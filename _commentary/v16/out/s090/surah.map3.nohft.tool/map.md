## Chains

All Quran passages outside this surah come from my own knowledge of the Quran. I checked their wording against the reference text before quoting. The one member that comes from tafsir memory and not from the dictionary is marked [memory].

### The two raised roads and the steep pass

Two raised roads are shown to the man. The road itself leads, and a guide walks ahead of him. One road climbs to a steep mountain pass, which is the road's hard stretch. Under it lies the drop between two mountains. A person can throw himself up and through the pass, or stand undecided with a hand on his chest, lag behind, or turn back on his heels. The pass is named from the same root as what comes at the end of a road: the outcome, the reward or the punishment. The surah names that outcome in 90:18–19. The dictionary describes the plunge as falling "from high to low" or entering "without guidance". In this surah the guidance has already been given (90:10), so the failure in 90:11 is a refusal to plunge, not a lack of a guide.

- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B001 — «النجد ما ارتفع من الأرض» (sihah); «النون والجيم والدال أصل واحد يدل على اعتلاء وقوة وإشراف» (maqayis) — two raised tracts, seen from below as heights to climb.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B002 — «وأمر نجد واضح وطريق نجد هاد» (ayn) — the road is plain and itself guides. The phrase joins نجد with هاد (90:10 هَدَيْنَٰهُ).
- 90:10 وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ — ه د ي B001 — «هديته الطريق والبيت هداية أي عرفته» (sihah); «تعريف الطرق» (mufradat) — showing the road and making it known.
- 90:10 وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ — ه د ي B003 — «الدليل يسمى هاديا لتقدمه» (ayn); «الهادية العصا لأنها تتقدم ممسكها» (maqayis) — the guide who goes first; the staff that goes ahead of the walker.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B004 [fixed expression] — «رجل نجد في الحاجة إذا كان ناجيا فيها أي سريعا» (sihah) — the quick, successful goer.
- 90:11 ٱقْتَحَمَ — ق ح م B001 — «انقحم الرجل انقحاما واقتحم اقتحاما إذا هوى من علو إلى سفل أو دخل في شيء من غير هداية» (jamhara); «قحم في الأمور قحوما رمى بنفسه فيها من غير دربة» (maqayis) — throwing oneself in. The jamhara phrase sets the plunge against هداية.
- 90:11 ٱقْتَحَمَ — ق ح م B002 — «قحم الطريق مصاعبه» (maqayis;sihah); «القحمة الأمر العظيم لا يركبها كل أحد» (ayn); «سميت المهالك قحما» (jamhara) — the road's hard stretches. Not "every أحد" takes them on, and they are called مهالك, which ties them to أَهْلَكْتُ.
- 90:11, 90:12 ٱلْعَقَبَةَ / ٱلْعَقَبَةُ — ع ق ب B012 — «العقبة الجبل الطويل يعرض للطريق وهو صعب شديد» (tahdhib); «العقبة طريق وعر في الجبل» (mufradat); «الأصل الآخر يدل على ارتفاع وشدة وصعوبة والعقبة طريق في الجبل» (maqayis) — the mountain set across the road; the rough way up.
- 90:11 ٱلْعَقَبَةَ — ع ق ب B002 — «العقب مؤخر القدم» (ayn) — the heel that pushes upward.
- 90:11 ٱلْعَقَبَةَ — ع ق ب B003 [fixed expression] — «رجع على عقبه وانقلب على عقبيه» (mufradat) — the reverse movement: turning back on the heels when one should climb.
- 90:11 ٱلْعَقَبَةَ — ع ق ب B006 — «عاقبة كل شيء آخره والعقبى جزاء الأمر» (sihah) — what lies at the end of the road, which 90:18–19 name.
- 90:6 أَهْلَكْتُ — ه ل ك B006 — «الهلك المهوى بين الجبلين» (maqayis;tahdhib); «مفازة هالكة من سلكها أي هالكة السالكين» (ayn;tahdhib) — the chasm between two mountains and the waste that kills those who cross it. The man's own boasting verb names the drop under the pass.
- 90:19 كَفَرُوا۟ — ك ف ر B013 — «الكفر الثنايا من الجبال» (ayn) — mountain passes and tracks over the ridge.
- 90:1 ٱلْبَلَدِ — ب ل د B005 — «تبلد أي تردد متحيرا» (sihah); «تبلد الرجل إذا وضع يده على صدره عند تحيره في الأمر» (maqayis) — the opposite of the plunge: standing at the foot of the climb, unsure, hand on chest.
- 90:1 ٱلْبَلَدِ — ب ل د B007 — «فرس بليد إذا تأخر عن الخيل السوابق» (tahdhib); «البلادة نقيض النفاذ والمضاء في الأمور» (tahdhib) — the horse that falls behind; the opposite of going through.

### Hardship borne in the middle

The human is set in the middle of hardship. To be "in kabad" is to be in the middle of a thing, and اقتحام is defined as "standing in the middle of a fearful hardship". The two words name the same position. The climber sweats from effort and distress and needs strength. On a famine day hunger comes with exhaustion. A road can wear out whoever travels it. The believers' answer is صبر: holding the self back from panic. Elsewhere the Quran turns that word around into a daring toward the fire.

- 90:4 كَبَدٍ — ك ب د B002 — «الكبد وهي المشقة» (maqayis;mufradat); «كابدت الأمر قاسيته في مشقة» (maqayis); «الكبداء الرحا التي تدار باليد سميت كبداء لما في إدارتها من المشقة» (tahdhib) — hardship endured; a hand-turned millstone as its picture.
- 90:4 كَبَدٍ — ك ب د B003 [fixed expression] — «كل شيء توسط شيئا فقد تكبده» (jamhara); «تكبد الفلاة إذا قصد وسطها ومعظمها» (tahdhib) — heading into the middle of the waste.
- 90:11 ٱقْتَحَمَ — ق ح م B001 — «الاقتحام توسط شدة مخيفة» (mufradat) — the same middle (توسط) as تكبّد: the plunge enters where the human already stands.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B005 — «نجد الرجل فهو منجود إذا كرب من حر أو غم أو ضيق أو وجع» (jamhara) — distress from heat, grief, narrowness or pain.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B006 — «نجد الرجل بالكسر ينجد نجدا أي عرق من عمل أو كرب» (sihah) — the sweat of labour on the climb.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B003 — «رجل نجد بين النجدة إذا كان جلدا قويا» (jamhara) — the toughness the climb asks for.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B010 — «منجد وهو الذي قد جرب الأمور وقاساها» (tahdhib) — the one hardened by having suffered through things.
- 90:14 مَسْغَبَةٍۢ — س غ ب B001 — «سغب الرجل إذا جاع ولا يكون السغب إلا الجوع مع التعب» (jamhara) — hunger with exhaustion: the day of the climb is also a day of fatigue.
- 90:6 أَهْلَكْتُ — ه ل ك B009 [fixed expression] — «استهلك الرجل في كذا وكذا إذا جهد نفسه واهتلك مثله» (tahdhib); «طريق مستهلك الورد أي يجهد من سلكه» (tahdhib) — wearing oneself out for something; a road that exhausts its travellers.
- 90:17 بِٱلصَّبْرِ — ص ب ر B001 — «الصبر حبس النفس عن الجزع» (sihah); «صبرت نفسي أي حبستها» (maqayis;tahdhib) — holding oneself still inside the hardship.
- 90:17 بِٱلصَّبْرِ — ص ب ر B006 — «أم صبور أمر لا منفذ له عنه» (tahdhib) — the affair with no way out: hardship that can only be carried through.
- 90:17 بِٱلصَّبْرِ — ص ب ر B013 — «الصبر الجرأة ومنه فما أصبرهم على النار» (tahdhib) — the reversal: "endurance" as daring toward the fire of 90:20.

### Clinging to the ground: matted wealth, the dust-bound poor

Three of the surah's words share one bodily picture, sticking to the ground (لصق بالأرض): لُّبَد, بَلَد and مَتْرَبَة. The man's wealth is لبد, layers pressed on each other like felt. The same root names whoever stays put and never travels, and a bird crouched flat on the earth. The town (بلد) is a place one stays in, and it is also the grave and the dust. The poor man is "ذو متربة", stuck to the dust. The reversal sits inside ترب itself: أترب means to become rich, as if one had wealth as much as dust. Against all this flatness stand the raised roads and the pass.

- 90:6 لُّبَدًا — ل ب د B001 — «أصل يدل على تكرس الشيء بعضه فوق بعض» (maqayis); «تلبدت الأرض بالمطر ولبد الندى الأرض» (sihah;tahdhib) — layers pressed into a mat; ground packed down by rain.
- 90:6 لُّبَدًا — ل ب د B002 — «يقول أهلكت مالا لبدا أي جما» (sihah); «مالا لبدا أي كثيرا متلبدا» (mufradat); «اللبد الكثير ومال لبد كثير وقد لبد بعضه ببعض» (tahdhib) — wealth massed and matted together. The dictionary quotes the surah's own phrase.
- 90:6 لُّبَدًا — ل ب د B003 — «لبد الشيء بالأرض لبودا تلبد بها أي لصق» (sihah); «اللبد أيضا الذي لا يسافر ولا يبرح» (sihah); «تلبد الطائر بالأرض أي جثم عليها» (sihah) — stuck to the earth; the one who never sets out.
- 90:6 لُّبَدًا — ل ب د B007 [fixed expression] — «ما له سبد ولا لبد ومعناه ما له قليل ولا كثير» (tahdhib) — the reversal: having nothing at all.
- 90:1 ٱلْبَلَدِ — ب ل د B010 — «بلد تبليدا ضرب بنفسه الأرض وأبلد لصق بالأرض» (sihah); «بلد الرجل بالأرض إذا لزق بها» (maqayis) — throwing oneself to the ground and sticking there.
- 90:1 ٱلْبَلَدِ — ب ل د B009 — «بلد بالمكان أقام به فهو بالد» (sihah) — staying in a place.
- 90:1 ٱلْبَلَدِ — ب ل د B002 — «الأصل الصدر ويقال وضعت الناقة بلدتها بالأرض إذا بركت» (maqayis) — the kneeling camel's chest set on the ground.
- 90:1 ٱلْبَلَدِ — ب ل د B001 — «البلد المقبرة ويقال هو نفس القبر وربما جاء البلد يعني به التراب» (tahdhib) — the graveyard, the grave, the dust. The phrase joins بلد with تراب (90:16).
- 90:16 مَتْرَبَةٍۢ — ت ر ب B002 — «ترب الرجل إذا افتقر كأنه لصق بالتراب» (maqayis;sihah;mufradat); «مسكين ذو متربة أي لاصق بالتراب» (sihah;mufradat) — the poor man pressed into the dust. The dictionary quotes the surah's phrase.
- 90:16 مَتْرَبَةٍۢ — ت ر ب B003 — «أترب إذا استغنى كأنه صار له من المال بقدر التراب» — the reversal: wealth as much as dust.
- 90:16 مَتْرَبَةٍۢ — ت ر ب B001 — «تربة الميت رمسه» (jamhara) — the dust that covers the dead.
- 90:16 مِسْكِينًۭا — س ك ن B001 — «السكون ذهاب الحركة» — the one who has stopped moving.

Outside the surah:
- 7:175–176 وَلَوْ شِئْنَا لَرَفَعْنَٰهُ بِهَا وَلَٰكِنَّهُۥٓ أَخْلَدَ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ — God about the man given "Our signs" who cast them off (scene opens 7:175). He could have been raised, but he clung to the earth. The passage ends ذَّٰلِكَ مَثَلُ ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا.

### Spent wealth: the boast, the display, the reckoning

The man talks: "I have destroyed heaps of wealth." His speech wants the spending seen and praised. The root of يره names doing a thing so that people see it (رئاء). The root of يحسب names both his merits as they are counted and the opposite way of counting, which is entering a loss in God's account. The tongue and lips God gave him (90:9) are also the dictionary's words for public praise. The lips also name the man drained dry by people asking him, which is what honest spending would look like. The same root of يحسب gives the fire from the sky that burns the rich man's garden.

- 90:6 يَقُولُ — ق و ل B001 — «القول من النطق» (maqayis) — the boast spoken aloud.
- 90:6 يَقُولُ — ق و ل B007 — «انتشرت له قالة حسنة أو قبيحة في الناس» (ayn) — talk spreading among people, the reputation the boast aims at.
- 90:6 أَهْلَكْتُ — ه ل ك B001 — «الهلاك على أوجه افتقاد الشيء واستحالة وفساد والموت وبطلان الشيء وعدمه» (mufradat) — wealth lost, spoiled, brought to nothing.
- 90:6 مَالًۭا — م و ل B001 — «تمول الرجل اتخذ مالا» (maqayis); «كانت أموال العرب أنعامهم» (ayn) — wealth acquired and kept, chiefly herds.
- 90:6 لُّبَدًا — ل ب د B002 — «مالا لبدا أي كثيرا متلبدا» (mufradat) — the amount he claims.
- 90:7 يَرَهُۥٓ — ر ء ي B005 — «وراءى فلان يرائي وفعل ذلك رئاء الناس وهو أن يفعل شيئا ليراه الناس» (maqayis) — doing a thing so that people see it. He wants people to see the spending, yet supposes no one sees him.
- 90:5, 90:7 أَيَحْسَبُ — ح س ب B004 — «الحسب الذي يعد من الإنسان»; «ما يعده الإنسان من مفاخر آبائه» — the merits a man counts up to boast of.
- 90:5, 90:7 أَيَحْسَبُ — ح س ب B005 — «احتسبت بكذا أجرا عند الله» — the opposite count: entering what was spent or lost into God's account.
- 90:5, 90:7 أَيَحْسَبُ — ح س ب B002 — «الحسبان الظن» — supposing in place of counting.
- 90:5, 90:7 أَيَحْسَبُ — ح س ب B007 — «حسبانا من السماء أي نارا تحرقها» — fire sent from the sky onto a garden.
- 90:9 وَلِسَانًۭا — ل س ن B006 — «لسان الناس عليك ثناؤهم» (tahdhib) — the tongue as people's praise of a man.
- 90:9 وَشَفَتَيْنِ — ش ف ه B004 [fixed expression] — «له في الناس شفة أي ثناء حسن» (sihah) — the lip as good repute.
- 90:9 وَشَفَتَيْنِ — ش ف ه B003 — «رجل مشفوه إذا كثر سؤال الناس إياه حتى نفذ ما عنده» (sihah) — a man whose stores run out because so many ask him: wealth really used up by giving.

### Measuring out: creation, power, lot

Before خلق is creating, it is measuring: marking a hide before it is cut. Its derived word خلاق is the lot measured out "for every أحد". يقدر is power, measure, and, in a fixed expression, measuring someone's provision short. أقسم belongs to a root of dividing into lots and of weighing one's affair. The dictionary defines it with يقدره. So 90:1, 90:4 and 90:5 meet in one operation: measuring and apportioning. The man supposes that no أحد can measure against him. The One who measured him out is the أحد.

### The hidden watcher and the eyes

The man supposes no one has seen him. The dictionary turns "no one" into "no eye": ما بها عين means "nobody is there". Watchers are everywhere in these roots. رقبة names watching and waiting, the guard, the lookout on a summit, and the hunter's blind. أدراك names the hunter who stalks prey he has not yet seen. عين names the spy and the eye that keeps someone in its care. The human (إنسان) is the small image of a person seen inside the pupil of an eye. The signs (آيات) are marks seen from afar, a man's figure on the horizon. The one who thought himself unseen is himself the figure in the eye.

- 90:7 يَرَهُۥٓ — ر ء ي B001 — «الرؤية إدراك المرئي بالحاسة» (mufradat) — seeing with the sense.

- 90:8 عَيْنَيْنِ — ع ي ن B017 — «ما بها عين متحركة الياء تريد أحدا له عين» (maqayis); «ما بها عائن، وكذلك ما بها عين، أي أحد» (sihah) — the dictionary equates عين with أحد: "no eye there" means "no one there". This joins 90:7 and 90:8.

### The made body: from liver to lips

The surah names the body piece by piece. The liver lies at the dark centre inside. Then come two eyes, a tongue, two lips, and a neck. The dictionary adds the jaw (فكّ, at 90:13), the hollow of the chest (بلدة, under البلد), the necklace bones of the upper chest (ترائب, under متربة), the lung (رئة, under يرى), and the right hand (يمين, at 90:18). One fixed expression reads "in kabad" as "upright and balanced", and the neck is named for standing upright. The body was made upright and whole, equipped with instruments to see and speak.

### The mouth: what goes out and what goes in

The mouth works two ways in this surah. Out of it comes the boast (يقول), through tongue and lips. Into it goes food (إطعام). The dictionary joins the two. "Feeding" can mean asking someone to talk (استطعمني الحديث). The tongue is called المقول, "the sayer". Face-to-face speech goes "from your mouth to his". The jaw (فك) opens to let things in: in a fixed expression, فككت الصبي means putting medicine into a child's mouth. Lips name the many mouths that ask. The reversal is the throat seized and squeezed, and the man pressing in at the tables.

- 90:6 يَقُولُ — ق و ل B002 — «المقول اللسان» (maqayis;ayn;sihah) — the dictionary names the tongue after saying, joining يقول (90:6) with لسان (90:9).

- 90:9 وَشَفَتَيْنِ — ش ف ه B002 — «المشافهة المخاطبة من فيك إلى فيه» (sihah) — speech passing from mouth to mouth.
- 90:9 وَشَفَتَيْنِ — ش ف ه B003 — «ماء مشفوة أي مطلوب مسؤول وهو الذي كثر عليه الناس وأنفدوه إلا أقله» (ayn) — water drunk almost dry by many mouths.

### The neck: rope and release

A neck has a rope thrown around it. A pledge is held locked. A gazelle is caught in the snare. To فكّ a رقبة is to open the jaw-like grip on it and let the person go, as the snared gazelle slips free. The dictionary joins the two words of 90:13 many times over. The neck is also the هادية, the part that leads, so an animal is led by the neck. A captive is called هدي, and the pass-root names the ransom taken for a captive. In the slave sense, ولد names the house-born slave. صبر names the one bound and held for death, and also the believer who holds back his own self in place of another's neck.

- 90:13 فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ — ف ك ك B002 — «فك الرهن وافتكه وفك الرقبة أي أعتقها» (sihah); «فك الرقبة تخليصها من إسار الرق وفك الرهن وفكاكه تخليصه من غلق الرهن» (tahdhib); «فككت رقبة فلان أعتقته» (ayn) — the dictionary's own phrase for the surah's pair: freeing a neck from the cords of bondage.
- 90:13 فَكُّ — ف ك ك B002 — «أفك الظبي من الحبالة إذا وقع فيه ثم انفلت» (tahdhib) — the snared gazelle getting free.

- 90:13 رَقَبَةٍ — ر ق ب B004 — «الرقبة اسم للعضو المعروف ثم يعبر بها عن الجملة واسما للمماليك» (mufradat) — the neck standing for the whole person, and for the owned.

### Lock and seal: opened, shut, covered

In the dictionary فكّ is "opening from being locked" (من الانغلاق), and أوصد is "locking it" (أغلقته). The two verbs face each other across the surah: the neck released in 90:13 and the fire shut over them in 90:20. The mufradat adds أطبقته وأحكمته: closed over like a lid and made fast. The root also names the threshold or forecourt (وصيد), and a stone pen built in the mountains to hold livestock (مال). كفروا belongs to the act of covering: night covers, a farmer covers seed with soil, a sheath encloses a palm blossom. Untying (حلّ) and stoppering a flask (صبر) join the same set of operations. The echo root ء ص د, which the dictionary withholds as not identical, records «نار مؤصدة أي مطبقة» (ayn).

- 90:13 فَكُّ — ف ك ك B001 — «أصل صحيح يدل على تفتح وانفراج» (maqayis); «فككت الشيء فانفك ككتاب مختوم تفك خاتمه» (ayn;tahdhib) — opening up; breaking the seal of a letter.
- 90:13 فَكُّ — ف ك ك B002 — «فكاك الرهن وهو فتحه من الانغلاق» (maqayis) — opening a pledge out of its locked state.
- 90:20 مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ — و ص د B001 — «أوصدت الباب أغلقته والموصد المطبق» (maqayis); «أوصدت الباب وآصدته أي أطبقته وأحكمته ومؤصدة مطبقة» (mufradat); «أصل يدل على ضم شيء إلى شيء» (maqayis) — the door locked and closed over like a lid.
- 90:20 مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ — و ص د B002 — «الوصيد فناء البيت والوصيد الباب» (ayn) — the forecourt and the door.
- 90:20 مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ — و ص د B003 — «الوصيدة كالحظيرة تتخذ للمال إلا أنها من الحجارة» (sihah); «الوصيدة حجرة تجعل للمال في الجبل» (mufradat) — a stone pen in the mountain built for مال, which is 90:6's word.
- 90:2 حِلٌّۢ — ح ل ل B001 [fixed expression] — «أصل الحل حل العقدة» (mufradat) — untying.
- 90:19 كَفَرُوا۟ — ك ف ر B001 — «كل شيء غطى شيئا فقد كفره» (ayn;sihah;tahdhib); «الكفر ضد الإيمان سمى لأنه تغطية الحق» (maqayis) — covering; denial as covering the truth.
- 90:19 كَفَرُوا۟ — ك ف ر B002 — «الليل كافر لأنه ستر بظلمته» (tahdhib) — night covering everything.
- 90:19 كَفَرُوا۟ — ك ف ر B008 — «يقال للزارع كافر لأنه يغطى الحب بتراب الأرض» (maqayis) — the farmer covering seed with the soil (تراب, 90:16).

### The famine day and the feeding

The day is not an ordinary day. It is "a day possessed of hunger", and the dictionary already uses يوم to mean an event that befalls people, "a day with days in it". In a famine year the nomads are plunged (تقحم) into settled lands (بلاد). The year itself is called هلك, and the land is barren. The poor who go from door to door looking for kindness are called هلاك, from the root of the man's boast. Food is سكن, what lets a person stay where he is, so the miskin is someone without it. The orphan is named for kindness that is slow to reach him or that forgets him. The dictionary's own example joins the orphan to hunger: يتيم ذو مسغبة. The answer to all this is إطعام, which "includes even water".

- 90:14 إِطْعَٰمٌۭ — ط ع م B002 — «استطعمه سأله أن يطعمه وأطعمته الطعام» (sihah) — asked for food, food given.
- 90:14 إِطْعَٰمٌۭ — ط ع م B001 — «أصل في تذوق الشيء والطعام هو المأكول والإطعام يقع حتى الماء» (maqayis) — feeding stretches to giving water.
- 90:14 يَوْمٍۢ — ي و م B003 — «اليوم: الكون، الكائنة من الكون إذا نزلت أو حدثت» (ayn;tahdhib); «اليوم الشديد: يوم ذو أيام» (ayn;tahdhib) — the day as a calamity that falls. The "يوم ذو …" pattern matches يوم ذي مسغبة.
- 90:14 مَسْغَبَةٍۢ — س غ ب B001 — «أصل واحد يدل على الجوع والمسغبة المجاعة» (maqayis); «يتيم ذو مسغبة أي ذو مجاعة» (sihah) — famine. The sihah example joins مسغبة to يتيم (90:15).

### Begetting and the line

The surah swears by a begetter and what he begot. In the dictionary the line passes through a womb, through a female near term (مقرب, the same form as مقربة), through loosening before birth and a pregnancy that becomes visible. It goes on into descendants (عقب) and a son grown old enough to be his father's companion (أصحب). The line can break: the orphan has lost the father, the رقوب is a parent whose children do not live. Peers born together are called both لدة (from ولد) and ترب. So the poor man ذا متربة can be heard beside the orphan ذا مقربة: one is near in kinship, the other born alongside, a peer. The surah ends with مرحمة, whose root is the womb.

- 90:3 وَلَدَ — و ل د B001 — «أصل صحيح وهو دليل النجل والنسل» (maqayis) — offspring and lineage.

### Nearness and connection: kin, companions, mutual counsel

The surah's last movement draws people together. The orphan is near in kin (مقربة). The believers "join" one another in counsel: the root of تواصوا is linking one thing to another, like plants growing into one unbroken carpet, and its counsel is "speech that is joined" (يوصل). Mercy comes from the womb that kinsfolk share. أصحاب is defined by nearness and closeness (مقارنة … ومقاربة). The reversal is the orphan as "anything isolated", a lone sand-ridge. The human (إنس) is defined by ease with others and freedom from estrangement.

- 90:15 مَقْرَبَةٍ — ق ر ب B001 — «أصل صحيح يدل على خلاف البعد» (maqayis) — nearness against distance.
- 90:17 بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ — ر ح م B002 — «الرحم القرابة تجمع بني أب» (tahdhib) — the tie that gathers one father's children.

- 90:17 وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ — و ص ي B003 — «تواصى القوم أي أوصى بعضهم بعضا» (sihah); «تواصى القوم إذا تواصلوا» (jamhara) — counselling one another; holding together.

### The oath: sworn, released, expiated

The surah opens by swearing (أقسم). Its root is the قسامة, oaths shared out among the kin of a slain man, and the dictionary defines يمين by it. The next ayah has حلّ, whose fixed expressions include تحلة القسم: what releases an oath, "what unties the knot of oaths". The right hand (يمين) of 90:18 is the oath's own word, borrowed from the hand. The root of كفروا names the expiation for a broken oath. The Quran's expiation for oaths is feeding the poor or freeing a neck, which are the pass of 90:13–14.

- 90:1 أُقْسِمُ — ق س م B004 — «أقسمت حلفت وأصله من القسامة» (sihah); «اليمين فالقسم وأصل ذلك من القسامة وهي الأيمان تقسم على أولياء المقتول» (maqayis) — the oath, from oaths shared out in a blood claim. The maqayis phrase joins قسم with يمين (90:18).
- 90:2 حِلٌّۢ — ح ل ل B005 [fixed expression] — «حللت اليمين تحليلا وتحلة وتحلة القسم» (maqayis); «تحلة أيمانكم أي ما تنحل به عقدة أيمانكم» (mufradat) — releasing the oath; one phrase holds حلّ, اليمين and القسم.
- 90:18 ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ — ي م ن B003 — «اليمين في الحلف مستعار من اليد» (mufradat); «اليمين القسم» (sihah) — the oath named from the right hand.
- 90:19 كَفَرُوا۟ — ك ف ر B009 — «تكفير اليمين فعل ما يجب بالحنث فيها» (sihah) — doing what a broken oath requires.
- 90:13 فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ — ف ك ك B002 — «فك الرقبة أي أعتقها» (sihah) — freeing a neck, one of the expiations.
- 90:14 إِطْعَٰمٌۭ — ط ع م B002 — «استطعمه سأله أن يطعمه وأطعمته الطعام» (sihah) — feeding, the other expiation.

Outside the surah:
- 5:89 وَلَٰكِن يُؤَاخِذُكُم بِمَا عَقَّدتُّمُ ٱلْأَيْمَٰنَ ۖ فَكَفَّٰرَتُهُۥٓ إِطْعَامُ عَشَرَةِ مَسَٰكِينَ … أَوْ تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍۢ … فَصِيَامُ ثَلَٰثَةِ أَيَّامٍۢ — God's ruling on oaths. In one verse: knotted oaths, expiation, feeding the poor, freeing a neck, days.

- 58:3–4 فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍۢ … فَإِطْعَامُ سِتِّينَ مِسْكِينًۭا — the same pair for a man who has said a forbidden word.

### Right and left: two companies on two sides

The two roads end in two sides. ميمنة is the right side, the right hand, and blessing. مشأمة is the left side and ill omen. The dictionary defines each against the other. أصحاب are those who keep to a side. The two directions have places on the ground. Facing the qibla, Yemen lies to the right and Sham to the left, so the town of 90:1 sits between the two regions named by these words. The trade journeys of Quraysh went to Yemen in winter and to Sham in summer [memory: tafsir on 106:2]. The root of أقسم also names separating into lots, and the surah closes by separating people. A man laid on his right side in the grave is said to تيمن.

- 90:18 ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ — ي م ن B002 — «اليمنة خلاف اليسرة والأيمن والميمنة خلاف الأيسر والميسرة» (sihah) — the right side against the left.
- 90:18 ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ — ي م ن B001 — «اليمن البركة وتيمنت به تبركت» (sihah); «أصحاب اليمين أصحاب السعادات والميامن» (mufradat) — blessing and good fortune.
- 90:18 ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ — ي م ن B004 — «اليمين القوة» (maqayis) — the right hand as strength.
- 90:19 ٱلْمَشْـَٔمَةِ — ش ء م B001 — «المشأمة وهي خلاف الميمنة» (maqayis) — the dictionary sets the surah's two words against each other.
- 90:19 ٱلْمَشْـَٔمَةِ — ش ء م B003 — «الشؤم نقيض اليمن» (sihah) — ill omen against blessing.
- 90:18 ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ — ي م ن B005 — «اليمن ما كان على يمين القبلة» (ayn) — Yemen, on the right of the qibla.
- 90:19 ٱلْمَشْـَٔمَةِ — ش ء م B002 — «الشأم أرض عن مشأمة القبلة» (maqayis) — Sham, on the left of the qibla.
- 90:18 ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ — ي م ن B007 — «التيمن الموت يقال تيمن فلان تيمنا إذا مات والأصل فيه أنه يوسد يمينه إذا مات في قبره» (tahdhib) — laid on the right side in the grave.
- 90:18, 90:19 أَصْحَٰبُ — ص ح ب B001 — «الصاحب الملازم» (mufradat) — those who keep to their side.
- 90:1 أُقْسِمُ — ق س م B003 — «التقسيم التفريق» (sihah) — separating into lots.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B001 — «النجد ما ارتفع من الأرض» (sihah) — the two roads that lead to the two sides.

### The town and the one alighting in it

The town (بلد) is a bounded place shaped by the people who settle in it. حلّ is alighting and staying in it. It also names being outside the sacred limit, or being someone whose life is not protected. It names an offering reaching the place where it is slaughtered, and punishment falling due and coming down. The dictionary ties حلّ to هدي through "the offering reaching its place" (محل الهدي). هدي also names the offering brought to the sanctuary and the man who comes to a tribe asking protection. مقربة's root names the offering that brings one near to God. The root of آمنوا names a safe dwelling. The root of أقسم names a truce between enemies.

- 90:1, 90:2 ٱلْبَلَدِ — ب ل د B001 — «البلد المكان المحيط المحدود المتأثر باجتماع قطانه وإقامتهم فيه» (mufradat) — the bounded place marked by its settled people.
- 90:2 حِلٌّۢ — ح ل ل B002 — «حل بالمكان حلا وحلولا ومحلا» (sihah); «المحل نقيض المرتحل والمحلة منزل القوم» (ayn;tahdhib) — alighting and staying.
- 90:2 حِلٌّۢ — ح ل ل B003 — «الحل ما جاوز الحرم» (sihah); «الحل الرجل الحلال والمحل الذي لا عهد له ولا حرمة» (tahdhib) — outside the sanctuary; the unprotected person.
- 90:2 حِلٌّۢ — ح ل ل B004 — «حل العذاب أي وجب ويحل أي نزل» (sihah); «من يحل فمعناه يجب ومن قرأ فيحل فمعناه فينزل ومحل الهدي» (tahdhib) — punishment falling due and descending; the offering's place. The tahdhib phrase joins حلّ with هدي (90:10).
- 90:10 وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ — ه د ي B005 — «الهدي ما يهدى إلى الحرم من النعم»; «حتى يبلغ الهدى محله» (sihah) — the animal brought to the sanctuary until it reaches its حلّ.
- 90:10 وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ — ه د ي B007 — «الهدي الرجل ذو الحرمة وهو أن يأتي القوم يستجيرهم أو يأخذ منهم عهدا» (tahdhib) — the man under protection, set against the unprotected حلّ.
- 90:15 مَقْرَبَةٍ — ق ر ب B005 — «القربان ما قرب إلى الله تعالى من نسيكة أو غيرها» (maqayis) — the offering that brings one near.
- 90:17 ءَامَنُوا۟ — ء م ن B001 — «مأمنه منزله الذي فيه أمنه» (mufradat) — the dwelling where one is safe.
- 90:1 أُقْسِمُ — ق س م B008 — «القسامة: الهدنة بين العدو وبين المسلمين» (tahdhib) — the truce.
- 90:16 مِسْكِينًۭا — س ك ن B002 — «يسكنون الدار» — dwelling in a house.

- 27:91 رَبَّ هَٰذِهِ ٱلْبَلْدَةِ ٱلَّذِى حَرَّمَهَا — the Prophet's words: the Lord who made this town sacred.

### Fire seen from afar: beacon and enclosure

The fire comes last, but its root makes it a light first. Fires used to be lit on high ground "so that people would be guided" by them, and the منار marks the road. A road that is najd is a road that guides. The root of إنسان gives "he caught sight of a fire" (آنس نارا), and fire is a سكن, something one rests beside. Night is كافر because it covers everything. The surah's last fire is no beacon on the road but a fire shut over its people.

- 90:20 نَارٌۭ — ن و ر B001 — «النور والنار سميا بذلك من طريقة الإضاءة» (maqayis) — fire as light.
- 90:20 نَارٌۭ — ن و ر B005 — «كانوا ينورون في الجاهلية ليهتدى ويقتدى بها» (ayn); «المنار: علم الطريق»; «ضرب المنار على طريقه ليهتدى بها» (sihah) — fire lit as a road-marker for guidance. Both phrases join the fire's root with هدى (90:10).
- 90:20 نَارٌۭ — ن و ر B003 [fixed expression] — «تنورت النار من بعيد: تبصرتها» (sihah) — making out a fire from far off.
- 90:10 ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ — ن ج د B002 — «وأمر نجد واضح وطريق نجد هاد» (ayn) — the plain road that leads.
- 90:10 وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ — ه د ي B001 — «هديته الطريق والبيت هداية أي عرفته» (sihah) — shown the way.
- 90:4 ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ — ء ن س B002 — «آنس من جانب يعني أبصر نارا» (tahdhib); «فإن آنستم منهم رشدا أي أبصرتم وآنست نارا» (mufradat) — catching sight of a fire.
- 90:7 يَرَهُۥٓ — ر ء ي B001 — «الرؤية إدراك المرئي بالحاسة» (mufradat) — seeing.
- 90:16 مِسْكِينًۭا — س ك ن B004 — «السكن النار التي يسكن بها» — the fire one rests beside.
- 90:19 كَفَرُوا۟ — ك ف ر B002 — «الليل كافر لأنه ستر بظلمته» (tahdhib) — the covering dark in which a fire is seen.
- 90:20 مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ — و ص د B001 — «أوصدت الباب أغلقته والموصد المطبق» (maqayis) — the fire closed in, no longer seen from the road.
- 90:5 أَيَحْسَبُ — ح س ب B007 — «حسبانا من السماء أي نارا تحرقها» — fire falling from the sky.
- 90:17 بِٱلصَّبْرِ — ص ب ر B013 — «الصبر الجرأة ومنه فما أصبرهم على النار» (tahdhib) — daring toward the fire.

## Interactions

- Pass ↔ Fire as beacon: «وطريق نجد هاد» (ayn) and «كانوا ينورون … ليهتدى» (ayn) give the raised road and the fire on it the same work, guiding. 20:10 stages fire, seeing and هدى together.
- Pass ↔ Guidance given: «اقتحم … إذا … دخل في شيء من غير هداية» (jamhara) sets the plunge against هداية, which 90:10 has already given.
- Pass ↔ Hardship: «الاقتحام توسط شدة مخيفة» (mufradat) and «كل شيء توسط شيئا فقد تكبده» (jamhara): plunging and being "in kabad" are the same middle position.
- Pass ↔ Clinging to the ground: rising (نجد «اعتلاء», عقبة «ارتفاع») against ل ب د B003 and ب ل د B010 (لصق بالأرض). 7:176 stages being raised versus clinging to the earth.
- Pass ↔ Famine: «القحمة السنة تقحم الأعراب بلاد الريف» (maqayis) joins اقتحم and البلد. The famine year plunges the starving into the towns.
- Pass ↔ Spent wealth: «سميت المهالك قحما» (jamhara) joins قحم with the root of أَهْلَكْتُ. 92:11 has wealth useless "when he falls".
- Pass ↔ Neck: «أخذت من أسيري عقبة إذا أخذت منه بدلا» (sihah) — the pass root names the ransom of a captive. «هادية الشاة الرقبة» (tahdhib) joins هَدَيْنَٰهُ and رَقَبَةٍ.
- Pass ↔ Right and left: ع ق ب B006 (عاقبة, العقبى) and the two roads leading to two sides. 92:4–14 and 76:3–4 stage roads followed by outcomes.
- Neck ↔ Lock and seal: «فكاك الرهن وهو فتحه من الانغلاق» (maqayis) against «أوصدت الباب أغلقته» (maqayis): opening from locked in 90:13, locked in 90:20.
- Neck ↔ Right and left: 69:25–34 has the left-hand book, the shackled neck, and failing to urge feeding the poor.
- Neck ↔ Oath: «تحلة أيمانكم أي ما تنحل به عقدة أيمانكم» (mufradat) makes the oath a knot that حلّ unties. 5:89 has عَقَّدتُّمُ ٱلْأَيْمَٰنَ with تَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍۢ.
- Oath ↔ Famine and feeding: 5:89 and 58:3–4 make feeding the poor and freeing a neck the expiation, which is exactly 90:13–14.
- Oath ↔ Right and left: «اليمين فالقسم» (maqayis) joins أُقْسِمُ (90:1) with ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ (90:18).
- Mouth ↔ Feeding: «استطعمني فلان الحديث» (maqayis) and «المقول اللسان» make speaking and eating one mouth's two traffics. 26:78–79 and 76:9 stage feeding with speech.
- Mouth ↔ Lock and seal: فكّ is both jaw-opening (B001, B009) and unsealing (ككتاب مختوم). 36:65 seals the mouth.
- Watcher ↔ Made body: «إنسان العين المثال الذي يرى في السواد» (sihah) joins إنسان, عين and يرى. «ما بها عين … أي أحد» (sihah) joins عين and أحد.
- Watcher ↔ Spent wealth: ر ء ي B005 رئاء. He spends to be seen yet thinks himself unseen. 2:264 stages both.
- Watcher ↔ Lock and seal: 2:7 and 45:23 lay a seal and a covering over the eyes, the cover of كفر on the seeing of 90:8.
- Measuring ↔ Spent wealth: ح س ب B001 counting against B002 supposing, and B005 counting with God. 104:2–3 جمع مالا وعدده يحسب.
- Measuring ↔ Pass and guidance: 87:2–3 ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ ۝ وَٱلَّذِى قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ ties 90:4, 90:5 and 90:10 into one sequence.
- Measuring ↔ Watcher: «الخلاق النصيب لأنه قد قدر لكل أحد نصيبه» (maqayis) and «ما بها عين … أي أحد» share أحد. The 'anyone' measured is the 'anyone' who might see.
- Clinging to the ground ↔ Spent wealth: ل ب د B002 heaped wealth and B003 stuck to the ground are one root. ت ر ب B002/B003 are poverty in the dust and wealth like dust.
- Clinging to the ground ↔ Lock and seal: «يقال للزارع كافر لأنه يغطى الحب بتراب الأرض» (maqayis) joins كفروا with the تراب of متربة.
- Begetting ↔ Nearness: مقربة is kin (B003) and also the female near delivery (B012). رحم is womb, kin and mercy. «اللدة مختصة بالترب» (mufradat) joins ولد and ترب.
- Begetting ↔ Neck: «الوليد الصبي والعبد» (sihah) — the begotten child and the bonded neck share a word.
- Famine ↔ Town: 106:3–4 has the House's Lord feeding the town from hunger and making it safe. Famine drives the nomads into the بلاد.
- Famine ↔ Begetting: «يتيم ذو مسغبة أي ذو مجاعة» (sihah) joins مسغبة and يتيم.
- Town ↔ Right and left: «اليمن ما كان على يمين القبلة» (ayn) and «الشأم أرض عن مشأمة القبلة» (maqayis) place the two sides on either hand of the sanctuary.
- Town ↔ Neck: حلّ is both untying (B001) and alighting (B002). هدي is the protected refugee and the captive (B007).
- Fire ↔ Hardship: «الصبر الجرأة ومنه فما أصبرهم على النار» (tahdhib). 2:175 turns the endurance of 90:17 into daring toward the fire of 90:20.
- Fire ↔ Lock and seal: مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ shuts the fire that the beacon sense had set on the open road. 104:6–8 stages the shut fire.
- Nearness ↔ Right and left: أصحاب (مقارنة … ومقاربة) names both the counsel-joined believers and the two final companies.

## Ayat

**90:1 لَآ أُقْسِمُ بِهَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدِ**
- Town: ٱلْبَلَدِ is the bounded, settled place. Scene: a bounded sacred town where someone alights. حلّ names him both as resident and as unprotected. The offering is brought there to reach its place, and the town is fed from hunger and made safe.
- Oath: أُقْسِمُ is the oath, from oaths shared out in a blood claim. Scene: an oath sworn, released by تحلة, named from the right hand, and expiated by feeding the poor or freeing a neck.
- Measuring: أُقْسِمُ is dividing into lots and weighing one's affair (يقدره). Scene: creation as measuring the hide; each person's lot measured. A man supposes no one has power over him or can measure his provision short. The One measured him.
- Right and left: أُقْسِمُ as التقسيم التفريق, and the town on whose right lies Yemen and on whose left lies Sham. Scene: two roads ending in two companies, right (blessing) and left (omen), with the sanctuary town between the regions named by those sides.
- Clinging to the ground: ٱلْبَلَدِ as sticking to the earth, staying, the kneeling camel's chest, and grave and dust. Scene: matted wealth and the dust-stuck poor, both flat on the ground, below the raised roads.
- Pass: ٱلْبَلَدِ as standing undecided with a hand on the chest, and as lagging behind. Scene: two raised, self-guiding roads; a steep pass over a chasm; a plunge, or hesitation, lagging, a turn on the heels.
- Famine: the بلاد into which famine plunges the nomads. Scene: a day that befalls, hunger with exhaustion, the orphan kindness reaches late, the poor man without the food that lets him stay, and feeding.
- Made body: ٱلْبَلَدِ as the chest hollow at the base of the throat. Scene: liver, eyes, tongue, lips, jaw, neck, chest and lung, made whole and upright.

**90:2 وَأَنتَ حِلٌّۢ بِهَٰذَا ٱلْبَلَدِ**
- Town: حِلٌّۢ as alighting and staying, as outside the sacred limit and unprotected, as the offering's place, and as punishment coming down. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Oath: حِلٌّۢ as تحلة القسم, what unties the knot of oaths. Placed straight after أُقْسِمُ. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Neck, and Lock and seal: حِلٌّۢ as untying the knot. Scenes: a neck in a rope or a pledge locked, opened and freed; a door and its threshold shut fast over a fire, with covering by night and soil.
- Nearness: حِلٌّۢ as the neighbour who lives alongside. Scene: kin drawn near, believers joined in counsel like a plant carpet, companions keeping close; against them the orphan standing alone.

**90:3 وَوَالِدٍۢ وَمَا وَلَدَ**
- Begetting: begetter, delivery, offspring, and the peer (لدة/ترب). Scene: a womb near term, loosening and visible pregnancy, delivery, the line of descendants. Its breaks are the orphan and the parent without surviving children. The surah closes on مرحمة, the womb's root.
- Neck: وَلَدَ as the house-born slave. Scene: as at 90:2.

**90:4 لَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ فِى كَبَدٍ**
- Hardship: كَبَدٍ as hardship endured (the hand-mill) and as the middle (تكبّد), the same middle as اقتحام. Scene: a human set in the middle of hardship, sweating and distressed, hungry and tired on a road that wears out its traveller. He holds himself back from panic, or else "endures" his way into the fire.
- Measuring: خَلَقْنَا as measuring the hide before cutting, and خلاق as each أحد's measured lot. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Made body: كَبَدٍ as the liver and as upright balance; خَلَقْنَا as a whole, even form. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Watcher: ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ as the little figure in the pupil, and as catching sight. Scene: a man who thinks no eye is on him, among the guard, the summit lookout, the hunter's blind and the stalker. He is himself the figure inside an eye.
- Fire: ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ as catching sight of a fire (آنس نارا). Scene: fire lit on the road to guide and seen from far off, fire to rest beside; at the end, fire shut in.
- Nearness: ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ as at ease, not estranged. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Begetting: خَلَقْنَا as the fully formed embryo. Scene: as at 90:3.

**90:5 أَيَحْسَبُ أَن لَّن يَقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ أَحَدٌۭ**
- Measuring: أَيَحْسَبُ is supposing against counting; يَقْدِرَ is power, measure, decree, and in a fixed expression measuring provision short; أَحَدٌۭ is "anyone at all" and, reversed, the One. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Spent wealth: أَيَحْسَبُ as counted merits, as counting with God, and as fire from the sky. Scene: a man boasting aloud of heaps of wealth destroyed, spending to be seen and praised by tongue and lips, supposing in place of counting; the wealth burnt, the stores that giving would truly drain.
- Fire: حسبان as fire sent from the sky. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Watcher: أَحَدٌۭ, which the dictionary equates with عين. Scene: as at 90:4.

**90:6 يَقُولُ أَهْلَكْتُ مَالًۭا لُّبَدًا**
- Spent wealth: يَقُولُ is the spoken boast and the talk spreading among people; أَهْلَكْتُ is loss; مَالًۭا is herds and wealth acquired; لُّبَدًا is massed. Scene: as at 90:5.
- Clinging to the ground: لُّبَدًا as layers pressed together, stuck to the ground, never travelling, and reversed as having nothing. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Mouth: يَقُولُ, with the tongue named المقول; أَهْلَكْتُ as the man pushing in at tables. Scene: speech going out (the boast, mouth-to-mouth talk) and food going in (tasting, asking to be fed, mouth into mouth). The jaw opens to receive; the reverse is the throat seized.
- Pass: أَهْلَكْتُ as the drop between two mountains and the desert that kills travellers. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Hardship: أَهْلَكْتُ as wearing oneself out, and the road that exhausts. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Famine: أَهْلَكْتُ as the hard year, barren land, and the destitute seeking kindness. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Lock and seal: مال, for which the stone pen (وصيدة) is built. Scene: as at 90:2.

**90:7 أَيَحْسَبُ أَن لَّمْ يَرَهُۥٓ أَحَدٌ**
- Watcher: يَرَهُۥٓ is seeing; أَحَدٌ is no one, and is "no eye". Scene: as at 90:4.
- Spent wealth: يَرَهُۥٓ as رئاء, doing a thing for people to see. Scene: as at 90:5.
- Begetting: أرأت, pregnancy made visible. Scene: as at 90:3.
- Made body: رئة, the lung. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Fire: seeing the fire. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Measuring: أَيَحْسَبُ, أَحَدٌ. Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:8 أَلَمْ نَجْعَل لَّهُۥ عَيْنَيْنِ**
- Watcher: عَيْنَيْنِ is the seeing eye, the protecting eye, the spy, and "no eye = no one". Scene: as at 90:4.
- Made body: the two eyes made. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Measuring: نَجْعَل glossed "جعل خلق؛ خلقنا". Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:9 وَلِسَانًۭا وَشَفَتَيْنِ**
- Made body: tongue and lips. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Mouth: the tongue is المقول, joining it to 90:6's يقول; the lips are mouth-to-mouth speech and water drunk dry by many mouths. Scene: as at 90:6.
- Spent wealth: tongue and lip as public praise; مشفوه as stores drained by askers. Scene: as at 90:5.

**90:10 وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ**
- Pass: ٱلنَّجْدَيْنِ are raised tracts and a plain road that guides; وَهَدَيْنَٰهُ is showing the road, the guide going first, and the staff going ahead. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Hardship: نجد as distress, sweat from labour, toughness, and being hardened. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Fire: «طريق نجد هاد» and the guiding beacon. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Neck: هادية = the neck; هدي = captive and protected man. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Town: هدي as the offering that reaches its محلّ, and the man asking protection. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Right and left: the two roads leading to the two sides. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Famine: نجد as distress. Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:11 فَلَا ٱقْتَحَمَ ٱلْعَقَبَةَ**
- Pass: ٱقْتَحَمَ is throwing oneself in, falling from high to low, entering without guidance; the hard stretches are مهالك. ٱلْعَقَبَةَ is the mountain set across the road, the heel, the turn back on the heels, and the outcome. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Hardship: ٱقْتَحَمَ as "standing in the middle of a fearful hardship". Scene: as at 90:4.
- Famine: the famine year that plunges nomads into the towns. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Neck: عقبة as the ransom for a captive. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Begetting: عقب as descendants. Scene: as at 90:3.

**90:12 وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلْعَقَبَةُ**
- Watcher: أَدْرَىٰكَ is being made to know, and the stalker eyeing prey not yet seen. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Pass: ٱلْعَقَبَةُ named again, the steep road and its outcome. Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:13 فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ**
- Neck: the dictionary's own pair, فك الرقبة أي أعتقها; the rope thrown round a neck; the snared gazelle getting free. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Lock and seal: فَكُّ as opening out of the locked state and breaking a seal. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Made body: فَكُّ as the jaw; رَقَبَةٍ as the back of the neck, upright. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Mouth: prising the jaws apart; فككت الصبي, putting something into a child's mouth. Scene: as at 90:6.
- Watcher: رَقَبَةٍ as watching, the guard, the summit lookout, the hunter's blind. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Begetting: متفككة, the body loosening near birth; رقوب, the parent without surviving children; رقيب, descendants. Scene: as at 90:3.
- Oath: freeing a neck as the expiation of an oath (5:89). Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:14 أَوْ إِطْعَٰمٌۭ فِى يَوْمٍۢ ذِى مَسْغَبَةٍۢ**
- Famine: إِطْعَٰمٌۭ is feeding, even with water; يَوْمٍۢ is a day that befalls; مَسْغَبَةٍۢ is famine and hunger with exhaustion. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Mouth: إِطْعَٰمٌۭ as tasting, asking to be fed, asking for talk, mouth into mouth, and the throat seized. Scene: as at 90:6.
- Hardship: مَسْغَبَةٍۢ as hunger with exhaustion. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Oath: feeding the poor as the expiation of an oath. Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:15 يَتِيمًۭا ذَا مَقْرَبَةٍ**
- Begetting: يَتِيمًۭا is the line cut at the father; مَقْرَبَةٍ is kin, and in its form the female near delivery. Scene: as at 90:3.
- Famine: the orphan whom kindness reaches late and forgets. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Nearness: مَقْرَبَةٍ against distance; يَتِيمًۭا as the isolated one. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Town: قربان, the offering that brings near. Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:16 أَوْ مِسْكِينًۭا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍۢ**
- Clinging to the ground: مَتْرَبَةٍۢ as stuck to the dust, reversed as wealth like dust, and the dust over the dead; مِسْكِينًۭا as having stopped moving. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Famine: مِسْكِينًۭا as poverty and lacking the food that lets one stay. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Begetting: ترب as peer, joined to لدة. Scene: as at 90:3.
- Made body: ترائب, the necklace bones of the upper chest. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Fire: السكن, the fire one rests beside. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Nearness: السكن, the loved one the heart rests in. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Town: dwelling in a house. Scene: as at 90:1.

**90:17 ثُمَّ كَانَ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ**
- Hardship: بِٱلصَّبْرِ is holding the self back from panic, the affair with no way out, and, reversed, daring toward the fire. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Nearness: وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ is linking, like an unbroken plant carpet, and counsel as joined-on speech; بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ is kinship from one womb; ءَامَنُوا۟ is a self at rest with fear gone. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Begetting: بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ, the womb. Scene: as at 90:3.
- Famine: بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ, tenderness that does good. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Neck: بِٱلصَّبْرِ as binding one's own self, and as the one bound for death. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Lock and seal: بِٱلصَّبْرِ as plugging a flask. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Town: ءَامَنُوا۟, the safe dwelling. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Fire: «فما أصبرهم على النار». Scene: as at 90:4.

**90:18 أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ**
- Right and left: ٱلْمَيْمَنَةِ is the right side, blessing, strength, Yemen on the right of the qibla, and the right side in the grave; أَصْحَٰبُ are those keeping close. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Oath: يمين as the oath named from the hand. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Nearness: أَصْحَٰبُ as nearness and protection. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Made body: the right hand. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Begetting: أصحب, the son grown into his father's companion. Scene: as at 90:3.

**90:19 وَٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا هُمْ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلْمَشْـَٔمَةِ**
- Right and left: ٱلْمَشْـَٔمَةِ is the left side "خلاف الميمنة", ill omen, and Sham on the left of the qibla. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Lock and seal: كَفَرُوا۟ as covering, the covering night, the farmer covering seed with soil, and the blossom's sheath. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Watcher: بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا as visible marks, a figure seen afar, aiming at someone by his figure. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Pass: كفر as mountain passes. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Oath: كفارة, the expiation of a broken oath. Scene: as at 90:1.
- Fire: the covering night. Scene: as at 90:4.

**90:20 عَلَيْهِمْ نَارٌۭ مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ**
- Fire: نَارٌۭ as light, as the guiding beacon, and as fire seen from far; مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ closes it in. Scene: as at 90:4.
- Lock and seal: مُّؤْصَدَةٌۢ is locked, closed over and made fast; it also names the threshold and the stone pen for wealth. Scene: as at 90:2.
- Hardship: the fire toward which the deniers "dare". Scene: as at 90:4.
- Neck: the shut fire as the place of shackled necks (69:30–32, 40:71, 76:4). Scene: as at 90:2.

## Not carried

- 1B Stone pen in the mountain: only الوصيدة was carried (into Lock and seal). Rock hollow, gravel and low wall have no foothold in the surah's wording.
- 1E Sacred limit and lawful exit: carried into The town; nothing left over.
- 2C Mountain core, ridge, Uhud: proper name and mountain middle make no scene the surah sets up.
- 2E Wilderness and disorientation: only the chasm and killing desert (ه ل ك B006) were carried. Circling bewilderment and remote villages were not.
- 2G Small-vessel passage: skiff, rudder and gear have no anchor in the surah.
- 3B Harmful gaze: the evil eye was not carried; the protective eye went into Watcher.
- 3C Mirror: not carried; only the pupil figure was used.
- 3E Banner: the راية/عقاب standard was not carried; the beacon went into Fire.
- 3F Dream, apparition, bodily indicator: no point of contact in the surah, apart from visible pregnancy, which went into Begetting.
- 3G Disclosure and target appraisal: "eye's appraisal" (ق ح م B007) is a fixed expression with no hold here.
- 4B Eloquence, verbal attack, tongue-cutting: not heard in 90:9's frame.
- 4C Message relay and shared language: only تواصوا was carried.
- 4E Fabrication and attribution: no support in the surah's wording.
- 4F Negotiation, ruling, definition: fixed technical expressions with no scene here.
- 5A–5F Question, pointing, time words, vocative, letters, proper names: grammatical functions and names with no image; ذ و و and ه ا ء have no entries in this dictionary.
- 6B Inward character, physique: only the upright, balanced form was carried (into Made body).
- 6C Aptitude and receptivity: no scene.
- 6D Transformation and commencement: too abstract; nothing concrete gained.
- 7C Newborn dependency: carried through the orphan in Begetting and Famine; nothing left over.
- 7E Old age, widowhood: only رقوب was carried.
- 8B Marriage and household: only حليل as neighbour was carried (into Nearness).
- 8C Confidant: only أنس was carried.
- 8D Cohort, siblings, present community: only the peer (ترب/لدة) was carried, into Begetting.
- 9B Wage and livelihood: only مشفوه was carried.
- 9D Food heap and crowding: only المتهالك at the tables was carried.
- 10A Security and trust: only the safe dwelling and the self at rest were carried.
- 10B Debt and term: only punishment falling due (حلّ B004) was carried.
- 10C Surety and payment hold: no scene in the surah.
- 10D Testament and executor: only وصي as joined-on speech was carried.
- 10E Survivorship gift, inheritance, ready cash: no anchor.
- 11D Correction and relapse: only the turn on the heels was carried (into The pass).
- 12B Ingratitude and disavowal: kept inside the covering sense of كفر; no separate chain.
- 12E Prayer response and "amen": no anchor.
- 12F Sacrifice and nearness: carried into The town.
- 13B Approximation and medium quality: no anchor.
- 13E Planning and opinion: only قسم as weighing one's affair and قدّر as scheming were carried.
- 13F Sufficiency: حسبك has no hold in 90:5–7's frame.
- 14B Sword and stick combat: no scene.
- 14C Feud and black livers: only the truce sense of قسامة was carried, into The town.
- 14D Retaliation and forced detention: only المصبورة was carried (into The neck).
- 14E Captive and asylum: carried into The neck and The town; nothing left over.
- 15D Heel and weak hock: the heel went into The pass; the hock was not carried.
- 15E Side, flank, fingertip: no anchor.
- 16A–16D Appearance, colour, scars, ornament: no image set up by the surah.
- 17A–17E Animal husbandry: only near-delivery, visible pregnancy and loosening before birth were carried (into Begetting).
- 18A Hunter's blind: carried into Watcher; nothing left over.
- 18B–18D Raid, raptor, tracking: not carried.
- 19A–19E Plants: only the farmer covering seed and the blossom's sheath were carried (into Lock and seal).
- 20A–20F Water and vessels: no anchor; feeding's "even water" is kept inside Famine.
- 21A–21G Cooking, medicine, coatings: only child dosing (فككت الصبي), the seized throat and mouth-to-mouth were carried, into The mouth.
