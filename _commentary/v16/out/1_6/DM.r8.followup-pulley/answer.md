**1. Why the pulley stayed out**

- **I tested relevance against the plain meaning.** My ledger reason, "no bearing on road, guidance or straightness", measured the image against what the ayah says. The brief asks for family images heard *beside* the word's meaning, and allows leaving a finding out only when it cannot contribute or its evidence fails. The evidence here is sound: tahdhib has «القامة البكرة التي يستقى بها الماء؛ النعامة الخشبة المعترضة ثم تعلق القامة», and sihah has «القامة البكرة بأداتها». So the evidence did not fail. I simply never tried the image.
- **The map's labels made it easy to drop.** The map calls these senses "rare or technical" and gives the rig no link to 1:6's meaning. Its Quran support (28:23, 22:45) is about wells, not guidance. I read "technical" as "peripheral" and did not look further.
- **My structure had no room for it.** My sections were built around road, walking, arrival and the scale. An apparatus that works vertically did not fit any of them, so I dropped it rather than asking whether it could build its own thesis.
- **I skipped the step the brief requires for mechanisms.** The brief says to explain a concrete object by its work before drawing its meaning. Had I done that, the image would have had something to offer: a pulley hangs from a beam over a well and lets water be drawn up, and that water keeps a camp alive.
- **I missed what the rig actually connects.**
  - The dictionary joins this ayah's root and 1:7's root in one phrase: the قامة hangs from the نعامة. Since 1:7 grammatically continues 1:6 (صراط is repeated), the thing from 1:6 hangs on the favour-beam of 1:7.
  - Sihah's «الماء ملك أمر أي يقوم به الأمر» ties the drawn water to 1:4's ملك and back to قوم.
  - A Quran scene joins guidance and a well directly. In 28:22 Moses, leaving Egypt, says «عسى ربي أن يهديني سواء السبيل», and in 28:23 he arrives at the water of Madyan, where people are drawing. The map cites 28:23 but not 28:22, and I did not check the ayah before it.
