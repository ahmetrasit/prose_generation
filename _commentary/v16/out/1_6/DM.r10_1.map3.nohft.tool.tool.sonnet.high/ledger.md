- memory: Arabic wording of 7:16 (Iblis's vow to sit in ambush on the straight road) — Quran 7:16
- memory: Arabic wording of 6:153 (one road against many paths that scatter) — Quran 6:153
- memory: Arabic wording of 26:182 (Shu'ayb to Madyan, "the straight scale"), 83:6 (the Day when people stand before the Lord of the worlds), 28:22 and 28:24 (Moses on the road to Madyan and at its well), 67:22 (walking face-down against walking upright on a straight road), 48:2 (completion of favour and guidance to a straight road), 20:123 (God to Adam and Eve at the descent) — Quran 26:182, 83:6, 28:22, 28:24, 67:22, 48:2, 20:123
- memory: that 2:196 speaks of the sacrificial animal reaching its place; and the Turkish popular image of the "Sırat köprüsü" as a bridge thinner than a hair and sharper than a sword — Quran 2:196; Turkish popular religious usage
- memory: 38:22 (the two litigants asking David to guide them to the level of the road) is available from the supplied text but I did not use it in the prose
- not written: the "cutting sword" sense of the root ص ر ط (السراط السيف القاطع الماضي في الضريبة) - it could have made the road a thing that cuts straight through, but the dictionary offers it as a separate sense and it adds nothing beyond the straightness already carried by ق و م
- not written: the shared root of ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ and "قوم" (the company whose road it is, 1:7 صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ) - it would have tied the straight road to the company that walks it, but the link is a wordplay on a shared root rather than a sense of this word in this ayah, and 1:7 can carry it
- not written: the Prophet being helped out of his house "يهادى بين رجلين" (from memory of hadith) - it would have given a concrete historical picture of the leaning walker, but I left it out to avoid attributing a historical scene to the ayah's word
- not written: the pulley and crossbeam sense of ق و م (القامة) from the well-head chain - it would have joined 1:6 to the water and draw-bucket imagery of the surah, but it belongs to the well chain at 1:7 and does not bear on the road theme here
- not written: the debt and blood-money chain - it would have made visible how the scale of ق و م meets 1:4's "دين", but I used only the scale and the coin, which serve the straightness theme directly
- not written: 36:66 "فاستبقوا الصراط" - it would have supported the road that takes its walker out of sight, but I could not verify the exact wording and it did not add beyond what the dictionary phrase already gives
- not written: 16:9 and 6:161, 3:51, 19:36, 43:64 (God's lordship and "this is a straight road", and the road that veers off) - they would have repeated what 6:153 and 43:43 already do for the theme of one road and its owner
- not written: 16:15-16 (roads, landmarks and stars for finding the way) - it would have added the landmark image of the guide, but the guide-in-front sense already gave the theme its centre
- not written: the remaining listed passages (92:12, 93:7, 87:3, 90:10, 29:69, etc.) - they would have added breadth to "God is the guide" but not sharpened the particular images of this ayah; 93:7 in particular rests on a disputed reading of ضالا that I did not want to build on
- not written: in the claim "Allah's gentle showing" I used only the dictionary's definition; I did not attempt to argue about the contested theological debate on whether hidaya is showing or enabling, since the supplied evidence does not settle it
