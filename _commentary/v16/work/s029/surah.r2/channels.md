## Parent Channels

### 1. P1 (29:1-13) Verification Under Pressure
- Semantic invariant: Pressure makes an inward quality externally legible by testing material, speech, and conduct.
- Surface relation: direct; 29:2-3,10 (ف ت ن), 29:3 (ص د ق), 29:2,10,12 (ق و ل), and 29:10 (ص د ر) stage profession, trial, truth, and what is held in breasts.
- Surprising reach: The passage's epistemic test acquires the concrete mechanics of fire-assay, diagnostic marking, hidden speech, and socially circulating noise.

#### Subchannel A. Fire-Assay and the Mark of Sound Material
- Reading type: mixed
- Scene or process: Metal or another material enters heat, changes visibly, and is judged by whether its solidity and promised performance survive.
- Active motifs: assaying metal and persons `quranic:root_001128:B001/m01`; burning and blackening by fire `quranic:root_001128:B002/m01`; material solidity and straightness `quranic:root_000852:B002/m01`; realization of promise in action `quranic:root_000852:B004/m01`; a distinguishing mark `quranic:root_001040:B002/m01`; a covering laid over what is beneath `quranic:root_001307:B001/m01`

- Synthesis: Trial is not only adversity but an assay that separates sound from unsound material. Truth is both a hard, straight substance and a promise realized under stress, while knowledge reads the resulting mark through any attempted covering.

#### Subchannel B. Spoken Profession and the Unvoiced Interior
- Reading type: mixed
- Scene or process: An utterance leaves the tongue while an unvoiced formulation, intention, and causal source remain within the person.
- Active motifs: articulated speech `quranic:root_001272:B001/m01`; speech held inward before utterance `quranic:root_001272:B012/m01`; inward intention `quranic:root_001533:B013/m01`; the source from which actions issue `quranic:root_000849:B004/m01`; knowledge as disclosure to a knower `quranic:root_001040:B001/m01`; the lying self `quranic:root_001290:B008/m01`

- Synthesis: The professed "we believe" is one layer of a speech event; beneath it sit an unspoken proposition, intention, and the source of subsequent action. Divine knowing therefore reaches the point where language is still interior and distinguishes the lying self before public wording can stabilize its claim.

#### Subchannel C. Injury Amplified as Public Circulation

## Cross-Pericope and Whole-Surah Channels

### X1. Nontransferable Liability and Entrusted Provision
- Pericopes: P1 (29:1-13) and P5 (29:57-69).
- Bridge: repeated scene signature with contrast and role reversal; both scenes contain a load, bearer, guarantor, transfer claim, and settlement, but they impose opposite transfer rules.
- Canonical placements: P1 Parent 2 / Subchannel A, "Load, Surety, and Inseparable Liability"; P5 Parent 19 / Subchannel A, "Load, Guarantor, and Entrusted Care".
- Synthesis: Moral liability cannot be detached from the actor, so a promised substitute only acquires an additional burden. Material provision, by contrast, is precisely what a creature unable to carry its future must entrust to a sufficient guardian. The bridge distinguishes evasion from reliance by asking whether the load is constitutive responsibility or a need properly placed in care.

### X2. Allegiance Reversed by Crisis and Safety
- Pericopes: P2 (29:14-27) and P5 (29:57-69).
- Bridge: contrast and reversal; the same roles of worshiper, binding object, allegiance, crisis, and repudiation recur with the direction of change inverted.
- Canonical placements: P2 Parent 4 / Subchannels B-C, "Affection as an Alliance Contract" and "Repudiation, Separation, and Expulsion"; P5 Parent 20 / Subchannels A-B, "Unstable Vessel and the Purified Call" and "Dry-Ground Rescue and Renewed Division".
- Synthesis: In P2 a stable cultic alliance is built through affection and later reverses into mutual disavowal. In P5 danger first strips divided allegiance down to one addressee, while restored safety permits partnership and denial to return. Read together, crisis does not mechanically produce fidelity; it exposes the bond's direction, and the post-crisis act reveals whether purification became durable allegiance.

### X3. Rescue as Separation and Relocation
- Pericopes: P2 (29:14-27) and P3 (29:28-44).
- Bridge: repeated scene signature and causal sequence; an enclosing threat is followed by an external rescuer, separation from danger, rapid movement, and relocation beyond the threatened field.
- Canonical placements: P2 Parent 7 / Subchannel B, "Extraction to Flood-Safe Height"; P2 S6, "Fire Attack and Extractive Rescue"; P3 S8, "Guests, Provision, and Extraction"; P3 S9, "Constricted Reach Opened by Rescue".
- Synthesis: Flood, fire, a hostile town, and constricted personal reach differ as threat media, but rescue keeps the same operation: it does not merely cancel harm in place; it opens an exit and changes the endangered party's location. The rescuing role therefore progresses from preserving companions to extracting a target and finally enabling a household whose own reach has failed.

### X4. Route Agency From Following to Guided Striving
- Pericopes: P1 (29:1-13), P2 (29:14-27), P3 (29:28-44), P4 (29:45-56), and P5 (29:57-69).

- Synthesis: The surah's road sequence develops moral agency through changing travel conditions. A path can recruit passive following, migration can sever inherited location, hostile power can block public passage, breadth can restore room for service, and exertion can culminate in disclosed routes. Guidance is thus neither bare direction nor frictionless motion; it is the restoration of traversable agency under pressure.

### X5. From Assay Mark to Inward Testimony
- Pericopes: P1 (29:1-13), P2 (29:14-27), P3 (29:28-44), and P4 (29:45-56).

### X6. Return Through Limit, Re-creation, and Revived Life
- Pericopes: P1 (29:1-13), P2 (29:14-27), and P5 (29:57-69).
