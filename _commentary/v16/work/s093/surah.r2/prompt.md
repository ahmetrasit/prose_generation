Read the surah as one text and write its map of image chains: the lexical images that run through several of
its ayat and join them into one scene, process or movement. A later writer will read one ayah at a time, with
only that ayah's own dictionary. The map lets that writer hear what the ayah's words carry in the surah as a
whole, including senses whose evidence sits under the words of other ayat.

Your evidence is the surah text, the dictionary of every root in the surah (each branch with the classical
dictionaries' own phrases), earlier readers' channel review (channels.md) and activation hypotheses (hft.md),
and your own knowledge of Arabic and the Quran. Where a member or passage comes from memory rather than from
the dictionary or the text, say so. Do not use tools, delegate, browse or inspect files.

The channel review and the HFT records are proposals by earlier readers. Ignore their judgements: grades,
strength or confidence labels, reading types, words such as "surprising", "exploratory" or "latent", and every
statement of what a reading may or may not do. Make your own judgement from the surah's words, the dictionary
phrases and the Quran. Do not rediscover what they already assembled: start from their chains, test each one
against the dictionary phrases and the text, and join, extend, split or correct them. Where their wording
abstracts a member, go back to the dictionary phrase and name what it actually says.

A chain belongs on the map when its members are senses the dictionary attests for words that stand in the
surah, and together they make one image or process that the surah's wording or sequence lets a listener hear.
A member need not be the sense that translates its word in its own ayah; a chain is heard across the surah,
not in one word. Say plainly when a member is a rare sense or is attested only inside a fixed expression, and
keep it. When the dictionary itself joins two of the surah's words in one phrase, quote that phrase: it is
the strongest evidence a chain can have. Keep a scene at the level of its objects, their parts and their
operation. When proposals share members, do not fold one into another's more abstract function unless nothing
concrete is lost. Carry every chain that meets this test, however unusual; leave out proposals that do not.

Write the map in English, with Arabic quoted exactly (surah wording from the text, dictionary phrases from the
dictionary). It is working material for the writer, not commentary prose.

   moves through the surah. Then its members, one line each: the ayah ref, the word as it stands in the text,

   image. Add Quran passages outside the surah, with exact refs, where they stage or confirm the chain.
2. `## Ayat`. For each ayah in order: which chains it opens, advances or completes, and what its words add to

3. `## Not carried`. Each channel subchannel and each HFT record you did not carry into a chain, one line each:

No ranking and no labels of strength or confidence. No list of what a writer must include. There is no length
target and no required number of chains or members.

# s093 Semantic Channel Discovery

## Parent Channels

### 1. Temporal Passage and Emergence
- Semantic invariant: Things occupy phases, move through ordered succession, and become manifest after absence.
- Surface relation: direct; 93:1 `ٱلضُّحَىٰ`, 93:2 `ٱلَّيْلِ` and `سَجَىٰ`, 93:4 `ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ` and `ٱلْأُولَىٰ`, and 93:11 `فَحَدِّثْ`.
- Surprising reach: The day-night sequence extends into reversal, ontological emergence, fresh life, and sudden incident.

#### Subchannel A. Daylight, Night, and Stilling
- Reading type: surface-primary
- Scene or process: Bright daytime opens and extends, then night covers the scene and settles into stillness.
- Active motifs: extended forenoon `ض ح و:B001/m01`; clear forenoon brightness `ض ح و:B005/m01`; night and darkness `ل ي ل:B001/m01`; night settling into stillness `س ج و:B001/m01`; daylight opening `ن ه ر:B002/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 93:1 `ٱلضُّحَىٰ`; 93:2 `ٱلَّيْلِ`, `سَجَىٰ`; 93:10 `تَنْهَرْ`
- Synthesis: Forenoon supplies both duration and radiance, while night supplies darkness and `سَجَىٰ` supplies the act of becoming still. The lexical opening of daylight completes a cycle in which illumination expands, darkness arrives, and motion subsides.

#### Subchannel B. Firstness, Laterness, and Return
- Reading type: mixed
- Scene or process: A sequence begins, reaches a later phase, may be deferred, and turns back toward its outcome.
- Active motifs: first position or onset `ء و ل:B001/m01`; later or subsequent state `ء خ ر:B001/m01`; deferment `ء خ ر:B002/m01`; return to outcome `ء و ل:B002/m01`; backward movement `ق ه ر:B005/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 93:4 `ٱلْءَاخِرَةُ`, `ٱلْأُولَىٰ`; 93:9 `تَقْهَرْ`
- Synthesis: Firstness and laterness establish ordered phases. Deferment lengthens the interval, while return to an eventual end and backward motion give that order a reversible shape rather than a static before-after contrast.

#### Subchannel C. Coming into Being and Disclosure
- Reading type: latent/lexical
- Scene or process: Something absent enters existence, is encountered as real, and is brought into view or speech.
- Active motifs: origination after nonbeing `ح د ث:B001/m01`; existent entity `و ج د:B002/m01`; manifestation and showing `ح د ث:B006/m01`; sudden incident `ح د ث:B005/m01`; initial emergence `ء و ل:B001/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 93:4 `ٱلْأُولَىٰ`; 93:6-8 `يَجِدْكَ` and `وَجَدَكَ`; 93:11 `فَحَدِّثْ`
- Synthesis: Beginning marks entry into a phase, occurrence marks entry into reality, existence makes the new thing available to encounter, and disclosure makes it publicly perceptible. A sudden incident is the disruptive form of the same emergence process.

#### Subchannel D. Freshness and Early Life
- Reading type: latent/lexical
- Scene or process: A life or phase remains close to its beginning and carries the freshness of recent arrival.
- Active motifs: youth and recentness `ح د ث:B002/m01`; newly delivered ewe `ر ب ب:B009/m01`; fresh beginning or early youth `ر ب ب:B009/m02`; first phase `ء و ل:B001/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 93:3, 93:5, 93:11 `رَبُّكَ` and `رَبِّكَ`; 93:4 `ٱلْأُولَىٰ`; 93:11 `فَحَدِّثْ`
- Synthesis: Recent birth, youth, and firstness converge on a scene whose defining state is proximity to origin. Freshness is therefore temporal as well as bodily.

### 2. Orientation, Search, and Passage
- Semantic invariant: A participant moves relative to a course, loses or recovers orientation, and reaches either a destination or retreat.
- Surface relation: direct; 93:3 `وَدَّعَكَ`, 93:6-8 `يَجِدْكَ` and `وَجَدَكَ`, and 93:7 `ضَآلًّا` and `فَهَدَىٰ`.
- Surprising reach: Moral guidance opens into physical finding, gait, front-rear orientation, farewell, and refuge.

#### Subchannel A. Straying and Gentle Guidance
- Reading type: surface-primary
- Scene or process: A traveler departs from the intended course and is gently directed back toward the road, purpose, or truth.
- Active motifs: deviation from the way `ض ل ل:B001/m01`; gentle direction to road or truth `ه د ي:B001/m01`; course, manner, and intended direction `ه د ي:B002/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 93:7 `ضَآلًّا`, `فَهَدَىٰ`
- Synthesis: Deviation supplies the displaced participant, guidance supplies the directing action, and the course or manner supplies the route that makes correction intelligible. The scene works equally as road-finding and restoration of purpose.

#### Subchannel B. Hidden, Lost, and Found
- Reading type: mixed
- Scene or process: An object disappears from view or memory, becomes hard to locate, and is recovered through finding.
- Active motifs: concealment or disappearance `ض ل ل:B002/m01`; lost object `ض ل ل:B003/m01`; forgotten object `ض ل ل:B004/m01`; finding and locating `و ج د:B001/m01`; directionless lost creature `ع ي ل:B008/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 93:6-8 `يَجِدْكَ` and `وَجَدَكَ`; 93:7 `ضَآلًّا`; 93:8 `عَآئِلًا`
- Synthesis: Disappearance, loss of location, and loss from memory are distinct ways an object becomes unavailable. Finding reverses each condition, while the directionless creature gives the search a mobile participant.

#### Subchannel C. Forward Motion and Agility
- Reading type: latent/lexical

#### Subchannel C. Grinding, Washing, and Polishing

- Synthesis: The leather case gathers discrete shafts or lots, the drink vessel holds liquid through time, and the provision container fixes food in stacked order. Their common operation is bounded keeping.

#### Subchannel B. Wrapping, Shrouding, and Concealment

#### Subchannel C. Carrier, Bier, and Supporting Frame

- Active motifs: carrier or bier `ء و ل:B008/m01`; structural timbers or carrying tool `ء و ل:B008/m02`; singular unmatched object `ي ت م:B002/m01`; leading projection or arrowhead `ه د ي:B003/m02`

- Synthesis: Timbers form the supporting frame, the bier gives that frame a carrying function, the singular object supplies its load, and the leading projection gives the assembly a directed front.

### 11. Herds, Wildlife, and Ritual Gifts
- Semantic invariant: Animals are gathered, grazed, lost, sheltered, bred, hunted, or transferred as offerings.
- Surface relation: indirect; 93:1 `ٱلضُّحَىٰ`, 93:5 `يُعْطِيكَ`, 93:6 and 93:9 `يَتِيمًا` and `ٱلْيَتِيمَ`, and 93:7 `فَهَدَىٰ`.
- Surprising reach: Human vulnerability and guidance extend into pastoral ownership, wildlife refuge, sacrifice, bird form, and herd dispersal.

#### Subchannel A. Herd, Grazing, and the Stray
- Reading type: latent/lexical
- Scene or process: A herd grazes early, depends on an owner, produces breeding stock, and risks losing animals to the open waste.
- Active motifs: gathered herd `ر ب ب:B014/m01`; livestock `ن ع م:B005/m01`; early grazing `ض ح و:B003/m02`; stray animal `ض ل ل:B005/m01`; directionless stray `ع ي ل:B008/m01`; breeding male retained for the herd `و د ع:B008/m01`; motherless animal `ي ت م:B001/m02`

- Synthesis: Herd and livestock establish collective ownership, early grazing establishes routine, and the retained male sustains reproduction. Straying breaks the owner-animal relation, while motherlessness creates the animal form of lost care.

#### Subchannel B. Offering and Sacrifice
- Reading type: latent/lexical

- Synthesis: Livestock supplies the object, selection as a sacrificial animal gives it ritual status, transfer directs it to the sanctuary, and giving explains the offering relation.

#### Subchannel C. Wildlife, Lure, and Refuge

#### Subchannel D. Ostrich Form and Dispersal
- Reading type: latent/lexical
- Scene or process: The ostrich supplies a visible body pattern that is transferred to tools and landmarks, then to the image of a group scattering in flight.
- Active motifs: ostrich `ن ع م:B006/m01`; ostrich-shaped structures and markers `ن ع م:B007/m01`; ostrich flight as group dispersal `ن ع م:B008/m01`

#### Subchannel C. Disposition Cultivated into Virtue

- Synthesis: Existence supplies the entity, otherness distinguishes it relationally, and singularity marks the stronger condition in which no peer or counterpart remains.
