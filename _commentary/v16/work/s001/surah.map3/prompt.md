===== _commentary/v16/prompts/map3/surah_map.md =====
Read the surah as one text and write its map of image chains: the lexical images that run through several of
its ayat and join them into one scene, process or movement. A later writer will read one ayah at a time, with
only that ayah's own dictionary. The map lets that writer hear what the ayah's words carry in the surah as a
whole, including senses whose evidence sits under the words of other ayat.

Your evidence is the surah text, the dictionary of every root in the surah (each branch with the classical
dictionaries' own phrases), earlier readers' channel review (channels.md) and activation hypotheses (hft.md),
and your own knowledge of Arabic and the Quran. Where a member or passage comes from memory rather than from
the dictionary or the text, say so. Do not use tools, delegate, browse or inspect files.

The channel review and the HFT records are proposals by earlier readers. Ignore their judgements: grades,
strength or confidence labels, reading types, words such as "surprising", "exploratory" or "latent", and every
statement of what a reading may or may not do. Make your own judgement from the surah's words, the dictionary
phrases and the Quran. Do not rediscover what they already assembled: start from their chains, test each one
against the dictionary phrases and the text, and join, extend, split or correct them. Where their wording
abstracts a member, go back to the dictionary phrase and name what it actually says.

A chain belongs on the map when its members are senses the dictionary attests for words that stand in the surah,
and together they make one image or process that the surah's wording or sequence lets a listener hear. A member
need not be the sense that translates its word in its own ayah; a chain is heard across the surah, not in one
word. A chain may join a sense and its reversal as well as the parts of one scene. Mark a member attested only
inside a fixed expression [fixed expression]; add no other label to a member or a chain, and where a source
records a phrase and rejects it, say so of that phrase alone. When the dictionary itself joins two of the
surah's words in one phrase, quote that phrase: it is the strongest evidence a chain can have. Keep a scene at
the level of its objects, their parts and their operation. When proposals share members, do not fold one into
another's more abstract function unless nothing concrete is lost. Carry every chain that meets this test,
however unusual; leave out proposals that do not.

Write the map in English, with Arabic quoted exactly (surah wording from the text, dictionary phrases from the
dictionary). It is working material for the writer, not commentary prose.

1. `## Chains`. For each chain, a `###` heading naming the image. One paragraph on what the image is and how it
   moves through the surah. Then its members, one line each: the ayah ref, the word as it stands in the text,
   the root and branch id, the dictionary's own phrase quoted exactly, and what this member contributes to the
   image. Add Quran passages outside the surah, with exact refs, where they stage or confirm the chain; for
   each, name the speaker and the situation in a few words, and include the ayah that opens its scene when the
   passage continues one.
2. `## Interactions`. Where chains meet: a shared member, a dictionary phrase that joins members of two chains,
   a Quran passage that stages two chains together, or one chain's scene needing another's. One line each: the
   chains, where they meet, the evidence.
3. `## Ayat`. For each ayah in order: the chains its words take part in, what its words add to each, and in one
   line the whole scene each chain makes across the surah, so that a writer who sees only this ayah sees the
   scene, not a fragment.
4. `## Not carried`. Each channel subchannel and each HFT record you did not carry into a chain: one short line
   each, its name and why, no prose.

No ranking and no labels of strength or confidence. No list of what a writer must include. There is no length
target and no required number of chains or members.

# S001 Semantic Channel Discovery

## Parent Channels

### 1. Devotional Dependence and Rule
- Semantic invariant: A dependent community turns toward a sovereign divine addressee through worship, service, obedience, and petition.
- Surface relation: direct; 1:1 `بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ`, 1:2 `لِلَّهِ رَبِّ`, 1:4 `مَٰلِكِ ... ٱلدِّينِ`, 1:5 `نَعْبُدُ ... نَسْتَعِينُ`, and 1:6 `ٱهْدِنَا`.
- Surprising reach: The devotional relation extends lexically from willing worship into ownership and coercive service, while lordship also carries the countervailing function of nurture.

#### Subchannel A. Worship and Obedient Submission
- Reading type: surface-primary
- Scene or process: Worshippers direct humble obedience to the one recognized as deity and organizing religious authority.
- Active motifs: worship as an act `ء ل ه:B001/m01`; the worshipped deity `ء ل ه:B001/m02`; obedient religious submission `د ي ن:B001/m01`; humble worship and service `ع ب د:B003/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 1:1 `ٱللَّهِ` and 1:2 `لِلَّهِ` (`ء ل ه`); 1:4 `ٱلدِّينِ` (`د ي ن`); 1:5 `نَعْبُدُ` (`ع ب د`)
- Synthesis: The scene joins the divine object of worship to the human act of worship, then specifies the relation as obedience enacted with humility. Religion is active here as an ordering relation rather than merely a named doctrine.

#### Subchannel B. Sovereignty, Ownership, and Service
- Reading type: mixed
- Scene or process: A lord and king possesses a domain, governs it, and stands over dependents whose service ranges from honored attendance to enslavement.
- Active motifs: divine lordship `ر ب ب:B001/m01`; proprietary mastery `ر ب ب:B001/m02`; kingship and sovereignty `م ل ك:B003/m01`; ownership and disposal of property `م ل ك:B002/m01`; the enslaved dependent `ع ب د:B001/m01`; coercive dominion `د ي ن:B004/m01`; the honored or venerated servant `ع ب د:B006/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 1:2 `رَبِّ` (`ر ب ب`); 1:4 `مَٰلِكِ` and `ٱلدِّينِ` (`م ل ك`, `د ي ن`); 1:5 `نَعْبُدُ` (`ع ب د`)
- Synthesis: Lordship and kingship supply the governing agent, ownership supplies the legal relation to a domain, and servitude supplies the dependent role. The honored servant and the enslaved subject expose opposite social realizations of the same vertical relation.

#### Subchannel C. Petition for Aid and Direction
- Reading type: surface-primary
- Scene or process: Dependents seek assistance and gentle direction toward an upright path, with compassionate benefaction as the answering function.
- Active motifs: requested aid and cooperation `ع و ن:B001/m01`; gentle guidance `ه د ي:B001/m01`; the traversable path `ص ر ط:B001/m01`; uprightness and rectitude `ق و م:B008/m01`; mercy enacted as benefaction `ر ح م:B001/m02`
- Ayah anchors: 1:1 and 1:3 `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ` / `ٱلرَّحِيمِ` (`ر ح م`); 1:5 `نَسْتَعِينُ` (`ع و ن`); 1:6 `ٱهْدِنَا`, `ٱلصِّرَٰطَ`, and `ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ` (`ه د ي`, `ص ر ط`, `ق و م`); 1:7 `صِرَٰطَ` (`ص ر ط`)
- Synthesis: Aid supplies the enabling force, guidance supplies the directing action, the path supplies its object, and straightness supplies the desired state. Mercy turns the request-response structure into beneficent rather than merely administrative rule.

### 2. Benefaction, Welfare, and Praise
- Semantic invariant: Good is conferred through compassion and maintenance, stabilized as welfare, and answered by praise or recognition.
- Surface relation: direct; 1:1 and 1:3 `ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ`, 1:2 `ٱلْحَمْدُ ... رَبِّ`, 1:5 `نَسْتَعِينُ`, and 1:7 `أَنْعَمْتَ`.
- Surprising reach: Tenderness expands into practical provisioning, repair, guardianship, and structural support, while praise can function either as gratitude or as a claim imposed by a patron.

#### Subchannel A. Compassionate Provision and Nurture
- Reading type: mixed
- Scene or process: A caregiver feels tenderness, nurtures a dependent over time, guards its affairs, and supplies or repairs what sustains a good condition.

# Focus 1:7

## embodied route
- reading: The verse makes a favored human trajectory the road's visible embodiment and distinguishes oppositional rupture from aimless deviation.
- mechanism: The verse does not identify a road by geometry alone. It makes bestowed well-being visible in a recipient group, so their lived trajectory becomes the road's positive continuity; opposition and deviation are rival ways of leaving that continuity.
- trace:
  - 1:7 **صِرَٰطَ** ص ر ط B001: The straight-road image supplies continuous movement, while the following relative phrase relocates that continuity into an embodied human route.
  - 1:7 **أَنْعَمْتَ** ن ع م B001: Bestowed favorable condition supplies the observable state by which the road-bearing group is identified.
  - 1:7 **غَيْرِ** غ ي ر B005: Otherness and exception turn the verse from a single route description into a contrast among trajectories.
  - 1:7 **ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ** غ ض ب B003: Defiance and opposition supply a relational mechanism for one trajectory's rupture rather than merely naming an emotion.
  - 1:7 **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** ض ل ل B001: Deviation from aim supplies the second failure mode: motion continues but no longer holds the road's direction.

## successful and failed disappearance
- reading: Both right passage and straying can involve disappearance; the decisive difference is whether disappearance completes directed transit or erases recoverable direction.
- mechanism: Two kinds of disappearance coexist. A soft, capacious passage can carry a traveler through so completely that the traveler vanishes into successful transit, whereas straying can also make something vanish but without destination or recoverability. Compact hardness acts as the obstructing condition that separates the two.
- trace:
  - 1:7 **صِرَٰطَ** ص ر ط B002: Disappearance through swallowing supplies a successful passage in which being taken out of sight is evidence of transit.
  - 1:7 **أَنْعَمْتَ** ن ع م B002: Softness and easeful living supply low resistance, making the favored passage traversable rather than abrasive.
  - 1:7 **ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ** غ ض ب B004: Hard compact rock supplies the blocking material state opposed to softened passage.
  - 1:7 **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** ض ل ل B002: Hiddenness and disappearance supply a failed vanishing that lacks the road's directed transit.

## decisive partition
- reading: The verse can stage a decisive partition among favorable passage, retaliatory exposure, and an unresolved loss that never arrives for settlement.
- mechanism: The road can image a decisive cut that separates outcomes. The exception pivot opens an alternative to retaliatory settlement, while the final group represents what never reaches settlement because it has gone unaccounted for.
- trace:
  - 1:7 **صِرَٰطَ** ص ر ط B003: A cutting implement passing cleanly through a stroke supplies the road as a decisive separator of outcomes.
  - 1:7 **غَيْرِ** غ ي ر B002: Compensation offered instead of retaliation supplies a legal fork at the verse's exception pivot.
  - 1:7 **ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ** غ ض ب B001: Anger rising toward retaliation supplies the threatened punitive settlement on one side of the fork.
  - 1:7 **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** ض ل ل B003: A lost and unlocated thing supplies the unresolved outcome that falls outside both favorable passage and settled retaliation.

## agency gradient
- reading: They encode different causal positions: actively furthered recipients, people bearing relational opposition, and agents continuing to deviate.
- mechanism: The three groups differ not only in outcome but in causal grammar. Favor actively carries recipients further; the angered group bears a condition produced in a relation of opposition; the strayers enact continuing deviation. The road is therefore a pattern of agency distribution.
- trace:
  - 1:7 **أَنْعَمْتَ** ن ع م B010: Adding and carrying an action further supplies active propulsion to the explicitly second-person benefaction.
  - 1:7 **ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ** غ ض ب B003: Mutual opposition supplies the relation whose result is borne by the passive angered group.
  - 1:7 **ٱلضَّآلِّينَ** ض ل ل B001: Deviation from aim supplies ongoing self-propelled divergence to the active-participial final group.
  - 1:7 **غَيْرِ** غ ي ر B003: Replacement and change of state make the exception pivot a switch among causal regimes rather than a flat list boundary.
