Read the surah as one text and write its map of image chains: the lexical images that run through several of
its ayat and join them into one scene, process or movement. A later writer will read one ayah at a time, with
only that ayah's own dictionary. The map lets that writer hear what the ayah's words carry in the surah as a
whole, including senses whose evidence sits under the words of other ayat.

Your evidence is the surah text, the dictionary of every root in the surah (each branch with the classical
dictionaries' own phrases), an earlier reader's channel review (channels.md),
and your own knowledge of Arabic and the Quran. Where a member or passage comes from memory rather than from
the dictionary or the text, say so. Do not use tools, delegate, browse or inspect files.

The channel review is an earlier reader's proposal. Ignore its judgements: grades,
strength or confidence labels, reading types, words such as "surprising", "exploratory" or "latent", and every
statement of what a reading may or may not do. Make your own judgement from the surah's words, the dictionary
phrases and the Quran. Do not rediscover what it already assembled: start from its chains, test each one
against the dictionary phrases and the text, and join, extend, split or correct them. Where its wording
abstracts a member, go back to the dictionary phrase and name what it actually says.

A chain belongs on the map when its members are senses the dictionary attests for words that stand in the surah,
and together they make one image or process that the surah's wording or sequence lets a listener hear. A member
need not be the sense that translates its word in its own ayah; a chain is heard across the surah, not in one
word. A chain may join a sense and its reversal as well as the parts of one scene. Mark a member attested only
inside a fixed expression [fixed expression]; add no other label to a member or a chain, and where a source
records a phrase and rejects it, say so of that phrase alone. When the dictionary itself joins two of the
surah's words in one phrase, quote that phrase: it is the strongest evidence a chain can have. Keep a scene at
the level of its objects, their parts and their operation. When proposals share members, do not fold one into
another's more abstract function unless nothing concrete is lost. Carry every chain that meets this test,
however unusual; leave out proposals that do not.

Write the map in English, with Arabic quoted exactly (surah wording from the text, dictionary phrases from the
dictionary). It is working material for the writer, not commentary prose.

# S103 Semantic Channel Discovery

## Parent Channels

### 1. Truthful Commitment Against Loss
- Semantic invariant: Human diminishment is countered when conviction, truth, purposeful action, and communal steadiness remain aligned.
- Surface relation: direct; 103:2 names the human being in `خُسْرٍ`, while 103:3 answers with `ءَامَنُوا۟`, `عَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ`, and reciprocal counsel in `ٱلْحَقِّ` and `ٱلصَّبْرِ`.
- Surprising reach: The ethical sequence opens into a social mechanism in which trusted companionship helps a community hold its course.

#### Subchannel A. Conviction Realized as Sound Action
- Reading type: surface-primary
- Scene or process: A person assents to truth, turns that assent into deliberate good action, and thereby opposes loss with soundness.
- Active motifs: faithful submission to truth `ء م ن:B002/m02`; truth opposed to falsehood `ح ق ق:B001/m01`; morally qualified intentional deed `ع م ل:B001/m02`; soundness opposed to corruption `ص ل ح:B001/m01`; general diminishment `خ س ر:B001/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 103:2 `خُسْرٍ`; 103:3 `ءَامَنُوا۟`, `عَمِلُوا۟`, `ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ`, `ٱلْحَقِّ`
- Synthesis: Conviction is not left as an inward state. It is matched to what is true, embodied in intended deeds, and expressed as a movement from corruption or decrease toward soundness.

#### Subchannel B. Reciprocal Counsel as Communal Steadiness
- Reading type: mixed
- Scene or process: People repeatedly counsel one another while trust, companionship, and self-restraint keep the group from dispersing under pressure.
- Active motifs: reciprocal counsel `و ص ي:B003/m01`; self-restraint against panic `ص ب ر:B001/m01`; steadfast endurance `ص ب ر:B001/m02`; companionship removing estrangement `ء ن س:B003/m01`; settled security and trust `ء م ن:B001/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 103:2 `إِنسَٰنَ`; 103:3 `ءَامَنُوا۟`, both occurrences of `تَوَاصَوْا۟`, and `ٱلصَّبْرِ`
- Synthesis: Mutual counsel becomes a sustaining social practice rather than a one-way instruction. Familiar presence removes isolation, trust stabilizes relations, and patience supplies the inward restraint needed for counsel to persist.

### 2. Entrusted Rights and Enforcement
- Semantic invariant: A binding claim is carried through appointed roles, contested speech, reconciliation, custody, or requital.
- Surface relation: indirect; 103:3 supplies the anchors `ٱلْحَقِّ`, `تَوَاصَوْا۟`, `عَمِلُوا۟`, and `ٱلصَّبْرِ`, whose lexical branches extend into legal and fiduciary relations.
- Surprising reach: Reciprocal exhortation expands into testamentary succession, surety, compelled testimony, and authoritative requital.

#### Subchannel A. Testament, Executor, and Surety
- Reading type: latent/lexical
- Scene or process: A binding instruction is transmitted to another person, placed under an executor, and secured by obligation and guarantee.
- Active motifs: transmitted charge `و ص ي:B002/m01`; testament after death `و ص ي:B002/m02`; appointed executor `و ص ي:B002/m03`; surety or guarantor `ص ب ر:B003/m01`; binding obligation `ح ق ق:B002/m01`; proprietary right `ح ق ق:B003/m01`; appointment to office `ع م ل:B003/m02`; protected charge requiring defense `ح ق ق:B007/m01`
- Ayah anchors: 103:3 `تَوَاصَوْا۟`, `ٱلصَّبْرِ`, `ٱلْحَقِّ`, `عَمِلُوا۟`
- Synthesis: The scene joins message, office, property, and person. A charge remains effective because someone is designated to carry it, a guarantor stands behind it, and the right itself imposes a duty of protection.

#### Subchannel B. Contested Right Reconciled by Deliberation
- Reading type: latent/lexical
- Scene or process: Opposing claimants each assert a right; reciprocal communication and adjudication convert the contest into restored concord.
- Active motifs: adversarial rights dispute `ح ق ق:B004/m01`; adjudication and verification `ح ق ق:B005/m02`; peacemaking `ص ل ح:B002/m01`; restored social concord `ص ل ح:B002/m02`; reciprocal communication `و ص ي:B003/m02`; human collective `ء ن س:B001/m01`

- Synthesis: The dispute is not erased but processed. Competing claims are stated and tested, communication remains reciprocal, and reconciliation removes the estrangement generated by the contest.

#### Subchannel C. Coercive Custody and Compelled Oath
- Reading type: latent/lexical

- Active motifs: restraint and withholding `ع ص ر:B006/m01`; captive held for execution `ص ب ر:B002/m01`; living target held for shooting `ص ب ر:B002/m02`; oath compelled by authority `ص ب ر:B002/m03`; human person `ء ن س:B001/m01`

- Synthesis: Loss becomes a claim that demands a proportionate answer. The lexical scene moves from deprivation to a formally satisfied right, with authority converting the claim into requital.

### 3. Work, Exchange, and Accounted Return
- Semantic invariant: Labor or property is evaluated by the return it yields, the right it creates, and the measure by which gain or shortfall is known.
- Surface relation: direct; 103:2 states `خُسْرٍ` and 103:3 states `عَمِلُوا۟`, establishing the loss-work axis that the lexical scenes specify economically.
- Surprising reach: Failed return acquires the tactile hardness of gravel and the bitterness of medicinal sap, while quantitative loss becomes an underfilled measure.
