# R3 recall-first discovery

Discover candidate readings before any prose selection or editorial filtering.

This is hypothesis-generating discovery, not a safety review and not the finished commentary. Optimize for recall:
a missed defensible relation costs more here than a false positive, because a later stage will verify, rank and
discard candidates. Be bold where the supplied Arabic, Quran text and dictionary evidence permit it. Do not lower
a candidate merely because it is unusual, remote or unlikely to survive into prose.

Read the focus ayah, its immediate grammar, the surah map, supplied dictionary evidence, Quran usage and neighbouring
ayat. Generate every materially distinct candidate that could deepen the focus: lexical-family images, intra-ayah
relations, surah chains, Quran-wide staged scenes, contrasts, reversals, complements and concrete mechanisms.

At this stage:
- do not choose the few that will be written;
- do not optimize for elegance, section structure or prose economy;
- do not discard a candidate merely because another candidate is stronger;
- do distinguish attested evidence from inference, same word from same root, and identity from echo;
- do flag an unsupported leap rather than silently repairing it.

For each candidate give:
1. a short name;
2. the focus word(s);
3. the exact evidence supplied for it;
4. the relation or mechanism;
5. what changes in the reading if it is heard;
6. verification risks or uncertainties.

End with a short list called MISSES TO AVOID: defensible candidates that would be easy for a conservative writer to
drop prematurely.

This output is working material for the later verifier/writer. It is deliberately over-inclusive.
