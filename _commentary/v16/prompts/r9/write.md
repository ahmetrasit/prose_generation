Write the Turkish reading of the focus Quranic ayah for a curious reader who
knows neither Arabic nor how lexical families work, and who already has the
plain meaning. Show what a translation cannot give: the supported latent
meanings and resonances of its words. Work from the supplied evidence and your
own knowledge of Arabic and the Quran; do not use tools. This is your own
interpretation, not a catalogue: maps and readings by earlier readers are
proposals, and what their members reveal together is yours to find or correct.
There is no length limit.

Discover before you write. First understand the ayah in its grammar and
situation. Then explore the attested senses beside one another, within and
across roots, and through the ayah's neighbours, its surah and the Quran; a
partial finding may gain its missing support from another. Look for Quran
passages that share the ayah's words or roots, and also for passages that stage
the same act, scene or stance without sharing a word with it. A chain that a
neighbouring ayah opens or completes can still be heard here, where this
ayah's words take part in it. Where the dictionary joins this ayah's root with
another root of the surah in one phrase, explore the join. Images from
different chains can complete one another: judge a member by the scene it
makes with the others, not alone. Let the major themes emerge from these
discoveries, and develop every supported finding that builds, complicates or
connects them. A surprise is welcome when the evidence supports it; rigour
comes from exact evidence, not from retreating to familiar readings.

Leave a finding out only when its evidence fails, or when you have tried it
and it still adds nothing: for a concrete object or mechanism, explain its
work, then look for a neighbouring ayah, another root of the surah or a Quran
scene that gives that work a place. Rarity, technicality, a mismatch with the
word's plain meaning here, or not fitting the sections you first planned are
not reasons. Decide each finding on its own, and give each its own ledger line
saying which evidence failed or what you tried.

Keep these guards:

- A family image is heard beside the word's meaning in this ayah, never in
  place of it; say so once, where the first one enters. Show where each image
  comes from: the word, its attested Arabic phrase in a reader tag, then the
  reading. Attribute the image to the word's family; the prose names no
  dictionary or lexicographer, but still quotes the phrase.
- Keep root identity, family images and your interpretive connections
  distinct. Same word, same root and analogy are different things; an echo
  root does not establish identity.
- Explain a concrete object or mechanism by its work before drawing its
  meaning; do not flatten it into a label.
- Never invent a sense, source, vowel, etymology, historical fact, citation or
  chronology.
- Where a key word lives in Turkish as a narrowed or shifted loanword, let the
  reader feel what the Turkish word no longer carries, once.
- When you use another Quran passage, assume the reader does not know it:
  give the speaker, the situation and the wording the connection needs.
- The prose never talks about its own sources or process and never hedges in
  the first person: no "hafızadan", "bildiğim kadarıyla", "bu bağı ben
  kuruyorum", branch IDs or workflow language. What comes from memory rather
  than the supplied texts goes in the ledger.

Write continuous prose in `##` sections, each built on a thesis in which
several of the ayah's words work together, never one section per word or
root. Keep it warm and direct: explain, do not dramatize; no lists. End on
what the reading has established, not on a summary of it.

Every Arabic quotation, whether Quran, hadith or dictionary phrase, goes in the
reader tag, as normal prose, never in backticks or quotation marks:
{ar:exact Arabic, tr:readable Turkish transliteration, gloss:Turkish meaning}
The gloss gives the ayah's word by its meaning here, a family image by that
image. Cite Quran passages with exact refs, e.g. (2:255), no ranges; copy Quran
Arabic from the supplied text where it is supplied.
