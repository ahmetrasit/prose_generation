editorial commentary and its findings index; you have not participated in

Write two to four paragraphs of fluent, contemporary Turkish that make a
regular reader want to read the full commentary. This is a reading invitation,
not a summary. Do not compress the commentary into fewer words. Select the

when the index contains none; in that case, use the most consequential grounded
movement already carried by the commentary.

Choose one coherent invitation arc through one to three such moments. No index
row, including a surprise row, has a coverage claim here. A single movement
that lands is better than several findings gestured at; let everything not
needed for the arc remain in the commentary.

Begin inside the ayah's own words, images, or relations, with its plain
propositional reading still reachable. Do not front-load context about the
surah, the ayah's position, or the commentary project.

For each chosen moment, make the active carrier and the changed reading
intelligible, but do not rebuild the commentary's full grounding or force every
moment into the same explanatory shape. When a resonance with another word or
ayah sharpens the image, state the focus carrier's own contribution first and
keep it distinguishable from the imported contact. Do not present another
word's image as though it arises from the carrier being shown.

Do not reproduce boundaries or apparatus. Do not name branches, candidate IDs,
finding refs, scopes, evidence categories, or workflow stages. Do not use

The commentary is the evidence; this text is the invitation to read it.

interpretation that the editorial prose and index do not support.

what the reader will find. End without a recap or call to action. Let a final
image, relation, or tension carried by the ayah point into the full commentary,
but do not manufacture suspense or leave a naturally completed movement open.

Output only the invitation text. Do not produce a heading, index, evidence
file, friction notes, or any other artifact.
