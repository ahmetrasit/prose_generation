TURKISH DISCOVERY PROSE

This is a Turkish prose writing step. The Turkish prose must be a thematic synthesis, not a catalogue of ayat and not
  a sequence of disconnected lexical observations. Use the completed ledger. Use the ledger’s row order, row_index values, target_arabic values,
  context_refs, context_summary, relationship_to_focus_branches,
  surprise_strength, and discovery notes as the prose source of truth.



PRIMARY SURFACE AS ANCHOR, NOT VETO

  1. Begin by recording the focus passage’s observable surface:

  - its explicit actions, participants, objects, relations, and sequence;
  - its strongest lexical and grammatical signals;
  - its immediate local context.

  Treat this surface as a reference coordinate for discovery, not as a settled
  interpretation, ceiling, or rejection filter.

  For every contextual, intertextual, lexical, or structural finding, ask:

  1. What part of the surface remains visible?
  2. What relation, emphasis, branch, or latent dimension changes?
  3. Does the finding add a layer, reframe the scene, or create productive tension?
  4. Does it introduce a competing reading that should coexist rather than be resolved?

  Allow four possible outcomes:

  - additive: preserves the surface and adds a new dimension;
  - reframing: preserves the wording but shifts the scene’s center of gravity;
  - tension: keeps multiple readings active without selecting a winner;
  - exploratory displacement: proposes a substantially different emphasis, clearly
    marked as speculative.

  Do not require every finding to confirm the initial reading. Do not reduce a finding
  to “mere association” simply because it changes the primary emphasis. Do not force
  apparently conflicting readings into one harmonized conclusion.

  When two readings have independent textual triggers, preserve both. Explain:

  - the trigger supporting each reading;
  - the branch or feature activated;
  - what each reading makes newly visible;
  - whether the readings reinforce, qualify, or compete with one another.

  The final synthesis must contain three distinguishable layers:

  1. the observable surface;
  2. the changed or expanded reading produced by the finding;
  3. any unresolved coexistence, tension, or speculative displacement.

  Preserving the surface is required for traceability, not interpretive control.
  The purpose of the analysis is to discover what the surrounding evidence allows the
  focus passage to become—not to protect an assumed primary interpretation from change.



  2. Cluster findings by activation mechanism, not merely by shared surah or root.

  Possible thematic clusters include:

  - material mud, human origin, and recognition;
  - solar perception, shelter, and situated seeing;
  - reaching, confluence, and the opening of a new task;
  - finding a people, resource access, and practical goodness;
  - binary choice, initiative, truth-testing, and authority;
  - punishment, repair, compensation, and conduct-bound governance.

  Create new themes when the evidence requires them. Do not force unrelated
  findings into one theme.

  3. Make every included finding traceable.

  For each finding included in prose, visibly provide this sequence:

  a. target ayah and surrounding context;
  	- “Surrounding context” means the context_refs and context_summary recorded
  in the ledger, supplemented only by the immediately relevant adjacent ayat
  from the Arabic source. Do not invent contextual claims or silently import
  external tafsir. If a connection is context-only, label it as such and do
  not present it as direct lexical branch activation.
  
  b. Arabic trigger or repeated structure;
  c. activated focus branch and its meaning;
  d. activation mechanism;
  e. change or expansion in the reading of 18:86;
  f. discovery status or uncertainty where relevant.

  Do not write “this broadens the meaning” without explaining exactly what in the
  target context causes the broadening.

  4. Give each row a substantive landing.

  For the complete 155-row task, include every strong and medium finding by default.
  Weak findings may be omitted from the prose only when they add no distinct movement
  or are fully represented by a stronger finding. The exhaustive ledger remains the
  record of all rows.

  A row may support more than one theme, but do not repeat its entire explanation.
  Cross-reference it through a new contribution.

  5. Synthesize rather than list.

  Within each theme:

  - open with the theme’s interpretive claim;
  - explain the relevant target contexts in connected paragraphs;
  - move from Arabic trigger to branch activation;
  - show how several findings reinforce, redirect, or complicate one another;
  - finish with the theme’s surprise reading.

  Use transitions such as:

  - “Bu bağlantı şu nedenle önemlidir...”
  - “Hedef ayetin bağlamı bu dalı yalnızca tekrar etmez; onu...”
  - “Buradaki sürpriz genişleme...”
  - “Bu okuma, <surah>:<ayah> ’daki...”
  - “Bağlantı güçlüdür; ancak keşif düzeyindeki sınırı şudur...”

  6. Preserve distinct mechanisms.

  If two ayat share the same root but do different work, keep them separate.
  For example, one target may activate a branch through human origin, while another
  activates it through refusal or status conflict. Do not merge both into “mud imagery.”

  Likewise, distinguish:

  - direct lexical activation;
  - repeated grammatical structure;
  - same-surah narrative development;
  - contextual or scene-level analogy;
  - context-only candidate activation.

  7. Keep references visible.

  Place each target reference beside the movement it supports, for example:

  (15:26), (15:28, 15:33), (18:17), (28:23)

  If several ayat support one movement, list every ayah explicitly. Do not use vague
  phrases such as “other verses” or hide the references in a final bibliography.

  8. Use Arabic selectively but visibly.

  Show the decisive Arabic trigger and provide a short Turkish gloss. Do not reproduce
  whole ayat repeatedly when a word or phrase is sufficient. Keep branch IDs in the
  ledger; in prose, use the branch meaning, adding the ID when technical traceability
  helps.

  9. Distinguish discovery from conclusion.

  Use exploratory language for inferred bridges:

  - “şu okuma açılabilir”
  - “bu bağlam ... yönünde yankılanır”
  - “keşif düzeyinde ...”
  - “bağlamsal aday olarak ...”

  Do not use exploratory material as if it were the direct translation or settled
  meaning of 18:86. At the same time, do not weaken every finding into a disclaimer;
  the prose should pursue the surprising reading fully before stating its boundary.

  10. End with layered synthesis.

  The final section must state separately:

  - which observable surface features remain stable;
  - which interpreted relationships are expanded or revised;
  - which tensions remain unresolved.
  - what relationships become newly visible;
  - what the strongest surprise reading is;
  - which parts remain speculative or context-dependent.


  The final synthesis may propose an integrative arc. Use only the stages supported by the
  ledger. Omit, reorder, or replace any stage when the findings require it.
  Do not force unrelated findings into one or more progressions.



PROSE SELECTION

  The ledger is exhaustive. The prose is selective but must normally include every
  strong and medium finding. Weak findings may be omitted only when they add no
  distinct movement or are fully represented by a stronger finding. Any finding
  included in prose must retain its trigger, contextual rationale, branch relation,
  and resulting surprise reading.

  If several findings support one theme, synthesize them under a title but preserve
  each ayah reference and the distinct reason it contributes. Do not merge separate
  findings into an unsupported generalization.
  
  A finding may have a direct branch activation, an indirect branch
  activation, or no confirmed branch activation but a contextual candidate.
  Do not force every contextual candidate into an activated branch.

  Write your output to:

  _commentary/v8_batch/raw/s018-regular-20260912/s018/<surah>_<ayah>/<surah>_<ayah>.global.inter-ayah.prose.tr.md



  The prose must:

  1. Be written in fluent Turkish.
  2. Use clear Turkish titles for themes.
  3. Group related findings rather than presenting a mechanical row-by-row list.
  4. Preserve all major strong and medium findings.
  5. Explain the rationale visibly inside the prose.
  6. Keep target ayah references near the claims they support.
  7. List multiple ayah references explicitly when several contribute to one movement.
  8. Keep Arabic triggers visible with concise Turkish glosses where useful.
  9. Mention the relevant focus branch and explain its relationship to the target context.
  10. State whether a connection is lexical, structural, contextual, or speculative when that distinction matters.
  11. Avoid compression: do not turn several distinct findings into one vague summary.
  12. End with an integrated synthesis of the strongest surprise readings.
  13. The result is a coherent surprise-reading commentary.

  A suitable prose structure is:

  # Title

  Short note explaining that this is exploratory discovery prose.

  ## Theme 1

  Detailed synthesis with triggers, context, branch relations, and surprise reading.

  ## Theme 2

  Detailed synthesis with triggers, context, branch relations, and surprise reading.

  ## Theme 3

  Detailed synthesis with triggers, context, branch relations, and surprise reading.

  Additional themes as needed.

  ## Birleşen keşif / Integrated discovery

  A coherent final synthesis that gathers the strongest latent activations while preserving exploratory status.
