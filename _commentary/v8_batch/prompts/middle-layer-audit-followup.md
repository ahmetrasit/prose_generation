# Commentary V5 fixed same-agent middle-layer audit

Before finalizing, perform a fresh audit of the same middle-layer task. Re-read
the complete filled prompt, the complete editorial source, and the current
reader prose. Do not treat your earlier report, paragraph citations, validator
success, or your first-pass reasoning as proof of semantic completeness.

Do not write a claim ledger, inventory, audit report, metrics file, span map,
manifest, scratch file, or any other sidecar. Repair only the same reader-prose
output named in the filled prompt. Keep every inventory, cluster plan, metric,
and audit checklist transient.

Re-inventory every source paragraph at atomic granularity. Do not cap a source
paragraph at one unit: separate every independent carrier, trigger, mechanism,
branch contribution, composite effect, changed reading, concrete detail,
contextual-ayah role, qualification, boundary, and live alternative. Recheck
every equivalence and overlap classification, rebuild the cross-paragraph
synthesis clusters, and locate one explicit substantive prose landing for every
unit. Confirm word by word that negation, possibility, attribution,
conditionality, agency, referent, sequence, and scope remain unchanged.

Next, read the prose without relying on the inventory. Confirm that each
paragraph gives enough carrier, trigger, mechanism, consequence,
distinguishing-detail, and boundary clues for a reader to decide whether to
open its cited editorial paragraphs. Confirm that synthesis is not
source-paragraph mirroring, citations have not produced fragments or
claim-ledger rhythm, long prose has useful navigation headings, every Quran
reference is explicit rather than an interval, and the Turkish is continuous
and grammatically sound.

Recompute the structural metrics yourself rather than trusting your first
pass. If the source has at least ten prose paragraphs and retained-word ratio
exceeds `0.80`, output/source paragraph ratio exceeds `0.75`, or fewer than
`0.40` of output prose paragraphs are multi-source, perform a mandatory
adversarial synthesis challenge even when both validators pass.

These values are diagnostic triggers only: they are not targets, hard limits,
or evidence that the prose must become shorter. During the challenge, inspect
every repeated setup, recap, defensive qualification, and sequence of
single-source paragraphs developing the same carrier, question, image,
mechanism, or consequence. Privately write the strongest continuous merged
alternative instead of merely asserting that a merge would be unsafe. Compare
that alternative unit by unit with the current prose. Adopt it only if every
distinct unit retains an explicit substantive landing, every mechanism,
concrete detail, modality, qualification, boundary, and live alternative stays
visible, citations remain locally meaningful, and the Turkish becomes easier
to follow rather than merely shorter.

If a tested merge fails, keep the passages separate for a concrete semantic
reason. “Separate source paragraphs” and “different topics” are not sufficient.
Never delete, generalize, make implicit, or overload a finding to cross a
metric boundary; an irreducible result may legitimately remain beyond every
warning signal.

After every repair, repeat the source-to-prose and prose-to-source checks and
rerun both validator commands from the filled prompt until they report `ok`.
Do not stop after describing a defect: repair the prose and re-audit it. Report
the final validator results, word counts, source/output paragraph counts,
multi-source and single-source paragraph counts, same-position singleton
count, both ratios, and any unresolved concern. Do not persist those metrics or
any other audit artifact.
