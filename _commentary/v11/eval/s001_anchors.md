## V5 and V11 — per-ayah distinctive findings (from compare_s001_v5.md, verbatim)

V5 anchors = the **v5-only** items (minus echo/alternative-root imagery); V11 anchors = the **v11-only** items and the
v11 surah chains (the regression guard). Limits subset (must appear where they limit a reading): 57:15, 6:70, 2:256,
7:186, 18:17, 9:115, 17:15.

### 1:1

**v5-only (substantive)**

- **The ism root's "sky" branch read as rain and mercy**:
  - HFT `delta_1_2_rain_womb_cultivation`, with rain imprinting the earth and the womb shaping life.
  - Prose 25:48: winds as heralds of mercy, pure water sent down from the sky.
  - Prose 22:65: the sky held back from falling, "raʾūf raḥīm".
  - v11 only notes the sky branch (كل ما علاك فأظلك) and does not use it.
- **Qur'an refs v11 lacks**:
  - 9:118, *tāba ʿalayhim li-yatūbū*: divine turning makes human return possible. v11 has only 2:37 and 2:160.
  - 23:75: mercy relieves them, yet they persist in transgression.
  - 26:5 and 43:36: turning away from the *dhikr* of al-Raḥmān. This extends v11's 25:60 resistance.
  - 67:20, "which army can help you besides al-Raḥmān?", the pair to 21:42.
  - 24:22 and 2:182: Raḥīm enacted as giving to kin and reconciling people.
- **The name as a seal that bears account**. HFT `delta_1_4_account_bearing_seal` binds naming, ownership and the closure of reckoning in 1:4. v11 has *wasm* as a mark on the deed but never ties it to *dīn*.
- **Remote HFT images**: narrow swallowing gate, recurrence as lullaby, name as a mark that recovers what was lost, interior reconciliation. Also the variant-root pressure of "narrow passage / inner ring". v11 has none of these. Low value.
- **Pause (waqf) and case-ending bridge** (`variant-and-pause-bridge`). v11 records the shādhdh rafʿ/naṣb reading but says nothing about waqf.
- **"Worship and longing" pressure on the name Allah** (أله, "longing"). **[v11 rejects]**: v11 says explicitly that "أله بمعنى تحير" is outside the branch.

**v11-only (substantive)**

1. **Rasm**: *bi-smi* is written without alif only in the verbless formula (1:1, 11:41, 27:30). Where the verb is stated, the alif stays (96:1, 56:74, 56:96, 69:52): "the formula took its own written shape where the verb was left empty."
2. **27:31 *allā taʿlū ʿalayya*** matches the lexicon's derivation of ism from *ʿuluww*: height is reserved for the name, and the addressee is asked to lower himself. Also 87:1.
3. **The "silhouette" branch** (سما لي شخص; سماوة الهلال): the name makes the unseen discernible without bringing it near. Linked to *khashiya'r-Raḥmāna bi'l-ghayb* (36:11, 50:33).
4. **The Kufan *wasm* derivation**, as a documented alternative. Paired with "the name mentioned *upon*" (6:118, 6:121, 22:36): the basmala is a mark placed on the deed. v5 explicitly denied the "damga" reading here.
5. **11:43, *lā ʿāṣima… illā man raḥim***: the ark as a concrete vessel of mercy, and "bismillāh" as boarding.
6. **Raḥmān is exclusively divine, Raḥīm is shared with humans** (9:128, 48:29). The formula descends from an unshareable name to a shareable trait. Raḥīm appears in rescue narratives through the Shuʿarāʾ refrain (26:9, 26:68…).
7. **Tradition and counting**:
   - In the Kufan and Meccan counts the basmala is ayah 1; in the Medinan and Basran counts 1:7 splits instead.
   - 15:87.
   - Ibn ʿĀmir's *dhī'l-jalāl* at 55:78, which tests the line between ism and musammā.

### 1:2

**v5-only (substantive)**

- **Qur'an refs v11 lacks**:
  - **28:30**, *innī ana'llāhu rabbu'l-ʿālamīn*: the proper name and the universal title in one speaker. A striking omission in v11.
  - **5:28**: Abel refuses to kill out of fear of "Allah rabb al-ʿālamīn".
  - **39:29**: a man owned by quarrelling partners against a man owned by one master, followed by *al-ḥamdu lillāh*. This is the clearest Qur'anic image of one Rabb against many.
  - **18:82**: the Rabb wills that the orphans reach maturity. This gives concrete form to *rabb* as cultivating over time.
  - **52:48**, *bi-aʿyuninā* with *sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbik*: under watch while waiting.
  - **93:3–5**: not forsaken, and a gift to come.
  - 29:63: rain revives the earth, followed by the command to say *al-ḥamdu*.
  - 14:39, 110:3, 32:15: ḥamd with a child, with istighfār, and with sujūd.
  - 100:6: ingratitude toward one's Rabb.
- **Dictionary**: *al-ʿaylam* ("sea") in the ʿ-l-m family, set against 18:109, where the sea serves as ink for "the words of my Rabb".
- **HFT outliers**: *ecological water cycle* (cloud, rain, plant; from *rabāb* "cloud"); *gestational lordship*; *repair after breakage*.
- **HFT `covenantal_bundle`** (رباب العهد). **[v11 rejects]**: v11 notes the contract branch "does not work directly".

**v11-only (substantive)**

1. **Pharaoh's *wa mā rabbu'l-ʿālamīn*** (26:23), with Moses unfolding the ʿālamīn as heavens, forebears, east and west (26:24–28). The magicians pin the title down with "Rabb of Moses and Aaron" (26:47–48).
2. **26:18, *a-lam nurabbika***: this is ر ب و, not ر ب ب. Pharaoh makes nurture a claim to lordship. v11 marks it as an analogy only.
3. **26:77–82**: Abraham describes his Rabb in the Fatiha's own order (1:2, 1:6, 1:4). Also 20:49–50, "gave each thing its form, then guided".
4. **26:97–98, *nusawwīkum bi-rabbi'l-ʿālamīn***, with 6:1 *yaʿdilūn*: the damned define their straying as setting an equal beside this title. This puts *ad-ḍāllīn* against 1:2.
5. **Ḥamd is praise for a chosen act** (the lexicon excludes المدح لما لا اختيار فيه), with 3:188. Praising the Rabb of the worlds therefore claims the worlds were willed. The *yataḥammadu bi'l-minna* branch is the counter-image.
6. **ʿālam as signpost**: العالم… اسم لما يعلم به; العلم الجبل; 16:16, *ʿalāmāt* and the star. The signs then feed 1:6.
7. **17:44**: ḥamd with no subject is praise that runs on before the reader.
8. **12:39 and 9:31**: many *arbāb*, one Rabb with many worlds.

### 1:3

**v5-only (substantive)**

- **Inter-ayah boundary refs** (from the evidence file):
  - **11:119**, *illā man raḥima rabbuk wa li-dhālika khalaqahum*: mercy as the purpose of creation amid difference.
  - **19:69**: defiance toward al-Raḥmān.
  - 42:8: He admits whom He wills into His mercy.
  - 48:25.
  - 23:75–76.
- **Mercy at work in human relations and in revelation** (published prose). v11 has none of these:
  - **3:159**, *fa-bimā raḥmatin… linta lahum*;
  - **30:21**, *mawadda wa raḥma* between spouses;
  - **16:64 and 17:82**: the Qur'an as *hudā*, *shifāʾ* and *raḥma*;
  - 7:204: listen to the Qur'an so that you may receive mercy;
  - 28:46: the warning as a mercy;
  - **42:28**: rain after despair, *yanshuru raḥmatahu*;
  - **18:82 and 18:98**: mercy as keeping safe for a set term; also 36:44;
  - 17:100 and 43:32: treasuries of mercy, and who apportions it;
  - **6:133**, *al-ghaniyyu dhū'r-raḥma*;
  - 17:57: hoping for His mercy while fearing His punishment;
  - **10:21, 41:50 and 23:75**: mercy tasted and then misused ("this is mine");
  - 36:23;
  - kin and family mercy: 21:84, 19:53, 7:151, 59:10, 4:23 (*rabāʾib*);
  - 57:28: two portions of mercy and a light to walk by.
- **HFT surprises v11 lacks**:
  - *mercy preserves against hardening*, set against the stone-like hardness of 1:7;
  - *mercy as supported delivery*: womb and path, the B004 postpartum branch. **[v11 rejects B004]**;
  - *accounted kinship*: raḥim with *dayn*;
  - *recognizable mark*;
  - *soothing utterance*.
- **Kinship as a continuing bond.** v11 covers this differently: it reverses the image, since at 60:3 kin ties are of no use on the Day and the bond is ʿubūdiyya (19:93).

**v11-only (substantive)**

1. **Morphology from usage rather than pattern theory**:
   - Raḥmān always occurs definite, as a subject and name;
   - Raḥīm often occurs indefinite, as a predicate taking *bi-* (2:143, 33:43);
   - asyndeton set against 57:3's *wa*-listed names.
2. **The na't/badal parse**: Raḥmān as a name (17:110) supports badal, Raḥīm supports na't. Also the shādhdh rafʿ/naṣb readings, which loosen the tie to 1:2.
3. **The name whose root holds womb and kin is the one that refuses a birth relation** (19:88–93, 21:26, 43:81). The bond is ʿabd, carried into 1:5.
4. **78:37–38 and 6:12 reproduce the order Rabb, Raḥmān, Day.** Al-Raḥmān governs the silence of the Day (20:108, 19:85). Resurrection is "what al-Raḥmān promised" (36:52).
5. **67:19, birds held aloft by al-Raḥmān**: "Rabb of the worlds" made concrete wing-beat by wing-beat. v5 also has 67:19, but as provision.
6. **7:156 shows the move from Raḥmān to Raḥīm inside one verse** (it encompasses all, then "I will write it for those who fear"). This previews the surah's narrowing from the worlds to one path. Also 21:107 *raḥmatan li'l-ʿālamīn*.
7. **15:49–50 and 19:45**: mercy carries awe. The hinge is "not a cushion".

### 1:4

**v5-only (substantive)**

- **The Qur'an's own book-of-deeds and scale verses for *yawm ad-dīn***:
  - 17:13–14, *iqraʾ kitābak*;
  - 18:49;
  - 45:28–29;
  - 39:69;
  - 69:18 and 50:22;
  - **21:47**, the mustard seed on the scales;
  - **99:7–8**.

  v11 builds its "record" idea only by analogy from 2:282 and never cites these.
- **Caveats on the debt analogy**:
  - **57:15 and 6:70**: no ransom is accepted.
  - **2:256**, *lā ikrāha fi'd-dīn*: a boundary on the "abase / enslave" branch of dīn.

  v11 builds the debt and enslavement branches without these checks.
- **Dīn as sincere devotion in crisis**: **10:22, 29:65, 31:32** (*mukhliṣīna lahu'd-dīn* in the storm, then relapse).
- **102:8**: asked about *an-naʿīm* on that day. This links 1:4 to 1:7's *anʿamta*.
- **Other refs**:
  - 11:105: no one speaks except by leave; wretched and happy;
  - 53:26;
  - 84:8, *ḥisāban yasīrā*;
  - 34:30 and 56:50: the appointed day;
  - 42:13, *aqīmū'd-dīn wa lā tatafarraqū*;
  - 42:21;
  - 79:40.
- **HFT readings**:
  - *aided liability*, *advance from the creditor*: the owner supplies the capacity to answer. This is partial: v11 has "only the creditor can forgive";
  - *cultivated maturity*, *gestated settlement*;
  - *decisive passage*, *hardened boundary*;
  - *customary regime change*. **[v11 rejects the ʿāda "habit" branch]**.
- **The adverbial *yawma* reading** (`qiraat-adverbial-contrast`) is only partly covered. v11 notes shādhdh *malaka yawma'd-dīn* but not the adverb contrast.

**v11-only (substantive)**

1. **Named qirāʾāt**: *mālik* is read by Ḥafṣ, Kisāʾī, Yaʿqūb and Khalaf; *malik* by Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Shuʿba and Ḥamza. The small-alif rasm carries both. The Qur'an-internal twins are 3:26 (the only other singular *mālik*) and 114:1–3 (*malik*), so the mushaf opens and closes on rabb with mālik/malik.
2. **82:17–19, *yawma lā tamliku nafsun li-nafsin shayʾā***: the Qur'an's own definition of *yawm ad-dīn*, built on the same m-l-k verb. "Mālik yawm ad-dīn" is its reverse face: when every hand is emptied, one hand remains.
3. **Dictionary: milk as a firm grip**: *malaktu'l-ʿajīn* ("kneaded firmly"); يده فيه قوية. Tied to *bi-yadihi'l-mulk* (67:1, 36:83) and to humans' derivative ownership (36:71).
4. **Dīn is also enslaving and possessing** (دانه… ملكته; المدين العبد). **56:86, *ghayra madīnīn* = *ghayra mamlūkīn***, the challenge to hold back one's own soul. Also 37:53 and 37:20–21 (*yawm al-faṣl*).
5. **Payment language**: 24:25, *yuwaffīhimu'llāhu dīnahum*; 3:185. **2:281 sits next to the debt contract of 2:282.**
6. **107:1–2**: denying *ad-dīn* is pushing away the orphan. 83:1–11 sets the scale scene with both of the Fatiha's phrases.
7. **12:76, *fī dīni'l-malik***: dīn as a king's law, which activates the *malik* reading.
8. **Dīn is already His** (16:52 *wāṣiban*, 39:3). The Day only reveals it. Also 26:82: hope directed to the owner of the Day.

### 1:5

**v5-only (substantive)**

- **Misdirected help-seeking and worship**:
  - **36:74**: they took gods "hoping to be helped". The sharpest foil to *iyyāka nastaʿīn*.
  - **25:43**: one who takes his desire as his god.
  - **13:14**: palms stretched toward water that never reaches the mouth.
  - **10:18 and 39:3**: worship justified as intercession or nearness. 39:3 is cited in v11, but only for *dīn khāliṣ*.
  - 5:76: worship of what has no power to harm or benefit.
- **Human mediation bounded**: **3:79**, a prophet may not call people to be his servants, though teaching stays; **71:3**, worship and obedience to the messenger stated separately.
- **Worship joined to social ethics**:
  - 4:36: worship with kindness to kin, neighbours and dependants;
  - 7:85: worship, then give full measure;
  - 2:83.
- **2:286**: the detailed petition for help, for when one forgets, errs or cannot bear the load.
- **Other refs**: 20:14; 6:102 (*wakīl*); 18:14 (hearts strengthened, they stood up); 22:77 and 53:62 (sujūd with ʿibāda).
- **HFT readings**:
  - *help as gift, not wage*;
  - *formed for service* (help builds capacity);
  - *present provision against debt*;
  - *obstacle-breaking aid*;
  - *seasoned conflict backing*, from *ḥarb ʿawān*, "renewed war". v11 notes only the "cow" ʿawān of 2:68;
  - *watchful-eye aid*. **[v11 rejects ع ي ن]**.

**v11-only (substantive)**

1. **The *iyyā* + ʿibāda construction in three persons**: 29:56 (God: *fa-iyyāya faʿbudūn*); 2:172 and 16:114 (conditional). 1:5 is the servants' reply in the same grammar.
2. **Day-of-gathering scenes built on the same construction**: 34:40–41, 10:28–29, 28:63 ("was it *you* they worshipped?"). 1:5 answers that question in advance, right after 1:4.
3. **Fronted object as a reply to a rival proposal** (39:14, 39:64, 39:66). Also 20:33 *nusabbiḥaka*: the attached "-ka" is detached and fronted in 1:5.
4. **ʿabd = mamlūk** (العبد وهو المملوك) links m-l-k from 1:4's owner to 1:5's owned. The lexicon separates ʿabd *bi'l-ījād* (a state) from ʿibāda (an act). One dictionary even glosses the verse itself (إياك نعبد إياك نطيع…).
5. **26:22, *ʿabbadta banī Isrāʾīl***: human enslavement is the counter-scene that "only You" cancels. 16:75 pairs ʿabd with mamlūk.
6. **Every *istiʿāna* in the Qur'an comes with patience** (2:45, 2:153, 7:128, 12:18), and 19:65 makes worship a burden that needs patience. The contrast is *bi-* as instrument versus "You" as direct object.
7. **Performative reading**: said in ṣalāt, 1:5 carries out 2:45 ("seek help through prayer"). 40:60 makes asking itself worship, so the second clause stays inside the first.
8. **Abraham's scene 26:70–82**: *naʿbudu aṣnāman* (object not fronted), and "do they hear you when you call?", since address needs a hearer. Plus concrete help: feeding, watering, healing.

**v11-only (substantive)**

1. **38:22 is the only other *ihdinā* in the Qur'an.** Litigants ask David to judge truly, not overreach, and guide them to "the middle of the path". v5 cites 38:22 but misses that it is unique. Together with **17:35 and 26:182** (*al-qisṭās al-mustaqīm*) and the lexicon's *qāma mīzān an-nahār* and *dīnār qāʾim*, this reads *mustaqīm* as a balance arm, which frames the two exclusions of 1:7.
2. **Lexicon: *hādī* = what goes in front**: the guide, and the staff that goes a step ahead (الدليل… لتقدمه; العصا هاديا). With 13:7. *Hidya* = direction, alongside qibla (هدية أمره), which ties to 2:142.
3. **Istiqāma = إذا انقاد واستمرت طريقته**: yielding to the lead and continuing without a break. Guidance and uprightness are one motion (81:26–29). Also *qawma*, the standing between rakʿas: the body asks for *mustaqīm* while standing in qiyām.
4. **7:16 and 15:41**: Iblīs lies in ambush on "Your straight path". The answer is *hādhā ṣirāṭun ʿalayya mustaqīm*. Also 16:9 and 92:12, "guidance is Ours to give".
5. **Traveller's uses of h-d-y**: 28:22 (Moses heading to Midian), 20:10 (hoping for a guide at the fire), 27:63.
6. **4:68–69**: the straight path, then *alladhīna anʿama'llāhu ʿalayhim… wa ḥasuna ulāʾika rafīqā*. The path is a caravan of companions. Also 37:118, the identical syntax for Moses and Aaron.
7. **23:73–74**: whoever does not believe in the hereafter swerves from *aṣ-ṣirāṭ*, which links 1:4 to 1:6. 37:22–23: the same imperative and the same noun lead to the path of Hell.
8. ***Ṣirāṭ* occurs 45 times and is never plural**, while *subul* is plural. Dispersing paths (6:153) differ from *subul as-salām* (5:16).

### 1:7

**v5-only (substantive)**

- **God as agent of *iḍlāl***: **7:186** (whom God leads astray has no guide), **18:17** (no *walī murshid* for him), **9:115** (He does not lead astray after guiding until He makes clear). This balances v11's stress that *ad-ḍāllīn* is "their own motion". v11 cites only Satan (36:62) and 28:15 *muḍill*.
- **17:15**: whoever strays, strays against himself; no one bears another's load.
- **38:26**: straying from God's path because they forgot *yawm al-ḥisāb*. This is a direct 1:7–1:4 bridge. v11 makes the bridge through 23:74 and 56:51 instead.
- **Blessing as a test**: **39:49**, "I was given it for my knowledge" (it is a trial); 89:15 and 73:11; **102:8**, asked about *an-naʿīm*.
- **Refs illustrating the *ghayr* branches**: **35:37**, *naʿmal ṣāliḥan ghayra'lladhī kunnā naʿmal* (substitution); **47:38**, *qawman ghayrakum*; 10:15. HFT `accountable_partition` reads ghayr as compensation and replacement.
- **Other refs**:
  - **43:59**: a servant "whom We favoured" (*anʿamnā ʿalayhi*) and made an example;
  - **14:36**: Abraham, "whoever disobeys me, You are *ghafūr raḥīm*";
  - 33:67 and 21:54: straying through leaders and ancestors;
  - 5:6 and 16:81: completion of favour as ease and protection.
- **HFT readings**: *hydrological flow* (ghayr "watering"); *gestational passage*; *watchful visibility* (ع ي ن); *fracture and standing*.

**v11-only (substantive)**

1. **The two ends of 1:7 pass through one life.** Moses says *bi-mā anʿamta ʿalayya* (28:17) and *wa ana mina'ḍ-ḍāllīn* (26:20) about the same act. Add 2:198 ("you were among the straying") and 93:7. The three classes are passable states of the "we", not fixed populations. v5 cites 28:17, 26:20 and 2:198 separately and draws no conclusion.
2. **6:77**, *la-in lam yahdinī rabbī la-akūnanna mina'l-qawmi'ḍ-ḍāllīn*: Rabb, guidance and *ḍāllīn* in one sentence, "the Fatiha's skeleton".
3. **Israel's history**: *anʿamtu ʿalaykum* (2:40, 2:47) turns to *ghaḍabin ʿalā ghaḍab* (2:61, 2:89–90). With 8:53, a blessing can turn. This grounds the *ṣifa* reading of *ghayri*: a blessing that has not turned to wrath.
4. ***Maghḍūb* occurs only here in the Qur'an.** Everywhere else wrath has God as stated agent (60:13, 58:14, 4:93, 48:6). The participle stays singular because its subject is *ʿalayhim*.
5. **20:81, *fa-yaḥilla ʿalaykum ghaḍabī… fa-qad hawā***: wrath "descends" on those who transgress in their provision. The same *ʿalā* carries favour and wrath, and it is absent for *ḍāllīn*.
6. **15:56**, only the *ḍāllūn* despair of their Rabb's mercy; with 60:13. This rings the ending back to the opening Raḥmān/Raḥīm.
7. **5:60**: both qualities in one group, so *wa lā* excludes qualities, not two populations. **7:30 and 18:104**: those astray think they are guided, so the prayer must name them. 37:69–70: straying also has footprints.
8. **Dictionary and tradition**:
   - *ad-ḍālla… lā yuʿrafu rabbuhā*: the stray camel's owner is its *rabb*, which gives a 1:2 ↔ 1:7 frame;
   - 16:9 uses the exact lexicon words *qaṣd* and *jāʾir*;
   - madd lāzim (six counts) falls on *ad-ḍāllīn*;
   - [recall] ʿAdī b. Ḥātim's Jews/Christians gloss is shown as exemplifying an open class;
   - āmīn, and the ḥadīth qudsī dividing the Fatiha.

### Surah-level chains

**What v5 has at surah level**:

- **Surah reading, 6 chains**:
  - **A.** Name as legible mark → ʿālamīn as signposts → *ihdinā/mustaqīm* as a readable route → *anʿamta*, lives as markers.
  - **B.** Protected gestation → sustained care: raḥim → rabb as cultivating → the pair returns in 1:3 → *nastaʿīn* as capacity → *ihdinā* as being kept on the path → *anʿamta* as softening and upkeep.
  - **C.** A given direction and the living answer: mālik's hand transfers → *iyyāka* → hidāya as *hadiyya* (gift) → the giver of *anʿamta*.
  - **D.** The recognized path walked together: ʿālamīn signs → *ṭarīq muʿabbad* → 67:22 supported upright walk → a communal, kin-like ni'ma.
  - **E.** From received good to measured answer: ḥamd as valuation → mālik/debt/account → *naʿbudu/nastaʿīn* → *al-qisṭās al-mustaqīm* → three relations.
  - **F.** Keeping the trace inside the passage: womb interior → *ṣirāṭ* as a swallowing passage → *ḍāllīn* hidden or lost.
- **7 channel briefs**:
  - *iḥsān–övgü–öğrenme döngüsü*: favour precedes praise;
  - *bakımla kurulan doğruluk*: uprightness built through care, repair, body made upright, and "apparent straightness can be blind";
  - *birlikte tutulan aidiyet*: leading the flock, loss as being unclaimed, cohesion against dispersal;
  - *bugünkü doğrultudan sonraki kalkışa*: q-w-m standing now and rising on the Day;
  - *çift yönlü takdim*: guidance as gift and offering;
  - *korunan ilişkinin sınırı*: covenant safe-conduct, wrath as a protective boundary;
  - *yaşam desteği ve yerleşim*: water system, rain-growth cycle, settlement.

**v5 chains v11 lacks**:

- **B, the gestation-to-cultivation arc.** v11 has no womb chain. It reads raḥim only lexically in 1:1 and 1:3, and in 1:3 it turns the womb image against a birth relation.
- **C and *çift yönlü takdim*, the gift/offering circuit through mālik, hidāya and anʿamta.** [v11 rejects the hady/hediyya bridge.]
- ***Yaşam desteği* (water, rain, settlement) and *korunan ilişki* (covenant).** Both absent. The covenant branch is [v11 rejects].
- ***Bakımla kurulan doğruluk* as structural repair and cohesion.** Absent as a chain. Its "apparent straightness can be blind" point is covered in 1:7 (7:30, 18:104).
- ***Bugünkü doğrultudan sonraki kalkışa*.** Partial: v11 has 83:6 standing, *qawma* in prayer, and 23:74, but not resurrection *qiyām* as a chain from 1:4 to 1:6.
- **A (mark → signpost → readable route → people as markers).** Only in pieces: 16:16 in 1:2 and *ihdinā* in 1:6. No surah chain.

**v5 chains v11 covers**:

- D, through "Yolun iki tanımı", "Biz'in yeri" and "Boyun eğişin sözlük ipliği".
- E, through "Hesap günü ve yoldaki ayrışma" and "Hamdin halkası".
- F, through "Yolda kaybolmak, yolu yitirmek" (the same observation without the womb start).
- *İḥsān–övgü*, through "Hamdin halkası" and the 1:2 ḥamd ↔ anʿamta link.
- *Birlikte tutulan aidiyet*, through "Sahip… sahibini yitiren" and 6:153.

**v11 chains that are new**:

1. **Ertelenen tümleç**: each ayah fills the previous gap (elided verb → object-less *nastaʿīn* → *ṣirāṭ* → 1:7). v5 has only the open start in 1:1.
2. **Edatların yolculuğu**: bi- → li- → iyyā- → ʿalā ×2 → no particle.
3. **Tek cümlenin uzaması**: 1:2–1:4 form one genitive sentence, and the break into address is the result of the chain. v5 hypothesis `h01` is only a seed of this.
4. **Adın dolması ve zamire çekilmesi**: the name fills with titles, then collapses into "You", against 12:40's empty names.
5. **Rahmetin üç görünüşü, öfkenin tek geçişi**: adjective → adjective → *anʿamta* as verb, against the unique agentless *maghḍūb*.
6. **Sahip, sahip olunan, sahibini yitiren**: rabb → mālik/madīn → ʿabd = mamlūk → *ḍālla* whose rabb is unknown. Also 15:56.
7. **Özne ve kip**: verbless → "we" → imperative → explicit agent → hidden agent → self-motion.
8. **Hamdin halkası**: the Fatiha opens with the last word of the saved (10:10, 39:75, 7:43).
9. **Boyun eğişin sözlük ipliği**: *inqiyād/dhull* recurs in the definitions of dīn, ʿibāda, *muʿabbad* and istiqāma.
10. **Namazda söylenen ve yapılan**: the Fatiha as a performative within ṣalāt.
11. **Kur'an'daki eşdizilimler**: 78:37–38, 6:12, 26:77–82, 36:60–62, 3:51, 6:77 reproduce the Fatiha's sequence.
12. **Kapsamın daralması**: from the worlds to one path, with 7:156.
13. **"Biz"in yeri**: the classes are passable states.
14. **Ses**: -īm/-īn rhyme, and the longest syllable falls on the word for straying.
