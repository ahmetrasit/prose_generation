# The brief (v11 surah pass, arm S): chains across the surah, and the surah's reading (Turkish)

Everything above this brief is evidence: the whole surah and the Fatiha (context.md); the branch table of the
surah's roots (every attested branch of every root, from the classical dictionaries); the seed sheet (image
systems a first whole-surah pass proposed, before the ayat were read one by one); and the findings ledger of every
ayah, each written when that ayah was read on its own (with the seeds in view). Nothing here is settled; judge it.

Reading one ayah at a time cannot see what only the whole surah shows: an image begun in one ayah and completed,
reversed or answered in another; a root, sound or form that returns in a new role; a scene whose parts are spread
over several ayat; the surah's movement as one argument (al-Biqāʿī's *naẓm* and the surah's purpose, *maqṣūd*).
That is your task. The familiar interpretation is one reading among the possible ones; a supported surprise is
welcome. Rigour comes from exact evidence, not from caution.

You may use any branch in the branch table (cite it `root Bnnn`), any finding in the ledgers, the seeds, and the
Quran from your own knowledge (quotations are checked afterwards). A seed that the ayah ledgers did not take up is
not refuted by that; judge it on the evidence. Keep every reading containable: it adds to the plain meaning and
never replaces it.

### Part 1 — chains (a record for the editor)

Every chain that runs across two or more ayat, as many as the surah supports. For each:

`### <short name>`
`- ayat: S:A, S:A, …` (the members, in the order the chain moves)
`- parts: <for each member: the word, its branch (root Bnnn) or finding, and its role — source, conduit, guide,
pressure, release, container, witness, loss, reversal …>`
`- movement: <how the parts act on one another across the ayat>`
`- staging: <where the Quran tells this scene openly, S:A refs>`
`- limits: <ayat that bound or complicate it>`
`- changes: <what the surah says through this chain that no single ayah says>`
`- grade: core | develop | note`
`- sources: <seed names and ayah-ledger findings it builds on, by ayah>`

Name mechanisms, not themes: a chain is complementary roles in motion, not a list of words from one field.

### Part 2 — the surah's reading (Turkish, for a reader who knows no Arabic)

The reader has complained that a compressed summary is impossible to follow: they cannot see what is coming from
where. So: after a short opening on how the surah moves from its first ayah to its last, give **every core chain
its own section** (`##` heading), and in it walk the reader through the chain explicitly — which ayah, which word,
which attested sense of that word (show the Arabic in a tag), what role it plays, how it hands on to the next
member, where the Quran tells the same scene openly, what bounds it, and what the reader now hears in the plain
reading that they could not hear before. Develop `develop` chains more briefly, in their own sections or together.
Close with the surah's purpose as the chains show it, and how it leads into the next surah. Do not retell the ayah
readings; the reader has them. There is no length limit: explicitness is the requirement.

Arabic in the reading is written `{ar:ARABIC, tr:transliteration, gloss:Türkçe karşılık, source:…}`: `source` is the
ayah it is quoted from (`source:29:45`) or the dictionary branch it is copied from (`source:ص ل و B006`), several
items comma-separated. No comma inside ar or tr, no colon inside gloss, no curly braces elsewhere. After a quotation
from another ayah also cite `(S:A)` in the prose.
