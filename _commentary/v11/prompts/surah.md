# The brief (v11 surah pass): chains across the surah (all in Turkish)

Everything above this brief is evidence: the whole surah and the Fatiha (context.md), and the findings ledger of
every ayah of the surah, each written when that ayah was read on its own. Each ledger line has its refs, roots and a
grade (core/develop/note).

Reading one ayah at a time cannot see what only the whole surah shows: an image that begins in one ayah and is
completed, reversed or answered in another; a root, sound or form that returns in a new role; a scene whose parts
are spread over several ayat; the surah's movement as one argument. That is your task. This follows al-Biqāʿī's
*naẓm* (the order and relations of the surah's parts) and al-Khūlī's reading of words through their uses; the
familiar interpretation is one reading among the possible ones, and a supported surprise is welcome.

Work from the ledgers and the surah text. Quran from memory is allowed (quotations are checked afterwards); do not
introduce dictionary senses the ledgers do not record.

## Your final message: three parts, with these exact marker lines

===== CHAINS =====
===== SURAH =====
===== AYAT =====

### Part 1 — chains (a record for the editor)

Every chain that runs across two or more ayat, as many as the surah supports. For each:

`### <short name>`
`- ayat: S:A, S:A, …` (the members, in the order the chain moves)
`- parts: <for each member: the word or image, and its role — source, pressure, release, container, witness, reversal …>`
`- movement: <how the parts act on one another across the ayat: what supplies, redirects, obstructs, reverses or completes what>`
`- changes: <what the surah says through this chain that no single ayah says>`
`- grade: core | develop | note`
`- ledgers: <the ayah-ledger findings it builds on, by ayah>`

Name mechanisms, not themes: a chain is complementary roles in motion, not a list of words from one field.

### Part 2 — the surah's reading

A Turkish reading of the whole surah for a reader who knows no Arabic, built on the `core` chains: how the surah
moves from its first ayah to its last, and what it lets one hear that the single-ayah readings could not. Do not
retell the ayah readings; assume the reader has them. Use `##` headings only where the movement needs them. Arabic
that does interpretive work is written `{ar:ARABIC, tr:transliteration, gloss:Türkçe karşılık}` (three fields, no
comma inside ar or tr, no colon inside gloss); after a quotation from another ayah cite `(S:A)`.

### Part 3 — for each ayah

`- S:A: <one or two sentences: where this ayah stands in the chains, and what the whole surah adds to it>`
