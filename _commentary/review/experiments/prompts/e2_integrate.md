Write the finished Turkish commentary of the focus Quranic ayah. The reader knows
neither Arabic nor how lexical families and associations work. Let the reader
see what the ayah makes possible to hear. Do not use tools, delegate, browse or
inspect files. Return only the reader's prose in Markdown. This is an
independent interpretation, not a catalogue, a translation expanded with
footnotes, or a report on an analytical workflow.

You receive two finished readings of this ayah, written independently from
different evidence, and the context file (the ayah, its words, an anchor
translation, the Fatiha and the whole surah). Compose one commentary from them.
- A reading that changes how the ayah is understood keeps its place. Where two
  readings illuminate each other, join them into one movement; where they are
  independent, let them coexist. No reading is declared the correct one.
- Never refute a reading from one of its members, and never drop a reading only
  because the other reading does not mention it. Where other ayat support,
  extend, shift or contradict a latent reading, say so after the reading has
  been developed; a contradiction is stated, not used to cancel it.
- Keep each reading's evidence: its Arabic quotations with their tags and
  sources, and the word or phrase in the ayah that activates it.
- Your own knowledge of Arabic and of the whole Quran is open to you. Mark a
  sense, phrase, count or report that you add from memory, rather than take
  from the two readings or the context file, with [bellek].
- A dictionary sense that belongs to a fixed phrase is heard here only as an
  echo of that phrase unless the phrase itself is in the ayah; say so in one
  clause when you use it. A word that the Quran uses in one recurring role
  elsewhere is stated as the Quran's usage there, with references, not as the
  word's meaning here.

First understand the focus in its immediate grammar and scene. Then develop
the consequential resonances into connected readings. Do not merely rewrite
each finding in a separate paragraph. Let a concrete detail make the next
detail necessary: what changes, through what operation, with what consequence
for the focus? An image chain earns space through the new understanding it
enables. Sustain its material specificity long enough for that consequence to
become visible, before any generic label.

Several independent readings can coexist without being subordinate to one
thesis. Give them the space their development needs. There is no word target,
section quota, required number of verses, or symmetry requirement. Select for
explanatory importance; novelty alone is neither a reason to include nor to
exclude. Use headings only when they help the reader follow a real movement.

Distinguish same word from same root, and textual repetition from interpretive
analogy. Let other ayat supply indispensable stages, contrasts and
consequences, rather than gathering references after an already finished
claim. Do not infer the chronology of revelation from surah order.

Show the non-Arabic reader where an image comes from: introduce the relevant
word, explain the attested family image or form, then demonstrate its
connection to another detail before drawing the reading. A dictionary sense of
a related noun does not become the translation of this verb. An image can
remain powerful as an analogy when stated accurately. Do not invent sources,
vowels, etymologies, lexical meanings, historical facts or citations.

Write natural, precise Turkish. Follow a question, image or developing insight,
not the order of the two readings. Retain productive tension and contrast.
Avoid repetitive constructions of the form "this image meets that image and
opens a field." Avoid restarting the same discovery in several sections. Do not
end every paragraph with a defensive disclaimer about what the verse does not
say. State an actual qualification at the point where the reader needs it,
then continue the reading. Internal labels, confidence ratings and workflow
language do not belong in the prose; do not mention the two readings.

Format Arabic in the established reader tag:
{ar:exact Arabic, tr:readable Turkish transliteration, gloss:Turkish meaning}
Every Arabic quotation must be intelligible in its paragraph. Use these tags as
normal prose, never inside backticks. Cite Quran passages with exact refs, e.g.
(S:A, S:A); do not use ayah ranges. Copy Quran Arabic from the context file or
the readings. Distinguish a dictionary quotation from Quran wording.

Before returning, read as someone encountering these relations for the first
time: can that person explain how the main images work together and reread the
focus with a changed understanding? Remove mere inventories, unsupported
bridges, repeated qualifications and restatements. Preserve the discoveries
that made the change possible.
