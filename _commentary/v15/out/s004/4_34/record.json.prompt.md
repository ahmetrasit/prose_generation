# Ayah reading

You are reading ayah 4:34 with every branch of every one of its words open, inside its neighbourhood and its surah. You produce the record from which its commentary will be written: everything you hear, each finding anchored and contained, each with what it makes perceptible. You do not write the commentary; you output JSON.

## Who this is for

One reader: a curious Turkish speaker with almost no Arabic grammar. Their Arabic arrives through loanwords, and those loanwords, theological ones above all, have shifted, narrowed or lost their meaning in Turkish. Canonical meanings are easy for them to find elsewhere; they are only the anchor. What this work gives them is what they cannot find elsewhere: what the Arabic words keep alive beneath the plain sense. Success: after reading, they understand the ayah and the surah better than before, and they are never disoriented.

## How a sense becomes heard

- Tafsir and translation choose one meaning; the Arabic keeps several alive at once. Other attested senses of a word's root can be heard beside the plain sense, and they often weave images that run through a surah and meet its main theme from several sides, each audible to a reader in a particular condition.
- Every attested branch of every root is open. Branches have no order. A branch is heard when something activates it: the ayah's own words, neighbouring words, words elsewhere in the surah, or a Quran passage that explains the ayah.
- The dictionary is both guard and supplier. Use the branches given (and the full entries you may read). A sense you recall that the dictionary does not attest must be marked as memory; never use it silently. No invented senses, sources, etymologies or chronology.
- Do not prune early. Keep partial, strange and minor activations. Combine fragments across words, branches and roles. Let a completed image strengthen its weak members. Only then ask what it shows about the plain reading. No plausibility filter, no confidence scores, no verdict on an image from one of its members.
- Integration, not aggregation: the branches of one root are often facets of one concept; finding that concept is itself a finding.
- Containment: every latent reading can be said in one sentence that keeps the plain reading intact. Never "not X but Y".
- The test that matters: what does this make perceptible in the plain reading that a paraphrase could not (more spatial, bodily, causal, relational, material, temporal, compositional)? Space in the commentary will follow that payoff; it is a matter of function, not of certainty.
- Readings coexist. Never declare one correct.

## What you have (below the line)

1. The ayah in its neighbourhood (±7 ayat; the focus is marked).
2. Its words with roots, lemmas and parts of speech.
3. The dictionary for its roots, one line per branch (branch | Arabic image | Arabic definition, trimmed). Alternative roots are marked ~alt with the reason.
4. Scene lines touching its words: first in the neighbourhood, then scenes that reach elsewhere in the surah (each with at most a few far words, those adding roles the neighbourhood lacks first, and a file listing every member). Members of one scene activate one another: several words supplying different parts of one scene are themselves an activation. Mechanical and generous: an ordering aid, not a worklist.
5. The plan from the window reading: the images this ayah should open, advance or complete, the images earlier ayat opened, and the window's movement. Use it; you are not bound by it. If the ayah opens an image the plan missed, record it.
6. The concordance for its lemmas: every use (lemmas used up to 30 times) or a use profile (frequent lemmas).
7. Variant readings.
8. Turkish loanword cards for its lemmas: what the Turkish reader hears, what the Arabic keeps.

You may read more from the paths listed at the end. Read only what a specific question needs; do not read in bulk.

## The record

- ref: 4:34.
- ground: the plain sense in two to four sentences, as a careful translator would give it.
- findings: every latent reading, local resonance, image member, root concept, Quran-loaded word, variant and grammar point you hear. For each:
  - id (F1, F2, …); kind: latent | image | local_resonance | concept | loaded_word | variant | grammar;
  - anchor: the word (ref S:A:W, surface) and the dictionary branch (root with spaces, branch Bnnn) the finding stands on (branch may be empty for grammar);
  - triggers: the words (ref, surface) that activate it and how (shared scene, sound, shared root, syntax, a neighbouring or explaining passage);
  - statement: one containment sentence (plain reading kept);
  - perceptible: what it makes perceptible in the plain reading;
  - image: the plan's image id, a new id you give, or "";
  - memory: true if any part rests on a sense the dictionary does not attest;
  - for_prose: lead | support | record. Lead: this ayah's commentary should make it perceptible; support: it can serve a lead finding; record: kept here, not needed in this ayah's prose. Decide by payoff for this reader in this ayah, respecting the plan.
- loaded_words: words the Quran uses with one recurring role (read it from the concordance: the role, and how many uses keep it), and where a usual translation departs from that role here.
- losses: for the key Turkish words the reader will meet for this ayah (from the loanword cards and your knowledge of Turkish; memory true for what the cards do not give): what the Turkish word loses or adds against the Arabic here.
- grammar: only grammar the Turkish reader cannot hear and that matters to a reading here, one line each; else empty.
- variants: variant readings that open or support a reading, one line each; else empty.
- reread: the plain reading read anew with the findings heard together, three to six sentences.
- disclosed: the image ids this ayah's commentary will make audible (so later ayat can build on them).
- set_aside: activations you considered and did not keep, each with why. Nothing is lost silently.

Do not audit your own findings for plausibility; the payoff test is the only test here. Evidence from the rest of the Quran will be gathered separately after this reading. Analysis in English; Arabic as in the text. Output only the JSON object required by the schema.

## Plan from the window reading

- opens: I8 The rising, the split, and the fitting — Ground rises up and juts out. In the shared bed, one sleeper turns away from the other. What lay between the two, the link joining them, splits into two halves, each on its own side. Each family sends one arbiter. If they both want repair, God fits the two back together, the way an arrow's notch is seated on the bowstring. Above it all, God is the One truly high and great.. Members: تَخَافُونَ 4:34:23 B001 fear of what is coming; نُشُوزَهُنَّ 4:34:24 B001 rising up, jutting out; نُشُوزَهُنَّ 4:34:24 B004 marital estrangement (plain sense; the dictionary notes it can come from either spouse); فَعِظُوهُنَّ 4:34:25 B001 a reminder that softens the heart; وَٱهْجُرُوهُنَّ 4:34:26 B001 cutting off contact; ٱلْمَضَاجِعِ 4:34:28 B002 the bed shared by two; وَٱضْرِبُوهُنَّ 4:34:29 B001 a blow (plain sense of the third step); أَطَعْنَكُمْ 4:34:31 B002 coming back into accord; عَلِيًّۭا 4:34:39 B001 the true height, against every rising up; كَبِيرًۭا 4:34:40 B001 greatness; شِقَاقَ 4:35:3 B001 cracking open; شِقَاقَ 4:35:3 B002 each on his own half, his own side; شِقَاقَ 4:35:3 B004 a group split by discord (plain sense); بَيْنِهِمَا 4:35:4 B003 the link that stands between them; فَٱبْعَثُوا۟ 4:35:5 B002 sending envoys; حَكَمًۭا 4:35:6 B005 the arbiter given authority to decide; أَهْلِهِۦ 4:35:8 B001 his people, the side he belongs to; أَهْلِهَآ 4:35:11 B001 her people; إِصْلَٰحًۭا 4:35:14 B002 removing the estrangement; يُوَفِّقِ 4:35:15 B001 two things made to fit together; يُوَفِّقِ 4:35:15 B006 seating the arrow's notch on the bowstring; بَيْنَهُمَآ 4:35:17 B003 the link restored; خَبِيرًۭا 4:35:22 B001 knowing what is inside the matter. Perceptible: The quarrel gets shape and direction. nushūz is ground rising and jutting. shiqāq is one thing cracking into two halves, each going to its side (shiqq). bayn is the joining link itself, and it is what cracks. The remedy mirrors the split: one arbiter from each half. Then God's tawfīq makes two things fit, like a notch meeting a string. It is God who fits them, and only if the two want repair. Ending 4:34 on 'God is High, Great' answers any rising-up in the household, from either spouse, with God's own height.
- completes: I6 The trodden road, the pull to the side, and the one who stands upright — A road has been trodden by those who went before. God guides the traveller along it. Others walk behind their appetites and want the traveller to swerve far off the road. To lean to one side is junāḥ; keeping to the side of what is forbidden is ijtināb. In the household, one person stands upright over the other to keep it straight, and is told not to go looking for a path against her.. Members: سَبِيلًا 4:22:17 B001 an evil road; جُنَاحَ 4:23:35 B001 leaning to one side (negated); جُنَاحَ 4:23:35 B003 leaning toward sin (plain sense); وَيَهْدِيَكُمْ 4:26:5 B001 gentle guidance along the way; سُنَنَ 4:26:6 B001 the beaten roads and ways of those before; قَبْلِكُمْ 4:26:9 B002 those who went ahead; يَتَّبِعُونَ 4:27:8 B001 walking behind; ٱلشَّهَوَٰتِ 4:27:9 B001 the pull of appetite; تَمِيلُوا۟ 4:27:11 B001 veering off to one side; عَظِيمًۭا 4:27:13 B001 the size of the swerve; تَجْتَنِبُوا۟ 4:31:2 B003 keeping away, to the side; قَوَّٰمُونَ 4:34:2 B004 guardianship and care; قَوَّٰمُونَ 4:34:2 B008 keeping straight and level; تَبْغُوا۟ 4:34:33 B003 overstepping in aggression; سَبِيلًا 4:34:35 B001 a road sought against her (forbidden). Perceptible: 4:26–28 become a road scene with a direction and a pull. God shows the trodden ways (sunan). Appetite's followers walk behind and want the traveller to veer far off. junāḥ, repeated as 'no blame on you', keeps its physical sense of leaning to a side, so each permission says: this does not tilt you off the road. In 4:34 qawwām is heard beside istiqāma (standing level), and the man's authority is bounded by the same road image: 'seek no path against them'.
- completes: I3 Rings of enclosure: the forbidden circle, the fortress, and water poured outside — Around a house lies a protected precinct: the kin one may never marry, and the lap where stepdaughters are raised. Past it stand the fortresses of married women. Beyond them is open ground where one may seek, but only as someone who builds a fortress, not as someone who pours water out on the ground or keeps secret companions. A false enclosure also appears: a grip that jams the exit so the woman cannot get out. Inside the true fortress, the wife guards what is hidden while the husband is away, because God guards.. Members: تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ 4:19:12 B004 false enclosure: constriction that keeps her from her right; تَعْضُلُوهُنَّ 4:19:12 B005 the blocked exit, like an obstructed birth; وَمَقْتًۭا 4:22:15 B002 nikāḥ al-maqt: the old name for breaching the inner ring by marrying the father's wife; حُرِّمَتْ 4:23:1 B011 the maḥārim, the protected kin; حُرِّمَتْ 4:23:1 B003 the protected precinct (ḥarīm) around a dwelling; حُجُورِكُم 4:23:23 B005 the lap or embrace where the child is sheltered; حُجُورِكُم 4:23:23 B001 the enclosure that bars; وَٱلْمُحْصَنَٰتُ 4:24:1 B003 married women, walled in by a bond; وَٱلْمُحْصَنَٰتُ 4:24:1 B001 the fortress whose interior cannot be reached; وَرَآءَ 4:24:14 B006 the ground beyond the rings; تَبْتَغُوا۟ 4:24:17 B001 the seeker who goes out; مُّحْصِنِينَ 4:24:19 B001 the seeker as fortress-builder; مُسَٰفِحِينَ 4:24:21 B001 pouring liquid out onto the ground; مُسَٰفِحِينَ 4:24:21 B002 union without contract (plain sense); أَخْدَانٍۢ 4:25:34 B002 hidden companionship of desire; أُحْصِنَّ 4:25:36 B003 being taken inside the walls through marriage; حَٰفِظَٰتٌۭ 4:34:17 B006 guarding what is sacred and the pledge; لِّلْغَيْبِ 4:34:18 B005 the husband's absence, and what is kept during it; حَفِظَ 4:34:20 B001 God as the outer guard of the fortress. Perceptible: The prohibitions of 4:22–24 become a map of concentric zones. First a protected precinct of kin, including a lap. Then walled-in married women. Then 'what is beyond that', where one may go out seeking. The difference between marriage and fornication becomes material: water kept inside walls versus water poured out onto the ground to be lost. ʿaḍl in 4:19 shows the bad counterpart of the fortress, a grip that jams the exit. 4:34 reaches the fortress interior: a wife who guards in the husband's absence, with God guarding.
- note: The qawwām stands upright to keep level, not to seek a path against her. Inside the fortress the wife guards in his absence, as God guards. nushūz is a rising-up, met by steps that end at the shared bed, and answered by God the High, the Great.

Window movement: The window opens by forbidding men to take women by force: taking them as inheritance, jamming their way out (ʿaḍl), reaching back for the gold already handed over. Against taking, it sets two things that cannot be undone: an arrival (afḍā, crossing to the other) and a cord both sides hold (mīthāq ghalīẓ). It then draws the rings of enclosure from the body of kinship (womb, breast, lap, loins) out to married women's fortresses and to the open ground beyond, where one may seek only as a fortress-builder, not as someone pouring water on the ground. At its centre, God's will is a guide on a trodden road and a hand lightening the load of a weak creature, against appetite's pull to swerve. The law then widens: devouring wealth feeds the fire that consumes the eater, while earned portions and God's surplus give each person a place. It ends in the household: a rising-up, a bond cracking in two, one arbiter from each side, and God fitting the two back together like a notch on a string. Throughout, taking and tearing are answered by binding, enclosing, allotting and fitting.
