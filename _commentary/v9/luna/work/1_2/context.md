ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

Her türlü övgü Allah'a, bütün varlıkların Rabbine aittir.

## Word notes (precomputed word analysis; support, not obligations)

- 1:2:1 ٱلْحَمْدُ: definite praise as a nominal clause topic; locally broader than thanks alone and not a finite act of praising — topics: definite nominal praise stands before the genitive chain; praise includes recognition, gratitude, and commendation; praise joins a recurring deity-lordship-worlds cluster; the opening stance moves from invocation to praise
- 1:2:2 لِ: prepositional governor assigning the praise relation to the following divine name as belonging, due-ness, and restriction — topics: the particle assigns praise as belonging and due; bound form makes the dependency audible; preposition shift turns invocation into attribution; grammar, form, and restriction converge
- 1:2:3 ٱللَّهِ: the fixed divine proper name as governed praise-recipient, then anchor for appositional expansion — topics: genitive governance makes the name praise-recipient; proper name carries worship and refuge pressure; the repeated name pivots into appositional expansion; variant smooths sound without changing referent; divine name joins praise, lordship, and worlds
- 1:2:4 رَبِّ: genitive appositional title and construct head: Lord, owner, master, nurturer, and sustainer in relation to the worlds — topics: apposition binds the name to cosmic scope; lordship is ownership plus formation and care; geminate noun compresses role into a title; lordship widens praise and anticipates later titles; grammar, polysemy, variants, and sound converge
- 1:2:5 ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ: the genitive plural worlds/communities governed by the preceding lordship title; scope is many knowable, sign-bearing orders rather than one inert realm — topics: genitive plural is the governed domain of lordship; plural form distributes lordship across many domains; worlds are knowable and sign-bearing orders; final totality completes the praise-lordship formula; sound pressure marks the ayah ending; invocation widens into knowable domains
