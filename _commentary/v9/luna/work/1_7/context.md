- 1:7:1 صِرَٰطَ: the same requested path from 1:6, now locally specified as the route of a favored community and bounded by exclusions rather than introduced as a new sentence — topics: cross-ayah appositive path specification; roadway and engulfing pressure within the path sense; path cooccurs with guidance and favor fields; heavy onset marks the path threshold; hapax signal limited to surface slot
- 1:7:2 ٱلَّذِينَ: definite masculine plural relative pronoun in a genitive construct chain, identifying the path through a group defined by the favor clause that follows — topics: relative pronoun defines the path's people by a clause; received plural keeps a recognizable collective; elided-onset variant tightens the construct link; petitioning us is oriented toward favored them
- 1:7:3 أَنْعَمْتَ: completed second-person divine bestowal of favor onto the group, with the favor-object compressed and the recipient expressed through the following upon-them phrase — topics: perfect verb sustains direct divine address; favor-object omitted into the upon-them frame; causative form transfers ease and favor; recognized favored-class formula remains compact; favor mediates the path and exclusions
- 1:7:4 عَلَيْهِمْ: upon-them prepositional complement marking the recipients as the locus of divine favor in the relative clause — topics: upon-them marks recipient locus of favor; upon-direction becomes benefactive in the favor frame; first endpoint prepares the opposite-valence mirror; recipient suffix remains stable through recitational variants
- 1:7:5 غَيْرِ: genitive exceptive boundary marker embedded in the path chain, excluding the following negative classes while allowing the exclusion to qualify either the people or the path relation — topics: genitive case embeds exclusion with live attachment options; alterity selected with affective boundary pressure; single hinge pivots favor into double exclusion; initial sound carries exclusion into wrath naming
- 1:7:6 ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ: definite passive participle naming the wrath-incurred class, with the affected recipients foregrounded and the wrath agent left unspoken — topics: definite passive participle foregrounds affected class; heated rising root pressure colors wrath receipt; recurrent formula appears without naming the group; wrath class pairs with straying and mirrors favor; emphatic-stop variant hardens sound without changing role
- 1:7:7 عَلَيْهِمْ: second upon-them complement, completing the passive wrath construction by marking the target on whom wrath is directed — topics: second suffix completes the passive wrath target; same upon-form carries adversarial valence here; repeated endpoint audibly binds blessing and wrath
- 1:7:8 وَ: coordinating conjunction that carries the second excluded class under the same restrictive frame rather than starting a new narrative unit — topics: conjunction coordinates the second negative class; coordination and negation arrive as one unit
- 1:7:9 لَا: negative particle that carries the prior exclusion across coordination to the final class and keeps that class outside the requested path — topics: negation extends the exclusion to the final class; negation blocks false equivalence with the favored route; canonical particle avoids symmetrical repeated exception; short negation releases into the long closing word
- 1:7:10 ٱلضَّآلِّينَ: definite genitive masculine plural active participle naming the astray as the final excluded class, with straying left absolute rather than tied to an explicit complement — topics: omitted complement makes straying absolute; genitive active participle remains governed by exclusion; root image expands straying beyond a wrong turn; final word answers the guidance request; active drift contrasts with passive wrath-reception; final word gathers path, favor, wrath, and straying axes; geminated and long final cadence seals exclusion; claim that both negatives are passive constructions
