### Surah
- [refs: 4:32 | roots: ف ض ل ب ع ض ر ج ل ن س و | core] Two ayat earlier the same formula مَا فَضَّلَ ٱللَّهُ بِهِۦ بَعْضَكُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍۢ appears, and it is at once split into a share for men and a share for women. So the preference in 4:34 stays a two-way "some over some", not "them over them". Coveting it is forbidden before the ayah even starts.
- [refs: 4:5 | roots: ق و م م و ل | core] Wealth is what God "made a support (qiyām) for you" (قِيَٰمًۭا). The second ground, spending from their wealth, makes the qawwām the one who passes on the very thing that holds life up.
- [refs: 4:127 | roots: ق و م ق س ط ن س و | core] In the section that answers the question about women, "standing for the orphans with equity" (أَن تَقُومُوا۟ لِلْيَتَٰمَىٰ بِٱلْقِسْطِ) is the model the surah uses for standing up for those under your hand.
- [refs: 4:135 | roots: ق و م ق س ط ش ه د | core] The same form qawwāmīn, tied to equity and witnessing "even against yourselves" (وَلَوْ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِكُمْ). Here the preposition ʿalā turns back on the one who stands.
- [refs: 4:36, 4:37, 4:38 | roots: ف ض ل ن ف ق م و ل ف خ ر ب خ ل | core] Right after the marriage passage come the arrogant and boastful, the stingy who hide God's faḍl, and those who spend for show. These are the two grounds of 4:34 (faḍl and spending) gone bad.
- [refs: 4:15 | roots: ن س و ش ه د | develop] It opens with the same وَٱلَّٰتِى, but there four witnesses are required. In 4:34 the trigger is only fear, and that shapes a mild, reversible procedure.
- [refs: 4:16 | roots: ت و ب ص ل ح ء ل ه ك و ن | core] Same shape: a procedure, then "if they…" and a stop, then إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ plus two names. There the names are about mercy; in 4:34 they are about height. The name fits the temptation.
- [refs: 4:35 | roots: خ و ف ص ل ح ح ك م | core] If a breach between the two is feared, arbiters come from both families. The procedure leaves the husband's hands, and the surah itself sets a limit on 4:34.
- [refs: 4:128, 4:129, 4:130 | roots: ن ش ز خ و ف ص ل ح | core] A wife may fear nushūz from her husband. The answer there is ṣulḥ (reconciliation), not a ladder of steps. Nushūz can come from either spouse.
- [refs: 4:19 | roots: ك ر ه ع ش ر | core] "It is not lawful for you to inherit women against their will (karh)", together with وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ. Karh (compulsion) is the opposite of the ṭawʿ (willing assent) in أَطَعْنَكُمْ.
- [refs: 4:20, 4:21 | roots: ف ض و ب ع ض و ث ق | develop] أَفْضَىٰ بَعْضُكُمْ إِلَىٰ بَعْضٍۢ ("one of you has gone in to the other", closeness) set against بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍۢ (preference). The wives took from the men a "solemn covenant".
- [refs: 4:1 | roots: ر ج ل ن س و ز و ج | develop] Men and women first appear as spread out from one soul and its mate.
- [refs: 4:90 | roots: س ب ل ع ل و ج ع ل | core] Once the other side withdraws and offers peace, "God has given you no way against them". This is the exact legal structure of "sabīl ʿalā": 4:34 uses the language that ends a hostility.
- [refs: 4:141, 4:15 | roots: س ب ل | develop] Across the surah, sabīl ʿalā/li means a lawful ground to act against someone.
- [refs: 4:95 | roots: ف ض ل م و ل ق ع د | develop] God preferred those who strive with their wealth and selves over "those who sit". Here faḍl is tied to giving and standing up, yet "to each God promised the best".
- [refs: 4:3, 4:9, 4:35, 4:128 | roots: خ و ف | develop] In the surah, clauses opening with fear work as legal triggers. They rest on apprehension, not certainty.
- [refs: 4:58, 4:63, 4:66 | roots: و ع ظ | develop] In the surah, God is the one who admonishes (يَعِظُكُم). Admonishing the hypocrites is paired with "an effective word into their souls".
- [refs: 4:124, 4:57, 4:122 | roots: ص ل ح | develop] Elsewhere in the surah al-ṣāliḥāt means deeds, done "by male or female". In 4:34 the same plural names women.
- [refs: 4:2, 4:31 | roots: ك ب ر | note] Kabīr describes the sin of eating orphans' wealth (حُوبًۭا كَبِيرًۭا). Here kabīr is God's own name.
- [refs: 4:59, 4:64, 4:80, 4:81 | roots: ط و ع | note] In the surah, obedience (ṭāʿa) points to God and the Messenger. In 4:81 "ṭāʿa" is said with the tongue only.
- [refs: 4:94, 4:101 | roots: ض ر ب | note] ضَرَبْتُمْ فِى means travel. This is a different construction from the transitive ٱضْرِبُوهُنَّ.
- [refs: 4:103 | roots: ق و م ق ع د ج ن ب | note] قِيَٰمًۭا وَقُعُودًۭا وَعَلَىٰ جُنُوبِكُمْ: the postures of standing and lying on one's side, in the same surah.
- [refs: 4:172, 4:173 | roots: ك ب ر | note] Istikbār (acting big) by humans is condemned. Kabīr is kept for God.
- [refs: 4:75, 4:98 | roots: ر ج ل ن س و ض ع ف | note] "The weak among men and women": the two classes stand together in weakness.
- [refs: 4:36 | roots: ص ح ب ج ن ب | note] وَٱلصَّاحِبِ بِٱلْجَنۢبِ [recall: some exegetes read "the companion at your side" as the wife].

### Quran
- [refs: 5:8 | roots: ق و م ق س ط ش ه د | core] قَوَّٰمِينَ لِلَّهِ شُهَدَآءَ بِٱلْقِسْطِ. The only other Quranic uses of this form are tied to justice and witnessing.
- [refs: 2:238 | roots: ح ف ظ ق و م ق ن ت | core] One command to all believers joins the three roots that 4:34 spreads between men and women: guarding, standing and devotion (qunūt).
- [refs: 33:35 | roots: ق ن ت ح ف ظ | core] qānitīn/qānitāt and ḥāfiẓīn/ḥāfiẓāt stand side by side. The qualities praised in women in 4:34 are the same ones praised in men.
- [refs: 3:43, 66:12, 16:120, 2:116, 30:26, 33:31 | roots: ق ن ت | develop] In the Quran qunūt is always toward God (in 33:31, God and His messenger). It is never toward a husband.
- [refs: 66:5 | roots: ق ن ت | note] qānitāt appears in a list of religious qualities of wives.
- [refs: 66:10, 66:11, 66:12 | roots: ص ل ح ق ن ت | develop] Two righteous husbands with faithless wives, then Pharaoh's wife, a believer under a tyrant, and Maryam "among the devout". A woman's righteousness and devotion are measured against God, not the husband.
- [refs: 12:81, 12:12, 12:64 | roots: غ ي ب ح ف ظ | core] The brothers had promised "we will guard him" and later say "we were not guardians of the unseen" (وَمَا كُنَّا لِلْغَيْبِ حَٰفِظِينَ). Jacob answers "God is the best guardian". The ṣāliḥāt do what the brothers disowned, and they do it "by what God has guarded".
- [refs: 12:52 | roots: غ ي ب خ و ن | develop] أَنِّى لَمْ أَخُنْهُ بِٱلْغَيْبِ: "I did not betray him in his absence". Loyalty to an absent master or husband, in the same story.
- [refs: 2:255 | roots: ق و م ح ف ظ ع ل و | core] al-Qayyūm, ḥifẓuhumā and al-ʿAliyy come together in one ayah. 4:34 gives God's standing and guarding to people in human measure and keeps the height for God.
- [refs: 22:62, 31:30, 34:23, 40:12 | roots: ع ل و ك ب ر | core] The pair ʿaliyy–kabīr is a fixed divine formula. Only 4:34 builds it with kāna and ends a legal ruling with it.
- [refs: 28:4, 44:31, 28:83 | roots: ع ل و | develop] Pharaoh "rose high in the land" and "was one who exalted himself". The home of the Hereafter is for those who want no ʿuluww. When people claim ʿuluww, it is condemned.
- [refs: 17:21 | roots: ف ض ل ب ع ض ك ب ر | develop] Worldly preference of some over some is set against the Hereafter's "greater" (akbar) ranks and preference. The ending kabīr puts tafḍīl in its place.
- [refs: 16:71 | roots: ف ض ل ر ز ق | develop] Preference is in provision, and the favoured do not pass it on. This links faḍl to the economic ground.
- [refs: 2:253 | roots: ف ض ل | note] Messengers too are preferred "some over some".
- [refs: 2:228 | roots: ر ج ل ن س و | develop] "They have rights like the rights over them, by the known good; and men have a degree over them." A rank that sits inside a mirror of rights.
- [refs: 58:11, 2:259 | roots: ن ش ز | core] Rising from a seat, and God raising the bones. The concrete image of nushūz is lifting up out of one's place.
- [refs: 2:231, 2:232, 58:3, 65:2 | roots: و ع ظ ن س و | core] In the law of marriage and divorce, God's waʿẓ is mostly addressed to husbands ("do not retain them to harm them… He admonishes you with it"). The one told to admonish is the one most admonished.
- [refs: 16:125 | roots: و ع ظ ح س ن | note] al-mawʿiẓa al-ḥasana: admonition has a standard of beauty.
- [refs: 73:10, 19:46, 74:5 | roots: ه ج ر | develop] "Leave them with a beautiful leaving." Hajr (leaving) can itself be "jamīl".
- [refs: 32:16, 3:154 | roots: ض ج ع | develop] The other two maḍājiʿ: sides drawn away from beds to call on the Lord, and beds as places of death. The bed is where a person is most exposed.
- [refs: 2:187, 30:21 | roots: ل ب س ز و ج | note] Spouses are garments for each other, bound by love and mercy. This is what the bed-space stands for.
- [refs: 2:73, 8:50, 47:27 | roots: ض ر ب | develop] Ḍaraba with a direct object that is a person or body means physical striking. The construction does not allow a lexical escape.
- [refs: 38:44 | roots: ض ر ب ح ن ث | develop] "Take a handful of stalks in your hand and strike with it, and do not break your oath." The Quran can shape a ḍarb so that it keeps a word while carrying almost no weight. Analogy only; the text does not name the one struck.
- [refs: 8:12, 47:4 | roots: ض ر ب | note] Ḍarb in war. The full physical range of the verb is present in the Quran.
- [refs: 24:31 | roots: ض ر ب | note] Ḍaraba with bi- (feet, head-coverings) is a different construction.
- [refs: 42:41, 42:42 | roots: س ب ل ب غ ي ظ ل م | core] "The way (against someone) is only against those who wrong people and commit baghy." A husband who keeps seeking a way against a compliant wife becomes the one the way opens against.
- [refs: 28:76 | roots: ب غ ي م و ل | develop] Qārūn "committed baghy against them" with his wealth. It warns the man whose standing rests on spending wealth.
- [refs: 9:91 | roots: س ب ل ح س ن | note] "There is no way against those who do good."
- [refs: 3:99, 7:45 | roots: ب غ ي | note] Baghā with a direct object means "to seek". So "seek a way", with ʿalā adding "against".
- [refs: 41:11, 3:83 | roots: ط و ع ك ر ه | develop] ṭawʿan aw karhan: the Quran opposes willing obedience to compulsion. أَطَعْنَكُمْ is on the ṭawʿ side.

### Readings and grammar
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: ح ف ظ ء ل ه | core] Variant اللهَ (accusative): "by their guarding of [what is due to] God" instead of "by what God has guarded". Either way, the women's guarding is tied to God.
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: — | note] عَلَيْهُنَّ / عَلَيْهِنَّ: a difference in sound only.
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: ق و م | core] قَوَّٰمُونَ is an intensive, repeated form in a verbless nominal sentence. It states an ongoing position, not an event.
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: — | core] Both grounds open with bi-mā ("because"). The first names the two sides of the comparison only as "some", in a masculine plural that can include both sexes.
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: — | develop] The fa- of فَٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ turns from the grounds to a positive portrait of women. qānitāt has no object, whereas أَطَعْنَكُمْ has an explicit "you".
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: — | core] The three imperatives are joined by wa-, not thumma. Grammar alone does not make them a sequence, but the order of mention moves from word, to shared space, to body.
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: — | core] in + past tense (أَطَعْنَكُمْ) is an open condition. فَلَا plus the jussive turns the commands into a prohibition. سَبِيلًا is indefinite: "any way at all".
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: ك و ن | develop] kāna with divine names states a lasting attribute.
- [refs: 4:34 | roots: ض ر ب | core] Form I, transitive, with a person as direct object: grammatically, the construction of physical striking.

### Fatiha
- [refs: 1:6 | roots: ق و م | develop] ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ: istiqāma is the measure of both qawwām and qunūt ("كل استقامة في طريق الدين قنوتا").
- [refs: 1:5 | roots: ع ب د | note] إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ: devotion is addressed to God alone, which supports reading qānitāt toward God.
- [refs: 1:7 | roots: ن ع م غ ض ب | note] عَلَيْهِمْ twice: "upon" can carry blessing or anger. This is the ambiguity of ʿalā that the ayah works with.
