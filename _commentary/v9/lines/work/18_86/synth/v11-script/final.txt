===== LEDGER =====

### Surah
- [refs: 18:86, 18:90, 18:93 | roots: ب ل غ و ج د | core] Three journey stops open with the same formula: حتى إذا بلغ ... وجد. Only the first has divine speech and a choice. At 18:90 God comments (كذلك وقد أحطنا بما لديه خبرا, 18:91), and at 18:93–94 the people speak. The first stop sets the ethic that the later stops carry out.
- [refs: 18:60, 18:61, 18:63, 18:65 | roots: ب ل غ ء خ ذ و ج د | core] Musa's journey has the same shape. He reaches a water limit (حتى أبلغ مجمع البحرين), something disappears into the water (فاتخذ سبيله في البحر سربا), and then comes a finding (فوجدا عبدا). In the focus ayah Dhul-Qarnayn reaches the setting place, the sun sinks into a spring, and he finds a people.
- [refs: 18:17, 18:86 | roots: غ ر ب ش م س ر ء ي | core] The verb غرب occurs in the Quran only in 18:17 and 18:86. In the cave the setting sun "cuts past" the sleepers (تقرضهم ذات الشمال) and spares them; here it goes down beside where people live. Both passages describe the sun from a human viewpoint: وترى in 18:17, وجدها here.
- [refs: 18:17, 18:90 | roots: ش م س س ت ر | develop] The surah sets people in three relations to the sun: sheltered from it (the cave), exposed with no cover (لم نجعل لهم من دونها سترا), and here living beside the place where it sets.
- [refs: 18:7, 18:84 | roots: ح س ن م ك ن ب ل و | core] The surah's test is لنبلوهم أيهم أحسن عملا, with the earth's adornment as the test. Dhul-Qarnayn has been given power "in the earth" (مكنا له في الأرض). The option حسنا is that same test, now put to someone who holds power.
- [refs: 18:2, 18:3, 18:87, 18:88, 18:56 | roots: ن ذ ر ب ش ر ح س ن ص ل ح ء م ن | core] His answer repeats what the Book is said to do at the surah's opening. The Book warns of بأسا شديدا and gives good news to المؤمنين الذين يعملون الصالحات أن لهم أجرا حسنا. He answers with punishment for the wrongdoer and الحسنى for من آمن وعمل صالحا. The ruler at the edge of the earth does what the Book does.
- [refs: 18:58 | roots: ء خ ذ ع ذ ب ر ح م ع ج ل | core] 18:58 reads وربك الغفور ذو الرحمة لو يؤاخذهم بما كسبوا لعجل لهم العذاب. It holds both roots of the choice (ع ذ ب, ء خ ذ) and the word ذو. Before the man titled ذو القرنين is offered punishment, the surah has shown the Lord who is ذو الرحمة holding it back.
- [refs: 18:59, 18:87 | roots: ظ ل م ه ل ك | develop] In 18:59 towns are destroyed لما ظلموا. His أما من ظلم uses the same criterion and limits punishment to it.
- [refs: 18:26, 18:87 | roots: ح ك م ر د د ر ب ب | core] 18:26 says God shares His judgement with no one (ولا يشرك في حكمه أحدا), yet here a choice is put in a man's hand. His answer sends the final punishment back to God: ثم يرد إلى ربه فيعذبه.
- [refs: 18:4, 18:15, 18:21, 18:50, 18:51, 18:56, 18:77, 18:102, 18:106 | roots: ء خ ذ | core] In the surah, اتخذ almost always names a wrong taking: a son for God, gods, Iblis as ally, the verses as mockery, servants as allies. The powerful take a masjid over the sleepers (18:21), and Musa suggests taking a wage (18:77). The one taking God Himself proposes is تتخذ فيهم حسنا.
- [refs: 18:61, 18:63 | roots: ء خ ذ | note] اتخذ سبيله في البحر (verb + فى) is built the same way as تتخذ فيهم.
- [refs: 18:50, 15:26 | roots: ق و ل ء د م | develop] The surah uses قلنا twice. In 18:50 it is the command to bow to Adam, the being made of حمأ, which Iblis refused. In 18:86 it is the choice about people found beside a حمئة spring.
- [refs: 18:83, 18:86, 18:94 | roots: ق ر ن | develop] The title is used by the questioners (18:83), by God in direct address (18:86) and by a helpless people (18:94). God's use comes first.
- [refs: 18:86, 18:88 | roots: ق و ل | core] قلنا becomes سنقول له من أمرنا يسرا. The divine speech he received turns into the speech he promises the people.
- [refs: 18:73, 18:88 | roots: ع س ر ي س ر ء م ر | develop] Musa asks لا ترهقني من أمري عسرا; Dhul-Qarnayn promises من أمرنا يسرا. The two phrases mirror each other: من أمر with hardship, من أمر with ease.
- [refs: 18:82, 18:88 | roots: ء م ر | develop] Khidr says وما فعلته عن أمري: hidden knowledge, and the authority is not his. Dhul-Qarnayn says من أمرنا: an open choice made by visible criteria. These are two models of power beside water.
- [refs: 18:71, 18:74, 18:77 | roots: ء ت ي | note] In the Khidr episodes the حتى إذا chain leads to acts that look harmful. Here it leads to a choice between harm and good.
- [refs: 18:84, 18:95, 18:98 | roots: م ك ن خ ي ر ر ح م | develop] مكنا له leads to ما مكني فيه ربي خير and then to هذا رحمة من ربي. His later acts carry out the حسن branch.
- [refs: 18:29, 18:87, 18:88 | roots: ش ي ء ء م ن | develop] 18:29 frames choice individually: فمن شاء فليؤمن ومن شاء فليكفر. The answer likewise turns the collective "them" into من ... من.
- [refs: 18:37 | roots: خ ل ق ت ر ب | note] The surah also recalls creation from earth: خلقك من تراب.
- [refs: 18:36, 18:49, 18:53 | roots: و ج د | note] In the surah, وجد often means what one finds at the end: one's deeds present (18:49), no escape (18:53), a hoped-for better outcome (18:36).
- [refs: 18:12, 18:32, 18:60, 18:93 | roots: ء ن ن | note] The surah keeps returning to pairs: two parties, two gardens, two seas, two barriers. The dual title and the binary choice belong to this pattern.
- [refs: 18:29, 18:33, 18:41 | roots: م و ه | note] Water across the surah: punitive water كالمهل, a river split open through the garden, water sunk away غورا.

### Quran
- [refs: 15:26, 15:28, 15:33 | roots: ح م ء خ ل ق | core] The root ح م ء appears elsewhere in the Quran only in من حمإ مسنون, the material of human creation (and in 15:33 Iblis's reason for refusing). The sun goes down into the stuff humans are made of.
- [refs: 6:78, 41:37 | roots: ش م س ء ف ل | core] The sun's setting shows it is no lord: فلما أفلت قال يا قوم إني بريء مما تشركون.
- [refs: 27:24 | roots: و ج د ش م س ق و م | core] وجدتها وقومها يسجدون للشمس has almost the same frame: find + her/it + a people + the sun. There a people worship the sun; here the sun is seen sinking into mud and a people is found beside it.
- [refs: 36:38 | roots: ش م س ج ر ي | note] والشمس تجري لمستقر لها is a cosmic statement. 18:86 is framed as perception (وجدها).
- [refs: 88:4, 88:5, 101:11 | roots: ح م ي ع ي ن | develop] حامية describes fire (نارا حامية, نار حامية), and 88:5 continues with عين آنية, a scalding spring for the punished. The variant reading brings the punishment spring into the scene.
- [refs: 88:12, 55:50, 55:66 | roots: ع ي ن ج ر ي | note] Paradise springs: عين جارية, عينان تجريان.
- [refs: 9:106 | roots: ع ذ ب ت و ب | core] إما يعذبهم وإما يتوب عليهم is God's own pair of options. Here the same kind of pair is handed to a man, but the second option is human-scale: goodness, not forgiveness.
- [refs: 3:128 | roots: ع ذ ب ت و ب ء م ر | core] The Prophet is told ليس لك من الأمر شيء أو يتوب عليهم أو يعذبهم. Dhul-Qarnayn is given the matter. The contrast shows how rare the moment is.
- [refs: 11:40 | roots: ق و ل | develop] حتى إذا جاء أمرنا وفار التنور قلنا احمل فيها has the same structure, حتى إذا ... قلنا. At the flood's boiling point God gives an order; here, at the setting point, He gives a choice.
- [refs: 21:69, 20:68, 2:35 | roots: ق و ل | note] قلنا followed by a vocative or direct address.
- [refs: 38:26 | roots: ح ك م خ ل ف | develop] يا داوود إنا جعلناك خليفة في الأرض فاحكم بين الناس بالحق: a ruler addressed and given a command. Dhul-Qarnayn is given a choice instead.
- [refs: 12:22, 28:14 | roots: ب ل غ ح ك م | develop] ولما بلغ أشده آتيناه حكما وعلما: "reaching" is followed by God granting judgement. Here "reaching" is followed by a judgement placed in his hands.
- [refs: 6:6 | roots: ق ر ن م ك ن ه ل ك | develop] من قرن مكناهم في الأرض: generations (qarn) that were given power in the earth and then destroyed. The noun in the title carries this Quranic history of empowered generations.
- [refs: 22:41 | roots: م ك ن | develop] الذين إن مكناهم في الأرض ... أمروا بالمعروف shows what the empowered are expected to do.
- [refs: 20:65, 7:115 | roots: ل ق ي | note] إما أن تلقي وإما أن نكون uses the same إما أن ... وإما أن choice formula, offered to Musa by the magicians.
- [refs: 47:4, 76:3 | roots: م ن ن ش ك ر | note] Other إما ... وإما pairs: captives freed by grace or ransom; humans grateful or ungrateful.
- [refs: 2:83, 29:8, 27:11 | roots: ح س ن | develop] حسنا as the object of an act: وقولوا للناس حسنا, بوالديه حسنا. Goodness is something one does to or among people.
- [refs: 10:26 | roots: ح س ن | note] للذين أحسنوا الحسنى stands behind 18:88's الحسنى.
- [refs: 28:23 | roots: و ج د م و ه | develop] At Madyan's water Musa "found a community" (وجد عليه أمة) and then did good. The pattern: arrive at water, find people, act.
- [refs: 53:14, 53:15 | roots: ع ن د ن ه ي | note] عند سدرة المنتهى / عندها جنة المأوى: at an utmost limit, something is found "at it" (عندها). An analogy only.
- [refs: 55:17 | roots: ش ر ق غ ر ب | develop] رب المشرقين ورب المغربين: the sun's two ends, in the dual.
- [refs: 8:33 | roots: ع ذ ب | note] ليعذبهم وأنت فيهم puts punishment and فيهم together.
- [refs: 25:53, 35:12 | roots: ع ذ ب | note] عذب فرات: sweet water, from the same root as punishment.
- [refs: 35:27 | roots: غ ر ب | note] غرابيب سود: the west's root also names crow-black.

### Readings and grammar
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ح م ء ح م ي | develop] Variant حامية: "hot" instead of "muddy". [recall] Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Shuʿba read it this way. [recall] Classical harmonization: a spring can be both muddy and hot.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: و ج د غ ر ب | core] وجدها تغرب: a verb of perception plus a circumstantial imperfect. The ayah reports what he found, from where he stood; it is not a cosmological statement.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ع ن د | develop] The pronoun in عندها is feminine and could point to the sun or to the spring. Either way, عند places the people beside the scene, not inside the spring.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ق و ل | develop] قلنا is first-person plural, the narrator's own voice. The channel (address or inspiration) is not stated. [recall] Whether Dhul-Qarnayn was a prophet is debated.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ع ذ ب ء خ ذ | core] The two branches are unequal in size. أن تعذب is bare, with no object and no measure. أن تتخذ فيهم حسنا names a domain (the people) and an object (a good). أن + subjunctive turns each branch into an option-unit.
- [refs: 18:86, 18:87 | roots: ع ذ ب | develop] The single تعذب of the offer becomes two acts in the answer: نعذبه (human, now), then يعذبه عذابا نكرا (God, later).
- [refs: 18:86, 18:88 | roots: ح س ن | develop] The indefinite حسنا is answered by the definite الحسنى.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: | develop] فيهم marks a domain: "among them" and "concerning them". The people are not the object that is taken.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ذ و | note] يا ذا is the vocative of the five-noun ذو, "possessor of".
- [refs: 18:88 | roots: ج ز ي | note] [recall] جزاءً الحسنى (Ḥafṣ) versus جزاءُ الحسنى in other readings.

### Fatiha
- [refs: 1:4, 18:87 | roots: م ل ك د ي ن | develop] مالك يوم الدين: the final reckoning is God's, and ثم يرد إلى ربه returns it to Him.
- [refs: 1:7, 18:87, 18:88 | roots: ن ع م غ ض ب | note] Humanity's two paths, أنعمت عليهم and المغضوب عليهم, are drawn by conduct. The answer sorts people by conduct, not as a group.
- [refs: 1:6, 18:85 | roots: ص ر ط س ب ب | note] The prayed-for path, alongside the سبب that Dhul-Qarnayn follows.

===== READING =====

## Where the road ends: a spring and a people

The ayah begins in the middle of a journey. The previous ayah said only that he followed a way (18:85). The opening words {ar:حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا, tr:hattâ izâ, gloss:ta ki … olunca} carry that walking to the moment of arrival. The verb is {ar:بَلَغَ, tr:belağa, gloss:vardı, erişti}. Classical dictionaries explain it as reaching the farthest point of an aim: {ar:البلوغ والبلاغ الانتهاء إلى أقصى المقصد والمنتهى مكانا كان أو زمانا أو أمرا, tr:el-bulûğ ve'l-belâğ el-intihâ ilâ aksa'l-maksad ve'l-müntehâ, gloss:yer, zaman ya da iş olsun, hedefin en uç noktasına, bitiş sınırına varmak}. What he reaches is {ar:مَغْرِبَ ٱلشَّمْسِ, tr:mağribe'ş-şems, gloss:güneşin battığı yer ya da vakit}: not a city but a limit. One of the old dictionaries describes the sun's setting as {ar:غروب الشمس كأنه بعدها عن وجه الأرض, tr:ğurûbu'ş-şems keennehû bu'duhâ an vechi'l-ard, gloss:güneşin batışı, sanki yeryüzünden uzaklaşmasıdır}. The man arrives at the place where the sun seems to leave the earth.

The sentence that follows is built from a particular point of view. It does not say "the sun sets in a spring". It says {ar:وَجَدَهَا تَغْرُبُ, tr:vecedehâ teğrubu, gloss:onu batarken buldu}. The verb *vecede* means finding something you searched for or came upon, and grasping it with the senses. The dictionary names perception "with one of the five senses" as one kind of this finding. So the picture that follows is the picture that met his eyes when he reached the limit. The Quran has its own cosmic sentence about the sun's course, {ar:وَٱلشَّمْسُ تَجْرِى لِمُسْتَقَرٍّۢ لَّهَا, tr:ve'ş-şemsu tecrî li-mustekarrin lehâ, gloss:güneş kendi karar yerine doğru akıp gider} (36:38). This ayah is not that kind of statement; it tells what a traveller found.

The surah has told the sun this way once before. The verb for the sun's setting appears in the whole Quran only twice, and both are in this surah. In the cave story: {ar:وَإِذَا غَرَبَت تَّقْرِضُهُمْ ذَاتَ ٱلشِّمَالِ, tr:ve izâ ğarabet takriduhum zâte'ş-şimâl, gloss:battığında onları sol yandan kesip geçer} (18:17). That verse also opens by setting up an observer: "you would see the sun". In the cave the setting sun cuts past the sleepers without touching them. Here it goes down right beside a place where people live. The surah places people in three relations to the sun: sheltered from it in the cave, living with no cover from it at the next stop ({ar:لَّمْ نَجْعَل لَّهُم مِّن دُونِهَا سِتْرًۭا, tr:lem nec'al lehum min dûnihâ sitrâ, gloss:onlarla güneş arasına bir örtü koymamıştık}, 18:90), and here living near the spot where it sinks.

## The eye that sinks into an eye

The place where the sun sinks is {ar:عَيْنٍ حَمِئَةٍۢ, tr:aynin hamieh, gloss:kara çamurlu bir göze}. Turkish happens to keep something of the Arabic here. *Göze* (a spring, a small source of water) comes from *göz*, the eye, just as Arabic *ayn* names both the eye and the water welling from the ground. The Arabic dictionaries go one step further: *ayn* also names the sun itself. {ar:طلعت العين وغابت العين، أي الشمس, tr:tala'ati'l-aynu ve ğâbeti'l-ayn, gloss:"göz doğdu, göz battı", yani güneş}. In context the word means a spring; nothing forces us to hear "sun" in it. Still, the word allows the scene to be heard this way: the sky's "eye", watched by a man's eye, goes down into the earth's "eye". The one who sees, the thing seen, and the place it vanishes into are all named with the same word.

The spring's adjective is harder. *Hamie* comes from the root of *hame'*, which the dictionaries define as {ar:الحمأ الطين الأسود المنتن, tr:el-hame'u't-tînu'l-esvedu'l-muntin, gloss:kara, kötü kokulu çamur}. For this very phrase they say {ar:عين حمئة أي ذات حمأة, tr:aynun hamie ey zâtu hame', gloss:çamurlu göze, içinde kara çamur bulunan göze}. Outside this ayah the root appears in the Quran in one phrase only, repeated three times in one scene: {ar:مِّنْ حَمَإٍۢ مَّسْنُونٍۢ, tr:min hame'in mesnûn, gloss:biçim verilmiş kara çamurdan} (15:26, 15:28, 15:33). That is the story of human creation. The last of the three verses is spoken by Iblis: he refuses to bow before a human made from this mud. So the Quran has used this word for one thing only, the material Adam was made from. At the edge of the world the sun sinks into the same material.

This matters because of what the Quran elsewhere says about the sun. Ibrahim watched it and at first said "this is my Lord"; when it set, he said {ar:فَلَمَّآ أَفَلَتْ قَالَ يَٰقَوْمِ إِنِّى بَرِىٓءٌۭ مِّمَّا تُشْرِكُونَ, tr:fe-lemmâ efelet kâle yâ kavmi innî berîun mimmâ tuşrikûn, gloss:batınca, "Ey kavmim, ben sizin ortak koştuklarınızdan uzağım" dedi} (6:78). Setting is what shows the sun is not a lord. A second passage is built almost like our sentence. The hoopoe reports to Sulayman: {ar:وَجَدتُّهَا وَقَوْمَهَا يَسْجُدُونَ لِلشَّمْسِ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ, tr:vecedtuhâ ve kavmehâ yescudûne li'ş-şemsi min dûnillâh, gloss:onu ve kavmini Allah'ı bırakıp güneşe secde ederken buldum} (27:24). The frame is the same: "find" plus a feminine object plus a people plus the sun. In Saba the people bow to the sun. At the western edge the sun is seen sinking into black mud, and then {ar:وَوَجَدَ عِندَهَا قَوْمًۭا, tr:ve vecede indehâ kavmâ, gloss:ve onun yanında bir halk buldu}. The most glorious visible body goes into the lowest matter, and right after it the text turns to the beings made of that matter. Sun and people meet here as creatures made of the same earth.

The pronoun in *indehâ*, "beside it", is feminine and could point to either the sun or the spring. The dictionaries explain *inde* as a word for nearness. It does not put the people inside the spring; it places them next to the scene. The people stand at the edge of the setting, beside its mud.

The spring also has a second reading. In the variant reading it is not *hamie* but *hâmiye*, "hot, burning". In the Quran this adjective is the adjective of fire: {ar:تَصْلَىٰ نَارًا حَامِيَةًۭ, tr:taslâ nâran hâmiyeh, gloss:kızgın bir ateşe girer} (88:4), and {ar:نَارٌ حَامِيَةٌۢ, tr:nârun hâmiyeh, gloss:kızgın bir ateş} (101:11). The next verse in the first passage is another spring: {ar:تُسْقَىٰ مِنْ عَيْنٍ ءَانِيَةٍۢ, tr:tuskâ min aynin âniyeh, gloss:kaynar bir gözeden içirilir} (88:5). So the two readings give the spring two tones. With "muddy", the spring reminds us of the mud humans came from. With "hot", it reminds us of a spring of the punished. Both are carried into the choice that follows. This is not proof of anything; the two readings let two sounds of the same scene be heard.

## "We said": a pair usually kept by God

Then the narration turns into address: {ar:قُلْنَا يَٰذَا ٱلْقَرْنَيْنِ, tr:kulnâ yâ ze'l-karneyn, gloss:dedik: Ey Zülkarneyn}. How this speech reached him is not said. The dictionary records a sense in which something that comes to mind without any heard address is also called "saying": {ar:في الإلهام فإن ذلك لم يكن بخطاب ورد عليه بل كان ذلك إلهاما فسماه قولا, tr:fi'l-ilhâm..., gloss:ilhamda; çünkü bu kendisine gelen bir hitap değildi, bir ilhamdı ve ona "söz" dendi}. Whatever the channel, the voice is the narrator's own "We". It is the God who speaks with that same *we* in {ar:إِنَّا مَكَّنَّا لَهُۥ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ, tr:innâ mekkennâ lehû fi'l-ard, gloss:biz ona yeryüzünde imkân ve güç verdik} (18:84).

The sequence "when … reached, We said" has an example elsewhere in the Quran: {ar:حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَا جَآءَ أَمْرُنَا وَفَارَ ٱلتَّنُّورُ قُلْنَا ٱحْمِلْ فِيهَا, tr:hattâ izâ câe emrunâ ve fâra't-tennûru kulne'hmil fîhâ, gloss:ta ki buyruğumuz gelip tandır kaynayınca "ona yükle" dedik} (11:40). At the boiling point of the flood, God's speech is an order. Elsewhere, "reaching" is followed by God's gift of judgement: {ar:وَلَمَّا بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥٓ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُ حُكْمًۭا وَعِلْمًۭا, tr:ve lemmâ belağa eşuddehû âteynâhu hukmen ve ilmâ, gloss:olgunluk çağına erişince ona hüküm ve ilim verdik} (12:22). A ruler addressed by name in the Quran also receives an order: {ar:يَٰدَاوُۥدُ إِنَّا جَعَلْنَٰكَ خَلِيفَةًۭ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَٱحْكُم بَيْنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلْحَقِّ, tr:yâ dâvûdu innâ ce'alnâke halîfeten fi'l-ardi fahkum beyne'n-nâsi bi'l-hakk, gloss:Ey Davud, seni yeryüzünde halife kıldık; insanlar arasında hakla hükmet} (38:26). What Dhul-Qarnayn hears at the western limit is not an order but a choice. The judgement itself is put in his hand.

How unusual this is shows when we see who in the Quran normally holds this very pair. Of a group left waiting for God's decision it says: {ar:إِمَّا يُعَذِّبُهُمْ وَإِمَّا يَتُوبُ عَلَيْهِمْ, tr:immâ yu'azzibuhum ve immâ yetûbu aleyhim, gloss:ya onları cezalandırır ya da tövbelerini kabul eder} (9:106). The two options are God's. The Prophet himself is told: {ar:لَيْسَ لَكَ مِنَ ٱلْأَمْرِ شَىْءٌ أَوْ يَتُوبَ عَلَيْهِمْ أَوْ يُعَذِّبَهُمْ فَإِنَّهُمْ ظَٰلِمُونَ, tr:leyse leke mine'l-emri şey'un ev yetûbe aleyhim ev yu'azzibehum, gloss:bu işte senin payına düşen bir şey yok; ya tövbelerini kabul eder ya da onları cezalandırır, çünkü onlar zalimdir} (3:128). This surah says the same thing even more sharply about God: {ar:وَلَا يُشْرِكُ فِى حُكْمِهِۦٓ أَحَدًۭا, tr:ve lâ yuşriku fî hukmihî ehadâ, gloss:hükmüne kimseyi ortak etmez} (18:26).

At the edge of the world a pair like this is handed to a man, but it changes as it is handed over. The first branch stays: punishing. The second is not the divine "turning toward them in forgiveness". It becomes something a human can do: {ar:أَن تَتَّخِذَ فِيهِمْ حُسْنًۭا, tr:en tettehize fîhim husnâ, gloss:aralarında bir güzellik, bir iyilik edinip kurman}. Forgiving belongs to God; bringing about something good among people is within human reach. The divine pair is translated into human scale.

A few pages earlier the surah prepared the ground for this lending. In the verse {ar:وَرَبُّكَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ذُو ٱلرَّحْمَةِ ۖ لَوْ يُؤَاخِذُهُم بِمَا كَسَبُوا۟ لَعَجَّلَ لَهُمُ ٱلْعَذَابَ, tr:ve rabbuke'l-ğafûru zu'r-rahmeh, lev yuâhızuhum bimâ kesebû le-accele lehumu'l-azâb, gloss:Rabbin bağışlayandır, rahmet sahibidir; yaptıklarından ötürü onları hemen hesaba çekseydi azabı çabucak verirdi} (18:58), the roots of both branches of our choice appear together. *Yuâhızu* (holding to account) shares a root with *tettehize* (to take, to adopt), and *azâb* shares a root with *tu'azzibe* (to punish). The title is there too: the Lord is *zu'r-rahmeh*, "possessor of mercy". This possessor of mercy has the means to punish and delays it. Only after the surah has shown this does the man called *zu'l-karneyn*, "possessor of the two horns", hear the option of punishing. The question is whether the human possessor will act like the divine one.

## The size of the two branches

Look at the grammar of the choice. Both branches are built the same way, "either that you … or that you …", and each act becomes a unit of choice after *en*. The resemblance stops there. The first branch is two words, {ar:أَن تُعَذِّبَ, tr:en tu'azzibe, gloss:cezalandırman}: it says neither whom nor how much. The second is longer and names both a place and an object: "among them" and "a good". Punishment is left bare, with no measure. Goodness is given a scene. We will see that Dhul-Qarnayn fills in the bare branch himself.

The punishment verb has a further sense in the dictionaries. The same form is recorded for weaning, for holding someone back from a thing: {ar:عذبته تعذيبا كقولك فطمته عن هذا الأمر, tr:azzebtuhû ta'zîben ke-kavlike fetamtuhû an hâze'l-emr, gloss:onu bu işten kestim, sütten keser gibi alıkoydum}. In this ayah the verb means punishment, and the next ayah seals that with {ar:عَذَابًۭا نُّكْرًۭا, tr:azâben nukrâ, gloss:görülmedik bir azap}. The old sense is still useful as a picture: this word can also mean taking someone away from something. Dhul-Qarnayn's answer uses punishment in exactly that way, aimed only at wrongdoing and at the wrongdoer.

The second branch's verb, *ittihâz* ("taking, adopting for oneself"), has a dark history in this surah. Almost every use names a wrong taking. {ar:ٱتَّخَذَ ٱللَّهُ وَلَدًۭا, tr:ittehaza'llâhu veledâ, gloss:Allah çocuk edindi} (18:4) is the claim that God has taken a son. {ar:ٱتَّخَذُوا۟ مِن دُونِهِۦٓ ءَالِهَةًۭ, tr:ittehazû min dûnihî âliheh, gloss:O'nu bırakıp ilahlar edindiler} (18:15) is people taking gods. {ar:أَفَتَتَّخِذُونَهُۥ وَذُرِّيَّتَهُۥٓ أَوْلِيَآءَ مِن دُونِى, tr:e fe tettehızûnehû ve zurriyyetehû evliyâe min dûnî, gloss:onu ve soyunu benden başka dostlar mı ediniyorsunuz} (18:50) is taking Iblis as an ally. {ar:وَٱتَّخَذُوٓا۟ ءَايَٰتِى وَمَآ أُنذِرُوا۟ هُزُوًۭا, tr:vettehazû âyâtî ve mâ unzirû huzuvâ, gloss:ayetlerimi ve uyarıldıkları şeyi alay konusu edindiler} (18:56) is taking the verses as mockery. The takings of the powerful appear too: those who won control over the sleepers' affair say {ar:لَنَتَّخِذَنَّ عَلَيْهِم مَّسْجِدًۭا, tr:le-nettehızenne aleyhim mescidâ, gloss:üzerlerine mutlaka bir mescit edineceğiz} (18:21). In Musa's words to Khidr there is the taking of a wage: {ar:لَتَّخَذْتَ عَلَيْهِ أَجْرًۭا, tr:la-tehazte aleyhi ecrâ, gloss:buna karşılık bir ücret alırdın} (18:77). Against this list, the one taking that God Himself proposes stands alone: taking a good among a people. The surah's common verb, used again and again for the wrong thing, is finally tied to the right object.

The object is *husn*. The dictionary shows this goodness is not only gentleness. It is what is found beautiful by reason, by inclination and by the senses: {ar:مستحسن من جهة العقل ومستحسن من جهة الهوى ومستحسن من جهة الحس, tr:mustahsen min cihati'l-akl ve mustahsen min cihati'l-hevâ ve mustahsen min cihati'l-hiss, gloss:akıl yönünden, gönül yönünden ve duyu yönünden güzel bulunan}. When the Quran uses this word as the object of an act, it means something done to or among people: {ar:وَقُولُوا۟ لِلنَّاسِ حُسْنًۭا, tr:ve kûlû li'n-nâsi husnâ, gloss:insanlara güzel söz söyleyin} (2:83), and {ar:وَوَصَّيْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ حُسْنًۭا, tr:ve vassayne'l-insâne bi-vâlideyhi husnâ, gloss:insana anne babasına güzellikle davranmasını buyurduk} (29:8). So the second option is more than mercy. It is to install and build something good among people. The rest of the story shows what that means. The same man later refuses payment and says {ar:مَا مَكَّنِّى فِيهِ رَبِّى خَيْرٌۭ, tr:mâ mekkennî fîhi rabbî hayr, gloss:Rabbimin bana verdiği imkân daha hayırlıdır} (18:95). He builds a barrier and calls it {ar:هَٰذَا رَحْمَةٌۭ مِّن رَّبِّى, tr:hâzâ rahmetun min rabbî, gloss:bu, Rabbimden bir rahmettir} (18:98). The good branch, not yet made concrete at the western edge, turns into iron and molten copper at the last stop.

The spring's mud gives a picture for this choice. The dictionaries record two opposite operations for a well that has filled with black mud: {ar:حمأت البئر إذا نزعت حمأتها وأحمأتها إذا ألقيت فيها الحمأة, tr:hame'tu'l-bi'ra izâ neza'tu hame'tehâ ve ahme'tuhâ izâ elkaytu fîhe'l-hame', gloss:kuyunun çamurunu çıkarınca "hame'tu", içine çamur atınca "ahme'tu" denir}. From the same root, one verb clears the well and the other fouls it. The ayah says nothing about wells. But the man standing beside the muddy spring faces a similar either/or about the people: add to the mud, or draw it out.

## A test given to power

Why does the surah put this choice in his hand? From its opening it has framed life on earth as a test: {ar:إِنَّا جَعَلْنَا مَا عَلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ زِينَةًۭ لَّهَا لِنَبْلُوَهُمْ أَيُّهُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًۭا, tr:innâ ce'alnâ mâ ale'l-ardi zîneten lehâ li-nebluvehum eyyuhum ahsenu amelâ, gloss:yeryüzündekileri ona süs yaptık ki hangisinin daha güzel iş yapacağını sınayalım} (18:7). The measure of the test is from the same root as our *husn*: "best in deed". Dhul-Qarnayn was given the widest reach "in the earth". The question at the edge of the world is that same question, now asked directly of someone who holds power.

The Quran knows well what happens to the powerful. The noun inside his title, *karn*, is mostly used in the Quran for generations. The dictionary gives the sense as {ar:القرن من الناس أهل زمان واحد, tr:el-karnu mine'n-nâsi ehlu zamânin vâhid, gloss:insanlardan karn, aynı çağın halkı}. The Quran uses it for those who were empowered and then destroyed: {ar:كَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا مِن قَبْلِهِم مِّن قَرْنٍ مَّكَّنَّٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ, tr:kem ehleknâ min kablihim min karnin mekkennâhum fi'l-ard, gloss:onlardan önce, yeryüzünde imkân verdiğimiz nice nesli helak ettik} (6:6). "We gave them power in the earth" is the same phrase used for Dhul-Qarnayn. In his title *karn* most likely means "horn" or "edge", not "generation", and the two senses should not be merged. Still, the Quranic history of the word hangs over the scene: a man given power, whose title recalls the generations that were given power and fell, is now offering a people either punishment or goodness. The Quran also says what the empowered are expected to do: {ar:ٱلَّذِينَ إِن مَّكَّنَّٰهُمْ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ أَقَامُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتَوُا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَأَمَرُوا۟ بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَنَهَوْا۟ عَنِ ٱلْمُنكَرِ, tr:ellezîne in mekkennâhum fi'l-ardi ekâmu's-salâte ve âtevu'z-zekâte ve emerû bi'l-ma'rûfi ve nehev ani'l-munker, gloss:onlar ki, kendilerine yeryüzünde imkân verirsek namazı kılar, zekâtı verir, iyiliği emreder, kötülükten alıkoyarlar} (22:41).

The dictionaries also record another image for *karn*: the first visible upper edge of the sun is called *karn*, and the title is explained by two projections or two ends. The ayah does not explain the title, and this is only one explanation. But the man standing at the western end of the sun's path goes next to its rising place (18:90). The Quran speaks of that path's two ends in the dual too: {ar:رَبُّ ٱلْمَشْرِقَيْنِ وَرَبُّ ٱلْمَغْرِبَيْنِ, tr:rabbu'l-meşrikayni ve rabbu'l-mağribeyn, gloss:iki doğunun Rabbi, iki batının Rabbi} (55:17). A dual title and a two-branched choice fit a man who travels between the two ends.

## The answer: sorting the collective

The choice was about "them" as a group: an unnamed people, spoken of with a plural pronoun. Dhul-Qarnayn's answer, in the very next ayat, first breaks that group apart: {ar:أَمَّا مَن ظَلَمَ, tr:emmâ men zaleme, gloss:zulmedene gelince} (18:87), and {ar:وَأَمَّا مَنْ ءَامَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَٰلِحًۭا, tr:ve emmâ men âmene ve amile sâlihâ, gloss:iman edip salih iş yapana gelince} (18:88). The binary is not applied to the whole people; it becomes a criterion that sorts individuals. The surah has already given this framework, {ar:فَمَن شَآءَ فَلْيُؤْمِن وَمَن شَآءَ فَلْيَكْفُرْ, tr:fe-men şâe fe'l-yu'min ve men şâe fe'l-yekfur, gloss:dileyen iman etsin, dileyen inkâr etsin} (18:29), and for the destroyed towns it had said {ar:لَمَّا ظَلَمُوا۟, tr:lemmâ zalemû, gloss:zulmettikleri zaman} (18:59). Dhul-Qarnayn ties the bare punishment branch to that criterion: only the one who does wrong.

He also divides the punishment in two: {ar:فَسَوْفَ نُعَذِّبُهُۥ ثُمَّ يُرَدُّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِۦ فَيُعَذِّبُهُۥ عَذَابًۭا نُّكْرًۭا, tr:fe-sevfe nu'azzibuhû summe yureddu ilâ rabbihî fe-yu'azzibuhû azâben nukrâ, gloss:onu cezalandıracağız; sonra Rabbine döndürülecek, O da onu görülmedik bir azapla cezalandıracak}. The single verb of the offer appears twice in the answer, first for a human act and then for a divine one. What was handed to him, he hands back at the last stage. Whatever power the human judge has ends at a point, and final reckoning belongs to the one the Fatiha calls {ar:مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ, tr:mâliki yevmi'd-dîn, gloss:din gününün sahibi} (1:4). The contradiction between the choice lent to him and 18:26 ("He shares His judgement with no one") is resolved by the man himself.

The goodness branch is transformed as well. The indefinite *husnâ* of the offer comes back as the definite {ar:فَلَهُۥ جَزَآءً ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ, tr:fe-lehû cezâen'il-husnâ, gloss:ona karşılık olarak en güzeli vardır}. Then the most striking step follows: {ar:وَسَنَقُولُ لَهُۥ مِنْ أَمْرِنَا يُسْرًۭا, tr:ve se-nekûlu lehû min emrinâ yusrâ, gloss:ona buyruğumuzdan kolaylık söyleyeceğiz}. The man who was addressed with "We said" now says "we will say". The speech that came down to him turns into the speech he will address to the people. The "ease" he promises answers a word in the Musa story. Musa had begged Khidr {ar:وَلَا تُرْهِقْنِى مِنْ أَمْرِى عُسْرًۭا, tr:ve lâ turhiknî min emrî usrâ, gloss:işimden ötürü bana zorluk yükleme} (18:73). There "from my affair, hardship"; here "from our affair, ease". The two phrases mirror each other.

This answer does not come from nowhere. At its start the surah describes the Book it praises in the same shape: {ar:لِّيُنذِرَ بَأْسًۭا شَدِيدًۭا مِّن لَّدُنْهُ وَيُبَشِّرَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَعْمَلُونَ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ أَنَّ لَهُمْ أَجْرًا حَسَنًۭا, tr:li-yunzira be'sen şedîden min ledunhu ve yubeşşira'l-mu'minîne'llezîne ya'melûne's-sâlihâti enne lehum ecran hasenâ, gloss:katından gelecek çetin bir azapla uyarmak ve salih işler yapan müminlere güzel bir ödülün kendilerini beklediğini müjdelemek için} (18:2). It warns of severe punishment and gives good news of a fine reward to believers who do good works. At the western edge, Dhul-Qarnayn's answer does exactly this: punishment for the wrongdoer, the finest reward for the one who believes and does good. A ruler given a choice at the edge of the world uses the choice the way the Book at the surah's opening does. Handing a man a choice turns out not to open the way to arbitrary power. The man takes the Book as his measure.

## Two limits beside water

The shape of this scene has a twin in the surah. Musa too set out saying he would keep going until he "reached" a place: {ar:لَآ أَبْرَحُ حَتَّىٰٓ أَبْلُغَ مَجْمَعَ ٱلْبَحْرَيْنِ, tr:lâ ebrahu hattâ ebluğa mecma'a'l-bahreyn, gloss:iki denizin birleştiği yere varıncaya kadar durmayacağım} (18:60). At the place he reached, something vanished into the water: {ar:فَٱتَّخَذَ سَبِيلَهُۥ فِى ٱلْبَحْرِ سَرَبًۭا, tr:fettehaze sebîlehû fi'l-bahri serabâ, gloss:balık denizde bir tünel gibi yolunu tuttu} (18:61). Then came a finding: {ar:فَوَجَدَا عَبْدًۭا مِّنْ عِبَادِنَآ, tr:fe-vecedâ abden min ibâdinâ, gloss:kullarımızdan bir kul buldular} (18:65). Dhul-Qarnayn's arrival has the same three steps: he reaches a limit, a body sinks into water, and there is a finding. The sun goes into the spring as the fish went into the sea, and at the place it vanished a people is found. The verb for the fish's "taking" its way is the same verb as the "taking" of goodness here.

The two endings differ in one important way. At Musa's water the power belongs to hidden knowledge: Khidr damages a ship, kills a boy and repairs a wall, and says at the end {ar:وَمَا فَعَلْتُهُۥ عَنْ أَمْرِى, tr:ve mâ fealtuhû an emrî, gloss:bunu kendi buyruğumla yapmadım} (18:82). His acts look like harm, and the good in them is explained only later. At Dhul-Qarnayn's spring the choice is open, its criteria are visible (who did wrong, who believed), and the man speaks "from our affair". Musa learned patience with hidden good. Dhul-Qarnayn is asked to choose visible good. The surah sets two kinds of power beside water and places them next to each other.

## Rereading the ayah

Now the ayah reads differently. A man with the widest reach arrives where the sun seems to leave the earth. What he sees is the sun going down into the black mud humans were made of: the sun is no lord, and at the edge the sky and humanity meet in the same lowliness. Beside it he finds a people with no name and no description. Then God speaks and hands him a pair of options that belong to Him, scaled down to human size: a bare punishment, or a goodness with a place and an object. The surah's history with the verb "take" is dark, and the history of those given power in the earth is darker still. So this choice is the test the surah announced at its opening, "who is best in deed", put to someone who holds power. The answer comes in the next ayat and shows how he passed it. He separated the people rather than treating them as one mass. He limited punishment to the wrongdoer and left the final reckoning to God. He turned the goodness he was offered into "ease" he would speak himself. The spring was muddy, and perhaps hot; the question was whether he would add to the mud or draw it out.
