### Surah
- [refs: 18:85, 18:86, 18:89, 18:90, 18:92, 18:93 | roots: ب ل غ و ج د | core] Each stop in the journey opens the same way: "he followed a means", then "until, when he reached", then "he found". 18:86 is the first of the three stops, and it is the only one where God speaks and offers a choice. It sets the rule for how he will govern.
- [refs: 18:60, 18:61, 18:65, 18:77, 18:86 | roots: ب ل غ و ج د | core] Moses's journey also runs toward a limit at water: he means to reach "the meeting of the two seas" (18:60), and he and his companion reach it (18:61). What follows is a finding: they find a servant (18:65) and a wall (18:77). Dhu al-Qarnayn's journey repeats this reach-then-find pattern at another edge of water. In both stories, the traveller finds more than he set out to reach.
- [refs: 18:17, 18:86, 18:90 | roots: غ ر ب ش م س ط ل ع | core] The surah describes the sun from where an observer stands. In 18:17 it is what "you would see" from the cave. In 18:90 it rises "upon a people". 18:86 uses the same finding verb for the sun's setting, which makes this the traveller's horizon rather than a claim about where the sun physically goes. Taken together, the sun measures who is sheltered and who is exposed: the sleepers are screened, the eastern people have no cover, and the western people live beside a dark spring.
- [refs: 18:50, 18:86 | roots: ق و ل ء خ ذ | core] Only two verses in the surah have God say "We said": 18:50 (the angels told to prostrate to Adam, followed by "will you take him [Iblis] and his offspring as allies") and 18:86. Both scenes combine "We said" with the verb "take". The first honours a being made of clay. The second comes right after black mud and a people.
- [refs: 18:4, 18:15, 18:21, 18:50, 18:51, 18:56, 18:77, 18:102, 18:106, 18:86 | roots: ء خ ذ | core] Through the surah, the Form VIII verb "take" is mostly used for wrong takings: taking a son, gods, allies, mockery of the signs, a mosque over the sleepers by those who prevailed, a wage. 18:86 gives the verb the opposite object: goodness "among them". The option reads as the surah's correction of its own list of wrong takings.
- [refs: 18:77, 18:94, 18:95 | roots: ء خ ذ خ ر ج | develop] Moses says "you could have taken a wage" (18:77). Later Dhu al-Qarnayn refuses the payment he is offered: "what my Lord established me in is better" (18:95). He does the "taking goodness" without charging for it.
- [refs: 18:7, 18:30, 18:104, 18:86 | roots: ح س ن | core] The surah's test is "which of them is best in deed" (18:7). The goodness option uses the same root, so the man given the choice is also being tested by it. 18:104 adds the warning case: people who think they are doing well.
- [refs: 18:86, 18:87, 18:88 | roots: ع ذ ب ح س ن ظ ل م | core] Dhu al-Qarnayn's answer switches "either… or…" (immā) to "as for… and as for…" (ammā). He does not apply one policy to the whole people. Punishment is for whoever wrongs; reward and ease go to whoever believes and does right.
- [refs: 18:74, 18:87 | roots: ن ك ر | core] The word "nukr" (monstrous, unheard-of) appears twice. Moses uses it of Khidr killing the boy: "you have done a monstrous thing". Dhu al-Qarnayn uses it only for the Lord's punishment. In his speech, the monstrous punishment belongs to God; his own punishment is the lesser one, followed by the return to God.
- [refs: 18:10, 18:16, 18:73, 18:88 | roots: ء م ر ي س ر ع س ر | develop] The surah repeats "from [someone's] affair + noun": right guidance (18:10), a comfortable arrangement (18:16), hardship (18:73: "do not burden me from my affair with hardship"). In 18:88 the man holding command promises "ease from our command", the exact opposite of what Moses asked to be spared.
- [refs: 18:58, 18:59, 18:87 | roots: ع ذ ب ظ ل م ء خ ذ | core] The surah's own principle is that God could rush punishment but delays it (18:58), and towns were destroyed "when they did wrong" (18:59). Dhu al-Qarnayn uses the same test, wrongdoing, and hands the final punishment back to God.
- [refs: 18:84, 18:86, 18:95, 18:98 | roots: م ك ن | develop] His power is given ("We established him", 18:84), he himself calls it given ("what my Lord established me in", 18:95), and it is temporary (18:98). The choice in 18:86 sits inside that frame.
- [refs: 18:82, 18:86 | roots: ء م ر | develop] Khidr's story ends with "I did not do it of my own command" (18:82): hidden direction. Dhu al-Qarnayn's story opens with an open grant of choice. The surah sets two ways of acting on God's authority side by side.
- [refs: 18:29, 18:86 | roots: ش ي ء | note] People are left to choose belief (18:29). Dhu al-Qarnayn is given a choice about how to respond to people.
- [refs: 18:17, 18:27, 18:36, 18:49, 18:53, 18:58, 18:69, 18:86 | roots: و ج د | note] Elsewhere in the surah "find" mostly appears in the negative: finding no guardian, no refuge, no escape. The two journeys are where finding actually happens.
- [refs: 18:28, 18:101 | roots: ع ي ن | note] Eyes in the surah: "do not let your eyes pass beyond them" (18:28) and eyes "under a cover" (18:101). These are root echoes only, weak for the focus.

### Quran
- [refs: 15:26, 15:28, 15:33 | roots: ح م ء | core] Apart from 18:86, the root ḥ-m-ʾ appears in the Quran only as the material humans were made from. The forms differ (the noun "mud" there, the adjective "muddy" here). Still, the scene moves from the black-mud spring straight to "a people", and then to God's speech.
- [refs: 15:33, 18:50, 18:86 | roots: ح م ء ء خ ذ | develop] Iblis refused because of the clay (15:33). Right after this mud, Dhu al-Qarnayn is asked whether he will take goodness among people. This is an analogy, not something the text states.
- [refs: 9:106, 18:86 | roots: ع ذ ب | core] "Either He punishes them or He turns to them" (9:106) is a divine either/or. 18:86 hands a similar-looking either/or to a human being.
- [refs: 6:6, 10:13, 18:84 | roots: م ك ن ق ر ن ه ل ك | develop] 6:6 uses "established them in the land" (makkana) of generations (qarn) that were later destroyed for their sins. 18:84 uses the same verb for Dhu al-Qarnayn, and his title shares the root qarn. The title's meaning is not settled, but the echo recalls what established power has done before.
- [refs: 6:78, 18:86 | roots: ش م س | develop] Abraham watches the sun set and turns away from worshipping it. That is another sunset seen from where someone stands.
- [refs: 2:258, 18:86 | roots: ش م س غ ر ب | develop] A king is challenged to bring the sun from the west and is silenced. Dhu al-Qarnayn does not command the sun; he finds it, and is given power over people instead. This connection is open (not stated by the text).
- [refs: 2:83, 29:8, 18:86, 18:88 | roots: ح س ن ق و ل | core] The same noun ḥusn is used for how to speak to people (2:83) and how to treat parents (29:8). This supports hearing it here as conduct toward the community. In 18:88 the promise "we will say to him ease" carries out a saying of good.
- [refs: 23:32, 8:33, 18:86 | roots: ف ي | note] "Among them" (fīhim) marks a community as the place where something is set: "We sent among them a messenger" (23:32), "while you are among them" (8:33).
- [refs: 88:12, 55:50, 55:66, 88:5 | roots: ع ي ن | develop] Paradise springs flow and gush. This one is dark mud. 88:5 has a scalding spring, which relates to the "hot" reading (see below).
- [refs: 55:17, 73:9, 37:5 | roots: غ ر ب ش ر ق | develop] "Lord of the two Easts and the two Wests" (55:17): the horizons belong to God. 73:9 puts east and west next to the command "take Him as guardian", the same verb "take" used for trust, not seizure.
- [refs: 4:58, 16:90, 38:26, 5:8, 49:9 | roots: ع د ل ح ك م | develop] Justice as the standard for judging people. David is told not to follow desire (38:26). These are the Quran's measures for the choice he is given.
- [refs: 17:15 | roots: ع ذ ب | note] "We do not punish until We send a messenger." The focus says nothing about any prior warning; the question stays open.
- [refs: 27:16, 18:84 | roots: ء ت ي | note] "Given of everything" is said of Solomon (27:16) and Dhu al-Qarnayn (18:84) alike: rulers whose capacity comes from God.
- [refs: 28:23, 18:86 | roots: و ج د | note] Moses at Midian's water finds a crowd and two women held back, then acts for them. Again a traveller at water, finding people, then deciding how to treat them.
- [refs: 3:37, 18:86 | roots: و ج د ع ن د ح س ن | note] "He found provision with her" (wajada ʿindahā): the same construction, near the root ḥ-s-n.
- [refs: 24:39 | roots: و ج د ع ن د | note] "Until, when he came to it, he found it was nothing, and found God there." The reach-and-find pattern ends in a reckoning.
- [refs: 51:36, 18:87 | roots: و ج د | note] There, the people found are labelled at once as "Muslims". The people in 18:86 are unlabelled until 18:87–88 sorts them by conduct.
- [refs: 27:24 | roots: ش م س و ج د | note] "I found her and her people prostrating to the sun." The focus gives no such detail, so this stays open.
- [refs: 2:231, 42:40, 17:33 | roots: ح س ن ع ذ ب | note] Other verses put limits on discretion and punishment.
- [refs: 25:53 | roots: ع ذ ب | note] [recall] "This is sweet and fresh" (ʿadhb furāt): the same root as "punish" also names sweet water. This is root-level only; it is not used in the reading.

### Dictionary
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ح م ء | core] B001: "الحمأ الطين الأسود المنتن"; "عين حمئة أي ذات حمأة". A black, foul-smelling mud spring, the opposite of flowing water.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ع ي ن | develop] The same root covers the eye (B001), a spring (B006) and the sun itself (B008: "عين الشمس مشبه بعين الإنسان"; "طلعت العين وغابت العين، أي الشمس"). In the dictionaries the sun is also called ʿayn, and here it sets into an ʿayn. This is image resonance, not the contextual meaning.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: غ ر ب | develop] B006 "غروب الشمس كأنه بعدها عن وجه الأرض": setting as moving away. B007 covers distance and estrangement. B004 "الغروب مجاري العين" (the channels of the eye's tears): the root of "setting" also touches eye-water.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: و ج د | core] B001 includes perception by the senses ("وجود بإحدى الحواس الخمس"). This supports reading the finding as what the traveller saw.
- [refs: 18:86, 18:87 | roots: ع ذ ب | develop] B005 "العذاب العقوبة" (punishment). B003 "عذبته تعذيبا كقولك فطمته عن هذا الأمر" means weaning or restraining someone from a matter. It complicates the picture, but 18:87 settles the meaning as punishment.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ح س ن | core] B001 "الحسن ضد القبح", with mufradat's three sources of approval: reason, inclination, senses. The word fuses beauty and moral goodness; it is an open noun, not the verb "to do good".
- [refs: 18:83, 18:86, 18:90 | roots: ق ر ن | develop] B006 includes the sun's first visible edge and explains the title by "two protrusions or two ends". B005 gives "القرن الأمة من الناس" (a generation). The title's sense stays open; the journey does reach both ends of the sun's course.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ع ن د | note] B004: nearness, not being inside. The people are "at" the spring, not in it.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ء خ ذ | note] B010: taking something for oneself, acquiring. The verb means adopting or instituting, not seizing.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ب ل غ | note] B001 "الانتهاء إلى أقصى المقصد": reaching the furthest point of an aim.

### Readings and grammar
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: ح م ء | note] [recall] The reading "ḥāmiya" ("hot") is attested (Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Shuʿba). The word notes flag it as an accepted variant. Hot and muddy can both hold (compare 88:5).
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: — | core] The two branches are not built the same way. "Punish" has no stated object (the people are affected directly). "Take" has goodness as its object and "among them" as its setting: goodness is placed in the community and stays there.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: — | develop] "Her" in "near her" (ʿindahā) is feminine and could refer to the sun or the spring. Either way the people are next to the scene, not inside it.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: — | develop] The verse closes on the open choice; the answer only comes in 18:87. The ayah leaves the choice hanging at its end.
- [refs: 18:86, 18:87 | roots: — | core] "Either/or" (immā … wa-immā) in the question becomes "as for… and as for…" (ammā … wa-ammā) in the answer: an open choice turned into sorting by conduct.
- [refs: 18:86 | roots: — | note] "Until, when" (ḥattā idhā) ends the following of 18:85 and opens a scene that happened, not a hypothetical.

### Fatiha
- [refs: 1:4, 18:87 | roots: م ل ك د ي ن | develop] "Then he is returned to his Lord": the human judge leaves final judgment to the "Master of the Day of Judgment" (1:4).
- [refs: 1:7, 18:87, 18:88 | roots: ن ع م غ ض ب | note] The Fatiha's two paths, those favoured and those under anger, mirror the two outcomes into which the people are sorted.
