### Surah
- [refs: 1:1, 1:2 | roots: سمو اله | core] The same divine name comes back under a different particle. In 1:1, bi- ("with/by the name") makes the name something the speaker starts with and leans on. In 1:2, li- ("belonging to") hands the most general word, al-ḥamd, over to that name. The invoked name becomes the one who receives praise.
- [refs: 1:2, 1:3, 1:4 | roots: ربب رحم ملك دين | core] Rabbi, ar-raḥmāni ar-raḥīmi and māliki yawmi d-dīn are all genitive titles attached to lillāhi. The praise sentence begun in 1:2 therefore runs grammatically to 1:4. The end of the ayah is a pause, not the end of the sentence.
- [refs: 1:2, 1:5 | roots: حمد عبد | core] 1:2 names no one who praises. The "we" appears only in naʿbudu (1:5), where the third person turns to "you". Praise is stated as a fact before any speaker claims it.
- [refs: 1:1, 1:2, 1:3 | roots: رحم ربب | core] The two mercy names of 1:1 return straight after rabbi l-ʿālamīn. This time they describe how the lordship works: lordship heard as mercy.
- [refs: 1:2, 1:4 | roots: ربب ملك علم | develop] The titles narrow in a telling way: from Lord of many worlds (plural, spread out in space) to owner of one day (singular, a point in time). The praise that 1:2 declares is tied in 1:4 to the day of reckoning.
- [refs: 1:2, 1:6 | roots: ربب هدي | core] The rabb who brings things to completion step by step is the one asked for guidance in ihdinā (1:6). The request is already inside the title (compare 20:50).
- [refs: 1:2, 1:7 | roots: حمد نعم | develop] anʿamta ʿalayhim names favour, and ḥamd takes in thanks for favour. The praise in 1:2 is not limited to that thanks.
- [refs: 1:2, 1:7 | roots: علم | develop] 1:7 sorts people into three groups (the favoured, those under anger, the astray). All of them are among the ʿālamīn whose Lord is praised, and the path requested is one path within that plurality.
- [refs: 1:2, 1:5 | roots: اله عبد | develop] The name that receives praise is explained lexically as "the one worshipped" (maʿbūd). The praise in 1:2 turns into naʿbudu in 1:5.

### Quran
- [refs: 6:45, 10:10, 37:182, 39:75, 40:65 | roots: حمد ربب علم | core] The exact formula al-ḥamdu lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn occurs five more times. In four of them it comes where something ends: after the wrongdoers are cut off (6:45), as the last call of the people of the Garden (10:10), as the last ayah of aṣ-Ṣāffāt (37:182), and after judgment as the last ayah of az-Zumar (39:75). 40:65 is the exception: it follows a call to sincere invocation. The Fatiha opens with what the Quran elsewhere keeps for the end.
- [refs: 39:75, 1:2 | roots: حمد قول | core] wa-qīla ("and it was said") is passive, with no named speaker. This matches the missing subject in 1:2: a verdict voiced by the whole scene.
- [refs: 10:10, 1:2, 1:5 | roots: حمد سبح | develop] In the Garden, the prayer opens with direct address (subḥānaka llāhumma) and ends with third-person ḥamd. The Fatiha reverses this: third-person ḥamd first, then direct address (1:5).
- [refs: 7:43, 1:2, 1:6 | roots: حمد هدي | core] The Garden people praise because they have been guided: allaḏī hadānā li-hāḏā. In the Fatiha, praise comes before the request for guidance. The end's verdict is said in advance.
- [refs: 6:1, 18:1, 34:1, 35:1, 1:2 | roots: حمد خلق نزل | develop] The other surah-openings with al-ḥamdu lillāh give a reason, a relative clause or participle naming a deed (creating, sending down the Book, owning, originating). 1:2 gives a title, rabb, instead: a standing relation rather than a single deed.
- [refs: 14:39, 23:28, 27:15, 35:34, 39:74 | roots: حمد | develop] Ḥamd is voiced for particular occasions: children in old age, rescue in the ark, knowledge received, grief removed, a promise fulfilled. 1:2 names no occasion, so its praise is unbounded.
- [refs: 14:8, 4:131, 31:26 | roots: حمد غني | core] ghaniyyun ḥamīd: God stays praiseworthy even if everyone on earth disbelieves. The praise in 1:2 is owed as a right, not dependent on human assent.
- [refs: 3:188 | roots: حمد فعل | core] Wanting to be praised for what one did not do is condemned. Ḥamd has to match an actual deed, so "all praise is God's" implies a claim about who actually acts.
- [refs: 28:70, 30:18, 34:1, 64:1 | roots: حمد | develop] lahu l-ḥamd holds in this world and the next, and in the heavens and the earth. Ownership of praise is spread over time and space, much as al-ʿālamīn spreads lordship.
- [refs: 17:44, 13:13 | roots: حمد سبح | core] Everything glorifies "with His praise", thunder included. This offers a Quranic answer to the unnamed praiser of 1:2: the worlds themselves already praise, and the human "we" joins them.
- [refs: 9:112, 17:79 | roots: حمد | note] al-ḥāmidūn names humans as those who praise; maqāman maḥmūdan is a praised station. The root runs from God, who owns praise, to humans who praise and are praised.
- [refs: 20:49, 20:50 | roots: ربب خلق هدي | core] Moses defines Rabb as the one who gave each thing its created form and then guided it: form first, then direction. This links 1:2 to 1:6.
- [refs: 26:77, 26:78, 26:79, 26:80, 26:81, 26:82 | roots: ربب خلق هدي دين | core] Abraham unfolds rabba l-ʿālamīn into a whole life: creation, guidance, food and drink, healing, death and revival, and forgiveness on yawma d-dīn. It reads as the Fatiha's own sequence spelled out (rabb → yawm ad-dīn → hidāya).
- [refs: 18:77, 18:82 | roots: ربب رحم شدد | core] The Lord wants the two orphans to reach full strength, and the wall is kept standing until then; this is called raḥmatan min rabbik. It is a concrete case of tarbiya (bringing to completion) named as mercy, which matches 1:2 → 1:3.
- [refs: 26:18, 26:23, 26:24 | roots: ربو ربب | develop] Pharaoh reminds Moses "a-lam nurabbika" (did we not raise you; root r-b-w, an echo, not the same root). He then asks "wa-mā rabbu l-ʿālamīn", and Moses answers with heavens, earth and what lies between. Raising one child is set against lordship of all the worlds.
- [refs: 79:24, 26:23 | roots: ربب | develop] "anā rabbukumu l-aʿlā": the rival lordship is still bound to "you", a single people. Human rabb-ship always carries a limited annexation.
- [refs: 17:24 | roots: ربو ربب رحم | develop] "rabbi rḥamhumā kamā rabbayānī ṣaġīrā": rabb, raising (the r-b-w echo) and mercy in one prayer, in the Fatiha's order of rabb → raḥma. This is an echo only, not a shared root.
- [refs: 39:29 | roots: حمد شرك سلم | core] One man owned by quarrelling partners against one man owned wholly by one master, followed at once by al-ḥamdu lillāh. 1:2 inverts the image: many worlds, one Lord. Plurality sits in what is ruled, not in the rulers.
- [refs: 12:39 | roots: ربب | note] [recall text] aʾarbābun mutafarriqūn… Scattered lords set against the One. Same contrast as 39:29.
- [refs: 9:31 | roots: ربب | note] Human authorities taken as arbāb. Rival lordship is the backdrop against which rabbi l-ʿālamīn is heard.
- [refs: 12:23, 12:42 | roots: ربب | note] rabb used of a human master, always annexed to a person (whom "rabbī" refers to in 12:23 is disputed). Supports the dictionary point that human rabb needs an annexation.
- [refs: 7:80, 26:165, 37:79, 68:52, 21:107, 2:47 | roots: علم | core] al-ʿālamīn as human communities: they "precede" one another in deeds, are reminded, receive mercy, and are ranked in favour. The worlds include those who can be addressed.
- [refs: 26:24, 45:36, 37:5, 78:37 | roots: ربب علم | develop] The worlds as cosmic domains. 45:36 lists rabb of heavens, rabb of earth, then gathers them as rabbi l-ʿālamīn.
- [refs: 16:16 | roots: علم هدي | develop] ʿalāmāt and stars as markers people steer by. Same root, different noun; with the dictionary's explanation of ʿālam this makes the worlds hearable as signs pointing to guidance.
- [refs: 29:63, 30:18, 30:19 | roots: حمد حيي | develop] Rain reviving dead earth leads directly to qul al-ḥamdu lillāh. Nurture visible in the world produces the formula.
- [refs: 6:162 | roots: ربب علم صلو | core] lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn, the same string, applied to salāt, sacrifice, life and death. The belonging declared in 1:2 covers the very prayer in which it is recited.
- [refs: 2:131, 6:71, 40:66, 27:44 | roots: سلم ربب علم | note] aslamtu / an nuslima li-rabbi l-ʿālamīn: the same title with li-, as personal submission.
- [refs: 83:6 | roots: قوم ربب علم | note] People stand li-rabbi l-ʿālamīn on the Day. It connects to 1:4; the link to standing in prayer is an analogy only.
- [refs: 81:29 | roots: شيأ ربب علم | note] Human willing depends on the will of Rabb al-ʿālamīn.
- [refs: 7:61, 7:67, 7:104, 26:16, 26:109, 26:127, 26:145, 26:164, 26:180 | roots: ربب علم رسل | note] The title as the prophets' shared formula for the source of their mission and their reward.
- [refs: 26:192, 32:2, 56:80, 69:43 | roots: نزل ربب علم | note] The title names the source of revelation.
- [refs: 100:6 | roots: ربب كند | note] The human is ungrateful to his Lord, the negative mirror of ḥamd.
- [refs: 114:1 | roots: ربب نوس | note] rabbi n-nās narrows the title to humanity, as refuge.
- [refs: 15:98, 52:48, 110:3, 32:15, 39:75 | roots: سبح حمد ربب | note] bi-ḥamdi rabbik: ḥamd and rabb joined under a command or in a scene of worship.
- [refs: 14:7, 35:34 | roots: شكر حمد | note] shukr is conditional on favour; God is shakūr. Supports the difference between ḥamd and shukr.
- [refs: 37:180, 37:181, 37:182 | roots: سبح سلم حمد | note] The surah ends: glorification, then peace on the messengers, then the formula. A closing sequence.
- [refs: 15:87 | roots: ثني | note] [recall] Identifying the "seven oft-repeated" with the Fatiha comes from outside the text; it is not established by the Quran itself.

### Dictionary
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: حمد | core] الحمد نقيض الذم; الحمد أعم من الشكر. Ḥamd is the opposite of blame and wider than thanks, so the praise is not tied to the speaker's own gain.
- [refs: 1:2, 3:188 | roots: حمد | core] The branch's "not" field excludes المدح لما لا اختيار فيه (praise of what involves no choice). Ḥamd is aimed at chosen deeds by an agent, which supports 3:188.
- [refs: 1:2, 6:45, 39:75 | roots: حمد | core] أحمدت فلانا إذا وجدته محمودا; أحمدت الأرض إذا رضيت سكناها أو مرعاها. Praise as the verdict after trying someone or something (land found good to live on or graze). This is a different form, used as an image only; it matches the formula's role as a closing verdict.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: حمد | note] محمود / محمد / الحميد: the one praised and the one with many praiseworthy qualities. Background for ḥamīd.
- [refs: 10:10 | roots: حمد | note] حماداك = the furthest reachable limit. A separate, pattern-bound branch; only a loose echo of ḥamd as the last word. Not used in the reading.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: حمد | develop] يتحمد بالمنة: holding one's favours over others to claim praise. The opposite of handing praise back to its owner.
- [refs: 1:5 | roots: حمد | note] أحمد إليك الله أي معك: praising God together with the addressee. Faintly anticipates the shared "we".
- [refs: 1:2, 1:5 | roots: اله | develop] فالإله الله تعالى لأنه معبود. The praised name is glossed as the one worshipped.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: ربب | core] ربب B001: ownership, command and management (ورب كل شيء مالكه; رببت القوم: سستهم). Used absolutely it is God's alone; for humans it must be annexed.
- [refs: 1:2, 18:82 | roots: ربب | core] ربب B002: رب الضيعة أي أصلحها وأتمها; رب فلان ولده; completing a favour; إنشاء الشيء حالا فحالا; ويكون الرب: المصلح. Lordship as improving, raising and completing in stages.
- [refs: 29:63 | roots: ربب | develop] ربب B008: الرباب: السحاب، سمي بذلك لأنه يرب النبات. The lexicographers' reason for a cloud's name, "because it nurtures the plants", gives the tarbiya sense a material image.
- [refs: 3:79 | roots: ربب | note] رباني: the scholar who educates people from small matters upward. Relevant to the open R-3:79 link.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: ربب | note] رُبّ (thick extract used to treat and strengthen a waterskin), لزوم (staying), the ship captain: separate branches, not used.
- [refs: 26:18, 17:24 | roots: ربو | develop] ECHO ربو B005: ربيته وتربيته أي غذوته (feeding and raising). A sound family only, not the root of رَبِّ. Usable as analogy where the Quran places the two side by side.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: علم | core] علم B003: العالمون كل جنس من الخلق فهو في نفسه معلم وعلم; العالم… في الأصل اسم لما يعلم به. The lexicographers' explanation of the name: each kind of creation is itself a sign, and "world" originally meant that by which something is known. This supplies the dictionary support that the U-علم open observation said was missing.
- [refs: 16:16 | roots: علم | develop] علم B002: flag, tall mountain, road marker; distinctive signs that guide. The same root family as the "sign" explanation.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: علم | note] علم B001: knowing as the opposite of ignorance. It stands behind the explanation "that by which something is known".

### Readings and grammar
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: حمد | core] A nominal sentence with no verb, no tense and no named praiser: praise is stated as a lasting ownership, not as an act.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: حمد | note] [recall] The definite article al- read as covering all praise, or as praise as a kind; both readings existed among the grammarians.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: — | core] li-: ownership, due right and restriction. It contrasts with bi- in 1:1.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: ربب علم | core] rabbi is a genitive title attached to the name. rabb is annexed to al-ʿālamīn, the widest possible annexation.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: علم | note] [recall] The -īn plural is normally used for rational beings, and whether ʿālam may take it at all was discussed. Fits the uses of al-ʿālamīn for communities.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: حمد | note] [recall] Non-canonical al-ḥamda (accusative, implying a verb) would make praise an act. The canonical nominative keeps it a standing fact.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: حمد | note] [recall] al-ḥamdi lillāh and al-ḥamdu lullāh: vowel harmony in non-canonical readings; the referent does not change.
- [refs: 1:2 | roots: ربب | note] [recall] rabba (accusative, as praise or specification) is a non-canonical reading.
- [refs: 1:4 | roots: ملك | note] [recall] Māliki / maliki (owner / king), both canonical: this affects how rabb → mālik reads.

### Fatiha
- [refs: 1:2, 10:10, 39:75 | roots: حمد | core] The Fatiha opens with the formula that elsewhere closes a story, and it is repeated in every prayer. The end's verdict is said at the start of the path.
- [refs: 1:2, 6:162 | roots: ربب علم صلو | develop] The prayer in which 1:2 is recited is itself declared lillāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn.
- [refs: 1:2, 1:5, 10:10 | roots: حمد | develop] The movement from third person to direct address reverses the Garden people's order.
