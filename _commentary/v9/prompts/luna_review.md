# Editorial review of one ayah reading (report only)

You review a finished Turkish reading of one Quranic ayah. You do not rewrite it. Read `PROSE_EDITORIAL_GUIDE.md`

## The assignment the reading had to meet

The reader already has the plain meaning. The reading develops the ayah's supported latent meanings and
resonances — senses the dictionaries attest for its words' roots, activated by the ayah's words, its construction,
the surah, the Fatiha or other Quranic usage — and builds a readable argument from them. The plan (below) lists its
connected readings: several roots' senses converging on one word of the ayah. Those convergences are the substance;
grammar and context support and explain them. A resonance may be the centre of a section while remaining a
resonance: a claim's kind is not its importance. Images the dictionaries supply are evidence, not decoration.

## What to review, in this order

1. **The latent layer** (the most important check):
   - Which connected readings of the plan are developed in the body? Are their contributing senses named, their
     images shown, and what they reveal together explained?
   - Did any core reading become only a mention, a disclaimer or an Ek Notlar line?
   - Is a boundary ("not the word's meaning here", "a sound echo, not the origin") stated once, or repeated so that
     it diminishes the reading?
   - Would the main argument stand essentially unchanged if the rare lexical material were removed?
2. **Argument and progression**: does each section add something the earlier ones did not; do quotations of other ayat
   each do identifiable work; are alternatives kept where they matter?
3. **Reader difficulty**: passages where a reader without notes must stop and reconstruct the meaning; unclear
   referents; mechanical cadence; decoration without inference; repeated explanations.
4. **The ending**: does it follow from what the body established?
