## Part 1 — the findings ledger (before any prose)

Before writing, collect every finding the evidence supports, so that nothing found is lost because the reading
took another direction. The ledger is kept as a record for the editor; it is not shown to the reader. Work through
the evidence in this order, and give each family its own heading:

1. `### Surah` — relations inside the surah: repeated roots and formulas, earlier and later ayat that prepare,
   answer, reverse or complete this one, the surah's argument around it.
2. `### Quran` — other ayat and scenes: the same word or root elsewhere, repeated formulas, parallel or contrasting
   scenes, the same people. Include what you know from the whole Quran, not only what the files list.
3. `### Dictionary` — attested senses and images of the ayah's roots that change, ground or complicate a reading
   (quote the source phrase exactly when you use one).
4. `### Readings and grammar` — variant readings (qirāʾāt), constructions, particles and forms that change the
   meaning.
5. `### Fatiha` — links to the Fatiha, when there are any.

One line per finding: what it shows about this ayah, its anchor (ayah refs, the Arabic word or phrase), and one of
`core`, `develop`, `note` for how much it could change the reading. Record findings you will not use in the prose
too; unusual findings are welcome when the evidence supports them. Do not pad: a family may be short. Plain
Markdown lines; Arabic in the ledger may be written plainly (no tags).

## Part 2 — the reading

Then write the reading, following the brief below exactly. Develop the `core` findings and the `develop` findings
that belong to the same movement; leave the rest in the ledger. The ledger's order is a way of looking, not the
reading's outline.
