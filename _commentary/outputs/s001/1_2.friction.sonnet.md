# Friction report — 1:2

- **Word count mismatch between word_analysis and QAC (documented, not silent).** word_analysis treats this ayah as 5 critical words (ٱلْحَمْدُ, لِ, ٱللَّهِ, رَبِّ, ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ); QAC treats it as 4 words (لِلَّهِ as one word with two morphemes). coverage.word_analysis_qac_alignment flags this explicitly as `consistent: false` and instructs against auto-repair. This is not ambiguous once read, but it did require a judgment call on how to render لِ and ٱللَّهِ in prose: I introduced the fused Quranic surface لِلَّهِ once, then discussed its two components (لِ, ٱللَّهِ) separately, following word_analysis's own five-word granularity rather than QAC's four. A different writer could reasonably present لِلَّهِ as a single span throughout instead. Not friction against the source, but a rendering choice worth flagging.

- **How far the "before and after" instruction may reach into later ayahs.** The prompt requires ayah-level prose to render v12_focus_trace_hermetic's before/after material "as reading experience," and separately requires that any later-ayah word being leaned on get "its own full span" first, and separately forbids layer 2 from carrying the surah's architecture or naming a recurring system. These three constraints pull against each other in practice: the strongest before/after material in this bundle (v12_cross_run_publication finding 1, the reader-walk retrospective surprise about رب vs. الضالين, and hermetic-trace context deltas c3/c6) all require citing words from 1:4 and 1:7. I resolved this by (a) giving each such word its own span before use, (b) keeping the claims tied to what 1:2's own words (حمد, رب) already carry rather than describing 1:4/1:7 as scenes in their own right, and (c) not naming any channel or surah thesis. But the line between "a local surprise anchored in this ayah's own word, activated by later context" and "carrying the surah's architecture" is a matter of degree the instructions describe but do not make mechanically checkable, and a reviewer could reasonably judge paragraph 8 as leaning further into 1:4/1:7 than a strictly ayah-2 reading should.

- **Root-letter display ban vs. root-level reader payoffs.** The instructions forbid displaying roots as spaced Arabic letters or letter-by-letter transliteration in prose, and require root discussion to anchor to the surface word instead. Several must_integrate topics (e.g. `nurture-mastery-owner-polysemy`, `knowledge-and-sign-bearing-pressure`) are *about* the root's branch structure rather than the inflected surface form alone. I worked around this by writing phrases like "Rab kelimesinin kök alanı" / "el-âlemîn'in kök alanı" instead of citing the root letters, which satisfies the letter of the rule but occasionally reads slightly more abstract than citing the root directly would have. No instruction conflict once the rule is applied consistently, but it constrains how root-branch material can be phrased.
