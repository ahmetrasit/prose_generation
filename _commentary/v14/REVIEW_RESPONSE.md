# Check of the quoted Sol review

The review is largely fair about the first Sol's prose and its accounting. Its model conclusion is stronger than
the experiment supports, and several operational findings have changed with the second run.

The first-run metrics (4,971 words, 44 paragraphs, median 113, 27 tags) reproduce exactly. The 23/30 integrated
anchor count is the v13 reviewer's recorded judgment in EVAL_ANCHORS.md; the table does not publish that Sol arm's
30 individual verdicts, so it is not independently reconstructed here. Both readability and preserved explanations
matter; a root-mention frequency proxy measures neither by itself. The review's praise of the balance sequence,
working frame, gift circuit, companions and Moses/marriage scene is supported by the prose.

The criticism of weak local anchoring is valid. Many dictionary and Quran claims have only Turkish prose or a
reference, which does not provide the lexical evidence of a source-bearing quotation. However, “the verifier sees
a fifth of the claims” is not a measured coverage rate. `verify_src.py` checks tagged Arabic against declared text;
it does not semantically verify the claim, even when a tag is present. Tags, distinct references and claims have
different denominators. The locally checked reader renderer links valid plain S:A references to ayat; that is
navigation, not a source hover or proof of the surrounding interpretation. More tags alone would not solve either
semantic verification or catalogue prose.

Serial touring remains a real issue. Short paragraphs improve local readability but do not supply a sustained
argument or reader orientation. The frozen v14 prompt permits headings rather than requiring an argument map.
Similarly, the composition pass receives already developed findings and a frozen disclosure plan; it was not a
clean test of the model's independent discovery ability. “Its own bridges are small” is a useful observation about
this output, not a demonstrated limitation of Sol across different instructions and inputs.

The second arm has now been independently reviewed, though not scored on the 30-anchor sheet. Its 23 tagged
quotations all pass exactly, with no repairs. It restores 20:10 and 17:97, cuts accounting from 44,781 to 20,173
bytes, and retains the principal explanatory developments. It also loses the earlier 18:1–2 Book connection while
claiming its larger source record connected. Accounting remains 32.1% of output and still overclaims. Thus the
first-run critique transfers in part, but its four source failures and 51.4% accounting figure are historical.
The exact evidence and limits are in [STABILIZATION_COMPARISON.md](STABILIZATION_COMPARISON.md).

The project's NORTH_STAR.md does state an Opus-high production preference based on older runs. That is a prior
project decision, not a fresh controlled finding that this Sol writer cannot transfer. The user explicitly said
max effort and cost are acceptable for this experiment. Billing remains unknown; the second run's observed elapsed
time is about 30 minutes. Neither unknown billing nor max effort should be used to reject its quality in this review.

The proposed new network followed by one writer is a reasonable test of planning under the new brief. Taking the
network's roles directly removes the script's assembly-ranking proxy. It still needs independent evidence that
those roles preserve important explanations and put each working part where the reader needs it. A new network,
new handover and different writer together cannot isolate a writer-model effect. No v13 call was initiated or
interrupted by this review.

Transfer the working mechanisms, source access, paragraph discipline and reader continuity. Replace bulky
self-accounting with minimal traceability and explicit partial use/deferrals, while keeping independent review.
Do not discard the writer on evidence that primarily diagnoses the input, contract and evaluation. A model
limitation remains possible; this comparison has not established it.
