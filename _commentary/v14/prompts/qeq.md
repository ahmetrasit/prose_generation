# v13 step 2 — the Quran explaining the findings (English records only)

The focus ayah's latent readings have been found (records `F<n>`), and the surah's images assembled (network entries
`I<n>`, when present). Now ask what the rest of the Quran says about them: does it support, expand, shift or
contradict each one? And which passages work on the ayah's own main axes? A last step writes the reader's prose from
your records and the findings.

## Rules

- Work from your knowledge of the whole Quran and the window text. Cite single ayat, never ranges. Give references
  only; no Arabic quotations (the prose step quotes, and every quotation is checked against the canonical text).
- Annotate the findings of every kind except `loss`, `grammar` and `fragment` (the prose step handles those; grammar's
  Quranic parallels belong to the axis pass). Write no line for a finding the Quran neither supports, expands, shifts
  nor contradicts.
- The Quran's traditional readings rest on the canonical sense of each word. **A passage read canonically never
  suppresses a latent reading.** When a passage seems to go against a finding, say exactly how: often it bounds or
  shifts it rather than contradicting it. Only a passage that states the opposite of the finding contradicts it; then
  the finding is marked `contradicts`, never dropped (the prose step decides how to present it).
- You annotate findings, never whole images: an image of the surah is not judged from one of its members here.
- Finding no passage that tells a latent scene openly is never a contradiction.
- Passages that touch none of the ayah's axes and none of the findings are left out.
- The words of the passages you bring can themselves activate branches of the focus words (a fourth scope, besides the
  ayah, the window and the surah): record those as new findings.
- A passage where the Quran tells a latent scene openly is valuable; its absence says nothing against a finding.
- Tag each cited passage that does special work: `S:A [staging]` when it tells a latent scene openly, `S:A
  [same-word]` when it uses the focus word (same lemma) itself. The prose step must use every tagged passage.
- "shifts" and "expands" say what the Quran adds beside the finding; they never reverse it.
- **Axis pass:** independently of the findings, bring the passages that explain the ayah's plain sense and grammar:
  the same wording or construction elsewhere, the Quran's own definitions of its terms, parallel scenes, passages that
  specify or limit what the ayah leaves general (lines of kind `axis`).

## Your final message

A line `===== QEQ =====`, then:
- for each finding `F<n>`, one line:
  `F<n> | supports|expands|shifts|contradicts | S:A [tag], S:A | <what the passages do to the finding, 1–2 sentences>`
- then new findings, one line each, in the step-1 record form with kind `qeq`:
  `Q<n> | qeq | words: S:A:W … | branches: … | trigger: S:A:W (qeq) … | with: F<m> … | shows: …`
  (for a passage that works on the ayah's axes without a branch, leave `branches:` empty and say its job in `shows:`).
- then the axis pass, one line each: `A<n> | axis | S:A [tag] … | <its job for the plain sense or grammar>`
English, compact.
