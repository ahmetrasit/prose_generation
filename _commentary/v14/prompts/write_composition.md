# Turkish commentary from an integrated explanation

Write the finished ayah commentary for a curious Turkish reader with almost no Arabic grammar. Their Arabic
loanwords may have narrowed or shifted. Start from the plain sense and lead them through the supplied explanation
so they can reread the ayah with a changed understanding.

The explanation is an interpretive proposal with selected evidence, not an authority. Understand and check it.
Its connected reasoning supplies the movement of the commentary; the source appendix is not an outline. Develop
how readings illuminate one another and change the ayah's action, relationships or address. Do not restart the
research as a survey of roots, image fragments or Quranic parallels. Include a source because it does explanatory
work here, not because it has been supplied. Quotation accuracy alone does not make an inference illuminating.

Keep distinct supported surprises that matter to the explanation. Do not replace them with a generic moral or
plain paraphrase. Preserve Turkish lexical losses and consequential grammar where the reader needs them, without
making either a separate tour. QeQ belongs where its wording or scene changes understanding. Multiple readings
can coexist; an attested related sense is not automatically the contextual translation or a proven etymology.

You receive the focus text, surrounding Quranic text, an integrated explanation, selected dictionary and usage
evidence, exact selected Quran passages and material qualifications. The full frozen research remains available
through the logged helper if a source, claim or omitted relationship needs checking. Retrieve for that question,
not to exhaust the archive. No previous target commentary, evaluation or external research is available. If no
previous-reader prose is supplied, do not pretend something has already been explained to the reader.

Use natural, precise Turkish and manageable paragraphs. Give orientation where the argument needs it; headings
are optional and must express real turns in understanding. The amount of prose follows the contribution. There
is no word target, quotation quota, fixed image count or requirement to mention every supplied source. Remove
repeated setup, redundant parallels and excursions that leave the reader's understanding unchanged. End by
rereading the ayah through what has been developed, without introducing a final batch of material.

Every Arabic quotation has exactly this form:

    {ar:ARABIC, tr:transliteration, gloss:Türkçe karşılık, source:SOURCE}

SOURCE is a Quranic S:A or spaced Arabic root plus Bnnn; comma-separated sources are allowed. Copy the exact
Arabic from supplied source lines or the logged helper. No commas inside ar/tr, colon inside gloss, extra fields
or other curly braces. Quote the short wording that carries a lexical or constructional inference at its point
of use. A bare reference can support narrative context; it cannot show an unfamiliar word's meaning. Use readable
Turkish transliteration, with intact words and ş, ç, c, ğ, â, î, û as appropriate. Keep gloss and contextual
interpretation distinct. Retain actual counter-evidence; concise containment should clarify, not repeatedly defend
the act of reading a resonance.

Return the commentary only. No JSON, inventory accounting, source-selection report, internal IDs, self-review,
workflow discussion or promise to publish unused material elsewhere. The research archive is preserved by code;
an independent review checks the result and any consequential losses after writing.
