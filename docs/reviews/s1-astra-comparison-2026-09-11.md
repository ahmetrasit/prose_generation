# S1 Astra High Comparison

Review date: 2026-09-11. Interim review of a developing run, not a final acceptance report.

## Overall Judgment

The new run is a substantial improvement in evidence handling and interpretive explanation. It is a mixed improvement as finished editorial prose. Its invitations are substantially closer to the intended experience: choosing a concrete resonance and showing how it expands the ordinary reading.

The improvement is not simply more findings or more references. The strongest change is that the new text distinguishes things the older text sometimes merged: possessing a resource versus reaching it; being shown a direction versus being able to follow it; preserving a developing life versus caring for the body that carried it; disappearing from view versus losing access to a still-existing place.

The costs are substantial length, repeated explanations, persistent defensive qualifications, and less exploratory breadth in some discovery decisions. There is also a significant, still-provisional retention gap in the current 1:5 editorial.

Do not revert discovery on the strength of this comparison. Preserve the analytical improvements; assess completion first, then address prose repetition and the few specific fidelity problems.

## Ayah-by-Ayah Findings

### 1:1: Better Attribution, Less Associative Breadth

**Gained:** The opening more carefully distinguishes ordinary naming, proposed name/mark relationships, concrete lexical images, and contextual support. It differentiates actual gestational development from naming and speech rather than presenting them as one event. Foster care, postpartum care, rain, and visible leadership receive more specific explanations. The wider-context reading of 9:118 adds an important movement: mercy can enable the return itself, not merely receive a return already accomplished.

**Lost or narrowed:** The earlier editorial's field was more adventurous: competitive reputation, poetic exchange, hunting/dispersal, a ceremonial captain, developmental body markers, and a name-based protected legal status. Several of these channels are rejected upstream in the new discovery, not merely hidden by the editor. For example, the protected-person reading now notices that the opening names Allah, not a refugee whose status has been certified. The camel entering a female herd is also not freely converted into a generic human entry scene.

These are real reductions in exploratory range, but several remove weak participant mappings. The old intensity should not be treated as independent evidence of validity. Conversely, not every more conservative judgment is demonstrably superior; the gain is better articulated selection criteria, not proof that all rejected possibilities are worthless.

**Invitation:** Clear improvement over the old grammar-led explanation. It selects gestation, development, and the return of the mercy pair. It makes the unfinished beginning intelligible as something that needs room and sustained care. Its repeated boundary language still consumes a noticeable share of the short text.

### 1:2: A Stronger Account of What Care Actually Does

**Gained:** The new prose differentiates source, access, maintenance, and benefit. Water in a well is not yet water available to the dependent life above it; the support and apparatus do distinct work. This is a genuine enlargement of praise for provision into praise for the conditions of access. Other useful developments include care preserving a benefit until its recipients can use it (18:82), praise not depending on actual human gratitude, and a shared Lord not requiring identical human responses (2:139).

The critique of a human benefactor claiming unlimited credit was already present. The new version makes it more explicit and better supported; it should not be counted as an entirely new discovery. Similarly, care, knowledge, legible signs, and the maintained route were already substantial old readings. Astra explains their separate operations more fully.

**Lost or weakened:** The old 3,220-word editorial was considerably easier to survey. At 8,664 words, the new account repeatedly revisits care, recognition, agency, and maintenance. Rejected poetic exchange, rivalry, and ceremonial combinations reduce speculative variety, though some had little developed presence in the old final prose anyway. They are discovery differences, not all reader-facing losses.

**Invitation:** One of the best upgrades. The old invitation mainly explained the sentence. The new one chooses the well, its water, the supporting wood, and the apparatus, then explains the important difference between having provision and making it usable. Each image has a legible contribution.

### 1:3: The Most Distinctive New Mercy Reading

**Gained:** The new text makes postpartum care materially specific: the mother who has carried the developing life can still need care after birth. Retained fetal membranes are distinguished from generic birth pain. This shifts attention from the successful arrival of the recipient to the continuing vulnerability of the carrier. The older text had gestation and pain, but did not develop this change of recipient nearly as clearly.

Other meaningful expansions include mercy as enabling conditions before harm, learning and expression before wrongdoing, and care beyond biological kinship. Marriage and gift acquire distinct relational functions. Contextual counterexamples also matter: relief need not produce gratitude, warnings can participate in care, and a merciful relation need not imply dependence of the giver on the recipient.

**Cost:** At 2.72 times the old length, the editorial becomes a dense analytical account. Repeated qualifications and clusters of references can interrupt the image before it has a chance to register. Much of the underlying womb/care insight is inherited and deepened, not discovered from nothing.

**Invitation:** A substantial improvement in surprise and specificity. Its strongest sentence-level movement is that the person needing care can change. The trade-off is that it now ends inside a highly specific medical image, giving less space to the old movement from sheltered formation into guided life. That is a selection trade-off, not evidence that the other reading was rejected.

### 1:4: Better Limits on the Debt Reading; Excessive Expansion

**Gained:** The new editorial more clearly distinguishes the differently vocalized debt term from the ayah's judgment/accounting term, and ready resources from an advance. The relation remains striking: the one who will assess the answer is also asked for the capacity to answer. It develops this through received benefits, promises in distress, and conduct after relief.

The refusal-of-ransom contexts prevent the economic analogy from implying that salvation can simply be purchased. Delegated earthly power, personal and collective records, and permission-dependent intercession receive more discriminating treatment. The gestation/maturing-obligation reading remains in the editorial; it has not disappeared from discovery or final long prose.

**Cost:** The editorial is now about 9,600 words versus 3,409. Several excellent passages compete for attention, while the debt/gestation connection becomes difficult to locate among many returns to accountability.

**Invitation:** Mixed, rather than an unqualified win. The new version is more precise about evidence, obligation, and the capacity to respond. But the previous invitation's gestation-and-maturing-debt channel is gone from this short selection. The new material about benefits and promises is clearer, but less imagistically surprising. This is an invitation-selection loss, not an upstream finding loss.

### 1:7: Better Causal and Moral Distinctions, Still Too Much Recapitulation

**Gained:** The prose no longer leans so easily on three mutually exclusive, fixed classes. It distinguishes the passive target of anger from the agent who opposes someone; an active participle is not made sufficient proof of chosen, continuous, irreversible rebellion. Actual overlap and differing agency are examined through contextual examples.

Several old overstatements are corrected. The prior editorial invoked 4:56 as restorative renewal, though the verse's setting is punishment. It also described a loss as disappearing before reaching accounting, and moved too readily from opposition to anger to hardened obstruction. The new editorial distinguishes repair from punitive replacement, expressly retains accountability, and gives the separate mechanisms and counterexamples rather than one necessary chain.

The best positive expansion is practical belonging: a person may retain the destination but lose access to it, forget the information needed to reach it, or lose the relationship that sustains movement. Disappearing while progressing is different from losing the trace by which one could return. Another strong development is that an accusation of error must be read in relation to who is speaking; the accusation itself is not automatically the narrator's verdict.

**Cost:** The fuller argument is more than twice the old length. Lines 205-211 repeat much of the place/memory/care argument. Other sections explain each image, summarize those functions, then qualify the summarized relation again. The old prose often felt more vivid because it allowed images to merge more freely; the new version rightly corrects several mergers but sometimes substitutes an audit-like cadence.

**Invitation:** Among the largest gains. The old version mainly walked through the grammatical exclusion. The new one selects passage, lost access, and memory, and gives them a connected interpretive payoff. The cost is less attention to the anger contrast, which is acceptable for a selective invitation so long as the full editorial retains it.

## Remaining Issues and Their Significance

### 1. The Presentation Follow-Up Improves Function More Than Economy

The requested clarification of each image's contribution is visibly working. Wood supports, apparatus enables access, water supplies, and care makes the supply usable. This is far more useful than an unexplained inventory of objects.

But the result often states the function twice: once while introducing each image, again in a summary of their relation, followed by several exclusions. The distinction between inherited material and genuinely new implications becomes harder to scan. This is the principal reader-facing regression across the set.

The issue is not that every qualification should disappear. Preserve the meaningful limits once, close to the interpretation they constrain. Remove duplicate restatement, not evidence or findings. This recommendation concerns editorial presentation, not new discovery instructions.

### 2. The Negative Tone Is Reduced in Density, Not Solved

As a deliberately crude check, occurrences of the Turkish negative copula stem rose from 123 to 176, while total prose length more than doubled. Their density therefore fell from roughly 4.6 to 3.0 per thousand words. This does not count other negative constructions or measure tone reliably.

The qualitative problem remains: recurring statements that the ayah does not describe literal organs, real transactions, actual engineering, a legal procedure, or a guaranteed result can dominate a paragraph's ending. Some are essential; repeated hypothetical exclusions are not equivalent to a warm, affirmative second-layer explanation.

The invitations generally do better because they hold one connected image longer. Nevertheless, 1:1 and 1:3 both begin from gestation, and 1:5 and 1:6 both center assisted walking. Their contributions differ, but the set offers less variety than the available findings would permit.

### 3. A Source-Polarity Problem Survives Selectively

The supplied `root_000973/B009` has an ambiguous short label, "brief delay and slight haste," but its detailed scope says "Brief non-delay and a modestly quick run." The Arabic scope gives the negated expression meaning that the person did not linger.

The current 1:5 editorial at line 95 explicitly preserves promptness and rejects turning it into a positive delay. Current 1:4 at line 91 and 1:7 at line 59 still use a short pause/delay in a changing-tempo interpretation; 1:6 at line 59 mixes little lingering and non-delay. The new run is therefore inconsistent in resolving the same supplied detail.

This matters because it changes the reading's action, not just its tone. It is not an established new regression: the old 1:7 also used short delay. The discrepancy partly originates in the packet's short label versus detailed scope. It needs targeted reconciliation, not a general retreat from imaginative readings.

### 4. Some Apparent Losses Are Corrections

The supplied `root_000973/B012` is a perfume mortar/preparation implement. It does not, by itself, supply burning incense or perfumed air. The previous 1:5 prose's smoking vessel went beyond that evidence. Rejecting the ceremonial combination, or retaining the captain without that mechanism, is a gain in fidelity even though it removes an attractive image.

Likewise, a legal analogy requires the named person, holder of responsibility, and protected person to occupy defensible roles. Merely disclaiming that it is literal law does not repair a faulty mapping. The new discovery more often performs this check.

I did not flag the different treatment of easy swallowing in 1:1/1:7 versus the restricted Mekayis-based connection in 1:6 as a simple contradiction. The supplied evidence differs in specificity; the 1:1 branch expressly includes ease and width of throat. A narrow restriction for one connection is not a ban on those details everywhere.

## Recommendation

Keep the new analytical work. The strongest gains are evidence-role discipline, broader and more discriminating contextual readings, recovery of 1:6's macro range, and invitations that actually select a surprising channel.

Before judging the set final, recheck 1:5's concrete-image retention after development stops. Then make tightly scoped editorial changes: remove duplicate explanations, keep each substantive qualification once, and preserve the now-clear contribution of each image. Reconsider invitation selection where the loss is meaningful, especially the gestation/debt reading in 1:4 and the overlap between 1:5 and 1:6.

The defensible model conclusion is: **this Astra-led run is analytically stronger overall, but not uniformly better edited, and one evolving run does not isolate the model effect from the new follow-up or normal run variation.** A clean future comparison would hold discovery packets, downstream prompts, follow-up, and completion status fixed, then compare matched runs or pass the same scope material to both editorial configurations.
