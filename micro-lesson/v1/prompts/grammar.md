# Engine: grammar

Apply common.md. Ask how this feature helps the reader understand the expression:
what does the form contribute, and how can the reader recognize that contribution?
Explore the full grammar coverage in ONTOLOGY.md:
word pieces, noun/verb morphology, pronouns, case and mood, phrases, clauses,
particles, participant relations, agreement, order and speech function. These are
directions for discovery, not a lesson checklist.

Explain the grammatical mechanism through the local expression. “Bi- ilişki kurar”
does not tell the beginner which relationship or how to read it. For example:
“بِسْمِ (bi-smi) sözünde ism ‘ad’dır; bi- burada ‘ile’ ilişkisini ekleyerek ‘adıyla’
anlamını kurar. Türkçe bu ilişkiyi ad-ıyla sözünün sonunda, Arapça ise bi-ism sözünün
başında gösterir.” The lesson explains the particle's meaning and placement through
a familiar Turkish construction. This is an example, not a required sentence formula.
Choose the meaningful mechanism for each passage: a participant role, a relation,
a negation, an ending or another useful distinction. Do not stop at naming a form,
pointing out matching letters or translating the whole phrase.

Ground the observation in the expression and available analysis. Morphology can
support a form claim without proving a rhetorical or theological inference.
Distinguish Arabic nominal case, verbal mood, derivation and Turkish case suffixes.
Not every genitive ends in -i; not every nâ is an object; not every fronted element
means only; not every imperative is a command in its speaking context.

Separate what each part contributes before explaining the whole expression.
For example, in لَمْ يُذْكَرِ the negative meaning requires lam; the verb alone
does not mean “anılmadı.” In nafs-i-hi, nafs supplies “self” and -hi supplies the
third-person reference; do not attribute “self” to the suffix. Use the supplied
morpheme analysis to settle such local questions, not Turkish translation alone.

Develop a useful feature beyond its first local explanation. For bi-, look for
distinct lessons about its ordinary relations, the noun or pronoun it attaches to,
case marking, limits of a single Turkish gloss, Quranic contrasts and special
constructions such as kafā bi-llāh. Retrieve the relevant verse or grammatical
account when needed. These are discovery directions, not a fixed number of lessons.
Explain a special construction on its own terms; a recognized construction need
not be labeled incorrect or irregular. Keep a documented alternative qualified.
See [the authored bi- family](../examples/bi-lesson-family.md) for distinct teaching
sentences across these directions; follow its explanatory approach, not its count.

Teach the useful distinction with a local or retrieved comparison. Keep one
coherent observation even if it teaches several concepts across grammar, discourse
and Turkish mapping. For example,
recognizing the imperative in ihdinâ and its prayer function can form one lesson.
The object suffix can be a separate lesson if it has independent learning value.

Introduce prerequisite knowledge locally when this passage supplies a natural
example. Otherwise a compact shared introduction can help. Do not turn a simple
lesson into a compressed paradigm or terminology course. Several short lessons can
together develop the construction's uses and boundaries; brevity limits each lesson,
not the depth or range of discovery.

Annotate by the taught content, not by this engine's name. Form-family meaning
belongs mainly to semantics and gloss tradeoffs mainly to mapping, but preserve
useful overlaps for the assembler to judge against the wording and evidence.
