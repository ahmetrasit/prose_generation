# Engine: mapping

Apply common.md. Ask what this occurrence teaches about understanding Arabic
through Turkish. The dictionary's existing branch-specific gloss work is the
foundation; do not generate a second independent dictionary analysis.

1. Identify the applicable branch and lexical unit using their actual boundaries,
   identity qualifications and lexicalization conditions. Root identity alone is
   insufficient. Read the relevant concept facets and their roles.
2. Read the existing concept, contextual and lexical Turkish glosses, applicability,
   excluded glosses, neighbor distinctions and error profiles. Use the reviewed
   gloss result together with its source definition. Distinguish encyclopedia
   `error_profile` from compact `error`; both describe the chosen gloss's cost.
3. Identify the exact gloss/reading being compared. It may come from the dictionary
   even when the commentary uses different wording; do not attribute it to the
   commentary. Include a compact locator identifying the branch and specific gloss.
4. Decide which preserved, missing, added, narrowed, broadened or displaced feature
   actually matters here. The profile is evidence to interpret, not a command to
   label the translation wrong. Narrowing may be appropriate contextual selection;
   another word in the expression may already supply an omitted feature.
5. Explain the useful distinction in ordinary Turkish, preserving the recognized
   contextual meaning. Give a usable clarification, not only a warning of loss.

Do not expand a branch's complete conceptual definition into the translation of
every occurrence. Do not copy a gloss profile onto every branch of its root. If a
profile mentions a source-variant facet, do not treat that as an obligatory word
meaning. Excluded glosses retain their specific exclusion scope.

Loanwords and ordinary non-loan translations can both reveal useful opportunities.
Do not generalize about all Turkish readers. A `drifted_loanword` label or a local
mismatch does not supply a historical trajectory: historical claims require actual
historical evidence. No explanation of salât/namaz or hidayet should replace the
local meaning with the broadest imagined root meaning.

Annotate multiple concepts as needed: a single contrast can teach a gloss cost,
a contextual selection principle and a grammatical relation. It remains one lesson
when those are facets of the same observation. Do not mechanically produce one
lesson for preserves, one for loses and one for adds if they repeat the same point.
