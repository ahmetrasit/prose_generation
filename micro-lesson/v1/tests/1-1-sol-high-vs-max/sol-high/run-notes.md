## Main linguistic findings

- `بِسْمِ` contains the `bi-` relation and `ism` “ad”; the action begun by that expression is not written in 1:1. The explicit verbs in 96:1 and 56:74 make the omission teachable without claiming a particular supplied verb as Quran text.
- The word-specific root analysis gives `اسم` a primary `س م و` derivation and a documented `و س م` alternative. The distinct name, visible shape, sky/covering, fame, physical mark, sign-reading, first-rain, and gathering branches were kept separate. “Ad” remains the local gloss in 1:1.
- `الله` is the special name in 1:1. The `إله` derivation is explicitly qualified in the source. The `و ل ه` derivation is a documented alternative, not the verse’s lexical meaning. Dictionary call and oath examples were labeled as dictionary examples.
- `الرحمن` and `الرحيم` share `ر ح م`, but the supplied branch does not license a fixed semantic formula from their patterns. The packet does support Rahîm applied to a human in 9:128, the bi- phrase naming the recipients in 33:43, Rahmân standing as an address/name, and `رحمة` as both compassionate orientation and beneficent action. The organ, kinship, and illness branches remain distinct.
- Indexed original dictionary entries were consulted for the commentary’s cross-verse links: `ٱهْدِنَا` at 1:6 versus sacrificial `هدي`, `رَبّ` versus a separate rearing action, `ٱلضَّآلِّينَ` versus a stray animal, and `أَنْعَمْتَ` versus livestock. Their common roots do not transfer the other branch’s meaning into the cited verse.

## Evidence limits, deferrals, and rejected transfers

The four deferred opportunities are recorded with exact anchors and questions in `page-output.json` and `lessons.md`: an asserted Turkish history of *isim* (p06); an exceptionless “Rahmân overfull / Rahîm continuously directed” pattern rule (p20); an asserted Turkish historical narrowing of *rahmet* and loss of its relation to *rahim* (p25); and a womb-to-divine-names image with specific fullness and direction claims (p27). The shared packet has no dated Turkish semantic attestations and no source phrase establishing the two pattern or womb formulas.

The reviewed dictionary projection and upstream source are different views of the same material, not two independent confirmations. Some upstream entries carry `editorial_review` or `repair`, and some review-response paths are unavailable here. I checked the local claim against the branch’s Arabic source phrase, identity boundary, gloss scope, and QAC occurrence rather than rejecting every marked entry. The Allah formation stayed explicitly qualified. The p24 `رحوم` lesson is limited to the cited postnatal camel example; the broader organ-illness branch is not turned into a claim that mercy itself requires bodily pain.

I did not promote the commentary’s “name rises physically,” “name is literally a camel brand,” “being marked proves ownership,” or “Rahmân/Rahîm are the womb’s fullness and inward direction” images into lexical definitions. Nor did I let `ihdinâ` mean transporting a sacrificial animal, `dâllîn` mean ownerless camels, or `en'amte` mean cattle. The first set can work as stated comparisons when their limits are clear; the latter branch transfers fail for the cited lexical units. The 1:1 phrase and the verse snippets in the commentary sometimes differ in diacritic encoding from the canonical packet; Quran anchors in the JSON were aligned to the exact `ayat/*.json` text, while the supplied Turkish readings were retained.
