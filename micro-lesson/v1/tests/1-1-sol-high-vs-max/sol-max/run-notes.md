## Linguistic findings and editorial choices

- 1:1 gives a bi- + ism construction and names without an explicit action verb. 96:1 and 56:74 name different actions beside bi-smi; neither action is copied into 1:1 as its written wording. Allah is a proper name in 1:1; ilâh is the general term for a worshipped being.
- Ism is locally “ad.” The reviewed س م و primary analysis and و س م alternative are confined to the documented ism lemma, including its plural; roof, rain, stamp and good reputation branches are separate lexical uses. The “good reputation” branch B008 has one cited source and is taught only as that source's usage.
- 19:65 semiyy can mean more than a namesake: the contextual gloss permits someone who would deserve the name as a peer. 15:75 supports “signs read to reach a conclusion,” with the branch-scope qualification recorded below.
- Rahmân and Rahîm share ر ح م, but a root and two patterns alone do not prove the commentary's “overflowing source / continuous direction to individuals” formula. 33:43 explicitly supplies the direction with bi’l-mü’minîn. 7:156 explicitly supplies breadth with “my mercy encompassed every thing.” 19:45 names punishment from Rahmân, which resists a one-dimensional gloss.
- رَحِم the organ, رَحْمَة the mercy field, and رَحِم the kinship relation were kept in their separate supplied branches. The organ-to-kin relation is actually stated in B002; 7:151 supports a local “enter mercy” image without equating mercy to the organ.
- Mapping decisions use the actual contextual or lexical gloss and its profile: “adıyla” preserves bi-smi's name relation; “adaş” can understate the 19:65 denklik use; “damgalamak” narrows B001 to heated marking; “mühürlemek” adds sealing/official validation; “aile” broadens the kinship use in 4:1; “rahim” can denote the organ correctly in Turkish but is less explanatory than “döl yatağı.” A glossary loss about a separate branch facet was not imposed on every verse occurrence.

## Evidence limits, deferrals and rejections

- The packet has exact ayah Arabic and QAC morphemes, reviewed word-specific root alternatives, and branch/gloss files. The same family can have upstream, finalized and reviewed-gloss views. Those views are evidence alternatives, not three independent review passes. Review-response files referenced by some status objects are unavailable locally; no independent editorial review is claimed.
- The root_000745 source entry carries an `editorial_review` flag and root_000552 a `repair` flag. B005 for ism has an accepted identity and coherent final definition, so its “ad” claim is used with the source's etymology kept distinct. B004 for رحم was reframed to include organ disease beyond postpartum pain; p24 teaches only the quoted postpartum example. The flags were not blanket rejection rules.
- The reviewed gloss for root_001650/B001 labels “tanıtıcı fiziksel iz bırakarak işaretleme” as `fit: none`, while its source concept map also includes the remaining mark and heated tool. Local lessons use the appropriate lexical unit and do not treat that broad fit label as a verdict on mark/tool passages.
- The root_001650/B002 concept definition is restricted to a visible sign in a person; the same branch's reviewed lu_009 explicitly covers signs of a situation, the reading required by 15:75. The displayed p14 lesson teaches the ayah's supported sign-reading phrase without asserting the disputed full B002 boundary. The branch attachment question is deferred.
- Three Turkish historical trajectories in commentary (isim, mevsim, rahmet) remain deferred because no period-based Turkish attestations were supplied. Contemporary gloss contrasts do not establish their change over time. Rahmân/Rahîm pattern formulas and p27's “house divided into fullness and inclination” remain deferred with separate, concrete source questions.
- Rejected as lexical lessons: turning the p13 stamp analogy into the literal meaning of bismillâh; turning the p24 postpartum pain example into a necessary “mercy pays in pain” meaning; turning p04's claimed change in meat into the definition of ism; making p11 “emptiness” a feature of the noun “name.” The commentary may use those as literary or theological interpretation, but the supplied linguistic evidence does not license them as word meanings.
