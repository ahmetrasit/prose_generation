# Claim map of the page: id, claim, terms, verses

## ¶1
1a: The opening wa in al-ʿaṣr is an oath particle rather than a conjunction, so al-ʿaṣr is in the genitive.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1
1b: The object of the oath in 103:1 is left unstated there and deferred to the following verse.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1, 103:2
1c: The first verse functions as a threshold: it lets the reader hear what is sworn by before the judgment in 103:2 is disclosed.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr, إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ, al-insān la-fī khusr  · verses: 103:1, 103:2
1d: Because the oath term is placed next to the judgment of loss, hearing what al-ʿaṣr means is presented as a way to hear what kind of loss is meant.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr, خُسْرٍ, khusr  · verses: 103:1, 103:2

## ¶2
2a: The Qurʾān also swears by times of day in 89:1, 93:1, 92:1, and 74:34: dawn, forenoon, night as it covers, and morning as it brightens.  · terms: وَٱلْفَجْرِ, al-fajr, وَٱلضُّحَىٰ, al-ḍuḥā, وَٱلَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ, al-layl idhā yaghshā, وَٱلصُّبْحِ إِذَآ أَسْفَرَ, al-ṣubḥ idhā asfar  · verses: 89:1, 93:1, 92:1, 74:34
2b: Most of those other oath phrases qualify the time by an action, whereas al-ʿaṣr is unqualified and alone; the paragraph takes this as leaving its scope open.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr, إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ, idhā yaghshā, إِذَآ أَسْفَرَ, idhā asfar  · verses: 103:1, 92:1, 74:34
2c: Al-ʿaṣr can name a long span or era, the part of a day near evening, and the prayer performed at that time takes its name from it.  · terms: العصر الدهر, al-ʿaṣr al-dahr, العصر العشي, al-ʿaṣr al-ʿashiyy, صلاة العصر, ṣalāt al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1
2d: The definite article in al-ʿaṣr points to a known time, but the verse does not choose among afternoon, the age in which people live, or time itself, leaving these senses to be heard together.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶3
3a: Modern Turkish separates the senses: asır means a hundred years and asri means contemporary, while the day's late afternoon is called ikindi.
3b: The Turkish word asır does not evoke afternoon's fading light or the root's images of squeezing, refuge, and whirlwind.  · terms: العصر, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1
3c: The verse's al-ʿaṣr holds the hour and the era together, so the end of a day and the end of a lifetime bear one name.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶4
4a: Arabic uses the dual al-ʿaṣrān for two paired limits of the day: night and day, or early morning and evening.  · terms: العصران الليل والنهار, al-ʿaṣrān al-layl wa-l-nahār, العصران الغداة والعشي, al-ʿaṣrān al-ghadāh wa-l-ʿashiyy
4b: The phrase bi-l-ghadāti wa-l-ʿashiyy in 6:52 is cited as the Qurʾān naming the morning-and-evening pair that Arabic calls the two ʿaṣrs.  · terms: بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ, bi-l-ghadāti wa-l-ʿashiyy, العصران, al-ʿaṣrān  · verses: 6:52
4c: The phrase ṭarafay al-nahār in 11:114 is cited as the Qurʾān's wording for the day's two edges, corresponding to the paired limits called al-ʿaṣrān.  · terms: طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ, ṭarafay al-nahār, العصران, al-ʿaṣrān  · verses: 11:114
4d: Calling both ends of the day al-ʿaṣr shows the word grasping time at the entry or exit of a period; afternoon is its closing edge.  · terms: العصر, al-ʿaṣr, العصران, al-ʿaṣrān  · verses: 103:1

## ¶5
5a: The expression jāʾanī fulān ʿaṣran describes someone arriving late and slowly.  · terms: جاءني فلان عصرا, jāʾanī fulān ʿaṣran
5b: The phrase nāma wa-mā nāma li-ʿaṣr means that someone slept but did not sleep even for a moment; al-ʿaṣār can also mean a while.  · terms: نام وما نام لعصر, nāma wa-mā nāma li-ʿaṣr, العصار الحين, al-ʿaṣār al-ḥīn
5c: The word spans both a long era and a fleeting moment, and both an ordinary part of the day and the time of a delayed arrival.  · terms: العصر, al-ʿaṣr, جاءني فلان عصرا, jāʾanī fulān ʿaṣran, العصار, al-ʿaṣār
5d: The same word refers to a young woman's age of youth and maturity, and also to a person's lifespan and old age.  · terms: بلغت عصر شبابها وإدراكها, balaghat ʿaṣra shabābihā wa-idrākihā, العصر, al-ʿaṣr
5e: Late afternoon has the least time left in the day, as work nears its end and light fades; al-ʿaṣr therefore carries a feeling of lateness and dwindling time within the hour.  · terms: العصر, al-ʿaṣr

## ¶6
6a: In 79:42 the question about the Hour is answered in 79:46 by reducing a lifetime, as seen from the Hour, to the span of one evening or one forenoon.  · terms: ٱلسَّاعَةِ, al-sāʿah, عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَىٰهَا, ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā  · verses: 79:42, 79:46
6b: Although 79:46 does not use al-ʿaṣr, its ʿashiyy (evening) is treated as the afternoon sense that explains al-ʿaṣr; viewed from the end, the long era becomes an afternoon.  · terms: عَشِيَّةً, ʿashiyyah, العصر, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 79:46, 103:1
6c: The plea in 35:37 to be returned for good work is met with the reminder that people had already been granted a lifespan in which anyone who would reflect could do so.  · terms: أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم, a-wa-lam nuʿammirkum, مَا يَتَذَكَّرُ فِيهِ مَن تَذَكَّرَ, mā yatadhakkaru fīhi man tadhakkar  · verses: 35:37
6d: The time granted for reflection is the span in which the loss of 103:2 and the faith and good works of 103:3 take place.  · terms: خُسْرٍ, khusr, إِيمَان, īmān, عَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَات, ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt  · verses: 35:37, 103:2

## ¶7
7a: The concrete meaning of the root is to press something until liquid is made to flow from it.  · terms: ضغط شيء حتى يتحلب, ḍaghṭ shayʾin ḥattā yataḥallab, عصر, ʿaṣara
7b: Grapes were pressed in a feed-bag-like sack; the sack holds the grapes while hand or weight crushes them, and the juice drains through its weave. The device and place for pressing have names from this process.  · terms: المعصار, al-miʿṣār, المعصرة, al-miʿṣarah
7c: The liquid that runs off a pressing is called ʿuṣārah, and anything whose water is squeezed out is called ʿaṣīr.  · terms: العصارة, al-ʿuṣārah, كل شيء عصر ماؤه فهو عصير, kullu shayʾin ʿuṣira māʾuhu fa-huwa ʿaṣīr
7d: What remains in the sack is pulp, so pressing divides a thing into a flowing essence and a dry mass left behind.  · terms: العصارة, al-ʿuṣārah

## ¶8
8a: Arabic speakers made the flowing essence of a pressing a figure for goodness and giving; a generous person is described with the verb ʿaṣara, and ʿuṣārah can mean the yield from land.  · terms: العصارة والمعتصر, al-ʿuṣārah wa-l-muʿtaṣar, يعصر فينا كالذي تعصر, yaʿṣiru fīnā ka-lladhī taʿṣir, العصارة الغلة, al-ʿuṣārah al-ghallah
8b: Clouds that bring rain are described as being squeezed or as dripping, and a group that receives rain is said to have been ʿuṣira.  · terms: المعصرات, al-muʿṣirāt, السحابة المعصر, al-saḥābah al-muʿṣir, عصر القوم, ʿuṣira al-qawm
8c: The Qurʾān's al-muʿṣirāt in 78:14 is read as rain-bearing, squeezed clouds, with the water they pour beginning a sequence that brings forth grain, plants, and layered gardens in 78:15–16.  · terms: مِنَ ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ, min al-muʿṣirāt, مَآءًۭ ثَجَّاجًۭا, māʾan thajjājā, حَبًّۭا وَنَبَاتًۭا, ḥabban wa-nabātan, جَنَّٰتٍ أَلْفَافًا, jannātin alfāfā  · verses: 78:14, 78:15, 78:16
8d: The paragraph reads 78:14–16 as a chain of blessing: squeezed cloud, then grain and vegetation, then gardens.  · terms: ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ, al-muʿṣirāt, حَبًّۭا وَنَبَاتًۭا, ḥabban wa-nabātan, جَنَّٰتٍ أَلْفَافًا, jannātin alfāfā  · verses: 78:14, 78:15, 78:16

## ¶9
9a: A tongue dried by thirst is called maʿṣūr, as if it had been squeezed until no water remained.  · terms: المعصور اللسان اليابس عطشا, al-maʿṣūr al-lisān al-yābis ʿaṭshan
9b: Iʿtiṣār can describe someone clearing a stuck morsel by drinking water in small sips, so squeezing here names a movement of rescue.  · terms: الاعتصار, al-iʿtiṣār, يعتصر بالماء, yaʿtaṣiru bi-l-māʾ
9c: The poet reverses that rescue image: if his throat were blocked by something other than water, he could clear it with water, implying that water itself blocks him and no further sip can save him.  · terms: لو بغير الماء حلقي شرق, law bi-ghayr al-māʾ ḥalqī shariq, بالماء اعتصاري, bi-l-māʾ iʿtiṣārī

## ¶10
10a: Set beside the verse's time sense, these root images make time look like pressure that extracts something from a person: it may leave a flowing essence that becomes generosity, or only a dry mass and a thirst-dried tongue.  · terms: العصر, al-ʿaṣr, المعصور, al-maʿṣūr, العصارة, al-ʿuṣārah  · verses: 103:1
10b: The loss in 103:2 is identified with the leftover side of what has been squeezed, and the surah's final word ṣabr is linked to the bitter essence squeezed from a tree, carrying the pressure image through to 103:3.  · terms: خُسْرٍ, khusr, صبر, ṣabr  · verses: 103:2

## ¶11
11a: Joseph's imprisonment in 12:35 is bounded by an indefinite period, which the paragraph connects to al-ʿaṣr as a time-span and as detention, while stressing that the Qurʾān uses a different word for prison.  · terms: حَتَّىٰ حِينٍ, ḥattā ḥīn, العصر, al-ʿaṣr, الحبس, al-ḥabs  · verses: 12:35
11b: In 12:36 the young prisoner's dream uses ʿaṣara for pressing grapes but names the pressed product, wine, rather than the grapes.  · terms: أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا, aʿṣiru khamran  · verses: 12:36
11c: Joseph interprets the dream as the young man serving wine to his master, and the following verse identifies him as the one expected to be released from prison.  · terms: فَيَسْقِى رَبَّهُۥ خَمْرًۭا, fa-yasqī rabbahu khamran, نَاجٍۢ, nājin  · verses: 12:41, 12:42
11d: The dictionary sense of al-ʿaṣr as a place of rescue is echoed by the scene: the one who squeezes in his dream is the prisoner who gets out.  · terms: العصر المنجاة, al-ʿaṣr al-manjāh, أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا, aʿṣiru khamran, نَاجٍۢ, nājin  · verses: 12:36, 12:42

## ¶12
12a: The released prisoner forgets Joseph, leaving Joseph in prison for several years; the prisoner remembers him only after a long interval.  · terms: فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ بِضْعَ سِنِينَ, fa-labitha fī al-sijni biḍʿa sinīn, بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ, baʿda ummah  · verses: 12:42, 12:45
12b: Joseph turns the king's dream of green and dry ears into a schedule: first seven years of planting, with most of the harvest left in its ear for storage.  · terms: سُنۢبُلَٰتٍ خُضْرٍۢ وَأُخَرَ يَابِسَٰتٍۢ, sunbulātin khuḍrin wa-ukhar yābisāt, فَذَرُوهُ فِى سُنۢبُلِهِۦٓ, fa-dharūhu fī sunbulih  · verses: 12:43, 12:47
12c: The paragraph connects the Arab crop term ʿaṣara, used when the ear's husks appear, with ʿaṣr as protection: the grain is held safe inside its husk, although the Qurʾān here says sunbul, not ʿaṣr.  · terms: قد عصر الزرع, qad ʿaṣara al-zarʿ, العصر, al-ʿaṣr, الحرز, al-ḥirz, سُنۢبُلِهِۦٓ, sunbulih  · verses: 12:47
12d: The seven hard years consume what was stored for them, except for a small amount the people preserve.  · terms: سَبْعٌۭ شِدَادٌۭ, sabʿun shidād, مَا قَدَّمْتُمْ لَهُنَّ, mā qaddamtum lahunna, تُحْصِنُونَ, tuḥṣinūn  · verses: 12:48
12e: The final year brings relief, and yaʿṣirūn in 12:49 is interpreted as people extracting a yield from their lands.  · terms: يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ, yughāthu al-nās, يَعْصِرُونَ, yaʿṣirūn  · verses: 12:49

## ¶13
13a: The Joseph story arranges several seemingly distant root senses on one calendar: imprisonment as a span, pressing in a dream, prolonged years of forgetting, grain sheltered in its husk, and pressing again in the year of abundance.  · terms: العصر, al-ʿaṣr, أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا, aʿṣiru khamran, عصر الزرع, ʿaṣara al-zarʿ, يَعْصِرُونَ, yaʿṣirūn  · verses: 12:35, 12:36, 12:42, 12:47, 12:49
13b: In this account pressing marks the end of scarcity: the wine-producing grapes are the share of those who patiently waited through and stored for the hard years.  · terms: أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا, aʿṣiru khamran, يَعْصِرُونَ, yaʿṣirūn, صبر, ṣabr  · verses: 12:36, 12:47, 12:48, 12:49
13c: The sworn time in 103:1 is presented as more than a period that consumes people: it can shelter what is stored within it and eventually let that store flow out.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶14
14a: Al-ʿaṣr can mean detention or restraint; asking mā ʿaṣraka asks what held or prevented someone, and ʿāṣir describes a person who keeps a tight grip and does not give.  · terms: العصر الحبس, al-ʿaṣr al-ḥabs, ما عصرك أي ما منعك, mā ʿaṣaraka, mā manaʿaka, فلان عاصر إذا كان ممسكا, fulān ʿāṣir idhā kāna mumsikan
14b: The root is used for a father withholding a child's property and for taking back a gift after giving it.  · terms: يعتصر الوالد على ولده في ماله, yaʿtaṣiru al-wālidu ʿalā waladih fī mālih, فاعتصرتها, fa-iʿtaṣartuhā
14c: The same root can describe both generosity and stinginess: squeezing gives when what is held flows out, and hoards when the hand keeps hold of it.  · terms: يعصر فينا, yaʿṣiru fīnā, عاصر, ʿāṣir, العصر, al-ʿaṣr

## ¶15
15a: A young woman at her first menstruation is called al-muʿṣir because she is kept at home, with a protected place made for her.  · terms: المعصر ساعة تطمث, al-muʿṣir sāʿata taṭmith, العصر, al-ʿaṣr
15b: The grain kept inside its husk shares this sense of shelter; holding can protect something at a threshold rather than merely deprive it.  · terms: عصر الزرع, ʿaṣara al-zarʿ, الحرز, al-ḥirz

## ¶16
16a: In 68:17–18 the Qurʾān likens the deniers' test to garden owners who swear to gather their crop in the morning and make no exception.  · terms: أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ, aṣḥāb al-jannah, لَيَصْرِمُنَّهَا مُصْبِحِينَ, la-yaṣrimunnahā muṣbiḥīn, وَلَا يَسْتَثْنُونَ, wa-lā yastathnūn  · verses: 68:17, 68:18
16b: The owners' purpose is to prevent any poor person from entering the garden to share its produce.  · terms: أَن لَّا يَدْخُلَنَّهَا ٱلْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكُم مِّسْكِينٌۭ, an lā yadkhulannahā al-yawma ʿalaykum miskīn  · verses: 68:24
16c: While the owners sleep, a visitation from God ruins the garden; on seeing it they first think they have lost their way, then recognize that they have been deprived.  · terms: طَآئِفٌۭ مِّن رَّبِّكَ, ṭāʾifun min rabbik, كَٱلصَّرِيمِ, ka-l-ṣarīm, إِنَّا لَضَآلُّونَ, innā la-ḍāllūn, مَحْرُومُونَ, maḥrūmūn  · verses: 68:19, 68:20, 68:26, 68:27
16d: The garden shows that a product kept back from others does not remain in its owner's hands; the owners' oath without exception is contrasted with this surah's oath followed by an exception in its third verse after the general judgment in the second.  · terms: لَا يَسْتَثْنُونَ, lā yastathnūn, وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr, خُسْرٍ, khusr, إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا, illā alladhīna āmanū  · verses: 68:17, 103:1, 103:2

## ¶17
17a: The root can mean attaching oneself firmly to something, and al-ʿaṣr can name the place of refuge or shelter.  · terms: تعلق بشيء وامتساك به, taʿalluq bi-shayʾ wa-imtisāk bih, العصر الملجأ, al-ʿaṣr al-maljaʾ
17b: The verb forms iʿtaṣara and taʿaṣṣara describe taking refuge in a person, while iʿtaṣara bi-l-makān means seeking shelter in a place; ʿuṣrat al-manjūd names refuge for a distressed, trapped person.  · terms: اعتصرت بفلان وتعصرت, iʿtaṣartu bi-fulān wa-taʿaṣṣartu, اعتصر بالمكان, iʿtaṣara bi-l-makān, عصرة المنجود, ʿuṣrat al-manjūd
17c: The expression mā baynahumā ʿaṣr wa-lā yaṣr means that two people have neither affection nor kinship between them.  · terms: ما بينهما عصر ولا يصر, mā baynahumā ʿaṣr wa-lā yaṣr, مودة ولا قرابة, mawaddah wa-lā qarābah
17d: The paragraph gives ʿunṣur, meaning a person's stock or lineage, an etymology from refuge al-ʿaṣr with an added nūn.  · terms: العنصر والعنصر الأصل والحسب, al-ʿunṣur, al-ʿunṣur al-aṣl wa-l-ḥasab, العصر وهو الملجأ, al-ʿaṣr wa-huwa al-maljaʾ
17e: The armor term al-maʿāṣir is derived from al-ʿaṣr because armor encloses and protects its wearer.  · terms: المعاصر الدروع, al-maʿāṣir al-durūʿ, العصر, al-ʿaṣr

## ¶18
18a: Squeezing and refuge are reconciled as forms of grasping: squeezing closes around something from outside, while a person seeking refuge firmly holds onto a shelter, as armor encloses and protects the body.  · terms: العصر, al-ʿaṣr, الملجأ, al-maljaʾ, المعاصر, al-maʿāṣir
18b: The expression for a distressed person's refuge reinforces this link: the one pressed or overwhelmed runs to a shelter.  · terms: عصرة المنجود, ʿuṣrat al-manjūd, الملجأ, al-maljaʾ
18c: Heard beside the oath in 103:1, the time named by al-ʿaṣr appears both as a span that constrains people and as a place of refuge.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr, الملجأ, al-maljaʾ  · verses: 103:1

## ¶19
19a: The cave story is used to show how refuge can close or suspend the experience of time: the youths withdraw from their community's worship and are told to seek the cave and God's mercy.  · terms: فَأْوُۥٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ, faʾwū ilā al-kahf, إِذْ أَوَى ٱلْفِتْيَةُ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ, idh awā al-fityatu ilā al-kahf  · verses: 18:16, 18:10
19b: Inside the cave the youths are made to sleep for counted years, but on waking they think they stayed only a day or part of one.  · terms: فَضَرَبْنَا عَلَىٰٓ ءَاذَانِهِمْ, fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim, لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍ, labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawm  · verses: 18:11, 18:19
19c: The story says they actually remained in the cave three hundred years and nine more; the paragraph hears refuge, a long era, and a duration mistaken for a moment together, although the word al-ʿaṣr itself is absent.  · terms: ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍۢ سِنِينَ وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا, thalātha miʾati sinīn wa-zdādū tisʿā, العصر, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 18:25
19d: The exception of believers in 103:3 is linked to the cave's refuge image, so escape from loss is heard as seeking shelter in a place.  · terms: إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا, illā alladhīna āmanū, العصر, al-ʿaṣr

## ¶20
20a: Iʿṣār names a wind that lifts dust from the ground and raises it toward the sky like a column; it can also name the dust as it rises in a spinning form.  · terms: الإعصار ريح تهب تثير الغبار, al-iʿṣār rīḥun tahubbu tuthīru al-ghubār, الإعصار الغبار الذي يسطع مستديرا, al-iʿṣār al-ghubār alladhī yasṭaʿu mustadīran
20b: This branch of the root depicts a moving wind gathering and turning what is on the ground, then pulling it upward into a column.  · terms: الإعصار, al-iʿṣār
20c: The same branch describes fragrance rising in the wake of a perfumed woman; ʿuṣrah is the scent's spreading and swelling.  · terms: مرت امرأة متطيبة لذيلها عصر, marrat imraʾatun mutaṭayyibatun li-dhaylihā ʿaṣr, العصرة, al-ʿuṣrah
20d: The paragraph groups dust and fragrance as two things that can billow upward behind something moving.  · terms: الإعصار, al-iʿṣār, العصرة, al-ʿuṣrah

## ¶21
21a: The paragraph reads 2:264–265 as contrasting showy spending, which is like soil washed from bare rock, with spending for God's approval, which is like a garden yielding abundantly under heavy or light rain.  · terms: صَفْوَانٍ عَلَيْهِ تُرَابٌ, ṣafwān ʿalayhi turāb, وَابِلٌ, wābil, جَنَّةٌ, jannah  · verses: 2:264, 2:265
21b: The question in 2:266 is read as a picture of a garden of date palms and grapes burned by a fiery whirlwind just when its owner is old and has weak children dependent on it.  · terms: جَنَّةٌۭ مِّن نَّخِيلٍۢ وَأَعْنَابٍۢ, jannatun min nakhīlin wa-aʿnāb, ٱلْكِبَرُ, al-kibar, ذُرِّيَّةٌۭ ضُعَفَآءُ, dhurriyyatun ḍuʿafāʾ, إِعْصَارٌۭ فِيهِ نَارٌۭ, iʿṣārun fīhi nār  · verses: 2:266
21c: The paragraph treats the verse's closing words as directing the reader to reflect on the preceding examples and garden image.  · terms: يُبَيِّنُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمُ ٱلْءَايَٰتِ, yubayyinu Allāhu lakum al-āyāt, تَتَفَكَّرُونَ, tatafakkarūn  · verses: 2:266

## ¶22
22a: The garden image brings several root senses together: grapes are fruit to be pressed, streams and produce are income, the owner is at life's afternoon in old age, and the only root word in the verse is iʿṣār, the whirlwind.  · terms: إِعْصَارٌۭ, iʿṣār, العصر, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 2:266
22b: The harvest that has awaited pressing through a lifetime is lost at the moment its owner has grown too old to plant again.  · terms: إِعْصَارٌۭ, iʿṣār, العصر, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 2:266
22c: The burned garden is presented as the most concrete image of 103:2's loss: heard beside al-ʿaṣr, the whirlwind shows time suddenly scattering what a person has accumulated.  · terms: إِعْصَارٌۭ, iʿṣār, وَٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr, خُسْرٍ, khusr  · verses: 2:266, 103:1, 103:2
22d: The fragrance image is the other possible aftermath of movement: after someone passes, a beautiful trace can remain instead of dust.  · terms: العصرة, al-ʿuṣrah, الإعصار, al-iʿṣār
