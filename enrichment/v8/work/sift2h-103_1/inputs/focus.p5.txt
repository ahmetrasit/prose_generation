[DURR-FULL:v8p621/r5] DURR-FULL d.911 · علي بن أبي طالب · reports · Ali’s reported reading expands al-Asr as “the vicissitudes of time” and says humanity remains in loss until the end of time. «عن علي بن أبي طالب أنه كان يقرأ والعصر ونوائب الدهر إن الإنسان لفي خسر وانه لفيه إلى آخر الدهر» [filed under 103:1-2]
[DURR-FULL:v8p621/r6] DURR-FULL d.911 · سعيد بن جبير · reports · Saʿid ibn Jubayr recites Ibn Masud’s expanded reading, adding that loss lasts until the end of time, except for believers and those doing good. «عن إسماعيل بن عبد الملك قال: سمعت سعيد بن جبير يقرأ قراءة ابن مسعود: والعصر إن الإنسان لفي خسر» [filed under 103:1-3]
[DURR-FULL:v8p621/r7] DURR-FULL d.911 · إبراهيم · reports · Ibrahim reports a reading attributed to Ibn Masud that adds “he is in loss until the end of time” before the four exceptions. «عن إبراهيم قال: قرأنا: والعصر إن الإنسان لفي خسر وانه لفيه إلى آخر الدهر» [filed under 103:1-3]
[DURR-FULL:v8p621/r8] DURR-FULL d.911 · عبد الله بن عتبة بن مسعود · reports · Asked about Ibn Masud’s reading, Abdullah ibn Utba recites the added phrase; when called an unbeliever for it, he says he believes in it. «إلى عبد الله بن عتبة بن مسعود فقال: كيف كان ابن مسعود يقرأ {والعصر} فقال: والعصر إن الإنسان لفي خسر وهو فيه إلى آخر الدهر» [filed under 103:1-2]
[DURR-FULL:v8p621/r9] DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says al-asr is an hour of the daytime. «عن ابن عباس {والعصر} قال: ساعة من ساعات النهار»
[DURR-FULL:v8p622/r1] DURR-FULL d.911 · محمد بن كعب القرظي · reports · Muhammad ibn Kaʿb says God swears by the afternoon. «عن محمد بن كعب القرظي {والعصر} قال: قسم أقسم به ربنا وتبارك وتعالى»

## ITQAN: al-Suyūṭī, d. 911 AH
[ITQAN:v1p176/r1] ITQAN d.911 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud mushaf order (via Jarir b. Abd al-Hamid): tiwal, mi'in, mathani, mufassal; al-Asr is in the mufassal; the mushaf lacks al-Fatiha and both Mu awwidhatayn. «حدثنا جرير بن عبد الحميد قال تأليف مصحف عبد الله بن مسعود»
[ITQAN:v1p180/r1] ITQAN d.911 · غيره · reports · Another scholar says one-word verses exist beyond the single case of madhamatan, e.g. al-Najm, al-Duha and al-Asr, and opening letters too. «وقال غيره بل فيه غيرها مثل»
[ITQAN:v1p180/r3] ITQAN d.911 · بعضهم · prefers · Some hold the correct view that verse division is known only by revelation from the Lawgiver, as with knowing the surah. «قال بعضهم الصحيح أن الآية إنما تعلم بتوقيف من الشارع»
[ITQAN:v1p183/r1] ITQAN d.911 · الموصلي · reports · Al-Mawsili divides surahs into three classes by counting: no disagreement, disagreement only in detail, and disagreement in both; al-Asr falls in the detail class. «قال الموصلي ثم سور القرآن على ثلاثة أقسام»
[ITQAN:v1p183/r2] ITQAN d.911 · المدني الأخير · reports · Last Medinan counter counts Watawasaw bil-haqq (103:3) as a verse and not wal-asr (103:1); the other counters do the reverse. «العصر ثلاث عد المدني الأخير» [filed under 103:1, 103:3]
[ITQAN:v1p37/r1] ITQAN d.911 · holds · Author grades the Bayhaqi narration listing Meccan surahs (al-Asr among them) as sound, all transmitters reliable scholars of Arabic. «هكذا أخرجه بطوله وإسناده جيد رجاله كلهم ثقات من علماء العربية المشهورين»
[ITQAN:v1p37/r2] ITQAN d.911 · عكرمة والحسن بن أبي الحسن · reports · Ikrima and al-Hasan (via Bayhaqi) list the Meccan surahs in order; al-Asr comes after al-Duha and Alam Nashrah, before al-Adiyat. «عكرمة والحسن بن أبي الحسن قالا أنزل الله من القرآن بمكة»
[ITQAN:v1p38/r2] ITQAN d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas (via Uthman b. Ata and his father, in Ibn al-Durays) gives the order of first revelations, with al-Asr after Alam Nashrah and before al-Adiyat. «عثمان بن عطاء الخراساني عن أبيه عن ابن عباس قال»
[ITQAN:v1p38/r3] ITQAN d.911 · البيهقي · reports · Bayhaqi notes that the same first-revelation hadith has a witness in Muqatil's tafsir and in a sound mursal narration cited earlier. «وللحديث شاهد في تفسير مقاتل وغيره مع المرسل الصحيح الذي تقدم»
[ITQAN:v1p77#2/r5] ITQAN d.911 · جابر بن زيد · reports · Abu Bakr b. al-Harith, via Jabir b. Zayd: a long Meccan order of revelation places al-Asr after al-Duha and Alam Nashrah, before al-Adiyat. «حدثنا أمية الأزدي عن جابر بن زيد قال أول ما أنزل الله من القرآن بمكة»
[ITQAN:v3p282/r1] ITQAN d.911 · ابن أبي الأصبع · reports · Ibn Abi al-Asbas book Al-Khawatir al-Sawanih on the secrets of surah openings is summarised here, with additions. «أفردها بالتأليف ابن أبي الأصبع في كتاب سماه»
[ITQAN:v3p282/r2] ITQAN d.911 · ابن أبي الأصبع · reports · Surahs open in ten kinds of speech: praise of God, letter-openers, calls, declarative sentences, oaths, and others. «افتتح سور القرآن بعشرة أنواع من الكلام»
[ITQAN:v3p282/r3] ITQAN d.911 · ابن أبي الأصبع · reports · Fifteen surahs open with oaths; al-Asr is one, its oath being by the later part of the day or by the whole time. «والعصر بالشطر الآخر أو بجملة الزمان»
[ITQAN:v3p57/r1] ITQAN d.911 · بعضهم · reports · One classification puts al-Asr in the group with both abrogating and abrogated verses; the no-abrogation group excludes it. «والذاريات والطور والواقعة والمجادلة والمزمل والمدثر وكورت والعصر»
[ITQAN:v3p57/r10] ITQAN d.911 · القاضي أبو بكر بن العربي · reports · Abrogation of ruling without recitation is very rare; many inflate the count, and Ibn al-Arabi clarified and perfected the matter. «فإن المحققين منهم كالقاضي أبي بكر بن العربي بين ذلك وأتقنه»
[ITQAN:v3p57/r2] ITQAN d.911 · rejects · Author doubts this classification and says it will become clear later; the no-abrogation group also names al-Asr as an exception. «كذا قال وفيه نظر يعرف مما سيأتي»
[ITQAN:v4p353/r1] ITQAN d.911 · unnamed · reports · Fifth kind of oath-and-consequent structure: an oath followed by the consequent, as in al-Asr followed by the statement that man is in loss. «والعصر إن الإنسان لفي خسر»
[ITQAN:v4p432/r1] ITQAN d.911 · ابن خالويه · reports · Ibn Khalawayh: no surah of three verses containing ten waws exists, except al-Asr to its end. «ولا سورة ثلاث آيات فيها عشر واوات إلا والعصر إلى آخرها»

## BURSEVI: İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127 AH
[BURSEVI:v10p390#2/r1] BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · The oath may refer to the afternoon itself, which has signs of divine power, or to the era of Prophethood, whose duration is likened to a day or half-day and which excels as the age of the best prophet, community, and revealed book. «ويقال اقسم بالعشى الذي هو ما بين الزوال والغروب كما سم بالضحى لما فيها جميعا من دلائل القدرة»
[BURSEVI:v10p390#2/r2] BURSEVI d.1127 · رسول الله · reports · A report says that if the Prophet's community remains upright, it has a day, and if not, half a day. «عليه السلام : إن استقامت أمتي فلها يوم وإن لم تستقم فلها نصف يوم»
[BURSEVI:v10p390#2/r3] BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Another reading takes ‘asr as time itself, filled with extraordinary events, and rejects blaming time for misfortune; God swears by it as a locus of divine signs and self-disclosure through attributes and acts. «ويقال اقسم بالدهر لانطوائه على أعاجيب الأمور القارة والمارة»
[BURSEVI:v10p390#2/r4] BURSEVI d.1127 · رسول الله · reports · The Prophet says not to revile time, for God is time. «لا تسبوا الدهر فإن الله هو الدهر»
[BURSEVI:v10p390#2/r5] BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The ‘asr gathers the signs sworn by elsewhere in the Qur’an, concluding the sequence of oaths about dawn, sun, moon, night, day, and duha. «فلما كان العصر جامعا لجميع الآيات التي أقسم الله بها في القرآن»
[BURSEVI:v10p390#2/r6] BURSEVI d.1127 · التأويلات النجمية · reports · The Najmiyya reading says God swears by time's complete continuity because it encompasses the Prophet's sainthood, prophethood, mission, and caliphate, citing reports about his pre-eternal prophetic status and the cosmos being created for him. «أقسم الله بكمال دوامه الزمان واستمراره لاشتماله على ولاية النبي عليه السلام ونبوته ورسالته وخلافته»
[BURSEVI:v10p390#3/r1] BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The loss is grave because the servant sins against the Lord and against immense benefaction; the indefinite form may also mark a distinctive kind of loss beyond ordinary human usage. «والذنب يعظم إما لعظم من في حقه الذنب أو لأنه في مقابلة النعمة العظيمة» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]

## ALUSI-FULL: al-Ālūsī, d. 1270 AH
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p227#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · مقاتل · reports · Muqatil says the oath is by the Asr prayer because of its virtue; the majority identify it as the middle prayer, supported by reports naming it and warning against missing it. «قال مقاتل أقسم سبحانه بصلاة العصر لفضلها لانها الصلاة الوسطى عند الجمهور»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p227#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · The author dismisses a story about a sinful woman being told her gravest sin was missing Asr prayer as unreliable material from an otherwise prominent transmitter. «ذكر ذلك الامام وهو لعمري امام في نقل مثل ذلك مما لايعول عليه عند أئمة الحديث»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p227#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Asr prayer is especially meritorious because people neglect it while absorbed in trade and work late in the day. «وخصت بالفضل لان التكليف في أدائها أشق لتهافت الناس في تجاراتهم ومكاسبهم آخر النهار»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p227#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says the oath is by the time of the Asr prayer, or by the creation of Adam on Friday; he also explains al-asr as evening, rich in signs of divine power like dawn. «وقيل أقسم عز وجل بوقت تلك الصلاة لفضيلة صلاته أو لخلق آدم أبي البشر عليه السلام فيه من يوم الجمعة»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p227#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjaj says al-asr means day or night, and cites Hamid ibn Thawr's verse about the two spans of time, day and night. «وقال الزجاج العصر اليوم والعصر الليلة وعليه قول حميد بن ثور»
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p227#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قيل · reports · Some interpret al-asr as morning and evening, the two cool periods; on this and the preceding view the oath is to one of two unspecified times. «وقيل العصر بكرة والعصر عشية وهما الابرادان»
