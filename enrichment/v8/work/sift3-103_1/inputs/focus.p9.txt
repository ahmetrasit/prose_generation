[BINTSHATI:v2p79/r2] BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · The author cites the prophetic report against blaming time, since God is al-dahr. «ولذلك قال - صلى الله عليه وسلم -: لا تسبوا الدهر فإن الله هو الدهر»
[BINTSHATI:v2p79/r3] BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · A report describes time as strangely both existent and like nothing, and compares a person's lifetime to a small part of it. «من أعجايب الدهر الدالة على كمال قدره خالقها: أن الدهر موجود يشبه المعدوم»
[BINTSHATI:v2p79/r4] BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author rejects loading asr with philosophical and mystical interpretations; the Quran explains signs of night and day in accessible terms, as in al-Qasas 28:71-72. «وترى أنهم حملوا لفظ العصر كل هذه التأويلات الفلسفية والإشارية مما»
[BINTSHATI:v2p80/r1] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi suggests the Quran chose asr instead of dahr because unbelievers were fond of invoking and exalting the latter word. «ولعله تعالى لم يذكر الدهر لعلمه أن الملحد مولع بذكره وتعظيمه»
[BINTSHATI:v2p80/r2] BINTSHATI d.1419 · النيسابوري · reports · Al-Nisaburi explains the oath as honoring the age and cites the report that people should not curse time because God is al-dahr. «على حين يذهب النيسابورى إلى أن وجه الإقسام بالعصر هنا»
[BINTSHATI:v2p80/r3] BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The author reads asr as the age that presses and tests humanity, extracting its essence; this familiar Arabic sense leads into the declaration that humanity is in loss. «تأتي كلمة العصر في سياقها من السورة، لافتة إلى ابتلاء الإنسان بالعصر» [filed under 103:1-2]
[BINTSHATI:v2p80/r4] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The oath's waw moves from a perceptible reality to a moral truth, a pattern the author also discusses in other surahs. «الواو هنا في موضعها الذي تطرد به الظاهرة الأسلوبية في اللفت إلى حسى مدرك»

## BINTSHATI-IJAZ: ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419 AH
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p244/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · Author says the waw of oath, in its place, marks the surah's opening as an assertion or testimony to what was denied. «فإذا جاءت للقسم فإن لها الصدارة، في مقام التوثيق لما يسبق إنكاره، أو الإقرار والشهادة»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p244/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · المفسرون · reports · Exegetes, mostly, read the oath-waw as glorifying the sworn-by object. «وقد اتجه بها المفسرون، أو جمهرتهم فيما أعلم، إلى تعظيم المقسَم به»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p244/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · rejects · Author rejects the exegetes' glory-by-wisdom reasoning and their neglect of the qualifiers attached to the sworn-by object. «وأما العظمة فلا يهون القول بها لمجرد لمح وجهٍ لظاهر الحكمة»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p244/r4] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · rejects · Exegetes often ignore the qualifier of the sworn-by object (e.g. the dawn-times of al-Duha). «ثم إنهم غالباً، لم يراعوا القيد في المقسم به»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p245/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · Author asks whether the oath-waw form, versus explicit aqsama, adds any rhetorical meaning. «فهل العدول عن: أقسم بالنجم، إلى "والنجم" لا يعطى أي ملحظ بياني؟»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p245/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · All surahs opening with the oath-waw, including al-ʿAsr, are Meccan; no Medinan surah begins this way. «وكلها سور مكية، ولم تأت سورة مدنية مبدوءة بهذه الواو.»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p251/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · In al-ʿAsr the waw places the human under the pressure of time, which squeezes and tests him. «الواو في موضعها الذي تطرد به الظاهرة الأسلوبية في اللفت إلى ابتلاء الإنسان بالزمن يعصره ويصهره»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p251/r3] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · Time's pressing extracts the human's essence, revealing whether his outcome is loss or salvation (exempting those who believe and do good and counsel truth and patience). «توطئة إيضاحية لبيان ما يستخلص العصرُ من عصارة هذا الإنسان» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p415/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · In the verse al-ʿaṣr means time (zaman, dahr); the word is a single-form Qurʾānic usage here. «العَصْر، بمعنى الزمن، في آية العصر»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p416/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · الراغب · rejects · Author rejects Raghib's derivation of time from the residue of what is pressed. «وأخذه "الراغب" من نفاية ما يُعصَر. وليس الوجه.»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p416/r5] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · The root ʿ-ṣ-r points to pressing to extract juice; the sense of time (dahr) is named for extracting man's essence through trial. «وتسمية الدهر عصراً، ملحوظ فيه أنه يستخلص عصارة الإنسان بالضغط والابتلاء والمعاناة»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p416/r6] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · General root meaning of ʿ-ṣ-r is pressing for juice, used literally of grapes and metaphorically of wine. «والعصر في كل صيغة واستعماله، يرجع إلى أصل دلالته على الضغط لاستخلاص العصارة»
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p567/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · The word ʿaṣr meaning time occurs in 103:1, alongside al-muʿṣirāt (78:14) and the verbs in Yusuf. «والمعصرات فى آية النبأ 14، والعصر بمعنى الدهر والزمن فى آية العصر» [filed under 103:1, 78:14]

## CORPUSCORANICUM: Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments)
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The three-rhyme sura is an opening single-member oath by late afternoon, followed by a rebuke; verse 3 exempts believers and differentiates good from bad people. «Die auf 3Kr reimende Sure besteht aus einem eingliedrigen Schwur „beim späten Nachmittag“ mit rügendem ʾinsān-Spruch als Schwuraussage» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · Neuwirth argues that oaths by prayer times preserve the recipient's liturgical experience and give the sura a hymn-like tone. «Neuwirth schreibt Schwüren bei Tages- und Nachtzeiten allgemein die Funktion zu»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r3] CORPUSCORANICUM · rejects · The commentator finds no hymn-like resonance in this sura and points to a critical discussion of Neuwirth's hypothesis elsewhere. «Eine solche hymnische Konnotation klingt jedoch im vorliegenden Text nirgends nach» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r4] CORPUSCORANICUM · Robinson · prefers · Robinson's secular reading makes ʿaṣr the time when merchants count a day's earnings, linking the oath's image of profit to the eschatological loss in verse 2. «Robinsons profane Deutung des ʿaṣr als des Zeitpunkts, zu dem Händler am Ende eines Markttags ihre Einnahmen berechnen» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r6] CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · A prayer-time meaning for afternoon nevertheless remains possible. «dass mit dem Nachmittag doch eine Gebetszeit gemeint sein könnte (so Neuwirth, Frühmekkanische Suren, 157)»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · reports · Arabic lexica offer competing senses for ʿaṣr: time or period, a time of day, morning, late afternoon or evening; the dual can mean day and night. «Die arabischen Lexika verzeichnen eine Reihe von z. T. gegensätzlichen Bedeutungen»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · prefers · Since other opening oaths name specific daytimes or night phases, ʿaṣr probably denotes a particular time rather than time in general. «stellt wohl auch ʿaṣr eine spezifische Zeitangabe dar und bezeichnet nicht allgemein „Zeitspanne“»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r3] CORPUSCORANICUM · Cuypers · reports · Cuypers reads ʿaṣr as a general span of time, though the commentator notes that he gives no further justification. «„Zeitspanne“ o. Ä. (so ohne weitere Begründung Cuypers 1999, 47)»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r4] CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · If ʿaṣr means late afternoon, this oath differs from all other early Meccan oaths naming night or stages of morning and forenoon. «Falls wirklich der Spätnachmittag intendiert ist, so hebt sich Q 103 von allen anderen frühmekkanischen Schwüren»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r5] CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Qur'anic oaths likely draw on a characteristic pre-Islamic seer expression, a relationship that has not yet been adequately studied. «wahrscheinlichen, aber bis dato noch nicht hinreichend untersuchten Anlehnung an ein charakteristisches Ausdrucksmittel altarabischer Seher»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r6] CORPUSCORANICUM · Spitaler · reports · Medina II joins the oath verse to verse 2 in its verse division. «Medina II zieht V. 1 zu V. 2 (Spitaler, Verszählung, 72)» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
[CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r7] CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · prefers · Neuwirth treats the oath as an independent verse, in keeping with other sura-opening oaths. «Wie die anderen Schwüre am Surenanfang ist aber auch wa-l-ʿaṣr als eigenständiger Vers zu sehen (Neuwirth, Studien, 35)»

## ISLAHI-TADABBUR: Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997)
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p433/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-ʿAṣr follows al-Takāthur’s warning against wasting life amassing wealth; people would have used life wisely if they knew they must answer for this great blessing. «waste all their lives in planning» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p433/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This sūrah explains life’s real value and the factors that make it a path to salvation or a cause of eternal punishment. «the real value and importance of life is divulged» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p433/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The oath points to a lifespan as a person’s real investment: using it well earns God’s favor, while wasting it brings Hell; time can serve either the person or Satan. «this time period is like a sword which can be used in both ways»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p433/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Only the present is available to shape; the past cannot return and the future is hidden, with its own demands. «which is hidden to him and no one knows its extent»
