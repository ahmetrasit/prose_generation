## TABATABAI: Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402 AH
[TABATABAI:103:1-3/r1] TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The surah condenses Quranic teachings and gathers its aims in the briefest statement; although it could be Meccan or Medinan, it seems more likely Meccan. «تخلص السورة جميع المعارف القرآنية وتجمع شتات مقاصد القرآن في أوجز بيان» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
[TABATABAI:103:1-3/r2] TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · Given the next verses' universal loss except for those who follow truth and persevere, al-asr most fittingly means the Prophet's era, when Islam arose and truth appeared over falsehood. «والأنسب لما تتضمنه الآيتان التاليتان من شمول الخسران للعالم الإنساني إلا لمن اتبع الحق»
[TABATABAI:103:1-3/r3] TABATABAI d.1402 · unnamed view · reports · One interpretation takes al-asr as late afternoon, whose fading day, approaching night, and waning sun point to divine governance. «وقيل المراد به وقت العصر وهو الطرف الأخير من النهار لما فيه من الدلالة»
[TABATABAI:103:1-3/r4] TABATABAI d.1402 · unnamed view · reports · Another interpretation takes al-asr as the afternoon prayer, the middle prayer and the best of the daily obligatory prayers. «وقيل المراد به صلاة العصر وهي الصلاة الوسطى التي هي أفضل الفرائض اليومية»
[TABATABAI:103:1-3/r5] TABATABAI d.1402 · unnamed view · reports · Some take al-asr as day and night, which are called the two asrs. «وقيل الليل والنهار ويطلق عليهما العصران»
[TABATABAI:103:1-3/r6] TABATABAI d.1402 · unnamed view · reports · Another reported meaning is time or the age, whose remarkable events indicate divine power. «وقيل الدهر لما فيه من عجائب الحوادث الدالة على القدرة الربوبية»
[TABATABAI:103:1-3/r7] TABATABAI d.1402 · unnamed reports · reports · Some narrations identify al-asr with the era of the Mahdi, when truth will fully prevail over falsehood. «وقد ورد في بعض الروايات أنه عصر ظهور المهدي عليه السلام لما فيه من تمام ظهور الحق»

## ASAD-NOTES: Muhammad Asad, d. 1412 AH
[ASAD-NOTES:103:1#n1/r1] ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asr denotes measurable time made up of a succession of periods. «The term "asr denotes "time" that is measurable, consisting of a succession of periods»
[ASAD-NOTES:103:1/r1] ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Unlike dahr, which denotes unlimited time without beginning or end, asr signifies passing time that cannot be recovered. «dahr, which signifies "unlimited time", without beginning or end: i.e., "time absolute"). Hence, asr bears the connotation of the passing or the flight of time - time which can never be recaptured»
[ASAD-NOTES:103:intro/r1] ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The surah was revealed shortly after surah 94, Ash-Sharh. «REVEALED shortly after surah 94» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]

## BINTSHATI: ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419 AH
[BINTSHATI:v2p75/r1] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Surah al-Asr is an early Meccan surah, conventionally ranked thirteenth in revelation, after al-Sharh and before al-Adiyat. «السورة مكية مبكرة، والمشهور في ترتيبها أنها الثالثة عشرة في النزول» [filed under 103:1-3]
[BINTSHATI:v2p75/r2] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The root sense of asr is pressing to extract juice, as in squeezing grapes; related words name the press, place of pressing, and stones used for it. «والمعنى الأصلي للعصر لغة: الضغط لاستخلاص العصارة»
[BINTSHATI:v2p75/r3] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Arabic also calls rain-bearing clouds mu'sirat, says a people asaru when they bring rain, and uses i'sar for a strong wind driving clouds. «وسميت السحب الممطرة معصرات لما تعتصر من المطر، وأعصر القوم أمطروا»
[BINTSHATI:v2p75/r4] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Arabic also uses asr metaphorically for confinement and extraction by pressure, and derives words for age and lineage from the image of pressing out an essence. «ومن هذه الدلالة اللغوية الأصلية على الضغط والإعتصار»
[BINTSHATI:v2p75/r5] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The age is called asr because experience and hardship press out a person's essence; late afternoon is also asr as the day is brought to its close, and the prayer takes its name from that time. «سمي الدهر عصراً، بملحظ من استخلاصه عصارة الإنسان بالضغط والتجربة»
[BINTSHATI:v2p75/r6] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Quranic uses of the root include squeezing grapes in Yusuf 12:36 and rain clouds in al-Naba 78:14, alongside the verb in Yusuf 12:49. «بمعناه اللغوي الأول في اعتصار الخمر بآية يوسف 36»
[BINTSHATI:v2p76/r1] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The root also appears for a whirlwind in al-Baqara 2:266 and for asr in the surah itself. «والإعصار في آية البقرة 266. والعصر، في سورة العصر»
[BINTSHATI:v2p76/r2] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الطبري · prefers · Among the meanings of the oath, al-Tabari reports both the age and a particular time of day, then prefers the age. «في تفسيره، ثم اختار الدهر»
[BINTSHATI:v2p76/r3] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi lists the afternoon prayer, understood with an omitted noun, and the age of Prophethood among the proposed meanings, without an explicit preference. «وقيل أنه صلاة العصر، على حذف المضاف، أو هو عصر النبوة»
[BINTSHATI:v2p76/r4] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الزمخشري · prefers · Al-Zamakhshari appears to prefer the afternoon prayer because of its merit and difficulty during the busy end of the workday; he cites the report comparing a missed prayer to losing one's family and property. «ولأن التكليف في أدائها أشق لتهافت الناس في تجارتهم ومكاسبهم آخر النهار»
[BINTSHATI:v2p76/r5] BINTSHATI d.1419 · محمد عبده · reports · Muhammad Abdu says Arabs gathered at afternoon to talk, so the oath corrects the belief that time itself is blameworthy when harmful conduct may occur then. «وكان من عادة العرب أن يجتمعوا وقت العصر، ويتحادثوا ويتذكروا»
[BINTSHATI:v2p76/r6] BINTSHATI d.1419 · ابن قيم الجوزية · prefers · Ibn Qayyim prefers reading asr as the age, as do most exegetes. «الراجح عند "ابن قيم الجوزية" أنه الدهر»
[BINTSHATI:v2p77/r1] BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The oath's waw retains its basic linguistic force of magnifying what is sworn by; al-Tabari did not construct elaborate arguments for the greatness of asr. «والسورة تبدأ بواو القسم وهو عندهم على أصل استعماله اللغوي»
[BINTSHATI:v2p77/r2] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi assembles six reasons for the greatness of the age, three for a particular time of day, six for the afternoon prayer, and a further case for the Prophetic age. «جمع الرازي ستة وجوه في عظمة العصر بمعنى الدهر»
[BINTSHATI:v2p77/r3] BINTSHATI d.1419 · محمد عبده · rejects · The author rejects Muhammad Abdu's claim that afternoon was the customary time for gatherings: evening may fit better, and objectionable talk is possible at any time. «ولا نعلم أن هذا الوقت في المألوف والعادة وقت اجتماع الناس وتذاكرهم»
[BINTSHATI:v2p77/r4] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi's reasons for magnifying the age include its extraordinary changes, the value of the final moment of life, and time's rank over space. «ولا تكاد أقوالهم تخرج عما استوفاه الفخر الرازي من وجوه عظمة العصر»
[BINTSHATI:v2p77/r5] BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The author follows al-Tabari, Ibn Qayyim, and most exegetes in reading asr as the age, and confines discussion to the arguments offered for that meaning. «ومن حيث آثرنا مع الطبري وابن القيم، وأكثر المفسرين، أن يكون العصر بمعنى الدهر»
[BINTSHATI:v2p78/r1] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi calls time a wonder encompassing fortune and hardship, health and illness, and wealth and poverty, while resisting simple judgments that it either exists or does not. «فعلمت حينئذ أن أشرف الأشياء حياتك في هذه اللمحة»
[BINTSHATI:v2p78/r2] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi says even a final instant can secure eternal paradise after a wasted lifetime, making time one of the greatest blessings. «إن بقية عمر المرء لا قيمة لها، فلو ضيعت ألف سنة»
[BINTSHATI:v2p78/r3] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi regards time as nobler than space and says the oath answers people who blame the age for calamities: the fault lies in the human being, not time. «إن الزمان أعلى وأشرف من المكان، فكان القسم بالعصر قسماً بأشرف النصفين»
[BINTSHATI:v2p78/r4] BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi says passing time subtracts from one's lifespan; without a corresponding gain, that decrease is itself loss, even when people welcome time passing. «ذكر العصر الذي بمضيه ينتقص عمرك، فإذا لم يكن في مقابلته كسب» [filed under 103:1-2]
[BINTSHATI:v2p78/r5] BINTSHATI d.1419 · ابن القيم · reports · Ibn Qayyim sees the ordered alternation of night and day, their changing light and temperature, and time divisions as signs of God's power and wisdom; the oath links human actions in time to their judgment. «فأقسم بالعصر الذي هو زمان أفعال الإنسان ومحلها، على عاقبة تلك الأفعال وجزائها»
[BINTSHATI:v2p78/r6] BINTSHATI d.1419 · النيسابوري · reports · Al-Nisaburi compares evening to the world's destruction and morning to resurrection, urging people to use their remaining lifespan for a trade without loss. «أن آخر النهار يشبه تخريب العالم وإماتة الأحياء كما أن أول النهار يشبه بعث الأموات»
[BINTSHATI:v2p79/r1] BINTSHATI d.1419 · النيسابوري · reports · Al-Nisaburi says the world's remaining life is like the time between afternoon and sunset, so people should hasten a trade without loss. «الدنيا ما بقى إلا بقدر ما بين العصر إلى المغرب، فعلى الإنسان أن يشتغل بتجارة لا خسران فيها»
