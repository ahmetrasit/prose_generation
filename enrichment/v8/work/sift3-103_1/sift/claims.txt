# Claim map of the page: id, claim, terms, verses

## ¶1
1a: The wāw of wa-l-ʿaṣr is the particle of oath (wāw al-qasam), not a conjunction joining two things, which is why al-ʿaṣr is in the genitive.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, wa-l-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1
1b: The thing sworn to (the answer of the oath) is not stated in 103:1 but in 103:2, inna l-insāna la-fī khusr; the first verse is a threshold whose ruling is withheld until the next verse.  · terms: إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ, inna l-insāna la-fī khusr  · verses: 103:1, 103:2
1c: What is sworn by is placed right beside the ruling it introduces, so that understanding what al-ʿaṣr is shows what kind of loss (khusr) is meant: the oath-object and the statement of loss are linked in meaning.  · terms: ٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr, خُسْرٍ, khusr  · verses: 103:1, 103:2

## ¶2
2a: Wa-l-fajr (89:1), wa-l-ḍuḥā (93:1), wa-l-layli idhā yaghshā (92:1) and wa-l-ṣubḥi idhā asfar (74:34) are cited as the Qurʾān's other oaths by times of day, the group to which wa-l-ʿaṣr belongs.  · terms: وَٱلْفَجْرِ, wa-l-fajr, وَٱلضُّحَىٰ, wa-l-ḍuḥā, وَٱلَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ, wa-l-layli idhā yaghshā, وَٱلصُّبْحِ إِذَآ أَسْفَرَ, wa-l-ṣubḥi idhā asfar  · verses: 89:1, 93:1, 92:1, 74:34, 103:1
2b: Unlike most of these time-oaths, where the time comes with a motion or qualifier (idhā yaghshā, idhā asfar), al-ʿaṣr stands alone with no qualification, and this bareness leaves the word's range of meaning open.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, wa-l-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1, 92:1, 74:34
2c: Lexically al-ʿaṣr names both time at large and a part of the day: al-ʿaṣr = al-dahr (a long time, an age) and al-ʿaṣr = al-ʿashiyy (late afternoon), and the afternoon prayer, ṣalāt al-ʿaṣr, takes its name from that hour.  · terms: العصر الدهر, al-ʿaṣr al-dahr, العصر العشي, al-ʿaṣr al-ʿashiyy, صلاة العصر, ṣalāt al-ʿaṣr
2d: The definite article points to a known time, but 103:1 does not say whether the afternoon of the day, the age in which man lives, or time itself is meant; the paragraph does not choose one referent and leaves all three to be heard together.  · terms: ٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶3
3a: Turkish has split the word: 'asır' now means century ('asri' contemporary) while the end of the day is 'ikindi', so the Turkish word loses the afternoon sense and the root's senses of pressing, refuge and whirlwind.  · terms: al-ʿaṣr
3b: The Qurʾānic word holds the hour and the age together, so that the end of a day and the end of a lifetime bear the same name.  · terms: ٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶4
4a: The Arabs used the dual al-ʿaṣrān for the two ends of the day: night and day (al-layl wa-l-nahār), or early morning and late afternoon (al-ghadāh wa-l-ʿashiyy).  · terms: العصران, al-ʿaṣrān, الغداة والعشي, al-ghadāh wa-l-ʿashiyy
4b: The phrase bi-l-ghadāti wa-l-ʿashiyy in 6:52 (the believers calling on their Lord morning and evening) is cited as the Qurʾān's own naming of the pair of day-ends that al-ʿaṣrān denotes.  · terms: بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ, bi-l-ghadāti wa-l-ʿashiyy  · verses: 6:52
4c: The phrase ṭarafayi l-nahār in 11:114, giving the times of prayer, is cited as another Qurʾānic naming of the day's two ends, the pair called al-ʿaṣrān.  · terms: طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ, ṭarafayi l-nahār  · verses: 11:114
4d: That ʿaṣr names the two ends of the day shows the word grasps time at its edge, where a period is entered and left; the afternoon sense in 103:1 is one such edge, the closing end of the day.  · terms: العصران, al-ʿaṣrān, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶5
5a: The root carries lateness: jāʾanī fulānun ʿaṣran means someone came late, slowly.  · terms: عصرا, ʿaṣran
5b: ʿAṣr also names a brief stretch, even a moment: nāma wa-mā nāma li-ʿaṣr (he lay down but did not sleep a moment) and al-ʿaṣār = al-ḥīn (a while); so ʿaṣr is both a long age and an instant.  · terms: نام وما نام لعصر, li-ʿaṣr, العصار الحين, al-ʿaṣār
5c: ʿAṣr names stages of a human life: of a girl, balaghat ʿaṣra shabābihā wa-idrākihā (she reached the age of youth and maturity), and a person's lifetime and old age are also called by this name.  · terms: بلغت عصر شبابها, ʿaṣr shabābihā
5d: The late-afternoon sense is read as time running short: the hour when least of the day remains and time narrows for what is undone, so that al-ʿaṣr is not only an hour but the lateness felt within it.  · terms: al-ʿaṣr, al-ʿashiyy  · verses: 103:1

## ¶6
6a: Lam yalbathū illā ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā (79:46), answering the question about the Hour (79:42), is cited as the Qurʾān's instance of a whole lifetime shrinking, seen from the end, into one part of a day; the paragraph uses ʿashiyya, the afternoon by which ʿaṣr is glossed, to show how the long age and the afternoon held in al-ʿaṣr turn into each other from the end.  · terms: عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَىٰهَا, ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā  · verses: 79:42, 79:46, 103:1
6b: The plea of the people of Hell in 35:37, rabbanā akhrijnā naʿmal ṣāliḥan, is cited as the Qurʾān's open instance of the work left too late, the lateness the paragraph hears in ʿaṣr.  · terms: نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا, naʿmal ṣāliḥan  · verses: 35:37
6c: The reply in 35:37, a-wa-lam nuʿammirkum mā yatadhakkaru fīhi man tadhakkar, is read as naming life as an allotted span given for reflection; the loss of 103:2 and the faith and righteous deeds of 103:3 are placed within this given span, i.e. al-ʿaṣr is heard as the lifetime granted.  · terms: نُعَمِّرْكُم, nuʿammirkum  · verses: 35:37, 103:2

## ¶7
7a: The root's most concrete sense is pressing: ḍaghṭu shayʾin ḥattā yataḥallab, pressing a thing until its liquid flows out.  · terms: ضغط شيء حتى يتحلب, ḍaghṭ shayʾ ḥattā yataḥallab
7b: The images drawn from the pressing sense do not replace the sense of 'time' in 103:1 but are heard alongside it; the paragraph keeps time as the verse's meaning.  · terms: al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1
7c: The vocabulary of pressing grapes: al-miʿṣār is a bag like a feed-bag in which grapes are pressed, al-maʿṣara the place of pressing, al-ʿuṣāra what flows from pressing, and ʿaṣīr anything whose juice has been pressed; the dregs remain in the bag.  · terms: المعصار, al-miʿṣār, المعصرة, al-maʿṣara, العصارة, al-ʿuṣāra, عصير, ʿaṣīr
7d: Pressing divides a thing in two: the essence that flows away and the dry mass left behind.  · terms: al-ʿaṣr

## ¶8
8a: The Arabs made al-ʿuṣāra and al-muʿtaṣar a figure for good and giving: of a generous man, yaʿṣiru fīnā ka-lladhī taʿṣir (he gives to us as you give), and al-ʿuṣāra also means al-ghalla, the yield of land.  · terms: العصارة والمعتصر, al-ʿuṣāra wa-l-muʿtaṣar, يعصر فينا, yaʿṣiru fīnā, العصارة الغلة, al-ʿuṣāra al-ghalla
8b: Pressing is carried to the sky: al-muʿṣirāt are clouds pressed with rain, saḥāba muʿṣir a cloud that yields rain as if milked, and ʿuṣira l-qawm means the people were rained upon.  · terms: المعصرات, al-muʿṣirāt, السحابة المعصر, al-saḥāba al-muʿṣir, عصر القوم, ʿuṣira l-qawm
8c: Al-muʿṣirāt in 78:14 (wa-anzalnā mina l-muʿṣirāti māʾan thajjājā) is taken as the rain clouds 'pressed' with rain, the Qurʾān naming these clouds with the root's word; other identifications of al-muʿṣirāt are not considered.  · terms: ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ, al-muʿṣirāt  · verses: 78:14
8d: 78:14-16, addressed to those disputing the news of the Resurrection, are read as a chain of blessing set off by the pressing of clouds: pressed cloud, then grain, plants and dense gardens.  · terms: ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ, al-muʿṣirāt, ثَجَّاجًۭا, thajjājā  · verses: 78:14, 78:15, 78:16

## ¶9
9a: The other end of pressing is dryness: al-maʿṣūr is the tongue dried out by thirst, a pressed tongue with no water left in it.  · terms: المعصور, al-maʿṣūr
9b: The same verb names a movement of rescue: al-iʿtiṣār is when one chokes on food and sips water little by little to get it down.  · terms: الاعتصار, al-iʿtiṣār
9c: The poet's verse law bi-ghayri l-māʾi ḥalqī shariqun kuntu ka-l-ghaṣṣāni bi-l-māʾi ʿtiṣārī is read as turning that image round: what chokes him is water itself, so no rescuing sip remains.  · terms: اعتصاري, iʿtiṣārī, كالغصان, ka-l-ghaṣṣān

## ¶10
10a: Heard beside the time of 103:1, these images show time as pressure: the days pass over a person and draw something out of him, either an essence that flows as gift, like rain from a cloud, or a dry mass like a thirst-dried tongue.  · terms: al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1
10b: The loss (khusr) of 103:2 is read as the dry remainder of what has been pressed.  · terms: خُسْرٍ, khusr  · verses: 103:2
10c: Ṣabr, the last word of 103:3, is also the name of a bitter juice pressed from a tree (aloe), which carries the pressing image to the end of the sura.  · terms: الصبر, ṣabr

## ¶11
11a: Ḥattā ḥīn in 12:35 is read as measuring Joseph's imprisonment by a span of time, and linked to ʿaṣr meaning a stretch of time and also detention (al-ḥabs); the paragraph says this is a connection, not shared wording, since the Qurʾān uses another word (sijn) for the prison.  · terms: حَتَّىٰ حِينٍۢ, ḥattā ḥīn, al-ḥabs  · verses: 12:35
11b: In aʿṣiru khamran (12:36) what the youth presses is grapes, but the sentence names it by the drink that will come of it: pressing is named by its product.  · terms: أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا, aʿṣiru khamran  · verses: 12:36
11c: Joseph's interpretation in 12:41 (fa-yasqī rabbahu khamran) is read as rescue for this youth, and 12:42 calls him the one who would be saved (nājin): the one who presses in his dream is the one who leaves the prison.  · terms: نَاجٍۢ, nājin, فَيَسْقِى رَبَّهُۥ خَمْرًۭا, fa-yasqī rabbahu khamran  · verses: 12:41, 12:42
11d: The lexical sense al-ʿaṣr = al-manjāh (place of rescue, escape) is heard echoing in this scene.  · terms: العصر المنجاة, al-ʿaṣr al-manjāh  · verses: 12:42

## ¶12
12a: In 12:42 it is the freed youth who forgets to mention Joseph to his master, and this forgetting prolongs Joseph's stay in prison by biḍʿa sinīn (some years).  · terms: فَلَبِثَ فِى ٱلسِّجْنِ بِضْعَ سِنِينَ, biḍʿa sinīn  · verses: 12:42
12b: Wa-ddakara baʿda umma in 12:45 is read as 'remembered after a long while' (umma as a span of time).  · terms: بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ, baʿda umma  · verses: 12:45
12c: Joseph's interpretation of the king's dream of seven green and seven dry ears (12:43) is read as a time plan: seven years of sowing with the harvest left in its ears (fa-dharūhu fī sunbulihi, 12:47), then seven hard years that eat what was stored except a little kept back (tuḥṣinūn, 12:48).  · terms: فِى سُنۢبُلِهِۦٓ, fī sunbulihi, تُحْصِنُونَ, tuḥṣinūn  · verses: 12:43, 12:47, 12:48
12d: When the sheaths of the ears appear, the Arabs said qad aʿṣara l-zarʿ, derived from al-ʿaṣr meaning al-ḥirz (protection) because the grain is guarded in its sheath; the paragraph links this to Joseph's plan of keeping grain in its ear, while noting the Qurʾān's word there is sunbul, not ʿaṣr.  · terms: عصر الزرع, aʿṣara l-zarʿ, العصر وهو الحرز, al-ʿaṣr al-ḥirz  · verses: 12:47
12e: Yaʿṣirūn in 12:49 is read as 'they press' (the year of relief in which pressing resumes) and is also reported as understood to mean 'they gain yield from their lands' (yastaghillūna bi-araḍīhim).  · terms: يَعْصِرُونَ, yaʿṣirūn, يستغلون بأرضيهم, yastaghillūna bi-araḍīhim  · verses: 12:49

## ¶13
13a: In the Joseph story the root's seemingly distant senses fall into one calendar: time spent in prison, pressing in the dream of the one to be rescued, forgotten and lengthening years, grain kept in its sheath, and the year of plenty when pressing begins again.  · terms: al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 12:35, 12:36, 12:42, 12:47, 12:49
13b: Pressing in this story marks the end of scarcity: the grapes to be pressed are the share of those who waited patiently through the hard years and stored.  · terms: يَعْصِرُونَ, yaʿṣirūn  · verses: 12:49
13c: Seen this way, the time sworn by in 103:1 is not only a span that consumes but one in which what is stored is protected and finally pressed out and flows.  · terms: ٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶14
14a: Al-ʿaṣr also means holding back: al-ʿaṣr = al-ḥabs, and mā ʿaṣaraka means 'what held you back, prevented you'.  · terms: العصر الحبس, al-ʿaṣr al-ḥabs, ما عصرك, mā ʿaṣaraka
14b: One who holds on and does not give is called ʿāṣir (= mumsik, tight-fisted).  · terms: عاصر, ʿāṣir
14c: The verb also names withholding and taking back property: yaʿtaṣiru l-wālidu ʿalā waladihi fī mālihi (the father withholds his child's property from him) and aʿṭaytu fulānan ʿaṭiyyatan fa-ʿtaṣartuhā (I gave a gift and then took it back).  · terms: يعتصر الوالد, yaʿtaṣiru l-wālid, فاعتصرتها, fa-ʿtaṣartuhā
14d: The same verb can name both open-handedness (the generous man who yaʿṣiru) and tight-fistedness, because both are gripping something in the hand; the difference is whether anything flows out of the grip.  · terms: يعصر, yaʿṣiru, عاصر, ʿāṣir

## ¶15
15a: Al-muʿṣir is a girl at the time of her first menstruation, so called because she is kept in the house and given a shelter (ʿaṣr); the grain kept in its sheath is within the same kind of shelter.  · terms: المعصر, al-muʿṣir
15b: Holding is not always blameworthy: here it means protecting one who stands at a threshold.  · terms: العصر الحبس, al-ʿaṣr al-ḥabs

## ¶16
16a: The story of the owners of the garden (68:17-27), who swore to harvest at dawn and meant to keep every poor person out (68:24), is cited as the Qurʾān's instance of what withholding produce brings: the withheld harvest did not stay in the withholders' hands (68:19-20, 68:26-27).  · terms: أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ, aṣḥāb al-janna, مِسْكِينٌۭ, miskīn  · verses: 68:17, 68:19, 68:20, 68:24, 68:26, 68:27
16b: Wa-lā yastathnūn (68:18) is read as 'they made no exception' in their oath.  · terms: وَلَا يَسْتَثْنُونَ, wa-lā yastathnūn  · verses: 68:18
16c: Fa-aṣbaḥat ka-l-ṣarīm (68:20) is read as the garden turning out at morning cut down and black.  · terms: كَٱلصَّرِيمِ, ka-l-ṣarīm  · verses: 68:20
16d: The garden owners swore an oath without exception, whereas the sura's oath is completed by the general ruling of 103:2 followed by the exception (illā) of 103:3.  · terms: وَلَا يَسْتَثْنُونَ, wa-lā yastathnūn  · verses: 68:17, 68:18, 103:2

## ¶17
17a: The root denotes clinging: taʿalluq bi-shayʾ wa-imtisāk bihi (hanging on to a thing and holding it tight), and the place clung to is called al-ʿaṣr = al-maljaʾ (refuge); iʿtaṣartu bi-fulān / taʿaṣṣartu means I took refuge with someone, and iʿtaṣara bi-l-makān he took refuge in a place.  · terms: العصر الملجأ, al-ʿaṣr al-maljaʾ, اعتصرت بفلان, iʿtaṣartu bi-fulān, تعصرت, taʿaṣṣartu
17b: The idiom ʿuṣrat al-manjūd ties this refuge to the hard-pressed: the refuge of one in distress.  · terms: عصرة المنجود, ʿuṣrat al-manjūd
17c: The bond between two people is named by the word through its absence: mā baynahumā ʿaṣrun wa-lā yaṣr, there is neither affection nor kinship between them.  · terms: ما بينهما عصر ولا يصر, mā baynahumā ʿaṣr wa-lā yaṣr
17d: Al-ʿunṣur (root, lineage) is said to come from al-ʿaṣr in the sense of refuge, with an added nūn: one's lineage is where one turns for refuge.  · terms: العنصر, al-ʿunṣur
17e: Al-maʿāṣir, coats of mail, are said to derive from al-ʿaṣr because one is protected by them.  · terms: المعاصر, al-maʿāṣir

## ¶18
18a: That one word names both squeezing and refuge is only an apparent contradiction: both are a grip. Pressing wraps a thing from outside, the one taking refuge grips something tightly, armour wraps and protects the body; the hard-pressed run to refuge, as the idiom ʿuṣrat al-manjūd says.  · terms: عصرة المنجود, ʿuṣrat al-manjūd, al-ʿaṣr al-maljaʾ
18b: Heard beside the time of 103:1, this image makes the time sworn by both a span that presses man and a place of refuge.  · terms: ٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1

## ¶19
19a: The youths' taking refuge in the cave (fa-ʾwū ilā l-kahf, 18:16; idh awā l-fityatu ilā l-kahf, 18:10) and their sleep (fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim, 18:11) are cited as the Qurʾān's instance of a refuge that closes time off.  · terms: فَأْوُۥٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ, fa-ʾwū ilā l-kahf, أَوَى, awā, فَضَرَبْنَا عَلَىٰٓ ءَاذَانِهِمْ, fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim  · verses: 18:10, 18:11, 18:16
19b: The sleepers' estimate labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawm (18:19) is set against wa-labithū fī kahfihim thalātha miʾatin sinīna wa-zdādū tisʿā (18:25), which the paragraph takes as the true length of their stay.  · terms: لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ, labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawm, ثَلَٰثَ مِا۟ئَةٍۢ سِنِينَ وَٱزْدَادُوا۟ تِسْعًۭا, thalātha miʾatin sinīna wa-zdādū tisʿā  · verses: 18:19, 18:25
19c: The Cave story shows three faces of ʿaṣr together, refuge, a long age, and the span the sleeper takes for a moment, though the word itself does not occur there.  · terms: al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 18:10, 18:19, 18:25
19d: The believers excepted in 103:3 lean on this refuge image: deliverance from loss is heard as taking refuge.

## ¶20
20a: Al-iʿṣār is a wind that blows, raises dust and lifts it to the sky like a column, and also the dust that rises in a spiral (plural aʿāṣīr): a wind that gathers what is on the ground by turning it and draws it up as an upright column.  · terms: الإعصار, al-iʿṣār, الأعاصير, al-aʿāṣīr
20b: The same branch describes rising scent: of a perfumed woman passing, li-dhaylihā ʿaṣar (scent rises behind her train), and al-ʿuṣra is the wafting and stirring of perfume.  · terms: لذيلها عصر, li-dhaylihā ʿaṣar, العصرة, al-ʿuṣra
20c: What links whirlwind and scent is a single motion: what swells and rises behind a moving thing is either dust or fragrance.  · terms: الإعصار, al-iʿṣār, العصرة, al-ʿuṣra

## ¶21
21a: Iʿṣārun fīhi nārun fa-ḥtaraqat in 2:266 is cited as the Qurʾān's use of the iʿṣār whirlwind, set in a parable of a garden whose owner has reached old age with weak children.  · terms: إِعْصَارٌۭ فِيهِ نَارٌۭ, iʿṣārun fīhi nār  · verses: 2:266
21b: The 2:266 parable is read in the series of parables on spending in God's way: after 2:264 (spending for show, like soil on a smooth rock washed bare by a downpour) and 2:265 (spending for God's pleasure, a hillside garden yielding double), and closing with kadhālika yubayyinu llāhu lakumu l-āyāti laʿallakum tatafakkarūn.  · terms: صَفْوَانٍ, ṣafwān, أَيَوَدُّ أَحَدُكُمْ, a-yawaddu aḥadukum  · verses: 2:264, 2:265, 2:266

## ¶22
22a: In the 2:266 garden several senses of the root stand together though only iʿṣār occurs as a word: grapes (aʿnāb, fruit to be pressed), rivers and all produce (yield), old age (al-kibar) as the afternoon of a life, and the fiery whirlwind that destroys it all.  · terms: أَعْنَابٍۢ, aʿnāb, ٱلْكِبَرُ, al-kibar, إِعْصَارٌۭ, iʿṣār  · verses: 2:266
22b: The produce awaited a lifetime is lost just when its owner has no strength left to plant again; this is read as the most concrete picture of the loss (khusr) of 103:2.  · terms: خُسْرٍ, khusr, ٱلْكِبَرُ, al-kibar  · verses: 2:266, 103:2
22c: Heard beside al-ʿaṣr, the whirlwind is the face of time that can scatter in a moment what time has accumulated.  · terms: إِعْصَارٌۭ, iʿṣār, ٱلْعَصْرِ, al-ʿaṣr  · verses: 103:1, 2:266
22d: The rising of scent stands at the other end of the same motion: behind one who passes, a fragrant trace rather than dust may remain.  · terms: العصرة, al-ʿuṣra
