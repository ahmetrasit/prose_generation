#85 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Ishaq says a man demolished the wall to build a sheep enclosure; one youth entered the changed city, where his old coin exposed the passage of centuries. «ألقى الله في نفس رجل من أهل البلد أن يهدم ذلك البنيان فيبني به حظيرة لغنمه» [filed under 18:11-12]
#86 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن إسحاق · reports · The youth's account convinced the town leaders and king that the sleepers were a sign confirming resurrection; the youths then died, and the king ordered a place of worship at the cave. «فعرفوا أنهم كانوا نياما بأمر الله تعالى وإنما أوقظوا ليكونوا آية للناس وتصديقا للبعث» [filed under 18:11-12]
#87 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · reports · In a final report, the youths asked to remain on earth rather than be placed in gold coffins; after their death the king established a mosque and annual feast. «فقالوا إنا لم نخلق من ذهب وقضة ولكن خلقنا من تراب فاتركنا كما كنا» [filed under 18:11-12]
#88 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الزجاج · reports · Striking over their ears means God put them to sleep and blocked their hearing, since a sleeper who hears wakes up. «قال الزجاج المعنى أنمناهم ومنعناهم السمع لأن النائم إذا سمع انتبه»
#89 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · reports · عددا can be an accusative verbal noun: count them as a count. «أحدهما على المصدر المعنى تعد عددا»
#90 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · reports · Alternatively, عددا modifies the years: years possessing a number. «والثاني أن يكون نعتا للسنين المعنى سنين ذات عدد»
#91 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Mentioning a count emphasizes abundance: a small number is evident, while many require counting. «والفائدة في ذكر العدد في الشيء المعدود توكيد كثرة الشيء»
#92 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · “We struck upon their ears” means God put them to sleep by placing a barrier that kept awakening sounds from reaching their hearing; “barrier” is omitted from the wording. «معناه أنمناهم وتقدير الكلام أنه تعالى ضرب على آذانهم حجابا يمنع»
#93 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj says describing countable things with “numbered” implies many, because small numbers need no enumeration. «ذكر العدد ههنا يفيد كثرة السنين»
#94 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الفراء · reports · Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj treat adadan as an adjective of years: years having a count, with either an omitted noun or the object named by its verbal noun. «نعت لسنين المعنى سنين ذات عدد أي معدودة هذا قول الفراء وقول الزجاج»
#95 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj also allows adadan to be an accusative cognate object, meaning they were counted with counting. «ويجوز أن ينتصب على المصدر ، المعنى تعد عدا»
#96 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The youths remained alive and unharmed through 309 years of sleep, shielded from the sun; Rāzī treats this as a karāma. «وبقاؤهم في النوم أحياء سالمين عن الآفات مدة ثلثمائة سنة وتسع سنين» [filed under 18:11, 18:12]
#97 RAZI-FULL d.606 · القاضي · reports · Al-Qāḍī objects that a prophet must have been present in that era for the extraordinary sleep to count as a prophetic miracle. «لا بد من أن يكون فيهم أو في ذلك الزمان نبي يصير ذلك علما له» [filed under 18:11, 18:12]
#98 RAZI-FULL d.606 · rejects · Rāzī replies that the sleep itself was ordinary, and its truth could not be verified until the exact sleepers were recognized after 309 years; therefore it cannot be a prophet’s miracle but is a gift to saints. «فامتنع جعل هذه الواقعة معجزة لأحد من الأنبياء فلم يبق إلا أن تجعل كرامة للأولياء» [filed under 18:11, 18:12]
#99 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The expression describes God putting the youths to sleep and is a notably eloquent Qur'anic phrase that challenged Arab eloquence. «عبارة عن إلقاء الله تعالى النوم عليهم»
#100 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Some take the following statement of 300 years plus nine as specifying the duration mentioned here. «بين الله تعالى عدد تلك السنين من بعد»
#101 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjaj explains the phrase as preventing them from hearing, since a sleeping person wakes when he hears. «أي منعناهم عن أن يسمعوا لأن النائم إذا سمع انتبه»
#102 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says their ears were blocked against incoming sounds by sleep. «ضربنا على آذانهم بالنوم أي سددنا آذانهم عن نفوذ»
#103 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · A further interpretation says God answered their prayer, diverted their people's harm, and made them sleep; the readings converge in meaning. «أي فاستجبنا دعاءهم، وصرفنا عنهم شر قومهم»
#104 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · قطرب · reports · Qutrub compares the idiom to striking a subject's hand to stop wrongdoing or a slave's hand to stop his trading activity. «هذا كقول العرب ضرب الأمير على يد الرعية إذا منعهم الفساد»
#105 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الأسود بن يعفر · reports · Al-Aswad's verse uses the phrase 'struck the earth over me' to witness this idiom; he was blind. «ضربت علي الأرض الأسداس»
#106 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Ears are singled out because sound most often disrupts sleep, and sleep deepens when hearing is inactive; a hadith describes Satan urinating in the ear of a long sleeper. «لأنها الجارحة التي منها عظم فساد النوم»
#107 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Adadan modifies years as counted; counting conveys a long duration since a small number needs no enumeration. It can also be an accusative of verbal noun, per Abu Ubayda. «نعت للسنين أي معدودة، والقصد به العبارة عن التكثير»
#108 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · أبو عبيدة · reports · Abu Ubayda parses adadan as an accusative cognate object. «وقال أبو عبيدة: «عددا» نصب على المصدر»
#109 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · God placed a barrier over their ears, meaning He put them into sleep in which sounds could not awaken them; the object is omitted idiomatically. «ضربنا عليهم حجابا يمنع السماع بمعنى أنمناهم إنامة لا تنبههم»
#110 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The cave and years are adverbial complements of the sleeping action; counted can imply many or few years depending on the reference point. «فى الكهف سنين } ظرفان لضربنا»
#111 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · God placed a curtain over their ears, meaning He caused deep sleep that sounds could not interrupt; the object 'barrier' is omitted. «أي ضربنا عليها حجابا من النوم يعني أنمناهم إنامة ثقيلة»
#112 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj says 'counted years' means years counted because a large number needs counting; a small number can be known without counting, whereas a counted home indicates few inhabitants. «أي تعد عددا لكثرتها لأن القليل يعلم مقداره من غير عدد»
#113 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Being struck on the ears is deep sleep of heedlessness from their spiritual world and perfection during immersion in bodily management until true maturity or voluntary or natural death. «أنمناهم نومة الغفلة عن عالمهم وكمالهم نومة ثقيلة»
#114 QASHANI d.736 · عليه السلام · reports · A prophetic saying calls people asleep and says they awaken when they die. «كما قال: " الناس نيام، فإذا ماتوا انتبهوا "»
#115 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · A hadith says Satan urinates in a man’s ear, meaning he sleeps so heavily that he does not rise at night. «وفي الحديث: «ذلك رجل بال الشيطان في أذنه»»
#116 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The omitted object of ḍarabnā is a barrier that prevents hearing, as in saying one built a tent over his wife. «ومفعول ضربنا محذوف أي حجابا من أن يسمع»
#117 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Sinīna is a time adverb governed by ḍarabnā; ʿadadan is a verbal noun used adjectivally or with an omitted preposition, meaning counted years and suggesting many. «وانتصب سنين على الظرف والعامل فيه فضربنا»
#118 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Striking over their ears is a vivid metaphor for heavy sleep, conveying forceful attachment and duration; ears are named because sleep deepens when hearing is suspended. «فضربنا على آذانهم استعارة بديعة للإنامة المستثقلة»
#119 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The metaphor resembles expressions for imposed humiliation, poll tax, conscription, and qualities fixed on a person, illustrated with lines by al-Farazdaq, al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur, and an unnamed poet. «وعبر بالضرب ليدل على قوة المباشرة واللصوق واللزوم ومنه ضربت عليهم الذلة»
#120 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhsharī allows ʿadadan to suggest few years because even a long period seems short to God, but the author rejects the comparison: 23:113 describes worldly life as short only when people see the promised punishment. «وقال الزمخشري: ويحتمل أن يريد القلة لأن الكثير قليل عنده»
#121 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · The object of فضربنا is omitted, understood as the sleep-preventing barrier. «مفعوله محذوف، أي: ضربنا الحجاب المانع»
#122 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · على آذانهم metaphorically means sleep's persistence; ears are named because striking them brings sleep. «مفعوله محذوف، أي: ضربنا الحجاب المانع»
#123 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · الأسود · reports · Al-Aswad's verse ضربت علي الأرض uses ضربت على for imposed covering or confinement. «كقول الأسود:
3127- ومن الحوادث لا أبالك أنني»
#124 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · الفرزدق · reports · Al-Farazdaq's spider verse likewise uses ضربت عليك for covering someone. «وقال الفرزدق:
3128- ضربت عليك العنكبوت بنسجها»
#125 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · سنين is an adverbial complement of ضربنا. «مفعوله محذوف، أي: ضربنا الحجاب المانع»
#126 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · reports · If عددا is a verbal noun, it can modify سنين with ذوات omitted, intensify the count, or be governed by an implied تعد. «مفعوله محذوف، أي: ضربنا الحجاب المانع»
#127 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · reports · If عددا is an adjective meaning “counted,” it only modifies سنين. «مفعوله محذوف، أي: ضربنا الحجاب المانع»
#128 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · God put the youths to sleep in the cave for many counted years. «ألقينا عليهم النوم حين دخلوا إلى الكهف»
#129 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Striking their ears means sealing them against sound with a deep sleep that ordinary noise could not disturb. «سددناها وأمسكناها عن السمع , وكان أصله ؛ ضربنا عليها حجابا بنوم ثقيل»
