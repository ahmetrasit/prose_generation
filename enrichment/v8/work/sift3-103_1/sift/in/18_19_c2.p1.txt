#44 RAZI-FULL d.606 · المفسرون · reports · Commentators say the youths carried coins bearing the image of the king of their time and that their city was what is now called Tarsus. «كانت معهم دراهم عليها صورة الملك الذي كان في زمانهم يعني بالمدينة التي يقال لها اليوم طرسوس»
#45 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The episode shows that taking steps to secure food is important and does not negate reliance on God. «وهذه الآية تدل على أن السعي في إمساك الزاد أمر مهم مشروع وأنه لا يبطل التوكل»
#46 RAZI-FULL d.606 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās glosses the purest food as lawful slaughter, since most inhabitants were Magians, though some secretly believed. «يريد ما حل من الذبائح لأن عامة أهل بلدهم»
#47 RAZI-FULL d.606 · مجاهد · reports · Mujāhid says they sought food farthest from the tyrant's injustice; other glosses understand the phrase as tastiest or cheapest. «وقال مجاهد : كان ملكهم ظالما»
#48 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj parses ayyuhā as subject, azkā as its predicate, and ṭaʿāman as a specification. «رفع بالابتداء ، و { أزكى } خبره»
#49 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The emissary should act discreetly while entering the city and buying food, and tell nobody where the youths are. «أي يكون ذلك في سر وكتمان يعني دخول المدينة وشراء الطعام»
#50 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Their questioning can itself be an intended purpose: it disclosed extraordinary aspects of God's power, and such disclosure is desirable for its own sake. «وذلك الانكشاف أمر مطلوب لذاته»
#51 RAZI-FULL d.606 · المفسرون · reports · Commentators say they entered the cave in the morning and awoke near day's end, explaining why they guessed a day or part of one. «قال المفسرون إنهم دخلوا الكهف غدوة وبعثهم الله في آخر النهار»
#52 RAZI-FULL d.606 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the speaker who returned the duration to God as their leader Yamlikhā, who inferred from their changed hair, nails, and faces that much time had passed. «قال ابن عباس هو رئيسهم يمليخا رد علم ذلك إلى الله تعالى»
#53 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم · reports · Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim read wariqikum with sukūn on rāʾ and fatḥa on wāw; Ibn Kathīr reads kasra on rāʾ and assimilates qāf into kāf. «قرأ أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم بورقكم ساكنة الراء مفتوحة الواو»
#54 RAZI-FULL d.606 · ابن محيصن · reports · Ibn Muḥayṣin's reading combines kasra on wāw with sukūn on rāʾ and assimilation, which Rāzī rejects because it creates two consecutive sukūns. «وعن ابن محيصن أنه كسر الواو وأسكن الراء وأدغم القاف في الكاف»
#55 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Wariq means silver, coined or uncoined; a report about ʿArfaja using a silver nose supports the uncoined sense, and several vowel forms are attested. «والورق اسم للفضة سواء كانت مضروبة أم لا»
#56 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الفراء · reports · Al-Farrāʾ lists the forms waraqu, warqu, and wariqu, and calls kasra on wāw the least good form. «ذكره الفراء والزجاج قال الفراء وكسر الواو أردؤها»
#57 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الأزهري · reports · Al-Azharī says riqqa, another word for silver, derives from wariq through the pattern of ṣila and ʿida. «قال الأزهري أصله ورق مثل صلة وعدة»
#58 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · عبد الرحمن بن عوف · reports · A Bukhari report says Abd al-Rahman contracted with Umayya ibn Khalaf to protect each other's dependents in Mecca and Medina; Umayya objected to the name al-Rahman. «كاتبت أمية بن خلف كتابا بأن يحفظني في صاغيتي بمكة وأحفظه»
#59 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الأصمعي · reports · Al-Asmaʿi explains saghiya as a man's adherents who incline toward him, from the verb meaning to incline. «صاغية الرجل الذين يميلون إليه ويأتونه وهو مأخوذ من صغا»
#60 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Agency is a delegated contract allowed because people need help managing affairs and not everyone can handle every task alone. «الوكالة عقد نيابة، أذن الله سبحانه فيه للحاجة إليه وقيام المصلحة»
#61 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Jurists support agency from this verse, 9:60, 12:93, and many hadiths, including the report of Urwa al-Bariqi. «وقد استدل علماؤنا على صحتها بآيات من الكتاب، منها هذه الآية»
#62 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · جابر بن عبد الله · reports · Jabir says he was sent to Khaybar and told by the Prophet to collect fifteen wasqs from his agent, using a hand on the collarbone as proof if asked. «إذا أتيت وكيلي فخذ منه خمسة عشر وسقا فإن ابتغى منك آية»
#63 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Agency is valid in any right that can be delegated, but not for a usurper or an unlawful act. «الوكالة جائزة في كل حق تجوز النيابة فيه»
#64 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Agency in this story arose from fear of discovery; scholars agree that people with an excuse may appoint agents, while most permit healthy present people too. «الوكالة إنما كانت مع التقية خوف أن يشعر بهم أحد»
#65 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · أبو حنيفة وسحنون · reports · Abu Hanifa and Sahnun disallow agency for a person present without an excuse; Ibn al-Arabi speculates Sahnun used this against oppressors, while al-Qurtubi permits it for virtuous people. «وقال أبو حنيفة وسحنون: لا تجوز»
#66 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · النبي ﷺ · reports · In Abu Hurayra's report, the Prophet repaid a camel debt with a better camel, and the creditor praised him; the Prophet said the best people pay best. «إن خيركم أحسنكم قضاء»
#67 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · This sound report shows a healthy person present may appoint others: the Prophet authorized his Companions to repay for him, contrary to Abu Hanifa and Sahnun. «فدل هذا الحديث مع صحته على جواز توكيل الحاضر الصحيح البدن»
#68 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن خويز منداد · reports · Ibn Khuwayz says the verse supports partnership because the youths shared their money, agency because they sent a buyer, and joint eating despite unequal appetites. «تضمنت هذه الآية جواز الشركة لأن الورق كان لجميعهم»
#69 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · The school allows a poor person to mix donated food with a rich person's meal and a business agent to mix provisions and eat together. «وقد قالوا في المضارب يخلط طعامه بطعام غيره»
#70 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن العربي · rejects · Ibn al-Arabi says the verse does not prove shared partnership because each youth may have given money separately; he relies instead on two hadiths. «ليس في الآية دليل على ذلك لأنه يحتمل أن يكون كل واحد منهم قد أعطاه منفردا»
#71 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عمر · reports · Ibn Umar cites the prohibition on taking two dates together without a companion's permission as evidence about shared food. «نهى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن الاقتران إلا أن يستأذن الرجل أخاه»
#72 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · The army-of-khabt report is less decisive because Abu Ubayda may simply have distributed equal portions rather than having everyone share one supply. «وهذا دون الأول في الظهور لأنه يحتمل أن يكون أبو عبيدة يعطيهم كفافا»
#73 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The permission to mix with orphans as siblings and the permission to eat together or separately support shared eating; the source cites 2:220 and labels the latter 24:16. «ليس عليكم جناح أن تأكلوا جميعا أو أشتاتا»
#74 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Baʿath means movement from stillness; here God awakened the youths in the same clothes and condition as before sleep. «البعث: التحريك عن سكون»
#75 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says their silver coins carried the image of the king of their time. «كان معهم دراهم عليها صورة الملك الذي كان في زمانهم»
#76 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · Azka food means meat slaughtered lawfully because townspeople sacrificed in the name of idols; Ibn Abbas also says most inhabitants were Magians. «أحل ذبيحة لأن أهل بلدهم كانوا يذبحون على اسم الصنم»
#77 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Other readings of azka are more blessed, tastier, or cheaper; they may have asked for enough food for several people, with rice, raisins, or dates proposed. «وقيل: «أزكى طعاما» أي أكثر بركة»
#78 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Rizqan means food; let the delegate act discreetly in town and tell nobody, lest discovery expose the rest of the youths. «أي في دخول المدينة وشراء الطعام»
#79 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · شاعر · reports · An unnamed poet uses baʿath for waking young men at dawn. «وفتيان صدق قد بعثت بسحرة»
#80 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The lām in li-yatasaʾalu is a consequence lām, not a purpose: their awakening resulted in their questioning, as in 28:8. «واللام في قوله «ليتساءلوا» لام الصيرورة وهي لام العاقبة»
#81 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · They entered the cave in the morning and awoke late in the day; their leader, called Tamlikha or Maksalmina, said God knew the duration. «وذلك أنهم دخلوه غدوة وبعثهم الله في آخر النهار»
#82 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas compares their silver currency to the hooves of a young camel. «قال ابن عباس: كانت ورقهم كأخفاف الربع»
#83 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم · reports · These readers pronounce the rāʾ in waraqikum with kasra; other readers make it still, and both are dialects. «قرأ ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم «بورقكم» بكسر الراء»
#84 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم · reports · They read waraqikum with a still rāʾ, removing the kasra as heavy; al-Zajjaj records a form with kasra on wāw. «وقرأ أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر عن عاصم «بورقكم» بسكون الراء»
#85 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الغزنوي · reports · The youths awoke hungry, and al-Ghaznawi identifies the dispatched youth Tamlikha as the youngest. «ويروى أنهم انتبهوا جياعا، وأن المبعوث هو تمليخا»
#86 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · The city was Ephesus, also called Tarsus; its earlier name was Ephesus and it was renamed Tarsus after Islam. Ibn Abbas says their coins bore the current king's image. «والمدينة: أفسوس ويقال هي طرسوس، وكان اسمها في الجاهلية أفسوس»
#87 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Their awakening is a second sign of God's power, teaching them about resurrection and prompting gratitude and certainty. «وكما أنمناهم آية بعثناهم آية على كمال قدرتنا»
