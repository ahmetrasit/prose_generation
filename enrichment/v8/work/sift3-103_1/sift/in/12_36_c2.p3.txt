#123 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The king's later request shows that Egyptian courtiers included people thought capable of interpreting dreams. «دل عليه قوله تعالى حكاية عن ملك مصر»
#124 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Aṣr means squeezing a moist substance to extract liquid; ʿaṣīr names the extracted liquid after its source. «الضغط باليد أو بحجر أو نحوه على شيء فيه رطوبة»
#125 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Bread is a baked portion of flour and water, also called a loaf; “its interpretation” refers either to the mentioned matter or to the dream as a class. «اسم لقطعة من دقيق البر أو الشعير»
#126 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The two fatayān were young male slaves; the context identifies them as servants of the king, as later reports will also say. «الفتى العبد وسياق الآيات يدل على أنهما كانا عبدين من عبيد الملك»
#127 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The break before ‘one of them said’ separates the dreams from the entry into prison; arānī recounts a past dream, and squeezing wine means squeezing grapes intended for wine. «وقوله { أعصر خمرا } أي أعصر عنبا كما يعصر ليتخذ خمرا»
#128 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The second prisoner dreamed of carrying bread that birds pecked from atop his head. «إني أرانى أحمل فوق رأسي خبزا تأكل الطير منه»
#129 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The repeated ‘said’ replaces repeating the verb ‘tell us’; the pronoun in ‘its interpretation’ refers contextually to what each man saw in his dream. «فاكتفى عن ذكر الفعل بقوله { قال } { وقال } وهذا من لطائف تفنن القرآن»
#130 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · They believed Joseph was among the good because they saw signs of goodness in him, and thought good people’s pure hearts read events more soundly. «نعتقدك من المحسنين لما نشاهد فيك من سيماهم»
#131 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Alternatively, the pronoun refers to the dreams, so Joseph promises to interpret them before the food arrives; the author finds this less likely in context. «وأما على تقدير عود ضمير { بتأويله } إلى ما رأياه من الرؤيا» [filed under 12:37, 12:36]
#132 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام · reports · The same report identifies the two young men as the king’s servants, a baker and a cupbearer, and says the baker had fabricated the dream and was the one crucified. «ودخل معه السجن فتيان يقول عبدان للملك أحدهما خباز والآخر صاحب الشراب» [filed under 12:36, 12:41]
#133 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · Joseph was called a benefactor because he cared for the sick, sought help for the needy, and treated fellow prisoners generously. «قال أبو عبد الله عليه السلام في قوله»
#134 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Dream interpretation appears in Jacob’s explanation of Joseph’s dream, Joseph’s interpretation of his own, his companions’ and the king’s dreams, and in waking predictions taught by God. «ما حكاه الله تعالى من بيان يعقوب تأويل رؤيا يوسف عليه السلام» [filed under 12:6, 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#135 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · Joseph's excellence included making room for people, borrowing for the needy, and helping the weak. «عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام في قول الله عز وجل { إنا نراك من المحسنين } قال»
#136 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qur'an also recounts Joseph's dream, his prison companions' dreams, and Pharaoh's dream. «ومنها ما حكاه تعالى من رؤيا يوسف عليه السلام إذ قال يوسف لأبيه يا أبت إني رأيت» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#137 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Everyone has encountered dreams that reveal hidden matters or future events, especially clear dreams requiring no interpretation; their correspondence to later events cannot be dismissed as coincidence. «ما منا واحد إلا وقد شاهد من نفسه شيئا من الرؤى والمنامات دله على بعض الأمور الخفية» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#138 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Dreaming is a mental act involving imagination, which recombines stored sensory images; bodily conditions, temperament, and emotions shape many ordinary dreams. «والمتخيلة من القوى الفعالة دائما ربما تدوم في عملها من جهة الأنباء الواردة عليها من ناحية الحس» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#139 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Naturalist critics correctly identify bodily and emotional causes that shape many dreams, but their account only shows that some dreams lack truth. «وهذا هو الذي ذكره منكروا حقيقة الرؤيا من علماء الطبيعة لا يزيد على تعداد هذه الأسباب» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#140 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The claim that every dream is unreal does not follow: some sound dreams reveal truths and have a real relation to external events. «وهو غير المدعى وهو أن كل منام ليس ذا حقيقة فإن هناك منامات صالحة ورؤيا صادقة» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#141 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Every dream has causes and thus an interpretation, but causes vary: bodily or temperamental influences, chance circumstances, and preoccupations can produce dreams without foretelling external events. «فقد ظهر مما بينا أن جميع الرؤى لا تخلو عن حقيقة بمعنى أن هذه الإدراكات المتنوعة المختلفة» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#142 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The relevant true dreams are those not caused by external bodily or accidental factors, nor internal dispositions, yet connected to external events and cosmic realities. «وإنما البحث في نوع واحد من هذه المنامات» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#143 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Because future or hidden events are absent from the dreamer's senses, the author proposes that the sleeping soul connects to their causes beyond the physical world. «ولذا قيل إن الارتباط إنما استقر بينها وبين النفس النائمة من جهة اتصال النفس بسبب الحادثة الواقعة» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#144 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · He distinguishes the physical world of changing matter, the imaginal world of immaterial forms that causes physical events, and the intellectual world of universal truths that causes the imaginal realm. «أن العوالم ثلاثة عالم الطبيعة وهو العالم الدنيوي الذي نعيش فيه والأشياء الموجودة فيها صور مادية» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#145 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A soul able to grasp intellectual realities may see universal causes directly; otherwise it represents them through familiar images, and a sound soul may perceive causes without alteration in a clear dream. «فإن كانت النفس كاملة متمكنة من إدراك المجردات العقلية أدركتها واستحضرت أسباب الكائنات» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#146 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Other true dreams use symbols or opposites, such as a crown for honor or darkness for ignorance; these require interpretation by returning each image to the reality first perceived. «وهذه هي التي تحتاج إلى التعبير بردها إلى الأصل الذي هو المشهود الأولى للنفس كرد التاج إلى الفخار» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#147 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Symbolic dreams divide into manageable transformations and tangled sequences of transformations; the latter are confused dreams that resist interpretation, unlike clear dreams and interpretable symbolic dreams. «المنامات التي تصرفت فيها النفس بالحكاية والتمثيل» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#148 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن سيرين · reports · In an example, Ibn Sīrīn interprets a dream of sealing people's mouths and sexual organs with a ring as becoming a Ramadan caller whose adhān makes people fast. «أن رجلا رأى في المنام أن بيده خاتما يختم به أفواه الناس وفروجهم فسأل ابن سيرين» [filed under 12:4, 12:36]
#149 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The two prisoners were the King's cup-bearer and baker, according to the Bible, which the Qur'an does not contradict; their offences are unspecified. «they were the King's cup-bearer and baker, both of them imprisoned for unspecified offences»
