#152 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · سعد · reports · Sa’d says the verse concerned six Companions, including Ibn Masud, who arrived early and drew near to hear the Prophet; Quraysh objected to their proximity. «نزلت هذه الآية في ستة من أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، منهم ابن مسعود»
#153 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Those the Prophet must not drive away are poor Muslims, devoted to their Lord in contrast to unbelievers who call on powerless beings. «عكس ما عليه الكفار في دعاء من لا يملك لهم ضرا ولا نفعا»
#154 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Morning and evening may mean both ends of each day, the two daily prayers, or continuous worship; their long constancy indicates sincerity rather than ostentation. «في طرفي النهار مطلقا أو بصلاتيهما أو يكون كناية عن الدوام»
#155 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The wealthy polytheists promised to follow Muhammad if he expelled his humble companions, whom they disparaged as insincere and driven by need. «وعدوه صلى الله عليه وسلم الاتباع إن طرد من تبعه ممن يأنفون من مجالستهم»
#156 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The Prophet has no stake in worldly gain from expelling the poor or accepting the rich; his mission calls people away from worldly life. «التي هو مبعوث للتنفير عنها»
#157 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The denial of responsibility for one another's account means the Prophet sees only outward conduct; he need not expel them over a fear that they are insincere or that they burden him. «ما عليك من حسابهم من شيء»
#158 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · An alternative reading of the denial of mutual accounting, linked to 18:28, concerns provision: the Prophet's invitation is for the afterlife, and neither the poor nor the Prophet depends on the other's provision. «يعني أن دعاءك إلى الله إنما مداره الأمر الأخروي»
#159 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · reports · The author adduces lexicons and usage showing that hisab can mean sufficiency or provision: 78:36 gives an abundant gift; the verb can mean feeding to satisfaction; and a report from Simak says hosts did not honor their guest. «صاحب القاموس وغيره : الحساب : الكافي»
#160 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · أبو عبيد الهروي · reports · Abu Ubayd explains ahsabtuhu as giving someone enough until he says, “That suffices me.” «أي أعطيته الكفاية حتى قال»
#161 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · reports · The phrase “without account” in 2:212 means without stinting or constriction; Ibn Faris likewise glosses ahsabtuhu as giving enough or what satisfies. «بغير تقتير وتضييق , وفي حديث سماك»
#162 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Expelling the poor would be unjustly putting things out of place and would not make the wealthy polytheists believe; guidance belongs to God alone. «وليس هدايتهم إلا إلينا»
#163 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · God tested groups through the Prophet's appointment and through differences of faith, wealth, and poverty; elites scorned the poor as unworthy of God's favor. «بالتخصيص بالإيمان والغنى والفقر ونحو ذلك» [filed under 6:52-53]
#164 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The elite's objection anticipates their later demand to receive what God's messengers received; God alone knows who merits preference through gratitude. «فالآية ناظرة إلى ما يأتي في هذه السورة من قوله تعالى» [filed under 6:52-53]
#165 DURR-FULL d.911 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · Quraysh leaders asked the Prophet to expel poor Muslim companions such as Suhayb, Ammar, Bilal, and Khabbab so that they might follow him; the warning verse was revealed. «عن عبد الله بن مسعود قال مر الملأ من قريش على النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم وعنده صهيب وعمار وبلال وخباب»
#166 DURR-FULL d.911 · عكرمة · reports · Quraysh nobles asked Abu Talib to have Muhammad expel poor and enslaved followers so they would join; Umar suggested testing their request, then verses about not expelling the believers were revealed. «عن عكرمة قال مشى عتبة بن ربيعة وشيبة بن ربيعة وقرظة بن عبد عمر وبن نوفل» [filed under 6:52, 6:53]
#167 SUYUTI-LUBAB d.911 · خباب · reports · Khabbab reports that Aqra ibn Habis and Uyayna ibn Hisn asked the Prophet to dismiss poor believers such as Suhayb, Bilal, Ammar, and Khabbab when Arab delegations arrived; the verse forbidding their expulsion was revealed. «جاء الأقرع ابن حابس وعيينة بن حصن فوجدا رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم مع صهيب وبلال وعمار وخباب قاعدا في ناس من الضعفاء»
#168 SUYUTI-LUBAB d.911 · ابن كثير · rejects · Ibn Kathir calls the report unusual because the verse is Meccan while Aqra and Uyayna converted much later, after the Hijra. «قال ابن كثير هذا حديث غريب فإن الآية مكية والأقرع وعيينة إنما أسلما بعد الهجرة بدهر»
#169 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · ‘Seeking His face’ is circumstantial to ‘they call’; it stresses that sincerity is the essential point of supplication. «يدعونه تعالى مخلصين له وقيد الدعاء بالإخلاص تنبيها على أنه ملاك الأمر»
#170 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · A Persian couplet says worship is good when intention is sincere; without that, it is like a kernel-less grain. «عبادت بإخلاص نيت نكوست وكرنه ه آيد زبى مغزوست»
#171 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Seeking God's face is among the strongest grounds for honor and is incompatible with expelling poor worshippers. «من أقوى موجبات الإكرام المنافي للأبعاد»
#172 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Quraysh not only disparaged poor believers' status but alleged their faith was false and that they gathered for food and clothing; God rejects this as grounds for expulsion. «في طعن فقراء المسلمين على وصفهم بكونهم موالي ومساكين بل طعنوا في إيمانهم»
#173 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The Prophet must judge by their outward mark of piety; any hidden defect in faith harms only them, not him, so he should welcome and nurture them. «ليس عليك إلا اعتبار ظاهر حالهم وهو اتسامهم بسمة المتقين»
#174 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The repeated denial of responsibility emphasizes that their reckoning is no concern of the Prophet, just as his is no concern of theirs. «مع أن الجواب قد تم بما قبله للمبالغة في بيان انتفاء كون حسابهم عليه»
#175 BURSEVI d.1127 · أبو السعود · reports · Abū al-Suʿūd says the repeated phrase emphasizes the point by pairing it with the uncontroversial reverse; ‘then you expel them’ answers the prohibition's logic. «قال المولى أبو السعود : وذكر قوله تعالى»
#176 BURSEVI d.1127 · السري السقطي · reports · Al-Sarī al-Saqaṭī says he found Bahlūl in a cemetery; Bahlūl replied that the dead do not harm him or gossip when he is away. «خرجت يوما إلى المقابر فإذا ببهلول فقلت له أي شيء تصنع هنا»
#177 BURSEVI d.1127 · بهلول · reports · Bahlūl left when hungry and recited that hunger is a pious act and prolonged hunger will eventually be followed by satisfaction. «أجالس قوما لا يؤذونني وإن غبت لا يغتابونني»
#178 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · A king's parable shows God adorning chosen lovers with righteous deeds and pure states: they need not display themselves to ordinary people, since God knows and their rank will be seen at the great gathering. «فهذا مثل من وفقهم الله تعالى للأعمال الصالحة والأحوال الزكية ولا حاجة لهم»
#179 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · A Persian couplet contrasts the noble servants of God with the speaker's group as wretched slaves. «أولئك خدام كرام وسادة ونحن عبيد السوء بئس عبيد»
#180 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Quraysh leaders asked the Prophet to expel poor wool-clad believers such as Ṣuhayb, ʿAmmār, Khabbāb, Bilāl, and Salmān so they could retain his company and status before Arab visitors. «حين رأوا في مجلسه الشريف فقراء المؤمنين»
#181 BURSEVI d.1127 · رسول الله · reports · The Prophet refused to expel believers, though he considered separating them to attract Quraysh; revelation taught that religion does not privilege the rich over the poor. «ما أنا بطارد المؤمنين»
#182 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Expulsion means removal; morning and evening remembrance signifies continuity, and whoever remembers continually remains with God. «الطرد الإبعاد وبالفارسية»
#183 BURSEVI d.1127 · النبي · reports · The Prophet says, ‘I am the companion of whoever remembers Me.’ «أنا جليس من ذكرني»
#184 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The poor worshippers seek God's face and pleasure alone, not worldly aims. «بذكرهم وعبادتهم {وجهه} تعالى ورضاه لا شيئا من أغراض الدنيا»
#185 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · ‘Thus We tested them’ points to God's giving poor believers precedence in religion by granting faith despite their poor worldly condition, while the rich had worldly precedence. «ذلك إشارة إلى مصدر ما بعده من الفعل»
#186 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The prefixed kāf intensifies the demonstrative: God tried them with a complete and remarkable trial. «والكاف مقحمة لتأكيد ما أفاده اسم الإشارة من الفخامة»
#187 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · After commanding the Prophet to warn the people who may become pious, God forbids doing so in a way that would lead him to expel the poor believers. «لما أمر النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم بانذار المذكورين لعلهم ينتظمون في سلك المتقين نهى»
#188 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Some accounts suggest the warning and no-expulsion verses came down together, while others do not indicate that. «ويفهم من بعض الروايات أن الآيتين نزلتا معا»
#189 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud reports Quraysh leaders seeing Suhayb, Ammar, Bilal, Khabbab, and other poor Muslims with the Prophet and demanding their expulsion, promising they might then follow him; the Quran was revealed concerning this. «مر الملأ من قريش على»
#190 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · خباب · reports · Khabbab reports that al-Aqraʿ and ʿUyayna looked down on the poor believers and demanded private access for tribal leaders, promising to meet the Prophet with the poor later; after Gabriel delivered the verse, the Prophet called the believers to him and then stayed with them when rising. «جاء الأقرع بن حابس التميمي وعيينة بن حصن الفزاري»
#191 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima reports that Quraysh nobles asked Abu Talib to have Muhammad expel poor slaves and allies so they would follow him; Umar suggested doing so temporarily, and verses warning and identifying the grateful were revealed, after which Umar apologized. «مشى عتبة وشيبة أبنا ربيعة وقرظة بن عبد عمرو»
