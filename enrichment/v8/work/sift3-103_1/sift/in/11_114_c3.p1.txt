#37 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · صاحب الذخائر · reports · The author of al-Dhakhāʾir argues that some good deeds expiate minor and major sins, citing reports promising forgiveness of past and future sins; Ibn al-Mundhir leans this way. «وذهب صاحب الذخائر إلى أن من الحسنات ما يكفر الصغائر والكبائر»
#38 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Critics call broad expiation ignorance and agreement with the Murjiʾa, arguing repentance would be pointless; al-Ālūsī replies that expiation differs from Murjiʾism and repentance remains obligatory for new and recurring delay sins. «إن الكبائر والصغائر تكفرها الطهارة والصلاة لظاهر الأحاديث»
#39 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Past expiation does not remove the ongoing duty to repent, just as a sleeper whose prayer time passed must still make it up; the Abū Hurayra report is specific and cannot be extended by analogy. «ولا يلزم من تكفير الله تعالى ذنوب عبده سقوط التكليف بالتوبة التي كلف بها تكليفا مستمرا»
#40 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الأشعري · reports · Repentance for minor sins is obligatory according to al-Ashʿarī, with al-Juwaynī and al-Anṣārī reporting consensus; ʿIzz al-Dīn ibn ʿAbd al-Salām says delay incurs renewed sin over time. «التوبة من الصغائر واجبة على ما نقل عن الأشعري وحكى إمام الحرمين وتلميذه الأنصاري الإجماع عليه»
#41 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The reminder benefits those who heed admonition, singled out because they are the ones who benefit. «أي عظة للمتعظين وخصهم بالذكر لأنهم المنتفعون بها»
#42 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhsharī says ‘that’ refers to the counsel about uprightness, avoiding tyranny and inclination toward wrongdoers, and establishing prayer at the named times; Abū Ḥayyān prefers prayer alone. «والإشارة إلى ما تقدم من الوصية بالإستقامة والنهي عن الطغيان والركون إلى الذين ظلموا»
#43 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الطبري · reports · Al-Ṭabarī takes the reference as the sūrah’s commands and prohibitions; others identify the Qurʾān, or prayer and repentance. «إشارة إلى الأوامر والنواهي في هذه السورة»
#44 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الواسطي · reports · Al-Wasiti says the lights of acts of obedience remove the darkness of acts of disobedience. «قال الواسطي : أنوار الطاعات تذهب بظلم المعاصي»
#45 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · يحيى بن معاذ · reports · Yahya ibn Muʿadh says God covers a believer's sin, then forgives it, then replaces it with good deeds, citing 25:70. «وقال يحيى بن معاذ : إن الله سبحانه لم يرض للمؤمن بالذنب حتى ستر ولم يرض بالستر حتى غفر»
#46 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Prayer at the stated times and good deeds erasing wrongs remind those who remember God of presence, purity, gatheredness, intimacy, and spiritual taste. «ذكرى للذاكرين تذكير لمن يذكر حاله عند الحضور مع الله تعالى في الصفاء والجمعية والأنس والذوق»
#47 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The command is to establish the prescribed prayer at its known times; prayer is the believer's ascent, as reports attest its lofty rank. «أمر بإقامة الصلاة المفروضة على ما علمت وقد ذكروا أن الصلاة معراج المؤمن وفي الأخبار ما يدل على علو شأنها»
#48 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · “Zulaf” is the plural of “zulfa,” whose Arabic broken plural denotes at least three; with two parts of the day, this indicates five prayers, including three during the night’s approaches to day. «“Zülef” “zülfe”nin cem‘i ve Arabîde cem‘in ekalli üç olduğu için bununla ikisi gündüz tarafında, üçü de geceden olmak üzere tam beş vakit namaz emredilmiş olduğu zâhirdir»
#49 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Each prayer is a good deed that continually erases prior minor sins; the five daily prayers expiate sins between them when major sins are avoided, as a hadith states. «beş vakit namaz arada vâki‘olan küçük günahlara keffaret olur»
#50 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · A hadith says one prayer expiates what lies between it and the next, provided major sins are avoided. «namaz, namaza kadar aralarındakine keffarettir, kebâirden ictinab ettikçe»
#51 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Because prayer restrains indecency and evil, regular prayer cultivates aversion to sins, avoidance of major sins, and remorse and repentance for any major sin already committed. «namaza devam edildikçe ale’l-umûm seyyiâta karşı hiss-i nefret uyanır»
#52 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · “That” may refer to the prayer or to the address from the command to be steadfast through this passage; either way, it is a reminder for people who heed. «O namaz yahut فَٱسْتَقِمْ emrinden buraya kadar olan hitâbe»
#53 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The positive commands are addressed grammatically to the Prophet but apply to everyone, while prohibitions address the community; this rhetorical shift implies that transgression and inclination to oppressors are inconceivable for the Prophet but possible among his community. «zâhiren müfred olarak Resûlullah’a hitab edilmiş, hâlbuki mâna itibariyle emir, umûma yapılmıştır» [filed under 11:112, 11:113, 11:114]
#54 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · On the reading that dawn counts among night prayers, the two day parts are noon and afternoon, while the three night-adjacent times are sunset, evening, and dawn; the author links this arrangement to 17:78. «“tarafeyi’n-nehâr”dan murad öğle ve ikindi, “zülefen mine’lleyl” de akşam, yatsı, sabah»
#55 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Noon and afternoon can be called the day’s two parts because dawn is its root, sunrise to noon its body, and noon through afternoon its prominent later divisions; another verse also speaks of the day’s parts. «Sabah gündüzün kökü, Güneş’in tulû‘undan zevâline kadar da gövdesi, zevalden sonra öğle ve ikindi de gurûbuna kadar tehavvülâtının bariz hadleri»
#56 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · Most scholars instead take the phrase as dawn and afternoon, or morning and sunset, supported by the wording “before sunrise and before sunset”; this leaves noon, which other verses state explicitly, unmentioned here. «sabah ve ikindi olması lâzım geleceğine kāil olmuşlardır»
#57 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · Al-Zamakhsharī and many others read one day part as before noon and the other as after it, yielding dawn, noon, and afternoon within the two parts and incorporating dawn as one of the day’s parts. «Sâhib-i Keşşâf gibi birçokları da bir tarafı zevalden evveli, bir tarafı da zevalden sonrası yani gudüv ve aşiy mânasına hamletmişlerdir»
#58 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · On that interpretation, the three night parts imply six obligatory prayers: the Prophet’s additional obligatory tahajjud and the community’s witr; other plural wording also suggests at least six. «farz namazların beş değil altı olması iktizâ eder»
#59 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Five prayers are certain through several proofs, while a sixth is only probable; therefore witr is a practical obligation (wājib), not an obligation of belief. «farz-ı i‘tikādî değil farz-ı amelî»
#60 ELMALILI d.1361 · prefers · Elmalılı prefers taking the two day parts as two of the customary three divisions, noon and afternoon, because the opposing “night approaches” clarify the phrase; with sunset, evening, and dawn, this gives the five daily prayers. «gündüzün iki ucu veya ortasının iki yanı demek olmayıp örfte başlıca üç sayılan etrâf-ı nehârdan ikisi demek olması bizce»
#61 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · These five prayers are established and performed with congregation; the call announcing the prayer is sunnah and congregational prayer is wājib. «tam beş vakit namazdır ki ikāmet ve cemaatle kılınır. İkāmet sünnet, cemaat vâcibdir»
#62 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The statement that good deeds remove sins explains the command to establish prayer; in context, “indeed” emphasizes the claim and signals its causal force. «مسوقة مساق التعليل للأمر بإقامة الصلوات»
#63 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Removing sins can mean good habits make sin easier to avoid, as prayer restrains indecency, or that good deeds erase guilt after a sin; both apply broadly. «وإذهاب السيئات يشمل إذهاب وقوعها بأن يصير انسياق النفس إلى ترك السيئات سهلا»
#64 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · prefers · Here “sins” means minor sins, limited by verses that exempt minor lapses and promise their erasure when major sins are avoided. «ومحمل السيئات هنا على السيئات الصغائر التي هي من اللمم»
#65 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · Bukhari reports Ibn Masud's account of a man who illicitly kissed a woman and asked the Prophet about it; the verse was revealed, and the Prophet said it applies to anyone in his community who does such a deed. «عبد الله بن مسعود رضي الله عنه : أن رجلا أصاب»
#66 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · Tirmidhi reports Ibn Masud's account of a man who confessed intimate contact short of intercourse; the Prophet later recited the verse and said it applies to everyone, not only that man. «عن ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه قال : جاء رجل»
#67 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · الترمذي · reports · Al-Tirmidhi grades the Ibn Masud report hasan sahih; he also gives two reports, from Muadh and Abu al-Yusr, and calls those weak. «قال الترمذي : هذا حديث حسن صحيح»
#68 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas and Qatada used the report's “it was revealed to him” wording to argue that this verse, unlike the rest of the surah, is Medinan. «حمل ابن عباس وقتادة على القول بأن هذه الآية مدنية دون بقية هذه السورة»
#69 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · If that wording is literal, the verse was inserted here because it fits the preceding command to be upright and the following command to be patient. «هذه الآية ألحقت بهذه السورة في هذا المكان»
#70 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Those who regard the whole surah as Meccan take the verse as a general command to pray, recited by the Prophet to a penitent; “revealed” can mean applying its general rule to his case. «وأما الذين رجحوا أن السورة كلها مكية»
#71 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · In another Tirmidhi transmission through Alqama and al-Aswad, Ibn Masud says the Prophet recited the verse, without saying it was newly revealed. «عن علقمة والأسود عن ابن مسعود»
