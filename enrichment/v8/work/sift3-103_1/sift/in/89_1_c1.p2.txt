#104 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Alternatively, the oaths describe the great resurrection: dawn is divine light appearing in the soul’s night; ten are dormant senses during self-disclosure; the pair is witness and witnessed before complete annihilation; the odd is the oneness after duality ends; night is selfhood disappearing. «الفجر الذي هو مبتدأ طلوع نور الحق وتأثيره في ليلة النفس وليال عشر من الحواس الراكدة الهادئة المظلمة» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#105 QASHANI d.736 · holds · A second alternative is the lesser resurrection of death: dawn is the sun rising from the west, the ten are dimmed senses, the pair spirit and body, the odd the separated spirit, and night the body’s darkness dispersing. «أي: فجر ابتداء ظهور نور الشمس الطالعة من مغربها» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#106 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الجمهور · reports · Most authorities classify the surah as Meccan; ʿAli ibn Abi Talha says it is Medinan. «هذه السورة مكية في قول الجمهور. وقال علي بن أبي طلحة: مدنية» [filed under 89:1-30]
#107 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The surah follows the prior account of humbled and flourishing faces by describing arrogant disbelieving peoples and the contented soul; it answers turning away and disbelief with a threat that God is watching. «ذكر الطوائف المتكبرين المكذبين المتجبرين الذين وجوههم خاشعة» [filed under 89:1-30]
#108 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · أبو الدينار الأعرابي · reports · Abu al-Dinar al-Aʿrabi adds tanwin to al-fajr, al-watr, and yasr; Ibn Khalawayh compares the usage with Arabs' pausal tanwin at rhyme endings, even on verbs or definite forms. «وقرأ أبو الدينار الأعرابي: والفجر، والوتر، ويسر بالتنوين في الثلاثة» [filed under 89:1, 89:4]
#109 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · A poem illustrates tanwin-like endings in open rhymes; grammarians discuss this in unrestricted rhymes and in ordinary pausal speech. «وهذا ذكره النحويون في القوافي المطلقة إذا لم يترنم الشاعر»
#110 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · prefers · Al-Fajr most naturally means dawn in general, not one specified day; other reports identify it with the Day of Sacrifice, Friday, Dhul-Hijjah, the night at Jamʿ, or the first day of Muharram. «أقسم به كما أقسم بالصبح»
#111 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbas also explains al-fajr as the whole day, the dawn prayer and its recitation, or the dawn of springs in rock. «وعن ابن عباس أيضا: الفجر: النهار كله»
#112 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الزمخشري وابن الأنباري · reports · The oath's answer is omitted: al-Zamakhshari supplies “We shall punish,” and Ibn al-Anbari supplies “Your Lord is watching.” «قال الزمخشري: وهو لنعذبن، يدل عليه قوله: ألم تر إلى قوله» [filed under 89:1-5]
#113 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · prefers · Abu Hayyan prefers an omitted answer inferred from the end of the prior surah: people return to God and their reckoning is His responsibility. «والذي يظهر أن الجواب محذوف يدل عليه ما قبله من آخر سورة الغاشية» [filed under 89:1-5]
#114 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن عباس · reports · The Jābir report interprets dawn as morning and the ten nights as the first ten of Dhū al-Ḥijja, also Ibn ʿAbbās’s and ʿIkrima’s view, preferred by al-Naḥḥās. «وفي هذا الحديث تفسيره عليه الصلاة والسلام الفجر بالصبح والليالي العشر بعشر النحر» [filed under 89:1-3]
#115 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · ابن الأنباري · reports · Ibn al-Anbari identifies ‘inna rabbaka la-bil-mirsad’ as the stated answer to the oath by dawn. «جواب هذا القسم قيل: مذكور وهو قوله إن ربك لبالمرصاد [الفجر: 14] قاله ابن الأنباري.»
#116 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · reports · Alternatively, the oath’s answer is omitted but understood as a promise to requite each person, indicated by the enumeration of what happened to earlier peoples. «وقيل: محذوف لدلالة المعنى عليه، أي: لنجازين أحد بما عمل»
#117 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhshari supplies ‘He will surely punish’, supported by the account of former peoples being struck down. «وقدر الزمخشري: " ليعذبن " قال: " يدل عليه ألم تر»
#118 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · الشيخ · reports · The Shaykh infers the omitted answer from the preceding sura’s ending: people return to God, who holds them to account. «وقدره الشيخ بما دلت عليه خاتمة السورة قبله، لإيابهم إلينا وحسابهم علينا»
#119 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · reports · An omitted noun may be supplied, making the oath by the dawn prayer or by the Lord of dawn. «وقيل: ثم مضاف محذوف، أي: وصلاة الفجر أو ورب الفجر»
#120 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · أبو الدينار الأعرابي · reports · Most readers do not nunate ‘al-fajr’, ‘al-watr’, and ‘yasr’; Abu al-Dinar al-A‘rabi nunates all three, treating verse endings like poetic rhymes. «والعامة على عدم التنوين في " الفجر " و " الوتر " و " يسر ". وأبو الدينار الأعرابي بتنوين الثلاثة» [filed under 89:1, 89:3, 89:4]
#121 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · ابن خالويه · reports · Ibn Khalawayh calls the poetic nunation ‘tanwin al-tarannum’, including nunation at verse endings even for verbs and definite nouns; his quoted couplet illustrates it. «قال ابن خالويه: " هذا ما روي عن بعض العرب أنه يقف على أواخر القوافي بالتنوين» [filed under 89:1, 89:3, 89:4]
#122 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · Some grammarians object to the label ‘tanwin al-tarannum’ and prefer ‘tanwin tark al-tarannum’; another type, ‘tanwin al-ghali’, appears on a closed rhyme and is disputed, with different rules from ordinary nunation. «وقد عاب بعضهم قول النحويين " تنوين الترنم " وقال: بل ينبغي أن يسموه بتنوين ترك الترنم» [filed under 89:1, 89:3, 89:4]
#123 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · The reader has treated the Qur’anic endings like rhyme endings; the author cites similar forms in al-Ahzab and al-Ra‘d as parallels. «والحاصل أن هذا القارىء أجرى الفواصل مجرى القوافي ففعل فيها ما يفعل فيها» [filed under 89:1, 89:3, 89:4]
#124 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · علي وابن عباس ومجاهد وعكرمة والسدي · reports · They say al-fajr is the morning dawn. «أما الفجر فمعروف، وهو: الصبح. قاله علي، وابن عباس، ومجاهد، وعكرمة، والسدي.»
#125 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · مسروق ومجاهد ومحمد بن كعب · reports · They restrict the dawn to the dawn of the Day of Sacrifice, the close of the ten nights. «وعن مسروق، ومجاهد، ومحمد بن كعب: المراد به فجر يوم النحر خاصة، وهو خاتمة الليالي العشر.»
#126 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · عكرمة · reports · ʿIkrima says the oath refers to the prayer performed at dawn. «وقيل: المراد بذلك الصلاة التي تفعل عنده، كما قاله عكرمة.»
#127 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · ابن عباس · reports · One report from Ibn ʿAbbās says dawn refers to the whole day. «وقيل: المراد به جميع النهار. وهو رواية عن ابن عباس.»
#128 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · After a man left Muadh's overly long prayer and Muadh called him a hypocrite, the Prophet warned Muadh against driving people away and recommended several short surahs, including al-Fajr. «فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "أفتان يا معاذ؟ أين أنت من {سبح اسم ربك الأعلى} {والشمس وضحاها} {والفجر}»
#129 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The conclusion of al-Ghashiya announces return and reckoning; renewed night and day, and Hajj rites performed in pilgrim clothing with talbiya and travel to sacred places, recall resurrection. «لما ختمت تلك بأنه لا بد من الإياب والحساب»
#130 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The oath’s Fajr is the dawn of the Sacrifice day, when pilgrims enter the sanctuary, leave consecration, and receive God’s hospitality; a new day follows one that has passed without a change in essence. «وهو فجر يوم النحر الذي هو أول الأيام الآخذة في الإياب إلى بيت الله الحرام»
#131 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Surat al-Fajr argues for return and reckoning, the ending of al-Ghashiya; dawn breaks after the prior day and sleepers rise from the lesser death of sleep to seek livelihood, then face reward or punishment. «مقصودها الاستدلال على آخر الغاشية الإياب والحساب»
#132 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The basmala portrays God as gathering people after their disintegration, granting explanation that prepares whom He wills for faith, and reserving contentment and gardens for His allies. «جامع العباد بعد تمزيقهم بما له من العظمة»
#133 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The Fajr sura's purpose is to support the return and reckoning mentioned at the end of Ghashiya: dawn's emergence resembles sleepers returning from death-like sleep to daytime work for reward or punishment. «مقصودها الاستدلال على آخر الغاشية الإياب والحساب» [filed under 88:26, 89:1]
#134 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The basmala depicts God gathering servants after dispersal through divine majesty, giving them clarifying guidance, and granting the chosen their gardens through mercy. «جامع العباد بعد تمزيقهم بما له من العظمة»
#135 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas and Ibn al-Zubayr say Surat al-Fajr was revealed in Mecca; the section also classifies the surah as Meccan and thirty verses. «عن ابن عباس قال: نزلت»
#136 DURR-FULL d.911 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · The Prophet says the best fasting after Ramadan is in God's month of Muharram, and the best prayer after the obligatory prayer is night prayer. «عن أبي هريرة قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: أفضل الصيام»
#137 DURR-FULL d.911 · علي بن أبي طالب · reports · Ali tells a man asking which month to fast after Ramadan to fast Muharram, God's month, which includes the day God accepted one people's repentance and forgave others. «عن النعمان قال: أتى عليا»
#138 DURR-FULL d.911 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · The Prophet rebuked Muadh for overlong prayer and directed him to recite shorter chapters, including the chapters beginning with glorification, the sun, dawn, and night. «عن جابر قال: أفتان يا» [filed under 89:1, 87:1, 91:1, 92:1]
#139 DURR-FULL d.911 · عبد الله بن الزبير · reports · Ibn al-Zubayr says God swears by the dawn. «عن عبد الله بن الزبير في قوله»
#140 DURR-FULL d.911 · ميمون بن مهران · reports · Maymun ibn Mihran says God may swear by any of His creation, while no one may swear except by God. «عن ميمون بن مهران قال:»
#141 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas interprets the dawn as daybreak. «عن ابن عباس في قوله»
