#88 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Thinking they had slept a day or part of one reflects a sleeper's inability to measure time; alternatively, they entered in the morning and awoke at noon, then reconsidered after seeing their hair and nails. «بناء على غالب ظنهم لأن النائم لا يحصي مدة نومه»
#89 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · reports · The line saying their Lord knows best may be one party correcting the others, or an objection by the others to their estimate. «ويجوز أن يكون ذلك قول بعضهم وهذا إنكار الآخرين عليهم»
#90 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Their silver waraqa may be coined or uncoined; variant readings lighten, double, or assimilate its consonants, and taking food shows that trusting God still permits provision. «والورق الفضة مضروبة كانت أو غير مضروبة»
#91 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The city is Tarsus; azka food means lawful and tasty, or more abundant and cheaper. «أحل وأطيب أو أكثر وأرخص»
#92 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The messenger should deal gently to avoid being cheated or act discreetly to avoid recognition, and must do nothing that alerts anyone. «وليتكلف اللطف في المعاملة حتى لا يغبن»
#93 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · As God made them sleep, He awakened them too, displaying His power over both sleep and resurrection. «وكما أنمناهم تلك النومة كذلك أيقظناهم اظهارا للقدرة على الانامة والبعث»
#94 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · Some scholars, longing for the Sacred House, said travel requires only packed provisions and trust in the Merciful. «وعن بعض العلماء أنه كان شديد الحنين إلى بيت الله ويقول ما لهذا السفر إلا»
#95 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · In “which of them,” the أهلها is elided, as in “ask the town”; azkā can mean more lawful, wholesome, plentiful, or cheaper. «أي أهلها فحذف كما في واسئل القرية»
#96 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · Ṭaʿāman is a specification (tamyīz), and the envoy should obtain provisions from the food he finds. «﴿ أزكى ﴾ أحل وأطيب وأكثر وأرخص»
#97 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · He should act gently in the purchase so he is not cheated, or remain discreet so no one recognizes him. «وليتكلف اللطف فيما يباشره من أمر المبايعة حتى لا يغبن أو في أمر التخفي»
#98 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The envoy must not cause anyone to discover them, even unintentionally; the source calls him the cause of their being noticed. «ولا يفعلن ما يؤدي إلى الشعر بنا من غير قصد منه فسمى ذلك اشعارا منه بهم»
#99 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · Their questioning helps them learn what happened, reflect on God's power, increase certainty, and give thanks for His favor. «ليسأل بعضهم بعضا ويتعرفوا حالهم وما صنع الله بهم»
#100 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The questioner was their leader, who asked how long they had stayed. «﴿ قال قائل منهم ﴾ رئيسهم ﴿ كم لبثتم ﴾»
#101 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · Their answer of a day or part of one rests on predominant conjecture and shows that reasoning and acting on such conjecture are permissible. «جواب مبني على غالب الظن وفيه دليل على جواز الاجتهاد والقول بالظن الغالب»
#102 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · Some answered that God knew best after signs or inspiration showed them the stay had been long and its exact duration was known only to Him. «انكار عليهم من بعضهم كأنهم قد علموا بالأدلة أو بالهام أن المدة متطاولة»
#103 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · A report says they entered in the morning and woke after noon, then judged from their long nails and hair that the sleep had lasted a long time. «روى أنهم دخلوا الكهف غدوة وكان انتباههم بعد الزوال»
#104 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · ابن عباس · prefers · Ibn ʿAbbās inferred that the sleepers were seven from the singular questioner, the plural answer, and a further plural reply. «وقد استدل ابن عباس رضي الله عنه على أن الصحيح أن عددهم سبعة»
#105 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · One of them was sent, namely Tamlīkha; wariq is silver, whether coined or uncoined, and several readers pronounce its rāʾ still. «كأنهم قالوا ربكم أعلم بذلك لا طريق لكم إلى علمه»
#106 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The city is Tarsus; taking provisions for travel is proper for those who trust God, unlike relying on chance or empty claims of reliance. «هي طرسوس وحملهم الورق عند فرارهم دليل على أن حمل النفقة وما يصلح للمسافر»
#107 QASHANI d.736 · holds · The purest food means the city's best knowledge, free of excess, disputation, dialectic, grammar, and empty appearances that cannot strengthen the soul; knowledge nourishes the heart. «أطيب وأفضل علما وأنقى من الفضول واللغو والظواهر»
#108 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Be discerning and choose a verified pure-hearted, upright, complete guide, not a superficial pretender who claims to teach what he lacks; companionship yields insight. «المحقق الزكي النفس، الرشيد السمت، الفاضل السيرة، النقي السريرة»
#109 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Alternatively, act discreetly so ignorant people do not learn their situation or faith; if the youths symbolize spiritual faculties, the envoy is thought, city their gathered faculties, and the best food intellect rather than illusion, imagination, or sensation. «من أهل الظاهر المحجوبين وسكان عالم الطبيعة المنكرين»
#110 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Their raising is genuine spiritual awakening: they discuss latent meanings and realities of their capacities to actualize them; Sufis call this first awakening wakefulness. «ليتباحثوا بينهم عن المعاني المودعة في استعدادهم»
#111 QASHANI d.736 · holds · The question of their duration repeats an earlier interpretation; the correct group leaves it to God, then sends one with silver to the city for insight and completion. «{ قالوا ربكم أعلم بما لبثتم فابعثوا أحدكم بورقكم هذه إلى المدينة } هذا هو زمان»
#112 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Silver signifies innate first principles that yield truths and divine knowledge; the city is a place of gathering or the city of knowledge, whose gate is ʿAlī. «ما معهم من العلوم الأولية التي لا تحتاج إلى كسب»
#113 QASHANI d.736 · عليه السلام · reports · A prophetic saying calls himself the city of knowledge and ʿAlī its gate. «قوله عليه السلام: " أنا مدينة العلم وعلي بابها "»
#114 QASHANI d.736 · holds · One envoy suffices because full perfection does not depend on everyone learning; the highest knowledge of one person can teach and awaken the rest. «لأن كمال الكل غير موقوف على التعليم والتعلم»
#115 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Warq means silver, whether minted or unminted. «الورق الفضة مضروبة وغير مضروبة»
#116 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · prefers · The two groups most likely are the youths themselves, as their later exchange shows; those saying the Lord knows best had realized the long duration, and the Quran first summarizes then retells the story at length. «والظاهر أن الحزبين هما منهم لقوله تعالى وكذلك بعثناهم ليتسائلوا بينهم» [filed under 18:12, 18:19]
#117 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الزجاج والزمخشري · reports · Al-Zajjāj and al-Zamakhsharī take the comparison in وكذلك to refer to God’s making the youths sleep: as He made them sleep, He raised them as a reminder of His power to cause death and resurrection, leading them to reflect, gain certainty, and give thanks. «وناسب هذا التشبيه قوله تعالى حين أورد قصتهم أولا مختصرة»
#118 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · أبو عمرو وحمزة وأبو بكر والحسن والأعمش واليزيدي ويعقوب وخلف وأبو عبيد وابن سعدان · reports · This group reads بورقكم with a still rāʾ; the other Seven and Zayd b. ʿAlī read its kasra. «ويعقوب في رواية، وخلف وأبو عبيد وابن سعدان بورقكم بإسكان الراء»
#119 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · أبو رجاء · reports · Abū Rajāʾ reads with kasra on wāw, a still rāʾ, and assimilation of qāf into kāf; Ibn Muḥayṣin is also reported with this assimilation, once with kasra rāʾ. «وقرأ أبو رجاء بكسر الواو وإسكان الراء وإدغام القاف في الكاف»
#120 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhsharī attributes kasra rāʾ with qāf-kāf assimilation to Ibn Kathīr, but Abū Ḥayyān says this conflicts with the transmitters; al-Zajjāj reports kasra wāw and still rāʾ without assimilation. «وقال الزمخشري: وقرأ ابن كثير بورقكم بكسر الراء وإدغام القاف في الكاف»
#121 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The comparison echoes the earlier compressed account: God first made them sleep for counted years, then raised them. «وناسب هذا التشبيه قوله تعالى حين أورد قصتهم أولا مختصرة فضربنا على آذانهم»
#122 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن عطية · reports · Ibn ʿAṭiyya says كذلك refers broadly to what God did for them, while the lām in ليتساءلوا marks an outcome rather than the purpose of their awakening. «وقال ابن عطية: الإشارة بذلك إلى الأمر الذي ذكره الله في جهتهم»
#123 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · The questioner may have been their leader Miksalmina or the companion carrying their money, Tamlikha; كم asks how many days they had slept. «قيل: كبيرهم مكسلمينا. وقيل: صاحب نفقتهم تمليخا وكم سؤال عن العدد»
#124 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The respondents appear genuinely uncertain; أو may distribute the answers between them, one saying a day and another part of a day. «والظاهر صدور الشك من المسئولين. وقيل: أو للتفصيل»
#125 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · One explanation says they slept at the day’s beginning and awoke near its end; their mistaken estimate follows their strongest impression and is not a lie, after which they defer the duration to God. «قيل: ناموا أول النهار واستيقظوا آخر النهار، وجوابهم هذا مبني على غلبة الظن»
#126 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhsharī says some youths corrected the others: evidence or divine inspiration had shown that the sleep lasted a long, indeterminate period known only to God. «إنكار عليهم من بعضهم وأن الله تعالى أعلم بمدة لبثهم»
#127 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Their hunger after waking explains the shift to sending someone for food: they leave the duration question to God and attend to their immediate need. «ولما انتبهوا من نومهم أخذهم ما يأخذ من نام طويلا من الحاجة إلى الطعام»
#128 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · Tamlikha was reportedly sent; the youths had brought coins for their expenses while fleeing, explaining their reference to these coins. «والمبعوث قيل هو تمليخا، وكانوا قد استصحبوا حين خرجوا فارين دراهم»
