#64 STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Another account says idolaters declared that Muhammad's Lord had forsaken and despised him. «Others say that it was in response to several idolaters saying, “Surely Muhammad’s lord has forsaken him and despised him”» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#65 STUDYQURAN · reports · A further account says illness kept the Prophet from night vigil for two or three nights; Umm Jamīl, Abū Lahab's wife, taunted him, and vv. 1–3 were revealed. «the Prophet became sick and could not offer his supererogatory night vigil» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#66 STUDYQURAN · holds · The sūrah consoles the Prophet: it assures him God will not abandon him, recalls God's favors, and ends by urging care for the downtrodden and preaching the message. «The main theme of the sūrah is consolation of the Prophet. It begins with an assertion that God would not abandon the Prophet» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3, 93:6, 93:7, 93:8, 93:9, 93:10, 93:11]
#67 STUDYQURAN · reports · Some say Sūrahs 93 and 94 were originally one, since Companions recited them together in one prayer cycle without a basmalah separating them. «the Companions of the Prophet would recite them together in a single prayer cycle» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3, 94:1, 94:2, 94:3, 94:4, 94:5, 94:6, 94:7, 94:8]
#68 STUDYQURAN · holds · Both sūrahs present God's bounties as relief: Sūrah 93 addresses external needs, Sūrah 94 spiritual relief; 94 recalls blessings, reassures, then commands exertion and turning to God. «Thematically both sūrahs speak of the bounties that God has provided the Prophet Muhammad as relief for the difficulties that beset him» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3, 93:6, 93:7, 93:8, 93:9, 93:10, 93:11, 94:1, 94:2, 94:3, 94:4, 94:5, 94:6, 94:7, 94:8]
