# 48 notes filed under 12:35 (group 2 of 2 of this verse's notes)
verse 12:35: ثُمَّ بَدَا لَهُم مِّنۢ بَعْدِ مَا رَأَوُا۟ ٱلْءَايَٰتِ لَيَسْجُنُنَّهُۥ حَتَّىٰ حِينٍۢ

#1 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Some say the women’s cuts show Joseph’s beauty made them the likely initiators; others count his refusal of them as evidence of innocence because the ruler and advisers heard it. Ikrima reports Ibn ʿAbbās also named marks on Joseph’s body and a knife mark. «وجه بعضهم عد القطع من الشواهد بأن حسنه عليه الصلاة والسلام الفاتن للنساء»
#2 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Ikrima asked Ibn ʿAbbās about the signs; he listed the shirt tear, marks on Joseph’s body, and a knife mark. Other signs may have been omitted, as prophetic miracles often are. «سألت ابن عباس رضي الله تعالى عنهما عن الآيات»
#3 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The subject of badā may be the verbal noun ‘decision,’ an opinion, or imprisonment understood from ‘they will imprison him’; the oath and response may be an object of implied saying or an explanatory clause. «وفاعل ( بدا ) ضمير يعود إما للبداء مصدر الفعل المذكور أو بمعنى الرأي»
#4 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · One view makes ‘they will surely imprison him’ the response to badā; another makes the full proposition its subject, as in 36:31 and 14:45, though most grammarians reject this. «وقيل : إن جملة ( ليسجننه ) جواب لبدا لأنه من أفعال القلوب»
#5 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · المازني · reports · Al-Māzinī selects the first subject analysis; Abū Ḥayyān selects the final one, where a pronoun refers to imprisonment understood from the sentence. «واختار المازني في الفاعل الوجه الأول»
#6 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو حيان · reports · Abū Ḥayyān selects the final analysis of the subject of badā. «واختار أبو حيان الوجه الأخير»
#7 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The decision to imprison Joseph arose when his wife persuaded her husband, who yielded to her and entrusted his authority to her; a report says she framed Joseph as the offender and demanded his imprisonment. «وكان ذلك البداء بإستنزال المرأة لزوجها ومطاوعته لها وحبه إياها»
#8 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الحسن · reports · Al-Ḥasan reads latasjunannahu as a direct address to the ruler or the ruler and advisers. «وقرأ الحسن لتسجننه على صيغة الخطاب بأن خاطب بعضهم العزيز ومن يليه»
#9 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās says ‘until a time’ means until city talk and rumors die down; the ruler’s apparent aim was temporary, while the wife wanted prison to humiliate Joseph and make people think him guilty. «قال ابن عباس : إلى إنقطاع المقال وما شاع في المدينة من الفاحشة»
#10 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · prefers · Reports give five, seven, or twelve years; al-Ālūsī avoids fixing a number and says only that the imprisonment was long. For most, ḥīn is an indefinite period, short or long. «وقال مقاتل : إنه عليه السلام حبس إثنتي عشرة سنة»
#11 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masʿūd reportedly read ʿattā with ʿayn, a Hudhayl dialect; ʿUmar wrote directing him to recite Quraysh’s ḥattā. «وقرأ ابن مسعود عتى بإبدال حاء ( حتى ) عينا وهي لغة هذيل»
#12 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Then marks a surprising later turn: after Joseph's innocence became clear, the authorities decided to imprison him. «كما هو شأنها في عطف الجمل فإن ما بدا لهم»
#13 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The chief wife feared gossip after the women dispersed, so she tried to cover Joseph's innocence by imprisoning him, making him appear guilty and perhaps returning him to her control. «فرامت أن تغطي ذلك بسجن يوسف عليه السلام حتى يظهر في صورة المجرمين»
#14 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · “Them” refers to the chief wife's group of advisers and commanders. «لجماعة العزيز من مشير وآمر»
#15 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The sentence with the oath lām is detached from “it appeared”; the omitted object can be inferred, showing suspension is not limited to verbs of thought, as Yunus ibn Habib held. «جواب قسم محذوف ، وهي معلقة فعل»
#16 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · يونس بن حبيب · reports · Yunus ibn Habib held that suspension is caused by an element with sentence-initial force and need not be limited to verbs of thought. «مذهب يونس بن حبيب ، لأن سبب التعليق»
#17 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Grammarians differed over whether a clause may serve as a subject: Hisham and Tha'lab allowed it generally, while al-Farra and others allowed it with a cognitive verb and suspension; this verse is cited as evidence, though suspension fits better. «فأجازه هشام وثعلب مطلقا ، وأجازه الفراء»
#18 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · “Until a time” is indefinite; if spoken by the authorities, the term was open-ended, while if Qur'anic narration, its length is omitted because it is irrelevant to the story. «فإن كان { حتى حين } من كلامهم كان المعنى»
#19 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The signs were proofs of Joseph's truth and the chief wife's falsehood. «دلائل صدق يوسف عليه السلام وكذب امرأة العزيز»
#20 TABATABAI d.1402 · علي بن الحسين · reports · A report from Imam al-Sajjād recounts Joseph’s resistance to the wife, the child’s shirt test, the women’s gathering and private approaches, Joseph’s plea for protection, and his eventual imprisonment. «وكان يوسف من أجمل أهل زمانه فلما راهق يوسف راودته امرأة الملك عن نفسه» [filed under 12:33, 12:35]
#21 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · Some took the response as granting a prayer for imprisonment, but the author rejects this: the later wording says only then did they decide to imprison him. «ولو كان دعاء بالسجن واستجابه الله سبحانه وقدر له السجن» [filed under 12:34, 12:35, 12:33]
#22 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The imprisonment begins a period of several years that brings Joseph close to the king and to Egypt’s authority; in prison he calls people to monotheism and first identifies his Abrahamic family. «وهو دخوله السجن ومكثه فيه بضع سنين وهو مقدمة تقربه التام عند الملك» [filed under 12:35, 12:22, 12:33]
#23 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Badā means that a new opinion appeared after it had not existed; ‘they’ refers to the master, his wife, and their circle of advisers and attendants. «البداء هو ظهور رأى بعد ما لم يكن يقال؟ بدا لي في أمر كذا»
#24 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The signs of Joseph’s innocence include the child’s testimony, his shirt torn from behind, the pair racing to the door, and perhaps the women’s amazement and the wife’s admission. «الشواهد والأدلة الدالة على براءة يوسف عليه السلام وطهارة ذيله» [filed under 12:35, 12:26, 12:28, 12:31, 12:32]
#25 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The lām is an oath of firm resolve; ‘until a time’ means until the public story of the seduction subsides and people forget it. «اللام فيه للقسم أي أقسموا وعزموا ليسجننه البتة»
#26 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · One possible motive was protecting the master’s household from shame and preventing public disorder or women’s infatuation with Joseph’s extraordinary beauty. «إنما عزموا على ذلك لمصلحة بيت العزيز وصونا لأسرته عن هوأن التهمة والعار»
#27 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · Later evidence shows the wife may have misled her husband into doubting Joseph’s innocence; she may have arranged or ordered prison both to protect herself and to punish him into obedience. «فقد كان سجنه بتوسل أو بأمر منها لتدفع بذلك تهمة الناس عن نفسها» [filed under 12:35, 12:32, 12:50, 12:51, 12:52]
#28 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام · reports · In the al-Qummi report through Abu al-Jarud, the signs preceding Joseph’s imprisonment were the child’s testimony, the shirt torn from behind, and the pair racing to the door while struggling. «في تفسير القمي في رواية أبي الجارود عن أبي جعفر عليه السلام»
#29 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · After the king had a troubling dream that others could not interpret, the cupbearer remembered Joseph and sought his interpretation. «وعند ذلك ادكر الساقي يوسف وتعبيره لمنامه» [filed under 12:30-35]
#30 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The phrase literally says that the idea occurred to them. «it occurred to them»
#31 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Joseph was jailed to appease his master's wife, not because the master believed him guilty; Asad illustrates the husband's weakness through Zamakhshari's simile. «Joseph was imprisoned not because his master believed him to be guilty, but because, in his weakness, he wanted to appease his wife»
#32 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Zamakhshari · reports · Zamakhshari likens the husband to a riding camel whose reins his wife held, illustrating his submission to her. «being entirely submissive to her, and behaving like a riding-camel whose reins she held in her hand" (Zamakhshari)»
#33 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Unlike Genesis' romantic account of Joseph's fortunes, the Qur'anic story illustrates God's unfathomable direction of human affairs: people may hate what is good for them or love what is bad for them. «the Qur'an uses it primarily as an illustration of God's unfathomable direction of men's affairs» [filed under 12:1, 12:2, 12:3, 12:4, 12:5, 12:6, 12:7, 12:8, 12:9, 12:10, 12:11, 12:12, 12:13, 12:14, 12:15, 12:16, 12:17, 12:18, 12:19, 12:20, 12:21, 12:22, 12:23, 12:24, 12:25, 12:26, 12:27, 12:28, 12:29, 12:30, 12:31, 12:32, 12:33, 12:34, 12:35, 12:36, 12:37, 12:38, 12:39, 12:40, 12:41, 12:42, 12:43, 12:44, 12:45, 12:46, 12:47, 12:48, 12:49, 12:50, 12:51, 12:52, 12:53, 12:54, 12:55, 12:56, 12:57, 12:58, 12:59, 12:60, 12:61, 12:62, 12:63, 12:64, 12:65, 12:66, 12:67, 12:68, 12:69, 12:70, 12:71, 12:72, 12:73, 12:74, 12:75, 12:76, 12:77, 12:78, 12:79, 12:80, 12:81, 12:82, 12:83, 12:84, 12:85, 12:86, 12:87, 12:88, 12:89, 12:90, 12:91, 12:92, 12:93, 12:94, 12:95, 12:96, 12:97, 12:98, 12:99, 12:100, 12:101, 12:102, 12:103, 12:104, 12:105, 12:106, 12:107, 12:108, 12:109, 12:110, 12:111]
#34 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Joseph’s imprisonment begins another trial: his powerful hosts knew the reality but judged a short prison term would silence gossip and lessen Zelicha’s infatuation by keeping him away. «they deemed it appropriate to send him to jail for some time. They must have thought that in this way people would stop gossiping about the whole issue»
