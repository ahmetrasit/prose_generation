#141 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · God woke them so two groups would debate how long they slept; their guesses proved unreliable, foreshadowing how people in Barzakh will perceive their span as brief. «they become divided into two groups and argue with each other about the period they had slept» [filed under 18:12, 18:19]
#142 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Just as God protected them, He awakened them so their mutual questioning would expose their inability to know the elapsed time; this expands the earlier summary. «The mutual questions and answers mentioned by the verse are the ones which are rather briefly referred to in verse 13 earlier» [filed under 18:19, 18:12]
#143 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Their mistaken sense of having slept briefly illustrates that after death in Barzakh and upon resurrection people will think their sleep lasted only a short time. «the life of barzakh after death would be quite similar. When people would rise on the Day of Judgement»
#144 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · “Pure food” means halal food: the youths fled a society unconcerned with lawful food and therefore instructed their companion to scrutinize what he bought. «they stressed on the person who was sent to fetch food to specially investigate and only bring back food that was ḥalāl»
#145 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · reports · Islahi allows that People of the Book may have lived in Raqim and been sensitive about food, but careful shopping could expose the youths. «It may well have been that some People of the Book were living in Raqīm»
#146 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Yatalattaf means carrying out a task cautiously, cleverly, and circumspectly. «means to accomlish a task in a very circumspect, clever and apprehensive way»
#147 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Wariq literally means silver and may refer to coins or other forms of money; Islahi uses the broader translation “currency.” «while I have translated it as “currency” because»
#148 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The verse leaves the exact discovery route unspecified; after investigation, people gradually learned the cave-dwellers' origins, possibly through the market trip. «investigating about his origins. Gradually they were able to know them» [filed under 18:21, 18:19]
#149 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Awakening the youths after a long fast without bodily decay exemplifies and proves that God can resurrect people after death. «hiçbir gıda almadıkları halde bedenlerinde herhangi bir bozulma olmadan uzun süre uyuttuktan sonra tekrar uyandırması, O’nun insanları öldükten sonra tekrar diriltebileceğine dair bir misal ve delildir.» [filed under 18:19, 18:20]
#150 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The youths disputed how long they had slept and, unaware of elapsed centuries and changes in the world, thought their persecutors’ threat remained current. «geçen süreyi ve dünyada meydana gelen değişiklikleri bilmedikleri için inkârcıların kendileri hakkındaki tehditlerinin devam ettiğini sanmışlardı.» [filed under 18:19, 18:20]
#151 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · They sent one companion into town for food and warned him to act carefully. «Bu sebeple yiyecek almak üzere şehre gönderdikleri arkadaşlarını dikkatli olması hususunda uyarmışlardı.» [filed under 18:19, 18:20]
#152 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The messenger was exposed by his ancient clothes, coin, and speech, which differed from the people of his time. «Ancak gencin asırlar öncesine ait kıyafeti, elindeki para ve konuşmasındaki farklılık onu ele verdi.» [filed under 18:19, 18:20]
#153 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · rivayetler · reports · Reports say the coin bore King Decius’s image; townspeople took the youth to the ruler, who was a monotheist Christian after Christianity had spread, then went with him to verify the story at the cave. «şehir halkı paranın üzerinde Kral Dakyanus’un (Decius) resmini görmüş ve adamın bir hazine bulduğunu sanarak kendisini devrin hükümdarına götürmüşlerdi.» [filed under 18:19, 18:20]

#158 STUDYQURAN · R, Z · reports · Others say they noticed their long hair and nails before concluding that God knew best how long they had slept. «only upon seeing the length of their hair and nails did they say, Your Lord knows best how long you have tarried (R, Z)»
#159 STUDYQURAN · holds · Their one-day estimate resembles the man awakened after a hundred years and Quranic accounts of people thinking their wait before resurrection was short. «their estimation of having tarried a day is identical to that made by a man who had doubted the resurrection»
#160 STUDYQURAN · holds · The parallel reactions connect the Cave story symbolically with death, resurrection, and the intervening barzakh. «the close symbolic connection of this story to death, resurrection, and the intermediate state, or barzakh, between them»
#161 STUDYQURAN · holds · The chosen youth carried a silver dirham stamped with the oppressive king’s image and was told to buy food discreetly to avoid persecution. «a silver dirham bearing the imprint of the oppressive king of their own time, and to enter the city in order to purchase food; but he was to do this discreetly»
