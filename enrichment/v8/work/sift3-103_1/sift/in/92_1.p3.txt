#115 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The word tajalli means appearing or becoming uncovered; its physical senses include recession of the hairline, polish that clears sight, display of a bride, and polishing a blade or mirror. «والتجلي لغة الظهور والإنكشاف»
#116 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The night/day oath parallels other early Meccan oaths and is especially close in wording to 91:3–4; the brightening day corresponds to ḍuḥā in 91:1 and 93:1. «Insbesondere zu 91:3.4 (wa-n-nahāri ʾiḏā ǧallāhā / wa-l-laili ʾiḏā yaġšāhā) besteht eine enge sprachliche Verwandtschaft» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#117 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Qur'anic oaths probably draw on a characteristic expression of pre-Islamic soothsayers, a connection not yet sufficiently studied. «Zu grundsätzlichen Hinweisen zu den koranischen Schwüren» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#118 CORPUSCORANICUM · reports · For the structure and function of this oath type, the commentary points to 93:1–2 and its parallels. «zu Aufbau und Funktion des hier vorliegenden Schwurtypus s. die Anmerkung zu 93:1.2 mit zahlreichen Parallelstellen» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#119 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Night/day and male/female form two cosmic oppositions; the second oath enigmatically points to God, who speaks explicitly in the first-person plural in verses 7–10. «Der einleitende Schwurabschnitt nennt zwei umfassende kosmische Gegensatzpaare» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#120 CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · Neuwirth reads the two elemental pairs as the structural matrix for differing human aims and their corresponding recompense. «die wiederum gegensätzliche Arten der Vergeltung nach sich ziehen.“ (Neuwirth, „Horizont“, 24)» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3, 92:4]
#121 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Sūrah al-Layl pairs with al-Shams; their central themes and inner and outer features correspond so closely that even a general reader can sense the harmony. «There is no essential difference between the central themes» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3, 92:4]
#122 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-Layl develops the preceding sūrah’s claim that the soul succeeds through purification by identifying what corrupts it and how it can be protected and purified. «the things which contaminate it are explained» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3, 92:4, 91:9, 91:10]
#123 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Verses 1–4 use evidence from the outer and inner worlds to establish that Judgment must come: the Hereafter is this world’s counterpart and makes it meaningful. «The evidence of the world around man and the world within him is presented to show that the Day of Judgement is bound to come.» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3, 92:4]
#124 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Oaths by natural phenomena in the Qur’an are intended to establish a premise. «the various oaths sworn by natural phenomena are meant to substantiate a» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#125 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Night and day, like male and female, are complementary pairs whose members fill one another’s void and together serve their created purpose; without a counterpart, their capacities and uses make no sense. «the male and female species form complementary pairs and together these pairs fulfil the purpose for which they have been created» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#126 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The harmony of opposites argues for monotheism and, here, for the Hereafter; 51:49 calls pairs a reminder that this world has a counterpart completing its purpose. «argument to substantiate the Hereafter» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#127 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · These oaths show the worth of the created things and the greatness of their Creator, while directing attention to the coming subject. «Bu yeminler de üzerine yemin edilen varlıkların değerini, onları yaratan gücün büyüklüğünü göstermekte; ayrıca gelecek konunun önemine dikkat çekmektedir» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#128 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · Using a past verb for a present event makes it vivid and immediate; the author illustrates this with the Prophet's turning toward heaven and the reproach for killing prophets. «حكاية الحال الفعل الماضي تضفي عليه الحركة والحيوية وتجعله كأنه معاصر» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#129 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · Arabic grammarians describe this vivid tense shift as transporting the listener to the event or bringing the event to the listener; past verbs have more temporal uses than the single schoolbook past. «إما ينقلك إلى الحدث أو ينقل الحدث إليك» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#130 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · Contrast makes meanings clearer and more visible; in the opening oath, night covers while day becomes manifest, and the object of يغشى is omitted to keep every possible object in view. «المفعول لفعل يغشى محذوف فلم يقل سبحانه ماذا يغشى الليل» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#131 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · Night's covering unfolds gradually, so يغشى is present tense; daylight appears at once at sunrise, so تجلى is past tense, as in the parallel verbs in al-Shams. «الليل يغشى شيئا فشيئا بالتدريج وهو ليس كالنهار الذي يتجلى دفعة واحدة» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#132 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · The surah's expressive pattern includes generality: it leaves broad references unspecified, including what the night covers, what is avoided, what is given, and what the two outcomes mean. «السورة كلها في العموم من قوله تعالى (والليل إذا يغشى)» [filed under 92:1-10]
#133 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · Sajā brings together stillness and darkness, matching revelation's suspension and loss of light; yaghshā and yasri imply movement and therefore fit less well. «كلمة سجى جمعت المعاني كلها التي تدل على انقطاع الوحي وسكونه» [filed under 93:2, 92:1, 89:4]
#134 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · The omitted object of “enshrouds” may be the world. «The omitted direct object can be seen as an allusion to the world, the earth, the day»
#135 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · The omitted object may be the earth. «The omitted direct object can be seen as an allusion to the world, the earth, the day»
#136 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · The omitted object may be the day. «The omitted direct object can be seen as an allusion to the world, the earth, the day»
#137 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · The omitted object may be all of creation. «The omitted direct object can be seen as an allusion to the world, the earth, the day»
#138 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Layl is early Makkan and often considered ninth in revelation order, though some call it Madinan or partly Madinan; it is named after the opening oath. «often considered the ninth in the chronological order of revelation»
#139 STUDYQURAN · holds · The first three verses echo the prior surah's polarity; the rest stresses diverse human ends and the opposed outcomes of good and evil deeds. «Similar to the first ten verses of the preceding sūrah, the first three verses of this sūrah focus upon the polarity inherent to the created order» [filed under 92:1, 92:21]
