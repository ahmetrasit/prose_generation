#41 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Isḥāq says the men meant that Joseph had done them a kindness by interpreting their dreams. «وقال ابن إسحاق: أرادا إخباره أنهما يريان له إحسانا عليهما ويدا»
#42 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · مجاهد · reports · Mujāhid says the two men truly saw these dreams and wanted to ask Joseph about them. «قال مجاهد: رأيا ذلك حقيقة فأرادا سؤاله»
#43 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن مسعود والشعبي · reports · Ibn Masʿūd and al-Shaʿbī say the prisoners pretended to dream in order to test Joseph. «وقال ابن مسعود والشعبي: استعملاها ليجرباه»
#44 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · The cupbearer, called Bunū, describes three grape clusters whose juice he pressed for the king. «والذي رأى عصر الخمر اسمه بنو قال: رأيت حبلة من كرم لها ثلاثة أغصان»
#45 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · The baker, called Malḥab, describes three baskets of bread carried on his head, from which birds ate. «والذي رأى الخبز اسمه ملحب قال: كنت أرى أن أخرج من مطبخة الملك»
#46 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The dream verb is like verbs of cognition: its subject and object may be pronouns referring to the same person, though ordinary paired pronouns such as اضربني are disallowed. «ورأى الحلمية جرت مجرى أفعال القلوب في جواز كون فاعلها ومفعولها»
#47 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Grapes are called wine in anticipation of what they become; some dialects use khamr for grapes directly. «وسمي العنب خمرا باعتبار ما يؤول إليه. وقيل: الخمر بلغة غسان اسم العنب»
#48 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · المعتمر · reports · Al-Muʿtamir recounts asking a Bedouin carrying grapes what he had; the man answered khamr, meaning grapes. «وقال المعتمر: لقيت أعرابيا يحمل عنبا في وعاء فقلت: ما تحمل؟ قال: خمرا»
#49 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · أبي وعبد الله · reports · Ubayy and ʿAbd Allāh read عنبا, but Abū Ḥayyān treats it as an explanatory gloss, since their transmitted mutawātir reading is خمرا and the form conflicts with the codex text. «وقرأ أبي وعبد الله: أعصر عنبا، وينبغي أن يحمل ذلك على التفسير لمخالفته سواد المصحف»
#50 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · قال أحدهما is an independent clause, not a circumstantial one: they did not say this while entering, and entry cannot become a dream; إني is the object of قال. «قوله تعالى: { قال أحدهما }: مستأنف لا محل له، ولا يجوز أن يكون حالا»
#51 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · reports · Some treat dream أراني like a verb of knowledge taking two objects, with أعصر as the second; others take أعصر as a circumstantial clause. «و " أراني " هنا متعدية لمفعولين عند بعضهم إجراء للحلمية مجرى العلمية»
#52 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · Dream cognition allows subject and object pronouns to refer to the same person, as here; ordinary verbs instead require an explicit reflexive or separate object. «وجرت الحلمية مجرى العلمية أيضا في اتحاد فاعلها ومفعولها ضميرين متصلين»
#53 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · When the causative hamza is added to this dream verb, it takes a third object, as in the cited construction in al-Anfal 8:43. «وإذا دخلت همزة النقل على هذه الحلمية تعدت لثالث، وقد تقدم هذا في قوله تعالى»
#54 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · reports · خمر means grapes metaphorically because grapes become wine; others say it literally meant grapes in Ghassan and Azd Oman speech. «والخمر: العنب أطلق عليه ذلك مجازا، لأنه آيل إليه كما يطلق الشيء على الشيء»
#55 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · المعتمر · reports · Al-Muʿtamir recounts an Arab carrying grapes who called them wine. «وقيل: بل الخمر: العنب حقيقة في لغة غسان وأزد عمان.»
#56 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · أبي وعبد الله · reports · Their explanatory reading أعصر عنبا does not establish synonymy or a recited variant; it is glossing, like Abdullah's explanatory ثريدا. «وعن المعتمر: " لقيت أعرابيا حاملا عنبا في وعاء فقلت: ما تحمل؟ فقال: خمرا.»
#57 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · تأكل الطير describes خبزا; فوق may modify carrying or be a circumstantial phrase from خبزا. «و " تأكل الطير " صفة لخبزا. و " فوق " يجوز أن يكون ظرفا للحمل»
#58 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · الشيخ · reports · Al-Shaykh and al-Zamakhshari take its pronoun in بتأويله as referring to what the two men recounted, like a demonstrative phrase; another view says each means his own dream. «قال الشيخ: " عائد على ما قصا عليه، أجري مجرى اسم الإشارة كأنه قيل بتأويل ذلك "»
#59 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · قتادة · reports · Qatada identifies the two prisoners as the king's cupbearer and baker. «قال قتادة: كان أحدهما ساقي الملك، والآخر خبازه»
#60 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · عبد الله · reports · A report cited by Ibn Jarir says the prisoners had no dreams and only pretended in order to test Yusuf. «عن عبد الله قال: ما رأى صاحبا يوسف شيئا»
#61 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · محمد بن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Ishaq names the wine official Nabwa and the baker Majlath. «قال محمد بن إسحاق: كان اسم الذي على الشراب "نبوا"»
#62 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · السدي · reports · Al-Suddi says the king jailed them because he suspected they had conspired to poison his food and drink. «قال السدي: وكان سبب حبس الملك إياهما أنه توهم»
#63 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · Yusuf was known in prison for generosity, trustworthiness, truthful speech, worship, dream interpretation, kindness to prisoners, visiting the sick, and meeting their needs. «يوسف، عليه السلام، قد اشتهر في السجن بالجود»
#64 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · reports · The two men loved Yusuf; he replied that affection had previously brought him harm through his aunt, father, and the Aziz's wife. «إنه ما أحبني أحد إلا دخل علي من محبته ضرر»
#65 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud recited the cupbearer's dream as squeezing grapes rather than wine; Ibn Abi Hatim transmits this reading from him. «عن ابن مسعود: أنه قرأها: "أعصر عنبا"»
#66 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · الضحاك · reports · Al-Dahhak explains wine here as grapes and says Omanis call grapes wine. «وقال الضحاك في قوله: {إني أراني أعصر خمرا}»
#67 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima gives the cupbearer's dream as planting a grapevine, squeezing its clusters, and serving the king; Yusuf said he would leave prison after three days and serve him wine. «وقال عكرمة: رأيت (3) فيما يرى النائم أني غرست حبلة من عنب»
#68 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · prefers · The majority view is that both prisoners truly dreamed and requested Yusuf's interpretation. «والمشهور عند الأكثرين ما ذكرناه، وأنهما رأيا مناما وطلبا تعبيره»
#69 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · فخر الدين الرازي · reports · Al-Razi says every stage in Joseph's story combined gentleness with hardship, blessing with trial, and eventual relief; harsh coercion can lead to the best outcome with fewer consequences. «قال الإمام فخر الدين الرازي في كتاب اللوامع : وعلى الجملة فكل أحوال يوسف»
#70 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The prison that appeared to humiliate Joseph instead became a source of honor and showed God's control of events while fulfilling his prior promise. «وكان سببا ظاهرا في الإهانة»
#71 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The two young inmates were the king's baker and cupbearer; the baker suspected that the cupbearer had helped him poison the king. «فتيان } : خباز الملك وساقيه , ورفع إليه أن الخباز أراد أن يسمه»
#72 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · أبو حيان · reports · Abu Hayyan says the word مع indicates companionship beginning at that point, so all three entered prison at the same time. «تدل على الصحبة واستحداثها , فهي تدل على دخول الثلاثة السجن في آن واحد - قاله أبو حيانز»
#73 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Joseph cared for fellow prisoners by comforting the grieving, visiting the sick, advocating for the poor, guiding people to good, and reminding them of God; they felt spiritually blessed by his company. «يسلي حزينهم , ويعود مريضهم , ويسأل لفقيرهم»
#74 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The prisoners and guard praised Joseph's face, character, and speech, and valued the reward, expiation, and purity they learned from him; the guard wished he could release him. «ما أحسن وجهك وأحسن خلقك وأحسن حديثك ! لقد بورك لنا في جوارك»
#75 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Joseph asked the two young men not to love him, explaining that love from his aunt, father, and his companion's wife had each preceded a new affliction; they loved him anyway. «فوالله ما أحبني أحد قط إلا دخل علي من جهته بلاء»
#76 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الشعبي · reports · Al-Sha'bi suggests the inmates may have invented the dreams to test Joseph, or perhaps they had seen nothing at all. «كما قال الشعبي - وإنما صنفا هذا ليختبراه به»
#77 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Their first dream describes a past scene: pressing means extracting liquid, and wine here means grapes destined to become wine. «والعصر : الاعتماد على ما فيه مائية ليحتلب منه»
#78 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Carrying is lifting an object with support for transport; bread is prepared food, and birds eating from it completes the second dream. «أي طعاما مهيأ للأكل بالخبز»
#79 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The inmates ask for interpretation, meaning the outcome to which the dream leads; they infer Joseph can interpret because they see him as excellent in all his affairs. «أي ما يرجع أمره ويصير إليه»
#80 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Joseph seizes their receptiveness to give the most important counsel first: sincere worship of God and rejection of associationism, before answering the dream. «وهو ما خلق العباد له من الاجتماع على الله»
#81 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Scholars should give essential counsel when asked a question and may describe their knowledge to help listeners accept it, provided this guides them toward God rather than self-praise. «فعلى كل ذي علم إذا احتاج إلى سؤاله أحد أن يقدم على جوابه نصحه بما هو الأهم له»
#82 DURR-FULL d.911 · يوسف · reports · Joseph says he can interpret their dreams before their next provision arrives, then predicts the cupbearer will return to service and the baker will be crucified and eaten by birds. «لا يأتيكما طعام ترزقانه في النوم إلا نبأتكما بتأويله في اليقظة ثم قال: {يا صاحبي السجن أما أحدكما»
#83 DURR-FULL d.911 · يوسف · reports · The men deny having dreamed anything, but Joseph says the matter is settled and asks the cupbearer to mention him to his master. «ففزعا وقالا: والله ما رأينا شيئا قال يوسف عليه السلام: {قضي الأمر الذي فيه تستفتيان»
