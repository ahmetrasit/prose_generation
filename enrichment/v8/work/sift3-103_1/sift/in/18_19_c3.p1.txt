#37 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Al-Ālūsī says making delicate food a rule impairs spiritual perfection; even if the report about ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Jīlānī is true, it could be accidental or for a lawful reason, and it conflicts with reports of the Prophet and major Companions' simple diet and clothing. «وعندي أن التزام ذلك يخل بالكمال وما يروى عن الشيخ قدس سره وأمثاله إن صح يحتمل»
#38 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Keeping the matter secret means concealing spiritual realities from people veiled from their light and insight. «أي من الأغبار المحجوبين عن مطالعة الأنوار والوقوف على الأسرار»
#39 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Their awakening means a return to sobriety after spiritual intoxication. «( وكذلك بعثناهم ) رددناهم إلى الصحو بعد السكر»
#40 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Their guess that they had slept a day or part of one reflects absorption that erased awareness of days and the distinction between moon and sun. «لأنهم كانوا مستغرقين لا يعرفون اليوم من الأمس ولا يميزون القمر من الشمس»
#41 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعضهم · reports · The brief estimate also symbolizes lovers' tendency to count union as short: even a long meeting seems brief, and the cited poem says a life passed without counting nights. «وقيل : إنهم استقلوا أيام الوصال وهكذا شأن عشاق الجمال»
#42 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Returning from intoxication to sobriety and spirituality to embodied life, they seek human food and apply spiritual principles to practical conduct. «ثم إنهم لما رجعوا من السكر إلى الصحو ومن الروحانية إلى البشرية طلبوا ما يعيش به الإنسان»
#43 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The shared purse teaches aspirants not to beg, rebuking mendicant shaykhs who make a habit of asking, perhaps for unlawful money. «الإشارة فيه أولا إلى أن اللائق بطالبي الله تعالى ترك السؤال ويرد به على المتشيخين»
#44 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Calling it “your silver” teaches companions to share resources rather than reserve something for one member, even though one of the youths may have been a shepherd without money. «وثانيا إلى أن اللائق بهم أن لا يختص أحدهم بشيء دون صاحبه ألا ترى كيف قال قائلهم ( بورقكم هذه )»
#45 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ذو النون · reports · Seeking the purest food teaches scrupulousness; Dhū al-Nūn says a knower does not extinguish the light of gnosis with the light of scruple. «وثالثا إلى أن اللائق بهم استعمال الورع ألا ترى كيف طلب القائل الأزكى وهو على ما في بعض الروايات الأحل ولذلك قال ذو النون»
#46 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Their awakening, like a sign of resurrection, first served to let them understand their own condition; knowing one's state is among the foremost concerns. «ilm-i hâl akdem-i umûr olduğundan ba‘slerinin ilk hikmeti»
#47 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Some estimated one day and others part of a day; people raised at resurrection will likewise make such an estimate, as in the passage about the passerby in 2:259. «Nitekim kıyamette ba‘s olunacaklar hep böyle sanacaklar (Sûre-i Bakara’da أَوْ كَٱلَّذِى مَرَّ عَلَىٰ قَرْيَةٍ ilh. [2/259] bak)»
#48 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · When some sensed they had slept longer, they ended the disagreement by acknowledging that their Lord knew the duration. «Rabbiniz a‘lemdir. Binâen‘aleyh ihtilâfı bırakınız»
#49 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · They sent one companion with their silver coin to find the cleanest food and bring provisions, acting carefully and keeping their presence secret. «ve size ondan bir rızık getirsin»
#50 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The Cave and Dhul-Qarnayn stories are the surah's primary purposes, placed at its beginning and end. «أن أهم غرض نزلت فيه سورة الكهف هو بيان قصة أصحاب الكهف ، وقصة ذي القرنين»
#51 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The surah's placement near the Qur'an's midpoint is a divinely inspired arrangement: one proposed midpoint letter is ت in وليتلطف (Q 18:19), another is ن in نكرا (Q 18:74), and the midpoint of its thirty parts is at Q 18:75. «وهو ( التاء ) من قوله تعالى : وليتلطف ( الكهف : 19 ) وقيل نصف حروف القرآن هو ( النون )» [filed under 18:19, 18:74, 18:75]
#52 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The revival benefits the sleepers themselves: it lets them see God’s protection and gives them direct knowledge of one aspect of resurrection, as Abraham asked about reviving the dead. «عطف لجزء من القصة الذي فيه عبرة لأهل الكهف بأنفسهم ليعلموا من أكرمهم الله به من حفظهم»
#53 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Kadhālika may compare awakening with the preceding sleep as an equally powerful sign, or compare the revival with itself to heighten wonder, as at 2:143. «ويجوز أن يكون تشبيه البعث المذكور بنفسه للمبالغة في التعجيب كما تقدم في قوله : { وكذلك جعلناكم أمة وسطا ( البقرة : 143 )»
#54 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The statement ‘they questioned one another’ is explained by the ensuing dialogue: a questioner asks how long they slept and the others answer. «وجملة قال قائل منهم } بيان لجملة { ليتساءلوا } . وسميت هذه المحاورة تساؤلا لأنها تحاور عن تطلب»
#55 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Those answering ‘a day or part of a day’ may have agreed together, or the wording may distribute the answers, with some saying a day and others part of one. «وأسند الجواب إلى ضمير جماعتهم : إما لأنهم تواطؤوا عليه ، وإما على إرادة التوزيع»
#56 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · After disagreeing, all apparently defer the duration to God; it may instead be one speaker’s answer collectively attributed because the others judged it correct. «ويجوز أن يكون قول بعضهم فأسند إليهم لأنهم رأوه صوابا»
#57 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Rather than continue debating the duration, they send someone for food, a ‘wise style’ that redirects the questioners to their more immediate need. «وهو قريب من الأسلوب الحكيم . وهو تلقي السائل بغير ما يتطلب تنبيها على أن غيره أولى بحاله»
#58 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Wariq means silver; the common reading has a kasra on rāʾ, while several readers use sukūn, and it refers to a minted silver coin or dirham. «والورق بفتح الواو وكسر الراء : الفضة . وكذلك قرأه الجمهور . ويقال ورق بفتح الواو وسكون الراء»
#59 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · An unnamed report says their coins were dirhams from the Roman ruler Decius; the city is Ephesus, identified earlier in the story. «والمراد بالورق هنا القطعة المسكوكة من الفضة ، وهي الدراهم قيل : كانت من دراهم ( دقيوس ) سلطان الروم»
#60 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Ayyahā means which place within the city, such as a market stall; azkā food is the purest or best, with ṭaʿāman specifying the comparative relation. «و { أيها } ما صدقه أي مكان من المدينة ، لأن المدينة كل له أجزاء كثيرة منها دكاكين الباعة»
#61 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Azka means better and more wholesome because zakā conveys increase in goodness and benefit; rizq means provision, as at 12:37. «والأزكى : الأطيب والأحسن ، لأن الزكو الزيادة في الخير والنفع . والرزق : القوت»
#62 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · The commands may address an unspecified delegate, or a known companion omitted in the narration; either way, the group must instruct him to return with food and act discreetly. «وصيغة الأمر في قوله : فليأتكم } و { ليتلطف } أمر لأحد غير معين سيوكلونه ، أي أن تبعثوه يأتكم»
#63 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · A saying counts the tāʾ in walyatalaṭṭaf as half of the Qur’an’s letters; Ibn ʿAṭiyya instead records the nūn in nakran at 18:74 as the midpoint. «قيل التاء من كلمة { وليتلطف } هي نصف حروف القرآن عدا . وهنالك قول اقتصر عليه ابن عطية»
#64 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Ishʿār means informing; its causative hamza derives from shaʿara, which can mean knowing, and the verb here takes one object. «والإشعار : الإعلام ، وهو إفعال من شعر من باب نصر وكرم شعورا ، أي علم . فالهمزة للتعدية»
#65 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The phrase means ‘do not inform anyone that you exist’; an omitted noun lets the warning cover every circumstance, including their number and location. «وقوله : { بكم } متعلق ب { يشعرن } . فمدخول الباء هو المشعور ، أي المعلوم» [filed under 18:19, 18:20]
#66 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The emphatic nūn warns of consequences; the threat in the following clause explains both the prohibition and its emphasis. «والنون لتوكيد النهي تحذيرا من عواقبه المضمنة في جملة { إنهم إن يظهروا عليكم يرجموكم } الواقعة تعليلا للنهي» [filed under 18:19, 18:20]
#67 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The cave sleepers mirror humanity absorbed in earthly adornment: people will awaken from worldly life and reckon its long span as a day or part of one. «ليس ذلك كله إلا آية إلهية هي نظيرة ما جرى على أصحاب الكهف» [filed under 18:7, 18:9, 18:19]
#68 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو جعفر · reports · A report from Abu Jaʿfar says the youths were money changers. «وفي الدر المنثور أخرج ابن أبي حاتم عن أبي جعفر قال كان أصحاب الكهف صيارفة»
#69 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · Al-Sadiq distinguishes money changers in speech from dealers in coins. «فقال كانوا صيارفة كلام ولم يكونوا صيارفة دراهم»
#70 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Tasāʾul is mutual questioning; waraq is dirhams or silver, coined or uncoined; yuẓhirū ʿalaykum can mean discover or overcome you. «التساؤل سؤال بعض القوم بعضا، والورق بالفتح فالكسر الدراهم، وقيل هو الفضة»
#71 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · “Likewise We awakened them” refers back to their long, remarkable sleep; the questioning was the immediate purpose of waking them. «كما أنمناهم في الكهف دهرا طويلا على هذا الوضع العجيب المدهش»
#72 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Their despair at the apparent triumph of coercive falsehood parallels the man revived after a century; their awakening shows that time's length does not destroy truth or revive falsehood. «فأماته الله مائة عام ثم بعثه»
#73 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Earthly life and its events pass like a brief dream, but worldly distraction keeps people from recognizing the truth until death or the end of the world. «وجدها كطائف في نومة أو سنة في مثل يوم»
#74 TABATABAI d.1402 · علي عليه السلام · reports · Ali says people are asleep and awaken when they die. «الناس نيام فإذا ماتوا انتبهوا»
