#79 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · At the Hour, worldly life will seem no longer than an evening or its forenoon; life passes quickly, the cautious escape its terrors, and the heedless reach everlasting calamity. «Uzun zannedilen dünya ömrü o kadar çabuk geçer»
#80 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The author cites 23:114, which asks how many years people remained on Earth, while rejecting worldly life as the meaning of the brief sojourn. «قال كم لبثتم في الأرض عدد سنين»
#81 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · The Qummi commentary says the period was part of a day. «عشية أو ضحاها } قال بعض يوم»
#82 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The comparison means that worldly life until resurrection will seem as brief as one afternoon or its morning, like a portion of one day. «بأن قرب الساعة من حياتهم الدنيا بحيث مثلهم حين يرونها مثلهم لو لبثوا بعد حياتهم في الأرض عشية أو ضحى تلك العشية»
#83 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · The “sojourn” is the interval from worldly life to resurrection, meaning the grave period; counting the whole worldly life for judgment supports this reading. «أن المراد باللبث لبث ما بين الحياه الدنيا والبعث أي لبثهم في القبور»
#84 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · rejects · One alternative makes the sojourn the interval from asking about the Hour until resurrection; the author objects that people witness the interval only at resurrection and that death, not their question, precedes it. «وقيل المراد به اللبث بين حين سؤالهم عن وقتها وبين البعث»
#85 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Like several other verses, this indicates that earthly time is an illusory concept that loses meaning in the ultimate reality of the hereafter. «a subtle indication of the illusory, earthbound nature of man's concept of "time" - a concept which, we are told, will lose all its meaning»
#86 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · This late Meccan surah was revealed shortly after surah 78 and is named for an-nazi'at in its first verse. «THIS late Meccan surah, revealed shortly» [filed under 79:1-46]
#87 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Qur'anic uses include duha opposite evening, the sun's light brought forth, and a specific time appointed for events; these usages weigh against interpreting it as the whole day. «وفي الاستعمال القرآني، نرى القرآن الكريم استعمل الضحى مقابلاً للعشية» [filed under 93:1, 79:29, 79:46, 7:98, 20:59]
#88 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Prophet should not be troubled by demands for the Hour’s timing: only God knows it, the Prophet’s duty is warning, and at its sight people will feel they stayed only a short while. «The time of arrival of the Day of Judgement is something which only the Almighty knows» [filed under 79:42, 79:46]
#89 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · At Judgment, people will see that their worldly stay seemed no longer than an evening or morning, so its arrival should not be dismissed as distant. «more than the first or last part of a day»
#90 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · After returning to life, people will see how brief worldly life was beside the everlasting afterlife. «İnsanlar, ikinci hayata döndüklerinde ebedî olan âhirete göre geçici olan dünya hayatının ne kadar kısa olduğunu anlayacaklardır»
#91 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Psychologically, the past seems short because it is finished; the open, uncertain future feels more important and interesting and can cause anxiety. «Psikolojik olarak insana geçmiş daima kısa bir zamanmış gibi gelir; çünkü geçmiş artık olmuş bitmiştir»
#92 STUDYQURAN · most commentators · reports · Most commentators say the time in the grave will seem brief before the resurrection and gathering before God. «Most commentators say that this verse means that the time in the grave will seem short»
#93 STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Others say earthly life will seem like only an hour compared with the Hereafter. «Others say the implication is that life on earth will seem as if it were only an hour»
#94 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · Here morning is said to represent worldly life as people see the Hereafter. «morning (ḍuḥā) is said to refer to the time period of the worldly life»
#95 STUDYQURAN · holds · The final verses answer disbelievers who taunted the Prophet for not knowing when the Hour would arrive. «respond to the disbelievers who chided the Prophet for not knowing when the Hour would come» [filed under 79:42, 79:46]
