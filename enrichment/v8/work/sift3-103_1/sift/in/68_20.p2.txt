#98 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The garden became like a fully harvested orchard with nothing left because fire consumed it; another view compares it to night because it was blackened. «كالبستان الذي صرمت ثماره لم بحيث لم يبق فيها شيء»
#99 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The garden became like an orchard whose fruit had been cut away, leaving nothing; الصريم is a passive-form adjective. «كالبستان الذي صرمت ثماره بحيث لم يبق فيها شيء ففعيل بمعنى مفعول»
#100 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas explains الصريم as black ash, or as a known barren stretch of sand in Yemen. «وقال ابن عباس كالرماد الأسود وهو بهذا المعنى لغة خزيمة وعنه أيضا الصريم رملة باليمن معروفة لا تنبت شيئا»
#101 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · مؤرج · reports · Muʾarrij compares it to a severed stretch of sand that grows nothing useful. «وقال مؤرج كالرملة انصرمت من معظم الرمل وهي لا تنبت شيئا ينفع»
#102 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · منذر والفراء وجماعة · reports · Al-Mundhir, al-Farra, and others understand it as night: the garden was burnt and black like night. «وقال منذر والفراء وجماعة الصريم الليل والمراد أصبحت محترقة تشبه الليل في السواد»
#103 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الثوري · reports · Al-Thawri understands الصريم as morning, because the garden had whitened like harvested crops; some say both night and day bear the name because each ends the other. «وقال الثوري كالصبح من حيث ابيضت كالزرع المحصود»
#104 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · By morning, the paradise-like orchard had become a sırım, a ruined and scorched husk. «Derhâl o cennet gibi bağ sabaha kadar sırıma dönüvermişti»
#105 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Sarīm means wholly cut or severed; it can describe cut fruit, a harvested field, a night portion, or barren sand. «tamamen kesilmiş veya kesik demektir»
#106 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · A transmitted interpretation takes the orchard itself, rather than only its fruit, as cut from the roots and burned black like ash. «o bağın meyvesinin değil, kendisinin kökünden kesilip yanmış, kül gibi karara kalmış olduğu mervîdir»
#107 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Their sworn plan came true, but to their harm and in an unforeseen way, not as they intended or by their own hand. «Demek ki yemin ettikleri fiil olmuştu, fakat gönüllerindeki gibi ve kendi elleriyle lehlerine değil» [filed under 68:17, 68:20]
#108 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · One interpretation takes الصريم as night, also a name for day because each cuts off the other; pairing it with أصبح creates antithesis. «والصريم قيل : هو الليل»
#109 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · جذيمة أو خزيمة · reports · Another reported dialectal meaning of الصريم is black ash. «وقيل الصريم : الرماد الأسود بلغة جذيمة أو خزيمة»
#110 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · A further reported meaning of الصريم is a known barren sand tract in Yemen. «وقيل الصريم : اسم رملة معروفة باليمن لا تنبت شيئا»
#111 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · He favors the word's many meanings because all of them can suit the verse; يصرمنها and الصريم also form paronomasia. «وإيثار كلمة الصريم هنا لكثرة معانيها وصلاحية جميع تلك المعاني لأن تراد في الآية»
#112 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The fāʾ in فتنادوا derives the call from their oath: at morning they summon one another to carry out the plan. «وفاء { فتنادوا } للتفريع على { أقسموا ليصرمنها مصبحين ، }»
#113 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A night visitation from God afflicted and surrounded the garden; it became like cut fruitless trees, a dark night after burning, or a barren patch of sand. «أي بلاء يطوف عليها ويحيط بها ليلا» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#114 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Sarim can mean trees stripped of fruit, a black night, or a piece of sand separated from other sand and unable to grow anything. «وهو الشجر المقطوع ثمره أو المعنى»
#115 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad places the surah probably third in revelation order and calls it one of the oldest Qur'anic portions. «In the chronological order of revelation, this surah most probably occupies the third place» [filed under 68:1-52]
#116 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Suyuti · reports · Suyuti holds that the surah followed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96; Asad says this conflicts with the best-authenticated reports that most of surah 74 came second. «Some authorities - among them Suyuti - incline to the view that it was revealed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96» [filed under 68:1-52]
#117 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Al-ṣarīm means cut or harvested; reports also gloss it as black ash in Khuzayma speech or a known Yemeni sand. «والصريم: المقطوع، والمحصود. ونقلوا أنه الرماد الأسود بلغة خزيمة»
#118 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. «أن هذه الجنة كانت لرجل صالح، حدودا قومه وبلده فقيل إنه من أهل اليمن» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:19, 68:20, 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24, 68:25, 68:26, 68:27, 68:28, 68:29]
#119 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · While the owners slept, the garden was made barren; in the morning they found it harvested, recognized their loss, and the middle brother reminded them of their neglect of God and His bounty. «وترك الجنة صريماً جرداء خلاء. فلما أصبحوا، تنادوا ليغدوا على حرثهم» [filed under 68:19-29]
#120 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The early Meccan account omits personal names, dates, and other details except where they serve its lesson; it identifies the people only as from Ethiopia or Yemen. «لا يتعلق فيها بذكر الأشخاص والأزمنه إلا ما كان من جوهر القصة» [filed under 68:17-29]
#121 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The punishment verse points to the lesson of the garden story: tyrants face punishment in this world, while the hereafter's punishment is greater. «يلفت إلى مناط العبرة فيما تلت الآيات من أمر أصحاب الجنة» [filed under 68:17-33]
#122 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Attributing the orchard’s destruction to God signals that the disaster arrived wholly unexpectedly and devastated it in an instant. «the calamity arrived without the slightest expectation that it would» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#123 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The calamity was so devastating that it miraculously reduced the orchard to nothing in a blink; ‘from your Lord’ reassures the Prophet amid Quraysh’s apparent ease. «it was so devastating in nature that it miraculously routed the orchard to nothingness in the blink of an eye» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#124 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The parable answers those who call the Prophet possessed because they feel secure: punishment can ruin even plans fixed by pledges when people wake to carry them out. «all of man’s schemes which he devises with the conviction of pledges and oaths are rendered to ruin» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#125 STUDYQURAN · Most commentators · reports · Most commentators place the story in Madinah and identify its owners as three Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen who inherited their father’s fertile garden; their father left produce for the poor, but they planned to withhold it and harvest secretly. «Most commentators maintain that these verses were revealed in Madinah and that the story refers to three either Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen» [filed under 68:17-34]
#126 STUDYQURAN · holds · The Arabic kaʾl-ṣarīm, rendered “like a garden plucked,” can also mean “like the blackness of night.” «Like a garden plucked translates kaʾl-ṣarīm, which can also mean “like the blackness of night”»
#127 STUDYQURAN · the Prophet · reports · The Prophet reportedly warned that disobedience can take away a servant’s sustenance, then recited this verse. «The Prophet reportedly said, “Avoid sins of disobedience. Verily, the servant commits sins»
#128 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Qalam is generally considered an early Makkan sūrah. «generally thought to be from the early Makkan period» [filed under 68:1-52]
#129 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qatādah · reports · These early commentators held that verses 1–16, 34–47, and 51–52 are Makkan, while 17–33 and 48–50 are Madinan. «the early commentators IbnʿAbbās and al-Qatādah (d. 117/726) maintained that vv. 1–16» [filed under 68:1-52]
#130 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 17–34 tell the parable of garden owners who lose their harvest because of miserliness. «the parable of the owners of the garden, who lost their harvest because of their miserliness» [filed under 68:17-34]
