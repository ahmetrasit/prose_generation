#46 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says الصريم is ash in the language of Khuzaima; another view takes it as a barren known sand tract in Yemen. «قاله ابن عباس. وقيل: الصريم رملة معروفة باليمن»
#47 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · في التفسير · reports · The garden became like that; a report says it was uprooted and placed where today's Hijazi town of al-Taif stands, explaining its name. «وفي التفسير: أن جنتهم صارت كذلك.»
#48 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · A heavenly calamity struck while they slept and left the orchard like a black night, according to Ibn Abbas, or like dry harvested stubble, according to al-Thawri and al-Suddi. «قال ابن عباس: أي كالليل الأسود. وقال الثوري، والسدي: مثل الزرع إذا حصد،» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#49 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · A Prophetic report warns that a sin can deprive a person of provision already prepared for him, and applies the warning to the garden owners’ lost crop. «عن ابن مسعود قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "إياكم والمعاصي، إن العبد ليذنب» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#50 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The destroying visitor is a slight act for God though devastating to the orchard; the phrase from your Lord frames the punishment as merciful correction intended to reform the Prophet's people. «عذاب مهلك محيط مع أنه أمر يسير جدا عند الله»
#51 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · rejects · Although it is said that a taif in this sense must occur at night, the author rejects that restriction by citing a taif from Satan that touches people. «ولا يكون الطائف بهذا المعنى إلا بالليل»
#52 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says they were people of Abyssinia whose father fed the needy from his garden; after his death, his sons swore to harvest early and give no poor person food. «عن ابن عباس في قوله» [filed under 68:17-24]
#53 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says the garden belonged to an Israelite elder who saved his annual provisions and gave away the excess; his sons opposed charity and resolved to exclude the needy. «عن قتادة قال: كانت الجنة» [filed under 68:17-24]
#54 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas identifies the visitor that passed over the garden as a command from God. «عن ابن عباس في قوله»
#55 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن جريج · reports · Ibn Jurayj identifies the visitor as a punishment, a fiery mass from a valley of Hell. «عن ابن جريج في قوله»
#56 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says God's command came upon the garden at night while its owners slept, leaving it like the dark night; Qatr ibn Maymun agrees. «عن قتادة في قوله: {فطاف عليها طائف من ربك وهم نائمون}» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#57 DURR-FULL d.911 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · The Prophet warns that sin can cause lost knowledge, missed night prayer, and withheld provision, then says the owners lost their garden because of their sin. «عن ابن مسعود قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: إياكم والمعاصي» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#58 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · A calamity encompassed the garden at night, as the following sleep indicates; it was fire sent from heaven. «أي على الجنة أي أحاط بها طائف بلاء طائف»
#59 BURSEVI d.1127 · الراغب · reports · Al-Rāghib defines ṭawf as circling around something to guard it, extended to a night visitor, jinn, image, or servant. «قال الراغب الطوف الدوران حول الشيء»
#60 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · They were asleep and heedless of the decree; sleep is a light death and death heavy sleep. «غافلون عما جرت به المقادير أو غافلون عن طوافه بالنوم»
#61 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · طاف عليها means the affliction came down and encircled the orchard. «أي أحاط نازلا على الجنة»
#62 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتادة · reports · Qatada glosses طائف as punishment, while Ibn Abbas calls it an event; al-Farra restricts a طائف to an event that comes at night. «وقول قتادة طائف أي عذاب بيان لحاصل المعنى ونحوه قول ابن عباس أي أمر وعن الفراء تخصيص الطائف بالأمر الذي يأتي بالليل»
#63 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن جريج · reports · Ibn Jurayj says a tongue of fire issued from their garden valley and destroyed it. «وكان ذلك على ما قال ابن جريج عنقا من نار خرج من وادي جنتهم»
#64 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · He rejects as unsound the story that Gabriel uprooted the orchard and carried it to the site of the city of al-Taif, as well as related relocation stories. «وقيل الطائف هو جبريل عليه السلام اقتلعها وطاف بها حول البلد ثم وضعها قرب مكة»
#65 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · النخعي · reports · Al-Nakhaʿi reads طيف in place of طائف. «وقرأ النخعي طيف»
#66 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · من ربك means the affliction originated from God. «﴿ من ربك ﴾ مبتديء من جهته عز وجل»
#67 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · وهم نائمون is circumstantial: the family were asleep, meaning the affliction reached the orchard at night, as Qatada reports. «في موضع الحال والمراد أتاها ليلا كما روي عن قتادة»
#68 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Alternatively, they were completely heedless of what destiny had brought upon them. «وقيل المراد وهم غافلون غفلة تامة عما جرت به المقادير»
#69 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · prefers · The literal sense of sleeping at night better fits the surrounding verses. «والأول أظهر من جهة السباق واللحاق»
#70 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · While they slept, a divine command or disaster from their Lord passed around the garden on every side. «Rabbin tarafından bir tâif, dolaşan bir emr-i ilâhî, bir âfet o bağın üzerinden her tarafını dolaşıverdi»
#71 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · One report says a fire emerged from the valley where the garden stood and burned it to its roots. «Bir rivayete göre bağın bulunduğu vâdiden bir ateş çıkıp o bağı kökünden yakıp kavurup bitirivermişti»
#72 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · طاف literally means walking around something; here it portrays the calamity as affecting the entire garden, as a specter does in 7:201. «الطواف : المشي حول شيء من كل جوانبه»
#73 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The passage admonishes people who do not share their wealth; the garden's worldly punishment does not prove its owners withheld obligatory zakat. «ويؤخذ من الآية موعظة للذين لا يواسون بأموالهم»
#74 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The preposition على with طاف gives the sense of taking control or descending upon the garden. «وعدي ( طاف ) بحرف ( على ) لتضمينه معنى : تسلط أو نزل»
#75 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The calamity's kind is left unnamed because its effect matters, not its type; attributing the action to a طائف functions like a passive construction. «ولم يعين جنس الطائف لظهور أنه من جنس ما يصيب الجنات من الهلاك»
#76 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · الفراء · reports · Al-Farra says a طائف only comes at night, associating the word with a dream specter; Ibn Ashur questions the proposed derivation from part of the night. «وعن الفراء : أن الطائف لا يكون إلا بالليل»
#77 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The phrase وهم نائمون either specifies when the calamity struck or, on al-Farra's reading, reinforces the nighttime sense and depicts the scene. «فقوله : { وهم نائمون } تقييد لوقت الطائف على التفسير الأول»
#78 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The indefinite طائف magnifies the event; the next verse identifies it as a ruinous calamity, with a report that a fiery blast burned the garden. «وتنوين { طائف } للتعظيم ، أي أمر عظيم وقد بينه»
#79 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · A reported explanation says a blast of fire struck and burned the garden. «قيل : أصابها عنق من نار فاحترقت»
#80 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · من ربك means sent from God: the destruction was punishment for failing to give thanks. «ف { من } للابتداء يعني : أنه عذاب أرسل إليهم»
#81 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Their punishment came before they actually withheld charity because their settled resolve and oath made them culpable as if they had already withheld it. «وعجل العقاب لهم قبل التلبس بمنع الصدقة لأن عزمهم على المنع وتقاسمهم عليه حقق أنهم مانعون»
#82 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A night visitation from God afflicted and surrounded the garden; it became like cut fruitless trees, a dark night after burning, or a barren patch of sand. «أي بلاء يطوف عليها ويحيط بها ليلا» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#83 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad places the surah probably third in revelation order and calls it one of the oldest Qur'anic portions. «In the chronological order of revelation, this surah most probably occupies the third place» [filed under 68:1-52]
#84 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Suyuti · reports · Suyuti holds that the surah followed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96; Asad says this conflicts with the best-authenticated reports that most of surah 74 came second. «Some authorities - among them Suyuti - incline to the view that it was revealed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96» [filed under 68:1-52]
#85 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. «أن هذه الجنة كانت لرجل صالح، حدودا قومه وبلده فقيل إنه من أهل اليمن» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:19, 68:20, 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24, 68:25, 68:26, 68:27, 68:28, 68:29]
#86 BINTSHATI d.1419 · قيل · reports · One report identifies the visitor who destroyed the garden as Satan; another identifies him as Gabriel, who uprooted and transported the trees around the Kaaba before planting them at Taif. «طاف طائف - قيل في رواية إنه الشيطان، وفي أخرى إنه جبريل»
#87 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · While the owners slept, the garden was made barren; in the morning they found it harvested, recognized their loss, and the middle brother reminded them of their neglect of God and His bounty. «وترك الجنة صريماً جرداء خلاء. فلما أصبحوا، تنادوا ليغدوا على حرثهم» [filed under 68:19-29]
#88 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The early Meccan account omits personal names, dates, and other details except where they serve its lesson; it identifies the people only as from Ethiopia or Yemen. «لا يتعلق فيها بذكر الأشخاص والأزمنه إلا ما كان من جوهر القصة» [filed under 68:17-29]
