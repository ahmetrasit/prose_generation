# 33 notes filed under 12:49 (group 2 of 2 of this verse's notes)
verse 12:49: ثُمَّ يَأْتِى مِنۢ بَعْدِ ذَٰلِكَ عَامٌۭ فِيهِ يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ وَفِيهِ يَعْصِرُونَ

#1 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Joseph shifts from addressing the group directly to saying people will receive rain or aid and press produce, indicating they will no longer need to manage their livelihoods. «ولعله لهذه الثلاثة غير السياق فقال»
#2 TABATABAI d.1402 · صاحب المنار · reports · The Manar author argues that the unusually specific final-year forecast cannot come from the dream and must come from revelation. «أن هذا التخصيص لم يعرفه يوسف عليه السلام إلا بوحي من الله لا مقابل له في رؤيا الملك»
#3 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Rain, fodder growth, or milking livestock best fits the imagery of fat and lean cows, explaining the specific mention of rain and pressing. «ومما تقدم يظهر أيضا أن الأنسب أن يكون المراد»
#4 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · The rain sense of yughath fits the reference to pressing; the claim that aid is more natural is unpersuasive except under a passive reading of yaʿsirun, they are rained upon. «وهذا المعنى الثاني أنسب بالنظر إلى قوله بعده»
#5 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · An objection that Egypt’s fertility depends on Nile flooding rather than rain fails because the floodwaters are supplied by rain in Sudan. «رد عليه بأن الفيضان نفسه لا يكون إلا بالمطر الذي يمده في مجاريه من بلاد السودان»
#6 TABATABAI d.1402 · لسان العرب · reports · The lexicon also defines ghayth as vegetation grown from rainwater, a sense that fits the following mention of pressing. «قال في لسان العرب والغيث الكلاء ينبت من ماء السماء»
#7 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Pressing can mean extracting juice or oil from fruit and plants, and some interpret it as milking livestock. «ويمكن أن يراد بالعصر الحلب أي يحلبون ضروع أنعامهم كما فسره بعضهم به»
#8 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The final year brings crops, rain, or divine relief and abundant produce or milk; the phrasing implies plentiful blessing for people and livestock. «وفيه كناية عن توفر النعمة عليهم وعلى أنعامهم ومواشيهم»
#9 TABATABAI d.1402 · البيضاوي · reports · Al-Baydawi treats the final fertile year as glad tidings beyond the interpretation of fat and lean cows; Joseph may have known it through revelation, the expected end of drought, or God’s custom of easing hardship. «ولعله علم ذلك بالوحى أو بأن انتهاء الجدب بالخصب أو بأن السنة الإلهية»
#10 TABATABAI d.1402 · صاحب المنار · reports · The Manar author says the final year’s exceptional abundance is extra information not entailed by the dream and therefore known to Joseph through revelation. «وقال صاحب المنار في تفسيره في الآية والمراد أن هذا العام»
#11 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The author says interpreters wrongly treat the dream as a forecast of seven fertile then seven barren years; Joseph instead states the required policy for surviving the anticipated famine. «لم يبن كلامه على أساس إخبارهم بما سيستقبلهم من السنين السبع المخصبة ثم السنين السبع المجدبة» [filed under 12:47, 12:48, 12:49]
#12 TABATABAI d.1402 · تفسير القمي · reports · Al-Qummī reports the king’s dream of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean cows and seven green ears with dry ones; the cupbearer recalled it after a time, and Joseph interpreted seven plentiful years, seven famine years, then rain. «ثم إن الملك رأى رؤيا فقال لوزرائه إني رأيت في نومي سبع بقرات سمان» [filed under 12:45, 12:46, 12:47, 12:48, 12:49]
#13 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · Al-Ṣādiq says the revealed wording is “what you bring near for them” and interprets the following year as one in which people receive rain. «قال الصادق عليه السلام إنما نزل ما قربتم لهن ثم يأتي من بعد ذلك عام فيه يغاث الناس» [filed under 12:48, 12:49]
#14 TABATABAI d.1402 · أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام · reports · When a man recited “they press” actively, ʿAlī corrected him: the verse means they are rained upon, not that they press wine, as 78:14 confirms. «فقال ويحك أي شيء يعصرون يعصرون الخمر؟»
#15 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · Al-Ṣādiq reads “they are rained upon” passively and takes yughāth from rain rather than rescue; al-ʿAyyāshī transmits the same interpretation. «إنما نزلت وفيه يعصرون أي يمطرون، أي بالبناء للمفعول ومنه يعلم أنه عليه السلام يأخذ قوله يغاث من الغيث» [filed under 12:48, 12:49]
#16 TABATABAI d.1402 · علي بن معمر عن أبيه عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · Al-ʿAyyāshī transmits the rain interpretation from al-Ṣādiq through ʿAlī ibn Muʿammar and his father. «وروى هذا المعنى أيضا العياشي في تفسيره عن علي بن معمر عن أبيه عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام»
#17 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Joseph interpreted Pharaoh's dream as seven productive years followed by famine, proposed storing surplus grain, and was appointed to Egypt's stores after the women and al-ʿAzīz's wife cleared him; prison thus led to kingship. «وفي هذه السنين انتصب يوسف لمقام عزة مصر» [filed under 12:42-57]
#18 TABATABAI d.1402 · جعفر الصادق · reports · Al-Sadiq reads yuʿṣirūn in the rain year as “they are rained upon,” citing 78:14; the author identifies the verb as passive and al-muʿṣirāt as clouds that rain. «عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام عام فيه يغاث الناس وفيه يعصرون بالياء يمطرون»
#19 TABATABAI d.1402 · علي بن معمر · reports · Al-Ayyashi transmits a similar explanation from Ali ibn Maʿmar and his father, and Qummi transmits it from Ali. «وروى العياشي مثل الحديث عن علي بن معمر عن أبيه عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام»
#20 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · reports · Yughath can mean that the people receive rain or are delivered from distress; either reading fits Egypt's crops, which depend on Nile floods fed by rainfall upstream. «depending on whether one connects the verbal form yughath with either of the infinitive nouns ghayth ("rain") or ghawth ("deliverance from distress")»
#21 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Unlike Genesis' romantic account of Joseph's fortunes, the Qur'anic story illustrates God's unfathomable direction of human affairs: people may hate what is good for them or love what is bad for them. «the Qur'an uses it primarily as an illustration of God's unfathomable direction of men's affairs» [filed under 12:1, 12:2, 12:3, 12:4, 12:5, 12:6, 12:7, 12:8, 12:9, 12:10, 12:11, 12:12, 12:13, 12:14, 12:15, 12:16, 12:17, 12:18, 12:19, 12:20, 12:21, 12:22, 12:23, 12:24, 12:25, 12:26, 12:27, 12:28, 12:29, 12:30, 12:31, 12:32, 12:33, 12:34, 12:35, 12:36, 12:37, 12:38, 12:39, 12:40, 12:41, 12:42, 12:43, 12:44, 12:45, 12:46, 12:47, 12:48, 12:49, 12:50, 12:51, 12:52, 12:53, 12:54, 12:55, 12:56, 12:57, 12:58, 12:59, 12:60, 12:61, 12:62, 12:63, 12:64, 12:65, 12:66, 12:67, 12:68, 12:69, 12:70, 12:71, 12:72, 12:73, 12:74, 12:75, 12:76, 12:77, 12:78, 12:79, 12:80, 12:81, 12:82, 12:83, 12:84, 12:85, 12:86, 12:87, 12:88, 12:89, 12:90, 12:91, 12:92, 12:93, 12:94, 12:95, 12:96, 12:97, 12:98, 12:99, 12:100, 12:101, 12:102, 12:103, 12:104, 12:105, 12:106, 12:107, 12:108, 12:109, 12:110, 12:111]
#22 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Joseph also promised a year after the drought when God answers people's pleas, sends abundant rain, and yields a large grape harvest. «He will send down abundant rain and they will get a thumping crop of grapes.»
#23 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Some people · reports · Some interpreters understand yugāth as rain. «Some people have interpreted the word #!#.ﻐﺎث as rain.»
#24 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · prefers · The literal sense is seeking help, which portrays drought's full effects; rain is a consequential translation rather than the word's literal meaning. «By taking the word in its literal meaning of “seeking help”, a comprehensive picture of the all-embracing affects of drought on people is portrayed.»
#25 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The extraordinary year is one in which people cry out and plead with God until their pleas are answered. «people will cry out and be forced to plead and beg the Almighty until finally their pleadings will be answered.»
#26 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Hardship drives even extreme polytheists and disbelievers to call on the one God and forget their imagined deities, as the Qur'an says. «the most extreme polytheists and disbelievers cry out for help from the one God and forget their hypothetical deities»
#27 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Joseph guided Egypt through the drought, winning the admiration of everyone from the king to ordinary people. «This period of drought was passed by the people of Egypt under the guidance of Joseph (sws). This made everyone from the king to the masses his great admirers.»
#28 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Yaʿṣirūn also has a consequential sense of abundant rain and productive grape vines; mentioning grape pressing subtly suits the king's wine-server. «The creepers of grapes will become very productive. People will profusely squeeze grapes.»
#29 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Joseph predicts a further abundant year in which God helps people and they press grapes, dates, olives, and sesame for juice and oil; this good year is not signaled in the dream but revealed by God, showing dreams should be interpreted by qualified people. «Kralın rüyasında bu bolluk yılına dair herhangi bir işaret yoktur. Hz. Yûsuf, Allah’ın verdiği bilgiye dayanarak bunu onlara müjdelemiştir»
#30 QIRAAT-ER · holds · The standard reading is active Form IV, third-person masculine plural. «3rd person masculine plural active IV — standard recitation»
#31 QIRAAT-ER · holds · The second-person plural reading changes the clause to direct address to Joseph's interlocutors. «2nd person plural active IV; direct address shifts to Joseph's interlocutors»
#32 STUDYQURAN · holds · Joseph’s interpretation covers the next fifteen years of Egypt’s agricultural cycle. «what would happen during the next fifteen years of Egypt’s agrarian cycle» [filed under 12:47, 12:48, 12:49]
#33 STUDYQURAN · R · reports · The final year would bring abundant bounty; pressing oil and wine signaled that food was plentiful again. «the pressing of oil and wine is an indication that there would be food in abundance once again; cf. Genesis 41:25–36.»
