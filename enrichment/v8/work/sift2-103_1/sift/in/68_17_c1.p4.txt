#158 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · At the first proclamation, Meccans followed such immoral leaders in denying God's signs and tormenting the Prophet, so he prayed for Mudar to face severe hardship like Joseph's years; God then afflicted them with seven years of famine. «“Allahım! Mudar’a darbeni şiddetlendir ve onlara Yûsuf’un seneleri gibi kıtlık ver”[^32]»
#159 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The orchard story shows that this divine law is not a chance event limited to the deniers of Muhammad's time but applies across generations. «sade onlara münhasır bir tesadüften ibaret olmayıp evvel ve âhir tatbik edilmesi iktizâ eden bir kanun olduğunu»
#160 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Here al-janna means an earthly paradise, a beautiful orchard, or at a deeper level a homeland; the story was familiar to Arabs, and its owners were those who possessed and managed the orchard. «burada “cennet”ten murad dünya cenneti, güzel bağ ve bostan demektir. Daha derin bakışla da vatan demektir»
#161 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Calling them the plural owners, unlike the single owner's garden in al-Kahf, points to common property and a social or national parable rather than an individually managed estate. «Sûre-i Kehf’te وَدَخَلَ جَنَّتَهُ [18/35] âyetinde olduğu gibi bir ferd misâliyle getirilmeyip أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ diye cemi‘sîgasıyla müşterek bir milk misâli olarak getirilmesi»
#162 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · The well-known account places the orchard near San'a in Yemen, at Sawran: a righteous owner gave God's due from its produce, but after his death his children withheld its benefit from people and begrudged God's due, bringing the narrated outcome. «Yemen’de San‘â’ya yakın, Savran denilen arzda sâlih bir adamın güzel bir bağı vardı»
#163 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Beginning the social parable with the owners' oath points to how communal enterprises begin with an agreement whose guarantee is the oath and whose consequences govern profit and loss. «her ictimâ‘î bir işin bir akid ve mukavele ile başladığını ve bunun yegâne müeyyidesi de yemin olduğunu»
#164 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The opening oath echoes the earlier “frequent swearer” and warns that people who make firm vows without considering their knowledge or power may swear falsely and bring disaster on themselves. «yukarıda كُلَّ حَلَّافٍۢ en başında zikredilmiş olduğu gibi burada da ondan başlanmış» [filed under 68:17, 68:10]
#165 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Their sworn plan came true, but to their harm and in an unforeseen way, not as they intended or by their own hand. «Demek ki yemin ettikleri fiil olmuştu, fakat gönüllerindeki gibi ve kendi elleriyle lehlerine değil» [filed under 68:17, 68:20]
#166 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · A reported interpretation sees the threatened mark on the nose as a humiliation on Judgment Day, because the man displayed arrogance and pride through his nose. «وقيل هذا وعيد بتشويه أنفه يوم القيامة»
#167 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The pronoun in بلوناهم refers to the deniers previously mentioned; the orchard parable begins a new section prompted by the preceding account of wealth and children. «ضمير الغائيبن في قوله : { بلوناهم } يعود إلى»
#168 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The parable warns that pride in ample provision has long led people to scorn truth and neglect gratitude; similar examples appear in the Cave and the story of Qarun. «فضرب الله للمشركين مثلا بحال أصحاب هذه الجنة لعلهم يستفيقون من غفلتهم وغرورهم»
#169 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Their trial was through blessings: Mecca enjoyed security, abundant provision, and easy trade across distant lands, yet its people rejected the messenger and failed to reflect on their gifts. «فالبلوى المذكورة هنا بلوى بالخير»
#170 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The Meccans resemble the orchard owners in turning from seeking God's pleasure and thanking Him for His gifts. «ووجه المشابهة بين حالهم وحال أصحاب الجنة المذكورة هنا هو الإعراض عن طلب مرضاة الله»
#171 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The parable implicitly threatens a reversal from prosperity to distress; though the immediate cause differs, the same kind of punishment later struck the Meccans with seven famine years after the Hijra. «وقد حصل ذلك بعد سنين إذ أخذهم الله بسبع سنين بعد هجرة النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى المدينة»
#172 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · The orchard story was associated with Darwan near Sanaa in Yemen; some say Darwan was the garden's name. «كانت ببلد يقال له : ضروان»
#173 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says the large garden belonged to a righteous, believing man from the People of the Book; the source does not identify him as Jewish or Christian. «غرسها رجل من أهل الصلاح والإيمان من أهل الكتاب قاله ابن عباس»
#174 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · بعض المفسرين · reports · Some say its owner was Jewish, reasoning that Yemen became Jewish in Bilqis's era or later and that Torah law required a tithe from fruit. «فقيل : كان يهوديا»
#175 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · بعض المفسرين · reports · Others place the owners shortly after Jesus, before Christianity spread in Yemen, which they date to the Abyssinian conquest near the Year of the Elephant. «وقال بعض المفسرين : كان أصحاب هذه الجنة بعد عيسى بقليل»
#176 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima says the owners were Abyssinians: their father shared the harvest with the poor, who supported orphans and widows, but his three wealthy sons became stingy and plotted to exclude them. «وعن عكرمة : كانوا من الحبشة كانت لأبيهم جنة وجعل في ثمرها»
#177 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · They planned to harvest before dawn before the poor arrived; their oath may have bound a hesitant brother, explaining why the middle brother later asks whether they would heed his warning. «وهذا يقتضي أن بعضهم كان مترددا في موافقتهم على ما عزموا عليه»
#178 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · The hesitant brother is said to have urged them to fear God, abandon their evil plan, and remember God's punishment; the others overruled him and secured the group's participation by oath. «قيل كان يقول لهم : اتقوا الله واعدلوا عن خبث نيتكم»
#179 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو جعفر · reports · Abu Jaʿfar says Meccans were tested by famine just as the garden owners were; their garden was al-Ridwan in Yemen, nine miles from Sanaʿa. «فقال ابن عباس فوالله الذي لا إله إلا هو هذا أنور في كتاب الله»
#180 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbas tells of a father who always gave each rightful person a share, then five sons who planned to keep an unusually abundant harvest from Muslim poor; four agreed and the moderate brother objected. «فقال ابن عباس فوالله الذي لا إله إلا هو هذا أنور في كتاب الله من الشمس الضاحية» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:22, 68:24, 68:28, 68:29]
#181 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The garden episode is a warning to the Prophet's deniers: like its owners, they arrogantly rely on wealth, children, and apparent causes without gratitude, then face sudden punishment, useless possessions, regret, and repentance too late. «وعيد لمكذبي النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم الرامين له بالجنون»
#182 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · If these verses were revealed with the preceding passage, their warning applies to the deniers generally and recalls the surprise defeat at Badr as worldly proof that apparent causes fail. «حتى يأتيهم الأجل ويفاجئهم عذاب الآخرة أو عذاب دنيوي من عنده كما فاجأهم يوم بدر»
#183 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · reports · Reports say the passage was revealed during a famine that struck Mecca after the Prophet prayed for years of hardship like Joseph's; the garden owners then parallel Mecca, though the final mutual blame does not fit easily. «وأما على ما رووا أن الآيات نزلت في القحط والسنة الذي أصاب أهل مكة وقريشا»
#184 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Balāʾ can mean a test or the affliction itself; sarm is cutting fruit, istithnaʾ is excluding some from a general rule or saying if God wills, and a night visitor is a punishment arriving at night. «البلاء الاختبار وإصابة المصيبة»
#185 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The surah consoles the Prophet after the polytheists accuse him of madness, promises reward and honors his great character, forbids compliance and compromise with them, and commands patience for God's judgment. «تعزي النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم إثر ما رماه المشركون بالجنون»
#186 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The surah's overall context is Meccan; the author considers it plausible that the seventeen verses about the garden owners were revealed in Medina. «ولا يخلو من وجه بالنسبة إلى الآيات السبع عشرة»
#187 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · God tests the garden owners by giving them affluence far beyond their moral deserts. «by bestowing on them affluence out of all proportion to their moral deserts»
#188 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad places the surah probably third in revelation order and calls it one of the oldest Qur'anic portions. «In the chronological order of revelation, this surah most probably occupies the third place» [filed under 68:1-52]
#189 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Suyuti · reports · Suyuti holds that the surah followed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96; Asad says this conflicts with the best-authenticated reports that most of surah 74 came second. «Some authorities - among them Suyuti - incline to the view that it was revealed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96» [filed under 68:1-52]
#190 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The surah is early Meccan except verses 17–33 and 48–50, and is commonly placed after al-ʿAlaq as the second revelation; despite ordering disputes it reflects the first revelation’s atmosphere. «السورة مكية مبكرة، إلا آيات (17: 33، 48: 50) فمدنية» [filed under 68:1, 68:17, 68:48]
#191 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Balāʾ means affliction or trial, while ibtilāʾ means examination. «البلاء المحنة، والإبتلاء الإمتحان.»
