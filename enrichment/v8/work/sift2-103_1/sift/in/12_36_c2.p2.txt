#84 DURR-FULL d.911 · يوسف · reports · On entering prison, Joseph announced that he interprets dreams; the two young men proposed testing him with dreams they had invented rather than actually seen. «فلما دخل يوسف عليه السلام السجن قال: إني أعبر الأحلام قال أحد الفتيين: هلم فلنجرب هذا العبد العبراني»
#85 DURR-FULL d.911 · holds · The cupbearer dreamed of pressing wine, and the baker of carrying bread from which birds ate. «فتراءيا من غير أن يكونا رأيا شيئا ولكنهما خرصا فعبر لهما يوسف خرصهما فقال الساقي: رأيتني أعصر خمرا»
#86 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · The two young prisoners were the king's food steward and cupbearer; Qatadah gives the same identification. «عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما في قوله {ودخل معه السجن فتيان} قال: أحدهما خازن الملك»
#87 DURR-FULL d.911 · محمد بن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Ishaq names the king's two servants Mujlab and Nabwa; one served wine and the other held another royal duty. «وأخرج ابن جرير وابن أبي حاتم عن محمد بن إسحق»
#88 DURR-FULL d.911 · مجاهد · reports · Joseph warned the two men not to love him because everyone who had loved him had brought him trouble: first his aunt, then his father, then the Aziz's wife; they persisted in their affection. «فحدثني عبد الله بن أبي نجيح عن مجاهد رضي الله عنه أن يوسف عليه الصلاة والسلام قال لهما»
#89 DURR-FULL d.911 · reports · Mujlab dreamed that birds ate bread carried over his head, while Nabwa dreamed of pressing grapes into wine. «وقد كانا رأيا حين ادخلا السجن رؤيا فرأى مجلب أنه رأى فوق رأسه خبزا»
#90 DURR-FULL d.911 · عمرو بن دينار · reports · Joseph said no one had suffered as he had: his father's love led to his being thrown into a well, and the Aziz's wife's love led to prison. «عن عمرو بن دينار قال: قال يوسف عليه السلام: ما لقي أحد في الحب ما لقيت»
#91 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas glosses Joseph's dream of pressing wine as pressing grapes. «عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما في قوله {إني أراني أعصر خمرا} قال: عنبا»
#92 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud recited the phrase as pressing grapes and swore that he received that wording from the Prophet. «عن ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه أنه قرأ [اني أراني أعصر عنبا] وقال: والله لقد أخذتها من رسول الله»
#93 DURR-FULL d.911 · الضحاك · reports · Al-Dahhak says khamr here means grapes, which people in Oman call khamr. «عن الضحاك رضي الله عنه في قوله {إني أراني أعصر خمرا} يقول: أعصر عنبا»
#94 DURR-FULL d.911 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid glosses taʾwīlihi as its interpretation or expression. «عن مجاهد رضي الله عنه {نبئنا بتأويله} قال: عبارته»
#95 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatadah says the prisoners admired Joseph's good treatment: he comforted mourners, treated the sick, worshipped diligently, and urged them to endure for reward. «وفي قوله {إنا نراك من المحسنين} قال: كان احسانه فيما ذكر لنا أنه كان يعزي»
#96 DURR-FULL d.911 · reports · The inmates said Joseph's presence had brought consolation, purification, and hope; he identified his lineage as Joseph son of Jacob, Isaac, and Abraham. «فمن أنت يا فتى قال: أنا يوسف ابن صفي الله يعقوب ابن ذبيح الله إسحق»
#97 DURR-FULL d.911 · reports · The jailer said he would improve Joseph's treatment though unable to release him; Joseph replied that this placed the jailer among the evil and destined him for the Fire's mountains. «قال: ذاك من الأخيار وأنت في الأشرار إنما تعد لي جبال أهل النار»
#98 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The two youths were the king’s cupbearer and baker; they were jailed after a poisoning plot, which the cupbearer abandoned but the baker carried out. «من عبيد الملك الأكبر وهو ريان بن الوليد»
#99 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The cupbearer dreamt of three grape clusters, squeezed them into the king’s cup, and served him; wine names the grapes by what they become. «ثلاثة أغصان عليها ثلاثة عناقيد من عنب»
#100 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The baker dreamt he carried bread above his head; above means on, as in the command to strike above the necks. «وقال الآخر وهو الخباز إني أريني كأني في مطبخ الملك»
#101 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The baker’s dream had three baskets of bread and foods, with birds of prey eating from the upper basket. «فوق رأسي ثلاث سلال فيها خبز وألوان الأطعمة»
#102 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Commentators differ on whether the prisoners truly dreamt or feigned dreams to test Joseph; the rescued cupbearer spoke truth, while the other lied and was crucified. «واختلف في أنهما هل رأيا رؤيا أو لم يريا شيئا فتحالما اختبارا ليوسف»
#103 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · They asked for interpretations of both dreams and their outcome; plural ‘tell us’ is narrative wording although each man asked separately, like messengers addressed individually. «وصيغة المتكلم مع الغير واقعة في الحكاية دون المحكي على طريقة قوله تعالى»
#104 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Seeing Joseph as good may mean they recognized his skill at dream interpretation, whose readings came true, or that he treated prisoners well and could relieve their distress. «يجوز أن تكون من الرؤية بالعين وأن يكون من الرؤية بالقلب»
#105 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Joseph comforted prisoners who had lost hope and suffered long grief, telling them to be glad, patient, and expect reward. «وكان في السجن ناس قد انقطع رجاؤهم وطال حزنهم»
#106 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The two young men were servants of the greater king Rayyān ibn al-Walīd: one baker and one cupbearer; an account says they were jailed after a poisoning plot against the king. «غلامان كانا للملك الأكبر الريان بن الوليد : أحدهما خبازه وصاحب طعامه والآخر ساقيه»
#107 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Isḥāq says the two loved Joseph when they saw him; Joseph begged them not to, since everyone who loved him suffered a trial, but they persisted. «فقد أخرج غير واحد عن ابن إسحق أنهما لما رأياه قالا له»
#108 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Calling their imprisonment ‘entering with him’ may suggest it became easier on seeing Joseph; another view says the wording associates Joseph with their prison status and avoids wording that denies his choice. Maʿa ordinarily implies concurrence from the action’s beginning. «والظاهر أن مع تدل على الصحبة والمقارنة لفاعل الفعل في ابتداء تلبسه بالفعل»
#109 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · صاحب الكشف · prefers · For the Queen’s ‘I submitted with Solomon,’ al-Kashf allows ‘with’ to mean submission to God and the Messenger, rather than simultaneous companionship; it suggests she had considered herself religious before her new Islam followed the Prophet’s guidance. «وقال صاحب الكشف : إنه لا يتعين المحكي عنها المعية الفاعل فجاز أن يراد أسلمت لله ولرسوله مثلا»
#110 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Some distinguish ongoing Islam from punctual entry, but al-Ālūsī prefers concurrence as the original meaning whenever possible. The object precedes the subject for emphasis; ‘with him’ precedes prison because companionship matters more. «واختير أن المقارنة هي الأصل ولا يعدل عنها ما أمكنت فتأمل»
#111 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The sentence follows an implied ‘they imprisoned him’; the two men entered with him. Al-sijn with fatḥa means the prison location, and ‘he said’ answers an implied question. «كأنه قيل : فلما بدا لهم ذلك سجنوه»
#112 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The cupbearer’s present-tense dream means he saw himself pressing wine, reportedly from a beautiful vine with three branches; grapes are named for the intended end product. Ubayy and Ibn Masʿūd’s ‘grapes’ is gloss, not canonical recitation. «وروي أنه قال : رأيت حبلة من كرم حسنة لها ثلاثة أغصان فيها عناقيد عنب»
#113 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عطية · reports · Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows ‘wine’ to be described as pressed because pressing is done for its sake; the common explanation is metonymy for its outcome. Al-Sijistānī heard reliable eloquent speakers use khamr as masculine. «وقال ابن عطية : يجوز أن يكون وصف الخمر بأنها معصورة لأن العصر من أجلها»
#114 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Aranī lets subject and object pronouns identify the same person as with verbs of perception: ‘I saw myself pressing wine.’ The baker’s dream has bread, or tharīd in Ibn Masʿūd’s muṣḥaf as a gloss, with birds eating from the upper basket. «وحاصله أرى نفسي أعصر خمرا»
#115 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · ‘Tell us its interpretation’ refers to both visions or what was seen; plural speech can narrate separate requests, as in 23:51. They call Joseph a benefactor for interpreting dreams or for his learning. «أي أخبرنا بتأويله بتعبيره وما يؤول إليه أمره»
#116 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتادة · reports · Qatāda reports that prison inmates were despondent; Joseph urged them to hope and be patient, and they praised his comfort. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says he tended the sick, made room for them, and gathered supplies for the needy. «أخرج ابن أبي حاتم وغيره عن قتادة قال : لما انتهى يوسف إلى السجن»
#117 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · All readers use kasra in al-sijn here, meaning the place of confinement, because entering applies to a place, not the act of imprisonment. «هنا بمعنى البيت الذي يسجن فيه»
#118 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · The two youths were the king's cupbearer and baker, imprisoned after the king became angry; an unnamed report says they were accused of poisoning his food and drink. «وهذان الفتيان هما ساقي الملك وخبازه غضب عليهما الملك فأمر بسجنهما»
#119 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Dream interpretation was a familiar Egyptian discipline, and God supported Joseph through it among the prisoners. «وكان تعبير الرؤيا من فنون علمائهم فلذلك أيد الله به يوسف عليه السلام بينهم»
#120 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The youths inferred Joseph's intelligence and assumed he could interpret dreams without having seen proof; “excellent” may mean skilled in interpretation or understanding. «أي المحسنين التعبير ، أو المحسنين الفهم»
#121 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Ihsan here means precision or proficiency, as in saying someone does not “do reading well.” «هو لا يحسن القراءة ، أي لا يتقنها»
#122 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Prisoners commonly exchanged dreams because they lacked news and conversation, and they hoped dreams would foretell escape. «لفقدانهم الأخبار التي هي وسائل المحادثة والمحاورة»
