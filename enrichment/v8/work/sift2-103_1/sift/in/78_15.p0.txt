# 42 notes filed under 78:15
verse 78:15: لِّنُخْرِجَ بِهِۦ حَبًّۭا وَنَبَاتًۭا

#1 MUQATIL d.150 · holds · The rain brings forth grain, including wheat, barley, and sesame, and plants such as wild trees and grass that grow in open country and renew annually. «يعني بالحبوب كل شيء يزرع ويحصد من البر والشعير والسمسم»
#2 NAHHAS d.338 · holds · Ḥabb is any grain with a husk; nabāt is grass, fodder, and similar growth. «فالحب كل ما كان له قشر والنبات الحشيش والكلأ ونحوهما»
#3 THALABI d.427 · holds · The gardens are closely packed and interwoven; Basran grammarians give alaf as the singular, though that is weak, while other grammarians give liff or lafi f and allow a collective plural formation. «وجنات ألفافا مجتمعه ملتفة بعضه ببعض» [filed under 78:15-16]
#4 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · الضحاك · reports · Grain means the seed in the husk that is harvested, while vegetation means pasture grass for grazing. «أحدهما : أن الحب ما كان في كمام الزرع الذي يحصد ، والنبات : الكلأ الذي يرعى ، وهذا معنى قول الضحاك .»
#5 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · عكرمة · reports · Another explanation is that grain means pearls and vegetation means grass; Ikrima says every rain drop produces grass on land or a pearl in the sea. «الثاني : أن الحب اللؤلؤ ، والنبات : العشب ، قال عكرمة : ما أنزل الله من السماء قطرة»
#6 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · reports · Alternatively, grain means what people sow and vegetation what they did not sow. «ويحتمل ثالثا : أن الحب ما بذره الآدميون ، والنبات ما لم يبذروه .»
#7 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · The water brings forth grain that people eat and vegetation that livestock eat. «يعني: ما يأكله الناس من الحبوب»
#8 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · Rain brings out grain for people, plants for livestock, and densely interwoven gardens. «{ لنخرج به حبا } مما يأكله الناس { ونباتا } مما ترعاه النعم» [filed under 78:15-16]
#9 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · The rain brings out grains people eat and plants the livestock eat. «{لنخرج به} أي بذلك الماء {حبا} وهو ما يأكله الناس {ونباتا} ما تنبته الأرض مما تأكله الأنعام.»
#10 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · حب refers to grains useful as food for animals, while نبات is grass used fresh by people or animals; the verse names both kinds of benefit. «و «الحب» : جنس الحبوب الذي ينتفع به الحيوان، و «النبات» : العشب الذي يستعمل رطبا لإنسان أو بهيمة»
#11 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The water brings out grain enclosed in crop husks and vegetation such as herbage and plants, encompassing earth's produce. «فالحب كل ما نضمنه كمام الزرع الذي يحصد والنبات الكلأ من الحشيش»
#12 TABRISI d.548 · reports · Another interpretation assigns grain to what people eat and vegetation to what livestock eat. «وقيل: حبا يأكله الناس ونباتا تنبته الأرض مما يأكله الأنعام»
#13 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الجمهور · reports · The grain is food for people; the plants are growth eaten by people and livestock. «أن الحب ما يأكله الناس والنبات ما تنبته الأرض مما يأكل الناس والأنعام»
#14 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الزجاج · reports · Everything harvested is grain, while pasture eaten by livestock is called vegetation. «كل ما حصد حب وكل ما أكلته الماشية من الكلإ فهو نبات»
#15 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عكرمة · reports · Alternatively, grain means pearls and vegetation means grass; every rain from heaven grows pearls in the sea and grass on land. «أن الحب اللؤلؤ والنبات العشب قال عكرمة ما أنزل الله من السماء قطرا»
#16 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · All terrestrial growth falls into three exhaustive classes: grain with husks, grasses without husks or stems, and trees that form gardens; 20:54 names the first two. «فثبت بالدليل العقلي انحصار ما ينبت في الأرض في هذه الأقسام الثلاثة» [filed under 78:15, 78:16]
#17 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Grain comes first because it is basic food, plants next because animals need them, and orchards last because fruit is less essential. «وإنما قدم الله تعالى الحب لأنه هو الأصل في الغذاء» [filed under 78:15, 78:16]
#18 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The water brings forth grains such as wheat and barley and vegetation such as fodder eaten by animals. «أي بذلك الماء { حبا } كالحنطة والشعير»
#19 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Rain brings out grain for human food and vegetation for livestock, such as straw and grass. «{ لنخرج به حبا ونباتا } ما يقتات به وما يعتلف من التبن والحشيش»
#20 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The water brings out grain, such as wheat and barley, and vegetation such as pasture. «﴿ حبا ﴾ كالبر والشعير ﴿ ونباتا ﴾ وكلأ»
#21 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The passage begins with grain because it is the staple food; نبات broadly includes all growth, including grain. «بدأ بالحب لأنه الذي يتقوت به، كالحنطة والشعير، وثنى بالنبات»
#22 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · God uses abundant, beneficial rain to grow grain stored for people and livestock, edible fresh plants, and gardens with varied fruits, colors, flavors, and scents. «أي: لنخرج بهذا الماء الكثير الطيب النافع المبارك {حبا} يدخر للأناسي والأنعام،» [filed under 78:15, 78:16]
#23 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The sky, earth, rain, and plants resemble a reproductive pair: sky is male, earth female, water semen, and vegetation their offspring; springs and pools are implied. «وجعل ذلك أشبه شيء بما يتولد بين الزوجين من الأولاد , فالسماء كالزوج والأرض كالمرأة , والماء كالمني» [filed under 78:14, 78:15, 78:16]
#24 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · مقاتل · reports · Muqātil says every sign just mentioned is more astonishing than resurrection. «قال مقاتل : وكل من هذا الذي ذكر أعجب من البعث» [filed under 78:10, 78:11, 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16]
#25 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Rain brings out staple grains first, such as wheat and barley, then other vegetation for enjoyment and animal fodder. «أي نجما ذا حب هو مقصوده لأنه يقتاته العباد»
#26 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The early authorities take the water to be the direct means of bringing forth crops; Ashʿaris say they come into being at the water's presence. «وهو على ظاهره عند السلف ومن اقتدى بهم وقالت الأشاعرة أي عنده حبا ونباتا»
#27 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The grain and vegetation are staple crops for people and fodder; grain is named first despite later sprouting because it is more noble and usually human food. «ما يقتاب به كالحنطة والشعير ويعتلف كالحشيش والتبن وتقديم الحب مع تأخره عن النبات»
#28 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The phrase explains the purpose or consequence of sending rain; lam marks consequence and bi marks causation. «İnzâlin hikmetini ve gayesini beyandır. Lâm âkıbet, bâ sebebiyet içindir.»
#29 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · ‘Bring forth’ rather than ‘grow’ portrays resurrection from the earth as the passage's main point; in Qaf, where favor is foregrounded, growth comes first and resurrection follows as an inference. «وجيء بفعل { لنخرج } دون نحو : لننبت ، لأن المقصود الإيماء إلى تصوير كيفية بعث الناس من الأرض»
#30 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Grain is a collective for edible seeds, including wheat, barley, millet, corn, rice, and pulses. «والحب : اسم جمع حبة وهي البرزة . والمراد بالحب هنا : الحب المقتات للناس»
#31 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Nabat is originally a verbal noun, then a conventional noun for useful vegetation whose seeds are not eaten, such as straw, clover, and grass. «والنبات أصله اسم مصدر نبت الزرع ،»
#32 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The water brings forth grain and vegetation that provide food for people and other animals. «أي حبا ونباتا يقتات بهما الإنسان وسائر الحيوان»
#33 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad identifies the surah as late Meccan (following Suyuti) and says its theme is life after bodily death, resurrection, and final judgment. «the continuation of human life after bodily death» [filed under 78:1-40]
#34 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Paired with al-Mursalat, this surah uses divine providence in the heavens and earth to establish Judgment as the necessary end of a purposeful world, rewarding the God-fearing and punishing rebels. «The basis of arguments is divine providence evident in every single part of the heavens and the earth.» [filed under 78:1, 78:2, 78:3, 78:4, 78:5, 78:6, 78:7, 78:8, 78:9, 78:10, 78:11, 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16, 78:17]
#35 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The surahs share an affirmative-question style aimed at deniers; each verse reproaches them while believers' glad tidings are understood against the warning. «The discourse begins with questions which can only be answered in the affirmative.» [filed under 78:1, 78:2, 78:3, 78:4, 78:5, 78:6, 78:7, 78:8, 78:9, 78:10, 78:11, 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16, 78:17]
#36 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The signs of providence, mercy, wisdom, and power throughout earth and heavens show that humanity is not unchecked or unaccountable; its fixed day of reckoning must arrive. «A call to reflect on the signs of God’s providence, mercy, wisdom and power» [filed under 78:6, 78:7, 78:8, 78:9, 78:10, 78:11, 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16, 78:17]
#37 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Rain produces grain for people, grass and vegetation for cattle, and luxuriant orchards. «provide grain for man and grass» [filed under 78:15, 78:16]
#38 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The seven firm heavens, sun, rain-bearing clouds, and life-sustaining growth display God's power to create a new realm, the afterlife. «Gökleri, alev alev yanarak dünyayı aydınlatan güneşi, bolca yağmur indirerek yeryüzünde birçok nimetin yetişmesine ve hayatın devam etmesine vesile olan bulutları yaratan yüce kudret» [filed under 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16]
#39 STUDYQURAN · holds · God remains the primary cause: rain is the means by which He produces grain, plants, and gardens, since He made every living thing from water. «God remains the primary cause for all things. Therefore, it is not the rain that produces grain» [filed under 78:15, 78:16]
#40 STUDYQURAN · holds · This late Makkan surah is named for the Great Tiding in verse 2, understood by most as the Quran or Resurrection; it is also called al-Muʿṣirāt and al-Tasāʾul for its rain-cloud and questioning themes. «a late Makkan sūrah, takes» [filed under 78:1-40]
