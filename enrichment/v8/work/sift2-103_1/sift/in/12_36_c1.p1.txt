#38 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · قتادة · reports · One prisoner was the king's baker and the other his cupbearer. «عن قتادة ودخل معه السجن فتيان قال : كان احدهما : خباز الملك على طعامه ، والاخر : ساقيه على شرابه»
#39 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · السدي · reports · The king imprisoned the baker for suspected poisoning and the cupbearer because he thought the cupbearer had colluded in the poisoning. «عن السدي ، قوله : ودخل معه السجن فتيان غضب الملك على خبازه ، بلغه انه سمه فحبسه ، وحبس الساقي»
#40 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · ابن اسحاق · reports · The prisoners served King Rayyan ibn al-Walid; their names were Majlath and Nabū, the latter serving wine. They admired Joseph, who warned that love for him had brought him repeated troubles, but they persisted in admiring his understanding. «عن محمد بن اسحاق : ودخل معه السجن فتيان : غلامان كانا للملك الاكبر : الريان ابن الوليد»
#41 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud recited “I see myself pressing grapes” in place of “pressing wine.” «عن زيد بن وهب قال : قرا عبد الله : اني اراني اعصر عنبا»
#42 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · الضحاك · reports · “Wine” here means grapes, which people in Oman call wine. «عن الضحاك قال احدهما اني اراني اعصر خمرا فالخمر : العنب ، وانما يسمى اهل عمان العنب : الخمر»
#43 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · مجاهد · reports · The two prisoners had seen dreams on entering prison: Majlath carried bread eaten by birds, and Nabū pressed wine; they asked Joseph to interpret them. «عن مجاهد وقد كانا رايا حين ادخلا السجن رؤيا فراى مجلث انه يحمل فوق راسه خبزا ياكل الطير منه»
#44 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · السدي · reports · The prisoners had invented their dreams to test Joseph's claim that he interpreted dreams; one said he pressed wine and the other carried bread eaten by birds. «عن السدي قال : وغضب يعني الملك على خبازه»
#45 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · أبو مجلز · reports · One of the two men who recounted a dream was lying, and the man who was crucified was the liar. «عن أبي مجلز قال : كان أحد الذين قصا على يوسف الرؤيا كاذبا ، قلت له : فالمصلوب هو الكاذب ؟ قال : نعم»
#46 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · مجاهد · reports · The prisoners asked Joseph to interpret their dreams because they saw him as a virtuous man, if he would do so. «عن مجاهد ، قال : فراى مجلث انه يحمل فوق راسه خبزا تاكل الطير منه ، وراى نبو انه يعصر خمرا»
#47 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · الضحاك · reports · Joseph tended sick inmates, made room for the crowded, and sought or collected help for anyone in need. «عند الضحاك بن مزاحم بخراسان اذ جاءه رجل فساله ، عن قول الله : نبئنا بتاويله»
#48 IBNABIHATIM d.327 · قتادة · reports · Joseph comforted grieving prisoners and treated the sick; his worship and devotion endeared him to them. «ثنا قتادة قوله : انا نراك من المحسنين قال : كان احسانه فيما ذكر لنا انه كان يعزى حزينهم»
#49 NAHHAS d.338 · holds · فتيان is dual of فتى; the dream omits 'in sleep'; hamzated نبئنا retains yā' unless hamza is lightened or replaced. «تثنية فتى وهو من ذوات الياء وقولهم الفتوة شاذ»
#50 IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB d.392 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masʿūd reads أعصر عنبا; the standard أعصر خمرا anticipates what the grapes will become, a future state named as though present. «ومن ذلك قراءة ابن مسعود: "إني أراني أعصر عنبا"7»
#51 THALABI d.427 · reports · The two prison newcomers were servants of King al-Akbar al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan: baker Majlath and cupbearer Banu; the king imprisoned them over an alleged poisoning plot. «وهما غلامان كانا للملك الأكبر الوليد بن الريان، أحدهما خبازه صاحب طعامه»
#52 THALABI d.427 · reports · The account says Egyptians bribed the two servants to poison the king’s food and drink; the cupbearer backed out, but the baker accepted the bribe and poisoned the food. «ضمنوا لهما مالا ليسما طعام الملك وشرابه فأجاباهم إلى ذلك»
#53 THALABI d.427 · الأصمعي · reports · Al-Asmaʿi says an Arab called grapes he carried khamr, and vinegar made from grapes was also called wine-vinegar by association. «قال الأصمعي: أخبرني المعتمر أنه لقي أعرابيا معه عنب، فقال: ما معك؟ قال: خمر»
#54 THALABI d.427 · holds · The repeated phrase that they are disbelievers in the Hereafter is analyzed as emphasis; another grammatical view treats the first pronoun as redundant and the second clause as the sentence. «وقيل: هم الأول جماد كقوله تعالى: أيعدكم أنكم إذا متم»
#55 THALABI d.427 · reports · The text compares the grammatical redundancy with a Quranic construction in which the first clause is treated as omitted and the next begins anew. «فصارت الأولى الملغاة والثانية ابتداء، وكافرون خبره»
#56 THALABI d.427 · القتيبي · reports · Al-Qutaybi allows literal wine by analogy with saying one pressed oil when olives were pressed. «قال القتيبي: وقد تكون هي الخمر بعينها كما يقال: عصرت زيتا»
#57 THALABI d.427 · الفراء وابن إسحاق · reports · Al-Farra and Ibn Ishaq say the prisoners call Joseph beneficent to them if he interprets their dreams; the phrase can be a polite appeal. «قال الفراء وقال ابن إسحاق: إنا نراك من المحسنين إلينا إن فعلت ذلك»
#58 THALABI d.427 · الضحاك · reports · Al-Dahhak says Joseph’s kindness in prison meant visiting the sick, making room for the cramped, gathering aid for the needy, and interceding for them. «في قوله: إنا نراك من المحسنين ما كان إحسانه؟ قال كان إذا مرض رجل»
#59 THALABI d.427 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says Joseph treated sick prisoners, comforted the grieving, and devoted himself to his Lord. «قتادة: بلغنا أن إحسانه كان يداوي مريضهم، ويعزي حزينهم»
#60 THALABI d.427 · reports · Another story says Joseph encouraged hopeless inmates with promises of reward and cleansing; his kindness made the jailer let him choose any cell. «فجعل يقول: أبشروا واصبروا تؤجروا، وإن لهذا لأجرا وثوابا»
#61 THALABI d.427 · holds · Joseph avoided immediately interpreting the dream because he knew one prisoner would suffer, and instead changed the subject. «فكره يوسف (عليه السلام) أن يعبر لهما ما سألاه لما علم في ذلك من المكروه على أحدهما»
#62 THALABI d.427 · holds · Most commentators understand Joseph’s promise to explain their food dreams before the food arrives as a claim to prophetic foreknowledge. «هذا قول أكثر المفسرين»
#63 THALABI d.427 · reports · Another view says he meant their ordinary waking food, telling them what it was, when they ate, and how much; they suspected divination, but he attributed knowledge to God. «وقال بعضهم: أراد به في اليقظة فقال: لا يأتيكما طعام ترزقانه تطعمانه وتأكلانه»
#64 THALABI d.427 · reports · The cupbearer warned the king not to eat, the baker warned him not to drink, and a test on an animal showed the food was poisoned, so both men were jailed. «فقال الملك للساقي: اشرب فشربه فلم يضره، وقال للخباز: كل من طعامك»
#65 THALABI d.427 · reports · Joseph had told prisoners he could interpret dreams; the two men approached to test him even though, Ibn Masʿud says, they had not actually dreamed anything. «قال عبد الله بن مسعود: ما رأى صاحبا يوسف شيئا، إنما كانا تحالفا أن يجربا علمه»
#66 THALABI d.427 · النبي · reports · Ibn Abbas transmits a prophetic warning that whoever falsely claims to have seen a dream will be ordered to tie two barley grains together on Judgment Day. «من أرى عينيه في المنام ما لم تريا كلف أن يعقد بين شعرتين يوم القيامة»
#67 THALABI d.427 · النبي · reports · The same report warns that secretly listening to people who dislike it brings molten lead poured into the listener’s ear. «ومن استمع لحديث قوم وهم له كارهون صب في أذنه الآنك»
#68 THALABI d.427 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says the two men loved Joseph at first sight; Joseph warned that those who loved him had each suffered a calamity, including his aunt, father, and the king’s wife. «قال مجاهد: لما رأى الفتيان يوسف قالا له: والله لقد أحببناك»
#69 THALABI d.427 · مجاهد · reports · The cupbearer’s dream showed a grape seed bearing three clusters which he squeezed into the king’s cup and served him. «فقال له الساقي: أيها العالم إني رأيت كأني غرست حبة من عنب»
#70 THALABI d.427 · مجاهد · reports · The baker’s dream showed three baskets of food above his head, eaten by birds; Ibn Masʿud’s reading clarifies the grape image as pressing grapes. «وقال الخباز: إني رأيت كأن فوق رأسي ثلاث سلال فيها الخبز»
#71 THALABI d.427 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masʿud reads the cupbearer as saying he pressed grapes, making explicit that the word khamr here means grapes. «ويدل عليه قراءة ابن مسعود أعصر عنبا»
#72 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says one prisoner was the king's food steward and the other his cupbearer; the king, al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan, suspected them of poisoning him and imprisoned them. «خازن الملك على طعامه»
#73 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · الضحاك · reports · Al-Dahhak says the prisoners called Joseph a benefactor because he visited their sick, consoled the grieving, and made room for cramped inmates. «لأنه كان يعود مريضهم ويعزي حزينهم ويوسع على من ضاق مكانه منهم»
#74 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · reports · Other explanations say Joseph encouraged patience and promised reward, was skilled in knowledge, accepted excuses, or put others' rights before his own. «معناه لأنه كان يأمرهم بالصبر ويعدهم بالثواب والأجر»
#75 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · ابن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Ishaq says they called Joseph a benefactor in the hope that he would interpret their dreams for them. «نراك من المحسنين»
#76 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says the prisoners told Joseph they loved him; Joseph asked them not to love him because the love of his aunt, father, and al-Aziz's wife had brought him tribulation, and he wanted love only from his Lord. «أحببناك حين رأيناك»
#77 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · holds · They are called fatayan because each was a slave, and slave may mean a young man regardless of age. «لأنهما كان عبدين»
#78 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid and Ibn Ishaq say the prisoners had true dreams and Joseph's interpretations came true; Abu Hurayra transmits the Prophetic maxim that the truest dreams belong to the most truthful speakers. «أنها كانت رؤيا صدق رأياها وسألاه عنها قال مجاهد وابن إسحاق»
