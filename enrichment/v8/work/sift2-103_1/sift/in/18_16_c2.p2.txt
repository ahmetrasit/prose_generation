#62 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Debates over their number and dog obscured their message and sacrifices; Islahi says the true history was later lost as Christianity’s teachings were distorted. «no one knew what their real message was and the extent of sacrifices they gave» [filed under 18:9-26]
#63 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Distorted reports about the youths circulated among Arab Christians, who prompted the question; the Qur’an answered to reassure the Prophet and companions in adversity. «he could receive great assurance and strength in the adverse circumstances he and his companions were passing through» [filed under 18:9-26]
#64 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ persecution and cave refuge parallel the Prophet and companions’ trials and their coming migrations to Abyssinia, the Thawr cave and Madinah. «the Prophet (sws) and his companions too had to face tremendous trials at the hands of the Quraysh» [filed under 18:9-26]
#65 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Marfaq means something useful to a person. «something useful for a person.»
#66 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The account suppresses the escalating conflict: imminent stoning led the youths to the cave they had chosen, where God assured them of mercy and provision if they took the initiative to leave their people and idols. «the danger of stoning them to death looked imminent, as is evident from verse 20» [filed under 18:16, 18:20]
#67 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · When servants fear God and set out toward Him, He provides the means they need, as also taught in al-Talaq. «It is stated thus in verses 2-3 of Sūrah al-Ṭalāq»
#68 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The guidance to seek refuge is a particular kind of divine inspiration, comparable to God's instruction to Moses' mother to place him in a box and set him afloat. «this is a particular type of divine revelation and is similar to the one sent to Moses’ mother»
#69 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · rejects · Islahi rejects the Sufi inference that this verse praises world-isolation: the youths fled mortal persecution, as the Prophet once sought refuge in Thawr, rather than pursuing monasticism. «These people had not adopted this life of isolation because of any inclination towards monasticism»
#70 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · rejects · Against exegetes who treat the promise of mercy and provision as the youths' own speech, Islahi says its certainty marks it as God's promise; a humble person could voice it only as prayer or yearning, as in verse 10. «constitutes the promise of divine help cannot be stated by a humble individual» [filed under 18:16, 18:10]
#71 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Unable to profess or practice monotheism safely amid persecution, the youths left their people with their dog and sought refuge in the cave. «imanlarını serbestçe ifade etme ve inançlarının gereğini yerine getirme imkânı bulamayacaklarını, hatta onların arasında hayat hakkına dahi sahip olamayacaklarını anlayınca, köpeklerini de yanlarına alarak mağaraya sığındılar.» [filed under 18:13, 18:14, 18:16]
#72 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · They prayed for mercy and a way out, hoping for God’s help; God accepted their prayer and caused them to sleep deeply for a long time. «Allah’ın yardımını ve bir kurtuluş yolu göstereceğini ümit ederek, “Rabbimiz! Bize rahmet et ve bize bir çıkış yolu hazırla!” diye dua ettiler.» [filed under 18:13, 18:16]
#73 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Replacing hamza with yāʾ softens the glottal stop, with no semantic change. «Hamza replaced by yāʾ — softening of glottal stop; no semantic change»
#74 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Marfiq may mean the elbow or a useful thing, shifting from an instrumental to a locative or nominal pattern. «Alternate canonical vocalization: marfiq (mafʿil pattern) = the elbow specifically, or a useful thing»
#75 STUDYQURAN · Ṭs · reports · The words are attributed to Tamlīkhā, also called Yamlīkhā or Iamblichus, the reported leader of the youths. «These are the words of Tamlīkhā (or Yamlīkhā, Iamblichus) the reported chief of the youths (Ṭs)»
#76 STUDYQURAN · holds · Tamlīkhā tells the youths to hide from disbelievers by taking refuge in the cave. «He instructs them to take refuge in the cave, to hide themselves from the disbelievers.»
#77 STUDYQURAN · IK · reports · The youths’ retreat parallels the Prophet and Abū Bakr hiding in a cave during the Hijrah while fleeing the pursuing Makkans. «as they sought to elude the pursuing Makkans (see 9:40 and commentary; IK)»
#78 STUDYQURAN · holds · In both episodes the cave symbolizes the heart’s interior, providing miraculous sanctuary and refuge from worldly evils. «the cave, which is a symbol of the interior“space” of the heart, offers miraculous sanctuary»
#79 STUDYQURAN · holds · The promise of ease answers the youths’ prayer for sound judgment in their affair in verse 10. «suggests a response to the youths’ supplication to God in v. 10 that He make them incline to sound judgment in their affair.»
