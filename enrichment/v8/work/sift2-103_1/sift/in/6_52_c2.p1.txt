#35 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · المشركون · reports · The polytheists objected to sitting with poor believers such as Salman, Suhayb, Bilal, and Khabbab, and asked that they be dismissed; the Prophet considered it but did not expel them. «ولا نرضى بمجالسة أمثال هؤلاء يعنون سلمان وصهيبا وبلالا وخبابا فاطردهم عنك»
#36 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The Prophet inclined toward their request only in hope of converting them and their people, thinking it would neither harm nor diminish the poor companions; revelation forbade the contemplated expulsion. «وكان النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم إنما مال إلى ذلك طمعا في إسلامهم، وإسلام قومهم،»
#37 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · سعد بن أبي وقاص · reports · In Muslim’s report, Sa‘d and five other companions were present when the polytheists asked the Prophet to dismiss them, and revelation came after he reflected on the request. «روى مسلم عن سعد بن أبي وقاص قال: كنا مع النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم ستة نفر،»
#38 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن · reports · Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and al-Hasan interpret morning and evening invocation as keeping the obligatory prayers in congregation. «قاله ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن»
#39 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Other explanations take the invocation as remembrance and Qur’an recitation, or as prayers at daybreak and evening for guidance and forgiveness. «وقيل: الذكر وقراءة القرآن. ويحتمل أن يريد الدعاء في أول النهار وآخره»
#40 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · ‘Seeking His face’ can mean sincerely seeking God’s obedience, or seeking God Himself, who is described as possessing the Face. «أي طاعته، والإخلاص فيها، أي يخلصون في عبادتهم وأعمالهم لله،»
#41 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Morning and evening are specified because people are usually occupied then, so one devoted to worship during busy hours is still more devoted in free time. «وخص الغداة والعشي بالذكر لأن الشغل غالب فيهما على الناس»
#42 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The Prophet later obeyed the command in 18:28 to remain with these worshippers, not looking away to seek worldly display. «وكان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم بعد ذلك يصبر نفسه معهم كما أمره الله في قوله:»
#43 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · After commanding warning for the heedless, God commands honoring and drawing near the devout, rather than expelling them to appease Quraysh. «بعدما أمره بإنذار غير المتقين ليتقوا أمره بإكرام المتقين وتقريبهم»
#44 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · قريش · reports · Quraysh offered to sit and talk with the Prophet if he expelled the poor Muslim worshippers, including Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, and Salman. «قالوا: لو طردت هؤلاء الأعبد يعنون فقراء المسلمين كعمار وصهيب وخباب وسلمان»
#45 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · The Prophet first said he would not drive away believers; when the request was repeated, he agreed to make them rise when Quraysh arrived. «فقال:
" ما أنا بطارد المؤمنين "»
#46 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · عمر · reports · Umar asked the Prophet to do this temporarily so they could see what the visitors would do; as a document and Ali were brought to write, the verse was revealed. «وروي أن عمر رضي الله عنه قال له: لو فعلت حتى ننظر»
#47 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · reports · Mentioning morning and evening can mean continuous worship, or the dawn and afternoon prayers; Ibn Amir reads ghudwah here and in al-Kahf. «والمراد بذكر الغداة والعشي الدوام، وقيل صلاتا الصبح والعصر.»
#48 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The worshippers seek God's face sincerely; making sincerity the qualification shows its centrality, and the prohibition consequently requires honoring them. «أي يدعون ربهم مخلصين فيه قيد الدعاء بالإخلاص تنبيها على أنه ملاك الأمر»
#49 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · reports · The accounting clause may mean the Prophet is not responsible for their faith or inner sincerity, even if their inner state is as the pagans alleged; their account is theirs and his is his. «ليس عليك حساب إيمانهم فلعل إيمانهم عند الله أعظم من إيمان»
#50 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · reports · Another view takes their account as their livelihood and poverty; a further view takes the pronouns to refer to the pagans, who are not accountable for the Prophet's account or vice versa. «وقيل ما عليك من حساب رزقهم أي من فقرهم. وقيل الضمير للمشركين»
#51 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Expelling the worshippers would make the Prophet one of the wrongdoers; the final clause answers the prohibition or follows from expelling as its consequence. «{ فتكون من الظلمين } جواب النهي ويجوز عطفه على فتطردهم»
#52 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · After commanding warning of the non-pious, God commands drawing the pious near and forbids their dismissal. «ولما أمر النبى عليه السلام بإنذار غير المتقين ليتقوا أمر بعد ذلك بتقريب المتقين»
#53 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · Calling on God morning and evening means continuous worship, or specifically dawn and afternoon prayers, or all five prayers; the Shami reading has the stated form of 'morning.' «أى عبادته ويواظبون عليها والمراد بذكر الغداة والعشى الدوام»
#54 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · Seeking God's face means sincerity; 'face' can denote a thing's essence and reality. «ووسمهم بالإخلاص فى عبادتهم بقوله ﴿ يريدون وجهه ﴾ فالوجه يعبر به عن ذات الشئ»
#55 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · Revelation followed the chiefs' demand to dismiss poor believers Bilal, Suhayb, Ammar, and others; the Prophet refused, they requested alternate days and a written agreement, then the poor moved aside as Ali was called to write. The Prophet threw away the paper and embraced them. «نزلت فى الفقراء بلال وصهيب وعمار وأضرابهم حين قال رؤساء المشركين لو طردت هؤلاء»
#56 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The believers' account is theirs alone and cannot transfer to the Prophet; the source compares the statement with a verse about Noah's reckoning. «حسباهم عليهم لازم لهم لا يتعداهم اليك كما أن حسابك عليك لا يتعداك اليهم»
#57 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The consequence clause answers the negation, while the next clause answers the prohibition; alternatively, being unjust is caused by dismissing them. «جواب النفى وهو ما عليك من حسابهم»
#58 QASHANI d.736 · holds · The dawn and dusk callers are complete people of unity; warnings benefit neither them, lost in God, nor those whose hearts are hardened. «أهل الوحدة الكاملون الواصلون، فإن الإنذار كما لا ينجع»
#59 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Their constant worship is presence of heart, witnessing of spirit, and orientation of the secret to God alone through eternal love, without seeking reward or fearing punishment. «يخصونه بالعبادة دائما بحضور القلب وشهود الروح وتوجه السر إليه»
#60 QASHANI d.736 · holds · They seek God's Essence rather than changing attributes or intermediaries, having witnessed the annihilation of means and even of their own selves. «لا يريدون بالعبادة إلا ذاته بالمحبة الأزلية لا يجعلون عبادتهم معللة بغرض»
#61 QASHANI d.736 · holds · They have no intermediary relation of account through the Prophet and, absorbed in God, do not involve themselves in his mission, aid to Islam, or opposition to disbelief. «لا واسطة بينهم وبين ربهم من ملك أو نبي»
#62 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Expelling them distracts their uninterrupted presence and makes the Prophet unjust; the following trial is that veiled people see only poverty and outward appearance and scorn their hidden rank. «عما هم عليه من دوام الحضور بإنهاضهم لشغل ديني»
#63 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · This segment reproduces Quranic verses 6:36–52 without commentary. «إنما يستجيب الذين يسمعون والموتى يبعثهم الله» [filed under 6:36, 6:52]
#64 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Ṭard means expulsion accompanied by humiliation; ṭarīd means an expelled person, and the author notes tribal names derived from these terms. «الطرد الإبعاد بإهانة والطريد المطرود»
#65 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · سعد بن أبي وقاص · reports · Saʿd ibn Abi Waqqas says the verse was revealed about six poor believers, including himself, Ibn Masʿud, Suhayb, ʿAmmar, al-Miqdad, and Bilal, after Quraysh demanded their expulsion. «قال سعد بن أبي وقاص: نزلت فينا ستة في وفي ابن مسعود وصهيب وعمار والمقداد وبلال قالت قريش»
#66 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · خباب بن الأرت · reports · Khabbab says the verse concerned weak believers whom the Prophet taught morning and evening; elite visitors al-Aqraʿ and ʿUyayna objected to sitting with them. «وقال خباب بن الأرت: فينا نزلت كنا ضعفاء عند النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم يعلمنا بالغداة والعشي»
#67 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · rejects · The author doubts this version because the verse is Meccan while those elites were not warned until Medina. «وهذا فيه بعد، لأن الآية مكية وهؤلاء الأشراف لم ينذروا إلا بالمدينة.»
#68 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · خباب بن الأرت · reports · A related report says the Prophet would rise and leave the poor believers, after which the instruction to remain with those who call on God was revealed. «وفي رواية عن خباب فإذا أراد أن يقوم قام وتركنا فأنزل الله تعالى واصبر نفسك»
#69 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن عباس · reports · Al-ʿAwfi's report from Ibn ʿAbbas says nobles would believe if the poor were moved back so they could pray behind the nobles; expulsion meant moving them from the row, not the gathering. «وروى العوفي عن ابن عباس أن ناسا من الأشراف قالوا: نؤمن بك وإذا صلينا خلفك فأخر»
#70 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Despite variations in the reports, their common point is that Arab notables asked the Prophet to send the poor believers away, prompting the verse. «ومضمونها أن ناسا من أشراف العرب سألوا من الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم طرد فقراء المؤمنين عنه فنزلت»
#71 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · After commanding warning for the unpious, God honors the pious and forbids their expulsion, describing their outward and inward sincerity in worship. «ولما أمر تعالى بإنذار غير المتقين لعلهم يتقون أردف ذلك بتقريب المتقين وإكرامهم ونهاه عن طردهم»
#72 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن عامر · reports · The majority read بالغداة; Ibn ʿAmir and several others read بالغدوة, Abu ʿAbd al-Rahman is also reported without the final هاء, and Ibn Abi ʿAbla reads plural forms بالغدوات والعشيات. «وقرأ الجمهور بالغداة. وقرأ ابن عامر وأبو عبد الرحمن ومالك بن دينار والحسن ونصر بن عاصم وأبو رجاء العطاردي بالغدوة.»
