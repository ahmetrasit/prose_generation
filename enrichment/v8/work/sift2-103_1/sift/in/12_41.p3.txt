#122 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatadah identifies the doomed man as the baker and the restored man as the cupbearer; both denied having dreamed, but Joseph's prediction stood. «عن قتادة رضي الله عنه قال: قال يوسف عليه السلام للخباز: إنك تصلب فتأكل الطير من رأسك»
#123 DURR-FULL d.911 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima recited the clause with the wording أما أحدكما فيسقي ربه خمرا. «عن عكرمة رضي الله عنه أنه قرأ {أما أحدكما فيسقي ربه خمرا}»
#124 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · After proving his knowledge, Joseph resumes the answer with a repeated address because interpretation is a new topic; he means the cupbearer without naming him, trusting the interpretation and observing good companionship. «بعد تحقيق الحق وبيانه لهما مقدار علمه الواسع شرع في إنبائهما»
#125 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Joseph interprets the cupbearer’s vine as the king and restored favor; three branches mean three days until release and return to service. The baker’s three baskets mean three days until release and crucifixion, with birds eating his head. «روي أنه عليه السلام قال له : ما رأيت من الكرمة وحسنها هو الملك»
#126 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · A reading has yusqā passive; another uses active asqā. Saqā and asqā can both mean giving drink, though convention distinguishes giving a drink from providing watering; ʿIkrima is also reported to read passively with riyyihi as second object. «وقريء ( فيسقى ) بضم الياء والبناء للفاعل من أسقى»
#127 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Quḍiya means completed or settled: the matter the two men asked about has been decided. «قضي ( أتم وأحكم ( الأمر الذي فيه تستفتيان»
#128 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The prisoners' visions foreshadow one man's rescue and the other's destruction; their questioning asks for the visions' interpretation. «نجاة أحدكما وهلاك الآخر ومعنى استفتائهما فيه سؤالهما عنه»
#129 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The report of Joseph's answer may be a rebuttal to the prisoners' denial or a confirmation of his interpretation; a lie by the cupbearer could have protected his companion, and another report says the baker was the one who denied it. «يحتمل أن يكون للرد عليهما حسبما ورد في الأثر يحتمل أن يكون تحقيقا لتعبيره»
#130 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The episode is cited for the maxim that dreams occur as first interpreted, expressed in the saying that a dream rests on a bird's wing until told, then falls. «المنام على جناح طائر إذا قص وقع»
#131 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud reports that the two prisoners feigned dreams to test Joseph; after he interpreted them, they said they had been playing and had seen nothing. «ما رأى صاحبا يوسف شيئا إنما تحالما ليجربا علمه»
#132 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعض المحققين · reports · An unnamed investigator says the settled matter is the dreams themselves, not the men's eventual fates, because an inquiry seeks an answer about an incident, not its outcome. «ذهب بعض المحققين إلى أن المراد به ما رأياه من الرؤيتين ونفى أن يكون المراد ما يؤول إليه أمرهما»
#133 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The investigator argues that asking for interpretation means asking about the dreams as events, analogizing the king's request to be advised about his dream. «لأن الإستفتاء إنما يكون في الحادثة لا في حكمها»
#134 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعض المحققين · reports · He reads the wording as magnifying the dreams: the text calls them an affair and their request an inquiry because such inquiry concerns obscure, momentous cases; the present tense prolongs the request until Joseph finishes answering. «وعبر عن ذلك بالأمر وعن طلب تأويله بالإستفتاء تهويلا لأمره وتفخيما لشأنه»
#135 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعض المحققين · reports · He explains the singular 'matter' despite two dreams as matching the prisoners' own singular wording, and says they asked about the image of the outcome rather than their crime or its cause. «على حسب ما وحداه في قولهما»
#136 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Al-Alusi objects that the investigator's distinction between the matter and the event is inconsistent: if the outcome and dream are treated as identical for predication, they can also be identical for inquiry. «بعد اعتبار العينية بين شيئين يكون صحة نسبة ما هو من أحوال أحدهما إلى الآخر»
#137 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الكثير · prefers · Many exegetes take the matter as the men's eventual outcome and understand the preposition causally: they asked about the dreams in order to learn their fate; Al-Alusi finds this the stronger, less strained reading. «ويرجح ما ذهب إليه الكثير أن فيه سلامة من نزع الخف قبل الوصول إلى الماء»
#138 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعضهم · reports · Another view takes the matter as the crime for which the men were accused and imprisoned, supplying an omitted noun meaning the consequence of that accusation. «المراد بالأمر ما اتهما به والكلام حينئذ على حذف مضاف»
#139 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Joseph's vocative stresses the importance of his interpretation and addresses them as fellow prisoners. «افتتح خطابهما بالنداء اهتماما بما يلقيه إليهما من التعبير»
#140 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · If Joseph spoke in the order shown, he gave both interpretations together in a veiled summary so the condemned man could work out his fate gradually. «في عبارة واحدة مجملة ، لأن في تأويل إحدى الرؤيين»
#141 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Alternatively, the verse may condense Joseph's original speech, in which he directly assigned each interpretation to one man. «كان في الآية إيجاز لحكاية كلام يوسف عليه السلام»
#142 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · “The matter is decided” confirms the interpretation because their urgent question concerned what would happen to them in prison. «تحقيق لما دلت عليه الرؤيا»
#143 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Istiftāʾ is asking for a fatwa: an explanation that resolves difficulty or uncertainty; the verb aftā retains its hamza and is linked by some to fatā, youth, because clear explanation strengthens the recipient. «الإخبار بإزالة مشكل ، أو إرشاد إلى إزالة حيرة»
#144 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The two predictions in this verse interpret the two prisoners’ dreams respectively: the cupbearer’s dream of pressing wine and the other prisoner’s dream of birds eating from his head. «معنى الآية ظاهر، وقرينة المناسبة قاضية»
#145 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو جعفر عليه السلام · reports · The same report identifies the two young men as the king’s servants, a baker and a cupbearer, and says the baker had fabricated the dream and was the one crucified. «ودخل معه السجن فتيان يقول عبدان للملك أحدهما خباز والآخر صاحب الشراب» [filed under 12:36, 12:41]
#146 TABATABAI d.1402 · أئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام · reports · An Imami report says the second prisoner admitted that he had invented his dream after hearing that he would be crucified; Joseph replied that the matter he had asked about was already decreed. «ما ورد من الرواية من طرق أئمة أهل البيت عليهم السلام أن الثاني من الصاحبين قال له إني كذبت»
#147 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The view that Joseph interpreted the dreams only by conjecture conflicts with his declaration that the matter was settled and God’s statement that He taught him interpretation. «أنه صرح لهما بعلمه في قوله» [filed under 12:41, 12:42]

#149 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Prophets seize opportunities to invite people to God's message; Joseph preached monotheism before interpreting the prisoners' dreams. «he first delivered the message of monotheism to them and after that intepreted their dream for them.»
#150 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · reports · Ibn Ashur reports that Egyptians of the time had about thirty gods representing natural forces or stars. «Rivayete göre o dönemde Mısırlılar’ın otuz dolayında tanrıları vardı; bunlar farklı tabiat kuvvetlerini veya bazı yıldızları temsil ediyorlardı (İbn Âşûr, XII, 276)» [filed under 12:39, 12:40, 12:41]
#151 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Joseph interprets one prisoner’s dream as his return to serving his master and serving wine, and the other’s as crucifixion with birds pecking his head. «birinin daha önce olduğu gibi efendisinin hizmetine gireceğini ve ona şarap sunacağını; diğerinin ise asılacağını, kafasını kuşların didikleyeceğini söylemiştir»
#152 STUDYQURAN · N and Kl · reports · The wine carrier would survive and regain his former post serving wine to Egypt’s king. «former position (N) as a wine server to the king of Egypt (Kl;»
#153 STUDYQURAN · My · reports · The baker would be crucified for his crime, and birds would eat from his head after death. «the baker would be crucified for his crime, and after his death the birds would eat from his head (My;»
