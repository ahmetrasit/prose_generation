#33 CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · Natural dualities such as day/night and male/female form a cosmic pattern for opposing human aims and their contrasting afterlife recompense. «das den gesamten natürlichen Kosmos durchziehende Phänomen der Dualität erscheint hier als makrokosmisches Gegenstück des moralischen Kontrasts von gut und böse» [filed under 93:1-2]
#34 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · In this sura, the dawn/night contrast anticipates the contrasts between afterlife and worldly life and between prior lack and divinely given fulfillment. «Denn der Surenfortgang ist maßgeblich durch Kontrastierungen geprägt: Dies gilt bereits für V. 4 mit seiner Entgegensetzung von Jenseits und Diesseits» [filed under 93:1-2]
#35 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Whether dawn and night are creation-theological here remains uncertain, while a specific liturgical reading is problematic without contemporary evidence for vigils. «auch für 93:1.2 eine spezifisch liturgische Deutung des Gegensatzes von Morgen und Nacht problematisch» [filed under 93:1-2]
#36 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Group II accumulative oaths usually name basic cosmic phenomena, especially paired opposites such as day and night or sun and moon. «Fast alle in Gruppe II begegnenden Schwüre ähneln strukturell stark 93:1.2: Als Schwurgegenstände erscheinen gewöhnlich grundlegende kosmische Gegebenheiten» [filed under 93:1-2]
#37 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Groups IIIa and IIIb also use cosmic oath images, but mostly in isolated verses, making them less prominent than group II series. «Auch in den Gruppen IIIa und IIIb erscheinen noch ähnliche Schwüre bei Himmelsphänomenen» [filed under 93:1-2]
#38 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · A second accumulative type is iconic: it names sacred or eschatological realities, as in the figs, olives, Sinai, and Mecca in 95:1-3. «Sie lassen sich am ehesten als ‚ikonische’ Schwüre beschreiben, die keine kosmischen, sondern sakral oder eschatologisch konnotierte Gegebenheiten benennen» [filed under 93:1-2]
#39 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The relation between cosmic oath objects and a sura's message is often unclear, unlike religious or eschatological objects such as Judgment Day or Sinai. «Anders als bei religiösen oder eschatologischen Schwurgegenständen wie dem Jüngsten Tag (85:2, 75:1) oder dem Berg Sinai (95:2) liegt der inhaltliche Bezug zum Rest der Sure» [filed under 93:1-2]
#40 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Cosmic opposites such as night and day may have belonged to the conventional language of pre-Islamic soothsayer oaths. «koranische Schwurgegenstände wie Tag und Nacht oder Sonne und Mond bereits aus altarabischen Orakelsprüchen vertraut gewesen sein dürften» [filed under 93:1-2]
#41 CORPUSCORANICUM · reports · Transmitted examples of soothsayer sayings invoke light and darkness, times of day, celestial phenomena, and the Kaaba, though their authenticity is uncertain. «deren Authentizität vorerst allerdings nicht sicher zu beurteilen ist» [filed under 93:1-2]
#42 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Such obvious cosmic realities make apt oath objects because they can affirm an important statement with self-evident force. «Sie stellen Gegebenheiten von schlechthinniger Evidenz dar, die sich als solche zur Bekräftigung bedeutsamer Äußerungen anbieten» [filed under 93:1-2]
#43 CORPUSCORANICUM · rejects · The Quranic oath series should not be reduced to leftovers from pre-Islamic oracular speech; their images may contribute to the message itself. «Die Konstatierung einer wahrscheinlichen literargeschichtlichen Kontinuität zwischen koranischen und vorkoranischen Schwüren sollte allerdings nicht dazu verleiten» [filed under 93:1-2]
#44 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Unlike 94 and 108, this sura signals revelation through an oath series associated with pre-Islamic soothsayer speech; that signal may explain its third-person reference to God. «Anders als die verwandten Suren 94 und 108, die ihren Offenbarungsanspruch durch einen unvermittelten Einsatz im Register göttlicher Wir-Rede deutlich machen» [filed under 93:1-2]
#45 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The contrast between dawn and night anticipates later contrasts: afterlife versus worldly life, divine fulfillment after lack, and hardship versus ease. «Der Kontrast zwischen diesen beiden gegensätzlichen Zeitangaben präfiguriert den im weiteren Textverlauf erscheinenden Gegensatzpaaren» [filed under 93:1-2]
#46 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Sūrat al-Ḍuḥā and the following Sūrah Alam Nashraḥ form a pair assuring the Prophet of success; the former reflects intense Makkan opposition, the latter emerging signs of success. «form a  pair. In» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 94:1, 94:2]
#47 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The discourse moves from day and night as models of developmental trials, to reassurance, promised better times, examples of past divine help, and duties arising from those favours. «Following is the sequence of the discourse» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3, 93:4, 93:5, 93:6, 93:7, 93:8, 93:9, 93:10, 93:11]
#48 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-Ḍuḥā is mid-morning, when people begin their daily work with renewed energy after a night’s rest. «refers to the time of mid-morning when people begin their daily routines and after resting the whole night start their day with a new vigour»
#49 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Day and night complement one another as divine mercy: night gives rest and day permits seeking bounty, sustaining the world together. «It is God’s great mercy that He created» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#50 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirlerin çoğu · reports · Most commentators take duha as the whole daytime, the counterpart of night in verse 2. «Duhâ kelimesi “kuşluk” anlamına gelmekle birlikte çoğu müfessirler, 2. âyetteki “gece”nin alternatifi olarak burada bütünüyle gündüz vakti için kullanıldığı kanaatindedirler»
#51 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Âşûr · reports · Ibn Ashur takes duha as morning here too, alluding to revelation's light spreading through the world like the morning sun covering the earth. «İbn Âşûr’a göre ise kelime burada da kuşluk vaktini ifade etmekte olup, bununla, tıpkı kuşluk vakti güneş ışığının yeryüzünü bütünüyle kaplaması gibi vahiy ışığının da dünyaya inip aydınlatmaya başladığına imada bulunulmuştur»
#52 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · أهل التفسير · reports · Exegetes report that revelation paused for days, distressing the Prophet, while opponents claimed his Lord had forsaken him; the surah responds and honors him. «يذكر أهل التفسير أن الوحي أبطأ على رسول الله» [filed under 93:1-3]
#53 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · The pause was a test of whether the Prophet valued revelation despite its burden, teaching preachers to persevere through hardship in their mission. «انه اختبار من الله تعالى للرسول الكريم» [filed under 93:1-3]
#54 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · Duḥā is the time when the sun rises high after sunrise. «الضحى في اللغة: هو وقت ارتفاع الشمس بعد الشروق»
#55 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · The oath contrasts the light of revelation, represented by the forenoon, with its interruption, represented by night; the order reflects revelation's prior illumination. «والضحى هنا يمثل نور الوحي وإشراقه» [filed under 93:1-3]
#56 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · Duḥā means the sun's bright rising and the start of people's activity, paired against the still night; daylight spans the whole day and fajr precedes the sun's light. «الضحى هو وقت إشراق الشمس أما النهار فهو كل الوقت من أول النهار» [filed under 93:1-2]
#57 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · The revelation’s light is represented by forenoon; the quiet night followed the revelation, so the order of the oath reflects the later interruption of revelation. «انقطاع الوحي هو الذي تأخر وليس العكس لذا جاء قسم الضحى أولاً ثم الليل» [filed under 93:1-2]
#58 STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Some identify morning brightness with the morning God spoke to Moses and night with the Night Journey and Ascension; others identify it as the moment Moses' opponents recognized truth and prostrated. «others say al-ḍuḥā refers to the hour at which» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#59 STUDYQURAN · al-Shawkānī · reports · When al-ḍuḥā precedes the night, al-Shawkānī says it refers to the whole day. «According to al-Shawkānī, when al-ḍuḥā precedes the mention of night, as in this context, it refers to the entire day.»
#60 STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Other symbolic readings make morning the Garden's light and night the Fire's darkness, or light in knowers' hearts and blackness in disbelievers' hearts. «It is also said that al-ḍuḥā is the light of the Garden and the night is the darkness of the Fire» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#61 STUDYQURAN · reports · Al-Ḍuḥā is early Makkan and ranked fourth by many, seventh by others; it may be the first sūrah revealed whole, though others give that distinction to al-Fātiḥah. «considered by many commentators to be fourth (others say seventh) in the chronological order of revelation and perhaps the first sūrah to be revealed in full at one time» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#62 STUDYQURAN · most · reports · The title most commonly means the rising sun's light; it can also mean the period from daybreak to noon, the whole sunlit day, or all creation made visible by sunlight. It also names a late-morning supererogatory prayer. «most common understanding of al-ḍuḥā as a reference to the light of the rising sun, but it can also refer to the period from daybreak until noon»
#63 STUDYQURAN · several commentators · reports · One occasion account says revelation paused for twelve, forty, or fifty days, after which an idolater taunted Muhammad that his satan had forsaken him, prompting reassurance. «According to several commentators, the Prophet had not received a revelation for some time (some say twelve days, others forty, and others fifty)» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
