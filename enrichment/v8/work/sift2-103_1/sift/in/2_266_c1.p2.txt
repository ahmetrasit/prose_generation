#84 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · أكثر المفسرين · reports · They connect this parable to 2:264: it pictures ostentatious spending that is nullified by reproach and harm. «راجعة في المعنى إلى قوله: {لا تبطلوا صدقاتكم بالمن والأذى}»
#85 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · مجاهد · reports · He sees a person neglecting obedience to God and occupied with worldly pleasures, only to face immense regret in the afterlife. «هذا مثل للمفرط في طاعة الله، المشتغل بملاذ الدنيا»
#86 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · ابن عباس · reports · He sees someone who ends a good life with corruption: Satan leads him into sin late in life until he dies in it, like the fiery whirlwind destroying the orchard. «هو مثل الذي يختم عمله بفساد، وكان يعمل عملا صالحا»
#87 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الفراء · reports · He explains past وأصابه الكبر after future أيود through the near-equivalent conditional particles أن and لو; examples occur in 2:221, 30:51, and 68:9. «عطف بماض على مستقبل، قال الفراء»
#88 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · An إعصار is a strong rotating wind rising like a column, also called a whirlwind; Ibn al-Aʿrabi records عصار and its use for a dust-raising wind. «ريح ترتفع وتستدير نحو السماء كأنها عمود»
#89 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · المفسرون · reports · The man is old and unable to work, with dependent young children; fire destroys his orchard when he needs it most, leaving the family helpless, just as hypocrites' deeds are nullified when repentance is no longer possible. «قال المفسرون: مثلهم كمثل رجل»
#90 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · God explains signs through accounts such as the passerby at the ruined town and Abraham, clarifying the proofs people need concerning divine oneness. «من قصة الذي يمر على قرية، وقصة إبراهيم»
#91 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · The orchard owner is old and unable to earn, has dependent young children, and loses his fruit garden to a fiery whirlwind when he needs it most. «فقدها أحوج ما كان إليها عند كبر السن وكثرة العيال وطفولة الولد»
#92 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · The owner and children are helpless and bewildered; similarly, God nullifies hypocrites' and show-offs' deeds, leaving them no repentance or reprieve from their sins. «كذلك يبطل الله عمل المنافق والمرائي»
#93 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · The verse's explanatory stories clarify God's signs concerning his oneness. «كمثل بيان هذه الأقاصيص { يبين الله لكم»
#94 DAMGHANI d.478 · holds · Iʿsar as a violent wind, hot or cold: 2:266 'a whirlwind with fire' means a strong wind. «فاصابها اعصار فيه نار فاحترقت يعني ريحا شديدة باردة او حارة»
#95 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · This parable continues the warning against nullifying charity through reminders and harm; the garden owner has a small, weak family. «أيود يعني: أيحب أحدكم أن تكون له جنة»
#96 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · An i'sar is a violent whirlwind rising like a column; the burning whirlwind represents a hypocrite's or show-off's apparently good work being destroyed when most needed. «الريح العاصف التي ترتفع إلى السماء كأنها عمود»
#97 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · The elderly owner cannot restore the burned garden and his weak children cannot help him, leaving them helpless; likewise, the hypocrite's work is void when no aid, repentance, or reprieve remains. «فصار أحوج ما يكون إليها وضعف عن إصلاحها لكبره وضعف أولاده»
#98 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · عبيد بن عمير · reports · Ubayd ibn Umayr narrates that Umar asked the Companions who the garden parable concerned; Ibn Abbas said it represented a show-off, and Umar likened it to a wealthy worshipper whose sins destroy his deeds. «قال عبيد بن عمير: قال عمر رضي الله عنه يوما لأصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
#99 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Palms and vines are singled out as the noblest and most useful trees, though the garden contains others; all fruits are then added. «لما كانا اكرم الشجر وأكثرها منافع خصهما بالذكر»
#100 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Fruits may mean all the benefits of the garden, as in the story of the two gardens in al-Kahf. «ويجوز ان يريد بالثمرات المنافع التي كانت تحصل له فيها»
#101 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The conjunction before ‘old age struck him’ can be circumstantial; alternatively, the wording follows the meaning of ‘would one wish if he had a garden and had grown old?’ «قلت الواو للحال لا للعطف ومعناه ان تكون له جنة وقد أصابه الكبر»
#102 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The interrogative hamza expresses disapproval; readings also have ‘gardens’ and ‘weak offspring.’ «للإنكار وقرىء له جنات وذرية ضعاف»
#103 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · An iʿṣār is a rotating wind that rises from the ground like a column into the sky. «الريح التى تستدير في الأرض ثم تسطع نحو السماء كالعمود»
#104 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The image concerns good deeds done without seeking God: on Judgment Day they are found void, producing the devastation of an elderly person whose dependent children lose their livelihood. «وهذا مثل لمن يعمل الأعمال الحسنة لا يبتغي بها وجه الله»
#105 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · ابن عباس · reports · Asked by Umar, Ibn Abbas interprets the image as a wealthy person whose good deeds are later drowned by sins after Satan leads him astray. «قال ضربت مثلا لعمل قال لأي عمل قال لرجل غني يعمل الحسنات»
#106 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · الحسن · reports · Al-Hasan says the old person with many children is most in need of his garden, just as a believer is most in need of his deeds when worldly life ends. «وإن احدكم والله أفقر ما يكون إلى عمله اذا انقطعت عنه الدنيا»
#107 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · السدي · reports · Al-Suddī says this is another parable for ostentatious spending; al-Ṭabarī prefers that view. «حكى الطبري عن السدي أن هذه الآية مثل آخر لنفقة الرياء، ورجح هو هذا القول،»
#108 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · الحسن · reports · Al-Ḥasan reads the word for garden in the plural. «وقرأ الحسن «جنات» بالجمع،»
#109 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · “From beneath them” means below the trees; the wāw in “and old age came upon him” and “he has weak offspring” introduces circumstantial clauses. «وقوله من تحتها هو تحت بالنسبة إلى الشجر، والواو في قوله وأصابه واو الحال،»
#110 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The wāw introduces a circumstantial clause, and ḍuʿafāʾ and ḍiʿāf are plural forms of ḍaʿīf. «وله وضعفاء جمع ضعيف وكذلك ضعاف»
#111 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · A Prophetic report says that intense heat is from Hell’s breath and that the Fire complained to its Lord. «كما تضمن قول النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: «إذا اشتد الحر فأبردوا عن الصلاة»
#112 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The iʿṣār is a violent, scorching wind that burns what it passes over. «فيها إحراق لكل ما مرت عليه»
#113 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن زيد · reports · Ibn Zayd reads the verse as a parable continuing the command not to nullify charity through reproach and harm. «وحكى عن ابن زيد أنه قرأ قول الله تعالى: يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تبطلوا صدقاتكم»
#114 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · prefers · Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers Ibn Zayd’s contextual reading and rejects a second specific parable for ostentation; more broadly, the image fits any hypocrite or unbeliever who thinks he has done good but finds nothing at the moment of need. «وهذا أبين من الذي رجح الطبري، وليست هذه الآية بمثل آخر لنفقة الرياء،»
#115 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · عمر بن الخطاب · reports · When ʿUmar asked the Companions what the verse meant and they deferred to God and His Messenger, he angrily demanded that they say either that they knew or did not know. «وقد سأل عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن هذه الآية»
#116 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās likens it to someone who does good throughout life but, near death, ends with the deeds of the damned; ʿUmar accepted this explanation. «فقال له ابن عباس هذا مثل ضربه الله كأنه قال: أيود أحدكم أن يعمل عمره»
#117 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن أبي مليكة · reports · Ibn Abī Mulayka reports that ʿUmar said the parable concerns one who does good until old age and then does evil when most in need of the good deeds. «وروى ابن أبي مليكة أن عمر تلا هذه الآية: أيود أحدكم، وقال: هذا مثل ضرب للإنسان»
#118 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The broad reading includes whatever the wording can cover; Mujāhid, Qatāda, and al-Rabīʿ share this general interpretation. «فهذا نظر يحمل الآية على كل ما يدخل تحت ألفاظها»
#119 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · مجاهد وقتادة والربيع وغيرهم · reports · They give an interpretation along these lines of a person’s good works being undone at the end. «وقال بنحو هذا مجاهد وقتادة والربيع وغيرهم»
#120 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Palms and grapevines are named for their superior standing among trees. «وخص النخيل والأعناب بالذكر لشرفهما وفضلهما على سائر الشجر»
#121 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The whirlwind’s fire may be literal, or it may be Hell’s breath producing a similar effect. «فإما أنه نار على حقيقته وإلا فهو نفسها يوجد عنه كاثرها»
#122 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · عبيد الله بن الحسن العنبري · reports · The judge ʿUbayd Allāh ibn al-Ḥasan al-ʿAnbarī interprets the clouds as being compressed by the winds. «وبهذا فسر عبيد الله بن الحسن العنبري القاضي»
#123 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن سيده · reports · Ibn Sīda reports that some exegetes explain muʿṣirāt as winds rather than clouds. «وحكى ابن سيده أن المعصرات فسرها قوم بالرياح لا بالسحاب»
#124 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj defines an iʿṣār as a violent wind rising from earth to sky, called a zawbaʿa. «وقال الزجاج: الإعصار الريح الشديدة تصعد من الأرض إلى السماء وهي التي يقال لها الزوبعة،»
#125 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · المهدوي · reports · Al-Mahdawī says the wind is called an iʿṣār because it twists like cloth being wrung; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this explanation weak. «قال المهدوي: قيل لها إعصار لأنها تلتف كالثوب إذا عصر»
#126 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · rejects · He regards the derivation from cloth being wrung as weak. «قال القاضي أبو محمد: وهذا ضعيف،»
#127 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · “Thus” points to the parables just described; “perhaps” expresses hope from a human perspective, since reflection may make a person fit to think. «والإشارة بذلك إلى هذه الأمثال المبينة، ولعلكم ترج في حق البشر،»
