# 162 notes filed under 18:19 (group 3 of 3 of this verse's notes)
verse 18:19: وَكَذَٰلِكَ بَعَثْنَٰهُمْ لِيَتَسَآءَلُوا۟ بَيْنَهُمْ ۚ قَالَ قَآئِلٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ كَمْ لَبِثْتُمْ ۖ قَالُوا۟ لَبِثْنَا يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ ۚ قَالُوا۟ رَبُّكُمْ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا لَبِثْتُمْ فَٱبْعَثُوٓا۟ أَحَدَكُم بِوَرِقِكُمْ هَٰذِهِۦٓ إِلَى ٱلْمَدِينَةِ فَلْيَنظُرْ أَيُّهَآ أَزْكَىٰ طَعَامًۭا فَلْيَأْتِكُم بِرِزْقٍۢ مِّنْهُ وَلْيَتَلَطَّفْ وَلَا يُشْعِرَنَّ بِكُمْ أَحَدًا

#1 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Calling the coin hadhihi suggests the speaker held it out; carrying provisions for livelihood is compatible with reliance on God, as the saying instructs one to tie the camel and trust. «وفي حملهم دليل على التأهب لأسباب المعاش لمن خرج من منزله بحمل النفقة»
#2 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعض الأجلة · reports · Some eminent scholars define the elect's trust as abandoning all causes, citing Khalid's drinking poison and reports of Saʿd, Abu Muslim, and Tamim facing extraordinary dangers. «إن توكل الخواص ترك الأسباب بالكلية»
#3 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The questioner is identified either as their leader Maksalmina or as Yamlikha, who managed their provisions. «قيل هو كبيرهم مكسلمينا وقيل صاحب نفقتهم يمليخا»
#4 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The questioner may have remarked on the unusual mismatch between their condition and what normally happens; another suggestion is that they were alarmed at missing prayer. «لما رأى من مخالفة حالهم لما هو المعتاد في الجملة»
#5 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · غير واحد · reports · They used aw for uncertainty because they could not establish how long they had slept; Alusi says sleep haze kept them from checking signs, whatever the time of sleep or waking. «أو للشك كما قاله غير واحد والمراد لم نتحقق مقدار لبثنا»
#6 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The well-known account has them sleep in the morning and wake late in the day; another says they could not tell whether it was the same or next day. «والمشهور أن نومهم كان غدوة وانتباههم كان آخر النهار وقيل فلم يدروا»
#7 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · One explanation takes aw as a correction: they first said a day, then noticed the sun had not set and corrected it to part of a day; Alusi maintains the straightforward reading is doubt. «ومن هنا قيل إن أو للإضراب وذلك أنهم لما انتبهوا آخر النهار»
#8 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو حيان · reports · Abu Hayyan reports a distributive reading: some said a day and others part of a day, each speaking from probable judgment; Alusi says this reading is unlikely and conjecture is unnecessary. «وحكى أبو حيان أنها للتفصيل على معنى قال بعضهم : لبثنا يوما»
#9 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · prefers · Their reply that God knows their stay best gently corrects the first group, displays proper etiquette, and marks the dialogue's two groups; the same speaker saying both is disfavored by the discourse structure. «وهذا رد منهم على الأولين على أحسن ما يكون من مراعاة حسن الأدب»
#10 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · The proposal that one person made both statements does not fit the passage's resumption and question-and-answer form. «وتعقب بأنه لا يساعده النظم الكريم فإن الاستئناف في الحكاية والخطاب في المحكي»
#11 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Their awakening is likened to their prolonged sleep; the correspondence is that both are signs of God's overwhelming power. «فالمشبه الإيقاظ والشبه به الإنامة المشار إليها ووجه الشبه كون كل منهما ءاية»
#12 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The purpose or consequence of awakening them was that they question one another, leading to the wisdoms narrated next; mentioning this effect also entails the other effects. «أي ليسأل بعضهم بعضا فيترتب عليه ما فصل من الحكم البالغة»
#13 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الخفاجي · prefers · Al-Khafaji favors reading the lām as consequential: questioning was an outcome, since God's purpose was to display His power rather than to cause their discussion. «وجعل غير واحد اللام للعاقبة واستظهره الخفاجي»
#14 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أحمد، إسحق · reports · Ahmad, Ishaq, and other imams allow those strong in certainty and reliance to cross deserts without provisions and to forgo earning or medical treatment; Ahmad defines trust as ending dependence on people. «وقد نص الإمام أحمد وإسحق وغيرهما من الأئمة على جواز دخول المفاوز بغير زاد»
#15 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتادة · prefers · Qatada interprets azka as best quality, which Alusi calls the best reading; it may also mean rice, dates, or raisins, though the youths are presumed to seek lawful food. «وقال قتادة : أي أجود وهو أجود»
#16 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The increase implied by zakāh can be spiritual and otherworldly, as in the reward and good outcome of seeking lawful food, or material and worldly, as in abundance, affordability, or quality. «وأصلها النمو والزيادة وهي تكون معنوية أخروية وحسية دنيوية»
#17 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Ay may be an interrogative subject with azka as predicate, or a relative object with an elided predicate; its suffix can refer to the city, its people, or the food understood from context. «وأي استفهام مبتدأ و ( أزكى ) خبره والجملة معلق عنها الفعل للاستفهام»
#18 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The pronoun in rizqan minhu may refer to the best food or to the coins, making min partitive, source-marking, or a substitute marker; in either case the requested provision should be lawful. «أي من ذلك الأزكى طعاما فمن لابتداء الغاية أو التبعيض»
#19 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · إبراهيم · reports · The account of Abraham refusing Gabriel's offer of help while being thrown into the fire illustrates reliance directly on God. «واستدل عليه بقول إبراهيم عليه السلام حين عرض له جبريل عليه السلام يوم ألقي في النار»
#20 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · The position that abandoning means of support is uniquely a Sufi doctrine is mistaken; it is also reported from others. «بل جاء عن غيرهم أيضا»
#21 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The city was their familiar home city, reportedly called Ephesus when they left and later Tarsus; this reconciles the two transmitted names. «وهي المدينة التي خرجوا منها قيل وتسمى الآن طرطوس وكان اسمها يوم خرجوا منها أفسوس»
#22 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Their immediate turn to obtaining food instead of investigating their situation exemplifies a wise redirection to what mattered then; the verse's fa also signals this. «وكان هذا القول صدر منهم إعراضا عن التعمق في البحث وإقبالا على ما يهمهم بحسب الحال»
#23 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says the people of the city sacrificed to idols; another report says they sacrificed pigs, so azka can mean lawful. «فإن أهل المدينة كانوا في عهدهم يذبحون للطواغيت كما روى سعيد بن منصور»
#24 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الضحاك · reports · Al-Dahhak says most of the townspeople's wealth had been usurped, supporting a request for the purest lawful food. «وقال الضحاك : إن أكثر أموالهم كانت مغصوبة»
#25 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن السائب، مقاتل · reports · Ibn al-Saʾib and Muqatil explain azka as more wholesome or pleasant; if it means lawful, it returns to the preceding interpretation. «وقال ابن السائب ومقاتل : أي أطيب»
#26 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · عكرمة، يمان بن ريان · reports · ʿIkrima says azka means more plentiful, while Yaman b. Rayyan says cheaper. «وقال عكرمة : أي أكثر وقال يمان بن ريان : أي أرخص»
#27 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The command to act gently means either to treat people tactfully so no quarrel exposes them, to hide their entry and exit, or to avoid being cheated; the next command warns not to let anyone in town notice them. «اللطف في المعاملة كيلا تقع خصومة تجر إلى معرفته أو ليتكلف اللطف في الاستخفاء دخولا وخروجا»
#28 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · On the concealment interpretations, wa-la-yushʿirannakum confirms and explains tact; the Imam takes it literally as do not inform anyone about you. «وفسره الإمام بلا يخبرن بكم أحدا فهو على ظاهره»
#29 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الحسن · reports · Al-Hasan reads the imperative lām in yatalattaf with kasra. «وقرأ الحسن ( وليتلطف ) بكسر لام الأمر»
#30 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتيبة الميال · reports · Qutayba al-Mayyāl reads yutalattaf with a passive verb and ḍamma on yāʾ. «وعن قتيبة الميال ( وليتلطف ) بضم الياء مبنيا للمفعول»
#31 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتيبة، أبو صالح، يزيد بن القعقاع · reports · They read yushʿirannakum as an active verb and aḥad as its nominative subject. «وقرأ هو وأبو صالح ويزيد بن القعقاع ( ولا يشعرن بكم أحد ) ببناء الفعل للفاعل ورفع أحد»
#32 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن العربي · reports · Ibn al-ʿArabi calls the passage the strongest proof for agency; al-Kiya also derives the permissibility of pooling group coins, jointly buying and sharing food, with an eater allowed extra money for his portion. «قال ابن العربي : وهي أقوىءاية في ذلك وفيها كما قال الكيا دليل على جواز خلط دراهم الجماعة»
#33 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Al-Ālūsī criticizes a shaykh who accepted money certainly taken from an oppressor, claiming the money was like hot coals that cooled hungry seekers, despite the shrine and votive offerings later attached to him. «والعجب أن رجلا من المتشيخين كان يأخذ من بعض الظلمة دنانير مقطوعا بحرمتها»
#34 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Gentle dealing with the food seller teaches good manners and kindness. «ورابعا إلى أنه ينبغي لهم التواصي بحسن الخلق وجميل الرفق»
#35 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعض أهل التأويل · reports · Some exegetes say the command means to select delicate food because they had not eaten and coarse food might harm their bodies; others say their spirits from the sacred realm suit only delicate things. «وقال بعض أهل التأويل : إنه أمر باختيار اللطيف من الطعام لأنهم لم يأكلوا مدة»
#36 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · يوسف بن الحسين · reports · Yūsuf ibn al-Ḥusayn says to buy delicate food for gnostics, while ascetics may eat whatever is found because they are still disciplining themselves; another view contrasts coarse diets of strivers with the gnostic's preference for delicacy. «وعن يوسف بن الحسين أنه كان يقول : إذا اشتريت لأهل المعرفة شيئا من الطعام فليكن لطيفا»
