#142 DURR-FULL d.911 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima identifies the dawn as morning, and elsewhere as dawn at Muzdalifa on the morning of the gathering. «عبد بن حميد عن عكرمة في قوله:»
#143 DURR-FULL d.911 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says the dawn is specifically that of the Sacrifice Day, not every dawn; Muhammad ibn Ka'b agrees. «ابن أبي حاتم عن مجاهد في قوله»
#144 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas identifies the dawn as the dawn prayer. «وأخرج ابن جرير عن ابن عباس»
#145 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas identifies the dawn as Muharram, the first dawn of the year. «عن ابن عباس في قوله»
#146 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · The Prophet said that, if he lived, he would order fasting one day before or after Ashura. «وأخرج البيهقي عن ابن عباس أن رسول الله»
#147 DURR-FULL d.911 · إبراهيم بن محمد بن المنتشر · reports · A traditional saying held that generosity to one's family on Ashura brings abundant provision throughout the year. «وأخرج البيهقي عن إبراهيم بن محمد بن المنتشر»
#148 DURR-FULL d.911 · عروة عن ابن عباس · reports · A report, which al-Bayhaqi weakened, says applying antimony to the eyes on Ashura prevents eye inflammation forever. «وأخرج البيهقي وضعفه عن عروة عن ابن عباس»
#149 DURR-FULL d.911 · جابر · reports · The Prophet interpreted the ten nights as the first ten of Dhu al-Hijjah, the odd as Arafah, and the even as the Day of Sacrifice. «أخرج أحمد والنسائي والبزار وابن جرير وابن المنذر وابن مردويه والحاكم وصححه والبيهقي في الشعب عن جابر» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3]
#150 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · He urged fasting the ninth and tenth of Muharram to differ from the Jews. «وأخرج البيهقي عن ابن عباس قال:»
#151 DURR-FULL d.911 · أبو جبلة · reports · While traveling with Ibn Shihab, he saw him fast Ashura; Ibn Shihab said Ramadan could be made up later, but Ashura would pass. «وأخرج البيهقي عن أبي جبلة قال:»
#152 DURR-FULL d.911 · أبو موسى · reports · Because Jews honored Ashura as a festival, the Prophet told Muslims to fast it. «وأخرج ابن أبي شيبة عن أبي موسى»
#153 DURR-FULL d.911 · أبو هريرة · reports · The Prophet described Ashura as a day the prophets used to fast and told Muslims to fast it. «وأخرج ابن أبي شيبة عن أبي هريرة»
#154 DURR-FULL d.911 · جابر · reports · The Prophet promised that whoever gives generously to family on Ashura will receive provision throughout the year. «وأخرج البيهقي عن جابر قال:»
#155 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن مسعود · reports · The Prophet promised yearlong provision to whoever expands provision for dependents on Ashura. «وأخرج البيهقي عن ابن مسعود قال:»
#156 DURR-FULL d.911 · أبو سعيد الخدري · reports · The Prophet promised that whoever gives generously to family on Ashura will receive provision for the rest of the year. «وأخرج ابن أبي الدنيا والبيهقي عن أبي سعيد الخدري»
#157 DURR-FULL d.911 · أبو هريرة · reports · The Prophet promised yearlong provision for generosity on Ashura; al-Bayhaqi said the individually weak chains gain strength together. «وأخرج البيهقي عن أبي هريرة قال:»
#158 DURR-FULL d.911 · أبو سعيد الخدري · reports · A report attributed to the Prophet identifies the ten as the ten of sacrifice, the pair with “We created you in pairs,” and the odd as God. «عن عطية في قوله: {والفجر} قال: هذا الذي تعرفون {وليال عشر} قال: عشر الأضحى» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3]
#159 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن مسعود · reports · His recitation ran from the dawn oath through the night oath; he said these oaths affirm that the Lord is ever watchful. «أخرج ابن المنذر عن ابن مسعود أنه قرأ» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4, 89:5]
#160 ITQAN d.911 · unnamed · reports · Oath by times such as dawn implies rites of the Hajj, which are pure worship, and honours Muhammad and Abraham. «فإنها أزمان تتضمن أفعالا معظمة من المناسك وشعائر الحج»
#161 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The false dawn resembles a wolf’s tail and carries no legal ruling; the true dawn triggers fasting and prayer. Its emergence spreads light and sends people and animals out for provision, resembling resurrection. «فجران مستطيل كذنب السرحان وهو الكاذب ولا يتعلق به حكم ومستطير وهو الصادق الذي يتعلق به الصوم والصلاة»
#162 BURSEVI d.1127 · الكاشفي · reports · Kashifi interprets dawn as the time when beloveds commune with God. «سوكند بصبح كه وقت مناجات دوستانست»
#163 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Other interpretations identify dawn as the morning of Arafah, when pilgrims go to the plain and perform supplications, citing the report “Hajj is Arafah.” «أو أقسم بصباح عرفة لأنه يوم شرف يتوجه فيه الحجاج إلى جبل عرفات»
#164 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Dawn may instead mean the Day of Sacrifice, a great day for the required circumambulation, shaving, and stoning; it is reported as the greater Hajj day. «أو صباح يوم النحر لأنه يوم عظيم أيضا ويقع فيه الطواف المفروض والحلق والرمي ويروى أن يوم النحر يوم الحج الأكبر»
#165 BURSEVI d.1127 · التبيان · reports · Tabyan offers further dawn images: the New Year, Friday as the poor person’s pilgrimage, miraculous water from the Prophet’s fingers or Moses’ stone, Salih’s she-camel from rock, springs, rain, or sinners’ tears. «انفجار آب از أصابع حضرت رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم»
#166 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The dawn imagery also urges repentance: if you carry a stain, wash it away with streams of tears. «بران ازدوسر شمه ديده جوى ورآلايشى داري ازخود بشوى»
#167 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الجمهور · reports · The Fajr sūrah is Meccan according to most interpreters, though ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭalḥah says it is Medinan; verse counts vary by regional enumeration. «مكية في قول الجمهور وقال علي بن أبي طلحة مدنية وآيها اثنتان وثلاثون آية في الحجازي»
#168 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The sūrah follows Ghāshiya's two kinds of faces by describing the arrogant deniers, then the blessed class through “O tranquil soul”; al-Suyūṭī sees its opening oath as affirming the previous sūrah's promise and threat and notes the parallel opening questions. «ولما ذكر سبحانه فيما قبلها وجوه يومئذ خاشعة ووجوه يومئذ ناعمة أتبعه تعالى بذكر الطوائف المكذبين»
#169 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · علي وابن عباس وابن الزبير · reports · The dawn is the familiar dawn, as transmitted from Ali, Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr, and others; Alusi considers the true dawn especially fitting for the oath because it governs fasting and prayer. «كما روي عن علي كرم الله تعالى وجهه وابن عباس وابن الزبير»
#170 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Dawn is named for splitting the night broadly; false dawn does not determine fasting or prayer, whereas true dawn does, and the likely referent is the true dawn in general. «وأصله شق الشيء شقا واسعاوسمى الصبح»
#171 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس ومجاهد · reports · Ibn Abbas and Mujahid identify the oath as the dawn of the Sacrifice day. «وعن ابن عباس ومجاهد فجر يوم النحر»
#172 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima identifies it as Friday's dawn. «عن عكرمة فجر يوم الجمعة»
#173 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الضحاك · reports · Al-Dahhak identifies it as the dawn of Dhu al-Hijja. «عن الضحاك فجر ذي الحجة»
#174 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · مقاتل · reports · Muqatil identifies it as the dawn of the night at Muzdalifa. «عن مقاتل فجر ليلة جمع»
#175 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · A report from Ibn Abbas identifies it as Muharram's dawn, the dawn of the year; a similar view is related from Qatada. «عن ابن عباس أنه قال هو فجر المحرم فجر السنة»
#176 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Other reports from Ibn Abbas take the dawn to mean the whole daytime, or the dawn prayer; the prayer interpretation is also related from Zayd ibn Aslam. «وعن الحبر أيضا أنه النهار كله وأخرج ابن جرير عنه أيضا»
#177 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Fajr is the first whiteness as night darkness breaks: the false dawn is vertical and the true dawn horizontal; fasting and prayer rulings attach only to the true dawn. «“Fecr” mâlum ki gece karanlığının çatladığı sabahın ilk beyazlığıdır»
#178 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · Jabir reports from the Prophet that the oath’s dawn and ten nights refer to the Adha ten; al-Tabari says this is correct because the authorities of interpretation agree. «Hazret-i Câbir’den de “Resûlullah sallallâhu aleyhi ve sellem عَشْرُ الأَْضْحَى,وَٱلْفَجْرِ وَلَيَالٍ عَشْرٍۢ’dır buyurdu” diye merfû‘an rivayet etmişlerdir.» [filed under 89:1, 89:2]
#179 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Dawn is the joyful instant when the world begins moving from darkness into light, so the generic dawn is a meaningful oath, as in 81:18. «Bu suretle fecir, cihânın zulmetten nûra geçmek üzere gülümsediği en neş’eli, mes‘ud bir demidir.»
#180 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · Some take the article as specific and understand the dawn of a blessed Friday or feast; Mujahid’s reported view most immediately points to the tenth of Dhul Hijjah, the Adha feast day. «Bu surette[^3] en mütebâdiri Mücâhid’den mervî olduğu üzere kurban bayramı gününün fecri olmaktır»
#181 ELMALILI d.1361 · prefers · Although generic dawn includes the feast dawn, the particular dawn more strongly conveys the collective joy of the occasion. «Evvelki buna dahi şâmil olursa da bunda daha ziyade bir hususiyet ve cem‘iyyet ile sürûra işaret vardır.»
#182 ELMALILI d.1361 · prefers · The reports identifying Dhul Hijjah’s nights, Arafah, and the sacrifice day best fit the sequence and surah’s purpose, include the possible senses, and urge worship during life’s limited span to attain afterlife joy. «Ma‘amâfîh Câbir ve İbn [5797] Abbas rivayetleri üzere aşr-i zilhicce geceleri ve arefe» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3]
#183 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The opening oaths direct attention to temporal events that show worldly change, move from darkness to light and grief to joy, and indicate the Creator’s lordship. «âlemdeki inkılâbâta delâlet eyleyen ve insanı zulmetten nûra, kederden sürûra götüren» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3]
