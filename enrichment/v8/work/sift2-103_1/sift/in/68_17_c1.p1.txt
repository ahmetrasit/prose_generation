<<notes for 68_17_c1, part 1 of 0..5>>
#40 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · المفسرون · reports · The exegetes say God tested the people of Mecca with famine as He tested the orchard owners: a group of Thaqif in Yemen who inherited farmland and gardens from their Muslim father, who gave the poor a share at harvest and processing. «كما بلونا} (¬1) الآية. قال المفسرون: بلونا أهل مكة بالجوع والقحط كما ابتلينا أصحاب الجنة بالجوع حين هلكت جنانهم. وهم قوم من ثقيف، كانوا باليمن»
#41 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · The heirs complained that their dependents were many and the wealth too little to keep giving to the poor as their father had, resolved to deprive them, and ended in ruin. «فقالت بنوه: العيال كثير، والمال قليل، ولا يسعنا أن نعطي المساكين كما كان يفعل أبونا. وعزموا على حرمان المساكين»
#42 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · مقاتل · reports · Muqatil says the story is a parable for Meccan unbelievers to reflect on and return to God. «قال مقاتل: وهذا مثل ضربه الله لكفار مكة ليعتبروا فيرجعوا،»
#43 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · المفسرون · reports · The exegetes name the orchard al-Darawan, near Sanaa; Saʿid ibn Jubayr puts it twelve miles away, while al-Kalbi says it was two farsakhs away and destroyed by fire. «قال المفسرون: وهي تسمى (¬6): الضروان # بقرب صنعاء»
#44 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · They swore to cut their fruit in the morning at a late-night or pre-dawn darkness called سدفة. «حلفوا ليقطعن ثمر نخلهم إذا أصبحوا بسدفة (¬4) من الليل.»
#45 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · مقاتل · reports · Muqatil says they planned to go secretly early and harvest without notifying the poor, whereas their father used to tell the poor so they would gather. «قال مقاتل: قالوا: اغدوا سرا إلى جنتكم فاصرموها ولا تؤذنوا المساكين وكان آباؤهم يخبرون المساكين»
#46 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · صرم means cutting a date bunch away from the palm; أرم النخل is used when the dates are ready to be cut. «ويقال: قد صرم العذق عن النخلة وأرم النخل إذا حان وقت صرامه»
#47 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · God tested Mecca's people with drought and famine as He tested orchard owners in Yemen by burning their garden. Their father used to feed the poor from it; after he died, his sons feared giving would leave them short, so they planned to harvest before the poor came. «{ إنا بلوناهم } امتحنا أهل مكة بالقحط والجوع { كما بلونا أصحاب الجنة } كما امتحنا أصحاب البستان بإحراقها»
#48 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · Trial, testing, and examination are synonyms; God's trial is the imposition of duties so acts occur and become manifest, although He knew beforehand they would occur, so testing is figurative of His treatment of the tested person. «والابتلاء من الله هو التعبد والتكليف، فكلفهم ليظهر من أعمالهم، فيعلم الله تعالى وقوعها كما علم من قبل»
#49 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · Ṣarm is cutting, especially harvesting date fruit; ṣarām and jadhādh name the harvest, and the separation between companions is also called ṣarm. «والصرم: أصله: القطع، والصرام والجذاذ: قطع ثمر النخل» [filed under 68:17, 68:22]
#50 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · The owners are blamed for denying the poor their due, swearing to do so, and omitting an exception; this supports requiring an exception when speaking about the future, since one cannot be sure the statement will come true. «ذمهم على منع الحق، وذمهم على القسم عليه، وذمهم على ترك الاستثناء» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#51 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · Intending an evil act is itself blameworthy: they were punished for the resolve even before committing the wrong, and actually taking property and denying rights is worse. «أن العزم على القبيح قبيح؛ لأنهم عوقبوا على عزمهم من غير منع وظلم بالمنع» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#52 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · God tested Mecca’s people with drought and hunger, as He tested the owners of the garden. «{إنا بلوناهم} يعني اختبرنا أهل مكة بالقحط والجوع {كما بلونا} ابتلينا {أصحاب الجنة}»
#53 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas recounts that a pious owner’s garden near Sanaa allowed poor people gleanings after harvest; his three heirs, deeming wealth too small for many dependents, swore to harvest before the poor without saying ‘if God wills,’ then found the garden burned black overnight. «عن ابن عباس: في قوله عز وجل: "إنا بلوناهم كما بلونا أصحاب الجنة" قال: كان بستان باليمن»
#54 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · ليصرمنها means they planned to cut and gather the fruit in the morning, before the poor learned of it. «أليجذنها وليقطعن ثمرها إذا أصبحوا قبل أن يعلم المساكين»
#55 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Mecca's people were tested with famine through the Prophet's supplication against them; the garden owners were pious worshippers near San'a. «إنا بلونا أهل مكة بالقحط والجوع بدعوة رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عليهم»
#56 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · حرد can mean a year withholding its good, camels withholding milk, or resolve and speed; the sons went out aiming to deprive the poor but found only deprivation themselves. «الحرد : من حردت السنة إذا منعت خيرها ؛ وحاردت الإبل إذا منعت درها»
#57 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Another reading على حرد means they could manage only anger at one another, as in the next phrase ‘blaming one another.’ «وقرىء : ( على حرد ) ، أي لم يقدروا إلا على حنق وغضب بعضهم على بعض»
#58 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Their father kept a year's food and gave the surplus to the poor, including missed grain, grapes, and dates; his sons feared hardship for their dependents and secretly swore to harvest early without leaving any share. «فكان يأخذ منها قوت سنته ويتصدق بالباقي ، وكان يترك للمساكين ما أخطأه المنجل»
#59 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · The sons did not make an exception in their oath, and God burned their garden; another report identifies them as Israelites. «فحلفوا ليصرمنها مصبحين في السدف خفية عن المساكين ، ولم يستثنوا في يمينهم»
#60 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · They planned to harvest early in the dark before dawn; not making an exception means not saying ‘if God wills.’ Though a condition, it functions as an exception because the meanings coincide. «{ مصبحين } داخلين في الصبح مبكرين { ولا يستثنون } ولا يقولون إن شاء الله»
#61 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The calamity that swept the garden was a trial or destruction; the reading طيف varies from طائف. «{ فطاف عليها } بلاء أو هلاك { طئف } كقوله تعالى : { وأحيط بثمره }»
#62 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · الصريم may mean a garden cut down, a night-blackened garden, dried daytime vegetation, an empty garden, or sandy ground; صارمين means harvesters. «{ فأصبحت كالصريم } كالمصرومة لهلاك ثمرها . وقيل : الصريم الليل»
#63 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The phrase غدوا على حرثكم uses على because they went to cut and harvest it; alternatively غدا is understood as ‘turned toward,’ as in the expression ‘the enemy went against them.’ «ويجوز أن يضمن الغدو معنى الإقبال»
#64 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · يتخافتون means whispering; خفي, خفت, and خفد all denote concealment, and الخفدود is a bat. «{ يتخافتون } يتسارون فيما بينهم . وخفى ، وخفت ، وخفد : ثلاثتها في معنى الكتم»
#65 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The phrase أن لا يدخلنها is explanatory; Ibn Masud reads without أن, supplying ‘they say.’ The ban on a poor person entering means not allowing him in. «وقرأ ابن مسعود بطرحها بإضمار القول ، أي تخافتون يقولون لا يدخلنها»
#66 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · A poetic line uses حرد for setting out quickly; birds called حراد are swift, so the sons thought they could promptly harvest and deny the poor their share. «أقبل سيل جاء من أمر الله … يحرد حرد الجنة المغلة»
#67 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · The verb يبدلنا has readings with and without doubling; راغبون إلى ربنا means seeking good from Him and hoping for His pardon. «{ أن يبدلنا } قرىء بالتشديد والتخفيف { إلى ربنا راغبون } طالبون منه الخير»
#68 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Their punishment resembles that of Mecca's people and the garden owners, but the punishment of the afterlife is greater. «{ كذلك العذاب } مثل ذلك العذاب الذي بلونا به أهل مكة وأصحاب الجنة عذاب الدنيا»
#69 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · قتادة · reports · When asked whether the garden owners belong to Paradise or Hell, Qatada replied that the question had burdened him. «وسئل قتادة عن أصحاب الجنة : أهم من أهل الجنة أم من أهل النار ؟ فقال : لقد كلفتني تعبا»
#70 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says the owners repented and were given a better garden. «وعن مجاهد : تابوا فأبدلوا خيرا منها»
#71 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud reports that they became sincere and God replaced the garden with one called al-Hayawan, whose grape clusters required a donkey to carry. «وروي عن ابن مسعود رضي الله عنه : بلغني أنهم أخلصوا وعرف الله منهم الصدق فأبدلهم بها جنة يقال لها الحيوان»
#72 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · حرد may instead be the proper name of the garden, or mean that they assumed their planned harvest and deprivation would succeed. «وقيل : { حرد } علم للجنة ، أي غدوا على تلك الجنة قادرين على صرامها عند أنفسهم»
#73 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · They first thought they had lost their way because the ruined garden looked unlike theirs; after recognizing it, they admitted they had been deprived through their own wrongdoing. «{ إنا لضالون } أي ضللنا جنتنا ، وما هي بها لما رأوا من هلاكها ؛ فلما تأملوا»
#74 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · أوسطهم means their most just and best member, from language for someone in the middle of their people; compare the description of the community as a middle community. «{ أوسطهم } أعدلهم وخيرهم ، من قولهم : هو من سطة قومه»
#75 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · prefers · لولا تسبحون most likely means remember God and repent of the corrupt plan; the speaker had urged them to repent before punishment, but they obeyed too late. «لولا تذكرون الله وتتوبون إليه من خبث نيتكم»
#76 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Another interpretation takes ‘glorify’ to mean making the exception ‘if God wills,’ since both entrusting matters to God and declaring His transcendence magnify Him. «وقيل : المراد بالتسبيح . الاستثناء لالتقائهما في معنى التعظيم لله»
