#46 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الفراء والزجاج · reports · They parse adadan as an adjective meaning counted years, with either an omitted noun or a passive verbal noun used as its object. «أنه نعت للسنين. المعنى: سنين ذات عدد، أي معدودة، هذا قول الفراء، والزجاج»
#47 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الزجاج · reports · He also allows adadan as an accusative cognate expression: We counted them with counting. «قال الزجاج: (ويجوز أن # ينتصب»
#48 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · God sealed their hearing with sleep for a counted number of years in the cave. «سددنا آذانهم بالنوم { في الكهف سنين عددا»
#49 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · ‘We struck over their ears’ is eloquent idiom for putting them to sleep; Qutrub compares it with striking someone's hand to prevent action. «والضرب معروف، (ضربنا على آذانهم) من فصيح الكلام أي: سلطت عليهم النوم،»
#50 JISHUMI d.494 · قطرب · reports · He compares the idiom with striking an official's or master's hand when stopping someone from acting. «قال قطرب: هو كقول العرب: ضرب الأمير على يد فلان إذا منعه عن العبث والفساد،»
#51 JISHUMI d.494 · الأسود بن يعفر · reports · His line about being struck upon the earth with barriers is evidence for the idiom; the source notes that he was blind. «قال الأسود بن يعفر وكان ضريرا: ومن الحوادث لا أبا لك أنني ... ضربت علي الأرض بالأسداد»
#52 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · A kahf is a cave in a mountain; rushd is reaching the path that leads to one's aim, opposite error. «والكهف: الغار في الجبل. والرشد: إصابة الطريق المؤدي إلى البغية،» [filed under 18:9-12]
#53 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · عجبا is the predicate of كان; رشدا is the object of هيئ; سنين is an accusative specification or governed by ضربنا; عددا describes the years or is a verbal noun, as Abū ʿUbayda says. «و عجبا نصب لأنه خبر (كان).» [filed under 18:9-12]
#54 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · Striking the ears means putting them to sleep; another interpretation is that sound was kept from reaching their hearing. «{فضربنا على آذانهم} أي: أنمناهم وألقينا عليهم النوم. وقيل: معناه منعنا نفوذ الأصوات إلى مسامعهم»
#55 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · The count of years either emphasizes their number or indicates a long period, since people do not usually count a small number. «وذكر العدد على سبيل التأكيد وقيل: ذكره يدل على الكثرة فإن القليل لا يعد في العادة.»
#56 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · God placed a barrier over their hearing, putting them into deep sleep in which sounds could not awaken them; the object, a covering, is omitted. «ضربنا عليها حجابا من أن تسمع ، يعني : أنمناهم إنامة ثقيلة»
#57 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Sinīna ʿadadan could mean many years or few, since many are enumerable; al-Zajjāj says a small number is known without counting, while a large number requires counting. «فيحتمل أن يريد الكثرة وأن يريد القلة»
#58 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · مجاهد عن ابن عباس · reports · In one account the youths lived under an idol-worshipping king; after learning belief from a grammarian or earlier believers, they rejected idolatry, were summoned and threatened with death, and answered with the words later quoted in 18:14-16. «روى مجاهد عن ابن عباس أن هؤلاء الفتية كانوا في دين ملك يعبد الأصنام ويذبح لها ويكفر بالله» [filed under 18:10-16]
#59 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · In one version the king delayed punishment, then the youths secretly planned to flee to a cave known to one of them. «فاذهبوا إلى منازلكم ودبروا رأيكم» [filed under 18:10-16]
#60 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Another account says the king's searchers found the cave and advised sealing it so hunger and thirst would kill the youths; God had already put them to sleep for their protection. «فأمر بالدخول عليهم فهاب الرجال ذلك، فقال له بعض وزرائه»
#61 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Striking over the ears expresses imposing sleep with force and close adherence; Arabic uses the same image for imposed humiliation, taxation, or military levies. «ويعبر عن هذا ونحوه ب «الضرب» لتبين قوة المباشرة»
#62 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · One report says God caused the youths to sleep as soon as they entered the cave, concealing their whereabouts from the kingdom. «فروت فرقة أن الله عز وجل «ضرب على آذانهم»»
#63 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · The line attributed to al-Farazdaq demonstrates the expression's sense of something firmly imposed or enveloping. «ضربت عليك العنكبوت بنسجها ...»
#64 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The ear is singled out because sleep strongly affects hearing and sleepers commonly wake through the ear; a Prophetic report describes Satan urinating in the ear of one who sleeps through the night. «الجارحة التي منها عظم فساد النوم، وقلما ينقطع نوم نائم»
#65 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The adjective 'counted' describes the years to intensify their number; al-Zajjaj also allows it as a cognate accusative. «وقوله عددا نعت للسنين، والقصد به العبارة عن التكثير»
#66 TABRISI d.548 · holds · God made them sleep for many counted years, answering their prayer and blocking sound from reaching them; this shows they remained alive and slept safely and peacefully. «معناه أنمناهم سنين ذات عدد وتأويله فأجبنا دعاءهم وسددنا آذانهم بالنوم الغالب»
#67 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The expression is among the Qur’an’s eloquent idioms and cannot be translated while preserving its wording’s full force. «وهذا من فصيح لغات القرآن التي لا يمكن أن يترجم بمعنى يوافق اللفظ»
#68 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The cave story also follows the Prophet’s grief over unbelievers: he should not despair, since God will support and protect him as He protected the youths. «أنه اتصل بقوله: { فلعلك باخع نفسك على آثارهم } أي فلا تأسف عليهم» [filed under 18:6, 18:9-12]
#69 TABRISI d.548 · holds · A third link is the promise to give believers good news: God will support them as He supported the people of the cave. «أنه اتصل بقوله: { ويبشر المؤمنين } أي وينصرهم كما نصر أصحاب الكهف» [filed under 18:2, 18:9-12]
#70 TABRISI d.548 · holds · ‘We struck their ears’ means that God imposed sleep on them; the phrase is eloquent, like saying an emir struck someone’s hand to prevent action. «ومعنى ضربنا على آذانهم سلطنا عليهم النوم وهو من الكلام البالغ في الفصاحة»
#71 TABRISI d.548 · قطرب · reports · Quṭrub compares the expression to the Arabic idiom ‘the emir struck his hand,’ meaning he prevented him from acting. «قال قطرب هو كقول العرب ضرب الأمير على يد فلان إذا منعه من التصرف»
#72 TABRISI d.548 · الأسود بن يعفر · reports · Al-Aswad ibn Yaʿfur’s line ‘the earth was struck against me with barriers’ illustrates the idiom of being blocked or confined. «قال الأسود بن يعفر وكان ضريرا:
ومن الحوادث لا أبا لك أنني
ضربت علي الأرض بالأسداد»
#73 TABRISI d.548 · reports · ‘Years, counted’ is either an adverbial time expression with counted as a verbal noun or an adjective meaning years that are counted; Zajjāj says the extra count emphasizes that they were many. «سنين نصب على الظرف وعددا منصوب على ضربين»
#74 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · The pursuers found the youths in the cave, but became terrified when trying to enter; the king had the entrance blocked so they would die there. «فكلما أراد رجل أن يدخل الكهف ارعب فقال قائل للملك أليس قلت إن قدرت عليهم قتلتهم»
#75 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says the youths fled a king who summoned them to idol worship, met a shepherd who followed their faith, and hid in the cave; two secret believers recorded their names and story on lead in a copper case. «ملكهم حين دعاهم إلى عبادة الأصنام الأصنام فمروا براع له فتبعهم على دينهم» [filed under 18:10-11]
#76 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عبيد بن عمير · reports · Ubayd ibn Umayr says the people searched for the missing youths but God concealed them; their names, lineage, and date were recorded on a tablet and kept in the king's treasury. «فقدهم قومهم فطلبوهم فعمى الله عليهم أمرهم فكتبوا أسماءهم وأنسابهم» [filed under 18:10-11]
#77 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · Wahb says a disciple barred from the city for refusing its idol entered a bathhouse, taught the youths, then fled with them and a shepherd's dog; the king's men sealed the cave after being frightened away. «أن أحد الحواريين جاء إلى مدينة أصحاب الكهف فأراد أن يدخلها فقيل له إن على بابها صنما» [filed under 18:10-11]
#78 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · In Wahb's account, the youths fled with the disciple, met a companion tending a field, and entered the cave at night intending to decide their next move in the morning. «فمروا على صاحب لهم في زرع» [filed under 18:10-11]
#79 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says noble youths independently gathered outside their city, one spoke of recognizing the Lord of heaven and earth, and all agreed before entering the cave. «كانوا أبناء عظماء المدينة وأشرافهم خرجوا فاجتمعوا وراء المدينة على غير ميعاد» [filed under 18:10-11]
#80 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says they were sons of Roman kings who separated themselves from their religion in the cave, where God caused them to sleep. «وقال قتادة كانوا أبناء ملوك الروم فتفردوا بدينهم في الكهف» [filed under 18:10-11]
#81 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · The third account says they affirmed that the Lord of the heavens and earth was their Lord and then resolved to enter the cave. «فقالوا ربنا رب السموات والأرض فأجمعوا أن يدخلوا الكهف» [filed under 18:10-11]
#82 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima says a Muslim nation disputed whether only the spirit or both body and spirit would be resurrected; its king prayed for a sign, and God awakened the cave youths. «جاءت أمة مسلمة وكان ملكهم مسلما فاختلفوا في الروح والجسد» [filed under 18:11-12]
#83 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · Wahb says a shepherd opened the cave to shelter his flock from rain, and God restored the youths' souls the next morning. «جاء راع قد أدركه المطر إلى الكهف فقال لو فتحت هذا الكهف وأدخلته غنمي من المطر»
#84 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن السائب · reports · Ibn al-Sa'ib says the landowner needed a sheep pen, removed the barrier, and opened the cave. «احتاج صاحب الأرض التي فيها الكهف أن يبني حظسرة لغنمه فهدم ذلك السد»
