#50 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Accounts differ on the ruined garden's appearance: black, or white like a harvested, dried crop. «فقيل: أصبحت سوداء، وقيل: بيضاء كالزرع اليابس المحصود»
#51 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · As the garden owners were punished in their orchard, Quraysh face punishment in their worldly life, while repentance remains open to survivors as it was for the brothers. «فكما حل بأولئك العقاب في جنتهم كذلك يحل بهؤلاء في جميع دنياهم وفي حياتهم» [filed under 68:17, 68:20]
#52 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · الفراء ومنذر وجماعة · reports · Al-Farra, Mundhir, and others take الصريم as night because the garden became black. «والصريم: قال الفراء ومنذر وجماعة: أراد به الليل من حيث اسودت جنتهم.»
#53 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · سفيان الثوري · reports · Sufyan al-Thawri takes الصريم as dawn because the garden became white like harvested grain. «وقال آخرون: أراد به الصبح من حيث ابيضت كالحصيد، قاله سفيان الثوري»
#54 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · الصريم can name either night or day because each is severed from the other. «والصريم، يقال لليل والنهار من حيث كل واحد منهما ينصرم من صاحبه»
#55 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas reports that الصريم means black ash in the language of Judhayma. «وقال ابن عباس: الصريم، الرماد الأسود بلغة جذيمة»
#56 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن عباس وغيره · reports · Ibn Abbas and others say الصريم is a known barren sandy tract in Yemen, used as a comparison for the garden. «وقال ابن عباس أيضا وغيره: الصريم، رملة باليمن معروفة لا تنبت فشبه جنتهم بها.»
#57 TABRISI d.548 · الجبائي · reports · One explanation says the garden became like a harvest whose fruit had all been cut away. «الصريم المصروم ثماره أي المقطوع والمعنى أنها صارت كأن جميع ثمارها قطعت عن الجبائي»
#58 TABRISI d.548 · الحسن · reports · Another explanation is a place from which all good has been cut off. «وقيل: الصريم الذي صرم عنه الخير فليس فيه شيء منه عن الحسن»
#59 TABRISI d.548 · مؤرج · reports · A third interpretation compares it to sand cut off from the bulk of the dune. «وقيل: كالصريم أي كالرملة انصرمت عن معظم الرمل عن مؤرج»
#60 TABRISI d.548 · reports · In Khuzaymī dialect, it can mean black ashes. «وقيل: كالرماد الأسود بلغة خزيمة»
#61 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس · reports · Al-ṣarīm means black ashes. «كالرماد الأسود قاله ابن عباس»
#62 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الفراء، ابن قتيبة · reports · Al-ṣarīm means night: the garden became black like night after burning; morning and night are each called ṣarīm because each cuts off from the other. «كالليل المسود قاله الفراء وكذلك قال ابن قتيبة أصبحت سوداء كالليل محترقة»
#63 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن قتيبة · reports · Alternatively, all the fruit was gone, as though it had been cut off. «وقد ذهب ما فيها من الثمر فكأنه قد صرم أي قطع وجذ»
#64 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Ṣarīm can mean cut off as a passive participle: burned trees resemble fruitless, cut trees; al-Ḥasan explains that all good had been cut away. «أي صرم عنها الخير فليس فيها شيء»
#65 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Other explanations compare the barren, burned garden to an isolated mass of sand, or to dawn after it has broken from night and left the garden dry and bare. «الصريم من الرمل قطعة ضخمة تنصرم عن سائر الرمال وجمعه الصرائم»
#66 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Another explanation says the burned garden became black like night; both night and day are called ṣarīm because each breaks off into the other. «والليل يسمى صريما وكذا النهار يسمى أيضا صريما»
#67 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Some say night is ṣarīm because darkness prevents activity, or because it cuts off sight. «لأنها تصرم نور البصر وتقطعه»
#68 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · If al-sarim means night, the garden was blackened; if it means day, its trees and crops had vanished and the soil was bare. «فإن كان أراد الليل فلاسوداد موضعها»
#69 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس والفراء · reports · Ibn Abbas, al-Farra, and others explain al-sarim as a dark night; the scorched garden became black like night. «أي كالليل المظلم عن ابن عباس والفراء وغيرهما»
#70 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · القشيري · rejects · Al-Qushayri objects that night cannot be called sarim because it stops activity, since day is also called sarim. «وفي هذا نظر لأن النهار يسمى صريما ولا يقطع عن تصرف»
#71 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · A cited poem describes the dark night as long and unable to clear by dawn, illustrating the night sense of al-sarim. «تطاول ليلك الجون البهيم فما ينجاب عن صبح بهيم»
#72 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas also says al-sarim means black ash, using a Khuzaymi dialect word. «الصريم الرماد الأسود بلغة خزيمة»
#73 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الثوري · reports · Al-Thawri says it means a harvested crop, with al-sarim understood as something cut down. «كالزرع المحصود. فالصريم بمعنى المصروم»
#74 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الحسن · reports · Hasan explains that its good was cut off, treating al-sarim as a passive form. «صرم عنها الخير أي قطع فالصريم مفعول أيضا»
#75 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · المؤرج · reports · Al-Mu’arrij compares it to a patch of sand separated from a larger dune, where nothing useful grows. «الرملة لا تنبت شيئا ينتفع به.»
#76 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الأخفش · reports · Al-Akhfash says al-sarim is the morning separated from night. «كالصبح انصرم من الليل»
#77 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · المبرد · reports · Al-Mubarrad interprets it as daytime, when nothing remains in the garden. «كالنهار فلا شيء فيها»
#78 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · شمر · reports · Shammir says both night and day are called sarim because each separates from the other; one view derives the night name from darkness stopping activity. «الصريم الليل والصريم النهار أي ينصرم هذا عن ذاك وذاك عن هذا»
#79 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The garden became like a harvested orchard, a blackened night, a whitened dry day, or sand; الصريم can be passive فعيل or either night or day because each separates from its opposite. «{ فأصبحت كالصريم } كالبستان الذي صرم ثماره بحيث لم يبق فيه شيء»
#80 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · The garden became like dark night because it burned black, or like dawn as a treeless white plain, or like a cut-off orchard deprived of fruit. «كالليل المظلم أي احترقت فاسودت أو كالصبح أي صارت أرضا بيضاء»
#81 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās gives two explanations of ṣarīm: black ash in Khuzaʿa speech, or a known barren sand in Yemen. «والصريم: الرماد الأسود بلغة خزيمة،»
#82 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الحسن · reports · Al-Ḥasan says the garden became ṣarīm because its good was cut off from it. «وقال الحسن: صرم عنها الخير، أي قطع.»
#83 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الثوري · reports · Al-Thawrī says ṣarīm can mean dawn because the garden turned white like harvested crop. «وقال الثوري: كالصبح من حيث ابيضت كالزرع المحصود.»
#84 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · مورج · reports · Murajj likens it to a sand dune separated from the main dune, barren of useful growth. «وقال مورج: كالرملة انصرمت من معظم الرمل، والرملة لا تنبت شيئا ينفع.»
#85 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الأخفش · reports · Al-Akhfash says it is like dawn separated from night. «وقال الأخفش: كالصبح انصرم من الليل.»
#86 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · المبرد · reports · Al-Mubarrad says it became like daylight, with nothing left in it. «وقال المبرد: كالنهار فلا شيء فيها.»
#87 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · شمر · reports · Shamr says ṣarīm can mean either night or day, each separating from the other. «وقال شمر: الصريم: الليل، والصريم: النهار،»
#88 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الفراء والقاضي منذر بن سعيد وجماعة · reports · They say the garden became like night because it blackened. «القاضي منذر بن سعيد وجماعة: الصريم: الليل من حيث اسودت»
#89 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · A heavenly calamity struck while they slept and left the orchard like a black night, according to Ibn Abbas, or like dry harvested stubble, according to al-Thawri and al-Suddi. «قال ابن عباس: أي كالليل الأسود. وقال الثوري، والسدي: مثل الزرع إذا حصد،» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#90 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · A Prophetic report warns that a sin can deprive a person of provision already prepared for him, and applies the warning to the garden owners’ lost crop. «عن ابن مسعود قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "إياكم والمعاصي، إن العبد ليذنب» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#91 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · reports · Al-sarim can mean trees stripped of fruit, something severed from its destination, a dark night, or black ash; the orchard was entirely ruined because the heirs sought everything and did not give charity. «كالأشجار التي صرم عنها ثمرها أو كالشيء الذي انقطع ما بينه وبين قاصده»
#92 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says they were people of Abyssinia whose father fed the needy from his garden; after his death, his sons swore to harvest early and give no poor person food. «عن ابن عباس في قوله» [filed under 68:17-24]
#93 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says the garden belonged to an Israelite elder who saved his annual provisions and gave away the excess; his sons opposed charity and resolved to exclude the needy. «عن قتادة قال: كانت الجنة» [filed under 68:17-24]
#94 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says God's command came upon the garden at night while its owners slept, leaving it like the dark night; Qatr ibn Maymun agrees. «عن قتادة في قوله: {فطاف عليها طائف من ربك وهم نائمون}» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#95 DURR-FULL d.911 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · The Prophet warns that sin can cause lost knowledge, missed night prayer, and withheld provision, then says the owners lost their garden because of their sin. «عن ابن مسعود قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: إياكم والمعاصي» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#96 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas glosses al-sarim as the black night. «عن ابن عباس في قوله»
#97 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · In a dialogue with Nafi ibn al-Azraq, Ibn Abbas says al-sarim means gold and cites an anonymous poem using the word al-sarim. «عن ابن عباس أن نافع»
