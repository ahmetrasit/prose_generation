#79 QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · The surah sets the varied human work and its differing recompense in a cosmic frame; its two opposed human outcomes match the contrasts of night and day and of male and female. «في إطار من مشاهد الكون وطبيعة الإنسان تقرر السورة حقيقة العمل والجزاء»
#80 QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · Night covers, envelops, and hides the world, while day appears, reveals, and makes things plain; they oppose one another in the cycle, appearance, qualities, and effects. «الليل حين يغشى البسيطة ، ويغمرها ويخفيها . والنهار حين يتجلى ويظهر»
#81 QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · The broad alternation of night and day affects the soul directly and evokes the mysterious universe, human inability to control it, change, and a power directing the cosmos. «ولهذا التقلب حديث وإيحاء . حديث عن هذا الكون المجهول الأسرار»
#82 QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · Their regular, precise order proves another hand directs the heavens; the one governing the cosmos also directs human lives and did not create people purposelessly. «بهذا الانتظام وهذا الاطراد وهذه الدقة . وأن الذي يدير الفلك هكذا يدير حياة البشر أيضا»
#83 QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · Even amid deniers’ attempts to divert attention, the human heart remains connected to the universe and instinctively recognizes its director, as well as through reflection. «ويدرك تلقائيا كما يدرك بعد التدبر والتفكر ، أن هنالك مدبرا»
#84 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The object of يغشى is omitted because the verb functions intransitively: the lesson is that night covers everything in darkness. «وحذف مفعول { يغشى } لتنزيل الفعل منزلة اللازم لأن العبرة بغشيانه كل ما تغشاه ظلمته .»
#85 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The passage alludes to night darkness as the original state of the solar system and daylight as following the sun's creation; time reckoning accordingly began with nights before days. «وأشير إلى أن ظلمة الليل كانت غالبة لضوء النهار وأن النهار يعقبها» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#86 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The time restrictions on night and day follow the explanation of their corresponding oaths in the preceding surah. «والقول في تقييد الليل بالظرف وتقييد النهار بمثله كالقول في قوله : { والنهار إذا جلاها والليل إذا يغشاها في السورة السابقة } ( الشمس : 3 ، 4 ) .» [filed under 92:1, 92:2]
#87 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · علقمة · reports · In a report in both Ṣaḥīḥs, Abū al-Dardāʾ asks how Ibn Masʿūd recited the oath; ʿAlqama gives the wording with الذكر والأنثى, and Abū al-Dardāʾ says he heard the Prophet recite it so. «عن علقمة قال : ( دخلت في نفر من أصحاب عبد الله ( يعني ابن مسعود ) الشام فسمع بنا أبو الدرداء» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#88 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Al-Zamakhsharī calls this the Prophet's reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains it as an early permitted variant that was later replaced by the final reading agreed upon by the reciters and written in Abū Bakr's muṣḥaf. «وتأويل ذلك : أنه أقرأها أبا الدرداء أيام كان القرآن مرخصا فيه أن يقرأ على بعض اختلاف ، ثم نسخ ذلك الترخيص» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:3]
#89 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The surah warns by contrasting divergent efforts: generous, pious believers who affirm the good promise reach lasting happiness, while misers who claim self-sufficiency deny it and approach misery; financial giving receives special emphasis. The surah may be Meccan or Medinan. «غرض السورة الإنذار وتسلك إليه بالإشارة إلى اختلاف مساعي الناس» [filed under 92:1-4]
#90 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The night swallows the day, as in 13:3; it may instead be understood as covering earth or sun. «إقسام بالليل إذا يغشى النهار على حد قوله تعالى يغشي الليل النهار»
#91 TABATABAI d.1402 · محمد بن مسلم · reports · Asked about divine oaths by created things, Imam al-Baqir answered that God may swear by any of His creatures, while creatures may swear only by God. «عن محمد بن مسلم قال قلت لأبي جعفر عليه السلام قول الله عز وجل»
#92 TABATABAI d.1402 · علي بن مهزيار · reports · Al-Faqih also transmits the ruling that God may swear by His creation but creatures may swear only by Him, through Ali ibn Mahziyar from Imam al-Baqir II. «ورواه في الفقيه بإسناده عن علي بن مهزيار عن أبي جعفر الثاني»
#93 TABATABAI d.1402 · القمي · reports · In al-Qummi’s gloss, the night covers the day, and the phrase is an oath. «قال حين يغشى النهار وهو قسم»
#94 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The surah is unanimously considered one of the earliest revelations, probably ninth in chronological order, and is named for the night mentioned in verse 1. «UNANIMOUSLY regarded as one of the very early revelations - most probably the ninth in the chronological order» [filed under 92:1-21]
#95 BINTSHATI d.1419 · المفسرون · reports · Exegetes supply an unstated object for the night covering: the whole day, the sun, or the earth and everything in it; they likewise explain daylight's appearing as the removal of night or the sun's light. «أو يغشى الشمس، كقوله تعالى» [filed under 92:1-2]
#96 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author sees no deleted or implied object; the night covering and day appearing are themselves the intended focus, so no object needs to be named. «يصرفنا عن تأويل محذوف أو مقدر، لنلتفت إلى أن الغشية والتجلي» [filed under 92:1-2]
#97 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Exegetes treat the oath's waw as magnifying night and day, but the author allows that a rhetorical device can depart from its original grammatical use, as interrogatives and commands do. «قد يعدل عن هذا الأصل لملحظ بلاغي في التعبير، كمثل عدوله في الاستفهام» [filed under 92:1-2]
#98 BINTSHATI d.1419 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says God swore by night and day because of their great importance as two signs that God turns over creation. «لعظم شأنهما، فهما آيتان عظيمتان يكورهما الله على الخلائق» [filed under 92:1-2]
#99 BINTSHATI d.1419 · أبو حيان · reports · Abu Hayyan explains the oath through the roles of night as the time creatures shelter and day as the time they spread out. «أقسم بالليل الذي فيه كل حيوان يأوى إلى مأواه» [filed under 92:1-2]
#100 BINTSHATI d.1419 · ابن القيم · reports · Ibn Qayyim says God swears by night in all its states because it is one of His signs. «إذ هو من آياته الدالة عليه» [filed under 92:1-2]
#101 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi details how night brings shelter, sleep, and rest while daylight reveals the world and enables work; their alternation serves human needs. «فلو كان الدهر كله ليلاً لتعذر المعاش، ولو كان نهاراً كله لبطلت الراحة» [filed under 92:1-2]
#102 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author says the general usefulness of alternating night and day is not the specific point of this oath; the Quran explains those benefits directly elsewhere. «وهذا الكلام في المصلحة من تعاقب الليل والنهار، هو من قبيل الحكمة» [filed under 92:1-2]
#103 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The Quran explains the practical wisdom of day and night directly, for example by contrasting endless night and day in al-Qasas 28:71-72. «والقرآن حين يقصد إلى أن يلفت إلى آيتي الليل والنهار» [filed under 92:1-2]
#104 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The author contrasts this oath's specific conditions of night and day with the many Quranic statements that explain their benefits and changing states. «بغير غشية الليل وتجلي النهار، نلمح السر البياني» [filed under 92:1-2]
#105 BINTSHATI d.1419 · ابن القيم · reports · Ibn Qayyim connects the pairings: night and day arise through celestial bodies, while male and female arise among earthly bodies; both are signs of God's lordship. «فإن إخراج الليل والنهار بواسطة الأجرتم العلوية» [filed under 92:1-3]
#106 BINTSHATI d.1419 · ابن القيم · reports · Ibn Qayyim also links time and the human actors within it to differing actions and rewards, comparing that difference to the contrasting pairs in the oath. «على اختلاف السعي كما اختلف الليل والنهار والذكر والأنثى» [filed under 92:1-4]
#107 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The author centers the surah's pattern of contrasts: visible differences between night and day and male and female prepare for differences in people's actions and their recompense. «ما يسيطر على السورة كلها من ملحظ التقابل والتفاوت» [filed under 92:1-4]
#108 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The oath's night, daylight, and creation frame divergent earthly endeavors, then contrasting afterlife fates: the denier's fire and the pious giver's satisfaction. «وعلى هذا النسق من البيان المعجز، يتم الربط بين المقسم به في أول السورة:» [filed under 92:1, 92:2, 92:4, 92:5, 92:6, 92:8, 92:9, 92:17, 92:18, 92:19, 92:20, 92:21]
#109 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Surah al-Layl is conventionally ninth in revelation, after al-Ala and before al-Fajr and al-Duha; although it follows al-Shams in the codex, al-Shams was revealed later. «أنها تاسعة السور، نزلت بعد سورة الأعلى، ثم نزلت بعدها» [filed under 92:1-21]
#110 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The nearby revelations of al-Layl, al-Fajr, and al-Duha develop a Quranic image pattern using gradations of light and darkness for guidance and misguidance. «يجلو الظاهرة الأسلوبية التي يعمد فيها البيان القرآني إلى جلاء المعنويات» [filed under 92:1-4]
#111 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The root gh-sh-y means covering; its derivatives describe a veil, dulling of perception in sleep or fainting, and the covering of sight or insight. «ومن غشية النعاس المعطلة للحس والإدراك، جاءت غشية الإغماء»
#112 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The Quran uses covering for rejection in Noah 71:7 and Hud 11:5, for the overwhelming manifestation at the lote tree, and for sleep, fainting, or a veil over heart and senses. «وفي الاستعمال القرآني، جاءت الغاشية على معناها في استغشاء الثياب حاجزاً دون السمع والبصر»
#113 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The root often describes the Day of Judgment and its punishments covering people, faces, or places. «وكثر مجئ الغشية في الحديث عن يوم القيامة»
#114 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The root can name terrors or darkness; the author understands night in this surah as a covering of deep darkness. «والغواشي الأهوال، أو الظلمات تلقى لفاعها الأسود»
