#34 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · A report interprets the human in loss as Abu Jahl ibn Hisham and identifies Ali and Salman among those who believe and do good. «أخرج ابن مردويه عن ابن عباس في قوله» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
#35 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The surah was revealed shortly after surah 94, Ash-Sharh. «REVEALED shortly after surah 94» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#36 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Surah al-Asr is an early Meccan surah, conventionally ranked thirteenth in revelation, after al-Sharh and before al-Adiyat. «السورة مكية مبكرة، والمشهور في ترتيبها أنها الثالثة عشرة في النزول» [filed under 103:1-3]
#37 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi says passing time subtracts from one's lifespan; without a corresponding gain, that decrease is itself loss, even when people welcome time passing. «ذكر العصر الذي بمضيه ينتقص عمرك، فإذا لم يكن في مقابلته كسب» [filed under 103:1-2]
#38 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The author reads asr as the age that presses and tests humanity, extracting its essence; this familiar Arabic sense leads into the declaration that humanity is in loss. «تأتي كلمة العصر في سياقها من السورة، لافتة إلى ابتلاء الإنسان بالعصر» [filed under 103:1-2]
#39 BINTSHATI d.1419 · المفسرون · reports · Exegetes differ between taking the human as all people and as particular Meccan opponents; reports name al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, al-As ibn Wa'il, al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Muttalib, Abu Lahab, or Abu Jahl. «مراداً بالإنسان جماعة من المشركين»
#40 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · The author notes that the exception of believers would break if humanity meant only the named Meccan opponents. «وهذا الاستثناء ينقطع إذا ما كان الإنسان خاصاً بالمعهودين»
#41 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The author rejects restricting humanity to named disbelievers: the general wording and the exception for believers would not fit people who never became believers. «ولا نقف عندما اختلفوا فيه، فالعبرة بعموم اللفظ لا بخصوص السبب» [filed under 103:2-3]
#42 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الطبري · reports · Al-Tabari understands humanity as a collective plural; al-Zamakhshari calls it the species generally, Abu Hayyan a generic noun, and Ibn Qayyim likely agrees. «الإنسان بمعنى الجمع لا بمعنى الواحد» [filed under 103:2-3]
#43 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The Quran's use of al-nas denotes humanity broadly, while al-ins appears in contexts paired with jinn and carries the sense of human beings as the opposite of wild creatures. «فشهد ذلك بأن دلالة الإنسية، بما تعني من نقيض الوحشية»
#44 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The preposition fī conveys being surrounded, immersed, or engulfed by loss. «من معنى الظرفية، في الغمر والإحاطة والإغراق» [filed under 103:2-3]
#45 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · Against al-Nīsābūrī, the indefinite form khusr is not simply an intensifier; she reads it as open-ended and unrestricted. «المبالغات كما ذهب النيسابورى. وإنما التنكير»
#46 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Indefiniteness may also convey awe, as al-Rāzī suggests: the loss is so great that only God comprehends it. «على ما قال الرازي. ووجهه عنده»
#47 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · Time's pressing extracts the human's essence, revealing whether his outcome is loss or salvation (exempting those who believe and do good and counsel truth and patience). «توطئة إيضاحية لبيان ما يستخلص العصرُ من عصارة هذا الإنسان» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#48 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The three-rhyme sura is an opening single-member oath by late afternoon, followed by a rebuke; verse 3 exempts believers and differentiates good from bad people. «Die auf 3Kr reimende Sure besteht aus einem eingliedrigen Schwur „beim späten Nachmittag“ mit rügendem ʾinsān-Spruch als Schwuraussage» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#49 CORPUSCORANICUM · rejects · The commentator finds no hymn-like resonance in this sura and points to a critical discussion of Neuwirth's hypothesis elsewhere. «Eine solche hymnische Konnotation klingt jedoch im vorliegenden Text nirgends nach» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#50 CORPUSCORANICUM · Robinson · prefers · Robinson's secular reading makes ʿaṣr the time when merchants count a day's earnings, linking the oath's image of profit to the eschatological loss in verse 2. «Robinsons profane Deutung des ʿaṣr als des Zeitpunkts, zu dem Händler am Ende eines Markttags ihre Einnahmen berechnen» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#51 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Robinson's reading is strengthened by Q 104, which also contrasts worldly wealth with damnation in the hereafter. «Robinsons Interpretation überzeugt auch angesichts des sich ergebenden Bezugs zu Q 104»
#52 CORPUSCORANICUM · Spitaler · reports · Medina II joins the oath verse to verse 2 in its verse division. «Medina II zieht V. 1 zu V. 2 (Spitaler, Verszählung, 72)» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#53 CORPUSCORANICUM · Zirker · reports · The German translation of the verse follows Zirker. «Die Übersetzung folgt Zirker.»
#54 CORPUSCORANICUM · prefers · The verse may mean that human conduct causes people to forfeit bliss in the hereafter. «Gemeint ist vielleicht, dass der Mensch aufgrund seines Verhaltens der jenseitigen Seligkeit verlustig geht»
#55 CORPUSCORANICUM · Torrey · reports · Torrey compares this eschatological loss with Luke 9:24; the Syriac Peshitta uses ḥsar, from the same root as Arabic ḫasira and ḫusr. «Von einem eschatologischen „Verlust“ ist, wie Torrey 1892, 32, anmerkt, auch in Lukas 9:24»
#56 CORPUSCORANICUM · reports · Philippians 3:7–8 offers another comparison for the notion of loss. «Vgl. a. Paulus Brief an die Philipper 3:7.8»
#57 CORPUSCORANICUM · Torrey · reports · Torrey documents many other Qur'anic examples of ḫasira and ḫusr used eschatologically. «Für einen eschatologischen Gebrauch von ḫasira, ḫusr im Koran gibt es noch zahlreiche weitere Belege»
#58 CORPUSCORANICUM · reports · An early Meccan parallel is the phrase ḫāsirah in Q 79:12. «In frühmekkanischer Zeit vgl. noch 79:12 (qālū tilka ʾiḏan karratun ḫāsirah)»
#59 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-ʿAṣr follows al-Takāthur’s warning against wasting life amassing wealth; people would have used life wisely if they knew they must answer for this great blessing. «waste all their lives in planning» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#60 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This sūrah explains life’s real value and the factors that make it a path to salvation or a cause of eternal punishment. «the real value and importance of life is divulged» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#61 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The sūrah briefly lays out the way to use life well and thereby fulfill the individual and collective duties on which salvation depends. «his eternal salvation depends» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
#62 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an’s guidance to organize personal and collective life for success in the Hereafter is condensed into these three verses. «the message which is conveyed by one hundred and fourteen sūrahs of the Qur’ ān has been succinctly put forth in three verses» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
#63 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Im ām Sh āfi‘ ī · reports · Imām Shāfiʿī is reported to say that reflective reading of Sūrah al-ʿAṣr alone would suffice for people. «Im ām Sh āfi‘ ī has pointed by saying that if people only read S ūrah al-‘A ṣr while reflecting on its contents, it will suffice for them» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
#64 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The lifespan is valuable yet rapidly consumed, so everyone is losing except those who believe and do good deeds. «all people are in a state of loss except those who» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#65 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Faith and righteous action fulfill life’s purpose: defying Satan brings mutual pleasure with God and Paradise, while surrendering to Satan’s lures forfeits an extraordinary favor. «the Almighty will be pleased with him» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
#66 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Im ām Far āhī · reports · Farāhī traces īmān to amn, with senses of giving peace, attesting or trusting, and having absolute faith; muʾmin is also a divine name because God grants safety to those who seek refuge. «Im ām Far āhī, whilst explaining the meaning of īmān in his exegesis, has written»
#67 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Im ām Far āhī · reports · Farāhī defines religious īmān as certainty joined with humility, trust, and adherence: a believer accepts God’s signs and commands, submits to Him, and accepts His decisions. «Im ām Far āhī, whilst explaining the meaning of īmān in his exegesis, has written»
#68 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Righteous deeds follow as faith’s necessary corollary: true faith should shape actions, and their absence shows that faith has not taken firm root. «When true faith is engendered in a person, just as its radiance lights up his inner-self»
#69 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Islahi explains that inward faith can be self-deception, so words alone do not prove belief; a person’s deeds must also testify to faith. «For this reason, two testimonies were needed to be required for it»
#70 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’anic address to those who believe with the tongue calls them to believe through their deeds. «O you who believe with the tongue! Believe through your deeds.»
#71 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Im ām Far āhī · reports · Farāhī explains ṣāliḥāt as actions that enable apparent and inward, worldly and religious, individual and collective development toward human potential. «Im ām Far āhī writes: God has regarded good deeds to be ṣāli ḥāt»
#72 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Righteous deeds accord with God’s wise design for the universe’s collective order, since the world is governed by purposeful prudence rather than play. «deeds will be regarded as righteous»
#73 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Faith is presented as the foundation and nucleus, with righteous deeds following as its explanation. «many branches sprout from a root» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
