#139 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · الجمهور · reports · Most interpreters take du'fayn as twofold: the thing plus an equal amount added; another view defines a double as two times the thing. «لأن ضعف الشيء مثله زائدا عليه , وضعفاه : مثلاه زائدا عليه , وقيل ضعف الشيء مثلاه»
#140 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · holds · Tall means dew, a lesser kind of rain; dew-grown crops are weaker and yield less than rain-grown crops, yet even small charity sustains and benefits. «الطل : الندى , وهو دون المطر , والعرب تقول : الطل أحد المطرين»
#141 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · holds · The parable compares abundant charity to a rain-fed crop and modest charity to a dew-fed crop: do not abandon small charity when unable to give much. «فأراد بهذا ضرب المثل أن كثير البر مثل زرع المطر كثير النفع , وقليل البر مثل زرع الطل قليل النفع»
#142 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · The truly impoverished person is one who imagines himself wealthy or accomplished, then discovers his bankruptcy and insignificance before God. «أفقر الخلق من ظن نفسه موسرا فيبين له إفلاسه» [filed under 2:264-266]
#143 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · The parables contrast sincere charity, which earns honor and increase, with vain spending, which brings rejection now and destruction later. «هذه آيات كثيرة ذكرها الله تعالى على جهة ضرب المثل للمخلص والمنافق» [filed under 2:264-266]
#144 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · Sincere deeds flourish like a healthy crop, whereas vain deeds resemble a failed trade whose goods and plans are lost. «مثل هؤلاء كالذي أنبت زرعا فزكا أصله ونما فصله» [filed under 2:264-266]
#145 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · This is a parable for those who spend seeking God's approval without reminding recipients of the gift or hurting them; the syntax omits “spending,” as in “God's horses, ride.” «هذا مثل ضربه الله عز وجل لمن ينفق، يريد ما عند الله ولا يمن ولا يؤذي»
#146 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الزجاج · reports · He reads تثبيتا as spending with certainty that God will reward it; the sincere are sure of that recompense, unlike hypocrites who give for show without expecting reward. «أي: وينفقونها مقرين بأنها مما يثيب الله عليها»
#147 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · السدي وأبو صالح وابن زيد · reports · They interpret تثبيتا as certainty. «فقال السدي»
#148 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الشعبي والكلبي · reports · They interpret it as affirmation or sincere belief that what they spend benefits them more than what they keep. «يعني: تصديقا من أنفسهم، يعلمون أن ما أخرجوا خير لهم مما تركوا»
#149 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · عطاء ومجاهد · reports · They take تثبيتا as deliberation: carefully selecting a pious, upright recipient for charity. «يتثبت في صدقته، فيضعها في أهل الصلاح والعفاف»
#150 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الحسن · reports · He says a person considering charity should test the motive: proceed if it is for God, but hold back if doubt is mixed into it. «فإن كان لله أمضى، وإن خالطه شك أمسك»
#151 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · The deliberation sense works because they steady themselves in seeking recipients and directing funds properly; Arabic similarly says one who restrains himself is restrained. «لأنهم ثبتوا أنفسهم تثبيتا في طلب المستحق، وصرف المال في وجهه»
#152 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · Rabwa means elevated ground and has several dialectal forms; al-Akhfash prefers the ḍamma form because its plural is usually الربى. «وقوله تعالى: {كمثل جنة بربوة}»
#153 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الأخفش · prefers · He prefers ربوة with ḍamma; it comes from rising or increasing, like الرابية, and is related to swelling breath and financial الربا. «قال الأخفش والذي نختاره: ربوة بالضم»
#154 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · An orchard on high ground yields more and grows greener when it has enough water; the Aʿsha's poem names the fertile gardens of al-Ḥazn as an example. «لأن الموضع المرتفع من الأرض إذا كان له ما يرويه من الماء فهو أكثر ريعا»
#155 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · Wābil is the strongest rain; akl means edible food or fruit, as in 14:25, and lexically can mean a person's worldly share, with variant vowel and plural forms. «وقوله تعالى: {أصابها وابل} وهو أشد المطر»
#156 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · المفسرون · reports · They take the crop in this verse to mean fruit. «قال المفسرون: الأكل: المراد به الثمر»
#157 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · ابن عباس في رواية عطاء · reports · The orchard produces in one year twice the crop another orchard produces in two years. «يريد: حملت في سنة من الريع ما يحمل غيرها في سنتين»
#158 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · Ṭall is a gentle rain with small drops; the source gives related verb and adjective forms and a customary prayer for abundant rain. «وهو المطر اللين الصغار القطر، يقال: طلت السماء تطل طلا»
#159 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · الفراء · reports · The conditional “if no heavy rain reaches it, then dew” has an omitted “if it receives dew,” as in the example about freeing two or one slave. «فيه إضمار المعنى: فإن يكن لم يصبها وابل فطل»
#160 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · Whether the orchard receives heavy rain or dew, its yield remains abundant; likewise God multiplies the sincere believer's reward whether the charity is small or great. «ثواب صدقة المؤمن المخلص، سواء قلت نفقته وصدقت»
#161 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · The sincere spender seeks God's pleasure and has inward conviction and belief in the reward, unlike a hypocrite. «من أنفسهم } بالثواب لا كالمنافق الذي لا يؤمن بالثواب»
#162 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · A garden on elevated ground yields more than one on low ground, and heavy rain makes it produce double its usual crop. «وهي ما ارتفع من الأرض»
#163 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · Even light rain leaves the elevated garden fruitful and blessed; likewise God multiplies a believer's reward whether the gift is small or large. «كذلك يضعف الله ثواب صدقة المؤمن قلت نفقته أم كثرت»
#164 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · A heavy downpour produces a crop twice that of other gardens' two-year yield. «حملت في سنة من الريع ما يحمل غيرها في سنتين»
#165 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · The garden simile leads into the example of the ostentatious spender and the person who neglects obedience until death. «ثم قرر مثل المرائي في النفقة والمفرط في الطاعة إلى أن يموت»
#166 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · The spenders seek God's pleasure; Qatada glosses 'confirming themselves' as expecting reward, while al-Shabi and al-Kalbi say they give with sincere belief in God's promise. «قال قتادة: احتسابا، وقال الشعبي والكلبي: تصديقا من أنفسهم»
#167 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · عطاء ومجاهد · reports · Ata and Mujahid read the phrase as placing or allocating their wealth; al-Hasan says they proceed with charity only once sure it is for God, holding back if doubt remains. «وقال عطاء ومجاهد: يثبتون أي يضعون أموالهم»
#168 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · On this interpretation, tathbit means deliberation, like the related verbal form in the command to devote oneself to God. «وعلى هذا القول يكون التثبيت بمعنى التثبت»
#169 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · المبرد والفراء · reports · Al-Mubarrad and al-Farra say a garden with palms is called janna, while one with grapevines is called firdaus. «قال (المبرد) (1) والفراء: إذا كان في البستان نخل فهو جنة»
#170 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · Ibn Amir and Asim read rabwa with an open ra, while the other readers use a damma; it means level high ground reached by flowing rivers, where plants grow best. «قرأ ابن عامر وعاصم بربوة وإلى ربوة في سورة المؤمنون بفتح الراء»
#171 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · Nafi, Ibn Kathir, and Abu Amr lighten the reading of 'it yielded'; others use a doubled consonant, and Nafi and Ibn Kathir also lighten 'its produce.' «قرأ نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو بالتخفيف، وقرأ الباقون بالتثقيل»
#172 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · عطاء · reports · Ata says the garden produced in one year what another garden yields in two. «قال عطاء: حملت في السنة من الريع»
#173 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima says it bore fruit twice in one year. «وقال عكرمة: حملت في السنة مرتين»
#174 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · Tall means light, gentle rain; al-Suddi identifies it as dew, which falls steadily. «أي فطش، وهو المطر الضعيف الخفيف ويكون دائما»
#175 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · The garden represents a sincere believer's work: God multiplies a pure giver's charity whether small or large, just as the garden yields with either little or heavy rain. «كذلك يضعف الله صدقة المؤمن المخلص الذي لا يمن ولا يؤذي سواء قلت نفقته أو كثرت»
#176 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · Even continuous light dew does the work of a strong downpour, and God sees what people do. «وذلك أن الطل إذا كان يدوم يعمل عمل الوابل الشديد»
#177 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Giving wealth, a close companion to life and especially difficult to surrender, disciplines the self, humbles it, restrains desire, and steadies faith and certainty. «وليثبتوا منها ببذل المال الذي هو شقيق الروح»
#178 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Alternatively, the phrase means confirming Islam and the reward from sincere hearts, since spending for God shows genuine belief in recompense. «وتصديقا للإسلام وتحقيقا للجزاء من أصل انفسهم»
#179 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · On the first reading, من is partitive, as in ‘he stirred some of his energy’; on the second it marks a starting point, as in envy from within themselves. «وعلى الثاني لابتداء الغاية كقوله تعالى»
#180 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid's reading وتبيينا من أنفسهم supports the interpretation that their spending demonstrates sincere faith to other believers. «وتعضده قراءة مجاهد ( وتبيينا من أنفسهم )»
#181 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Giving wealth establishes a part of oneself, while giving both wealth and life establishes the whole self. «ومن بذل ماله وروحه معا فهو الذي ثبتها كلها»
#182 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The passage connects steadfastness to striving for God's cause with wealth and selves. «وتجاهدون في سبيل الله بأموالكم وأنفسكم»
#183 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The givers' expenditure grows in God's regard like a garden on high ground, where trees produce better and finer fruit. «بمكان مرتفع وخصها لأن الشجر فيها ازكى وأحسن ثمرا»
