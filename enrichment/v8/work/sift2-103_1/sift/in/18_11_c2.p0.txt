# 14 notes filed under 18:11 (group 2 of 2 of this verse's notes)
verse 18:11: فَضَرَبْنَا عَلَىٰٓ ءَاذَانِهِمْ فِى ٱلْكَهْفِ سِنِينَ عَدَدًۭا

#1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · After their circumstances became known, groups built memorials and claimed them; Christian retellings gradually reduced their story to miracles and unsupported details. «Gradually, this tradition became confined to some miraculous feats and baseless details about them» [filed under 18:9-26]
#2 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Debates over their number and dog obscured their message and sacrifices; Islahi says the true history was later lost as Christianity’s teachings were distorted. «no one knew what their real message was and the extent of sacrifices they gave» [filed under 18:9-26]
#3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Distorted reports about the youths circulated among Arab Christians, who prompted the question; the Qur’an answered to reassure the Prophet and companions in adversity. «he could receive great assurance and strength in the adverse circumstances he and his companions were passing through» [filed under 18:9-26]
#4 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ persecution and cave refuge parallel the Prophet and companions’ trials and their coming migrations to Abyssinia, the Thawr cave and Madinah. «the Prophet (sws) and his companions too had to face tremendous trials at the hands of the Quraysh» [filed under 18:9-26]
#5 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · These verses summarize the whole story before its details, keeping its purpose in view so secondary details do not obscure it; the Qur'an often uses this narrative pattern. «this verse and the next two present a summary of the whole anecdote» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:12]
#6 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The idiom of patting or sealing ears developed meanings of stopping someone from hearing and affectionately lulling them to sleep, from patting a child's ears to sleep. «to put a seal on the ears or to pat someone on the ears»
#7 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The description of sleep is an eloquent metaphor for putting them to sleep with loving care. «an eloquent metaphor and connote putting someone to sleep in a very loving and caring manner»
#8 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The phrase literally says God closed their ears; the translation ‘put them into a deep sleep’ explains that hearing was also disabled so they would not wake during that period. «lafzî tercümesi “kulaklarını kapattık” şeklindedir. Bu durum, uyuyan gençlerin anılan süre içinde uyanmamaları için işitme duyularının da çalışmaz hale getirildiğini ifade eder.»
#9 STUDYQURAN · R, Z · reports · The phrase literally says God struck them upon the ears, preventing sounds from waking them. «literally, “struck them upon the ears,” meaning that He made them incapable of hearing sounds that could awaken them (R, Z)»
#10 STUDYQURAN · IK, Th, Ṭs · reports · Another explanation is that God put them into a very deep slumber. «that He put them into a very deep slumber (IK, Th, Ṭs)»
#11 STUDYQURAN · Z · reports · A number of years can designate either a long or a short period. «A number of years may refer to a long or short period of time (Z)»
#12 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verse 25 gives their sleep as 309 years, yet they experienced it as a day or part of one in verse 19. «v. 25 gives the number of years as 309, but the Companions of the Cave experienced this long amount of time as but a day or part of a day (v. 19)»
#13 STUDYQURAN · Ṭs · reports · The description indicates they remained asleep and did not die. «the companions did not die, but remained in a deep sleep (Ṭs)»
#14 STUDYQURAN · holds · The youths' centuries-long sleep represents an intermediate realm between life and death, analogous to the state between individual death and universal resurrection. «their sleep represents an intermediate realm between life and death» [filed under 18:9, 18:11, 18:12, 18:25]
