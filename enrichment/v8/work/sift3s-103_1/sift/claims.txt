# Claim map of the page: id, claim, terms, verses

## ¶1
1a: The initial ve in ve'l-asr (103:1) is an oath particle rather than a conjunction joining two things.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, ve'l-asr  · verses: 103:1
1b: The noun following the oath particle in ve'l-asr is read with a final kasra because the particle governs the genitive.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, ve'l-asr  · verses: 103:1
1c: The proposition sworn to is deferred from 103:1 to inne'l-insâne le-fî husr in 103:2; the opening oath leaves the reader awaiting that judgment.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, ve'l-asr, إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ, inne'l-insâne le-fî husr  · verses: 103:1, 103:2
1d: Placing the oath by asr immediately before the judgment of husr makes the meaning of asr a way of understanding the kind of loss asserted in 103:2.  · terms: وَٱلْعَصْرِ, ve'l-asr, خُسْرٍ, husr  · verses: 103:1, 103:2

## ¶2
2a: Ve'l-fecr (89:1), ve'd-duhâ (93:1), ve'l-leyli (92:1), and ve's-subhi (74:34) are cited as Qur'anic precedents for swearing by portions of the day, placing the oath by asr in that pattern.  · terms: وَٱلْفَجْرِ, ve'l-fecr, وَٱلضُّحَىٰ, ve'd-duhâ, وَٱلَّيْلِ, ve'l-leyli, وَٱلصُّبْحِ, ve's-subhi, asr  · verses: 89:1, 93:1, 92:1, 74:34
2b: Izâ yağşâ in 92:1 and izâ esfer in 74:34 qualify their times by covering and brightening; asr in 103:1 has no such qualification, leaving its semantic breadth open.  · terms: إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ, izâ yağşâ, إِذَآ أَسْفَرَ, izâ esfer, asr  · verses: 92:1, 74:34
2c: Asr can denote dehr, an extended stretch of time or an age.  · terms: العصر الدهر, el-asru'd-dehr
2d: Asr also denotes aşiyy, the late portion of the day near evening.  · terms: العصر العشي, el-asru'l-aşiyy
2e: Salâtu'l-asr takes its name from the time of day at which the afternoon prayer is performed.  · terms: صلاة العصر, salâtu'l-asr
2f: The definite article in asr points to a known time but does not decide between the day's afternoon, one's historical age, and time itself; the verse lets these possibilities be heard together.  · terms: asr

## ¶3
3a: Turkish separates meanings held together by Arabic asr: asır now denotes a century, asri means contemporary, and the late day is named by the different word ikindi.  · terms: asr, asır, asri, ikindi
3b: Turkish asır does not evoke either the afternoon's fading light or the Arabic root's squeezing, refuge, and whirlwind senses.  · terms: asır, asr
3c: The Qur'anic word asr holds a time of day and an age together, giving the end of a day and the end of a human life the same name.  · terms: asr

## ¶4
4a: Arabic usage calls night and day together el-asrân, the two asrs.  · terms: العصران الليل والنهار, el-asrâni'l-leylu ve'n-nehâr
4b: Another use of el-asrân pairs early morning with late afternoon as the two asrs.  · terms: العصران الغداة والعشي, el-asrâni'l-gadâtu ve'l-aşiyy
4c: Bi'l-gadâti ve'l-aşiyyi in 6:52 is cited as the Qur'an's own morning-and-evening pair corresponding to the two ends of the day named by el-asrân.  · terms: بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ, bi'l-gadâti ve'l-aşiyyi, العصران, el-asrân  · verses: 6:52
4d: Tarafeyi'n-nehâr in 11:114 is cited as another Qur'anic expression of the day's two edges, here used to specify prayer times and connected to the pair named by el-asrân.  · terms: طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ, tarafeyi'n-nehâr, العصران, el-asrân  · verses: 11:114
4e: Naming the day's two ends with asr presents time through its edges, the entry into and exit from a period; afternoon is specifically the day's closing edge.  · terms: asr

## ¶5
5a: Asran in câenî fulânun asran describes someone coming late or slowly, giving asr a sense of delayed arrival.  · terms: جاءني فلان عصرا أي بطيئا, câenî fulânun asran, asr
5b: The expression nâme ve mâ nâme li-asr uses asr for even a moment of sleep that the person failed to obtain.  · terms: نام وما نام لعصر, nâme ve mâ nâme li-asr
5c: Asâr can mean hîn, a period or portion of time; together with the moment usage, this extends the root from long ages to very short intervals.  · terms: العصار الحين, el-asâru'l-hîn, asr
5d: Asr names stages of a human life, including a girl's attainment of youth and maturity and a person's lifespan or old age.  · terms: بلغت عصر شبابها وإدراكها, belegat asra şebâbihâ ve idrâkihâ, asr
5e: Afternoon is read as the time when light and remaining opportunities are running out, so asr evokes felt lateness within a shrinking time rather than merely naming an hour.  · terms: asr

## ¶6
6a: The question about the Hour in 79:42 is followed in 79:46 by a measure of perceived duration: aşiyyeten ev duhâhâ makes a whole life seem only an afternoon or its forenoon when viewed from its end.  · terms: عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَىٰهَا, aşiyyeten ev duhâhâ  · verses: 79:42, 79:46
6b: Aşiyyeten in 79:46 is connected to the afternoon sense used to explain asr, although the verse does not use asr itself; the connection illustrates a long age becoming a brief afternoon in retrospective perception.  · terms: عَشِيَّةً, aşiyyeten, asr  · verses: 79:46
6c: Na'mel sâlihan gayra'llezî kunnâ na'mel in 35:37 is cited as an instance of the good work desired only after its opportunity has passed.  · terms: نَعْمَلْ صَٰلِحًا غَيْرَ ٱلَّذِى كُنَّا نَعْمَلُ, na'mel sâlihan gayra'llezî kunnâ na'mel  · verses: 35:37
6d: E-ve lem nu'ammirkum in 35:37 presents a lifespan as an already granted interval sufficient for reflection, rather than an opportunity still available to be reclaimed.  · terms: أَوَلَمْ نُعَمِّرْكُم, e-ve lem nu'ammirkum  · verses: 35:37
6e: The granted lifespan evoked by 35:37 is the interval within which both the surah's loss and its faith and righteous action take place.  · verses: 35:37

## ¶7
7a: The root's squeezing images are heard alongside the temporal sense of asr in the oath, without replacing that temporal sense.  · terms: asr
7b: The concrete act of asr is pressing a thing until its liquid flows out, described as dagtu şey'in hattâ yetehalleb.  · terms: ضغط شيء حتى يتحلب, dagtu şey'in hattâ yetehalleb, asr
7c: Mi'sâr denotes a bag like a feed bag in which grapes are placed and squeezed.  · terms: المعصار, el-mi'sâr
7d: Ma'sara denotes the place or receptacle in which grapes are squeezed.  · terms: المعصرة, el-ma'sara
7e: Usâra names what flows out as a result of squeezing.  · terms: العصارة ما سال عن العصر, el-usâratu mâ sâle ani'l-asr
7f: Asîr can designate anything whose liquid has been extracted by squeezing.  · terms: كل شيء عصر ماؤه فهو عصير, kullu şey'in usira mâuhû fe-huve asîr
7g: Squeezing is interpreted as a division into flowing essence and the dry residue or pulp left behind.  · terms: asr

## ¶8
8a: Arabic usage makes usâra and mu'tasar images of goodness and giving: the extracted essence serves as a figure for beneficence.  · terms: العصارة, usâra, المعتصر, mu'tasar
8b: Ya'siru in ya'siru fînâ ke'llezî ta'sir can mean giving generously, applying the squeezing verb to a benefactor.  · terms: يعصر فينا كالذي تعصر, ya'siru fînâ ke'llezî ta'sir
8c: Usâra can also mean galle, the yield or income obtained from land.  · terms: العصارة الغلة, el-usâratu'l-galle
8d: Mu'sırât and mu'sır describe clouds that are squeezed by rain or release rain as if being milked, extending the squeezing image to the sky.  · terms: المعصرات, mu'sırât, السحابة المعصر, es-sehâbetu'l-mu'sır
8e: Usira'l-kavm means that a community received rain.  · terms: عصر القوم أي مطروا, usira'l-kavm
8f: Mu'sırât in 78:14 is read specifically as the squeezing or rain-releasing clouds, making the verse a Qur'anic instance of that lexical image.  · terms: ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ, mu'sırât  · verses: 78:14
8g: Mine'l-mu'sırâti in 78:14, followed by li-nuhrice bihî in 78:15 and the gardens of 78:16, is used to trace a chain of abundance initiated by cloud-squeezing: rain yields grain, vegetation, and gardens.  · terms: ٱلْمُعْصِرَٰتِ, mu'sırât, لِّنُخْرِجَ بِهِۦ, li-nuhrice bihî  · verses: 78:14, 78:15, 78:16

## ¶9
9a: Ma'sûr can mean a tongue dried by thirst, pictured as something squeezed until no water remains.  · terms: المعصور اللسان اليابس عطشا, el-ma'sûru'l-lisânu'l-yâbisu atşâ
9b: I'tisâr names sipping water little by little to dislodge food stuck in the throat, giving the root an action of rescue.  · terms: الاعتصار, el-i'tisâr
9c: The poet's conditional lev bi-gayri'l-mâi halkî şarikun implies that water itself is choking him; the usual rescuing sip is therefore unavailable.  · terms: لو بغير الماء حلقي شرق, lev bi-gayri'l-mâi halkî şarikun, اعتصاري, i'tisârî

## ¶10
10a: Heard beside the squeezing images, asr as time becomes a pressure that extracts either a beneficent essence, like a gift or cloud's rain, or leaves a dried mass.  · terms: asr
10b: The loss of the surah's second verse is interpreted through the dry residue left behind after squeezing.
10c: Sabr, the surah's closing word, also names a bitter essence squeezed from a tree; this lexical image carries squeezing pressure through to the surah's end.  · terms: sabr

## ¶11
11a: Hattâ hîn in 12:35 frames imprisonment as a measured interval, connecting Yusuf's confinement with asr's interval and detention senses; this is a conceptual connection, since the Qur'anic prison word is different.  · terms: حَتَّىٰ حِينٍۢ, hattâ hîn, asr  · verses: 12:35
11b: A'siru hamrâ in 12:36 means squeezing grapes, but names what is squeezed by the resulting wine: an instance of naming by the product.  · terms: أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا, a'siru hamrâ  · verses: 12:36
11c: The wine-serving interpretation in 12:41 and the description nâcin minhumâ in 12:42 identify the dreamer who squeezes as the one who escapes prison; this is linked to asr as mencât, a place of deliverance.  · terms: أَعْصِرُ, a'siru, نَاجٍۢ مِّنْهُمَا, nâcin minhumâ, العصر المنجاة, el-asru'l-mencât  · verses: 12:36, 12:41, 12:42

## ¶12
12a: Bid'a sinîn in 12:42 and ba'de ummeh in 12:45 are cited for the prolonged waiting caused by the freed youth's delayed remembrance of Yusuf.  · terms: بِضْعَ سِنِينَ, bid'a sinîn, بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ, ba'de ummeh  · verses: 12:42, 12:45
12b: The green and dry ears in 12:43 and the instructions of 12:47-48 are read together as Yusuf's plan for managing time: abundance must provision the coming years of scarcity.  · terms: خُضْرٍۢ, hudrin, يَابِسَٰتٍۢ, yâbisât, سَبْعٌۭ شِدَادٌۭ, seb'un şidâdun  · verses: 12:43, 12:47, 12:48
12c: Asara'z-zer' is said of crops when the ears' enclosing husks become apparent.  · terms: قد عصر الزرع, kad asara'z-zer'
12d: The agricultural expression asara'z-zer' is derived from asr meaning hirz, a protective enclosure, because the grain shelters within its husk.  · terms: عصر الزرع, asara'z-zer', العصر, asr, الحرز, hirz
12e: Fe-zerûhu fî sunbulihî in 12:47 is cited as an instance of the protective work pictured by asara'z-zer': grain is kept in its husk for scarcity, although the verse says sunbul and does not use asr.  · terms: فَذَرُوهُ فِى سُنۢبُلِهِۦٓ, fe-zerûhu fî sunbulihî, عصر الزرع, asara'z-zer'  · verses: 12:47
12f: Ye'kulne mâ kaddemtum lehunne and mimmâ tuhsınûn in 12:48 depict hard years consuming prior stores while a little remains protected, extending the contrast between time's consumption and preservation.  · terms: يَأْكُلْنَ مَا قَدَّمْتُمْ لَهُنَّ, ye'kulne mâ kaddemtum lehunne, مِّمَّا تُحْصِنُونَ, mimmâ tuhsınûn  · verses: 12:48
12g: The pairing of yugâsu'n-nâsu with ya'sirûn in 12:49 makes renewed squeezing a sign of relief and abundance after the hard years.  · terms: يُغَاثُ ٱلنَّاسُ, yugâsu'n-nâsu, يَعْصِرُونَ, ya'sirûn  · verses: 12:49
12h: Ya'sirûn in 12:49 can also be understood as yestagıllûne bi-aradîhim, obtaining produce or income from their land.  · terms: يَعْصِرُونَ, ya'sirûn, يستغلون بأرضيهم, yestagıllûne bi-aradîhim  · verses: 12:49

## ¶13
13a: Yusuf's story brings the root's otherwise distant senses into one temporal sequence: confinement's duration, dream-squeezing and escape, extended waiting, protected grain, and renewed squeezing in abundance.  · terms: asr
13b: Squeezing in this story marks the end of scarcity; its fruit belongs to those who preserved supplies and waited patiently through the hard years.
13c: Through that sequence, the time sworn by is understood as an interval that can preserve what is stored until its essence flows, alongside its power to consume.  · terms: asr

## ¶14
14a: Asr can mean habs, detention or withholding, and mâ asarak asks what detained or prevented someone.  · terms: العصر الحبس, el-asru'l-habs, ما عصرك, mâ asarak
14b: Âsir describes someone tightfisted, who holds on to possessions rather than giving them away.  · terms: فلان عاصر إذا كان ممسكا, fulânun âsirun izâ kâne mumsikâ
14c: Ya'tasıru can describe a father withholding his child's property from the child.  · terms: يعتصر الوالد على ولده في ماله, ya'tasıru'l-vâlidu alâ veledihî fî mâlih
14d: I'tisâr can describe retracting and taking back a gift already given.  · terms: أعطيت فلانا عطية فاعتصرتها, a'taytu fulânen atıyyeten fa'tasartuhâ
14e: The same root can describe generosity and stinginess because both involve grasping: squeezing that lets liquid flow figures giving, while grasping that releases nothing figures withholding.  · terms: asr

## ¶15
15a: Mu'sır, the girl at her first menstruation, is derived here from being kept at home and provided with an asr or protective enclosure.  · terms: المعصر, el-mu'sır, عصرا, asr
15b: The confined girl and the grain enclosed in its husk illustrate a beneficial form of holding: protecting something at a threshold of development.  · terms: المعصر, el-mu'sır, asr

## ¶16
16a: Lâ yestesnûn in 68:18 is read as the garden owners' making an oath without exception, connected to their intended exclusion of the poor expressed by lâ yedhulennehe'l-yevme aleykum miskîn in 68:24.  · terms: وَلَا يَسْتَثْنُونَ, ve lâ yestesnûn, مِسْكِينٌۭ, miskîn  · verses: 68:17, 68:18, 68:24
16b: The transition from a planned harvest in 68:17 and exclusion in 68:24 to the ruined garden in 68:19-20 and mahrûmûn in 68:27 is used to show that produce withheld from others does not remain in the withholder's hands.  · terms: مِسْكِينٌۭ, miskîn, مَحْرُومُونَ, mahrûmûn  · verses: 68:17, 68:24, 68:19, 68:20, 68:26, 68:27
16c: Ke's-sarîm in 68:20 is read as the garden becoming cut down and darkened by morning.  · terms: كَٱلصَّرِيمِ, ke's-sarîm  · verses: 68:20
16d: The garden owners' exceptionless oath in 68:17-18 is contrasted with the oath of al-Asr, whose general judgment of loss is completed by the third verse's exception.  · terms: وَلَا يَسْتَثْنُونَ, ve lâ yestesnûn  · verses: 68:17, 68:18

## ¶17
17a: The root can mean attaching oneself to something and holding fast to it, connecting asr to an act of seeking support.  · terms: تعلق بشيء وامتساك به, teallukun bi-şey'in ve imtisâkun bih, asr
17b: Asr denotes melce', a refuge; i'tasartu or teassartu can mean taking refuge with a person, and i'tasara bi'l-mekân taking refuge in a place.  · terms: العصر الملجأ, el-asru'l-melce', اعتصرت بفلان وتعصرت, i'tasartu bi-fulânin ve teassartu, اعتصر بالمكان, i'tasara bi'l-mekân
17c: Usratu'l-mencûd specifically names the refuge of someone distressed or hemmed in.  · terms: عصرة المنجود, usratu'l-mencûd
17d: Asr can name affection or kinship between people, as shown by mâ beynehumâ asrun ve lâ yasr for the absence of such a bond.  · terms: ما بينهما عصر ولا يصر, mâ beynehumâ asrun ve lâ yasr
17e: Unsur denotes origin or ancestry, understood here as a place to which a person can return for refuge.  · terms: العنصر, el-unsur
17f: Unsur is said to derive from asr meaning refuge through the addition of a nûn.  · terms: العنصر, el-unsur, العصر, asr, الملجأ, melce'
17g: Meâsir, coats of armour, is derived from asr because armour encloses and protects the wearer.  · terms: المعاصر الدروع, el-meâsiru'd-durû', asr

## ¶18
18a: Squeezing and refuge are reconciled as two forms of grasping or enclosure: squeezing surrounds from outside, the refugee clings to support, and armour surrounds protectively.  · terms: asr
18b: The distressed person's refuge in usratu'l-mencûd links pressure to the need for shelter: the one who is hemmed in seeks refuge.  · terms: usratu'l-mencûd
18c: Heard together with asr's time sense, the refuge image makes the time sworn by both a duration that presses on a person and a place in which to seek shelter.  · terms: asr

## ¶19
19a: Fe'vû ile'l-kehfi with yenşur lekum rabbukum min rahmetih in 18:16, and eve'l-fityetu ile'l-kehf with the prayer for rahmeh in 18:10, present taking shelter after religious separation as a means of receiving divine mercy.  · terms: فَأْوُۥٓا۟ إِلَى ٱلْكَهْفِ, fe'vû ile'l-kehfi, رَحْمَتِهِۦ, rahmetih, رَحْمَةًۭ, rahmeh  · verses: 18:16, 18:10
19b: Fe-darabnâ alâ âzânihim with sinîne adedâ in 18:11 is read as time being closed off within the refuge, shielding the sleepers from the passage of many years.  · terms: فَضَرَبْنَا عَلَىٰٓ ءَاذَانِهِمْ, fe-darabnâ alâ âzânihim, سِنِينَ عَدَدًۭا, sinîne adedâ  · verses: 18:11
19c: Yevmen ev ba'da yevm in 18:19 is contrasted with the three hundred years plus nine in 18:25 to connect refuge, a long age, and subjectively brief time: three faces of asr appear without the word itself occurring.  · terms: يَوْمًا أَوْ بَعْضَ يَوْمٍۢ, yevmen ev ba'da yevm, asr  · verses: 18:10, 18:11, 18:19, 18:25
19d: The believers excepted from loss in the surah's third verse are connected to the cave's refuge image; the youths' shelter makes escape from loss imaginable as taking refuge.  · verses: 18:16, 18:10, 18:11, 18:19, 18:25

## ¶20
20a: I'sâr names a wind that lifts dust from the ground into the sky in a column.  · terms: الإعصار, el-i'sâr
20b: I'sâr can also denote the revolving dust itself, with eâsîr as its plural.  · terms: الإعصار الغبار الذي يسطع مستديرا والجمع الأعاصير, el-i'sâru'l-gubâru'llezî yesta'u mustedîrâ, eâsîr
20c: The whirlwind image is understood as gathering earthly material through rotation and drawing it upward into a vertical column.  · terms: i'sâr
20d: Asr can describe scent rising behind a perfumed woman's trailing garment, and usra the diffusion or swelling of fragrance.  · terms: لذيلها عصر, li-zeylihâ asr, العصرة, usra
20e: Rising dust and spreading perfume are connected as two forms of something swelling and ascending in the wake of movement.  · terms: i'sâr, asr, usra

## ¶21
21a: The rain-stripped rock of 2:264, especially fe-terakehû saldâ, is cited as the image of spending for display whose apparent substance is washed away.  · terms: فَتَرَكَهُۥ صَلْدًۭا, fe-terakehû saldâ  · verses: 2:264
21b: The elevated garden in 2:265 is cited as the contrasting image of spending for God's approval: it gives a doubled yield and remains productive whether it receives heavy rain or drizzle.  · verses: 2:265
21c: I'sârun fîhi nâr in 2:266 places the root's whirlwind image inside the sequence of parables about spending, specifically a garden's destruction when its owner has become old and has dependent, weak children.  · terms: إِعْصَارٌۭ فِيهِ نَارٌۭ, i'sârun fîhi nâr, ٱلْكِبَرُ, kiber, ذُرِّيَّةٌۭ ضُعَفَآءُ, zurriyyetun duafâ  · verses: 2:266
21d: Leallekum tetefekkerûn in 2:266 is cited as the verse's statement that this garden-and-whirlwind example is offered to prompt reflection.  · terms: لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَفَكَّرُونَ, leallekum tetefekkerûn  · verses: 2:266

## ¶22
22a: The garden example gathers several senses associated with the root: grapes suggest squeezing, irrigated produce suggests income, old age suggests life's afternoon, and the destructive wind supplies the explicit i'sâr word. Only the whirlwind is actually named by the root in the verse.  · terms: asr, i'sâr
22b: The garden's destruction is read as a lifetime's accumulated fruit lost precisely when its owner no longer has the strength to plant again.
22c: That irrecoverable garden is presented as the most concrete image of the surah's second-verse loss; heard beside asr, the whirlwind figures time's ability to scatter accumulated gains in an instant.  · terms: asr, i'sâr
22d: The fragrance image provides another outcome of the same rising movement: a person's passing can leave a beautiful trace behind.
