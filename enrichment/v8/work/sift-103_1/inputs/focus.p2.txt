[TABRISI:103:3/r1] TABRISI d.548 · holds · The root of ʿaṣr means squeezing cloth to extract water; the term came to mean a time in which events can be pressed or late afternoon. Poems illustrate “the two ʿaṣrs” as morning/evening and night/day. «أصل العصر عصر الثوب ونحوه وهو فتله لإخراج مائه»
[TABRISI:103:3/r18] TABRISI d.548 · holds · Though brief, the sūra demonstrates the Qurʾan's inimitability by encompassing what people need to know and practice in religion. «وفي هذه السورة أعظم دلالة على إعجاز القرآن ألا ترى أنها مع قلة حروفها» [filed under 103:1-3]
[TABRISI:103:3/r20] TABRISI d.548 · ابن مسعود، علي · reports · A reading attributed to Ibn Masʿūd adds “indeed, humanity is in loss”; the report from ʿAlī says this remains through the end of time. «وقيل: إن في قراءة ابن مسعود { والعصر إن الإنسان لفي خسر }» [filed under 103:1-3]
[TABRISI:103:3/r3] TABRISI d.548 · ابن عباس، الكلبي، الجبائي · reports · They say the oath is by time, whose alternating nights and days provide a lesson for perceptive people. «أقسم سبحانه بالدهر لأن فيه عبرة لذوي الأبصار»
[TABRISI:103:3/r4] TABRISI d.548 · الحسن، قتادة · reports · They identify the oath as late afternoon, the day's final edge as the sun sets and night arrives; this complements the oath by forenoon, and both times are revered by the adherents of the two religions. «هو وقت العشي عن الحسن وقتادة فعلى هذا أقسم سبحانه بالطرف الأخير من النهار»
[TABRISI:103:3/r5] TABRISI d.548 · مقاتل · reports · Maqātil says the oath is by the mid-afternoon prayer, the middle prayer. «أقسم بصلاة العصر وهي الصلاة الوسطى عن مقاتل»
[TABRISI:103:3/r6] TABRISI d.548 · ابن كيسان · reports · Ibn Kaysān takes ʿaṣr as night and day, together called the two ʿaṣrs. «هو الليل والنهار ويقال لهما العصران عن ابن كيسان»

## IBNJAWZI-NUZHA: Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597 AH
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p181/r10] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA d.597 · holds · Twenty-fourth: Abu Lahab (Abd al-ʿUzza) is the insan named in al-ʿAsr 103:2. «والرابع والعشرون : أبو لهب بن عبد العزى بن عبد المطلب [ ومنه ] قوله تعالى في العصر» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p277/r1] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA d.597 · holds · Khusran is defined as naqs (diminution); in common usage it is the loss of part of the capital. «الخسران : النقص . وهو في التعارف نقص جزء من رأس المال»

## RAZI-FULL: Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606 AH
[RAZI-FULL:v32p80/r1] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · One view takes العصر as time or the age; a report says the Prophet swore by time, but al-Razi refuses to treat its wording as Qur'an because that would invalidate prayer. «ما روى عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه أقسم بالدهر»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p80/r2] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Time contains astonishing opposites such as health and illness or wealth and poverty, and resists a simple account of existence because past and future are absent while the indivisible present cannot be divided. «أن الدهر مشتمل على الأعاجيب»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p80/r3] RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · A final moment of life can redeem a wasted thousand years through repentance and eternal salvation, so time is among the greatest blessings. «فلو ضيعت ألف سنة ، ثم تبث في اللمحة الأخيرة من العمر»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p80/r4] RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Night and day are opportunities to remember and give thanks; the Qur'an also connects time and what occupies it to God's dominion. «فلذلك أقسم به ونبه على أن الليل والنهار فرصة يضيعها المكلف»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p80/r5] RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Arabs blamed misfortune on time, but the oath teaches that time is a blessing and the human being who wastes it is at fault. «أنهم كانوا يضيفون الخسران إلى نوائت الدهر»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p80/r6] RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · As each age passes, life shrinks; unless a person gains something in return, the loss of lifespan is itself a loss. «أنه تعالى ذكر العصر الذي بمضيه ينتقص عمرك» [filed under 103:1-2]
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r1] RAZI-FULL d.606 · مقاتل · reports · Muqatil says the oath is by the Asr prayer, citing the Hafsa recension of 2:238 and a reported reading of 5:106 as referring to Asr. «القول الثالث : وهو قول مقاتل : أراد صلاة العصر»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r10] RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Razi says the parable identifies the Prophet's era as the time assigned to him and his community; the oath can mean his era, alongside his place and lifespan (90:2; 15:72). «فهذا الخبر دل على أن العصر هو الزمان المختص به وبأمته»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r11] RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The Prophet and his community are like the setting in which events occur; since God honors that setting, their rejection of his call makes their loss and abandonment all the greater. «فإذا وجب تعظيم حال الظرف فقس حال المظروف ، ثم وجه القسم ، كأنه تعالى يقول : أنت يا محمد حضرتهم ودعوتهم ، وهم أعرضوا عنك»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r2] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · A Prophetic report likens missing the Asr prayer to losing one's family and wealth, supporting its special merit. «قوله عليه السلام : ( من فاتته صلاة العصر فكأنما وتر أهله وماله )»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r3] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · One argument for the Asr interpretation is that people find it harder to pray as trade and livelihood occupy them late in the day. «لتهافت الناس في تجاراتهم ومكاسبهم آخر النهار واشتغالهم بمعايشهم»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r4] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · An unnamed report tells of a woman confessing adultery, killing her child in a vessel of vinegar, and selling it; the Prophet named penalties and linked the matter to abandoning Asr. «أما الزنا فعليك الرجم ، أما قتل الولد فجزاؤه جهنم»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r5] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Asr closes the day's acts of worship like repentance closes deeds; swearing by it magnifies the prayer and urges its performance, through which loss becomes profit. «أن صلاة العصر بها يحصل ختم طاعات النهار ، فهي كالتوبة بها يختم الأعمال ، فكما تجب الوصية بالتوبة كذا بصلاة العصر»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r6] RAZI-FULL d.606 · النبي · reports · The Prophet says God will neither look at nor speak to three people on Judgment Day, including a man who lies under oath after Asr. «قال النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم : ( ثلاثة لا ينظر الله إليهم يوم القيامة»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r7] RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Although prayer is a human act, God swears by it as a noble command He prescribed, not as our deed. «والجواب : أنه ليس قسما من حيث إنها فعلنا ، بل من حيث إنها أمر شريف تعبدنا الله تعالى بها»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r8] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · An unnamed fourth view takes the oath as referring to the era of the Messenger. «القول الرابع : أنه قسم بزمان الرسول عليه السلام»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81#2/r9] RAZI-FULL d.606 · النبي · reports · A Prophetic parable compares workers hired from dawn to noon, noon to afternoon, and afternoon to sunset; the last group receives double pay, prompting the others' complaint and God's reply that this is His grace. «قوله عليه السلام : ( إنما مثلكم ومثل من كان قبلكم»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81/r1] RAZI-FULL d.606 · أبو مسلم · reports · Abu Muslim reads العصر as one end of the day, the afternoon, paired with the morning's signs of resurrection, judgment, and the evening's signs of death and destruction. «وهو قول أبي مسلم : المراد بالعصر أحد طرفي النهار»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81/r2] RAZI-FULL d.606 · الحسن · reports · Al-Hasan says afternoon signals that markets are closing: whoever has failed to earn must face dependents and creditors, a picture of the approaching Judgment and unprepared worldly life. «قال الحسن رحمه الله : إنما أقسم بهذا الوقت»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81/r3] RAZI-FULL d.606 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · A prophetic report says that one who swears falsely after the afternoon prayer will face God's displeasure and lack of regard on Judgment Day. «قوله عليه السلام : ( من حلف بعد العصر كاذبا»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81/r4] RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · As midmorning in the previous sura assures the righteous of rising fortunes, the afternoon oath warns the doomed of decline; its remaining daylight urges repentance. «فكما أقسم في حق الرابح بالضحى فكذا أقسم في حق الخاسر بالعصر»
[RAZI-FULL:v32p81/r5] RAZI-FULL d.606 · بعض السلف · reports · A seller of ice taught a student the sura's meaning by crying, Pity the one whose capital melts: age passes without gain and leaves its owner at a loss. «وعن بعض السلف : تعلمت معنى السورة من بائع الثلج» [filed under 103:1-2]
[RAZI-FULL:v32p85/r8] RAZI-FULL d.606 · سلام بن المنذر · reports · A report has Salām ibn al-Mundhir reading al-ʿaṣr with a kasra on ṣād; al-Rāzī attributes it to stopping for breath or another impediment, not to carrying pause into liaison. «ما يروى عن سلام بن المنذر أنه قرأ ، والعصر بكسر الصاد ولعله وقف لانقطاع نفس أو لعارض منعه من إدراج القراءة»

## QURTUBI-FULL: al-Qurṭubī, d. 671 AH
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:1/r1] QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس وغيره · reports · Ibn Abbas and others say al-asr means time or age; its changing conditions point to the Creator. «أي الدهر قاله ابن عباس وغيره. فالعصر مثل الدهر»
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:1/r10] QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · The oath may also have an omitted object, meaning by the Lord of the age. «وقيل: معناه ورب العصر.»
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:1/r11] QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · مالك · reports · Malik says a person who swears not to speak to someone for an asr has sworn off speech for a year, the most expansive interpretation and consistent with his stricter approach to oaths. «قال مالك: من حلف ألا يكلم رجلا عصرا: لم يكلمه سنة.»
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:1/r12] QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الشافعي · reports · Al-Shafi'i says the oath is fulfilled by refraining for an hour, unless the speaker intended another duration. «وقال الشافعي: يبر بساعة، إلا أن تكون له نية،»
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:1/r13] QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن العربي · reports · Ibn al-Arabi prefers al-Shafi'i's hour unless the person swearing is Arab, in which case the stated intended meaning is accepted if plausible, except the shortest reading. «وقال الشافعي: يبر بساعة، إلا أن تكون له نية، وبه أقول إلا أن يكون الحالف عربيا»
[QURTUBI-FULL:103:1/r2] QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الشاعر · reports · A poem uses the contrast of a day, month, and age to illustrate time's scale. «ويوم الهوى شهر وشهر الهوى دهر»
