#84 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masʿud's reading ʿattā ḥīn is a Hudhayl dialect form. «وفي قراءة ابن مسعود ( عتى حين ) وهي لغة هذيل ، وعن عمر رضي الله عنه»
#85 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · عمر · reports · After hearing a man recite ʿattā ḥīn, ʿUmar asked his teacher and wrote Ibn Masʿud to teach Quraysh speech rather than Hudhayl speech. «فكتب إليه : إن الله أنزل هذا القرآن فجعله عربيا وأنزله بلغة قريش»
#86 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · After Joseph refused sin, the wife of al-Aziz allegedly told her husband that the Hebrew boy had publicly disgraced her and was explaining matters on his own terms; she demanded either release so she could defend herself and accuse him, or his imprisonment while she remained confined. They then chose prison. «ويئست منه امرأة العزيز طالبته بأن قالت لزوجها»
#87 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says Joseph was put on a donkey, a drum was beaten, and he was announced through Egypt's markets as a Hebrew who desired his master and deserved prison; Abu Salih says Ibn Abbas wept whenever recounting it. «قال ابن عباس: فأمر به فحمل على حمار»
#88 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · بدا means appeared, with a deleted subject estimated as بدو or رأي; the plural لهم refers to the king alone because the decision involved consultation, and ليسجننه is an oath clause. «ظهر، والفاعل ب بدا محذوف تقديره»
#89 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · سيبويه · reports · Sibawayh's explicit position is that ليسجننه cannot be the grammatical subject of بدا, since a sentence cannot be its subject; the claim that it is the subject is wrong, and the sentence instead explains the omitted subject. «ولا يجوز أن يكون الفاعل ببدا ليسجننه لأن الفاعل لا يكون جملة بوجه»
#90 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · مجاهد وغيره · reports · Interpreters identify the signs as the torn shirt; Ikrima says Joseph's scratched face accompanied the shirt episode, and al-Suddi says the women cut their hands. «أنها قد القميص، قاله مجاهد وغيره»
#91 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The point is that they decided on prison after signs had exonerated Joseph; scratched faces and cut hands do not prove innocence, whereas the shirt report does. If the speaker was an infant it was a great miracle; if an adult, the shirt offered an inference. 'Signs' may also mean whatever proofs were clear in degree. «ومقصد الكلام إنما هو أنهم رأوا سجنه بعد بدو الآيات المبرئة له من التهمة»
#92 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Alternatively, 'after they saw the signs' may mean that the overall circumstances showed Joseph was innocent, without identifying any one specific sign. «أي من بعد ما ظهر لهم من وجوه الأمر»
#93 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Ḥīn means an unspecified span of time, potentially short or long; the Qur’an uses it in both senses. «في كلام العرب وفي هذه الآية الوقت من الزمن غير محدود يقع للقليل والكثير»
#94 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · عكرمة · reports · He takes ḥīn here to mean seven years. «يراد به سبعة أعوام، وقيل: بل يراد بذلك سنة»
#95 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Another reported interpretation takes the period as one year; the author relates these durations to what was disclosed during Joseph’s imprisonment. «وهذا بحسب ما كشف الغيب في سجن يوسف»
#96 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · عمر بن الخطاب · reports · Umar heard a Hudhayli reading عتى حين and wrote Ibn Masud to teach people in Quraysh Arabic rather than Hudhayl’s dialect. «بلغة قريش، فبها أقرئ الناس، ولا تقرئهم بلغة هذيل»
#97 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas counts three slips by Joseph: the episode with her, asking the cupbearer to remember him, and accusing the brothers of theft, which prompted the reply about Benjamin. «عثر يوسف عليه السلام ثلاث عثرات» [filed under 12:35, 12:42, 12:70, 12:77]
#98 TABRISI d.548 · holds · Although the women were previously mentioned, the masculine plural 'them' refers to the king; another reading takes it as Zuleikha and her helpers, with masculine agreement for a mixed group. «أراد به زليخا وأعوانها»
#99 TABRISI d.548 · reports · The signs proving Yusuf's innocence were the shirt torn from behind and the women's cut hands, according to Qatada and others. «العلامات الدالة على براءة يوسف وهي قد القميص من دبره وجز الأيدي عن قتادة»
#100 TABRISI d.548 · reports · Another interpretation takes the signs as evidence that the women had despaired of obtaining Yusuf. «العلامات الدالة على الإياس منه»
#101 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The grammatical subject of بدا is omitted and understood as بداء; the decision that appeared was the decision to imprison him. «وقوله بدا فاعله مضمر وتقديره ثم بدا لهم بداء»
#102 TABRISI d.548 · السدي · reports · Al-Suddi says Zuleikha told her husband that Yusuf had publicly disgraced her by saying she had seduced him, and demanded release to explain herself or his imprisonment. «قال السدي: وذلك إن المرأة قالت لزوجها إن هذا العبد قد فضحني»
#103 TABRISI d.548 · reports · One explanation says they imprisoned Yusuf so the public would assume he was guilty, since criminals are the ones imprisoned. «الغرض من الحبس أن يظهر للناس أن الذنب كان له»
#104 TABRISI d.548 · reports · Another explanation is that the prison was near Zuleikha, who wanted to see Yusuf when she looked out. «كان الحبس قريبا منها فأرادت أن يكون بقربها حتى إذا أشرفت عليه رأته»
#105 TABRISI d.548 · عكرمة، الكلبي، الجبائي · reports · The imprisonment lasted seven years according to ʿIkrima, five according to al-Kalbi, or until the affair was forgotten and Yusuf's news faded according to al-Jubbaʾi. «إلى سبع سنين عن عكرمة»
#106 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · أبو صالح عن ابن عباس · reports · One view says the signs leading to Joseph's imprisonment were the torn shirt and his cousin's judgment against the wife. «أحدها أنها شق القميص وقضاء ابن عمها عليها رواه أبو صالح عن ابن عباس»
#107 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس عن أبي صالح · reports · The otherwise unbounded word “a while” is estimated as five years in one report from Ibn Abbas. «أحدها خمس سنين رواه أبو صالح عن ابن عباس»
#108 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس · reports · Another report from Ibn Abbas estimates one year. «والثاني سنة روي عن ابن عباس أيضا»
#109 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima estimates seven years. «والثالث سبع سنين قاله عكرمة»
#110 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عطاء · reports · Ata interprets the term as lasting until the gossip had died down. «والرابع إلى انقطاع القالة قاله عطاء»
#111 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الماوردي · reports · Al-Mawardi says the term was unspecified; the author prefers this because the jailers set no fixed duration, while commentators estimated Joseph's actual stay. «والخامس أنه زمان غير محدود ذكره الماوردي وهذا هو الصحيح لأنهم لم يعزموا على حبسه مدة معلومة»
#112 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مجاهد عن ابن عباس · reports · A second view includes the shirt being cut, the witness, the women's hand-cutting, and their awe at Joseph. «والثاني أنها قد القميص وشهادة الشاهد وقطع الأيدي وإعظام النار إياه رواه مجاهد عن ابن عباس»
#113 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الماوردي · reports · Al-Mawardi says the signs were Joseph's beauty and chastity. «والثالث جماله وعفته ذكره الماوردي»
#114 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · Wahb says the women urged the wife to imprison Joseph, hoping seclusion would let them entice him and stop public gossip that she hated him. «أن يستهوينه حين يخلو لهن في السجن وقلن متى سجنتيه قطع ذلك عنك قالة الناس»
#115 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · When Joseph remained unmoved, she later asked the master to imprison him because his presence exposed her; he agreed and treated him harshly. «فلما يئست قالت لسيدها إن هذا العبد قد فضحني وقد أبغضت رؤيته فائذن لي في سجنه»
#116 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · السدي · reports · Al-Suddi reports that she demanded permission either to leave and clear herself or to jail Joseph as he had jailed her; the master and advisers chose imprisonment. «قالت إما أن تأذن لي فأخرج وأعتذر بعذري وإما أن تحبسه كما حبستني»
#117 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjaj says Aziz first ordered only that Joseph be ignored, then changed his mind. «قال الزجاج كان العزيز أمر بالإعراض فقط ثم تغير رأيه عن ذلك»
#118 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن الأنباري · reports · One reading of “then it appeared to them” is that imprisonment emerged as their plan through discussion and deliberation. «أحدهما ثم بدا لهم أي ظهر لهم بالقول والرأي والفكر سجنه»
#119 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن الأنباري · reports · Alternatively, they decided about Joseph that they must imprison him; the lām answers an unspoken oath. «والثاني ثم بدا لهم في يوسف بدء فقالوا والله لنسجننه فاللام جواب يمين مضمرة»
#120 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · After Joseph's innocence became evident, the wife falsely accused him of publicly defaming her and pressed her husband to choose between letting her leave to defend herself and imprisoning Joseph; the Aziz chose imprisonment to quiet scandal. «إن هذا العبد العبراني فضحني في الناس»
#121 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The signs of Joseph's innocence included the shirt torn from behind, scratches on her face, and the Aziz's judgment that her plot was great; other decisive signs were suppressed to hide the scandal. «والمراد من الآيات براءته بقد القميص من دبر»
#122 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Badā means that their opinion changed from what it had been before. «لأن البداء عبارة عن تغير الرأي عما كان عليه»
#123 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Grammarians reject making one verb the subject of another and therefore paraphrase the construction as then their imprisonment appeared advisable; al-Razi challenges the grammatical premise. «تقدير الكلام ثم بدا لهم سجنه»
#124 RAZI-FULL d.606 · rejects · He argues that a verb can be the subject of a statement: if the predicate says an item is a verb, either the verb itself is discussed or the alternative makes the statement false. «بل نقول في هذا المقام»
#125 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The continuation argues that if the subject in ضرب فعل is the verbal form, it is a noun and calling it a verb is false; if it is the verb, the example proves verbs can be discussed as subjects. «المخبر عنه هو هذه الصيغة وهي اسم»
