#258 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · An ʿiṣār is a fierce wind; its fire burns the garden’s fruit and destroys its trees, leaving the elderly owner helpless. «ولهذا قال تعالى: {وله ذرية ضعفاء فأصابها إعصار} وهو الريح الشديد»
#259 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · ابن عباس · reports · An alternate Ibn Abbas report depicts a man wasting his garden in youth, then losing it in old age when weak children cannot restore or support him. «عن ابن عباس قال: ضرب الله له مثلا حسنا، وكل أمثاله حسن، قال»
#260 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · The ruined garden parallels the disbeliever at resurrection: he has no good to seek renewed standing and loses reward when most desperate for it. «كذلك الكافر يوم القيامة، إذ رد إلى الله عز وجل، ليس له خير فيستعتب»
#261 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · Ibn Kathir notes that this interpretation is reported by al-Bukhari alone. «وهو من أفراد البخاري، رحمه الله»
#262 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · رسول الله · reports · Al-Hakim reports the Prophet praying that his broadest provision come in old age and near the end of life. «أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم كان يقول في دعائه: "اللهم اجعل أوسع رزقك علي عند كبر سني وانقضاء عمري"»
#263 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · The signs teach readers to consider and understand parables, then apply them to their intended meanings. «أي: تعتبرون وتفهمون الأمثال والمعاني، وتنزلونها على المراد منها»
#264 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The question expresses intense desire for an orchard, its inner space screened by trees; dates and grapes specify what the earlier garden parable left general. «أي حديقة تستر داخلها وعين هنا ما أبهمه في المثل الأول»
#265 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الحرالي · reports · The date palm is useful in its entirety, from its lasting leaves and nourishing fruit to its wood, resembling a believer whose whole person benefits others. «في كليتها نفعها حتى في خشبها طعام للآدمي بخلاف سائر الشجر»
#266 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الحرالي · reports · The grapevine branches upward, downward, and in every direction, resembling a pious believer honored through piety in every direction. «بل يتفرع علوا وسفلا ويمنة ويسرة»
#267 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Running rivers indicate fertile, generous land; the author contrasts this irrigated garden with the earlier rain-fed ridge orchard, since irrigation failure costs more than rain-fed cultivation. «فإن الجائحة في السقي أشد على المالك منها في البعل»
#268 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The garden's abundance includes every kind of fruit in addition to dates and grapes, making its destruction especially devastating. «له فيها من كل الثمرات»
#269 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The owners children are young and weak, compounding his own weakness from old age and making the garden essential to his dependents. «بالصغر كما ضعف هو بالكبر»
#270 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الحرالي · reports · Iʿṣār is an intensified form linked with squeezing out hidden contents; al-Ḥarālī describes a fierce wind within cloud, with a cold edge likened to one kind of fire. «صيغة اشتداد بزيادة الهمزة والألف فيه من العصر وهو الشدة المخرجة لخبء الأشياء»
#271 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الحكيم · reports · A sage's maxim reverses the sequence of thought and action: the end of work is the beginning of thought, and the end of thought is the beginning of work. «أول الفكرة آخر العمل وأول العمل آخر الفكرة»
#272 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الأصفهاني · reports · Al-Aṣfahānī defines an iʿṣār as a wind that swirls across the ground and then rises toward the sky like a column. «ريح تستدير في الأرض ثم تسطع نحو السماء كالعمود»
#273 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The fiery whirlwind burns the garden and leaves its aged owner helpless, burdened with dependents and stripped of wealth; the analogy depicts ruin at the moment of greatest need. «وبقي صاحبها بمضيعة مع ضعفه وثقل ظهره بالعيال وقلة المال»
#274 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الحرالي · reports · The first garden image is ruined by an affliction from beneath, at the root; this one is struck from above, at the branches, marking two directions from which harm can come. «فجعل المثل الول في الحب أي الذي على الصفوان»
#275 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Someone who spoils charitable deeds through abuse faces a loss like the orchard owner and his family when their hopes are destroyed. «فحال من رأى في أعماله أو آذى في صدقة ماله في يوم القيامة وأهواله» [filed under 2:264, 2:266]
#276 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · عمر · reports · In a report recorded by al-Bukhārī in the tafsir, ʿUmar asks the Prophet's Companions what the garden verse means after they remain silent. «روى البخاري رضي الله تعالى عنه في التفسير عن عبيد بن عمير قال قال عمر»
#277 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās explains the garden as a person's deeds: a wealthy man obeys God, then Satan leads him into sins until they drown his deeds. «قال ابن عباس رضي الله تعالى عنه»
#278 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · God clarifies all signs so people may reflect and benefit; the passage distinguishes those whose reflection multiplies their yield from those whose unconsidered actions let desire bring disaster. «أي ليكون حالكم حال من يرجى أن يحمل نفسه على الفكر»
#279 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · الحرالي · reports · Worship has no good without reflection; like a builder, a person must consider both the start and end of religious action, preparing its beginnings and later consequences. «لا خير في عبادة إلا بتفكر»
#280 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Asked by Umar about the garden parable, Ibn Abbas says it depicts a wealthy person who obeys God, then Satan draws him into sins that drown his deeds. «عن ابن عباس قال: قال عمر يوما لأصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
#281 DURR-FULL d.911 · عمر · reports · Umar invites Ibn Abbas to explain his whispered thought; Ibn Abbas says the parable concerns a deed, and Umar agrees it means a person's deeds needed most at old age and resurrection. «قال: قل يا ابن أخي ولا تحقر نفسك قلت: عنى بها العمل قال: وما عنى بها العمل»
#282 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · A person who spent youth doing good but becomes old with weak dependents, then loses his garden in a fiery storm, resembles a disbeliever left without good or help at resurrection. «عن ابن عباس قال: ضرب الله مثلا حسنا»
#283 DURR-FULL d.911 · السدي · reports · The garden is a parable for ostentatious charity: at resurrection the donor needs his charity but finds showmanship has burned its reward. «عن السدي في الآية قال: هذا مثل آخر لنفقة الرياء إنه ينفق ماله»
#284 DURR-FULL d.911 · مجاهد · reports · It depicts someone who neglects obedience until death; old age and helpless children cannot help the ruined garden, just as all becomes regret after death. «عن مجاهد في الآية قال: هذا مثل المفرط في طاعة الله حتى يموت»
#285 DURR-FULL d.911 · عمر · reports · Umar interprets the parable as a person doing good until late life, when he most needs his deeds, then committing evil. «عن ابن أبي مليكة أن عمر تلا هذه الآية فقال: هذا مثل ضرب للإنسان يعمل عملا صالحا»
#286 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · The parable is a person who begins with good deeds but persists in sin late in life and dies in that state; the fiery whirlwind represents that final evil. «عن ابن عباس في الآية قال: ضربت مثلا للعمل يبدأ فيعمل عملا صالحا»
#287 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas tells Umar the garden means good work ruined by an evil final deed when a person most needs a good ending; Umar is struck by the interpretation. «قال: يا أمير المؤمنين هذا مثل ضربه الله فقال: أيحب أحدكم أن يكون عمره يعمل بعمل أهل الخير»
#288 DURR-FULL d.911 · عائشة · reports · The Prophet prayed that his broadest provision come in old age and near the end of his life. «عن عائشة قالت: كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يدعو اللهم اجعل أوسع رزقك علي»
#289 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · An i'sar containing fire is a wind with intensely hot, scorching air. «عن ابن عباس في قوله {إعصار فيه نار} قال: ريح فيها سموم شديدة»
#290 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · In a lexical exchange with Ibn al-Azraq, he defines i'sar as a violent wind and cites Arabic poetry as evidence. «عن ابن عباس إن نافع بن الأزرق سأله عن قوله {إعصار} قال: الريح الشديدة قال: وهل تعرف العرب ذلك»
#291 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · God clarifies signs so people reflect; believers should understand the parables, as also stated at 29:43. «عن قتادة في قوله {كذلك يبين الله لكم الآيات لعلكم تتفكرون} قال: هذا مثل ضربه الله فاعقلوا عن الله أمثاله»
#292 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The interrogative hamza denies that anyone should wish this, unlike a question denying an act already occurring; he illustrates the difference with two Arabic examples. «الهمزة لإنكار الوقوع كما في قوله أأضرب أبي لا لإنكار الواقع»
#293 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · A garden can mean its dense trees; if it means the land containing them, trees must be supplied for rivers to flow beneath them. «والجنة تطلق على الأشجار الملتفة المتكاثفة وهو الأنسب»
#294 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The syntax of ‘all fruits’ makes it a description of provision, as in the phrase ‘each of us has an assigned station.’ «الظرف الأول خبر والثاني حال والثالث مبتدأ»
#295 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · ‘All fruits’ means a great variety, not literally every fruit, as in the promise of every kind of fruit in Paradise. «وليس المراد بالثمرات العموم بل إنما هو التكثير»
#296 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Palms and grapes are singled out as the noblest and most useful trees; other trees are included by giving these prominence. «النخيل والأعناب لما كانا أكرم الشجر وأكثرها نفعا خصهما بالذكر»
#297 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Old age intensifies need for the garden's benefits while making it harder to earn a living; weak young dependents cannot work or support the owner. «كبر السن الذي هو مظنة شدة الحاجة إلى منافعها»
#298 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The whirlwind is a violent revolving wind rising from the ground toward the sky like a column, carrying fire that ruins the garden. «ريح عاصفة تستدير في الأرض ثم تنعكس منها ساطعة إلى السماء»
