#85 THALABI d.427 · أنس · reports · The Prophet says God approved his example of sitting with poor believers and calls their assembly a gathering of earlier prophets and righteous people. «إن الله رضي لي أن أتأسى بمجالسكم وأن الله معنا»
#86 THALABI d.427 · عائذ بن عمرو · reports · After Abu Bakr rebuked Salman, Suhayb, and Bilal for insulting Abu Sufyan, the Prophet warned that upsetting them upsets God; they forgave Abu Bakr. «لعلك أغضبتهم إن كنت أغضبتهم فقد أغضبت ربك»
#87 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · holds · Seeking God's face may mean intending Him, since Arabic uses face for the thing itself to magnify it, or seeking His obedience by aiming toward what He directs. «معنى قوله : { يريدون وجهه } فيه قولان : أحدهما : يريدون بدعائهم , لأن العرب تذكر وجه الشيء إرادة له»
#88 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · الحسن · reports · Al-Hasan says each person answers for their own deeds, so neither group bears the other's reward or punishment. «أحدها : يعني ما عليك من حساب عملهم من شيء من ثواب أو عقاب . { وما من حسابك عليهم من شيء }»
#89 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · ابن بحر · reports · Ibn Bahr interprets reckoning as provision: neither group is responsible for maintaining the other, since his reading of 78:36 describes a complete, sufficient gift. «والثالث : ما عليك كفايتهم ولا عليهم كفايتك , والحساب الكفاية كقوله تعالى : ^ { عطاء حسابا } ^ [ النبأ : 36 ]»
#90 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · reports · Quraysh leaders asked Muhammad to expel weak believers including Bilal, Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, and Ibn Masud, promising to follow if he did; Umar urged a trial, and the Prophet considered it before the verse came down. «يا محمد اطرد عنا موالينا وحلفاءنا فإنما هم عبيدنا وعتقاؤنا , فلعلك إن طردتهم نتبعك»
#91 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas and Mujahid interpret those calling on their Lord morning and evening as performing the five prayers. «أنها الصلوات الخمس , قاله ابن عباس , ومجاهد .»
#92 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · إبراهيم النخعي · reports · Ibrahim al-Nakhai interprets calling on their Lord as remembrance of God; other views say Qur'an recitation or worship. «والثاني : أنه ذكر الله , قاله إبراهيم النخعي .»
#93 MAWARDI-FULL d.450 · أبو جعفر · reports · Abu Jafar interprets the devotional calling as honoring the Qur'an; al-Dahhak says it is worship of God. «والثالث : تعظيم القرآن , قاله أبو جعفر . والرابع : أنه عبادة الله , قاله الضحاك .»
#94 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · They have no worldly or afterlife request and no concern beyond their Master; having devoted themselves to God, they receive His devoted care. «ولا هم سوى حديث مولاهم»
#95 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · Having no protector or intercessor besides God means relying neither on other creatures nor on one’s own deeds or states, but on divine care and mercy. «لا معتمد لهم من أفعالهم ، ولا مستند من أحوالهم»
#96 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · The command protects the poor and vulnerable companions after some people sought to remove them from the Prophet’s gathering; God took their side and showed the effect of humble prayer. «هذه وصية له - - في باب الفقراء والمستضعفين»
#97 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · The Prophet should look past their ragged outward appearance to the burning longing in their inner lives. «لا تنظر يا محمد إلى خرقتهم على ظاهرهم وانظر إلى حرقتهم في سرائرهم»
#98 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · God made their inward state public and testified that they sought His face; without that testimony no one could confidently claim that a creature seeks God. «قال : { يريدون وجهه } فشهد لهم بالإرادة»
#99 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · In strict theology, will attaches to originated things, whereas God’s eternal, self-subsisting essence is beyond origination; this divine desire is not ordinary choice or merely a lexical sense. «وحقيقة الصمدية متقدسة عن الاتصاف بالحدثان»
#100 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · The most precise account of spiritual desire is a need arising in the heart that robs the seeker of rest until reaching God. «أكثر تحقيقها أنها احتياج يحصل في القلوب يسلب القرار من العبد حتى يصل إلى الله»
#101 QUSHAYRI d.465 · reports · A poem depicts longing overcoming a traveler who crosses wilderness without fear of lions or wolves. «لا أسدا أخشى ولا ذيبا يغلبني شوقي فأطوي السرى»
#102 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · Morning and evening limit the companions’ outward acts, but their inward desire for God’s face lasts without interruption; the phrase about seeking His face is circumstantial. «ودامت إرادتهم فاستغرقت جميع أوقاتهم لأنها من الأحوال الباطنة»
#103 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · The poor are light in burden; God, rather than the Prophet or the companions, takes account of each person and gives each the outcome of his deeds. «فالفقير خفيف الظهر لا يكون منه على أحد كثير مؤنة»
#104 QUSHAYRI d.465 · holds · The superior should be grateful and the lesser patient; in love, gratitude is the lesser person’s path and can equal the superior’s gratitude. «أما الفاضل فليشكر ، وأما المفضول فليصبر»
#105 QUSHAYRI d.465 · reports · Two poems express gratitude even for a beloved’s blame or harm, because the beloved’s name and actions remain precious. «أتاني منك سبك لي فسبي»
#106 WAHIDI-ASBAB d.468 · سعد · reports · Saʿd says the verse was revealed about six poor companions—himself, Ibn Masʿūd, Ṣuhayb, ʿAmmār, al-Miqdād, and Bilāl—after Quraysh demanded their expulsion. «عن المقدام بن شريح عن أبيه عن سعد قال: نزلت هذه الآية فينا ستة في وفي ابن مسعود وصهيب وعمار والمقداد وبلال»
#107 WAHIDI-ASBAB d.468 · خباب بن الأرت · reports · Khabbāb says the verse concerned weak companions who sat with the Prophet morning and evening to learn the Qur’an and religion and hear about Paradise, Hell, death, and resurrection. «عن خباب بن الأرت قال: فينا نزلت، كنا ضعفاء عند النبي - صلى الله عليه وسلم - بالغداة والعشي، فعلمنا القرآن والخير»
#108 WAHIDI-ASBAB d.468 · الأقرع بن حابس وعيينة بن حصن · reports · Al-Aqraʿ b. Ḥābis and ʿUyayna b. Ḥiṣn demanded that the Prophet expel the poor companions before they would sit with him; the verses were revealed in response. «فجاء الأقرع بن حابس التميمي وعيينة بن حصن الفزاري فقالا: إنا من أشراف قومنا وإنا نكره أن يرونا معهم فاطردهم إذا جالسناك»
#109 WAHIDI-ASBAB d.468 · ابن مسعد · reports · Ibn Masʿūd reports that Quraysh leaders objected to the Prophet’s companions Khabbāb, Ṣuhayb, Bilāl, and ʿAmmār, asking whether they were to become their followers. «عن ابن مسعد قال: مر الملأ من قريش على رسول الله - صلى الله عليه وسلم - وعنده خباب بن الأرت وصهيب وبلال وعمار»
#110 WAHIDI-ASBAB d.468 · الربيع · reports · Al-Rabīʿ says poor companions, including Bilāl, ʿAmmār, Ṣuhayb, and Salmān, took the Prophet’s seats first; nobles asked him to draw them near, and the verse followed. «عن الربيع قال: كان رجال يسبقون إلى مجلس رسول الله - صلى الله عليه وسلم - ومنهم بلال وعمار وصهيب وسلمان»
#111 WAHIDI-ASBAB d.468 · عكرمة · reports · ʿIkrima reports that Banū ʿAbd Manāf’s chiefs asked Abū Ṭālib to have Muhammad dismiss his freedmen, servants, and poor followers; ʿUmar suggested trying this, and apologized after revelation. «قال عكرمة: جاء عتبة بن ربيعة وشيبة بن ربيعة ومطعم بن عدي والحارث بن نوفل في أشراف بني عبد مناف»
#112 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud reports that Quraysh leaders asked Muhammad to expel Suhayb, Khabbab, Bilal, Ammar, and other poor Muslims, promising to follow him if he did. «عن عبد الله بن مسعود قال: (مر الملأ من قريش»
#113 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · خباب بن الأرت · reports · Khabbab says Uyayna ibn Hisn and al-Aqra ibn Habis asked the Prophet to remove poor enslaved believers while Arab delegations visited, so they would not be seen with them. «لا يحسن بنا الجلوس مع هؤلاء العبيد الفقراء، فأقمهم»
#114 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · خباب بن الأرت · reports · In Khabbab's account, the Prophet agreed and summoned Ali to write their demand, but revelation came; he set aside the page, turned to the poor Muslims, and drew near to them. «فأنزلت هذه الآية وما بعدها، فنحى رسول الله»
#115 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · ابن الأنباري · reports · Ibn al-Anbari says the Prophet considered prioritizing wealthy leaders to attract their people, intending good rather than contempt for the poor, but God forbade it. «كان قد هم بتقديم الرؤساء وأولي الأموال على الضعفاء وذوي المسكنة»
#116 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says calling on God morning and evening means performing the obligatory dawn and afternoon prayers; Hasan, Mujahid, Qatada, and al-Dahhak similarly define it as prescribed prayer. «بالصلاة المكتوبة يعني: صلاة الصبح وصلاة العصر»
#117 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · إبراهيم · reports · Ibrahim offers remembrance of God at the two ends of the day as one meaning, but also reports that the phrase means the five obligatory prayers, as all exegetes hold. «(الدعاء هاهنا الذكر، أي: يذكرون ربهم طرفي النهار)»
#118 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · ابن عامر · reports · Ibn Amir reads al-ghuduwa with waw; Abu Ali favors the common al-ghadāh because it can be indefinite and take the article, while ghuduwa is ordinarily a definite proper time-name. «وقرأ ابن عامر: (بالغدوة) (¬5) بالواو»
#119 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · أبو علي · reports · Abu Ali says the manuscript's waw does not establish that reading, since ṣalāh is also written with waw for alif; Sibawayh treats ghuduwa and bukra as names for times, with Yūnus reporting their definite use without nunation. «وإن كانت قد كتبت في المصحف بالواو لم تدل على ذلك»
#120 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · أبو علي · reports · For Ibn Amir's reading, Abu Ali cites al-Khalil's permission to say 'today, ghuduwa and bukra' like ḍaḥwa, and time-names sometimes take the article when construed as generic. «زعم الخليل أنه يجوز أن يقول: أتيتك اليوم غدوة وبكرة»
#121 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · holds · The author explains that a proper name becomes shareable and generic when stripped of its individual reference, making forms such as 'the Zayds' possible; the discussion cites Ibn Mayada. «فإذا شورك في اسمه فقد خرج عن أن يكون علما معروفا»
#122 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says the poor worshippers seek God's reward and act for His pleasure without associating anything with Him. «يطلبون ثواب الله ويعملون ابتغاء مرضاة الله»
