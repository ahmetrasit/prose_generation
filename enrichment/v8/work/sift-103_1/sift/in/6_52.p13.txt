#501 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Quraysh leaders disparaged the companions as poor menial workers and argued that respectable people could not join a religion that welcomed them; Muhammad must not lose true companions to win these elites. «his companions were all poor who did menial jobs and how could God select such lowly sections of the society for His religion»
#502 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · If the elites persist in error, they alone bear its consequence; turning away genuine seekers would leave the Prophet liable for neglecting those deserving his care. «If he goes after these wretched elements, they will not be held responsible on his behalf»
#503 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Addressing Muhammad rather than the Quraysh directly strikes at their arrogance and implies they are unworthy of direct address. «instead of addressing them, the Prophet (sws) is addressed. This is to make evident to them that they are not even worthy of being addressed directly.»
#504 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The verb used for driving the poor away is harsh and means to disregard someone disparagingly, matching what Quraysh wanted Muhammad to do to his companions. «it means to disparagingly disregard someone.»
#505 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Jesus (sws) · reports · Jesus’ lost-sheep parable depicts a shepherd seeking one straying sheep instead of being content with the ninety-nine. «Jesus (sws) has stated a parable also: “What do you think? If a man owns a hundred sheep»
#506 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The lost-sheep parable illustrates God’s affection for people who return after error and a prophet’s concern for his nation’s faith. «Just as this parable shows the great affection God has for people who turn to Him after being in error»
#507 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Prophet should warn those who fear God and the hereafter, ignore demands for miracles, and welcome poor believers seeking God’s pleasure without turning them away to attract arrogant elites. «As for those who seek God’s pleasure and want to listen to his message, he must not distance them from himself» [filed under 6:51-55]
#508 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Prophetic concern for people reflects God’s affection and is praiseworthy, but it has limits: the Prophet must not pursue the wayward at the expense of loyal companions. «God does not want his prophet to go after those people who have gone astray at the expense of his real companions.»
#509 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · reports · Farāhī discusses important points on this topic in his commentary on Sūrah ‘Abas; Islahi says he will give his own view later. «Ustādh Far āhī has written some very important points related to this topic in his exegesis of Sūrah ‘Abas.»
#510 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The poor believers seek only their Creator’s pleasure, so the promised mercy and forgiveness answer their actual desire rather than worldly status or riches. «The only desire they had was to please their creator and master.» [filed under 6:52, 6:54]
#511 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · reports · A report in Muslim and other hadith collections says Quraysh notables demanded the poor, vulnerable Companions leave before they would meet Muhammad; he refused, then considered their lesser demand before this verse stopped him. «Müslim’in Sahîh’i (“Fezâilü’s-sahâbe”, 45-46) ve diğer bazı hadis kaynaklarında yer alan bir rivayete göre»
#512 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Habbâb · reports · After the verse, the Prophet and poor Muslims grew so close that they sat knee to knee, and he waited for them to rise before leaving so as not to hurt them. «Habbâb’ın anlattığına göre bu âyetin inmesinden sonra Hz. Peygamber’le yoksul ve kimsesiz müslümanlar arasındaki yakınlık o kadar artmıştır»
#513 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müfessirlerin çoğu · rejects · Most commentators, noting reports that the whole surah descended at once rather than in parts, deny that this verse was occasioned by that incident; even if so, the incident only occasions its universal ethical teaching. «müfessirlerin çoğu bu sûrenin, parça parça değil, tamamının bir defada indiğini bildiren haberleri de dikkate alarak»
#514 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · some interpreters · reports · Some interpreters take the pronoun ‘they’ in the accountability clause to refer to the polytheists. «“Onların hesaplarından sana sorumluluk yoktur...” cümlesindeki “onlar” zamiriyle müşriklere işaret edildiğini düşünenler olmuşsa da»
#515 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · ağırlıklı görüş · prefers · The predominant view is that ‘they’ refers to the poor Muslims mentioned earlier. «ağırlıklı görüş, bununla yukarıda anılan müslümanların kastedildiği yönündedir»
#516 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müşrikler · reports · The polytheists alleged that the poor Muslims gathered around the Prophet for shelter, food, and drink rather than sincere devotion. «Rivayete göre müşrikler, Hz. Peygamber’in çevresinde toplanan söz konusu kişilerin, aslında –samimi dindarlıklarından değil– barınma imkânı buldukları, yiyip içtikleri için orada toplandıklarını ileri sürmüşlerdi»
#517 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · one view · reports · One interpretation says the verse separates the Prophet’s responsibility from the poor Muslims’ responsibility: each bears their own deeds, so he should remain close to them rather than wrong them. «herkesin kendi yapıp ettiklerinin sorumluluğunu yine kendisinin taşıdığı bildirilerek Resûl-i Ekrem’in onlara olan ilgisini sürdürmesi istenmiş»
#518 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · another interpretation · reports · Another reading says the Prophet should not send worshipping poor people away to earn a living: their provision is God’s responsibility, not his calculation. «herkesin rızkını verenin Allah Teâlâ olduğuna işaret buyurulmuştur (Râzî, XII, 236-237)»
#519 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Human worth in Islam rests on faith, reverence for God, and spiritual nobility rather than rank, wealth, or lineage; the verse also shows the Prophet’s character formed by Qur’anic principles. «mevki, zenginlik ve soyuna göre değil iman zenginliğine, Allah’a saygı ve ruh yüceliğine göre değer taşıdığını ortaya koyması»
#520 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Humiliating people valued by God for faith and conduct is injustice, even if the hypothetical offender were a prophet. «farzımuhal bu kimse peygamber bile olsa– zalim olarak gösterilmesi son derece ilgi çekicidir»
#521 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Ghudwa as an instance noun contrasts with ghadāh as general morning, shifting from habitual to specific morning. «ghudwa (instance noun) vs ghadāh (general morning) — shifts from habitual to specific single-morning reading»
#522 STUDYQURAN · reports · Prominent Makkans wanted to join the Prophet’s teaching circle but disliked association with lower-status followers and asked him to send them away. «This verse was reportedly revealed when a group of prominent Makkans indicated that they wished to join the Prophet’s teaching circle»
#523 STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī · reports · Al-Rāzī holds that the Prophet did not sin by initially appearing willing to accommodate the demand because his intention was good. «cannot be said to have sinned in his apparent initial willingness to accommodate the Makkans’ demand (R)»
#524 STUDYQURAN · R, Ṭ · reports · Morning and evening invocation may mean the five canonical prayers performed at their appointed times. «refer to praying the five canonical prayers at their prescribed times»
#525 STUDYQURAN · R, Ṭ · reports · Morning and evening invocation may mean supererogatory remembrance of God or Quran recitation. «performing supererogatory acts of invoking God»
#526 STUDYQURAN · R, Ṭ · reports · The invocation may include all these practices together. «or to all of them (R, Ṭ)»
#527 STUDYQURAN · R · reports · Desiring God’s Face can mean literally hoping to see it, as a sincere lover longs to see the beloved’s face. «with the hope of seeing God’s Face, for any sincere lover longs to see the face of the beloved (R)»
#528 STUDYQURAN · IK, R · reports · Desiring God’s Face can also metaphorically mean sincerity of intention among the Prophet’s followers. «it refers metaphorically to sincerity of intention among the Prophet’s followers (IK, R)»
#529 STUDYQURAN · holds · The verse echoes the Quranic principle that no person bears another’s burden. «echoes the Quranic idea that none shall bear the burden of another (6:164; 17:15; 35:18; 39:7; 53:38)»
#530 STUDYQURAN · Z · prefers · Syntactically, “their reckoning” most likely refers to the pious worshippers’ own reckoning, so the Prophet judges piety by outward acts. «that the Prophet need only judge his followers’ piety on the basis of their outward actions (Z)»
#531 STUDYQURAN · Some commentators · reports · Some commentators say the verse responds to a Quraysh accusation that the poor followed the Prophet for material support rather than religious sincerity. «Some commentators explain this as a response to an accusation made by some of the Quraysh»
#532 STUDYQURAN · R · reports · On that reading, the Prophet need not assess possible ulterior motives behind his followers’ apparent piety. «the verse was meant to indicate that the Prophet had no responsibility to assess the possible ulterior motives behind his»
#533 STUDYQURAN · Ṭ · reports · Some reports say the Makkans asked only that the poor followers be moved to the back rows of prayer, not expelled altogether. «Some reports indicate the Prophet was not asked to send away these followers altogether, but rather to send them to the back rows of prayer»
#534 STUDYQURAN · Z · prefers · More plausibly, the reckoning concerns the prominent Makkans; the Prophet need not court their conversion by yielding to their demand to dismiss sincere followers. «their embracing Islam that he should accede to their demand and “drive away”his pious followers at their behest (Z)»
