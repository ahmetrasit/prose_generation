#171 TABRISI d.548 · ابن عباس، علي بن الحسين، أبو عبد الله · reports · The stay of several years is specified as seven years in reports from Ibn Abbas, ʿAli ibn al-Husayn, and Abu ʿAbd Allah. «أي سبع سنين عن ابن عباس وروي ذلك عن علي بن الحسين ع وأبي عبد الله»
#172 TABRISI d.548 · رسول الله · reports · A report from the Prophet marvels that Yusuf sought help from a creature instead of the Creator; another says his request to be remembered prolonged his stay. «عجبت من أخي يوسف ع كيف استغاث بالمخلوق دون الخالق»
#173 TABRISI d.548 · أبو عبد الله · reports · In a report from Abu ʿAbd Allah, Gabriel asks Yusuf who gave him his beauty, his father's love, the caravan, rescue from the well, and protection from the women; Yusuf answers that God did, and is told to remain in prison for turning to a creature. «جاء جبرائيل ع فقال: يا يوسف من جعلك أحسن الناس»
#174 TABRISI d.548 · holds · Seeking human help to avert harm is permitted and can even be required; the Prophet sought help from the Emigrants and Helpers, so the reports describe a temporary reproach for departing from Yusuf's usual reliance on God, not a prohibition. «الاستعانة بالعباد في دفع المضار والتخلص من المكاره جائز غير منكر ولا قبيح»
#175 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · The man Joseph thought would be saved was the cupbearer. «قوله تعالى ( وقال للذي ظن أنه ناج منهما ) يعني الساقي»
#176 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الضحاك عن ابن عباس · reports · Al-Dahhak transmits a definition of biḍʿ as twelve years. «والثاني اثنتا عشرة سنة قاله الضحاك عن ابن عباس»
#177 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima defines biḍʿ as seven years. «والثالث سبع سنين قاله عكرمة»
#178 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الحسن · reports · Al-Hasan defines biḍʿ as between five and seven. «والرابع أنه ما بين الخمس إلى السبع قاله الحسن»
#179 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid defines biḍʿ as between four and nine. «والخامس أنه ما بين الأربع إلى التسع قاله مجاهد»
#180 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · قتادة · reports · Qatada defines biḍʿ as between three and nine, or ten. «والسادس ما بين الثلاث والتسع والعشر قاله قتادة»
#181 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الفراء والأخفش · reports · Al-Farra says biḍʿ is below ten; al-Akhfash gives the range as one through ten. «والثامن أنه ما دون العشرة قاله الفراء وقال الأخفش البضع من واحد إلى عشرة»
#182 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · أبو عبيدة وابن قتيبة · reports · Abu Ubayda says biḍʿ is less than a decade and half of it; Ibn Qutayba narrows it to one through four. «والتاسع أنه مالم يبلغ العقد ولا نصفه قاله أبو عبيدة قال ابن قتيبة يعني ما بين الواحد إلى الأربعة»
#183 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · أبو عبيدة عن الأثرم · reports · A report from Abu Ubayda transmitted by al-Athram defines biḍʿ as between three and five. «وروى الأثرم عن أبي عبيدة البضع ما بين ثلاث وخمس»
#184 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس والضحاك وقتادة · reports · The total prison stay is estimated as twelve years by Ibn Abbas, fourteen by al-Dahhak, or seven by Qatada. «وفي جملة ما لبث في السجن ثلاثة أقوال أحدها اثنتا عشرة سنة قاله ابن عباس»
#185 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مالك بن دينار · reports · Malik ibn Dinar relates that God rebuked Joseph for taking the cupbearer as an intermediary and lengthened his imprisonment; Joseph wept that affliction had distracted his heart. «لما قال يوسف للساقي اذكرني عند ربك قيل له يا يوسف اتخذت من دوني وكيلا لأطيلن حبسك»
#186 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says “thought” means knew. «أحدهما أنه بمعنى العلم قاله ابن عباس»
#187 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · قتادة · reports · Qatada takes “thought” in its ordinary sense of belief short of certainty. «والثاني أنه الظن الذي يخالف اليقين قاله قتادة»
#188 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · “Remember me with your master” means tell the king that a young man is imprisoned unjustly; the king was al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan. «أي عند صاحبك وهو الملك وقل له إن في السجن غلاما حبس ظلما واسم الملك الوليد بن الريان»
#189 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · أبو صالح عن ابن عباس · reports · One view says Satan made the cupbearer forget to mention Joseph to the king. «فأنسى الشيطان الساقي ذكر يوسف لربه قاله أبو صالح عن ابن عباس»
#190 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Ishaq also assigns the forgetting to the cupbearer, who forgot Joseph before his master. «وبه قال ابن إسحاق»
#191 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مجاهد ومقاتل والزجاج · reports · Mujahid, Muqatil, and al-Zajjaj instead say Satan made Joseph forget God and seek relief from the king; this was deliberate, not absentmindedness. «والثاني فأنسى الشيطان يوسف ذكر ربه وأمره بذكر الملك ابتغاء الفرج من عنده»
#192 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Joseph remained in prison additional years, as punishment for depending on a created person. «أي غير ما كان قد لبث قبل ذلك عقوبة له على تعلقه بمخلوق»
#193 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس عن النبي · reports · Ibn Abbas's report to the Prophet explains biḍʿ as between seven and nine, in the story of Abu Bakr's wager over the Romans. «ما بين السبع إلى التسع»
#194 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Joseph could infer that they served the king from their use of rabb for their master in the later statements about serving wine and asking to be remembered. «هما غلامان كانا للملك الأكبر بمصر أحدهما صاحب طعامه» [filed under 12:36, 12:41, 12:42]
#195 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · The referent of ظن is disputed: if Joseph, it may mean certain knowledge when his interpretation came by revelation, as in 2:46 and 69:20, or ordinary probability if he interpreted by learned principles. «اختلفوا في أن الموصوف بالظن هو يوسف عليه السلام أو الناجي»
#196 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Alternatively the survivor is the one who expected escape; since the two men did not believe Joseph's prophethood, his prediction gave them only an expectation. «والقول الثاني : أن هذا الظن صفة الناجي ، فإن الرجلين السائلين»
#197 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Joseph asks the surviving cupbearer to mention to the king that he was wronged by his brothers, who sold him, and in the case for which he was imprisoned. «اذكر عنده أنه مظلوم من جهة إخوته لما أخرجوه وباعوه»
#198 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · One interpretation says Satan made Joseph forget to remember God, faulting his reliance on a human instead of entrusting his need to God alone, as Abraham did when Gabriel offered help in the fire. «الشيطان أنسى يوسف أن يذكر ربه»
#199 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · On this reading, turning to the cupbearer let Satan gain influence and prolonged Joseph's trial in prison. «رجوع يوسف إلى المخلوق صار سببا لأمرين»
#200 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Al-Razi's own lifelong experience is that relying on others in affairs brings hardship, while relying on God's favor brings the desired result in the best way. «والذي جربته من أول عمري إلى آخره أن الإنسان كلما عول في أمر من الأمور على غير الله صار ذلك سببا إلى البلاء»
#201 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Some prefer the reading about the survivor forgetting Joseph because Satan's suggestion fits the survivor better and seeking help from people to escape injustice is lawful. «ومن الناس من رجح القول الثاني لأن صرف وسوسة»
#202 RAZI-FULL d.606 · prefers · Al-Razi prefers that Satan made Joseph forget God, appealing to the inner truths of worship; the verse's wording also weighs against the alternative, which would say Satan made him forget to mention Joseph to his lord. «واعلم أن الحق هو القول الأول وما ذكره هذا القائل الثاني تمسك بظاهر الشريعة»
#203 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Human help against injustice is permitted but spiritually imperfect for the most devoted; since God still praises Joseph and does not censure him for sexual wrongdoing, Joseph is innocent of the accusations made by ignorant traditionalists. «لا إنكار عليه إلا أنه لما كان ذلك مستدركا من المحققين المتوغلين في بحار العبودية»
#204 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Satan can whisper suggestions but cannot erase knowledge from the heart; he can distract a person with other work so the knowledge is not recalled. «الشيطان يمكنه إلقاء الوسوسة ، وأما النسيان فلا»
#205 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Critics say asking the king for help conflicts with Joseph's rejection of other gods; al-Razi clarifies that rabb here means human master, like master of a house or garment. «كما يقال : رب الدار ، ورب الثوب»
#206 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Joseph's earlier denial of all association with God makes reliance on a human appear inconsistent with total surrender to God. «وذلك نفي للشرك على الإطلاق»
#207 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Seeking people's help against oppression is legally allowed, but for the spiritual elect the better course is to detach from means and focus on the source of causes. «الاستعانة بالناس في دفع الظلم جائزة في الشريعة»
#208 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · A second explanation says Joseph could properly ask the cupbearer but should have qualified the request by mentioning God, such as saying if God wills. «إلا أنه كان من الواجب عليه أن لا يخلي ذلك الكلام من ذكر الله مثل أن يقول إن شاء الله»
#209 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Another view makes the forgotten reminder the cupbearer's failure to mention Joseph to the king, which kept Joseph imprisoned; the author cites reports supporting blame for seeking a human intermediary. «أن الشيطان أنسى ذلك الفتى أن يذكر يوسف للملك»
#210 RAZI-FULL d.606 · النبي · reports · A prophetic report says, May God have mercy on Joseph: had he not asked to be remembered by the king, he would not have remained in prison. «رحم الله يوسف لو لم يقل اذكرني عند ربك ما لبث في السجن»
#211 RAZI-FULL d.606 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says Joseph was disciplined for turning to someone other than God. «وعن قتادة أن يوسف عليه السلام عوقب بسبب رجوعه إلى غير الله»
#212 RAZI-FULL d.606 · إبراهيم التيمي · reports · Ibrahim al-Taymi recounts that Joseph asked the prison companion to remember him before a lord other than the Lord Joseph had named. «وعن إبراهيم التيمي أنه لما انتهى إلى باب السجن»
#213 RAZI-FULL d.606 · مالك · reports · Malik reports that God reproached Joseph for appointing the cupbearer as an agent and would lengthen his imprisonment; Joseph wept and regretted the request. «وعن مالك لما قال يوسف للساقي اذكرني عند ربك»
