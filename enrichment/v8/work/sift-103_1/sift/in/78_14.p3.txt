#144 DURR-FULL d.911 · عكرمة · reports · Ikrima identifies al-muʿṣirāt as clouds and thajjāj as pouring or abundant. «عبد بن حميد وابن جرير عن عكرمة»
#145 DURR-FULL d.911 · الربيع بن أنس · reports · Al-Rabi ibn Anas says al-muʿṣirāt means from heaven and thajjāj means pouring. «عبد بن حميد وابن المنذر عن الربيع بن أنس»
#146 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · The report notes the wording in Fadl ibn Abbas's codex for the rain verse. «عن قتادة في مصحف الفضل»
#147 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada reports Ibn Abbas's reading identifying the source as winds. «عن قتادة في مصحف الفضل بن عباس»
#148 DURR-FULL d.911 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says al-muʿṣirāt are winds, reads bi-l-muʿṣirāt, and glosses thajjāj as pouring. «عن مجاهد {وأنزلنا من المعصرات}»
#149 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The plural “We” signals the majesty and collectivity of the Divine Essence, Names, and Attributes. «النون للعظمة وللإشارة إلى جمعية الذات والأسماء والصفات»
#150 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Muʿṣirāt are clouds the winds are about to squeeze into rain; the imminence prefix means they are ready, not that the rain has already fallen. «هي السحائب إذا أعصرت أي شارفت أن تعصرها الرياح فتمطر ولم تعصرها بعد فالإنزال من المستعد»
#151 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The same imminence prefix appears in harvest-ready crops and a girl reaching puberty, supporting the sense “nearing the time.” «يقال أحصد الزرع إذا حان له أن يحصد وأعصرت الجارية»
#152 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Muʿṣirāt may alternatively mean winds about to squeeze out rain; the causative reading is unsuitable because the winds themselves are the squeezers. «ويجوز أن يكون المراد من المعصرات الرياح التي حان لها أن تعصر فتمطر فهي أيضا اسم فاعل»
#153 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Thajjaj means abundant, successive flowing; the related hadith explains the pilgrimage’s loud talbiya and the pouring of sacrificial blood, while al-Zajjaj glosses it as pouring. «والمراد تتابع القطر حتى يكثر الماء فيعظم النفع به»
#154 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · There is no conflict with rain being sent from the sky: it may descend through clouds, or arise through celestial causes such as the sun lifting water from earth and seas into clouds. «فإن ابتداء المطر إن كان من السماء يكون الإنزال منها إلى سحاب ومنه إلى الأرض»
#155 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس وأبو العالية والربيع والضحاك · reports · They understand المعصرات as clouds; the word can derive from a verb whose hamza marks the approach of their time to release rain. «هي السحائب على ما روي عن ابن عباس وأبي العالية والربيع والضحاك»
#156 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الأعرج · reports · Al-Aʿraj has a variant of ثجاجا, and the word مثاجح names places where water flows together. «وقرأ الأعرج ثجا جاء بجيم ثم حاء مهملة ومثاجح الماء مصابه»
#157 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو النجم العجلي · reports · Abu al-Najm's line about a maiden supports أعصرت for a young woman nearing menstruation, illustrating the time-nearing sense. «قال أبو النجم العجلي تمشي الهوينا مائلا خمارها قد عصرت أو قد دنا أعصارها»
#158 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن كسيان · reports · Ibn Kaysan derives the clouds' name from their giving aid and rain, as though rain were extracted from them by squeezing. «ولذا قال ابن كسيان سميت السحائب بذلك لأنها تغيث فهي من العصرة»
#159 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة · reports · They also explain المعصرات as winds, which compress the clouds until they rain. «وعن ابن عباس أيضا ومجاهد وقتادة أنها الرياح لأنها تعصر السحاب فيمطر»
#160 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · المازني · reports · Al-Mazini takes them as clouds possessing cyclonic winds, which necessarily bring rain. «والمازني اعتبر النسبة أيضا إلا أنه قال المعصرات السحائب ذرات الأعاصير»
#161 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن الزبير وابن عباس والفضل وعبد الله بن يزيد وعكرمة وقتادة · reports · They read بالمعصرات with a causal or instrumental yāʾ, supporting the interpretation as winds; on that reading some construe من as causal, while others understand it as origin. «وأيد تفسيرها بالرياح بقراءة ابن الزبير وابن عباس وأخيه الفضل وعبد الله بن يزيد»
#162 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو الحسن وابن جبير وزيد بن أسلم ومقاتل وقتادة · reports · They say المعصرات are the heavens; the author objects that the heavens are not squeezed to produce rain, and calls the proposed reconciliation far-fetched. «وعن أبي الحسن وابن جبير وزيد ابن أسلم ومقاتل وقتادة أيضا أنها السماوات وتعقب بأن السماء لا ينزل منها الماء بالعصر»
#163 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · ثجاجا means pouring abundantly; the author prefers the intransitive sense because it is more common, though al-Zajjaj takes it transitively. «واختير جعل ما في النظم الكريم من اللازم لأنه الكثر في الاستعمال وجعله الزجاج من المتعدي»
#164 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · A hadith describes the best pilgrimage as loud talbiya and the pouring of sacrificial blood; the objection that clouds could only yield a little water is dismissed as a relative comparison. «ومن المتعدي ما في قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم أفضل الحج العج والثج»
#165 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Thajjaj describes abundant water or liquid pouring and rushing, whether intransitively or as something poured. «Suyun veya bir mâyi‘in kesretle dökülüp şarlayarak akması veya akıtılmasıdır»
#166 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The surah has five attested names: al-Naba, Amma yatasa'alun, Amma, al-Tasa'ul, and al-Mu'asirat; al-Itqan lists four. It is Meccan and was placed after al-Ma'arij and before al-Nazi'at in Jabir ibn Zayd's revelation order. «فهذه خمسة أسماء . واقتصر في ( الإتقان ) على أربعة أسماء : عم»
#167 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Medinan, Syrian, and Basran counters count forty verses; Meccan and Kufan counters count forty-one. «وعد آيها أصحاب العدد من أهل المدينة والشام والبصرة أربعين»
#168 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The surah rebukes mockery of the Quran and resurrection, proves resurrection through creation and humanity's first creation, warns of punishment, and contrasts believers' reward. «اشتملت هذه السورة على وصف خوض المشركين في شأن القرآن وما جاء به مما يخالف معتقداتهم»
#169 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · The Qummi commentary glosses al-muʿṣirāt as clouds and thajjāj as successive pouring. «قال من السحاب { ماء ثجاجا } قال صبا على صب»
#170 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Al-muʿṣirāt are rain clouds, while thajjāj means water pouring abundantly. «المعصرات السحب الماطرة وقيل الرياح»
#171 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · reports · Some identify al-muʿṣirāt as winds that compress clouds to produce rain; on this reading min should mean bi. «وقيل الرياح التي تعصر السحب لتمطر والثجاج الكثير الصب للماء»
#172 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad identifies the surah as late Meccan (following Suyuti) and says its theme is life after bodily death, resurrection, and final judgment. «the continuation of human life after bodily death» [filed under 78:1-40]
#173 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · نافع · reports · Nafiʿ asked about al-muʿṣirāt (78:14); the answer given: clouds press one another so water comes out between them. «وسأل نافع عن قوله تعالى: {وَأَنْزَلْنَا مِنَ الْمُعْصِرَاتِ}»
#174 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · rejects · Author sees no need to restrict al-muʿṣirāt to two clouds; the explanation is a gloss. «ولا نرى ضرورة لقيد المعصرات بسحابتين»
#175 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · الراغب · reports · Raghib does not restrict to two clouds; he explains muʿṣirāt as clouds pressed by rain, and others say they bring the storm. «و"الراغب" لم يحدد سحابتين، بل فسر المعصرات بالسحائب التي تعتصر بالمطر»
#176 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · rejects · Author rejects the view that muʿṣirāt bring the storm (iʿṣār), since the context of the verse and Qurʾānic usage do not support it. «وما نقله في المعصرات من قولٍ بأنها تأتي بالإعصار، لا يؤنس إليه سياق الآية»
#177 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · The word ʿaṣr meaning time occurs in 103:1, alongside al-muʿṣirāt (78:14) and the verbs in Yusuf. «والمعصرات فى آية النبأ 14، والعصر بمعنى الدهر والزمن فى آية العصر» [filed under 103:1, 78:14]
#178 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Paired with al-Mursalat, this surah uses divine providence in the heavens and earth to establish Judgment as the necessary end of a purposeful world, rewarding the God-fearing and punishing rebels. «The basis of arguments is divine providence evident in every single part of the heavens and the earth.» [filed under 78:1, 78:2, 78:3, 78:4, 78:5, 78:6, 78:7, 78:8, 78:9, 78:10, 78:11, 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16, 78:17]
#179 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The surahs share an affirmative-question style aimed at deniers; each verse reproaches them while believers' glad tidings are understood against the warning. «The discourse begins with questions which can only be answered in the affirmative.» [filed under 78:1, 78:2, 78:3, 78:4, 78:5, 78:6, 78:7, 78:8, 78:9, 78:10, 78:11, 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16, 78:17]
#180 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The signs of providence, mercy, wisdom, and power throughout earth and heavens show that humanity is not unchecked or unaccountable; its fixed day of reckoning must arrive. «A call to reflect on the signs of God’s providence, mercy, wisdom and power» [filed under 78:6, 78:7, 78:8, 78:9, 78:10, 78:11, 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16, 78:17]
#181 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-muʿṣirāt describes water-laden clouds or clouds producing rain, with no essential difference between these senses. «clouds replete with water and also»
#182 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Māʾ thajjāj means pounding, widespread, continuous rain; rain displays sky–earth harmony and supports monotheism, providence, and accountability. «pounding, widespread and continuous rain.»
#183 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The seven firm heavens, sun, rain-bearing clouds, and life-sustaining growth display God's power to create a new realm, the afterlife. «Gökleri, alev alev yanarak dünyayı aydınlatan güneşi, bolca yağmur indirerek yeryüzünde birçok nimetin yetişmesine ve hayatın devam etmesine vesile olan bulutları yaratan yüce kudret» [filed under 78:12, 78:13, 78:14, 78:15, 78:16]
#184 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · Some read muʿṣirāt as the winds that bring rain clouds. «is read by some as “the winds” that bring the rain clouds»
