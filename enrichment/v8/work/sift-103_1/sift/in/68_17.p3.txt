#119 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · Another account locates it at Ṣurawān near Ṣanʿāʾ after Jesus; the owner left poor neighbors dates and grapes missed by harvesters, but his sons decided to stop sharing because they had dependents. «وكان صاحبها ينزل للمساكنين ما أخطأه المنجل وما في أسفل الأكراس» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#120 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The sons swore to harvest early in darkness and secretly, keeping the poor from their customary share; the oath's response predicts that they would cut the crop. «فحلفوا ليصرمنها مصبحين في السدف خفية من المساكين،» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#121 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · مصبحين is a circumstantial phrase for the subject of ليصرمنها and means entering the morning, as in 37:137 and the proverb about hearing of a journeyer who has arrived by morning. «هذا حال من فاعل " ليصرمنها "»
#122 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · The kāf in كما is an accusative adjective of an omitted verbal noun, with ما either a verbal noun or relative pronoun; إذ attaches to بلونا. «في موضع نصب نعتا لمصدر محذوف،»
#123 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · ليصرمنها answers the oath, but shifts person from their reported first-person words; exact quotation would be لنصرمنها. «جواب للقسم، وجاء على خلاف منطوقهم،»
#124 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · The garden owners are an example for Quraysh: God gave them the great mercy of Muhammad’s mission and abundant blessings, but they answered with denial, rejection, and opposition. «هذا مثل ضربه الله تعالى لكفار قريش فيما أهدى إليهم من الرحمة العظيمة»
#125 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · The heirs swore to harvest at dawn so that no poor person or petitioner would learn of the crop and receive charity; they made no exception in their oath. «حلفوا فيما بينهم ليجذن ثمرها ليلا لئلا يعلم بهم فقير ولا سائل» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#126 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · سعيد بن جبير · reports · Some early scholars say the brothers lived in Yemen near Sana’a or in Abyssinia; their righteous father maintained the garden, kept a year’s provisions, and gave the surplus to the poor, but his sons called this foolish and were punished by losing crop, profit, and charity. «قال سعيد بن جبير: كانوا من قرية يقال لها ضروان» [filed under 68:17, 68:29]
#127 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · God tests Quraysh and others by granting worldly ease; they mistake this for divine favor and look down on poor believers, though meeting the Prophet itself tests whether they act well or badly. «فغرهم ذلك وظنوا أنهم أحباب»
#128 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · reports · The testing may also refer to the famine the Prophet invoked against Mecca; even after eating carrion, its people did not repent. «أو كان ابتلاؤنا لهم بالقحط الذي دعا عليهم به رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم حتى أكلوا الجيف فما تابوا»
#129 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The famous orchard lay near Sanaa at al-Darwan; its owner customarily left fallen or missed fruit for the poor, but his sons resolved to harvest before sunrise so no poor person could claim any. «كان صاحبه ينادي الفقراء وقت الصرام»
#130 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · The expression for cutting the orchard conveys a decisive, complete severing that would prevent the poor from sharing its fruit. «عبر به عن الجذاذ بدلالته على القطع البائن المعزوم عليه المستأثل»
#131 DURR-FULL d.911 · سعيد بن جبير · reports · Sa'id ibn Jubayr locates the garden in a place in Yemen called Dar, six miles from Sana'a. «عن سعيد بن جبير في قوله»
#132 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says these are people whose story God has recounted and made clear. «عن قتادة في قوله: {إنا بلوناهم كما بلونا أصحاب الجنة}»
#133 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن جريج · reports · Ibn Jurayj reports that Abu Jahl ordered the captives at Badr bound in the mountains rather than killed; the verse likens his power over them to the garden owners' power over their orchard. «عن ابن حريج أن أبا»
#134 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas identifies the owners of the garden as People of the Book. «عن ابن عباس في قوله»
#135 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says they were people of Abyssinia whose father fed the needy from his garden; after his death, his sons swore to harvest early and give no poor person food. «عن ابن عباس في قوله» [filed under 68:17-24]
#136 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says the garden belonged to an Israelite elder who saved his annual provisions and gave away the excess; his sons opposed charity and resolved to exclude the needy. «عن قتادة قال: كانت الجنة» [filed under 68:17-24]
#137 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · God tested Mecca’s people with seven years of famine after the Prophet’s supplication; they ate carrion, hides, bones, and blood in ingratitude. «والمعنى أنا ابتلينا أهل مكة بالقحط والجوع سبع سنين»
#138 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The owners were people of Ṣanʿāʾ, identified by one source as three brothers; reported locations include Darwān near Ṣanʿāʾ and Yemen. «وأصحاب الجنة قوم من أهل صنعاء وفي كشف الأسرار سه برادر بودند»
#139 BURSEVI d.1127 · السهيلي · reports · Al-Suhaylī locates the orchard at Darwān, several farsakhs from Ṣanʿāʾ. «وقال السهيلي : هي جنة بضروان وضروان»
#140 BURSEVI d.1127 · فتح الرحمن · reports · Fatḥ al-Raḥmān locates it in Yemen shortly after Jesus was raised; the generous father kept a year’s provisions and gave the surplus to the poor at harvest. «وفي فتح الرحمن الجنة بستان يقال له ضروان باليمن»
#141 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · After their father died, sons feared his charitable practice would leave too little for their families, so they swore to harvest early in the remaining darkness. «فلما مات أبوهم قال بنوه إن فعلنا ما كان يفعل أبو ناضاق علينا الأمر» [filed under 68:17-18]
#142 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Ṣirām means cutting dates or fruit; their early-morning harvest was to begin before night darkness fully passed. «الصرام والصرم قطع ثمار النخيل» [filed under 68:17-18]
#143 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · ‘They did not make an exception’ means they did not say ‘if God wills’; the apparent sense may instead be that they did not reserve a portion for the poor. «أي لا يقولون إن شاء الله وتسميته استثناء مع إنه شرط» [filed under 68:17-18]
#144 BURSEVI d.1127 · أبو حيان · reports · Abū Ḥayyān reads the phrase as not turning back from their resolve to bar the poor. «وقال أبو حيان : ولا ينثنون عما عزموا عليه من منع المساكين» [filed under 68:17-18]
#145 BURSEVI d.1127 · تاج المصادر · reports · Tāj al-Maṣādir defines istithnāʾ as saying ‘if God wills’; its root suggests repetition because the exception is stated generally and then specifically. «قال في تاج المصادر الاسثناء إن شاء الله كفتن» [filed under 68:17-18]
#146 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · God tested the people of Mecca with drought following the Prophet's prayer against Mudar for years like Joseph's years. «أصبنا أهل مكة ببلية وهي القحط بدعوة رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم وقوله اللهم اشدد وطأتك على مضر»
#147 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · In كما بلونا, kāf marks an implied cognate accusative and ما is a verbal noun; alternatively ما means الذي. «فالكاف في محل نصب مصدر مقدر وما مصدرية وقيل بمعنى الذي»
#148 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The Garden was a familiar orchard near the people in Yemen; its owner used to give God's due, but his heirs withheld its produce from the poor. «كانت بأرض اليمن بالقرب منهم قريبا من صنعاء لرجل كان يؤدي حق الله تعالى منها»
#149 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن جرير · reports · Ibn Jarir's report places the garden in Sawran, Yemen, six miles from Sanʿa. «عن ابن جرير بأرض في اليمن يقال لها صوران بينها وبين صنعاء ستة أميال»
#150 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says the owners were Abyssinians whose father fed the poor; after he died, his sons called him foolish and vowed to feed no poor person. «عن ابن عباس هم ناس من الحبشة كانت لأبيهم جنة وكان يطعم منها المساكين»
#151 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says an Israelite elder gave away his annual surplus, despite his sons' objections, and his sons vowed to stop the charity after his death. «عن قتادة أنه قال كانت لشيخ من بني إسرائيل وكان يمسك قوت سنته ويتصدق بالفضل»
#152 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Another account says the owner left gleanings and leftover fruit for the poor; after his death, his sons feared for their dependents and secretly vowed to harvest early. «وكان يترك للمساكين ما أخطأه المنجل وما في أسفل الأكداس»
#153 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The phrase إذ أقسموا is grammatically governed by بلونا. «كما قال عز وجل ﴿ إذ أقسموا ﴾ معمول لبلونا»
#154 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · They swore to cut the fruit once ripe, entering the morning; the wording reports their oath rather than quoting it directly, since a direct quotation would use first-person plural. «﴿ مصبحين ﴾ داخلين في الصباح وهذا حكاية لقسمهم لا على منطوقهم»
#155 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Sarm means to cut or harvest a vine and its fruit, or sever something completely; although they meant to gather the fruit, their oath said they would certainly cut the garden itself in the morning. «“Sarm” bağ kesmek, üzüm ve meyve devşirmek mânasına geldiği gibi, bir şeyi kökünden kesip tamamen ayırmak mânasına da gelir»
#156 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The owners could not control the garden's fate or even know with certainty that they themselves would live until morning. «gerek o bağın gerek kendilerinin» [filed under 68:17-18]
#157 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · God tested the deniers who trusted in wealth and sons, making those supposed blessings a trial because they used them to block good and show pride rather than give thanks. «Muhakkak ki Biz onlara bir belâ verdik yani mal ve oğullarına güvenip de öyle diyenleri»
#158 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · At the first proclamation, Meccans followed such immoral leaders in denying God's signs and tormenting the Prophet, so he prayed for Mudar to face severe hardship like Joseph's years; God then afflicted them with seven years of famine. «“Allahım! Mudar’a darbeni şiddetlendir ve onlara Yûsuf’un seneleri gibi kıtlık ver”[^32]»
