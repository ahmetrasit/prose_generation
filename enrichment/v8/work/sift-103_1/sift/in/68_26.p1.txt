#42 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · إنا لضالون means they had strayed from gratitude; calling that misguidance metaphorically signals their recognition that the disaster was punishment for ingratitude. «ومعنى { إنا لضالون } أنهم علموا أنهم كانوا في ضلال»
#43 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The emphatic wording conveys grief and regret because they had only just recognized their earlier heedlessness. «وأكدوا الكلام لتنزيل أنفسهم منزلة من يشك في أنهم ضالون طريق الخير»
#44 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Alternatively, they may literally have thought they had lost the road to the garden; بل then corrects them: this is their garden, destroyed and stripped of its produce. «ويحتمل أن يكون الضلال حقيقيا ، أي ضلال طريق الجنة»
#45 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · On seeing the ruined orchard, they first say they have gone astray from right conduct in trying to harvest and exclude the poor; then they correct themselves: they have been deprived of the crop. «عن الصواب في غدونا إليها بقصد الصرم ومنع المساكين» [filed under 68:26, 68:27]
#46 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · reports · Another view takes their first words to mean that they had lost the way to their orchard; the author instead explains them as moral error. «وقيل المراد إنا لضالون طريق جنتنا وما هي بها»
#47 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad places the surah probably third in revelation order and calls it one of the oldest Qur'anic portions. «In the chronological order of revelation, this surah most probably occupies the third place» [filed under 68:1-52]
#48 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Suyuti · reports · Suyuti holds that the surah followed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96; Asad says this conflicts with the best-authenticated reports that most of surah 74 came second. «Some authorities - among them Suyuti - incline to the view that it was revealed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96» [filed under 68:1-52]
#49 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. «أن هذه الجنة كانت لرجل صالح، حدودا قومه وبلده فقيل إنه من أهل اليمن» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:19, 68:20, 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24, 68:25, 68:26, 68:27, 68:28, 68:29]
#50 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · While the owners slept, the garden was made barren; in the morning they found it harvested, recognized their loss, and the middle brother reminded them of their neglect of God and His bounty. «وترك الجنة صريماً جرداء خلاء. فلما أصبحوا، تنادوا ليغدوا على حرثهم» [filed under 68:19-29]
#51 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The early Meccan account omits personal names, dates, and other details except where they serve its lesson; it identifies the people only as from Ethiopia or Yemen. «لا يتعلق فيها بذكر الأشخاص والأزمنه إلا ما كان من جوهر القصة» [filed under 68:17-29]
#52 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The punishment verse points to the lesson of the garden story: tyrants face punishment in this world, while the hereafter's punishment is greater. «يلفت إلى مناط العبرة فيما تلت الآيات من أمر أصحاب الجنة» [filed under 68:17-33]
#53 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The orchard was so devastated that they could not recognize it at first and thought they had lost their way; they soon saw that their prized possession was gone. «All their desires and wishes were dashed to the ground» [filed under 68:26, 68:27]
#54 STUDYQURAN · Most commentators · reports · Most commentators place the story in Madinah and identify its owners as three Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen who inherited their father’s fertile garden; their father left produce for the poor, but they planned to withhold it and harvest secretly. «Most commentators maintain that these verses were revealed in Madinah and that the story refers to three either Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen» [filed under 68:17-34]
#55 STUDYQURAN · IK, JJ · reports · “We are astray” may mean they thought they had arrived at the wrong place or that they recognized their wrongdoing. «Here we are astray indicates that they thought they had arrived at the wrong place (IK, JJ)»
#56 STUDYQURAN · holds · Their initial belief that they were astray alludes to opponents of Muhammad thinking they are guided but eventually realizing their error, as the brothers first mistake their punishment for a wrong location and then recognize it as earned. «It can also be seen as an allusion to the distinction between the believers and the disbelievers mentioned in vv. 5–7»
#57 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Qalam is generally considered an early Makkan sūrah. «generally thought to be from the early Makkan period» [filed under 68:1-52]
#58 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qatādah · reports · These early commentators held that verses 1–16, 34–47, and 51–52 are Makkan, while 17–33 and 48–50 are Madinan. «the early commentators IbnʿAbbās and al-Qatādah (d. 117/726) maintained that vv. 1–16» [filed under 68:1-52]
#59 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 17–34 tell the parable of garden owners who lose their harvest because of miserliness. «the parable of the owners of the garden, who lost their harvest because of their miserliness» [filed under 68:17-34]
