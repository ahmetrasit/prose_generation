#80 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · مجاهد عن ابن عباس · reports · In one account the youths lived under an idol-worshipping king; after learning belief from a grammarian or earlier believers, they rejected idolatry, were summoned and threatened with death, and answered with the words later quoted in 18:14-16. «روى مجاهد عن ابن عباس أن هؤلاء الفتية كانوا في دين ملك يعبد الأصنام ويذبح لها ويكفر بالله» [filed under 18:10-16]
#81 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · In one version the king delayed punishment, then the youths secretly planned to flee to a cave known to one of them. «فاذهبوا إلى منازلكم ودبروا رأيكم» [filed under 18:10-16]
#82 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · One account says they hid their escape by playing with a polo mallet and ball as they moved toward the cave; another says they escaped while being taken to a festival. «فخرجوا فيما روي يلعبون بالصولجان والكرة وهم يدحرجونها إلى نحو طريقهم»
#83 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · People recorded the youths' names, fathers, rank, and disappearance on lead or copper tablets; some say two secretly believing judges hid them, either at their homes or in the king's construction over the cave. «من رصاص أو نحاس، وجعلوه على باب المدينة»
#84 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · The persecuting king is also said to have been a Roman ruler who sought to abolish the religion of Jesus and killed believers publicly. «وكان يريد أن يذهب فيما ذكر، دين عيسى»
#85 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The youths sought God's mercy, interpreted by commentators as provision, and a good way out of their situation; the author says this was a worldly prayer, while they trusted their afterlife outcome. «دعوا الله تعالى بأن يؤتيهم من عنده رحمة»
#86 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The majority read rashadan with both vowels open; Abu Raja reads rushdan with a rounded first vowel and silent second, while the majority reading better matches surrounding verse endings. «وقرأ الجمهور «رشدا» بفتح الراء والشين، وقرأ أبو رجاء»
#87 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The words may also include mercy in the afterlife, and the author recommends this verse as a sufficient prayer for a believer's worldly needs. «وينبغي لكل مؤمن أن يجعل دعاءه في أمر دنياه هذه الآية فقط»
#88 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · A separate account says the youths saw their city's festival idolatry, believed, and left the people to avoid sharing their punishment. «فرأى الفتيان ما يمتثله الناس في ذلك العيد من الكفر وعبادة الأصنام والذبح لها»
#89 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · وهب بن منبه · reports · Wahb's account has a disciple of Jesus work in a bathhouse, convert the city's noble youths, then flee with them after the prince and a prostitute died there and the disciple was blamed. «وروى وهب بن منبه أن أمرهم إنما كان أن حواريا لعيسى ابن مريم،»
#90 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · عبيد بن عمير · reports · Ubayd ibn Umayr says the noble youths became believers one by one, met by chance beneath a tree, learned that their aims matched, and then fled together after confiding in pairs. «وقال عبيد بن عمير: إن أصحاب الكهف كانوا فتية أبناء العظماء»
#91 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Accounts say the dog belonged to a youth who hunted, or that a shepherd and his dog joined the group; its name is given as Himran or Qitmir. «وأما الكلب فروي أنه كان كلب صيد لبعضهم»
#92 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The youths took refuge in the cave to escape their people and protect their religion, asking God for mercy, rescue, and a way to right guidance. «أي اذكر لقومك إذا التجأ أولئك الشبان إلى الكهف وجعلوه مأواهم هربا بدينهم إلى الله»
#93 TABRISI d.548 · ابن عباس · reports · The prayer may ask God to provide a safe way out of the cave. «وقيل هيىء لنا مخرجا من الغار في سلامة عن ابن عباس»
#94 TABRISI d.548 · قيل · reports · Alternatively, they ask to be guided to a course that will save them, or for an arrangement that earns God’s approval. «وقيل معناه دلنا على أمر فيه نجاتنا لأن الرشد والنجاة بمعنى»
#95 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The youths were believers who concealed Islam from a ruler named Daqiyānūs in Ephesus; he worshiped idols and killed opponents, though another account calls him a Magian who promoted that religion. «وكان اسم الملك دقيانوس واسم مدينتهم أفسوس وكان ملكهم يعبد الأصنام ويدعو إليها»
#96 TABRISI d.548 · عبيد بن عمير · reports · ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr says the youths were close associates of the king who hid their faith from one another until they met, disclosed it, and fled to the cave. «عن عبيد بن عمير وقيل إنهم كانوا قبل بعث عيسى ع»
#97 TABRISI d.548 · قيل · reports · One account says they followed the religion of Jesus after the people of the Gospel had passed away; another places them before Jesus’s mission. «على دين المسيح لما برح أهل الإنجيل»
#98 TABRISI d.548 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Isḥāq transmits from Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and ʿIkrima, from Ibn ʿAbbās, that Quraysh sent al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith and ʿUqba ibn Abī Muʿayṭ to Jewish scholars in Medina to ask about three signs: ancient youths, a world traveler, and the spirit. «عن سعيد بن جبير وعكرمة عن ابن عباس أن النضر بن الحرث بن كلدة وعقبة بن أبي معيط» [filed under 18:9-10]
#99 TABRISI d.548 · أحبار اليهود · reports · The Jewish scholars told the envoys to ask about the youths, the traveler who reached the East and West, and the spirit; if Muḥammad answered the first two but not the last, he was still a prophet. «فقال لهما أحبار اليهود اسألوه عن ثلاث فإن أخبركم بهن فهو نبي مرسل» [filed under 18:9-10]
#100 TABRISI d.548 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · When asked, the Prophet said he would answer the next day but did not say ‘if God wills’; revelation then ceased for fifteen nights, and Meccans spread rumors while the delay distressed him. «فجاؤوا إلى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فسألوه فقال:» [filed under 18:9-10]
#101 TABRISI d.548 · ابن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Isḥāq says the cave chapter then came through Gabriel with answers about the youths and traveler, along with the revelation about the spirit. «ثم جاءه جبرائيل ع عن الله سبحانه بسورة الكهف وفيها ما سألوه عنه عن أمر الفتية والرجل الطواف» [filed under 18:9-10]
#102 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The cave story also follows the Prophet’s grief over unbelievers: he should not despair, since God will support and protect him as He protected the youths. «أنه اتصل بقوله: { فلعلك باخع نفسك على آثارهم } أي فلا تأسف عليهم» [filed under 18:6, 18:9-12]
#103 TABRISI d.548 · holds · A third link is the promise to give believers good news: God will support them as He supported the people of the cave. «أنه اتصل بقوله: { ويبشر المؤمنين } أي وينصرهم كما نصر أصحاب الكهف» [filed under 18:2, 18:9-12]
#104 TABRISI d.548 · holds · Awā means to return or take refuge; futiya is a collective plural for young men, like ṣabī/ṣibya and ghulām/ghilma, though such formations are not fully productive. «والأوي الرجوع والفتية جمع فتى وفعلة من أسماء الجمع وليس بناء يقاس عليه»
#105 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjaj says āwaw ilayhi means they went to the cave and made it their refuge. «قال الزجاج معنى أووا إليه صاروا إليه وجعلوه مأواهم»
#106 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Fitya is a plural of fatā, like ghulām/ghilma and ṣabī/ṣabiya; it is a collective form that does not generate parallel forms such as “ravens” or “wealthy men.” «والفتية جمع فتى مثل غلام وغلمة وصبي وصبية وفعلة من أسماء الجمع»
#107 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · reports · Some commentators understand fitya to mean young men; Ibn Qutayba says fatā can mean a fully grown man, as in the verse about believing women. «وقال بعض المفسرين الفتية بمعنى الشبان»
#108 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · “From Your presence” means from You; raḥma means provision, and prepare our affair means set it right. «من لدنك أي من عندك رحمة أي رزقا وهيئ لنا أي أصلح لنا من أمرنا»
#109 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Rushd means guidance toward what brings one near to God and is the opposite of error. «هيئ لنا أي أصلح لنا من أمرنا رشدا أي ارشدنا إلى ما يقربنا منك»
#110 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says the youths fled a king who summoned them to idol worship, met a shepherd who followed their faith, and hid in the cave; two secret believers recorded their names and story on lead in a copper case. «ملكهم حين دعاهم إلى عبادة الأصنام الأصنام فمروا براع له فتبعهم على دينهم» [filed under 18:10-11]
#111 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عبيد بن عمير · reports · Ubayd ibn Umayr says the people searched for the missing youths but God concealed them; their names, lineage, and date were recorded on a tablet and kept in the king's treasury. «فقدهم قومهم فطلبوهم فعمى الله عليهم أمرهم فكتبوا أسماءهم وأنسابهم» [filed under 18:10-11]
#112 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · Wahb says a disciple barred from the city for refusing its idol entered a bathhouse, taught the youths, then fled with them and a shepherd's dog; the king's men sealed the cave after being frightened away. «أن أحد الحواريين جاء إلى مدينة أصحاب الكهف فأراد أن يدخلها فقيل له إن على بابها صنما» [filed under 18:10-11]
#113 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · وهب بن منبه · reports · In Wahb's account, the youths fled with the disciple, met a companion tending a field, and entered the cave at night intending to decide their next move in the morning. «فمروا على صاحب لهم في زرع» [filed under 18:10-11]
#114 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says noble youths independently gathered outside their city, one spoke of recognizing the Lord of heaven and earth, and all agreed before entering the cave. «كانوا أبناء عظماء المدينة وأشرافهم خرجوا فاجتمعوا وراء المدينة على غير ميعاد» [filed under 18:10-11]
#115 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says they were sons of Roman kings who separated themselves from their religion in the cave, where God caused them to sleep. «وقال قتادة كانوا أبناء ملوك الروم فتفردوا بدينهم في الكهف» [filed under 18:10-11]
#116 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · The third account says they affirmed that the Lord of the heavens and earth was their Lord and then resolved to enter the cave. «فقالوا ربنا رب السموات والأرض فأجمعوا أن يدخلوا الكهف» [filed under 18:10-11]
