#338 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi regards time as nobler than space and says the oath answers people who blame the age for calamities: the fault lies in the human being, not time. «إن الزمان أعلى وأشرف من المكان، فكان القسم بالعصر قسماً بأشرف النصفين»
#339 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi says passing time subtracts from one's lifespan; without a corresponding gain, that decrease is itself loss, even when people welcome time passing. «ذكر العصر الذي بمضيه ينتقص عمرك، فإذا لم يكن في مقابلته كسب» [filed under 103:1-2]
#340 BINTSHATI d.1419 · ابن القيم · reports · Ibn Qayyim sees the ordered alternation of night and day, their changing light and temperature, and time divisions as signs of God's power and wisdom; the oath links human actions in time to their judgment. «فأقسم بالعصر الذي هو زمان أفعال الإنسان ومحلها، على عاقبة تلك الأفعال وجزائها»
#341 BINTSHATI d.1419 · النيسابوري · reports · Al-Nisaburi compares evening to the world's destruction and morning to resurrection, urging people to use their remaining lifespan for a trade without loss. «أن آخر النهار يشبه تخريب العالم وإماتة الأحياء كما أن أول النهار يشبه بعث الأموات»
#342 BINTSHATI d.1419 · النيسابوري · reports · Al-Nisaburi says the world's remaining life is like the time between afternoon and sunset, so people should hasten a trade without loss. «الدنيا ما بقى إلا بقدر ما بين العصر إلى المغرب، فعلى الإنسان أن يشتغل بتجارة لا خسران فيها»
#343 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · The author cites the prophetic report against blaming time, since God is al-dahr. «ولذلك قال - صلى الله عليه وسلم -: لا تسبوا الدهر فإن الله هو الدهر»
#344 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · A report describes time as strangely both existent and like nothing, and compares a person's lifetime to a small part of it. «من أعجايب الدهر الدالة على كمال قدره خالقها: أن الدهر موجود يشبه المعدوم»
#345 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author rejects loading asr with philosophical and mystical interpretations; the Quran explains signs of night and day in accessible terms, as in al-Qasas 28:71-72. «وترى أنهم حملوا لفظ العصر كل هذه التأويلات الفلسفية والإشارية مما»
#346 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الرازي · reports · Al-Razi suggests the Quran chose asr instead of dahr because unbelievers were fond of invoking and exalting the latter word. «ولعله تعالى لم يذكر الدهر لعلمه أن الملحد مولع بذكره وتعظيمه»
#347 BINTSHATI d.1419 · النيسابوري · reports · Al-Nisaburi explains the oath as honoring the age and cites the report that people should not curse time because God is al-dahr. «على حين يذهب النيسابورى إلى أن وجه الإقسام بالعصر هنا»
#348 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The author reads asr as the age that presses and tests humanity, extracting its essence; this familiar Arabic sense leads into the declaration that humanity is in loss. «تأتي كلمة العصر في سياقها من السورة، لافتة إلى ابتلاء الإنسان بالعصر» [filed under 103:1-2]
#349 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The oath's waw moves from a perceptible reality to a moral truth, a pattern the author also discusses in other surahs. «الواو هنا في موضعها الذي تطرد به الظاهرة الأسلوبية في اللفت إلى حسى مدرك»
#350 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · Author says the waw of oath, in its place, marks the surah's opening as an assertion or testimony to what was denied. «فإذا جاءت للقسم فإن لها الصدارة، في مقام التوثيق لما يسبق إنكاره، أو الإقرار والشهادة»
#351 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · المفسرون · reports · Exegetes, mostly, read the oath-waw as glorifying the sworn-by object. «وقد اتجه بها المفسرون، أو جمهرتهم فيما أعلم، إلى تعظيم المقسَم به»
#352 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · rejects · Author rejects the exegetes' glory-by-wisdom reasoning and their neglect of the qualifiers attached to the sworn-by object. «وأما العظمة فلا يهون القول بها لمجرد لمح وجهٍ لظاهر الحكمة»
#353 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · rejects · Exegetes often ignore the qualifier of the sworn-by object (e.g. the dawn-times of al-Duha). «ثم إنهم غالباً، لم يراعوا القيد في المقسم به»
#354 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · Author asks whether the oath-waw form, versus explicit aqsama, adds any rhetorical meaning. «فهل العدول عن: أقسم بالنجم، إلى "والنجم" لا يعطى أي ملحظ بياني؟»
#355 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · All surahs opening with the oath-waw, including al-ʿAsr, are Meccan; no Medinan surah begins this way. «وكلها سور مكية، ولم تأت سورة مدنية مبدوءة بهذه الواو.»
#356 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · In al-ʿAsr the waw places the human under the pressure of time, which squeezes and tests him. «الواو في موضعها الذي تطرد به الظاهرة الأسلوبية في اللفت إلى ابتلاء الإنسان بالزمن يعصره ويصهره»
#357 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · Time's pressing extracts the human's essence, revealing whether his outcome is loss or salvation (exempting those who believe and do good and counsel truth and patience). «توطئة إيضاحية لبيان ما يستخلص العصرُ من عصارة هذا الإنسان» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#358 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · In the verse al-ʿaṣr means time (zaman, dahr); the word is a single-form Qurʾānic usage here. «العَصْر، بمعنى الزمن، في آية العصر»
#359 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · الراغب · rejects · Author rejects Raghib's derivation of time from the residue of what is pressed. «وأخذه "الراغب" من نفاية ما يُعصَر. وليس الوجه.»
#360 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · The root ʿ-ṣ-r points to pressing to extract juice; the sense of time (dahr) is named for extracting man's essence through trial. «وتسمية الدهر عصراً، ملحوظ فيه أنه يستخلص عصارة الإنسان بالضغط والابتلاء والمعاناة»
#361 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · General root meaning of ʿ-ṣ-r is pressing for juice, used literally of grapes and metaphorically of wine. «والعصر في كل صيغة واستعماله، يرجع إلى أصل دلالته على الضغط لاستخلاص العصارة»
#362 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · The word ʿaṣr meaning time occurs in 103:1, alongside al-muʿṣirāt (78:14) and the verbs in Yusuf. «والمعصرات فى آية النبأ 14، والعصر بمعنى الدهر والزمن فى آية العصر» [filed under 103:1, 78:14]
#363 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The three-rhyme sura is an opening single-member oath by late afternoon, followed by a rebuke; verse 3 exempts believers and differentiates good from bad people. «Die auf 3Kr reimende Sure besteht aus einem eingliedrigen Schwur „beim späten Nachmittag“ mit rügendem ʾinsān-Spruch als Schwuraussage» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#364 CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · Neuwirth argues that oaths by prayer times preserve the recipient's liturgical experience and give the sura a hymn-like tone. «Neuwirth schreibt Schwüren bei Tages- und Nachtzeiten allgemein die Funktion zu»
#365 CORPUSCORANICUM · rejects · The commentator finds no hymn-like resonance in this sura and points to a critical discussion of Neuwirth's hypothesis elsewhere. «Eine solche hymnische Konnotation klingt jedoch im vorliegenden Text nirgends nach» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#366 CORPUSCORANICUM · Robinson · prefers · Robinson's secular reading makes ʿaṣr the time when merchants count a day's earnings, linking the oath's image of profit to the eschatological loss in verse 2. «Robinsons profane Deutung des ʿaṣr als des Zeitpunkts, zu dem Händler am Ende eines Markttags ihre Einnahmen berechnen» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#367 CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · A prayer-time meaning for afternoon nevertheless remains possible. «dass mit dem Nachmittag doch eine Gebetszeit gemeint sein könnte (so Neuwirth, Frühmekkanische Suren, 157)»
#368 CORPUSCORANICUM · reports · Arabic lexica offer competing senses for ʿaṣr: time or period, a time of day, morning, late afternoon or evening; the dual can mean day and night. «Die arabischen Lexika verzeichnen eine Reihe von z. T. gegensätzlichen Bedeutungen»
#369 CORPUSCORANICUM · prefers · Since other opening oaths name specific daytimes or night phases, ʿaṣr probably denotes a particular time rather than time in general. «stellt wohl auch ʿaṣr eine spezifische Zeitangabe dar und bezeichnet nicht allgemein „Zeitspanne“»
#370 CORPUSCORANICUM · Cuypers · reports · Cuypers reads ʿaṣr as a general span of time, though the commentator notes that he gives no further justification. «„Zeitspanne“ o. Ä. (so ohne weitere Begründung Cuypers 1999, 47)»
#371 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · If ʿaṣr means late afternoon, this oath differs from all other early Meccan oaths naming night or stages of morning and forenoon. «Falls wirklich der Spätnachmittag intendiert ist, so hebt sich Q 103 von allen anderen frühmekkanischen Schwüren»
#372 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Qur'anic oaths likely draw on a characteristic pre-Islamic seer expression, a relationship that has not yet been adequately studied. «wahrscheinlichen, aber bis dato noch nicht hinreichend untersuchten Anlehnung an ein charakteristisches Ausdrucksmittel altarabischer Seher»
#373 CORPUSCORANICUM · Spitaler · reports · Medina II joins the oath verse to verse 2 in its verse division. «Medina II zieht V. 1 zu V. 2 (Spitaler, Verszählung, 72)» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#374 CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · prefers · Neuwirth treats the oath as an independent verse, in keeping with other sura-opening oaths. «Wie die anderen Schwüre am Surenanfang ist aber auch wa-l-ʿaṣr als eigenständiger Vers zu sehen (Neuwirth, Studien, 35)»
#375 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-ʿAṣr follows al-Takāthur’s warning against wasting life amassing wealth; people would have used life wisely if they knew they must answer for this great blessing. «waste all their lives in planning» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#376 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This sūrah explains life’s real value and the factors that make it a path to salvation or a cause of eternal punishment. «the real value and importance of life is divulged» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#377 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The oath points to a lifespan as a person’s real investment: using it well earns God’s favor, while wasting it brings Hell; time can serve either the person or Satan. «this time period is like a sword which can be used in both ways»
