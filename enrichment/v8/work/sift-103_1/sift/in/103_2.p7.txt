#277 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Righteous deeds accord with God’s wise design for the universe’s collective order, since the world is governed by purposeful prudence rather than play. «deeds will be regarded as righteous»
#278 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Faith is presented as the foundation and nucleus, with righteous deeds following as its explanation. «many branches sprout from a root» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
#279 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Despite its brevity, Surah al-Asr contains a rich summary of the Qur'an's religious and moral duties and counsels. «Kur’an-ı Kerîm’deki bütün dinî ve ahlâkî yükümlülüklerin, öğütlerin özü sayılmaya değer» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#280 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İmam Şâfiî · reports · Al-Shafi'i is quoted as saying that this short surah alone would suffice humanity if nothing else had been revealed, since it embraces all Qur'anic sciences. «Şayet Kur’an’da başka bir şey nâzil olmasaydı, şu pek kısa sûre bile insanlara yeterdi. Bu sûre Kur’an’ın bütün ilimlerini kucaklıyor» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#281 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Mehmet Âkif Ersoy · reports · Akif's poem presents true faith, righteous conduct, truth, and steadfastness as the sequence that saves humanity from loss. «Başta îmân-ı hakîkî geliyor sonra salâh Sonra hak sonra sebât: İşte kuzum insanlık Dördü birleşti mi yoktur sana hüsrân artık» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#282 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · God swears by this precious reality to stress time's value and warn that a person who fails to use it well ends in loss. «zamanın önemine dikkat çekmiş; onu iyi değerlendirmeyen insanın sonunun» [filed under 103:1, 103:2]
#283 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Here, loss means the punishment of the hereafter, the greatest loss for someone who has wasted life and time, as Ibn Ashur is cited. «Burada “ziyan”la âhiret azabı kastedilmiştir. Çünkü zamanı ve ömrü boşa geçirmiş insan için en büyük ziyan odur»
#284 SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The exception here requires faith and good works, whereas al-ʿAṣr adds mutual exhortation to truth and patience; that added practice prevents further loss of reward. «فلم يقل مثل ما قال في سورة العصر: {والعصر * إِنَّ الإنسان لَفِى خُسْرٍ * إِلاَّ الذين آمَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ الصالحات» [filed under 95:6, 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#285 STUDYQURAN · holds · This reading applies to individuals and humanity's history: each generation falls a degree below the previous one. «the fall of humanity continues as the historical cycle unfolds, and each generation is a degree below the generation before it.» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#286 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 2–3 answer the oath and parallel 95:5–6. «Vv. 2–3 are the response to the oath in v. 1 and are similar in meaning to 95:5–6» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]
#287 STUDYQURAN · Bg and Q · reports · Mankind can mean disbelievers; another reading takes it as humanity generally. «The term mankind can be seen as a reference to disbelievers (Bg, Q) or to humanity in general (Q).»
#288 STUDYQURAN · holds · In light of the declining day, mankind can refer to humanity in the final phase of the historical cycle. «Seen in light of the reference to the declining day, it can also be taken as a reference to humanity during the latter days of the historical cycle.»
#289 STUDYQURAN · holds · Disbelief increases disbelievers only in loss; Qur'anic loss encompasses both this world and the Hereafter. «the disbelief of the disbelievers increases them in naught but loss»
#290 STUDYQURAN · holds · Losers lose what they value most in this life, not necessarily material goods, and later lose their souls and families at Resurrection. «The losers are thus those who lose what they value most in this life, although not necessarily purely material things, and in the next life»
#291 STUDYQURAN · holds · Spiritually, loss can mean losing faith and attachment to God; if ʿaṣr means time, misuse of time leaves people constantly losing. «From a spiritual perspective, loss can also be seen as a reference to losing faith and attachment to God.»
#292 STUDYQURAN · reports · Al-ʿAṣr is Makkan and likely follows Sūrah 94; ʿaṣr can mean late afternoon, its last hour, afternoon, the afternoon prayer, or an epoch. «most likely follows Sūrah 94» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#293 STUDYQURAN · reports · Companions reportedly did not part after meeting until one recited the whole sūrah and greeted the other with peace. «whenever two Companions of the Prophet would meet they would not part company until one of them had recited al-ʿAṣr in its entirety» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#294 STUDYQURAN · holds · Read after Sūrah 102, ʿaṣr can mean time generally: vying for increase wastes it, and time's blessing is what people will be questioned about. «When viewed in relation to the previous sūrah, ʿaṣr can be understood as a reference to time in general, meaning that vying for increase (102:1) prevents people from spending their time» [filed under 102:1, 102:8, 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#295 STUDYQURAN · holds · These three short verses can be seen as the whole Qur'an's message: humanity loses unless it turns to God. «From one perspective, these three short verses contain the entire message of the Quran, namely, that human beings are in loss unless they turn to God.» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
#296 STUDYQURAN · al-Shāfiʿī · reports · Al-Shāfiʿī is reported to have said that if people pondered this sūrah, it would suffice them. «the famous legal scholar al-Shāfiʿī is reported to have said,“Were the people to ponder this sūrah, it would suffice them”» [filed under 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]
