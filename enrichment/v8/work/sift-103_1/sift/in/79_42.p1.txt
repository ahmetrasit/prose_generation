#45 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · A weakly transmitted report says Meccan polytheists mockingly asked when the Hour would come; its timing belongs to God, Muhammad warns those who fear it, and the world will seem only an evening or morning. «بسند ضعيف عن ابن عباس» [filed under 79:42-46]
#46 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The mockers ask when God will establish the Hour; ayyan means “when,” and mursa is the fixing or establishing of its time. «إرساؤها أي إقامتها يريدون متى يقيمها الله ويثبتها»
#47 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Because a time cannot directly predicate an event, the grammatical estimate is “at what time is its appointed establishment”; the questioners mockingly invoke the Hour’s terrifying descriptions. «فأيان ظرف بمعنى متى وأصله أي آن ووقت والمرسي مصدر بمعنى الإرساء»
#48 BURSEVI d.1127 · عائشة · reports · A report from Aisha says the Prophet wanted to ask God about the Hour’s time, but was told its knowledge was not his and belonged to the Lord alone. «عائشة رضي الله عنها فرموده كه حضرت رسول عليه السلام ميخواست كه وقت آن ازخدا بسد حق تعالى فرمود توازد انستن قيامت» [filed under 79:42-43]
#49 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · They ask when the Hour will be established; مرساها is a time noun from rasa, meaning becoming fixed or firm, as with firmly rooted mountains. «أي متى إرساؤها أي إقامتها يريدون متى يقيمها الله تعالى ويكونها ويثبتها»
#50 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Another gloss makes مرساها its endpoint or place of settlement, as for a ship; the author notes the mismatch between a question about when and a place comparison, then proposes a metaphor of the distant day as a traveler. «وجوز أن يكون المرسى بمعنى المنتهى أي متى منتهاها ومستقرها»
#51 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Mursa is a verbal noun of bringing something to rest, or a place or time meaning endpoint; like a ship anchored, the question asks when God will bring the Hour to pass. «Burada bir istiare var demek olur. Yani Allah o Sâ‘at’i ne vakit getirip dikecek»
#52 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The question about the Hour begins an explanatory resumption prompted by the disbelievers' repeated, mocking questions about its timing and their demand that it be hastened. «استئناف بياني منشؤه أن المشركين كانوا يسألون عن وقت حلول الساعة»
#53 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · They imagine the delay disproves the Hour, reasoning that an angry God would immediately retaliate like an impetuous human; the Quran rejects this human analogy. «وكان سؤالهم استهزاء واستخفافا لأنهم عقدوا قلوبهم على استحالة وقوع الساعة»
#54 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · After proving resurrection and describing its punishment, the passage answers the question about when the Great Calamity will arrive. «لما قضي حق الاستدلال على إمكان البعث بإقامة الدليل وضرب الأمثال»
#55 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The questioners are the disbelievers who earlier asked whether they would be restored to their former state. «فضمير ( يسألون ) عائد إلى المشركين أصحاب القلوب الواجفة»
#56 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The imperfect verb presents their question as recurring and repeated. «وحكي فعل السؤال بصيغة المضارع للدلالة على تجدد هذا السؤال وتكرره»
#57 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Calling the Hour ‘the Hour’ rather than repeating ‘the Great Calamity’ gives the sentence independent force and varies the naming. «والساعة : هي الطامة فذكر الساعة إظهار في مقام الإضمار لقصد استقلال الجملة»
#58 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Ayyan asks for a specific time; the question implies that the event is remote or even impossible, while marsa is a ship's mooring metaphor for the Hour's arrival. «والاستفهام مستعمل في الاستبعاد كناية وهو أيضا كناية عن الاستحالة»
#59 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The reply addresses the apparent request for timing and their use of delay to deny the Hour: it will occur, and the waiting will seem no longer than part of a day. «فأجيبوا على طريقة الأسلوب الحكيم ، أي إن طال تأخر حصولها فإنها واقعة» [filed under 79:42, 79:45]
#60 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The passage answers repeated questions about the Hour by saying only God knows when it will come. «تعرض لسؤالهم عن وقت قيام الساعة ورد له بأن علمه ليس لأحد إلا الله فقد خصه بنفسه»
#61 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The imperfect “they ask” suggests ongoing questioning: after hearing about resurrection, the mockers repeatedly demanded a date; marsā is a verbal noun meaning establishment or settling. «الظاهر أن التعبير بيسألونك لإفادة الاستمرار فقد كان المشركون بعد ما سمعوا حديث القيامة»
#62 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · The Qummi commentary glosses the question as “when does it come?” and says its knowledge belongs to God. «قال متى تقوم؟ فقال الله { إلى ربك منتهاها } أي علمها عند الله» [filed under 79:42, 79:44]
#63 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · A weak-chain report says Meccan polytheists mockingly asked the Prophet when the Hour would come, and these verses were revealed. «بسند ضعيف عن ابن عباس قال إن مشركي مكة سألوا النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم فقالوا متى تقوم الساعة استهزاء منهم»
#64 TABATABAI d.1402 · عائشة · rejects · A report says the Prophet answered Bedouins by pointing to the youngest person and saying their Hour would come if he lived a century; the author rejects this as an unsuitable date-setting, since revelation repeatedly says only God knows the Hour. «كانت الأعراب إذا قدموا على النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم سألوه عن الساعة فينظر إلى أحدث إنسان فيهم»
#65 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · This late Meccan surah was revealed shortly after surah 78 and is named for an-nazi'at in its first verse. «THIS late Meccan surah, revealed shortly» [filed under 79:1-46]
#66 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The Qur’an’s warning has reached its culmination, then it settles the question of the Hour by saying its final knowledge belongs to God. «لا يدع الموقف دون أن يعقب عليه بحسم عقدته، والرد على سؤالهم عن الساعة»
#67 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · In Arabic, sāʿah first denotes a portion of time, later conventionally specified as sixty minutes; the Qur’anic eschatological usage is distinctive. «ولفظ الساعة في العربية، يعني الجزء من الوقت، ثم تحدد. بستين دقيقة»
#68 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · When indefinite, sāʿah in the Qur’an denotes a short, unspecified span, while the definite Hour consistently means the Afterlife’s decisive event. «أما حين يستعمل القرآن "الساعة" معرفة بـ: ال، فتلك - دائماً - هي ساعة الآخرة»
#69 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The Hour is presented as active and momentous through verbs of coming and rising, marking the change in cosmic order. «أن هذه "الساعة" تنفرد دون ساعات الزمان كله، بأنها الحاسمة الفاصلة التي يتغير فيها نظام الزمن»
#70 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · In ordinary Arabic, the definite sāʿah can mean the present moment; later the word also named the timekeeping instrument. «ويستعمل معرفا بـ (ال) للعهد، ظرف زمان للوقت الحاضر، فيقال: أزورك الساعة»
#71 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Prophet should not be troubled by demands for the Hour’s timing: only God knows it, the Prophet’s duty is warning, and at its sight people will feel they stayed only a short while. «The time of arrival of the Day of Judgement is something which only the Almighty knows» [filed under 79:42, 79:46]
#72 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Prophet is assured that the questioners tease him about when Judgment will arrive, while his responsibility is to warn and give the heedful a chance to prepare. «the only responsibility of the Prophet (sws) was to inform people»
#73 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The word ayān asks when a future event occurs, not where; marṣāhā's port and anchoring image makes the question sarcastic. «it would be erroneous to translate it as “where;”»
#74 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Disbelieving polytheists repeatedly asked the Prophet when Resurrection would occur to put him in difficulty, sometimes mockingly wishing it would come soon because they deemed it impossible. «Müşrikler kıyamet ve âhirete inanmadıkları için her fırsatta Hz. Peygamber’e kıyametin ne zaman kopacağını sorarak onu zor duruma düşürmeye çalışıyor»
#75 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The Prophet wished he could answer, hoping a response might help them believe. «Hz. Peygamber ise onların iman etmelerine vesile olur ümidiyle, “Keşke sorularına cevap vermek mümkün olsaydı!” diye temennide bulunuyordu»
#76 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · The question is attributed to Makkan idolaters who asked to mock, not to gain information. «the idolaters of Makkah, who did not ask in order to be informed, but only to mock»
#77 STUDYQURAN · holds · The wording derives from a nautical metaphor, asking when the Hour will berth. «derives from a nautical metaphor meaning, “When shall it berth?”»
#78 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · Some take the nautical image to imply that the Hour is near. «Some take the use of this metaphor to imply that it is near at hand»
#79 STUDYQURAN · holds · The final verses answer disbelievers who taunted the Prophet for not knowing when the Hour would arrive. «respond to the disbelievers who chided the Prophet for not knowing when the Hour would come» [filed under 79:42, 79:46]
