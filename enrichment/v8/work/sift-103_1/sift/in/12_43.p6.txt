#232 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Two inmates’ dreams and Joseph’s correct interpretations ultimately create the opportunity for him to interpret the reigning king’s dream, after which the king entrusts him with control of the kingdom. «He does so and the interpretation proves to be absolutely true. Ultimately, this paves the way of he interpreting the dream seen by the reigning king.» [filed under 12:36, 12:43, 12:54]
#233 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · When God wills a plan, its means begin to arise on their own, however unlikely it may seem. «its means and resources start coming into being out of their own accord.»
#234 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The king considered his dream significant and suspected his naive courtiers could not interpret it. «The king regarded this dream to be very significant.»
#235 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The dream suppresses the second half of its image: the dry ears consume the green ears, as verse 48 clarifies; the translation supplies the omission. «seven dry ears were eating away the seven green ones.»
#236 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · reports · The king is identified as one of six Hyksos rulers who ruled Egypt from 1700 to 1580 BCE after entering through Sinai; historians’ ‘shepherd kings’ label suggests they were Syrian Arabs not yet settled before conquering Egypt. «ülkede milâttan önce 1700’den 1580’e kadar hüküm süren altı Hiksos kralından biri olduğu bildirilmektedir»
#237 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · reports · The Hebrews may share origins with the Hyksos: Hebrews descended from nomadic tribes that migrated from Arabia through Mesopotamia to Syria; Joseph’s favor later enabled Israelites to become an ethnic and cultural community in Egypt. «İbrânîler de daha önce Arabistan yarımadasından Mezopotamya’ya, sonra Suriye’ye göç eden bedevî kabilelerden birinin soyundan gelmektedir»
#238 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · By Moses’ time the Hyksos era had ended and a Coptic Pharaoh ruled Egypt, killing Israelite boys and sparing girls out of fear of their threat (2:49). «artık Mısır’ı Kıptî soyundan gelen Firavun yönetiyordu. Ülkesi için bir tehlike oluşturacağı endişesiyle İsrâiloğulları’nın erkek çocuklarını öldürüyor, kız çocuklarını hayatta bırakıyordu»
#239 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Eliding hamza from ruʾyā yields rūyāya, a canonical phonological simplification without semantic change. «Hamzless form: the hamza in رُؤْيَا is elided, yielding rūyāya»
#240 QIRAAT-ER · holds · At pause, final yāʾ of the hamzless reading becomes sukūn. «Pause form (waqf) of the hamzless reading — final yāʾ becomes sukūn»
#241 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Assimilating the wāw of rū- to the following yāʾ yields ruyyāya without semantic change. «the wāw of rū- assimilates to the following yāʾ producing ruyyāya — phonological but not semantic change»
#242 QIRAAT-ER · holds · The hamzless form of al-ruʾyā elides hamza without changing meaning. «Hamzless form of الرؤيا — elision of hamza; canonical in multiple readings; no semantic change»
#243 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Assimilating wāw to yāʾ produces ruyyā, a phonological variant only. «the wāw assimilates to yāʾ producing ruyyā — phonological variant only»
#244 QIRAAT-ER · holds · The first-person yāʾ is vocalized in this canonical form but silent in the standard reading. «Vocalized yāʾ al-mutakallim (ya al-idafa) vs. silent in standard reading; both canonical»
#245 STUDYQURAN · Kl and JJ · reports · The notables were high-ranking members of the king’s entourage, and he asked them to interpret his dream. «high offices in his kingdom (Kl), to give him their opinion means that he wanted them to interpret the dream for him (JJ)»
#246 STUDYQURAN · holds · Visions here means true or veridical dreams, as discussed at 12:4. «By visions here are meant true or veridical dreams (see 12:4c).»
#247 STUDYQURAN · holds · The verb ʿabara comes from ʿ-b-r, meaning to cross from one side to another; in dream interpretation it describes moving from symbolic dream forms to their actual meanings. «moving from the imaginal and symbolic forms in dreams to the actual meanings they symbolize (Bḍ)»
