#299 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The parable depicts Judgment Day grief when good deeds are cancelled by ostentation or injury just when their doer needs them. «يفعل الأفعال الحسنة ويضم إليها ما يحبطها كرياء وإيذاء»
#300 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Its spiritual parallel is the seeker who reaches the unseen realm, then turns back to illusion and makes his effort vain. «في عالم الملكوت وترقى بفكره إلى جنات الجبروت ثم نكص على عقبيه»
#301 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · ‘Thus’ points back to clear explanations of striving and spending for God and the stories of Abraham and Ezra; the signs establish monotheism and the truth of religion. «البيان الواضح الذي بين فيما مر من الجهاد والإنفاق في سبيل الله»
#302 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The signs call readers to reflect, learn from their lessons, and act accordingly. «تتفكروا فيها وتعتبروا بما فيها من العبر»
#303 BURSEVI d.1127 · القشيري · reports · Qushayrī contrasts sincere and hypocritical spending: some receive replacement and honor, while others waste, lose their wealth, and have their deeds nullified. «ذكرها الله على جهة ضرب المثل للمخلص والمنافق والمنفق في سبيل الله والمنفق في الباطل»
#304 BURSEVI d.1127 · القشيري · reports · Qushayrī likens faithful deeds to a flourishing crop and failed deeds to a ruined bargain that leaves an old person without income. «أنبت زرعا زكا أصله ونما فضله وعلا فرعه وكثر نفعه»
#305 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Works need sincerity because their fruits depend on their root. «فلا بد من إخلاص الأعمال فإن الثمرات تبتنى على الأصل»
#306 BURSEVI d.1127 · معاذ بن جبل · reports · Muʿādh reports that, when he was sent to Yemen, the Prophet advised him to make his religion sincere, making a little work sufficient. «حين بعث إلى اليمن يا رسول الله أوصني»
#307 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Treat ostentation by removing its roots and establishing its opposite; its main causes are love of this world and preferring immediate pleasure to the afterlife. «قطع عروقه واستئصال أصوله وذلك بإزالة أسبابه وتحصيل ضده»
#308 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Before each worship act, inspect the heart, remove thoughts of ostentation, resolve on sincerity, and keep resisting them. «فعليك في أول كل عبادة أن تفتش قلبك وتخرج منه خواطر الرياء»
#309 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Ostentation enters in three stages: expecting people to notice, wanting their praise and status, then accepting and settling on that desire; reject each. «العلم باطلاع الخلق أو رجاؤه ثم الرغبة في حمدهم وحصول المنزلة»
#310 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The opening asks whether anyone would love this outcome; the hamza is reproachful, and a report says ʿUmar read yuḥibbu, ‘would love.’ «أي أيحب أحدكم وكذلك قرأ عمر»
#311 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · An iʿṣār is a rotating whirlwind, descending or rising, generated when competing air currents collide; it can grow strong enough to uproot trees or snatch boats. «أي ريح تستدير على نفسها وتكون مثل المنارة وتسمى الزوبعة وهي قد تكون هابطة وقد تكون صاعدة»
#312 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The garden is centered on palms and grapes, the most valuable core species, without excluding others; the Qur’an names the palm tree itself but grape fruit, perhaps because the palm is wholly useful. «على معنى أنهما الركن والأصل فيها»
#313 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Palm trees are analyzed either as a collective plural or a collective noun; grapes is the plural of ʿinaba, while ʿinbāʾ is a feminine-marked form that does not decline. «وقيل : جمع نخل وهو إسم جنس جمعي»
#314 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The expression janna may mean the dense trees themselves or land containing them; the first is more fitting because rivers run beneath it without supplying an omitted ‘trees.’ «والأول أنسب بقوله تعالى»
#315 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · In ‘he has therein of every fruit,’ the prepositional phrase can be a fronted predicate, while ‘of every fruit’ describes an omitted provision or fruit; min may be extra under al-Akhfash. «الظرف الأول في محل رفع جبر مقدم والثاني حال من الضمير المستتر في الخبر والثالث نعت لمبتدأ محذوف»
#316 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · ‘Every fruit’ means many kinds rather than literally all; reading fruit as benefits or as general after the two named kinds is also discussed, with the latter rejected. «ليس المراد بالثمرات العموم بل إنما هو الكثير»
#317 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The garden owner is struck by old age, a more forceful expression than simply being old; the circumstantial clause highlights his urgent need and inability to restore his livelihood. «أي أثر فيه علو السن والشيخوخة وهو أبلغ من كبر»
#318 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الفراء، أبو حيان · reports · Al-Farrāʾ allows the coordinated past tense to represent a future result; Abū Ḥayyān objects that this makes the old age part of the wish, which the author finds unconvincing to dismiss. «وقيل : الواو للعطف ووضع الماضي موضع المضارع»
#319 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The weak offspring are dependents unable to earn a living; ḍuʿafāʾ is a plural of ḍaʿīf, and a variant reads ḍiʿāf. «في موضع الحال من الضمير في أصابه»
#320 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The signs are explained clearly so people may reflect on their lessons, take heed, and act accordingly. «أي كي تتفكروا فيها وتعتبروا بما تضمنته من العبر وتعملوا بموجبها»
#321 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Alternatively, people should direct thought to what perishes in this world and what endures in the next, thereby giving and seeking the afterlife rather than grieving over worldly loss. «أو لعلكم تعملون أفكاركم فيما يفنى ويضمحل من الدنيا وفيما هو باق لكم في الأخرى»
#322 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The fire-bearing whirlwind is named and described for its rhetorical force; it may wrap like a twisted cloth or squeeze clouds and passing bodies, and tanwīn magnifies the fire. «وتذكير الضمير لإعتبار التذكير فيه وإنما سمى ذلك الهواء إعصارا لأنه يلتف كما يلتف الثوب المعصور»
#323 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās interprets iʿṣār as any violent wind and its fire as the scorching samūm. «وروي عن ابن عباس أن الإعصار الريح الشديدة مطلقا وأن المراد من النار السموم»
#324 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · This image portrays the grief of a donor whose good deeds are nullified just when old and needy on Judgment Day, like an elderly parent losing the family’s garden. «وهذا كما روي عن السدي تمثيل حال من ينفق ويضم إلى إنفاقه ما يحبطه في الحسرة والأسف إذا كان يوم القيامة»
#325 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · عطاء · reports · ʿAṭāʾ reports that ʿUmar asked about the parable; Ibn ʿAbbās explained a formerly righteous person who, near death and most in need of a good ending, commits sins and burns his deeds, impressing ʿUmar. «وأخرج عبد بن حميد عن عطاء أن عمر بن الخطاب»
#326 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · A version transmitted by al-Bukhārī, al-Ḥākim, Ibn Jarīr, and others has ʿUmar ask the Companions about the verse and Ibn ʿAbbās describe a rich obedient person corrupted by Satan until his deeds burn. «وفي رواية البخاري والحاكم وابن جرير وجماعة»
#327 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · prefers · The Ibn ʿAbbās interpretation is stronger than limiting the parable to charity invalidated by reproach or ostentation, since he is the Qur’an’s interpreter and ʿUmar accepted it. «وأنت تعلم أن هذا لا يدفع أحسنية ذلك لا سيما وقد قاله ترجمان القرآن وإرتضاه الأمير المحدث»
#328 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The image asks whether anyone would want a rich orchard to burn just when old age and helpless young dependents make its loss devastating. «ve bu hâlde elleri ermez, güçleri yetmez yavrucukları dahi bulunsun»
#329 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Faith and good deeds are like gardens whose reward is destroyed at Judgment by disbelief, ostentation, reminders, hurt, and other corrupt motives. «imansızlık, riyâ, menn ü ezâ ve sâir ağrâz-ı fâside gibi seyyiât da yevm-i kıyâmette o ateşli bora gibidir»
#330 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The Islamic polity is also a garden that prepares the way to eternal blessing; coercive, unbelieving injustice, immorality, and loss of resolve can devastate both worldly life and the afterlife. «dâr-ı İslâm dahi böyle hüsn-i infak ve a‘mâl-i sâliha mahsûlü bir bağa, bir cennete»
#331 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · God explains signs so people reflect, draw sound analogies from them, apply principles to their circumstances, reach conclusions, and act toward their aims. «ibretler alasınız, güzel güzel kıyaslara yol bulasınız, bunların her birini delil, asıl, düstur, mebde’»
#332 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Thought is the process of arranging known matters to discover what is unknown. «“fikir”, umûr-i ma‘lûmeyi tertib ile mâlumdan meçhûlü bulmaktır»
#333 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The discussion turns toward where, how, and to whom obligatory and voluntary spending should be given. «o infakları nereden yapacağız, zekâtları, sadakaları nereden ve nasıl ve kimlere vereceğiz mi diyeceksiniz»
#334 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The new parable answers the hearer's expectation after two favorable spending images, depicting the disgraceful opposite of sincere charity unaccompanied by reminders or harm. «استئناف بياني أثاره ضرب المثل العجيب للمنفق في سبيل الله بمثل حبة»
#335 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Its likeness is failure and despair just as hope nears its harvest, contrasting the sincere spender and illustrating the ruin of showy or injurious giving. «ووجه الشبه هو حصول خيبة ويأس في وقت تمام الرجاء وإشراف الإنتاج»
#336 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The owner is old, unable to earn elsewhere, and has weak dependents; ضعيف means young or limited, and can also mean poor, as 2:282 and Abū Khālid al-ʿAttābī's verse show. «بأنه ذو عيال فهو في حاجة إلى نفعهم وأنهم ضعفاء أي صغار إذ الضعيف»
#337 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The poem of al-Aʿshā heightens the old man's dependence on his garden and anticipation of its yield, sharpening the audience's sense of impending loss. «وقد أصابه الكبر فلا قدرة له على الكسب غير تلك الجنة»
