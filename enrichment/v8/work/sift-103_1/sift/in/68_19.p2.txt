#89 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The Quran leaves the story's date unstated and gives no basis for the strange claims that Satan or Gabriel uprooted the trees and carried them to the Kaaba and Taif. «دون أن يشير من قرب أو بعد، إلى ما يسيغ الروايات الغريبة» [filed under 68:17-19]
#90 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Yunus 10:24 offers a useful parallel for understanding the garden story as a picture of worldly life suddenly cut down. «لأن نستأنس في فهم آيات القلم، بآية يونس في مثل الحياة الدنيا» [filed under 68:17-19]
#91 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The punishment verse points to the lesson of the garden story: tyrants face punishment in this world, while the hereafter's punishment is greater. «يلفت إلى مناط العبرة فيما تلت الآيات من أمر أصحاب الجنة» [filed under 68:17-33]
#92 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Attributing the orchard’s destruction to God signals that the disaster arrived wholly unexpectedly and devastated it in an instant. «the calamity arrived without the slightest expectation that it would» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#93 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The calamity was so devastating that it miraculously reduced the orchard to nothing in a blink; ‘from your Lord’ reassures the Prophet amid Quraysh’s apparent ease. «it was so devastating in nature that it miraculously routed the orchard to nothingness in the blink of an eye» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#94 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The parable answers those who call the Prophet possessed because they feel secure: punishment can ruin even plans fixed by pledges when people wake to carry them out. «all of man’s schemes which he devises with the conviction of pledges and oaths are rendered to ruin» [filed under 68:19, 68:20]
#95 STUDYQURAN · Most commentators · reports · Most commentators place the story in Madinah and identify its owners as three Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen who inherited their father’s fertile garden; their father left produce for the poor, but they planned to withhold it and harvest secretly. «Most commentators maintain that these verses were revealed in Madinah and that the story refers to three either Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen» [filed under 68:17-34]
#96 STUDYQURAN · holds · The visitation was a scourge sent by God. «Visitation here indicates some form of scourge sent by God»
#97 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Qalam is generally considered an early Makkan sūrah. «generally thought to be from the early Makkan period» [filed under 68:1-52]
#98 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qatādah · reports · These early commentators held that verses 1–16, 34–47, and 51–52 are Makkan, while 17–33 and 48–50 are Madinan. «the early commentators IbnʿAbbās and al-Qatādah (d. 117/726) maintained that vv. 1–16» [filed under 68:1-52]
#99 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 17–34 tell the parable of garden owners who lose their harvest because of miserliness. «the parable of the owners of the garden, who lost their harvest because of their miserliness» [filed under 68:17-34]
