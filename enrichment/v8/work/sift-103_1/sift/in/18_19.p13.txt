#556 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾān omits names, ancestry, birth, upbringing, occupations, social standing, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors, and the dog’s identity because guidance does not depend on them; historians and early exegetes filled these gaps, producing divergent accounts. «إلى غير ذلك من الأمور التي لا يتوقف غرض الهداية على العلم بشيء منها كما يتوقف عليه غرض البحث التاريخي» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#557 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · After 309 years God raised the youths to show how He had rescued them; they awoke disoriented, and some guessed they had slept a day or part of one while others deferred to God’s knowledge. «بعثهم بعد هذا الدهر الطويل وهو ثلاثمائة وتسع سنين من يوم دخلوا الكهف» [filed under 18:12, 18:19, 18:25]
#558 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Because they were hungry, the youths sent one with their silver to find wholesome food and instructed him to act discreetly, fearing stoning or forced return to their religion. «فإنكم جياع وليتلطف الذاهب منكم إلى المدينة في مسيره إليها وشرائه الطعام ولا يشعرن بكم أحدا»
#559 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · God allowed their discovery after the persecuting generation and its rule had passed; the new population, dominated by monotheists and disputing resurrection, would receive a sign of it. «القوم الذين اعتزلوهم وفارقوهم يوم دخلوا الكهف قد انقرضوا وذهب الله بهم وبملكهم وملتهم» [filed under 18:19, 18:21]
#560 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The messenger expected the same city, but its people and conditions had changed over three centuries; his old silver exposed him as one of the vanished youths, and the news brought the townspeople to the cave. «وهو يظن أنها التي فارقها البارحة لكنه وجد المدينة قد تغيرت بما لا يعهد مثله في يوم» [filed under 18:19, 18:21]
#561 TABATABAI d.1402 · أهل الرواية والتاريخ · reports · Most historians date the story between Jesus and Muḥammad; it is absent from the two Testaments, though some Muslim reports say Quraysh heard it from Jews, while Christians preserved it extensively. «معظم أهل الرواية والتاريخ على أن القصة وقعت في الفترة بين النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وبين المسيح» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#562 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Christian accounts resemble Ibn Isḥāq’s report from Ibn ʿAbbās in some respects but differ from Muslims on the number of sleepers, whether they had a dog, and the length of their sleep. «أن المصادر السريانية تذكر عدد أصحاب الكهف ثمانية في حين يذكره المسلمون» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#563 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · After centuries as a ruined, forgotten site, Jordanian excavation uncovered Rujayb; the author considers its archaeological signs and some Muslim reports the strongest fit for the Qurʾānic cave. «وقامت عدة من الأمارات والشواهد الأثرية على كونه هو كهف أصحاب الكهف المذكورين في القرآن» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#564 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Amman stands on the site of Philadelphia, a major and beautiful city before Islam that remained under Roman control from the early second century until the Muslim conquest. «كانت هي وما والاها تحت استيلاء الروم منذ أوائل القرن الثاني الميلادي حتى فتح المسلمون الأرض المقدسة» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#565 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Other proposed locations include a cave on Mount Qāsiyūn near Damascus, one at Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and a cave near Nakhchivan. «والكهف الثالث كهف بجبل قاسيون بالقرب من الصالحية بدمشق الشام» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#566 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · No evidence identifies any proposed cave as the Qurʾānic one; the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with the story’s Roman setting and the limits of Roman power. «ولا شاهد يشهد على كون شيء من هذه الكهوف هو الكهف المذكور في القرآن الكريم» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#567 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · most commentators · reports · Most commentators take li in li-yatasa'alu to express purpose: so that the youths might ask one another. «which most commentators take to mean "so that they might ask one another"»
#568 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · prefers · Asad instead reads li as lam al-aqibah, marking causal sequence, which he renders as and they began to ask. «li in li-yatasa'alu (which most commentators take to mean "so that they might ask one another") is not a particle»
#569 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The matching question and answer in 2:259 link both allegories to God's power to create life, end it, and resurrect. «exactly the same question is asked and exactly the same wondering answer is given»
#570 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The story also exposes the deceptive, earthbound human conception of time. «the deceptive, purely earthbound character of the human concept of "time".»
#571 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Some sleepers understood that the interval had no independent reality, as with death and resurrection; this explains the two viewpoints in verse 12. «the lapse of time between their "falling asleep" and their "awakening" had no reality of its own»
#572 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers passage answers an objection from the Prophet’s opponents and presents their real story without needless details. «their real life aside from needless details becomes evident» [filed under 18:9-26]
#573 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ trials parallel the Prophet and his companions’ preaching phase; perseverance brings God’s easing of hardship and provision, and He does not let His persevering servants lose their way. «God will ease out their hardships the way He did so for them» [filed under 18:9-26]
#574 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This overview is offered as sufficient to establish the sūrah’s coherence and central theme before its detailed exegesis. «This concise analysis of the meanings of the sūrah is sufficient to explain its coherence and central theme» [filed under 18:1-110]
#575 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers question was planted by the People of the Book and posed by Quraysh to harass and corner the Prophet, not to seek truth. «These irrelevant questions were obviously not meant to find out the real truth» [filed under 18:9-26]
#576 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Opponents planned to exploit either an inability to answer or an answer conflicting with their own beliefs. «if the Prophet (sws) was able to answer them and the answer was against their beliefs» [filed under 18:9-26]
#577 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an generally counsels ignoring such questions, but answers when useful; the truthful account both answers and trains the Prophet and companions in perseverance during comparable trials. «if some question is beneficial or its answer can be beneficial for education» [filed under 18:9-26]
#578 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an preserves the useful, true part of the cave story, correcting false accretions and restoring a major chapter in truth-seekers’ history. «a very important chapter in the history of the seekers of the truth was cleansed from all sorts of wrong details» [filed under 18:9-26]
#579 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The dwellers’ identities, date and ancestry cannot be known securely; beyond the Qur’anic account, claims are folk tales, and further inquiry is discouraged. «Who were the cave-dwellers, when did they inhabit the earth and whose ancestors were they are questions that cannot be answered with certainty» [filed under 18:9-26]
#580 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Islahi’s opinion is that the youths were ancestors of Christians, not Quraysh or Jews, and belonged to the early Christian period under Roman persecution. «in my opinion, they belonged to the initial period of the Christians when they were persecuted by the Romans» [filed under 18:9-26]
#581 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · He says they boldly preached monotheism and accountability, faced imminent stoning, then took refuge in a cave where God supplied extraordinary protection. «They fearlessly invited people to monotheism and warned them of the accountability in the hereafter» [filed under 18:9-26]
#582 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · After their circumstances became known, groups built memorials and claimed them; Christian retellings gradually reduced their story to miracles and unsupported details. «Gradually, this tradition became confined to some miraculous feats and baseless details about them» [filed under 18:9-26]
#583 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Debates over their number and dog obscured their message and sacrifices; Islahi says the true history was later lost as Christianity’s teachings were distorted. «no one knew what their real message was and the extent of sacrifices they gave» [filed under 18:9-26]
#584 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Distorted reports about the youths circulated among Arab Christians, who prompted the question; the Qur’an answered to reassure the Prophet and companions in adversity. «he could receive great assurance and strength in the adverse circumstances he and his companions were passing through» [filed under 18:9-26]
#585 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ persecution and cave refuge parallel the Prophet and companions’ trials and their coming migrations to Abyssinia, the Thawr cave and Madinah. «the Prophet (sws) and his companions too had to face tremendous trials at the hands of the Quraysh» [filed under 18:9-26]
