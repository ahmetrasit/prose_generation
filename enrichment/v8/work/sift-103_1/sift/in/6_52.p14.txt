#535 STUDYQURAN · holds · The Prophet refused to drive away his poorer followers, as Noah refused similar demands, though he was prepared to have them depart temporarily when prominent Makkans visited. «The Prophet refused to “drive away” his poorer followers (in 11:27–29 and 26:111–14 Noah refuses to do the same)»
#536 STUDYQURAN · IK, R, Ṭ, W · reports · The verse was revealed to forbid the Prophet from driving away his pious followers who worship morning and evening. «This verse was then revealed forbidding him from “driving away” his pious followers who call upon their Lord morning and evening (IK, R, Ṭ, W)»
#537 STUDYQURAN · Ṭ, W · reports · Quran 18:28 is linked to a similar circumstance and is reportedly a revelation about that situation. «reportedly revealed in a similar circumstance (Ṭ, W)»
#538 STUDYQURAN · holds · Quran 80:1–10 recounts the incident in which the Prophet is admonished for showing displeasure when a poor blind man came instead of wealthier Makkans. «about the incident in which the Prophet is admonished for registering displeasure when his teaching attracted a poor blind man rather than the wealthier Makkans»
#539 STUDYQURAN · holds · These passages raise the question of how the Prophet could appear to favor status-conscious Makkans over sincere followers, given that driving them away would make him a wrongdoer. «These passages raise the question of how the Prophet could seem to favor these prominent Makkans»
#540 STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī · reports · Al-Rāzī says the Prophet’s possible initial willingness to accommodate the Makkans was intended only to facilitate their acceptance of Islam. «al-Rāzī argues that even if the Prophet were initially prepared to accede to the Makkans’ demand»
#541 STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī · reports · Al-Rāzī reasons that the poor followers could be instructed another time, so no spiritual harm would come to them. «his intention was only to facilitate their acceptance of Islam, and since the Prophet could continue to instruct his poorer followers at another time»
#542 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Anʿām is Makkan and addresses the Prophet and Muslims' struggle with Makkan idolatry; its central aim is to refute idolatry and invented ritual practices. «al-Anʿām clearly addresses the challenges faced by the Prophet and the Muslims engaged in a religious struggle with the idolatrous Makkans» [filed under 6:1-165]
#543 STUDYQURAN · holds · The surah refutes worship of idols, celestial bodies, and jinn, and challenges the Makkans' invented religious practices. «The primary concern of the sūrah is to refute through powerful arguments various kinds of idolatry in general—including the worship of idols, celestial bodies, and jinn» [filed under 6:1-165]
#544 STUDYQURAN · Many · reports · Many regard it as a late Makkan surah, reflecting the Prophet's final effort to persuade the Makkans before his 622 migration to Madinah. «Many consider this sūrah to be a late Makkan one and thus to reflect the culmination of the Prophet’s struggle and effort to persuade the Makkans» [filed under 6:1-165]
#545 STUDYQURAN · holds · The Prophet is addressed throughout and given arguments and challenges for disbelievers; the surah sums up the Quranic case against idolatry. «The Prophet is directly addressed throughout the sūrah and given specific arguments and challenges to pose to the disbelievers in Makkah» [filed under 6:1-165]
#546 STUDYQURAN · reports · A hadith reports that al-Anʿām descended all at once with seventy thousand angels praising and glorifying God. «According to a ḥadīth, “Sūrat al-Anʿām was sent down all at once, accompanied by seventy thousand angels, hymning glorifications and praises”» [filed under 6:1-165]
#547 STUDYQURAN · al-Zamakhsharī · reports · A longer version adds that the seventy thousand angels seek forgiveness for whoever recites al-Anʿām with each verse, day and night. «A longer version of thisḥadīth cited by al-Zamakhsharī adds» [filed under 6:1-165]
