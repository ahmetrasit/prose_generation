#121 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · ʿAṣr is pressing something to extract what it contains; clouds are called muʿṣirāt because rain is pressed from them, and a people who receive rain are said to have entered a rainy state. «العصر: مصدر عصر يعصر عصرا، وهو الاعتماد على الشيء ليستجلب ما فيه»
#122 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · The cupbearer’s “wine” means grapes, named for what they become; another view says khamr means grapes in Oman’s language. «{إني أراني أعصر خمرا} أي: عنبا، سمى العنب خمرا باسم ما يؤول إليه، كما يقال:»
#123 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · The cupbearer saw three grape clusters in a garden and squeezed them into the king’s cup to serve him. «فإذا بأصل حبلة عليها ثلاث عناقيد من عنب فجنيتها، وكان كأس الملك بيدي فعصرتها فيه وسقيت الملك فشربه.»
#124 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · The baker saw three baskets above his head containing bread and assorted foods, from which birds of prey ate. «كأن فوق رأسي ثلاث سلال فيها الخبز وألوان الأطعمة وسباع الطير تنهش منه»
#125 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · Interpreting a dream means explaining what it signifies and how its outcome will unfold; “one of the good” means skilled in interpretation, with ihsan here meaning knowledge. «{نبئنا بتأويله} أخبرنا بتفسيره وتعبيره وما يؤول إليه أمر هذه الرؤيا. {إنا نراك من المحسنين}»
#126 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · Joseph cared for prisoners, visited the ill, made room for them, helped them obtain necessities, and devoted nights to worship. «كان إذا مرض إنسان في السجن عاده وقام عليه، وإذا ضاق»
#127 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · Another account says Joseph comforted the prisoners, introduced his prophetic lineage, and the keeper let him choose his quarters; he asked the two dreamers not to love him because love had repeatedly brought him hardship. «فجعل يسليهم ويقول: أبشروا واصبروا تؤجروا، فيقولون:»
#128 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · Because Joseph knew one prisoner would suffer, he initially avoided their request and instead demonstrated his prophetic sign and called them to monotheism. «فلما قصا عليه الرؤيا كره يوسف أن يعبر لهما ما سألاه لما علم في ذلك من المكروه على أحدهما، فأعرض عن سؤالهما وأخذ في غيره»
#129 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · The two young men served Egypt’s king al-Rayyan ibn al-Walid ibn Sharwan: one was the baker and the other the cupbearer; the king imprisoned them after a poisoning plot. «ملك مصر الأكبر، أحدهما: خبازه وصاحب طعامه، والآخر: ساقيه وصاحب شرابه.»
#130 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · A group bribed the two to poison the king; the cupbearer withdrew while the baker accepted. The cupbearer warned the king, survived a test, and the baker’s food killed an animal, so both were jailed. «فضمنوا لهذين مالا ليسما الملك في طعامه وشرابه فأجاباهم، ثم إن الساقي نكل عنه، وقبل الخباز الرشوة فسم الطعام،»
#131 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · reports · Joseph taught dream interpretation in prison; Ibn Masud says the two men invented dreams to test him, while others say they had real dreams that troubled them. «قال ابن مسعود: ما رأيا شيئا وإنما تحالما ليجربا يوسف. وقال قوم: بل كانا رأيا حقيقة،»
#132 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Maʿa marks companionship being newly entered into, so the two youths entered prison in Yusuf's company. «يدل على معنى الصحبة واستحداثها ، تقول : خرجت مع الأمير»
#133 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The pronoun in nabbiʾnā bi-taʾwīlihi refers to the story they recounted and functions like a demonstrative pronoun. «والضمير يجري مجرى اسم الإشارة في نحوه كأنه قيل : نبئنا بتأويل ذلك»
#134 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The youths were the king's baker and cupbearer, imprisoned on suspicion that they were poisoning him when Yusuf was admitted. «{ فتيان } عبدان للملك : خبازه وشرابيه : رقي إليه أنهما يسمانه»
#135 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Their statements describe past dreams; khamr names grapes by what they become, and in the language of Oman it means grapes directly. «{ إنى أرانى } يعني في المنام ، وهي حكاية حال ماضية { أعصر خمرا } يعني عنبا»
#136 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masʿud reads ʿinaban, grapes, in place of khamran. «وفي قراءة ابن مسعود ( أعصر عنبا )»
#137 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Muḥsinīn may mean skilled interpreters of dreams, learned men, or people who treat fellow prisoners well. «{ من المحسنين } من الذين يحسنون عبارة الرؤيا ، أي : يجيدونها»
#138 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · قتادة · reports · Qatāda reports that Yusuf comforted despondent prisoners, urging them to be patient and promising reward; they praised his character and asked who he was. «وعن قتادة : كان في السجن ناس قد انقطع رجاؤهم وطال حزنهم»
#139 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · A prison report says Yusuf identified himself through his lineage: son of Jacob, Isaac, and Abraham, and the guard promised him good treatment despite being unable to free him. «أنا يوسف ابن صفي الله يعقوب ابن ذبيح الله إسحاق ابن خليل الله إبراهيم»
#140 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Yusuf asked the two men not to love him, saying that love had brought him calamity through his aunt, father, and the Aziz's wife. «أنشدكما بالله أن لا تحباني ، فوالله ما أحبني أحد قط إلا دخل علي من حبه بلاء»
#141 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · الشعبي · reports · Al-Shaʿbī says the two men feigned dreams to test Yusuf: the cupbearer dreamed of three grape clusters pressed for the king, while the baker dreamed of three food baskets attacked by birds. «وعن الشعبي أنهما تحالما له ليمتحناه»
#142 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Some say the grape is called wine by what it will become; others say this is the language of Oman’s Azd. «قيل: إنه سمى العنب خمرا بالمئال»
#143 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · الأصمعي · reports · Al-Asmaʿi recounts an Arab carrying grapes who answered that he was carrying wine, meaning grapes. «ما تحمل؟ قال: خمرا، أراد العنب»
#144 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Ubay ibn Kaʿb and Ibn Masud read the dream as pressing grapes rather than wine. «في قراءة أبي بن كعب وعبد الله بن مسعود «إني أراني أعصر عنبا»»
#145 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Alternatively, the phrase can describe wine as pressed, since pressing is done for its sake. «العصر لها ومن أجلها»
#146 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · The bread may have been tharīd; Ibn Masud’s codex explicitly says tharīd above the dreamer’s head. «فوق رأسي ثريدا تأكل الطير منه»
#147 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · الجمهور · reports · The majority understand the prisoners’ praise to mean that Joseph was excellent in knowledge. «قال الجمهور: يريدان في العلم»
#148 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · الضحاك وقتادة · reports · Al-Dahhak and Qatada understand the praise as Joseph’s kind conduct and good treatment of fellow prisoners. «من المحسنين في جريه مع أهل السجن وإجماله معهم»
#149 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن إسحاق · reports · Ibn Ishaq says they meant Joseph had done them a personal kindness by interpreting their dreams. «ونحا إليه ابن إسحاق»
#150 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Joseph won the prisoners’ affection through his speech and virtue, comforting the sad, visiting the ill, helping the poor, and urging good. «وكان يسلي حزينهم ويعود مريضهم ويسأل لفقيرهم ويندبهم إلى الخير»
#151 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · When the prison keeper offered Joseph his choice of rooms, Joseph asked him not to love him, recalling the harm that followed his aunt’s, father’s, and the governor’s wife’s affection. «لا تحبني يرحمك الله، فلقد أدخلت علي المحبة مضرات»
#152 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud says the two prisoners staged the dreams to test Joseph’s interpretation skill. «فروي عن ابن مسعود أن الفتيين استعملا هاتين المنامتين ليجرباه»
#153 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid says the two prisoners really saw the dreams and wanted to ask Joseph about them. «عم مجاهد أنهما رأيا ذلك حقيقة، فأرادا سؤاله»
#154 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · One prisoner, reportedly named Banu, dreamed of a vine with three branches and bunches of grapes that he pressed for the king. «فكنت أعصرها وأسقي الملك»
#155 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · The other, reportedly named Majlath, dreamed he carried three baskets of bread on his head as birds ate from the top. «وعلى رأسي ثلاث سلال فيها خبز، والطير تأكل من أعلاه»
#156 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The two prisoners entered with Joseph either at the same time or separately; مع does not settle the timing. «تحتمل أن تكون باقتران وقت الدخول، وأن لا تكون بل دخلوا أفذاذا»
#157 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · The two young men reportedly served the great king al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan: one was his baker and the other his cupbearer. «الريان- أحدهما: خبازه، والآخر: ساقيه»
#158 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Fatā means a young man, but can also refer to an enslaved person or a free servant; the two may fit all these senses. «قد تقع اللفظة على المملوك وعلى الخادم الحر»
#159 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · السدي · reports · Al-Suddi says the king suspected the baker of trying to poison him and imprisoned both men because the cupbearer agreed. «الخابز منهما أراد سمه، ووافقه على ذلك الساقي»
#160 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The citation to call people to God's path with wisdom frames the account of Yusuf's religious address to the prisoners. «ادع إلى سبيل ربك بالحكمة والموعظة الحسنة»
#161 TABRISI d.548 · reports · The two prisoners said they had come to love Yusuf; he replied that affection for him had repeatedly brought him hardship: his aunt's love led to a theft accusation, his father's to the well, and the Aziz's wife's to prison. «لا تحباني فوالله ما أحبني أحد إلا دخل علي من حبه بلاء»
#162 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الزجاج · reports · The mention of the two youths entering prison implies that Joseph himself had been imprisoned, though this was not stated. «قال الزجاج فيه دليل على أنه حبس وإن لم يذكر ذلك»
#163 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · reports · The two “youths” may have been young or old, since an enslaved man could be called a fatā at any age; Ibn al-Anbari ties the label to their being owned. «فتيان جائز أن يكونا حدثين أو شيخين لأنهم يسمون المملوك فتى»
