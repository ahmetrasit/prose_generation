#164 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · قيل · reports · Another view takes the pronouns to refer to the polytheists: they are not responsible for the Prophet's account nor he for theirs, so eagerness for their conversion must not lead him to expel believers. «لا يؤاخذون بحسابك ولا أنت بحسابهم ، حتى يهمك إيمانهم»
#165 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · In the syntax, فتطردهم responds to the negation and فتكون to the prohibition; alternatively, it coordinates as a consequence because injustice results from expelling them. «جواب النهي . ويجوز أن يكون عطفا على»
#166 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The people addressed were poor, socially weak believers, including Bilal, Ammar, Ibn Umm Abd, Murthid al-Ghanawi, Khabbab, Suhayb, Subayh, Dhul-Shimalayn, and al-Miqdad. «المراد ب الذين ضعفة المؤمنين في ذلك الوقت في أمور الدنيا بلال وعمار وابن أم عبد»
#167 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Some unbelievers said their status and rank prevented them from mixing with the poor; if Muhammad expelled them, the elites would follow him. A report also attributes the advice to Abu Talib, and Quraysh leaders gathered to press it; the author thinks the proposal was likely a ruse. «قال بعضهم للنبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: نحن لشرفنا»
#168 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says some unbelievers asked to move the poor believers out of the front prayer row so they could take their places and believe. «إن بعض الكفار إنما طلب أن يؤخر هؤلاء عن الصف الأول في الصلاة»
#169 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · خباب بن الأرت عن الطبري · reports · Al-Tabari transmits that al-Aqraʿ ibn Habis and similar Arab notables asked for a separate meeting without slaves or allies and for a written agreement; Muhammad considered it before the verse came down. «أسند الطبري إلى خباب بن الأرت أن الأقرع بن حابس ومن شابهه من أشراف العرب قالوا للنبي»
#170 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · المهدوي · reports · Al-Mahdawi says all the pronouns in the passage refer to the believers; Hasan says their accounting concerns their deeds, as most interpreters hold. «فعلى هذا تجيء الضمائر كلها للمؤمنين»
#171 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The first min is partitive and the second is emphatic; the two fa-verbs follow the prior negation and prohibition, and being among wrongdoers means putting things in the wrong place. «ومن الأولى للتبعيض والثانية زائدة مؤكدة»
#172 TABRISI d.548 · reports · A report describes al-Aqraʿ b. Ḥābis, ʿUyayna b. Ḥiṣn, and other leaders disparaging the poor believers and asking the Prophet to dismiss them when Arab delegations came; he agrees, but Gabriel reveals the prohibition and the Prophet tears up their proposed written pledge. «جاء الأقرع بن حابس التميمي وعيينه بن حصين الفزاري وذووهم من المؤلفة قلوبهم»
#173 TABRISI d.548 · النبي · reports · After the revelation, the Prophet tells the believers that their Lord has prescribed mercy, then quotes God's instruction to keep patient company with them and says he will live and die among them. «فكنا نقعد معه فإذا أراد أن يقوم»
#174 TABRISI d.548 · holds · God forbids His Messenger from acceding to the polytheists' demand to expel the believers who worship Him at dawn and evening. «ثم نهى سبحانه رسوله عليه واله السلام عن إجابة المشركين»
#175 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The cited words from Noah say that their reckoning rests with the Lord alone, supporting the interpretation that God alone will judge the believers deeds. «إن حسابهم إلا على ربي لو تشعرون»
#176 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The lām in the outcome clause marks consequence: God acted so they might be patient and grateful, and their question was the eventual outcome. «هذه لام العاقبة المعنى فعلنا هذا ليصبروا ويشكروا»
#177 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The question whether God knows the grateful is a confirming question, as illustrated by Jarīr poetry. «هذا استفهام تقرير أي إنه كذلك كقول جرير»
#178 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The polytheists despised the poor believers because of their poverty and devotion, wanting them set below themselves in the gathering; the verse rebuts this social contempt. «وهذا لأن المشركين أزدروهم لفقرهم وحاجتهم إلى الأعمال الدينية»
#179 TABRISI d.548 · holds · A second interpretation says the Prophet is not responsible for believers livelihood, so poverty is no reason to tire of them and expel them. «ما عليك من حساب رزقهم من شيء فتملهم وتطردهم»
#180 TABRISI d.548 · holds · God provides both the poor believers and the Prophet, so neither party depends on the other for provision. «أي ليس رزقهم عليك ولا رزقك عليهم وإنما يرزقك وإياهم الله»
#181 TABRISI d.548 · ابن زيد · reports · Ibn Zayd interprets the warning as saying the Prophet would wrong the believers if he expelled them; Ibn ʿAbbās says such expulsion would harm the Prophet himself through disobedience. «لهم بطردهم عن ابن زيد»
#182 TABRISI d.548 · ابن الأنباري · reports · Ibn al-Anbārī says the warning is severe because the Prophet had considered preferring wealthy leaders to attract their followers, intending good rather than contempt for the poor, but God forbade it. «عظم الأمر في هذا على النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم وخوف الدخول في جملة الظالمين»
#183 TABRISI d.548 · holds · God tests wealthy leaders through poor believers: a proud notable may refuse faith when a poorer person has preceded him, lest he lose precedence. «ابتلينا هؤلاء الرؤساء من قريش بالموالي فإذا نظر الشريف إلى الوضيع قد آمن قبله»
#184 TABRISI d.548 · holds · God is described as testing though He needs no test because He treats people as a tester would; the outcome clause means the affair resulted in their asking whether God favored these believers. «وهو لا يحتاج إلى الاختبار لأنه عاملهم معاملة المختبر»
#185 TABRISI d.548 · الجبائي · reports · Al-Jubbāʾī interprets the test as the difficult obligation imposed on Arab nobles to believe and give precedence to those who believed first; the clause lets believers accept the Prophet's honor toward them rather than denying God's favor. «المعنى في فتنا شددنا التكليف على إشراف العرب بأن أمرناهم بالإيمان»
#186 TABRISI d.548 · ابن عامر · reports · Ibn ʿĀmir reads the temporal phrase with wāw as al-ghudwa throughout the Qurʾan; the other readers read al-ghadāh with alif. «قرأ ابن عامر بالغدوة والعشي في كل القرآن بواو والباقون بالغداة بالألف»
#187 TABRISI d.548 · أبو علي · reports · Abū ʿAlī argues al-ghadāh is preferable because it is an indefinite noun that can take the article, whereas ghudwa is a proper name and does not become indefinite; the grammarians' reports also support ghudāh. «قال أبو علي: الوجه الغداة لأنها تستعمل نكرة»
#188 TABRISI d.548 · الخليل · reports · The reading ghudwa is also defended: al-Khalīl allows it as a temporal adverb like ḍuḥwa, and other time names can appear definite without the article. «ووجه قراءة ابن عامر أن سيبويه قال زعم الخليل أنه يجوز أن تقول»
#189 TABRISI d.548 · holds · “Then you would expel them” answers the preceding negation, and “then you would be among the wrongdoers” is subjunctive as the result of the prohibition not to expel. «فتطردهم جواب للنفي في قوله ما عليك من حسابهم»
#190 TABRISI d.548 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masʿūd reports Quraysh leaders passing the Prophet while he sat with poor Muslims—Ṣuhayb, Khabbāb, Bilāl, and ʿAmmār—and asking him to expel them so the leaders might follow him. «روى الثعلبي بإسناده عن عبد الله بن مسعود قال مر الملأ من قريش على رسول الله»
#191 TABRISI d.548 · سلمان · reports · Salmān and Khabbāb say the verse was revealed concerning them. «وقال سلمان وخباب: فينا نزلت هذه الآية»
#192 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · سعد بن أبي وقاص · reports · The verse concerned six poor Companions, including Sa'd, Ibn Masud, Suhayb, Ammar, al-Miqdad, and Bilal; Quraysh demanded the Prophet expel them rather than be seen following them. «نزلت هذه الآية في ستة في وفي ابن مسعود وصهيب وعمار والمقداد وبلال»
#193 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · خباب بن الأرت · reports · Khabbab says poor believers learned from the Prophet morning and evening; al-Aqra and Uyayna demanded they be removed, then pressed for a written agreement, until Gabriel brought the warning against expelling them and the Prophet welcomed them. «كنا ضعفاء عند النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم يعلمنا بالغداة والعشي ما ينفعنا»
#194 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن مسعود · reports · Quraysh leaders passing by the Prophet and poor Companions asked whether he expected them to become followers of those men. «مر الملأ من قريش على رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم وعنده خباب وصهيب»
#195 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عكرمة · reports · Nobles of Abd Manaf asked Abu Talib to make Muhammad expel clients and slaves; Umar suggested trying it, but the verse came and Umar apologized. «فقالوا لو أن ابن أخيك يطرد عنه موالينا وعبيدنا كان أعظم في صدورنا»
#196 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · أبو صالح عن ابن عباس · reports · The verse concerns the clients Bilal, Suhayb, Khabbab, Ammar, Mihja, Salman, Amir ibn Fuhayra, and Salim, and another verse also addressed them. «أن هذه الآيات نزلت في الموالي منهم بلال وصهيب وخباب وعمار ومهجع»
#197 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · العوفي عن ابن عباس · reports · Some nobles offered belief if the Prophet delayed the poor believers and let them pray behind the nobles; this report seeks moving them from the prayer row, unlike the others' request to remove them from his gathering. «نؤمن لك وإذا صلينا فأخر هؤلاء الذين معك فليصلوا خلفنا فعلى هذا»
#198 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عمر وابن عباس · reports · Calling upon their Lord means the obligatory prayers. «أنه الصلاة المكتوبة قاله ابن عمر وابن عباس»
#199 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Morning stands for the whole day and evening for the night; if a person's daytime worship is sincere, nighttime worship is even purer. «نبه بالغداة على جميع النهار وبالعشي على الليل لأنه إذا كان عمل النهار»
#200 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid glosses the phrase as the five daily prayers. «قال مجاهد هي الصلوات الخمس»
#201 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · مجاهد وقتادة · reports · Another report from Mujahid and Qatada identifies the prayers as dawn and afternoon. «وفي رواية عن مجاهد وقتادة قالا يعني صلاة الصبح والعصر»
