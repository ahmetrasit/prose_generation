#532 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · reports · Another reported interpretation says they carefully decide where to place their money. «وقيل هو التثبت أي يتثبتون أين يضعون أموالهم»
#533 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · reports · A further interpretation says they verify their spending, proceeding if it is for God and withholding if ostentation intrudes. «وقيل هو التثبت في الانفاق، فإن كان لله أمضى»
#534 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · reports · Another interpretation makes it a resolve of the self to obey God. «وقيل التثبيت توطين النفس على طاعة الله تعالى»
#535 TABATABAI d.1402 · قيل · reports · Another interpretation is to establish oneself in faith by habituating oneself to give for God's sake. «وقيل هو تمكين النفس في منازل الإيمان بتعويدها على بذل المال»
#536 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The author says none of these reported explanations fits the wording without strained interpretation. «بأن شيئا من الأقوال لا ينطبق على الآية إلا بتكلف»
#537 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · The passage first excludes ostentation, which invalidates a deed from the outset, and spending later spoiled by mann or harm; it then describes sincere givers who hold fast to their pure intention. «وهم الذين ينفقون أموالهم ابتغاء وجه الله ثم يقرون أنفسهم على الثبات»
#538 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Giving to those in need from faith and sincere inner conviction for God's pleasure yields in proportion to intention, like an orchard protected from harmful winds that bears much in heavy rain and some in drizzle. «Allah rızâsı için ve içlerinden gelerek, gönüllerindeki inancın yönlendirdiği bir karara dayanarak muhtaçlara yardım edenler, bu niyet, şuur ve samimiyetin derecesine göre sonuç alacaklardır»
#539 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Changing fatḥa to ḍamma on rāʾ gives rubwa instead of rabwa; both dialectal forms mean elevated ground. «rubwa and rabwa are dialectal variants of the same meaning (elevated ground)»
#540 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Sukūn on kāf lightens pronunciation while preserving meaning; both forms are canonical. «Canonical variant: sukūn on kāf (uklahā vs ukulahā). Lighter pronunciation; same meaning»
#541 STUDYQURAN · IK, Ṭ · reports · IK and Ṭ understand giving out of inner confirmation as expressing certainty of faith in the act of giving. «means that the givers affirm and have certainty of faith in their act of giving (IK, Ṭ)»
#542 STUDYQURAN · Z · reports · Z reads inner confirmation as confirmation of God’s Truth or certainty. «that they do so because they have confirmation of the Truth of God or certainty (Z)»
#543 STUDYQURAN · Q, R · reports · Q and R understand the phrase as giving carefully and confirming that charity is placed where it will be used properly. «they take care and confirm where they place their charity to make sure that it is used properly (Q, R)»
#544 STUDYQURAN · holds · Rain imagery contrasts charity spoiled by bad intention with giving done for God’s pleasure and secure faith. «These verses compare the merit of two kinds of giving through the symbolism of rain striking the earth» [filed under 2:264, 2:265]
#545 STUDYQURAN · holds · Rain on a fertile garden doubles its yield, symbolizing charity given with the right intention. «the garden to double its yield, symbolizing the fruit of charity with the right intention»
#546 STUDYQURAN · holds · Even without a downpour, a softer rain lets the garden thrive and bear fruit. «even if the amount of rain is not great, the garden will at least thrive normally and bear its fruit»
#547 STUDYQURAN · Some commentators · reports · Some commentators say elevated oases gather rich earth and flourish; because streams do not reach them, rain alone nourishes them. «Some commentators mention that certain kinds of oases flourish and have rich earth collect on them when they are slightly elevated»

#549 STUDYQURAN · al-Rāzī · reports · Al-Rāzī argues hills lack streams and face too much wind, while hollows flood; he says level ground is best and prefers a meaning tied to growth. «Al-Rāzī argues that a garden upon a hill would not be reached by streams and would be exposed to too much wind»
#550 STUDYQURAN · reports · A hadith says God grows a lawfully earned date given in charity until it becomes mountain-sized or larger. «When a person gives a lawfully earned date in charity, God takes it in His Hand and causes it to grow»
