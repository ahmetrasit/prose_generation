#196 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Iftāʾ is giving a fatwa or explanation, as discussed in the earlier prison scene. «الإخبار بالفتوى . وتقدمت آنفا»
#197 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The preposition fi figuratively marks an interpretation attached to the dream, like an explanation attached to something concise. «للظرفية المجازية التي هي بمعنى الملابسة»
#198 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Putting “for the dream” before its verb observes the cadence and foregrounds the dream; its definite article is generic. «للرعاية على الفاصلة مع الاهتمام بالرؤيا»
#199 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The lām reinforces the delayed verb's connection to its object; al-Zamakhshari says careful scholars prefer the light form ʿabara, not the doubled form. «لام التقوية لضعف العامل عن العمل بالتأخير»
#200 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Ibn Ashur cites a verse recited by al-Mubarrad from an unnamed Bedouin as evidence for dream-interpreter wording: “I saw my dream, then interpreted it; I was an interpreter of dreams.” «على بيت أنشده المبرد في كتاب»
#201 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Dream interpretation was a practiced Egyptian science with priestly rules; both the prisoners' request and the king's consultation indicate that the court included interpreters. «وكان الكهنة منهم يعدونه من علومهم»
#202 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · The Torah says Pharaoh summoned Egyptian magicians and sages but found no interpreter; dream interpretation was associated with priests because it concerned the unseen. «جميع سحرة مصر وجميع حكمائها»
#203 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The passage recounts Joseph’s release, rise in Egypt, and the king’s renewed investigation that fully establishes his innocence. «تتضمن الآيات قصة خروجه عليه السلام من السجن ونيله عزة مصر»
#204 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The king reports his own dream to the officials; the wording may describe a repeated dream, as with the earlier prisoners’ dreams. «رؤيا للملك يخبر بها الملأ والدليل عليه»
#205 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Saman means fat and ʿijaf means emaciated; the latter is an exceptional feminine adjective plural, with ʿujuf offered as its regular plural. «والسمان جمع سمينة والعجاف جمع عجفاء بمعنى المهزولة»
#206 TABATABAI d.1402 · المجمع · reports · Al-Majma defines fatwa as an answer about a ruling, though it may answer about the matter itself and then is not technically a fatwa. «قال في المجمع الفتيا الجواب عن حكم المعنى وقد يكون الجواب عن نفس المعنى»
#207 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Taʿbir comes from crossing: the interpreter passes from the dream’s familiar image to the reality represented beyond it. «وهو على أي حال مأخوذ من عبور النهر ونحوه كأن العابر يعبر من الرؤيا إلى ما وراءها»
#208 TABATABAI d.1402 · الكشاف · reports · Al-Kashshaf explains that fat qualifies the cows rather than the numeral to distinguish the seven by a kind of cow; making it describe the numeral would distinguish by species and then add a second description. «إذا أوقعتها صفة لبقرات فقد قصدت إلى أن تميز السبع بنوع من البقرات»
#209 TABATABAI d.1402 · الكشاف · reports · Al-Kashshaf says the dry ears must also number seven because the passage centers on seven cows and seven green ears; coordinating dry ears as a subset would conflict with the word others. «الكلام مبني على انصبابه إلى هذا العدد في البقرات السمان والعجاف والسنابل الخضر»
#210 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Egyptian king asks his council to explain a dream of seven fat cows eaten by seven thin cows, alongside seven green ears and others dry. «وقال ملك مصر لملئه إني أرى في منامي سبع بقرات سمان يأكلهن سبع بقرات مهازيل» [filed under 12:43, 12:44]
#211 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · The dream depicts the practical measures the king must take for a coming famine: repeated planting, preserving grain in ears, and rationing reserves, rather than simply forecasting years. «أن الذي رآه الملك من الرؤيا إنما كان مثال ما يجب عليه من اتخاذ التدبير لإنجاء الناس من مصيبة الجدب» [filed under 12:43, 12:47, 12:48]
#212 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Cows represent yearly provisions: fat cows picture plentiful harvests from sustained planting, lean cows picture shortages, and their eating the fat ones pictures famine consuming saved food. «فحكت السنين المخصبة والمجدبة أي الرزق الذي يرتزقون به فيها في صورة البقرة» [filed under 12:43, 12:47, 12:48]
#213 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The green ears represent stores to be saved and the dry ears indicate preserving grain unthreshed; the dream shows the work required to protect food supplies. «وحكت ما يجب عليهم في حفظ ذخائر الرزق بالسنبلات اليابسة قبال السنبلات الخضر» [filed under 12:43, 12:47, 12:48]
#214 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The dry ears are not expressly numbered because the dream depicts duties for famine management, not the famine years themselves; numbering would be needed under a forecast reading. «ومما ذكرنا أيضا تظهر النكتة في ترك توصيف السنبلات اليابسة»
#215 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Dream interpretation appears in Jacob’s explanation of Joseph’s dream, Joseph’s interpretation of his own, his companions’ and the king’s dreams, and in waking predictions taught by God. «ما حكاه الله تعالى من بيان يعقوب تأويل رؤيا يوسف عليه السلام» [filed under 12:6, 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#216 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qur'an also recounts Joseph's dream, his prison companions' dreams, and Pharaoh's dream. «ومنها ما حكاه تعالى من رؤيا يوسف عليه السلام إذ قال يوسف لأبيه يا أبت إني رأيت» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#217 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · European natural scientists generally deny that dreams are real or scientifically connected to external events, while some psychologists study them; authentic dreams disclose future events or hidden facts in ways chance cannot explain. «الباحثين من علماء الطبيعة من أوربا لا يرون لها حقيقة» [filed under 12:4, 12:43]
#218 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Everyone has encountered dreams that reveal hidden matters or future events, especially clear dreams requiring no interpretation; their correspondence to later events cannot be dismissed as coincidence. «ما منا واحد إلا وقد شاهد من نفسه شيئا من الرؤى والمنامات دله على بعض الأمور الخفية» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#219 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Dreaming is a mental act involving imagination, which recombines stored sensory images; bodily conditions, temperament, and emotions shape many ordinary dreams. «والمتخيلة من القوى الفعالة دائما ربما تدوم في عملها من جهة الأنباء الواردة عليها من ناحية الحس» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#220 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Naturalist critics correctly identify bodily and emotional causes that shape many dreams, but their account only shows that some dreams lack truth. «وهذا هو الذي ذكره منكروا حقيقة الرؤيا من علماء الطبيعة لا يزيد على تعداد هذه الأسباب» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#221 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The claim that every dream is unreal does not follow: some sound dreams reveal truths and have a real relation to external events. «وهو غير المدعى وهو أن كل منام ليس ذا حقيقة فإن هناك منامات صالحة ورؤيا صادقة» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#222 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Every dream has causes and thus an interpretation, but causes vary: bodily or temperamental influences, chance circumstances, and preoccupations can produce dreams without foretelling external events. «فقد ظهر مما بينا أن جميع الرؤى لا تخلو عن حقيقة بمعنى أن هذه الإدراكات المتنوعة المختلفة» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#223 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The relevant true dreams are those not caused by external bodily or accidental factors, nor internal dispositions, yet connected to external events and cosmic realities. «وإنما البحث في نوع واحد من هذه المنامات» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#224 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Because future or hidden events are absent from the dreamer's senses, the author proposes that the sleeping soul connects to their causes beyond the physical world. «ولذا قيل إن الارتباط إنما استقر بينها وبين النفس النائمة من جهة اتصال النفس بسبب الحادثة الواقعة» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#225 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · He distinguishes the physical world of changing matter, the imaginal world of immaterial forms that causes physical events, and the intellectual world of universal truths that causes the imaginal realm. «أن العوالم ثلاثة عالم الطبيعة وهو العالم الدنيوي الذي نعيش فيه والأشياء الموجودة فيها صور مادية» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#226 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A soul able to grasp intellectual realities may see universal causes directly; otherwise it represents them through familiar images, and a sound soul may perceive causes without alteration in a clear dream. «فإن كانت النفس كاملة متمكنة من إدراك المجردات العقلية أدركتها واستحضرت أسباب الكائنات» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#227 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Other true dreams use symbols or opposites, such as a crown for honor or darkness for ignorance; these require interpretation by returning each image to the reality first perceived. «وهذه هي التي تحتاج إلى التعبير بردها إلى الأصل الذي هو المشهود الأولى للنفس كرد التاج إلى الفخار» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#228 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Symbolic dreams divide into manageable transformations and tangled sequences of transformations; the latter are confused dreams that resist interpretation, unlike clear dreams and interpretable symbolic dreams. «المنامات التي تصرفت فيها النفس بالحكاية والتمثيل» [filed under 12:4, 12:36, 12:43]
#229 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Joseph interpreted Pharaoh's dream as seven productive years followed by famine, proposed storing surplus grain, and was appointed to Egypt's stores after the women and al-ʿAzīz's wife cleared him; prison thus led to kingship. «وفي هذه السنين انتصب يوسف لمقام عزة مصر» [filed under 12:42-57]
#230 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad identifies the king as probably a Hyksos ruler and argues their Arab origins and kinship with Hebrews explain his confidence in Joseph and the later Israelite settlement in Egypt. «This king seems to have been one of the six Hyksos rulers who dominated Egypt»
