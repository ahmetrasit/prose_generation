#367 TABATABAI d.1402 · الصادق عليه السلام · reports · Al-Qummī glosses ṣafwān as a large rock, wābil as rain, tall as nighttime dew on plants, and iʿṣār as winds. «والصفوان هي الصخرة الكبيرة التي تكون في المفازة» [filed under 2:264, 2:265, 2:266]
#368 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The connected themes suggest this passage was revealed as one unit: spending grows seven hundredfold or more, sincere giving bears fruit, ostentation and reminders or harm ruin reward, good wealth should be given, the poor detained in God's path are recipients, and the reward is great. «اتحادها في بيان أمر الانفاق» [filed under 2:261, 2:262, 2:263, 2:264, 2:265, 2:266, 2:267, 2:268, 2:269, 2:270, 2:271, 2:272, 2:273, 2:274]
#369 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The passage orders its teaching around purpose (giving for God, not people), manner (without reproach or injury), quality (good rather than bad wealth), recipient (the needy constrained in God's path), and reward. «وهو أن يكون لله لا للناس» [filed under 2:261, 2:262, 2:263, 2:264, 2:265, 2:266, 2:267, 2:268, 2:269, 2:270, 2:271, 2:272, 2:273, 2:274]
#370 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The verse says charity is nullified when followed by reminders or hurt; it does not establish nullification of other sins through charity, despite a broader inference some may draw. «ولا دلالة في الآية على غير المن والأذى بالنسبة إلى الصدقة»
#371 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Because the address is to believers and the show-off is not a believer in the relevant sense, the prohibition targets reminders and harm; the show-off is an analogy whose deed was invalid from the start, unlike a gift later voided. «عمل المرائي باطل من رأس وعمل المان والمؤذي وقع أولا صحيحا»
#372 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The coordinated present-tense clauses mean the show-off does not believe in God's call to spend and the promised Judgment reward, not that he disbelieves in God altogether; ostentation in a deed entails such unbelief regarding it. «ليس المراد من عدم إيمان المرائي عدم إيمانه بالله سبحانه رأسا»
#373 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Ṣafwān and ṣafā are smooth stone, ṣald is hard smooth stone, and wābil is heavy, forceful rain. «والوابل المطر الغزير الشديد الوقع»
#374 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The plural pronoun in 'they cannot' refers by sense to the single show-off, and the clause states the shared feature linking stone and ostentatious giver. «والضمير في قوله لا يقدرون، راجع إلى الذي ينفق رئاء لأنه في معنى الجمع، والجملة»
#375 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · God's statement that He does not guide disbelievers generalizes the rule and explains why the show-off is a case of disbelief. «بيان للحكم بوجه عام وهو أن المرائي في ريائه»
#376 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Rain and soil normally foster life, but a smooth stone sheds the soil under heavy rain and cannot sustain seed; likewise an insincere heart cannot receive mercy or reward despite an outwardly good act. «فإنه مسلوب الاستعداد لا يقبل قلبه الرحمة والكرامة»
#377 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The parable shows that acceptance of a deed requires sincere intention and seeking God's face. «وقد ظهر من الآية أن قبول العمل يحتاج إلى نية الإخلاص وقصد وجه الله»
#378 TABATABAI d.1402 · النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم · reports · A report transmitted by both Sunni and Shi'i authorities states that deeds are only according to intentions. «وقد روى الفريقان عن النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم أنه قال»
#379 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Giving with reminders or injury resembles showy spending by those lacking faith, like soil washed from a smooth rock: it brings no reward, while sincere giving without expectation or harm brings blessing and recompense. «Yalçın ve pürüzsüz bir kayanın üzerindeki toprak şiddetli yağmur aldığında sıyrılıp yere inmekte, düz yerlerdeki toprağa bereket getiren yağmur bu kayada toprağı yok etmektedir»
#380 QIRAAT-ER · holds · The hamza is resolved to yāʾ, smoothing the pronunciation without changing meaning. «Hamza resolution: riʾāʾ → riyāʾ; the hamza is replaced by yāʾ»
#381 STUDYQURAN · holds · Giving only to be seen is the worst model of charity, as also described in the Quran’s passage about ostentatious worshippers. «The worst model of charity is one who gives only for appearance’s sake, as in 107:6–7»
#382 STUDYQURAN · holds · Rain imagery contrasts charity spoiled by bad intention with giving done for God’s pleasure and secure faith. «These verses compare the merit of two kinds of giving through the symbolism of rain striking the earth» [filed under 2:264, 2:265]
#383 STUDYQURAN · holds · Rain washes dust from a smooth rock and leaves no fertile benefit, illustrating giving without sincere intention. «When a downpour strikes the earth on a smooth stone, it can only wash it away»
