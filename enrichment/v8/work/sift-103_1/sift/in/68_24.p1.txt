#43 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbas tells of a father who always gave each rightful person a share, then five sons who planned to keep an unusually abundant harvest from Muslim poor; four agreed and the moderate brother objected. «فقال ابن عباس فوالله الذي لا إله إلا هو هذا أنور في كتاب الله من الشمس الضاحية» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:22, 68:24, 68:28, 68:29]
#44 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · They went quietly, consulting in secret to keep poor people from noticing and claiming a share of the harvested fruit. «والخفت الإخفاء والكتمان، والحرد المنع» [filed under 68:23, 68:24]
#45 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Since Biblical times the poor have been understood to possess a right to a share of the harvest, as 6:141 states. «the poor have a right to a share in the harvest of the fields and gardens owned by their more fortunate fellow-men»
#46 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The owners' resolve to deny the poor this right is the parable's second, specifically social, sin and connects with vv.10-13. «the second type of sin to which the above parable points: and inasmuch as it is a social sin, it connects with verses 10-13»
#47 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad places the surah probably third in revelation order and calls it one of the oldest Qur'anic portions. «In the chronological order of revelation, this surah most probably occupies the third place» [filed under 68:1-52]
#48 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Suyuti · reports · Suyuti holds that the surah followed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96; Asad says this conflicts with the best-authenticated reports that most of surah 74 came second. «Some authorities - among them Suyuti - incline to the view that it was revealed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96» [filed under 68:1-52]
#49 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Takhāfut means whispering to one another so no one else hears the plan. «والتخافت: أن يتحدث بعضهم إلى بعض في خفوت» [filed under 68:23, 68:24]
#50 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. «أن هذه الجنة كانت لرجل صالح، حدودا قومه وبلده فقيل إنه من أهل اليمن» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:19, 68:20, 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24, 68:25, 68:26, 68:27, 68:28, 68:29]
#51 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · While the owners slept, the garden was made barren; in the morning they found it harvested, recognized their loss, and the middle brother reminded them of their neglect of God and His bounty. «وترك الجنة صريماً جرداء خلاء. فلما أصبحوا، تنادوا ليغدوا على حرثهم» [filed under 68:19-29]
#52 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The early Meccan account omits personal names, dates, and other details except where they serve its lesson; it identifies the people only as from Ethiopia or Yemen. «لا يتعلق فيها بذكر الأشخاص والأزمنه إلا ما كان من جوهر القصة» [filed under 68:17-29]
#53 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The punishment verse points to the lesson of the garden story: tyrants face punishment in this world, while the hereafter's punishment is greater. «يلفت إلى مناط العبرة فيما تلت الآيات من أمر أصحاب الجنة» [filed under 68:17-33]
#54 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The whispered prohibition against a needy person entering repeats the parable’s portrayal of the owners’ stinginess already signaled in verse 18. «This is another portrayal of their stinginess.»
#55 STUDYQURAN · Most commentators · reports · Most commentators place the story in Madinah and identify its owners as three Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen who inherited their father’s fertile garden; their father left produce for the poor, but they planned to withhold it and harvest secretly. «Most commentators maintain that these verses were revealed in Madinah and that the story refers to three either Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen» [filed under 68:17-34]
#56 STUDYQURAN · holds · The brothers’ exclusion of the poor violated an ancient Near Eastern custom allowing the poor to glean and the Quranic command to give the harvest due on harvest day. «These verses represent a violation of ancient Near Eastern custom in which the poor are allowed to glean a share of the harvest» [filed under 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24]
#57 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Qalam is generally considered an early Makkan sūrah. «generally thought to be from the early Makkan period» [filed under 68:1-52]
#58 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qatādah · reports · These early commentators held that verses 1–16, 34–47, and 51–52 are Makkan, while 17–33 and 48–50 are Madinan. «the early commentators IbnʿAbbās and al-Qatādah (d. 117/726) maintained that vv. 1–16» [filed under 68:1-52]
#59 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 17–34 tell the parable of garden owners who lose their harvest because of miserliness. «the parable of the owners of the garden, who lost their harvest because of their miserliness» [filed under 68:17-34]
