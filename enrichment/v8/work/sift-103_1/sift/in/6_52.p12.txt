#466 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Al-Tabataba'i treats these similar stories as applications of the verses, not separate revelation occasions: the sura came down all at once, and the same proposal may have recurred. «إنما هي من قبيل ما نسميه تطبيقا بمعنى أنهم وجدوا مضامين بعض الآيات تقبل الانطباق على بعض القصص الواقعة» [filed under 6:52, 6:53]
#467 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Reports giving occasions of revelation for this Meccan surah often relate events merely associated with the verses; paraphrase, weak transmission, and fabrication prevent treating them as exclusive causes. «فلا اعتماد في أمثال هذه السورة من السور التي نزلت دفعة»
#468 TABATABAI d.1402 · عمر بن عبد الله بن المهاجر · reports · A report says the verse came after some people proposed that the Prophet expel the poor Companions of the Suffa. «عن عمر بن عبد الله بن المهاجر أن الآية نزلت في اقتراح بعض الناس»
#469 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · He rejects that as a specific occasion because the surah was revealed all at once in Mecca, before the Hijra. «ويضعفه ما تقدم في نظيره أن السورة إنما نزلت دفعة وفي مكة قبل الهجرة»
#470 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Reports assigning preceding verses to enemies of the Prophet’s family are applications or inner interpretations, not occasions of revelation, given the surah’s Meccan, single-period descent. «والظاهر أنها جميعا من قبيل الجري والتطبيق أو الأخذ بباطن المعنى» [filed under 6:52, 6:54]
#471 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · God has no bodily face or material direction; “face” is what something faces others with, so it can metaphorically denote the direction or aspect by which it is encountered. «لكن وجه الشيء لما كان ما يستقبل به غيره كانت الجهة بمعنى الناحية»
#472 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Righteous works can be a face oriented toward God, while evil works face Satan; divine attributes and names are also ways creation encounters God. «وبهذه العناية تصير الأعمال الصالحة وجها لله تعالى كما أن الأعمال الطالحة وجه للشيطان»
#473 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The phrase “possessor of majesty and honor” grammatically describes the face, not the Lord, in the verse about God's enduring face. «فإن ظاهر الآية أن قوله { ذو الجلال والإكرام } نعت للوجه دون الرب»
#474 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Anything related to divine nearness—God's names, attributes, religion, good deeds, prophets, angels, martyrs, and forgiven believers—is a face of God. «وكذا كل من يحل في ساحة قربه كالأنبياء والملائكة والشهداء وكل مغفور له من المؤمنين وجه له تعالى»
#475 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · These near-to-God realities endure by God's permanence; “everything perishes except His face” identifies them as remaining in His secure presence. «فدل الحصر الذي في الآية على أن ذلك كله وجه لله سبحانه»
#476 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · What worshippers seek from God is His face, including divine favor, mercy, satisfaction, and the means of nearness to Him. «فكل ذلك وجهه تعالى لأن صفات فعله تعالى كالرحمة والمرضاة والفضل ونحو ذلك من وجهه»
#477 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · “Account” originally denotes numerical reckoning and extends to measuring deeds for recompense; because reward is God's, the reckoning is His responsibility. «فالحساب على الله تعالى أي في عهدته وكفايته»
#478 TABATABAI d.1402 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم · reports · A report calls those who deny divine decree and claim independent human will and power the Magians of this community. «قال فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم " ألا إن لكل أمه مجوسا»
#479 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Al-Tabataba'i explains the hadith as condemning those who deny God's relation to human acts and make humans independent creators, like Zoroastrian dualism. «وهو أن لإرادة الله سبحانه تعلقا ما بأعمال العباد»
#480 TABATABAI d.1402 · المعتزلة · reports · The Mu'tazilites reinterpret the hadith to target those who affirm decree, claiming they resemble Magians by assigning good and evil to a creator other than humans. «وقد أول المعتزلة وهم النافون للقدر الرواية بأن المراد بالقدرية المثبتون للقدر»
#481 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The manuscript readings that combine “no decree” with denial of human will contradict each other, so al-Tabataba'i attributes that combination to scribal corruption. «والقول بالقدر يلازم القول بنفي استقلاله بالمشيئة والقدرة»
#482 TABATABAI d.1402 · عبد الله بن مسعود · reports · Ibn Mas'ud reports that Quraysh leaders asked the Prophet to expel poor Muslims such as Suhayb, Ammar, Bilal, and Khabbab so they might follow him. «مر الملأ من قريش على النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وعنده صهيب وعمار وبلال وخباب» [filed under 6:51, 6:52]
#483 TABATABAI d.1402 · بعض المفسرين · reports · Some commentators say the people singled out are believers who affirm resurrection and are the poor believers mentioned in the next verse. «في المؤمنين القائلين بالحشر، وأنهم هم الذين عنوا في الآية التالية» [filed under 6:51, 6:52]
#484 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The context indicates that Meccan polytheists asked the Prophet to expel poor believers; this resembles elites in Noah's community demanding the same of him. «أن المشركين من قومه صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم اقترحوا عليه أن يطرد عن نفسه الضعفاء المؤمنين به» [filed under 6:52, 6:53]
#485 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · reports · Traditions place the revelation of this and the next verse after Meccan pagan leaders offered to consider Islam if Muhammad dismissed former slaves and other low-status followers; he rejected their demand. «some of the pagan chieftains at Mecca expressed their willingness to consider accepting Islam on the condition»
#486 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad says the historical occasion is not a full explanation: the verse's wording concerns people who believe in God and seek His grace, even if they are not Muslims in the current sense. «while not being Muslims in the current sense of this word»
#487 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The enduring instruction applies to all Qur'an followers: do not repel anyone who believes in God, even with incomplete Qur'anic beliefs; instead explain the teaching patiently. «they are enjoined not to repulse anyone who believes in God - even though his beliefs may not fully answer»
#488 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The reciprocal accountability clause means people answer to God alone for beliefs or conduct that differ from Qur'anic teaching. «all are accountable to God alone»
#489 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The literal phrase means that repelling them would make the addressee one of the evildoers. «so that thou shouldst repulse them and thus be of the evildoers»
#490 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · In the four-sūrah sequence, al-An‘ām conclusively presents truth to Quraysh, while al-A‘rāf warns them. «Sūrah al-An‘ām conclusively communicates the truth to the Quraysh. Sūrah al-A‘rāf sounds a warning to them.» [filed under 6:1-165]
#491 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-Anfāl prepares believers for jihād and announces Quraysh's removal from custodianship of the House of God. «the divine decision is taken to depose the Quraysh from the custodianship of the House of God.» [filed under 6:1-165]
#492 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Al-Tawbah issues Quraysh an ultimatum and gives the Hypocrites a final warning. «an ultimatum is sounded to the Quraysh and the Hypocrites are given a final warning.» [filed under 6:1-165]
#493 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Qur’an Group 1 primarily addresses the People of the Book and only secondarily Quraysh; Group 2 reverses that order. «Group 1 of the Qur’ ān is directed to the People of the Book. If the Quraysh are addressed, it is only in a secondary way.» [filed under 6:1-165]
#494 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Group 2 argues mainly from human nature, intellect, and the world, whereas Group 1 uses matters the People of the Book acknowledged. «In Group 2, arguments are mostly drawn from the signs found in human nature and intellect and in the world around human beings.» [filed under 6:1-165]
#495 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · In Group 1 believers replace the People of the Book as religious leaders; in Group 2 they replace Quraysh as custodians of the House. «In Group 1, the People of the Book are deposed from religious leadership and their place has been given to the believers.» [filed under 6:1-165]
#496 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The sūrah invites Quraysh to monotheism, the hereafter, and Muhammad’s prophethood, warning them with the fate of nations that rejected messengers and using Abraham’s argument to identify his true followers. «They are invited to profess faith while arguments in favour of monotheism, the hereafter» [filed under 6:1-165]
#497 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This section tells Muhammad to leave sign-demanders, warn those fearing God and the hereafter, and preserve access for poor believers rather than appease arrogant elites. «those who were demanding signs and punishments. However, he should warn through this book those who fear God and the hereafter.» [filed under 6:51-55]
#498 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The believers seeking religious understanding are worthy of the Prophet’s attention, while the poor’s presence is itself a trial for wealthy opponents. «Nor should he turn away the poor among the believers merely to please these arrogant elements.» [filed under 6:51-55]
#499 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Prophet is not accountable for arrogant people’s refusal, and neither are they accountable for his duties in a way that could justify his turning believers away; doing so would be injustice. «Similarly, these people will not be held responsible on the Day of Judgement for the duties imposed on him»
#500 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Muhammad must not abandon sincere worshippers to appease wealthy, arrogant people who disparage his poor companions. «he should not waste any more time on the wretched among them who are inebriated by their status»
