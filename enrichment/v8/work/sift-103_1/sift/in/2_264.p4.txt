#176 RAZI-FULL d.606 · القفال · reports · Al-Qaffāl gives a third reading: the words return to the believers addressed by the command, shifting from direct address to third person as in Yūnus 10:22. «قال القفال رحمه الله : وفيه وجه ثالث»
#177 RAZI-FULL d.606 · المعتزلة · reports · On the Muʿtazilite account, God does not guide disbelievers by leading them away from reward and Paradise because of their own choices; another view glosses this as removal of faith. «على قول المعتزلة : إنه تعالى يضلهم عن الثواب»
#178 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The parable shows that reproachers, injurers, and hypocrites seem to have deeds, but at Resurrection their work vanishes because it was never done for God. «فإذا كان يوم القيامة اضمحل كله وبطل لأنه تبين أن تلك الأعمال ما كانت لله تعالى»
#179 RAZI-FULL d.606 · المعتزلة · reports · The Muʿtazilites instead say the charity first earned reward and reproach and injury later removed it, as rain removes dust from stone. «وأما المعتزلة فقالوا : إن المعنى أن تلك الصدقة أوجبت الأجر والثواب»
#180 RAZI-FULL d.606 · prefers · Al-Rāzī maps outward work to dust, the hypocrite or reproaching donor to the stone, and Judgment Day to rain; the rival reading maps reproach to rain. «الأول : ما ذكرنا أن العمل الظاهر كالتراب»
#181 RAZI-FULL d.606 · القفال · reports · Al-Qaffāl offers a seed analogy: sincere deeds grow like seed in fertile garden soil, while the reproacher's deed is seed stored on sterile stone and yields nothing when needed. «قال القفال رحمه الله تعالى ، وفيه احتمال آخر» [filed under 2:264, 2:265]
#182 RAZI-FULL d.606 · rejects · In reply to the objection that rain cleans stone, al-Rāzī says only the stated likeness matters; al-Qāḍī adds benefits of dust, but al-Rāzī relies on his first explanation. «والجواب أن وجه التشبيه ما ذكرناه»
#183 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · One interpretation is that reminder and harm erase the gift's excellence, not its reward: a show-off earns nothing like a disbeliever, while a sincere donor originally merits reward but loses the gift's excellence. «وقيل: المراد بالآية إبطال الفضل دون الثواب»
#184 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الكسائي · reports · Ṣald describes smooth stone that grows nothing; al-Kisa'i gives the verb and adjective forms, including a smooth bald forehead. «قال الكسائي: صلد يصلد صلدا بتحريك اللام فهو صلد بالإسكان»
#185 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · The author describes ṣald as smooth, non-growing stone and gives a smooth bald forehead as an example before citing al-Asmaʿi’s verse. «هو كل ما لا ينبت شيئا ومنه جبين أصلد»
#186 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Another view says only the reward after the donor begins reminding and harming is lost; earlier reward remains, but further multiplication stops, as charity otherwise grows like a mountain. «وقد قيل: إنما يبطل من ثواب صدقته من وقت منه وإيذائه»
#187 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · prefers · The author prefers the first interpretation of the preceding disagreement as the more apparent one. «والقول الأول أظهر والله أعلم»
#188 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الأخفش · reports · Al-Akhfash says ṣafwān is a collective with singular ṣafwāna; another reported view treats ṣafwān as singular, like 'stone.' «جمع واحده صفوانة قاله الأخفش»
#189 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الكسائي · reports · Al-Kisa'i calls ṣafwān singular and gives ṣafwān, ṣafī, and ṣafiyy as plurals; al-Mubarrad rejects ṣafī as the plural of ṣafā, like qafā/qafī. «وقال الكسائي: صفوان واحد وجمعه صفوان وصفي وصفي، وأنكره المبرد»
#190 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · سعيد بن المسيب · reports · Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib and al-Zuhri read ṣafwān with a fatḥa on the fāʾ, a dialectal form; Qutrub also records the form. «وقرأ سعيد بن المسيب والزهري «صفوان» بتحريك الفاء، وهي لغة»
#191 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · النحاس · reports · Al-Nahhas allows ṣafwān to be singular or plural but judges singular more likely because the verse says dust was on it; he rejects al-Kisa'i's plural analysis as linguistically unsound. «قال النحاس: صفوان وصفوان يجوز أن يكون جمعا ويجوز أن يكون واحدا، إلا أن الأولى به»
#192 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The poem mentioning the crow's day supports the weak analysis of krawān as a plural of karwān; ṣafī is likewise a plural of ṣafā, like ʿaṣā. «والضعيف في العربية كروان جمع كروان وصفي وصفي جمع صفا مثل عصا»
#193 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Wābil means heavy rain; the same root describes a severe taking in 73:16 and severe blows or punishment. «والوابل: المطر الشديد. وقد وبلت السماء تبل، والأرض موبولة»
#194 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · 'They cannot do anything' refers to the show-off, disbeliever, and donor who reminds recipients: they cannot benefit from the reward of their spending when they need it, because it was for other than God. «أي على الانتفاع بثواب شيء من إنفاقهم»
#195 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Spending is called their 'earnings' because they intended to acquire a return through it. «فعبر عن النفقة بالكسب لأنهم قصدوا بها الكسب»
#196 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الماوردي · reports · Al-Mawardi says the example concerns the show-off losing reward and the donor who reminds losing the excellence of the gift. «وقيل: ضرب هذا مثلا للمرائي في إبطال ثوابه، ولصاحب المن والأذى في إبطال فضله ذكره الماوردي»
#197 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · النقاش · reports · Al-Naqqash says aṣlad means hairless in the Hudhayl dialect. «قال النقاش: الأصلد الأجرد بلغة هذيل»
#198 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · 'Invalidating' means that the particular charity followed by reminders or harm is not accepted and loses reward; it does not invalidate unrelated good deeds, since sins do not annul good deeds as a general rule. «الصدقة التي يمن بها ويؤذي، لا غيرها»
#199 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The harmful donor is compared to a show-off who gives for reputation, like a disbeliever seeking praise; rain strips dust from a smooth rock and exposes its barren surface, just as these motives expose the intention and erase the gift. «مثل الله تعالى الذي يمن ويؤذي بصدقته بالذي ينفق ماله رئاء الناس لا لوجه الله تعالى»
#200 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Most scholars say God does not accept a donation when He knows its donor will later reproach or harm the recipient; another view says God gives the recording angel a sign not to record it. «قال جمهور العلماء في هذه الآية: إن الصدقة التي يعلم الله»
#201 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Arabic idiom calls a gift used for reproach a 'black hand,' an unasked gift a 'white hand,' and a gift prompted by a request a 'green hand.' «ولما يعطي عن غير مسألة: يد بيضاء»
#202 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · بعض البلغاء · reports · A rhetorician says that reminding someone of a favor forfeits gratitude, while admiring one's own deed destroys its reward. «وقال بعض البلغاء: من من بمعروفه»
#203 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن سيرين · reports · When Ibn Sirin heard a man enumerate favors to another, he told him to stop: beneficence has no good in it once it is counted aloud. «وسمع ابن سيرين رجلا يقول لرجل:»
#204 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · رسول الله · reports · A Prophetic report warns that reminding others of a favor erases gratitude and reward, then recites this verse; no grading is stated. «وروي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه قال:»
#205 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · مالك · reports · Malik disliked paying obligatory alms to relatives for fear of seeking their praise, reminding them, or receiving repayment that compromises sincerity; he preferred strangers and having another distribute it when the ruler is unjust. «كره مالك لهذه الآية أن يعطي الرجل صدقته الواجبة أقاربه لئلا يعتاض منهم الحمد والثناء»
#206 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · If voluntary secret charity loses its reward, it simply becomes as though never done; if obligatory alms loses reward, the giver faces a threat as though he failed to perform it. «وهذا بخلاف صدقة التطوع السر لأن ثوابها إذا حبط سلم من الوعيد وصار في حكم من لم يفعل»
#207 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Grammatically, the comparison's kāf can be accusative as an adjective for an omitted verbal noun or can function circumstantially. «الكاف في موضع نصب، أي إبطال «كالذي»»
#208 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Believers must avoid ostentation, reproach, and harm, which are here insinuated to be qualities of disbelievers. «بأن الرئاء والمن والأذى على الإنفاق من صفات الكفار»
#209 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Reproach and harm cancel the reward of charity, as does the hypocrite's public show of giving without seeking God's pleasure or the afterlife. «لا تحبطوا أجرها بكل واحد منهما.»
#210 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The comparison may make the donors like the show-off, or make their charity like his; the syntax allows a source or circumstantial comparison, and riya may mark purpose, state, or manner. «{ كالذي ينفق ماله رئاء الناس ولا يؤمن بالله واليوم الآخر } كإبطال المنافق الذي يرائي بإنفاقه»
#211 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · A smooth stone carries dust that a heavy rain strips away, leaving it bare; the image depicts spending that produces no benefit or reward. «{ كمثل صفوان } كمثل حجر أملس. { عليه تراب فأصابه وابل }»
#212 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The plural pronoun can refer to the generic donor by meaning or by number; a cited line likewise shifts from singular alladhi to plural hum. «{ لا يقدرون على شيء مما كسبوا } لا ينتفعون بما فعلوا رئاء ولا يجدون له ثوابا»
#213 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The kāf grammatically describes an omitted verbal noun: do not nullify charity with invalidation like that of the hypocrite who gives for display and lacks faith in God and the Last Day. «الكاف نصب صفة مصدر محذوف والتقدير بطلا مثل ابطال الذى»
#214 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The smooth rock covered with dust represents a hypocrite's useless spending; a downpour strips it bare, leaving no reward for what was earned. «مثله ونفقته التى لا ينتفع بها ألبته بحجر أملس عليه تراب»
#215 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The singular relative can denote a class or group, explaining why the text shifts to plural 'they cannot'; alternatively the kāf is circumstantial. «أراد بالذى ينفق الجنس أو الفريق الذى ينفق»
