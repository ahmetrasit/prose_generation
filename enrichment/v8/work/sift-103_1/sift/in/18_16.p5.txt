#214 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Iʿtizāl means physical or inward avoidance; ma may be relative or infinitival, and on either reading the exception can be connected, with an implied noun needed for one infinitival construction. «والاعتزال تجنب الشيء بالبدن أو بالقلب وكلا الأمرين محتمل هنا»
#215 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Their advice is to withdraw from the people and their worship, then worship God alone somewhere safe; its fulfillment confirms their sincerity and the preceding counsel. «وإذا اجتنبتم عنهم وعما يعبدون فأخلصوا له العبادة في موضع تتمكنون منه»
#216 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Min amrikum may alternatively be an implied circumstantial phrase modifying marfiqan. «وجوز أن يكون حالا من ( مرفقا ) فيتعلق بمحذوف»
#217 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Putting lakum first signals early that the following provision benefits them and builds anticipation; their statement reflects trust, hope, and certainty in God. «وتقديم ( لكم ) لما مر مرارا من الايذان من أول الأمر بكون المؤخر من منافعهم»
#218 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الفراء · reports · Al-Farra takes faʾwu as the answer to idha; another analysis makes it evidence of an implied answer: first separate inwardly, then physically if needed. «وإلى كون ( فأووا ( جواب إذ ذهب الفراء وقيل إنه دليل الجواب»
#219 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Both conditional analyses are challenged because idha without ma is not conditional; the claim that it can be conditional is weak or merely semantic shorthand. «واعترض كلا القولين بأن إذ بدون ما لا تكون للشرط»
#220 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Accordingly, idha is causal or temporal, attached either to an implied command or to the stated command; Abu al-Baqa attaches it to an implied “they said to one another,” which the author calls astonishing. «فهي هنا تعليلية أو ظرفية وتعلقها قيل بأووا محذوفا دل عليه المذكور»
#221 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتادة · reports · A report from Qatada gives Ibn Masud's codex the explanatory wording “what they worship besides God”; Harun reports “besides us” in another codex. «وفي مصحف ابن مسعود كما أخرج ابن جريج وابن أبي حاتم عن قتادة»
#222 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · في البحر · holds · The codex variants are explanatory glosses, not readings, because they conflict with the Uthmanic consonantal text. «وفي البحر أن المصحفين تفسير لا قراءة لمخالفته سواد الإمام»
#223 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Yanshur means to spread and expand; rabb is the guardian who guided them to faith; mercy covers both worlds; yuhayyiʾ means to make easy, and the omitted object is good or similar benefit. «يبسط لكم ويوسع عليكم ( ﴿ ربكم ﴾ مالك أمركم»
#224 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Min amrikum may attach to yuhayyiʾ as source or partitive, or, according to Ibn al-Anbari, mean God will replace their hard situation with a refuge. «على ما في بعض الحواشي متعلق بيهيء ومن الابتداء الغاية»
#225 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن الأنباري · reports · Ibn al-Anbari reads min as substitution: God prepares a refuge in place of their difficult circumstances, as in the cited verse and a poem wishing for Zamzam water. «وقال ابن الأنباري : للبدل والمعنى يهييء لكم بدلا عن أمركم الصعب»
#226 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · A further parsing makes mā negative with an unrestricted exception: God inserts the youths' monotheistic declaration parenthetically between 'when you withdraw' and 'take refuge in the cave'; al-Kashf explains the parenthesis by the following clause. «وجوز أن تكون ما نافية والاستثناء مفرغ والجملة إخبار من الله تعالى عن الفتية بالتوحيد»
#227 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says every prophet was sent as a young man and every scholar received knowledge while young, citing Abraham, Moses, and the cave youths. «أخرج الطبراني وابن المنذر عن ابن عباس قال : ما بعث الله تعالى نبيا»
#228 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · The possibilities that a contemporary prophet told the youths their refuge would be prepared, or that one of them was a prophet receiving revelation, are unsupported conjecture. «ولا يخفى أن ما ذكر مجرد احتمال من غير داع»
#229 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو جعفر · reports · Abu Jaʿfar, al-Aʿraj, Shayba, Humayd, Ibn Saʿdan, Nafiʿ, Ibn Amir, and others read marfiqan with mīm open and faʾ kasra; another reading reverses the vowels. «وقرأ أبو جعفر والأعرج وشيبة وحميد وابن سعدان ونافع وابن عامر»
#230 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjaj and Thaʿlab say both vowel patterns mean either something useful or the bodily joint. «ولا فرق بينه وبين ما هو بكسر الميم وفتح الفاء معنى على ما حكاه الزجاج وثعلب»
#231 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الفراء · reports · Al-Farra says he knows only kasra for a useful object or the elbow; al-Kisai says the elbow alone takes the open-mim form, which Abu Hatim disputes as meaning a place. «ونقل مكي عن الفراء أنه قال : لا أعرف في الأمر وفي اليد وفي كل شيء إلا كسر الميم»
#232 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Other authorities call the two forms dialect variants for a useful thing, while distinguishing the elbow's form; Hejaz speakers and other Arabs differ, and Muadh permits both vowels open. «وقيل : هما لغتان فيما يرتفق به وأما من اليد فبكسر الميم وفتح الفاء»
#233 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The verse supports migration to preserve religion and condemns remaining in an unbelieving land when remaining requires professing disbelief. «هذا واستدل بالآية على حسن الهجرة لسلامة الدين وقبح المقام في دار الكفر»
#234 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Leaving the people and their worship symbolizes leaving the company of passion and turning away from everything besides God; taking refuge in the cave means retreating into solitude with the Beloved. «وإذ اعتزلتموهم وما يعبدون إلا الله ) أي وإذ خرجتم عن صحبة أهل الهوى»
#235 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعض العارفين · reports · A gnostic says solitude from everything besides God brings union with Him, which comes only after withdrawal; he compares this to the Prophet's retreat in Ḥirāʾ before revelation. «قال بعض العارفين : العزلة عن غير الله تعالى توجب الوصلة بالله عز وجل»
#236 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The youths expect their retreat to bring God's mercy and ease; the light of faith ultimately leads to the mercy of the All-Merciful and success in their aim. «zira nûr-i imanın âkıbet rahmet-i Rahmân’a kavuşturacağı muhakkaktır»
#237 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Their prayer to receive mercy and right guidance was followed by God putting them to sleep, either to shield them from oppressors' distress or after the cave entrance was walled. «kulaklarının üzerine vurulup yani o zâlimler tarafından bir keder işitilmemek için yatırılıp veyahut mağaranın kapısına bir binâ kurdurulup senelerce uyutuldular»
#238 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · Al-Sadiq says they concealed belief and outwardly showed disbelief, so God rewarded them twice; other reports say they wore zunnar belts and attended festivals. «عن أبي بصير عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام قال إن أصحاب الكهف أسروا الإيمان وأظهروا الكفر» [filed under 18:16, 18:20]
#239 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The youths’ departure was migration from a society that prevented open monotheism and threatened stoning or forced conversion; their declaration at some point does not rule out taqiyya earlier. «ولا يرد عليه أن ظاهر قوله تعالى حكاية عنهم» [filed under 18:14, 18:16, 18:20]
#240 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Accounts differ on whether the ruler learned of their opposition before or after escape, whether they planned together or met by chance in the wilderness, and whether a shepherd or only his dog followed them. «وفي بعضها أنهم تواطؤوا على الخروج فخرجوا وفي بعضها أنهم خرجوا على غير معرفة من بعضهم لحال بعض» [filed under 18:10, 18:16]
#241 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Some reports say the ruler searched unsuccessfully, others found them asleep and walled up the cave until a shepherd broke in; still others say they met the king and died, or died before his arrival and were hidden, after which a mosque was built. «فأمر أن يبنى على باب الكهف بنيان ليحتبسوا فيموتوا جوعا وعطشا جزاء لعصيانهم» [filed under 18:16, 18:21]
#242 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Their coordinated flight and abandonment of family, property, and homeland showed opposition, but their first declaration can be understood as private resolve rather than public defiance; either way, the verses allow taqiyya earlier. «فقد كانوا على خطر عظيم لو ظهر عليهم القوم ولم ينته أمرهم إلا إلى أحد أمرين الرجم أو الدخول في ملة القوم» [filed under 18:14, 18:16, 18:20]
#243 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · A report says they left without knowing one another’s faith or arranging a meeting, exchanged pledges in the wilderness, then disclosed and found they shared one belief. «وفي تفسير العياشي أيضا عن أبي بكر الحضرمي عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام قال خرج أصحاب الكهف على غير معرفة ولا ميعاد»
#244 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says the youths left separately from an idolatrous city and cautiously questioned one another before making pledges, agreeing that God alone is Lord and seeking refuge in the cave. «فبلغ ذلك ابن عباس فأنشأ يحدث عنهم» [filed under 18:14, 18:16]
#245 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The cave story circulated in detailed reports from al-Qummī, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Ibn Isḥāq, Wahb ibn Munabbih, and al-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr, among others. «وردت قصة الكهف مفصلة كاملة في عدة روايات عن الصحابة والتابعين وأئمه أهل البيت» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#246 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The transmitted accounts disagree even on major features, while reports about dates, rulers, lineage, appearance, names, and the title أصحاب الرقيم diverge still more. «من الاختلاف في متونها بحيث لا تكاد تتفق في جهة بارزه من جهات القصة» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
