#174 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Other derivations say the wind squeezes clouds, or that muʿṣirāt are pregnant clouds or clouds compressed by winds; Ibn Sidah says some took muʿṣirāt to mean winds. «وقيل إنما قيل للريح إعصار لأنه يعصر السحاب»
#175 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن زيد · reports · Ibn Zayd and al-Suddi describe the iʿṣār as a violent, scorching wind; Ibn Abbas calls it a wind with intense samūm heat. «ابن زيد: الإعصار ريح عاصف وسموم شديدة وكذلك قال السدي»
#176 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عطية · reports · Ibn Atiyya says the whirlwind's intense heat or cold is a breath of Hell, citing Prophetic reports about delaying prayer in severe heat and the Fire's complaint. «قال ابن عطية: ويكون ذلك في شدة الحر ويكون في شدة البرد»
#177 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas and others explain the parable as a disbeliever's or hypocrite's livelihood orchard burning when its elderly owner and weak children can neither replace nor sustain it; the afterlife offers no second chance to restore deeds. «وروي عن ابن عباس وغيره: أن هذا مثل ضربه الله تعالى للكافرين والمنافقين»
#178 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · السدي · reports · Al-Suddi, preferred by al-Tabari, takes the garden as a second parable for ostentatious spending; Ibn Abbas also reports a likeness to deeds of show-offs that are void when most needed. «حكى الطبري عن السدي أن هذه الآية مثل آخر لنفقة الرياء،»
#179 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن زيد · reports · Ibn Zayd connects the garden directly to the prohibition against nullifying charity with reminders and harm in 2:264. «وحكي عن ابن زيد أنه قرأ قول الله تعالى:»
#180 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عطية · prefers · Ibn Atiyya says the connection to the preceding context is clearer than treating this as a separate parable for ostentatious spending; more broadly it covers any hypocrite or disbeliever whose apparent good deed vanishes when needed. «قال ابن عطية: وهذا أبين من الذي رجح الطبري، وليست هذه الآية»
#181 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · In the established Bukhari report, Ibn Abbas says the garden represents a wealthy person who obeys God, then follows Satan into sins until his life's work is burned away; Umar approves the explanation. «قال ابن عباس: ضربت مثلا لعمل»
#182 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · عمر بن الخطاب · reports · Umar asks the Companions what the garden verse means, urges them to answer rather than defer, and accepts Ibn Abbas's interpretation; another report says a person may end a righteous life with evil deeds when he most needs his work. «خرج البخاري عن عبيد بن عمير قال: قال عمر بن الخطاب»
#183 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن أبي مليكة · reports · Ibn Abi Mulayka reports Umar explaining that someone may do good until old age but commit evil at the end of life, when he most needs his good deeds. «وروى ابن أبي مليكة أن عمر تلا هذه الآية. وقال: هذا مثل»
#184 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid, Qatada, al-Rabi, and others support a broad reading that applies to every case covered by the wording. «وبنحو ذلك قال مجاهد وقتادة والربيع وغيرهم»
#185 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Palms and grapes are singled out because they are superior and especially prized among trees; 'all fruits' means every kind grows there. «وخص النخيل والأعناب بالذكر لشرفهما وفضلهما على سائر الشجر»
#186 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The past-tense 'old age struck him' is coordinated with the future 'that he should have'; alternative analyses link it to 'would wish,' treat it as circumstantial, or understand an implicit 'if he had a garden.' «عطف ماضيا على مستقبل وهو «تكون»»
#187 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The opening hamza is interrogative for repudiation; the garden is said to consist of palms and vines for their excellence and abundance, with all fruits indicating the other kinds or benefits too. «{ أيود أحدكم } الهمزة فيه للإنكار.»
#188 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The old age makes poverty and dependence more difficult, while weak offspring are too young to earn; the conjunction may be circumstantial or coordinated by sense. «{ وأصابه الكبر } أي كبر السن، فإن الفاقة والعالة في الشيخوخة أصعب»
#189 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · An i'sar is a violent whirlwind rising from earth toward the sky like a column; the garden's fiery destruction pictures the anguish of finding good deeds nullified by ostentation or harm. «والإعصار ريح عاصفة تنعكس من الأرض إلى السماء مستديرة كعمود»
#190 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The parable also evokes someone who rises spiritually toward the divine realm, then turns back to worldly illusion and sees the effort scattered like dust. «ثم نكص على عقبيه إلى عالم الزور والتفت إلى ما سوى الحق»
#191 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · God explains the signs so people will think and draw a lesson from them. «أي تتفكرون فيها فتعتبرون بها.»
#192 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The interrogative hamza expresses denial of the prospect of such a loss. «فى ﴿ أيود أحدكم ﴾ للانكار أن تكون له»
#193 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The garden is described with dates and grapes because they are the noblest, most beneficial trees; all fruits are then added by generalization. «لما كانا أكرم الشجر وأكثرها منافع خصهما بالذكر»
#194 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The conjunction before old age is circumstantial, as is the clause about weak offspring; the garden is the aged owner's livelihood and theirs. «أولاد صغار للحال أيضا والجملة فى موضع الحال من الهاء فى أصابه»
#195 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · An iʿṣār is a whirlwind that rotates over the ground and rises skyward like a column; fire is raised by the ظرف because the ظرف functions as an adjective of the whirlwind. «ريح تستدير فى الأرض ثم تسطع نحوالسماء كالعمود»
#196 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · The garden's destruction depicts ostentatious good deeds found null at resurrection, causing the anguish of an elderly person whose weak children depend on the ruined garden. «فيتحسر عند ذلك حسرة من كانت له جنة جامعة للثمار»
#197 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · As with the preceding explanation of monotheism and religion, the signs are clarified to prompt reflection and awaken people. «كهذا البيان الذى بين فيما تقدم»
#198 QASHANI d.736 · holds · The garden depicts any good deed, including charity, done for God's pleasure when the ego later emerges; conflicting ego motions become a whirlwind seized by Satan for whispering. «تمثيل لحال من عمل صالحا إنفاقا كان أو غيره متقربا به إلى الله»
#199 QASHANI d.736 · holds · Self-regard or ostentation is like fire that consumes the deed precisely when it is most needed. «فكان ذلك النفث نارا أحرقت عملها أحوج ما يكون إليه»
#200 QASHANI d.736 · أمير المؤمنين علي عليه السلام · reports · ʿAlī asks God to forgive acts of devotion whose intentions his heart later contradicted. «كما قال أمير المؤمنين علي عليه السلام: { اللهم اغفر لي ما تقربت به إليك»
#201 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · نخيل is a collective or broken plural like نخل; al-Rāghib derives it from the trees’ selection and notes the date palm’s distinctive pollination and growth. «النخيل: اسم جمع أو جمع تكسير، كنخل اسم الجنس،»
#202 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · عنب is a collective name for grapes, with عنبة as its singular; أعناب is its plural and عنباء is another extended form. «العنب: ثمر الكرم، وهو اسم جنس، واحده عنبة،»
#203 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj describes إعصار as a powerful whirlwind that raises dust toward the sky; another view identifies it as a deadly hot wind that compresses clouds. «الإعصار: ريح شديدة ترتفع فيرتفع معها غبار إلى السماء يسميها العامة الزوبعة، قاله الزجاج،»
#204 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · احتراق is intransitive, while أحرقت is transitive; حرق ناب can describe a man’s tooth grinding in anger, with a cited line read using either nominative or accusative ناب. «الاحتراق: معروف وفعله لا يتعدى، ومتعديه رباعي،»
#205 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · السدي · reports · He takes the final garden parable as another image of the show-off. «فقال السدي: هذا مثل آخر للمرائي»
#206 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن زيد · reports · He reads it as a parable for someone who reminds a recipient of charity. «وقال ابن زيد: هو مثل للمان في الصدقة»
#207 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · مجاهد وقتادة والربيع وغيرهم · reports · They apply the parable to someone who falls short in obedience. «وقال مجاهد، وقتادة، والربيع، وغيرهم: للمفرط في الطاعة»
#208 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن جريج · reports · He applies it to a person given youth and wealth who fails to act until both are taken away. «وقال ابن جريج: لمن أعطي الشباب والمال، فلم يعمل حتى سلبا»
#209 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · ابن عباس · reports · He compares many acts of obedience ending in a final sin to a garden ruined just when its elderly owner most needs it; a similar report is related from Umar. «وقال ابن عباس: لمن عمل أنواع الطاعات كجنة فيها من كل الثمرات»
#210 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الحسن · reports · Hasan says few understand this image: an old, weakened person with many children loses his garden when most dependent on it, just as people most need their deeds after worldly life ends. «وقال الحسن: هذا مثل قل والله من يعقله»
#211 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The interrogative hamza conveys denial and improbability; أحد means any one of you distributively, not an item restricted by a negative construction. «والمعنى على التبعيد والنفي، أي: ما يود أحد ذلك»
#212 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الحسن · reports · He reads جنات in the plural. «وقرأ الحسن: جنات، بالجمع»
#213 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · Dates and grapes are singled out as the most honored and useful trees; the Qur'an names date palms while naming grapes by their fruit because every part of the palm is useful. «لما كان النخيل والأعناب أكرم الشجر وأكثرها منافع، خصا بالذكر»
#214 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · He analyzes the clause as a subject and predicate, then discusses competing parses for min. «وهذه الجملة مركبة من مبتدأ وخبر»
#215 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الأخفش · reports · He treats min in من كل الثمرات as extra, yielding له فيها كل الثمرات, with a generalizing expression rather than literal totality. «فعلى مذهب الأخفش: من، زائدة، التقدير: له فيها كل الثمرات»
