#135 TABRISI d.548 · holds · رئاء الناس is either a circumstantial expression describing the spender or a purpose for the spending. «رئاء الناس مصدر وضع موضع الحال من الضمير في ينفق»
#136 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The clause about dust describes the smooth stone; صلدا is a circumstantial state, and the inability clause also functions circumstantially. «عليه تراب جملة في موضع جر بكونه صفة صفوان»
#137 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The plural verb they cannot refers to the generic class denoted by الذي, not to the singular wording. «والواو عائد إلى معنى الذي لأنه جنس لا إلى لفظه»
#138 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The likeness concerns both the boasting donor and hypocrite: when they perform a deed outside its commanded form, they earn no reward, which is what invalidating means. «فإنهما إذا فعلا الفعل على غير الوجه المأمور به»
#139 TABRISI d.548 · reports · The phrase about reminders can mean boasting to the recipient or reminding God; injury is harm to the recipient. «أي بالمنة على السائل وقيل بالمنة على الله»
#140 TABRISI d.548 · reports · A further interpretation is that God does not guide unbelievers to Paradise through their works, or does not grant them the extra care and success he gives believers. «لا يعطيهم ما يعطي المؤمنين من زيادة الألطاف والتوفيق»
#141 TABRISI d.548 · holds · Riya' derives from seeing and describes giving so others see it. صفوان is a smooth stone; al-Kisa'i gives its plural as صفي, while al-Mubarrad rejects that and uses صفا. «وجمع في رئاء الناس بين همزتين»
#142 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The root of turab/dust relates to soil and poverty; ذا متربة is one seated in dust from need, and ترب can also mean a peer of the same age. «مسكينا ذا متربة [البلد: 16] لأنه قعد على التراب للفقر»
#143 TABRISI d.548 · holds · The word at-taraib names chest bones; al-wabil is heavy rain, while the root of wabāl conveys severity and bad consequence. «والوابل المطر الشديد الوقع»
#144 TABRISI d.548 · reports · The quoted, unnamed poet uses صلد of hard smooth rock isolated from good; صلد also describes sterile ground, a miser, an unproductive flint, and things resistant to flow. «ولا بصفا صلد عن الخير معزل»
#145 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Do not nullify charity means do not nullify its reward, as in the case of a hypocritical show-off who does not believe in God. «لا تبطلوا ثوابها كما تبطل ثواب صدقة المرائي الذي لا يؤمن بالله»
#146 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن قتيبة · reports · Safwan is smooth stone, wabil is heavy rain, and sald means smooth; al-Zajjaj also calls it smooth stone, while Tha'lab glosses sald as bare or clean. «قال ابن قتيبة الصفوان الحجر والوابل أشد المطر والصلد الأملس»
#147 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس وقتادة · reports · “It left it bare” means the stone had nothing on it afterward. «وروي عن ابن عباس وقتادة ﴿ فتركه صلدا ﴾ قالا ليس عليه شيء»
#148 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · The stone-and-rain image represents a show-off whose charity yields no reward on Judgment Day. «وهذا مثل ضربه الله تعالى للمرائي بنفقته لا يقدر يوم القيامة على ثواب شيء»
#149 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أصحابنا · rejects · Al-Razi's school reads لا تبطلوا as a command not to perform charity in a null form: if the original motive was other than God, no valid reward ever arose to be removed later. «بل المراد به أن يأتي بهذا العمل باطلا»
#150 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أصحابنا · holds · If old and new merits do not conflict, the new event cannot erase the old; if they do conflict, the new event has no priority, and prevention is easier than reversal. «إن لم يكن بينهما منافاة لم يلزم من طريان الطارىء زوال النافي»
#151 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أصحابنا · holds · A later event cannot erase what existed only in the past, what exists now without contradiction, or what has not yet come into existence. «في الوجود في الماضي وهو محال»
#152 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أصحابنا · holds · Explaining the prior merit's disappearance by the arrival of the later event is circular, since the later event requires the prior resistance already to have disappeared. «فلو جعلنا زوال النافي معللا بطريان الطارىء لزم الدور وهو محال»
#153 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أبو هاشم · reports · Abu Hashim's mutual-balancing account has each event erase some of the other; if both resulting absences occur, the causes must simultaneously exist and not exist, an impossibility. «والأول هو الموازنة وهو قول أبي هاشم وهو باطل»
#154 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أبو علي الجبائي · rejects · Abu Ali al-Jubba'i says the later punishment alone removes prior reward; al-Razi objects that the earlier good then provides no benefit or protection, contrary to 99:7 and justice. «وأما الثاني : وهو قول أبي علي الجبائي فهو أيضا باطل»
#155 RAZI-FULL d.606 · القاضي · reports · Al-Qadi says the prohibition clarifies that taunting and harm cancel the reward, not the already completed act of charity; the verse compares this cancellation with a clearer case. «فالمراد إبطال أجرها وثوابها»
#156 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The first parable compares the taunting donor to a show-off who is also an unbeliever, whose reward's failure is clearer than the donor's. «لأن بطلان أجر نفقة هذا المرائي الكافر أظهر»
#157 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The smooth stone is the unbeliever, the thin soil is the apparent donation, and the downpour is unbelief or taunting that washes away the deed's reward. «فالكافر كالصفوان ، والتراب مثل ذلك الإنفاق»
#158 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الجبائي · reports · Al-Jubba'i argues that reward and punishment cannot both be deserved because each is an eternal pure benefit or harm; otherwise the same outcome would be both just punishment and unjust denial of reward. «فلو استحق ثواب طاعته وعقاب معصيته لوجب أن يستحق النقيضين»
#159 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · If a minor sin cancels only some equal parts of reward, selecting those parts without a distinguishing cause is arbitrary; if it cancels all reward, that is rejected by agreement, so minor sins cancel none. «فالصغيرة الطارئة إذا انصرف تأثيرها إلى بعض تلك الاستحقاقات دون البعض مع استواء الكل»
#160 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · If a major punishment exceeds prior reward, one part cannot be singled out to cancel it without cause; if every part independently cancels it, multiple independent causes produce the same effect, which is impossible. «والقسم الثاني باطل ، لأنه حينئذ يجتمع على إبطال الجزء الواحد من الثواب»
#161 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Good and blameworthy consequences can coexist without erasing each other: a servant who saves his master from an enemy deserves praise but also blame for exposing the master's property to theft. «فاشتغل العبد بمحاربة ذلك العدو وقتله فذلك الفعل من العبد يستوجب استحقاقه للمدح»
#162 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · A later act cannot alter the causal force of an earlier act in the past; absent such an effect, the earlier entitlement remains, and the past is stronger than a later event. «لأن ذلك الفعل إنما يكون موجودا في الزمان الماضي»
#163 RAZI-FULL d.606 · المعتزلة · reports · The Muʿtazilites read the smooth stone and rain as charity that first earned reward, then lost it through reproach and injury. «فهذا يشهد لتأويلهم ، لأنه تعالى جعل»
#164 RAZI-FULL d.606 · prefers · Al-Rāzī reads the ostentatious unbeliever as a deed invalid from inception; the dust is the outward charitable act, only apparently attached to the smooth stone, like charity with corrupt intent. «لا نسلم أن المشبه بوقوع الغبار على الصفوان»
#165 RAZI-FULL d.606 · المعتزلة · rejects · The Muʿtazilites claim one grave sin may outweigh decades of sincere obedience because God's immense gifts make disobedience exceptionally grave; al-Rāzī rejects this as irrationally unjust. «قال الجبائي : إنه لا يمتنع أن تكون الكبيرة الواحدة أعظم من كل طاعة»
#166 RAZI-FULL d.606 · rejects · Al-Rāzī argues that one hour of faith can erase seventy years of unbelief, so reason likewise resists saying one hour of sin erases seventy years of faith. «إيمان ساعة يهدم كفر سبعين سنة»
#167 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The command may prohibit charity that was invalid from the outset through ostentation, which does not support the Muʿtazilite doctrine of later cancellation. «وذلك أن ينوي بالصدقة الرياء والسمعة»
#168 RAZI-FULL d.606 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās says reproach is toward God for the gift and injury toward the petitioner; al-Rāzī finds this possible but says the other view is clearer. «قال ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما : لا تبطلوا صدقاتكم بالمن على الله»
#169 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الباقون · prefers · The other authorities take reproach and injury as directed toward the poor recipient; al-Rāzī considers this the clearer reading. «وقال الباقون : بالمن على الفقير»
#170 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The first parsing makes the comparison an ellipsis for invalidating charity like the ostentatious person's; both acts lack God's pleasure, supporting invalidity from the outset. «قولان الأول : أنه متعلق بمحذوف والتقدير»
#171 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · A second parsing makes the comparison an accusative of state: donors must not invalidate charity while resembling someone who gives to be seen. «والقول الثاني : أن يكون الكاف في محل النصب على الحال»
#172 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Riyāʾ is the verbal noun for showing one's deed to another person, with forms compared to other Arabic verbal nouns. «الرياء مصدر ، كالمراءاة يقال : راأيته رياء ومراءاة»
#173 RAZI-FULL d.606 · أبو عبيد · reports · Abū ʿUbayd transmits al-Aṣmaʿī's view that ṣafwān, ṣafā, and ṣafwā are equivalent names for a smooth stone; al-Rāzī also defines wابل and صلد as heavy rain and barren, smooth stone. «وحكى أبو عبيد عن الأصمعي أن الصفوان والصفا والصفوا واحد»
#174 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · One reading of the pronoun says no one can recover the seed once the dust washes away, supporting al-Qaffāl's interpretation that the deed cannot benefit its giver. «أحدهما : أنه عائد إلى معلوم غير مذكور»
#175 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · A second reading refers the pronoun to the ostentatious spender, with the generic expression functioning as a general class. «والثاني : أنه عائد إلى قوله { كالذي ينفق ماله }»
