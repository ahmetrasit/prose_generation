#42 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · A sound report says both combatants who meet with swords are in Hell, including the victim because he was eager to kill his opponent. «إذا التقى المسلمان بسيفيهما فالقاتل والمقتول في النار» [filed under 68:17-19]
#43 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Qurtubi also points to the discussion under the verse about those who do not persist in wrongdoing. «ولم يصروا على ما فعلوا» [filed under 68:17-19, 3:135]
#44 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · They did not say God willing; this is called exception because it excludes a condition, or because willing harvest and not harvesting until God wills are equivalent formulations. «{ ولا يستثنون } ولا يقولون إن شاء الله، وإنما سماه استثناء لما فيه من الإخراج»
#45 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · Alternatively, they failed to reserve the poor’s share, as their father had done. «أو { ولا يستثنون } حصة المساكين كما كان يخرج أبوهم»
#46 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · They did not say “if God wills”; this is called an exception although formally it is a condition, since both expressions mean that the act occurs only if God wills. «ولا يقولون إن شاء الله وسمى استثناء وإن كان شرطا صورة»
#47 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · One account places the garden in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ; its owner paid God's due, but his sons withheld it after inheriting and the garden was destroyed. «كانت بأرض اليمن بالقرب منهم قريبا من صنعاء لرجل كان يؤدي حق الله منها،» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#48 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · Another account locates it at Ṣurawān near Ṣanʿāʾ after Jesus; the owner left poor neighbors dates and grapes missed by harvesters, but his sons decided to stop sharing because they had dependents. «وكان صاحبها ينزل للمساكنين ما أخطأه المنجل وما في أسفل الأكراس» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#49 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · holds · The sons swore to harvest early in darkness and secretly, keeping the poor from their customary share; the oath's response predicts that they would cut the crop. «فحلفوا ليصرمنها مصبحين في السدف خفية من المساكين،» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#50 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · مجاهد · reports · Mujāhid explains “they made no exception” as not saying إن شاء الله; al-Zamakhsharī follows him. «وقال مجاهد: معناه: لا يقولون إن شاء الله، بل عزموا على ذلك»
#51 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhsharī explains why saying “if God wills” is called an exception: it has the practical force of not doing something unless God wills. «لأنه يؤدي مؤدى الاستثناء من حيث إن معنى قولك: لأخرجن إن شاء الله،»
#52 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · prefers · ولا يستثنون begins a new sentence; a circumstantial parse is weak because negated present verbs do not take an introductory wāw, and no hidden topic is needed. «هذه مستأنفة. ويضعف كونها حالا»
#53 SAMIN-DURR d.756 · holds · لا يستثنون may mean they did not reconsider their resolve to withhold, or that they did not say إن شاء الله; calling it an exception is justified because the condition and exception mean the same. «لا يثنون عزمهم على الحرمان»
#54 IBNKATHIR-FULL d.774 · holds · The heirs swore to harvest at dawn so that no poor person or petitioner would learn of the crop and receive charity; they made no exception in their oath. «حلفوا فيما بينهم ليجذن ثمرها ليلا لئلا يعلم بهم فقير ولا سائل» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#55 BIQAI-FULL d.885 · holds · Not making an exception means they neither said inshallah nor used another phrase returning the matter to God's will; such an exception removes some of the oath's absolute force. «لا يطلبون ولا يوجدون ثنيا»
#56 DURR-FULL d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says they were people of Abyssinia whose father fed the needy from his garden; after his death, his sons swore to harvest early and give no poor person food. «عن ابن عباس في قوله» [filed under 68:17-24]
#57 DURR-FULL d.911 · قتادة · reports · Qatada says the garden belonged to an Israelite elder who saved his annual provisions and gave away the excess; his sons opposed charity and resolved to exclude the needy. «عن قتادة قال: كانت الجنة» [filed under 68:17-24]
#58 DURR-FULL d.911 · أبو صالح · reports · Abu Salih says their exception was the phrase Subhan Allah. «عن أبي صالح في قوله»
#59 BURSEVI d.1127 · الرغب · reports · Al-Rāghib defines exception as wording that removes part of a preceding general statement or removes a ruling from its stated form. «قال الرغب الاستثناء إيراد لفظ يقضتي رفع بعض»
#60 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · After their father died, sons feared his charitable practice would leave too little for their families, so they swore to harvest early in the remaining darkness. «فلما مات أبوهم قال بنوه إن فعلنا ما كان يفعل أبو ناضاق علينا الأمر» [filed under 68:17-18]
#61 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Ṣirām means cutting dates or fruit; their early-morning harvest was to begin before night darkness fully passed. «الصرام والصرم قطع ثمار النخيل» [filed under 68:17-18]
#62 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · ‘They did not make an exception’ means they did not say ‘if God wills’; the apparent sense may instead be that they did not reserve a portion for the poor. «أي لا يقولون إن شاء الله وتسميته استثناء مع إنه شرط» [filed under 68:17-18]
#63 BURSEVI d.1127 · أبو حيان · reports · Abū Ḥayyān reads the phrase as not turning back from their resolve to bar the poor. «وقال أبو حيان : ولا ينثنون عما عزموا عليه من منع المساكين» [filed under 68:17-18]
#64 BURSEVI d.1127 · تاج المصادر · reports · Tāj al-Maṣādir defines istithnāʾ as saying ‘if God wills’; its root suggests repetition because the exception is stated generally and then specifically. «قال في تاج المصادر الاسثناء إن شاء الله كفتن» [filed under 68:17-18]
#65 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · One reading is that they did not say inshaAllah; although that is technically a condition, its effect matches an exception. «ولا يقولون إن شاء الله تعالى وتسميته استثناء مع أنه شرط من حيث أن مؤداه مؤدى الإستثناء»
#66 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الإمام · reports · The Imam derives the literal sense of an exception from restraint or turning back: a condition prevents an oath from taking effect. «وقال الإمام أصل الإستثناء من الثني وهو الكف والرد في التقييد بالشرط رد لانعقاد ذلك اليمين»
#67 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Alternatively, they did not turn back from their plan to deprive the poor; the syntax apparently links the phrase to their swearing. «وقيل أي ولا يثنون عما هموا به من منع المساكين»
#68 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Using the present tense instead of the expected past evokes their failure to make an exception and signals their error. «وفي العدول إلى المضارع نوع تعبير وتنبيه على مكان خطئهم»
#69 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · في الكشف · reports · Al-Kashf construes the clause as a circumstantial phrase: they were in the state of not making an exception. «وفي الكشف هو حال أي غير مستثنين»
#70 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · A further interpretation is that they excluded no share for the poor as their father had done; this may attach to their harvesting oath or its timing. «وقيل المعنى ولا يستثنون حصة المساكين كما كان يخرج أبوهم»
#71 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The owners could not control the garden's fate or even know with certainty that they themselves would live until morning. «gerek o bağın gerek kendilerinin» [filed under 68:17-18]
#72 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · They made no exception such as “if God wills,” if they reached morning safely, or if no accident befell them, because their overconfident resolve did not consider failing their vow or a reversal of fortune. «“inşaallah, Allah izin verirse” yahut “sağ sâlim sabaha çıkarsak, bir kazâ ve âfete uğramazsak” gibi bir şart»
#73 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · Alternatively, their failure to make an exception means they wanted to give nothing to anyone else and did not consider that anyone besides them might harvest the garden. «Yahut kendilerinden başka kimseye bir şey vermek istemiyorlar, onu kendilerinden başka birisinin kesebileceğini hesâba almıyorlardı»
#74 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn ʿAbbas tells of a father who always gave each rightful person a share, then five sons who planned to keep an unusually abundant harvest from Muslim poor; four agreed and the moderate brother objected. «فقال ابن عباس فوالله الذي لا إله إلا هو هذا أنور في كتاب الله من الشمس الضاحية» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:22, 68:24, 68:28, 68:29]
#75 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The garden owners agreed at night to harvest in the morning; not making an exception means they relied on themselves and outward causes without acknowledging God's will. «وكأنهم ائتمروا وتشاوروا ليلا فعزموا على الصرم صبيحة ليلتهم»
#76 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Another possible sense of not making an exception is that they set aside no share of the fruit for the poor. «أو المعنى قالوا وهم لا يعزلون نصيبا من ثمارهم للفقراء والمساكين»
#77 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · They planned without saying 'if God wills'; this is the parable's first lesson and links it to the question about their wealth in vv.14-15. «which points to the first lesson to be derived from this parable»
#78 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad places the surah probably third in revelation order and calls it one of the oldest Qur'anic portions. «In the chronological order of revelation, this surah most probably occupies the third place» [filed under 68:1-52]
#79 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Suyuti · reports · Suyuti holds that the surah followed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96; Asad says this conflicts with the best-authenticated reports that most of surah 74 came second. «Some authorities - among them Suyuti - incline to the view that it was revealed immediately after the first five verses of surah 96» [filed under 68:1-52]
#80 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The apparent story is that the garden owners planned to harvest everything at dawn and leave nothing; the author rejects the gloss that they merely omitted saying in shāʾ Allāh. «وظاهر النص، أن خطيئتهم التي أخذوا بها، هي التصميم على صرم جنتهم خفية» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
