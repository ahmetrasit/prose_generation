#219 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Across parallel oaths, dawn or day is contrasted with night; the ten nights vary this same contrast and uniquely quantify the night. «In all diesen Parallelstellen werden der Morgen bzw. der Tag mit der Nacht kontrastiert; wenn in 89:1.2 als Schwurgegenstände der Tagesanbruch und die „zehn Nächte“ genannt werden, so handelt es sich dabei um eine Variation desselben Kontrasts.» [filed under 89:1, 89:2]
#220 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Despite objections to Neuwirth's liturgical reading of cosmic oath series, a cultic interpretation of the ten nights is persuasive. «ist eine kultische Deutung der in V. 2 genannten „zehn Nächte“ doch einleuchtend» [filed under 89:1, 89:2]
#221 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The passage foregrounds cosmic duality—dawn and night, odd and even—which elsewhere aligns with moral good and evil and their eschatological recompense. «das insbesondere in Schwurpassagen aus Gruppe II prominente Motiv kosmischer Dualität (Tagesanbruch – Nacht, gerade – ungerade) im Vordergrund» [filed under 89:1, 89:2]
#222 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · A likely authentic poem by Umayya b. abī ṣ-Ṣalt supports a cultic or liturgical connotation for dawn, opening with a greeting to the Lord at each dawn. «ein sehr wahrscheinlich authentisches Gedicht Umayya b. abī ṣ-Ṣalts mit den Worten „Sei gegrüsst, Herr, bei jeder Morgendämmerung (faǧr) …“ beginnt»
#223 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Qur'anic oath formulas probably draw on a characteristic expression of pre-Islamic soothsayers, though this connection remains insufficiently studied. «Zu grundsätzlichen Hinweisen zu den koranischen Schwüren sowie ihrer wahrscheinlichen, aber bis dato noch nicht hinreichend untersuchten Anlehnung an ein charakteristisches Ausdrucksmittel altarabischer Seher» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#224 CORPUSCORANICUM · reports · For the oath type in this passage, the commentary directs readers to its discussion of 93:1–2. «zum hier vorliegenden Schwurtypus s. die Anmerkung zu 93:1.2» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#225 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The opening oath resembles those in earlier suras 93, 95, 100, and 103 and roughly contemporary suras 91 and 92. «Die Sure setzt mit einer Schwureinleitung ein, wie sie bereits in den früheren Suren 93, 95, 100 und 103 und den in etwa gleichzeitigen Suren 91 und 92 erscheint» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#226 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The oath's cosmic oppositions, day/night and odd/even, resemble 91 and 92, where such duality anticipates moral and afterlife oppositions. «Wie in Q 91, 92 und 93 ist in der Schwurpassage ein Tag-Nacht-Gegensatz zentral» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#227 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The preceding surah's signs establish God's power, wisdom, and providence, which entail a Day of accountability and reward; otherwise His mercy and wisdom would be contradicted. «when He possesses great power and wisdom» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4, 89:5]
#228 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Prophet's role is to warn through the signs in heaven and earth, even when people cannot see them; he cannot guide the blind. «His only responsibility is to warn them through these signs even if they are not able to see them. He cannot guide the blind.» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4, 89:5]
#229 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The oaths establish that God alone governs the world, brings things forth and removes them, grants respite, and acts within no rival's authority. «In His hands are the reins of everything. It is He Who brings forth everything and it is He Who makes them disappear.» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4, 89:5]
#230 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Fajr is the moment night darkness disappears and daylight appears at the horizon; unlike fajr, subh spans dawn and the time after sunrise, so it is qualified in oaths. «but also the time after sunrise.»
#231 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The dawn oath resembles oaths in al-Muddaththir: night recedes and morning appears as evidence that the Day of Judgment is a great event. «the night bears witness when it turns its back and the morning when it appears» [filed under 89:1, 89:4]
#232 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Dawn's daily transformation symbolizes the appointed arrival of Judgment: after death people will rise as if from sleep, just as night gives way to morning; a waking supplication alludes to this. «The time of fajr brings the message of a great change»
#233 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Oaths should be read as evidence for the surah's claim, not assumed to concern sacred objects; identify the claim and compare parallel Qur'anic passages. «meant to substantiate a claim.» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4, 89:5]
#234 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The first and last oaths are primary and contrast dawn with departing night; the middle oaths are secondary signs between them supporting the opening claim. «Further deliberation on the first four verses shows that the oaths» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#235 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Contrasting forces of nature coexist in harmony; only one wise, powerful sovereign can reconcile them, since competing deities would destroy the world. «these conflicting elements have great harmony and accord between them.» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4, 89:5]
#236 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Pairs reach completion with their counterparts; likewise this world is incomplete without an afterlife, where God will judge the conflict of good and evil and reward or punish people. «there should come a day of justice in which the Lord of the worlds, with full authority, control and knowledge hold people accountable and then reward His good people and punish those who had spread disorder» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4, 89:5]
#237 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Without a Day of Judgment, good and evil would be treated alike and creation would be purposeless; 68:35–36 makes the same objection to denying accountability. «are We to treat alike the obedient and the criminals?» [filed under 89:1, 89:4]
#238 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Daylight and night sustain material life, while ease and hardship train the moral and spiritual self in gratitude and patience, preparing people for the state described in 89:28. «they will be happy with their Lord» [filed under 89:1, 89:4]
#239 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The alternation of night and day shows that every created thing is governed by God, so even powerful people cannot escape His control. «It is He who releases and restrains it and it is He who sets it free.» [filed under 89:1, 89:4]
#240 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The oath's signs of release and restraint emphasize God's control, as illustrated by the possibility of perpetual night or day in 28:71–72. «no one, however much powerful he may be, should arrogantly reckon that he is beyond His control.» [filed under 89:1, 89:4]
#241 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Fajr means dawn breaking, morning brightness, or the time of daybreak. «fecr kelimesi masdar olarak “tan yerinin ağarması”, isim olarak belirttiğimiz anlamı yanında, “sabah aydınlığı, şafak vakti, tan yerinin ağarma zamanı” gibi anlamlara gelmektedir»
#242 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirler · reports · The unstated oath's answer is taken either to affirm resurrection, Judgment, punishment, and reward, or to be verse 14: God watches all, knows deeds, and recompenses them. «Müfessirlere göre Allah Teâlâ bu dört âyette kendi katında önemli olan varlıklara yemin ederek öldükten sonra dirilme, kıyamet, hesap, ceza ve mükâfatın gerçekleşeceğini vurgulamıştır» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]
#243 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Dawn's light and the waking of living things resemble resurrection; God swears by it to direct attention to what follows (cf. al-Takwir 81:18). «Tan yerinin ağarma zamanı ortalığın aydınlanmaya, canlıların da uyanmaya başlaması, bir çeşit yeniden dirilmeye benzediği için yüce Allah sabah aydınlığına yemin ederek aşağıda anlatılacak konulara dikkat çekmiştir»
#244 STUDYQURAN · some · reports · Some take the dawn and ten nights as the beginning of Muḥarram and its first ten nights. «Some see these verses as a reference to the break of dawn at the beginning of Muḥarram» [filed under 89:1, 89:2]
#245 STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Others identify the dawn with the Feast of Sacrifice and the ten nights with the first ten of Dhu’l-Ḥijjah. «Others see them as a reference to the break of dawn at the Feast of Sacrifice» [filed under 89:1, 89:2]
#246 STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Another interpretation points to dawn during Ramadan and its last ten nights, when the Quran was first revealed. «Others see them as a reference to the break of dawn during Ramadan and the last ten nights of Ramadan» [filed under 89:1, 89:2]
#247 STUDYQURAN · most commentators · reports · Most commentators classify al-Fajr as early Makkan, though some call it Madinan; it is probably tenth in revelation order. «though some maintain that it is Madinan»
#248 STUDYQURAN · holds · The surah's opening oaths lead into accounts of tyrants, human vacillation under fortune, rebuke of stinginess, Judgment Day, and the peaceful believing soul. «The sūrah begins with a series of oaths (vv. 1–4) followed by brief descriptions of the punishments» [filed under 89:1, 89:30]
