#267 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · rejects · Against exegetes who treat the promise of mercy and provision as the youths' own speech, Islahi says its certainty marks it as God's promise; a humble person could voice it only as prayer or yearning, as in verse 10. «constitutes the promise of divine help cannot be stated by a humble individual» [filed under 18:16, 18:10]
#268 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The story in 18:9–26 illustrates the coming of the Hour and resurrection, making these ideas easier for people to grasp. «kıyametin kopacağını ve âhirette ölülerin diriltileceğini insanların daha kolay kavrayabilmeleri için 9-26. âyetlerde ibret verici bir olaya» [filed under 18:9, 18:10]
#269 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Meccans questioned the Prophet after learning of the story from Jewish scholars in Medina; the story answered their test, and verse 9’s question reflects the event’s astonishing nature. «Medine’deki yahudi âlimlerinden Ashâb-ı Kehf hakkında bilgi alan Mekkeliler, olayı hayret verici buldular ve Hz. Peygamber’i imtihan etmek için olay hakkında ona sorular sordular.» [filed under 18:9, 18:10]
#270 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Kahf means a large, wide mountain cave; Companions of the Cave names people said to have slept there for years and then awakened. «Kehf “dağda bulunan büyük ve geniş mağara”; Ashâb-ı Kehf ise, “mağara arkadaşları” demek olup bir mağarada yıllarca uyutulduktan sonra tekrar uyandırıldıkları haber verilen kişiler» [filed under 18:9, 18:10]
#271 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Although reports connect the youths to Jesus’ religion, the Quran does not identify them and presents only selected details for instruction; the author summarizes their flight, long sleep, awakening, and the townspeople’s recognition of resurrection. «Kur’an-ı Kerîm, bu konuda açıklama yapmamış, ancak ibret alınması için bazı yönleriyle tasvir etmiştir. Olay kısaca şöyledir» [filed under 18:9, 18:10]
#272 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The Quran gives neither the event’s place and date nor the youths’ number; guesses about their number are baseless speculation, known only to God. «Kur’an-ı Kerîm olayın nerede ve ne zaman meydana geldiğine dair bilgi vermediği gibi, bu gençlerin sayıları hakkındaki iddiaları da, “karanlığa taş atma”» [filed under 18:9, 18:10]
#273 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · reports · Long-sleep-and-awakening stories also occur in Indian scriptures, the Talmud’s accounts of people sleeping 70 or 60 years, and Christianity’s Seven Sleepers of Ephesus. «Hint kutsal kitaplarında bir tek kişinin uzun süre uykuda kalması olayına rastlandığı gibi, yahudilerin kutsal kitabı olan Talmud’da da bir şahsın yetmiş yıl» [filed under 18:9, 18:10]
#274 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Replacing hamza with yāʾ changes the root from h-y-ʾ to h-w-y and may shift prepare toward make desirable or incline. «Hamza replaced by yāʾ: hayyiʾ → hayyī. Root changes from h-y-ʾ to h-w-y»
#275 QIRAAT-ER · holds · Eliding hamza after the dhāl of idh creates smooth liaison without semantic change. «the hamza of awā is elided after the dhāl of idh, producing a smooth liaison»
#276 STUDYQURAN · IK, Ṭs · reports · The youths, the Companions of the Cave, fled to God for the sake of their religion. «fleeing to God for the sake of their religion (IK, Ṭs)»
#277 STUDYQURAN · R, Ṭs, Z · reports · Their prayer sought guidance, knowledge, provision, patience, safety from enemies, and eventual deliverance. «God would grant them guidance and knowledge, provision, patience in their trial, security from their enemies, and eventual deliverance (R, Ṭs, Z)»
#278 STUDYQURAN · Prophet · reports · Their prayer is compared with a hadith prayer asking God to make the ends of all affairs virtuous. «as in the prayer of the Prophet reported in a ḥadīth, “O God, make virtuous the ends of all our affairs” (IK)»
#279 STUDYQURAN · Ṭs · reports · Some say they asked God eventually to grant them a safe way out of the cave. «praying that God would eventually grant them a safe exit from the cave (Ṭs)»
#280 STUDYQURAN · Aj · reports · The cave is where they withdrew after despairing of the world’s kindness and sought God’s protection alone. «having despaired of its kindness, and sought the protection of God alone (Aj)»
#281 STUDYQURAN · holds · A mystical interpretation makes the cave a symbol of the heart. «From a mystical perspective, the cave represents the heart.»
#282 STUDYQURAN · Th · reports · Some reports say the youths spent their cave years praying, praising God, and devoting themselves to Him. «the youths spent their time in the cave praying and praising God and devoting themselves fully to Him (Th)»
#283 STUDYQURAN · ST · reports · For some, asking for mercy meant asking God to preserve them through His remembrance. «their plea for mercy was a plea for God to preserve them in or through the remembrance of Him (ST)»
#284 STUDYQURAN · holds · Their prolonged life can symbolize remembrance that takes a person beyond ordinary time. «the extended life of the youths of the cave can be seen as symbolic of this experience.»
#285 STUDYQURAN · Th, Ṭs · reports · They sought refuge from King Daqyānūs, also called Daqyūs in another report. «seeking refuge from a king named Daqyānūs (Th, Ṭs) or Daqyūs (Q)»
#286 STUDYQURAN · Ṭs · reports · Daqyānūs may Arabize Decius, the king in the Christian account who tried to force idol worship. «which may be an Arabization of Decius, the name of the king in the Christian account, who was attempting to force his subjects to worship idols (Ṭs)»
#287 STUDYQURAN · al-Ṭabrisī · reports · Al-Ṭabrisī also reports a possibility from some Christian sources that the persecuting king was Zoroastrian. «Al-Ṭabrisī also mentions the possibility, as suggested in some Christian sources, that it was a Zoroastrian king trying to force his Christian subjects to follow his religion (Ṭs)»
#288 STUDYQURAN · Ṭs, Q · reports · Some accounts place the youths among the king’s courtiers; another says they were sons of local noble families. «the youths, who may have been among the king’s courtiers (Ṭs) or the sons of local noble families (Q)»
#289 STUDYQURAN · Th, Ṭs · reports · Some reports say the youths lived in Ephesus and hid their belief from fear of persecution. «Some report that the youths, who may have been among the king’s courtiers (Ṭs) or the sons of local noble families (Q), lived in Ephesus and were believers hiding their faith out of fear of persecution (Th, Ṭs)»
#290 STUDYQURAN · Th · reports · Others identify them as Christians preserving their faith and practices amid corruption, oppressive rule, and idolatry. «Others indicate that they were Christians attempting to maintain the beliefs and practices of Christianity at a time of great religious corruption, oppressive kings, and a turn toward idolatry (Th)»
#291 STUDYQURAN · Th · reports · After learning they were in a cave, the king sealed its entrance, expecting hunger and thirst to kill them. «the king eventually learned that the youths had sought refuge in a cave. He thus had the entrance to the cave sealed, thinking that they would die of hunger and thirst (Th)»
#292 STUDYQURAN · R, Z · reports · The mercy they requested from God’s Presence is explained as coming from His storehouse of Mercy. «from God’s storehouse of Mercy (R, Z)»
#293 STUDYQURAN · holds · Khiḍr’s paired gift of mercy and knowledge echoes the youths’ prayer for mercy from God’s Presence. «echoes the supplication of the youths of the cave in v. 10, where they pray to God seeking mercy from Thy Presence» [filed under 18:65, 18:10]
#294 STUDYQURAN · holds · Moses asks for sound judgment; the cave youths likewise ask God for sound judgment in their affairs. «in v. 10, the youths of the cave also pray to God to grant them sound judgment in their affairs» [filed under 18:66, 18:10]
