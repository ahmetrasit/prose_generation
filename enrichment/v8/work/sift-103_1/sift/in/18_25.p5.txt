#208 DURR-FULL d.911 · الضحاك · reports · When people asked whether the duration was days, months, or years, the verse specifying years and nine more was revealed. «عن الضحاك قال: لما نزلت هذه الآية {في كهفهم ثلاث مائة} قيل: يا رسول الله أياما أم شهورا أم سنين»
#209 DURR-FULL d.911 · مجاهد · reports · Mujahid explains 300 years and nine more as the count of their stay. «عن مجاهد في قوله: {ثلاث مائة سنين وازدادوا تسعا} يقول: عدد ما لبثوا»
#210 SUYUTI-LUBAB d.911 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas reports that after people asked whether the Cave-dwellers' three hundred years meant years or months, the verse adding nine was revealed; al-Dahhak also transmitted it. «وأخرج ابن مردويه أيضا عن ابن عباس قال أنزلت ﴿ ولبثوا في كهفهم ثلاث مائة ﴾ فقيل يا رسول الله سنين أو شهورا فأنزل الله ﴿ سنين وازدادوا تسعا ﴾»
#211 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The years clarify the youths' counted time in the cave while alive and asleep. The phrase is apposition, not a specification; the latter parse would imply 600 years under al-Khalīl or 900 under the usual plural minimum. «عطف بيان لثلاثمائة لا تمييز وإلا لكان أقل مدة لبثهم عند الخليل ستمائة سنة»
#212 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · On the tanwīn reading of مائة, the genitive plural follows; on the possessive reading, a plural stands for a singular, as in أعمالا. «هذا على قراءة مائة بالتنوين وأما على قراءة الإضافة فأقيم الجمع مقام المفرد»
#213 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The extra nine years convert the People of the Book's solar reckoning to the Arabs' lunar reckoning, with three years' difference per century; the source gives the lengths of solar and lunar years. «أي : تسع سنين وهو إشارة إلى أن ذلك الحساب على اعتقاد أهل الكتاب شمسي»
#214 BURSEVI d.1127 · الكاشفي · reports · He calculates 300 solar years as 309 lunar years plus two months and nine days. «قال الكاشفي : (وبتحقيق سيصدسال شمسي سيصدونه سال قمري ودوماه نوازده روز باشد)»
#215 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · مجاهد · reports · The statement that the youths stayed three hundred years and nine more is an independent sentence explaining the earlier summary that God kept them asleep for a number of years; Mujāhid is cited for this explanation, which several scholars choose. «وهي جملة مستأنفة مبينة كما قال مجاهد لما أجمل في قوله تعالى ( فضربنا على آذانهم في الكهف سنين عددا )»
#216 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعضهم · reports · The nine years may reconcile the solar-year count of the People of Scripture with the lunar-year count of Arabs, and this explanation is attributed by some to ʿAlī; al-Ālūsī details astronomical objections to the claimed nine-year difference. «فقيل هو الإشارة إلى أنها ثلثمائة بحساب أهل الكتاب واعتبار السنة الشمسية وثلثمائة وتسع بحساب العرب»
#217 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الخفاجي · reports · Al-Khafājī says the two counts are explicit in context: three hundred by the People of the Book's reckoning, then nine extra by the questioners' reckoning; the difference is approximate because the remaining fraction is below half a year. «وبحث فيه الخفاجي بأن وجه الدلالة فيه ظاهر لأن المعنى لبثوا ثلثمائة سنة»
#218 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الطيبي · reports · Al-Ṭībī proposes that the sleepers nearly awoke after three hundred years but something kept them sleeping nine additional years; al-Ālūsī objects that the verse would then need to say their sleep increased. «وقال الطيبي في توجيه العدول : إنه يمكن أن يقال : لعلهم لما استكملوا ثلثمائة»
#219 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Al-Ālūsī rejects the nine-year sleep-extension proposal because it would require an unexpressed meaning, that their sleep itself grew stronger for nine years. «وتعقب بأن هذا يقتضي أن يكون المراد وازدادوا نوما أي قوى نومهم في تسع سنين»
#220 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعضهم · reports · Some say the People of the Book disagreed over the sleepers' duration as they did their number; the verse resolves the disagreement, and the nine-year phrase confirms the answer like the exception in the account of Noah's thousand years. «وقال أيضا : يجوز أن يكون أهل الكتاب قد اختلفوا في مدة لبثهم كما اختلفوا في عدتهم»
#221 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · قتادة · reports · Another view says the sleepers briefly awoke and then returned to sleep, explaining the increase; al-Ālūsī considers this closer than the solar-versus-lunar reckoning explanation. «وقيل إنهم انتبهوا قليلا ثم ردوا إلى حالتهم الأولى فلذا ذكر الازدياد»
#222 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن عباس · reports · A view attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās treats the duration statement as the People of the Book's quoted speculation, not God's assertion; he argues that a true duration would not be followed by saying God knows best, and cites the prior reports as conjecture. «ونسب ذلك إلى ابن عباس فقد أخرج ابن أبي حاتم وابن مردويه عنه رضي الله تعالى عنه أنه قال»
#223 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Al-Ālūsī doubts this attribution: Ibn ʿAbbās accepted the seven-sleepers view even though it too is followed by God's knowing their number, so the same wording cannot distinguish rejection here. «فقد صح عنه القول بأن عدة أصحاب الكهف سبعة وثامنهم كلبهم مع أنه تعالى عقب القول بذلك بقوله سبحانه ( قل ربي أعلم بعدتهم )»
#224 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masʿūd's reading says that people said they remained in the cave, supporting the possibility that the duration is quoted speculation; al-Ālūsī still says the following divine statement does not by itself signal rejection. «نعم قرأ ابن مسعود ( قالوا لبثوا كهفهم ) وهو يقتضي أن يكون من كلام الخائضين»
#225 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الخفاجي · reports · On the quoted-speech interpretation, al-Khafājī assigns the pronoun in “they increased” to the People of the Book: some said three hundred, others added nine; al-Ālūsī rejects this as weak. «وقال الخفاجي : إن ضمير عليه لأهل الكتاب بخلافه على الأول ويظهر فيه وجه العدول»
#226 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعضهم · rejects · Al-Ālūsī rejects the suggestion that the nine is unspecified as to years, months, days, or hours: a following unqualified count takes the unit of the preceding explicit number. «وقيل إنه تعالى ما قال ( وازدادوا تسعا ) كانت التسع مبهمة لا يدري أنها سنون أم شهور»
#227 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The accusative “nine” is the one object of the verb “increased”; although Abū al-Baqāʾ says “increase” can take two objects, its derived form here takes one, as usage reported by al-Rāghib also supports. «ونصب ( تسعا ) على أنه مفعول ( ازدادوا ) وهو مما يتعدى إلى واحد»
#228 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو البقاء · reports · Abū al-Baqāʾ and Ibn al-Ḥājib parse “years” in the common reading as an accusative substitute for “three hundred”; al-Zamakhsharī instead calls it an explanatory apposition, which the Sea commentary rejects for Basran grammar. «فقال أبو البقاء وابن الحاجب : هو منصوب على البدلية من ( ثلثمائة )»
#229 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعضهم · reports · Some prefer explanatory apposition because substitution would seem to make the number itself unintended; a report says listeners asked whether the three hundred meant days, months, or years, after which “years” was revealed. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows either parsing. «وادعى بعضهم أنه أولى من البدلية لأنها تستلزم أن لا يكون العدد مقصودا»
#230 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الزجاج · reports · A parsing of “years” as a numeral specification is criticized because it seems to make the stay nine hundred years: the specification should count one item per hundred, and years are plural; al-Ālūsī replies that plural specification follows the underlying collective usage. «وقيل : على التمييز وتعقب بأنه يلزم عليه الشذوذ من وجهين»
#231 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj also allows “years” to modify “hundred” in the genitive, semantically describing the whole number as in an Antarah verse about forty-two milk-camels; the passage continues its grammatical defense in the next segment. «وجوز الزجاج كون ( سنين ) مجرورا على أنه نعت ( مائة ) وهو راجع في المعنى إلى جملة العدد»
#232 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو علي · reports · Abū ʿAlī explains the poetic construction by taking “milch-camel” collectively, allowing its plural adjective to describe the whole numbered group; a collective plural in singular form can specify a numeral, as in “twenty persons.” «وقال أبو علي : لا يمتنع أن يكون الشاعر اعتبر حلوبة جمعا وجعل سودا وصفا لها»
#233 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · حمزة والكسائي · reports · Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, and several others recite “three hundred years” with “years” genitive by annexation; the same objection about making the duration nine hundred would apply only if it were a numeral specification. «وقرأ حمزة والكسائي وطلحة ويحيى والأعمش والحسن وابن أبي ليلى وخلف»
#234 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The plural genitive is possible though uncommon: the plural ending may function like a replacement for the singular's deleted final consonant, and the Quranic reading itself suffices as Arabic evidence despite the lack of another attested example. «لكن قالوا : إن الجمع المذكور فيها قد أجرى مجرى العاري عن علامة الجمع»
#235 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبي · reports · Ubayy reads “three hundred year” with the singular genitive, the usual construction, as does the Ibn Masʿūd codex. «وقرأ أبي ( ثلثمائة سنة ) بالإضافة والإفراد كما هو الاستعمال الشائع»
#236 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الضحاك · reports · Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reads “three hundred years” with the nominative plural, understood as the predicate of an omitted “they are.” «وقرأ الضحاك ( ثلثمائة سنون ) بالتنوين ورفع سنون على أنه خبر مبتدأ محذوف»
#237 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الحسن وأبو عمرو · reports · Al-Ḥasan and Abū ʿAmr, in the narration of al-Luʾluʾī, read “nine” with an open tāʾ, a dialectal form. «وقرأ الحسن وأبو عمرو في رواية اللؤلؤي عنه ( تسعا ) بفتح التاء وهو لغة فيه»
#238 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · بعض أهل التأويل · rejects · Some allegorize the three hundred and nine years as twenty-five years by treating a Quranic year as a month; al-Ālūsī rejects this as baseless, contrary to Muslim consensus and weak in faith, insisting the possible outward duration should stand. «زعم بعض أهل التأويل أن مجموع ذلك خمس وعشرون سنة واعتبر السنة التي في الآية شهرا»
