#247 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · One cause is the story’s prominence among People of the Book: Quraysh learned it from them, and its Christian images and monuments spread it widely, so communities reshaped it to fit their beliefs. «أن القصة مما اعتنت به أهل الكتاب كما يستفاد من رواياتها أن قريشا تلقتها عنهم» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#248 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A second cause is Muslims’ broad collection of outside reports, especially through converts such as Wahb ibn Munabbih and Kaʿb al-Aḥbār; later transmitters treated such material as prophetic reports. «اختلط بهم قوم من علماء أهل الكتاب دخلوا في الإسلام كوهب بن منبه وكعب الأحبار» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#249 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾān selects key lessons that serve guidance instead of supplying a complete historical account; it is a book of guidance, not history. «فما كتاب الله بكتاب تاريخ وإنما هو كتاب هدى» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#250 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾānic narrative first shows the youths’ truth-bearing withdrawal and long sleep, then their awakening and exchange that exposes them, and finally their discovery and the mosque built over them. «أورد أولا شطرا من محاورتهم يشير إلى معنى قيامهم لله وثباتهم على كلمة الحق» [filed under 18:10, 18:14, 18:16, 18:19, 18:21]
#251 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾān omits names, ancestry, birth, upbringing, occupations, social standing, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors, and the dog’s identity because guidance does not depend on them; historians and early exegetes filled these gaps, producing divergent accounts. «إلى غير ذلك من الأمور التي لا يتوقف غرض الهداية على العلم بشيء منها كما يتوقف عليه غرض البحث التاريخي» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#252 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Seeing that remaining would force them to conceal truth or conform to idolatry, the youths chose to affirm God as Lord of heaven and earth, reject other gods as false, and withdraw to the cave trusting in divine mercy and guidance. «علموا أن سبيلهم أن يقوموا على التوحيد ورفض الشرك ثم اعتزال القوم» [filed under 18:14, 18:16]
#253 TABATABAI d.1402 · أهل الرواية والتاريخ · reports · Most historians date the story between Jesus and Muḥammad; it is absent from the two Testaments, though some Muslim reports say Quraysh heard it from Jews, while Christians preserved it extensively. «معظم أهل الرواية والتاريخ على أن القصة وقعت في الفترة بين النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وبين المسيح» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#254 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Christian accounts resemble Ibn Isḥāq’s report from Ibn ʿAbbās in some respects but differ from Muslims on the number of sleepers, whether they had a dog, and the length of their sleep. «أن المصادر السريانية تذكر عدد أصحاب الكهف ثمانية في حين يذكره المسلمون» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#255 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · After centuries as a ruined, forgotten site, Jordanian excavation uncovered Rujayb; the author considers its archaeological signs and some Muslim reports the strongest fit for the Qurʾānic cave. «وقامت عدة من الأمارات والشواهد الأثرية على كونه هو كهف أصحاب الكهف المذكورين في القرآن» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#256 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Amman stands on the site of Philadelphia, a major and beautiful city before Islam that remained under Roman control from the early second century until the Muslim conquest. «كانت هي وما والاها تحت استيلاء الروم منذ أوائل القرن الثاني الميلادي حتى فتح المسلمون الأرض المقدسة» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#257 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Other proposed locations include a cave on Mount Qāsiyūn near Damascus, one at Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and a cave near Nakhchivan. «والكهف الثالث كهف بجبل قاسيون بالقرب من الصالحية بدمشق الشام» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#258 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · No evidence identifies any proposed cave as the Qurʾānic one; the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with the story’s Roman setting and the limits of Roman power. «ولا شاهد يشهد على كون شيء من هذه الكهوف هو الكهف المذكور في القرآن الكريم» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#259 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · They most likely rose during an assembly enforcing idol worship, publicly declared monotheism, and fled under mortal threat; a report that six were the king's advisers supports this. «الأظهر هو الوجه الأول» [filed under 18:14, 18:16]
#260 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Public confession does not conflict with reports that they had concealed their faith: they could have practiced caution before suddenly declaring belief and leaving. «ولا ينافي ذلك ما سيأتي من الروايات أنهم كانوا يسرون إيمانهم ويعملون بالتقية» [filed under 18:14, 18:16]
#261 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Iʿtizāl is withdrawal, nashr is spreading, and mirfaq, with the cited vowelings, means gentle treatment or kind benefit. «الاعتزال والتعزل التنحي عن أمر، والنشر البسط، والمرفق بكسر الميم وفتح الفاء وبالعكس وبفتحهما المعاملة بلطف»
#262 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · After leaving the people and their gods, they advise hiding in the cave, confident God will extend mercy and provide ease; these are the same gifts they requested on entering. «وهذان اللذان تفرسوا بهما من نشر الرحمة وتهيئة المرفق هما اللذان سألوهما بعد دخول الكهف» [filed under 18:16, 18:10]
#263 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The exception in “what they worship except God” is disconnected: the passage does not imply idolaters worshipped God alongside idols, contrary to other interpretations. «والاستثناء في قوله { وما يعبدون إلا الله } استثناء منقطع»
#264 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Mirfaq means anything beneficial, concrete or abstract; here it is spiritual provision associated with abandoning the world and withdrawing into seclusion. «anything by which one benefits", whether concrete or abstract»
#265 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers passage answers an objection from the Prophet’s opponents and presents their real story without needless details. «their real life aside from needless details becomes evident» [filed under 18:9-26]
#266 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ trials parallel the Prophet and his companions’ preaching phase; perseverance brings God’s easing of hardship and provision, and He does not let His persevering servants lose their way. «God will ease out their hardships the way He did so for them» [filed under 18:9-26]
#267 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This overview is offered as sufficient to establish the sūrah’s coherence and central theme before its detailed exegesis. «This concise analysis of the meanings of the sūrah is sufficient to explain its coherence and central theme» [filed under 18:1-110]
#268 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers question was planted by the People of the Book and posed by Quraysh to harass and corner the Prophet, not to seek truth. «These irrelevant questions were obviously not meant to find out the real truth» [filed under 18:9-26]
#269 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Opponents planned to exploit either an inability to answer or an answer conflicting with their own beliefs. «if the Prophet (sws) was able to answer them and the answer was against their beliefs» [filed under 18:9-26]
#270 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an generally counsels ignoring such questions, but answers when useful; the truthful account both answers and trains the Prophet and companions in perseverance during comparable trials. «if some question is beneficial or its answer can be beneficial for education» [filed under 18:9-26]
#271 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an preserves the useful, true part of the cave story, correcting false accretions and restoring a major chapter in truth-seekers’ history. «a very important chapter in the history of the seekers of the truth was cleansed from all sorts of wrong details» [filed under 18:9-26]
#272 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The dwellers’ identities, date and ancestry cannot be known securely; beyond the Qur’anic account, claims are folk tales, and further inquiry is discouraged. «Who were the cave-dwellers, when did they inhabit the earth and whose ancestors were they are questions that cannot be answered with certainty» [filed under 18:9-26]
#273 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Islahi’s opinion is that the youths were ancestors of Christians, not Quraysh or Jews, and belonged to the early Christian period under Roman persecution. «in my opinion, they belonged to the initial period of the Christians when they were persecuted by the Romans» [filed under 18:9-26]
#274 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · He says they boldly preached monotheism and accountability, faced imminent stoning, then took refuge in a cave where God supplied extraordinary protection. «They fearlessly invited people to monotheism and warned them of the accountability in the hereafter» [filed under 18:9-26]
#275 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · After their circumstances became known, groups built memorials and claimed them; Christian retellings gradually reduced their story to miracles and unsupported details. «Gradually, this tradition became confined to some miraculous feats and baseless details about them» [filed under 18:9-26]
