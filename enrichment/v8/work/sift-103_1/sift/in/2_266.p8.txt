#338 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The destructive ʿiṣār is a violent hot wind, likened emphatically to fire or the simūm; it burns the garden's trees to dry wood and brings disaster at the moment of hope. «فأحرقت الجنة أي أشجارها أي صارت أعوادها يابسة»
#339 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · أيود is a warning and repudiating question, like the question about eating a brother's flesh; the unstated analogue is someone whose charity is followed by reminders and harm. «استفهام إنكار وتحذير كما في قوله»
#340 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · ابن عباس · reports · In the report of ʿUmar's question to the Companions, Ibn ʿAbbās interprets the garden as a wealthy person's good deeds, later consumed when Satan tempts him to sin near life's end; al-Bukhārī transmits the exchange. «روى البخاري أن عمر بن الخطاب سأل يوما أصحاب رسول الله»
#341 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Likewise signals that God clarifies signs through this kind of explanation, bringing abstract truths near through tangible images so people reflect on consequences and avoid heedlessness. «نحو ما في قوله تعالى»
#342 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The plural form 'helpers' hints that there are supporters in cases of wrongdoing; the excerpt begins mid-discussion, so its precise antecedent is not preserved here. «ومن هنا يظهر وجه إتيان الأنصار بصيغة الجمع فإن في مورد مطلق الظلم أنصارا» [filed under 2:261-274]
#343 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Withholding spending is a grave wrong that is not expiated or waived as a minor lapse; repentance concerning people's rights requires returning the right to its owner. «وهو ترك الإنفاق لا يقبل التكفير ولو كان من الصغائر لقبله فهو من الكبائر» [filed under 2:261-274]
#344 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The author says this wrong receives no intercession at the Resurrection, citing the question to the people of Saqar and their failure to feed the poor, followed by the denial that intercession helps them. «فما تنفعهم شفاعة الشافعين» [filed under 2:261-274]
#345 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Because such a wrongdoer is not approved by God and intercession is only for those whose religion He approves, the wording 'seeking God's pleasure' is significant. «إذ لا شفاعة إلا لمن ارتضى الله دينه» [filed under 2:261-274]
#346 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Refusing all aid to present, needy poor is a destructive major sin; 41:7 describes withholding zakat as associationism and disbelief in the afterlife, although that Meccan verse predates the formal zakat system. «وويل للمشركين الذين لا يؤتون الزكاة وهم بالآخرة هم كافرون» [filed under 2:261-274]
#347 TABATABAI d.1402 · الصادق عليه السلام · reports · Al-Qummī glosses ṣafwān as a large rock, wābil as rain, tall as nighttime dew on plants, and iʿṣār as winds. «والصفوان هي الصخرة الكبيرة التي تكون في المفازة» [filed under 2:264, 2:265, 2:266]
#348 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The connected themes suggest this passage was revealed as one unit: spending grows seven hundredfold or more, sincere giving bears fruit, ostentation and reminders or harm ruin reward, good wealth should be given, the poor detained in God's path are recipients, and the reward is great. «اتحادها في بيان أمر الانفاق» [filed under 2:261, 2:262, 2:263, 2:264, 2:265, 2:266, 2:267, 2:268, 2:269, 2:270, 2:271, 2:272, 2:273, 2:274]
#349 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The passage orders its teaching around purpose (giving for God, not people), manner (without reproach or injury), quality (good rather than bad wealth), recipient (the needy constrained in God's path), and reward. «وهو أن يكون لله لا للناس» [filed under 2:261, 2:262, 2:263, 2:264, 2:265, 2:266, 2:267, 2:268, 2:269, 2:270, 2:271, 2:272, 2:273, 2:274]
#350 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Wudd is love with an element of wishing; janna is dense tree growth that covers and shades the earth, so rivers flow beneath the garden rather than beneath the land itself. «الجنة الشجر الكثير الملتف كالبستان سميت بذلك لأنها تجن الأرض»
#351 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · This reading of garden accords with 23:50, which describes a settled elevation with flowing water, and with the repeated image of gardens whose water flows below them. «ولذلك قال تعالى في مثل الربوة وهي الأرض المعمورة»
#352 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Min in 'of palms and grapes' explains the garden's predominant crop rather than excluding other plants; that is why the verse adds that it contains every kind of fruit. «للتبيين، ويفيد معنى الغلبة دون الاستيعاب»
#353 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Old age together with weak children makes the garden an indispensable source of livelihood: a young owner, a childless elder, or an elder with strong children could each fall back on another means. «وقد جمع تعالى في المثل بين إصابة الكبر ووجود الذرية الضعفاء»
#354 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · When an elderly owner with frail children loses the garden to fire, neither he nor they can restore their strength, former resources, or the lost orchard, leaving them without a means or hope. «فلا صاحب الجنة يمكنه أن يعيد لنفسه الشباب والقوة أو الأيام الخالية»
#355 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · An iʿṣār is a column of dust twisting between sky and earth, like cloth twisted during wringing. «والاعصار الغبار الذي يلتف على نفسه بين السماء والأرض»
#356 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The ruined-garden parable depicts charity nullified by reminders and harm: a spoiled deed cannot be restored to soundness. «وهذا مثل ضربه الله للذين ينفقون أموالهم ثم يتبعونه منا وأذى»
#357 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Such corruption comes from inner loves of wealth and status, pride, conceit, and miserliness that block reflection and discernment; contemplation would expose the harm. «إنما تصدر من الناس ومعهم حالات نفسانية كحب المال والجاه والكبر»
#358 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · holds · The root of iʿṣār (a whirlwind with fire in 2:266) relates to pressing; the wind-sense is close to the root's sense of squeezing. «وتفسير الإعصار بالريح الشديدة، قريب، مع ملحظ دلالة مادته على»
#359 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · الجوهري · reports · Jawhari defines iʿṣār as a wind raising dust that rises like a column. «وقال الجوهرى: والإعصار ريح تهب تثير الغبار»
#360 BINTSHATI-IJAZ d.1419 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas, via a chain the author notes from Tabari, glosses iʿṣār as a wind carrying hot poison. «أسند عن ابن عباس، قال: ريح فيها سموم شديدة»
#361 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · Charity given for other purposes reduces wealth but earns no reward; at the afterlife, the donor is left empty-handed like an old man whose orchard burns just when he needs it. «tıpkı gençliğinde yaşlılık yılları için hazırladığı serveti yanıp kül olmuş ihtiyar gibi– elleri boş kalıverirler»
#362 STUDYQURAN · R · reports · R reads the garden as charity’s fruit and the fiery whirlwind as later boasting and insults that destroy it, leaving the giver with nothing for the Hereafter or weak descendants to support him. «For some this verse is a parable of those who follow up their charitable giving with preening and injury»
#363 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās · reports · Ibn ʿAbbās interprets the verse as people doing good works and later evil works that wipe out the earlier deeds’ value. «According to another interpretation attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās, this verse refers to those who do good works»
#364 STUDYQURAN · reports · Every kind of fruit may mean many kinds of fruit. «Every kind of fruit can mean something like “many kinds of fruit.”»
#365 STUDYQURAN · reports · Rivers running below is a common image for Paradise. «With rivers running below is a common image symbolizing the paradisal state»
