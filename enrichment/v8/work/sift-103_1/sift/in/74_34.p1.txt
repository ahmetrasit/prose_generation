#48 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The oath pairs night retreat with its opposite, the morning's unveiling; idbar is the reverse of the night's arrival, and isfar is the clearing of dawn. «وإدبار الليل مقابل إقباله، وإسفار الصبح انجلاؤه وانكشافه» [filed under 74:33, 74:34]
#49 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · After the first five verses of surah 96, revelation paused for an uncertain six months to three years, deeply distressing the Prophet and making him fear his first experience was illusory. «AFTER the Prophet's earliest revelation» [filed under 74:1-56]
#50 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Khadijah's support sustained the Prophet; after his vision of Gabriel, revelation became intense and continuous, as Bukhari and Muslim report. «revelation became intense and continuous» [filed under 74:1-56]
#51 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Asad places the surah at the beginning of the Meccan mission despite the possibility that some verses came later, and sees it as outlining the Qur'an's central themes: God, resurrection, moral responsibility, and afterlife as consequences of earthly conduct. «the surah outlines almost all the fundamental concepts» [filed under 74:1-56]
#52 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Nature’s manifestations argue for the Hereafter and expose heedlessness; the Qur’an warns so people can prepare, while evaders are left with stubbornness as their excuse. «An argument on the Hereafter from the manifestations of nature is presented» [filed under 74:32, 74:33, 74:34, 74:35, 74:36, 74:37, 74:38, 74:39, 74:40, 74:41, 74:42, 74:43, 74:44, 74:45, 74:46, 74:47, 74:48]
#53 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The moon's slow phases answer why Judgment is delayed: humanity likewise advances through stages, and the Day comes at God's appointed time, when the universe's purpose is fulfilled. «The moon is cited to them as an example.» [filed under 74:33, 74:34]
#54 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The emphatic negation precedes the oath because the audience's denial is so objectionable that the speaker states rejection before giving its proof. «Examples of such negation are found in various» [filed under 74:33, 74:34]
#55 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Qur'anic oaths substantiate a following claim; here they answer disbelievers asking why the promised Judgment has not arrived yet. «are meant to substantiate a claim which is generally stated after the oath.» [filed under 74:33, 74:34]
#56 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The signs of God follow a prescribed plan; only He knows its benefits, and justice will appear at its appointed time despite demands for haste. «Only He knows its benefits and expediencies.» [filed under 74:33, 74:34]
#57 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Night yielding to morning illustrates this world's darkness giving way to Judgment at its appointed time, as surely as daily dawn arrives. «just as there is no sign of the morning in the darkness of night but a time comes when the morning arrives» [filed under 74:33, 74:34]
#58 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The daily alternation of night and dawn rehearses Judgment; nature continually supplies evidence of that Day for sincere truth-seekers. «at some place or the other a continuous rehearsal of the Day of Judgement is going on» [filed under 74:33, 74:34]
#59 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The oaths by the moon, departing night, and dawning morning draw attention both to divine signs and to the importance of the warning to humanity. «Yüce Allah gece karanlığında dünyayı aydınlatan ay, aydınlanmak üzere olan gece, aydınlanıp ışığı her tarafa yayılmış olan sabah üzerine yemin ederek» [filed under 74:32, 74:33, 74:34, 74:35, 74:36]
#60 STUDYQURAN · Āl, Ṭ · reports · “Nay” rejects claims that people can know God’s hosts’ number or overcome them; the oath also recalls other oaths on the moon and the night-day alternation. «Nay! expresses a rejection of those who suppose that they can know the number of God’s hosts or that they can overcome them» [filed under 74:32, 74:33, 74:34]
#61 STUDYQURAN · Bg · reports · Some commentators consider the whole surah Makkan, while others assign its final section, verses 31–56, to the Madinan period. «according to some commentators, Makkan in its entirety (Bg), but others maintain that the last section (vv. 31–56) is from the Madinan period» [filed under 74:1-56]
#62 STUDYQURAN · holds · The surah continues the previous one’s account of the Prophet covering himself at the onset of revelation, then stresses his role as warner. «both are said to respond to the Prophet’s covering himself when the experience of the revelation began» [filed under 74:1-56]
