#396 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · A report has God tell Moses that each drop of ablution water used by Aḥmad and his community earns a garden as broad as the sky. «يتوضأ أحمد وأمته كما أمرتهم وأعطيهم بكل قطرة»
#397 BURSEVI d.1127 · الحافظ · reports · Ḥāfiẓ's Persian couplet praises prayer and devoted appeal purified by tears and heart's blood. «خوشا نماز ونياز كسى كه ازسردرد بآب ديده»
#398 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The highest good deed is knowledge of God, following monotheism, and opposing the lower self; remembrance purifies souls and hearts, strengthens obedience, and defeats Satan's schemes. «العلم بالله ، وطريقه التوحيد ، وخلاف هوى النفس»
#399 BURSEVI d.1127 · النبي · reports · When companions ask whether ‘There is no god but God’ is a good deed, the Prophet says it is the best of good deeds. «لا إله إلا الله من الحسنات قال»
#400 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The verse signals constant remembrance, worship, and obedience by day and night, with time for human needs, daytime work, and sleep to restore tired faculties for worship. «وفي الآية إشارة إلى إدامة الذكر والطاعة والعبادة في الليل والنهار»
#401 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · In the inward reading, light from good deeds, remembrance, and watchfulness at day edges and night approaches erases the darkness of time spent on bodily needs. «أي أن أنوار الحسنات وهي الأعمال الصالحة والذكر والمراقبة»
#402 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The luminous spirit would be lost through attachment to the dark body unless sacred good works nurture it, lift it toward spirituality and divine unity, and dispel bodily darkness. «تعلق الروح النوراني العلوي بالجسد الظلماني السفلي موجب لخسران الروح»
#403 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · He compares this nurture to a seed that would be lost unless water made one seed into seven hundred, with God multiplying further as He wills. «كما أن إلقاء الحبة في الأرض موجب لخسران الحبة»
#404 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · One reading assigns the afternoon edge of day to noon and afternoon prayers, and the night approaches to sunset and night prayers, covering all five daily prayers. «الظهر والعصر لأن ما بعد الزوال عشى»
#405 BURSEVI d.1127 · النبي · reports · The Prophet compares five daily prayers to bathing five times in a river at one's door, which leaves no dirt behind. «لو أن نهرا بباب أحدكم يغتسل منه كل يوم خمس مرات»
#406 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Another reading takes the day's ends as morning and sunset and night approaches as night prayer plus tahajjud, obligatory for the Prophet; it cites the night vigil command. «وفسر بعضهم طرفي النهار بالصبح والمغرب وزلف الليل بالعشاء»
#407 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · Other interpretations include witr according to Abū Ḥanīfa, or night prayer, witr, and tahajjud together, matching the plural ‘approaches.’ «أو الوتر على ما ذهب إليه أبو حنيفة أو مجموع العشاء والوتر والتهجد»
#408 BURSEVI d.1127 · reports · A similar passage is read as morning before sunrise, noon and afternoon before sunset, and sunset and night prayers at night. «أي : بصلاة الصبح [ق : 39-79]»
#409 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Good deeds, especially the five prayers, erase consequences of minor sins rather than undoing sins already committed; prayers, Fridays, and Ramadans expiate between them when major sins are avoided. «على الإطلاق لا سيما الصلوات الخمس»
#410 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · Prayer also restrains people from committing sins, as another verse says it keeps one from indecency and wrongdoing. «إن الصلوة تنهى عن الفحشاء والمنكر»
#411 BURSEVI d.1127 · أبو اليسر الأنصاري · reports · Abū al-Yusr was tempted by a woman buying dates; he drew her home, embraced and kissed her but stopped before intercourse when she warned him to fear God, then regretted it. «أن أبا اليسر الأنصاري كان يبيع التمر فأتته امرأة»
#412 BURSEVI d.1127 · أبو بكر · reports · Abū al-Yusr confessed to Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, who advised concealing the sin and repenting, but he could not keep silent and went to the Prophet. «فأتى أبا بكر رضي الله عنه فأخبره»
#413 BURSEVI d.1127 · holds · The Prophet told him to wait for God's command; after the afternoon prayer the verse came, and prayer expiated his act for everyone. «فلما صلى صلاة العصر نزلت هذه الآية»
#414 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Ṭalḥa, Ibn Abī Isḥāq, and Abū Jaʿfar read zuluf with a rounded lām; Mujāhid and Ibn Muḥayṣin read it with a silent lām, and a variant has zulfa, a synonymous form. «وقرأ طلحة وابن أبي إسحاق وأبو جعفر ( زلفا ) بضم اللام»
#415 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Good deeds expiate sins and remove liability, not sins as entities; another view says their records are erased. The view that they prevent sinning is remote and contrary to reports. «أي يكفرنها ويذهبن المؤاخذة عليها وإلا فنفس السيئات أعراض وجدت فانعدمت»
#416 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The apparent scope is obligatory and voluntary good deeds, though some restrict it to obligations. Reports about affirmation matching angels, fasting ʿArafa, and gratitude after eating or dressing support voluntary acts as expiators. «والظاهر أن المراد من الحسنات ما يعم الصلوات المفروضة وغيرها من الطاعات»
#417 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو اليسر · reports · The occasion is reported as Abū al-Yusr regretting he kissed a woman; after waiting for revelation, the Prophet said prayer atoned for it. Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, and Ibn al-Musayyib also transmit this view. «أبا اليسر من الأنصار قبل امرأة ثم ندم فأتى»
#418 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · prefers · Those who identify good deeds as obligatory prayers likely cite one important instance in the general category; the occasion does not restrict the verse. «والظاهر أن ذلك منهم إقتصار على بعض مهم من أفراد ذلك العام وسبب النزول لا يأبى العموم»
#419 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Establish the prescribed prayers fully; other reported meanings are to maintain them regularly or perform them at the start of their times. «أي المكتوبة ومعنى إقامتها أداؤها على تمامها»
#420 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The two ends mean the beginning and end of the day. Zulf are hours near the day; al-Layth and Thaʿlab say the first part of night, while Abū ʿUbayda, al-Akhfash, and Ibn Qutayba say any night hour, supported by al-ʿAjjāj’s verse. «فإنه من أزلفه إذا قربه»
#421 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Zulf is coordinated with the two ends, and min al-layl qualifies it. End prayers are variously dawn and afternoon, dawn and sunset, or dawn then noon and afternoon; Abū Ḥayyān favors dawn and afternoon, while noon as an endpoint is questioned. «وهو عطف على ( طرفي النهار ) و ( من الليل ) في موضع الصفة له»
#422 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Most identify the night prayers as sunset and evening; a report via al-Ḥasan from Ibn ʿAbbās specifies ʿatama. Another reading makes zulf night prayers drawing near to God, perhaps the Prophet’s night prayer or evening and witr. «والمراد بصلاة الزلف عند الأكثر صلاة المغرب والعشاء»
#423 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · Most take ‘sins’ as minor sins and say major sins require repentance, citing Muslim’s report through al-ʿAlāʾ that five prayers expiate the interval if major sins are avoided. «والمراد بالسيآت عند الأكثرين الصغائر لأن الكبائر لا يكفرها»
#424 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · البلقيني · reports · Al-Balqīnī says 4:31 concerns avoiding major sins throughout life, whereas the prayer report concerns each day; al-Samhūdī objects that lifelong avoidance entails daily prayer, then discusses multiple expiators retaining their rewards. «وأجاب البلقيني بأن ذلك غير وارد لأن المراد بالآية أن تجتنبوا في جميع العمر»
#425 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · Al-Ālūsī rejects a lifelong reading of 4:31: the reward for avoiding major sins at each time expiates minor sins committed then, as al-Qāḍī and al-Ghazālī support. «والظاهر أن المراد من ذلك أن ثواب إجتناب الكبائر في كل وقت يكفر الصغائر»
#426 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الغزالي · reports · Al-Ghazālī says restraint expiates only when someone has ability and desire to commit a major sin; abstaining from incapacity or lack of desire does not earn that effect. «إجتناب الكبيرة إنما يكفر الصغيرة إذا إجتنبها مع القدرة والإرادة»
#427 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · النووي · reports · Al-Nawawī says that when an expiatory act coincides with major sins but no minor sins, one may hope that the major sins are lightened. «أنه صادف فعل المكفر كبيرة أو كبائر ولم يصادف صغيرة»
#428 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · prefers · Al-Ālūsī reconciles the evidence by making avoidance of major sins a limit on expiating all interval sins, not a condition for any expiation: prayers expiate minors and all interval sins when majors are avoided. «ما اجتنبت الكبائر في الخبر ليس قيدا لأصل التكفير بل لشمول التكفير سائر الذنوب التي بين الصلوات الخمس»
#429 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · ابن حجر · reports · Ibn Ḥajar says avoiding major sins entails performing the five prayers, since abandoning prayer is major; al-Ālūsī says this argument merits examination. «وعلى تقدير ورد السؤال فالتخلص عنه سهل وذلك لأنه لا يتم إجتناب الكبائر إلا بفعل الصلوات الخمس»
#430 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · المحب الطبري · reports · Al-Muḥibb al-Ṭabarī records two views: the majority make avoidance of major sins a condition; another treats it as an exception, so expiation excludes major sins, which he calls stronger. «إختلف العلماء في أمر تكفير الصغائر بالعبادات هل هو مشروط بإجتناب الكبائر على قولين»
#431 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الزركشي · reports · Al-Zarkashī reports disagreement over requiring repentance for expiation; al-Baḥr says the requirement is explicit among rigorous legal theorists. «ذكر الزركشي أنهم إختلفوا في أن التكفير هل يشترط فيه التوبة أم لا»
#432 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · السبكي · reports · Al-Subkī says acceptance of repentance from unbelief is certain by grace, but acceptance for sins is disputed; other authorities differ over whether its expiation is presumptive or certain. «والمنقول عن السبكي أنه قال : إن قبول التوبة عن الكفر مقطوع به تفضلا»
