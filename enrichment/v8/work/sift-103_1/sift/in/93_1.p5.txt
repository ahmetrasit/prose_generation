#197 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The quality selected for the oath suits its context; any impression of glorification strengthens the attention rather than defining the device's main purpose. «واختيار المقسم به تراعى فيه الصفة التي تناسب الموقف» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#198 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The daily alternation from bright duha to a quiet night is a sensory image that prepares for the unseen pause in revelation, without suggesting that heaven abandoned earth. «تألق الضوء في ضحوة النهار، ثم فتور الليل إذا سجا وسكن» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#199 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The sensory contrast between light and darkness can clarify unseen guidance and truth or misguidance and falsehood. «معاني من الهدى والحق، أو الضلال والباطل، بماديات من النور والظلمة» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#200 BINTSHATI d.1419 · فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري · reports · They compare alternating night and day, which wax and wane by wisdom, to revelation being sent and withheld according to divine purposes. «فمرة تزداد ساعات الليل وتنقص ساعات النهار، ومرة بالعكس، فلا تكون الزيادة لهوى» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#201 BINTSHATI d.1419 · فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري · reports · They also say the oath answers the disbelievers' taunt: they were unable to prove their claim that God had abandoned Muhammad. «هاتوا الحجة، فعجزوا، فلزمه اليمين بأنه ما ودعه ربه ما قلاه» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#202 BINTSHATI d.1419 · فخر الدين الرازي ونظام الدين النيسابوري · reports · A further interpretation compares night and day alternately prevailing to how no created people are exempt from such change. «أنظر إلى جوار الليل مع النهار، لا يسلم أحدهما عن الآخر» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#203 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الشيخ محمد عبده · reports · Abduh says the Prophet's distress during the pause was relieved by renewed revelation: duha represents life-giving first revelation, while quiet night restores strength for later work. «فآتاه الله ما كان في شوق إليه، وثبته بالوحي وبشره أن تلك الفترة لم تكن عن ترك ولا عن قلى» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#204 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author's sensory analogy is nearly lost in Abduh's account of night as rest and preparation for work. «ويوشك الملحظ البيلني، أن يتوه وسط هذا الكلام في الضحى تقوى به الحياة» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#205 BINTSHATI d.1419 · ابن قيم الجوزية · reports · Ibn al-Qayyim comes close to matching the bright duha after darkness with revelation after its pause, but frames the oath as proof of God's lordship, wisdom, and mercy. «وكان "إبن قيم الجوزية" أقرب إلى إدراك الملحظ البياني» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#206 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author says Ibn al-Qayyim's appeal to doubts about God's lordship misses the stated occasion, which concerns a claim that God abandoned Muhammad. «الموقف كان ارتياباً من المشركين في ربوبية الله وحكمته ورحمته، وإنما كان - على قول المفسرين في سبب النزول - كلاماً» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#207 BINTSHATI d.1419 · بعض المفسرين · reports · Some interpret the order of duha then night as preferring life until believers reach maturity, avoiding despair first and then fear of divine plotting. «إنه إشارة أن الحياة أولى للمؤمن من الموت إلى أن تحصل كمالاته الممكنة» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#208 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author finds no clear connection between the oath and its answer in the accounts of al-Tabari, al-Zamakhshari, and Abu Hayyan, and notes they do not compare it with God's explicit denials of oaths. «لم يتعرض "إبن جرير الطبري" لبيان ارتباط المقسم به» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#209 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الطبري · reports · Al-Tabari reports disagreements over whether duha means the whole day or one daytime hour, and whether saja means night arriving, departing, settling, or becoming still. «فهم النهار كله، وهو ساعة من ساعات النهار» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#210 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الطبري · reports · Al-Tabari selects daylight, understood as visible sunlight, for duha and stillness among its people for saja. «واختار "الطبري" من هذه الأقوال في الضحى» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#211 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhshari defines duha as the early day when the sun rises and shines, while also reporting that it can mean the whole day; saja is settled darkness or quiet people and sounds. «هو صدر النهار حين ترتفع الشمس وتلقى شعاعها، وقيل: أريد بالضحى النهار» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#212 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الشيخ محمد عبده · reports · Abduh defines duha as sunlight in the day's prime and saja as the quiet and inactivity of night-time inhabitants. «هو ضوء الشمس في شباب النهار. وفي سجا الليل: هو ما تجده من سكون أهله» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#213 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Although the words can carry several lexical senses, the rhetoric of this particular setting admits only the single sense that best fits it. «البلاغة لا تجيز إلا معنى واحداً في المقام الواحد» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#214 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The sensory uses of the duha root center on visibility and exposure; most commonly, duha names the defined early daytime when the sun's light becomes clear, and it can name an act at that time. «ودلالة الوضوح هي الملحوظة في كل الإستعمالات الحسية للمادة»
#215 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Qur'anic uses include duha opposite evening, the sun's light brought forth, and a specific time appointed for events; these usages weigh against interpreting it as the whole day. «وفي الاستعمال القرآني، نرى القرآن الكريم استعمل الضحى مقابلاً للعشية» [filed under 93:1, 79:29, 79:46, 7:98, 20:59]
#216 BINTSHATI d.1419 · يعقوب · reports · Yaqub explains that the sacrificial day is called adha from the sheep sacrificed at duha. «وقال "يعقوب" في الأضحى: يسمى اليوم أضحى»
#217 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The appointment of people at duha in 20:59 and its use opposite evening in 79:46 show that duha means a specific daytime period, not the whole day; the same reading fits the sun's duha in 91:1. «مما يبعد تفسير الضحى بأنه النهار كله» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#218 BINTSHATI d.1419 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhshari says duha was chosen because it was when God spoke to Moses and when Moses met the sorcerers; the author rejects this anachronistic reading because the cited passages were revealed much later. «أقسم بالضحى، لأنها الساعة التي كلم فيها موسى عليه السلام» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#219 BINTSHATI d.1419 · النيسابوري والرازي · reports · Al-Nisaburi and al-Razi liken the duha hour to the whole night, just as Muhammad is said to equal all prophets and their communities. «توازي جميع الليل، كما أن محمداً - صلى الله عليه وسلم - يوازي جميع الأنبياء وأممهم» [filed under 93:1, 93:2]
#220 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The author excludes esoteric readings that turn duha and night into the Prophet's face and hair, male and female relatives, knowledge and forgiveness, or Islam's rise and later estrangement. «ولا نقف بعد هذا عند تأويلات الإشاريين بأن الضحى وجه محمد، - صلى الله عليه وسلم - والليل شعره» [filed under 93:1, 93:2, 93:3]
#221 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The Quran distinguishes dynamic fāʿilāt oath series from accumulative lists of separately named phenomena; 93:1-2 and 103:1 are the earliest accumulative examples in group I. «Generell lassen sich im Koran zwei distinkte Typen von Schwüren unterscheiden: einerseits die erstmals in 100:1–5 (Gruppe I) belegten fāʿilāt-Serien» [filed under 93:1-2]
#222 CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · Neuwirth reads the night and dawn oath images as evoking times of prayer or devotion and the worshipper's liturgical experience. «Neuwirth weist u. a. darauf hin, dass die in 93:2 genannte Nacht» [filed under 93:1-2]
#223 CORPUSCORANICUM · Neuwirth · reports · Neuwirth proposes that dawn and night images retain worship-related light and darkness perceptions and give these suras a hymn-like tone. «Vor diesem Hintergrund formuliert Neuwirth die Hypothese, die Zeitangaben in den weiter oben aufgeführten kosmischen Schwurpassagen würden auf Gebets- oder Andachtszeiten anspielen» [filed under 93:1-2]
#224 CORPUSCORANICUM · rejects · The prayer evidence postdates group II, including the likely later emergence of the vigil practice described in 73, so it cannot securely explain these early oaths. «Erstens sind alle koranischen Belege für zu bestimmten Tageszeiten abgehaltene Andachts- oder Gebetsübungen später als Gruppe II der frühmekkanischen Suren» [filed under 93:1-2]
#225 CORPUSCORANICUM · rejects · The following statements and themes usually point to judgment and accountability rather than worship; cosmic oath suras mostly end in warning or threat. «Zweitens legen die auf die betreffenden Schwüre folgenden Schwuraussagen sowie die jeweils im weiteren Textverlauf angesprochenen Themen häufig keineswegs eine liturgische Interpretation nahe» [filed under 93:1-2]
#226 CORPUSCORANICUM · rejects · Neuwirth's broad claim that day-and-night oath suras generally express closeness between God and humanity needs further examination. «Neuwirths Feststellung, dass sich „in den mit Schwüren bei Tag- und Nachtzeiten eingeleiteten Suren“ generell eine „enge Annäherung von Gott und Mensch“ mitteilt» [filed under 93:1-2]
#227 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · Short group IIIa and IIIb oaths may chiefly create emphasis and mark textual breaks. «rhetorische Mittel zur Erzeugung von Emphase und zur Markierung von Textzäsuren» [filed under 93:1-2]
#228 CORPUSCORANICUM · prefers · Cosmic oaths point to God's creative omnipotence as evidence that resurrection and judgment are possible, a reasoning made explicit in later passages. «dass die sich in der Schöpfung erweisende göttliche Allmacht als Argument für die grundsätzliche Möglichkeit einer Auferweckung und Aburteilung der Toten dienen soll» [filed under 93:1-2]
