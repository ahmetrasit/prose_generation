#228 TABRISI d.548 · holds · In syntax, seeking God's approval is a purpose, تثبيتا coordinates with it, the hill phrase modifies the garden, and the twofold produce describes its state. «ابتغاء مرضاة الله مفعول له وتثبيتا معطوف عليه»
#229 TABRISI d.548 · سعيد بن جبير والسدي والشعبي · reports · Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Suddi, and al-Sha'bi explain تثبيتا من أنفسهم as strong conviction and insight in religion. «بقوة اليقين والبصيرة في الدين عن سعيد بن جبير»
#230 TABRISI d.548 · الحسن ومجاهد · reports · Al-Hasan and Mujahid say spenders confirm for themselves where their charity should be placed; the author answers that confirming themselves is itself a valid sense of making firm. «وقيل معناه أنهم يثبتون أين يضعون صدقاتهم»
#231 TABRISI d.548 · أبو علي الجبائي · reports · Abu Ali al-Jubba'i reads تثبيتا as making oneself steadfast in obedience to God. «وقيل معناه وتوطينا لنفوسهم على الثبوت على طاعة الله»
#232 TABRISI d.548 · holds · A garden on high ground yields better and more than land receiving pooled runoff. «لأن نبتها يكون أحسن وريعها أكثر من المستغل»
#233 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · The sincere spenders seek God's pleasure. «أي طلبا لرضاه»
#234 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن قتيبة · reports · Rabwa means elevation; anything elevated and increased is called rabā, including usury in trade. «الربوة الارتفاع وكل شيء ارتفع وزاد فقد ربا ومنه الربا في البيع»
#235 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الشعبي وقتادة والسدي · reports · “Establishing themselves” means spending with certainty and belief. «أنه الإنفاق على يقين وتصديق وهذا قول الشعبي وقتادة والسدي في آخرين»
#236 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الحسن ومجاهد وأبو صالح · reports · “Establishing themselves” means carefully considering where to place their expenditure. «أنه التثبيت لارتياد محل الإنفاق فهم ينظرون أين يضعونها»
#237 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Janna means a garden; Mujahid and Asim al-Jahdari read ḥabba instead. «الجنة البستان وقرأ مجاهد وعاصم الجحدري حبة بالحاء»
#238 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي · reports · These readers pronounce rabwa with a ḍamma on the rāʾ. «وقرأ ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي بربوة بضم الراء»
#239 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · عاصم وابن عامر · reports · They pronounce rabwa with a fatḥa on the rāʾ. «وقرأ عاصم وابن عامر بفتح الراء»
#240 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الحسن والأعمش · reports · They pronounce the rāʾ in rabwa with a kasra. «وقرأ الحسن والأعمش بكسر الراء»
#241 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن عباس وأبو رزين · reports · They read rabāwa with an added alif and a fatḥa; Ubayy ibn Ka'b and Asim al-Jahdari add the alif but use a ḍamma, and variants occur in al-Mu'minun too. «وقرأ ابن عباس وأبو رزين برباوة بزيادة ألف وفتح الراء»
#242 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الزجاج · reports · Rabwa has three vowel variants and denotes high land with enough water, which yields more than low ground. «يقال ربوة وربوة وربوة ورباوة والموضع المرتفع من الأرض إذا كان له ما يرويه»
#243 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن كثير ونافع · reports · They read akulaha with a light, unvowelled kāf everywhere; Abu Amr agrees when the word is attached to a feminine noun, while Asim, Ibn Amir, Hamza, and al-Kisa'i always use the heavier form. «قرأ ابن كثير ونافع أكلها والأكل بسكون الكاف حيث وقع»
#244 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · Akul means fruit, and du'fin means twofold. «وأكلها أي ثمرها ﴿ ضعفين ﴾ أي مثلين»
#245 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن قتيبة · reports · Tall means weaker rain. «فأما الظل فقال ابن قتيبة هو أضعف المطر»
#246 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الزجاج · reports · Tall is continuous, fine rain with small drops that scarcely make water channels run. «هو المطر الدائم الصغار القطر الذي لا تكاد تسيل منه المثاعب»
#247 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · The future form describes a past possibility: if no downpour struck, a light drizzle did; the garden owner and sincere believer are not disappointed by either rain. «وهذا لفظ مستقبل وهو لأمر ماض فمعناه فإن لم يكن أصابها وابل فطل»
#248 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · The garden prospers under light rain or a downpour, just as a sincere believer's giving bears fruit in either case. «فكذلك نفقة المؤمن المخلص»
#249 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · الخطابي · reports · Basir means the One who sees; its form fa'il functions like the active participle muf'il. «والبصير من أسماء الله تعالى معناه المبصر قال الخطابي وهو فعيل بمعنى مفعل»
#250 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The contrasting sincere donors spend for two ends: seeking God's pleasure and تثبيت of themselves. «غرض هؤلاء المنفقين من هذا الانفاق أمران»
#251 RAZI-FULL d.606 · القفال · reports · Al-Qaffāl offers a seed analogy: sincere deeds grow like seed in fertile garden soil, while the reproacher's deed is seed stored on sterile stone and yields nothing when needed. «قال القفال رحمه الله تعالى ، وفيه احتمال آخر» [filed under 2:264, 2:265]
#252 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Repeated giving for God's pleasure creates a lasting disposition: even an accidental lapse quickly returns the heart toward the sacred, spiritually aligning the person with angelic and holy realities. «صيرورة هذا الابتغاء والطلب ملكة مستقرة في النفس»
#253 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj says تثبيت is confident assurance that God will not waste or disappoint the donors' work, unlike the hypocrite who sees giving as wasted without belief in reward. «قال الزجاج : المراد من التثبيت أنهم ينفقونها»
#254 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الحسن ومجاهد وعطاء · reports · Al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, and ʿAṭāʾ interpret تثبيت as careful placement of alms among people of integrity; al-Ḥasan says a donor gives when the motive is truly for God. «قال الحسن ومجاهد وعطاء : المراد أن المنفق»
#255 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The word ربوة means elevated, swelling ground; the author favors fertile soil that rises and grows with rain over interpreters' ordinary hill, citing Q 22:5 and the contrast with sterile stone. «وأصلها من قولهم : ربا الشيء يربو إذا ازداد وارتفع»
#256 RAZI-FULL d.606 · عاصم وابن عامر · reports · ʿĀṣim and Ibn ʿĀmir read ربوة with fatḥa on rāʾ, a Tamīm dialect; the other readers use ḍamma, the more common Qurashī form, among seven variants. «قرأ عاصم وابن عامر { بربوة } بفتح الراء»
#257 RAZI-FULL d.606 · القاضي · reports · Al-Qāḍī says تثبيت means training oneself to preserve obedience and avoid what corrupts it, including subsequent reproach and injury. «يوطنون أنفسهم على حفظ هذه الطاعة وترك ما يفسدها»
#258 RAZI-FULL d.606 · مجاهد · reports · Mujāhid's reading, وتثبيتا من بعض أنفسهم, supports construing تثبيت as reassuring others that the donors' faith and sincerity are genuine. «ويعضده قراءة مجاهد { وتثبيتا من * بعض * أنفسهم }»
#259 RAZI-FULL d.606 · صاحب الكشاف · reports · The Kashshāf explains that struggle subdues part of the self attached to worldly life and wealth; spending money stabilizes part, while spending life stabilizes all, as in al-Ṣaff 61:11. «ومعشوقها أمران : الحياة العاجلة والمال»
#260 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Al-Rāzī proposes that the heart becomes firm through God's remembrance; spending brings calm only when done solely for worship, illustrated by ʿAlī's words in al-Insān 76:9. «أن ثبات القلب لا يحصل إلا بذكر الله»
#261 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · He also cites the report about Abū Bakr's spending as seeking only God's face, and notes the rational principle that repeating acts produces stable dispositions. «ووصف إنفاق أبي بكر فقال»
#262 RAZI-FULL d.606 · المفسرون · rejects · Exegetes say a garden on a hill yields better, but al-Rāzī objects that elevation or a valley each has disadvantages and prefers fertile swelling soil, supported by Q 22:5 and the stone contrast. «البستان إذا كان في ربوة من الأرض كان أحسن وأكثر ريعا»
#263 RAZI-FULL d.606 · ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو · reports · Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, and Abū ʿAmr lighten أكلها; the remaining readers intensify it. The word means edible fruit or produce, as in Ibrāhīm 14:25, with lexical poetry cited. «قرأ ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو { أكلها } بالتخفيف»
#264 RAZI-FULL d.606 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj reads ضعفين as twice as much; ʿAṭāʾ says a single year's yield equals another garden's two-year yield, while al-Aṣamm and Abū Muslim give related doubled-yield readings. «قال الزجاج : { اتت أكلها * ضعفين } يعني مثلين»
#265 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Ṭall is light rain. Either a fertile garden preserves its full yield despite receiving only light rain, or it still yields some fruit; sincere giving is never wasted, little or much. «الطل : مطر صغير الفطر»
#266 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · God sees the amount and manner of spending and its motives, then recompenses each person accordingly, for good with good and evil with evil. «هو تعالى عالم بكمية النفقات وكيفيتها»
#267 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · A rabwa is a slightly elevated place, usually with deep soil and better growth, which explains why it is chosen for the parable. «والربوة: المكان المرتفع ارتفاعا يسيرا، معه في الأغلب كثافة تراب»
#268 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عطية · rejects · Ibn Atiyya rejects al-Tabari's identification with the 'gardens of al-Ḥazn': those are the gardens of Najd, whose growth and air he says are superior to Tihama's. «قال ابن عطية: ورياض الحزن ليست من هذا كما زعم الطبري، بل تلك هي الرياض المنسوبة إلى نجد»
#269 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · An Arab woman compared her husband to a Tihama night, invoked as evidence that Tihama's air is unpleasant except at night. «وقلما يصلح هواء تهامة إلا بالليل ولذلك قالت الأعرابية:»
#270 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · السدي · reports · Al-Suddi reads rabwa as rabāwa, a low-lying place; Ibn Atiyya calls the wording awkward, and the author says it is untenable because the root means increase and elevation. «وقال السدى: «بربوة» أي برباوة، وهو ما انخفض من الأرض»
#271 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الفراء · reports · Al-Farra explains 'a severe seizure' in 69:10 as increased and compares it with taking more than one gave, as evidence for the root's sense of increase. «وقال الفراء في قوله تعالى:»
