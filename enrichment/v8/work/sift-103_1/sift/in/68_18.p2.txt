#81 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. «أن هذه الجنة كانت لرجل صالح، حدودا قومه وبلده فقيل إنه من أهل اليمن» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:19, 68:20, 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24, 68:25, 68:26, 68:27, 68:28, 68:29]
#82 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The garden owner harvested enough for his family, gave surplus to the needy, and allowed gleaners to collect missed grain and grapes. «ويتصدق بالباقي على المحتاجين، ويترا ما يخطئه المنجل» [filed under 68:17-18]
#83 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · His sons resented this generosity and, after his death, resolved to gather the crop secretly and leave nothing for anyone in need. «وكان بنوه يضيقون بذلك ويحاولون حمله على بما يملك» [filed under 68:17-18]
#84 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The early Meccan account omits personal names, dates, and other details except where they serve its lesson; it identifies the people only as from Ethiopia or Yemen. «لا يتعلق فيها بذكر الأشخاص والأزمنه إلا ما كان من جوهر القصة» [filed under 68:17-29]
#85 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The Quran leaves the story's date unstated and gives no basis for the strange claims that Satan or Gabriel uprooted the trees and carried them to the Kaaba and Taif. «دون أن يشير من قرب أو بعد، إلى ما يسيغ الروايات الغريبة» [filed under 68:17-19]
#86 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Yunus 10:24 offers a useful parallel for understanding the garden story as a picture of worldly life suddenly cut down. «لأن نستأنس في فهم آيات القلم، بآية يونس في مثل الحياة الدنيا» [filed under 68:17-19]
#87 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The punishment verse points to the lesson of the garden story: tyrants face punishment in this world, while the hereafter's punishment is greater. «يلفت إلى مناط العبرة فيما تلت الآيات من أمر أصحاب الجنة» [filed under 68:17-33]
#88 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · rejects · Although exegetes unanimously take the phrase to mean they omitted ‘if God wills,’ Islahi rejects that reading: the construction is unsuitable, and istithnāʾ is not explicit enough without contextual evidence. «Although all the exegetes are unanimous in this interpretation, I personally am not convinced of it»
#89 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · prefers · He reads istithnāʾ literally: they swore to take all the fruit and leave none for the poor, rejecting a customary charitable gleaning practice also stated in Leviticus. «what they swore was that they would pick all the fruit and leave nothing for the poor»
#90 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The oath about leaving nothing emphasizes the same stinginess as Quraysh leaders’ forbidding good, especially Abū Lahab and his associates. «the picture of the stinginess of Abū Lahab and his associates»
#91 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu · reports · Most commentators say the garden owners resolved what they would do the next day without saying 'if God wills.' «Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu 18. âyeti, “Bahçe sahipleri ‘Allah izin verirse’ demeden ertesi gün yapacakları iş hakkında karar verdiler” şeklinde açıklamışlardır»
#92 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı yorumlar · reports · An alternative interpretation is that they made no provision for the poor's share. «“yoksulların payını ayırmaksızın” şeklinde de bir yorum vardır»
#93 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · When intending future action, people should say inshaallah because human will and power alone cannot make an event occur; God's will is also necessary. «Gelecekte bir işi yapmaya niyet ederken “inşaallah” diyerek işi Allah’ın iradesine bağlamak gerekir»
#94 STUDYQURAN · Most commentators · reports · Most commentators place the story in Madinah and identify its owners as three Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen who inherited their father’s fertile garden; their father left produce for the poor, but they planned to withhold it and harvest secretly. «Most commentators maintain that these verses were revealed in Madinah and that the story refers to three either Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen» [filed under 68:17-34]
#95 STUDYQURAN · Bg, IK, Z · reports · “Made no exception” can mean the brothers did not say “If God wills,” showing they relied on themselves rather than God. «Made no exception is taken to indicate that they did not say, “If God wills” (Bg, IK, Z)»
#96 STUDYQURAN · holds · The brothers’ failure to make an exception indicates self-reliance rather than trust in God. «and thus trusted in themselves rather than in God (R)»
#97 STUDYQURAN · holds · Alternatively, “made no exception” can mean that they left no part of the harvest for the poor. «It could also be a reference to the harvest, indicating that they left no portion for the poor»
#98 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Qalam is generally considered an early Makkan sūrah. «generally thought to be from the early Makkan period» [filed under 68:1-52]
#99 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qatādah · reports · These early commentators held that verses 1–16, 34–47, and 51–52 are Makkan, while 17–33 and 48–50 are Madinan. «the early commentators IbnʿAbbās and al-Qatādah (d. 117/726) maintained that vv. 1–16» [filed under 68:1-52]
#100 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 17–34 tell the parable of garden owners who lose their harvest because of miserliness. «the parable of the owners of the garden, who lost their harvest because of their miserliness» [filed under 68:17-34]
