#169 TABATABAI d.1402 · الزمخشري · reports · Al-Zamakhsharī says counted years can suggest abundance or scarcity, since a long span is small in God's reckoning. «قال في الكشاف إن توصيف السنين بالعدد يحتمل أن يراد به التكثير أو التقليل»
#170 TABATABAI d.1402 · الزجاج · reports · Al-Zajjāj says a small quantity is already easy to grasp without counting, while a large quantity needs to be counted. «وقال الزجاج إن الشيء إذا قل فهم مقدار عدده فلم يحتج أن يعد»
#171 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · The author prefers that counted signals a small, easily numbered span, fitting the passage's denial that the story is uniquely astonishing; the sleepers were not dead. «وكون الغرض من التوصيف بالعدد هو التقليل هو الملائم للسياق»
#172 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The cave story circulated in detailed reports from al-Qummī, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Ibn Isḥāq, Wahb ibn Munabbih, and al-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr, among others. «وردت قصة الكهف مفصلة كاملة في عدة روايات عن الصحابة والتابعين وأئمه أهل البيت» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#173 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The transmitted accounts disagree even on major features, while reports about dates, rulers, lineage, appearance, names, and the title أصحاب الرقيم diverge still more. «من الاختلاف في متونها بحيث لا تكاد تتفق في جهة بارزه من جهات القصة» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#174 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · One cause is the story’s prominence among People of the Book: Quraysh learned it from them, and its Christian images and monuments spread it widely, so communities reshaped it to fit their beliefs. «أن القصة مما اعتنت به أهل الكتاب كما يستفاد من رواياتها أن قريشا تلقتها عنهم» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#175 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A second cause is Muslims’ broad collection of outside reports, especially through converts such as Wahb ibn Munabbih and Kaʿb al-Aḥbār; later transmitters treated such material as prophetic reports. «اختلط بهم قوم من علماء أهل الكتاب دخلوا في الإسلام كوهب بن منبه وكعب الأحبار» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#176 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾān selects key lessons that serve guidance instead of supplying a complete historical account; it is a book of guidance, not history. «فما كتاب الله بكتاب تاريخ وإنما هو كتاب هدى» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#177 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾān omits names, ancestry, birth, upbringing, occupations, social standing, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors, and the dog’s identity because guidance does not depend on them; historians and early exegetes filled these gaps, producing divergent accounts. «إلى غير ذلك من الأمور التي لا يتوقف غرض الهداية على العلم بشيء منها كما يتوقف عليه غرض البحث التاريخي» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#178 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The narrative presents the youths sleeping in the cave with their dog for the stated long period, protected by the cave’s position and God’s care. «ثم دخلوا الكهف واستقروا على فجوة منه وكلبهم باسط ذراعيه بالوصيد» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:17, 18:18, 18:25]
#179 TABATABAI d.1402 · أهل الرواية والتاريخ · reports · Most historians date the story between Jesus and Muḥammad; it is absent from the two Testaments, though some Muslim reports say Quraysh heard it from Jews, while Christians preserved it extensively. «معظم أهل الرواية والتاريخ على أن القصة وقعت في الفترة بين النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وبين المسيح» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#180 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Christian accounts resemble Ibn Isḥāq’s report from Ibn ʿAbbās in some respects but differ from Muslims on the number of sleepers, whether they had a dog, and the length of their sleep. «أن المصادر السريانية تذكر عدد أصحاب الكهف ثمانية في حين يذكره المسلمون» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#181 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · After centuries as a ruined, forgotten site, Jordanian excavation uncovered Rujayb; the author considers its archaeological signs and some Muslim reports the strongest fit for the Qurʾānic cave. «وقامت عدة من الأمارات والشواهد الأثرية على كونه هو كهف أصحاب الكهف المذكورين في القرآن» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#182 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Amman stands on the site of Philadelphia, a major and beautiful city before Islam that remained under Roman control from the early second century until the Muslim conquest. «كانت هي وما والاها تحت استيلاء الروم منذ أوائل القرن الثاني الميلادي حتى فتح المسلمون الأرض المقدسة» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#183 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Other proposed locations include a cave on Mount Qāsiyūn near Damascus, one at Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and a cave near Nakhchivan. «والكهف الثالث كهف بجبل قاسيون بالقرب من الصالحية بدمشق الشام» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#184 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · No evidence identifies any proposed cave as the Qurʾānic one; the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with the story’s Roman setting and the limits of Roman power. «ولا شاهد يشهد على كون شيء من هذه الكهوف هو الكهف المذكور في القرآن الكريم» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#185 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The three verses summarize their flight and prayer for rescue, their counted years of sleep, and their awakening to determine which faction counted the duration. «إذ رجع الشبان إلى الكهف فسألوا عند ذلك ربهم قائلين ربنا هب لنا من لدنك» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:12]
#186 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Each opening verse gives one of the story's three summary elements; later verses add what happened after the people discovered them. «فلإجمال القصة أركان ثلاثة تتضمن كل واحدة من الآيات الثلاث واحدا منها» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:12]
#187 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · The phrase can mean that God cut the youths off physically or metaphorically from outside sounds and bustle. «God caused them to remain cut off - physically or metaphorically - from the sounds and the bustle of the outside world»
#188 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · classical commentators · reports · Classical commentators understand the veiling of ears as putting the youths to sleep. «The classical commentators take the above phrase to mean that God "veiled their ears with sleep"»
#189 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers passage answers an objection from the Prophet’s opponents and presents their real story without needless details. «their real life aside from needless details becomes evident» [filed under 18:9-26]
#190 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ trials parallel the Prophet and his companions’ preaching phase; perseverance brings God’s easing of hardship and provision, and He does not let His persevering servants lose their way. «God will ease out their hardships the way He did so for them» [filed under 18:9-26]
#191 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This overview is offered as sufficient to establish the sūrah’s coherence and central theme before its detailed exegesis. «This concise analysis of the meanings of the sūrah is sufficient to explain its coherence and central theme» [filed under 18:1-110]
#192 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers question was planted by the People of the Book and posed by Quraysh to harass and corner the Prophet, not to seek truth. «These irrelevant questions were obviously not meant to find out the real truth» [filed under 18:9-26]
#193 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Opponents planned to exploit either an inability to answer or an answer conflicting with their own beliefs. «if the Prophet (sws) was able to answer them and the answer was against their beliefs» [filed under 18:9-26]
#194 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an generally counsels ignoring such questions, but answers when useful; the truthful account both answers and trains the Prophet and companions in perseverance during comparable trials. «if some question is beneficial or its answer can be beneficial for education» [filed under 18:9-26]
#195 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an preserves the useful, true part of the cave story, correcting false accretions and restoring a major chapter in truth-seekers’ history. «a very important chapter in the history of the seekers of the truth was cleansed from all sorts of wrong details» [filed under 18:9-26]
#196 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The dwellers’ identities, date and ancestry cannot be known securely; beyond the Qur’anic account, claims are folk tales, and further inquiry is discouraged. «Who were the cave-dwellers, when did they inhabit the earth and whose ancestors were they are questions that cannot be answered with certainty» [filed under 18:9-26]
#197 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Islahi’s opinion is that the youths were ancestors of Christians, not Quraysh or Jews, and belonged to the early Christian period under Roman persecution. «in my opinion, they belonged to the initial period of the Christians when they were persecuted by the Romans» [filed under 18:9-26]
#198 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · He says they boldly preached monotheism and accountability, faced imminent stoning, then took refuge in a cave where God supplied extraordinary protection. «They fearlessly invited people to monotheism and warned them of the accountability in the hereafter» [filed under 18:9-26]
