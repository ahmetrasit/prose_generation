<<notes for 18_19, part 12 of 0..14>>
#520 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Humanity will also awaken when the world's adornment is folded up and the human world is brought to extinction. «ويوم آخر وهو يوم يطوى فيه بساط الدنيا وزينتها»
#521 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The questioning is the real purpose of awakening and aligns with the purpose stated in 18:12. «قوله تعالى { ليتساءلوا بينهم } غاية لبعثهم»
#522 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · Treating the questioning as only a subordinate purpose lacks textual evidence; treating it as a mere consequence does not answer the objection that it is a small outcome of a great miracle. «وهذا مع عدم شاهد عليه من جهة اللفظ تكلف ظاهر»
#523 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · One youth asked the others how long they had slept, apparently sensing a long interval from the heaviness of their sleep. «وكأن السائل استشعر طولا في لبثهم مما وجده من لوثة النوم الثقيل»
#524 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · “A day or part of a day” is one speaker's uncertain estimate based on the sun's changed position, not a division in which some said a day and others part of one. «وقد أجاب بمثله شخص واحد بعينه قال تعالى»
#525 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Observed sun, shadows, light, and darkness cannot establish an exact full day; here “day” commonly means slightly more than a night and day. «الأمور الخارجية التي يستدل بها الإنسان وخاصة من نام ثم انتبه من شمس وظل»
#526 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Saying “your Lord knows” states an epistemic truth: humans know external events only through indicators, while God alone directly encompasses each thing and event. «فإن الإنسان محجوب عما وراء نفسه لا يملك بإذن الله إلا نفسه»
#527 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The speakers who appeal to God's knowledge show higher monotheistic insight than those who estimate a day; they are a different group, not the factions in 18:12. «أن القائلين منهم { ربكم أعلم بما لبثتم } كانوا أعلى كعبا في مقام المعرفة»
#528 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The dialogue implies at least seven youths: one asks, and each of two plural replies requires at least three people. «ومن هنا يستفاد أن القوم كانوا سبعة أو أزيد»
#529 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · They propose sending one of their own to buy food; “which one” refers grammatically to the city but means its residents. «الضمير في { أيها } راجع إلى المدينة والمراد بها أهلها من الكسبة استخداما»
#530 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The requested errand is to obtain food for them to eat, introduced as a proposal to the group. «وفيه أمر أو عرض لهم أن يرسلوا رسولا منهم إلى المدينة ليشتري لهم طعاما»
#531 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Zakiy food may mean good, lawful, or pure food; the comparative form favors the first sense. «وزكاء الطعام كونه طيبا وقيل كونه حلالا وقيل كونه طاهرا»
#532 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · In “bring you sustenance from it,” the pronoun most naturally refers to the food; taking it to refer to the silver is remote and requires an extra implied pronoun. «والضمير في { منه } للطعام المفهوم من الكلام»
#533 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Talaṭṭuf is acting gently and showing care; in context the envoy should deal smoothly with residents to avoid a quarrel that reveals them. «والمراد على ما يعطيه السياق ليتكلف اللطف مع أهل المدينة في ذهابه ومجيئه»
#534 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · A broader reading of tact as general caution in transactions is not supported by the explicit following clause. «وقيل المعنى ليتكلف اللطف في المعاملة وإطلاق الكلام يدفعه»
#535 TABATABAI d.1402 · الراغب · reports · Al-Rāghib traces ẓuhūr to visibility, from which the word extends to learning of something, finding it, or prevailing over it. «والكلمة على ما ذكره الراغب مأخوذة من الظهر بمعنى الجارحة»
#536 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · The author prefers “discover your location” for yuẓhirū ʿalaykum because it covers how the powerful townspeople could then capture and overpower the youths. «وظاهر السياق أن يكون { يظهروا عليكم } بمعنى يطلعوا عليكم ويعلموا بمكانكم»
#537 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The choice of stoning signals the townspeople's hostility to the youths' faith and the likelihood of a collective attack. «وفي اختيار الرجم على غيره من أصناف القتل إشعار بأن أهل المدينة عامة كانوا يعادونهم لدينهم»
#538 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · “Return you into their creed” uses a verb carrying the sense of insertion, hence the preposition fi rather than ilā. «الإعادة مضمن معنى الإدخال ولذا عدي بفي دون إلى»
#539 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Being returned would likely mean enforced participation in idol worship and loss of their true religious practices, not only verbal assent. «أن لا يقنع القوم بمجرد اعترافهم بحقية الملة صورة دون أن يثقوا بصدقهم في الاعتراف»
#540 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A captive or weak person trapped in an unbelieving society may comply under coercion while keeping faith inwardly, as 16:106 and 3:28 allow. «وقد قال تعالى إلا من أكره وقلبه مطمئن بالإيمان»
#541 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Someone free to leave who voluntarily enters a hostile society and loses the ability to profess faith has wronged himself; 4:97 rebukes those who claim they were helpless despite a spacious earth. «وأما من كان بنجوة منهم وهو حر في اعتقاده وعمله ثم ألقى بنفسه في مهلكة الضلال»
#542 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The author rejects proposed harmonizations: coerced apostasy leading to Satanic influence, return by invitation alone, or a period when dissimulation was forbidden lack textual support. «وقد أجابوا عن الإشكال بوجوه أخر غير مقنعة منها أن الإكراه على الكفر»
#543 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The exchange portrays close fellowship: they share religious insight, delegate equally, use communal money, advise tact, and care for one another's safety. «وسياق ما حكي من محاورتهم أعني قوله { لبثتم } إلى تمام الآيتين سياق عجيب دال على كمال تحابهم»
#544 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The emphatic phrase “your silver, this” is unusual for a food errand; the coin's age—three centuries—caused the youths to be recognized. «ولعلها إنما ذكرت في الآية مع خصوصية الإشارة لأنها كانت هي السبب لظهور أمرهم»
#545 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · They entered the cave with the dog and slept until that king and city disappeared, then awoke in a new era and initially thought they had slept a day or part of one. «فناموا حتى أهلك الله ذلك الملك وأهل المدينة وذهب ذلك الزمان وجاء زمان آخر وقوم آخرون» [filed under 18:11, 18:19]
#546 TABATABAI d.1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام · reports · One youth went for food with old currency, found a changed city and mutually unintelligible people, and was questioned after asking for provisions. «فرأى مدينة بخلاف التي عهدها ورأى قوما بخلاف أولئك لم يعرفهم ولم يعرفوا لغته»
#547 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · A later just king’s era began before God awakened them; they disputed whether they slept a day or longer, then sent one with an old coin to buy food, exposing the changed city and era. «ثم إن ذلك الملك ذهب وجاء ملك آخر فعبد الله وترك تلك الأوثان وعدل في الناس فبعثهم الله»
#548 TABATABAI d.1402 · ابن عباس · reports · The coin seller alerted officials, who identified the youths from the tablet; the messenger entered the cave while the crowd could not find or enter it, and they built a mosque to pray and seek forgiveness for them. «فقالوا لنتخذن عليهم مسجدا فاتخذوا عليهم مسجدا يصلون عليهم ويستغفرون لهم» [filed under 18:19, 18:21]
#549 TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The report’s claim that some said two days or more conflicts with the Quran’s day or part of a day and with ordinary evidence; differing about duration is a disagreement in judgment, not blameworthy dissent in action. «وأما احتمال اليومين وأزيد فمما لا سبيل إليه ولا شاهد يشهد عليه عادة»
#550 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The cave story circulated in detailed reports from al-Qummī, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Ibn Isḥāq, Wahb ibn Munabbih, and al-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr, among others. «وردت قصة الكهف مفصلة كاملة في عدة روايات عن الصحابة والتابعين وأئمه أهل البيت» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#551 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The transmitted accounts disagree even on major features, while reports about dates, rulers, lineage, appearance, names, and the title أصحاب الرقيم diverge still more. «من الاختلاف في متونها بحيث لا تكاد تتفق في جهة بارزه من جهات القصة» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#552 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · One cause is the story’s prominence among People of the Book: Quraysh learned it from them, and its Christian images and monuments spread it widely, so communities reshaped it to fit their beliefs. «أن القصة مما اعتنت به أهل الكتاب كما يستفاد من رواياتها أن قريشا تلقتها عنهم» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#553 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · A second cause is Muslims’ broad collection of outside reports, especially through converts such as Wahb ibn Munabbih and Kaʿb al-Aḥbār; later transmitters treated such material as prophetic reports. «اختلط بهم قوم من علماء أهل الكتاب دخلوا في الإسلام كوهب بن منبه وكعب الأحبار» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#554 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾān selects key lessons that serve guidance instead of supplying a complete historical account; it is a book of guidance, not history. «فما كتاب الله بكتاب تاريخ وإنما هو كتاب هدى» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#555 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾānic narrative first shows the youths’ truth-bearing withdrawal and long sleep, then their awakening and exchange that exposes them, and finally their discovery and the mosque built over them. «أورد أولا شطرا من محاورتهم يشير إلى معنى قيامهم لله وثباتهم على كلمة الحق» [filed under 18:10, 18:14, 18:16, 18:19, 18:21]
