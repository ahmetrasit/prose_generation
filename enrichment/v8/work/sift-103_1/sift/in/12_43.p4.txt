#161 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · rejects · The claim that أخر would be needless under an omitted seven is unsound: both competing grammatical analyses are recognized as apparent by Arab grammarians, so the word can emphasize the intended reading rather than be verbose. «فغير سديد على ما في الكشف لأن القائل بالتقدير يدعي الظهور في الإستقلال»
#162 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · reports · The address يا أيها الملأ is to notables thought to possess knowledge; one report says the king gathered magicians, soothsayers, and dream interpreters. «خطاب للأشراف ممن يظن به العلم يروى أنه جمع السحرة والكهنة والمعبرين»
#163 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · صاحب الفرائد · reports · The author of al-Fara'id objects that numeral discriminators normally appear in genitive construction: with seven lean, an omitted cows is needed, whereas adding lean could make the adjective stand for cows and avoid ellipsis. «واعترض صاحب الفرائد بأن الأصل في العدد التمييز بالإضافة»
#164 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · القطب · reports · Al-Qutb replies that the preceding mention of seven cows already identifies the lean seven as cows, so إضافة عجاف would make an adjective do the work of a discriminator; treating it as standing for cows applies only when it is an adjective. «تعقب ذلك القطب بأنه هب أن الأصل في العدد التمييز بالإضافة»
#165 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · الطيبي · reports · Al-Tibi says describing the number before its discriminator stresses both count and quality, while naming the discriminator first makes the description the focus; omission in all three parallel images serves the scene's movement from ease to hardship. «إن المميز إذا وصف ثم رفع به الإبهام والإجمال من العدد آذن بأنهما مقصودان في الذكر»
#166 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The king asks them to interpret the dream and state its consequence; another view says he addresses his companions and calls the task a fatwa to honor them and magnify the vision. «أي عبروها وبينوا حكمها وما تؤول إليه من العاقبة»
#167 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · Dream interpretation is the habitual passage from a seen dream-image to its corresponding external or inward reality; عبور means crossing, and تأويل names what the dream comes to. «وهي الإنتقال من الصورة المشاهدة في المنام إلى ما هي صورة ومثال لها»
#168 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · holds · The light form عبر الرؤيا is more established and forceful in linguistic usage than the doubled form; although some deny the latter, al-Mubarrad's Bedouin poem attests it. «عبرت الرؤيا بالتخفيف عبارة أقوى وأعرف عند أهل اللغة»
#169 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · المبرد · reports · Al-Mubarrad cites a Bedouin poet's line about seeing and interpreting a dream as evidence that the doubled verb تعبير is attested. «ما أنشده المبرد في الكامل لبعض الأعراب»
#170 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو حيان · prefers · Abu Hayyan takes the lām in للرؤيا as an emphatic or strengthening lām for a delayed weakened verb; other accounts call it explanatory, make it indicate eagerness to interpret, or treat الرؤيا as a predicate of كان, which Al-Alusi considers strained. «وقيل واختاره أبو حيان إنها لتقوية الفعل المذكور لأنه ضعف بالتأخير»
#171 ALUSI-FULL d.1270 · أبو جعفر · reports · Abu Ja'far assimilates the hamza in الرؤيا after changing it to wāw and then yā because a preceding wāw is unvocalized; grammarians call this irregular because the wāw substitution is not obligatory. «وقرأ أبو جعفر بالإدغام في الرؤيا وبابه بعد قلب الهمزة واوا»
#172 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Islamic scholars classify dreams as divine instruction, self-generated recollections with no independent value, or satanic false associations; the latter two are ahlām. «Ulemâ-yı İslâm, rüya hâdiselerini üç sınıf üzerine tasnif etmişlerdir» [filed under 12:43-44]
#173 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Dream interpretation cannot be solved by logic alone; people ignorant of the unseen and God's knowledge and wisdom may consequently claim all dreams are empty fantasies. «esrâr-ı gaybiyeden büsbütün gāfil olanlar, Rabbü’l-âlemîn’in alîm ve hakîm olduğunu bilmeyenler» [filed under 12:43-44]
#174 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · A prophetic report that a dream is one forty-sixth of prophecy points to its epistemic value, while the report that glad tidings remain after prophecy ends highlights its emotional value. «hadîs-i şerîfi ilmî haysiyetine işaret olduğu gibi» [filed under 12:43-44]
#175 ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · The Prophet's mission lasted twenty-three years, with revelation in its first six months arriving through dreams like true dawn; six months is one forty-sixth of that span. «bu yirmi üç sene müddetin ilk altı ayındaki vahiy de hep fecr-i sâdık gibi zuhur eden rüya ile olmuştu» [filed under 12:43-44]
#176 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Dreams and even confused dreams can matter for self-knowledge because they stir feelings, not only because they may convey knowledge. «ahlâmın bile ma‘rifetü’n-nefste şâyân-ı mütalaa bir ehemmiyeti vardır» [filed under 12:43-44]
#177 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Waking perception combines a present sensation with memories of similar past sensations; association among them lets a person recognize and describe what is seen or heard. «birtakım suver ü me‘ânî dahi tedâ‘î ve tevârüd etmiş» [filed under 12:43-44]
#178 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Sleep also exposes the self to external or internal impressions and lets past memories associate, producing dream experiences without voluntary attention or control. «hâtırât-ı mâziyesi tedâ‘î edebilir» [filed under 12:43-44]
#179 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Associations made only from past memories form empty dreams, whereas associations prompted by an influence from God form true dreams. «Taraf-ı Hak’tan bir tesir üzerine cereyan eden tedâ‘îler de rüyayı teşkil ederler» [filed under 12:43-44]
#180 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Unlike waking perception, dreams unfold involuntarily beyond choice; this makes direct divine instruction more evident in dreams, even though their meaning is less clear. «rüya hâdisesi ise nefsin irade ve intihâbı fevkinde ve mücerred cebrî bir surette cereyan eder» [filed under 12:43-44]
#181 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Bright waking visions, especially in darkness or with closed eyes, can later prove true like dreams; both reveal reality to the spirit through an imaginal realm between meaning and matter. «âlem-i me‘ânî ile mücerred âlem-i madde arasında mu‘allak» [filed under 12:43-44]
#182 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The king convenes a large body of leading scholars and sages to hear his troubling dream. «meşhur ulemâ ve hukemâsının a‘yânından mürekkeb büyük bir heyet toplamış»
#183 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · A non-prophet's dream or interpretation generally yields no certain knowledge, though it can produce individual conviction at varying degrees of confidence. «enbiyâdan mâ‘adâsının rüyası ve tâbiri sûret-i umûmiyede ilm-i yakīn ifade etmez» [filed under 12:43-44]
#184 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Actual events can verify a dream's interpretation: some dreams occur exactly, some are interpreted as they are seen, and some bring an inarticulate but compelling sense of truth. «Rüyanın asıl tevili vâkı‘ât ile tebeyyün eder» [filed under 12:43-44]
#185 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The king's dream belongs to the kind that its dreamer senses to be true but cannot interpret, which is why he asks the court for an explanation. «Mevzû‘-i bahis olan Mısır melikinin rüyası da böyle olduğundan» [filed under 12:43-44]
#186 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Unlike the court, the king seems to recognize his vision as a meaningful dream rather than an empty jumble, even though he cannot interpret it himself. «Mevzû‘-i bahis olan Mısır melikinin rüyası da böyle olduğundan» [filed under 12:43-44]
#187 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · His request for a ruling means the dream is important to him and he wants its interpretation to resolve his difficulty. «bu rüya, benim için pek mühim bir mesele oldu, bunu tâbir edip müşkilimi hallediniz»
#188 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Although ordinary speech treats ru'yā and hulm as synonyms, ru'yā derives from seeing and points through its mental image to an underlying reality. «“Rüya”, “rü’yet” mânasından me’huz olduğu için bunda hayâlin mâverâsında bir hakikat görülmüş bulunur» [filed under 12:43-44]
#189 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Dream interpretation crosses from imagined forms to the reality behind them; in Arabic, ubūr or ibāra is more eloquent than the customary ta'bīr. «“ubûru’r-rü’yâ”, “ibâretü’r-rü’yâ” denilmek Arabîde daha fasihtir» [filed under 12:43-44]
#190 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Ta'wīl al-aḥādīth can mean grasping the real import of words or the eventual meaning and outcome of events, since ḥadīth can mean speech or an event. «“Ehâdîs” “hadîs”in cem‘i, “hadîs” de söz veya hâdis, yani hâdise demek olabileceğine göre» [filed under 12:43-44]
#191 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The difference between true dreams and empty dreams parallels the difference between truthful and false speech or accurate and mistaken perceptions while awake. «yakaza hâlinde doğru bir söz veya hakīkī bir ihtisas ile eğri bir sözün veya yanlış bir ihtisâsın farkları ne ise rüya ile ahlâm beynindeki fark da o demektir» [filed under 12:43-44]
#192 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Dreams usually encode a person's hidden consciousness as a riddle or symbolic parable, and interpreting them requires bestowed insight, from intuition or inspiration up to revelation. «ekseriyetle rüyalar örf-i âm ile değil, ferdin nefsindeki gizli şuur husûsiyetlerine göre söyleyen bir muamma» [filed under 12:43-44]
#193 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · The seven ears are joined to the seven cows; the wording recalls the parable of a grain producing seven ears. «و { وسبع سنبلات } معطوف على { سبع بقرات }»
#194 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · reports · Prophetic biography reports that Kisra sent for the soothsayer Satiḥ to interpret a dream around the Prophet's birth, counted among prophetic portents; Kisra was alarmed and sent Abd al-Masih. «أن كسرى أرسل إلى سطيح الكاهن»
#195 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Al-malaʾ means the leading or distinguished people. «الملأ : أعيان الناس . وتقدم»
