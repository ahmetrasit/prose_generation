#79 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · They blamed one another because some proposed the plan, some accepted it, some opposed it, and others remained silent in approval. «{ يتلاومون } يلوم بعضهم بعضا ؛ لأن منهم من زين ، ومنهم من قبل»
#80 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · After their father died, the brothers judged his generosity mistaken and resolved to harvest without admitting or giving anything to a poor person. «فمات الشيخ، فقال ولده: نحن جماعة وفعل أبينا كان خطأ، فلنذهب إلى جنتنا ولا يدخلها علينا مسكين»
#81 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · God likens Quraysh's test through Muhammad and his guidance to the brothers' test through their father's practice and their religious obligations. «فشبه الله تعالى قريشا بهم، في أنهم امتحنهم بمحمد صلى الله عليه وسلم وهداه كما امتحن أولئك»
#82 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · As the garden owners were punished in their orchard, Quraysh face punishment in their worldly life, while repentance remains open to survivors as it was for the brothers. «فكما حل بأولئك العقاب في جنتهم كذلك يحل بهؤلاء في جميع دنياهم وفي حياتهم» [filed under 68:17, 68:20]
#83 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · كثير من المفسرين · reports · Many exegetes say Quraysh's seven years of famine correspond to the brothers' loss of their garden. «وقال كثير من المفسرين: السنون السبع التي أصابت قريشا هي بمثابة ما أصاب أولئك في جنتهم.»
#84 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · ليصرمنها means to harvest it; صرام is picking dates or fruit from any tree. «وقوله تعالى: ليصرمنها أي ليجدنها، وصرام النخل: جد ثمره وكذلك في كل شجرة»
#85 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · مصبحين means when they entered the morning. «ومصبحين معناه: إذا دخلوا في الصباح»
#86 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · The people tested in the parable are Quraysh; the garden belonged to brothers whose father retained his own provision and gave the surplus to the poor. «وقوله تعالى: إنا بلوناهم يريد قريشا، أي امتحناهم، وأصحاب الجنة فيما ذكر قوم إخوة»
#87 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · One account says their father took the poor with him at harvest so they could share the crop. «وقيل بل كان يحمل المساكين معه في وقت حصاده وجذه، فيجذيهم منه»
#88 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · God tested the people of Mecca with hunger and drought as He tested the owners of the garden when their garden was destroyed. «يعني أهل مكة أي ابتليناهم بالجوع والقحط كما بلونا أصحاب الجنة حين هلكت جنتهم»
#89 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · أهل التفسير · reports · Interpreters tell of a believing man in Yemen, after Jesus, who kept what he needed and gave the surplus and gleanings to the poor; after his death, his three sons planned to harvest before anyone came and deny the poor their share. «وكان مؤمنا وذلك بعد عيسى بن مريم عليهما السلام وكان يأخذ منه قدر قوته وكان يتصدق بالباقي» [filed under 68:17-18]
#90 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · God gave the wealthy man money and children as a test, intending gratitude and obedience; those who fail are stripped of blessings and afflicted. «إنما أعطاه المال والبنين على سبيل الابتلاء والامتحان»
#91 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The orchard owners were tested to thank God and give the poor their due, just as the wealthy man should have used his blessings rightly. «كما كلفنا أصحاب الجنة ذات الثمار ، أن يشكروا»
#92 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · One report says a Muslim man of Thaqīf owned a fertile estate near Ṣanʿāʾ and regularly gave the poor a generous share at harvest; after his death, his sons withheld it and God burned the orchard. «روي أن واحدا من ثقيف وكان مسلما»
#93 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Another report says the orchard owners were Israelites. «وقيل : كانوا من بني إسرائيل»
#94 RAZI-FULL d.606 · مقاتل · reports · Muqātil says they planned to go secretly at dawn, cut the harvest, and keep the poor from learning of it; ṣaram means cutting fruit, and aṣrama means the palms are ready to harvest. «معناه أغدوا سرا إلى جنتكم»
#95 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · Most commentators say they did not make an exception by saying 'if God wills' because they were overconfident they could harvest; another view says they refused to set aside the poor's usual share. «فالأكثرون أنهم إنما لم يستثنوا بمشيئة الله تعالى» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#96 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says a righteous owner had three sons and gave the poor all fallen grapes, missed fruit, stray grain, and spilled threshed produce. «كانت تلك الجنة دون صنعاء بفرسخين، غرسها رجل من أهل الصلاح» [filed under 68:17-18]
#97 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · The father’s charity supported orphans, widows, and poor people; after he died, the sons complained of fewer assets and more dependents. «وكان يعيش في ذلك في حياة أبيهم اليتامى والأرامل والمساكين، فلما مات أبوهم» [filed under 68:17-18]
#98 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · The sons agreed to go before the poor at dawn and secretly harvest the garden so the needy would not know. «فتحالفوا بينهم ليغدون غدوة قبل خروج الناس ثم ليصرمنها ولا تعرف المساكين» [filed under 68:17-18]
#99 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · They meant to cut the date clusters at dawn under night’s dimness; sarm means cutting, and asrama means that the palms are ready to harvest. «ليقطعن ثمر نخيلهم إذا أصبحوا بسدفة من الليل لئلا ينتبه المساكين لهم»
#100 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Qurtubi says people can be held accountable for a firm intention: the garden owners were punished before carrying out their plan, as in the threat concerning wrongdoing in the Sacred Mosque. «ومن يرد فيه بإلحاد بظلم نذقه من عذاب أليم» [filed under 68:17-19, 22:25]
#101 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم · reports · A sound report says both combatants who meet with swords are in Hell, including the victim because he was eager to kill his opponent. «إذا التقى المسلمان بسيفيهما فالقاتل والمقتول في النار» [filed under 68:17-19]
#102 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Qurtubi also points to the discussion under the verse about those who do not persist in wrongdoing. «ولم يصروا على ما فعلوا» [filed under 68:17-19, 3:135]
#103 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The parable addresses Meccans: wealth was a test meant to elicit gratitude, but their arrogance and hostility to Muhammad were followed by famine and drought. «أعطيناهم أموالا ليشكروا لا ليبطروا»
#104 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The garden story was locally known: a Yemenite owner gave God’s due, but after his death his sons withheld it and God destroyed the orchard. «وكانت لرجل يؤدي حق الله تعالى منها فلما مات صارت إلى ولده، فمنعوا الناس خيرها»
#105 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · الكلبي · reports · Al-Kalbi locates the garden two farsakhs from Sanaa and says God burned it. «كان بينهم وبين صنعاء فرسخان ابتلاهم الله بأن أحرق جنتهم»
#106 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Another account places the garden at Dawran, one farsakh from Sanaa, and says its owners were miserly after Jesus was raised. «هي جنة بضوران»
#107 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · One account says the owners harvested at night to keep the poor away; at dawn they found the garden uprooted and blackened. «وكانوا بخلاء فكانوا يجدون التمر ليلا من أجل المساكين»
#108 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · One account says Gabriel swept the garden away, circled it around the Kaaba, and placed it where Taif now stands, explaining its name and fertility. «وكان الطائف الذي طاف عليها جبريل عليه السلام فاقتلعها»
#109 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · البكري · reports · Al-Bakri instead derives Taif’s name from a man of Sadaf who built a wall around the town. «سميت الطائف لأن رجلا من الصدف يقال له الدمون، بنى حائطا»
#110 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · prefers · Qurtubi says harvesters should share crops with poor people present, based on 6:141; the right is distinct from zakat, and he prefers the view that night harvesting was prohibited to avoid depriving them. «وإنما قلنا الأول أصح لأن العقوبة كانت بسبب ما أرادوه من منع المساكين»
#111 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · God tested Meccans with drought as He tested garden owners outside Sanʿāʾ; their righteous father let the poor glean after harvest, but his heirs feared poverty and planned a secret dawn harvest. «{ إنا بلونهم } بلونا أهل مكة شرفها الله تعالى، بالقحط. { كما بلونا أصحب الجنة } يريد البستان»
#112 BAYDAWI-FULL d.685 · holds · The father left poor people what the sickle missed and wind dropped, or the substantial crop gathered under a palm; the sons swore to cut it early while hiding from the poor. «وكان ينادي الفقراء وقت الصرام ويترك لهم ما أخطأه المنجل وألقته الريح»
#113 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · holds · God tested Mecca's people with famine and hunger after the Prophet prayed that Mudar suffer hardship like Joseph's years. «امتحنا أهل مكة بالقحط والجوع حتى أكلوا الجيف والرمم بدعاء النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم»
#114 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · The garden belonged to people of Ṣarwān near Ḍarwān outside Ṣanʿāʾ; their father took his family's food and gave the rest to the poor. «هم قوم من أهل الصلات كانت لأبيهم هذه الجنة بقربة يقال لها ضرو أن»
#115 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · After their father died, the sons feared poverty and resolved to harvest secretly at dawn before the poor appeared; they did not make an exception. «فلما مات قال بنوه أن فعلنا ما كان يفعل أبونا ضاق علينا الامر»
#116 NASAFI-FULL d.710 · reports · Al-Ḥasan says the owners were unbelievers, while the majority holds they were otherwise; the blight destroyed their orchard. «وقال الحسن كانوا كفارا والجمهور على الأول»
#117 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · النبي · reports · The Prophet prayed that God tighten His grip on Muḍar and impose years of famine like Joseph's years; Abū Ḥayyān cites this before the garden parable. «اللهم اشدد وطأتك على مضر واجعلها عليهم سنين كسني يوسف»
#118 ABUHAYYAN-FULL d.745 · reports · One account places the garden in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ; its owner paid God's due, but his sons withheld it after inheriting and the garden was destroyed. «كانت بأرض اليمن بالقرب منهم قريبا من صنعاء لرجل كان يؤدي حق الله منها،» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
