#236 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾānic narrative first shows the youths’ truth-bearing withdrawal and long sleep, then their awakening and exchange that exposes them, and finally their discovery and the mosque built over them. «أورد أولا شطرا من محاورتهم يشير إلى معنى قيامهم لله وثباتهم على كلمة الحق» [filed under 18:10, 18:14, 18:16, 18:19, 18:21]
#237 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The Qurʾān omits names, ancestry, birth, upbringing, occupations, social standing, dates, rulers, cities, persecutors, and the dog’s identity because guidance does not depend on them; historians and early exegetes filled these gaps, producing divergent accounts. «إلى غير ذلك من الأمور التي لا يتوقف غرض الهداية على العلم بشيء منها كما يتوقف عليه غرض البحث التاريخي» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#238 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The narrative presents the youths sleeping in the cave with their dog for the stated long period, protected by the cave’s position and God’s care. «ثم دخلوا الكهف واستقروا على فجوة منه وكلبهم باسط ذراعيه بالوصيد» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:17, 18:18, 18:25]
#239 TABATABAI d.1402 · أهل الرواية والتاريخ · reports · Most historians date the story between Jesus and Muḥammad; it is absent from the two Testaments, though some Muslim reports say Quraysh heard it from Jews, while Christians preserved it extensively. «معظم أهل الرواية والتاريخ على أن القصة وقعت في الفترة بين النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم وبين المسيح» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#240 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Christian accounts resemble Ibn Isḥāq’s report from Ibn ʿAbbās in some respects but differ from Muslims on the number of sleepers, whether they had a dog, and the length of their sleep. «أن المصادر السريانية تذكر عدد أصحاب الكهف ثمانية في حين يذكره المسلمون» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#241 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · After centuries as a ruined, forgotten site, Jordanian excavation uncovered Rujayb; the author considers its archaeological signs and some Muslim reports the strongest fit for the Qurʾānic cave. «وقامت عدة من الأمارات والشواهد الأثرية على كونه هو كهف أصحاب الكهف المذكورين في القرآن» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#242 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Amman stands on the site of Philadelphia, a major and beautiful city before Islam that remained under Roman control from the early second century until the Muslim conquest. «كانت هي وما والاها تحت استيلاء الروم منذ أوائل القرن الثاني الميلادي حتى فتح المسلمون الأرض المقدسة» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#243 TABATABAI d.1402 · reports · Other proposed locations include a cave on Mount Qāsiyūn near Damascus, one at Petra, a Scandinavian cave with seven uncorrupted Roman-looking bodies, and a cave near Nakhchivan. «والكهف الثالث كهف بجبل قاسيون بالقرب من الصالحية بدمشق الشام» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#244 TABATABAI d.1402 · prefers · No evidence identifies any proposed cave as the Qurʾānic one; the Scandinavian and Caucasus claims conflict with the story’s Roman setting and the limits of Roman power. «ولا شاهد يشهد على كون شيء من هذه الكهوف هو الكهف المذكور في القرآن الكريم» [filed under 18:9, 18:10, 18:11, 18:12, 18:13, 18:14, 18:16, 18:17, 18:18, 18:19, 18:21, 18:25, 18:26]
#245 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · The three verses summarize their flight and prayer for rescue, their counted years of sleep, and their awakening to determine which faction counted the duration. «إذ رجع الشبان إلى الكهف فسألوا عند ذلك ربهم قائلين ربنا هب لنا من لدنك» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:12]
#246 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · Each opening verse gives one of the story's three summary elements; later verses add what happened after the people discovered them. «فلإجمال القصة أركان ثلاثة تتضمن كل واحدة من الآيات الثلاث واحدا منها» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:12]
#247 TABATABAI d.1402 · holds · After leaving the people and their gods, they advise hiding in the cave, confident God will extend mercy and provide ease; these are the same gifts they requested on entering. «وهذان اللذان تفرسوا بهما من نشر الرحمة وتهيئة المرفق هما اللذان سألوهما بعد دخول الكهف» [filed under 18:16, 18:10]
#248 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · holds · Rushd means consciousness of what is right; the prayer introduces the Cave allegory and previews what verses 13 onward explain fully. «which latter phrase gives the meaning of the term rushd in this context»
#249 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers passage answers an objection from the Prophet’s opponents and presents their real story without needless details. «their real life aside from needless details becomes evident» [filed under 18:9-26]
#250 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ trials parallel the Prophet and his companions’ preaching phase; perseverance brings God’s easing of hardship and provision, and He does not let His persevering servants lose their way. «God will ease out their hardships the way He did so for them» [filed under 18:9-26]
#251 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · This overview is offered as sufficient to establish the sūrah’s coherence and central theme before its detailed exegesis. «This concise analysis of the meanings of the sūrah is sufficient to explain its coherence and central theme» [filed under 18:1-110]
#252 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The cave-dwellers question was planted by the People of the Book and posed by Quraysh to harass and corner the Prophet, not to seek truth. «These irrelevant questions were obviously not meant to find out the real truth» [filed under 18:9-26]
#253 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Opponents planned to exploit either an inability to answer or an answer conflicting with their own beliefs. «if the Prophet (sws) was able to answer them and the answer was against their beliefs» [filed under 18:9-26]
#254 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an generally counsels ignoring such questions, but answers when useful; the truthful account both answers and trains the Prophet and companions in perseverance during comparable trials. «if some question is beneficial or its answer can be beneficial for education» [filed under 18:9-26]
#255 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The Qur’an preserves the useful, true part of the cave story, correcting false accretions and restoring a major chapter in truth-seekers’ history. «a very important chapter in the history of the seekers of the truth was cleansed from all sorts of wrong details» [filed under 18:9-26]
#256 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The dwellers’ identities, date and ancestry cannot be known securely; beyond the Qur’anic account, claims are folk tales, and further inquiry is discouraged. «Who were the cave-dwellers, when did they inhabit the earth and whose ancestors were they are questions that cannot be answered with certainty» [filed under 18:9-26]
#257 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Islahi’s opinion is that the youths were ancestors of Christians, not Quraysh or Jews, and belonged to the early Christian period under Roman persecution. «in my opinion, they belonged to the initial period of the Christians when they were persecuted by the Romans» [filed under 18:9-26]
#258 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · He says they boldly preached monotheism and accountability, faced imminent stoning, then took refuge in a cave where God supplied extraordinary protection. «They fearlessly invited people to monotheism and warned them of the accountability in the hereafter» [filed under 18:9-26]
#259 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · After their circumstances became known, groups built memorials and claimed them; Christian retellings gradually reduced their story to miracles and unsupported details. «Gradually, this tradition became confined to some miraculous feats and baseless details about them» [filed under 18:9-26]
#260 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Debates over their number and dog obscured their message and sacrifices; Islahi says the true history was later lost as Christianity’s teachings were distorted. «no one knew what their real message was and the extent of sacrifices they gave» [filed under 18:9-26]
#261 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Distorted reports about the youths circulated among Arab Christians, who prompted the question; the Qur’an answered to reassure the Prophet and companions in adversity. «he could receive great assurance and strength in the adverse circumstances he and his companions were passing through» [filed under 18:9-26]
#262 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The youths’ persecution and cave refuge parallel the Prophet and companions’ trials and their coming migrations to Abyssinia, the Thawr cave and Madinah. «the Prophet (sws) and his companions too had to face tremendous trials at the hands of the Quraysh» [filed under 18:9-26]
#263 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The verb rashada means to be guided and granted steadfastness; the expression about one’s affair means guidance in that matter. «The verb رَشَدَ means that he was guided and bestowed with steadfastness»
#264 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The young believers' sense of truth made them disregard danger and expediency when the truth awakened in them. «these were young people. When the sense of honour for the truth once awakens in them»
#265 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Their prayer for guidance and perseverance was wise and apt at this precarious moment. «they supplicated before God for guidance and perseverance at this precarious instant»
#266 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · These verses summarize the whole story before its details, keeping its purpose in view so secondary details do not obscure it; the Qur'an often uses this narrative pattern. «this verse and the next two present a summary of the whole anecdote» [filed under 18:10, 18:11, 18:12]
