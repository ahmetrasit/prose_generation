#193 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The Qur’an usually uses janna for Paradise, but also uses it for earthly gardens: singular in nine verses, dual in five, and plural twelve times; context decides. «وهو الاستعمال الغالب للفظ جنة وجنات في القرآن الكريم - نحو مائة وعشرين مرة - على أنها جاءت بدلالتها الأولى على الجنة المعروفة في الدنيا،»
#194 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Ṣarm means cutting, especially harvesting crops and picking fruit, then developed a figurative sense. «والصرم: القطع، ومنه حصد الزرع وجني الثمر، ثم أخذ دلالته المجازية على»
#195 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · The apparent story is that the garden owners planned to harvest everything at dawn and leave nothing; the author rejects the gloss that they merely omitted saying in shāʾ Allāh. «وظاهر النص، أن خطيئتهم التي أخذوا بها، هي التصميم على صرم جنتهم خفية» [filed under 68:17, 68:18]
#196 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. «أن هذه الجنة كانت لرجل صالح، حدودا قومه وبلده فقيل إنه من أهل اليمن» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:19, 68:20, 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24, 68:25, 68:26, 68:27, 68:28, 68:29]
#197 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The garden owner harvested enough for his family, gave surplus to the needy, and allowed gleaners to collect missed grain and grapes. «ويتصدق بالباقي على المحتاجين، ويترا ما يخطئه المنجل» [filed under 68:17-18]
#198 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · His sons resented this generosity and, after his death, resolved to gather the crop secretly and leave nothing for anyone in need. «وكان بنوه يضيقون بذلك ويحاولون حمله على بما يملك» [filed under 68:17-18]
#199 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The early Meccan account omits personal names, dates, and other details except where they serve its lesson; it identifies the people only as from Ethiopia or Yemen. «لا يتعلق فيها بذكر الأشخاص والأزمنه إلا ما كان من جوهر القصة» [filed under 68:17-29]
#200 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The Quran leaves the story's date unstated and gives no basis for the strange claims that Satan or Gabriel uprooted the trees and carried them to the Kaaba and Taif. «دون أن يشير من قرب أو بعد، إلى ما يسيغ الروايات الغريبة» [filed under 68:17-19]
#201 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · Yunus 10:24 offers a useful parallel for understanding the garden story as a picture of worldly life suddenly cut down. «لأن نستأنس في فهم آيات القلم، بآية يونس في مثل الحياة الدنيا» [filed under 68:17-19]
#202 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The plural pronoun in we tested them refers to the deniers and every oath-breaking, contemptible person. «عائد على المكذبين وكل حلاف مهين»
#203 BINTSHATI d.1419 · أبو حيان · reports · One interpretation compares Quraysh's trial to the garden owners' punishment by citing the seven-year famine, when Quraysh ate hides. «قول من قالوا: النازل بقريش المماثل لأمر الجنة، هو الجدب»
#204 BINTSHATI d.1419 · أبو حيان · reports · Another interpretation compares the garden owners' planned deprivation of the poor with Quraysh's vow before Badr to kill the Prophet and his companions, followed by their defeat. «تشبيه بلاء قريش ببلاء أصحاب الجنة، هو أن أهل الجنة عزموا»
#205 BINTSHATI d.1419 · rejects · The Badr interpretation reads later history into the passage, since Badr occurred about five years after this surah; the warning addresses tyrants generally. «النص، على أي حال، صريح في ردع كل الطغاة المتجبرين»
#206 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The punishment verse points to the lesson of the garden story: tyrants face punishment in this world, while the hereafter's punishment is greater. «يلفت إلى مناط العبرة فيما تلت الآيات من أمر أصحاب الجنة» [filed under 68:17-33]
#207 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The orchard parable warns Quraysh that present peace and blessings do not last: God can remove them suddenly and leave people to face the consequences. «the peace and security they have today should not mislead them to think that they would remain under the same conditions forever»
#208 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The orchard parable exposes the hollowness of Quraysh leaders’ authority: God can remove it instantly, after which their grief and repentance will be useless. «their authority which has made them so conceited and proud that they are making fun of the warnings»
#209 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The plural pronoun ‘them’ refers to the previously described people collectively, confirming that the portrait is of Quraysh leadership as a whole rather than one individual. «this character is not that of a specific individual; it is that of the total leadership of the Quraysh»
#210 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The definite article in ‘the orchard’ is generic in parables, painting a complete situation rather than identifying a particular orchard or historical owners in Yemen or Sana. «Their purpose is only to portray the overall situation so that the reader gets to know the complete picture»
#211 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The owners confidently swore they would harvest the next morning because they believed their orchard had survived every weather change and was secure from calamity. «their orchard had braved various phases of weather change and had become safe and secure from all calamities»
#212 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · The garden parable warns Meccan polytheists who failed to thank God for blessings; a pious owner shared harvest with the poor, but his sons planned to exclude them and a night calamity destroyed the crop. «Hasat zamanı geldiğinde fakirleri çağırır, bahçenin ürünlerinden onlara ikramda bulunurdu»
#213 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · reports · A report says the garden owner's sons invoked their large family to justify cutting the poor's share and secretly harvesting early, after which a disaster ruined the produce. «oğulları, aile fertlerinin çokluğunu ileri sürerek yoksulların payını kesmeye ve bahçenin ürününü sabahleyin erkenden gizlice toplamaya karar vermişler»
#214 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · God promises more blessings to the grateful and punishment to the ungrateful; the Meccan opponents lost prosperity and trade after rejecting the Prophet and eventually fell before Muslims. «verdiği nimete şükredenlere daha fazla nimet vereceğini, nankörlük edenleri de cezalandıracağını haber vermiştir»
#215 STUDYQURAN · Most commentators · reports · Most commentators place the story in Madinah and identify its owners as three Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen who inherited their father’s fertile garden; their father left produce for the poor, but they planned to withhold it and harvest secretly. «Most commentators maintain that these verses were revealed in Madinah and that the story refers to three either Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen» [filed under 68:17-34]
#216 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Qalam is generally considered an early Makkan sūrah. «generally thought to be from the early Makkan period» [filed under 68:1-52]
#217 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qatādah · reports · These early commentators held that verses 1–16, 34–47, and 51–52 are Makkan, while 17–33 and 48–50 are Madinan. «the early commentators IbnʿAbbās and al-Qatādah (d. 117/726) maintained that vv. 1–16» [filed under 68:1-52]
#218 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 17–34 tell the parable of garden owners who lose their harvest because of miserliness. «the parable of the owners of the garden, who lost their harvest because of their miserliness» [filed under 68:17-34]
