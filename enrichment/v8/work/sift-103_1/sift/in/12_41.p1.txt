#40 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · قتادة · reports · Qatāda agrees that يوسف's words finish the interpretation requested by the prisoners without guaranteeing its fulfillment. «قال ابن عباس (¬1) وابن مسعود (¬2) وقتادة (¬3) وغيرهم (¬4)، قالوا: لما عبر رؤياهما،»
#41 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · prefers · Al-Wāḥidī prefers the revelation-based certainty reading as more fitting to a prophet's state. «والصحيح هو الأول؛ لأنه أشبه بحال الأنبياء (¬5)،»
#42 WAHIDI-BASIT d.468 · reports · The word الاستفتاء was explained earlier in the commentary on al-Nisāʾ. «وذكرنا معنى الاستفتاء في سورة النساء (¬6).»
#43 WAHIDI-WAJIZ d.468 · holds · Joseph predicts that one companion will serve his master wine and the other will be crucified and eaten by birds; whether or not the dreams were genuine, the interpretation will happen. «يعني: سيقع بكما ما عبرت لكما»
#44 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · Qaḍā means completing and executing something fully; hence لما قضي means when it was finished, and a qāḍī is so named. «وقضاء الشيء: إحكامه وإمضاؤه، والفراغ منه على التمام، ومنه: (لما قضي)»
#45 JISHUMI d.494 · holds · Istiftāʾ is asking for a legal opinion; fatwā answers the legal question itself, not the reasoning behind it. «والاستفتاء: طلب الفتيا، وهو استفعال منه، والفتيا: جواب حكم المعنى»
#46 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · The cupbearer will serve wine to his master, the king; after three days he will be released and restored to his former position. «{يا صاحبي السجن أما أحدكما} وهو صاحب الشراب، {فيسقي ربه} [يعني الملك] (2) {خمرا}»
#47 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · holds · The baker will be released after three days only to be crucified, so birds eat from his head. «فيصلب فتأكل الطير من رأسه»
#48 BAGHAWI-FULL d.516 · ابن مسعود · reports · Ibn Masud says both men admitted they had invented the dreams and were only playing; Joseph answered that God’s decree was settled regardless. «قال ابن مسعود: لما سمعا قول يوسف قالا ما رأينا شيئا إنما كنا نلعب، قال يوسف: {قضي الأمر الذي فيه تستفتيان}»
#49 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · The one who will serve wine is the cupbearer; one reading has him give his lord a drink, while ʿIkrima's reading uses a passive construction. «يريد الشرابي { فيسقى ربه } سيده»
#50 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · عكرمة · reports · ʿIkrima reads yusqā rabbahu, with the passive meaning that he will be given drink to satisfy him. «وقرأ عكرمة ( فيسقي ربه ) أي يسقي ما يروي به على البناء للمفعول»
#51 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Yusuf interprets the cupbearer's vine as the king and his good standing, with three days in prison before release; the baker's three baskets mean three days before execution. «ثم تخرج وتعود إلى ما كنت عليه ، وقال للثاني : ما رأيت من السلال»
#52 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · holds · Although the men asked about different dreams, al-amr means their shared accusation of poisoning and the outcome of their imprisonment: one would escape and one die. «المراد بالأمر ما اتهما به من سم الملك وما سجنا من أجله»
#53 KASHSHAF-FULL d.538 · reports · Alternatively, the men had fabricated the dreams to test Yusuf; he told them the outcome would occur whether they had spoken truthfully or lied. «وقيل : جحدا وقالا : ما رأينا شيئا ، على ما روي أنهما تحالما له»
#54 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Joseph reportedly told Banu he would return to his former position serving the king, and Majlath would be crucified, after three days. «أما أنت فتعود إلى مرتبتك وسقاية ربك، وقال لمجلث»
#55 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · One report says both prisoners denied having seen dreams and said they had invented them to test Joseph; another says only the man sentenced to crucifixion denied it, while a third says they saw them but later denied it. «ما رأينا شيئا وإنما تحالمنا لنجربك»
#56 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · reports · Some read يسقي from saqā and others from asqā, two verbs with the same meaning; ʿIkrima and al-Jaḥdari read the passive form, meaning he will be given wine to drink. «بضم الياء وفتح القاف أي ما يرويه»
#57 IBNATIYYA-FULL d.542 · holds · Joseph reports knowledge of the unseen granted by God and says the matter has already been decreed. «عن غيب علمه من قبل الله تعالى: إن الأمر قد قضي»
#58 TABRISI d.548 · reports · The cupbearer's three grape clusters meant three more days in prison, then release on the fourth day and restoration to his post; Yusuf calls his master 'lord' as one says lord of a house or estate. «أما العناقيد الثلاثة فإنها ثلاثة أيام تبقى في السجن»
#59 TABRISI d.548 · reports · The other prisoner's baskets meant three more days before he was crucified and birds ate from his head; Yusuf's certain forecast shows he spoke by revelation of the unseen, not ordinary interpretation. «أما السلال الثلاث فإنها ثلاثة أيام تبقى في السجن ثم يخرجك الملك فيصلبك»
#60 TABRISI d.548 · أبو عبيدة · reports · Abu ʿUbayda says buḍʿ denotes a span from three to five years. «البضع ما بين الثلاث إلى الخمس عن أبي عبيده» [filed under 12:39-42]
#61 TABRISI d.548 · قطرب، مجاهد · reports · Qutrub defines buḍʿ as three to seven; this definition is also reported from Mujahid. «وقول قطرب مروي عن مجاهد» [filed under 12:39-42]
#62 TABRISI d.548 · الأصمعي، قتادة · reports · Al-Asmaʿi gives three to nine, as reported by al-Zajjaj, and this range is transmitted from Qatada. «وقول الأصمعي مروي عن قتادة» [filed under 12:39-42]
#63 TABRISI d.548 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas defines buḍʿ as less than ten; most commentators specify seven years in this verse. «وقال ابن عباس: وهو ما دون العشرة» [filed under 12:39-42]
#64 TABRISI d.548 · الكلبي · reports · Al-Kalbi says the seven years in prison came after the five years already spent there. «قال الكلبي: وهذه السبع سوى الخمسة التي كانت قبل ذلك» [filed under 12:39-42]
#65 TABRISI d.548 · أبو عبد الله · reports · Abu ʿAbd Allah says Gabriel taught Yusuf a prayer to recite after every obligatory prayer, asking God for relief and provision from expected and unexpected sources. «وروي عن أبي عبد الله ع قال: علم جبرائيل ع يوسف في حبسه» [filed under 12:39-42]
#66 TABRISI d.548 · أبو عبد الله · reports · A report through Shuʿayb al-ʿAqraqufi says that when the term ended, Yusuf prayed for release by invoking Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, and Jacob; Abu ʿAbd Allah tells the questioner to make the same prayer through Muhammad, ʿAli, Fatima, Hasan, Husayn, and the Imams. «وروى شعيب العقرقوفي عنه ع قال: لما انقضت المدة» [filed under 12:39-42]
#67 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن السائب · reports · Ibn al-Sa'ib says rabb here means master; Joseph predicts that the cupbearer will serve wine to his master after three days and regain his old position. «الرب هاهنا السيد قال ابن السائب لما قص الساقي رؤياه على يوسف»
#68 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · ابن السائب · reports · Ibn al-Sa'ib says Joseph tells the baker that in three days the king will execute and crucify him, and birds will eat from his head. «وقال للخباز بئس ما رأيت»
#69 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · holds · “The matter is decided” means the interpretation has been settled, whether the prisoners' dreams were true or false. «أي فرغ منه وسبقع بكما صدقتما»
#70 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · reports · One answer to why Joseph was certain is that revelation told him; the declaration that the matter was decided shows it was revealed. «أنه حتم ذلك لوحي أتاه من الله وسبيل المنام المكذوب فيه أن لا يقع تأويله»
#71 IBNJAWZI-ZAD d.597 · reports · The alternative says he did not guarantee the events: “the matter is decided” may mean only that he has finished answering their question. «أنه لم يحتم بدليل قوله وقال للذي ظن أنه ناج منهما»
#72 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Joseph could infer that they served the king from their use of rabb for their master in the later statements about serving wine and asking to be remembered. «هما غلامان كانا للملك الأكبر بمصر أحدهما صاحب طعامه» [filed under 12:36, 12:41, 12:42]
#73 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · Joseph interprets the cupbearer's vine as a return to service after three days, and the baker's three baskets as execution and birds eating from his head. «أما حسن العنبة فهو حسن حالك»
#74 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · The two men later said they had seen nothing; one view is that they invented the dreams to test Joseph, while another says they disliked his answer. «واختلف فيما لأجله قالا ما رأينا شيئا فقيل إنهما وضعا هذا الكلام ليختبرا علمه»
#75 RAZI-FULL d.606 · ابن عباس · reports · Ibn Abbas says Joseph gave the interpretation rather than revelation; the verse's description of expectation as ظن also argues against certainty from revelation. «لأن ابن عباس رضي الله تعالى عنهما نقل أنه إنما ذكره على سبيل التعبير»
#76 RAZI-FULL d.606 · reports · He considers revelation possible: God may have disclosed each man's outcome when asked, though it appeared as dream interpretation; alternatively, Joseph's interpretive judgment need not mean an inevitable outcome. «فلما نزل الوحي بذلك الغيب عند ذلك السؤال وقع في الظن أنه ذكره على سبيل التعبير»
#77 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · Joseph told the cupbearer he would return to serving the king after three days, while the other man would be crucified after three days and eaten by birds; whether dreamed or not, the decree was settled. «قال للساقي: إنك ترد على عملك الذي كنت عليه من سقي الملك بعد ثلاثة أيام»
#78 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · reports · Some linguists use سقى and أسقى synonymously; al-Nahhas says most distinguish giving someone water to drink from providing a source of water. «وحكى أهل اللغة أن سقى وأسقى لغتان بمعنى واحد، كما قال الشاعر:
سقى قومي بني»
#79 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · A false dream interpretation does not bind someone; Joseph's interpretation became a decree because he was a prophet and God made it occur to confirm his prophethood. «لا يلزمه وإنما كان ذلك في يوسف لأنه نبي، وتعبير النبي حكم، وقد قال: إنه»
#80 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · قتادة · reports · A report from Qatada says a man told Umar he dreamed of alternating lush growth and drought; Umar predicted alternating faith and apostasy ending in death as an unbeliever, and the man said he had dreamed nothing. «فقد روى عبد الرزاق عن معمر عن قتادة قال: جاء رجل إلى عمر بن الخطاب»
