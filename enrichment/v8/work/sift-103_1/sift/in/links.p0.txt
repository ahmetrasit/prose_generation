# 23 notes filed under other verses, naming 103:1

#1 TABRISI d.548 · reports · An alternative describes people as complaining in hardship and hoarding and withholding during prosperity; the verse's human is a particular type, as in the Sūrat al-ʿAṣr passage, not all humanity. «ظلوم في الشدة يشكو ويجزع كفار في النعمة يجمع يمنع ولم يرد بالإنسان ها هنا العموم»   [filed under 14:34]
#2 RAZI-FULL d.606 · holds · The surahs al-Asr and al-Kawthar mention faith, good works, prayer, and sacrifice; what remains is exhorting truth and patience by publicly rejecting idolatry. «بقي عليك التواصي بالحق والتواصي بالصبر»   [filed under 109:1]
#3 QURTUBI-FULL d.671 · holds · The generic definite al-nafs parallels generic human statements qualified by exceptions; the verse forbids killing any protected life, Muslim or treaty-holder, except for a legally valid cause. «وهذه الآية نهي عن قتل النفس المحرمة، مؤمنة كانت أو معاهدة إلا بالحق» [filed under 6:151, 70:19, 70:22, 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]   [filed under 6:151]
#4 IBNKATHIR d.774 · rejects · The old-age interpretation does not fit the exception, since believers too may become elderly; the intended meaning is the Fire, as in the exception in al-ʿAṣr. «ولو كان هذا هو المراد لما حَسُن استثناء المؤمنين من ذلك ؛ لأن الهَرَم قد يصيبُ بعضهم ،   وإنما المراد ما ذكرناه»   [filed under 95:5]
#5 BURSEVI d.1127 · التأويلات النجمية · reports · They corrupt the innate seed of monotheism by associating partners and rejecting the prophets, failing to water it with faith and righteous deeds; thus they lose human perfection as a seed loses its potential without water. «يفسدون بذر التوحيد الفطري في أرض طينتهم بالشرك والإعراض عن قبول دعوة الأنبياء»   [filed under 2:27]
#6 ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Being worthy of the sanctuary requires reforming surrounding injustice, establishing safety, helping the poor, striving in God’s path, and holding sincerely to Islam, as summarized in Sūrat al-ʿAṣr. «bütün cihâna karşı o etrafın cehalet ve şekāvetini ıslah ve emniyet ü âsâyişini tesis» [filed under 106:3, 106:4]   [filed under 106:3]
#7 QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · Quṭb says the rightly guided period embodied the civilization and banner of faith, good works, and communal exhortation outlined by Sūrat al-ʿAṣr. «هذه بعض ملامح تلك الحقبة السعيدة التي عاشتها البشرية في ظل الدستور الإسلامي»   [filed under 103:3]
#8 IBNASHUR-FULL d.1393 · holds · Qur'an 103:1–3 makes patience complete the means of success; the Qur'an also links patience with leadership, Israel's favor, and divine companionship, as the author notes through al-Ghazali. «وحسبك بمزية الصبر أن الله جعله مكمل سبب الفوز»   [filed under 2:45]
#9 ASAD-NOTES d.1412 · Asad · holds · Surah 100 is Meccan and was revealed after surah 103. «Mecca Period REVEALED after surah 103» [filed under 100:1-11]   [filed under 100:1]
#10 BINTSHATI d.1419 · prefers · As with al-Duha and al-Adiyat, the author reads the opening wāw as directing attention to sensory, observable realities that prepare for an unseen matter; he says the approach suits several other Meccan oath surahs. «القرآن يعدل في هذا الأسلوب عن الأصل اللغوي للتعظيم بالقسم إلى استعمال بلاغي» [filed under 79:1, 79:2, 79:3, 79:4, 79:5]   [filed under 79:1]
#11 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The oath's waw redirects attention from honorification to an undeniable sensory scene that prepares persuasion about disputed unseen matters. «ونعود فنقول إن مثل هذا القسم بالواو في القرآن الكريم ظاهرة أسلوبية عدل فيها البيان القرآني بالقسم عن أصل استعماله الأول للتعظيم،»   [filed under 89:4]
#12 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The passage is among the earliest Quranic oath sequences, alongside 103:1, 95:1-3, and 93:1-2, all early group I texts. «Der Passus stellt zusammen mit 103:1, 95:1–3 und 93:1.2» [filed under 100:1-5]   [filed under 100:1]
#13 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The opening is also compared with the parallel opening of Q69 and the openings of Q52, 55, 83, 89, 93, 95, 103, and 106. «die parallele Eröffnung von Q 69 zu vergleichen, s. aber auch Q 52, 55, 83, 89, 93, 95 (vgl. Kommentar), 103 sowie 106»   [filed under 101:1]
#14 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · This theological lexicon recasts the negatively charged early Meccan human as homo eschatologicus, belonging to both worlds under God’s rule. «Der in Frühmekka durchweg negativ konnotierte Mensch (al-insān, siehe etwa Q 103) wird mit der Einführung des Kollektivs al-ʿālamīn»   [filed under 1:2]
#15 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The opening oath resembles those in earlier suras 93, 95, 100, and 103 and roughly contemporary suras 91 and 92. «Die Sure setzt mit einer Schwureinleitung ein, wie sie bereits in den früheren Suren 93, 95, 100 und 103 und den in etwa gleichzeitigen Suren 91 und 92 erscheint» [filed under 89:1, 89:2, 89:3, 89:4]   [filed under 89:2]
#16 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The Quran distinguishes dynamic fāʿilāt oath series from accumulative lists of separately named phenomena; 93:1-2 and 103:1 are the earliest accumulative examples in group I. «Generell lassen sich im Koran zwei distinkte Typen von Schwüren unterscheiden: einerseits die erstmals in 100:1–5 (Gruppe I) belegten fāʿilāt-Serien» [filed under 93:1-2]   [filed under 93:2]
#17 CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · The formula for those who believe and do good works is characteristic of late Meccan and Medinan texts; the note refers to the literary-critical discussion of 103. «Zu der für spätmekkanische und medinensische Texte charakteristischen Formulierung allaḏīna ʾāmanū wa-ʿamilŭ ṣ-ṣāliḥāt»   [filed under 95:6]
#18 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The loss means failing to earn virtue in worldly life, whose true value is virtue; such a life becomes a curse and ends in doom, as Sūrah al-ʿAṣr shows. «his life will become a curse for him and he will be doomed»   [filed under 7:53]
#19 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Virtue requires both practice and exhortation; urging others is every society member’s duty and will be accounted for, a theme developed at 103:1-3. «Urging others to adopt virtue is the responsibility»   [filed under 90:9]
#20 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Patience accompanies sympathy because virtuous deeds oppose inner desire and feel like climbing a slope; ṣabr means perseverance and steadfastness needed for that effort. «Those who are devoid of this quality lack valour and bravery»   [filed under 90:17]
#21 SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The exception here requires faith and good works, whereas al-ʿAṣr adds mutual exhortation to truth and patience; that added practice prevents further loss of reward. «فلم يقل مثل ما قال في سورة العصر: {والعصر * إِنَّ الإنسان لَفِى خُسْرٍ * إِلاَّ الذين آمَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ الصالحات» [filed under 95:6, 103:1, 103:2, 103:3]   [filed under 95:6]
#22 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · The Tin exception concerns deliverance from the depths of Hell, so faith and good works suffice; al-Asr concerns loss, where omitting mutual exhortation forfeits additional reward. «وذلك لاختلاف الموطنين، فإن سورة العصر في بيان الخسران الذي يصيب الإنسان، وسورة التين فيما يُنجي من دركات النار»   [filed under 95:6]
#23 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 2–3 answer the oath and parallel 95:5–6. «Vv. 2–3 are the response to the oath in v. 1 and are similar in meaning to 95:5–6» [filed under 103:2, 103:3]   [filed under 103:3]
