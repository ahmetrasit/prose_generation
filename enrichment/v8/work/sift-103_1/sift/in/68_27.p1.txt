#41 BINTSHATI d.1419 · reports · Exegetes elaborate the garden story: it belonged to a righteous man who left it to his people and town; reports place it in Yemen near Ṣanʿāʾ, Abyssinia, or among the People of Scripture. «أن هذه الجنة كانت لرجل صالح، حدودا قومه وبلده فقيل إنه من أهل اليمن» [filed under 68:17, 68:18, 68:19, 68:20, 68:21, 68:22, 68:23, 68:24, 68:25, 68:26, 68:27, 68:28, 68:29]
#42 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · While the owners slept, the garden was made barren; in the morning they found it harvested, recognized their loss, and the middle brother reminded them of their neglect of God and His bounty. «وترك الجنة صريماً جرداء خلاء. فلما أصبحوا، تنادوا ليغدوا على حرثهم» [filed under 68:19-29]
#43 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The early Meccan account omits personal names, dates, and other details except where they serve its lesson; it identifies the people only as from Ethiopia or Yemen. «لا يتعلق فيها بذكر الأشخاص والأزمنه إلا ما كان من جوهر القصة» [filed under 68:17-29]
#44 BINTSHATI d.1419 · holds · The punishment verse points to the lesson of the garden story: tyrants face punishment in this world, while the hereafter's punishment is greater. «يلفت إلى مناط العبرة فيما تلت الآيات من أمر أصحاب الجنة» [filed under 68:17-33]
#45 ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · The orchard was so devastated that they could not recognize it at first and thought they had lost their way; they soon saw that their prized possession was gone. «All their desires and wishes were dashed to the ground» [filed under 68:26, 68:27]
#46 STUDYQURAN · Most commentators · reports · Most commentators place the story in Madinah and identify its owners as three Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen who inherited their father’s fertile garden; their father left produce for the poor, but they planned to withhold it and harvest secretly. «Most commentators maintain that these verses were revealed in Madinah and that the story refers to three either Christian or Jewish brothers in Yemen» [filed under 68:17-34]
#47 STUDYQURAN · holds · “We are deprived” recalls 56:67 and broadens the story to all harvests: God, not people, determines whether crops flourish; it may also mean “we are rendered destitute.” «This verse is similar to 56:67; it reflects the broader application of this story to all manner of harvesting»
#48 STUDYQURAN · holds · Al-Qalam is generally considered an early Makkan sūrah. «generally thought to be from the early Makkan period» [filed under 68:1-52]
#49 STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Qatādah · reports · These early commentators held that verses 1–16, 34–47, and 51–52 are Makkan, while 17–33 and 48–50 are Madinan. «the early commentators IbnʿAbbās and al-Qatādah (d. 117/726) maintained that vv. 1–16» [filed under 68:1-52]
#50 STUDYQURAN · holds · Verses 17–34 tell the parable of garden owners who lose their harvest because of miserliness. «the parable of the owners of the garden, who lost their harvest because of their miserliness» [filed under 68:17-34]
