[TABATABAI:95:7/r2] TABATABAI d.1402 · بعض من جعل الخطاب في فما يكذبك للنبي · reports · Some take the address to the Prophet, read ما as ‘who’ and الحكم as ‘judgment,’ asking who can call the judgment-day recompense false when God will judge between him and its deniers. «وبعض من جعل الخطاب في قوله»
[TABATABAI:95:7/r3] TABATABAI d.1402 · rejects · The interpretation that turns the verse into a question about who accuses the Prophet of falsehood is unnecessarily strained. «وأنت خبير بأن فيه تكلفا من غير موجب»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:95:7:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · With bi-, kaḏḏaba means declaring something false; in this construction dīn means judgment rather than religion, as parallel alternation with yawm al-dīn shows. «so dass dīn in solchen Kontexten allgemein mit „Gericht“ und nicht mit „Religion“ zu übersetzen ist»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:95:7:anmerkung/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · Birkeland 1937 · prefers · By analogy with 25:19, Birkeland reads kaḏḏaba transitively and bi- as a domain phrase: what could still accuse you of lying about the Judgment? «Birkeland 1937 argumentiert deshalb dafür, 95:7 analog zu 25:19 zu übersetzen»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:95:7:anmerkung/r3] CORPUSCORANICUM · modern translations · reports · Most modern translations instead ask what could still make the addressee deny the Judgment after God's manifestations of power in verses 4–5. «Die meisten modernen Übersetzungen geben den Vers im Gegensatz dazu mit „Was lässt dich da»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:95:7:anmerkung/r4] CORPUSCORANICUM · prefers · The author notes Birkeland's objection that the mainstream reading makes kaḏḏaba causative to itself, but says Arabic has no separate causative form from Form II and entertains a sense of telling lies about Judgment. «Möglicherweise ist kaḏḏaba + Akk. + bi- hier auch einfach im Sinne von „jemanden in Bezug auf etwas Lügen äußern lassen“ zu verstehen»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:95:7-8:kommentar/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · Birkeland · reports · If verse 5 already concerns the Last Judgment, Birkeland takes baʿdu temporally: after damnation, what could still accuse one of lying about Judgment? «Bezieht man mit Birkeland bereits V. 5 auf das Jüngste Gericht»
[CORPUSCORANICUM:95:7-8:kommentar/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · holds · If verses 4–5 describe youth and old age, baʿdu means after these arguments or signs: the duality of divine care and severity supports belief in resurrection and Judgment. «Bezieht man V. 4.5 dagegen auf Jugend und Altersschwäche des Menschen»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p379/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · On the first reading, no evidence contradicts belief in reward and punishment; the interrogative ma exposes denial driven by blind following or stubbornness and calls people to reflect. «He finds no arguments to support him in this matter.»
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p380/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Imām Farāhī · holds · Farāhī says the first question, under either reading, urges belief after the evidence and protection from doubts raised inwardly or by others. «Im ām Far āhī explains the purport of both these interrogative sentences thus:»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:95:7/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · After recalling human creation, abilities, and gifts, the passage says sound thought should lead to faith and denial has no sound intellectual or evidential basis. «bütün bu kanıtlara rağmen dini inkâr etmenin ilim ve akıl yönünden sağlam bir dayanağının bulunamayacağı»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:95:7/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · reports · The word dīn may also mean the Hereafter and Day of Judgment, which ultimately is a requirement of religion and belief. «Âyetteki dîn kelimesini “âhiret ve yargı günü” olarak anlamak da mümkündür»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17#3/r1] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · After the evidence just given, nothing should make a person deny the final judgment. «والمعنى: أي شيء يجعلك أيها الإنسان مكذّباً بالجزاء بعد هذه الدليل الواضح؟»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17#3/r4] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The sura combines transmitted evidence for judgment, through the revelations given to Moses, Jesus, and Muhammad, with rational evidence from human creation and development. «فالدليل النقلي هو ما أخبرت به الرسالات السماوية، وقد ذكر من هذه الرسالات كبراها وهي رسالات موسى وعيسى ومحمد»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17#3/r5] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The word dīn is chosen instead of recompense, reckoning, or resurrection because it fits the preceding revelations and can mean both religion and judgment; the creator gives humanity the best way to live. «وذلك لما تقدم ذكر مواطن الرسالات ناسب ذلك ذكر الدين، لأن هذه أديان، ولأنه قد يراد بذلك معنى (الدين) علاوة على معنى الجزاء»
[STUDYQURAN:95:7/r1] STUDYQURAN · holds · The question addresses people who deny revelation, resurrection, and other parts of religion. «As translated, this verse is a question posed to those who deny the revelation, the Resurrection, and other aspects of religion.»
[STUDYQURAN:95:7/r2] STUDYQURAN · some · reports · Another reading addresses belief in the Prophet: after the proofs, who could deny the Messenger; some consider this grammatically problematic. «It may also be addressing belief in the Prophet specifically»
[STUDYQURAN:95:7/r3] STUDYQURAN · holds · The verse expresses amazement that people who came from a drop and attained beautiful stature still deny resurrection. «The verse also indicates amazement that anyone could witness human beings coming from a drop»

### notes filed under 98:7
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p407/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · holds · Humans are created in the best mould and can become God's best creatures by recognizing their worth, but indifference can reduce them to the lowest; their ascent and descent have no limit, hence the sages' counsel to know oneself. «The Almighty has created him in the best of moulds with great abilities.»
