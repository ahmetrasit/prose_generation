## ELMALILI: Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361 AH
[ELMALILI:95:1-3#3/r3] ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Mecca's security makes it holier in this respect than contested Holy Land sites and creates an ascent in the oaths from material taste toward spiritual security. «mânevî emniyet zevkine doğru yükselen bir terakki vardır»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3#3/r6] ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · The familiar names Tīn, Zaytūn, Sinai, and the secure city evoke sacred places and their graded religious and worldly blessings, preparing for the answer about human creation. «isimlerinin tanınmış olan ahid mânalarıyla zihinlere sarâhaten veya delâleten ifade ettiği bu mâna»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r1] ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Although tīn literally means fig, he prefers retaining Tīn and Zaytūn as proper names when they designate places, since translating a proper name by its lexical sense misidentifies its referent. «burada öyle terceme etmek pek muvâfık olmayacaktır. Zira müfessirînin birçoğunun beyânına göre»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r10] ELMALILI d.1361 · ابن عباس، فخر الدين الرازي · reports · Al-Rāzī attributes to Ibn ʿAbbās two Holy Land mountains: the fig mountain is Jesus's mission site, and the olive mountain is Syria, where most Israelite prophets were sent. «İmâm Râzî bunu İbn Abbas’ın kavli olmak üzere naklederek şöyle der»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r11] ELMALILI d.1361 · ابن زيد، كعب، ابن عباس · reports · Reported mosque identifications vary: Ibn Zayd says the Damascus and Jerusalem mosques; Kaʿb says Damascus and Iliya; one Ibn ʿAbbās report says Noah's mosque and Jerusalem. «İbn Zeyd Tîn Dımaşk mescidi, Zeytûn Beyt-i Makdis mescidi demiş; Ka‘b da»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r12] ELMALILI d.1361 · كعب، شهر بن حوشب · reports · Town identifications also vary: Kaʿb says Damascus and Jerusalem, while Shahr b. Hawshab says Kufa and Syria; Kufa means the land where Noah lived. «Şehr b. Havşeb de “Tîn” Kûfe, “Zeytûn” Şam demiştir»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r13] ELMALILI d.1361 · prefers · He favors the sacred-place resonance: the familiar fruits and their productive lands, mountains, towns, and mosques evoke beneficial provision, righteous effort, and places where prophetic religion began; together they point to the Holy Land and a secure city. «Bunlar da Tûr-i Sînâ ve Mekke gibi din menşe’i mübarek, şerefli yerler sayılmış olduğundan»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r2] ELMALILI d.1361 · حسن، مجاهد، عكرمة، إبراهيم النخعي، عطاء، مقاتل، كلبى، ابن عباس · reports · One reported view takes tīn and zaytūn literally as familiar fruits or their trees; the cited glosses call them the figs and olives people eat or press. «Hasan’dan, Mücâhid’den, İkrime’den, İbrâhîm Nehaî’den, Atâ’dan, Mukātil’den, Kelbî’den ve daha bazılarından»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r3] ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Without a contextual sign of metaphor, the evident meaning is the familiar fruits; their value as food, fruit, medicine, and trade may explain the oath. «zeytin diye bildiğimiz meyveler olmaktır»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r4] ELMALILI d.1361 · holds · Figs and olives may represent important provisions generally, while Sinai and the secure city represent places of spiritual provision; the oath can thus encompass material and spiritual sustenance. «ehemmiyetli me’kûlâtı temsil edecek birer misâl»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r5] ELMALILI d.1361 · reports · The two fruits may also allude to female and male, as the oath in al-Layl suggests; on this view they can be translated literally. «bu iki meyvenin dişi ve erkekten kinaye olmaları ihtimâli de ba‘îd değildir»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r6] ELMALILI d.1361 · birçok müfessirîn، آلوسي · prefers · Another view takes Tīn and Zaytūn as blessed places rather than fruit, supported by their pairing with Sinai and the secure city; al-Ālūsī favors this view. «Âlûsi de “birçoğunun zâhib olduğu üzere bunlar mübarek, şerefli buk‘alara kasemdir”»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r7] ELMALILI d.1361 · قتادة · reports · Qatāda, as transmitted by Ibn Jarīr, identifies Tīn as the mountain where Damascus stands and Zaytūn as the mountain where Jerusalem stands. «İbn Cerîr de Katâde’den: “Tîn”, Dımaşk’ın bulunduğu cebel, “Zeytûn” Beyt-i Makdis’in»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r8] ELMALILI d.1361 · عكرمة، ربيع · reports · ʿIkrima calls them two mountains; Rabīʿ locates them between Hamadan and Hulwan or among the mountains of Syria. «İkrime bir rivayette de bunlar iki cebeldir»
[ELMALILI:95:1-3/r9] ELMALILI d.1361 · أبو حبيب حارث بن محمر · reports · A report identifies them as Mount Tīnā and Mount Zaytā, named for the figs and olives that grow well there. «“Zeytûn” “Tûr-i Zeytâ” denilen dağlardır»

## QUTB-ZILAL: Sayyid Quṭb, d. 1386 AH
[QUTB-ZILAL:95:1/r1] QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · The surah’s central truth is that the human being’s sound innate nature aligns with faith and reaches its intended completion through it, while deviation makes the person fall low. «الحقيقة الرئيسية التي تعرضها هذه السورة هي حقيقة الفطرة القويمة التي فطر الله الإنسان عليها»
[QUTB-ZILAL:95:1/r10] QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · prefers · He cannot be certain of a specific interpretation, but considers places or memories connected to faith—or perhaps humanity’s original life in the Garden—the likeliest reading, fitting the surah’s central truth. «إن الأقرب أن يكون ذكر التين والزيتون إشارة إلى أماكن أو ذكريات ذات علاقة بالدين والإيمان»
[QUTB-ZILAL:95:1/r2] QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · holds · Mount Sinai is where Moses was called, and the secure city is Mecca and the Sacred House; both have clear ties to religion and faith. «وطور سينين هو الطور الذي نودي موسى»
[QUTB-ZILAL:95:1/r3] QUTB-ZILAL d.1386 · قول مأثور · reports · One reported view takes the fig as a reference to Mount Tina near Damascus. «قيل : إن التين إشارة إلى طور تينا بجوار دمشق»
