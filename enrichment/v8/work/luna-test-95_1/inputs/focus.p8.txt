## KURANYOLU-TEFSIR: Hayreddin Karaman, Mustafa Çağrıcı, İbrahim Kâfi Dönmez, Sadrettin Gümüş
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:95:1/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · holds · God swears by four signs of His knowledge, artistry, and power—fig and olive as bodily food, Sinai as revelation's spiritual food, and secure Mecca—to affirm that He made humans best and endowed them to bear physical and spiritual duties (cf. 17:70). «incire, zeytine, mânevî gıdası olan vahyin indiği Sînâ dağına ve “emin belde”ye (Mekke), insanların muhtaç oldukları maddî ve mânevî ikramların mükemmel örneklerine yemin ederek insanı en güzel biçimde yarattığını»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:95:1/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirler · reports · One symbolic view takes fig and olive as lands rich in those trees, Palestine and Syria, symbols of the blessing of prophetic teachings conveyed there. «incir ve zeytin, mecaz olarak bu ağaçların çokça bulunduğu toprakları, yani Akdeniz’in doğusunda bulunan Filistin ve Suriye’yi simgelemektedir»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:95:1/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirler · reports · Other symbolic interpretations identify the fig with Mecca's Sacred Mosque and the olive with Jerusalem's al-Aqsa Mosque. «“tîn” ve “zeytûn” kelimeleri hakkında, ilkiyle Mekke’deki Mescid-i Haram’ın, ikincisiyle Kudüs’teki Mescid-i Aksâ’nın kastedildiği»
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:95:1/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Şevkânî · rejects · Shawkani argues that these symbolic interpretations lack rational or transmitted evidence. «Şevkânî’nin de haklı olarak belirttiği gibi bu tür yorumların aklî ve naklî dayanağı yoktur»

## SAMARRAI-ASRAR: فاضل صالح السامرائي
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17#3/r11] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The ending returns to the opening: the one who sent the great revelations and their exalted laws is the wisest judge. «فإن الذي أنزل هذه الشرائع العظيمة وما تضمنته من أحكام سامية هو أحكم الحاكمين»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r1] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · reports · The opening oath may refer to the familiar fig and olive trees. «والتين والزيتون قد يكون قصد بهما الشجران المعروفان»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r10] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · مفسرون · reports · Another interpretation takes fig and olive as two Holy Land mountains, called Tour Tina and Tour Zaita in Syriac, where those trees grow. «وقيل: إن المقصود بالتين والزيتون جبلان من الأرض المقدسة يقال لهما بالسريانية طور تينا وطور زيتا»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r11] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · rejects · On the mountain interpretation, the connection between the two names and what follows is not clear without strained reasoning. «والعلاقة بين التبين والزيتون وما بعدهما ليس ظاهرة على هذا إلا بتكلُّف»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r12] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · مفسرون · reports · A reported interpretation identifies three places of major prophetic missions: Jerusalem for Jesus, Sinai for Moses, and Mecca for Muhammad. «هذه محال ثلاثة بعث الله في كل واحد منها نبياً مرسلاً من أولي العزم أصحاب الشرائع الكبار»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r13] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · reports · Al-Tibyān reads the oath as the three prophetic places and explains them as successive manifestations of Jesus, Moses, and Muhammad and their major religious communities. «جاء في (التبيان في أقسام القرآن)»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r14] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · reports · Al-Tibyān says the oath ascends from the place of Jesus to Moses and then Muhammad, paralleling a Torah passage about Sinai, Seir, and Paran. «ونظير هذا بعينه من التوراة التي أنزلها الله على كليمه موسى: (جاء من طور سيناء»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r15] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · prefers · The author prefers the three prophetic locations because their relation to one another is clear on that reading. «وهذا هو الراجح فيما أرى لأن المناسبة بين هذه المحالّ المُقْسَمِ بها ظاهرة على هذا»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r16] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The sequence begins with fig then olive, with olive ranked higher because God calls it blessed; it serves as fruit, food, and lamp oil. «والزيتون أشرف وأفضل من التين فقد شهد الله له أنه شجرة مباركة»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r2] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · مفسرون · reports · Exegetes explain the paired trees as two kinds, one whose fruit has no seed and one whose fruit has a seed. «ذكر المفسرون لاختيار هذين الشجرين للقسم بهام أسبابا عدة، فقد ذكروا أنه أقسم بنوعين من الشجر»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r20] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The oath progresses from fig to olive to Sinai to the secure city, moving from the noble to the noblest and ending at the home of the final, most honored mission. «فأنت ترى أنه تدرّج من التين إلى الزيتون إلى طور سينين إلى بلد الله الأمين»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r3] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · رسول الله · reports · A report says the Prophet received a plate of figs, ate them, and told his companions that if any fruit came from Paradise, it would be this one because Paradise's fruit has no seeds. «أنه أُهديَ لرسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم طبق من تين فأكل منه وقال لأصحابه»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r4] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · reports · A report says Adam used fig leaves to cover his nakedness after it was exposed in Paradise. «وقد ذكر أن آدم خصف من ورقه ليستر عورته حين انكشفت في الجنة»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r6] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · reports · The author speculates that the eight verses might correspond to Paradise's eight gates, but leans toward this being pure conjecture. «فإن عدد آيات هذه السورة ثمانية وهُنَّ بعددِ أبواب الجنة. قد يكون هذا القول خَرْصاً محِضاً وأنا أميلُ إلى ذلك»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r7] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · reports · He notes numerical patterns elsewhere: eight repetitions of the blessing question in descriptions of Paradise and seven in warnings, corresponding to Paradise's and Hell's gates. «عند الكلام في وصف الجنة ثماني مرات بعدد أبواب الجنة»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r8] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · reports · Other proposed numerical patterns say Sura al-Qadr has thirty words for Ramadan's days and places huwa at word 27 to signal the 27th night. «وقالوا: إن سورة القدر ثلاثون كلمة بعدد أيام شهر رمضان، وإن قوله: (هي) في قوله تعالى»
[SAMARRAI-ASRAR:r17/r9] SAMARRAI-ASRAR · holds · The author cautions that many such numerical correspondences may simply be coincidences. «وعلى أي حال فإن كثيراً من هذه العلاقات ربما كانت موافقات والله أعلم»

## SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT: فاضل صالح السامرائي
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p420/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · بعض المفسرين · reports · Some exegetes identify the oath's fig and olive as the familiar trees, one fruit seedless and the other containing seeds. «فقد ذكروا أنه أقسم بنوعين من الشجر»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p420/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · النبي · reports · A report says the Prophet ate a plate of figs and called it a fruit from Paradise because Paradise's fruit has no seeds; Adam also used fig leaves to cover himself. «أن التين من شجر الجنة فقد روي أنه أُهدي لرسول الله»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p420/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · reports · The olive is described as a blessed tree in the Qur'an. «وأما الزيتون فإنه شجرة مباركة كما جاء في التنزيل العزيز.»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p421/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · rejects · The author doubts that the fig's alleged Paradisal origin explains the surah's eight verses and eight gates of Paradise, calling that connection likely conjecture despite parallels elsewhere. «وقد يكون هذا القول خرصاً محضاً وأنا أميل إلى ذلك»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p421/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · reports · He cites proposed numerical correspondences: eight Paradise descriptions, seven warnings for Hell, thirty words in al-Qadr, and its word “she” as the twenty-seventh word; he warns many such links may be coincidences. «وعلى أي حال فإن كثيراً من هذه العلاقات ربما كانت موافقات»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p421/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · بعض المفسرين · reports · Another interpretation identifies the fig and olive with two Holy Land mountains, called Ṭūr Tīnā and Ṭūr Zaytā in Syriac, where those trees grow. «إن المقصود بالتين والزيتون جبلان من الأرض المقدسة»
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p422/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · holds · The sequence begins with the fig and the more excellent olive, which God calls blessed; olive serves as fruit, food accompaniment, and lamp oil. «فقد شهد الله له أنه شجرة مباركة»
