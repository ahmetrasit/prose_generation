[STUDYQURAN:95:1/r2] STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Some read them symbolically: fig as Damascus's mosque, perhaps Noah's mosque at Mount Jūdī, and olive as Jerusalem's Holy House; others identify the two mountains or cities. «the fig symbolizes the mosque (meaning place of prostration, as there would not have been a building for that purpose at the time of the revelation) in Damascus»
[STUDYQURAN:95:1/r3] STUDYQURAN · others · reports · Another interpretation makes the fig the mosque of the Companions of the Cave and the olive the mosque in Jerusalem; another sees two Levantine mountains. «Another interpretation takes the fig as a reference to the “mosque” of the Companions of the Cave (18:9) and the olive as a reference to the “mosque” in Jerusalem»
[STUDYQURAN:95:1/r4] STUDYQURAN · others · reports · A further view makes the fig the Garden leaves Adam and Eve used to cover themselves and the olive the blessed tree; these may foreshadow human abasement and intellect's light. «Others say that the fig refers to the leaves of the Garden (7:22), with which Adam and Eve covered themselves, and the olive refers to the blessed olive tree in 24:35»
[STUDYQURAN:95:intro/r1] STUDYQURAN · reports · Al-Tīn is likely an early-middle Makkan sūrah revealed after Sūrah 85, though some consider it Madinan; it is named for the fig in verse 1. «A Makkan sūrah, al-Tīn was most likely revealed in the early part of the middle Makkan period, shortly after Sūrah 85»
[STUDYQURAN:95:intro/r3] STUDYQURAN · the Prophet · reports · A hadith says whoever recites the sūrah through its closing question and answers “Indeed” is among its witnesses. «According to an oft citedḥadīth, the Prophet said, “Whosoever among you recites By the fig and the olive through to is not God the most just of judges»
