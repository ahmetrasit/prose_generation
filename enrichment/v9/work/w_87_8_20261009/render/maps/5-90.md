# 5:90: 30 questions

## 5:90/q01 Why are believers addressed here?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ · interpretation]
- 5:90/q01/p1 All believers are called to abandon entrenched pre-Islamic habits and obey as believers. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan say the desires remained among believers; Biqaʿi says faith entails submission. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 5:90/q02 What does khamr cover?  [ٱلْخَمْرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names grape wine technically or any intoxicant; the reports and Medina's non-grape drinks support the broader application.
- 5:90/q02/p1 Every intoxicating or mind-clouding drink, and in the broadest reading every intoxicating substance. — ʿUmar names barley, wheat, grapes, dates, honey and raisins and defines what veils intellect; Ibn ʿAbbas and prophetic reports generalize to intoxicating fermented drinks, including maize and sult. Ibn ʿUmar and Anas report little grape wine in Medina. Asad includes drugs; Study Quran cites ʿUmar. Al-Tabatabaʾi reports a distinction between divine prohibition of wine and prophetic prohibition of other intoxicants, and Musa b. Jaʿfar classifies substances by consequences. [25 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عمر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أنس بن مالك,ابن عمر,عمر); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,موسى بن جعفر عليه السلام); ASAD-NOTES(all lexicographers); STUDYQURAN(ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 5:90/q02/p2 Specifically fermented grape juice that intoxicates in quantity, though some who state this extend the ruling beyond it. — Tabrisi states this lexical definition and separately transmits broader reports; Alusi reports grape juice as one sense and broader use as another. Study Quran calls grape wine the technical sense. [3 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb)]

## 5:90/q03 Why is wine called khamr?  [ٱلْخَمْرُ · meaning]
turns on: The explanations derive the name from its effect on reason, its fermentation, or separation of liquid from sediment.
- 5:90/q03/p1 It covers or clouds the intellect. — Zajjaj, Jishumi, Tabrisi, Razi, Asad and ʿUmar connect the name to covering; Jishumi gives vessels, khimar, crowds, dough, sorrow and concealed testimony as related usage. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(all lexicographers); STUDYQURAN(ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb)]
- 5:90/q03/p2 It is named for being left to ferment and change in smell. — Ibn al-Aʿrabi supplies this etymology in Jishumi and Razi; al-Khalil associates its derivation with fermentation and boiling. [3 notes: JISHUMI(ابن الأعرابي,الخليل); RAZI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي)]
- 5:90/q03/p3 It is named for straining the clear liquid from settled sediment. — Saʿid b. al-Musayyib gives this explanation. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن المسيب)]

## 5:90/q04 What is maysir?  [ٱلْمَيْسِرُ · meaning]
turns on: The notes differ between a general class of gambling, a broader distraction criterion, and specific pre-Islamic forms.
- 5:90/q04/p1 Gambling of every kind, including wagering with arrows, dice, chess and small games. — Mujahid includes Persian dice, arrows and every gambling kind; Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn ʿUmar and others gloss gambling. Reports extend it to children's walnuts and eggs, knucklebones, chess and backgammon. Zajjaj, Wahidi and Study Quran identify camel gambling as its original or paradigmatic case. Ibn Qutayba explains yassartu and yāsir; Jishumi and Tabrisi derive it from yusr, ease, while Azharī says it first named the apportioned camel. Tabrisi also reports a folk etymology of the left hand as easing work by assisting the right. [41 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ابن عمر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله,علي بن أبي طالب,مجاهد وعطاء وطاوس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الأزهري); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الباقر,قيل); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عمر,الأعرج,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,راشد بن سعد، حمزة بن حبيب,عطاء، مجاهد، طاوس,علي بن أبي طالب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); STUDYQURAN]
- 5:90/q04/p2 Anything that distracts from God's remembrance and prayer. — Al-Qasim b. Muhammad gives this broader functional definition; it is reported by Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Kathir. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(القاسم بن محمد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(القاسم بن محمد)]
- 5:90/q04/p3 The older usage includes arrow lotteries over wealth and produce, or trading meat for one or two sheep. — Abu al-Aʿraj gives arrow stakes; Saʿid b. al-Musayyib gives the meat exchange; Ibn Kathir repeats both. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو الأعرج,سعيد بن المسيب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأعرج,سعيد بن المسيب)]

## 5:90/q05 How did the old camel maysir work, and how many shares were there?  [ٱلْمَيْسِرُ · reports]
turns on: The descriptions agree on arrow allotment but transmit twenty-eight, ten, or no known number.
- 5:90/q05/p1 A camel was slaughtered and divided by named arrows; winners received shares and could distribute them to the needy. — Ibn Qutayba locates it in harsh winter, ties the charitable benefit to 2:219, and says winners were praised while abstainers were called baram; Zajjaj describes wooden named arrows. Ibn ʿAtiyya notes the game offered pleasure and benefit; Abu Hayyan says defenders prized gains and victory; Qurtubi identifies commercial gain as a benefit. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 5:90/q05/p2 The camel was divided into twenty-eight shares. — Al-Aṣmaʿī gives this number; Wahidi transmits the same dispute. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(الأصمعي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي وأبو عمرو الشيباني وأبو عبيدة)]
- 5:90/q05/p3 The camel was divided into ten shares. — Abu ʿAmr al-Shaybani gives this number; Wahidi transmits it. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو عمرو الشيباني); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي وأبو عمرو الشيباني وأبو عبيدة)]
- 5:90/q05/p4 The number of shares is unknown. — Abu ʿUbayda says he does not know; Wahidi transmits it. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو عبيدة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي وأبو عمرو الشيباني وأبو عبيدة)]

## 5:90/q06 What are the anṣāb?  [ٱلْأَنصَابُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the erected objects are worshipped idols, sacrificial altars, or, in one report, games.
- 5:90/q06/p1 Erected idols or stones worshipped as idols. — Ibn Qutayba, the majority in Mawardi and many later commentators identify worshipped idols; the root means upright, with naṣb singular and varying vocalization. Tabrisi cites al-Aʿsha's line about an erected idol. Alusi distinguishes unshaped worshipped stones from fashioned aṣnām; Asad extends the reference metaphorically to saint worship, magical objects and taboo. [24 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الجمهور); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; THALABI; TABRISI(الأعشى); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)]
- 5:90/q06/p2 Stones or altars at which animals were sacrificed to idols. — Muqatil and Abu Muslim specify sacrificial stones; Ibn ʿAbbas is so transmitted by Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Kathir. Ibn ʿAtiyya says some accorded the stones special merit; the wife of al-Tufayl mentions Dhu al-Shara. Kuran Yolu locates stones around the Kaʿba; Study Quran ties the altars to 5:3. [11 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(امرأة الطفيل بن عمرو الدوسي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، عطاء، سعيد بن جبير، الحسن); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:90/q06/p3 Backgammon and chess. — Qurtubi reports this as an alternative attributed to some, alongside idols. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 5:90/q07 What are the azlām?  [ٱلْأَزْلَٰمُ · referent]
turns on: The word is used for both divining and gambling lots; the 5:3 food context is al-Tabatabaʾi's deciding clue, while al-Azhari rejects the gambling identification here.
- 5:90/q07/p1 Divining arrows or lots consulted before undertaking an action. — Accounts give two marked commands, three arrows marked yes, no and blank, and arrows kept at the Kaʿba; Suraqa used them during the Hijra. Bukhari says a zalam lacks feathers. Divination concerned travel, war, residence and trade. Kuran Yolu calls it predictive and opposed to monotheism. [26 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق وغيره,سراقة بن جعشم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، عطاء، سعيد بن جبير، الحسن); BIQAI-FULL(البخاري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(المعترض,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 5:90/q07/p2 Pebbles used as lots for whether to go on a campaign or stay. — Saʿid b. Jubayr reports pebbles; al-Thawri is reported similarly. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 5:90/q07/p3 Arrows or other implements used to allot gambling shares. — Wahidi reports this but says al-Azhari rejects the identification; Ibn ʿAtiyya and Tabrisi acknowledge gambling arrows, while Elmalılı includes sticks and dice. Al-Tabatabaʾi prefers lottery arrows because of 5:3's food context and says the word would otherwise be ambiguous; he opposes taking the verse as a ban on istikhara. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI+]

## 5:90/q08 What does rijs mean here?  [رِجْسٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: The glosses emphasize repulsiveness, sin or evil, divine displeasure, or the resulting legal ban.
- 5:90/q08/p1 Filth or something vile and repugnant, chiefly moral or religious foulness in this context. — Ibn Qutayba gives stench as root sense; Zajjaj and Wahidi describe ugly deeds and a loud-sound root attested by a poet's rajjās and thunder clouds. Tabrisi, Razi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Samin and Alusi preserve that derivation. Ibn ʿAtiyya distinguishes rijz punishment and riks excrement, while Ibn Durayd calls rijs evil. Bursevi contrasts rational repugnance with natural najās. Ibn ʿAshur includes visible and inward filth and cites 9:125 and 33:33. Al-Tabatabaʾi says benefits in 2:219 do not yield lasting good. [43 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن دريد,الراغب,الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن دريد,الزجاج); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 5:90/q08/p2 Sin or evil. — Muqatil calls it sin; Ibn Zayd and Zayd b. Aslam evil; Saʿid b. Jubayr sin. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن جبير)]
- 5:90/q08/p3 God's displeasure or wrath. — Ibn ʿAbbas gives this gloss in Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 5:90/q08/p4 That which is forbidden. — Mawardi derives the legal sense from basic filth and prohibition. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 5:90/q09 What does the singular predicate rijs describe, and why is it singular?  [رِجْسٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: The issue is whether a singular generic predicate directly covers all four nouns or ellipsis supplies a genitive or repeated predicates.
- 5:90/q09/p1 It predicates filth of all four named things directly; as a generic noun or verbal noun it can remain singular. — Abu Hayyan and Samin say direct predication is stronger and compare 9:28; Alusi says the verbal noun does not vary in number, and Ibn ʿAshur says the singular identifies the items with filth. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 5:90/q09/p2 An omitted genitive such as the affair, use, or action of the four things is the subject described as filth. — Zamakhshari and Abu al-Baqa supply the omitted term; Tabrisi spells out drinking or handling wine, worshipping idols and drawing lots. Abu Hayyan and Samin reject the need for deletion. [5 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 5:90/q09/p3 Rijs predicates wine alone, with predicates of the remaining coordinated nouns understood by ellipsis. — Abu al-Baqa, Baydawi, Biqaʿi and Alusi give this parse; Biqaʿi says one collective predicate is insufficient. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:90/q09/p4 Rijs predicates the last noun, with earlier predicates elided. — Samin gives the reverse parse and compares 9:62. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 5:90/q10 How does min ʿamal al-shayṭān attach grammatically?  [مِّنْ عَمَلِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical force of min and whether an omitted genitive changes the causal reading.
- 5:90/q10/p1 It qualifies rijs as an adjective in the nominative sense. — Samin gives the iʿrāb; Alusi adds that min can mark causal origin in Satan's temptation. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:90/q10/p2 Min marks origin from Satan's work. — Alusi allows this alternative and questions the claim that an omitted genitive makes causal connection or originative min unnecessary. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 5:90/q11 In what sense are these things Satan's work?  [مِّنْ عَمَلِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether attribution means his incitement or his origination of the practices.
- 5:90/q11/p1 Satan invites, adorns, and incites people to the practices, although the physical things are God's creation. — Muqatil and Abu Hayyan compare 28:15; Tabari says God neither urges nor approves them. Tabrisi links intoxication with loss of reason, gambling with base traits, idols with shirk and lots with poor judgment. Razi cites 9:28 and 24:26 for impurity's association. Ibn ʿAshur and others call the attribution an intensification of repulsion. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:90/q11/p2 Satan originated the practices and people followed him. — Qurtubi offers this alongside incitement and embellishment. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 5:90/q12 What does ijtanibūhu mean?  [فَٱجْتَنِبُوهُ · meaning]
- 5:90/q12/p1 Keep apart from it, put it to one side, and leave the condemned practices. — Tabari says reject and refrain; Wahidi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Biqaʿi explain the spatial image of keeping apart; Bursevi applies it to impurity. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 5:90/q13 To what does the singular pronoun in 'avoid it' refer?  [فَٱجْتَنِبُوهُ · referent]
turns on: The singular can resume the unified predicate, the whole list, an implied act, or Satan's work; direct reference is defended as rhetorically stronger.
- 5:90/q13/p1 It refers to rijs, the unified filth covering all four items. — Saʿid b. Jubayr says the phrase embraces the list; Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir and Ibn ʿAshur give rijs as antecedent; Abu Hayyan and Samin prefer direct reference as stronger, with the fāʾ consequence noted by Ibn ʿAshur. [12 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 5:90/q13/p2 It refers to the named things collectively. — Baydawi and Nasafi give this as a possibility; Ibn ʿAshur says the command concerns their harmful intended use. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:90/q13/p3 It refers to an omitted noun meaning their conduct, consumption, or practice. — Zamakhshari, Abu al-Baqa and Razi offer the ellipsis; Abu Hayyan rejects its necessity, and Samin prefers direct reference. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 5:90/q13/p4 It refers to Satan's work, or Satan himself. — Tabrisi and Nasafi allow Satan's work; Alusi also mentions Satan himself. [3 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 5:90/q14 What force does the command to avoid these practices have?  [فَٱجْتَنِبُوهُ · law]
turns on: The command's imperative force is accepted; the disputed auxiliary inference is whether the word rijs alone yields prohibition.
- 5:90/q14/p1 It establishes a binding prohibition of wine, gambling, idol rites and divining lots. — Muqatil and Saʿid b. Jubayr compare avoiding idols in 22:30; Ibn ʿAtiyya and Qurtubi add hadith and consensus. Tabrisi gives four proofs: impurity, Satanic attribution, imperative abstention and success conditioned on avoidance. Qurtubi says command and rebuke are stronger than the declarative ban of 6:145; Ibn ʿAshur reads the listed harmful uses as prohibited. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 5:90/q14/p2 The label rijs itself proves prohibition by analogy to other forbidden filth. — Some invoke 6:145's carrion, blood and pork; Ibn ʿAtiyya and Abu Hayyan report the inference as problematic. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 5:90/q15 What does 'so that you may prosper' promise?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The glosses identify success as prosperity and reward, with one report emphasizing meeting God.
- 5:90/q15/p1 Abstention is a means to success, reward and prosperity with God. — Muqatil and Saʿid give the simple gloss; Tabari explains prosperity with God and cross-refers 2:189. Muhammad b. Ishaq specifies escaping punishment and gaining promised reward; Tabrisi says winning reward; Biqaʿi says attaining aims. Ibn ʿAshur adds that other persistent sins condition hope and compares 2:21. Kashshaf and Nasafi infer indulgence entails ruin. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,محمد بن إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:90/q15/p2 The phrase expresses hope of meeting God tomorrow. — Muhammad b. Kaʿb al-Qurazi gives this eschatological gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب القرطبي)]

## 5:90/q16 Why this emphatic wording and sequence of the four practices?  [إِنَّمَا ٱلْخَمْرُ وَٱلْمَيْسِرُ وَٱلْأَنصَابُ وَٱلْأَزْلَٰمُ رِجْسٌۭ مِّنْ عَمَلِ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنِ فَٱجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Different explanations focus on cumulative emphasis, the leading position of wine, a progression of harms, or shared pagan custom.
- 5:90/q16/p1 The stacked expressions reinforce the ban, especially on wine and gambling, by placing them with pagan rites and tying avoidance to success. — Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and al-Tabatabaʾi enumerate innamā, idols, filth, Satan, the imperative and success; Zamakhshari adds 22:30. Ibn ʿAtiyya says idols in the list make other practices base. Wahidi, Tabrisi and multiple hadith comparisons liken drinkers to idol worshippers. Bursevi and Elmalılı say wine and gambling are the chief target; Zamakhshari says 5:91 singles them out. Ibn ʿAbbas reports Companions equated wine with shirk. Burhan reads the wording as a reordered call to abstain from idols as impurity. [24 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الباقر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BURHAN]
- 5:90/q16/p2 Wine comes first because the occasion concerned its harms and it encompasses sin. — Abu Hayyan gives this reason for the order. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 5:90/q16/p3 The order follows linked corruptions: wine wastes wealth, gambling corrupts wealth, idols show open shirk and arrows hidden shirk. — Biqaʿi develops this pairwise order and says all are pagan customs sharing worldly and religious harm. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 5:90/q16/p4 The four are grouped because all were pre-Islamic polytheist customs requiring avoidance. — Zamakhshari states this grouping; Abu Hayyan says altar stones remove lingering reverence or idol grouping assimilates the other practices. Kuran Yolu warns about shrine vows, graveside sacrifices and modern superstitions. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 5:90/q17 What harms and benefits of wine and gambling explain the ban?  [* · interpretation]
- 5:90/q17/p1 Their limited apparent benefits are outweighed by moral, social and religious harms. — Abu Hayyan records claims of wine relieving worry, giving courage and generosity and gambling yielding wealth, then says wine destroys reason and wealth and leads to killing, hatred and sins while gambling takes wealth unjustly; a poet illustrates waste. Kashshaf and Razi point to 5:91's enmity and diversion from remembrance and prayer. Kuran Yolu adds observable social and bodily harm and argues moderate use cannot control the greater harm; al-Tabatabaʾi says 2:219's benefits have no lasting human good. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 5:90/q18 Are small quantities of intoxicating non-grape drinks forbidden?  [ٱلْخَمْرُ · law]
turns on: The dispute concerns whether the non-grape drink's small amount follows its intoxicating large amount; the necessity note concerns exceptional need.
- 5:90/q18/p1 Yes: any drink intoxicating in a large amount is forbidden even in a small amount. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and Abu Hayyan report the majority; both report consensus for any small or large amount of pure grape wine. Biqaʿi states no amount distinction; Ibn Kathir transmits every intoxicant unlawful; al-Tabatabaʾi transmits every intoxicant as wine. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أكثر الأمة,الأمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 5:90/q18/p2 A non-intoxicating amount of a non-grape drink may be consumed even if much would intoxicate. — Abu Hanifa and some Kufans hold this; Ibn ʿAtiyya rejects it. Abu Hayyan notes the dispute. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حنيفة وبعض فقهاء الكوفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 5:90/q18/p3 Dire necessity can permit otherwise banned intoxicants. — Asad restricts the exception to unavoidable need such as illness or accident, citing 5:3. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 5:90/q19 Do chess, backgammon and games without stakes fall under the ban?  [ٱلْمَيْسِرُ · law]
turns on: Whether the shared harms suffice to extend the prohibition to unstaked games; the juristic rulings differ.
- 5:90/q19/p1 Yes, even without stakes, because they invite obsession, hostility and distraction from prayer and remembrance. — Qurtubi extends wine and gambling's common rationale; Ibn Kathir transmits hadith against nard, grades one marked-dice report gharīb, notes Abu Musa's report may be his own statement, and lists Malik, Abu Hanifa and Ahmad as forbidding chess. Ibn ʿUmar says chess worse than nard. Al-Qasim's maysir definition is functional. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو موسى الأشعري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمر,علي بن أبي طالب,مالك، أبو حنيفة، أحمد); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 5:90/q19/p2 Chess is disliked rather than categorically forbidden. — Ibn Kathir reports al-Shafiʿi's ruling; Study Quran says neither chess nor backgammon was legally banned and both remain popular. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي); STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 5:90/q20 Is wine itself ritually impure?  [رِجْسٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether rijs denotes physical impurity and what to infer from street disposal.
- 5:90/q20/p1 Yes: its description as rijs and the order to avoid it establish impurity. — Qurtubi reports the majority and defends inference from general texts and analogy; he replies that Medina's road disposal was practical because homes lacked wells and latrines and also publicly showed destruction. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور العلماء)]
- 5:90/q20/p2 No: wine is pure as a substance, while drinking it is prohibited. — Qurtubi attributes this to Rabiʿa, al-Layth, al-Muzani and others; Saʿid b. al-Haddad cites Companions pouring it into Medina's streets with no prophetic ban on doing so. Qurtubi rejects that inference. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ربيعة والليث بن سعد والمزني,سعيد بن الحداد القروي); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 5:90/q21 Does avoidance prohibit wine ownership, sale and other uses?  [فَٱجْتَنِبُوهُ · law]
turns on: The disagreement turns on whether the command excludes every utility or only the objects' harmful intended use.
- 5:90/q21/p1 Yes: wine must be abandoned, sold neither directly nor for profit, and has no lawful ordinary utility. — Qurtubi cites the Prophet's refusal of a wine gift and order to pour it out; he infers no ownership or sale, and reports consensus against selling wine and blood. Tabrisi includes buying, selling and all dealings; Ibn Kathir transmits multiple pour-out and market-container reports, a refusal to return or gift stock, and prohibitions on its price; Thaʿlabi transmits curses covering makers, traders, carriers and drinkers. Study Quran includes sale proceeds. Ibn Kathir calls the Khaybar carrier report gharīb and notes Bahrain compensation for orphans and reuse of emptied vessels. Qurtubi compares the permitted use of a tanned dead sheep hide and extends the wine-sale analogy to animal dung, recording Malik's dislike, Ibn al-Qasim's permission for utility, and the Shafiʿi agreement with Malik. [22 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة وابن عباس,ابن عمر); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عمر,تميم الداري,جابر بن عبد الله,عبد الله بن عمر); STUDYQURAN]
- 5:90/q21/p2 Wine can be owned for limited utility such as relieving choking or extinguishing fire. — Ibn Khuwayz Mindad attributes this to Malik; Qurtubi says the attribution is unknown and follows only if wine is held pure, then rejects it because the Prophet ordered pouring it away. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 5:90/q21/p3 Avoidance concerns harmful intended uses, not mere touching, showing or historically preserving the objects. — Ibn ʿAshur gives examples of inspection, display, historical preservation and allowing wine to become vinegar. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 5:90/q22 May wine be converted into vinegar or used as a preserve?  [فَٱجْتَنِبُوهُ · law]
turns on: The dispute concerns deliberate alteration after the order to pour wine away, with separate distinctions for spontaneous vinegar and other preserves.
- 5:90/q22/p1 Vinegar formed by itself is lawful, but a Muslim should pour wine out rather than deliberately convert it. — Qurtubi reports Malik, ʿUmar, Qabisa, Ibn Shihab, Rabiʿa and Shafiʿi positions on spontaneous vinegar and endorses Malik's ban on treatment. Majority jurists prohibit treatment; reports from ʿUthman b. Abi al-ʿAs and Anas describe prophetic refusal even for orphans' wine. Qurtubi argues the order to empty it would make no sense if conversion were lawful. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور الفقهاء,عثمان بن أبي العاص,مالك وأصحابه); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)+]
- 5:90/q22/p2 Wine may be deliberately converted to vinegar, then eaten. — Thawri, Awzaʿi, al-Layth and Kufans allow conversion; a report from Ashhab says Malik permits Christian-made vinegar and the same from a Muslim who should repent. Qurtubi allows that an early ban might have targeted new drinking habits. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أشهب عن مالك,الثوري والأوزاعي والليث بن سعد والكوفيون); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)-]
- 5:90/q22/p3 Wine may be made into a changed preserve with musk or salt. — Abu Hanifa permits it if wine's state changes; Iraqis cite a weak report from Abu al-Darda about sun and salt tanning it. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,أبو حنيفة)]
- 5:90/q22/p4 Treatment is confined to making vinegar, not other preserves. — Muhammad b. al-Hasan states this narrower allowance; Qurtubi notes opposition by ʿUmar and ʿUthman b. Abi al-ʿAs to conversion. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,محمد بن الحسن)]

## 5:90/q23 What practices analogous to arrows and idols are included or excluded?  [ٱلْأَنصَابُ ٱلْأَزْلَٰمُ · law]
turns on: Whether a guidance practice claims divination or merely resolves practical hesitation while relying on God.
- 5:90/q23/p1 Augury, written omens and shrine customs resemble the condemned pagan reliance. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan mention bird augury and omens from books; Kuran Yolu cautions against shrine vows and sacrifices at graves, and modern lottery superstition. Asad reads anṣāb as idolatry broadly. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 5:90/q23/p2 Istikhara and favorable omens after thought and consultation are permissible guidance, not forbidden azlām. — Al-Tabatabaʾi cites Sunni and Shiʿi reports on the Prophet welcoming good omens and Ahl al-Bayt reports allowing rosary guidance. He says the practice ends indecision, claims no hidden knowledge or divine governance, and is not a binding ruling; its outcome can still be good or bad. [5 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:90/q24 What incident prompted this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted occasions name different people and incidents; Kuran Yolu says no specific cause is certain.
- 5:90/q24/p1 Saʿd b. Abi Waqqas was struck with a camel jawbone after drinking and tribal boasting, then complained to the Prophet. — Muqatil names ʿUthban b. Malik; the reports vary whether Saʿd was struck on nose or head, and whether an Anṣari hosted. Wahidi says Muslim transmits Saʿd's account. Elmalılı adds that Saʿd recited satire. Al-Tabatabaʾi observes public accounts differ sharply. [16 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مصعب بن سعد); WAHIDI-ASBAB(سعد بن أبي وقاص); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير,مصعب بن سعد عن أبيه); QURTUBI-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(سالم بن عبد الله,سعد بن أبي وقاص); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); TABATABAI(سعد بن أبي وقاص); STUDYQURAN(Companions of the Prophet)]
- 5:90/q24/p2 Two Anṣari clans became drunk and fought, then blamed each other for injuries and acquired rancor. — Ibn ʿAbbas reports this through Mawardi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, Suyuti and Durr; Durr adds the later 5:93 question. A broader report says Muslims drank, fought and spoke offensive words. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 5:90/q24/p3 ʿUmar repeatedly prayed for a clear ruling, received successive wine passages, and declared 'we have stopped.' — Abu Maysara and ʿUmar reports describe 2:219, 4:43 and the final verse; Elmalılı repeats his response. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); WAHIDI-ASBAB(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو ميسرة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو ميسرة عن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ميسرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمر); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ömer)]
- 5:90/q24/p4 Hamza's intoxicated attack on ʿAli's camels occasioned the prohibition. — Abu Hayyan reports the incident; a report from Jaʿfar al-Ṣadiq names sakarkah, places revelation before Uhud and includes Hamza's reconciliation and martyrdom. Its continuation recounts his shroud and conduct at Uhud. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 5:90/q24/p5 An intoxicated Companion's misrecitation at Maghrib helped prompt the final ban. — Abu Hayyan gives this occasion; Abu Hurayra and Suyuti place drunken misrecitation before 4:43 in a staged account. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو هريرة)]
- 5:90/q24/p6 ʿUmar drank, struck ʿAbd al-Raḥman b. ʿAwf and lamented Badr's dead before the final revelation. — Al-Tabatabaʾi reports Zamakhshari's staged narrative. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الزمخشري)]
- 5:90/q24/p7 Hostility after gambling losses may have been an occasion. — Kuran Yolu reports Tabari listing this with ʿUmar and Saʿd and saying no cause is certain. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]

## 5:90/q25 When and through what stages was wine prohibited?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notes disagree on the count and order of prior passages, whether prior rules were abrogated, and the date of this revelation.
- 5:90/q25/p1 A staged sequence culminated here: 2:219, then 4:43, then this decisive prohibition. — Ibn ʿUmar, Abu Hurayra, Saʿid b. Jubayr, Qurtubi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Ibn ʿAshur and others recount benefits acknowledged, prayer-time restraint and final abstention; 5:93 reassured about those who died before the ban. Qurtubi calls 2:219 and 4:43 abrogated; Elmalılı calls this third and final. Kuran Yolu invokes ʿAʾisha's warning against an abrupt initial ban and compares failed American prohibition. Qurtubi says apparent pre-ban permission conflicts with the juristic claim that every religious law forbade intoxication, which jurists ground in reason's role in welfare. [26 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عمر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر,سعيد بن جبير); ITQAN(ابن عمر); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو هريرة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Âişe); TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 5:90/q25/p2 There are four wine-related revelations, with 16:67 also counted before the final passage. — Bursevi calls this fourth; Study Quran orders 16:67, 2:219, 4:43 and 5:90. Al-Shaʿbi instead gives 2:219 then 16:67 then al-Maʾida; al-Tabatabaʾi rejects the implied abrogations. [3 notes: BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(الشعبي); TABATABAI-]
- 5:90/q25/p3 The law was always prohibited; the progression was in manner of address and practical obligations, without legal abrogation. — Al-Tabatabaʾi says 7:33 and 2:219 already prohibit, with 4:43 a particular warning, and rejects al-Shaʿbi's abrogation chain; Abu Jaʿfar says every prophet's mission included wine's ban, while people were moved gradually to avoid abandonment of religion. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 5:90/q25/p4 The prohibition was revealed after Uhud in year three of the Hijra. — Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Ibn ʿAshur give this dating; Ibn ʿAshur says the verse was then placed later in al-Maʾida. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:90/q25/p5 The prohibition came a few days after the Battle of the Trench. — Biqaʿi reports al-Aṣbahani's date. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الأصبهاني)]
- 5:90/q25/p6 The ban is Medinan and among the last revelations of al-Maʾida. — Qurtubi reports Muslim agreement; al-Tabatabaʾi calls these the latest wine-ban verses. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 5:90/q26 When did a new prohibition bind someone who had not yet heard it?  [* · sciences]
- 5:90/q26/p1 It binds when news of abrogation reaches the person. — Qurtubi explains a man gifting wine while unaware of the ban and the people of Quba changing prayer direction on receiving news; he endorses this timing. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أهل قباء); QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 5:90/q27 How did hearers react when the prohibition was announced?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports distinguish communal response at the ban from one man's earlier abstention.
- 5:90/q27/p1 They immediately desisted and poured out wine. — Anas says jars of date wine were emptied without further question; Burayda reports companions draining remaining cups and pouring out stores; Abu Hurayra says the Prophet announced the ban after earlier hinting a ruling would come. ʿUmar said they had stopped. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,بريدة); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ömer)]
- 5:90/q27/p2 ʿUthman b. Maẓʿun had renounced wine even before Islam and lamented its loss when the verse was recited. — ʿAbd al-Rahman b. Sabit says he feared intoxication would cloud reason and lead to unwanted marriage. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن سابط)]

## 5:90/q28 What warnings and prophetic reports reinforce the wine prohibition?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct reports and warnings, not competing legal interpretations; their details and stated chain assessments differ.
- 5:90/q28/p1 Wine brings severe moral and afterlife consequences for drinkers and those helping its trade. — Thaʿlabi transmits deprivation of Paradise wine, venom, rejected worship until repentance, curses for production and trade, no coexistence of wine and faith, idol-worship likeness, mistrust and reputational harm; a poet says nabidh opens evil. Ibn Abi Hatim reports Judgment Day thirst for drinkers and drink for abstainers. Ibn Kathir gives later reports on lost prayer, forty mornings, Hell's extract, deprivation of Paradise wine, God not looking at habitual drinkers, and Paradise barred; he notes Bukhari's chain doubts for one report. ʿUthman recounts an ascetic driven to adultery and murder by wine. [20 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن عمرو); THALABI(أبو هريرة,أبو هريرة وابن عباس,ابن عباس,ابن عمر,الشاعر,عثمان بن عفان,علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد,ابن عباس,ابن عمر,البخاري,عبد الله بن عمر,عبد الله بن عمرو,عثمان بن عفان); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI]
- 5:90/q28/p2 This verse has a Torah counterpart abolishing play, music, dancing, instruments, songs and wine. — ʿAbd Allah b. ʿAmr transmits the claim; Ibn Kathir grades the chain sound. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن عمرو); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 5:90/q28/p3 Reports also prohibit associated drinks, instruments and gambling. — Ibn Kathir reports bans on kubah, qinin or lutes, mizr and ghubayra, with two reports transmitted by Ahmad alone; a separate report names wine and gambling. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو,قيس بن سعد بن عبادة)]
- 5:90/q28/p4 A questioner was told he was among those who do good. — Muqatil reports the Prophet's reply without identifying the question in this excerpt. [1 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 5:90/q28/p5 Had earlier scriptures and prophets continued, the community would have received an especially severe wine ban. — Ibn ʿAbbas says its matter is deferred to Judgment Day. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 5:90/q29 How does the verse connect with nearby rulings and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The links operate at different scales: preceding permission, earlier lexical discussions, and the following explanation and reassurance.
- 5:90/q29/p1 After allowing lawful foods and warning against self-denial, the passage identifies truly forbidden drink and pagan practices. — Tabari recalls companions who renounced lawful women, sleep and meat at 5:87; Razi and Abu Hayyan link 5:87–88 with the ban; Tabrisi says it turns from pre-Islamic custom to Islamic law; Biqaʿi says drink follows food and compares 5:3. Elmalılı says avoid unlawful impurity after refusing to ban wholesome things. Study Quran notes these are the sura's wine and gambling rulings. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:90/q29/p2 The verse recalls earlier explanations of wine, gambling, altars and arrows in al-Baqara and at 5:3. — Wahidi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, Razi, Qurtubi, Tabrisi, Baydawi, Biqaʿi, Bursevi and Elmalılı point back; the repeated cross-references carry lexical details rather than fresh disagreement. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 5:90/q29/p3 The next verse specifies hostility, hatred and neglect of remembrance and prayer, while 5:93 answers concern for earlier drinkers. — Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi and al-Tabatabaʾi tie 5:91 to worldly and religious harms; Abu Hurayra's report supplies 5:93's reassurance. Al-Tabatabaʾi says the verses form a coherent, nearly simultaneous unit; Samarrai compares added warning and knowledge in al-Maʾida to al-Taghabun's lack of a prior comparable ban. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 5:90/q30 What inward lessons are drawn near this verse?  [* · inward]
- 5:90/q30/p1 A seeker should surrender to God's decree and serve steadily rather than demand closeness by oath. — Bursevi cites al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya: an oath from intense longing is excused yet idle under contentment and a lapse under surrender; the seeker should accept approach or estrangement. Persian verse, Ibn al-Farid and the Mathnawi urge prompt use of time and patience in hardship. [5 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الفارض,المثنوي)]
