# 92:7: 21 questions

## 92:7/q01 What does the future marker sa- imply?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · grammar]
- 92:7/q01/p1 Facilitation continues from now until the end of life. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 12:98; the previously described deeds already occurred, so the promise sustains and increases them. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:7/q02 Why does God speak in the first-person plural?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 92:7/q02/p1 It marks God's authoritative promise and the divine power needed to ease obedience. — Biqāʿī says obedience, though intrinsically easy, burdens the self; Corpus Coranicum says the plural authorizes the paired promises alongside the oath. [2 notes: BIQAI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:7/q03 What does nuyassiruhu mean here?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · meaning]
- 92:7/q03/p1 God prepares and makes a person ready for a course or outcome. — Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı compare saddling and bridling a horse for riding; Ibn ʿĀshūr says taysīr literally makes something easy to obtain, citing 20:26 and 54:17, and treats 'prepare' as figurative in one analysis. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI(مفسّرین); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 92:7/q04 How does the object and li- phrase work with nuyassiruhu?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the ordinary object-beneficiary relation is inverted, li- expresses purpose with figurative preparation, or the words name states while the verb remains literal.
- 92:7/q04/p1 The literal syntax is rhetorically inverted: God eases the easy path for the person. — Ibn ʿĀshūr observes that normally the direct object is what becomes easy and li- marks its beneficiary, as 20:26 and 54:17 show; inversion intensifies the facilitation. He compares presenting a she-camel to a watering trough. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:7/q04/p2 The verb figuratively means prepare; li- gives the purpose, with entry into Paradise or Hell understood. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares easy entry to speed rather than delay (50:44); Hell's parallel wording is mocking. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:7/q04/p3 The verb remains literal without inversion: the easy and hard terms name states, and facilitation means persistence in deeds. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the Bukhārī report that each is facilitated for the work for which created. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:7/q05 Whom does the pronoun refer to?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the preceding description is taken generally or identified with the reported occasions involving Abū Bakr or the palm purchase.
- 92:7/q05/p1 The giver who fears God and affirms His promise, described in the preceding verses; the pattern applies generally. — Ṭabarī identifies the generous God-fearing believer; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clauses mark faith, with omitted object of 'fears' understood as God and compares 74:43–44. Rāzī says the sūra's human striving and Fire warning show general meaning (92:4, 92:14). [9 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); RAZI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 92:7/q05/p2 Abū Bakr is the exemplary giver. — Al-Thaʿlabī says revelation concerned him; reports describe freeing tortured or weak converts, with his father urging strong slaves for protection and Abū Bakr seeking God's reward. The Bilāl account names Umayya ibn Khalaf and a garment and ten coins; Ibn Masʿūd also names Ubayy among opposed figures. Ibn ʿAbbās names Abū Sufyān as miser in a report through al-Kalbī and Abū Ṣāliḥ. [18 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير عن أبيه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 92:7/q05/p3 Abū al-Daḥdāḥ is the giver, set against a stingy palm owner. — ʿAṭāʾ's versions say a palm dropped dates into a poor neighbor's yard, the owner refused a promised palm in Paradise, and Abū al-Daḥdāḥ bought or traded his orchard for it and gave it to the family. Ibn ʿAbbās's version says forty palms; reports vary in detail. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI]

## 92:7/q06 What does God make easy for this person in practice?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promised ease is chiefly enabled conduct, renewed conduct, a pleasant state in both lives, or entry to Paradise; Ṭabāṭabāʾ explicitly favors the afterlife emphasis.
- 92:7/q06/p1 God enables pleasing and righteous deeds until obedience becomes easy. — Ṭabarī locates these deeds in this life and Paradise as their reward; Māwardī and Qurṭubī stress making the means easy; Qushayrī adds love of faith and excellence, hatred of disobedience, and desire for nearness. Kashshāf compares opening the breast to Islam (6:125); Rāzī contrasts hypocritical sluggishness (2:45, 4:142, 9:38). Ṭabrisī stresses repeated, eager obedience; Islahi adds removal of obstacles and strengthened desire (29:69); Kur'an Yolu says generosity becomes a stable trait; Quṭb describes calm ease spreading through worldly life. [36 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Zamakhsharī); IBNASHUR(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI-]
- 92:7/q06/p2 God eases a return to earlier obedience, especially renewed giving. — Farrāʾ and Ibn Qutayba describe a return to righteous acts; Muqātil specifies repeated giving, while Qushayrī mentions resuming worship. Rāzī contrasts return to withholding and denial of financial rights; Ibn ʿĀshūr reasons that acts already described precede the future promise. [9 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; QUSHAYRI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:7/q06/p3 God readies the person for an easy and pleasant worldly and afterlife condition, culminating in Paradise. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says both lives are good and pleasing, while the opposite condition is bad in both; Abū Ḥayyān, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ discuss a comfortable state or disposition. Biqāʿī mentions mercy, a good life and Paradise; Elmalılı includes easy reckoning (84:8–9). Ṭabāṭabāʾ explicitly prefers an ease-filled afterlife reached through deeds over mere ease of doing them. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(المفسرون); QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI+]
- 92:7/q06/p4 God makes entry into Paradise easy and honorable. — Ṭabrisī mentions the angels' greeting and good news; Rāzī cites 13:23–24 and 39:73 for greeting at entry; Ibn ʿĀshūr's preparation analysis supplies entry understood after li- and likens quick entry to ease (50:44). [7 notes: TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI]

## 92:7/q07 What is al-yusrā?  [لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether yusrā names deeds or their course, Paradise and its reward, or a broader easy state spanning both lives.
- 92:7/q07/p1 The easy course of goodness, obedience and deeds pleasing to God. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly glosses it as good; Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Baghawī and Nasafī call it approved deeds. Al-Qaffāl says obedience is named for its restful outcome, contrasted with sins ending in hardship; some describe the route to Paradise. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it broadly means what benefits without hardship. [29 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; TAB; MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI(القفال); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:7/q07/p2 Paradise, the good destination or ease of afterlife bliss. — Zayd ibn Aslam and Mujāhid are named repeatedly; Ṭabrisī includes angelic welcome; Rāzī says the promised Paradise makes strenuous worship bearable. Baghawī maps it to Abū al-Daḥdāḥ's reward; Elmalılı and Islahi cite the easy reckoning in 84:7–9. Ṭabāṭabāʾ prefers this afterlife sense over good works alone. [33 notes: IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); NAHHAS(الضحاك); MAWARDI(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زيد بن أسلم); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI(زيد بن أسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNKATHIR(زيد بن أسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNASHUR(زيد بن أسلم ومجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]
- 92:7/q07/p3 The pleasant and least burdensome condition or easy disposition in this life and the next. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān explicitly include both lives; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī speak of a trait leading to rest and Paradise. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an easy rank or place; Qur'an Yolu includes a greater capacity for virtue, and Quṭb details ease radiating into everyday affairs. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(المفسرون); IBNASHUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QUTB-ZILAL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:7/q08 What form and implied noun underlie al-yusrā?  [لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 92:7/q08/p1 It is the feminine 'easier', qualifying an implied way, trait, deed, outcome, or class of deeds. — Jishumī relates yusr to comfort and abundance. Rāzī proposes a group of acts, an implicit feminine khilla or faʿla, return to an act, or a feminine way; Islahi suggests al-ʿāqibah, outcome. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the definite article may be generic or referential, and feminine forms may categorize deeds by consequences. [8 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:7/q09 What force does the feminine form yusrā have compared with yusr?  [لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · meaning]
- 92:7/q09/p1 It conveys the extreme of ease. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts yusrā and ʿusrā with verbal nouns yusr and ʿusr, comparing the Qur'anic greatest seizure and greatest Fire (79:25; 92:14). [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 92:7/q10 How is al-yusrā read?  [لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · readings]
- 92:7/q10/p1 Abū Jaʿfar reads the sīn with ḍamma in both al-yusrā and al-ʿusrā. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports his vocalization; Qiraat-ER says inserting a short vowel between sīn and rāʾ is phonological and leaves root and meaning intact. NASHR reports that Abū ʿAmr also gives the sīn ḍamma in the paired yusr and ʿusr forms, including dhū ʿusra; it cross-references Abū Jaʿfar's ḍamma across those related forms here and elsewhere. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(أبو جعفر,أبو عمرو)]

## 92:7/q11 Why are the two paths called easy and hard?  [لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording names deeds by their eventual effects or figures the resulting condition through a rhetorical transfer.
- 92:7/q11/p1 The names describe their outcomes: obedience ends in ease and rest, sin in toil and hardship. — Al-Qaffāl gives the causal explanation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the deeds take their names from their eventual results. Rāzī adds that expected Paradise makes hard worship easy to bear. Zamakhsharī's account is reported by Abū Ḥayyān and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ. [9 notes: RAZI(القفال); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 92:7/q11/p2 The wording uses a metaphor, metonymy, or figurative attribution for a comfortable outcome. — Ālūsī names the three rhetorical analyses; Corpus Coranicum notes that pairing near-homophonous yusr and ʿusr with yassara veils the destinations and makes facilitation toward hardship paradoxical, with comparison to 94:5–6. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:7/q12 What does facilitation teach about deeds and divine decree?  [* · theology]
turns on: How divine preparation and prior knowledge relate to human acts; the reports repeatedly answer the temptation to rely on decree by commanding work.
- 92:7/q12/p1 Destinations are written or known beforehand, yet people must act; each is enabled for the deeds associated with that end. — ʿAlī's funeral report appears across sources and is graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ by Tirmidhī in Qurṭubī; Ibn ʿĀshūr names Bukhārī. Variant questions come from two young men, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, Jābir and Surāqa, and a man after 54:49; Muslim and Aḥmad routes are noted. Ibn Kathīr says good intention receives success and evil intention abandonment, within decree; Biqāʿī invokes prior decree. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the writing as God's exact prior knowledge, actions as signs and means, and says the hadith's opening writing is not the verse's main subject. [49 notes: IBNHIBBAN(شعبة,علي بن أبي طالب); WAHIDI-ASBAB(علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(علي); KASHSHAF(النبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI(علي); RAZI-FULL(علي); QURTUBI(رسول الله,علي عن النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الغلامان,علي عن النبي,غلامان شابان عن النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(النبي); DURR(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,بشير بن كعب الأسلمي,جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR(علي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 92:7/q12/p2 God grants the believer special enabling so obedience outweighs disobedience. — Rāzī reports this inference of his Ashʿarī associates. Abū Ḥayyān objects to a Muʿtazilite implication in Zamakhsharī's account, adding that taysīr prepares for either outcome by contrast; Ibn Kathīr connects intended good or evil to divine success or abandonment. [3 notes: RAZI(الأصحاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 92:7/q13 Do deeds lead to further deeds of the same kind?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:7/q13/p1 Good deeds bring further good deeds, while sins bring further sins. — Early authorities give this maxim; Ibn Kathīr relates it to the following opposite clause. Ibn ʿĀshūr says prior giving, piety and affirmation cause and are rewarded by continued deeds; Elmalılı says good actions multiply means to the goal, and Kur'an Yolu says the habit of generosity stabilizes. [8 notes: IBNKATHIR(بعض السلف); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 92:7/q14 What occasions or incidents are reported for this promise?  [* · reports]
turns on: The narratives identify different acts and people as the setting of the promise; the palm-sale versions also vary in the exchange price.
- 92:7/q14/p1 Abū Bakr freed vulnerable converts despite his father's advice to free strong protectors. — Ibn Isḥāq and ʿĀmir ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr say he sought God's face or reward; Ibn Kathīr locates the freeing in Mecca. Some versions emphasize tortured converts; a Bilāl version names Umayya ibn Khalaf and a garment and ten coins. Another Ibn ʿAbbās route names Abū Sufyān as the opposed miser. [18 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير عن أبيه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 92:7/q14/p2 A stingy palm owner denied fallen dates to a poor family; Abū al-Daḥdāḥ bought the tree and donated it. — ʿAṭāʾ says the owner refused the Prophet's offer of a Paradise palm. Versions say Abū al-Daḥdāḥ traded an orchard; Ibn ʿAbbās says forty palms. The buyer receives a promised Paradise palm. The contrasting figures' efforts are mapped to verses 5–10. Qurṭubī reports that in this story al-ḥusnā is the reward and the easy way is Paradise. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI]
- 92:7/q14/p3 At sunrise two angels pray for replacement of the spender's gift and ruin of the hoarder's wealth. — Māwardī transmits Qatāda through Khulayd from Abū al-Dardāʾ; the Prophet then recites these verses, with no grading stated. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]

## 92:7/q15 How does this promise fit the sūra's paired paths?  [* · links]
- 92:7/q15/p1 It is the good half of the paired sequence: conduct leads to felicity, while opposed conduct leads to hardship. — Ṭabarī contrasts the giver with the self-sufficient miser; Rāzī explains divergent striving and reward or punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Corpus Coranicum name paired giving/stinginess, piety/self-sufficiency, affirmation/denial and ease/hardship; Ibn ʿĀshūr says naming giving encourages generosity. Corpus Coranicum notes symmetric behavior-to-consequence sequences, cross-rhyme, and the last stanza's reversed order, with outer blessed sections joined by ittaqā/atqā (92:5, 17). Abū Ḥayyān links the sūra to success and failure in 91:9–10. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:7/q16 What other scriptural passages illuminate this promise?  [* · links]
- 92:7/q16/p1 It echoes the promise of an easy way in 87:8 and relates to other passages on ease, guidance and reward. — Quṭb calls 87:8 the promise to the Messenger; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the pairing appears only there and here. Corpus Coranicum compares ease/hardship in 94:5–6, Study Quran adds God's desire for ease in 2:185 and appointment of ease for reverence in 65:4, and Asad points to his 87:8 note. Burhān says the Prophet used al-Layl when describing Paradise, alongside 32:17 and 56:30. [6 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:7/q17 What practical response does the contrast of ease and hardship call for?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:7/q17/p1 Follow God's commands and strive for the good end despite difficulty in resisting desire. — Sāmarrāʾī says ease and hardship in either life belong to God; Islahi says the virtuous struggle against base desires, grow stronger, and finally reach the company and Paradise of 89:29–30. The young men in Qurṭubī's report resolve to work after hearing the Prophet's teaching. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL(الغلامان)]

## 92:7/q18 How does God's facilitation unfold over a person's life?  [* · theology]
- 92:7/q18/p1 God progressively leads the giver through good deeds toward an end already present in divine knowledge. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes the visible, gradual unfolding of facilitation and distinguishes it from God's prior knowledge of the outcome. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 92:7/q19 What is the counterpart of this easy outcome?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:7/q19/p1 Al-ʿusrā is hardship or punishment, including Hell. — Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a condition of hardship or its place, citing 74:9–10 for the hard day of disbelievers. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 92:7/q20 Which deeds mark the giver and the contrasting miser?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:7/q20/p1 The giver pays God's due, avoids prohibitions and believes His promise; the miser withholds that due and denies the promise. — Qatāda adds self-sufficiency to the miser's profile. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 92:7/q21 Can divine preparation concern both outcomes?  [* · theology]
- 92:7/q21/p1 God may withhold favors so obedience is hard, and taysīr may prepare a person for either outcome. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Zamakhsharī's formulation, including paths to Paradise and Hell and 6:125; he rejects what he calls its Muʿtazilite implication and offers preparation for both outcomes by contrast. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
