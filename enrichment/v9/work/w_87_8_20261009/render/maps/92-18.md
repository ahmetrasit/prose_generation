# 92:18: 22 questions

## 92:18/q01 Who is the Godfearing giver?  [ٱلَّذِى · referent]
turns on: Whether the named occasion limits the wording, and whether the Medinan palm story can be the occasion of an early Makkan sūra.
- 92:18/q01/p1 Abu Bakr. — Tustari calls him the truthful one, who abandoned the perishable for the everlasting; Thaʿlabi, Baghawi, and others connect the verse to his freeing persecuted Muslims. Itqan claims consensus and denies general wording. Kuranyolu and Study Quran report this as an occasion while allowing a general message. [14 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن الزبير,عروة عن أبيه); BAGHAWI(ابن الزبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن الزبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عروة); BIQAI-FULL(الزبير بن بكار); STUDYQURAN(others); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAGHAWI+; ITQAN+; THALABI+; TUSTARI+; RAZI(الشيعة)-]
- 92:18/q01/p2 Abu al-Dahdah. — Thaʿlabi and Qurtubi connect him to the donated date palm; Qurtubi contrasts him with its miserly owner. Study Quran recounts this as a Medinan report, but doubts it as the occasion if the sūra is early Makkan. [6 notes: THALABI(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(several commentators); QURTUBI; THALABI+; STUDYQURAN-]
- 92:18/q01/p3 Ali. — Al-Razi reports the Shiʿi identification, linked to alms given while bowing in 5:55; they reject the Abu Bakr report. [2 notes: RAZI(الشيعة)]
- 92:18/q01/p4 Anyone who gives seeking purification; Abu Bakr can be one instance. — Ibn Ashur says an unusual named instance does not restrict general wording. Kuranyolu says the message and rulings remain general regardless of occasion. Study Quran's early Makkan dating favors a general condemnation and praise. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)+; STUDYQURAN+; ITQAN-]

## 92:18/q02 What does يُؤْتِى convey as a word?  [يُؤْتِى · meaning]
- 92:18/q02/p1 Giving readily and generously. — Bint al-Shati compares a flood and a fruiting tree that yield readily; she cites 2:265, 13:35, 14:25, and 18:33. She says Quranic forms of zakat recur with giving, citing 23:4 and 24:37, 21:73, 4:162, 2:177, 9:11, 9:18, 24:56, 2:43, 2:83, 2:110, 2:277, 4:77, 9:5, 22:41, 22:78, 5:12, and 30:39. [3 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 92:18/q02/p2 Giving or spending wealth. — Alusi glosses the verb as giving and spending. [1 notes: ALUSI]

## 92:18/q03 Where or for what is the wealth given?  [يُؤْتِى مَالَهُۥ · interpretation]
- 92:18/q03/p1 It is spent in charity, obedience, and good causes, including help to the poor. — Baydawi, Biqai, and Alusi specify good causes; Nasafi specifies the poor. Ibn Kathir says obedience to God, purifying what God granted in religious and worldly life. Jishumi includes charity and other obedient acts. Qutb adds that giving is voluntary, without seeking thanks. Qurtubi identifies Abu al-Dahdah's payment for the palm as an instance of spending for God's sake. [13 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; QURTUBI]

## 92:18/q04 Does the giving denote an obligation or voluntary good works?  [يُؤْتِى مَالَهُۥ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's giving is read as imposed alms or as freely chosen good works.
- 92:18/q04/p1 It includes fulfilling the wealth obligations God imposed, including obligatory alms. — Tabari says the obligations purify the giver of sins. Jishumi names charity and obligatory alms. Bint al-Shati describes religious zakat as obligatory wealth and says God's blessing and reward cause growth. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BINTSHATI; TAB+]
- 92:18/q04/p2 The apparent setting is recommended or voluntary giving. — Ibn Atiyya explicitly calls the apparent sense recommended works; Qutb describes the giver as acting voluntarily. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNATIYYA+]

## 92:18/q05 What does مَالَهُۥ denote?  [مَالَهُۥ · meaning]
- 92:18/q05/p1 Useful owned property and its yields; in some Arab usage, specifically date palms. — Ibn Ashur names livestock, land, private wells, and trees as generic māl. He says some Arabs, including Yathrib's people, used the word for date palms. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:18/q06 Must the giver give all his wealth?  [مَالَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The distinction between what the generic wording requires and what a report says one giver did.
- 92:18/q06/p1 No; the generic construct does not imply totality. — Ibn Ashur says māl in this construction is generic, so the verse does not require surrendering everything owned. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:18/q06/p2 Abu Bakr spent all forty thousand he had. — Urwa's account in Thaʿlabi says he freed Bilal and others and gave away all forty thousand; this is a reported individual act, not a rule about the verse's grammar. [1 notes: THALABI(عروة عن أبيه)]

## 92:18/q07 What does يَتَزَكَّىٰ mean lexically?  [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes the giver's sought purity or the act of paying zakat.
- 92:18/q07/p1 To seek purification and growth in goodness. — Ibn Atiyya gives both purity and increase. Ibn Ashur defines tazakki as seeking zakāʾ, growth in goodness; Bint al-Shati traces growth to ripening fruit and explains nonphysical goodness and blessing, self-praise in 53:32, and refinement in 3:164 and 62:2. Corpus Coranicum compares 91:9 and 91:15. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ELMALILI; QURTUBI]
- 92:18/q07/p2 To pay zakat or alms. — Kashshaf offers the verb form for giving zakat; Nasafi and Abu Hayyan also derive it from zakāh or tazkiya. Bint al-Shati says all 32 Quranic occurrences of the religious term zakat mean obligatory wealth, and compares 9:103 where wealth is purified; Study Quran notes the shared root. [8 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]

## 92:18/q08 What purification does the giver seek?  [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Which object or aspect of purification the commentators emphasize: standing before God, removal of sins, or the giver and wealth together.
- 92:18/q08/p1 Purity and growth before God, with His acceptance and pleasure rather than public praise. — Abu Ishaq, Wahidi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, and Qutb reject ostentation and reputation. Qutb adds self-exaltation, repayment, and thanks; Ibn Ashur adds benefit and increased reward and contrasts idolaters' pride, display, mischief, and debauchery. Elmalili includes spiritual growth and prosperity. Corpus Coranicum says the longer portrait makes generosity ethical purification for the Lord's face, not repayment. [26 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; THALABI]
- 92:18/q08/p2 Cleansing himself from sins. — Tabari and Nahhas state this directly; Alusi offers it as an alternative to seeking pure, growing wealth before God. [4 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:18/q08/p3 Purifying both the person and the wealth God granted. — Ibn Kathir includes the self, wealth, and gifts in religion and worldly life. Islahi says believers spend to cleanse their wealth; Study Quran says charity purifies giver and wealth. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 92:18/q09 What inward attachments are removed by this giving?  [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · inward]
- 92:18/q09/p1 Love of rivals, attachment to otherness, attention to anything besides God, and hidden shirk. — Qashani names each attachment as what the gift purges. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 92:18/q10 How is يَتَزَكَّىٰ related grammatically to يُؤْتِى?  [يُؤْتِى يَتَزَكَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the imperfect states the giver's circumstance, substitutes for a preceding clause or verb, or depends on omitted purpose particles; objections concern case matching and the source of nominative inflection.
- 92:18/q10/p1 An accusative circumstantial clause describing the subject as he gives. — Jurjani says an affirmative imperfect circumstantial clause normally lacks wāw, citing this verse, 74:6, 7:186, and poetry. Abu Hayyan calls it usual and al-Zamakhshari's substitute strained; Bint al-Shati prefers it. Ibn Ashur says this parse conveys the giver's motive and increased reward. [20 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(صاحب الكشاف); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); TABRISI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(بعض النحاة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN+; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)+]
- 92:18/q10/p2 A substitute for the relative clause, with no independent syntactic position. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, and others report or allow this. Alusi records the objection that the relative clause has no case to follow, and the response that correspondence can mean matching the presence or absence of case; al-Sayyid Isa defines a follower as a second item that would be inflected if inflectable. Abu Hayyan and Bint al-Shati call it strained. [16 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(صاحب الكشاف); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(السيد عيسى); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)-]
- 92:18/q10/p3 The verb alone substitutes for the preceding verb. — Alusi reports this second substitution. Critics say its nominative is already explained by absence of a governor; the response allows a case to be intended despite an independent license, and al-Sayyid Isa states the follower test. [6 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(السيد عيسى)]
- 92:18/q10/p4 An implied purposive li-an precedes the verb; both lām and an are omitted. — Tabrisi invokes deletion of an with a line by Tarafa. Alusi reports that deleting lām then an permits a raised or subjunctive verb, illustrated by two transmitted readings of Tarafa's aḥḍur. [4 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 92:18/q11 How is يَتَزَكَّىٰ read?  [يَتَزَكَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether the tāʾ of tazakkā remains pronounced or assimilates into zāy.
- 92:18/q11/p1 The common reading is yatazakkā, imperfect of tazakkā. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin call it the majority or common reading. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن بن علي بن الحسن بن علي)]
- 92:18/q11/p2 Al-Hasan ibn Ali reads yazakkā, assimilating tāʾ into zāy. — Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, and Alusi attribute the assimilated form to al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib; Alusi Full writes it يزكي. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الحسن بن علي بن الحسن بن علي بن أبي طالب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن بن علي بن الحسن بن علي بن أبي طالب); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن بن علي بن الحسن بن علي); ALUSI(الحسن بن علي بن الحسن بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن بن علي بن الحسن بن علي بن أبي طالب)]

## 92:18/q12 What does the giving teach about favors and repayment?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:18/q12/p1 The giver seeks God's reward without settling a favor owed to anyone. — Razi says repaying a benefactor merely settles a debt; reward follows acting on God's command. Ibn Kathir says Abu Bakr owed no one repayment although Arab leaders benefited from him. Qutb rejects thanks and repayment, and Kuranyolu says all blessings come from God. Corpus Coranicum links the ethical motive to seeking the Lord's face. Qurtubi says Abu al-Dahdah owed nobody a favor to repay when he paid for the palm; he acted only for God's sake. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM; THALABI; QURTUBI]

## 92:18/q13 What happened when Abu Bakr freed persecuted Muslims?  [* · reports]
- 92:18/q13/p1 He freed Bilal and six others before the migration. — Ibn Ishaq names Amir ibn Fuhayra, Umm Umays, Zahra, al-Nahdiyya and her daughter, and a Banu al-Muammal girl. Baghawi Full's list varies by omitting Zahra but says six more. Urwa says seven including Bilal and Amir. Reports emphasize their vulnerability and torture. [12 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن الزبير,عروة عن أبيه); BAGHAWI(ابن الزبير,محمد بن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن الزبير,عروة); DURR-FULL(عروة); BIQAI-FULL(الزبير بن بكار); STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 92:18/q14 What did Abu Bakr say when advised to buy stronger protectors?  [* · reports]
- 92:18/q14/p1 He sought protection for himself, meaning a purpose other than a worldly defender. — Ibn al-Zubayr in Thaʿlabi says he pursued his own aim; Baghawi says protection for his own back, and Baghawi Full says protection precisely. Biqai's reports use a similar reply yet distinguish his purpose from buying a worldly defender. Study Quran phrases it as seeking no defense. The reported wording varies. [6 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن الزبير); BAGHAWI(ابن الزبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر,الزبير بن بكار); STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 92:18/q15 How did Abu Bakr obtain Bilal's freedom?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on whether the payment was gold, an unnamed stronger enslaved man, or Nustas.
- 92:18/q15/p1 He bought Bilal with gold. — Aʿta from Ibn Abbas says Bilal endured torture, the Prophet reassured him, then Abu Bakr bought him with gold. Study Quran says the Prophet told Abu Bakr Bilal was tortured for God; idolaters later alleged an earlier favor prompted the purchase. [2 notes: THALABI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN]
- 92:18/q15/p2 He exchanged a stronger enslaved man for Bilal and freed him. — Hisham ibn Urwa says Abu Bakr challenged Umayya's torture before the exchange; Baghawi Full also reports the exchange. [2 notes: BAGHAWI(هشام بن عروة عن أبيه); BAGHAWI-FULL(عروة)]
- 92:18/q15/p3 He traded Nustas, a wealthy enslaved man who refused Islam, for Bilal. — Saʿid ibn al-Musayyib says the emancipation was solely for God and God promised repayment in the hereafter. [1 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن المسيب)]
- 92:18/q15/p4 He bought Bilal from Umayya; the consideration is unspecified. — Biqai locates the purchase amid midday torture and Bilal's repeated 'One, One'; idolaters claimed repayment of a past favor, which revelation refuted. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 92:18/q16 What did Bilal endure before he was freed?  [* · reports]
- 92:18/q16/p1 Umayya tortured him for his monotheistic faith while he affirmed God's oneness. — Ibn Ishaq puts the torture under a boulder in Mecca's noonday heat and quotes 'Ahad, Ahad.' Aʿta adds the Prophet's reassurance; Study Quran's account says Bilal had desecrated Kaʿbah idols and the Prophet promised the One would save him. [6 notes: THALABI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI(محمد بن إسحاق,هشام بن عروة عن أبيه); BAGHAWI-FULL(عروة); BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 92:18/q17 What happened in the palm tree account associated with this passage?  [* · reports]
- 92:18/q17/p1 Abu al-Dahdah bought a disputed palm and gave it for a poor neighbor after its owner refused the Prophet's Paradise offer. — Thaʿlabi describes fruit falling into a neighbor's yard; Study Quran says the owner snatched dates from poor children, refused the offer, and Abu al-Dahdah paid forty palms before the Prophet transferred the tree. Qurtubi maps miser and giver to the sūra's contrasting outcomes. Study Quran doubts this is the occasion if the sūra is early Makkan. [6 notes: THALABI(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(several commentators); QURTUBI; STUDYQURAN-]

## 92:18/q18 What is reported about Zahra's sight?  [* · reports]
- 92:18/q18/p1 Her sight returned after she denied that idols had power over her blindness. — Baghawi, Baghawi Full, and Biqai say Quraysh attributed her impaired sight to idols; she rejected their power and recovered. The accounts vary in naming her Zahra or Umm Umays. [3 notes: BAGHAWI(محمد بن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(عروة); BIQAI-FULL(زهرة)]

## 92:18/q19 What other reports illustrate Abu Bakr's devotion or standing?  [* · reports]
- 92:18/q19/p1 He told the Prophet he had kept God and His Messenger for himself. — Tustari reports the Prophet's question about what Abu Bakr retained after giving. [1 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 92:18/q19/p2 The Prophet hoped Abu Bakr would be called from every gate of Paradise. — Ibn Kathir Full reports a saying that gates call those who spend two kinds of wealth in God's path; asked whether anyone receives every call, the Prophet named Abu Bakr as hoped for. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 92:18/q19/p3 The Prophet said God and believers would accept only Abu Bakr. — Biqai treats the report as supporting Abu Bakr's unique closeness to the Prophet and his contrast with the worst unbeliever. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي)]
- 92:18/q19/p4 Bilal later became the first caller to prayer and one of the Prophet's treasurers. — Study Quran supplies this aftermath of his freeing. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 92:18/q20 How does this verse connect with the sūra's contrasting figures and outcomes?  [* · links]
- 92:18/q20/p1 The giver's conduct contrasts with denial, hoarding, and the fire; purification leads toward rescue and satisfaction. — Biqai contrasts cleansing bad traits with denial in 92:16. Bint al-Shati contrasts the miser's useless wealth in 92:11 with the gift in 92:18 and frames both by the opening oath. Islahi describes Quraysh's warning, believers' response, fire and protection. Kuranyolu links 92:14 with 92:17–20. Corpus Coranicum sees the two antitheses of conduct and recompense in 5–13 and 14–21. [10 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:18/q20/p2 The final stanza reverses and mirrors the earlier portrayal of saved and damned. — Corpus Coranicum says the blessed outer sections share ittaqā in 92:5 and atqā in 92:17; earlier behavior precedes outcome, whereas final warning and promise precede conduct. Its longer portrait of the pious clarifies ethical purification and the Lord's face. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:18/q20/p3 The promise of Abu Bakr's satisfaction parallels God's promise to satisfy the Prophet. — Biqai says Abu Bakr is the closest in the community to the Prophet's station; the report of God's and believers' acceptance supports this. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي)]
- 92:18/q20/p4 The sūra's wealth motif suits emphatic wording of God's ownership. — Samarrai says a lām of emphasis on divine ownership fits the repeated concern with wealth and possession; he contrasts 53:25, where that wealth context is absent. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:18/q21 What rhetorical work does the contrast around this verse perform?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:18/q21/p1 Reciprocal omission lets the denier's opposite and giver's opposite be understood. — Biqai calls this ḥatbāk: first naming denial implies its opposite for the pious; later naming giving implies its opposite for the denier. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 92:18/q21/p2 The relative descriptions make conduct the cause of each outcome. — Ibn Ashur says clauses describing most wretched and most pious indicate why each receives the stated fate. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 92:18/q22 What grammatical principle applies to agreement in the verse's modifying construction?  [* · grammar]
- 92:18/q22/p1 Agreement normally follows the preceding word's form; agreement by meaning is less common. — Burhan states this rule for adjectives and similar constructions. [1 notes: BURHAN]
