# 51:3: 17 questions

## 51:3/q01 What does al-jāriyāt mean as a word?  [فَٱلْجَٰرِيَٰتِ · meaning]
- 51:3/q01/p1 It is the plural of jāriyah, derived from the verb 'to run'. — Jishumī gives the plural and derivation; in the same note he also lists recompense, religion, reckoning, and custom as senses of dīn. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 51:3/q02 What are the things that run smoothly?  [فَٱلْجَٰرِيَٰتِ يُسْرًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the four oath descriptions name distinct created beings or stages of wind activity; the wording also permits clouds, celestial bodies, and multiple possible bearers.
- 51:3/q02/p1 Ships sailing easily through water. — Mujāhid and ʿAlī identify ships; ʿAlī's four identifications are winds, clouds, ships, angels (ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Zajjāj, Kashshāf, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Kūrānyolu). Muqātil contrasts water-laden clouds. Māwardī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī say wind takes ships where directed; Wāḥidī and Baghawī stress easy gliding through water; Biqāʿī notes heavy cargo. Ibn Kathīr calls this the majority view. Elmalılı and Kūrānyolu include comparable trains and automobiles. Ibn Kathīr and Itqān report ʿUmar's Prophetic attribution; Ibn Kathīr questions its elevation. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says the wording also admits other referents. Study Quran cites Bayḍāwī, Ibn Jawzī, Qurṭubī, and Ṭabarī. [36 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(علي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ(علي); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب وغيره); TABRISI(علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الجمهور,علي بن أبي طالب,عمر بن الخطاب); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ITQAN(عمر بن الخطاب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(علي عليه أفضل السلام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Ali,müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, JJ, Q, Ṭ); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 51:3/q02/p2 Clouds moved easily by wind or wherever God directs them. — Māwardī gives divine direction or ease; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Abū Ḥayyān say wind drives them; Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī say God directs them across lands. Biqāʿī describes their free travel across the sky. Qurṭubī cites familiar Arab usage and al-Aʿshā's comparison of a woman's measured walk with a cloud's movement. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعشى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); BIQAI-FULL+]
- 51:3/q02/p3 Stars or planets moving in their orbits or stations. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah calls them orbiting stars and says the wording accommodates all interpretations; Ṭabrisī names seven planets: Sun, Moon, Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus, Mercury. Ibn Kathīr says this reading makes the oath ascend from lower to higher created beings. Study Quran attributes the planet view to Bayḍāwī. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 51:3/q02/p4 Winds moving gently, with all four oath descriptions naming their successive actions. — Kashshāf says winds form, carry, and move clouds, then distribute rain; Bayḍāwī includes winds running in their courses. Abū Ḥayyān reports Zamakhsharī's wind account. Kūrānyolu reports Rāzī favoring winds that form and carry water-vapor clouds heavier than mountains, drive them, and distribute rain. Iṣlāḥī says winds slow after raising dust and gathering clouds. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)+; TABATABAI-]

## 51:3/q03 What does yusran mean here?  [يُسْرًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether yusran describes ease of motion or is glossed as subjection.
- 51:3/q03/p1 With ease, smoothly, and with little effort. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah specifies little effort; Qurṭubī says ease of movement is established Arab usage and cites al-Aʿshā's cloud simile, neither slow nor rushed. Kashshāf and Nasafī paraphrase it as easy running; Abū Ḥayyān calls it easy movement. Ibn Qutaybah, Samīn, and the ship glosses also use this sense. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأعشى); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 51:3/q03/p2 Maysara: they are made subservient. — Ibn Qutaybah reports this as an alternative gloss. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 51:3/q04 How does yusran function grammatically?  [يُسْرًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether yusran describes an omitted verbal noun, serves directly as a circumstantial phrase, or intensifies the sense as a verbal noun.
- 51:3/q04/p1 It qualifies an omitted verbal noun, 'an easy running,' and can function circumstantially. — Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyyah, Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Abū Ḥayyān supply the omitted running. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says an adjective of an omitted verbal noun can serve as a circumstantial expression; Abū Ḥayyān attributes the circumstantial analysis to Sībawayh. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 51:3/q04/p2 It can be a direct circumstantial expression meaning 'easy' or 'eased'. — Samīn lists this among grammatical possibilities. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 51:3/q04/p3 It can be the verbal noun 'ease itself' used for emphasis. — Samīn gives this emphatic analysis in addition to easy running and circumstantial readings. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 51:3/q05 How is the sīn in yusran read?  [يُسْرًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the sīn is quiescent or bears ḍamma; the fuller form may reflect a nominal pattern or dialect.
- 51:3/q05/p1 With quiescent sīn, yusran. — Nashr attributes quiescence to al-Nahrawānī's transmission from ʿĪsā ibn Wardān through Abū ʿAmr. [1 notes: NASHR(النهرواني عن عيسى بن وردان)]
- 51:3/q05/p2 With ḍamma on sīn, fuller yusuran. — Qirāʾāt-ER describes this variant against sukūn and suggests an archaic nominal pattern or dialectal variant. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(skn)]

## 51:3/q06 How do the fāʾs order the four oath descriptions?  [فَٱلْجَٰرِيَٰتِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural participles describe several beings or one subject acting in an ordered chain.
- 51:3/q06/p1 They sequence different beings: winds, clouds, ships, then angels. — Kashshāf links winds to clouds they drive, ships moved by their blowing, and angelic apportionment of provisions through rain and sea trade. Bayḍāwī says increasing evidence of power orders distinct beings. Abū Ḥayyān calls them successive groups; ʿAlī's reported identifications support this order. Ibn Kathīr's star option would instead rise from lower to higher beings. [12 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(علي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Ali,Zemahşerî ve Râzî); TABATABAI(علي عليه أفضل السلام); KASHSHAF-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 51:3/q06/p2 They sequence one wind's actions, from scattering dust through moving clouds to distributing rain. — Kashshāf details blowing, scattering dust and gravel, moving and spreading clouds, then rain. Bayḍāwī says one kind can be ordered by actions; Abū Ḥayyān cites a poetic sequence of raiding, booty, and return. Rāzī, as reported by Kūrānyolu, prefers this; Iṣlāḥī appeals to a classical Arabic sequence illustrated by Ibn Zayyābah's horse couplet, then reads the sequence as winds. Biqāʿī gives a related wind–vapor–cloud–water causal sequence, with water divided among distant lands as mercy or punishment. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî ve Râzî); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)+; TABATABAI-]

## 51:3/q07 What is sworn by in this oath?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the expressed creations are oath objects, their Lord is implied, or supplying an unstated object is unnecessary.
- 51:3/q07/p1 The created things named in the oath. — Baghawī and Ṭabrisī say God swears by these creations; Ṭabrisī reports Abū Jaʿfar and Abū ʿAbd Allāh's distinction that people may swear only by God whereas God may swear by any creature He wills. Jishumī, Biqāʿī, and Kūrānyolu discuss the created signs. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله); JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 51:3/q07/p2 The Lord and power of the named things. — Qushayrī gives this outward explanation. Ṭabrisī reports a view that supplies their Lord because only God may be sworn by. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(قيل)]
- 51:3/q07/p3 No object of oath need be supplied beyond the stated expressions. — Farāhī, reported by Kūrānyolu, says an oath need not name an object sworn by and interpreters need not assume the object is being glorified. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Abdülhamîd el-Ferâhî)]

## 51:3/q08 Why does the passage swear by these things?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which force of the oath is emphasized: signs of creative power, proof of resurrection, governance through causes, reinforcement of the claim, or evocative wording.
- 51:3/q08/p1 Their benefits and ordered existence reveal God's creative power and oneness. — Jishumī points to winds and clouds as power and blessings; Baghawī to divine making. Ṭabrisī adds human benefits, oneness, and artistry. Biqāʿī says measured winds, suspended water-laden clouds, regulated ships, and angelic distribution disclose a wise Creator; excess, cessation, or stillness can destroy. Ibn Barjān says each oath accords with significant qualities that guide those God wills despite distracting appearances. Kūrānyolu points to atmospheric laws, biosphere changes, and angels in light of 51:7, 20, 22–23, 47–48; the plural participles invite possible referents. [7 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 51:3/q08/p2 The phenomena provide evidence for resurrection and the promised recompense. — Biqāʿī reports Bayḍāwī: power over extraordinary phenomena that defy ordinary nature proves power to raise the dead. Kūrānyolu similarly reports Bayḍāwī: the One who establishes extraordinary order and balance can fulfill resurrection. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says proofs of lordship substantiate the promised Day of Recompense. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(البيضاوي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Beyzâvî); TABATABAI]
- 51:3/q08/p3 The four signs exemplify divine governance through causes on land, sea, and sky, completed by angels. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls angels intermediaries and the four objects general means administering cosmic affairs; he says this broader account is more eloquent than Rāzī's all-wind reading and cites Shīʿī and Sunnī reports from ʿAlī. Biqāʿī describes wind, cloud, water, ships, and angels as regulated. [4 notes: TABATABAI(علي عليه أفضل السلام); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 51:3/q08/p4 The oath strengthens the claim, prompts reflection, and communicates meanings through its chosen qualities. — Biqāʿī reports Rāzī: God swears by wonders to reinforce belief and reflection, while humans invoke God as witness to hidden and public claims. Kūrānyolu says the oaths attract attention and stress seriousness; it reports Farāhī that meaning, rather than glorification, is primary and that an oath reinforces speech or resolve. Farāhī notes human oaths may honor an object or hearer, elevate a swearer, or invoke a curse on perjury; Ibn Barjān says the object's qualities matter. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان,الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Abdülhamîd el-Ferâhî)]
- 51:3/q08/p5 Swearing by unnamed qualities is elegant concision that opens several imagined referents. — Kūrānyolu reports Ibn ʿĀshūr's explanation of the omitted bearers of the participles and the resulting breadth of imagination. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]

## 51:3/q09 What claim do the opening oaths affirm?  [* · interpretation]
- 51:3/q09/p1 The promised judgment and recompense are certainly true. — Zajjāj parses the oath objects as genitive and cites 51:5 as its answer. Qushayrī says what is promised is true. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says successive oaths intensify the assertion of recompense; Kūrānyolu identifies verses 5–6 and Iṣlāḥī names the certainty of threatened punishment and reward or punishment in the Hereafter. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 51:3/q10 How do the oaths fit the sūra's argument about resurrection?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection traced is the sūra's internal argument or its continuation of the preceding sūra.
- 51:3/q10/p1 The opening and close promise the Day of Recompense, using proofs of lordship against its deniers. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the prophetic call warned of worldly destruction and lasting punishment, with good news too; resurrection and accountability are central to monotheism and prophethood. He says polytheists deny resurrection to undermine religion and the messenger. The opening calls that denial conjecture and describes the Day and the pious and deniers. Iṣlāḥī says wind effects and striped clouds substantiate both threatened punishment and final reward or punishment; winds and clouds can bring mercy or punitive storms. Kūrānyolu connects earth, sky, and heavenly provision in 51:22 to atmospheric and angelic signs. [8 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 51:3/q10/p2 The opening continues Sūrat Qāf's account of death and afterlife, warning that delay does not mean God overlooks wrongdoing. — Biqāʿī reports Ibn al-Zubayr linking the oath and judgment to Qāf 50:19, then citing 14:42 and 3:178 on divine respite for wrongdoers. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 51:3/q11 What is reported about ʿAlī's questioning episode?  [* · reports]
- 51:3/q11/p1 ʿAlī invited questions, identified the four oath figures, and rebuked Ibn al-Kawwāʾ for provoking rather than learning. — Abū al-Ṭufayl reports ʿAlī claimed knowledge of the future and of whether each verse descended by night or day, on plain or mountain. His answers name winds, clouds, ships, and angels. In the same exchange he calls Dhū al-Qarnayn a righteous servant, neither prophet nor king, struck on both sides of his head; calls the rainbow a sign with Noah protecting from drowning; and locates al-Bayt al-Maʿmūr in the seventh heaven beneath the Throne, entered daily by seventy thousand angels who never return. Kashshāf and Ṭabrisī mention a pulpit exchange; Ṭabrisī also names Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid as transmitters, and Abū Ḥayyān cites Ibn ʿAbbās's report. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أبو الطفيل,علي); KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); TABRISI(علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي)]

## 51:3/q12 What is reported about ʿUmar and Ṣubaygh concerning these oaths?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reliability of the chain raising ʿUmar's explanation to the Prophet.
- 51:3/q12/p1 ʿUmar attributed the interpretation of winds, ships, and angels to the Prophet while answering Ṣubaygh. — Ibn Kathīr cites al-Bazzār through Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyab; Itqān reports ʿUmar saying he would not assert this without hearing the Prophet, naming 51:1, 3–4. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); ITQAN(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 51:3/q12/p2 The report's soundest form stops at ʿUmar; its elevation to the Prophet is weak. — Ibn Kathīr cites al-Bazzār's criticism of two transmitters and says ʿUmar punished Ṣubaygh for apparently stubborn, contentious questions. He records two beatings of one hundred lashes, confinement, and a ban on company, later lifted after Ṣubaygh swore his troubling thoughts had gone. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 51:3/q13 What inward reading is given for the smooth-running ships?  [فَٱلْجَٰرِيَٰتِ يُسْرًۭا · inward]
turns on: Whether the image concerns souls and hearts, lovers, or reliance on providence amid peril.
- 51:3/q13/p1 The ships figure souls moving through works and stages of nearness, or hearts traversing seas of divine attributes. — Qāshānī ties this movement to inspirations and reads the oath by preparers, receptive capacities, and emanators as establishing their combined inevitable consequence. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 51:3/q13/p2 The ships represent lovers and beloved ones carried by providence through divine unity. — Bursawī likens their sails to catching winds of providence and their voyage to easy passage through the sea of divine unity. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 51:3/q13/p3 The ship evokes hope that divine providence carries mystics safely through turbulent seas of power. — Qushayrī compares sailors carrying poor passengers without charge in hope of the ship's safety with mystics' hope of being carried. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 51:3/q14 What other spiritual or sea accounts accompany this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate accompanying reports and a spiritual comment, not competing accounts of one event.
- 51:3/q14/p1 Remembering the basmala empowers the tongue, while knowing it overwhelms the heart and draws lovers' spirits. — Qushayrī calls the basmala a word of might: hearts tremble in its company, intellects are conquered, and lovers' spirits captivated. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 51:3/q14/p2 An angel controls the sea's provision; without that charge, the sea would cover the earth. — Bursawī reports this from Ibn ʿUmar. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عمر)]
- 51:3/q14/p3 Sea travel is permitted for a campaign, major pilgrimage, or minor pilgrimage; fire and sea lie beneath the sea. — Bursawī quotes a Prophetic report giving these three travel purposes and successive layers of fire and sea; he supplies no grading. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 51:3/q14/p4 The seas ask nightly to drown sinners, but God commands them to remain still. — Bursawī attributes this report to Kaʿb. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كعب)]
- 51:3/q14/p5 Solomon sought refuge from the sea after seeing only half its enormous ruler emerge between sunrise and noon. — Bursawī reports Solomon ibn David's question to a sea creature and its statement that half its body had yet to appear. [1 notes: BURSEVI(سليمان بن داود)]

## 51:3/q15 What is the sūra's place of revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 51:3/q15/p1 It is Meccan. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the context shows this and that no one has disputed it. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 51:3/q16 What meter is ascribed to the opening oath sequence?  [* · rhetoric]
- 51:3/q16/p1 The sequences in al-ʿĀdiyāt and al-Dhāriyāt are classified as basīṭ meter. — Burhān reports this classification, citing 100:1–2 and 51:1–3. [1 notes: BURHAN(قالوا)]

## 51:3/q17 What oath scholarship is cited alongside this passage?  [* · reports]
- 51:3/q17/p1 Few older scholars wrote separately on Quranic oaths; Farāhī later wrote a substantial study drawing on Ibn Qayyim and Rāzī. — Kūrānyolu surveys Ibn Qayyim's dedicated work and reports that ʿAbd al-Ḥamīd al-Farāhī's Imʿān fī Aqsām al-Qurʾān treats general principles, earlier scholarship, the history and human need for oaths, and examples. Farāhī's stated principles about meanings, implicit objects, and reinforcement are mapped under the oath questions. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Abdülhamîd el-Ferâhî)]
