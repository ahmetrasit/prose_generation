# 87:9: 18 questions

## 87:9/q01 What is conveyed in the reminder?  [فَذَكِّرْ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dhikrā denotes the Qur'an, the testimony, recollection through explanation, or admonition about God.
- 87:9/q01/p1 Convey and admonish with the Qur'an or revelation. — Mujāhid names the Qur'an (Māwardī). Wāḥidī calls dhikrā admonition. Jishumī includes promises, warnings and rulings; Ālūsī and Elmalılı include revelation's legal rules and knowledge; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the imperative calls for continued proclamation. [19 notes: MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR]
- 87:9/q01/p2 Remind people of God through hope and fear, His greatness, and warning of punishment. — Ibn Shajara names hope and fear (Māwardī); Ṭabarī names God's greatness, admonition and punishment. [3 notes: TAB; MAWARDI(ابن شجرة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة)]
- 87:9/q01/p3 Admonish people generally. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the bare command; Abū ʿAlī says delivery of the universal mission establishes proof regardless of acceptance. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; JISHUMI(أبي علي)]
- 87:9/q01/p4 Remind through the testimony that there is no god but God. — Muqātil identifies the reminder with the testimony. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 87:9/q01/p5 Explain matters so understanding yields recollection and needed knowledge. — Jishumī says explanation produces recollection and leads to necessary knowledge. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 87:9/q02 Whom is the Prophet to remind?  [فَذَكِّرْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the audience is universal, immediately Meccan, or restricted to receptive hearers.
- 87:9/q02/p1 Direct attention to receptive people likely to benefit. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām explains benefit as acceptance; Abū Muslim mentions hopeful receptivity; Ibn Kathīr says to address suitable hearers; Islahi says to leave the obdurate. [9 notes: MAWARDI(يحيى بن سلام); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يحيى بن سلام); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:9/q02/p2 The immediate hearers are Mecca's people or Quraysh. — Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī specify Mecca; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān identify Quraysh as those reproached; Corpus Coranicum proposes a wider audience at Mecca's Kaʿba cult. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA(بعض الحذاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:9/q02/p3 All people or God's servants are within the call. — Ṭabarī explicitly says everyone, even people whose belief seems hopeless. Abū ʿAlī calls the mission universal; Biqāʿī says everyone in every circumstance; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes Abū Jahl despite divine foreknowledge. [10 notes: TAB; JISHUMI(أبي علي); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:9/q02/p4 The messenger's call can exemplify a general appeal to every believer. — Corpus Coranicum attributes this observation to Robinson. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:9/q03 How does فَذَكِّرْ follow the earlier verses?  [فَذَكِّرْ · links]
turns on: How preserved recitation and ease prepare the public call.
- 87:9/q03/p1 The earlier promises of preserved recitation and ease prepare the Prophet for reminding. — Rāzī reads 87:8 as completing the Prophet so he may guide others; Abū Ḥayyān says recitation should benefit him and his audience; Qutb emphasizes the mission's weight; Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes purification from shirk; Ibn ʿĀshūr says fāʾ makes the command a result of what preceded it. [14 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI]
- 87:9/q03/p2 The second stanza makes the revelation of verse 6 public through admonition. — Corpus Coranicum says praise comes first, perhaps with close followers, then speech to a wider audience; it connects this to 73:2–5 and 74:2–3. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:9/q04 What does the omitted object of فَذَكِّرْ imply?  [فَذَكِّرْ · grammar]
- 87:9/q04/p1 The omission makes the command general. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says verse 87:10 subsequently identifies those who will heed. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 87:9/q05 What does the condition mean for the duty to remind?  [فَذَكِّرْ إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether in is a real condition, an ellipsis, a rebuke, or an affirmative or temporal use; and whether initial delivery differs from repetition.
- 87:9/q05/p1 Remind whether or not the hearers benefit; an unspoken alternative is understood. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, al-Zahrawī and al-Jurjānī explain the omitted opposite. Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī and others compare 16:81: heat protection implies cold. Rāzī cites 24:33, 2:172, 4:101, 2:283, 2:229 to show a condition need not imply its converse. Abū ʿAlī says the universal call establishes proof; al-Ḥasan distinguishes admonition for believers and proof for unbelievers. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects only the need to supply explicit omitted words. [41 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن أحمد النيسابوري); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الجرجاني); QURTUBI-FULL(الجرجاني); NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء والنحاس والزهراوي والجرجاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء والنحاس والزهراوي والجرجاني); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(غيره); ALUSI(الفراء والنحاس والجرجاني والزهراوي,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Baghawi and Razi); STUDYQURAN(Bg); JISHUMI(أبي علي); IBNASHUR; ELMALILI(Müfessirîn)+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)+; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:9/q05/p2 Remind where benefit is expected; after proof, fruitless repetition can cease. — Kashshāf links resistance to 50:45 and 43:89; Rāzī and Bayḍāwī console the grieved Prophet. Ālūsī prefers the literal condition because 87:10 names the fearful, citing 18:6, 50:45 and 53:29. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says further reminders to the wretched are futile; Elmalılı says harmful repetition is not obligatory. Biqāʿī says even faint hope warrants calling, since outcomes are unseen. [31 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; BIQAI-; BIQAI-FULL-]
- 87:9/q05/p3 The clause affirms that reminder has benefited or always offers benefit. — Muqātil reads in as qad; Quṭrub reads it as causal 'since' or affirmative 'indeed'; Ibn Shajara says reminder benefits in every case; Jishumī says it increases obedience and curbs sin; Elmalılı says an effective admonition offers benefit even if refused; Qutb says someone benefits somewhere. Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes universal benefit using 2:6–7. [18 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI(ابن شجرة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI(ابن شجرة,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); ITQAN(قطرب); BURSEVI; ALUSI(قطرب); ELMALILI(Müfessirîn); QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA-; TABATABAI-]
- 87:9/q05/p4 The condition rebukes resistant hearers and suggests benefit is unlikely. — Kashshāf likens it to preaching to tax collectors; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn cite a poem about addressing a lifeless hearer. Rāzī compares inviting one who may answer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this reading because 87:10 promises heed by the fearful. [19 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الشاعر,بعض الحذاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN(غيره); TABATABAI(قيل); SAMIN-DURR+; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-]
- 87:9/q05/p5 In means 'when' or 'since': remind when the reminder benefits. — Māwardī attributes a temporal sense to Ibn Shajara; Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī cite 3:139, while Ālūsī reports opponents in al-Mughnī; Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the reading to al-Māwardī. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI(أهل العربية); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(الكوفيون); TABATABAI]
- 87:9/q05/p6 The condition means 'if the hearers accept the reminder.' — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām supplies acceptance as the sense. [3 notes: MAWARDI(يحيى بن سلام); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يحيى بن سلام)]
- 87:9/q05/p7 The condition can console the Prophet while telling him to remind for reward despite nonresponse. — Ālūsī reports this alternative, then prefers the literal condition; Elmalılı cites the Prophet's compassion and distress, with 52:48, 9:128 and 26:3. [3 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 87:9/q05/p8 The condition urges hearers to accept truth and gain benefit. — Rāzī compares saying the truth is clear 'if you understand'; means more strongly motivate when people know the intended end will be achieved. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 87:9/q06 Who benefits from the reminder, and how?  [نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether benefit means acceptance, establishment of proof, or an opportunity offered despite resistance.
- 87:9/q06/p1 The God-fearing, believers, God's allies, and prepared hearers heed. — Sulamī and Thaʿlabī refer to 87:10; Qushayrī says God enables the receptive; Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes allies from enemies; Qāshānī frames readiness inwardly; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it inclines the fearful to truth; Study Quran cites 50:45. [14 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); QASHANI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:9/q06/p2 Sealed-hearted or persistently arrogant people do not gain benefit. — Qushayrī cites a saying in which light and dark are equal to an unseeing person; Wāḥidī contrasts the heedful with arrogant people; Ibn ʿAbbās names God's enemies; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 2:6–7. [14 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); QASHANI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:9/q06/p3 Believers receive admonition, while unbelievers receive conclusive proof. — Al-Ḥasan gives the two effects; Abū ʿAlī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī add that initial proclamation establishes proof even for avoiders. [4 notes: QURTUBI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); JISHUMI(أبي علي); TABATABAI]
- 87:9/q06/p4 Someone can benefit in every land and generation, even amid widespread hardness. — Qutb makes the broad claim; Elmalılı distinguishes an offered benefit from a hearer's desire to accept; Kur'an Yolu says at least some of the universal audience benefit. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI(Müfessirîn); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 87:9/q07 How should reminders be repeated after an initial call?  [فَذَكِّرْ إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · law]
turns on: The distinction between first delivery and repetition, and whether lack of future benefit can be known.
- 87:9/q07/p1 The first call is obligatory; repetition can cease after proof and entrenched refusal. — Rāzī distinguishes an obligatory universal first call from further repetition; Ālūsī says God disclosed sealed hearts to the Prophet; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the first reminder completes proof; Kur'an Yolu cites 53:29 for turning away from mockers; Elmalılı says harmful repetition may be left. [13 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:9/q07/p2 Keep calling universally because humans cannot know that someone will never benefit. — Biqāʿī invokes unseen spiritual opportunities and the Prophet's refusal to despair; he reads turning away as ending grief, not the call. Ibn ʿĀshūr says even Abū Jahl was invited. [10 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 87:9/q07/p3 There is no fixed count; custom determines sufficient repetition. — Rāzī and his fuller version both explicitly defer the measure to custom. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 87:9/q08 What teaching practice does the verse support?  [فَذَكِّرْ إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · law]
- 87:9/q08/p1 Teach what each hearer can grasp and needs. — Ibn Kathīr cites two sayings of ʿAlī: incomprehensible teaching can be a trial, and people may then accuse God and His Messenger of falsehood. ʿIṣām al-Dīn says call disbelievers first to faith and remind a believer neglecting prayer about prayer. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR(علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي); ALUSI(عصام الدين)]

## 87:9/q09 How is the conditional sentence attached grammatically?  [إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the imperative supplies the response or the clause is an aside.
- 87:9/q09/p1 The command supplies the omitted answer: if it benefits, remind. — Ṭabrisī and Nasafī explicitly analyze the response as supplied by the preceding imperative. [2 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI]
- 87:9/q09/p2 The clause is parenthetical and does not restrict the general command. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet cannot know beforehand who benefits and objects only to explicitly supplying al-Farrāʾ's omitted words. [2 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 87:9/q10 Why use إن and the feminine verb?  [إِن نَّفَعَتِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form marks uncertain reception or emphasizes the value of reminding.
- 87:9/q10/p1 In hints some will not benefit; the feminine verb marks even a weak chance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr points to 87:10 as the answer to the hint; Biqāʿī says faint hope is enough and links the feminine form with weakness. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:9/q10/p2 The condition presents the favorable case to strengthen or ease the call. — Rāzī says certainty of attaining an end motivates action and naming the better case can imply its opposite; Elmalılı says the condition emphasizes the command while easing it. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Müfessirîn)]

## 87:9/q11 How does the next verse clarify the reminder?  [إِن نَّفَعَتِ ٱلذِّكْرَىٰ · links]
- 87:9/q11/p1 Verse 87:10 identifies the God-fearing as those who heed. — Sulamī, Thaʿlabī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu appeal to 87:10; Bursevī reports al-Qāshānī's view that the concise transition is expanded there. Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses it against a reading that expects no hearer to benefit. [9 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); THALABI; BURSEVI(القاشاني); ALUSI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:9/q12 What inward lesson follows from the command?  [* · inward]
- 87:9/q12/p1 Prepared souls can be completed through preaching; disciples seek permission, purification, and more good. — Qāshānī conditions benefit on receptivity; Biqāʿī compares the Prophet to a physician and relates a disciple's need for a shaykh's permission to God's spiritual opportunities. [7 notes: QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:9/q13 What does the report about Moses and Pharaoh illustrate?  [* · reports]
- 87:9/q13/p1 The duty to invite is distinct from God's knowledge that the hearer will not heed. — Rāzī reports that God commanded Moses to speak gently to Pharaoh while affirming that Pharaoh would neither remember nor fear, citing 20:44; he says the command does not imply uncertainty. [3 notes: RAZI(الله); RAZI-FULL]

## 87:9/q14 What surrounding rhetorical and reading details are recorded?  [* · rhetoric]
- 87:9/q14/p1 Context prepares figurative judgments before a metaphor appears. — Al-Jurjānī says attaching له to تجوب makes the eye a figurative subject and changing order weakens the image; he cites five clouds for fingers, cued by thunderbolt, sword and heads, and fires for swords, cued by rejection of justice and faith. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 87:9/q14/p2 A nearby verb has a third-person reading referring to al-ashqā. — Jishumī attributes it to Abū ʿAmr, Yaʿqūb and Qutayba from al-Kisāʾī; others use direct address, preferred as Ubayy's reading and the majority's. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب وقتيبة عن الكسائي)]

## 87:9/q15 What broader setting and themes surround the verse?  [* · links]
- 87:9/q15/p1 The surah sets reminder amid divine preservation, ease, praise, oneness, and recompense. — Qutb says al-Aʿlā is Meccan with nineteen verses and the Prophet recited it at Eid and Friday prayer with al-Ghāshiya; he describes cosmic praise, revelation, afterlife and earlier scriptures. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:9/q15/p2 The admonition belongs to early Meccan public proclamation. — Corpus Coranicum proposes a Kaʿba cult setting, with parallels in 73:2–5 and 74:2–3. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:9/q15/p3 Related passages pair admonition with uncertainty about its effect. — Corpus Coranicum points to 73:19 for dhikr terms and cites 80:4, 50:45, 51:55 and 88:21–22. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:9/q16 Does one reported view restrict the command to particular people?  [فَذَكِّرْ · referent]
- 87:9/q16/p1 Yes, one view restricts it to particular people. — Qurṭubī reports the view without identifying those people. [1 notes: QURTUBI(قيل)]

## 87:9/q17 What obligation does the command establish?  [فَذَكِّرْ · law]
- 87:9/q17/p1 Remembering and calling people to God are obligatory. — Jishumī draws this duty from the imperative to remind. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 87:9/q18 How is the following verb for taking heed formed?  [* · grammar]
- 87:9/q18/p1 Yadhdhakkaru is the response form of dhakkara, with tāʾ assimilated to dhāl. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two articulation points are close. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
