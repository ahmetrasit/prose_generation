# 87:11: 11 questions

## 87:11/q01 What does وَيَتَجَنَّبُهَا describe the person doing?  [وَيَتَجَنَّبُهَا · meaning]
- 87:11/q01/p1 He deliberately turns away from and refuses to receive the reminder. — Al-Farrāʾ says he does not remember; al-Zajjāj supplies al-dhikrā as the understood object. Jishumī explains tajannub as turning to one side from what conflicts with one. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains its form as taking a position beside something, a picture of deliberate distance. Naṣafī adds nonacceptance; Bursevī adds refusal to listen receptively. Biqāʿī says he forces his originally upright nature away from a reminder delivered by the noblest messenger. Muqātil describes slighting or neglecting monotheism. Qutb says he will neither hear nor benefit. [16 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:11/q02 What does the pronoun ـهَا refer to?  [وَيَتَجَنَّبُهَا · referent]
- 87:11/q02/p1 It refers to the reminder or admonition. — Al-Wāḥidī attributes this to al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj; Corpus Coranicum points back specifically to verse 9 and notes Paret's comparison with Q 92:17, where subject and object are reversed and the object is Hellfire. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء، الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:11/q03 Who is ٱلْأَشْقَى, and how strong is the description?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · referent]
turns on: Whether the elative marks all unbelievers, a distinct extreme of hostility, the contrast between response groups, or a specific occasion; the notes also differ on whether it functions as a true superlative.
- 87:11/q03/p1 The unbeliever as a class, more wretched than a sinful believer. — Māwardī ties the condition to unbelief; Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī state the comparison with the sinner. Ṭabrisī specifies sinners who disbelieve in God and worship another. Bursevī includes all unbelievers as an alternative. Ālūsī reports this but prefers the persistent denier. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the definite article is generic and includes every polytheist; he prefers this over restriction to named men, because unbelief leads to eternal Hell. Kur'an Yolu calls them rejecters of God. The Study Quran describes them as deniers of return to God. [18 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR+]
- 87:11/q03/p2 The particularly hostile or obstinate unbeliever, most wretched among unbelievers. — Kashshāf, Nasafī and Ālūsī cite deep enmity toward the Messenger; Bayḍāwī says deep immersion in unbelief. Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī explain the extreme degree by rejection of Muhammad, the noblest messenger, while believers in him surpass believers in earlier messengers. Rāzī contrasts knowledgeable, hesitant and defiant hearers: the obstinate denier refuses the call and benefits from no warning. Ālūsī prefers the persistent denier of resurrection and truths that should awaken fear. [19 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)+; ALUSI+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 87:11/q03/p3 The wretched one as God already knows or has decreed him to be. — Thaʿlabī and Wāḥidī appeal to God's foreknowledge; Baghawī and Qurṭubī say he is wretched in God's knowledge. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls his misery decreed and says he rejects the reminder through disbelief; his fuller account contrasts those fearing God and the Hereafter, who heed it. [8 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 87:11/q03/p4 The most wretched of the two response groups: those who avoid the reminder. — Ṭabarī says they are the party whom the reminder did not benefit and who enter the great Fire. Abū Muslim explicitly compares the fearers and avoiders. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the definite article as marking a general class, while Qutb calls the word the absolute endpoint of wretchedness. Rāzī says an Arabic superlative need not presuppose a separate class of merely wretched people, comparing Q 25:24. [6 notes: TAB; TABRISI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:11/q03/p5 A particular named opponent or opponents. — Reports variously name al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra and ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa, sometimes adding Umayya ibn Khalaf or Abī; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports exegetes who limit the definite description to al-Walīd or ʿUtba. The differing lists and occasion reports are set out in the report question. [13 notes: NAHHAS(الحسين بن وقاد); KASHSHAF(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR(بعض المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-]
- 87:11/q03/p6 The unbelievers of Muhammad's community, more wretched than earlier communities' unbelievers. — Ālūsī reports an analogy: this community's believers are more blessed than earlier believers, so its unbelievers are more wretched; Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī make the corresponding comparison concerning Muhammad's followers and rejecters. [5 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:11/q03/p7 Simply a wretched person, with the elative used like a positive adjective. — Rāzī reports this alternative, compares Q 30:27's use of 'easy,' and cites an Arabic poetic line for the usage. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 87:11/q03/p8 The hypocrite in the date palm account. — The report through ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās makes verse 11 refer to the hypocrite who refused the Prophet's offer; Qurṭubī's fuller account also reports this identification. No grading is supplied. [3 notes: QURTUBI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 87:11/q04 What does wretchedness mean here?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · meaning]
- 87:11/q04/p1 It is the condition of severe punishment and afterlife misery caused by unbelief. — Jishumī calls shaqwa a condition leading to severe punishment, opposite felicity. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines its religious sense as humiliation and torment in the afterlife from unbelief and says one who dies a believer is not wretched. Corpus Coranicum notes the eschatological sense in Q 92:15 and possible threat in Q 91:12. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:11/q05 Why does the person avoid the reminder, and how responsible is he?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes variously stress personal choice, contempt, lack of fear, or divine foreknowledge; only Jishumī explicitly disputes determinism.
- 87:11/q05/p1 His refusal is his own blameworthy, avoidable act. — Jishumī says he has the capacity to do otherwise, explicitly arguing against determinist created acts and capacity. Biqāʿī says he forces his upright nature against the reminder, a sign of aversion; Islāḥī says rejection reflects the receiver's condition rather than a flaw in the message or preacher. [6 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:11/q05/p2 He lacks fear of God or the Hereafter and clings to disbelief or worldly delusion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says delusion and pleasure leave him seeking no escape; Ṭabāṭabāʾ contrasts him with the fearful. Islāḥī says evaders treat this life as all there is, and that loss of nature's God-given light leaves some unresponsive until the Judgement trumpet. The Study Quran contrasts deniers of return with both certain and uncertain believers who fear God. Ālūsī specifies persistent denial of resurrection or other fear-producing truths; Qutb describes a dead, dull soul anxiously preoccupied with trifles. [9 notes: ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI+]
- 87:11/q05/p3 He shuns it out of contempt for the reminder and hatred of its people. — Qatāda contrasts God-fearing people who remember with those who disdain the reminder and its people; Ibn Abī Ḥātim includes hostility as well as disdain. [5 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 87:11/q05/p4 His wretchedness is already known to God or decreed. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Qurṭubī phrase his state in God's foreknowledge; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says his misery was decreed. These notes do not themselves say he lacks capacity. [8 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 87:11/q06 What reports identify people or events behind the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted occasion or identification differs in number and identity of opponents, and one account instead concerns a hypocrite and ʿUthmān.
- 87:11/q06/p1 It was revealed concerning al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra and ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa. — The two names occur as an unnamed report in Kashshāf, Qurṭubī, Nasafī and Ālūsī; Ibn ʿĀshūr says some restrict the subject to either one. Rāzī says the wording remains general despite occasion reports. [12 notes: KASHSHAF(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR(بعض المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:11/q06/p2 The most wretched are ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa, al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, and Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn Waqād supplies this three-person identification in al-Naḥḥās. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الحسين بن وقاد)]
- 87:11/q06/p3 Occasion reports name al-Walīd, ʿUtba, and Abī. — Rāzī gives this variant list while arguing that the verse's wording applies generally and its sequence also has a rational basis. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 87:11/q06/p4 The verse concerns a hypocrite who refused a date palm exchange for Paradise. — A report through ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says the Prophet offered him a tree in Paradise; ʿUthmān then gave the petitioner his orchard for the leaning date tree, and verse 14 concerns ʿUthmān. Qurṭubī gives no grading. Bursevī relates the same basic event. His related illustrations contrast a man choosing eighty sheep over Paradise with an old woman choosing Paradise over a hundred sheep for revealing Joseph's bones to Moses. [4 notes: QURTUBI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 87:11/q07 What fate follows the avoidance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe both the following verses' afterlife punishment and, in Qutb, a present worldly misery.
- 87:11/q07/p1 He enters the great Fire and endures continuing punishment without restful death or beneficial life. — Ibn Jawzī calls it greater and more dreadful than worldly fire. Ḥasan, cited by Rāzī, identifies Hell in contrast with this world's lesser fire; other accounts distinguish levels of fire or make it the lowest level for unbelievers, with Q 4:145 on hypocrites. Ibn Kathīr relates the neither-dying-nor-living state to Q 43:77 and 35:36. Qutb says the punishment has no known end; Islāḥī says evaders wish for death in vain. Ibn ʿĀshūr says verse 12 explains the initially ambiguous description 'most wretched.' [13 notes: TAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:11/q07/p2 His earthly life is already spiritually dead, anxious, and confined to trivial concerns. — Qutb says the dull soul cannot perceive existence's truths or respond to its signs, before the unending afterlife punishment described in verses 12–13. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:11/q08 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes describe different scales of structure: the immediate two-group contrast and the larger turn to public proclamation.
- 87:11/q08/p1 It contrasts fearful hearers with avoiders and pairs each response with its outcome. — Qutb says the Prophet delivers reminder and God deals with hearers according to response. Corpus Coranicum says verses 10–11 contrast good and bad responses, with Hell in verses 12–13 balanced by promise in 14–15; its fuller structural account calls verses 10–11 and 12–15 a chiasm, comparing Q 92:5–11 and 14–21. Biqāʿī describes reciprocal ellipsis: fear and happiness imply the second group's hard-heartedness and misery, while their cause and outcome imply the first group's opposites. Al-Mulāwī illustrates such cause-outcome correspondence with planning and operating a waterwheel. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the comparison implies the avoider does not fear. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(الملوي); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:11/q08/p2 It is part of the messenger's public admonition after the opening praise and assurance of revelation. — Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza again addresses the messenger: after praising God, perhaps with close followers, he turns to a wider audience, making the revelation of verse 6 public in purpose. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:11/q09 What does the verse imply for the Prophet's practice of reminding?  [* · interpretation]
- 87:11/q09/p1 He should give the reminder without pursuing or forcing those who persistently evade it. — Islāḥī says the fearful will heed it, while the obdurate face their own fate; their rejection does not show a defect in the message or preacher. Ṭabāṭabāʾ says one reminder establishes proof for the wretched, further reminders do not help, and cites Q 53:29 for turning away. Qutb says the Prophet fulfills his duty by reminding, after which people diverge. [4 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:11/q10 What other Qur'anic usage illuminates ٱلْأَشْقَى?  [ٱلْأَشْقَى · links]
- 87:11/q10/p1 Early parallels use the elative for the damned or a threatened sinner; later passages use the positive shaqiyy. — Corpus Coranicum points to the damned in Q 92:15 and the Thamūd compatriot in Q 91:12, perhaps with an eschatological threat; it notes that the elative disappears from later texts, with the positive in Q 19:4, 19:32, 19:48 and 11:105. Rāzī compares Q 25:24 to show the superlative need not entail a separate comparison class, and Q 30:27 plus poetry for an elative used positively. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; RAZI-FULL]

## 87:11/q11 What wider sūrah setting frames this warning?  [* · sciences]
- 87:11/q11/p1 Al-Aʿlā is a Meccan sūrah of nineteen verses, moving from cosmic praise and assurance of the Prophet to warning and recompense. — Qutb says the Prophet loved the sūrah and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes on the same day. He describes the cosmos as a sanctuary of praise, the Prophet's promised preservation and ease, and the sūrah's foundations of divine oneness, revelation, afterlife recompense, and roots in earlier scriptures (87:18–19). [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
