# 94:2: 18 questions

## 94:2/q01 What does waḍaʿnā mean here?  [وَوَضَعْنَا · meaning]
- 94:2/q01/p1 God put down or removed the burden, bringing relief. — Damghānī compares 7:157. Ibn al-Jawzī gives putting down from above as the root sense and distinguishes other Qurʾānic uses: childbirth (3:36; 65:4), setting balances and record (21:47; 39:69), spreading earth (55:10), and swift movement (9:47); al-Yazīdī explains īḍāʿ as haste. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites childbirth (3:36; 46:15; 65:4,6; 35:11; 41:47), war's burdens (47:4), weapons (4:102), and shackles (7:157). Elmalılı allows unloading wholly or partly; Biqāʿī stresses decisive and irreversible removal. Ibn al-Jawzī also records waḍīʿ for low status and waḍʿ for an animal's quick gait. [18 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(اليزيدي,بعض المفسرين); TABRISI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; TABATABAI]

## 94:2/q02 How is this clause joined to the preceding question?  [وَوَضَعْنَا · grammar]
- 94:2/q02/p1 The negative question affirms that God expanded the chest, so the following past-tense clause coordinates with that affirmed meaning. — Al-Mubarrad says coordination with the surface question would demand a present-tense form. Biqāʿī stresses that interrogative plus negation affirms the act. Islahi says the initial interrogative governs verse 2 as well and compares Sūrah al-Nabaʾ. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); RAZI(المبرد); RAZI-FULL(المبرد); TABRISI; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 94:2/q03 Why does ʿanka come before the burden?  [عَنكَ وِزْرَكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the fronted pronouns supply a needed clarification or naturally emphasize and personalize an already clear construction.
- 94:2/q03/p1 Fronting ʿanka emphasizes the Prophet's personal relief and brings the glad news sooner. — Bursevī and Ālūsī say it creates suspense; Ālūsī adds that the reverse order would disturb cadence. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ʿanka conveys care without repeating laka and completes a general-to-specific expression. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and ʿAbduh treat this as ordinary Arabic emphasis, exclusive address, and close connection, not redundant wording. [7 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده); BINTSHATI+]
- 94:2/q03/p2 The addressed prepositional phrases clarify initially vague objects or are inserted for generality followed by specification, exclusivity, or emphasis. — Al-Nīshābūrī calls laka an insertion. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects that clarification would require a pause before laka, whereas readers join it, and ṣadraka and the subsequent objects already identify what is meant. Al-Kashshāf instead sees suspense then disclosure in the parallel expressions. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(النيسابوري,مفسرون); KASHSHAF; BINTSHATI-]

## 94:2/q04 What is signaled by God's use of 'We'?  [وَوَضَعْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural magnifies divine action or includes angelic participation.
- 94:2/q04/p1 It magnifies the gift beyond comprehension. — Al-Rāzī gives this as one explanation of the plural. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 94:2/q04/p2 It includes the angels as God's agents. — Al-Rāzī says their presence strengthened the Prophet and intimidated opponents. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 94:2/q05 What alternate readings of this phrase are reported?  [وَوَضَعْنَا عَنكَ وِزْرَكَ · readings]
turns on: Which transmitted wording replaces waḍaʿnā and whether the object is wizr or wiqr.
- 94:2/q05/p1 Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh reads wa-ḥalalnā ʿanka wiqraka, 'We released your load.' — Al-Suddī cites this to support lifting the load. Some reports render the same variant 'relieved' or 'removed'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī give the codex wording. Abū ʿAmr reports prophetic approval of the variants. [19 notes: FARRA; TAB(عبد الله); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI(ابن مسعود,السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF(ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA(أبو عمرو,ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أنس بن مالك وأبو عمرو); QURTUBI(ابن مسعود,عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 94:2/q05/p2 Anas reads a form meaning 'We released' or 'We lowered/removed' the burden. — Al-Kashshāf gives ḥalalnā; Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives ḥaṭaṭnā ʿanka wizraka; al-Qurṭubī records both. The versions differ in the verb, while retaining release/removal as the sense. [6 notes: KASHSHAF(أنس); KASHSHAF-FULL(أنس); IBNATIYYA(أنس بن مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أنس بن مالك وأبو عمرو); QURTUBI(أنس); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 94:2/q05/p3 Ubayy's codex reads wa-ḥaṭaṭnā ʿanka wiqraka. — Abū ʿAmr reports prophetic approval of all these variants. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA(أبو عمرو,أبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أنس بن مالك وأبو عمرو)]

## 94:2/q06 What does wizr mean and how is its weight pictured?  [وِزْرَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verse activates the general load image or the specialized moral sense of sin.
- 94:2/q06/p1 A heavy load, used figuratively for moral sin or distress. — Ibn Qutayba cites 20:87 and 29:13 for sins carried as loads. Jishumī cites 6:31; al-Ṭabrisī says sins are heavy because of punishment. Wāḥidī and Rāzī connect the image to backs carrying burdens. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces mountain and refuge, then wazīr as a sharer of load, and cites physical and moral uses (75:11; 20:29; 25:35; 20:87; 47:4; 6:31,164; 35:18; 39:7; 16:25; 20:100; 17:15; 53:38). Al-Naḥḥās gives wazara and mawzūr and notes a reported maʾzūrāt as phonetic accommodation if authentic. Ibn ʿĀshūr highlights distress and hardship. [23 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS(الحسن,النبي); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); IBNATIYYA; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI]
- 94:2/q06/p2 Its Qurʾānic sense here is specifically sin. — Majāz gives sin or sinful burden; al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, and others apply the weight image to forgiveness; al-Nīsābūrī? No separate reason is given beyond the metaphor. [8 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Bir kısım müfessirîn)]

## 94:2/q07 What burden did God remove from the Prophet?  [وِزْرَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether wizr names actual sin, prevented sin, inward distress, or a real but divinely eased responsibility; evidence includes the heavy-back image, 48:2, prophetic protection, and the sequence with chest expansion.
- 94:2/q07/p1 His sins, especially acts before prophethood, were forgiven. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Ḥasan, Ibn Zayd and others report past sin; al-Ṭabarī describes the weight of the Jāhiliyya. Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn Kathīr and others compare 48:2; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes socializing with his people, eating their slaughtered animals, and gathering arrows at al-Fijār while denying idol worship. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's reported disliked gatherings fit this range. Qatāda says the sins weighed heavily. Elmalılı rejects this reading as disproportionate to the back-crushing image; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls some alternatives weak or out of context. [70 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; TAB(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(شريح بن عبيد الحضرمي,مجاهد); NAHHAS(الحسن); THALABI(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك); MAWARDI(قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; JISHUMI(الحسن,مجاهد والضحاك وابن زيد وأبو علي); BAGHAWI(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد وقتادة والضحاك); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة وابن زيد والحسن وجمهور المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة والضحاك والفراء وابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI(قتادة والحسن والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(شريح بن عبيد الحضرمي,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(شريح بن عبيد الحضرمي,مجاهد); BINTSHATI(الطبري,قتادة); ELMALILI(Bir kısım müfessirîn); STUDYQURAN(many commentators); ELMALILI-]
- 94:2/q07/p2 He was forgiven both earlier and later sins. — Al-Zajjāj states this expressly; Ibn al-Jawzī transmits it. The cross-reference is 48:2. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 94:2/q07/p3 The burden was specifically shirk or association with God. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is transmitted this way by al-Ṭabarī and others. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ corrects the attribution to the shirk of Muhammad's people; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Muhammad never worshipped idols. [5 notes: TAB(الضحاك); TAB-FULL(الضحاك); BINTSHATI(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 94:2/q07/p4 It was minor lapses, mistakes, error, forgetfulness, or leaving the better action. — Al-Ḥusayn b. al-Faḍl specifies error and inadvertence; Wāḥidī and others say even forgiven small lapses distressed him. Al-Nasafī explains leaving the preferable for a good act and prophetic reproach. Al-Muḥāsibī says remorse makes small sins heavy. Al-Qāḍī objects that obligatory pardon could not count as a favor. [18 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT(قيل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(الحسين بن الفضل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(المحاسبي); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); QURTUBI(الحسين بن المفضل,المحاسبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل,المحاسبي); NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BINTSHATI(مفسرون); ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-; ELMALILI-]
- 94:2/q07/p5 God prevented sin and kept him pure, so the removed burden is one never incurred. — Al-Qushayrī and Abū Ḥayyān describe a metaphor of removal from one who had no such load. Some say he remained pure for forty years before revelation. Al-Rāzī cites an occasion when God made him sleep through a wedding with music. Biqāʿī excludes failure in duty because God protected him. [14 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI(قيل); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)]
- 94:2/q07/p6 God lightened the duties and responsibility of prophethood and aided him in carrying them. — ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz b. Yaḥyā, Abū ʿUbayda, al-Kisāʾī, and others say the mission's load became bearable; al-Māwardī says superhuman prophetic burdens need extra strength. Sahl says divine support carried him; al-Qu shayrī reports the same. Al-Qāshānī describes moving from undifferentiated witness to a mission of command and prohibition. Qutb says revelation and receptive hearts made delivery gentler; Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers success arranged for the mission. [30 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم,سهل); THALABI(عبد العزيز بن يحيى وأبو عبيدة); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكسائي); WAHIDI-QT(قيل); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد العزيز بن يحيى وأبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA(أبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبيدة وغيره); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(عبد العزيز بن يحيى وأبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد العزيز بن يحيى وأبو عبيدة); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]
- 94:2/q07/p7 The burden was concern for his people's sins or obstinate unbelief. — Some say the community's sins are attributed to him because they occupied his heart. Al-Rāzī says God reassured him against immediate punishment (8:33) and promised intercession. Al-Kashshāf mentions grief at obstinacy; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ transmits ʿAbduh's image of psychological pressure. Islahi says failed preaching increased his fear; Ṭāhā 20:1–3 reassures him. [14 notes: THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده,مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 94:2/q07/p8 It was persecution and the strain of warning hostile opponents, eased by victory and protection. — Abū Muslim's view is called best by Jishumī. Al-Rāzī contrasts early insults with later endurance of bloodied attacks. Biqāʿī says condemning ancestral religion provoked threats, while signs and 5:67 protected the Prophet; even worried relatives could not stop him. Elmalılı notes hostility after the first revelation and a promise of protection or 93:4–5. [9 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)+]
- 94:2/q07/p9 It was distress at pre-Islamic idolatry and confusion before revelation about truth or worship. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he saw Quraysh's idols without clear instruction; al-Rāzī says he could not stop corruption of Abraham's religion or knew no adequate way to serve a generous God. Elmalılı joins both anxieties and says revelation made service possible. Ibn ʿĀshūr links guidance at 93:7 to relief from perplexity; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers the pre-revelation confusion of 93:7. Islahi includes the search for truth. [16 notes: IBNATIYYA(بعض المتأولين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الراغب,الشيخ محمد عبده,مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI+]
- 94:2/q07/p10 It was ignorance of the revealed laws, relieved by teaching. — Al-Kashshāf gives this alongside forgiveness and establishing the Prophet's excuse after he delivered the message; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists the view among reported explanations. [5 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)]
- 94:2/q07/p11 It was the initial terror or heaviness of receiving Gabriel and revelation. — Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī report a fear so great he almost cast himself from a mountain, then Gabriel appeared and familiarity relieved it. Elmalılı cites Gabriel's power at 53:5–6 and gradual habituation. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites promises to preserve recitation and ease the path (87:6,8). [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI]
- 94:2/q07/p12 It was grief after the deaths of Abū Ṭālib and Khadīja. — Al-Rāzī conditionally links the sūrah to those deaths and says the heavenly ascent and angelic greetings relieved their loss and raised the Prophet's renown. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 94:2/q07/p13 It was loneliness, mockery, and worry during the pause in revelation. — Elmalılı says continued revelation and al-Ḍuḥā gradually removed or lightened such anxieties. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 94:2/q07/p14 It was the inner self's attachment to creatures, removed by exclusive repose in God. — Tustarī says the Prophet became receptive through God; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says awareness of creatures was removed from his inner secret. Al-Qushayrī combines divine witnessing with endurance of people. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI]
- 94:2/q07/p15 It was a dark clot taken from his heart when Gabriel opened and cleansed his chest. — Ibn ʿAbbās says Gabriel filled the heart with knowledge, faith, and wisdom; this made extraordinary burdens bearable. Biqāʿī accepts this as a lexical interpretation. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 94:2/q07/p16 It was material need and distractions from livelihood, eased by provision. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links removal to guidance and enrichment in 93:7–8; provision freed him for reflection on reform. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 94:2/q07/p17 It was the load of his mission after expanded capacity, removed by making the call succeed. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues from the sequence after chest expansion and prefers this contextual reading; some alternative readings seem to him weak or out of context. Qutb describes enabling the mission and opening hearts. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI+]
- 94:2/q07/p18 It was simply a heavy load lifted from him. — Al-Suddī states removal of the Prophet's weight without identifying a more specific burden. [1 notes: QURTUBI(السدي)]

## 94:2/q08 How can the Prophet's burden be called sin or described as so heavy?  [وِزْرَكَ · theology]
turns on: Whether a prophetic fault occurred, and whether deep remorse or protected innocence explains the severe imagery.
- 94:2/q08/p1 Small forgiven lapses felt heavy because prophets are intensely remorseful or of greater rank. — Al-Muḥāsibī says concern and sorrow magnified the weight. Jishumī adds that higher standing and greater blessings make a sin graver, or that the particular matter was grave. Muʿtazilites cited by Rāzī say a small sin could still affect reward seriously. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA(المحاسبي); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); QURTUBI(المحاسبي); QURTUBI-FULL(المحاسبي); ELMALILI]
- 94:2/q08/p2 The sin reading is problematic if forgiveness was obligatory or if the image implies an enormous fault. — Al-Qāḍī says obligatory forgiveness cannot be counted as a favor. Elmalılı says a back-crushing wizr does not fit minor lapses attributable to prophets, preferring hardship; Jishumī says the objection vanishes under Abū Muslim's opponents reading. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); JISHUMI; ELMALILI+]
- 94:2/q08/p3 The burden can be described as removed because divine protection prevented it from occurring. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this a deliberate hyperbole, as if removing a load from one who never bore it; Biqāʿī excludes failure in an obligation. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 94:2/q08/p4 The reported shirk should be understood as the people's shirk, not Muhammad's. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ corrects al-Ḍaḥḥāk's attribution; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say the Prophet never worshipped an idol. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الضحاك)+; TAB(الضحاك)-; TAB-FULL(الضحاك)-]

## 94:2/q09 How does the removal relate to the burden's weight on the back?  [وِزْرَكَ · rhetoric]
- 94:2/q09/p1 The back image portrays an almost crushing weight now lifted. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Thaʿlabī compare a travel-worn camel; al-Farrāʾ takes naqīḍ as the creak or sound of a breaking back. Al-Qushayrī says only God's carrying it prevented breaking. Biqāʿī compares a new saddle creaking; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says paired removal and bent back magnify oppression; Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses audible pressure. Corpus Coranicum notes comparable relief from a back burden in Psalm 81:7. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(الفراء); QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 94:2/q10 What is the purpose of reminding the Prophet of this favor?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: What the listing of favors is meant to evoke in its addressee.
- 94:2/q10/p1 The reminder calls forth gratitude and prepares for further blessing. — Al-Ṭabarī states this of the opening favors. [1 notes: TAB]
- 94:2/q10/p2 It reassures him that hardship in calling to virtue is followed by relief and success. — Islahi sees inner expansion leading to the repeated law of relief after hardship and completion of the mission; signs of success answer earlier Makkan opposition. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 94:2/q10/p3 The address also extends through the Prophet to every true follower of the Qurʾān. — Asad states this scope of address. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 94:2/q11 How does burden removal connect with chest expansion and raised remembrance?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the passage traces relief by inner capacity, mission success, or prior guidance and provision.
- 94:2/q11/p1 Chest expansion enables lifting the burden, followed by the Prophet's raised standing. — Biqāʿī sees knowledge and wisdom adding virtue while removal takes away vices. Qutb says the opened heart makes mission work lighter. Elmalılı links ongoing revelation, spiritual strength, and the Qurʾān's raising his name. Al-Ḥasan links forbearance and knowledge in the chest with the heavy load and raised renown. Al-Rāzī explains chest expansion as removing satanic whispering (114:5) and replacing it with good impulses; al-Tirmidhī locates reason and knowledge in the heart, which worry and greed can constrict. Al-Rāzī also gives reciprocal divine care or benefit returning to the Prophet as senses of laka. [9 notes: BIQAI; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(محمد بن علي الترمذي)]
- 94:2/q11/p2 The sequence recalls favors before and after prophethood, with burdens removed through guidance and provision. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the removal to 93:7–8; Islahi says Sūrah 93 names earlier favors while Sūrah 94 points to those after Islam spread. Asad says 93 addresses outward needs and 94 spiritual relief. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 94:2/q12 What reported divine conversation includes this verse among God's favors?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the report of the Prophet's question and God's answer is acceptable, and which favors each transmission includes.
- 94:2/q12/p1 When the Prophet asked about gifts or miracles granted earlier prophets, God recalled His favors to him. — The transmitted lists vary: orphaned shelter, guidance, enrichment, chest expansion, burden removal, and raised remembrance; some add God's choice of him as a close friend. Baghawī notes other transmitters add chest expansion and burden removal. Al-Qāḍī criticizes this report: prophets ask only by permission, and God's reply sounds reproachful. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,غيره عن حماد); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(عدي بن ثابت); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]

## 94:2/q13 How is this surah's direct divine speech related to modes of revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported mode might account for divine speech in these verses; the notes present possibilities without settling one.
- 94:2/q13/p1 God may have addressed the Prophet directly, with parts of al-Ḍuḥā and al-Sharḥ possible examples. — Al-Itqān says no certain Qurʾānic instance is known for this mode, though the end of al-Baqara and parts of the two sūrahs may qualify; it also cites speech while awake on the Night Journey and a dream report about the exalted assembly. [3 notes: ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عدي بن ثابت)]
- 94:2/q13/p2 Revelation could arrive with a ringing or soundlike experience. — A report from Ibn ʿUmar describes the Prophet's fear and silence; al-Khaṭṭābī says indistinct continuous sound later becomes understood, while another view calls it wing flutter. Al-Itqān says it clears hearing and was the most severe mode; some associate it with warnings. [6 notes: ITQAN(الخطابي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل)]
- 94:2/q13/p3 The angel could deliver words into the heart, appear as a man, or come in sleep. — Al-Itqān cites the Holy Spirit breathing words into the heart, says this could coincide with sound or human appearance, and records the human appearance as easiest. Some assign al-Kawthar's revelation to sleep. [6 notes: ITQAN(أبو عوانة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قوم)]

## 94:2/q14 How does this surah relate to Sūrah al-Ḍuḥā?  [* · links]
- 94:2/q14/p1 It continues al-Ḍuḥā's account of divine favors and reassurance. — Abū Jaʿfar b. al-Zubayr says both enumerate favors. Biqāʿī likens their order to a parent first listing evident favors then earlier provision; Asad says the surah followed almost immediately. Islahi sees 93 addressing earlier trials and 94 the emerging fulfillment of its promised success. Ālūsī cites the Night Journey favor report as a thematic bridge. Study Quran contrasts 93's outward needs with 94's spiritual relief. Corpus Coranicum suggests Psalm 4:2's spaciousness in distress for the opening and notes the direct divine 'We' also at 97:1 and 108:1. [11 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 94:2/q15 Were al-Ḍuḥā and al-Sharḥ treated as one surah?  [* · sciences]
- 94:2/q15/p1 Some treated them as one, reciting both in one prayer cycle without a second basmala. — Asad names Ṭāwūs b. Kaysān and ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, citing Rāzī; Study Quran attributes the practice to Companions. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Ta'us ibn Kaysan and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz); STUDYQURAN]

## 94:2/q16 When and where was al-Sharḥ revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the whole surah or only two later verses belong to Madinah.
- 94:2/q16/p1 Most call it Makkan, revealed just after Sūrah 93. — Asad says almost immediately after al-Ḍuḥā; Study Quran reports the majority chronology. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 94:2/q16/p2 Ibn ʿAbbās calls it Madinan. — Study Quran reports this attribution. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 94:2/q16/p3 Some place only verses 5–6 in Madinah. — Study Quran records this partial assignment. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]

## 94:2/q17 What merit is reported for reciting this surah?  [* · sciences]
- 94:2/q17/p1 Recitation is likened to visiting the distressed Prophet and relieving him. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this report. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 94:2/q18 How should sounds in wizraka be pronounced?  [وِزْرَكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether verse-end harmony warrants emphatic rāʾ or the regular light pronunciation prevails.
- 94:2/q18/p1 Make the zāy clear so it does not become sīn. — Ibn al-Jazarī stresses the voiced zāy when quiescent beside a voiceless consonant. His surrounding articulation cautions also cover the single controlled rise of geminated rāʾ, open low sīn after emphasis, shīn's spread, and ṣād's distinctions from sīn and zāy. [5 notes: NASHR]
- 94:2/q18/p2 The rāʾ in wizraka can be emphatic to harmonize with the verse endings. — Makkī and several authorities use emphasis; some works permit both and call emphasis more common. [2 notes: NASHR(مكي)]
- 94:2/q18/p3 The rāʾ can be light according to the regular rule. — Other authorities apply the rule; al-Dānī read emphasis with Abū al-Fatḥ but chose lightness. [2 notes: NASHR(الآخرون)]
