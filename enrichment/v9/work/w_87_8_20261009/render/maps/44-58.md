# 44:58: 15 questions

## 44:58/q01 How does the opening fāʾ connect this verse to what precedes?  [فَإِنَّمَا · links]
turns on: Which part of the clause the fāʾ is taken to introduce.
- 44:58/q01/p1 It draws a conclusion from the preceding argument and returns to the Book announced at the sūra’s opening. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ summarizes the sūra’s aims in their order and recalls the Book of 44:2–3. Iṣlāḥī says it joins the verse to the preceding sections, like an orator’s closing return to his opening topic. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 44:58/q01/p2 It introduces the restricted assertion that the Qur’an was made easy by the Prophet’s tongue. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this as one possible attachment of the fāʾ. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 44:58/q01/p3 It introduces the goal that the audience remember, with the preceding phrase preparing for that goal. — Ibn ʿĀshūr presents this as the other possible attachment of the fāʾ. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:58/q02 What force does innamā have here?  [فَإِنَّمَا · rhetoric]
- 44:58/q02/p1 It restricts by reversal, answering pagans who received an eloquent, accessible revelation yet responded with doubt and mockery. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects their response to 44:9. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:58/q03 What does the object of ‘We made it easy’ refer to?  [يَسَّرْنَٰهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the implicit object is identified as the Book itself or, more specifically, its comprehension.
- 44:58/q03/p1 The pronoun denotes the Qur’an or Book, understood though unspoken here. — Thaʿlabī, Baghawī and Ṭabrisī make the unspoken Qur’an explicit. Nasafī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran point to the Book at the sūra’s opening, especially 44:2–3. Rāzī calls it the opening’s clear, beneficial Book; Qurṭubī says the close urges adherence to this unnamed Qur’an. [11 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 44:58/q03/p2 What is understood as the omitted object is comprehension of the Qur’an. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted noun is supplied by context and identifies the ease specifically with comprehension. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:58/q04 In what sense did God make the Qur’an easy?  [يَسَّرْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ease names intelligible Arabic, facilitated recitation, or suitability for teaching despite complex content; Iṣlāḥī explicitly disputes a merely bland sense.
- 44:58/q04/p1 He made it accessible for understanding and remembrance through clear Arabic suited to its audience. — Muqātil and Wāḥidī speak of ease through Muḥammad’s tongue. Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Kuran Yolu connect ease with Arabic and intelligibility; the last says it lacks tangled expressions or concepts beyond its audience. Ibn Kathīr calls the language supremely eloquent and lucid. Asad says revelation is understandable in the Prophet’s human tongue. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes comprehension, rather than mere wording, the implied object, recalls 54:17, and says delivery through the best of messengers makes remembrance natural despite the audience’s doubt and play at 44:9. Study Quran reports that ease helps explain the ‘clear Book.’ Biqāʿī calls it greatly accessible. Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 43:3; Asad and Iṣlāḥī direct readers to 19:97. [30 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 44:58/q04/p2 God facilitated the Qur’an’s utterance and recitation on the Prophet’s tongue. — Muqātil and Wāḥidī say it was made easy upon his tongue; Baghawī glosses the preposition as ‘upon.’ Ṭabrisī explicitly adds that recitation was made easy for him; Qurṭubī extends ease to those who recite. Bursevī reports Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq’s claim that without this divine easing no creature could utter even a letter. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(جعفر الصادق)]
- 44:58/q04/p3 God equipped the Qur’an to fulfill its purpose of education and reminder, including intricate truths that call for reflection. — Iṣlāḥī defines taysīr as making something suitable, firm and sound for its purpose, like preparing and equipping a riding horse. He says majestic, inimitable styles convey intricate wisdom to hearts, while its subject matter still requires deep deliberation. He rejects a reading of ease as mere bland simplicity. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 44:58/q04/p4 Its ease means that it is merely simple or bland. — This view appears only as the reading Iṣlāḥī disputes; he says ease serves instruction despite intricate content. [0 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 44:58/q05 How can the Qur’an be easy when it is called a heavy charge?  [يَسَّرْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
- 44:58/q05/p1 Its language and recitation are easy, while obeying its duties can be demanding. — Bursevī reconciles this verse with 73:5 by distinguishing easy language from heavy duties and lighter recitation from harder action. He reports a gnostic’s anecdote: a scholar chose Qur’an recitation over a sacrifice for his sick son because speech is on the tongue while giving wealth strains a heart attached to money. Iṣlāḥī adds that the Qur’an’s intricate wisdom still demands deep thought. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعض العرفاء); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 44:58/q06 What grammatical role does the bāʾ in ‘by your tongue’ play?  [بِلِسَانِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the bāʾ describes accompaniment or the cause or means of the easing.
- 44:58/q06/p1 It marks accompaniment. — Samīn al-Ḥalabī specifies bāʾ of accompaniment while glossing the phrase as ‘in your language.’ [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 44:58/q06/p2 It marks cause or means: the Prophet’s Arabic makes the Qur’an intelligible. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the language is that of the Arabs, in keeping with each messenger speaking his people’s language in 14:4. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:58/q07 What does ‘by your tongue’ mean, and why name the Prophet’s tongue?  [بِلِسَانِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether lisān chiefly indicates the Prophet’s Arabic, his oral recitation, or the human medium through which revelation reached hearers; Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects to the unlettered-prophet reading on contextual grounds.
- 44:58/q07/p1 It means revelation in the Prophet’s Arabic language, intelligible to his people. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn identify the language as Arabic. Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain lisān as a metaphor from the speech organ to language; Bursevī cites a Prophetic description of Arabic as the tongue of the pre-Islamic people, and Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 26:195. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the possessive honors the Prophet although the language belongs to the Arabs, comparing 14:4. Biqāʿī stresses his effortless eloquence among Arabs who prize it. Iṣlāḥī specifies the pure Quraysh dialect as exemplary eloquent Arabic and says its use leaves hearers responsible if they fail to understand. [24 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 44:58/q07/p2 It means the Qur’an was conveyed orally on the unlettered Prophet’s tongue, a sign of prophethood. — Ālūsī reports an unnamed view that oral delivery rather than writing reflects the Prophet’s unlettered state. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the same explanation as a sign of prophethood but says it does not suit the verse’s summary context. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 44:58/q07/p3 The human tongue mediates revelation because direct hearing of divine speech would overwhelm and kill its hearers. — Bursevī presents this as a hint in the wording. Asad likewise calls the medium the Prophet’s human tongue, though without the claim about fatal direct hearing. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 44:58/q07/p4 It refers to ease upon the Prophet’s tongue in utterance and recitation. — Baghawī glosses the phrase ‘upon your tongue’; Ṭabrisī says recitation was made easy for him. Muqātil and Wāḥidī use the same upon-the-tongue formulation. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 44:58/q08 What does ‘so that they may remember’ ask the hearers to do?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ · interpretation]
- 44:58/q08/p1 Understand the Qur’an, take heed, believe, and put its guidance into practice. — Muqātil and Wāḥidī add that the people did not believe. Baghawī and Qurṭubī speak of admonition; Qurṭubī adds refraining from wrongdoing. Ṭabrisī specifies commands, prohibitions, promises and threats. Bayḍāwī says they recall through it what they had not recalled before. Ibn Kathīr and Bursevī add action. Ālūsī joins understanding, remembering and acting. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls laʿalla a marker of purpose and compares 46:12. Study Quran reports that conveyance should lead to admonition and belief, comparing 19:97. Iṣlāḥī names truths already available through intellect and nature, lessons in self and world, and earlier prophets, especially Abraham. Biqāʿī adds recognition of inimitability as evidence of divine oneness and the messenger: eloquent Arabs would otherwise match it; reflection on divine power, grandeur and wisdom makes resurrection and final judgment certain. [26 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ)]

## 44:58/q09 Who are ‘they,’ and what does the stated purpose imply about God's will for belief?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether ‘they’ includes everyone or refers specifically to believers.
- 44:58/q09/p1 God intended everyone to believe and know Him, and intended no one’s unbelief. — Rāzī reports this inference from al-Qāḍī’s reading of the purpose clause. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)-]
- 44:58/q09/p2 The pronoun refers to a particular group, identified as believers, so the verse does not establish a universal intent to believe. — Rāzī reports his school’s answer to al-Qāḍī; the restriction of the pronoun carries their reply. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا)+]

## 44:58/q10 What is the verse’s role in the structure of Sūrat al-Dukhān?  [* · links]
- 44:58/q10/p1 It concludes and summarizes the sūra by returning to the opening’s clear Book and urging response to the Qur’an. — Kashshāf calls it the sūra’s summary and a reminder through the clear Book. Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Iṣlāḥī, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran connect the ending to the opening Book at 44:2–3. Qurṭubī adds the blessed night and 97:1, and says the unnamed Qur’an is urged upon readers. Ālūsī says it condenses prior exposition and recalls the command to warn. Iṣlāḥī calls clear Arabic revelation a favor to Quraysh and a full conveyance of truth; his orator analogy explains the final reminder. Biqāʿī says the conclusion gathers divine oneness, creation, revelation, past peoples, warning, glad tidings and resurrection despite disbelievers’ denial. Iṣlāḥī describes the intervening argument: denial of life after death causes Quraysh’s rebellion, then arguments for reward and punishment precede the fates of believers and disbelievers. Study Quran reports that ease helps explain the opening title ‘clear Book.’ [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 44:58/q11 What other Qur’anic passages illuminate this wording?  [* · links]
- 44:58/q11/p1 The verse echoes statements that the Qur’an was made easy for remembrance. — Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 54:17; Study Quran points to the repeated refrain at 54:17, 22, 32 and 40. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this echo supports ease of comprehension for the purpose of remembrance. Iṣlāḥī refers to his discussions of 19:97 and Sūrat al-Qamar. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 44:58/q11/p2 Other passages about Arabic revelation and reminder clarify its purpose. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 43:3, Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 46:12, Asad directs readers to 19:97, and Study Quran’s reported view compares 19:97 for conveyance that leads to belief. Ibn ʿĀshūr also compares each messenger’s language at 14:4, the metaphor of tongue at 26:195, and continuing reminder at 19:97. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(JJ)]

## 44:58/q12 What follows if the hearers refuse this reminder?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَذَكَّرُونَ · interpretation]
- 44:58/q12/p1 Their rejection leaves them facing the threatened punishment, while the Prophet continues warning and waits for its outcome. — Muqātil and Wāḥidī say the people did not believe. Bursevī and Ālūsī connect the failure to heed with the Prophet’s instruction to await what befalls them; Ālūsī says this broadens the specific warning just given. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording implies continuing to remind them through the Qur’an despite resistance, comparing 19:97. Iṣlāḥī says rejection of the favor leads to the fate of earlier rejecters, and the hearers’ own history recalls the fate of rebellious people; his account of the close opposes valuing the Qur’an, which brings success, to arrogance, which brings destruction. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 44:58/q13 What recitation merits are reported for Sūrat al-Dukhān?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the time of recitation and in the promised benefit.
- 44:58/q13/p1 Reciting it at night brings seventy thousand angels seeking forgiveness for the reciter by morning. — Baghawī and Kashshāf both transmit this report from the Prophet; Kashshāf names the sūra Ḥā Mīm al-Dukhān. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 44:58/q13/p2 Reciting the sūra mentioning the Smoke on Friday night brings forgiveness by morning. — Kashshāf reports this as another Prophetic promise. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 44:58/q14 How is the preceding phrase ‘as a favor from your Lord’ construed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic attachment of the preceding expression.
- 44:58/q14/p1 It states the purpose of God’s act. — Ṭabrisī presents this as one grammatical explanation of the preceding phrase. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 44:58/q14/p2 It is governed by an implied verb, ‘He gave them.’ — Ṭabrisī presents this as another possible construction. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 44:58/q14/p3 It confirms the preceding description as God’s favor. — Ṭabrisī presents this as a third possibility. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 44:58/q15 What inward teaching is drawn from remembrance on tongues?  [يَتَذَكَّرُونَ · inward]
- 44:58/q15/p1 God grants remembrance to the tongues of servants He chooses and withholds it from others, who therefore cannot remember Him. — Sulamī reports this saying from Ibn ʿAṭāʾ. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
