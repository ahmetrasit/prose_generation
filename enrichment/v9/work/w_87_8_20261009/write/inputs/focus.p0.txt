# 87:8/q01 What does God's facilitation of the Prophet involve? [وَنُيَسِّرُكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether facilitation names the Prophet's readiness for a course or the breadth of affairs God makes easy for him.
- 87:8/q01/p1 God prepares and enables the Prophet to carry out an easy course.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taysīr as making a task easy for its doer and says the surface wording is a metaphor for empowering the Prophet; his readiness extends to preserving the Qurʾān, conveying the law and gaining good outcomes. Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise stress readiness and capacity; Biqāʿī includes contemplation and an inclination to ease. Corpus Coranicum compares Q 92:7 and 92:10.
  holders (15 notes): JISHUMI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM

- 87:8/q01/p2 God makes all of the Prophet's affairs easy and grants success in what he undertakes.
  reasons: ʿAṭāʾ extends the promise to all Muhammad's affairs; Shawkānī applies it to every religious or worldly undertaking. Al-Rāzī points to the unmatched transformation of an orphan among an ignorant people into a universal guide.

# 87:8/q02 Why is the Prophet himself the object of 'We ease'? [نُيَسِّرُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the direct object describes a prepared person figuratively or an inverted expression for easing the way itself.
- 87:8/q02/p1 The wording makes the Prophet fit for the easy way until ease is a settled capacity or disposition.
  reasons: Rāzī says the agent is made ready for the act and cites the Prophetic saying and Q 92:7; Bayḍāwī says this wording suits coordination with 'We shall make you recite.' Biqāʿī says the Prophet comes to love ease; Bursawī and Ālūsī say mastery becomes a settled trait; Elmali̇lı says success becomes ingrained. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the surface reading as metaphorical preparation, cites the ordinary object construction and Moses' prayer at Q 20:26, and says the lām marks the goal. Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Moses' pledge at Q 7:105.

  notes: RAZI:87:8/r6 RAZI:87:8/r8 RAZI-FULL:v31p130/r6 BAYDAWI:87:8/r2 BAYDAWI-FULL:87:8/r2 BIQAI:87:8/r3 BIQAI-FULL:v8p397/r4 BURSEVI:v10p315#2/r4 ALUSI:87:8/r2 ALUSI-FULL:v30p107/r1 ELMALILI:87:7-13/r4 IBNASHUR:87:8/r3 IBNASHUR:87:8/r7 IBNASHUR:87:8/r13 IBNASHUR:87:8/r14 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p282/r2 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p282/r3 TABATABAI:87:1-19#4/r1

  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as an alternative that keeps the verb literal; he says inversion strongly identifies the facilitated person with the facilitated way, comparing an Arabic camel-and-trough expression, a verse of al-ʿAjjāj and Q 28:76.

# 87:8/q03 What does the divine plural in 'We ease' convey? [نُيَسِّرُكَ · rhetoric]
- 87:8/q03/p1 It magnifies the gift by magnifying its giver.
  reasons: Rāzī compares other divine first-person plurals, citing Q 97:1, 12:2, 15:9 and 108:1 across the two notes.

# 87:8/q04 What does the lām in 'for the easy way' express? [لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:8/q04/p1 It marks the purpose or end for which the Prophet is equipped, or his readiness to receive it.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the benefited person or purpose normally follows the verb with a lām, citing Moses' prayer at Q 20:26; here it marks the way of ease and its acceptance.

# 87:8/q05 What is the form of al-yusrā, and what noun does it qualify? [الْيُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:8/q05/p1 It is a feminine elative adjective, with an omitted noun most likely meaning the religious law or way.
  reasons: Ṭabarī calls it the feminine built from yusr. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the feminine of al-aysar and says its fuʿlā form intensifies ease; the omitted head can be feminine singular or a plural construed as feminine, and context favors the law the Messenger conveys. Iṣlāḥī likewise identifies an adjective with an implicit noun.

# 87:8/q06 What is the easy way promised in al-yusrā? [الْيُسْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'the easy way' names conduct, the law, revelation, Paradise, the whole mission, worldly and afterlife outcomes, inner ease or the transition out of hardship.
- 87:8/q06/p1 It is the easy, tolerant, upright religious law.
  reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk contrasts an easy religion with hardship; other sources call it the tolerant Ḥanīfī law. Ibn Kathīr adds a straight, just law without deviation or distress. Biqāʿī says what God establishes is easiest and best suited to its time. Ibn ʿĀshūr favors the omitted 'law' because delivering it defines the Messenger's office. Study Quran cites Q 2:185 and 94:5–6.
