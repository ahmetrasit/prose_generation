# 87:8/q10 Which reports illustrate the Prophet's ease and teaching about it? [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different sayings, daily habits and episodes offered as illustrations of the promise.
- 87:8/q10/p1 He chose the easier of two choices and was gentle and cheerful at home.
  reasons: Quṭb transmits ʿĀʾisha's description; Ibn ʿĀshūr also cites the choice of the easier option.
  holders (2 notes): QUTB-ZILAL(الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:8/r4 IBNASHUR:87:8/r16
- 87:8/q10/p2 His clothing, food and sleeping arrangements followed what was available without fuss.
  reasons: Ibn al-Qayyim, as quoted by Quṭb, reports varied clothing and turban styles; pleasant available food without criticizing what he declined, including a lizard eaten in his presence; and sleep on a bed, mat, ground, couch or black cloth.
  holders (3 notes): QUTB-ZILAL(ابن قيم الجوزية)
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:8/r5 QUTB-ZILAL:87:8/r6 QUTB-ZILAL:87:8/r7

# 87:8/q11 How does this promise fit the sūrah and related passages? [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the verse to different contexts: the immediate recitation promise, other consolatory passages, the sūrah's wider themes and the intervening knowledge clause.
- 87:8/q11/p1 It consoles the Prophet as the sūrah moves from revelation's preservation to aid for his mission.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr links the promise to 87:6 and says the Creator who proportioned and guided also equipped the Prophet for revelation and easy law; a later reference says the promise of easing difficulties continues 87:8. Iṣlāḥī says the preceding sūrah warns stubborn deniers, whereas this sūrah addresses the Prophet and begins a series easing preaching fears; verses 6–8 promise maturing, preserved revelation with a divine exception and relief from known hardships. Quṭb describes the sūrah as good news of preservation and ease.
  holders (9 notes): QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r3 IBNASHUR:87:8/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p272/r3 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p277/r3 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p281/r5 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p413/r5 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r2 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r4
- 87:8/q11/p2 The ease here recalls the facilitated outcomes of Q 92 and the consoling ease after hardship in Q 94.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum compares Q 92:7 and 92:10, where the blessed are eased toward ease and the damned toward hardship, and Q 94:1, 94:5–6, where consolation likewise addresses the Prophet.
  holders (3 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1-8:kommentar/r8 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:8:anmerkung/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:8:anmerkung/r2
- 87:8/q11/p3 The sūrah's easy message belongs with its themes of divine oneness, revelation and final recompense.
  reasons: Quṭb calls al-Aʿlā Meccan with nineteen verses and reports that the Prophet loved it and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes on one day; he cites 87:18–19 on earlier scriptures.
  holders (2 notes): QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r4
- 87:8/q11/p4 God's public and hidden knowledge reinforces the preservation promise near this verse.
  reasons: Al-Durr transmits Qatāda's report that the Prophet forgot nothing except what God willed and that divine knowledge includes inner whispering; Saʿīd b. Jubayr says it includes what a person conceals inwardly.
  holders (2 notes): DURR(سعيد بن جبير، قتادة)
  notes: DURR:87:6/r6 DURR:87:6/r7
sources: CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al.
