[TABRISI:5:4#2/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Training hunting animals teaches them through God-given reason to distinguish trained from untrained; game caught by an untrained dog is unlawful unless slaughtered while alive.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس، عدي بن حاتم، عطاء، الشعبي، طاوس، السدي | reports | Ibn Abbas, ʿAdi ibn Hatim, Ata, al-Shaʿbi, Tawus, and al-Suddi add that a trained dog must not eat the prey.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Life is established by movement of an eye, ear, or tail; the animal must then be slaughtered by cutting the windpipe and jugulars.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس، الحسن، السدي | reports | Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, and al-Suddi say to pronounce God’s name before releasing the dog; the author calls this the stronger reading, rather than pronouncing it over slaughter.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The command to pronounce God’s name is clear evidence that naming God is obligatory for game and slaughter.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Piety here requires avoiding game caught by an untrained dog, not held for its owner, or taken without God’s name being invoked.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · عدي بن حاتم | reports | ʿAdi ibn Hatim transmits the Prophet’s instruction not to eat game a dog has eaten, because it caught it for itself.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو يوسف، محمد | reports | Abu Yusuf and Muhammad set three successful instances as the threshold for a dog to count as trained.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · أصحابنا | reports | The author’s school gives no fixed count: a dog is trained once it displays the stated qualities; a Magian’s dog may be taught and used immediately.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · أهل البيت | reports | According to the author’s report from the Prophet’s family, only a dog’s catch is lawful without slaughter; other predators must be ritually slaughtered.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · سلمان، سعد بن أبي وقاص، ابن عمر | reports | Those allowing other trained predators say an animal is trained by returning to its handler, chasing and catching prey, and answering the call; it remains trained whether or not it eats.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي، الشعبي، عكرمة | reports | Ali, al-Shaʿbi, and ʿIkrima are reported to hold that prey eaten by the hunting animal is not lawful.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | “Eat what they caught for you” supports the view that a dog-eaten catch is unlawful, because the dog caught that prey for itself.
[TABRISI:5:4#3/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The handler must pronounce God’s name when releasing the dog; if omitted, the kill is unlawful unless the prey is found alive and slaughtered.
[TABRISI:5:4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The common reading is mukallibīn with a doubled lām; Ibn Masʿud and al-Hasan are reported to read it with a light lām.
[TABRISI:5:4/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو رافع | reports | When people asked what was lawful from the animals whose dogs they had been ordered to kill, the Prophet was silent and this verse was revealed; useful dogs were then permitted, while harmful or useless dogs were forbidden or killed.
[TABRISI:5:4/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو حمزة الثمالي، الحكم بن ظهيرة | reports | Abu Hamza al-Thumali and al-Hakam ibn Zahir report that Zayd al-Khayl and ʿAdi ibn Hatim asked about six dogs that caught wild cattle and gazelles, some alive and some dead; the verse allowed eating what they caught.
[TABRISI:5:4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Iklāb means inciting a dog to hunt and training it; mukallib can mean someone with many dogs or a dog hunter and trainer.
[TABRISI:5:4/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Al-ṭayyibāt means lawful things, though it is also glossed as pleasurable; jawāriḥ are hunting birds and predators that earn food for their owners.
[TABRISI:5:4/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | A line by al-Aʿshā uses jāriḥ for one who acquires; the poet’s line explains the word as earning.
[TABRISI:5:4/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Mādhā aḥilla lahum has two parses: mā alone may be a noun with dhā as its predicate, or mādhā may be one subject with aḥilla as its predicate.
[TABRISI:5:4/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Mukallibīn is circumstantial, and tuʿallimūnahunna is another circumstantial phrase attached to it.
[TABRISI:5:4/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One grammatical view treats min in “what they catch for you” as extra, making all their catch permitted, as in the cited verse. | mentions 24:43
[TABRISI:5:4/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another view treats min as partitive: only the permitted portion is eaten, since a catch includes forbidden blood, offal, and glands.

[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p417/r1] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · author | holds | Ṭayyibāt is plural of ṭayyib, opposite to foul; it describes what pleases the self, including food and scent, and figuratively what is lawful.
[IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p417/r5] IBNJAWZI-NUZHA, d. 597 · author | reports | The verse uses ṭayyibāt for slaughtered animals. | mentions 5:5
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | One occasion report says the verse came after people asked what from the dogs they had been ordered to kill remained lawful; al-Hakim included it in his Sahih from Abu Rafiʿ, from the Prophet.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r10] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو سليمان الدمشقي | holds | Birds are trained differently from land predators: hunting birds are trained by eating, while dogs and cheetahs are trained by leaving the catch uneaten.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r2] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | The dog-killing order followed Gabriel's refusal to enter because a house contained a dog or image; they then found a puppy in one of their houses.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r3] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | A second occasion report says ʿAdi ibn Hatim and Zayd al-Khayl, whom the Prophet renamed Zayd al-Khayr, asked about their dog and falcon catches: some could be ritually slaughtered, others not, while carrion was forbidden.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r4] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الزجاج | holds | The question asks what is lawful; the answer supplies good things and the game caught by trained animals, with the latter phrase omitted because the context makes it clear.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r5] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | reports | The two explanations of good things are permitted slaughtered animals, or what Arabs find good and palatable so long as it is not forbidden.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r6] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | Jawarih are predatory land animals or birds used for hunting, such as dogs, cheetahs, falcons and hawks, that accept training; Ibn ʿAbbas says every hunting animal is a jarih.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r7] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · ابن قتيبة | reports | One etymology of jawarih derives it from acquisition: the animals earn their owners a livelihood, just as a woman with no breadwinner is called one with no jarih.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r8] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · الماوردي | reports | An alternative etymology calls them jawarih because they usually wound what they hunt.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p290/r9] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو سليمان الدمشقي | reports | A trained beast answers when called, goes out when sent to hunt, and holds the catch for its owner rather than itself; not eating the catch is a sign of this.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p292/r1] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو صالح، ابن عمر، سعيد بن جبير، عطاء، الضحاك، السدي، الفراء، الزجاج، ابن قتيبة | reports | Mukkalibin can mean owners of dogs; al-Zajjaj glosses mukallib or kilabi as a person who hunts with dogs.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p292/r10] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · author | holds | On the preferred distinction, a dog or cheetah eating its catch makes it unlawful, while a falcon or hawk eating its catch remains lawful; Abu Hanifa and his school agree.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p292/r11] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · مالك | reports | Malik permits a catch eaten by a dog, cheetah or falcon, and permits a dog-killed catch if the dog did not eat it.
[IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v2p292/r12] IBNJAWZI-ZAD, d. 597 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa does not permit a dog-killed catch merely because the dog did not eat it; if live game dies before one can slaughter it, it is permitted, but if slaughter was possible it is not.
