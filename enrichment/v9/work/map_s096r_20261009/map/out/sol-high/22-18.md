# 22:18: 39 questions

## 22:18/q01 What does 'Have you not seen' mean?  [أَلَمْ تَرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the address describes knowledge inferred or a particular inward vision.
- 22:18/q01/p1 Know or perceive intellectually, with the heart. — Ṭabarī says the heart knows; al-Rāzī says God's report gives this knowledge without sight. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it intellectual recognition from creation's states. Elmalılı also allows hearing of the matter. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 22:18/q01/p2 The Prophet may be addressed with a confirmatory inward vision. — Ibn ʿĀshūr relates his established knowledge to al-Raʿd and al-Naḥl; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 53:11–12. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 22:18/q02 Who is addressed by 'Have you not seen'?  [أَلَمْ تَرَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular address is generic or directed particularly to Muhammad.
- 22:18/q02/p1 The Prophet is addressed, while the point extends to responsible or capable listeners. — Al-Ṭabrisī includes all morally responsible people; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the address generic and reproachful; al-Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī include anyone capable of knowledge. Al-Itqān describes a generally evident address. [5 notes: TABRISI; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 22:18/q02/p2 The Prophet is the particular addressee. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the question as confirming his established knowledge; Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows a specific prophetic address and inward vision. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 22:18/q03 What does sujūd mean in this verse?  [يَسْجُدُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sujūd denotes observable posture, actual unseen worship, shadow movement, created subjection, or ontological dependence.
- 22:18/q03/p1 Submission, humility, and obedience under God's governance and will. — Al-Zajjāj prefers obedient worship and cites 41:11, stones fearing God (2:74), mountains and birds with David (21:79), and universal praise (17:44); its manner is unknown. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī cite created form, provision, health and affliction. Al-Rāzī compares 16:40 and 34:10. Al-Rāghib gives submission as the root sense, with voluntary and imposed modes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya's poet speaks of hills yielding beneath hooves. Objects' testimony to monotheism is stressed by Iṣlāḥī. [55 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,النحاس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشاعر); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات,الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 22:18/q03/p2 Nonhuman creatures' created form is a sign of their Maker that summons rational observers to prostrate. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites a poem on every thing bearing a sign of unity; al-Wāḥidī treats this as humility before the Creator and cites hills prostrating under hooves. Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects that this confuses prostration with glorification. Al-Zajjāj rejects mere created form because stones fear God and mountains glorified with David. Al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr allow testimony through lowliness and created dependence. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ(الشاعر,قوم); THALABI(أهل الحقائق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ZAJJAJ-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 22:18/q03/p3 The creatures truly prostrate, though humans cannot perceive the manner. — Al-Wāḥidī cites stones, mountains and universal praise (2:74, 21:79, 17:44); al-Qurṭubī invokes a sound-chain sun report, later identified as Muslim's, entailing life and intellect. Al-Zajjāj says the manner requires a report; theologians say God must create discernment in inanimate matter. Al-Nasafī compares imperceptible glorification (17:44). [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أرباب الأصول); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ZAJJAJ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 22:18/q03/p4 The listed beings prostrate by their shadows moving with the sun. — Mujāhid explains the shadows; Ṭabarī gives sunrise and decline, Muqātil adds zenith, Iṣlāḥī says their direction opposite the sun identifies the Creator as worthy of prostration. The cited parallels are 13:15 and 16:48–49; even a garment prostrates by its shade. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 22:18/q03/p5 For humans it can mean bodily worship, while for nonhumans it means figurative or compelled submission. — Al-Rāzī and al-Samīn discuss a homonym carrying two senses; Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly permits literal and figurative meanings together, citing 60:2 and 83:1 as analogies. Al-Ālūsī cites the lexical definition of forehead placement and disputes using the verse to prove a homonym because nonhuman real prostration is possible. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects a rule limiting multiple senses to negative sentences. [27 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كمال,التفتازاني); IBNASHUR-FULL(برهان علي المرغيناني); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:18/q03/p6 Everything prostrates through intrinsic dependence on God for existence. — Al-Rāzī says contingent beings need the Necessary Being at origin and continuously (53:42); al-Qaffāl links that need and acknowledgment to 17:44. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says all humans necessarily share this ontological abasement. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); TABATABAI]
- 22:18/q03/p7 Prostration is inward recognition of what reason requires even in a disbeliever. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this as a separate view of the unbeliever's prostration. [1 notes: THALABI(قيل)]

## 22:18/q04 What is the lexical range of sujūd?  [يَسْجُدُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the baseline is broad Arabic submission or the bodily act.
- 22:18/q04/p1 Its basic sense is humility or submission, extending to bodily worship and a specific legal act. — Al-Jishumī distinguishes physical and legal forms; al-Rāghib distinguishes willing rewarded acts and imposed subjection. The hills-under-hooves poem illustrates nonritual submission. [6 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشاعر); ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر)]
- 22:18/q04/p2 In customary or legal usage it means placing the head or forehead down. — Ibn Kamāl cites a lexicon; al-Taftāzānī says placing the back of the head does not count. Al-Ālūsī therefore treats nonhuman uses as metaphorical if this definition is adopted. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن كمال,التفتازاني)]

## 22:18/q05 What do 'those in the heavens' and 'those on earth' include?  [مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: Whether man includes nonrational beings by predominance or denotes rational inhabitants only.
- 22:18/q05/p1 All upper and lower creatures, including rational and nonrational beings. — Al-Farrāʾ includes angels in heaven and mountains, jinn and other earthly kinds; Ibn Kathīr adds humans, animals and birds. Al-Bayḍāwī explains man for mixed beings by grammatical predominance; al-Ālūsī prefers inclusion. Elmalılı includes angels and souls. Al-Burhān explains rational predominance and personification. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(سيبويه); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 22:18/q05/p2 The two phrases name rational inhabitants: heavenly angels and earthly humans or jinn. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies the earthly humans as people who were worshipped; separately named nonrational beings then follow. Al-Ālūsī allows a rational-only reading. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:18/q05/p3 Heavenly inhabitants all glorify, while only some earthly inhabitants prostrate. — Yaḥyā b. Sallām marks this difference. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 22:18/q06 Why is man repeated, and how does its use compare with other verses?  [مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَن فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether repetition emphasizes universality and how relative pronouns accommodate mixed classes.
- 22:18/q06/p1 Its repetition stresses God's universal sovereignty while the verse details heavenly and earthly beings. — Al-Biqāʿī says either broad or rational-only use makes lower creatures' submission follow all the more; al-Burhān notes repetition in several suras. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 13:15, where man is unrepeated in a general statement; 22:18 lists specifics. [3 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 22:18/q06/p2 The choice of man and mā follows the mix and usual rationality of the creatures named. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says dābba normally denotes nonrational creatures, hence mā in 16:49; coordination can join rational beings after mā and nonrational after man. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 22:18/q07 Why name the sun, moon, stars, mountains, trees, and beasts separately?  [وَٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ وَٱلنُّجُومُ وَٱلْجِبَالُ وَٱلشَّجَرُ وَٱلدَّوَآبُّ · rhetoric]
turns on: What rhetorical work their surprising status and human worship do.
- 22:18/q07/p1 Their worship by people makes their subjection to God pointed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Himyar's sun, Kināna's moon, and Arab star cults, plus fire, stone and wood idols and cattle or roosters. Abū Ḥayyān/Biqāʿī list Aldebaran–Tamīm, Sirius–Lakhm, Pleiades–Ṭayyiʾ, Mercury–Asad, al-Mirzam–Rabīʿa; al-Ālūsī gives some variant tribal attributions and names Ghatafān's al-ʿUzzā tree. Ibn Kathīr cites 41:37. Winds and air remain unnamed. [16 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:18/q07/p2 Their prostration seems surprising, making the examples instructive. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī pair apparent improbability with their worship by some; Elmalılı describes them as named examples of all things subject to God. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 22:18/q08 How do the sun, moon, and stars prostrate?  [وَٱلشَّمْسُ وَٱلْقَمَرُ وَٱلنُّجُومُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the prostration is a setting event, visible motion, or real unseen humility.
- 22:18/q08/p1 They prostrate when setting and wait for permission to resume their course. — Abū al-ʿĀliya says each remains prostrate until permitted to turn toward rising; Muqātil says beneath the Throne before evening prayer. The Abū Dharr report from the Prophet has the sun beneath the Throne asking leave and one day ordered back. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية الرياحي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية,أبو ذر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية)]
- 22:18/q08/p2 Their courses, risings, settings, and phases display submission. — Bursevī ties these movements to human benefit; Iṣlāḥī describes daily standing, kneeling and prostration of the sun and lunar phases and stars as signs. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:18/q08/p3 They really humble themselves as created beings. — Ibn Kathīr cites the eclipse report: eclipses do not mark a person's death or birth, and luminaries humble themselves at divine manifestation; Ṭāwūs and ʿAbdallāh b. ʿAmr say the moon weeps from fear of God. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(طاوس,عبد الله بن عمرو)]

## 22:18/q09 How do mountains, trees, and animals prostrate?  [وَٱلْجِبَالُ وَٱلشَّجَرُ وَٱلدَّوَآبُّ · interpretation]
turns on: What observable or reported act is called their prostration.
- 22:18/q09/p1 Through their shadows. — Muqātil locates the motion at sunrise and after zenith; Ibn Kathīr cites shadows right and left (16:48); al-Ḍaḥḥāk includes animals and birds when shadows return. [5 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:18/q09/p2 Through their created properties and useful products. — Bursevī specifies springs and minerals in mountains, shade and fruit in trees, and animal design; all states remain subject to divine giving and affliction. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 22:18/q09/p3 Animals remember or glorify God. — A report from Aḥmad forbids treating animals' backs as pulpits because the animal may remember God more than its rider. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 22:18/q10 What does al-dawābb denote?  [وَٱلدَّوَآبُّ · meaning]
- 22:18/q10/p1 All animals or moving creatures. — Ibn Kathīr says all animals; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes cattle and a rooster among creatures once worshipped. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 22:18/q11 How is al-dawābb read?  [وَٱلدَّوَآبُّ · readings]
turns on: Whether the geminate bāʾ is retained; the light form is contested.
- 22:18/q11/p1 The majority double the bāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts the usual reading with al-Zuhrī's rare form. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور والزهري)]
- 22:18/q11/p2 Al-Zuhrī, and by one report Ibn Waththāb, lighten the bāʾ. — Abū al-Faḍl and Abū al-Baqāʾ explain avoidance of gemination or two sukūns; Ibn Jinnī calls it weak in analogy and transmission, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya says its parallels are poetic only. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزهري); SAMIN-DURR(الزهري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور والزهري); ALUSI-FULL(الزهري وابن وثاب)]

## 22:18/q12 Who are the many people who prostrate?  [وَكَثِيرٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · referent]
turns on: Whether kathīr identifies worshippers directly or an elliptic group defined by reward.
- 22:18/q12/p1 Believers, Muslims, monotheists, or obedient worshippers. — Al-Ḥasan limits this prostration to Muslims; Mujāhid calls the group believers. Elmalılı says kathīr is a substantial portion, not necessarily a majority. Believers voluntarily worship although all humans undergo imposed subjection. Tawus says only Adam's descendants are excepted from universal prostration, many of them prostrating. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(طاوس,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 22:18/q12/p2 Those destined for Paradise. — Ibn ʿAbbās' report treats the first many as people in Paradise, paired with the many punished; this accompanies an analysis with a missing predicate. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 22:18/q13 Why say 'many people' when earthly beings were already included?  [وَكَثِيرٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the qualification marks the kind of submission or the size of a group.
- 22:18/q13/p1 It distinguishes voluntary worship, which earns reward, from humanity's involuntary created subjection. — Al-Rāzī says it prevents assuming all humans worship as all angels do; al-Biqāʿī calls the singled-out prostration praiseworthy. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says ritual falling to the ground expresses servanthood; Iṣlāḥī contrasts free choice with instinctive universal obedience. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 22:18/q13/p2 Only a substantial portion of humans is intended. — Elmalılı says kathīr neither means all nor necessarily the majority; Bursevī says the obedient group is small relative to the condemned, about one in a thousand. [2 notes: ELMALILI; BURSEVI]

## 22:18/q14 How is the first kathīr construed?  [وَكَثِيرٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the first kathīr is coordinated with the verb or begins an elliptical independent statement.
- 22:18/q14/p1 It coordinates with earlier prostrating subjects. — Al-Naḥḥās describes the listed items as conjoined to man; Ibn ʿĀshūr lets the single verb carry literal human and figurative nonhuman senses. Al-Samīn allows shared submission, homonymy, or mixed literal and figurative senses; al-Ālūsī objects that generic subjection would include all people and make kathīr pointless. [11 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كمال); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:18/q14/p2 It starts a new clause with an implied 'prostrate' of bodily worship. — Al-Samīn distinguishes human posture from nonhuman submission; Bursevī says coordination would mix literal and metaphorical senses; al-Ālūsī prefers this reading as encouragement to worship. Al-Mughnī objects that a homonymous stated verb may not license omission; al-Khafājī defends implication when senses are related. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي,المغني); ELMALILI(bazı müfessirîn); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 22:18/q14/p3 It is a subject with a missing predicate, such as 'rewarded' or 'obedient'. — Ibn ʿAbbās' Paradise report supports 'rewarded'; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī infer the opposite of the punishment clause. Abū al-Baqāʾ offers obedient or rewarded. Abū Ḥayyān calls some associated readings weak and al-Samīn finds this analysis of little use. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Razi); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 22:18/q14/p4 Kathīr is the subject and 'of the people' its predicate: the righteous are people in the fullest sense. — Al-Rāghib says figurative 'people' can mean those exercising reason and remembrance; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call this strained or weak. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:18/q14/p5 The two human groups are contrasted after the description ends at 'beasts'. — Ibn ʿAbbās explains the groups as many in Paradise and many punished; this requires detaching the first kathīr from the creatures' prostration. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 22:18/q15 What does the second 'many' refer to?  [وَكَثِيرٌ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ · referent]
turns on: Whether those punished stand outside worship or also prostrate involuntarily, and whether jinn are included.
- 22:18/q15/p1 Unbelievers who refuse prostration, monotheism, or obedience and consequently deserve punishment. — Al-Farrāʾ says punishment entails abandonment of obedience; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the consequence is named in place of the refusal. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies polytheists condemned by warning. Iṣlāḥī says they disgraced themselves and sought to belittle God. Al-Ālūsī resists extending the group to jinn because 'of the people' restricts only the first group. Al-Qāshānī links the condemned multitude to eternal decree, Satanic traits, and misfortune despite inward compulsion. [36 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو بكر بن عياش,بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QASHANI]
- 22:18/q15/p2 Disbelievers among humans and jinn whose shadows nevertheless prostrate. — Muqātil extends this group to jinn; Mujāhid says the condemned person prostrates with his shadow. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also allows compelled submission in hardship. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:18/q15/p3 Their suffering follows necessarily from their worldly attitude. — Asad says the result is deserved rather than an arbitrary punishment in the conventional sense; Study Quran glosses 'became due' as deserved. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 22:18/q16 How is the second kathīr joined and parsed?  [وَكَثِيرٌ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the conjunction continues the prostrating list or starts a contrasted clause, and whether omitted material is supplied.
- 22:18/q16/p1 It begins an independent nominative subject with 'punishment became due' as predicate; refusal to prostrate is implied. — Al-Farrāʾ says the resumptive pronoun in ʿalayhi licenses nominative and cites 6:39, 26:224, 41:17; accusative coordination is possible by comparison. Al-Kisāʾī and al-Farrāʾ favor the nominative after a pause at the first kathīr; al-Wāḥidī notes Nāfiʿ, al-Kisāʾī, and Abū Ḥātim's stop. Ibn ʿAṭiyya points to the following humiliation warning; al-Ālūsī prefers this apparent analysis. [18 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو بكر بن عياش,بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي والفراء); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; NAHHAS+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 22:18/q16/p2 It coordinates with the earlier prostrators: the condemned also prostrate under compulsion. — Mujāhid names their shadows; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds submission under hardship, and al-Samīn clarifies that this coordinates clauses with a subject and 'prostrates' predicate, not the isolated word kathīr. All things undergo God's changes of state. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:18/q16/p3 The second kathīr repeats the first to magnify the many punished. — Al-Zamakhsharī's analysis makes 'punishment became due' the predicate of the coordinated repeated subject. Al-Samīn defends repetition by analogy with 'a thousand and a thousand'; an anonymous قبر line supplies poetic evidence. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Baḥr call the analysis weak. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(البحر)-]
- 22:18/q16/p4 An accusative construction supplies an omitted verb: God humbled many. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this further possibility; al-Farrāʾ permits accusative coordination, contrasting the nominative's resumptive structure. [2 notes: FARRA; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 22:18/q17 How is the second kathīr read?  [وَكَثِيرٌ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the word denotes a multitude or describes punishment as great.
- 22:18/q17/p1 The standard reading has kathīr, 'many'. — The usual text is the reference point for Junāḥ's variant. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جناح بن حبيش); SAMIN-DURR(جناح بن حبيش)]
- 22:18/q17/p2 Junāḥ b. Ḥubaysh reads kabīr, 'great', with bāʾ replacing thāʾ. — The resulting wording says great punishment is due to him. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جناح بن حبيش); SAMIN-DURR(جناح بن حبيش)]

## 22:18/q18 How is ḥaqqa read and construed?  [حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is active, passive, or replaced by an emphatic accusative.
- 22:18/q18/p1 The usual active ḥaqqa means punishment became due. — Study Quran says it means deserved; al-Farrāʾ makes the clause imply refusal to prostrate. [2 notes: FARRA; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 22:18/q18/p2 A variant gives ḥuqqa with ḍamma, passive. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report the ḍamma form. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:18/q18/p3 Another reading has ḥaqqan in the accusative as emphasis with an understood verb. — Al-Rāzī calls it emphasis that punishment truly applies; al-Samīn says al-ʿadhāb is the verb's agent; Abū Ḥayyān calls the accusative a cognate expression. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:18/q19 How does the verse balance the two human groups?  [وَكَثِيرٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ وَكَثِيرٌ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ · rhetoric]
turns on: How 'many' and the omitted opposites express the difference between rewarded obedience and condemned refusal.
- 22:18/q19/p1 Ellipsis gives each group the unstated opposite: reward for prostrators and refusal for the punished. — Al-Biqāʿī says each expressed half supplies the other; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says naming punishment shows it follows the condemned person's own act and prepares the warning. al-Wāḥidī reports a stop after the first group. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 22:18/q19/p2 Both are called many, even if the prostrating group is much smaller. — Bursevī says about one in a thousand belong to the first; Ibn Masʿūd says one person on truth can be called the great majority, and another view says a few are 'many' in gathered strength. Reports give 999 to one and a white hair on a black ox; Bursevī says counting angels, houris, and youths reverses the total of mercy and wrath. Al-Nīsābūrī gives divine independence, believers' visible honor, and protection of the beloved among enemies as reasons. [6 notes: BURSEVI(ابن مسعود,النيسابوري,بعضهم)]
- 22:18/q19/p3 The contrast reflects human choice and moral rank. — Iṣlāḥī says chosen rejection disgraces humans among universally obedient creatures and describes a narrowing hierarchy from believers through the rare people of union, where the perfect human is creation's aim. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 22:18/q20 What does yuhin mean, and how does Arabic distinguish related forms?  [يُهِنِ · meaning]
- 22:18/q20/p1 To humiliate, disgrace, or diminish someone's worth. — Al-Dāmghānī relates the root to humiliating punishment and cites 15:88 and 16:59; Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes hawn with fatḥa as calm dignity. Abū ʿUbayda distinguishes hūn with ḍamma, disgrace, from hawn, gentleness. Al-Rāghib separates chosen praiseworthy humility from imposed blameworthy humiliation; al-Jishumī traces ahānah and a mortar name to hawn. [7 notes: THALABI; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو عبيدة,الراغب)]

## 22:18/q21 Whom does God humiliate here, and how?  [وَمَن يُهِنِ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether humiliation is described as damnation, decreed wretchedness, or divine vengeance.
- 22:18/q21/p1 The disbeliever or refuser of worship, whom God renders wretched and destined for Hell. — Yaḥyā b. Sallām specifies admission to the Fire; Ibn ʿAbbās says treating God's worship lightly brings disgrace and banishment. Al-Ālūsī and Bursevī connect decreed misery with disposition or known choice of evil; al-Qāshānī speaks of a locus of wrath and punishment. Iṣlāḥī says the person first disgraces himself through rejected truth. - 22:18/q21/p2 God may disgrace through vengeance; no benefactor can reverse it. — Al-Māwardī offers vengeance and beneficence as a distinct explanatory pair. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 22:18/q22 What does 'no one can honor him' mean?  [فَمَا لَهُۥ مِن مُّكْرِمٍ · interpretation]

- 22:18/q22/p1 No one can remove God's humiliation or grant the condemned person Paradise, felicity, aid, or intercession. — Yaḥyā contrasts Hell and Paradise; al-Farrāʾ says there is no helper; al-Ṭabrisī says reward and punishment belong to God alone. Ibn ʿĀshūr names victory and intercession. Study Quran compares divine guidance, aid and abandonment in 18:17 and 3:160. Al-Ālūsī calls a specific intercessor reading far-fetched. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء، علي بن عيسى,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:18/q22/p2 No one can bestow beneficence against God's vengeance. — Al-Māwardī gives this as another pairing for disgrace and honor. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 22:18/q23 What does mukrim mean grammatically?  [مُّكْرِمٍ · grammar]

## 22:18/q24 How is mukrim read?  [مُّكْرِمٍ · readings]

- 22:18/q24/p1 The general reading has kasra on rāʾ, mukrim, an active participle. — Al-Ṭabarī rejects another vocalization on authoritative reader agreement. ## 22:18/q25 How is 'whoever God humiliates' parsed?  [وَمَن يُهِنِ ٱللَّهُ فَمَا لَهُۥ مِن مُّكْرِمٍ · grammar]
- 22:18/q25/p1 Man is a fronted conditional object; the fāʾ introduces its answer. — Al-Samīn gives this construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 22:18/q26 What does 'God does what He wills' affirm?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: How broad divine willing is and how it relates to acts, eternal attributes, and human conduct.
- 22:18/q26/p1 God freely honors and humiliates, rewards and punishes, and His will cannot be opposed. — Ṭabarī cites God's ownership of creation and command and 21:23; al-Biqāʿī says any independent opponent would prevent God's doing whatever He wills. The ʿAlī report tests a disputant with creation, illness, healing and afterlife destination; related versions appear in Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī and al-Durr. Al-Ālūsī prefers the universal scope over limiting the clause to honor and humiliation. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء، علي بن عيسى,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي); BIQAI-FULL(علي); DURR-FULL(علي); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(جعفر بن محمد عن أبيه); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 22:18/q26/p2 Divine willing honors some with faith and humiliates others with disbelief or punishment. — Al-Wāḥidī connects honor to faith and humiliation to disbelief; al-Qurṭubī says the condemned end in Hell and no one can object; al-Ṭabrisī names favor and vengeance. [5 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 22:18/q26/p3 The verse refutes the claim that God wills things without doing them. — Al-Nasafī explicitly identifies a Muʿtazilite doctrine and argues from 'does whatever He wills'. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 22:18/q26/p4 Divine will is an attribute of action inferred from acts or prepared causes, not an eternal attribute distinct from God's essence. — Al-Riḍā's report denies eternal willing and choosing as monotheism; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says human will names knowledgeable agency when action follows, while God's willing says He did or prepared what He knew best. An eternal distinct will would introduce an uncreated co-eternal. He rejects reducing will to knowledge of the best as an empty extra attribute. [5 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)]
- 22:18/q26/p5 Will and people's deeds and beliefs together explain decreed felicity or misery. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives this explanation; Abū Ḥayyān identifies Muʿtazilite doctrine in it and objects. Bursevī speaks of eternal knowledge of the person's evil choice. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 22:18/q27 Why end with 'God does what He wills'?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يَشَآءُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the closing assertion is unrestricted or explained through its immediate contrasts.
- 22:18/q27/p1 It explains irreversible punishment and humiliation and asserts universal power. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says emphatic inna makes it a cause of the preceding parenthetical statements; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it indirectly asserts power. Al-Ālūsī prefers the universal assertion rather than context-limited honor and humiliation. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:18/q27/p2 Its context is specifically divine reward and punishment, felicity and misery, or beneficence and vengeance. — Al-Māwardī records the three explanatory pairs through Yaḥyā, al-Farrāʾ and his own addition. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء، علي بن عيسى,يحيى بن سلام)]

## 22:18/q28 What does the verse establish about divine power and independent interpretation?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the verse is used as evidence for divine power or for reasoned access to religious meaning.
- 22:18/q28/p1 God perfectly subjects creation and directs it according to His will. — Al-Jishumī makes universal subjection evidence of perfect power. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 22:18/q28/p2 The verse's intended meaning can be reached by examining proof without an Imam. — Al-Jishumī rejects the Imāmī position and says reasoning from evidence yields knowledge of religion against claims that such knowledge is necessary or unreasoned. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 22:18/q29 What inward lesson is drawn from prostration and humiliation?  [يَسْجُدُ وَمَن يُهِنِ ٱللَّهُ · inward]
turns on: How authors extend the verse's contrast into spiritual states.
- 22:18/q29/p1 Gnostics worship knowingly while rejecters' very bodies testify to God. — Al-Qushayrī cites a couplet on every thing bearing a sign of oneness; Bursevī's Taʾwīlāt contrasts chosen gnosis with compelled need. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات)]
- 22:18/q29/p2 The condemned wear spiritual deprivation; the faithful wear graded piety and intimacy with God. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts shirk, falsehood and afterlife pitch (23:108) with piety against association, sin, heedlessness and dependence on other than God, then silk or cloaks of love and solitude in the afterlife. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 22:18/q29/p3 The elect adorn Paradise, and the rare person of union has inviolable honor. — Al-Qushayrī says the elect transcend states, stations and created servitude, with an illustrative pearl couplet. Bursevī says annihilation of self is true prostration and cites the sacred report that harming God's friend declares war on Him. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الله)]
- 22:18/q29/p4 Prostration offers greater delight than worldly fortune. — Bursevī cites a devotional couplet and the Futūḥāt's call to witnessing, reflection and joining the obedient group. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 22:18/q30 What prostration of recitation is associated with this verse and Sūrat al-Ḥajj?  [يَسْجُدُ · law]
turns on: How the reports establish practice and the sūra's count.
- 22:18/q30/p1 This verse is a recognized place for prostration of recitation, and the sūra has two such places. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports juristic consensus for this passage. A report from ʿUqba b. ʿĀmir says the Prophet confirmed two prostrations and urged their practice; ʿUmar performed two at al-Jābiya, and ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ reports fifteen Qurʾanic prostrations including both in this sūra. A mursal Khālid report agrees. Ibn Kathīr treats the reports as mutual corroboration; al-Tirmidhī called Ibn Lahīʿa's chain weak, which Ibn Kathīr disputes because he explicitly claimed hearing. Abū Dāwūd says another connected route is unsound. Kāshifī calls this the sixth Qurʾanic prostration by consensus. [9 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو داود,خالد بن معدان عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عقبة بن عامر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر,عمرو بن العاص عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 22:18/q30/p2 The Prophet and Companions prostrated on reciting this verse. — Muqātil reports the event; a dream report through Ibn ʿAbbās describes a tree praying while prostrate and the Prophet repeating its words. A report from Abū Hurayra says Satan weeps when Adam's child prostrates at a prostration verse, recalling his own refusal and Fire. [3 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عباس)]

## 22:18/q31 How does the verse connect with its immediate context?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link centers on the adjacent group contrast, idols, prior sūra, or divine witnessing.
- 22:18/q31/p1 It continues the contrast between rival groups and explains their judgment through submission, reward, humiliation and punishment. — Al-Ālūsī says it explains the deeds behind the preceding division; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God witnesses, judges and separates disputants while even refusers remain inwardly subdued. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the dual demonstrative in the next verse points to worshippers of God and polytheists. Iṣlāḥī describes the larger passage's monotheism, Hereafter, cosmic signs, and respective outcomes. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 22:18/q31/p2 It renews proof of God's sole divinity after the helpless beings invoked in 22:12–13. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the intervening material a digression; Iṣlāḥī sees the creatures' submission as an indirect proof of monotheism, exposing the disgrace of worshipping inferior beings. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:18/q31/p3 It answers an implied question about God's sovereignty after the prior sūra's account of omnipotence, will and knowledge. — Al-Biqāʿī explicitly links the sūras and asks how God's rule extends across creatures and their states. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:18/q31/p4 It may show that God witnesses everything or rebuke doubters of His judgment. — Al-Ālūsī reports both functions, adding that evidence should remove the doubt. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:18/q32 Which other verses illuminate creation's prostration and the humiliation warning?  [* · links]

- 22:18/q32/p1 Verses on universal submission, shadows and praise illuminate the same theme. — Commentators cite 13:15, 16:48–49, 17:44, 21:79, 24:41, 34:10 and 55:6; Iṣlāḥī says 24:41 treats visible obedience and inward praise, while Asad directs readers to 13:15 and 16:48–49. ## 22:18/q33 What is reported about the sūra's place and circumstances of revelation?  [* · sciences]

## 22:18/q34 How does implied meaning or concision help explain this verse's unstated halves?  [وَكَثِيرٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ وَكَثِيرٌ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether brevity works through inferred opposites or a change of sense in an implied word.
- 22:18/q34/p1 Its compact contrast implies the omitted reward and refusal. — Al-Biqāʿī says prostration in the first group implies its absence in the second, and punishment in the second implies reward for the first. Al-Burhān calls concise expression eloquent when shorter than the usual amount for its meaning; it cites the Prophet's gift of comprehensive concise speech and examples 7:199 and 6:82. Al-Rummānī says valid implication is economical expression and divine speech conveys every sound implication of its wording. [11 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RUMMANI]
- 22:18/q34/p2 A supplied governor with another sense approaches istikhdām. — Al-Biqāʿī compares a repeated unexpressed word in another sense to a pronoun recalling a word in a changed sense. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 22:18/q35 What grammar supports or limits omission around kathīr?  [وَكَثِيرٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ وَكَثِيرٌ حَقَّ عَلَيْهِ ٱلْعَذَابُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether nominative resumption, accusative government, or coordination best handles the two many clauses.
- 22:18/q35/p1 A fronted nominative may be resumed by a later pronoun, supporting independent predication. — Sībawayh cites time and place nouns with a later pronoun; al-Farrāʾ says ḥaqqa ʿalayhi resumes the second kathīr. Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats it as a detached subject. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 22:18/q35/p2 An accusative may be governed as an adverb or by an understood verb; it is the more common form in Sībawayh's analogous constructions. — Sībawayh accepts several accusative analyses and prefers the recognized accusative; al-Farrāʾ permits accusative kathīran, and al-Qurṭubī supplies an omitted verb meaning God humbled many. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; QURTUBI-FULL; SIBAWAYH+]
- 22:18/q35/p3 Omitting a resumptive pronoun is possible in poetry but weak in prose. — Sībawayh cites Abū al-Najm's object omission, Imruʾ al-Qays's 'a garment I wore', al-Namir's day/month expressions, Jarīr's adjectival clause and al-Ḥārith's omitted object. He says an adjective completes its noun and cannot become an independent clause whose missing pronoun is replaced with accusative. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو النجم العجلي,الحارث بن كلدة,النمر بن تولب,امرؤ القيس,جرير)]
- 22:18/q35/p4 Coordination brings a second noun under the same governor, while a circumstantial wāw opens a separate proposition. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī explains the shared governor for coordination, and distinguishes the wāw of a resumed circumstance from an integrated predicate by comparing 'Zayd came while he was hurrying' with 'Zayd came hurrying'. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 22:18/q36 How are particle clauses and their implied relations analyzed?  [أَلَمْ تَرَ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يَشَآءُ · grammar]
- 22:18/q36/p1 Particles operate on whole predications, and causal or emphatic clauses may stand independently. — Sībawayh says inna after illā can begin independently if ungoverned, with lām marking initial position as in 25:20; in an anger example illā anna has the sense 'only because'. His oath examples include mā inna. Al-Jurjānī says negation, interrogation and condition govern an attributed action, compares lā rajula, kaʾanna, law and lawlā, and says particle plus noun or verb is incomplete except vocative with implied verb. Ibn ʿĀshūr says emphatic inna here conveys causal explanation. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH; JURJANI-DALAIL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:18/q37 What morphology or Arabic poetry is cited in discussing this verse?  [وَكَثِيرٌۭ يُهِنِ · meaning]
turns on: The difference between a morphological analysis and poetic evidence for lexical usage.
- 22:18/q37/p1 Kathīr is an adjective on the faʿīl pattern with an added yāʾ. — Sībawayh gives the pattern for nouns and adjectives; kathīr is his verse example. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 22:18/q37/p2 Poetry is a witness to difficult Arabic meanings, not a source from which the Qurʾān is derived. — Ibn al-Anbārī answers critics using the Qurʾān's Arabic character; Ibn ʿAbbās calls poetry the Arabs' register, and Abū ʿUbayd says his poetic citations served as evidence. ## 22:18/q38 What is known about the transmitted Ibn ʿAbbās poetry questions cited for rare words?  [يُهِنِ · reports]
- 22:18/q38/p1 The recensions differ, and their manuscript relationships can be traced. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the Cairo K and Ṭ copies likely descend from a fourth-century manuscript; Ṭ may derive from K, with two omitted disruptive passages and no recorded copyist/date. The opening places Ibn ʿAbbās at the Kaʿba challenged by Nāfiʿ and Najda; only al-Mubarrad has the ʿUmar b. Abī Rabīʿa episode. The Ẓāhiriyya manuscript and al-Ṭabarānī share 31 questions through ʿUthmān al-Ḥarrānī; K and Ṭ align with abridged al-Itqān through Ibn al-Ṭastī. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ names the manuscript, al-Haythamī and source works as editorial comparanda. [6 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(نافع بن الأزرق ونجدة بن عويمر)]

## 22:18/q39 What illustrative simile analysis accompanies discussion of expression?  [* · rhetoric]
- 22:18/q39/p1 Unusual composite likenesses require attention to coordinated motion and appearance. — Al-Jurjānī explains yiqtāʿuhā as a calf climbing onto its kneeling mother and likens its irregular limbs to a person climbing a ladder. He says novel composite configurations are rarer than wheel, mill or arrow motions, and analyzes a shaking mirror's light moving inward from the rim. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
