# 31:16: 29 questions

## 31:16/q01 How is yā bunayya read?  [يَٰبُنَىَّ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vocalization of the final yāʾ differs by reader and occurrence.
- 31:16/q01/p1 The final yāʾ is doubled and read with kasra. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya lists Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī and Abū Bakr; Ibn Kathīr's middle Luqmān occurrence is undisputed. Qirāʾāt ER associates kasra with possessive force. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل,نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); NASHR(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER]
- 31:16/q01/p2 The final yāʾ is doubled and read with fatḥa. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Ḥafṣ and al-Mufaḍḍal from ʿĀṣim; al-Nashr says Ḥafṣ opens all six occurrences, with other readers agreeing at specified verses. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل,نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); NASHR]
- 31:16/q01/p3 Some transmissions give the yāʾ sukūn here. — Ibn Abī Barrah reports Ibn Kathīr with silence in the first occurrence, kasra in the second, fatḥa in the third; Qunbul has silence in first and third. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل)]

## 31:16/q02 Why repeat the address to Luqmān's son?  [يَٰبُنَىَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the address is taken only as tenderness and attention or also as a warning.
- 31:16/q02/p1 It affectionately renews the son's attention before counsel. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī classify it as affectionate address, comparing Abraham's yā abat and Aaron's yā ibn umm. Ibn ʿĀshūr says repetition renews attention. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:16/q02/p2 It also stresses the child's smallness before divine wrath. — Al-Biqāʿī connects tenderness with the son's vulnerability. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 31:16/q03 To what does innahā refer?  [إِنَّهَآ · referent]
turns on: Whether the implied subject is a deed, the son's specific question, an anticipatory matter pronoun, or an inward allotment.
- 31:16/q03/p1 A good or evil deed, or an act of obedience or disobedience. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers an open reference because both deeds receive recompense, against restricting it to sin. Al-Fārisī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others infer the deed from context; a feminine act can be implicit. Abū Isḥāq also explicitly says the phrase may concern a person's smallest deed. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني والباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الباقون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TAB-FULL+]
- 31:16/q03/p2 A sin, injustice, or evil deed in particular. — Basran grammarians, Muqātil's narrative, al-Baghawī, and Ibn Kathīr connect the pronoun to a hidden sin. Ibn Kathīr calls this most likely. Al-Ṭabarī argues that it excludes good deeds. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); THALABI(بعض النحاة); BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور القراء); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TAB-FULL-]
- 31:16/q03/p3 The matter, story, or situation subsequently explained by the conditional clause. — Abū Isḥāq gives the story construction; Basrans accept it, Kufans limit it to feminine expressions. Al-Jishumī, al-Samīn, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī call it a pronoun of the matter. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it focuses attention. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني والباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(نافع، والأعرج، وأبو جعفر); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TAB-FULL+]
- 31:16/q03/p4 The thing or question the son had asked about, including a seed. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq allow the queried matter; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite the sea-seed question. Ibn ʿAṭiyya sees the physical hidden thing as the apparent sense. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:16/q03/p5 Primordial allotments, sincerity, and divine gifts. — Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya gives this inward referent. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 31:16/q03/p6 The son himself, if hiding from judgment. — Al-Ālūsī reports this possibility but says it does not fit the answer. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 31:16/q04 Why use inna and a feminine matter pronoun?  [إِنَّهَآ · rhetoric]
- 31:16/q04/p1 They focus and strongly affirm God's complete knowledge and power. — Ibn ʿĀshūr counts the call, inna and the matter pronoun as three emphatic markers; the pronoun alerts the hearer to the following proposition. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Thaʿlabī cite a poetic parallel for anticipatory feminine agreement. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:16/q05 How is the shortened in tak formed, and why is it short?  [إِن تَكُ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical elision and rhetorical reasons for this particular short form.
- 31:16/q05/p1 The nūn of takun is omitted after the conditional jussive. — Bursevī describes loss of original wāw before two quiescent letters and nūn omission by analogy with weak sounds or frequent use; the nūn returns before a following quiescent letter and is not deleted in lam yaṣun or lam yakhan. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 31:16/q05/p2 Its brevity evokes the tiny or initially unspecified thing. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Biqāʿī read omission as mirroring smallness; al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts initially unspecified location with the named locations after fa-takun. [3 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:16/q06 What does mithqāl ḥabbatin min khardal mean?  [مِثْقَالَ حَبَّةٍۢ مِّنْ خَرْدَلٍۢ · meaning]
- 31:16/q06/p1 The weight or equivalent measure of one mustard seed, an extremely small amount. — Yaḥyā, Abū ʿUbayda, and al-Ṭabarī gloss it as seed weight; al-Jishumī derives mithqāl from th-q-l and cites poetry and 4:40. Bursevī says any scale weight and the least weighing unit. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies an instrument noun and connects 21:47. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:16/q06/p2 The word ḥabbah denotes a plant seed or grain. — Bursevī calls mustard familiar and among the smallest food grains. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a seed capable of propagating its kind, citing 2:261 and 6:95, and describes mustard's small pungent medicinal seeds. [3 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:16/q07 How is mithqāl read?  [مِثْقَالَ · readings]
turns on: The variant case ending, with corresponding different uses of kāna.
- 31:16/q07/p1 Accusative mithqāla is read by the other or majority readers. — Al-Fārisī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Naḥḥās's discussion, and Ibn ʿĀshūr report it. It is usually the predicate of incomplete kāna, with an understood deed or matter as subject. [13 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); JISHUMI(نافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أهل المدينة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع); QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور القراء); NASAFI-FULL(مدني والباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; NASHR(المدنيان); IBNASHUR-FULL(الباقون)]
- 31:16/q07/p2 Nominative mithqālu is read by Nāfiʿ and other Medinan readers. — Sources name Nāfiʿ, sometimes Abū Jaʿfar and al-Aʿraj; al-Nashr says two Medinans read this in both parallel verses. Complete kāna is the leading parse; agreement follows ḥabbah or feminine sense. [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); JISHUMI(نافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع); TABRISI(أهل المدينة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني والباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(نافع); IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASHR(المدنيان); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المولى الجامي); ALUSI-FULL(نافع، والأعرج، وأبو جعفر); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وأبو جعفر)]

## 31:16/q08 How do tak and mithqāl work grammatically?  [تَكُ مِثْقَالَ حَبَّةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The nominative or accusative reading, and whether kāna is complete, incomplete, inactive, or has an omitted predicate.
- 31:16/q08/p1 With accusative mithqāl, kāna is incomplete: an implicit deed or matter is its subject and mithqāl its predicate. — Al-Ṭabarī gives an implicit unknown subject; Abū ʿAlī makes it a wrong or deed, with reward or punishment unless forgiven. Al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr agree on copular construction. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(صاحب المقالة الأولى); ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور القراء); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(الباقون)]
- 31:16/q08/p2 With nominative mithqāl, kāna is complete and mithqāl is its subject; its feminine agreement follows ḥabbah or the feminine thing meant. — Al-Zajjāj takes it as equivalent to 'if it is a seed'; al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī take the measured good or bad deed. Al-Naḥḥās rebuts Abū Ḥātim's objection with اجتمعت أهل اليمامة; poetry and 6:160 support semantic agreement. Others cite annexation to ḥabbah. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة,صاحب هذه المقالة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); FARISI-HUJJA; JISHUMI(نافع); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(نافع); BURHAN; BURSEVI(المولى الجامي); ALUSI-FULL(نافع، والأعرج، وأبو جعفر); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وأبو جعفر); NAHHAS(أبو حاتم)+]
- 31:16/q08/p3 A nominative may also be analyzed with an omitted predicate or with kāna inactive. — Al-Ṭabarī reports a Kufan indefinite lacking an overt predicate and a Basran nominative that needs none; he himself supplies an implicit locative predicate. Al-Jishumī also reports an inactive kāna analysis. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(صاحب المقالة الأولى,صاحب هذه المقالة); THALABI; JISHUMI(نافع)]
- 31:16/q08/p4 Tak can simply mean 'occur' and therefore needs no predicate. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī take it as taqaʿ. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 31:16/q09 What does the seed-sized thing represent?  [حَبَّةٍۢ مِّنْ خَرْدَلٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the seed is a physical example, a moral deed, or allotted provision.
- 31:16/q09/p1 A tiny good or evil deed, to be brought for reckoning. — Qatāda and many commentators include both good and evil, making the warning fearful and hopeful. Al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabarī and later sources connect 99:7–8 or 21:47; al-Rāzī says a rock can secure it yet God knows it. [38 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الزركشي); STUDYQURAN]
- 31:16/q09/p2 A physical small thing or seed displaying God's power to retrieve it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this the apparent sense, citing the son's ocean question; on it the verse itself does not promise reward or threaten punishment. The kasra reading of fa-takun supports a covered physical thing. Luqmān's sea-seed demonstration is also reported. [14 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الكريم الجزري); TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الكريم الجزري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:16/q09/p3 A tiny measure of provision that will reach its recipient. — Al-Qurṭubī connects it to trust in allotted provision and a Prophetic exhortation to Ibn Masʿūd; Jāmī reads bringing it as nourishment. The inward reading extends this to ordained shares. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,المولى الجامي)]

## 31:16/q10 How is fa-takun read?  [فَتَكُن · readings]
turns on: The reported vocalization and derivation of the verb.
- 31:16/q10/p1 The common verb means 'then it is' in the specified place. — Al-Biqāʿī says retaining nūn stresses the thing's persistence; al-Rāzī says fa joins minuteness with concealment. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 31:16/q10/p2 A kasra on kāf derives the verb from a bird settling in its roost or shelter. — Ibn Jinnī cites nesting poetry; reports vary between light and doubled nūn and between settling in a nest and being covered. Qatāda and al-Jazarī are named transmitters; the reading supports a concrete thing. [13 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عبد الكريم الجزري); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الكريم الجزري); TABRISI(عبد الكريم الجزري); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الكريم الجزري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الكريم الجزري,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الكريم الجزري,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(عبدالرحيم الجزري,قتادة)]
- 31:16/q10/p3 A passive variant has ḍamma on tāʾ and a doubled open nūn. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī attribute it to Muḥammad ibn Abī Fajjah al-Baʿlabakkī. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن أبي فجة البعلبكي); SAMIN-DURR(محمد بن أبي فجة البعلبكي); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن أبي فجة البعلبكي)]

## 31:16/q11 What does the fa in fa-takun add?  [فَتَكُن · rhetoric]
- 31:16/q11/p1 It joins the tiny size and secure concealment in one increasingly difficult case. — Al-Rāzī says the sequence combines both conditions, though neither prevents God's knowledge. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 31:16/q12 What is ṣakhrah here?  [صَخْرَةٍ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular rock names a cosmological object or serves as an ordinary image of an inaccessible place; reports also differ in its placement.
- 31:16/q12/p1 An ordinary rock or mountain used as an extreme example of concealment. — Qatāda calls it a mountain; al-Ṭabrisī describes a large boulder. Iṣlāḥī says Arabic ṣakhrah can mean a mountain or passes. Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī prefer the straightforward hyperbole and doubt cosmological reports. Al-Zajjāj says m-q-l means to dive: a seed in rock is more hidden than one in water. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(السدي,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(المولى الجامي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(al-Ṭabarī); ZAJJAJ; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 31:16/q12/p2 The green rock below the earth or seven earths, bearing or supporting the earth. — Al-Kalbī says earth rests on it; Ibn ʿAbbās says sinners' deeds are written there and sky's green comes from it; al-Thawrī also cites green sky. - 31:16/q12/p3 The cosmological rock beneath the earth, beyond heaven and earth, in a chain of fish, water, slab, angel, rock, and wind. — Al-Suddī's report through Companions cites 68:1 and says the rock is neither heaven nor earth. Versions differ: fish back, bull horn, wind, or angel standing on it; these are retained as distinct reports below. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، وعبد الله، وناس من أصحاب النبي); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس والسدي); TABRISI(السدي)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 31:16/q12/p4 Sijjīn, the place beneath earth where unbelievers' evil deeds are recorded. — Al-Māwardī, al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī mention or adopt this identification; al-Nasafī says it is outside earth. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 31:16/q12/p5 A rock on which a bull rests, neither in heaven nor earth. — Al-Rāzī attributes this to some exegetes. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]

## 31:16/q14 Why mention a rock as well as heavens and earth?  [صَخْرَةٍ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ ٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the rock lies inside the earth and is repeated for emphasis or denotes a special realm outside it.
- 31:16/q14/p1 The rock intensifies the image although it lies within the earth. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī compare repeated creation in 96:1–2; al-Qurṭubī adds 17:1. Al-Fārisī says both inaccessible rock and earth reinforce inescapability. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 31:16/q14/p2 The rock is a distinct place outside heaven and earth. — Al-Suddī's cosmology supplies the reason; al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī report it as a possible explanation. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، وعبد الله، وناس من أصحاب النبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي)]
- 31:16/q14/p3 The specific rock is named before comprehensive heaven and earth. — Al-Rāzī says particular before general is natural, illustrating with houses of named people followed by elsewhere; al-Zamakhsharī allows an ellipsis, 'or another place'. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 31:16/q14/p4 The locations represent four impediments: minute size, barrier, distance, darkness. — Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī align seed, rock, heaven and earth with those impediments. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues greater and nearer things are thus covered a fortiori. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:16/q14/p5 The examples move from hard rock, to remote heaven, then familiar earth. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī explain the sequence this way. Ibn ʿĀshūr says earth completes all terrestrial locations; al-Biqāʿī says repeated aw marks each realm. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 31:16/q15 What does fī ṣakhratin imply?  [فِى صَخْرَةٍ · grammar]
turns on: How the preposition and indefinite noun mark the hiding place.
- 31:16/q15/p1 Any rock's interior is meant. — Bursevī notes the indefinite form means any rock; Jāmī says hard stone is especially resistant to retrieval. [3 notes: BURSEVI(المولى الجامي)]
- 31:16/q15/p2 Fī can be spatial, idiomatic, or mean ʿalā for firm placement. — Al-Ālūsī lists the options and considers the spatial sense strongest. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:16/q16 What are the heavens and earth in this image?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Literal remote places versus inward correspondence.
- 31:16/q16/p1 The upper and lower worlds, including their most remote surfaces. — Bursevī reports the heavens' convex surface and earth's concave surface; al-Ālūsī notes these extremes are possible but not required by the wording. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 10:61 for exhaustive scope. [4 notes: BURSEVI(بعض التفاسير); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:16/q16/p2 The heavens and earth are inward worlds of spirit and heart. — Bursevī applies the rock to selves, heavens to spirits and earth to hearts; al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya makes rock nonexistence, heavens form and meaning, and earth outward structure. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 31:16/q17 What does yati bihā Allāh mean?  [يَأْتِ بِهَا ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether bringing is physical retrieval, public manifestation, recompense, or a gloss for divine knowledge.
- 31:16/q17/p1 God brings the deed forth at resurrection for reckoning and just recompense. — Al-Ṭabarī includes good and evil; al-Zajjāj and many others compare 99:7–8; Ibn Kathīr invokes just scales at 21:47. Al-Ālūsī allows literal production or making it present by mention and admission. [27 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:16/q17/p2 God manifests or reveals the hidden thing, proving nothing escapes Him. — Ibn Qutayba gives 'make manifest'; al-Rāzī says showing it to witnesses is stronger than knowledge alone. A Prophetic report says a deed performed within a sealed rock would emerge to people. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:16/q17/p3 God knows the hidden thing and its location. — Some interpreters, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī and Abū Mālik gloss bringing it as knowing it. Al-Ṭabarī knows no idiom of 'bring' meaning 'know' but accepts the point that God knows its place; al-Wāḥidī calls it a meaning rather than direct exegesis. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو مالك); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك); TAB-FULL-]
- 31:16/q17/p4 God actually retrieves or supplies a tiny physical thing or provision. — Muqātil says He subtly extracts the hidden seed; al-Qurṭubī uses it for destined provision and Jāmī for nourishment. The sea-seed demonstration gives a physical analogue. [4 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(المولى الجامي)]
- 31:16/q17/p5 God requites an equivalent good or evil rather than physically producing the deed. — Al-Māwardī gives equivalent requital as one formulation; the other includes knowing and later recompensing. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 31:16/q25 What grammatical examples illuminate ṣakhrah as a unit noun?  [صَخْرَةٍ · grammar]
- 31:16/q25/p1 Ṣakhrah is a feminine unit of the collective ṣakhr; some pluralize it ṣukhūr. — Sībawayh describes -ah unit formation, -āt for few and collective for many; ṣukhūr follows analogy with badrah/budūr and maʾnah/maʾūn. He surveys further patterns: sakhlah/sikhāl, ʿinabah/ʿinab, sidrah/sidr, ḥabbah/ḥabb, weak and doubled forms, and rare ḥalqah/ḥalaq. Abū al-Khaṭṭāb and Yūnus supply usage; al-Musayyib's poetry attests ḥuqqah's plural. [16 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو الخطاب,المسيب بن علس,يونس عن أبي عمرو)]
