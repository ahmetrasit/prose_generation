# 2:129: 33 questions

## 2:129/q01 What does 'raise' mean here?  [وَٱبْعَثْ · meaning]
turns on: The root's range includes dispatch, stirring, and revival.
- 2:129/q01/p1 Send or commission a messenger. — Jishumi and Abu Hayyan gloss baʿth as dispatch; Ibn al-Jawzi cites this verse and 62:2 for the usage. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:129/q01/p2 The root can also mean rousing or reviving. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives stirring a resting she-camel and reviving; Jishumi mentions raising the dead. Ibn al-Jawzi notes Buʿath as the Aws–Khazraj battle day. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 2:129/q02 Who are 'among them'?  [فِيهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun reaches back to Abraham's descendants or the submitted nation, to Mecca's people in 2:126, or to a narrower lineage.
- 2:129/q02/p1 Abraham and Ishmael's descendants, the submitted community of their line. — Muqatil, Thalabi, Baghawi, Tabarsi, Biqai and Bursevi specify the joint descendants; Wahidi says the two antecedents converge on Abraham's Arab descendants. Razi connects 2:128; Tabataba'i limits the requested nation to their descendants, not all believers or addressees. Asad observes that fīhim literally means 'within them'. [22 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن وقتادة وجماعة من أهل العلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل والفراء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 2:129/q02/p2 The people of Mecca, linked to the preceding prayer for its inhabitants. — Thalabi and Baghawi report the view; Ibn al-Jawzi connects it with 2:126; Abu Hayyan cites 62:2 and the unlettered. Abu Ali in Jishumi says only Muhammad among Mecca's descendants fits, unlike Israelite prophets. Alusi calls the Mecca antecedent remote. [6 notes: THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل والفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:129/q02/p3 Muhammad's community, especially Banu Hashim in al-Sadiq's report. — Abu al-Aliya and others say Muhammad's community; al-Sadiq's report identifies the community with Banu Hashim and grounds the Imams in Abraham's lineage, citing 2:127–128 and 14:35–36. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); JISHUMI(الحسن وقتادة وجماعة من أهل العلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 2:129/q03 Why ask that the messenger be 'from them'?  [رَسُولًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The practical benefit of an insider and the lineage restriction both explain the wording.
- 2:129/q03/p1 Shared lineage makes him recognizable, trusted, compassionate, honored, and easier to follow. — Qatada says face and lineage were known; Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai and Alusi cite upbringing, trustworthiness, concern and acceptance. Razi adds completion and continuity of their religion and Abraham's joy in descendants' honor. Bursevi distinguishes being sent among a people from necessarily belonging to them; minhum supplies the latter. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:129/q03/p2 The prayer seeks a prophet from the descendants near Mecca, thereby singling out Muhammad. — Jishumi's Abu Ali contrasts Israelite prophets; Razi says none was sent to Mecca and its environs except Muhammad; Tabarsi and Biqai call him the sole prophet of the joint Abraham–Ishmael line. Ibn Kathir says his mission extends to Arabs, non-Arabs, humans and jinn. [6 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:129/q04 Who is the requested messenger?  [رَسُولًۭا · referent]
- 2:129/q04/p1 Muhammad. — Al-Suddi, al-Rabiʿ and many later commentators identify him; Razi and Kuranyolu cite commentator agreement. Tabarsi, Baydawi, Biqai, Bursevi and Alusi argue he alone came from the joint Abraham–Ishmael line; Ibn Kathir says the mission reaches all peoples and jinn. Study Quran points to matching language in 2:151. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الربيع,السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وقتادة وجماعة من العلماء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(إجماع المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 2:129/q05 When was Abraham's prayer fulfilled?  [وَٱبْعَثْ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًۭا · reports]
- 2:129/q05/p1 God answered it by sending Muhammad at the end of time. — Al-Rabiʿ and Abu al-Aliya say Abraham was told of the delayed fulfillment; Abu Hayyan says it was answered in full with Qur'anic revelation; Ibn Ashur sees fulfillment among Arabs embracing Islam before and after the Hijra despite exceptions. Muqatil cites 62:2; Study Quran cites 2:151. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الربيع,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,قتادة); BURSEVI(راوٍ غير مسمى); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 2:129/q06 What did Muhammad say about this prayer and the first signs of his mission?  [وَٱبْعَثْ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًۭا · reports]
- 2:129/q06/p1 He identified himself as Abraham's prayer and Jesus's glad tidings, often adding his mother's vision. — Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Thalabi, Baghawi, Wahidi, Razi and later works transmit forms of the statement; longer versions say his status as final prophet was written while Adam was still clay and the mother saw light reaching Syrian palaces. Wahidi, Razi, Ibn Kathir and Kuranyolu relate Jesus's announcement of Ahmad in 61:6; Abu Hayyan calls his version sound, whereas Nasafi and Alusi state no grading. Al-Dahhak locates Abraham's utterance while raising the House. Alusi says 'prayer' can mean its outcome, recipient, or emphatically the prayer itself, and Ishmael shared it despite Abraham alone being named. ## 2:129/q07 What does the mother's vision of Syrian light signify?  [رَسُولًۭا · reports]
- 2:129/q07/p1 It foretells the mission's reach and faith's later refuge in Syria. — Ibn Kathir says the vision was a pregnancy dream that spread among her people; Syria would be a refuge near the end, Jesus would descend by Damascus's eastern white minaret, and a report says a group would remain on truth there. Other accounts simply report light illuminating Syria's palaces; Ibn Kathir adds that prophets' mothers have such visions. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(العرباض بن سارية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,العرباض بن سارية); BURSEVI(النبي); QASHANI(النبي محمد)]

## 2:129/q08 What lineage and wider scriptural announcements are connected with this messenger?  [رَسُولًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · reports]
turns on: The accounts differ on the specified people and on how the later lineage and announcements are described.
- 2:129/q08/p1 Muhammad is traced through Ishmael's Arabized descendants, and earlier scripture is said to announce him. — Baghawi and Abu Hayyan transmit Ibn Abbas's ten non-Israelite prophets, though Abu Hayyan notes his excerpt lists nine. Kuranyolu recounts Ishmael's settlement among Jurhum, twelve children, the Mustaʿriba, and Adnan as Muhammad's twenty-first ancestor; it reports Muslim readings of John's Paraclete and earlier scriptural announcements, citing 7:157 and 61:6. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:129/q08/p2 Bursevi calls this an invocation for a messenger among the Israelites. — This is Bursevi's specific formulation, despite his nearby identification of Muhammad as the answer. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 2:129/q09 What does rasūl mean and how is it derived?  [رَسُولًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the immediate etymon is the message or the camel's forward movement.
- 2:129/q09/p1 A sent messenger, on faʿūl from risāla. — Thalabi and Qurtubi derive it from message or dispatch; dispatched parties are rusul, arrivals in succession irsālan, and milk rasl because it flows from the udder. [4 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:129/q09/p2 It may derive from a camel moving easily ahead of others. — Ibn al-Anbari supplies marsāl or rasla; Thalabi and Qurtubi add dispatched groups and successive arrivals as related usage. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري)]

## 2:129/q10 What does Ubayy's reading add?  [فِيهِمْ رَسُولًۭا · readings]
- 2:129/q10/p1 It adds 'among their later generations'. — Mawardi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit Ubayy's wording; Alusi takes it as supporting Muhammad's identification. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبي بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبي)]

## 2:129/q11 How are ibʿath, fīhim and minhum parsed?  [وَٱبْعَثْ فِيهِمْ رَسُولًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Which expression the prepositional phrase attaches to.
- 2:129/q11/p1 Ibʿath is coordinated with tab; fīhim modifies ibʿath or an implied 'messenger among them'. — Tabarsi gives both attachments and treats the latter as circumstantial. Asad notes the literal 'within them'. [2 notes: TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 2:129/q11/p2 Minhum is an accusative adjective of rasūlan with an implicit attachment. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin infer 'a messenger belonging to them'; it stresses the messenger's origin among his audience. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:129/q12 How is yatlū parsed?  [يَتْلُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The clause may describe the messenger, state his circumstance, or hypothetically answer the imperative.
- 2:129/q12/p1 It is an accusative adjective of rasūlan. — Nahhas and Tabarsi give this parse; al-Samin prefers it as the best order. Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi also allow it. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:129/q12/p2 It is circumstantial of rasūlan or its already specified referent. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi allow the circumstantial parse. Al-Samin says the prior minhum adjective makes rasūlan specific enough. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:129/q12/p3 It is circumstantial of the pronoun in minhum. — Al-Samin says the implied existential verb governs the circumstantial clause. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:129/q12/p4 Outside recitation it could be jussive as the prayer's response. — Nahhas marks this as a non-Qur'anic grammatical possibility. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 2:129/q13 How are the verbs after yatlū connected?  [وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · grammar]
- 2:129/q13/p1 They are coordinated with yatlū. — Nahhas parses teaching the Book and wisdom and purifying them as coordinated verbs. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 2:129/q14 How is 'wa-yuʿallimuhum' pronounced or read?  [وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ · readings]
turns on: The phonetic status of the weakened vowel and the Hijazi–Tamimi usage.
- 2:129/q14/p1 The mīm's vowel can be faintly shortened, rather than wholly absent. — Abu ʿAmr lightly rounds or lowers the vowel before hāʾ; al-Farisi says omitted vowel remains grammatically intended and only ḍamma/kasra permit ikhtilās. Sībawayh treats the apparent sukūn as faint pronunciation. Ibn Jinni attributes light forms to Tamim and explains vowel loss by clustered movements; Abū Zayd's yaktubūn and Abū ʿAmr's bāriʾikum are parallels. Ibn Jinni also defends an actually still mīm from Sībawayh's hearing and poetic meter against Abu al-ʿAbbas. [8 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,سيبويه); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عباس ناقلا عن أبي عمرو); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB+]
- 2:129/q14/p2 Hijazi usage geminates the relevant forms. — Abu ʿAmr as transmitted by Ibn Jinni contrasts Hijazi gemination of yuʿallimuhum and yalʿanuhum with light Tamimi forms; Ibn Jinni says gemination needs no special defense. [2 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(عباس ناقلا عن أبي عمرو)]

## 2:129/q15 How is the implied speech construction explained?  [وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether qawl is syntactically ellipted or supplied by the sense of an overt verb.
- 2:129/q15/p1 An implicit qawl is omitted. — Ibn Jinni sides with the Basrans; Ibn Masʿud's cited reading makes the speech verb overt. His parallels include a poem, calling Antarah, and the angels' greeting; the poem's meter and Sībawayh's transmission answer Abu al-ʿAbbas's objection. [4 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن مسعود); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB+]
- 2:129/q15/p2 The existing wording itself carries the sense of speech. — Ibn Jinni reports the Kufan account: akhbarānā can itself mean qālā lanā, without an omitted verb. [2 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB]

## 2:129/q16 What does reciting the signs mean?  [يَتْلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether āyāt here designates recited revelation, its evidentiary content, religious exposition, or narrated events.
- 2:129/q16/p1 Reciting and conveying God's revealed Qur'anic words. — Muqatil, Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabarsi and Abu Hayyan identify the signs with scripture. Biqai stresses continuous sequential reading; Abu Hayyan stresses articulation, delivery and pauses, citing the Prophet's recitation to Ubayy. Tabarsi says ʿalayhim attaches to yatlū. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:129/q16/p2 Presenting God's proofs of oneness, attributes and the truth of prophecy. — Mawardi says divine proof; Jishumi reports proofs but prefers Qur'an because it is what one recites. Razi says the Prophet reminds hearers of the Creator's attributes; Baydawi, Nasafi, Zamakhshari via Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Bursevi and Kuranyolu include monotheism and prophetic truth. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); JISHUMI+]
- 2:129/q16/p3 Explaining their religion to them. — Mawardi reports it as an alternative; al-Fadl gives this gloss through Abu Hayyan. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفضل)]
- 2:129/q16/p4 Relating past and future events up to resurrection. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi report this alternative for the signs. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 2:129/q17 What does āya mean in Arabic?  [ءَايَٰتِكَ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical use as sign versus a grouped textual unit.
- 2:129/q17/p1 A sign, or a bounded unit of connected speech. — Thalabi says āya signifies a sign; Baghawi says a stretch of speech up to its break. [2 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 2:129/q17/p2 A collection or group of letters. — Thalabi and Baghawi give this sense; al-Shaybani cites kharaja al-qawm bimā fīhim, a gathered company. [3 notes: THALABI(الشيباني,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 2:129/q18 Why mention recitation before teaching, and how do the tasks differ?  [يَتْلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتِكَ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clauses describe stages, functions, or several aspects of one scripture.
- 2:129/q18/p1 Recitation gives the wording and proof; teaching supplies meaning and practice. — Jishumi and Tabarsi say recitation establishes the Qur'an's inimitability and prophecy, while teaching explains law, unity, justice and evidence. Razi says recitation preserves wording by mass transmission, guards against alteration and is worship in prayer; teaching its proofs, rulings and secrets is the higher goal. Abu Hayyan says hearing precedes reflection, and details teaching of lawful/forbidden, duties, parables, promises, threats and the afterlife. Qurtubi and Ibn Atiyya say the Prophet supplies material and methods for inquiry. [16 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:129/q18/p2 The Book's descriptions as recited signs, written scripture and proof can overlap. — Thalabi cites al-Hutayʾa for coordinating distinct descriptions; Jishumi reports 'written' and 'proof' as descriptions of one Book. Qurtubi distinguishes its wording, meanings and intended rulings as signs, Book and wisdom. [3 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:129/q18/p3 Teaching may cause understanding or may only provide its means. — Abu Hayyan explains why a learner does not necessarily learn; the Messenger communicates and facilitates, while God grants understanding. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:129/q19 What is 'the Book' here?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether kitāb denotes the revealed Qur'an, writing as a skill, or a part of scripture.
- 2:129/q19/p1 The Qur'an. — Ibn Zayd, Hasan and many others identify it; Ibn Abbas in Wahidi includes its duties, rulings, Sunnah and earlier prophetic laws. Biqai calls it comprehensive of scriptures and recalls comprehensive speech; Alusi says the requested scripture need not have been explicitly named Qur'an before its answer. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة ومقاتل بن حيان وأبو مالك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN]
- 2:129/q19/p2 Writing with the pen. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports this alternative from Ibn Abbas. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 2:129/q19/p3 The unambiguous verses, while wisdom designates ambiguous verses. — Razi reports this division within scripture. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:129/q20 What is the wisdom taught by the messenger?  [ٱلْحِكْمَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paired term is prophetic instruction outside the Book, Qur'anic content, learned and lived discernment, judgment, or a distinct spiritual quality.
- 2:129/q20/p1 The Prophet's Sunnah and explanation of law. — Qatada, al-Shafi'i and many commentators identify it; Shafi'i's followers argue separation from the Book marks extra-scriptural teaching and reply that rational proofs are independently accessible. Abu Hayyan says Sunnah clarifies generalities, ambiguities and omitted details and supplies further rulings; a report says the Prophet was given Qur'an and twice as much wisdom. Ibn Kathir treats religious understanding as compatible. Alusi challenges the assertion that soul-perfecting truths must lie outside the Qur'an, citing 6:38 and 16:89. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن، أبو مالك، مقاتل بن حيان، قتادة، يحيى بن أبي كثير); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أصحاب الشافعي,الشافعي وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة ومقاتل بن حيان وأبو مالك); DURR-FULL(الحسن,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(أصحاب الشافعي)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:129/q20/p2 Religious knowledge, understanding and observance, especially rulings known through the Messenger. — Malik and Ibn Zayd describe knowledge, fiqh and following it; Ibn Zayd calls it divinely placed heart-light and cites 2:269, 3:48 and 7:175 to show signs without wisdom do not benefit. Tabari prefers prophetic clarification of God's rulings. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan add innate insight; Biqai describes all prescribed commands as wisdom protecting worldly and afterlife paths. Razi says this includes benefit and interpretation. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مالك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن زيد بن أسلم عن أبيه); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); JISHUMI(ابن زيد,مالك بن أنس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك); TABRISI(ابن زيد,مالك بن أنس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك وابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل,مالك وأبو رزين); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); BIQAI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:129/q20/p3 The Qur'an itself, its understanding, admonitions and lawful and forbidden matters. — Muqatil stresses exhortation and halal/haram; Mujahid stresses understanding; Wahidi and others treat wisdom as another name or quality of the Book, sometimes repeated for emphasis. Razi reports its embedded rationale and benefit; Alusi distinguishes its wording from inner truths. Thalabi's al-Hutayʾa line supports coordination of synonymous content. [21 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مجاهد,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد,مقاتل); TABRISI(قيل,مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(من الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعضهم,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:129/q20/p4 Sound knowledge joined to right action, speech and conduct. — Ibn Qutayba and Abd Allah ibn Muslim require both; Abu Jaʿfar says sound speech yields sound action or state; Razi adds putting each thing in its place; Baydawi and Elmalili connect it with perfection of the soul and 2:269. [12 notes: THALABI(أبو جعفر محمد بن يعقوب,ابن قتيبة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن مسلم); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن يعقوب,مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 2:129/q20/p5 An admonishing saying that restrains evil and urges virtue. — Al-Buraydi, Ibn Durayd and others include rebuke and guidance; the Prophetic saying 'some poetry is wisdom' supports it. Abu Hayyan reports Ibn Zayd similarly. [7 notes: THALABI(أبو بكر محمد بن الحسن البريدي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن دريد,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر بن دريد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI(ابن دريد)]
- 2:129/q20/p6 Judgment, adjudication, or what one is obliged to do. — Tabari derives it from ḥukm separating truth and falsehood; Thalabi cites a poem calling silence proper ḥukm, Qurtubi says the interpretations are close; Razi reports divine judgments taught to the messenger. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(الشاعر,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:129/q20/p7 Prophethood. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports this from al-Suddi. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي)]
- 2:129/q20/p8 A God-given inner light or force that cools attachment to worldly life. — Ibn Zayd calls it light in the heart; Yahya ibn Muadh likens it to a troop cooling worldly heat; Tabarsi and Abu Hayyan report light or force, and Alusi a cure for worldly ardor. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(يحيى بن معاذ); TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك وأبو رزين,يحيى بن معاذ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:129/q20/p9 Putting things in their proper places. — Thalabi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi give this concise definition; Razi also includes it in right speech and action. [4 notes: THALABI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:129/q20/p10 Resembling God insofar as humans can. — Razi transmits al-Qaffal's report of a philosophers' definition. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفلاسفة)]
- 2:129/q20/p11 Idolatry and sins. — Thalabi reports this unusual alternative without supporting argument. [1 notes: THALABI(قيل)]

## 2:129/q21 Why is it called ḥikma?  [ٱلْحِكْمَةَ · meaning]
turns on: Which root use is emphasized: restraint, adjudication, or obligatory right action.
- 2:129/q21/p1 Its root involves restraint and judgment. — Al-Asmaʿi says a judge checks injustice and a bridle restrains an animal; Ibn al-Jawzi says wisdom prevents ignorance; Razi derives it from 'I restrained'; Tabari derives it from ḥukm separating true from false. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL]
- 2:129/q21/p2 It denotes right action or obligation. — Thalabi cites an anonymous line where silence is proper ḥukm though rarely practiced. [2 notes: THALABI(الشاعر,قيل)]

## 2:129/q22 What does 'purify them' mean?  [وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether purification is cleansing, moral guidance, alms collection, or a future testimony.
- 2:129/q22/p1 Cleanse them of shirk, unbelief, doubt, sins and bad character. — Muqatil, Ibn Jurayj, Hasan and many others say this; Razi cites 7:157 and infers polytheists among Ishmael's descendants; Abu Hayyan distinguishes inward shirk and doubt from outward duties that remove sins. Bursevi distinguishes neglected duties and committed wrongs. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 2:129/q22/p2 Lead them to obedience, sincerity and good worship. — Ibn Abbas says obedience and sincerity, sometimes through zakat; Wahidi says guiding to the best worship. Mawardi says following his religion makes them pure before God; Tabarsi says he makes them obedient and sincere. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:129/q22/p3 Invite and motivate them toward faith and righteous action. — Al-Jubba'i and Ibn al-Jawzi describe a call to what makes people pure; Razi prefers warnings, promises, exhortation and practical engagement because a messenger cannot coerce inner states, and says it best serves Abraham's prayer for salvation. [4 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:129/q22/p4 Collect the alms due from their wealth. — Thalabi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Abu Hayyan report this. Alusi deems it far-fetched. [5 notes: THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:129/q22/p5 Attest to their purity and uprightness on Judgment Day. — Ibn Kaysan links their testimony for the prophets in 2:143 to the messenger's testimony for them; al-Asamm and others describe final judgment. Razi prefers an active purification reading, and Alusi calls the testimony reading far-fetched. [5 notes: THALABI(ابن كيسان); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كيسان); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); TABRISI(الأصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:129/q23 What does the root of yuzakkīhim signify?  [وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: The second note traces a later use of the same root rather than a competing verse gloss.
- 2:129/q23/p1 Purification and growth in good. — Majaz compares zakiyya in 18:75; Jishumi says a pure soul is zakiyya; Wahidi says tazkiya attributes untainted increase in deeds, and Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say zakat stays within cleansing or growth. [6 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:129/q23/p2 The term al-Nafs al-Zakiyya became a historical epithet. — Jishumi says Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Hasan was called this after predictions of his death at a place of that name, where he was killed. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:129/q24 Why does purification follow teaching here and precede it elsewhere?  [وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The audience's needs and the relation between knowledge and moral cleansing.
- 2:129/q24/p1 Here knowledge precedes removal of vice or cultivation of practice. — Razi says perfection needs theoretical truth and practical good before deficiencies are removed; Biqai says Islam already grounds purification here and these descendants need instruction; Bursevi sees theory followed by practical faculty. Alusi calls teaching adornment and purification emptying of defilement, with adornment first for nobility. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:129/q24/p2 Other verses reverse the order to fit their audiences. — Burhan notes the change; Biqai says the unlettered in 62:2 first needed release from major shirk, while believers in 3:164 needed cleansing from attachment to spoils after defeat. [3 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:129/q25 What does al-ʿAzīz mean?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ · meaning]
turns on: Which root nuance—prevailing, peerlessness, vengeance, strength, honor or rarity—best applies to God.
- 2:129/q25/p1 The unconquerable and prevailing, whose will cannot be stopped. — Al-Kisa'i cites 38:23 and the proverb of victory; Ibn Kaysan cites 35:44; al-Mufaddal says no hand or refusal reaches God. Tabarsi and Razi stress capacity; Alusi favors overpowering. Alusi rejects peerless and avenger as distant alternatives. Thalabi also cites an unexplained proverb, min ʿazīz, in connection with the word. [29 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI(ابن كيسان,الكسائي,المفضل بن سلمة,قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق والمفضل,الكسائي وابن الأنباري); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كيسان,الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كيسان); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:129/q25/p2 The peerless One, without equal. — Ibn Abbas and Qurtubi invoke 42:11, 'nothing is like Him'; al-Farra links the root to rarity. Alusi calls the peerless gloss distant. [8 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الإمام الغزالي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:129/q25/p3 The avenger, mighty in punishment. — Al-Kalbi cites 3:4; Abu al-Aliya emphasizes punishment and Ibn Ishaq support of believers against unbelievers. Alusi calls this gloss distant. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:129/q25/p4 The strong one, or one who grants strength and honor. — Thalabi cites 36:14 for strengthening and 3:26 for bestowing honor; Abu Hayyan repeats both. Root usage includes hard camel flesh, difficult matters, hard ground and a powerful bird. Wahidi resists physical severity as a divine attribute. [10 notes: THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو الشيباني,أبو كبير الهذلي); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 2:129/q25/p5 The rare, eminent, urgently needed and hard to reach. — Al-Ghazali's definition in Bursevi combines these conditions; al-Farra's root explanation stresses scarcity. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإمام الغزالي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)]

## 2:129/q26 How does the root ʿ-z-z vary by form and usage?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ · meaning]
- 2:129/q26/p1 It can denote prevailing, strength, hardness, preciousness or rarity. — Al-Samin distinguishes vowels: ḍamma for prevailing (38:23), fatḥa for strength, kasra for rarity. Thalabi gives hardened camel flesh and 'difficult for me'; Wahidi cites al-Shaybani's lean camel, Abu Kabir's powerful bird and hard azāz ground, but denies physical hardness of God. Tabarsi adds honor through association. [7 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو الشيباني,أبو كبير الهذلي,الفراء); TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:129/q27 What does al-Ḥakīm mean?  [ٱلْحَكِيمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ḥakīm emphasizes knowledgeable essence or the precision of divine action.
- 2:129/q27/p1 Wise and precise in command, speech, deeds and placement. — Tabari connects ḥakīm to accuracy; Abu al-Aliya says wise in command and Ibn Ishaq wise in argument to servants. Ibn Atiyya, Baydawi and Ibn Kathir say deeds are ordered in their proper places; Biqai's al-Harali says God's intended act cannot be undone. Abu Hayyan recalls the earlier explanation at 2:32. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,محمد بن إسحاق); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); BURSEVI]
- 2:129/q27/p2 The all-knowing One. — Jishumi and Tabarsi present this as an essential attribute; Razi includes all-knowledge in the closing praise. Alusi regards a merely knowing gloss as distant. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:129/q28 How do might and wisdom function as divine attributes?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ · theology]
turns on: The distinction between essence and action attributes and whether wisdom entails an intrinsic limit on divine power.
- 2:129/q28/p1 Power is essential; wise action is an attribute of action. — Jishumi makes ʿazīz an essential capacity and ḥakīm either essential knowledge or precise action; Tabarsi agrees. Razi says essential power and freedom from need are eternal, whereas relative invulnerability and wise acts can be action attributes. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin place essential might before active wisdom. [9 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:129/q28/p2 God cannot perform a vile act, since wisdom excludes folly. — Al-Nazzam argues divine wisdom is essential, folly intuitively incompatible with it, and impossibilities outside divine power. Razi responds that on his doctrine no divine act is folly, so the objection disappears. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(النظام)+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:129/q28/p3 Divine ʿizza must not imply bodily hardness or severity. — Wahidi permits invulnerability and transcendence beyond reach but explicitly rejects a physically severe or strong description. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق والمفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 2:129/q29 Why close this prayer with al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm in this order?  [إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْحَكِيمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The rhetorical pairing of divine ability and right ordering, plus the syntax of the closing address.
- 2:129/q29/p1 God has the power to send and protect the messenger and the wisdom to choose and teach through him. — Tabarsi says the names answer the prayer's need for power and knowledge of benefit and hidden thoughts; Razi says knowledge and power ensure sound acts, revelation and answered prayer. Burhan pairs commissioning with might and teaching with wisdom; Biqai says enemies cannot undo God's ordering. Abu Hayyan adds noblest placement of prophecy, essence before action and verse-end fit; Itqan says power enables judgment, though other contexts order names differently. [10 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 2:129/q29/p2 The second-person anta follows the earlier parsing pattern. — Abu Hayyan refers the possible iʿrāb of anta to his discussion at 2:127 without restating it. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:129/q30 What is the overall sequence and purpose of the requested prophetic tasks?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The listed tasks can be read as full prophetic conditions or as the culmination of Abraham's family prayer.
- 2:129/q30/p1 Recitation, explanation of scripture and wisdom, and purification together provide guidance and human perfection. — Tabarsi says these cover conveying revelation, explaining it and teaching Sunnah, and seek grace to adhere to scripture and law; Ibn Ishaq describes learning good and evil to practice and avoid them. Kuranyolu calls them basic functions of prophethood; Islahi sees the same tasks in 62:2. Baydawi and Bursevi describe theoretical knowledge and practical purification; Elmalili calls wisdom mastery of knowledge and action. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:129/q30/p2 Abraham sought durable guidance for his descendants through a messenger. — Razi says he wanted religion established for them now and later; Biqai says, after requesting direct teaching for himself, he asks for the usual intermediary for descendants. Abu Hayyan sees the prayer's climax as worldly and otherworldly happiness through security, provision and guidance. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:129/q31 How does this prayer connect to its surrounding verses and parallel passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes link the verse either to adjacent prayers and fulfillment passages or to broader scriptural patterns.
- 2:129/q31/p1 It follows prayers for Mecca and a submitted offspring and is answered in parallel Qur'anic passages. — Jishumi notes the turn from offspring to prophet; Abu Hayyan says it follows security, provision and a submissive community. Muqatil points to 62:2; Study Quran says 2:151 repeats the description; Islahi notes 62:2's matching functions. Ibn Ashur says the next prayer signals fulfillment despite limited exceptions. [6 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:129/q31/p2 The pairing of Book and Wisdom recurs elsewhere. — Study Quran names Jesus, Israel's children and Abraham's House at 3:48, 5:110, 45:16 and 4:54. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 2:129/q31/p3 The People of the Book are censured for opposing the very messenger sought by Abraham. — Biqai identifies Abraham as father of the Arabs and says the messenger would purify opponents by guidance. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:129/q32 Why is Abraham paired with Muhammad in later blessings?  [* · links]
turns on: Different reported grounds for naming the two together in prayer.
- 2:129/q32/p1 The pairing repays Abraham's prayer for Muhammad and his prayer for lasting praise. — Razi reports both explanations, citing 26:84 for lasting good mention; Elmalili says the community's prayers over Muhammad's and Abraham's households express thanks, and Kuranyolu notes the established Allahumma salli and barik tradition. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:129/q32/p2 They are paired as fathers of the community and of mercy. — Razi cites Abraham as religious father at 22:78, Ibn Masud's reading, 9:128 and the Prophet's description of himself as like a father in compassion. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:129/q32/p3 They are paired as heralds of pilgrimage and faith. — Razi cites Abraham's call in 22:27 and Muhammad's call in 3:193. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:129/q32/p4 Abraham's title Khalīl Allāh marks his standing. — Kuranyolu notes that hadith and other sources call him God's friend. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:129/q33 What inward guidance is read from the messenger's purification?  [وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · inward]
- 2:129/q33/p1 A perfected guide leads seekers from attachment to other than God toward certainty. — Bursevi says messengers order outer life and illumine the inner, their saintly heirs share in purification, and a guide is needed lest Satan guide the seeker. Hafiz's Persian line warns against the path without a guide; the perfected mentor recites inner and outer signs, yielding joy and companionship of the truthful. Bursevi ends by praying for protection from obstacles. [5 notes: BURSEVI(الحافظ)]
