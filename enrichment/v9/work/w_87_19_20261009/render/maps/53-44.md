# 53:44: 8 questions

## 53:44/q01 How is وَأَنَّهُۥ connected to the preceding discourse?  [وَأَنَّهُۥ · links]
turns on: Whether the opening is read wa-anna as a continuation or wa-inna as an independent assertion.
- 53:44/q01/p1 Wa-anna continues the sequence of truths attributed to the scriptures of Moses and Abraham. — STUDYQURAN says verses 42–50, apart from verse 46, begin wa-anna and continue 36–41; its introduction describes the transition from reflection on creation to enduring truths in those scriptures. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN+]
- 53:44/q01/p2 Wa-inna makes each statement an independent assertion, 'truly' or 'surely'. — STUDYQURAN reports this alternative reading of wa-anna. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 53:44/q02 How is the sequence وَأَنَّهُۥ هُوَ pronounced in Ruways's transmission?  [وَأَنَّهُۥ هُوَ · readings]
turns on: The optional assimilation transmitted from Ruways, including the first two Najm instances reported through Abu al-Ala from al-Nakhkhas.
- 53:44/q02/p1 Assimilation is transmitted at this sequence. — NASHR lists this verse among Ruways's optional assimilation sites; Abu al-Ala, from al-Nakhkhas, reports assimilation at the first two Najm occurrences of innahu huwa. Other Ruways transmitters include these passages among those with both sound pronunciations. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو العلاء عن النخاس,الباقون عن رويس)]
- 53:44/q02/p2 Clear pronunciation without assimilation is also transmitted. — NASHR reports that other transmitters from Ruways give both assimilation and clear pronunciation as sound transmissions at the listed passages, including this verse. [2 notes: NASHR(الباقون عن رويس)]

## 53:44/q03 What does the separating pronoun هُوَ assert?  [هُوَ · rhetoric]
- 53:44/q03/p1 It emphasizes that God alone truly causes death and life. — ABUHAYYAN says it singles out the true agent of this series despite human claims, citing 2:258. BURHAN says others may claim death or life, unlike creation of the sexes in 53:45, where the pronoun is absent; he also cites 2:258. ALUSI adds repeated attribution. IBNASHUR says it counters attribution to time, citing 45:24, and that the past tense concerns observed acts rather than an afterlife denied by pagans. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BURHAN+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 53:44/q04 Why are no objects expressed after أَمَاتَ وَأَحْيَا?  [أَمَاتَ وَأَحْيَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verbs have deliberately omitted objects or are used intransitively.
- 53:44/q04/p1 The objects are omitted to assert God's agency generally, without naming recipients. — JURJANI places these verbs with أضحك وأبكى and أغنى وأقنى: an omitted object is better left unspoken where context permits. His illustration from a fragment attributed to Sumayya shows that a suppressed sense should be restored only when context requires it. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(سمية); JURJANI-DALAIL+]
- 53:44/q04/p2 The two verbs are used intransitively, displaying God's wondrous power and hinting at resurrection. — IBNASHUR compares their use to 53:43 and says human creation witnesses the possibility of resurrection. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 53:44/q05 What deaths and lives do أَمَاتَ وَأَحْيَا describe?  [أَمَاتَ وَأَحْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: Which bodily, spiritual, social, or figurative transitions the two verbs name, and whether they refer to this world, the grave, or resurrection.
- 53:44/q05/p1 Death in this world is followed by life at resurrection. — THALABI, WAHIDI, BAGHAWI, IBNJAWZI, QURTUBI, NASAFI, and others report or hold this. WAHIDI attributes it to earlier commentators. TABRISI adds that God raises the dead for recompense. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); WAHIDI-BASIT(المتقدمون من المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 53:44/q05/p2 God causes fathers to die and brings children to life. — WAHIDI says later commentators give this reading and compares 45:24. Sources variously say sons or children. [7 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المتأخرون من المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI]
- 53:44/q05/p3 God brings life from sperm or a drop of fluid. — NAHHAS specifies placing the spirit in a person who had been a fluid drop; THALABI, WAHIDI, QURTUBI, and BURSEVI describe the sperm and living offspring or being. WAHIDI cites 30:19 among parallels for later explanations. [5 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المتأخرون من المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; NAHHAS+]
- 53:44/q05/p4 Unbelief or estrangement is death; faith, recognition, or gnosis is life. — TUSTARI specifies enemies' hearts darkened by disbelief and friends' hearts lit by faith and gnosis; THALABI adds separation versus knowledge and communion. BAGHAWI distinguishes the indefinite 'died' unbeliever from the definite 'lived' believer. QURTUBI cites 6:122 and 6:36; WAHIDI cites 30:19 and 6:122. Ja'far adds turning away, disobedience, and lights of conformity; BURSEVI says denial versus recognition. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المتأخرون من المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الثعلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(ST); SULAMI(جعفر); TUSTARI+]
- 53:44/q05/p5 God ordains or brings about the causes of death and life. — MAWARDI calls this ordaining the causes; QURTUBI says God causes the circumstances; BURSEVI reports the same explanation. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 53:44/q05/p6 God creates death and life themselves. — Ibn Bahr's interpretation is reported by MAWARDI and QURTUBI with 67:2 as evidence. TABRISI says God alone creates both and argues that one who could cause death could cause its opposite, while no one except God can give life. BURSEVI adds that a killer only dismantles bodily structure; God causes death by customary order. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن بحر); BURSEVI+; TABRISI+]
- 53:44/q05/p7 Death and life are famine or barrenness and fertility. — MAWARDI says death may be barrenness and life fertility; QURTUBI says drought and fertility. BURSEVI lists fertility and famine as another pair, varying the order of the terms. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 53:44/q05/p8 Death and life are sleep and waking. — MAWARDI and QURTUBI give sleep followed by waking; BURSEVI lists waking and sleeping in reverse verbal order. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 53:44/q05/p9 Death is turning from remembrance or obedience; life is restored remembrance or obedience. — Al-Qasim in SULAMI says people die to remembrance and obedience and revive through them, leaving no excuse; THALABI reports his turning away from remembrance version. MAWARDI reports death through disobedience and life through obedience; Ja'far adds killing souls through disobedience and reviving hearts through conformity. [4 notes: SULAMI(القاسم,جعفر); THALABI(القاسم); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 53:44/q05/p10 Death comes by God's justice and life by His grace or favor. — SULAMI and THALABI attribute the pairing to Ibn Ata; QURTUBI attributes a justice and favor version to Ata. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(ابن عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 53:44/q05/p11 Death is veiling or concealment; life is disclosure or manifestation. — SULAMI attributes veiling and self-disclosure to al-Nasrabadi. QUSHAYRI includes concealment and manifestation among inward pairings; BURSEVI attributes that pairing to realized Sufis. [3 notes: SULAMI(النصرآباذي); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); BURSEVI(المحققون)]
- 53:44/q05/p12 Death is withholding or miserliness; life is generosity or giving. — THALABI and QURTUBI give withholding and miserliness versus generosity and giving; BURSEVI also lists miserliness and generosity, withholding and giving. [3 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 53:44/q05/p13 Ascetics' selves die through discipline, while gnostics' hearts live through witnessing or spiritual qualities. — QUSHAYRI gives struggle and witnessing; BURSEVI reports Qushayri's discipline and witnessing, and describes the self's death to bodily desires and animal pleasures versus the heart's life through spiritual qualities and divine character. He says the self and heart can alternate in dominance until settled assurance subdues the self. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); BURSEVI(القشيري); BURSEVI+]
- 53:44/q05/p14 Death and life occur in this world and then in the grave. — QUSHAYRI says grave life brings either rest or punishment; BURSEVI reports death at the appointed time and revival in the grave. [2 notes: BURSEVI; QUSHAYRI+]
- 53:44/q05/p15 Death is awe and life is intimacy. — QUSHAYRI lists this inward pairing; BURSEVI attributes it to realized Sufis. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); BURSEVI(المحققون)]
- 53:44/q05/p16 Death is turning away and life is turning toward. — QUSHAYRI lists this pairing; Ja'far in SULAMI speaks of death through turning away and life through gnosis. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 53:44/q05/p17 God simply causes living beings to die and brings the dead to life. — MUQATIL gives this direct account without specifying resurrection or another stage. [1 notes: MUQATIL+]
- 53:44/q05/p18 Death is ignorance and life is knowledge. — BURSEVI reports this among the further explanatory pairs. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 53:44/q05/p19 Death is annihilation of the person and life is subsistence through God. — BURSEVI reports this as another saying of Qushayri. [1 notes: BURSEVI(القشيري)]
- 53:44/q05/p20 Obedience and sin form another inward death-and-life pairing. — QUSHAYRI lists obedience and sin among inward pairs without specifying in this note which corresponds to death and which to life. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 53:44/q06 What rhetorical effect comes from pairing death and life in this order?  [أَمَاتَ وَأَحْيَا · rhetoric]
turns on: The stated grounds include temporal sequence, warning, rhyme, correspondence with the previous pair, and antithesis.
- 53:44/q06/p1 Death precedes life because life follows prior death or lifelessness. — BURHAN says life follows death. BURSEVI gives sperm before living being, heart death before revival, and bodily death before resurrection in the grave. [2 notes: BURHAN+; BURSEVI+]
- 53:44/q06/p2 Placing death first subdues creatures and makes the warning arrive sooner. — BURHAN says the sequence subdues creatures; BURSEVI says it hastens the warning's impact. [2 notes: BURHAN+; BURSEVI+]
- 53:44/q06/p3 The reversed order matches death with the preceding weeping and life with laughter. — BIQAI notes that the reversal from the previous pair is rhetorically apt for these associations. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]
- 53:44/q06/p4 Death comes first for rhyme. — BURSEVI gives rhyme as one explanation of the order. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]
- 53:44/q06/p5 The pair is an antithesis, setting life against death. — IBNASHUR identifies the device as antithesis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 53:44/q06/p6 The sequence recalls life between two deaths or periods of nonexistence. — BURHAN says human life resembles death and leads back to death. BURSEVI says nonexistence precedes existence and existence ends in annihilation, warning against being deceived by life between two deaths or existence between two absences. [2 notes: BURHAN+; BURSEVI+]

## 53:44/q07 Who causes death and life, and what role can other causes have?  [* · theology]
- 53:44/q07/p1 God alone truly causes or creates death and life; other agents and causes depend on Him. — TABRISI argues that only God can give life and cites recompense; IBNKATHIR and STUDYQURAN cite 67:2, while STUDYQURAN also lists 2:258, 3:156, 7:158, 9:116, 10:56, 22:6, 23:80, 40:68, 44:8, and 57:2. BURSEVI and ALUSI say a killer dismantles the body while God brings death through His customary order; BURSEVI calls the servant's share an acquired act. BIQAI allows apparent causes. TABATABAI permits natural and non-natural intermediaries, such as human joy, organs, and angels, but denies them independent agency. IBNASHUR says human creation witnesses the power that makes resurrection possible. ISLAHI says exclusive life-giving rules out rival lordship and calls people to turn to God; KURANYOLU extends dependence on God's will to all events. [22 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن بحر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURHAN+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; MUQATIL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+]

## 53:44/q08 How does the verse fit the surrounding passage and related scriptural claims?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect it to different parts of the surrounding argument and to distinct scriptural parallels.
- 53:44/q08/p1 It forms part of a series of divine acts showing dependence on God's power across life and the universe. — KURANYOLU identifies verses 43–49 as examples of universal dependence. ISLAHI groups joy, grief, life, death, provision, children, prosperity, and poverty and urges constant turning to God. IBNASHUR relates the verbs to 53:43; STUDYQURAN says death and life show power over every phase of existence and nonexistence. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; STUDYQURAN+]
- 53:44/q08/p2 These acts echo Abraham's declaration of separation from his people's worship. — ISLAHI cites 26:78–82 and notes slight variation in the acts listed. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 53:44/q08/p3 The surrounding appeal directs those claiming descent from Abraham and Moses to their teachings and sacrifices, not inherited entitlement to Paradise. — ISLAHI says the assumed entitlement is condemned in this setting. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 53:44/q08/p4 The verse's giving life can be read alongside God's reviving a spiritually dead person in 6:122. — THALABI explicitly links the descriptions; WAHIDI and QURTUBI also cite 6:122 for the faith-and-unbelief interpretation, while QURTUBI adds 6:36. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المتأخرون من المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI+]
