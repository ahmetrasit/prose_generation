# 92:13: 14 questions

## 92:13/q01 What do the Hereafter and the first life designate?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the paired terms name the realms, their rewards, Paradise specifically, or inward aspects of the soul.
- 92:13/q01/p1 The two abodes: the next life and this world. — Al-Wāḥidī calls them the two abodes. Al-Jishumī explains that al-ūlā is the feminine of al-awwal with 'abode' elided, while the latter abode is resurrection, Paradise, or Fire. Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the two-abode reading. Corpus Coranicum points to 93:4 for the paired terms. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:13/q01/p2 The rewards of this life and the next. — Al-Kalbī and al-Farrāʾ are named by al-Māwardī; al-Wāḥidī attributes this to al-Kalbī and cites 4:134. Abū Ḥayyān cites Abraham's worldly reward and next-worldly righteousness in 29:27. Study Quran says punishment in both worlds follows by implication and cites 53:25, 4:134, and 79:25. Abū Ṣāliḥ's Ibn ʿAbbās report adds that seeking these rewards elsewhere misses the way. [12 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI(الكلبي والفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي والفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BINTSHATI-]
- 92:13/q01/p3 Paradise in the case of the Hereafter, and this world in the case of the first life. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ. [2 notes: QURTUBI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس)]
- 92:13/q01/p4 Inwardly, the afterlife is the soul of spirit and the first life the soul of nature. — Al-Tustarī says people are guided toward one or the other. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 92:13/q02 Why use al-ūlā instead of al-dunyā?  [ٱلْأُولَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 92:13/q02/p1 Al-ūlā better conveys the breadth of God's ownership. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the Qur'an pairs al-ākhira with al-ūlā, never al-dunyā: al-ūlā includes all earlier affairs of heaven and earth, while al-dunyā concerns human worldly life. He cites 2:201, 2:86, and 4:77. Al-dunyā derives from أدنى and can mean nearer, lower, lesser, or inferior, senses ill suited to vast dominion. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:13/q03 How does the emphatic lām in la-l-ākhira work?  [لَلْءَاخِرَةَ · grammar]
- 92:13/q03/p1 It is a non-governing lām of initiation moved into an inna clause to emphasize its delayed noun. — Al-Naḥḥās says the same emphatic construction appears in the adjoining declaration. Al-Itqān explains that the lām moves from initial position after inna to avoid consecutive emphatics; it can enter a subject, an inna predicate, or its delayed noun, citing لأَنْتُمْ أَشَدُّ رَهْبَةً and إِنَّ رَبِّي لَسَمِيعُ الدُّعَاءِ as well as this verse. It also notes that the lām makes an imperfect verb present in sense. [3 notes: NAHHAS; ITQAN]

## 92:13/q04 Why emphasize God's ownership with the lām here?  [لَلْءَاخِرَةَ · rhetoric]
- 92:13/q04/p1 The emphasis fits the sūra's repeated concern with wealth and possession. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 53:25, whose context lacks the wealth theme and omits this emphatic lām. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:13/q05 Why is the Hereafter placed before the first life?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order chiefly answers deniers, expresses the afterlife's greater weight, follows this passage's context, or preserves cadence.
- 92:13/q05/p1 The deniers especially need the afterlife stressed. — Al-Biqāʿī says the words address deniers. He rejects making rhyme the sole explanation, citing the end of al-Tawba (9:129) and noting that other wordings could also rhyme; Qur'anic ordering should not be treated as poetic meter. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(قائلون)-]
- 92:13/q05/p2 The afterlife merits priority because it is better and enduring, as is its severer punishment. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites the same order in the promise to the Prophet at 93:4 and the threat concerning Pharaoh at 79:25; she explicitly rejects a mere rhyme explanation. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(قائلون)-]
- 92:13/q05/p3 The order suits this passage's focus on God's ownership of both realms. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Qaṣaṣ 28:70–73, where the worldly realm comes first amid worldly blessings and thanksgiving; al-Layl omits praise and puts the Hereafter first. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:13/q05/p4 The fronting preserves the verse ending. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports that some interpreters give this explanation; al-Biqāʿī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ dispute a rhyme-only account. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(قائلون); BIQAI-; BIQAI-FULL-; BINTSHATI-]

## 92:13/q06 What does it mean that both lives belong to God?  [لَنَا ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether possession concerns the realms and everything in them, their rewards, or God's recompense within them.
- 92:13/q06/p1 God owns and governs both realms and all they contain, disposing of them as He wills. — Al-Ṭabarī says God gives or withholds from creatures. Muqātil is cited for dominion. Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr affirm His ownership and direction. Al-Rāzī's second interpretation says He grants both abodes as He wills. Al-Biqāʿī says He gives and withholds. Al-Ālūsī includes reward, punishment, and the fates of generous and miserly people; Study Quran reports unlimited disposal. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 5:17 for people's lack of claim beyond God's grant. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī grounds ordinary ownership in all things' dependence on God for existence and cites 13:41, 12:21, and 14:27 against any rival. [32 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); THALABI; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Sh); BINTSHATI+; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 92:13/q06/p2 God owns the rewards of the two lives. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Kalbī are cited for rewards. Abū Ḥayyān adduces Abraham's reward in this life and his righteousness in the next at 29:27. Study Quran extends the reading by implication to punishment in each. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects al-Zamakhsharī's restriction to reward because the context contains warning as well as glad tidings. [12 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI(الكلبي والفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي والفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BINTSHATI-]
- 92:13/q06/p3 God recompenses people in both worlds. — Al-Māwardī offers this as a further reading besides dominion and ownership of rewards. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 92:13/q07 Where should people seek the good of either life?  [لَنَا ٱلْءَاخِرَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse directs all requests to the owner or also promises worldly provision to whoever chooses the higher life.
- 92:13/q07/p1 They should seek both lives and their good from God, their sole owner. — A report in al-Sulamī and the explanations of al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, and Ibn al-Jawzī say seeking either from another misses the way. Al-Rāzī says people should seek happiness in both from God. Al-Qurṭubī's Abū Ṣāliḥ report cites 4:134. Al-Qushayrī is not cited here. Qutb says there is nowhere to flee from God; al-Iṣlāḥī says neither strategy, racial standing, nor deities secure benefit and cites 53:25. He compares self-credit for wealth to Qārūn's claim at 28:78. Kuranyolu and al-Sāmarrāʾī urge seeking good and following God's guidance in both worlds. [18 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:13/q07/p2 Turning toward God for the higher life brings the lower life as well. — Al-Qāshānī says one who chooses the higher necessarily receives the lower; he adduces 5:66, which describes provision from above and below. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 92:13/q08 How does God's ownership bear on guidance and human responsibility?  [* · theology]
turns on: How sovereign granting and ownership relate to guidance by explanation, non-compulsion, human choice, and claims upon God.
- 92:13/q08/p1 God's dominion is unaffected by human guidance or misguidance; each person's response benefits or harms that person. — Al-Ṭabrisī says obedience adds nothing to God's dominion and disobedience subtracts nothing. Al-Rāzī associates this first interpretation with the Muʿtazila. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ālūsī likewise say refusal harms the refuser and cite, in al-Ālūsī, 10:108 and 17:15. Elmalılı says no one can dispense with His guidance. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says worldly response shapes the final outcome without resolving free will; she favors the explanation attributed to al-Ṭabarī over an unnamed interpretation that raises a responsibility concern. [17 notes: TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:13/q08/p2 God guides through explanation, commands, promises, warnings, and revelation without forcing acceptance. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī reason that compulsion would undo moral responsibility or testing. Al-Iṣlāḥī says God guides through His Messenger while accepting or refusing is people's responsibility; he addresses Quraysh in verses 11–14. Al-Rāzī says this interpretation suits the Muʿtazila. [6 notes: TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:13/q08/p3 God grants guidance and the fortunes of both worlds to whom He wills. — Al-Ṭabarī says God grants obedience and honor here and reward later, while leaving others to disobedience, humiliation, and punishment. Al-Rāzī says people should seek happiness in both from Him and prefers this account as fitting his school over the Muʿtazilite account. Al-Bayḍāwī says He gives what He wills; al-Biqāʿī says the preceding guidance does not create an obligation upon God. [7 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BIQAI; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 92:13/q08/p4 Guidance is God's favor, while people have no claim on Him beyond what He grants. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 5:17 and links this claim to the previous verse's statement of guidance. He adds that God disclosed the order of next-worldly recompense and established worldly causes and consequences. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:13/q09 How do conduct and outcomes in both lives relate?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse foregrounds reward for guidance, both promise and threat, or the distinct ways God orders results in the two lives.
- 92:13/q09/p1 Guided people receive reward in both abodes. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites Abraham's descendants receiving prophecy and scripture at 29:27; al-Bayḍāwī also offers the reward of following guidance. Abū Ḥayyān cites Abraham's worldly reward and next-worldly righteousness. Al-Qāshānī says choosing the higher brings the lower as well, citing 5:66. [8 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QASHANI; BINTSHATI-]
- 92:13/q09/p2 Both favorable and adverse outcomes depend on people's response to guidance under God's rule. — Al-Ṭabarī sets obedience and honor against disobedience, humiliation, and punishment. Study Quran says a rewards reading implies punishments as well. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses both glad tidings and warning and says response or refusal determines the final outcome. Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes that ease and hardship may arise in either life; givers seek the afterlife while misers seek this world. [7 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); BINTSHATI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:13/q09/p3 Afterlife recompense follows God's disclosed order, and worldly results follow causes He established. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says God commands people to preserve the worldly system and guides them; neglect of its safeguards makes them deserve the consequences. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:13/q10 How does this declaration connect with the surrounding sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is the completion of 92:12 and support for the coming outcomes, or the structural hinge between the sūra's contrasted groups.
- 92:13/q10/p1 It completes the prior statement about divine guidance and supports the ensuing warning and promise. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the conjunction to 92:12 and says guidance is a favor. Al-Biqāʿī says the declaration removes any inference that guidance obligates God. Al-Iṣlāḥī reads verses 11–14 as an admonition to Quraysh: revelation guides without forcing and both worlds are God's. Corpus Coranicum says God's self-predication separates the second and third stanzas and underwrites threats and promises; Cuypers calls it the sūra's center. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:13/q10/p2 It is a hinge in the sūra's paired contrasts of conduct and recompense. — Corpus Coranicum traces the saved and damned through verses 5–13 and 14–21, and reports Neuwirth's placement of verse 11 with the theological predication in verses 12–13. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reads the givers as seeking the Hereafter and misers as seeking this life, with ease or difficulty possible in either. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:13/q11 What inward orientation do the two lives call for?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual orientation to the two lives the interpreter foregrounds.
- 92:13/q11/p1 The wise view the afterlife with certainty and the world as a lesson. — Al-Sulamī reports this saying from an unnamed authority. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 92:13/q11/p2 People are guided toward either the soul of spirit or the soul of nature. — Al-Tustarī identifies the afterlife with the first and the first life with the second. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 92:13/q11/p3 Turning to God and choosing the higher brings both lives. — Al-Qāshānī adduces 5:66's image of provision from above and below. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 92:13/q12 What did the Prophet and other prophets leave as inheritance?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the report names scripture, knowledge, or the disposition of any property left behind.
- 92:13/q12/p1 The Prophet left the Qur'an, described as what is between two covers. — Ibn Kathīr transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's answer and Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥanafiyya's agreement, saying al-Bukhārī alone transmitted it. He explains that the Sunna clarifies the Qur'an, while God's Book is the primary inheritance, citing 35:32. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 92:13/q12/p2 Prophets leave knowledge, and taking it yields an abundant share. — Abū al-Dardāʾ reports that prophets leave no dinar or dirham. Ibn Kathīr explains that they called people to the Hereafter rather than accumulating and transmitting worldly goods. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 92:13/q12/p3 The Prophet left no inheritable property; what he left was charity. — Ibn Kathīr supports the absence of money, enslaved persons, and other property with ʿAmr ibn al-Ḥārith's statement. He quotes the Prophet's rule, 'We are not inherited; what we leave is charity.' Abū Bakr publicly affirmed it when asked about the inheritance; Ibn Kathīr lists ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī, ʿAbbās, Ṭalḥa, al-Zubayr, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf, Abū Hurayra, and ʿĀʾisha among those who accepted his report. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق)]

## 92:13/q13 Does an occasion concerning Abū Bakr limit the passage?  [* · reports]
- 92:13/q13/p1 Some apply verses 5–19 to Abū Bakr, while their message and rulings remain general. — Kuranyolu reports the proposed occasion and states that general applicability does not depend on identifying a specific occasion. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 92:13/q14 How are worldly and afterlife existence related?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:13/q14/p1 They are two stages of one continuous human life. — Asad describes human existence here and hereafter as a continuous entity. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
