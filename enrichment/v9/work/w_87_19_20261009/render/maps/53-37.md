# 53:37: 19 questions

## 53:37/q01 How is Abraham's name read?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · readings]
- 53:37/q01/p1 Ibrāhīm is read Ibrāhām, with long ā in place of long ī. — QIRAAT-ER calls both canonical and explains Ibrāhām as a phonological adaptation of the foreign name. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Lvw)]

## 53:37/q02 How is 'and Abraham' construed?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase is analyzed as coordination with Moses or as an explanatory clause.
- 53:37/q02/p1 Abraham is coordinated with Moses, standing for Abraham's scriptures. — Al-Samīn and Bursevī specify coordination with Moses; Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān supply the implied scriptures. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 53:37/q02/p2 The Abraham clause occupies a nominative explanatory position for the following no-bearer principle. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this grammatical analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 53:37/q03 Why does Moses precede Abraham here?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which historical or audience feature accounts for the prophets' order.
- 53:37/q03/p1 The order moves from the nearer, better-known Moses to Abraham. — Bursevī says Moses had a revealed book whereas Abraham did not in the same sense. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 53:37/q03/p2 Moses comes first because his scripture is more extensive. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts 87:18–19, where Abraham precedes Moses by chronology. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:37/q03/p3 Abraham is mentioned first because his scrolls were more familiar to the audience. — Al-Ālūsī gives this explanation, though the note's ordering differs from the present wording. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 53:37/q04 What does the mention of Abraham refer to?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the scrolls are identified generally as revealed writings or specifically with the testing words of 2:124.
- 53:37/q04/p1 It refers to Abraham's revealed scrolls and what was written in them. — Al-Zajjāj and Qushayrī frame the preceding question as asking about the contents of Moses' and Abraham's writings; Ṭabrisī identifies Torah and Abraham's pages. Islāḥī allows writings preserving Abraham's teachings even with later exegesis. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:37/q04/p2 Abraham's scrolls may be the words with which God tested him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr proposes this by linking the scrolls with 2:124 and Abraham's conveying those words to his people. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:37/q05 How many scrolls or books were attributed to Abraham?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · reports]
- 53:37/q05/p1 Abraham received ten scrolls. — Abū Dharr's report through Ibn al-Jawzī also gives Moses ten before the Torah. Bursevī's Abū Dharr report totals 104 books, assigning ten to Abraham, ten to Adam, fifty to Seth, thirty to Idrīs and four later books. Ibn ʿĀshūr estimates the ten old-script sheets at roughly forty Qur'anic verses. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو ذر); BURSEVI(أبو ذر الغفاري); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:37/q06 What counsel is reported from Abraham's scrolls?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · reports]
turns on: The reports preserve different admonitions from the scrolls.
- 53:37/q06/p1 A king should answer the oppressed person's prayer instead of amassing wealth. — Bursevī's scroll report includes even an oppressed unbeliever. [1 notes: BURSEVI(صحف إبراهيم)]
- 53:37/q06/p2 A wise person should allot time to prayer, reflection, self-accounting and lawful needs, and guard the tongue. — Bursevī reports that speech counts among one's deeds and one should know one's era. [1 notes: BURSEVI(صحف إبراهيم)]

## 53:37/q07 Why are Abraham and Moses singled out?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the selection highlights the historical rule against transferred guilt or addresses communities claiming prophetic ancestry.
- 53:37/q07/p1 Abraham first opposed holding people responsible for relatives' crimes, and Moses confirmed or continued the rule. — Al-Samīn, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı place the earlier custom between Noah and Abraham or before Abraham and say his law ended it through Moses. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân)]
- 53:37/q07/p2 Their teachings correct those who claim descent from them grants salvation. — Islāḥī says Quraysh claimed Abraham as ancestor and leader, while the People of the Book claimed both prophets; their teaching and sacrifices, rather than descent, determine the point. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:37/q08 How is وَفَّىٰ read?  [وَفَّىٰٓ · readings]
turns on: Doubling or lightening the fāʾ, with some commentators assigning distinct emphases.
- 53:37/q08/p1 The common or majority reading doubles the fāʾ: waffā. — Jishumī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn identify it as common or majority; Zamakhsharī and Qurṭubī say doubling intensifies or indicates complete fulfillment. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 53:37/q08/p2 It is also read with a light, undoubled fāʾ: wafā. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr is repeatedly named; other transmitters include Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī, Abū Mālik, Ibn al-Sumayfaʿ, Abū Umāma, Zayd ibn ʿAlī and a report from the Prophet. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says interpretation remains the same; Qurṭubī glosses truthfulness in word and deed, Jishumī keeping promise and covenant, and Qāshānī the primordial covenant. [12 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI(سعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير وأبو مالك وابن السميفع,النبي وأبو أمامة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير وأبو عمران الجوني وابن السميفع اليماني); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وقتادة); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو أمامة الباهلي وسعيد بن جبير وأبو مالك الغفاري وابن السميفع وزيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو أمامة الباهلي وسعيد بن جبير وابن السميفع); ALUSI-FULL(أبو أمامة الباهلي وسعيد بن جبير وأبو مالك الغفاري وابن السمقيع وزيد بن علي)]

## 53:37/q09 What does وَفَّىٰ mean and imply?  [وَفَّىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verbal force is full completion, covenant fidelity, or emphatic expression of unusually severe trials.
- 53:37/q09/p1 It means to fulfill or complete fully, reaching the end of what is due. — Ibn Qutayba says attaining the intended end; Thaʿlabī and Wāḥidī liken tawfiya to full payment of a right or wages, citing 3:57 and 35:30; Jishumī distinguishes giving in full from meeting one's due. Naḥḥās, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Bursevī and al-Samīn stress completion or breadth, and 2:124 supplies a parallel. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:37/q09/p2 It emphasizes faithful keeping of a covenant or promise. — Rāzī prefers covenant-fulfillment as the apparent sense and connects it to the confirmed dream in 37:105–106; Bayḍāwī says Abraham went to great lengths for his covenant. Bursevī likewise links intensification to exceptional fidelity. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:37/q09/p3 The doubled verb is more emphatic because Abraham's trials were exceptionally severe. — Al-Zajjāj proposes the explanation, repeated by Ibn al-Jawzī; Zamakhsharī and Nasafī describe intensification of complete fulfillment. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 53:37/q11 How is Abraham's fulfillment related grammatically to the following no-bearer statement?  [وَفَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the next clause functions as the object of fulfillment or as independent scroll content, and whether it is understood as fronted.
- 53:37/q11/p1 On the message-delivery reading, the following 'that' clause is governed by 'fulfilled'. — Wāḥidī says the particle an is accusative under waffā in the first interpretation. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 53:37/q11/p2 The no-bearer statement is content of Moses' and Abraham's scrolls, not a restriction on what Abraham fulfilled. — Ṭabarī gives this analysis and rejects limiting the general verb to that one teaching; Ibn ʿAbbās says the rule through the rest is in Moses' scrolls. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); TAB-FULL+]
- 53:37/q11/p3 The no-bearer clause is fronted in meaning and belongs with the mention of both sets of scrolls. — Ṭabarī reports this in conjunction with the son-sacrifice interpretation. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 53:37/q12 Why is Abraham described as one who fulfilled?  [ٱلَّذِى وَفَّىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the epithet chiefly explains his exceptional merit, its universal recognition, or its rebuke to descendants.
- 53:37/q12/p1 His extraordinary trials warrant the epithet. — Al-Ālūsī says the sacrifice alone suffices; Bursevī lists fire, sacrifice, migration, leaving family in a barren valley and guest-seeking; Bayḍāwī adds refusing Gabriel's help. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 53:37/q12/p2 All major audiences accepted this praise of Abraham. — Rāzī says Jews, polytheists and Muslims agreed on Abraham's fidelity, whereas polytheists might hesitate to praise Moses. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:37/q12/p3 His faithfulness exposes descendants who claim his favor while abandoning his teaching. — Ibn ʿĀshūr indicts Arabs' idolatry despite Abraham's transmission; Islāḥī contrasts Abraham's standing through obedience with descendants' reliance on ancestry. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:37/q13 What inward fulfillment is attributed to Abraham?  [ٱلَّذِى وَفَّىٰٓ · inward]
turns on: The inward accounts locate fulfillment in surrender, witnessing, or spiritual unity and service.
- 53:37/q13/p1 He left the self behind, bearing every trial as a blessing in God's presence. — Sulamī reports the unnamed saying. [1 notes: SULAMI(anonymous)]
- 53:37/q13/p2 He fulfilled by sincere, faithful witnessing in every condition and deed. — Sulamī reports Jaʿfar's definition. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 53:37/q13/p3 He surrendered self, heart, son and wealth to God. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ gives four offerings: self to fire, heart to the Merciful, son to sacrifice, wealth to brothers; Abū al-Qāsim recalls ransom of the son at 37:107. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو القاسم,ابن عطاء)]
- 53:37/q13/p4 He surrendered existence in annihilating unity, then served and conveyed in steadfast subsistence. — Qāshānī also allows completion of the divine testing words and, with the light reading, fidelity to the primordial covenant until attaining unity, citing 6:79. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 53:37/q14 How does the verse and its scroll citation connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which prior argument and audience the earlier-scriptures citation principally addresses.
- 53:37/q14/p1 The scrolls restate individual recompense already raised in 53:30–31. — Rāzī links both passages through recompense; Asad says the prophets conveyed lasting ethical truths, with 2:124 as a parallel. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 53:37/q14/p2 The citation challenges a person who gave briefly then withheld, possibly a scriptured person. — Rāzī proposes the turn from idolaters to a book-holder at 53:33 and says his scrolls already taught individual responsibility; the bodily-rights contrast is to 53:34. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل)]
- 53:37/q14/p3 The earlier scriptures counter polytheist claims of angelic intercession and set out creation, duties and recompense. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī links them to rejection of idols as God's daughters, creation and governance, resurrection, destruction of Noah's, ʿĀd's, Thamūd's and the overturned cities' peoples; the sūra then warns of the Hour and commands prostration. Study Quran calls these enduring truths after creation and transitions. [5 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 53:37/q15 What occasion of revelation is reported for the passage?  [* · reports]
- 53:37/q15/p1 It was revealed after a man unable to get a mount from the Prophet was offered transport to catch the army. — Al-Durr reports this from Darrāj Abū al-Samḥ. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(دراج أبو السمح)]

## 53:37/q16 What other report is preserved alongside these notes?  [* · reports]
- 53:37/q16/p1 Ibn ʿAbbās says the Kursī is the place of the two feet and no one can measure the Throne. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq transmits this separate report without a stated connection to the verse. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس)]

## 53:37/q17 When was the teaching about Abraham's fulfillment known?  [* · reports]
- 53:37/q17/p1 The teaching preceded Abraham. — Al-Naḥḥās transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿIkrima. [1 notes: NAHHAS(ابن عباس)]

## 53:37/q18 How does the preceding question particle introduce this citation?  [* · grammar]
- 53:37/q18/p1 The preceding am is disconnected and means 'Rather, has he not been informed?' — Al-Samīn gives the grammatical analysis as bal alam yunabbaʾ. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 53:37/q19 How does Tabari assess the two prophetic explanations of fulfillment?  [* · reports]
- 53:37/q19/p1 He would accept either report if sound, but both chains need verification. — Ṭabarī raises concerns about the reports connecting fulfillment to four early prayers or morning and evening glorification. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
