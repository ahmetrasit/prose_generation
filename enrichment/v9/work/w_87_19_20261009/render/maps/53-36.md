# 53:36: 19 questions

## 53:36/q01 What does 'am lam yunabba' mean?  [أَمْ لَمْ يُنَبَّأْ · meaning]
- 53:36/q01/p1 Rather, has he not been told or informed? — Wahidi al-Basit and Nasafi gloss yunabba as being informed; Abu Hayyan and Alusi explain am as 'rather'; Bursevi frames it as the possibility that he never heard the scriptural teaching. Farra glosses the full phrase أم لم ينبأ as ألم ينبأ, a direct 'has he not been informed?' Qushayri expresses the question as whether the addressee has heard the scriptural teaching. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; FARRA; QUSHAYRI]

## 53:36/q02 How may the final sound of 'yunabba' be read?  [يُنَبَّأْ · readings]
- 53:36/q02/p1 Its final hamza may be softened to a long ā without changing the meaning. — Qiraat-ER says the softened ending is less abrupt. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 53:36/q03 What rebuke does the question convey?  [أَمْ لَمْ يُنَبَّأْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ in whether they stress the failed excuse of ignorance or the obligation to consult earlier revelation.
- 53:36/q03/p1 The person turning away cannot claim complete ignorance of the earlier teaching. — Razi says one who fully knows needs no instruction and one wholly unaware, like a sleeper, is not addressed; neither condition excuses this person. Bursevi states the question as whether he has never been told. Qushayri frames the question as whether the unbeliever has heard the teaching in Moses' and Abraham's scriptures. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; QUSHAYRI]
- 53:36/q03/p2 If he rejects Muhammad, he should seek the earlier messengers' teaching on fearing God and escaping punishment. — Ibn Ashur calls this second interrogative another rebuke. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:36/q04 Whom does the question address?  [أَمْ لَمْ يُنَبَّأْ · referent]
turns on: Razi makes the audience depend on whether bima means the message's shared teachings or the actual contents received by a scriptured community.
- 53:36/q04/p1 It addresses Quraysh primarily and the People of the Book secondarily. — Islahi says Quraysh claimed Abraham as ancestor and religious leader, while the People of the Book claimed Moses and Abraham. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:36/q04/p2 It can address both idolaters and the People of the Book. — Razi says the Prophet proclaimed teachings such as monotheism and resurrection found in Moses' scrolls, so both audiences heard their substance. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:36/q04/p3 It specifically addresses the People of the Book. — On Razi's alternative, bima points to the scriptures' actual contents, received by that community; he also proposes that the person who ceased giving God's rights when Muhammad came may have possessed a book. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:36/q04/p4 It addresses the unbeliever. — Qushayri explicitly identifies the person asked whether he has heard the scriptural teaching as the unbeliever. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 53:36/q05 How does 'bima fi suhufi Musa' point to the earlier teaching?  [بِمَا فِى صُحُفِ مُوسَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The contrast is between the teachings in substance and the actual scriptural contents, with different implications for the audience.
- 53:36/q05/p1 It points to teachings found in Moses' scrolls, conveyed again by the Prophet. — Razi gives monotheism and resurrection as examples and says this reading reaches idolaters as well as the People of the Book; Tabatabai says preceding Qur'anic passages present the material as wisdom, admonitions, stories, and lessons without calling them excerpts. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 53:36/q05/p2 It points to the very contents as they stand in those scriptures. — Razi says this reading especially addresses the People of the Book, who received the scriptures; Zajjaj says the text goes on to state what the scrolls contain. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ZAJJAJ]

## 53:36/q06 Which teachings are recalled from the scrolls?  [بِمَا فِى صُحُفِ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes propose a particular nearby rule, an earlier-scriptures statement about the afterlife, or a broader body of common prophetic teaching; Razi favors the nearby rule without excluding further contents.
- 53:36/q06/p1 Chiefly, no soul bears another's burden and each person answers for his own deeds. — Nahhas says none is punished in another's place; Thalabi and Jishumi connect the teaching to Abraham's message and God's individual accounting. Razi prefers this as the most apparent content from 53:38–39, while allowing the scrolls to contain more. [4 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:36/q06/p2 The afterlife is better than worldly life. — Razi offers this as another proposed content, citing the first scriptures in 87:18–19. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:36/q06/p3 Foundational beliefs occur in every scripture, including monotheism and resurrection. — Razi cites 6:90 for following earlier guidance in religious fundamentals; legal branches may differ between communities. He also names monotheism and resurrection as teachings found in Moses' scrolls. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:36/q06/p4 They convey enduring wisdom and ethical truths through admonitions, stories, and lessons. — Tabatabai says the earlier Qur'anic presentation of these teachings takes those forms; Asad says Moses and Abraham exemplify prophets conveying enduring ethical truths. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 53:36/q06/p5 Prophetic descent alone does not entitle someone to Paradise; the prophets' teachings and sacrifices matter. — Islahi directs those claiming descent from Abraham and Moses to what the prophets taught and did. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:36/q07 What does 'suhuf' mean, and why is it plural?  [صُحُفِ · meaning]
- 53:36/q07/p1 It denotes written sheets or scrolls, plural because there were many parts or tablets. — Al-Raghib, reported by Bursevi, derives sahifa from a flat spread surface such as a face or writing sheet, gives saha'if and suhuf as plurals, and says a mushaf gathers sheets. Razi prefers numerous scrolls over merely counting Moses and Abraham as two possessors, citing the tablets in 7:154 and 7:150; Tabatabai says Abraham's scripture had many parts. Al-Qahestati, reported by Bursevi, says mushaf has three possible vowels on its mim and denotes a collection containing the Quran. [4 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب,القهستاتي); TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+]

## 53:36/q08 What variant reading is reported for 'suhuf'?  [صُحُفِ · readings]
- 53:36/q08/p1 The word is also read with a light consonant. — Kashshaf reports the lighter pronunciation without giving a change of meaning. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 53:36/q09 What are Moses' scrolls?  [صُحُفِ مُوسَىٰ · referent]
- 53:36/q09/p1 They are the Torah, described as its books or tablets. — Moses' scrolls are repeatedly identified as the Torah; Baydawi also calls his tablets the Torah. Razi relates the plural to the many tablets (7:154; 7:150). Ibn Ashur notes that 87:18–19 also names the earlier scriptures. [18 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]

## 53:36/q10 Why are Moses' scrolls named before Abraham's?  [صُحُفِ مُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanations appeal to familiarity and scale of scripture, the audience and context, scripture chronology, or the absence of a required order.
- 53:36/q10/p1 Moses' scripture was more familiar, extensive, accessible, or weighty for the audience. — Razi proposes that Moses' scrolls were numerous and familiar while Abraham's were less known. Baydawi calls the tablets more familiar and substantial; Burhan cites wider circulation and the verse endings; Biqai says the book was greatest after the Quran and available for consultation; Bursevi invokes the nearer, better-known figure; Ibn Ashur cites broader guidance and law, sparse Abrahamic material, and familiarity through the People of the Book (28:48); Study Quran reports the greater familiarity from Bḍ. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 53:36/q10/p2 The order here reflects the immediate audience, including Jews, rather than an invariant prophetic order. — Razi offers addressing Jews first as an explanation; he observes that Moses is often named later where the Prophet's dealings with polytheists and Jews and polytheist respect for Abraham shape the context. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:36/q10/p3 The order follows the order in which the scriptures descended. — Razi gives this as a possible rationale for a different ordering in the earlier sura, comparing 87:1. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:36/q10/p4 No special rationale is required; either order is eloquent. — Razi initially says eloquent speech allows either ordering, while later listing possible contextual explanations; he compares the earlier sura at 87:1. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:36/q11 Why are Moses and Abraham invoked together?  [صُحُفِ مُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain the pairing through audience familiarity and claimed ancestry, shared perennial teaching, or the history of individual accountability.
- 53:36/q11/p1 Their scriptures and legacies were familiar to the hearers who claimed a connection to them. — Ibn Ashur says Arabs knew Abraham's hanifiyya, sometimes claimed to follow it, as with Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl, and knew Moses' scrolls through the People of the Book (28:48). Islahi says Quraysh claimed Abraham and the People of the Book claimed both. Kuran Yolu says these were the best-known prophets to the audience; Biqai notes Israelite and Arab claims to Abraham's legacy. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:36/q11/p2 They attest the enduring truths revealed across prophetic history. — Asad cites 2:124 for the prophets' transmission of ethical truth; Kuran Yolu calls these core revelations; Study Quran says the section turns to perennial truths in both scriptures. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:36/q11/p3 Their scrolls are singled out in connection with the rule against liability for another's offense. — Qurtubi reports al-Hudhayl ibn Shurahbil's account that between Noah and Abraham people were held liable for relatives' offenses. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الهذيل ابن شرحبيل)]

## 53:36/q12 What are the scrolls attributed to Abraham in the continuation?  [صُحُفِ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'scrolls of Abraham' means written revelation given to him or later written preservation of teachings transmitted orally.
- 53:36/q12/p1 They are multiple written records of revelation sent to Abraham. — Ibn Atiyya calls them books sent from heaven; Tabrisi and Qurtubi pair Abraham's pages with Moses' books, Qurtubi citing 87:19; Ibn Ashur calls them written revelation named also in 87:18–19; Tabatabai says their plural reflects many parts. Abu Dharr's report, as given by Ibn Ashur and Bursevi, specifies ten written pages or scrolls. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو ذر); TABATABAI; BURSEVI(أبو ذر الغفاري); BURSEVI-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 53:36/q12/p2 Abraham had no formal revealed book; the attribution can denote later texts preserving his orally transmitted teaching. — Bursevi contrasts Moses' truly revealed book with Abraham. Islahi says Abraham's teaching reached both branches orally, was better preserved among Israelites through prophets, became blurred among illiterate Ishmaelites, and was later included and partly altered in the Torah; he says attribution remains sound even if later exegesis appears in the texts. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:36/q13 How many scrolls does Abu Dharr's report assign to Abraham?  [صُحُفِ · reports]
- 53:36/q13/p1 Ten written scrolls or pages were given to Abraham. — Ibn Ashur reports Abu Dharr asking the Prophet about revealed books and receiving the number ten for Abraham. Bursevi's Abu Dharr report gives 104 books overall: ten pages for Adam, fifty for Seth, thirty for Idris, ten for Abraham, then the Torah, Gospel, Psalms, and Quran. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو ذر); BURSEVI(أبو ذر الغفاري)]

## 53:36/q14 What did Abraham fulfill in the following description?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The reports variously specify comprehensive obedience, particular acts of worship, or the rule of individual liability as the fulfillment.
- 53:36/q14/p1 He fully discharged God's commands, trials, vows, and duty of servitude. — Zajjaj lists his resolve to sacrifice his son, endurance of his people's hostility and the fire, and circumcision. Mawrdi reports Ibn Abbas on obedience, Hasan on every command and vow, and trials of sacrifice, fire, and being called a liar. Ibn Kathir reports Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-Thawri that he fulfilled every command. Tabrisi glosses waffa as completing his commands; Tabatabai says he perfectly discharged servitude and cites the completed trials of 2:124. Thalabi includes conveying God's message and the rule against another's burden. Qushayri specifies that Abraham fulfilled what was required of him concerning himself, his wealth, and his child. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير والثوري); TABRISI; TABATABAI; QUSHAYRI]
- 53:36/q14/p2 He fulfilled each day's worship with four midmorning rakas. — Mawrdi reports this from the Prophet through Abu Umama. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النبي)]
- 53:36/q14/p3 He fulfilled morning and evening glorification. — Mawrdi reports through Anas and his father that Abraham recited the glorification verse at both times, citing 30:17. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النبي)]
- 53:36/q14/p4 He established the principle that no one bears a relative's offense. — Mawrdi reports al-Hudhayl that Abraham opposed the earlier practice of charging a person with a child's or parent's offense, connecting this to 53:38; Thalabi includes this principle in his mission. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الهذيل); THALABI]

## 53:36/q15 What changed in liability for relatives' offenses around Abraham?  [* · reports]
- 53:36/q15/p1 Abraham rejected an earlier practice of charging people for relatives' offenses, and individual liability prevailed afterward. — Mawrdi reports al-Hudhayl on liability for a child's or parent's offense; Qurtubi reports al-Hudhayl ibn Shurahbil that this occurred between Noah and Abraham; Elmalili reports Abu Hayyan that before Abraham people were charged for relatives' or masters' crimes and that from Abraham's law through Moses' they were not. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الهذيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الهذيل ابن شرحبيل); ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân)]

## 53:36/q16 How does the scrolls passage connect to its surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize either continuity with the rebuke and recompense in 53:30–33 or the section's broader movement into scriptural truths and commentary on Quraysh.
- 53:36/q16/p1 It continues the theme of recompense and redirects the person turning away to prior teaching. — Razi connects 53:30–31 with the scrolls and 53:38 because both explain recompense; he proposes a shift from idolaters to a scriptured person who stopped giving God's rights when Muhammad came (53:33, 53:38). Ibn Ashur calls the second question another rebuke, and Kuran Yolu says it follows the earlier criticism. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:36/q16/p2 It marks a turn from reflection on creation to enduring scriptural truths and their application to Quraysh. — Study Quran describes the third section moving from creation to truths in Moses' and Abraham's scriptures. Islahi says the passage extends the original scriptural statement and becomes commentary on Quraysh from verse 40 onward. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:36/q17 What occasion is reported for the passage's revelation?  [* · reports]
- 53:36/q17/p1 It followed an offer of transport to a man who could not obtain a mount from the Prophet and wanted to catch the army. — Durr reports this account from Darraj Abu al-Samh. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(دراج أبو السمح)]

## 53:36/q18 What distinction is drawn between 'the right' and 'any right' in nearby wording?  [* · rhetoric]
- 53:36/q18/p1 The definite phrase identifies a particular justification for killing; the indefinite denies any justification and stresses aggression. — Samarrai gives this contrast for the nearby wording. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 53:36/q19 What distinction is reported between al-nabiyyin and al-anbiya'?  [* · meaning]
- 53:36/q19/p1 Al-nabiyyin describes those being informed, whereas al-anbiya' names prophets after becoming prophets. — Samarrai reports al-Kubaisi's distinction and adds 'God knows best.' [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(الدكتور أحمد الكبيسي)]
