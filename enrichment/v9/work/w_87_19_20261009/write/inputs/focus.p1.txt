# 87:19/q07 Is the name Ibrāhīm foreign or Arabic? [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the variant forms reflect adaptation of a foreign name or an Arabic root.
- 87:19/q07/p1 It is a foreign name adapted in Arabic.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī says the reading variants reflect Arabs' adaptation of foreign names and calls this the sounder view.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+
  notes: ALUSI:87:19/r15 ALUSI-FULL:v30p111#3/r14
- 87:19/q07/p2 It is Arabic, from barhama, intense looking.
  reasons: Al-Kirmānī's Wonders offers this derivation; al-Ālūsī reports it while preferring foreign origin.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI-FULL(الكرماني); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p111#3/r15 | against: ALUSI:87:19/r15 ALUSI-FULL:v30p111#3/r14
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; ALUSI-FULL = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270

# 87:19/q08 Why is Abraham named before Moses? [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Chronology and merit, or the relative prominence of admonition and law in the two scriptures.
- 87:19/q08/p1 Their historical and revelatory order, also precedence in merit, determines the sequence.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī gives chronology and merit; al-Itqān and al-Burhān cite this pair as an example of chronological ordering that may combine with honor.
  holders (4 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN
  notes: BIQAI:87:19/r3 BIQAI-FULL:v8p402/r4 BURHAN:v3p239#4/r5 ITQAN:v3p38/r1
- 87:19/q08/p2 Abraham's scrolls suit admonition more closely; Moses' law-focused book therefore comes last.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī invokes Abū Dharr's hadith without grading it; he says Moses' book has fewer admonitions but forceful warnings, including curses against Torah violators and foretelling rejection of its good news about Muhammad.
  holders (4 notes): BIQAI(أبو ذر); BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:19/r1 BIQAI:87:19/r2 BIQAI-FULL:v8p402/r3 BIQAI-FULL:v8p402/r4
sources: BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BIQAI-FULL = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; ITQAN = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911

# 87:19/q09 How many revealed books or scrolls were there, and how were they apportioned? [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Whether the ten additional sheets belong to Adam or Moses and whether Abraham receives ten or twenty.
- 87:19/q09/p1 A report counts 104: ten to Adam, fifty to Seth, thirty to Idrīs, ten to Abraham, plus four named books.
  reasons: Abū Dharr's account names Torah, Gospel, Psalms and Furqān/Qur'an; al-Thaʿlabī adds that Idrīs was first to write with a pen. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes that another transmission instead gives Moses ten and omits Adam. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Abraham's ten without grading the transmission.
