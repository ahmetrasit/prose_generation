# 87:19/q17 How does the ending connect to the sūra's opening and the next sūra? [* · links]
turns on: These describe different, compatible connections made by the closing words.
- 87:19/q17/p1 The ending returns to opening glorification through purification, showing guidance and purpose.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī joins 87:1 and 87:14: glorifying God and cleansing the soul are the sūra's central aim; guidance proves creation was not purposeless. He defines tazkiya as removal of impurities, transcendence, and acquiring divine qualities as possible. Qutb describes the cosmic hymn and the Prophet's promised preservation and ease; Study Quran traces the movement from glorification to contrasting hearers and earlier revelation.
  holders (6 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN
  notes: BIQAI:87:19/r4 BIQAI:87:19/r5 BIQAI:87:19/r6 BIQAI-FULL:v8p402/r5 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r3 STUDYQURAN:87:intro/r7
- 87:19/q17/p2 Sūrat al-Ghāshiya explains this ending by affirming and depicting the afterlife.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī points to 88:1, the fates of pious and wicked, and signs of God's power.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI-FULL:v8p404#s088/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p404/r1
- 87:19/q17/p3 Repetition of ṣuḥuf joins verses 18 and 19 by concatenation.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum compares early Qur'anic and pre-Islamic poetic chains that stretch syntax over short verses, citing 96:1-2,4-5,15-16; 106:1-2; 26:132-33; 40:36-37.

- 87:19/q18/p1 Q 53:36-42 also names the scrolls and develops Abraham's fulfillment, personal responsibility, striving, recompense and return to God.
  reasons: Ibn Kathīr cites 53:36-42 and Ibn ʿAbbās' link from 53:37 to the rule that no bearer bears another's burden. # 87:19/q20 How was this sūra recited in prayer and with what response? [* · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted occasions and responses associated with recitation.
- 87:19/q20/p1 The Prophet recited al-Aʿlā with other sūras in voluntary, Eid and Friday prayers.
  reasons: ʿĀʾisha reports al-Aʿlā and al-Kāfirūn before witr, followed by al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq and al-Nās in witr. Qutb and Study Quran mention Eid and Friday use with al-Ghāshiya, sometimes on the same day; Study Quran also records a recommendation for evening prayer. A report from al-Nuʿmān concerns al-Ghāshiya on Friday with another sūra.
  holders (4 notes): BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); ALUSI-FULL

# 87:19/q23 Can revealed wording disappear through abrogation? [* · sciences]
- 87:19/q23/p1 God can cause recitation and script to be forgotten and discontinued, as happened to earlier scriptures; Qur'anic abrogation ended with the Prophet's death.
  reasons: Abū Bakr al-Rāzī argues that God orders people to stop reciting and writing the text, so it fades; al-Burhān and al-Itqān preserve his argument.
  holders (2 notes): BURHAN(أبو بكر الرازي); ITQAN(أبو بكر الرازي)
  notes: BURHAN:v2p38/r13 ITQAN:v3p69/r2
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; ITQAN = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911
