# 87:19/q01 What does ṣuḥuf mean here? [صُحُفِ · meaning]
- 87:19/q01/p1 Written sheets or scrolls, hence revealed books or scriptures.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and Kuran Yolu derive the plural from ṣaḥīfa; al-Bursawī distinguishes a single written sheet from a muṣḥaf, a collected set. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ṣuḥuf an irregular but more eloquent plural. Asad treats it as synonymous with kitāb and translates revelations. Corpus Coranicum suggests an Old South Arabian loan and observes early Qur'anic use for revealed texts or a heavenly archetype (81:10; 80:13-14; 74:52; 53:36-37), distinct from profane poetry; later 20:133 and 98:2 echo early passages, while kitāb and zubur become usual terms (78:29; 84:7,10; 26:196).

# 87:19/q02 How is ṣuḥuf pronounced? [صُحُفِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the ḥāʾ takes ḍamma or sukūn; al-Ālūsī identifies the latter as Tamīm usage.
- 87:19/q02/p1 With ḍamma on the ḥāʾ, the majority reading.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies this as the majority pronunciation.
  holders (1 notes): IBNATIYYA
  notes: IBNATIYYA:87:19/r1
- 87:19/q02/p2 With a silent ḥāʾ.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes it through Hārūn to Abū ʿAmr and also to al-Aʿmash; al-Ālūsī adds ʿIṣmah's transmission from Abū ʿAmr and calls it a Tamīm dialect.
  holders (2 notes): IBNATIYYA; ALUSI(الأعمش وهرون وعصمة عن أبي عمرو)
  notes: IBNATIYYA:87:19/r1 ALUSI:87:18/r7
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; IBNATIYYA = Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542

# 87:19/q03 Which scriptures are named by the scrolls of Abraham and Moses? [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the genitive marks recipients or subjects, whether Moses' scrolls include the Torah, and whether Abraham had a distinct written revelation.
- 87:19/q03/p1 Revelations given to Abraham and Moses, with Moses' scripture understood as the Torah.
  reasons: Muqātil says Abraham's scrolls have since been taken away. Al-Wāḥidī reports both a pre-Torah Mosaic scroll view and a Torah reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls Moses' many scrolls the Torah's books, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies al-Ṣādiq's tablets with the Torah, citing 7:145,150,154. Al-Qurṭubī says the meaning, rather than the exact wording, occurs in them. References to 53:36 appear in al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī.

- 87:19/q03/p3 The names broadly evoke biblical scripture or tradition, rather than particular Abrahamic and Mosaic books.
  reasons: Ahrens calls this a rough designation for the Old Testament or whole Bible, arguing that 53:36 onward paraphrases biblical material. # 87:19/q04 How does the phrase relate grammatically to the earlier scrolls? [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the named pair exhausts the preceding reference or illustrates the wider set of revealed scriptures.
- 87:19/q04/p1 It is an apposition specifying the earlier scrolls of 87:18.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās calls ṣuḥuf an appositional substitute. Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe specification after the general phrase; al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī say initial vagueness magnifies the named scriptures.

# 87:19/q05 What does calling these scrolls earlier mean? [صُحُفِ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ · interpretation]
- 87:19/q05/p1 Earlier than the Qur'an, not necessarily the first scriptures ever revealed.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr points to reports of scrolls given to messengers before Abraham and cites 53:50,56; a Bukhārī report preserves the saying about shame from earliest prophecy.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:19/r1 IBNASHUR:87:19/r2
sources: IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

# 87:19/q06 How is Abraham's name read? [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · readings]
turns on: The presence of alif and yāʾ and the vowel on hāʾ; witnesses give differing details for some transmitted forms.
- 87:19/q06/p1 Ibrāhīm, with alif, yāʾ and kasra on hāʾ, is the common reading.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call it the majority form; Corpus Coranicum prefers it on Horovitz's argument that it parallels Ismāʿīl and occurs in Umayya b. Abī ṣ-Ṣalt, despite readings closer to biblical forms.
  holders (3 notes): ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; CORPUSCORANICUM+

  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions omission; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report fatḥa and kasra variants; al-Ālūsī reports both and the full account also
