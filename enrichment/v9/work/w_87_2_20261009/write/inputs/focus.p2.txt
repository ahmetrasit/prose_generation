# 87:2/q09 How does this creation clause bear on God's exaltedness? [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · theology]
turns on: How the surrounding acts establish divine worthiness of praise and what the superlative language does or does not imply.
- 87:2/q09/p1 It shows exalted power and lordship through creation and proportioning, in a way befitting God's majesty.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī rejects a spatial reading because distance does not establish worthiness of praise, while these acts display power. Al-Qurṭubī's teacher says the clauses explain God's exaltedness above creatures.
  holders (2 notes): QURTUBI(بعض أشياخي); RAZI-FULL+
  notes: RAZI-FULL:v31p125/r4 QURTUBI:87:3/r17
- 87:2/q09/p2 The descriptions 'Great' and 'Highest' do not imply multiple gods or a comparison with another lord.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī rejects that objection because God's oneness is already established and no other lord is mentioned.
  holders (1 notes): RAZI-FULL
  notes: RAZI-FULL:v31p125#2/r1
sources: QURTUBI = al-Qurṭubī, d. 671; RAZI-FULL = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606

# 87:2/q10 What inward significance is given to creation and fashioning? [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · inward]
turns on: Whether fashioning's spiritual consequence is distinctive guidance, readiness for the perfect spirit, or receptivity to an allotted effusion.
- 87:2/q10/p1 Humans receive a special gift of guidance; pride belongs in guidance and piety rather than created form.
  reasons: Al-Sulamī cites 49:13; Bursawī reports a related view that creatures share basic creation but differ through special guidance.
  holders (2 notes): SULAMI; BURSEVI(بعضهم)
  notes: SULAMI:v2p389#2/r1 BURSEVI:v10p313/r6
- 87:2/q10/p2 The outward human constitution is proportioned to receive the most complete spirit and every perfection.
  reasons: Al-Qāshānī speaks of a particular temperament; Bursawī transmits the same reading as the body receiving the perfect spirit.
  holders (2 notes): QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)
  notes: QASHANI:87:1-4/r2 BURSEVI:v10p313/r4
- 87:2/q10/p3 Each being is formed to receive its allotted divine effusion according to innate capacity.
  reasons: Bursawī attributes this interpretation to the Najmiyya.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI(النجمي)
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p313/r5
sources: BURSEVI = İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127; QASHANI = ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736; SULAMI = al-Sulamī, d. 412

# 87:2/q11 How do creation and fashioning support the command to glorify God? [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · links]
turns on: Whether the clause's immediate lesson is the observable proof warranting glorification or the gradual divine ordering that calls for patience.
- 87:2/q11/p1 They answer why the Lord is known and worthy of glorification by displaying observable divine power, wisdom, and guidance.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī calls creation and guidance a central prophetic proof: Abraham says his Creator guides him (26:78), Moses describes the giver of each thing's form and guidance (20:49–50), and the opening revelation joins creation and teaching (96:1–4). He says their abundant marvels are directly observable. Jishumī says the sūra answers the preceding warning with God's attributes and power. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn al-Zubayr connect the preceding servants' or unbelievers' plots with the command to glorify and the demonstration of power and wisdom. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the first four verses prove absolute lordship; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they indicate oneness through human creation and sustenance.
  holders (16 notes): JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI(الأستاذ أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7372/r1 KASHSHAF:87:2/r1 KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p739/r4 IBNATIYYA:87:2/r1 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p468#3/r3 RAZI:87:2/r1 RAZI:87:2/r2 RAZI:87:2/r3 RAZI:87:2/r4 RAZI:87:2/r5 RAZI-FULL:v31p125#2/r6 RAZI-FULL:v31p125#2/r7 BIQAI:87:2/r4 BIQAI-FULL:v8p395/r1 TABATABAI:87:1-19/r10 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p272/r2
- 87:2/q11/p2 The ordered acts explain the gradual maturing of created physical and intellectual capacities and counsel patient obedience.
  reasons: Iṣlāḥī says verses 1–5 address the Prophet's preaching fears; divine plans unfold by wisdom, so delays are wise and hardship can benefit people in both lives.

# 87:2/q12 How does this clause relate to neighboring sūras and later verses of this sūra? [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace different links: the preceding sūra's warning and resurrection argument, the Prophet's later promises in this sūra, and the sūra's broader themes.

  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī cites 86:5 and 86:8–9; Ibn al-Zubayr also ties the plot at 86:15 to the command at 87:1 and creation and guidance at 87:2–3 as evidence against slander. # 87:2/q14 What variant wording of the glorification including this verse is reported? [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · readings]
- 87:2/q14/p1 The wording 'Glory be to my Lord, the Most High, who created and fashioned' is read as the commanded glorification.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī reports this as Ubayy ibn Kaʿb's reading. Al-Rāzī reports ʿAlī and Ibn ʿUmar reading the command as 'Glory be to my Lord, the Most High' followed by the relative clause; he says it supplies the words of glorification.

  notes: TAB:87:1/r3 RAZI:87:1/r29
sources: RAZI = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606; TAB = al-Ṭabarī, d. 310
