# 87:2/q01 How does 'who created' attach grammatically to the preceding words? [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause modifies Lord or name despite the intervening adjective and the problem of assigning creation to a name, or instead begins an implicit predication or praise.
- 87:2/q01/p1 It qualifies 'Lord' or 'Most High' as another adjective.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās says both adjectives can share one referent and compares the idiom yā Yazīd al-karīm dhū al-jumma. Abū Ḥayyān reports the possible attachment to rabb, while al-Ālūsī objects that an adjective of another noun intervenes.
  holders (5 notes): NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN(قالوا); ALUSI; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-
  notes: NAHHAS:v5p203/r11 NAHHAS:v5p203/r12 ABUHAYYAN:87:1/r10 ALUSI:87:1/r36 ALUSI:87:2/r23 | against: ALUSI:87:1/r38 ALUSI:87:2/r25 ALUSI-FULL:v30p103#2/r1
- 87:2/q01/p2 It qualifies 'name'; creation is predicated of the name through its named referent or creative attribute.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports the grammatical possibility if al-aʿlā qualifies ism. Al-Ālūsī finds a name's creating implausible and questions the proposed metonymy. Elmalılı says creation may aptly be assigned to the Creator-name as the effect of its attribute of bringing into being, citing Sunni and Sufi formulations.

- 87:2/q01/p3 It stands independently as the predicate of an omitted subject or as an accusative of praise.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says this allows al-aʿlā to qualify ism. Al-Ālūsī prefers this solution to the intervening-adjective and name-as-creator difficulties; Elmalılı also prefers treating the clause independently.

# 87:2/q02 What does 'created' mean here? [خَلَقَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes origination, an initial form, or the assembly of parts before their proportioning.
- 87:2/q02/p1 God brought things into existence from nonexistence.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says God can bring about whatever He wills without difficulty. Elmalılı says the Creator, as such, transcends creatures' origination, possibility, and need for a cause.
  holders (3 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI
  notes: BIQAI:87:2/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p393#2/r11 ELMALILI:87:2/r1
- 87:2/q02/p2 Creation can mean making an initial sketch or form that is subsequently perfected.
  reasons: Iṣlāḥī cites the bird example at 3:49 for the sense of making an effigy; for a human, the initial sketch passes through stages before completion.
  holders (2 notes): ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p273/r6 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p274/r1
- 87:2/q02/p3 Creating gathers the parts of a composed creature.
  reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes gathering parts in creation from placing them proportionately through fashioning.
  holders (1 notes): TABATABAI
  notes: TABATABAI:87:1-19/r9
sources: BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BIQAI-FULL = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; ELMALILI = Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361; ISLAHI-TADABBUR = Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997); TABATABAI = Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402

# 87:2/q03 Whom or what does God create and fashion, and in what way? [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The unstated object of 'created' may be every creature, living beings, humanity, Adam, or composed beings; the accounts also differ over whether fashioning concerns form, useful capacities, equal rulings, understanding, worship, or stages of gestation. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the usual rule favoring a general omitted object; al-Ālūsī rejects unsupported restrictions.
- 87:2/q03/p1 God creates all things and fashions them in sound, balanced, precise order without flaw.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī glosses taswiyah as adjustment; al-Naḥḥās adds that the results are good. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, and Abū Ḥayyān see harmony as evidence of a knowing, wise maker; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says it displays power and oneness. Al-Rāzī attributes the order to God's knowledge and power. Al-Ālūsī says each essence and attribute is formed as wisdom requires, rejects narrower objects as unsupported, and notes that the omitted object gives general scope. Al-Qurṭubī recalls his explanation at 82:7; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites precise fingertips and 91:7 and 75:38. Q 67:3–4 is cited by Study Quran for the absence of flaw. Al-Ṭabrisī says creatures are alike in sound construction, and another account says God forms things according to His will and wisdom as testimony to His oneness; al-Biqāʿī says even opposed elements are harmonized. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted object is general by the usual rule when context does not specify it, adducing the majority, ʿAṭāʾ, and 20:50.
