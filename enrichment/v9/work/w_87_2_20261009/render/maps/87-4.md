# 87:4: 13 questions

## 87:4/q01 What does God's bringing out the pasture mean?  [أَخْرَجَ · interpretation]
- 87:4/q01/p1 God causes vegetation to emerge and grow from the earth. — Al-Kashshāf explicitly glosses akhraja as causing growth; Ibn ʿĀshūr says bringing it out of the earth is making it grow. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Jishumī locate its emergence in the earth. Al-Biqāʿī adds rain from clouds and says the growth occurs initially and again. [7 notes: KASHSHAF; IBNASHUR; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:4/q02 What does al-marʿā denote?  [ٱلْمَرْعَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term covers all earth-grown plants, specifically grazed growth, a reported human referent, or an inward image.
- 87:4/q02/p1 Vegetation broadly, including plants beyond livestock fodder. — Abū Razīn and Ibrāhīm gloss it simply as vegetation. Al-Thaʿlabī describes green, yellow, red, and white plants; Qatāda also lists yellow, red, and white sprouts. Al-Māwardī cites a verse of Zafar ibn al-Ḥārith about pasture growing over ruins; his fuller text gives the verse's contrast with lasting human grief. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says plants sustain livestock and benefit people directly. Al-Rāzī includes fruits, crops, and grasses; Ibn Kathīr says every kind of plant and crop. Elmalılı includes grasslands, trees, and fruits for human and animal use and cites 79:31–33. Quṭb expressly extends the term beyond livestock pasture to provisions for walking, hidden, and flying creatures. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the whole plant kingdom, including trees, is recalled through a useful example. Study Quran likewise says all vegetation. [22 notes: TAB(أبو رزين,قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو رزين,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(إبراهيم); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:4/q02/p2 The growth or fodder grazed by livestock, often fresh grass or green forage. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies varied earth-grown plants and grasses serving as livestock feed. Al-Māwardī explains the name through grazing and cites Zafar's verse. Al-Baghawī gives green, yellow, red, and white grasses; Ibn ʿAbbās calls it green herbage, as reported by al-Rāzī and al-Būrsawī. Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī stress tender, fresh growth; al-Ālūsī adds moist and verdant. Al-Biqāʿī says rain clouds produce grass and other fodder. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies grazed vegetation. Al-Rāzī's fuller note juxtaposes Ibn ʿAbbās's green fodder with the author's broader definition. [23 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; MAWARDI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI]
- 87:4/q02/p3 Girls or daughters. — Ibrāhīm's gloss is transmitted by Ibn Abī Ḥātim. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(إبراهيم)]
- 87:4/q02/p4 Inwardly, worldly adornment and sustenance on which the animal soul and its serving powers graze. — Al-Qāshānī includes worldly benefits, food, and drink in this figurative pasture. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:4/q03 How is the noun al-marʿā formed and used?  [ٱلْمَرْعَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:4/q03/p1 It may be a noun of grazing place or a verbal noun used for what is grazed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both nominal possibilities while identifying the referent as vegetation grazed by livestock. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 87:4/q03/p2 Its root also carries the senses of guarding and tending. — Al-Jishumī notes these related root senses alongside the pasture gloss. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 87:4/q04 How is al-marʿā connected grammatically?  [ٱلْمَرْعَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:4/q04/p1 It is coordinated in the genitive. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this case analysis and glosses the noun as what grazing animals eat. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 87:4/q05 Why does the verse name pasture specifically?  [ٱلْمَرْعَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:4/q05/p1 It evokes the benefit livestock provide people and preserves the verse ending while recalling the wider plant kingdom. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says forage is one useful kind of the whole plant kingdom, including trees; naming it rather than plants in general brings livestock's human benefit to mind and fits the verse ending. [2 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 87:4/q06 How does the pasture's growth relate to its later dark state?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The force and attachment of aḥwā, the meaning of ghuthāʾ, and whether the sequence ends in decay or lush growth; Ibn Jarīr appeals to transmitted explanations, whereas Iṣlāḥī appeals to usage and poetry.
- 87:4/q06/p1 God brings forth living pasture and subsequently turns it into dark, dry stubble or refuse. — Muqātil specifies winter herbage drying after fresh green growth. Al-Thaʿlabī says its varied colors become dry and brittle and reads aḥwā as black from age and drying. Al-Burhān notes intervening stages between emergence and dark stubble. Al-Būrsawī transmits al-Jawharī's definitions of ghuthāʾ as flood-borne scraps and qumāsh as things collected from here and there. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls the change an instance of divine governance. Corpus Coranicum treats the cycle as recurring; Kuran Yolu also offers living things receiving life and dying as a metaphor. Study Quran says blackened stubble illustrates creation and destruction. [8 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); THALABI; BURHAN; BURSEVI(الجوهري); TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 87:4/q06/p2 Aḥwā describes pasture as it emerges dark green; it is then made dry debris, with the apparent word order postponed. — Al-Zajjāj construes aḥwā as a circumstantial accusative of pasture and likens the later brittle stubble to debris atop floodwater. Ibn Kathīr reports Arab-language scholars who move the dark-green description before the making of debris. Ibn Jarīr objects that this conflicts with transmitted exegetical explanations. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKATHIR(بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير)-]
- 87:4/q06/p3 The pasture is made luxuriant and blackish green; ghuthāʾ and aḥwā describe fertile growth rather than decay. — Iṣlāḥī, following Imām al-Farāhī, cites Qaṭṭāmī's valley couplet on dense, layered undergrowth to support ghuthāʾ as luxuriant vegetation. He says aḥwā is the reddish or greenish blackness of freshness and fertility and qualifies ghuthāʾ; he explicitly rejects decay-blackness and waste readings. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 87:4/q07 What do the perfect verbs imply about the time of this act?  [أَخْرَجَ · grammar]
- 87:4/q07/p1 They present an ongoing, recurring act of creation and sustenance rather than an inherently past event. — Corpus Coranicum calls the perfects aspectual and points to the recurring growth and decay in verses 4–5. It compares 53:43–49, 96:1–5, and 95:4–5. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:4/q08 What does the producing of pasture demonstrate?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes draw different lessons from the same growth and change: divine exclusivity, resurrection, governance over life, or gradual nurturing.
- 87:4/q08/p1 God alone can produce it, whereas the idols cannot. — Al-Rāzī uses the production of grass to contrast God's power with the worshipped idols; his fuller account says God alone brings forth pasture. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:4/q08/p2 The yearly restoration of plants from soil demonstrates God's power to resurrect the dead. — Al-Biqāʿī calls vegetation the clearest horizon sign of resurrection: pasture returns after breaking down into soil, and gathering the dead is no harder for God than restoring plants. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:4/q08/p3 Its growth and change show divine governance and power over living creation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites growth and the transition to dark refuse as divine governance. Study Quran speaks of God's power over creation and destruction of living things; Kuran Yolu offers a metaphor of life followed by death. [3 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:4/q08/p4 The successive stages display nurturing that brings creation to completion little by little. — Al-Ālūsī reads the successive descriptions as gradual development. Iṣlāḥī says verses 1–5 show the gradual maturation of creation and of human physical and intellectual capacities. [2 notes: ALUSI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:4/q09 How does the verse connect with the preceding descriptions of God?  [* · links]
turns on: Each source selects a different link in the surrounding sequence: beneficiaries, kinds of signs, scale of imagery, or relative-clause structure.
- 87:4/q09/p1 After blessings particular to humans, it mentions a blessing for other creatures. — Al-Rāzī gives this shift in both his shorter and fuller accounts. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:4/q09/p2 It moves from signs within the self to a visible sign across the horizons. — Al-Biqāʿī identifies vegetation as the strongest horizon sign of resurrection after signs within the self. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:4/q09/p3 It completes a survey of vast existence and glorification with an evocative plant image. — Quṭb describes this movement from cosmic scope to plant life. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:4/q09/p4 The repeated relative pronoun highlights each divine attribute separately. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the repetition before measuring and producing pasture singles out each attribute, while fa-hadā marks guidance as the effect of measure. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:4/q10 How is this wording linked to Q 79:31?  [* · links]
- 87:4/q10/p1 Both passages speak of God bringing out pasture; the other passage also names water and human and animal provisions. — Corpus Coranicum observes the verbal parallel with 79:31, which brings out water and pasture. Elmalılı cites 79:31–33 for plants, trees, and fruits used by humans and animals. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ELMALILI]

## 87:4/q11 How does the wider sūra frame the verse?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize different sūra-wide frames: cosmic praise, doctrinal foundations, or the change in addressee and reassurance.
- 87:4/q11/p1 The whole cosmos becomes a sanctuary of praise, and the Prophet receives assurance of preservation and ease. — Quṭb describes the cosmic glorification and good news for the Prophet's mission and affairs. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:4/q11/p2 Its foundations are God's oneness as creator, revelation, and afterlife recompense, rooted in earlier scripture. — Quṭb adds the ease of message, messenger, and community and points to 87:18–19. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:4/q11/p3 The sūra shifts from warning persistent deniers to reassuring the Prophet about preaching. — Iṣlāḥī says the previous sūra urges patience because the deniers' respite is temporary and they cannot escape God. Here and for at least ten following sūras, the primary address turns from stubborn Quraysh to the Prophet and dispels his fears; verses 1–5 show gradual maturation. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:4/q12 Where was this sūra revealed and how many verses does it have?  [* · sciences]
- 87:4/q12/p1 It is Meccan and has nineteen verses. — Quṭb states both facts. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:4/q13 What is reported about the Prophet's attachment to and recitation of this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 87:4/q13/p1 He loved it and recited it with al-Ghāshiyah at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes both on one day. — Quṭb cites ʿAlī for the Prophet's love of the sūra and Muslim for the prayer recitations, linking al-Ghāshiyah to 88:1. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
