# 87:3: 18 questions

## 87:3/q01 How is قَدَّرَ read?  [قَدَّرَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the light form merely shares the doubled form's sense or also conveys ability or ownership.
- 87:3/q01/p1 The majority double the dāl, reading qaddara. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī prefer this by the readers' agreement or consensus. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows decree or measured proportion; al-Qurṭubī relates it to estimation (25:2). Al-Samīn and Rāzī compare the forms in 77:23. [25 notes: FARRA; IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي); THALABI(علي); JISHUMI(الكسائي وعلي بن أبي طالب وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي); BAGHAWI(الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA(جمهور القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الكسائي); RAZI(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(علي والسلمي والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(علي والسلمي والكسائي); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; IBNASHUR(الجمهور والكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); FARRA+; TAB-FULL+; TAB(الكسائي)+]
- 87:3/q01/p2 Al-Kisāʾī reads the dāl lightly, qadara; reports also attribute it to ʿAlī and al-Sulamī. — Al-Kisāʾī is called the sole reader among the canonical readers; al-Farrāʾ attributes al-Sulamī's form to ʿAlī. Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Wāḥidī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the two forms mean the same. Jishumī allows a distinction between power and apportionment; al-Qaffāl reads the light form as ownership and governance; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention power or measure; Qirāʾāt-ER lists measuring, determining, estimating, or ability; Study Quran says it may affirm God's direct power over creation. ## 87:3/q02 What does قَدَّرَ mean?  [قَدَّرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the determination names the universal assignment of measure or a particular allotted feature; the light reading also permits power or ownership.
- 87:3/q02/p1 God assigned each created thing its particular measure, properties, capacities, acts, circumstances, and destiny. — Al-Rāzī cites 15:21 and lists essence, size, duration, place, beauty, guidance and misguidance. Elmalılı cites 65:3 and 15:21, stressing divine knowledge and will and the unknown secret of destiny; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes particular existence arising from an undetermined plenitude and cites 15:21, 54:49, 25:2, 57:22, and 64:11. Ibn ʿĀshūr says measure starts with length, capacity, weight, or count and here means ordered organs and powers. Al-Biqāʿī gives hand, leg, ear, and eye examples. Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī include kinds, species, individuals, qualities, acts, and lifespans. [25 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; RAZI(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 87:3/q02/p2 It means forming or shaping a creature, especially in the womb. — Al-Dāmghānī calls it shaping well in the womb; al-Māwardī describes formation there and emergence fully formed. Study Quran reports the human-specific account of forming the first human after breathing in the Spirit and providing nourishment. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:3/q02/p3 It means assigning a particular provision, sustenance, or useful benefit. — ʿAṭāʾ says every animal receives what suits it; al-Wāḥidī's additional report has God determine the offspring He wills. The accounts of livelihood distinguish assigned food from God's placement of useful properties in things. [35 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); THALABI(عطاء,قيل); MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(عطاء وأبو مسلم); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي,عطاء); QURTUBI(عطاء,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 87:3/q02/p4 It means determining males and females and their reproductive course. — Al-Wāḥidī says God determined male and female animal forms; Muqātil and al-Kalbī pair that with the guidance of mating. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats reproductive powers as preserving the species. [10 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; MAWARDI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI(مقاتل والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل والكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:3/q02/p5 It means fixing gestation, including a term of about nine months with possible variation. — Al-Suddī's report in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī specifies nine months, less, or more; other transmitters state only the allotted period until birth. [13 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); THALABI(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ALUSI(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 87:3/q02/p6 It means decreeing felicity and misery, or sins followed by repentance. — Mujāhid and al-Wāsiṭī describe assigned destinies; Tustarī distinguishes God's care for the felicitous from leaving the wretched to themselves. Al-Sulamī separately reports allotted sins followed by repentance. [14 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TUSTARI; SULAMI(الواسطي,بعضهم); THALABI(الواسطي,قيل); MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI(الواسطي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الواسطي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي,مجاهد); QURTUBI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 87:3/q02/p7 It means creating people for recompense. — Al-Māwardī's fifth possibility pairs this creation with guidance to action. [2 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 87:3/q02/p8 Other lexical senses include making or appointing, knowing, and the light form's power or ownership. — Al-Dāmghānī lists making or appointing and knowledge without examples in the excerpt. Al-Qaffāl derives the light reading as owning and governing what God creates; al-Qurṭubī also allows possession, while Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention power. Qirāʾāt-ER gives measuring, determining, estimating, and ability. [12 notes: DAMGHANI; RAZI(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); ALUSI(الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي); QIRAAT-ER; STUDYQURAN]

## 87:3/q03 What is God said to guide creatures toward?  [فَهَدَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase is unrestricted or names a particular kind of guidance, and whether religious guidance is read as its principal sense.
- 87:3/q03/p1 Every creature is guided toward its allotted function, course, or benefit; particular examples do not limit the verse. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, and Elmalılı expressly keep the scope general. Al-Ṭabarī cites lack of evidence for restriction; Elmalılı cites 20:50. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and Bursawī name inclination, inspiration, signs, and revelation. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives speech, craft, grazing, nursing, and bee architecture; al-Biqāʿī says ordainment needs guidance for completeness. Elmalılı includes planetary orbits, attraction, gases, stones, crystals, salt, coal, and cells. Qutb says provision accompanies creatures throughout life; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 15:21, 80:20, and 2:148. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls this creational guidance: God directs each kind and individual toward its created perfection, acts, appointed course, and lifespan, citing 20:50 for God's giving each thing its form and then guiding it. [44 notes: TAB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNATIYYA+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]

These reports describe an outcome, while other Mujāhid reports describe moral alternatives. ## 87:3/q04 Does فَهَدَىٰ also imply 'led astray'?  [فَهَدَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the single named act conventionally carries its opposite by ellipsis.
- 87:3/q04/p1 Yes; 'led astray' is left unspoken because guidance evokes its opposite. — Al-Zajjāj's grammarians cite 14:4; al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī compare 16:81, which names protection from heat without naming cold; al-Nasafī cites 16:93 and 35:8. Al-Farrāʾ is repeatedly named as proponent. [16 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ(بعض النحويين); NAHHAS(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء)-; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)-]
- 87:3/q04/p2 No; the proposed ellipsis is rejected. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects al-Farrāʾ's proposal and prefers the verse's general account of positive guidance. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)+; ALUSI(الفراء)+]

## 87:3/q05 Is God said to compel or command misguidance?  [فَهَدَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether directing to, mentioning, or decreeing error entails compelling or commanding it.
- 87:3/q05/p1 No; God commands and approves obedience while forbidding sin and misguidance. — Qatāda says God neither compels nor commands sin and does not approve it, answering any reading of guidance that would make Him do so. [2 notes: RAZI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 87:3/q05/p2 The verse's guidance can include a path to error or an implied act of leading astray. — Mujāhid's reports say God guides toward good and evil or right and error; al-Farrāʾ proposes the implicit opposite. These sources do not all make the same claim about compulsion. [5 notes: MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; FARRA]

## 87:3/q06 What does the fa in فَهَدَىٰ express?  [فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:3/q06/p1 Guidance follows as the effect or completion of determination. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the fa linking creation and proportioning: each measured creature is directed to its allotted use. Al-Biqāʿī says creation needs proportion for balance, while ordainment needs guidance for completeness. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:3/q07 Who is the unspoken object of فَهَدَىٰ?  [فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether general wording encompasses all created things or only beings capable of apprehending direction.
- 87:3/q07/p1 It is left general, then contextually limited to creatures able to apprehend direction. — Ibn ʿĀshūr includes humans and animals with perception and will but excludes fruiting trees and seed production, which he calls determined rather than guided. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:3/q07/p2 The created object may be humans, humans and animals, or all creation. — Study Quran reports these possibilities for 'created' in the preceding verse; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the corresponding human-specific reading of determination and guidance. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR]

## 87:3/q08 How are divine attributes linked by the conjunctions and relative pronoun?  [وَٱلَّذِى قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:3/q08/p1 The repeated relative pronoun and conjunction emphasize distinct divine predicates. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the repetition amplification. Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū al-Baqāʾ say conjunction makes each adjective independent; al-Burhān compares 68:10 and 57:3 and says conjunction suits attributes that differ widely. Al-Biqāʿī says wāw joins ordainment to proportion to establish both attributes together. [6 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:3/q09 Do the perfect verbs describe only completed past acts?  [قَدَّرَ فَهَدَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:3/q09/p1 No; their perfect form is aspectual, and divine creation is ongoing and sustaining. — Corpus Coranicum points to the repeated growth and decay of pasture in verses 4–5 and compares 53:43–49, 96:1–5, and 95:4–5. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:3/q10 What inward guidance does the verse describe?  [فَهَدَىٰ · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual stage or disposition the guidance addresses.
- 87:3/q10/p1 Heedless people build the world, while worshippers prefer the afterlife. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts the objects of their efforts. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:3/q10/p2 Ascetics recognize the world's passing nature and reject it. — Al-Qushayrī gives this as another degree of guidance. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:3/q10/p3 Scholars contemplate created signs and remain with their inferences. — Al-Qushayrī says they recognize God through examining His signs and creation. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:3/q10/p4 Seekers turn toward the glory of God's attribute and devote themselves to Him. — Al-Qushayrī says they prefer Him and expend their effort seeking Him. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:3/q10/p5 Gnostics enter the sanctity of His description, watching and then witnessing Him. — Al-Qushayrī portrays successive stages of awareness. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:3/q10/p6 Monotheists abandon familiar things and seek God alone in His sovereign sublimity. — Al-Qushayrī names this as a distinct orientation. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:3/q10/p7 Knowers finally rest in incapacity before God beyond proof, explanation, vision, separation, and union. — Al-Qushayrī locates the divine beyond these modes. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 87:3/q10/p8 God plants human perfection within a person and guides its actualization through purification. — Al-Qāshānī says refinement brings the potential perfection forth. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:3/q11 What reports illustrate divine determination and guidance?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported incident is used to illustrate the point.
- 87:3/q11/p1 God decreed all creatures' destinies fifty thousand years before creating heaven and earth. — Ibn ʿAmr reports the Prophet's statement; the report also says the Throne was over water. Bursawī cites its timing. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR(عبد الله بن عمرو); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:3/q11/p2 A very old blind snake found a healing plant and regained sight. — Al-Kashshāf calls it a thousand-year-old snake; one version says fresh fennel, another fresh rue in a distant garden. Al-Qurṭubī repeats the fennel version. The plant names vary in the notes. [3 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI]
- 87:3/q11/p3 Animals show directed instinct in hunting, nest-building, and storing food. — Bursawī names a weasel catching a mouse, spiders and bees making nests, and ants splitting damp seeds and drying them so they do not sprout. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 87:3/q11/p4 A diving bird escaped a crow that kept stealing its fish by dragging the crow underwater. — Bursawī attributes the observation to an unnamed observer. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 87:3/q11/p5 A monkey exposed a seller who diluted milk by throwing the price of the added water into the sea. — Bursawī reports this as a warning against dishonest sale. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:3/q11/p6 A dog repeatedly exposed a well containing its murdered owner, leading to the killer's confession and execution. — Bursawī transmits the story as an instance of animal guidance. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 87:3/q11/p7 A palm tree weakened from attachment to another tree and recovered when bound to one of its branches. — Bursawī presents the account as another instance of directed natural behavior. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 87:3/q12 How does this verse support the call to glorify God and relate to the surrounding sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding passage or theological purpose supplies the connection.
- 87:3/q12/p1 Creation, proportion, determination, and guidance display God's power, wisdom, and worthiness of glorification. — Al-Rāzī says they answer how the Lord is known and why He merits praise; Abraham's words in 26:78, Moses' reply in 20:49–50, and the first revelation in 96:1–4 use the same proof. The wonders are abundant and observable. Al-Qurṭubī's teacher says the clauses show divine exaltedness. Elmalılı says finite creatures imply the Lord's transcendence and grounds for trust amid disbelievers' schemes. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(بعض أشياخي); QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:3/q12/p2 The verse's acts counter the disbelievers' schemes and prepare the Prophet for his mission. — Jishumī connects it with the preceding warning; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn al-Zubayr tie the plot in 86:15 to God's power and wisdom in 87:1–3. Iṣlāḥī says al-Ṭāriq promises only temporary respite and this sūra turns to reassure the Prophet. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the One who shaped him will secure his retention of revelation and ease his law (87:6, 87:8). [8 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI(الأستاذ أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:3/q12/p3 The verse parallels other statements that creation is measured and creatures directed. — Ibn Kathīr cites Moses' 20:50 and the predestination report; Asad links 25:2 and 20:50. Corpus Coranicum links qaddara to 80:19 and hadā to 93:7; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 15:21, 80:20, and 2:148. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 20:50 for God's giving each thing its form and then guiding it, linking it with 87:2–3. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; TABATABAI]
- 87:3/q12/p4 The ordered creation described in the verse bears on the sūra's themes of oneness, revelation, and recompense. — Qutb describes the cosmos as a place of praise, the Prophet's preservation and ease, and the message's roots in earlier scriptures; Study Quran says creation is properly formed without flaw and cites 67:3–4. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]

## 87:3/q13 Does 'Great' alongside 'Highest' imply more than one deity?  [* · theology]
- 87:3/q13/p1 No; God's oneness is established, and the wording does not say He is higher than another lord. — Al-Rāzī rejects the objection to the opening description of God. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 87:3/q14 What reports concern the sūra's recitation and revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which report or practice is in view.
- 87:3/q14/p1 The sūra was revealed after Companions asked what to say in prostration. — Abū Hurayra reports that the Prophet then instructed them to say 'Glory to my Lord, the Most High.' [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 87:3/q14/p2 The Prophet stopped Ḥaḍramī from appending his own line about a pregnant woman to the verses. — The report says he called the recitation sufficient. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(حضرمى بن عامر)]
- 87:3/q14/p3 The Prophet loved the sūra and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayer. — Qutb cites ʿAlī for the Prophet's love and Muslim for the paired recitation, sometimes on the same day. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:3/q15 What are the sūra's place and verse count?  [* · sciences]
- 87:3/q15/p1 It is Meccan and has nineteen verses. — Qutb states this while describing the Prophet's fondness for the sūra. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:3/q16 How is the sūra's rhyme ending pronounced?  [* · readings]
- 87:3/q16/p1 Its rhyme endings from al-Aʿlā through Mūsā are subject to imāla. — Ibn al-Jazarī says their treatment appeared earlier in his imāla chapter. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 87:3/q17 How does varying related Qur'anic expressions convey meaning?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Which purpose of expressive variation is being discussed.
- 87:3/q17/p1 Taṣrīf expresses one meaning through varied, connected meanings and forms, clarifying it from several sides. — Al-Rummānī illustrates derivatives of ملك, from owner to king and dominion, and عرض, from appearance and turning away to poetic meter; the linked senses illuminate a shared core. [4 notes: RUMMANI]
- 87:3/q17/p2 The varied telling of Moses' story sustains eloquence, strengthens admonition, and resolves doubts about the miracle. — Al-Rummānī names al-Aʿrāf, Ṭā Hā, and al-Shuʿarāʾ and gives these three purposes. [4 notes: RUMMANI]
- 87:3/q17/p3 Variation at the highest eloquent level demonstrates the Qur'anic challenge because God can produce its like. — Al-Rummānī contrasts meaningful challenges within possibility with impossible alternatives such as five times five being other than twenty-five or one hundred having a different unique square root. [2 notes: RUMMANI]

## 87:3/q18 How extensive and beneficial is divine guidance?  [* · interpretation]
- 87:3/q18/p1 It extends through religious and worldly human needs and through the beneficial instincts of other creatures. — Al-Zamakhsharī says its range in humans, beasts, birds, and other creatures exceeds description. Al-Naḥḥās reports that God guides creatures to their interests. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says creational guidance reaches each kind and each individual through its acts, appointed course, and lifespan. [4 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NAHHAS(بعضهم); TABATABAI]
