# 4:42: 32 questions

## 4:42/q01 What day does يَوْمَئِذٍ indicate?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ · referent]
- 4:42/q01/p1 The Day of Resurrection when prophetic witnesses are brought. — Al-Ṭabarī and Study Quran connect it to 4:41, when a witness is brought from each community and Muḥammad over his community; al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya locate the wish on that very day. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 4:42/q02 What governs يَوْمَئِذٍ grammatically?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ يَوَدُّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the time phrase attaches to the wish or to the preceding witness clause.
- 4:42/q02/p1 It is an adverb governed by يَوَدُّ. — Al-Naḥḥās also allows yawm to be indeclinable; al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān say it modifies the wish. Al-Ālūsī prefers this and judges the alternative remote. [7 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 4:42/q02/p2 It is governed by the previous verse's شَهِيدًا, while يَوَدُّ describes the day. — Abū al-Baqāʾ posits an omitted fīhi in the adjective and cites 2:48. Al-Samīn sees a clear problem; al-Ālūsī calls it remote. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 4:42/q03 How is the form يَوْمَئِذٍ constructed?  [يَوْمَئِذٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical treatment of yawm and idh, and which omitted earlier clause the tanwīn replaces.
- 4:42/q03/p1 Yawm is annexed to idh, and idh's tanwīn compensates for an omitted clause. — Al-Wāḥidī says idh normally takes a nominal or verbal clause and cites 40:71, 2:30, 2:72, 70:11 and 30:43; al-Quṭāmī's poetry illustrates a nominal clause. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow either both prior witness clauses, the first, or the last; those choices affect whether الرسول is generic or Muḥammad. Al-Ṭabrisī calls the tanwīn a mark of completion. [9 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(القطامي); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:42/q03/p2 Yawm can be built as indeclinable by annexation to indeclinable idh. — Al-Ḥawfī gives the rule that an adverb annexed to an indeclinable may be built; Abū Ḥayyān approves, reasoning that annexation specifies idh like a noun. Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows an adverbial accusative or a built accusative. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 4:42/q03/p3 Idh's kasra merely breaks adjacent consonants, rather than marking genitive case. — Al-Wāḥidī cites a poetic line ending 'and you were then sound' with idh unattached to anything preceding it. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 4:42/q04 What does يَوَدُّ mean here?  [يَوَدُّ · meaning]
- 4:42/q04/p1 It means to wish or desire. — Abū Ḥayyān glosses it with yatamannā; al-Wāḥidī refers to his earlier discussion at 2:96. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 4:42/q05 Who are ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description is read as its outward referent or an inward state.
- 4:42/q05/p1 The disbelievers, especially those who denied God or His oneness. — Al-Ṭabarī says they deny God's oneness; Muslim al-Baṭīn identifies those wishing for leveling as disbelievers. Al-Ālūsī says the relative can focus on Muhammad's deniers or refer to disbelievers generally, especially them. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مسلم البطين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:42/q05/p2 In an inward reading, they are souls veiled from God and truth. — Al-Qāshānī calls disbelief a veil from truth; al-Ālūsī similarly speaks of being veiled from God. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:42/q06 How are كَفَرُوا۟ and عَصَوُا۟ related?  [كَفَرُوا۟ عَصَوُا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the conjunction joins verbs of one relative clause, an elided second relative, or a circumstantial clause.
- 4:42/q06/p1 The two verbs describe one group who both disbelieved and disobeyed. — Al-Rāzī says conjunction implies distinct sins; Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent syntax. Al-Ālūsī says عصوا remains within the relative clause; al-Burṣawī also distinguishes sins beyond disbelief. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 4:42/q06/p2 An omitted second relative pronoun yields two groups: disbelievers and those who disobeyed. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this; al-Ālūsī says it depends on al-Farrāʾ's allowance for eliding a relative pronoun. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); BURSEVI]
- 4:42/q06/p3 عَصَوُا۟ is circumstantial: they disbelieved while having disobeyed. — Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies an understood qad. Al-Samīn rejects his calling it parenthetical, since it belongs within the relative clause's dependencies, analogizing مسرعين in a sentence about those who came hastily. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 4:42/q07 What does their disobedience to the Messenger mean?  [عَصَوُا۟ ٱلرَّسُولَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether عصوا is read as additional legal sins, the Prophet's governing commands, or inward failure to follow him.
- 4:42/q07/p1 It comprises acts of disobedience beyond disbelief. — Al-Rāzī argues that otherwise the conjunction is redundant and infers that disbelievers face Islam's practical obligations and added punishment; al-Ālūsī likewise infers accountability for derivative duties. Al-Burṣawī agrees on distinct sins. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:42/q07/p2 It means defying the Messenger's commands as guardian. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes commands from the Messenger's own governing authority from God's legal rulings. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 4:42/q07/p3 In an inward reading, it is failure to follow him and a veil from religion. — Al-Qāshānī calls it veiling from religion; al-Ālūsī says failure to follow him. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:42/q08 Who does ٱلرَّسُولَ designate?  [ٱلرَّسُولَ · referent]
turns on: Which earlier witness clause supplies the context, and whether the singular title is generic.
- 4:42/q08/p1 Muhammad specifically. — Al-Ṭabarī names Muhammad as witness over his community; al-Samīn says a tanwīn replacing only the preceding وجئنا بك makes this specific. Al-Ālūsī says the designation honors Muhammad. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:42/q08/p2 The class of messengers, including Muhammad. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the singular denotes the class; Abū Ḥayyān permits either reference, and al-Ālūsī says Muhammad is chiefly included. Asad favors every apostle who preached God's message; al-Samīn relates the generic sense to the first witness clause. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 4:42/q09 Why say ٱلرَّسُولَ instead of addressing the Prophet directly?  [ٱلرَّسُولَ · rhetoric]
- 4:42/q09/p1 The title honors his office and heightens the rebuke of its deniers. — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts وعصوك and calls the mission humanity's noblest charge and a source of felicity in both lives. Al-Samīn emphasizes rank and dignity; al-Ālūsī adds condemnation of those who deny him. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:42/q10 How is the wāw in عَصَوُا۟ pronounced?  [عَصَوُا۟ · readings]
turns on: Which vowel resolves adjacent sukūns and whether the original kasra is retained.
- 4:42/q10/p1 With ḍamma. — Al-Zajjāj prefers it to resolve two adjacent sukūns; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī also allow kasra. Abū Ḥayyān attributes ḍamma to the majority; al-Jishumī says the normal kasra is blocked. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ZAJJAJ+]
- 4:42/q10/p2 With kasra. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn attribute it to Abū al-Sammāl and Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar; the latter two say kasra preserves the original liaison vowel. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال ويحيى بن يعمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر وأبو السمال); SAMIN-DURR(يحيى وأبو السمال)]

## 4:42/q11 How is تُسَوَّىٰ read?  [تُسَوَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: Passive leveling versus reflexive leveling, and whether the reflexive tāʾ assimilates or is omitted; al-Ṭabarī also weighs ease of pronunciation and 78:40.
- 4:42/q11/p1 With ḍamma on tāʾ and a light sīn: a passive verb, 'the earth is made level.' — Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿAmr and others are named, although source lists vary; the agent is unexpressed and Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān identify God as agent. Al-Ṭabarī notes all three readings have the dust sense. Qirāʾāt ER calls this earth's passive leveling. [15 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الباقون); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي والباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير، عاصم، أبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم وأبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو وابن كثير وعاصم); NASHR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]
- 4:42/q11/p2 With fatḥa on tāʾ and doubled sīn: assimilated reflexive تتسوى. — Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir are regularly named, with Medinan and Syrian readers or Abū Jaʿfar/Yazīd in some lists. The tāʾ assimilates into sīn, giving the earth active/reflexive agency; al-Rāzī compares 27:47, 10:24 and 6:26. Ibn al-Jawzī reports Abū ʿAlī's explanation that the people become level with earth. [20 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أهل المدينة والشام); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي والباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,نافع، ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL(مدنى وشامى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن عامر); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر ويزيد); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]
- 4:42/q11/p3 With fatḥa on tāʾ and light sīn: a tāʾ is omitted from تتسوى. — Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are commonly named, Khalaf by Ibn al-Jazarī and Ibn ʿĀshūr; some report Kufans except ʿĀṣim. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this lighter reading, avoiding two doubled consonants and fitting 78:40; imāla is praised by Abū ʿAlī and reported by later sources. Al-Thaʿlabī compares 11:105. [18 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أهل الكوفة إلا عاصما); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي والباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي,حمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة، الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة وعلى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة والكسائي); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); TAB-FULL+]

## 4:42/q12 How may the pronoun in بِهِمُ be pronounced?  [بِهِمُ · readings]
turns on: Dialectal pronoun vowels, adjacency to bāʾ, and the liaison vowel at the word boundary.
- 4:42/q12/p1 With ḍamma on both hāʾ and mīm. — Ibn Khālawayh says this restores their original vowels before bāʾ; al-Zajjāj allows ḍamma on mīm. Qirāʾāt ER identifies ḍamma on hāʾ in bihumu with Hijazi and Tamimi dialects. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; QIRAAT-ER]
- 4:42/q12/p2 With kasra on both hāʾ and mīm. — Ibn Khālawayh attributes the vowels to adjacency to bāʾ and the following word boundary; Qirāʾāt ER calls mīm's kasra a liaison vowel before a pause or consonant. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; QIRAAT-ER]
- 4:42/q12/p3 With kasra on hāʾ but original mīm vowel. — Ibn Khālawayh says the hāʾ follows neighboring bāʾ, and the following wāw drops for ease. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 4:42/q13 How does لَوْ function after يَوَدُّ?  [يَوَدُّ لَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether لو is a nominalizing particle like أن or a conditional with understood object and answer.
- 4:42/q13/p1 It nominalizes its clause, which is the object of يَوَدُّ. — Abū Ḥayyān compares أن and says there is no conditional answer. Al-Samīn cites 2:96; al-Ṭāhir ibn ʿĀshūr also compares 2:96. Al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī use this to express the wish for burial or nonresurrection. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,غير مسمى); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:42/q13/p2 It remains conditional, with an omitted object of يَوَدُّ and omitted result. — Abū Ḥayyān supplies 'they would be pleased' as the result. Al-Samīn attributes this to the majority and says the object is earth's leveling; al-Ālūsī says context supplies both omissions. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:42/q14 What is the sense of ٱلْأَرْضُ and leveling?  [ٱلْأَرْضُ تُسَوَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether الأرض denotes ordinary ground or the grave in the image.
- 4:42/q14/p1 Earth is the ground or soil made flat or joined into one level. — Al-Farrāʾ glosses leveling with soil; al-Ṭabrisī compares flattening and joining fingertips at 75:4. Asad gives the literal sense that earth becomes level with them. [3 notes: FARRA; TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 4:42/q14/p2 Earth can mean the grave. — Al-Dāmghānī offers this lexical sense in the wish to be leveled with earth. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 4:42/q15 How does the construction تُسَوَّىٰ بِهِمُ ٱلْأَرْضُ work?  [تُسَوَّىٰ بِهِمُ ٱلْأَرْضُ · grammar]
turns on: The force of بِ, which entity is actually leveled, and whether the active or passive reading supplies the subject.
- 4:42/q15/p1 The earth levels over them, with بِ meaning عَلَى. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes this to al-Ḥasan; Abū ʿUbayda and others apply it to active readings. Al-Samīn applies it to the passive reading too. Study Quran notes the active subject supports swallowing or caving in. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة وجماعة); SAMIN-DURR; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:42/q15/p2 The wording reverses the semantic relation: they become level with earth. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Sībawayh's comparable usage; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn compare expressions where the understood leveling goes in the opposite direction. Ibn al-Jawzī quotes Abū ʿAlī on the expanded construction. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR]
- 4:42/q15/p3 The بِ marks accompaniment: earth is leveled together with their lying bodies. — Al-Burṣawī reports a scholar who proposes this to avoid reversing the construction; he says the resulting sense barely differs. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الأفاضل)]
- 4:42/q15/p4 The passive has God as the unstated agent who levels the earth. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God causes either the covering or dust outcome; Abū Ḥayyān likewise takes God as agent for the two interpretations. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 4:42/q16 What do they wish would happen with the earth?  [لَوْ تُسَوَّىٰ بِهِمُ ٱلْأَرْضُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether leveling means burial, conversion to dust, nonresurrection, animal-like dust, trampling, ransom, or an inward erasure; the readings and بِ are used to support some views.
- 4:42/q16/p1 The earth would split or rise, swallow them, and close level over them. — Muqātil, Mujāz, Qatāda, Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Jurayj give variants of entering, being swallowed or covered; Ibn ʿAbbās adds mountains flattened over them. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers earth rising to hide their bodies, citing a Bedouin satire quoted by al-Mubarrad and the shame of exposure. Ibn Kathīr cites the terrors and 78:40; al-Qurṭubī situates the wish after limbs expose them. [36 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI(قتادة وعبيدة); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة وأبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة، أبو عبيدة، مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة وجماعة,قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 4:42/q16/p2 They would themselves become dust or one substance with earth. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAbbās and many later sources present this sense; 78:40 is repeatedly cited. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the earth wording an indirect allusion to their becoming dust and illustrates indirect attribution with Ziyād al-Aʿjam's line about virtues beneath Ibn al-Ḥashraj's tent. Al-Ṭabarī says God creates them as dust. [18 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير، عاصم، أبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:42/q16/p3 They had never been raised or created and had remained earth beneath level ground. — Al-Naḥḥās says they were made from dust; al-Thaʿlabī attributes the view to Ibn Kaysān. Al-Ṭabrisī connects it to foreknowledge of eternal fire and 78:40; Ibn al-Jawzī names Ibn Kaysān and al-Zajjāj. Some say never resurrected; others extend it to never created. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قيل); THALABI(ابن كيسان); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كيسان، الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(مفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN]
- 4:42/q16/p4 They would share the beasts' fate when God turns animals and birds to dust. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Kalbī describe the beasts' transformation; Abū Hurayra adds livestock and birds at the gathering. Al-Ṭabrisī accepts the account if compensation to animals ends, rejecting eternal compensation, and cites 78:40 through others. [11 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ(أهل التفسير); THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة); RAZI-FULL(مفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI+]
- 4:42/q16/p5 The gathered people would walk over them as over the ground. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies the trampling image; al-Kalbī makes a similar point of dust walked over by the assembled people. [3 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); TABRISI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 4:42/q16/p6 They would exchange all the earth's contents as ransom for themselves. — Abū al-Qāsim al-Ḥusayn invokes the criminal's wish to ransom himself with his sons at 70:11; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn gloss the earth's contents as the ransom. [4 notes: THALABI(أبو القاسم الحسين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:42/q16/p7 In an inward reading, their souls would be erased or made blank of false beliefs and vices. — Al-Qāshānī calls this leveling the earth of preparedness; al-Ālūsī says their souls would be effaced of corrupt beliefs and destructive vices. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:42/q16/p8 They wish for death or cancellation of their existence. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats earth-leveling as a metaphor for death and cites 78:40; al-Biqāʿī says the terrifying scene makes sinners wish for nonexistence. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 4:42/q17 How is وَلَا يَكْتُمُونَ joined to the preceding wish?  [وَلَا يَكْتُمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the wāw coordinates a fresh report, a wished-for object, or a contemporaneous circumstance, and whether لو is nominal or conditional.
- 4:42/q17/p1 It is a separate assertion coordinated with يَوَدُّ or the preceding clauses. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj and later sources say God reports both their wish and nonconcealment. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn distinguish joining يود from joining لو and its consequence, yielding three reported clauses; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an independent sentence with the same subject. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī joins its syntactic position to يود. [19 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(آخرون); ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); THALABI(آخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الفراء، الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الفراء والزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:42/q17/p2 It joins the object of يَوَدُّ: they wish both for leveling and for having concealed nothing. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAbbās and others cite regret over their lie that they were not polytheists. ʿAṭāʾ instead identifies what they wished not to have concealed as Muhammad's status. Al-Ālūsī favors joining the omitted object of يود over attachment to تسوى. Abū Ḥayyān regards this with conditional لو as remote; al-Samīn records the same qualification. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); TABRISI(ابن عباس,عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,طائفة,قيل); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 4:42/q17/p3 It is circumstantial: they wish for leveling while unable to conceal. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Burṣawī describe that state. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow the state to qualify either بهم under تسوى or الذين كفروا under يود, with different requirements for لو; Abū Ḥayyān doubts the latter since concealment occurs at other stations and conditional لو interrupts the relation. Ibn ʿĀshūr places inability to hide during the wish. [10 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 4:42/q17/p4 The clauses concern different places or episodes of Judgment. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a group explaining the wording's apparent contrast by separate settings; Abū Ḥayyān says stations and groups differ. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طائفة)]

## 4:42/q18 What is the حَدِيثًا that they would not conceal?  [حَدِيثًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the hidden report is an afterlife denial, concealed knowledge of Muhammad in worldly life, deeds generally, or inner marks.
- 4:42/q18/p1 Their afterlife denial of polytheism or other deeds. — Most exegetes identify 'We were not polytheists' at 6:23; Ibn ʿAbbās links regret or exposure to concealing shirk. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it is regret for their lie rather than a ransom bargain. Abū Ḥayyān contrasts this afterlife view with ʿAṭāʾ's worldly view. [20 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الجمهور,طائفة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:42/q18/p2 Muhammad's status, description, command or message that they concealed in worldly life. — ʿAṭāʾ supplies this interpretation in multiple sources; Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported with it by al-Rāzī, adding disbelief and hypocrisy. Al-Ālūsī calls a particular syntactic attachment of it to تسوى very remote. [6 notes: THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); TABRISI(عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء)-]
- 4:42/q18/p3 All of their deeds and speech. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Bayḍāwī invoke the organs' disclosure of what mouths deny; Ibn Kathīr says they acknowledge everything they did. Al-Biqāʿī includes concealment of divine signs and proofs in life. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:42/q18/p4 In an inward reading, the imprint of false beliefs and vices in their souls. — Al-Qāshānī and al-Ālūsī say no inner imprint can be hidden once all is exposed. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:42/q19 Why can they conceal nothing from God?  [وَلَا يَكْتُمُونَ ٱللَّهَ حَدِيثًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether exposure comes through bodily testimony, divine knowledge, Hell's keepers, confession, or the inward disclosure of the soul.
- 4:42/q19/p1 Their limbs testify after their mouths are sealed, exposing them. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly tells how denial of shirk fails when hands, feet or other body parts speak, citing 6:23 and 36:65. Al-Akhfash allows the implicit subject to be the limbs; al-Ṭabrisī also says the limbs themselves conceal nothing. Study Quran cites 24:24 and 41:20–22. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds prophets, angels and others as witnesses. [38 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الكلبي وجماعة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN]
- 4:42/q19/p2 Their deeds are already manifest to God's all-encompassing knowledge, so concealment cannot help. — Al-Akhfash distinguishes failed attempted concealment from limbs' inability. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes the claim that all concealment fails from the claim that concealment simply brings no benefit. Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj and others explain the independent clause this way. [19 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(آخرون); ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); THALABI(آخرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء، الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء والزجاج,طائفة); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:42/q19/p3 The keepers of Hell testify against them. — Al-Kalbī and a group provide this account in al-Thaʿlabī. [1 notes: THALABI(الكلبي وجماعة)]
- 4:42/q19/p4 They confess or cease to conceal when their deeds are exposed. — Ibn Kathīr says they acknowledge everything; al-Ḥasan and Qatāda locate confession after matters are clear. Al-Ālūsī says they know concealment brings no benefit. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they cannot risk concealment after seeing witnesses and consequences. [7 notes: NAHHAS(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:42/q19/p5 In an inward reading, no imprint in their souls can remain hidden. — Al-Qāshānī and al-Ālūsī apply the nonconcealment to the soul's corrupt beliefs and vices. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:42/q20 How does يَكْتُمُونَ take its objects?  [يَكْتُمُونَ ٱللَّهَ حَدِيثًۭا · grammar]
- 4:42/q20/p1 It takes two objects, with a preposition understood before God. — Al-Samīn reconstructs ولا يكتمون من الله حديثا, treating the construction as reaching one object by an omitted preposition. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 4:42/q21 How does this verse relate to the denial of polytheism in 6:23?  [وَلَا يَكْتُمُونَ ٱللَّهَ حَدِيثًۭا · links]
turns on: Whether denial and disclosure occur at different stages, through different organs, only as a wish, as sincere misunderstanding, or whether the cited lie is worldly.
- 4:42/q21/p1 Different stages of Judgment explain denial at one point and disclosure or confession later. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports that polytheists deny shirk, hoping for forgiveness after seeing Muslims pardoned or admitted to Paradise; God seals their mouths and their limbs testify. Al-Ḥasan lists silence, whispers, lies, confession, pleas for return, and bodily testimony, citing 20:108, 16:28, 67:11 and 36:65. Al-Ṭabarī's version has all gather in one place; al-Qurṭubī adds a dispute about this community's testimony at 2:143. [32 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,عكرمة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس,الحسن); JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,طائفة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 4:42/q21/p2 Their tongues deny while their limbs give truthful testimony. — Al-Burhān explicitly distinguishes lying tongues from limbs commanded to speak; Ibn ʿAbbās's accounts repeatedly describe the same transition. [3 notes: BURHAN; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس)]
- 4:42/q21/p3 The nonconcealment is itself part of their wish, so no actual denial need be posited. — Al-Rāzī reports this reconciliation of the apparent conflict. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 4:42/q21/p4 They described what they sincerely believed, not intending concealment. — Jishumī says they report their understanding before truth becomes clear; al-Ṭabrisī attributes to al-Balkhī the view that they did not regard their approach to God as shirk and were compelled to abandon wrongdoing and lies. Ibn al-Anbārī says idol worship seemed obedience to them; Qāḍī Abū Yaʿlā adds that this does not make the utterance true. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(أبو القاسم البلخي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,القاضي أبو يعلى); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري,القاضي)]
- 4:42/q21/p5 The lie in 6:23 describes worldly lying, since nobody lies in the afterlife. — Jishumī reports Abū ʿAlī's interpretation and agrees with his reason. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)+]

## 4:42/q22 When and why do the disbelievers make this wish?  [يَوَدُّ لَوْ تُسَوَّىٰ بِهِمُ ٱلْأَرْضُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate trigger is witness testimony, the animals' transformation, or awareness of judgment and punishment.
- 4:42/q22/p1 After witnesses and bodily testimony expose their denial, they dread public shame and punishment. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās place the wish after mouths are sealed and limbs speak. Al-Biqāʿī links it to witnesses standing, exposure and coming rebuke. Ibn Kathīr names terror, disgrace and exposure; Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasizes humiliation and the desire to shrink from view. [16 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:42/q22/p2 After seeing animals become dust, they wish to escape eternal punishment in the same way. — Al-Kalbī says beasts and birds are turned to dust and the gathered people walk over them; al-Qurṭubī adds realization that disbelievers remain eternally in Hell. Al-Ṭabrisī discusses whether animal compensation ends. [7 notes: FARRA; THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة)]
- 4:42/q22/p3 The Day's terrors and prospect of eternal fire alone move them to wish for disappearance. — Al-Ṭabrisī cites their knowledge of punishment and eternal Fire; al-Ālūsī says the severity and doubled terror of that day compound the worldly descriptions; Study Quran mentions fear of accountability and fire. [4 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 4:42/q23 How does this scene connect to the preceding witness verse and the next verse?  [* · links]
turns on: The scope of coherence: the immediate witness scene or the larger passage about giving and judgment.
- 4:42/q23/p1 It clarifies the 'how?' raised by the witnesses and displays the terror of those exposed. — Al-Ālūsī calls it a resumption explaining the terrible state; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a clarifying restart after the question in 4:41; al-Burṣawī says the next verse describes the dreadful state. Al-Biqāʿī uses the scene to pivot toward prayer and purification as the means of purity through faith and obedience. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:42/q23/p2 The surrounding seven verses urge kindness and spending, promise reward, and condemn withholding or show. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this broader span of the sūra as the setting for 4:42. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 4:42/q24 What purpose does the public witness scene serve?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the note stresses the scene's moral effect or its function of exposing deeds.
- 4:42/q24/p1 It intensifies remorse for the negligent, joy for believers, and the call to obedience. — Jishumī says witness testimony exposes wrongdoers, shows God's just requital against the Mujabbira's accusations, and pictures the scene as a divine kindness encouraging obedience and avoidance of sin. [3 notes: JISHUMI]
- 4:42/q24/p2 It exposes every deed under testimony by limbs, prophets, angels and others. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their shameful acts are present before God as witnesses testify; al-Biqāʿī says they carry all burdens and are requited for concealing God's signs. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 4:42/q25 Who can serve as a witness for a community?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the neighboring witness language is generic enough to include nonprophetic witnesses.
- 4:42/q25/p1 A prophet, an angel, a believer, or another person may be a witness. — Jishumī says the text does not determine which and both prophetic and nonprophetic persons are rationally possible; he reports angels and believers as candidates and holds that prophets testify against communities. [3 notes: JISHUMI]
- 4:42/q25/p2 The witness is a prophet testifying to a community's deeds. — Jishumī gives this account as his own explanation of the prophetic witnesses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 4:42/q26 Does witness testimony require an infallible person or an upholder of truth in every era?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether bearing witness presupposes infallibility or continuous truth upholders.
- 4:42/q26/p1 No infallible person is required; testimony does not entail infallibility. — Jishumī rejects that deduction from the verse and says even a nonprophetic witness is rationally possible. [3 notes: JISHUMI; JISHUMI(أبو علي)+]
- 4:42/q26/p2 People upholding the truth are required in every era. — Jishumī reports Abū ʿAlī's requirement but says it is not a necessary condition. [0 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)-]

## 4:42/q27 What happened when Ibn Masʿūd recited the witness passage?  [* · reports]
- 4:42/q27/p1 At the Prophet's request, Ibn Masʿūd recited until this verse, then wept and stopped. — Jishumī reports the episode and suggests his tears reflected gratitude for the Prophet's station or compassion for sinners. [2 notes: JISHUMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 4:42/q28 How is the Ibn ʿAbbās report about apparent Quranic discrepancies transmitted?  [* · reports]
- 4:42/q28/p1 A questioner raised four apparent discrepancies; Ibn ʿAbbās answered them according to different settings or meanings. — Al-Itqān names Saʿīd ibn Jubayr as transmitter and lists afterlife denial, questioning, creation sequence and 'was Allah.' Al-Ḥākim authenticated the full report; Ibn Ḥajar says its original appears in an authenticated collection and groups four questions. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's exchange with Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq through Ibn Jarīr. [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس,الحاكم,سعيد بن جبير); ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 4:42/q29 How does the accompanying report reconcile no kinship or questions with later questioning?  [* · links]
- 4:42/q29/p1 They are different stages between the two trumpet blasts. — Ibn ʿAbbās says after the first blast there are no ties or questions, whereas after the second people rise and question one another; the reports cite 23:101, 37:27, 52:25 and 39:68. [3 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 4:42/q30 How does the accompanying report reconcile the order of earth and heaven's creation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 'after' marks creation's initial sequence or the later spreading and furnishing of earth.
- 4:42/q30/p1 Earth was created first; heavens were formed from smoke; earth was spread and furnished afterward. — Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes earth's initial creation from its later spreading with mountains, rivers, trees and seas, in connection with 41:9 and 79:30. Mujāhid agrees earth preceded heaven and glosses 'after that' as 'along with that,' allowing maʿa and baʿda the same Arabic sense. Al-Baghawī gives two days for earth, four for its features and provisions, and two for heavens. [6 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 4:42/q31 How does the accompanying report explain past-tense divine attributes?  [* · theology]
- 4:42/q31/p1 God always had and continues to have the described attributes. — Ibn ʿAbbās says descriptions like 'forgiving' and 'merciful' did not begin and cease; the apparently differing revealed statements each convey what God intended, though people may not know the meaning. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن عباس)]

## 4:42/q32 What debt-forgiveness report appears with these notes?  [* · reports]
- 4:42/q32/p1 God forgave a creditor who treated debtors leniently and gave the poor more time. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim relates Ḥudhayfa's account of easing wealthy debtors and deferring the poor, with ʿUqba ibn ʿĀmir or Abū Masʿūd confirming hearing it from the Prophet.
