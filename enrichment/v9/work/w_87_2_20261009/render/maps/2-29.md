# 2:29: 47 questions

## 2:29/q01 Who does the opening 'He' identify?  [هُوَ · referent]
- 2:29/q01/p1 God, the creator and knower named in the preceding verse. — Ṭabarī links the pronoun to God in 2:28; Biqāʿī's al-Ḥarālī sees the unseen sustaining essence disclosed by His names. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:29/q02 How is huwa read in inward remembrance?  [هُوَ · inward]
- 2:29/q02/p1 It indicates God's unique unseen essence and becomes a center of repeated remembrance. — Al-Ālūsī reports people of God treating huwa as a divine name. A mystical linguistic account reads hāʾ as original and wāw as extra; dropping wāw in dual and plural points to the sole existent. Spiritual elders repeat the name with every breath while its referent eludes analogy. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أهل الله)]

## 2:29/q03 How does the opening pronoun function grammatically?  [هُوَ · grammar]
- 2:29/q03/p1 It is the subject of a nominal sentence, with the following relative clause as predicate. — Naḥḥās and al-Samīn give the subject-predicate analysis. Abū Ḥayyān describes huwa as a detached masculine singular third-person pronoun, whose referent is fixed by its predicate. Al-Ālūsī notes the majesty conveyed by this construction. [5 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:29/q04 How is the hāʾ of huwa pronounced here and in related forms?  [هُوَ وَهُوَ · readings]
turns on: Whether a reader preserves the detached pronoun's vowel or lightens it after adjacent particles, and which particles license the lightening.
- 2:29/q04/p1 It retains its original vowel, ḍamma. — Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir and Ḥamza; transmitters from Nāfiʿ also give it. Ibn Khālawayh says this preserves the pronoun's independent original form. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the majority reading. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، عاصم، ابن عامر، حمزة,نافع: ابن جماز، أبو بكر بن أبي أويس، ورش); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 2:29/q04/p2 The hāʾ is quiescent after attached particles, with differences over which particles count. — Ibn Mujāhid names al-Kisāʾī and transmissions from Nāfiʿ. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguish wāw, fāʾ, lām, thumma and rare lakin or hamza environments. Lightening follows close attachment and Arabic syllable smoothing; al-Wāḥidī cites 24:52 and al-Khalīl's verse; al-Samīn cites 2:74, 28:61, 22:64 and 29:64. [21 notes: IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي,نافع: قالون، إسماعيل بن أبي أويس، خلف، ابن سعدان عن إسحق); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(بعض القراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو,الخليل); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر وأبو عمرو والكسائي وقالون); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عون عن الحلواني عن قالون,الكسائي وقالون عن نافع وأبو عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو وعلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حمدون,أبو عمرو والكسائي وقالون); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(قالون وأبو عمرو والكسائي وأبو جعفر)]
- 2:29/q04/p3 The pronoun's wāw itself has dialectal light, geminated, still, and poetic omitted forms. — Abū Ḥayyān associates gemination with Hamdān, stillness with Asad and Qays; al-Ālūsī also mentions Ḥimyar. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:29/q05 What does khalq mean here?  [خَلَقَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khalq designates actual origination or prior determination in this context.
- 2:29/q05/p1 God originated the thing after nonexistence. — Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī define divine creation as bringing it into existence; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī contrast human making after initial composition and cite poetic usage. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:29/q05/p2 Khalq can mean determining or measuring a thing. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this lexical sense; al-Ālūsī uses determination or divine intention as one solution to the creation-order difficulty. [4 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:29/q06 How is khalaqa read?  [خَلَقَ · readings]
- 2:29/q06/p1 The standard reading is Form I perfect active khalaqa. — Qirāʾāt-ER states the standard vocalization. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 2:29/q07 What does the verse mean by creating earthly things 'for you'?  [لَكُم مَّا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the lām names practical benefit, religious evidence, general gift, or the human vocation behind those gifts.
- 2:29/q07/p1 They serve human worldly use and religious reflection. — Ṭabarī includes livelihood, obedience and proofs of oneness; al-Wāḥidī distinguishes use from reflection and says even harmful beasts deter sin. Rāzī lists animals, plants, minerals, mountains and crafts; Abū Ḥayyān adds food, clothing, marriage, transport and scenery. Al-Bayḍāwī includes images of afterlife joy and pain; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses changing times and the visible kinds. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the heaven's making as part of the favor to humanity. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(Şeyh Sa‘dî); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:29/q07/p2 Its primary benefit is reflection and proof of divine oneness and resurrection. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī appeal to the surrounding life, death and heaven signs; al-Qurṭubī adds that snakes and scorpions can warn of punishment. Al-Jishumī's al-Aṣamm uses seven heavens from smoke as a resurrection proof; al-Wāḥidī says direct sight of danger affects the heart more than a report. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(المفسرون); JISHUMI(الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي); IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:29/q07/p3 The things were subjected as a favor, provision and blessing to humanity. — Qatāda repeatedly explains subjection for Adam's children; al-Ṭabarī emphasizes favor and evidence; Biqāʿī says every earthly element supports the rest. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 2:29/q07/p4 The cosmos is for humanity so that humanity may wholly serve God. — Al-Sulamī's Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and Bursevī's inward source urge detachment from possessions; Elmalılı cites 51:56 for worship as the human end. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ELMALILI]

## 2:29/q08 What is included in 'what is on earth'?  [مَّا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: Whether earth denotes the physical globe and its contents or the entire lower region.
- 2:29/q08/p1 The earthly contents, such as animals, plants, minerals and all useful products. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the land-and-sea globe and says its container's creation is implied. Bursevī takes the things contained; al-Wāḥidī and Elmalılı allow some things to harm some individuals. Abū Ḥayyān links this with 2:22's earlier gift of earth as a resting place. [6 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:29/q08/p2 The lower realm, including earth itself and everything below the upper realm. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī allow earth to mean the lower regions; al-Ālūsī also invokes the encompassing Throne or conceptual direction. Abū Ḥayyān finds this distant from the phrase's literal scope, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says earth literally names the globe. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 2:29/q08/p3 The clause covers whatever people need. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this narrower explanation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:29/q09 How are mā and its following phrase parsed?  [مَّا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
- 2:29/q09/p1 Mā is a relative pronoun and accusative object of khalaqa, with fī al-arḍ as its relative clause. — Ṭabarī calls mā relative; al-Zajjāj makes it the object. Naḥḥās and al-Samīn give the accusative and relative-clause analysis. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:29/q10 What does the lām in 'for you' express?  [لَكُم · grammar]
turns on: Which function of the Arabic lām fits the context.
- 2:29/q10/p1 Purpose, cause, and benefit: for your sake. — Ibn Kaysān glosses it min ajlikum; Abū Ḥayyān prefers benefit. Al-Ālūsī says fronting the phrase hastens glad tidings. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كيسان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:29/q10/p2 Ownership, permissive use, or a broader relation of particularity. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn list these linguistic possibilities; al-Ālūsī notes 17:7 as a counterexample to an automatic inference of benefit and answers through beneficial particularization. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:29/q11 Does the verse establish original permissibility or ownership?  [لَكُم · law]
turns on: Whether a statement of human benefit is also a legal grant, what reason can establish before revelation, and whether a collective grant entails individual ownership.
- 2:29/q11/p1 Useful things are presumptively permissible unless evidence forbids them. — Al-Kashshāf and jurists infer permission from creation for benefit; al-Ṭabrisī requires proof for exclusive control. Al-Bayḍāwī distinguishes collective grant from every individual's ownership. Elmalılı grounds permission in revealed text rather than reason. [15 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الكرخي وأبو بكر الرازي والمعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الرازي والبيضاوي وصاحب الكشاف); ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 2:29/q11/p2 Things are originally forbidden until authorization. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says most proponents except necessities such as breathing and motion; Abū Ḥayyān states their principle that one should not act without leave. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(القائلون بالحظر); QURTUBI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض أهل الحديث وبعض المعتزلة)]
- 2:29/q11/p3 Judgment is suspended before revelation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers suspension for a genuinely novel case; Abū al-Ḥasan and most Mālikīs say reason creates no ruling. Ibn al-Ṣāʾigh replies that cases always have a text, precedent or continuing default. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes post-revelation harms from benefits. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الصائغ); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الحسن وأصحابه وأكثر المالكية والصيرفي,ابن الصائغ,المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو بكر بن فورك عن ابن الصائغ,قوم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الإمام الرازي,المالكية وجمهور الحنفية); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القائلون بالوقف)+]
- 2:29/q11/p4 The verse grants no legal default; it gives proof of God's power and favor. — Ibn al-ʿArabī and Ibn ʿĀshūr read the address as reminder to disbelievers, not legislation. Al-Qurṭubī argues that benefit need not require tasting and some goods are lethal. Abū Ḥayyān notes the lām is equivocal. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:29/q11/p5 All things are common to everyone, with no exclusive private claim. — Rāzī reports permissivists; Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr answer that a grant to humanity collectively can be distributed to individuals under separate proof. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أهل الإباحة); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 2:29/q11/p6 Eating clay is forbidden because it is earth itself, not what is in earth. — Rāzī answers that minerals and veins lie within earth and mention of contents does not exclude earth; Abū Ḥayyān also reports this inference. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:29/q11/p7 Unrestricted permission cancels duties and prohibitions for realized lovers. — Bursevī attributes this to libertine Sufis and rejects it as contradicting revealed commands and prohibitions. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أهل الإباحة من المتصوفة الجهلة); BURSEVI-]

## 2:29/q12 What human rights limit the use of creation granted 'for you'?  [لَكُم · law]
- 2:29/q12/p1 The collective grant protects each person's life, honor, liberty, intellect, religion and property from another's unauthorized use. — Elmalılı says people were not created as one another's possessions: suicide and self-sale are impermissible; consent can transfer wealth, grounding contracts, while core human rights belong to God and are inviolable. [4 notes: ELMALILI]

## 2:29/q13 Does creating 'for you' mean God acts for a purpose?  [لَكُم · theology]
turns on: Whether a wise benefit is a divine goal, and whether such a goal would entail divine need.
- 2:29/q13/p1 Divine acts have wisdoms and purposes benefiting creatures. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports proponents citing 51:56 and distinguishes divine acts from rulings grounded in human interests; al-Ṭabrisī infers purposeful action. [5 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عرفة,جماعة المثبتين,جمهور الفقهاء والمتكلمين)]
- 2:29/q13/p2 God does not act to attain an end that benefits or perfects Him; purposive language describes an act's outcome. — Rāzī argues need, inability, eternal goal or infinite regress would follow; known unbelievers' burdens contradict a rule of always securing welfare. He reads 51:56 analogically. Al-Bayḍāwī calls human benefit an outcome rather than God's need; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ashʿarī reasoning. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أصحاب الأشعري)]
- 2:29/q13/p3 The verse itself need not settle the philosophical dispute about final causes. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says all Muslims affirm divine will, choice, knowledge and wisdom, while disagreeing over labeling wisdom as a final cause. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:29/q14 What does 'all' add, and how is it parsed?  [جَمِيعًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the word is read as collective scope or as implying a shared time of creation.
- 2:29/q14/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial qualifier of mā, emphasizing the whole rather than simultaneous creation. — Sībawayh, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Zamakhsharī and others give the circumstantial parse. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī contrast jamīʿan with maʿan and deny that it proves simultaneity; Biqāʿī says human needs rely on all earthly elements. [10 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 2:29/q14/p2 The root means gathering or joining, opposed to separation. — Al-Jishumī adduces Friday and the place Jamʿ as names from gathering; al-Ṭabrisī also glosses the root. Al-Jishumī uses gathering and separation as accidents indicating bodies are originated. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 2:29/q15 What does thumma mark between the earth and heaven clauses?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the conjunction orders events, ranks the greater act, or structures the recital.
- 2:29/q15/p1 A higher rank or a new stage in the discourse, not necessarily later time. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 90:17; al-Bayḍāwī and Biqāʿī stress heaven's greater rank. Ibn ʿĀshūr says one subject linked by thumma can carry imagined delay in rank, while Elmalılı traces order of human awareness from self to earth to sky; al-Qāshānī marks the difference of realms. [22 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; BAYDAWI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:29/q15/p2 It marks temporal succession, perhaps with intervening earth preparation. — Abū Ḥayyān says this is the ordinary force; one account allows an interval for mountains and provisions in 41:9–10. Ibn ʿĀshūr says when subjects differ, temporal succession is normal; if earth preceded heaven both time and rank can be present. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:29/q15/p3 It joins or orders the presentation without specifying the order of events. — Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguish discourse order from event order; Asad translates it 'and'. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 2:29/q16 Which came first, earth or heaven, and how are the creation verses reconciled?  [خَلَقَ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · links]
turns on: Whether khalq and spreading are distinct events, whether the sky existed as smoke before its sevenfold shaping, and how strictly thumma and baʿda convey temporal order.
- 2:29/q16/p1 Earth's body was created first; heaven was fashioned next; earth was spread and furnished afterward. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās and most early exegetes reconcile 2:29 and 41:9–12 with 79:30 by separating creation from spreading. Ibn Kathīr likens construction to foundations rising upward; Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions geological cooling and crust movement. Rāzī objects that a body cannot exist unspread and contents require spread earth; al-Ālūsī replies it began small and its contents existed as materials. [37 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وعمرو بن عبيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود وناس من أصحاب رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد وغيره من المفسرين); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل,مجاهد); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور ومنهم مجاهد والحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام الرازي)-]
- 2:29/q16/p2 Heaven or its vapor preceded earth; the sky was later fashioned into seven. — Qatāda, Muqātil and al-Suddī are reported for heaven first. Al-Qurṭubī prefers primordial vapor, then earth, then fashioned heaven, then spreading; Ibn ʿĀshūr favors heaven first because 79:30 is stronger for sequence, with astronomy and Genesis as further support. Al-Ālūsī says investigators regard visible earthly contents as later. Al-Qurṭubī prefers the account with primordial vapor before earth, despite an Ibn ʿAbbās variant reversing the order, and says speculation cannot decide it. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(المحققون,بعض,بعض القوم); IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة والسدي ومقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:29/q16/p3 The earth-first wording names divine determination or initial material, while heaven's actual form came before earthly contents. — Al-Ālūsī uses khalq as determination or intention, cites 41:11's command to heaven and earth, and reads towns and provisions as matter and origins; the order of each sūra fits its theme. [10 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المحققون,بعض القوم)]
- 2:29/q16/p4 The textual order is rhetorical, so it need not establish chronological earth-first creation. — Rāzī prefers enumerating blessings; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn Kathīr report discourse-order resolutions. Elmalılı traces human perception and allows a narrower chronological sense in which the earth's separate body affects later heavenly ordering. [7 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 2:29/q16/p5 The question remains difficult because cited verses appear to order the events differently. — Rāzī sets out the apparent clash of 2:29, 41:9–10 and 79:27–30; Abū Ḥayyān reports both camps; al-Ālūsī says it troubled readers since the Companions. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض الملحدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(القرطبي)]

## 2:29/q17 What creation-day schedule do the reports give?  [خَلَقَ ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · reports]
turns on: How the six days are allocated, especially the Saturday report's attribution.
- 2:29/q17/p1 Earth Sunday and Monday, provisions and mountains Tuesday and Wednesday, heavens Thursday and Friday; Adam in Friday's last hour. — ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām and reports through Ibn ʿAbbās give six days, with Friday also the hour of Resurrection. Ibn al-Jawzī transmits another allocation of earth and contents over four days then heaven over two. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن سلام); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الحسن ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,عبد الله بن سلام); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي)]
- 2:29/q17/p2 Soil Saturday, mountains Sunday, trees Monday, harmful things Tuesday, light Wednesday, animals Thursday, Adam late Friday. — Abū Hurayra transmits this as Prophetic, but Ibn Kathīr reports ʿAlī b. al-Madīnī, al-Bukhārī and al-Bayhaqī tracing the schedule to Kaʿb and explaining its mistaken elevation to the Prophet. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(البيهقي,علي بن المديني والبخاري وغيرهما)-]
- 2:29/q17/p3 The earth and its provisions took four days and the heavens two, totaling six. — Rāzī bases this on 41:9–10; the al-Suddī narrative names Tuesday and Wednesday for mountains and plants. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]

## 2:29/q18 From what substance did heaven and earth arise according to the reports?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلسَّمَآءِ · reports]
turns on: Which reported primordial material and sequence is being followed.
- 2:29/q18/p1 Water under the Throne produced smoke or vapor for heaven and dried into earth, which was divided into seven. — Al-Suddī's multi-Companion report details water, smoke, one earth, seven earths, then seven heavens; Ibn ʿAbbās describes heating water into rising vapor. Ibn ʿAmr adds wind, clay and foam; al-Bayhaqī says water itself was created by God without prior material. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدى); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم,ابن عباس,ابن عباس وابن مسعود وناس من أصحاب رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,البيهقي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,عبد الله بن عمرو,علي)]

## 2:29/q27 What form and force does the number seven have?  [سَبْعَ · grammar]

## 2:29/q28 Do seven heavens exclude additional heavens or spheres?  [سَبْعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٍۢ · interpretation]

## 2:29/q29 What physical or cosmic entities are the seven heavens?  [سَبْعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٍۢ · referent]

- 2:29/q29/p6 The great celestial bodies of the solar system are the likely referent. — Ibn ʿĀshūr admits only God knows their full nature and presents this as a scientific likelihood. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:29/q30 What did astronomers infer about spheres beyond the seven?  [سَبْعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٍۢ · reports]

- 2:29/q30/p1 A fixed-star sphere and an outer daily-rotating sphere yield nine. — Rāzī reports faster planetary and slower fixed-star motion, and the claim that stars move only with their spheres. - 2:29/q31/p3 Heaven is a contained wave or is whiter than milk; it was formed from smoke and water. — Abū al-Jald answers Ibn ʿAbbās with the wave description; Kaʿb gives whiteness, and ʿAlī swears to smoke and water. A report attributes the sky's green appearance to a rock beneath the earths. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو الجلد,سفيان الثوري,علي,كعب)]
- 2:29/q31/p4 Each heaven contains inhabitants and features; the lowest bears protective stars. — Al-Suddī includes angels, seas, hail mountains and unknown beings; reports mention decoration and protection from devils, then God's settling over the Throne. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(السدي)]
- 2:29/q31/p5 The heaven containing the Throne is chief among heavens, and the inhabited earth chief among earths. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this rank distinction in al-Durr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 2:29/q32 How many earths are there, and what are they like?  [سَبْعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٍۢ · reports]
turns on: What 'their like' in 65:12 means and the reliability of specific reports.
- 2:29/q32/p1 There are seven earths corresponding in number to the heavens. — Al-Qurṭubī prefers this reading of 65:12 and adduces reports that a land thief will be encircled by seven earths and that tawḥīd outweighs seven heavens and earths. Al-Dāwūdī says the seven earths are contiguous. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الداودي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:29/q32/p2 The earths are alike in thickness and distances between them, rather than necessarily in count. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this alternative construal of 'their like' in 65:12. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:29/q32/p3 Each earth contains counterparts to familiar prophets and Adam. — A report attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās names counterparts; al-Bayhaqī judges its chain sound but content extremely anomalous and uncorroborated. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(البيهقي)-]

## 2:29/q33 What was the first created thing according to these reports?  [خَلَقَ · reports]
turns on: The scope of 'first' and whether water, pen, or light and darkness are counted.
- 2:29/q33/p1 Water, or water and other willed things, preceded later creation. — A Prophetic report says everything was created from water; al-Bayhaqī says God created water without prior matter or model. Ibn ʿAmr, Ibn al-Zubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās list water, light, darkness, wind and earth while declining to explain their source, which Ibn ʿAbbās relates to God's creative act. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم,ابن عباس,البيهقي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن الزبير,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص)]
- 2:29/q33/p2 The pen was first and wrote destiny through Resurrection. — Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿUbāda transmit Prophetic reports; al-Bayhaqī qualifies first as after water, wind and Throne. Another Ibn ʿAbbās account puts the pen before fish, vapor and mountains. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,البيهقي,عبادة بن الصامت)]
- 2:29/q33/p3 Light and darkness were made first before heavens from smoke and the earth's later provisions. — Ibn Isḥāq gives this sequence and the six-day completion. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق)]

## 2:29/q34 What does the ending say God knows?  [وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the universal close is applied chiefly to creation's details, hidden disbelief, or the final outcomes of gifts.
- 2:29/q34/p1 Every created thing and all its details, including deeds, secrets and resurrection. — Muqātil includes resurrection; Ṭabarī singles out hypocrites' hidden unbelief and Jewish scholars' concealment, Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Saʿīd b. Jubayr on deeds. Ibn Kathīr cites 67:14; Rāzī argues precise creation entails knowledge of universals and particulars. Al-Ālūsī stresses nothing is excluded. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); IBNASHUR-FULL(جميع المليين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:29/q34/p2 Specifically the disbelief, hypocrisy and concealed description of the Prophet addressed in context. — Ṭabarī ties the ending to 2:8; al-Wāḥidī names the concealed description. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:29/q34/p3 God knows people's outcomes and what benefits His gifts will yield. — Al-Ṭabrisī says this makes gifts true blessings; the inward Bursevī source says God knows each thing's purpose and praise. [2 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 2:29/q35 What is the meaning and grammatical force of ʿalīm?  [عَلِيمٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ʿalīm is taken as simple equivalence to ʿālim or as an intensive form.
- 2:29/q35/p1 It means knowing, with an intensive force of complete, far-reaching knowledge. — Ṭabarī gives ʿālim; Ibn ʿAbbās says complete knowledge. Sībawayh treats faʿīl as intensive; al-Jishumī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī distinguish ʿalīm's breadth from multiple acts of knowing. Abū Ḥayyān rejects a contrast in which ʿalīm is self-taught and ʿālim externally taught. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; TABRISI(سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:29/q35/p2 The final clause is nominal: huwa is subject and ʿalīm its predicate. — Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī and al-Samīn give this parse; bi-kulli shayʾ attaches to ʿalīm. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:29/q36 Why does ʿalīm govern 'everything' with bi?  [بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌۭ · grammar]
- 2:29/q36/p1 Intensive forms of knowing or ignorance take bi, and the fronted prepositional phrase depends on ʿalīm. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn distinguish bi for knowing, li for other direct verbs, and preservation of the base verb's preposition for prepositional verbs; they cite 53:32, 57:6 and 11:107. Al-Ālūsī compares the forms to the elative; Abū Ḥayyān notes an omitted verb analysis for apparent direct objects. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:29/q37 Does God's knowledge include nonexistent things and particulars?  [بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌۭ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'thing' reaches nonexistents and particulars, and whether divine knowledge is an eternal attribute or newly acquired.
- 2:29/q37/p1 It encompasses particulars and universals, existent and nonexistent, through one eternal unchanging knowledge. — Abū Ḥayyān says even if 'thing' were confined to existents other verses prove nonexistents; he and al-Qurṭubī describe one eternal attribute, without sense-acquisition or distraction. Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr reject philosophers' restriction to universals. Al-Ālūsī denies limiting 'thing'. Al-Qurṭubī cites 4:166, 11:14, 7:7, 35:11 and 6:59 on revelation, judgment, pregnancy and the unseen. [14 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجهمية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(جميع المليين)]
- 2:29/q37/p2 God is knowing by essence, without a separate attribute of knowledge. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the Muʿtazilite and Jahmī position and rejects the latter; Rāzī records a Muʿtazilite inference from 12:76 and counters with 4:166. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجهمية)-]
- 2:29/q37/p3 Knowledge of created things arises newly when they arise. — Al-Jishumī attributes this to Hishām b. al-Ḥakam and says the verse refutes it. [0 notes: JISHUMI-]

## 2:29/q38 Why does the verse end with divine knowledge?  [وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the ending emphasizes the knowledge needed for creation, awareness of the addressees, or preparation for Adam's story.
- 2:29/q38/p1 Precise, faultless creation reveals and requires complete knowledge. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr reason from ordered work to a knowing maker; al-Biqāʿī and al-Itqān find the ending especially apt. The knowledge also assures resurrection, since God knows scattered body parts. Al-Burhān notes that subtle verse endings fit their recited attributes even when another ending first seems expected. [20 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; BURHAN]
- 2:29/q38/p2 It reassures or warns the addressed people that hidden unbelief and future outcomes are known. — Ṭabarī names hypocrites and scholars; al-Ṭabrisī notes God knows the effects of His gifts; Abū Ḥayyān treats the close as extending knowledge from beginning to end. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:29/q38/p3 It prepares the following Adam story, where knowledge is central to humanity's role. — Biqāʿī and al-Sāmarrāʾī connect God's knowledge in 2:29 to Adam's taught knowledge in 2:31–32 and the qualifications for vicegerency. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 2:29/q39 What theological claims follow from universal creative knowledge?  [وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَىْءٍ عَلِيمٌۭ · theology]
turns on: How universal knowledge interacts with divine will, human action, and wisdom.
- 2:29/q39/p1 An ordered creator knows particulars; human acts and divine willing cannot be separated from detailed knowledge. — Rāzī uses specification by measure to argue for will and against Muʿtazilite human origination of acts whose details humans do not know; the verse also counters philosophers who deny divine knowledge of particulars. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:29/q39/p2 The creator is powerful, knowing, purposeful and deserving of gratitude. — Al-Ṭabrisī draws this from creation for even disbelievers; Elmalılı says divine knowledge underlies the prepared fit between causes, effects and human life. [3 notes: TABRISI; ELMALILI]

## 2:29/q40 What inward reading is given to earthly gifts and human purpose?  [لَكُم مَّا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · inward]
turns on: Whether gifts primarily call for grateful use, detachment, or ascent through realms.
- 2:29/q40/p1 Gifts should strengthen obedience and prompt gratitude, trust and generosity. — Al-Sulamī and al-Qurṭubī report that blessings point to God's generosity and promised afterlife; Abū ʿUthmān says monotheism is the first gift. Al-Qurṭubī adduces reports on giving, replacement, the two angels, Asmāʾ and ʿĀʾisha; fear of shortage is poor trust. [16 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عثمان,أرباب المعاني,أسماء بنت أبي بكر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة,عمر بن الخطاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عثمان,بعض المنسوبين للحقائق)]
- 2:29/q40/p2 The cosmos is yours while you are God's; relinquish attachment to blessings and see the Benefactor. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, Baghdadi mystics and al-Nūrī or al-Thawrī urge detachment; al-Jāmī calls both worlds witnesses to oneness. Bursevī's inward source says every atom praises God. [11 notes: SULAMI(أبو الحسن النوري,ابن عطاء,بعض البغداديين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطاء,الثوري,بعض البغداديين); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الجامي)]
- 2:29/q40/p3 The earthly and heavenly realms reflect body and spirit and stages of knowing God. — Al-Qāshānī distinguishes creation and command; Elmalılı urges ascent from sense to intelligible and an awareness of celestial vocation. [6 notes: QASHANI; ELMALILI]

## 2:29/q41 How are the seven heavens read inwardly?  [سَبْعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٍۢ · inward]
turns on: Which inward scheme is used: faculties, cosmic subtle layers, or Sufi stations.
- 2:29/q41/p1 They are seven graded spiritual faculties and stations. — Al-Qāshānī and Bursevī name lower malakūt, psychic powers or jinn, then soul, heart, intellect, secret, spirit and hidden secret; ʿAlī's knowledge of heaven's paths becomes knowledge of asceticism, trust and contentment. Al-Qāshānī notes philosophical and Sufi terminology differs. [5 notes: QASHANI(أمير المؤمنين); BURSEVI(علي)]
- 2:29/q41/p2 They belong to a vast subtle hierarchy above the material universe. — Kur'an Yolu transmits a mystical account in which the material cosmos is below the first heaven; each higher heaven expands greatly, then come Kursī and Throne, and beyond lies the world of command with subtle heart, spirit and secret. Miʿrāj reports populate the heavens. [8 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(hadisler,tasavvuf ve irfan kolu)]
- 2:29/q41/p3 They correspond to spiritual gardens and paths of Jalwatī and Khalwatī practice. — Bursevī reports Aftāda Efendi's twelve stations and relates seven heavens to seven gardens and two routes through divine oneness or Names. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ الشهير بافتادة أفندي)]

## 2:29/q42 How does the verse connect to the preceding creation and resurrection argument?  [* · links]
turns on: How the new reminder of cosmic gifts advances the prior rebuke and resurrection proof.
- 2:29/q42/p1 It adds visible cosmic creation and human provision to the prior life, death and resurrection proof. — Muqātil cites 40:57; al-Wāḥidī says creating heaven and earth makes re-creation credible. Rāzī calls it a second universal blessing after life and points to 2:28 and 45:13. Ibn ʿĀshūr parallels reasons for worship in 2:21–22; al-Biqāʿī says the cosmic gift unfolds the earlier human summary. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الأصم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:29/q42/p2 It warns disbelievers, especially hypocrites and People of the Book, while inviting repentance and gratitude. — Ṭabarī links 2:6 and the scriptural audience; al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān see favor after warning; al-Biqāʿī pictures a threat that may draw deniers back. - 2:29/q42/p3 The unjoined opening clause can be a new independent proof or a close consequence of the preceding gift of life. — Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give both analyses; the benefit of created goods depends on people first being alive. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:29/q43 How does the verse prepare the story of Adam and vicegerency?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link centers on Adam's entrusted domain, his knowledge, or the story's moral warning.
- 2:29/q43/p1 It establishes humanity's honored domain and God's knowledge, the two themes of Adam's appointment. — Ṭabarī says the story shows honor, disobedience, repentance and Iblīs's fate and proves Muhammad's prophethood to scriptural readers. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says vicegerency requires an entrusted earth and knowledge or capacity, developed by Adam's test and teaching. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 2:29/q43/p2 The passage discloses human dignity and a duty to use the created realm in God's way. — Elmalılı combines benefit, worship and prepared spiritual ascent; al-Qushayrī stresses that creation points to God's lordship rather than becoming an object of worship. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; ELMALILI]

## 2:29/q44 What larger Quranic links are drawn for the earth and heavens?  [* · links]
turns on: Which parallel verses and inferred omitted elements expand the brief account.
- 2:29/q44/p1 Other verses describe creation's subjection, cosmic layering and provision from heaven. — The Study Quran cites 16:5, 16:13, 22:65, 31:20, 45:13, 41:12, 67:3, 65:12 and 71:15. Elmalılı invokes 51:22 for provision from heaven and air and light reaching earth. [9 notes: STUDYQURAN(Mujāhid); ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:29/q44/p2 The wording omits seven earthly levels and heavenly benefits by reciprocal implication. — Biqāʿī calls this ḥatbāk: the mentioned seven heavens imply earthly levels, while earthly benefits imply celestial benefits. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:29/q45 What rhetorical details mark the move from earth to heaven?  [ثُمَّ ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ فَسَوَّىٰهُنَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: What rhetorical work the order and the paired verbs do.
- 2:29/q45/p1 The shift magnifies the higher creation and recalls God's governance over feared events from above. — Biqāʿī and al-Ḥarālī emphasize heaven's beauty, purity and the reassurance that God governs what may descend; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls for renewed attention to the greater sign. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:29/q45/p2 Istawā and sawwāhun echo one another in sound while linking divine direction to perfected order. — Al-Ālūsī calls the correspondence graceful; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it modified paronomasia. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:29/q45/p3 The verse starts with earth because it is nearer human experience. — Biqāʿī and Elmalılı say the tangible earth is the more immediate sign; al-Ālūsī contrasts al-Baqara's human-benefit context with al-Nāziʿāt's display of power. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:29/q46 How is the word samāwāt written in the early script?  [سَمَٰوَٰتٍۢ · sciences]
- 2:29/q46/p1 Its omitted weak letters follow ordinary early writing practice. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the scribes used their contemporary script rather than inventing a distinct Quranic orthography. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 2:29/q47 What additional report or maxim accompanies the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which ancillary tradition is cited.
