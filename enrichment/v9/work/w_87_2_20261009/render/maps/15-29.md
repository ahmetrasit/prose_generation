# 15:29: 25 questions

## 15:29/q01 How does the conditional account of Adam's creation connect with the preceding narrative?  [فَإِذَا · links]
- 15:29/q01/p1 It continues the account of a remarkable clay-made human and the command that followed. — Muqātil summarizes the creation, breathing and command; Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts Adam's clay body with angels of light; Rāzī says the first human evidences a powerful, choosing God before the narrative turns to angelic obedience and Iblīs's refusal; Asad summarizes the sequence. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 15:29/q01/p2 The opening 'when' belongs to a conjoined narrative with an omitted 'remember'. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the parallel openings in al-Baqara and al-Aʿrāf. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 15:29/q02 What does سَوَّيْتُهُۥ mean?  [سَوَّيْتُهُۥ · meaning]
- 15:29/q02/p1 God completed and proportioned Adam's human form, arranging its parts so it was ready to receive life or spirit. — Al-Kalbī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī and others gloss shaping and completion; Jishumī explains taswiya as equal measure or judgment; Rāzī also proposes balanced bodily temperaments and mixtures, citing 76:2. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran stress readiness for the spirit; Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies each organ in its fitting place; Kūran Yolu includes the clay stages and completed biological development; al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya reads the form as receptive to divine manifestation. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الشيخ عز الدين); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 15:29/q03 Was Adam's bodily formation gradual or instantaneous?  [سَوَّيْتُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the temporal sequence describes stages in Adam's bodily formation or conveys an allegorical account of immediate divine willing.
- 15:29/q03/p1 His bodily parts were assembled and ordered in stages before the spirit came. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads this sequence in the wording, takes 3:59's 'Be' as applying to the soul rather than bodily formation, and compares 23:14's new creation after bodily formation; Kūran Yolu says the earth, clay, and dry clay stages took an unknown duration. [4 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 15:29/q03/p2 The creation account is allegorical and divine creation requires no elapsed time. — Asad adduces 2:117, 3:47, 3:59, 6:73, 16:40, 19:35, 36:82 and 40:68, alongside creation and prostration parallels. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 15:29/q04 What is the literal sense of نَفَخْتُ?  [نَفَخْتُ · meaning]
- 15:29/q04/p1 Blowing is sending air into a body or cavity. — Wāḥidī and Jishumī define air entering; Rāzī specifies air moved through another body's cavities; Qurṭubī and Bayḍāwī compare entry into a cavity; Ālūsī specifies air from mouth or elsewhere and a cavity able to hold it; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes compressed air from pursed lips; Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows mouth or another means. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 15:29/q05 What does God's breathing into Adam signify?  [وَنَفَخْتُ فِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'breathing' describes the creation of life figuratively or the entry of a created spirit; the notes deny corporeal breath from God.
- 15:29/q05/p1 It figuratively signifies imparting or creating life or spirit in receptive Adam, without a physical divine breath. — Jishumī and Nasafī equate it with giving life; Abū Ḥayyān denies an actual breath or breathed object; Biqāʿī and Ghazālī compare a wick catching fire; Bursevī and ʿIzz al-Dīn speak of soul-light appearing in a receptive, balanced form, like a polished mirror, and Bursevī cites a Persian mirror couplet. Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad use metaphor; Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies the spirit's coming to exist in attachment to the body; Asad includes consciousness. [17 notes: JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الشيخ عز الدين); ALUSI-FULL(حجة الإسلام); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 15:29/q05/p2 God caused an actual spirit to enter Adam, with the breathing image describing its flow or a mediated act. — Ṭabrisī says God made spirit flow into Adam; Ibn al-Jawzī compares its movement through the body to wind. Abū Jaʿfar reports God created a spirit and breathed into Adam from it; Abū ʿAbd Allāh says an angel was ordered to breathe it in, so God's attribution need not describe direct physical breath. [4 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 15:29/q06 How does the spirit relate to Adam's bodily life?  [فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the animating spirit is conceived as a subtle bodily presence, an immaterial director, or the body's new spiritual creation.
- 15:29/q06/p1 The spirit animates the prepared body, then may separate while persisting as a distinct self. — Ṭabarī says the breathing made Adam living. Bayḍāwī describes attachment to heart vapor, spreading through arteries; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares a rapidly formed vaporous or electrical force; Ālūsī reports the animal spirit as heart-generated vapor carrying the rational soul's control, and the subtle body's passage through the organs and survival after separation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads 23:13–14 as the bodily form becoming a new spiritual creation and 32:11 as the spirit taken at death while the body remains. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(المحققون); TABATABAI]
- 15:29/q06/p2 The immaterial spirit governs the body through a nonspatial attachment while the body remains receptive. — Ālūsī reports this as the immaterialists' account, comparing attachment to an inspired lover's bond; some verificationists distinguish a permeating subtle spirit from a higher divine-command spirit illuminating it. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الذاهبون إلى التجرد,بعض المحققين)]

## 15:29/q07 What is the nature of the spirit referred to here?  [رُّوحِى · theology]
turns on: The notes differ over bodily substance, immaterial substance, life as an accident, heart temperament, transcendent command, and divine power; several also acknowledge limited knowledge of its nature.
- 15:29/q07/p1 It is a created subtle body that gives life and persists apart from the changing visible body. — Wāḥidī and Baghawī define it as life-giving subtle body; Qurṭubī cites reports and identifies nafs and rūḥ as names for one referent. Ālūsī attributes this to the Sunni majority and Juwaynī; Laqqānī calls it luminous, self-living and imperishable, permeating organs like rosewater; verificationists compare oil in sesame, with persistent particles outlasting bodily change. Al-Jalāl says proofs of selfhood beyond the visible frame do not exclude a subtle body; Ālūsī says its special divine property prevents a regress of spirits. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجلال,اللقاني,المحققون,جمهور أهل السنة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 15:29/q07/p2 It is an immaterial, self-subsisting substance, distinct from the body and bodily accidents. — Ālūsī reports Ghazālī and verificationists: universal intelligibles cannot inhere in indivisible or divisible matter; the Shaykh cites direct self-awareness; Plato cites imagined vast nonexistent forms; further arguments cite unified perception, organ awareness and continuity of personal identity as the frame changes. The author of al-Hayākil notes awareness of self when bodily parts are forgotten; Ālūsī defends the possibility of distinct spaceless creatures and says only God's independence is unique. Al-Jalāl replies that these proofs may establish only distinction from the visible frame. [18 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أفلاطون,الإمام,الشيخ,جماعة من المحققين,جمهور المتكلمين,حجة الإسلام وجماعة من المحققين,صاحب الهياكل)]
- 15:29/q07/p3 The spirit is life itself, an accident inhering in the body. — Qurṭubī reports identification with life; Ālūsī reports a visible-frame account, a later argument from it against servants creating acts, and al-Qāḍī's attribution to most theologians and Ustādh Abū Isḥāq. The Imam objects that the person persists while bodily parts change; this account raises the accident-in-an-accident problem. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي,بعض المتأخرين من المعاصرين,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,جمهور المتكلمين)-]
- 15:29/q07/p4 The spirit is the balanced temperament of the heart's blood. — Ālūsī reports this as a further account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 15:29/q07/p5 It is a real transcendent divine-command being, the person's essential self rather than a physical function. — Elmalılı links the bestowed spirit to 17:85; Kūran Yolu says 17:85 leaves its nature little known yet affirms a real metaphysical being linking humans to the divine realm, and makes spirit the true self. Ghazālī calls it divine reality; Study Quran reports commentators who take al-rūḥ more literally. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes its self-existence and independence at death; Ibn al-Jawzī and Ālūsī stress limits of knowledge. [12 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Gazzâlî); STUDYQURAN(other commentators); TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL(المناوي)]
- 15:29/q07/p6 It is God's power or a specially chosen created spirit from His power. — Study Quran reports the power interpretation; Abū ʿAbd Allāh and Samāʿa say spirit comes from God's power or malakūt without diminishing Him; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it active power emanating from essential power. Abū Jaʿfar calls it a specially chosen created spirit, favored above other spirits, from which Adam was breathed. [7 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,سماعة); STUDYQURAN(many commentators); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 15:29/q08 Are human souls all of one species?  [رُّوحِى · theology]
turns on: Whether shared faculties establish one essence, or persistent personal and prophetic differences imply distinct soul kinds.
- 15:29/q08/p1 Human souls share one species. — Ālūsī reports Ghazālī and the Shaykh, but says the Shaykh supplies no proof. Advocates argue that essential difference would make souls composite and bodily, that all people share capacities for knowledge and moral traits, and that all self-knowing immaterial selves apprehend the same cognitive and volitional essence. Ālūsī replies that composition need not imply body, induction of equal capacities is inadequate, and the self-knowledge argument would assimilate all separate beings. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الغزالي والشيخ من الفلاسفة,غيره); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 15:29/q08/p2 Souls may differ in species. — Ālūsī reports a small group and the author of al-Muʿtabar, who cites differences in knowledge, strength, anger, restraint and prophetic power despite similar temperaments. Ālūsī regards this as weak persuasion, while his critiques leave common species unproved. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شرذمة وصاحب المعتبر,صاحب المعتبر); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 15:29/q09 Did individual human souls exist before their bodies?  [رُّوحِى · theology]
turns on: How souls become distinct, whether origination requires bodily matter, and how reports of prior spirits or everlasting souls are understood.
- 15:29/q09/p1 They originate when particular bodies become fit to receive them. — Ālūsī reports Ghazālī, Aristotle and followers, comparing the soul to an image appearing in a polished mirror. Ghazālī argues one preexistent soul cannot divide and already multiple souls lack individuating matter or attributes; Ālūsī further grounds individuality in bodily attachment and persisting self-awareness. He answers division, accidental distinction and transmigration objections, yet records objections about circular or infinite differentiation, essential differences, earlier bodies and endless accidents; he calls al-Muʿtabar's memory argument weak. [20 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المعترضون,حجة الإسلام,صاحب المعتبر)]
- 15:29/q09/p2 Souls preexist bodies, or are eternal. — Ālūsī reports the ungraded two-thousand-years-prior spirits report and the ancient philosophers' arguments: origination would require matter, infinite prior bodies would yield impossible infinite surviving souls, and originated things could not be everlasting. Ghazālī instead reads the report as angels preceding cosmic bodies; Ālūsī rejects the inferences from matter, infinity and everlastingness. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قدماء الفلاسفة); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 15:29/q10 What do reports of Muhammad's being a prophet before Adam's completion mean?  [رُّوحِى · reports]
turns on: Whether 'first' means temporal existence or priority in divine plan.
- 15:29/q10/p1 They say Muhammad was first in creation and already a prophet while Adam was between water and clay. — Ālūsī cites two Prophetic sayings. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه الصلاة والسلام)]
- 15:29/q10/p2 They refer to divine decree and intended purpose, not Muhammad's actual prior bodily existence. — Ālūsī reports an architectural analogy: the final house is first in design; God's plan and the Preserved Tablet precede realization. Human creation aims at nearness through prophecy, completed in Muhammad, the final historical prophet and purest descendant of Adam. The Pen and Tablet can be understood as spiritual teacher and learner; 96:4 supports the analogy. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 15:29/q11 Why is the spirit called رُّوحِى, 'My spirit'?  [مِن رُّوحِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the possessive chiefly marks ownership and honor, immediate creation, likeness in certain qualities, or power as the spirit's source.
- 15:29/q11/p1 It is God's created possession, singled out for honor and distinction. — Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī explain an honorific ownership genitive; Qurṭubī compares God's house, camel, earth, sky and month and links 4:171, as does Bayḍāwī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls rūḥ a generic class term; Abū Jaʿfar compares God's attributed created image to the Kaʿba; Abū Ḥayyān adds direct creation and placement. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]
- 15:29/q11/p2 The attribution marks direct divine creation without an intermediary and special nearness. — Bursevī says God personally shaped Adam and breathed, linking the divine breath to knowledge of all gracious and majestic Names and to the 'shadow' of 25:45; al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya relates special rank to 50:16. Ghazālī says no intermediate origin or matter was used and also allows honorific attribution; Abū Ḥayyān likewise mentions direct creation. [6 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(حجة الإسلام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 15:29/q11/p3 The spirit's likeness to divine qualities or its origin in divine power explains the attribution. — Ghazālī compares sunlight from the sun and cites spacelessness and universal knowledge while distinguishing degrees; Ālūsī says creaturely dependence preserves God's uniqueness. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's reports derive spirit from God's power without treating it as a lost divine part; Study Quran reports a power reading. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(حجة الإسلام); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,سماعة); STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]

## 15:29/q12 Why is the divine act phrased in the singular here?  [نَفَخْتُ · rhetoric]
- 15:29/q12/p1 Adam's spirit was breathed directly after his creation, whereas Mary's conception through Gabriel uses the plural of majesty. — Sāmarrāʾī makes the contrast. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 15:29/q13 What does the فَ in فَقَعُوا۟ convey?  [فَقَعُوا۟ · grammar]
- 15:29/q13/p1 It links the command to the 'when' condition, marking immediate succession after formation and breathing. — Rāzī emphasizes no intervening delay; Nasafī calls the imperative the answer to idhā and infers that a command can precede its execution. Iṣlāḥī likewise says the angels received the command before Adam's creation, to act after his perfection and breathing. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 15:29/q13/p2 The falling diction conveys easy, prompt obedience. — Biqāʿī says the angels fell almost involuntarily once commanded. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 15:29/q14 What action is commanded by فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ?  [فَقَعُوا۟ سَٰجِدِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the falling verb requires bodily prostration or can describe a lesser gesture.
- 15:29/q14/p1 The angels must fall down in actual prostration before Adam. — Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect faqʿū to waqaʿa, 'fall'; Dāmghānī notes waqaʿa can mean fall prostrate. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the open qāf before a guttural and cites Abū al-Akhzar's poetic 'prostrating head'; Ālūsī and Bursevī reject mere bowing. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says face-down falling intensifies submission. [15 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الأخزر الحماني); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس)-]
- 15:29/q14/p2 The command means deference, submission, a gesture, or mere bowing rather than ordinary physical prostration. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this interpretation to reject it; Ālūsī also rejects the suggested bowing because the verb says to fall down. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; BURSEVI-]

## 15:29/q15 How is سَٰجِدِينَ parsed?  [سَٰجِدِينَ · grammar]
- 15:29/q15/p1 It is accusative as a circumstantial qualifier. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the parsing. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 15:29/q16 To whom was the angels' prostration directed?  [لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'to him' makes Adam the honored recipient of prostration or its direction while God remains the object of worship.
- 15:29/q16/p1 It was directed to Adam as a greeting and honor, while worship remained God's alone. — Ṭabarī, al-Kalbī, Wāḥidī, Abū al-Qāsim, Baghawī and Qurṭubī distinguish greeting from worship; Wāḥidī and Study Quran compare Joseph's family in 12:100 and his dream in 12:4. Qurṭubī says God may favor whom He wills, including prophets over angels; Biqāʿī, Bursevī and Elmalılı emphasize honoring Adam in obedience to God. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو القاسم); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 15:29/q16/p2 The angels worshipped God with Adam serving as their qibla or direction. — Abū ʿAlī compares Adam with the Kaʿba; Qurṭubī and Study Quran report this view; Ālūsī presents both alternatives and cites Ḥassān's line about facing the qibla; Bursevī compares Adam displaying divine marvels to a qibla. Rāzī states the exegetical question. [6 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI]

## 15:29/q17 Which angels were commanded to prostrate?  [فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether this general command includes the highest angels in light of 7:206.
- 15:29/q17/p1 The command may include all heavenly angels. — Rāzī records this as one scope allowed by the general wording. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 15:29/q17/p2 The command may include only some angels or the angels of earth, excluding those nearest God. — Rāzī reports the narrower scopes and an argument from 7:206 that the nearest angels prostrate to God alone; he says that specific statement takes priority over this verse's general wording. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض الناس)]

## 15:29/q18 What happened when angels were commanded to prostrate?  [فَقَعُوا۟ لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the narrated refusal involved earlier groups of angels as well as Iblīs.
- 15:29/q18/p1 Successive angelic groups refused and were burned, then another group obeyed while Iblīs remained among the first unbelievers. — Ṭabarī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās; Ibn ʿAṭiyya repeats Ṭabarī's report, saying another group was created and obeyed except Iblīs. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 15:29/q18/p2 The angels obeyed, but Iblīs refused because he judged Adam by clay against his own fire. — Kūran Yolu says Iblīs preferred limited reasoning over God's command, missed Adam's spiritual origin and was cursed until Resurrection; Study Quran also says he overlooked the Hands, Spirit and knowledge by focusing on material origin. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 15:29/q19 Why was Adam worthy of the angels' prostration?  [لَهُۥ سَٰجِدِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the prostration indicates Adam's superior station or a trial of angels considered superior.
- 15:29/q19/p1 His perfected form, spirit, knowledge and divinely granted qualities made him worthy of honor. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says worth lies in qualities and effects rather than material or conventional purity; Elmalılı and Iṣlāḥī tie elevation to spirit and perfected ability; Kūran Yolu ties rank to a transcendent spirit explained to angels by 2:30,33. Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya adds perfected form and knowledge; Study Quran reports the interpretation that humans stood above angels before the fall. Sulamī's inward reports add repentance, names and nearness. [10 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); STUDYQURAN; SULAMI(أبو عثمان,بعضهم)]
- 15:29/q19/p2 The command tested superior angels, giving them an opportunity for great reward. — Qurṭubī reports al-Qaffāl's Muʿtazilite account, which maintains angelic superiority over Adam. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القفال)]

## 15:29/q20 What inward meaning is found in Adam's preparation and the angels' submission?  [سَوَّيْتُهُۥ رُّوحِى سَٰجِدِينَ · inward]
turns on: The reports variously focus on Adam's disclosed qualities, the human duty to preserve them, and the perfected person's symbolic station.
- 15:29/q20/p1 Adam's spirit receives special divine nearness, knowledge and will, so angelic dispute yields to submission. — Abū ʿUthmān names spirit and repentance; al-Wāsiṭī says the spirit's knowledge and will depend on God's knowing and willing, its own action and speech are effaced, and its formed, attributed state brings it near. Another Sulamī report says the angels first saw only the form, then recognized spirit, repentance, names and unseen friendship. Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says the spirit descends through angels, jinn, devils and animals, gathering their qualities before joining Adam's specially prepared form. [8 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,الواسطي,بعضهم); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 15:29/q20/p2 The divine spark enables ascent and connection with God when preserved, and spiritual darkness when squandered. — Iṣlāḥī links the perfected abilities and divine spark to the angelic honor and says neglect makes a human worse than an animal. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 15:29/q20/p3 The perfected person's prayer can mirror both sides of Adam's prostration at an inward level. — Bursevī interprets a perfected shaykh's funeral prayer as the spirit both prostrating and being prostrated to, while the outward legal prayer remains supplication. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 15:29/q21 Does intellect and faith persist after bodily death?  [رُّوحِى · theology]
- 15:29/q21/p1 Intellect, perception, faith and sainthood belong to the spirit and persist after death. — Bursevī recounts a dream in which his shaykh performed ablution after death, ascended, returned for his own funeral and affirmed continuing earthly intellect and perception; Bursevī infers these spiritual qualities do not change. [3 notes: BURSEVI]

## 15:29/q22 What rule about washing the deceased does Bursevī draw?  [رُّوحِى · law]
- 15:29/q22/p1 The deceased should be washed because death agony ends ablution; even a perfected believer follows the outward rule. — Bursevī says the complete believer remains pure since ritual hadath is not impurity, yet outwardly lacks ablution and is washed like an imperfect person. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 15:29/q23 What human tendencies follow from the spirit's transcendent origin?  [رُّوحِى · interpretation]
- 15:29/q23/p1 Humans seek perfection, knowledge, freedom and power, but need sound knowledge and freedom from transient passions to sustain their spiritual bond. — Kūran Yolu relates human worth to spirit and distinguishes true from illusory perfection. It cites a Sufi on an inward Pharaoh-like claim, and Ghazālī on the spirit's dislike of servitude, love of mastery and self-preservation, desire to use or know beings, and knowledge, will and power as true perfection and divine attributes. [7 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Gazzâlî,أحد كبار الصوفية)]

## 15:29/q24 How are commands addressed to angels grammatically gendered?  [فَقَعُوا۟ · grammar]
- 15:29/q24/p1 They use masculine forms throughout the Qur'an. — Sāmarrāʾī cites اسجدوا, أنبئوني and فقعوا له ساجدين. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH]

## 15:29/q25 How does this verse relate to other Qur'anic passages?  [* · links]
- 15:29/q25/p1 It repeats and echoes other creation and prostration accounts. — Study Quran says it repeats 38:72, compares 32:9, and cites 2:34, 7:11, 17:61, 18:50, 20:116 and 38:73–74; it also compares Joseph's family's bowing in 12:100 with his dream in 12:4. Rāzī compares breathing into Adam and Mary, noting that the wording about Jesus in 66:12 can grammatically refer to Mary in whose womb he was. Kūran Yolu says repeated accounts vary with context to teach origin and Creator. [5 notes: STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 15:29/q25/p2 Within this sūra, Makkan idolaters' rebellion is juxtaposed with Iblīs's refusal to bow. — Study Quran's introduction draws the comparison. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
