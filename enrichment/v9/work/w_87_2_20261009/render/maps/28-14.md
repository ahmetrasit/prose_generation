# 28:14: 18 questions

## 28:14/q01 What does reaching Moses's ashudd denote, and at what age?  [بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ashudd marks puberty, peak bodily growth, a broad life span, or a fixed reported age; al-Ālūsī questions fixing an age without a reliable report.
- 28:14/q01/p1 Completion or peak of physical strength and growth, without a fixed age. — Abū ʿUbayda and al-Ṭabarī take the expression as vigor; al-Zajjāj says utmost bodily power; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say growth no longer increases. Biqāʿī includes all capacities. Al-Ālūsī prefers this general sense because timing varies by place, era and circumstance and a precise age needs a reliable report; he questions the inference from 46:15. Ibn Qutayba refers back to an earlier explanation. Abū Isḥāq allows the literal sense of attaining maturity. [14 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:14/q01/p2 Thirty-three years. — Mujāhid, Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās are repeatedly cited; al-Thaʿlabī's Ibn ʿAbbās report also says Adam's descendants were allotted sixty years and invokes 35:37. Sources commonly pair thirty-three for ashudd with forty for istiwāʾ; al-Kalbī is also reported for thirty-three. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس,سائر المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وقتادة,الكلبي,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس,مجاهد وغيره); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد، ابن عباس); TABRISI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 28:14/q01/p3 Puberty or adolescence, approximately fifteen years. — Rabīʿa, Zayd ibn Aslam, Mālik and other exegetes identify the stage with puberty; al-Qurṭubī prefers it, citing the marriage-age test in 4:6. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports roughly fifteen, Muḥammad ibn Qays fifteen, and Iṣlāḥī calls it adolescence, requiring later poise. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ربيعة,زيد بن أسلم,مالك); MAWARDI-FULL(ربيعة ومالك,محمد بن قيس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); STUDYQURAN(R); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL(ربيعة ومالك)+]
- 28:14/q01/p4 Eighteen years. — Muqātil and Ibn Jubayr give eighteen; another group reports it, and a report from Imam al-Ṣādiq gives eighteen. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls eighteen the usual age. [5 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 28:14/q01/p5 Twenty years. — Al-Suddī, Mujāhid, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and other reports give twenty. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي)]
- 28:14/q01/p6 Forty years. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās and other exegetes identify full strength or full maturity itself with forty, though many other reports apply forty to istiwāʾ. [7 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 28:14/q01/p7 Eighteen through thirty years. — Al-Kalbī and Ibn ʿAbbās give a bodily-strength span; Ibn ʿAbbās describes a plateau from thirty to forty, then decline. Rāzī endorses the trajectory, observing that bodily power wanes while intellect increases. Bursawī gives the same span. [6 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)+]
- 28:14/q01/p8 Thirty to forty years. — Al-Zajjāj gives this range; some support it from 46:15, which couples full strength and forty, though al-Ālūsī says the inference is difficult. Al-Bayḍāwī associates this span with completed growth and maturing reason. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+]
- 28:14/q01/p9 Thirty-four years. — Sufyān al-Thawrī supplies thirty-four; al-Qurṭubī presents it as the farthest limit of ashudd. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سفيان الثوري); MAWARDI-FULL(سفيان); QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان الثوري)]
- 28:14/q01/p10 Thirty years. — Al-Suddī, an unnamed report and another group give thirty. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 28:14/q01/p11 Twenty-five years. — ʿIkrima and another group give twenty-five. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 28:14/q01/p12 A few years after thirty, without an exact year. — Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Wāḥidī likewise says above thirty. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 28:14/q01/p13 Between thirty-three and thirty-nine. — Reported by al-Zajjāj as one view. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل)]
- 28:14/q01/p14 Eighteen through forty. — Al-Wāḥidī attributes this range to Muqātil. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 28:14/q01/p15 Fifteen through forty. — Reported by al-Jishumī as one interpretation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 28:14/q01/p16 Eighteen through eighty. — Al-Jishumī attributes this unusually broad span to al-Kalbī. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الكلبي)]
- 28:14/q01/p17 Sixty-three years. — Al-Jishumī attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda while also saying istiwāʾ occurred at forty. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس وقتادة)]
- 28:14/q01/p18 Approximately seventeen through forty. — Al-Ālūsī reports this from al-Zajjāj. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 28:14/q01/p19 Thirty-six years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to a large group. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة عظيمة)]
- 28:14/q01/p20 The point at which proof and moral responsibility are complete. — Al-Ḥasan defines the stage by completion of obligation rather than a number. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن)]
- 28:14/q01/p21 Visible maturity with complete physical and mental strength. — Al-Jishumī reports signs of maturity and both faculties becoming complete. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 28:14/q02 What is the grammatical number and singular of ashudd?  [أَشُدَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Different analyses take the form as a plural of shidda or shadd, a plural without singular, or a singular noun; authorities' attributions differ.
- 28:14/q02/p1 It is the plural of shidda. — Sībawayh's analysis is reported by al-Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿAṭiyya; al-Jishumī and al-Nasafī compare niʿma/anʿum, while Abū al-Haytham adds the sense of strength and toughness. Al-Māwardī instead attributes shidda to al-Kisāʾī; al-Naḥḥās also lists the alternatives. [6 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); MAWARDI-FULL(الكسائي); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); RAZI-FULL(أبو الهيثم); NASAFI-FULL]
- 28:14/q02/p2 It is the plural of shadd. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Kisāʾī are cited by Abū ʿUbayda, likening shadd/ashudd to baḥr/abḥur; al-Māwardī assigns this to Sībawayh. Rāzī says al-Farrāʾ treats shadd as analogical but unattested. Attributions vary across sources. [5 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS(سيبويه); MAWARDI-FULL(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره); RAZI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 28:14/q02/p3 It is plural in sense but has no singular of its own form. — Abū ʿUbayda gives this analysis, also transmitted by al-Māwardī. Bursawī describes a singular form built on a plural pattern. [3 notes: MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BURSEVI]
- 28:14/q02/p4 It is a singular noun. — Al-Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿAṭiyya record this alternative. [2 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 28:14/q02/p5 Ashadd is not the only afʿal singular without tāʾ. — Abū Isḥāq cites the lexicographers' aṣbaʿ; al-Naḥḥās calls denial of this comparison mistaken. [1 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق)]

## 28:14/q03 What does Moses's istiwāʾ denote, and at what age?  [وَٱسْتَوَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether istiwāʾ completes bodily form, intellectual poise, or both, and whether reports fix it at forty or another age.
- 28:14/q03/p1 Mature, settled completion or balance of body and youth, without a fixed age. — Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and al-Zamakhsharī describe settled completion; al-Zamakhsharī cites al-Laqīṭ's poetry. Ibn Shajara emphasizes balanced bodily power; al-Bayḍāwī allows stature or intellect. Al-Ālūsī calls it explanatory of the preceding term. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says timing varies by person. [16 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة,ابن قتيبة); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:14/q03/p2 Forty years. — A large set of reports pairs forty with earlier bodily strength; Bursawī and al-Ālūsī cite 46:15, and al-Ālūsī mentions a poem and calls forty usual but not certain. The Study Quran relates forty to the traditional prophetic age and Muḥammad's first revelation. [36 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد، قتادة، زيد بن اسلم، الثوري); THALABI(ابن عباس,سائر المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وقتادة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس,مجاهد وغيره); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة); TABRISI(مجاهد وقتادة وابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(Q,Ṭ)]
- 28:14/q03/p3 Completion of intellectual power, judgment, or temperamental poise. — Rāzī prefers this distinction from bodily strength, explaining that intellect increases as physical power wanes. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ālūsī and Iṣlāḥī prefer mental completion; Iṣlāḥī says youth without poise is insufficient. Al-Junayd adds sound insight and readiness for divine address; the Study Quran reports adult sure footing. [10 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL(الجنيد); BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj,R); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:14/q03/p4 Bodily completeness, shape and stature. — Rāzī records bodily-constitution readings; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares crops that thicken and stand upright in 48:29, distinguishing Moses's full tall build from Joseph's. A Prophetic description likens Moses to men of Shanūʾa, helping explain his fatal blow. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 28:14/q03/p5 Growth of Moses's beard. — Ibn Qubayṣa/Abū Qubayṣa, Ibn Qutayba and an Imam al-Ṣādiq report make the sign of maturity the beard. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن قبيصة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); DURR-FULL(أبو قبيصة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 28:14/q03/p6 The span from thirty to forty, followed by decline. — Ibn ʿAbbās report transmitted by al-Suyūṭī and al-Ālūsī. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 28:14/q03/p7 Sixty years. — Makkī and Ibn Jarīr are separately reported for sixty; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the view weak. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير)]
- 28:14/q03/p8 A settled adulthood between twenty-one and forty-two. — Biqāʿī gives this interval after youth is complete. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:14/q03/p9 A stage from twenty-two to beyond forty. — Al-Jishumī reports this further age range. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 28:14/q04 How do ashudd and istiwāʾ relate to each other?  [بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ وَٱسْتَوَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the second term repeats the first or names a further completion, and whether that further completion is intellectual or physical.
- 28:14/q04/p1 They are synonymous or the second reinforces the first. — Rāzī reports one view of balanced temperament and mature constitution; al-Ālūsī calls istiwāʾ explanatory; some commentators in the Study Quran agree. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects the synonymous reading. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 28:14/q04/p2 Ashudd is bodily strength; istiwāʾ is mature intellect and judgment. — Rāzī favors this, as physical power wanes while intellect increases around forty; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī prefer it, the latter allowing age to vary by circumstance. Iṣlāḥī says youthful ability needs poise; the Study Quran places psychological maturity around forty. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:14/q04/p3 Ashudd is strength or adulthood; istiwāʾ is completed bodily form. — Rāzī reports the strength/form and adulthood/perfection distinctions. Ibn ʿĀshūr holds that the latter term describes Moses's distinctive stature, comparing 48:29. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:14/q04/p4 They mark distinguishable stages, but the exact distinction is left open. — Ibn al-Jawzī says commentators distinguish them and directs the reader to his earlier discussions of Joseph and al-Anʿām. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 28:14/q05 What are the ḥukm and ʿilm given to Moses?  [حُكْمًۭا وَعِلْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gift is prophethood itself, preparatory wisdom and learning, or specific faculties or religious texts; opponents of prophethood invoke narrative sequence and the reward clause.
- 28:14/q05/p1 Wisdom, sound understanding or reason, with religious knowledge before prophethood. — Mujāhid says religious learning, reason and action before prophethood; Ibn Isḥāq specifies Moses's and his forefathers' religion, its laws and limits. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and others give overlapping wisdom, jurisprudence and knowledge glosses. Rāzī prefers this against prophethood, citing wisdom paired with revelation at 33:34, prior upright character, and the reward clause. Al-Ālūsī prefers a prior gift because of narrative order and 28:13. Variants specify Abraham's law, God, or interests of both worlds. Al-Suddī specifically glosses ʿilm as understanding. [39 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد,محمد بن اسحاق); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,مجاهد,محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(السدي,مجاهد,محمد بن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,من ذهب إلى أن الإيتاء كان قبل الهجرة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:14/q05/p2 Prophethood and its accompanying knowledge or religious science. — Al-Suddī and Mujāhid are cited; Rāzī says the waw cannot itself date this relative to the killing. Al-Ālūsī reports a response that the gift summarizes the promised future mission after 28:13, while later verses detail it. Other versions specify religious law or both worlds. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول,السدي,بعضهم); JISHUMI-; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; STUDYQURAN(R)-]
- 28:14/q05/p3 The bearing and conduct of wise, learned people before prophethood. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī say Moses did nothing foolish. Al-Bayḍāwī says this fits narrative order; al-Ālūsī explicitly prefers that fit over commissioning before the subsequent killing and migration. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:14/q05/p4 Knowledge is the Torah, and ḥukm is the prophets' Sunna. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 33:34, the pairing of Scripture and wisdom in the Prophet's wives' houses; al-Ālūsī reports his view. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 28:14/q05/p5 Ḥukm is intellect or rational judgment. — ʿIkrima says intellect; Asad calls it ability to distinguish right from wrong, Iṣlāḥī decision-making power, and the Study Quran reports judgment between people. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(M)]
- 28:14/q05/p6 Ḥukm is wisdom, and ʿilm is knowledge of God. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this pairing; Asad speaks of deep spiritual consciousness; Iṣlāḥī adds that both capacities have levels and here precede prophetic wisdom. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:14/q05/p7 Ḥukm is understanding and intellect; ʿilm is prophethood. — Mujāhid's report in al-Durr assigns prophethood specifically to ʿilm. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 28:14/q05/p8 Ḥukm is knowledge. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this direct gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 28:14/q05/p9 Ḥukm is strength. — Al-Māwardī transmits Mujāhid's gloss. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 28:14/q05/p10 Ḥukm is the Quran. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim records this alternative from Mujāhid. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد)]
- 28:14/q05/p11 The gift consists of the disciplines of sacred law. — Al-Jishumī lists this separately among the alternatives. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 28:14/q05/p12 Ḥukm and ʿilm are theoretical and practical wisdom. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading of the pair. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 28:14/q05/p13 God granted judgment and knowledge, with no narrower gloss. — Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Kathīr affirm the gift without identifying a more specific referent. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 28:14/q05/p14 Prophetic wisdom before formal investiture, with knowledge of religious matters. — The Study Quran transmits Ṭ's distinction between prophetic wisdom and formal investiture. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 28:14/q06 When did Moses receive this gift relative to his mission and the killing?  [ءَاتَيْنَٰهُ حُكْمًۭا وَعِلْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause describes prior preparation or anticipates a later mission, and whether waw establishes chronology.
- 28:14/q06/p1 Before prophethood, and before the killing of the Egyptian. — Mujāhid, al-Zajjāj and other interpreters make it preparation; Rāzī explicitly places the gifts before the killing. Ibn ʿĀshūr says commissioning occurred only after Moses left Midian, citing 28:29. Al-Ālūsī traces the subsequent killing, migration and return, while Iṣlāḥī also puts prophetic wisdom later. [21 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(من ذهب إلى أن الإيتاء كان قبل الهجرة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:14/q06/p2 The verse anticipates Moses's later prophetic commission after his migration and return. — Al-Kāshifī places the commission after the Midian migration, on return to Egypt; al-Ālūsī reports supporters who take this verse as a summary of the promise in 28:13, later told in detail. Ibn ʿĀshūr points to 28:29. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:14/q06/p3 The conjunction does not determine whether prophethood preceded or followed the killing. — Rāzī says the waw introducing the entry into the city lacks necessary sequence; al-Ālūsī's reported prophethood advocates likewise say waw need not order events. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول)]
- 28:14/q06/p4 Moses attained the described spiritual maturity after the events beginning in verse 15. — Asad cites Moses's statement at 26:20 as evidence. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 28:14/q07 How did preprophetic learning affect Moses's standing among the Israelites?  [ءَاتَيْنَٰهُ حُكْمًۭا وَعِلْمًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the notes describe a following, active leadership and inspired knowledge, or palace education and moral awareness.
- 28:14/q07/p1 He knew ancestral religion and had followers among the Israelites. — Ibn Isḥāq specifies the rules and limits of Moses's and his fathers' religion; al-Wāḥidī says Israelites listened and followed, while al-Qurṭubī gives the number nine before prophethood. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق)]
- 28:14/q07/p2 He led Israelites before migration and knew useful ancestral teachings through inspiration or transmitted reports. — Al-Ālūsī says Israelites brought important affairs to him and obeyed his commands; knowledge could be inspired or inferred from prophetic ancestors' beliefs and laws or reliable reports. He considers this perhaps preferable to a Kashshāf report. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(من ذهب إلى أن الإيتاء كان قبل الهجرة); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:14/q07/p3 Palace education accompanied God's gift, and Moses already saw Pharaoh's injustice. — Kur'an Yolu says Moses was educated in the palace and before his mission recognized Pharaoh's oppression of the Israelites. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:14/q08 Must prophets be commissioned at forty?  [وَٱسْتَوَىٰٓ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُ حُكْمًۭا وَعِلْمًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the report about forty is universal; the opposing note supplies younger prophetic examples.
- 28:14/q08/p1 A report says every prophet was commissioned at forty. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī record the universal claim; Bursawī says most hold Muḥammad was commissioned at forty and some extend this to all prophets. The Study Quran calls forty the traditional age of prophethood and Muḥammad's first revelation. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الجمهور); STUDYQURAN(Q); BURSEVI(بعضهم)-]
- 28:14/q08/p2 Forty is not a requirement for every prophet. — Bursawī reports Jesus at thirty-three, Joseph at eighteen, and John at two or three; he also cites Sahl al-Tustarī's spiritual knowledge before puberty. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 28:14/q09 Why is this stage fitting for Moses's divine gift or mission?  [وَلَمَّا بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ وَٱسْتَوَىٰٓ ءَاتَيْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give preparation and miraculous divine choice as distinct explanations.
- 28:14/q09/p1 Mature intellect, insight and character prepare him for divine address and prophethood. — Al-Junayd says readiness follows sound insight and complete reason. Al-Qushayrī says God completes the preparation of eminent people and prophets. Rāzī calls prophethood the highest rank requiring knowledge and uprightness; al-Biqāʿī calls wisdom and knowledge anticipatory preparation. [6 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(الجنيد)]
- 28:14/q09/p2 God's choice at this age signals exceptional, unacquired growth beyond ordinary human decline. — Biqāʿī cites 36:68: ordinary people stop gaining faculties after youth, then decline; chosen prophets and righteous followers can still gain strength and knowledge without acquisition, making Moses's innate gift miraculous. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:14/q10 What does wisdom or knowledge require of its possessor?  [حُكْمًۭا وَعِلْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe practical action and inward self-knowledge as dimensions of the gift.
- 28:14/q10/p1 A truly wise and learned person acts on knowledge; failure to act is ignorance. — Al-Zajjāj cites 2:102; al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī and al-Nasafī repeat that acting is integral. Al-Biqāʿī defines wisdom as perfected action through knowledge, and says knowledge is supported by wisdom. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:14/q10/p2 The gifts include clear self-knowledge and awareness of new favors from God. — Al-Junayd's inward gloss ties the gift to readiness for divine address. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]

## 28:14/q11 Who are the muḥsinīn in the reward clause?  [ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the clause identifies believers, the historical exemplars Moses and his mother, or a quality of virtuous conduct.
- 28:14/q11/p1 Those who believe in God or affirm His oneness. — Muqātil says believers; al-Kalbī says people of monotheism. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل)]
- 28:14/q11/p2 Moses, his mother, and by extension everyone who does good. — Al-Qurṭubī cites her obedience and trust in placing Moses in the river and God's safe return of him; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Biqāʿī extend the pattern. The Study Quran reports the mother's reward as her son, his return, and his intelligence. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 28:14/q11/p3 People who use their abilities fully and well. — Iṣlāḥī says wisdom rewards this use, whereas unused youth and abilities are wasted. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:14/q11/p4 People endowed with virtues who walk the path of justice. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward characterization. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 28:14/q12 What is the recompense promised to doers of good, and how is it related to the gift?  [وَكَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِى ٱلْمُحْسِنِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gift itself is recompense, and whether prophethood can be described as a reward for deeds.
- 28:14/q12/p1 God grants wisdom and knowledge as reward for obedience and goodness, leading to Paradise. — Al-Ṭabarī names Moses's obedience, patience and avoidance of prohibitions and extends reward to every obedient messenger and servant. Al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq, al-Wāḥidī and al-Nasafī explain that knowledge and wisdom lead to Paradise. Ibn Isḥāq connects the gift with the reward formula; Iṣlāḥī links wisdom to using ability well. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:14/q12/p2 God repays Moses's mother, Moses, and other good-doers with deliverance and further gifts. — Al-Qurṭubī lists his mother's trust, her child's safe return and Moses's later reason, wisdom and prophethood; al-Biqāʿī stresses their reliance on God alone. The Study Quran reports a son, his return and judgment; Bursawī says those who join their pious company receive the best recompense. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 28:14/q12/p3 Prophethood itself is not earned by deeds. — Al-Jishumī says Moses's character qualified him for the mission, not that deeds earned it. Rāzī argues that calling prophethood recompense would imply every good-doer becomes a prophet; al-Ālūsī says it sits uneasily with the clause. The Study Quran transmits Rāzī's merit objection. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول)-]
- 28:14/q12/p4 On a prophethood reading, the clause may generalize reward, or reward higher degrees of prophetic nearness. — Al-Ālūsī reports defenders who count God's gifts to Moses and his mother as recompense by generalizing attribution; he allows increased nearness to God as recompense while denying that prophethood itself is earned. Prophets differ in nearness. He also notes that the every-benefactor objection need not defeat the reading. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول)]
- 28:14/q12/p5 Divine favors arrive at their appointed time, so seekers should remain patient. — Bursawī says delay should not cause despair and illustrates repayment of charity with a report of a beggar, a wolf taking a donor's child, her prayer, and the child's rescue unharmed. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 28:14/q13 What inward stages are described by Moses's maturity?  [بَلَغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ وَٱسْتَوَىٰٓ · inward]
turns on: The inward readings identify different balances or stages.
- 28:14/q13/p1 Ashudd is spiritual youth and perfected primordial nature; istiwāʾ is perfection followed by detachment from the soul's attributes. — Al-Qāshānī maps both successive stages onto spiritual development. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 28:14/q13/p2 Istiwaʾ is balance of hope and fear, humility and dignity, dependence and autonomy. — The Study Quran transmits Aj's inward account of the poise at which true wisdom starts. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 28:14/q14 How does this wording relate to Joseph's story and other Quranic parallels?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes explain the added wording through Moses's stature or the different age of revelation, while also noting broader parallels.
- 28:14/q14/p1 It closely parallels Joseph 12:22 and the same recompense formula elsewhere. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī refer explanation to Joseph; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes Joseph lacks the added istawāʾ, which he relates to Moses's tall full build. Asad notes near identity. The Study Quran cites 6:84, 12:22, 37:80, 105, 110, 121, 131, 77:44, and its notes at 29:69 and 37:80. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:14/q14/p2 Joseph received revelation in youth, whereas Moses received it after forty, explaining why their maturity wording differs. — Bursawī contrasts 12:22, which mentions only full strength, with this verse's added istiwāʾ. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 28:14/q14/p3 The maturity language also relates to legal independence of orphans. — The Study Quran links 12:22, 6:152, 17:34 and the maturity-age discussion at 4:6. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 28:14/q15 How does verse 14 fit the surrounding account of Moses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the maturity notice summarizes an earlier stage or later spiritual growth.
- 28:14/q15/p1 It is a chronological interlude after the promise to Moses's mother was fulfilled. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the promise in 28:7 was fulfilled by the return in 28:13, prompting this overview. Al-Ālūsī also tests readings against the narrative sequence; Asad instead places spiritual maturity after the events beginning at 28:15, citing 26:20. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:14/q15/p2 The telling of Moses and Pharaoh varies in wording, order and detail across retellings for distinct nuances. — Al-Burhān surveys 28:3–43 and contrasts Joseph's collected narrative in one sūra with earlier scripture's separate single tellings. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 28:14/q15/p3 Moses grew up with Pharaoh and his wife after weaning, before the later killing and flight to Midian. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports his childhood household; the Study Quran summarizes the killing in 28:15–20 and flight in 28:21–22. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); STUDYQURAN]
- 28:14/q15/p4 The described maturity followed the events beginning at verse 15. — Asad cites 26:20. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 28:14/q16 What circumstances and location are reported for Moses's entry into the city in the next verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ on the city's identification and whether Moses entered at midday or at night.
- 28:14/q16/p1 Moses came from Khanin, two farsakhs away, at midday. — Muqātil names the village, distance and time. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 28:14/q16/p2 He concealed his opposition to Pharaoh and entered the city at night. — Kur'an Yolu surmises that his opposition spread among relatives, leading him to hide. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:14/q16/p3 The city was the Egyptian city where Pharaoh resided. — Kur'an Yolu attributes this to most commentators. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğunluğu)]
- 28:14/q16/p4 The city was Aynişems, formerly an independent settlement. — Attributed to al-Ḍaḥḥāk by Kur'an Yolu. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Dahhâk)]
- 28:14/q16/p5 The city was Cairo. — Attributed to al-Shawkānī by Kur'an Yolu. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 28:14/q16/p6 The city was Memphis, northern Egypt's center. — Kur'an Yolu reports this identification from some. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:14/q17 What is said of the sūra's revelation setting?  [* · sciences]
- 28:14/q17/p1 The whole of al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī transmits this from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:14/q18 What ancillary Arabic poetic usage is recorded with the verse's discussion?  [* · grammar]
