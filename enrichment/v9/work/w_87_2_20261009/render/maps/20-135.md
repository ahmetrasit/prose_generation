# 20:135: 23 questions

## 20:135/q01 Who is waiting, and for what?  [كُلٌّۭ مُّتَرَبِّصٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reciprocal wait names an unspecified outcome or a particular death, calamity, triumph, or demanded judgment.
- 20:135/q01/p1 The Prophet's party and its opponents each await how their affair ends and which side prevails. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Jishumī extend the wait to God's command and Resurrection; al-Rāzī includes before death, at death, and after death. Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions relief. Al-Biqāʿī relates mutual waiting to persistent objections to guidance and punishment. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكبير); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:135/q01/p2 The unbelievers await Muhammad's death, while he awaits punishment for them in this life. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā name the Meccan expectation; al-Baghawī and al-Bursawī report their hope that time's reversals would remove him. Al-Jishumī transmits the contrast as another view. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 20:135/q01/p3 Believers await God's promised triumph or punishment of opponents, while opponents await a setback for believers. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī contrast the promise with the opponents' hoped calamity; Ibn al-Jawzī specifies worldly punishment. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ adds the advance of religion and completion of divine light. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); TABATABAI]
- 20:135/q01/p4 Each side awaits the other's destruction. — Al-Jishumī transmits this as another explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 20:135/q01/p5 Everyone hopes that their chosen way of life will prove right. — Asad extends the statement across beliefs and circumstances. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:135/q01/p6 The Prophet waits for the punishment demanded by his opponents, while they are told to wait for it. — Iṣlāḥī connects this to demands for a sign or promised doom and says the Prophet's task is warning. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:135/q02 What does tarabbuṣ mean?  [مُّتَرَبِّصٌۭ فَتَرَبَّصُوا۟ · meaning]
- 20:135/q02/p1 It means deliberate waiting or awaiting. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses deliberateness; al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ gloss it as waiting. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:135/q03 Why is mutarabbis singular after kull?  [كُلٌّۭ مُّتَرَبِّصٌۭ · grammar]
- 20:135/q03/p1 Its singular form agrees with the grammatical form of kull. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī state this agreement; Abū Ḥayyān compares 17:84. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:135/q04 What force does the command to wait carry?  [فَتَرَبَّصُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the imperative is identified by its audience or its threatening force.
- 20:135/q04/p1 It is a threat and warning to the opponents. — Al-Māwardī, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ and Study Quran say the disclosure rebukes or frightens unbelievers; al-Rāzī says the question expresses no doubt. Ibn Kathīr compares 25:42 and 54:26; Study Quran cites 10:20. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:135/q04/p2 It instructs the opponents to wait for what they seek. — Al-Nasafī identifies the addressees as opponents; Muqātil says the Meccans are answered with the same waiting; al-Baghawī reports their hope for the Prophet's death. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 20:135/q05 What alternative wording is reported for the command and its sequel?  [فَتَرَبَّصُوا۟ فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ · readings]
turns on: Which transmitted alternative changes the imperative, the future particle, or both.
- 20:135/q05/p1 One reading replaces 'wait' with 'enjoy yourselves'. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī report fa-tamattaʿū. Abū Rāfiʿ, as quoted by al-Zamakhsharī, remembers from the Prophet 'Enjoy yourselves; soon you will know.' [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو رافع); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:135/q05/p2 One reading has fa-sawfa taʿlamūn in the sequel. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes the remembered form to Abū Rāfiʿ via al-Zamakhsharī; the report in al-Kashshāf also couples enjoyment with soon knowing. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو رافع); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو رافع)]

## 20:135/q06 When and through what event will they learn the outcome?  [فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether disclosure is a worldly victory, death, or the Last Day.
- 20:135/q06/p1 A victory or God's command in this life will reveal the guided side. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite victory; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ takes the verse to foretell triumph. Al-Biqāʿī identifies the conquest of Mecca, when former opponents embraced Islam amid hope of clemency and fear of punishment. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:135/q06/p2 The Resurrection or Judgment Day will make the truth clear. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Jishumī place God's command and Resurrection together; Yaḥyā and al-Nasafī specify the road to Paradise, as do alternative views in al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:135/q06/p3 The truth may emerge before death, at death, or after death through reward and punishment. — Al-Rāzī lists all three times; al-Bursawī's report from al-Kabīr names jihad and ascendant power before death, reward and punishment afterward; Study Quran allows this life or death. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكبير); STUDYQURAN]
- 20:135/q06/p4 The appointed divine decision will eventually disclose the truth. — Iṣlāḥī urges patient waiting for God's decision; Kur'an Yolu connects it with the divinely set time of punishment and a blessed future for the God-conscious. Al-Bursawī quotes a poem in which clearing dust reveals whether the rider has a horse or a donkey. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 20:135/q07 How is the first man clause parsed after 'you will know'?  [فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ مَنْ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلصِّرَٰطِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether man is an independent interrogative that suspends ʿalima or a relative object governed by it.
- 20:135/q07/p1 Man is an interrogative nominative subject, with aṣḥāb its predicate; the verb is suspended from governing the question. — Al-Ṭabarī compares 18:12; al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq reject accusative government of an interrogative. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say the clause fills two object slots, or one if ʿalima means recognition. Al-Nasafī places both clauses in the accusative slot. [12 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والفراء وأبو إسحاق والنحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:135/q07/p2 Man may be accusative as the verb's object, understood as a relative pronoun with an omitted resumptive. — Al-Farrāʾ compares 2:220; al-Ṭabarī allows the object parse. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say this uses the Kūfan allowance of deleting the relative-clause pronoun, disallowed by Basrans. Abū Isḥāq objects that an interrogative cannot be governed by a prior verb. [7 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والفراء وأبو إسحاق والنحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق)-]

## 20:135/q08 Who are the people of the level path?  [مَنْ أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلصِّرَٰطِ ٱلسَّوِىِّ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wording names the believing community, leaves the contrast formally open, focuses on the Prophet, or contrasts a worse path with the guided one.
- 20:135/q08/p1 The Prophet and believers are the guided side, in contrast to their opponents. — Yaḥyā explicitly identifies the Prophet and believers; al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī and others formulate believers versus opponents. Al-Biqāʿī says the Prophet's followers flourish in both lives. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:135/q08/p2 The question leaves open which of 'us or you' possesses the path until the outcome discloses it. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq frame it as an equivalence question; al-Wāḥidī and al-Bursawī voice 'we or you?' The promised knowledge, rather than present doubt, is the point. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والفراء وأبو إسحاق والنحاس); BURSEVI]
- 20:135/q08/p3 The Prophet himself is the intended guided one. — Al-Bursawī reports Kāshifī: the Prophet is guided and guides others; al-Ālūsī permits both descriptions to refer to him. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:135/q08/p4 An alternative construal asks which people follow a worse path and which person is guided. — Al-Zajjāj presents this alternative contrast between the worse way and the guided one. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 20:135/q09 What does 'the level path' mean?  [ٱلصِّرَٰطِ ٱلسَّوِىِّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the path is explained as a road, religion, justice, or its destination.
- 20:135/q09/p1 It is the straight, upright way or road without deviation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses ṣirāṭ as road; al-Biqāʿī adds broad, clear, unbending and fit for reaching one's aim; al-Bursawī calls it balanced. Ibn Kathīr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ call it a straight road. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:135/q09/p2 It is the upright or true religion, specifically Islam in al-Suddī's gloss. — Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Wāḥidī calls it upright religion; Yaḥyā reports al-Suddī's Islam gloss; al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī identify true religion. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:135/q09/p3 It is justice or the just way. — Muqātil gives justice; al-Suddī as reported by al-Durr does likewise; Yaḥyā reports al-Suddī's just religion. Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Zamakhsharī's justice gloss for sawāʾ. [5 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); DURR-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 20:135/q09/p4 It is the road to Paradise or everlasting bliss. — Yaḥyā describes the just road to Paradise; al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī offer this as Judgment Day sense; al-Nasafī says everlasting bliss. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 20:135/q10 How is the consonant of ṣirāṭ recited?  [ٱلصِّرَٰطِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the consonant is pronounced with sīn or with ṣād shaded toward zāy.
- 20:135/q10/p1 The sīn pronunciation is a canonical dialectal reading. — Qirāʾāt ER calls sīn etymologically original and ṣād an emphatic Qurayshī adaptation. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(ibdāl|sp)]
- 20:135/q10/p2 Ishmām shades ṣād toward zāy. — Qirāʾāt ER describes a sound intermediate between ṣād and sīn. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(ibdāl)]

## 20:135/q11 How is al-sawiyy read, and what do the variants signify?  [ٱلسَّوِىِّ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowels, hamza and feminine or diminutive morphology determine whether the adjective means level, middle, or bad.
- 20:135/q11/p1 The usual al-sawiyy means straight or level. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call it majority or general reading on faʿīl; al-Ālūsī gives the straight gloss. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:135/q11/p2 Al-sawāʾ or al-siwāʾ means the middle, best, just, or even path. — Abū Mijlaz and ʿImrān ibn Ḥudayr are named by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī; al-Qurṭubī cites al-Zamakhsharī's middle, justice and upright glosses. The notes differ in vowel transcription. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مجلز، عمران بن حدير); SAMIN-DURR(أبو مجلز وعمران بن حدير); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مجلز وعمران بن حدير)]
- 20:135/q11/p3 Al-sūʾ or al-sūʾā makes the path bad or evil, contrasting it with the guided person. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with al-sūʾ; Ibn Yaʿmar and al-Jaḥdarī with the feminine form, which treats ṣirāṭ as feminine. Al-Qurṭubī notes 1:6 uses masculine. Al-Naḥḥās defends the unhamzated form by substitution; Abū Ḥātim challenges its derivation. [17 notes: NAHHAS(يحيى بن يعمر وعاصم الجحدري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,يحيى بن يعمر وعاصم الجحدري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الجحدري، ابن يعمر); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,يحيى بن يعمر والجحدري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الجحدري وابن يعمر); NAHHAS(أبو حاتم)-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)-]
- 20:135/q11/p4 A diminutive variant derives from sawāʾ, the even or middle way. — Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh prefer this origin, comparing ʿaṭāʾ to ʿaṭiyy; they object that a diminutive of sūʾ should retain hamza. Al-Samīn also discusses a reversed yāʾ-to-wāw change in the doubled-wāw form. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 20:135/q11/p5 A diminutive variant derives from sūʾ, the bad way. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives this analysis; Abū al-Baqāʾ agrees, and al-Samīn notes hamza replacement is allowed. Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh object that hamza should remain. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 20:135/q11/p6 The unhamzated feminine form is morphologically unacceptable. — Abū Ḥātim argues that sūʾ would yield al-sūʾā and sawāʾ another form, transcribed al-siyyā or al-siyy; al-Naḥḥās counters with permitted hamza substitution. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو حاتم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم); NAHHAS-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-]

## 20:135/q12 How is the second man connected grammatically?  [وَمَنِ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second man is interrogative or relative, and, if relative, which prior constituent it coordinates with.
- 20:135/q12/p1 It is another interrogative nominative subject with an implied predicate, parallel to the first question. — Al-Samīn says the resumptive is omitted; al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī treat both clauses as the content of knowing. Al-Naḥḥās says it coordinates with the first man. [8 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:135/q12/p2 It is a relative nominative pronoun coordinated with the first clause or its subject. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī allow coordination with the suspended question or subject; al-Samīn cites Abū al-Baqāʾ that this coordinates a declarative with an interrogative. Al-Ālūsī requires ʿalima to mean recognition if an object would otherwise be omitted. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:135/q12/p3 It coordinates with aṣḥāb, describing the one who is guided. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī list this alternative; al-Ālūsī says both descriptions can belong to the Prophet. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:135/q12/p4 It is genitive by coordination with ṣirāṭ, meaning the path of the guided one. — Al-Samīn gives the path of those who followed guidance; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī allow ṣirāṭ to mean the Prophet. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:135/q13 What does 'was guided' mean?  [ٱهْتَدَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the phrase states guidance simply or stresses its achieved benefit.
- 20:135/q13/p1 It means being rightly directed, reaching truth, or escaping error. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses right direction; Ibn Kathīr adds reaching truth; al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī specify guidance out of error. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:135/q13/p2 It means attaining guidance's benefit and avoiding harm, beyond merely possessing a path. — Al-Biqāʿī distinguishes possession from actual knowledge and use of the path. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:135/q14 Why pair 'people of the path' with 'who was guided'?  [أَصْحَٰبُ ٱلصِّرَٰطِ ٱلسَّوِىِّ وَمَنِ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pairing distinguishes prior error, the use of a path, or the contrast created by a variant reading.
- 20:135/q14/p1 The first can name those who never strayed; the second those who strayed then found guidance. — Al-Farrāʾ gives this distinction; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī transmit it. [3 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)]
- 20:135/q14/p2 Possessing the path differs from attaining and benefiting from its guidance. — Al-Biqāʿī says possession alone does not ensure knowledge or use; the second expression asks who gained benefit and avoided harm. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:135/q14/p3 On the negative-path readings, the second clause explicitly contrasts evil with guidance. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the variant divides the parties; Abū Ḥayyān says this contrast is explicit unlike the majority reading; al-Ālūsī says comparison is absent in the usual and first anomalous readings. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:135/q15 How does the final warning address its audience?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the notes explain its address and ellipsis or its threatening force.
- 20:135/q15/p1 The final warning shifts to direct address and omits a parallel clause made clear by context. — Iṣlāḥī contrasts it with the indirect address in 20:133–134 and takes the shift as heightened anger. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:135/q15/p2 The closing statement is a severe threat, warning, and intimidation. — Al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī identify its force; Ibn Kathīr compares 25:42 and 54:26; al-Rāzī says it rebukes without implying doubt. [5 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 20:135/q16 Was the command to wait abrogated?  [فَتَرَبَّصُوا۟ · sciences]
turns on: Whether the appeal to 9:5 establishes abrogation; Ibn al-Jawzī denies it.
- 20:135/q16/p1 An unnamed view says the sword verse, conventionally 9:5, abrogated it. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports the claim only to reject it as baseless. [0 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل)-]
- 20:135/q16/p2 The abrogation claim is baseless. — Ibn al-Jawzī explicitly rejects the unnamed claim. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل)+]

## 20:135/q17 What inward path does the ending describe?  [ٱلصِّرَٰطِ ٱلسَّوِىِّ ٱهْتَدَىٰ · inward]
- 20:135/q17/p1 The guided reach God through spiritual stations and detachment from everything else; attachment to other things cuts people off. — Al-Bursawī gives this inward reading; Khujandī's couplet says union begins with separation from all else. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الخجندي)]

## 20:135/q18 What does respite leave people accountable for?  [* · theology]
- 20:135/q18/p1 God has removed excuses through respite and guidance, so the decisive proof belongs to Him. — Al-Bursawī adds a report, citing 17:15, of three who plead excuse: one dying in the interval, a person without reason, and a child; their response to a fire test discloses their standing. Kur'an Yolu says hearers retain freedom of choice until truth becomes clear. [3 notes: BURSEVI(أبو سعيد الخدري رضي الله عنه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:135/q19 What duty and patience does this ending leave the Prophet?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:135/q19/p1 He must warn, worship, and patiently await God's decision while leaving unresponsive people to their choice. — Iṣlāḥī warns that haste opened Adam to deception, says prayer fosters patience, and says divine provision removes need to court the wealthy. Kur'an Yolu urges steady worship and effort; Iṣlāḥī says a delivered warning benefits willing hearers without making the Prophet answerable for others. [10 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:135/q20 How does the ending connect with the sūra's opening and preceding debate?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link stressed is the opening consolation or nearby demands for judgment.
- 20:135/q20/p1 The conclusion returns to the opening consolation: the Prophet has delivered guidance and need not exhaust himself over rejection. — Al-Biqāʿī compares 20:2 and divine forbearance; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 20:2–3; Iṣlāḥī says the Moses account and final section return to the opening; al-Ṭībī says the Prophet may worship within his capacity and leave those warning will not benefit. Study Quran describes the closing warning to Makkans. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:135/q20/p2 The final warning answers demands for immediate punishment after the message has been delivered. — Iṣlāḥī connects the conclusion with demands for a sign or doom; Kur'an Yolu emphasizes the fixed time for punishment and continued perseverance. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:135/q21 How does the sūra relate to its setting and the next sūra?  [* · links]

## 20:135/q22 What reports promise a virtue for reciting this sūra?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which of two reported rewards or recitations is described.
- 20:135/q22/p1 A reciter of Ṭā Hā receives the Resurrection reward of the Emigrants and Helpers. — Al-Kashshāf reports this from the Prophet without grading; al-Bayḍāwī repeats it. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:135/q22/p2 The people of Paradise recite no Qur'an except Ṭā Hā and Yā Sīn. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī report this from the Prophet without grading; al-Bursawī transmits it from al-Kashshāf. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله); BURSEVI(الكشاف)]

## 20:135/q23 What makes a literary resemblance or attribution apt?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether an attributed likeness follows an actual, graspable relation.
- 20:135/q23/p1 A genuine analogy requires intelligible resemblance and fitting proportions, not merely a simile marker or borrowed word. — Al-Jurjānī says forced likeness is awkward; discovering hidden rational similarity resembles finding a pearl, while a composite object's parts must fit its form and proportions. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 20:135/q23/p2 A skilled imitator may be called a learner following a predecessor, so a craft may be attributed to that predecessor. — Al-Jurjānī gives this as an example of accurate imitation and attribution. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
