# 16:17: 14 questions

## 16:17/q01 What does 'creates' mean here?  [يَخْلُقُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the cited sense is shaping or origination; the notes do not directly oppose the senses.
- 16:17/q01/p1 To form or shape. — Ibn al-Jawzi reports the exegetes' Qur'anic usage, citing formation from clay and the contrast between idols and those who make them. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 16:17/q01/p2 To originate things and bring them into being. — Ibn Atiyya speaks of originating; al-Razi and Abu Hayyan speak of originating creation, and al-Biqa'i of continual creation as God wills. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 16:17/q02 Who is the one who creates?  [مَن يَخْلُقُ · referent]
- 16:17/q02/p1 God, creator of the things and benefits previously mentioned. — Muqatil names the preceding created things; al-Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas and 31:11; al-Alusi includes whatever God wills; Qatada in al-Durr calls Him Creator and Provider. The other listed sources identify Him directly. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 16:17/q03 Why is creation singled out from the preceding divine acts?  [يَخْلُقُ · rhetoric]
- 16:17/q03/p1 Creation is the greatest and clearest of those acts, or each earlier act is itself a particular creation. — Al-Alusi offers both explanations for this choice of word. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:17/q04 Who are those who do not create?  [مَن لَّا يَخْلُقُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase directly denotes idols, all worshipped beings, all noncreators, or rational noncreators from whom the case of idols follows a fortiori.
- 16:17/q04/p1 The idols worshipped besides God. — Muqatil names al-Lat, al-Uzza, Manat and Hubal. Yahya, Qatada, Jishumi, al-Qurtubi and al-Durr add that idols cannot harm or benefit; Yahya also denies their control of death, life and resurrection. Ibn Kathir notes that the idols themselves are created; the Study Quran specifies Makkan idols. Al-Alusi reports this as a narrower alternative. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:17/q04/p2 All beings worshipped besides God, including rational beings and idols. — Al-Baydawi and Abu Hayyan explicitly broaden the class. Al-Samin and al-Burhan use a mixed class to explain the personal relative; al-Tabataba'i speaks of all deities invoked apart from God. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; TABATABAI]
- 16:17/q04/p3 All beings that create nothing, whether intelligent or inanimate. — Al-Alusi prefers this broad scope and says the form man favors rational beings without excluding idols; he says idols fall within the denial directly or a fortiori. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 16:17/q04/p4 Rational noncreators immediately, with the idols' inferiority following even more strongly. — Al-Razi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin give the a-fortiori argument, citing the challenge about idols' limbs in 7:195. Al-Sakkaki, reported by al-Burhan, applies the wording to a living capable being; al-Alusi lists this as an alternative and allows idols to follow a fortiori. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(السكاكي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)]

## 16:17/q05 How can the personal relative man apply to noncreators?  [مَن لَّا يَخْلُقُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether man follows the worshippers' personification, formal matching, a mixed referent, an a-fortiori rational referent, broader Arabic usage, or a distinction from ma.
- 16:17/q05/p1 Worshippers treat idols as rational, effective persons or gods, so the verse addresses them in their own terms. — Al-Wahidi cites 7:195; Jishumi also notes that creating is an act associated with rational agents. Al-Razi cites 16:20, and al-Qurtubi 7:195. Al-Samin adds a bird-flock poem using man of birds. Al-Burhan says this wording makes the intended target clear to the audience. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 16:17/q05/p2 It corresponds to the preceding man for the Creator or is licensed by pairing the two. — Al-Tabrisi links the form to the preceding creation clause. Al-Farra compares a rider and mount grouped under man and cites 24:45; al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Qurtubi relay the comparison. Al-Razi, al-Samin and Ibn Ashur describe stylistic or syntactic matching, and al-Qurtubi notes proximity to the Creator's pronoun. [11 notes: TABRISI; FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:17/q05/p3 The referents form a mixed group in which rational beings determine the pronoun. — Al-Samin says ma would also be permissible. Al-Burhan cites 24:45, where man includes snakes and other crawling animals in a mixed group; Abu Hayyan and al-Baydawi also allow a mixed rational and irrational class. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:17/q05/p4 Man refers first to rational noncreators, making the case against inert idols stronger. — Al-Razi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin use the a-fortiori argument and 7:195; al-Burhan reports al-Sakkaki's living, capable referent. Al-Alusi reports the option. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(السكاكي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)]
- 16:17/q05/p5 Man can refer to nonrational things in this grammatical setting without further explanation. — Al-Farra allows this when nonhuman and human are grouped; al-Samin reports Qutrub's rule that man can apply to nonrational things under a condition. Al-Burhan notes that relative man can take a singular verb by form and a plural predicate by meaning, citing 2:135. [3 notes: FARRA; SAMIN-DURR(قطرب); BURHAN]
- 16:17/q05/p6 Man asks about the Creator, whereas ma asks about kinds, and God belongs to no kind. — Al-Qurtubi reports al-Mahdawi's comparison of Pharaoh's questions and Moses' replies in 20:49 and 26:23. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي)]
- 16:17/q05/p7 The man/ma contrast reflects their different grammatical functions and frequency. — Al-Burhan reports that ma is common because nonrational cases are numerous and man suits the rarer rational cases; al-Ibyari restricts the distinction to relatives, since conditionals require a verb and do not govern nouns. [2 notes: BURHAN(الإبياري,قيل)]

## 16:17/q06 What does the interrogative fa add to the comparison?  [أَفَمَن · rhetoric]
- 16:17/q06/p1 It directs denial at an imagined inference of likeness from God's preceding mighty acts. — Al-Alusi explains the fa after the interrogative hamza in this way. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:17/q07 Why does the comparison put the Creator before the noncreator?  [أَفَمَن يَخْلُقُ كَمَن لَّا يَخْلُقُ · rhetoric]
- 16:17/q07/p1 The reversal exposes idolaters' act of lowering God to the level of powerless creatures by equating them in worship. — Al-Razi frames the objection that the comparison might be reversed; al-Zamakhshari, reported by Abu Hayyan and al-Samin, answers through their equation in naming and worship. Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Burhan, al-Biqa'i and al-Itqan stress the sharper rebuke gained by lowering the higher party; al-Burhan says it confronts the idolaters on their own terms. Al-Alusi adds that possession properly precedes absence. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:17/q08 What does the comparison establish about God and worship?  [أَفَمَن يَخْلُقُ كَمَن لَّا يَخْلُقُ · interpretation]
turns on: The latter notes add implications about accountability or divine choice; they do not contest the denial of equality.
- 16:17/q08/p1 Creator and noncreator cannot be equal; worship and divinity belong to the Creator alone. — Muqatil names the four idols. Al-Thalabi and al-Qurtubi cite 31:11 and 46:4 or 35:40 as challenges to show another's creation. Al-Wahidi's exegetes compare gratitude owed to a benefactor with that owed to a nonbenefactor. Yahya, Jishumi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi and Qatada add idols' inability to benefit or harm; Yahya also denies their power over resurrection and glosses al-nushur as resurrection; al-Razi adds their lack of awareness, power and choice. Al-Biqa'i invokes God's knowledge, wisdom, power, free choice and universal benefits. Ibn Ashur says the question repudiates the polytheists' partnership claim; al-Tabataba'i says created existence and effects belong to God. Asad and others stress reflection on the contrast. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التأويل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 16:17/q08/p2 The verse also directs readers to monotheism and accountability in the Hereafter. — Islahi names both implications without developing a separate argument in this note. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 16:17/q08/p3 Differences among created things show that God acts by choice and can bring about whatever He wills. — Al-Biqa'i argues that differences in existence and qualities have no explanation except choice. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 16:17/q09 Does the creation argument settle whether humans create their own acts?  [يَخْلُقُ لَّا يَخْلُقُ · theology]
turns on: Whether any act of creation would imply divinity, or the verse concerns God's cosmic creation and the idols' incapacity while allowing limited human agency.
- 16:17/q09/p1 Yes; creation belongs uniquely to God, so human acts are created by Him, not by their human doers. — Al-Wahidi's associates say a person creating his acts would partner God in origination. Al-Razi's associates argue that if any creature could create, the verse's inference to divinity would absurdly make it a god. Al-Nasafi and al-Alusi invoke the verse against Mu'tazilite creation of acts. Al-Alusi rejects al-Shihab's denial of this inference as a likely textual corruption or lapse. The Study Quran reports the Ash'arite position that God creates all things, including human acts. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحابنا); RAZI-FULL(بعض أصحابنا); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarite theologians)]
- 16:17/q09/p2 No; humans may create their acts in a limited sense, while the verse contrasts God with powerless idols or concerns the creation of the whole cosmos. — Al-Razi reports the Mu'tazilites' distinction between God creating heavens, earth, people, animals, plants, seas, stars and mountains and a servant's acts. They say superiority over idols does not make every capable human divine; 7:195 likewise makes a walking human superior to a legless idol without divinizing the human. The Study Quran reports limited human creation through intelligence and agency. Al-Shihab, reported by al-Alusi, says an intransitive universal 'creates' permits a particular affirmation about humans. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب); STUDYQURAN(Some Muʿtazilites); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 16:17/q10 Whom does the closing call address?  [أَفَلَا تَذَكَّرُونَ · referent]
- 16:17/q10/p1 The polytheists or idolaters, especially the pagan Arabs addressed by the verse. — Yahya and Ibn Abbas, as reported by al-Wahidi, contrast the believers who already take heed. Ibn al-Jawzi calls them to do as believers did. Ibn Ashur and al-Tabataba'i identify the polytheists; Kuranyolu specifies pagan Arabs of Muhammad's time. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 16:17/q11 What does 'will you not remember' ask people to do?  [أَفَلَا تَذَكَّرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tadhakkarun denotes actual recollection of an already grasped truth or figurative knowledge when no earlier conception is assumed.
- 16:17/q11/p1 Reflect on the evident difference between Creator and idols and abandon the error of associating them. — Muqatil connects reflection with affirming oneness; Yahya and Ibn Abbas contrast believers' heeding. Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi and Abu Hayyan call it a rebuke. Al-Baydawi says slight attention suffices because the error is obvious; al-Biqa'i says even some angles of visible evidence suffice. Kuranyolu urges reconsidering beliefs after reasoning from creative power and favors. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:17/q11/p2 Literally recall a truth once conceived but now overlooked. — Al-Alusi says the equality issue is already clear and calls only for recollection of a prior conception. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:17/q11/p3 Metaphorically know or recognize the omitted object, the lack of equality. — Al-Alusi reports some who supply 'lack of equality' as the omitted object because there was no prior conception to recall. He reports two analyses: explicit metaphor in the estimated object, or implicit metaphor there with the assertion of remembering as an imaginative elaboration. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,قيل)]

## 16:17/q12 How is tadhakkarun read?  [تَذَكَّرُونَ · readings]
- 16:17/q12/p1 Asim fully assimilates ta into dhal; both forms are canonical variants without a stated difference in meaning. — The Qiraat-ER note reports the assimilation and the existence of the other canonical form without naming its reader. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Assim)]

## 16:17/q13 How does the verse develop the surrounding signs and the sura's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: These notes describe successive parts of the passage rather than opposing explanations.
- 16:17/q13/p1 It concludes the preceding signs of God's creation and blessings by rejecting worship of idols. — Al-Razi says the ordered signs show a wise Creator and His blessings. Ibn Ashur locates the conclusion after evidence of God's creative acts since 16:3 and their call for gratitude. The Study Quran names nature's benefits and surveys livestock, crops, heavenly bodies, sea, mountains, streams and travel landmarks as the sura's spiritual signs. Al-Tabataba'i connects creation, countless blessings, divine knowledge, ownership and governance in the closing verses; Kuranyolu recalls God's creative power and favors. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:17/q13/p2 The following verses extend the reminder to inexhaustible blessings, forgiveness and mercy, and God's knowledge of hidden and open states. — Kuranyolu says 16:18 offers hope to people who recognize the Creator despite past errors and 16:19 warns even outward believers against retaining old errors, ill will or hostility. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 16:17/q14 How does creation prove exclusive lordship against the polytheists?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether creation alone answers a polytheist who already accepts God as Creator but assigns delegated governance and intercession to idols.
- 16:17/q14/p1 Creation by itself and blessings are two independent proofs of divine oneness. — Al-Tabataba'i reports this account only to reject it: creation alone proves a maker and unity in creating, while polytheists already accepted God as Creator. [0 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 16:17/q14/p2 Creation proves God alone is the true benefactor and owner of all effects, so the combined argument defeats delegated lordship. — Al-Tabataba'i says polytheists accepted God's creation of everything, including idols, but claimed idols had delegated control and intercession. God's inalienable ownership of all beings and benefits, joined to His knowledge and governance in the passage, answers that claim. [4 notes: TABATABAI]
