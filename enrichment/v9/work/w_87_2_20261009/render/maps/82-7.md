# 82:7: 17 questions

## 82:7/q01 How does ٱلَّذِى attach grammatically to the preceding verse?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative expression continues the preceding construction or begins a detached one.
- 82:7/q01/p1 It follows the preceding expression as apposition, explanatory apposition, or adjective. — Al-Samīn lists these three connected analyses. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 82:7/q01/p2 It is detached, with nominative or accusative case. — Al-Samīn gives both case possibilities for detachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 82:7/q02 What does خَلَقَكَ say God did?  [خَلَقَكَ · meaning]
- 82:7/q02/p1 God brought the human into existence initially, from nothing or a drop before shaping him. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī describe formation from a drop into a hearing, seeing person; al-Qurṭubī locates it in the womb and names the paired limbs. Elmalılı says creation precedes shaping, balancing, forming, and composing; Islahi sees initial and final stages in the creation vocabulary. Muqātil states the sequence of creating, proportioning, and making upright. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:7/q03 What does فَسَوَّىٰكَ describe?  [فَسَوَّىٰكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the shaping denotes bodily function, staged maturation, matching measure, or each person's specific endowment.
- 82:7/q03/p1 God fashioned a sound, ordered body with organs and faculties fitted to their uses. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī specify hearing and sight after embryonic origin. Al-Bayḍāwī names healthy, proportioned organs ready for their uses; Bursawī gives grasping, walking, and seeing. Al-Biqāʿī stresses each part's intended benefit, temperament, and structure. Study Quran calls parts and faculties sound and functional; al-Qushayrī calls the fitting wise and notes differing beauty and height. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 82:7/q03/p2 God developed the human through stages until mature and fit to receive the spirit. — Elmalılı includes perfection and ordering of body, limbs, and faculties and cites 18:37; Islahi says the vocabulary moves from making or sketching and creating to perfecting, alluding to initial and final stages. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 82:7/q03/p3 Taswiya means bringing something into matching measure, outwardly or inwardly. — Al-Jishumī gives the measuring sense and allows both bodily form and inward character. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 82:7/q03/p4 God equipped each person with qualities and abilities suited to that person's life and environment. — Asad specifies individual suitability rather than only a general bodily pattern. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 82:7/q04 May the adjacent kāf sounds be assimilated?  [خَلَقَكَ فَسَوَّىٰكَ · grammar]
- 82:7/q04/p1 The adjacent identical kāf consonants may be assimilated or left separate under the stated vowel conditions. — Al-Akhfash describes assimilation and distinguishes the cases by whether the first or second letter bears a vowel. [1 notes: AKHFASH]

## 82:7/q05 How is فَعَدَلَكَ read?  [فَعَدَلَكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether dāl is doubled or light, and whether fī in the next verse can govern a sense of turning; both readings are transmitted.
- 82:7/q05/p1 With a doubled dāl, fa-ʿaddalaka, Form II. — Al-Ṭabarī names Medinan, Meccan, Syrian, and Basran readers; al-Naḥḥās agrees. Al-Jishumī names Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, and Yaʿqūb. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Samīn, Ibn al-Jazarī, and al-Durr report the other or majority readers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says narrators agree the Prophet doubled the dāl when speaking on sighting the crescent, and al-Durr transmits Abū Hurayra's report of the Prophet's doubled recitation, authenticated by al-Ḥākim. QIRAAT-ER identifies thorough adjustment, rectification, or proportional calibration as Form II. Al-Akhfash and Ibn Khalawayh explain the doubled sense; al-Zajjāj reports both pronunciations. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both as sound but prefers doubling because Arabic more readily combines adjustment with fī. Al-Farrāʾ and Abū ʿUbayd prefer it according to al-Thaʿlabī, citing 95:4; al-Wāḥidī reports their preference and al-Farrāʾ's objection to fī with turning. Al-Jishumī likewise reports al-Farrāʾ's preference. Al-Qurṭubī says Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim choose it with 95:4. Study Quran prefers it if verse 8 continues verse 7. [26 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(من قرأ بالتشديد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أهل الحرمين وأهل البصرة وأهل الشام والكوفيون); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر، والآخرون,الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي,جمهور الناس); TABRISI(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر); SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون); NASHR(الكوفيون); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QIRAAT-ER(Vr); JISHUMI(الفراء)+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم)+; STUDYQURAN+; TAB-FULL+; THALABI(الفراء وأبو عبيد)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد,الفراء)+]
- 82:7/q05/p2 With a light dāl, fa-ʿadalaka, Form I. — Al-Akhfash and Ibn Qutayba report the light form. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn, Ibn al-Jazarī, and al-Qurṭubī identify Kufan readers; al-Jishumī additionally names Abū Jaʿfar, ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī. Al-Ṭabarī accepts this established reading too. Al-Naḥḥās rejects al-Farrāʾ's grammatical objection: fī begins the next clause with rakkabaka. Abū ʿAlī, reported by al-Wāḥidī, gives the light form a proportioning sense that avoids the objection. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, Elmalılı, and Bursawī allow balance in the light form; Study Quran allows turning in any direction. [31 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(من قرأ); TAB-FULL(من قرأ بالتخفيف); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أهل الحرمين وأهل البصرة وأهل الشام والكوفيون); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(أهل الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الفراء,الكوفيون); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكوفيون); TABRISI(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون: عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(الكوفيون); SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون); NASHR(الكوفيون); BIQAI-FULL(الكوفيون); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; NAHHAS(الفراء)-]

## 82:7/q06 What does فَعَدَلَكَ mean?  [فَعَدَلَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ʿadl denotes anatomical balance, specifically human uprightness, turning to God-chosen forms, inward capacities, or movement between deficient and perfected states; the two readings and verse 8 affect several explanations.
- 82:7/q06/p1 God made the human body balanced and proportionate, especially its corresponding limbs. — Al-Akhfash and al-Naḥḥās say the two forms can share this sense; al-Naḥḥās cites poetry about equal numbers killed on two sides. Al-Ṭabarī adds uprightness. Ibn Khalawayh and Muqātil, as quoted by al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Elmalılı, specify paired eyes, ears, hands, and feet; al-Samīn says neither hand or foot is longer nor eye wider. Al-Wāḥidī explains distinct organs rather than one undifferentiated form. Bursawī cites anatomists on matching bones, veins, arteries, and nerves. Elmalılı compares balanced fingertips at 75:4 but says this reading alone does not mark human uniqueness. Al-Bayḍāwī also mentions proportionate structure, and al-Bīqāʿī balanced temperament. [36 notes: MUQATIL; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(من قرأ بالتشديد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(الفراء وأبو عبيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,أبو علي الفارسي,الفراء,عطاء عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر، والآخرون,الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم); BAYDAWI-FULL(الكوفيون); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Mukātil); QIRAAT-ER(Vr)]
- 82:7/q06/p2 God made humans upright and beautiful in a distinctively human form. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ describe uprightness and beautiful appearance. Ibn Kathīr calls it the finest stature and form. Elmalılı prefers Ibn ʿAbbās's straight back unlike crooked-backed beasts, because it is more specifically human and fits after shaping; al-Bīqāʿī says the light reading turns from deformity to uprightness; Bursawī cites 95:4 for departure from an ugly animal form. [8 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الكوفيون); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ELMALILI(İbn Abbas)+]
- 82:7/q06/p3 God turned or directed the human into whatever form He wished. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Khalawayh, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and al-Qurṭubī give alternatives such as beautiful or ugly, tall or short; al-Ṭabarī includes resemblance to a relative, al-Naḥḥās adds health or illness, and al-Farrāʾ's account connects the light reading to verse 8. Al-Samīn derives this from ʿudūl; Study Quran says God can turn people in any direction. Al-Akhfash says He formed the person in this way. Al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī name light-reading transmitters. [14 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(من قرأ); TAB-FULL(من قرأ بالتخفيف); NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(أهل الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر، والآخرون); QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون: عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(الكوفيون); SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; NAHHAS(الفراء)-]
- 82:7/q06/p4 God balanced bodily form with human capacities, reason, thought, power, or the faculties of felicity. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives faculties leading to felicity beside anatomical balance. Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, reported by al-Wāḥidī and Elmalılı, includes capacities unavailable to others and superiority among creatures; Elmalılı cites 15:29 and 17:70 and calls them divine generosity. Asad includes bodily needs and emotional drives alongside intellectual and spiritual perception. Study Quran notes inner qualities such as faith or disobedience, or felicity and wretchedness understood as salvation and damnation, citing 11:105. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebû Ali Fârisî); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Qu and R)]
- 82:7/q06/p5 God successively turned the human from deficient forms toward perfection. — Elmalılı calls this a possible light-reading sense of repeated selection toward the best form. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 82:7/q06/p6 God can turn the perfected human toward a lower state. — Elmalılı connects this alternative to the warning against delusion by generosity and to the Hereafter, citing 95:5. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 82:7/q07 How does فِيٓ أَىِّ صُورَةٍ in verse 8 relate to فَعَدَلَكَ?  [فَعَدَلَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fī governs ʿadalaka or the following rakkabaka, and whether a turning sense requires ilā.
- 82:7/q07/p1 It continues the adjustment verb, favouring the doubled reading. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the doubled reading because Arabic more naturally says adjustment with fī. Al-Farrāʾ, quoted by al-Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī, says turning takes ilā, not fī; al-Wāḥidī says attachment of verse 8 to the light verb is weak. Study Quran says doubling is grammatically better if verse 8 continues verse 7. [4 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); STUDYQURAN+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+; NAHHAS-]
- 82:7/q07/p2 The clause ends at فعدلك; fī belongs with the following ركبك. — Al-Naḥḥās uses this attachment to reject al-Farrāʾ's objection to the light reading. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 82:7/q08 What account of shaping in the womb is linked to this verse?  [فَعَدَلَكَ · reports]
- 82:7/q08/p1 Three forty-day stages precede an angel mixing the person's soil with the embryo. — Ibn Khalawayh connects the account to divine shaping and cites 3:6 and 23:14; the surviving excerpt stops in mid-account. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 82:7/q09 What prophetic rebuke uses the language of this verse?  [* · reports]
- 82:7/q09/p1 God rebukes Adam's child for arrogance and hoarding after creating, shaping, and straightening him. — Busr ibn Jaḥḥāsh transmits the Prophet's account: the person withholds wealth and only promises charity when death reaches the collarbones. Al-Mizzī notes a corroborating route through Yaḥyā ibn Ḥamza, Thawr ibn Yazīd, and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Maysara. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بسر بن جحاش القرشي,يحيى بن حمزة)]

## 82:7/q10 What inward readings are given for the human's creation and adjustment?  [* · inward]
turns on: The two reported inward explanations emphasize knowledge and faith or humanity's relation to other creatures.
- 82:7/q10/p1 God perfected the human form through knowledge and adjusted it through faith. — Bursawī reports al-Junayd's formulation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الجنيد)]
- 82:7/q10/p2 God created the human and subjected all created things to him without subjecting him to them. — Bursawī reports Dhū al-Nūn's formulation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ذو النون)]

## 82:7/q11 How does human creation establish God's generosity and lordship?  [* · theology]
- 82:7/q11/p1 Creating and fitting the human shows God's lordship and generosity; existence itself is the first blessing. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls the description a confirmation of lordship and manifestation of generosity. Bursawī says existence is better than nonexistence; Elmalılı calls existence the foundation of every blessing, and calls reason and excellence gifts in light of 15:29 and 17:70. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 82:7/q12 What does human formation imply about resurrection and Judgment?  [* · theology]
- 82:7/q12/p1 The One who created and formed humans first can recreate them and will judge them. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī infer re-creation from origination. Islahi says first creation makes resurrection no difficulty and that power, creativity, care, wisdom, mercy, and justice in human formation show a purposeful life and a coming day of reward and punishment. Elmalılı's possible descent from perfection warns of the Hereafter with 95:5. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:7/q13 What response does human creation demand?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is the obligation arising from divine formation or the opportunity and surveillance before Judgment.
- 82:7/q13/p1 It demands gratitude and reverent fear rather than arrogance, ingratitude, or denial. — Al-Biqāʿī says the One who proportions can also disfigure or punish. Kuranyolu says bodily and mental faculties and all blessings make rebellion and denial ingratitude. Ibn Kathīr's prophetic report rebukes arrogance and hoarding after God's gifts. Islahi says human formation discloses purpose and accountability; refusal to meet the test merits punishment. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بسر بن جحاش القرشي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 82:7/q13/p2 God's present respite gives wrongdoers time to reform before Judgment. — Islahi says refusal after this opportunity leaves no excuse; assigned angels meanwhile record every word and deed, so delay does not conceal anything. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:7/q14 What does human balance imply about human nature and rank?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the inferred significance is harmonious human nature, exceptional faculties, or vicegerent duty.
- 82:7/q14/p1 Bodily drives and intellectual or spiritual perception coexist in divinely intended human nature. — Asad includes bodily needs and emotional urges with intellectual and spiritual perceptions and concludes there is no inherent moral conflict between flesh and spirit. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 82:7/q14/p2 Human reason, thought, power, and excellent form mark superiority over other creatures. — Elmalılı reports Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī's account and cites 15:29 and 17:70; al-Wāḥidī also reports al-Fārisī's finely proportioned human with distinctive capacities. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); ELMALILI(Ebû Ali Fârisî)]
- 82:7/q14/p3 Humanity's middle place among creatures fits vicegerency and the middle community. — Islahi connects fulfilling this duty to the eternal kingdom of Heaven and cites 95:4. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:7/q15 How does this verse serve al-Infiṭār's argument beside al-Takwīr?  [* · links]
- 82:7/q15/p1 Al-Infiṭār argues from God's creativity, power, wisdom, justice, and mercy in human creation to Judgment. — Islahi contrasts al-Takwīr's reasoning from the Qur'an's authenticity, source, revelation, and bearer with al-Infiṭār's signs of human formation. He calls the two sūras counterparts sharing form, style, meanings, and opening cataclysm; both address rich, arrogant Quraysh trusting their buildings and ignoring warnings. He says rejecters of al-Takwīr's Qur'anic proof cannot distinguish radiant dawn from murky night. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 82:7/q16 When was the sūra containing this verse revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The dating of the sūra within the Meccan period; the note gives no specific deciding evidence.
- 82:7/q16/p1 Early in the Meccan period. — Asad reports this dating from some authorities. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 82:7/q16/p2 More likely in the last group of Meccan revelations. — Asad reports the later dating as the more likely view. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)+]

## 82:7/q17 What hadith of concealed and forgiven sins appears in the commentary?  [* · reports]
- 82:7/q17/p1 God draws the believer near, covers him, elicits acknowledgment of sins, then says He concealed them in life and forgives them today. — Bursawī cites it as a sound hadith; the believer fears ruin before the declaration of forgiveness. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
