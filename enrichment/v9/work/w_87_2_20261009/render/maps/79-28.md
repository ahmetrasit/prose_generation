# 79:28: 8 questions

## 79:28/q01 What does samkaha denote?  [سَمْكَهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether samk names vertical height above earth, the roof or built support, or the thickness within the sky; several sources allow more than one usage.
- 79:28/q01/p1 The sky's height or upward extent. — Jishumī explains al-masmūkāt from height, contrasts upward samk with downward depth, and cites ʿAlī's prayer and al-Farazdaq's line about raising the sky. Rāzī and Elmalılı make the same upward/downward distinction; Qurtubī cites samakta and asmiq fi al-daym, and Samīn cites transitive samaka, intransitive samuka, and a high camel hump. Al-Qāmūs, quoted by Biqāʿī, also gives stature. Abū Ḥayyān associates the height with five hundred years. [15 notes: JISHUMI(الفرزدق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(القاموس); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]
- 79:28/q01/p2 The sky's roof or highest canopy. — Naḥḥās calls the sky a roof over earth. Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Qurtubī, and Nasafī speak of raising its roof. Al-Qāmūs, quoted by Biqāʿī, gives roof; Study Quran identifies the canopy with the sky's highest level. [7 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(القاموس); STUDYQURAN]
- 79:28/q01/p3 Its structure, construction, or supporting framework. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss it as construction or building, the former specifying construction without pillars; Ṭabarī transmits both. Al-Farrāʾ, quoted by Thaʿlabī and Qurtubī, uses samk for what carries or supports a structure; the related expressions denote building, height, and camel humps. Samīn cites the house's supporting beam and the star names al-Simāk, Rāmiḥ and Aʿzal, and an unnamed poet's line on raising heaven. Itqān allows construction alongside height. [8 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN]
- 79:28/q01/p4 The sky's thickness between its lower and upper surfaces. — Bayḍāwī gives thickness as an alternative to height above earth. Abū Ḥayyān, quoted by Biqāʿī, defines the distance between the sky's near and upper surfaces. Bursevī reports the same as a five-hundred-year journey. Ālūsī allows thickness and calls its upper-to-lower measure depth; Elmalılı notes an explanation involving thickness in breadth and length. Al-Qāmūs, quoted by Biqāʿī, also gives the span from a house's top to bottom. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان,القاموس); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 79:28/q02 What does raising its samk describe?  [رَفَعَ سَمْكَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the raised object is the sky's own height or structure, or the heavenly bodies above people.
- 79:28/q02/p1 God built and lifted the sky or its ceiling high above earth. — Mujāhid, Ṭabarī's Mujāhid report, Rāzī's 'others,' and Elmalılı stress that it stands without pillars; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts its unreachable unsupported vastness with human buildings needing reachable materials and supports. Qatāda, reported by Ṭabarī, describes raising its structure. Kashshāf and Samīn describe a vast extent in the upward direction; Ibn Jawzī says it was lifted into the air, Ibn Kathīr adds that it was extended, and Bursevī describes its rise from earth. The five-hundred-year estimates are mapped separately. [21 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI+; ELMALILI+]
- 79:28/q02/p2 God raised the heavenly bodies above us. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ attributes this reading to Muḥammad ʿAbduh and finds it unpersuasive. [0 notes: BINTSHATI(محمد عبده)-]

## 79:28/q03 Is a distance specified for the sky's height or thickness?  [سَمْكَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reported five-hundred-year journeys establish a literal measure, or the verse only states vast height; astronomical calculations are also noted without figures.
- 79:28/q03/p1 The distance is a journey of five hundred years. — Muqātil, Kashshāf, Rāzī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Bursevī give the figure for height or separation from earth; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports it from commentators in Kashshāf and Baḥr. Bursevī also reports five hundred years for thickness. Ālūsī says sound reports give this distance between earth and the lowest heaven, between successive heavens, and across each heaven's thickness; he reads it as literal customary travel and a definite number. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects that the verse gives no measure, Qur'anic cosmic signs do not use such measures, and travel time varies with speed across eras. [9 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BINTSHATI(مفسرون في الكشاف والبحر); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+; BINTSHATI-]
- 79:28/q03/p2 The height is immense, with no fixed distance established by this wording. — Kashshāf and Samīn call its upward extent vast; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ emphasizes its unreachable height and rejects the five-hundred-year measure as unsupported by the text or stable travel time. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]
- 79:28/q03/p3 Celestial sizes and distances from earth have been calculated by astronomers. — Rāzī reports the astronomers' calculations without giving figures in this note. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحاب الهيئة)]

## 79:28/q04 What does fa-sawwaha mean?  [فَسَوَّىٰهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taswiya concerns a seamless surface, completed construction, proportion and wise order, or the placement and furnishing of individual celestial bodies.
- 79:28/q04/p1 God made the sky even and seamless, without disparities, cracks, or flaws. — Ṭabarī specifies no high or low part and even height and extent; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention a smooth surface without rises or depressions. Muqātil, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn Jawzī, Qurtubī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, and Study Quran emphasize absence of flaws, seams, breaks, fissures, or unequal elevation. Rāzī's reported view and Study Quran link this to 67:3, the latter also to 67:4. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ accepts this as a possible close sense. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 79:28/q04/p2 God completed, perfected, and set the sky right as its construction required. — Kashshāf gives this alongside smoothness; Rāzī reports composing and proportioning it; Bayḍāwī says the verb can mean setting right. Abū Ḥayyān gives perfected construction; Ibn Kathīr adds leveling and adornment with stars. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ accepts completion, repair, and precise construction as textually possible. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BINTSHATI(مفسرون)+]
- 79:28/q04/p3 God proportioned and ordered the sky and its parts according to wisdom. — Jishumī defines taswiya as matching one thing to another in form and meaning. Biqāʿī says the thicknesses of each heaven and earth and the spaces between them were proportioned so none exceeds another. Ālūsī says each part received what wisdom requires, including stars; Elmalılı cites 55:7 on the balance. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each part was arranged in its right place and compares 38:72. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses straightness, balance, equilibrium, and precision; Asad says 87:2 is the earliest chronological use of sawwā in this sense. [10 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI(مفسرون); ALUSI-FULL+; BINTSHATI+]
- 79:28/q04/p4 God completed the sky by furnishing it with stars, celestial spheres, and epicycles. — Bayḍāwī gives stars, spheres, and other features as an explanation. Ālūsī reports the version with stars, accessories, and epicycles but objects that it identifies heavens with spheres without early support and rests on weak astronomical proofs; he leaves the truth to God. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 79:28/q04/p5 God placed each heavenly body in its proper position. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ attributes this to Muḥammad ʿAbduh but rejects it as a poor fit for the Qur'anic sense of evenness and precision. [0 notes: BINTSHATI(محمد عبده)-]

## 79:28/q05 Does taswiya establish that the sky is spherical?  [فَسَوَّىٰهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether unqualified proportion requires a sphere, or leaves the sky's overall shape undetermined.
- 79:28/q05/p1 Complete proportion implies a genuinely spherical sky. — Rāzī reports advocates arguing that the unqualified wording requires complete proportion: other shapes have edges and unequal distances, while sphericity causes no religious harm. Ālūsī reports many scholars held that the heavens have simple, similar parts and a genuinely spherical form; their dependence on a freely choosing creator makes this religiously unobjectionable. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بالقول الأول); ALUSI-FULL(كثير); ELMALILI-]
- 79:28/q05/p2 The wording does not establish the sky's overall shape. — Elmalılı rejects the inference to a perfect sphere and says the detail is known to God. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]

## 79:28/q06 How does this statement relate to the preceding mention of building the sky?  [رَفَعَ سَمْكَهَا فَسَوَّىٰهَا · links]
- 79:28/q06/p1 It explains how God built the sky. — Rāzī says the passage turns from stating that God built heaven to explaining its manner. Samīn says raising its height explains that manner, and Bursevī says the vast rise from earth does so. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 79:28/q07 How does this verse's creation sequence relate to Fussilat's sequence?  [* · links]
- 79:28/q07/p1 An apparent conflict is alleged: Fussilat puts earth's creation before heaven, while this passage puts heaven's construction before earth's spreading. — Ibn Qutayba reports the objection, citing 41:9 and 41:12 against 79:27, 79:28, and 79:30; the note gives no resolution. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(قالوا)]

## 79:28/q08 What does the sky's creation show in the sura's argument?  [* · interpretation]
- 79:28/q08/p1 Its vast, ordered creation demonstrates God's power to resurrect people. — Biqāʿī places the signs within the sura's purpose of establishing resurrection, gathering, and final fate after earlier suras' exposition; its oaths signal a decisive climax. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the question makes deniers answer for themselves: recreating decayed bones is no harder than making the sky. Kur'an Yolu calls the vast ordered cosmos evidence to resurrection deniers, citing 40:57, and treats pillarless heavens as a sign of limitless power, citing 13:2; it also includes night and day, citing 2:164 and 78:10–11. Study Quran includes the sky, night and day, and spread earth as dimensions of creation showing power to create and resurrect. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
