# 16:20: 14 questions

## 16:20/q01 Who are those invoked besides God?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the expression is restricted to ordinary idols, covers every object of worship, or points specifically to saints, angels or ancestral symbols; Kur'an Yolu disputes the evidence for ancestor worship.
- 16:20/q01/p1 The idols worshipped by the polytheists. — Muqātil names al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, Manāt and Hubal among the Meccan idols. Al-Ṭabrisī says the idols are made from created stone, wood and like materials. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the address narrows to the polytheists. The common early understanding preferred by Kur'an Yolu takes them as lifeless idols. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(eski tefsirler)+]
- 16:20/q01/p2 All that is worshipped apart from God, including idols. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly extends the relative expression to every object of worship. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī call them worshipped deities, while al-Thaʿlabī speaks of invoked beings and Iṣlāḥī of baseless deities. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 16:20/q01/p3 Deceased saints to whom followers ascribe divine or semi-divine qualities. — Asad points to the following description in 16:21. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 16:20/q01/p4 Angels worshipped or petitioned as God's daughters and assigned divine functions. — Kur'an Yolu reports this proposal and explains the following reference to death as angels' mortality, like human mortality. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:20/q01/p5 Carved idols that may symbolize Arab ancestors. — Kur'an Yolu reports Ateş's proposal but objects that there is no evidence that pre-Islamic Arabs deified ancestral spirits; the author retains the common early interpretation. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ateş); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 16:20/q02 What does calling upon them mean?  [يَدْعُونَ · meaning]
- 16:20/q02/p1 Calling them gods or worshipping them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says people call the idols gods. Bursawī says invoking a deity often denotes worshipping it in Qur'anic usage. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 16:20/q03 How is the verb 'call upon' read, with its surrounding verbs?  [يَدْعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the surrounding speech continues direct address, shifts to a report about polytheists, or uses a passive verb; individual transmitters and explanatory readings are reported differently.
- 16:20/q03/p1 Second-person tāʾ in 'you call upon' and in the two preceding verbs. — Al-Fārisī names Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī. Other accounts call this the majority or remaining readers; al-Ālūsī also names Mujāhid, al-Aʿraj, Shayba, Abū Jaʿfar and a report from Hubayra. It continues the prior direct address, including 16:15, 16:17 and 16:22, or follows an implied 'Say' according to al-Fārisī. Al-Ṭabrisī says it addresses the listeners; Qirāʾāt-ER says the accusation thereby becomes personal. Accounts differ over particular transmitters. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير، وأبو عمرو، ونافع، وابن عامر، وحمزة، والكسائي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عاصم); JISHUMI(الباقون); TABRISI(الباقون); RAZI-FULL(الباقون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور ومجاهد والأعرج وشيبة وأبو جعفر وهبيرة عن عاصم); QIRAAT-ER(Imp)]
- 16:20/q03/p2 Third-person yāʾ in 'they call upon,' after second-person tāʾ in the preceding two verbs. — The usual report attributes this to ʿĀṣim, especially Abū Bakr, and several accounts include Yaʿqūb or Sahl; al-Ṭabrisī specifies exceptions in a transmission through Abū Bakr, while al-Qurṭubī also names Hubayra from Ḥafṣ. Al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī explain it as a shift to information about the polytheists and their gods, sometimes addressed to the Prophet. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the contrast clarifies that the tāʾ version addresses polytheists. Reports of ʿĀṣim's transmitters differ. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم، عن عمرو بن الصباح,عاصم); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عاصم); JISHUMI(أبو بكر عن عاصم ويعقوب الحضرمي); BAGHAWI-FULL(عاصم ويعقوب); TABRISI(عاصم غير الأعشى والبرجمي عن أبي بكر ويعقوب وسهل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عاصم); RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر وحفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عاصم); SAMIN-DURR(عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(عاصم); IBNASHUR-FULL(عاصم ويعقوب)]
- 16:20/q03/p3 Third-person yāʾ in all three verbs: they conceal, disclose and call upon. — Al-Fārisī records reports through Hubayra from Ḥafṣ and through al-Kisāʾī from Abū Bakr; al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī report it from Ḥafṣ, and al-Ālūsī cites a transmission from Abū ʿAmr and Ḥamza. Al-Fārisī says an implied 'Tell them' can introduce these statements about what they hide, show and worship. The transmitter attributions vary. [6 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الكسائي عن أبي بكر عن عاصم,حفص عن عاصم، عن هبيرة); RAZI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر وحفص); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة)]
- 16:20/q03/p4 The opening verb is passive: those who are called upon or summoned. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies ḍamma on yāʾ and fatḥa on ʿayn in Muḥammad al-Yamānī's reading. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also transmit it; al-Ālūsī explains that disbelievers summon and worship the idols. Al-Samīn mentions a passive variant without naming its reader. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد اليماني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد اليماني); SAMIN-DURR(عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(محمد اليماني)]
- 16:20/q03/p5 Al-Aʿmash and Ibn Masʿūd's companions use 'He knows what they show and conceal' before second-person 'you call upon.' — Abū Ḥayyān reports the variant يعلم الذي يبدون وما يكتمون; he and al-Ālūsī say such forms diverge from the codex consonantal text and are explanatory readings, not Qur'an. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش وأصحاب عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش وطلحة)]
- 16:20/q03/p6 Ṭalḥa uses 'what they hide and what they disclose' before second-person 'you call upon.' — Abū Ḥayyān reports ما يخفون وما يعلنون. He and al-Ālūsī classify such variants as explanatory because they depart from the codex consonantal text. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(طلحة); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش وطلحة)]

## 16:20/q04 Why use a relative pronoun for rational beings for idols?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the rational form follows the worshippers' conception of idols or the breadth of the verse's target; Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives both reasons.
- 16:20/q04/p1 It speaks of idols as persons in keeping with the worshippers' belief in their divinity and agency. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the unbelievers credited idols with influence. Abū Ḥayyān says they were worshipped and believed divine. Kur'an Yolu notes similar personification in the question about God's partners at 16:27; al-Ālūsī says treating them as rational also permits rhetorical matching. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:20/q04/p2 It accommodates all categories that can be worshipped besides God. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the argument rebuts every kind of worship apart from God and the relative form can cover categories suited to it. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 16:20/q05 What does 'besides God' mean and how does it function?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
- 16:20/q05/p1 It is circumstantial and means passing beyond God. — Bursawī traces dūn from a lower place to difference of rank and then more broadly to crossing a boundary or rule. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 16:20/q06 What does their failure to create anything mean?  [لَا يَخْلُقُونَ شَيْـًۭٔا · interpretation]
- 16:20/q06/p1 The invoked beings have no creative capacity whatsoever. — Muqātil says not even a fly. Yaḥyā relates it to 37:95–96. Jishumī adds that idols can neither benefit nor harm. Bursawī says creation lies outside their power; al-Ālūsī stresses the absolute negation. Al-Qāmūs, cited by Bursawī, defines the Creator as one who originates without a prior model. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the claim makes explicit what 16:17 implied; Study Quran cites 25:3 and 37:95–96. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(ikinci yorum); STUDYQURAN]

## 16:20/q07 Who or what makes them in 'they are created'?  [وَهُمْ يُخْلَقُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'created' names God's origination of their existence or the worshippers' carving of their forms; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows both processes while assigning the implied creator to God.
- 16:20/q07/p1 God creates them or their underlying substance; they depend on Him for existence. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this apparent sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God creates the stone even if humans carve it, citing 37:96. Al-Ālūsī says both instances of creation have their ordinary meaning and the unstated agent is obviously God. Bursawī says contingent beings depend on a creator in essence and existence; al-Ālūsī adds their continuance. Study Quran cites 37:95–96 and 25:3 to contrast created idols with uncreated God. Other notes simply state that idols are created without naming the agent. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 16:20/q07/p2 People make the idols by carving, shaping or fashioning images. — Muqātil says worshippers carve them by hand; Yaḥyā invokes 37:95–96 and reports al-Suddī's explanation that they are formed into images. Al-Ṭabrisī specifies created stone and wood. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's view that idols cannot do what their makers do. Al-Ālūsī reports this as a second sense of creation but says using carving for the first creation clause would not establish worthiness of worship. Kur'an Yolu draws out that these idols cannot know resurrection's time when even their makers cannot. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(ikinci yorum)]

## 16:20/q08 Why add 'they are created' after saying they create nothing?  [وَهُمْ يُخْلَقُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the added createdness mainly supplies an independent premise paired with noncreation or unfolds the earlier creator/noncreator inference; al-Ālūsī discusses both and prefers the pairing explanation.
- 16:20/q08/p1 It adds an independent defect: lack of creative power does not itself prove createdness or dependence. — Al-Rāzī says it goes beyond the earlier noncreation statement. Al-Ālūsī says noncreation does not entail createdness and their possible natures need an agent to exist and continue. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it advances the proof against their divinity. An objection reported by al-Ālūsī says pairing noncreation with createdness explains the repetition of the earlier creator contrast; al-Ālūsī prefers this pairing account. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل عليه); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 16:20/q08/p2 It completes the inference from the preceding universal creator versus noncreator distinction. — An authority reported by al-Ālūsī says 16:17 denies likeness because a noncreator cannot share creation with the Creator. Al-Ālūsī answers an objection that this premise was already specified to God and idols: the universal wording and evident contextual referents still let the earlier premise serve the proof. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل عليه)-]

## 16:20/q09 What is the effect of the passive 'are created' and the pronoun's placement?  [وَهُمْ يُخْلَقُونَ · rhetoric]
- 16:20/q09/p1 The passive and fronted pronoun emphasize that these beings undergo creation, with the agent left obvious. — An authority reported by al-Ālūsī says the passive sharpens the contrast with their inability to create and leaves God unnamed because the agent is clear. Al-Ālūsī says putting 'they' first adds emphasis and both creation verbs retain their ordinary sense. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]

## 16:20/q10 What theological conclusion follows from their inability to create and their createdness?  [* · theology]
- 16:20/q10/p1 They cannot be divine or worthy of worship; God alone has the required creative power and independent existence. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the two defects a comprehensive denial of divine attributes. Jishumī argues that originated bodies need an originator unlike bodies. Al-Bayḍāwī says a deity must exist necessarily. Al-Biqāʿī adds God's perfect power and knowledge. Al-Rāzī requires a true deity to live without dying. Iṣlāḥī calls their worship foolish; al-Ālūsī says spelling out obvious defects exposes the idolaters' folly. Asad and Study Quran stress that the worshipped beings are created while God is uncreated. [10 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(eski tefsirler); STUDYQURAN]

## 16:20/q11 Where does this clause end before the following words?  [وَهُمْ يُخْلَقُونَ · grammar]
- 16:20/q11/p1 The clause ends at 'they are created'; 'dead' starts a new sentence. — Jishumī supplies 'they are dead' for the new sentence and takes the later ayyān as an accusative time adverb. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 16:20/q12 How does the next verse's description of death bear on these invoked beings?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ · links]
turns on: Whether the subsequent 'dead' refers to lifeless idols, deceased saints, mortal angels, or spiritually dead unbelievers.
- 16:20/q12/p1 The idols are literally lifeless, without even animal awareness. — Al-Baghawī says they are dead, not alive; al-Rāzī contrasts them with a deity who must always live. Abū Ḥayyān says the following 'not living' intensifies the point and denies even animal perception or rational knowledge. Kur'an Yolu prefers this early understanding because seeking help from an inanimate object is irrational. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(eski tefsirler)+]
- 16:20/q12/p2 The dead beings are deceased saints. — Asad takes those invoked as saints granted divine or semi-divine qualities and points to 16:21. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 16:20/q12/p3 The angels invoked are mortal, like humans. — Kur'an Yolu reports this explanation alongside the proposal that the invoked beings are angels treated as God's daughters. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 16:20/q12/p4 The unbelievers are figuratively dead through heedlessness and lack of reason. — Kur'an Yolu reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's figurative reading of the following 'dead and lifeless.' [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)]

## 16:20/q13 How does this verse develop the surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes relate it both to the general creator contrast in 16:17 and to the developing account of God's favors and knowledge through 16:19; these links can coexist.
- 16:20/q13/p1 It makes explicit the contrast in 16:17 between the Creator and noncreators. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the denial of idols' creative power and knowledge had already been implied and that this clause is joined to 16:17; al-Ālūsī reports an inference from the universal distinction to the idols' dissimilarity to God. Al-Rāzī says the second phrase adds their createdness to the earlier contrast. An objection reported by al-Ālūsī says the 16:17 reasoning had already identified God and idols; al-Ālūsī responds that its universal wording can still serve the proof. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة,قيل عليه); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 16:20/q13/p2 After presenting God's favors, power and knowledge of intentions, it refutes worship of others. — Jishumī says the passage moves from God's power and favors as Creator to direct argument against other worship. Al-Ṭabrisī adds His knowledge of intentions. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects this clause with the statement at 16:19 that God knows what people conceal. Al-Ālūsī sees it as exposing the worshipped gods' obvious unfitness and their worshippers' folly. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:20/q14 How does the mention of God here compare with the parallel in al-Aʿrāf?  [ٱللَّهِ · links]
- 16:20/q14/p1 Al-Naḥl says 'besides God,' whereas al-Aʿrāf says 'besides Him.' — Al-Burhān records the wording contrast without supplying a further explanation. [1 notes: BURHAN]
