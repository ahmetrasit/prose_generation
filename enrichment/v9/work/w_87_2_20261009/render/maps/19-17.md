# 19:17: 31 questions

## 19:17/q01 What was the screen Mary placed between herself and others?  [حِجَابًۭا · referent]
turns on: What physical barrier ḥijāb denotes: fabric, walls, a mountain, clothing, divine shade, or an unspecified screen.
- 19:17/q01/p1 A curtain or hanging screen. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls it a screen or curtain (Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī); Rāzī finds a curtain more likely than a wall or other barrier. Al-Kāshifī says it blocked people's view. Ibn al-Jawzī defines ḥijāb as what prevents sight and compares the screen of the Prophet's wives. [11 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z); YAHYA-SALLAM(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 19:17/q01/p2 A wall or walls. — Al-Suddī is cited repeatedly for walls; Baghawī and Study Quran present this beside the curtain account. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي عن أشياخه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z)]
- 19:17/q01/p3 A mountain between Mary and her people. — Muqātil invokes the mountain hiding the sun in 38:32, a year's journey from Mount Qāf; Ibn ʿAbbās reports a mountain, and al-Durr says God was preparing to honor Mary and announce Jesus. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 19:17/q01/p4 A general barrier separating Mary from her family or people. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr and others say it hid her from view without specifying its substance. Al-Dāmghānī says ittakhadhat can mean drawing or spreading the screen. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; MAWARDI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:17/q01/p5 Shade from the sun provided by God. — Ibn ʿAbbās says the shade screened Mary from others; Ibn al-Jawzī says the sun shaded her so none could see. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 19:17/q01/p6 Clothing used as a screen. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān preserve this alternative alongside the wall account. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي)]

## 19:17/q02 Why did Mary withdraw behind the screen?  [فَٱتَّخَذَتْ حِجَابًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the seclusion was principally devotion, ritual washing, or grooming; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes the textual basis of the detailed menstrual report.
- 19:17/q02/p1 She sought solitude for worship, prayer, or meditation. — Al-Māwardī says she withdrew from people for worship; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes Zechariah finding provision in her sanctuary (3:37); Asad says she wished to pray and meditate undisturbed; Iṣlāḥī calls it iʿtikāf. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:17/q02/p2 She secluded herself to bathe or purify herself after menstruation. — Al-Kalbī relates Zechariah moved her to his home with her sister during menstruation; ʿIkrima says she stayed at her aunt's home then washed, sometimes unclothed; al-Farrāʾ says she raised the screen at menstruation. Reports vary as to whether she met Gabriel while washing, after dressing, or after entering her home; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the wording does not support the detailed menstrual account. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الكلبي); FARRA; ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z); TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q02/p3 She went aside to comb her hair, specifically to remove lice in one report. — Ibn ʿAbbās mentions combing with purification; ʿAṭāʾ specifies removing lice; Ibn ʿĀshūr also allows hair-combing. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,عطاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:17/q03 Where was Mary when she made the screen and Gabriel arrived?  [فَٱتَّخَذَتْ حِجَابًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reported setting of her withdrawal and whether the detailed mosque and home narrative is warranted.
- 19:17/q03/p1 At her aunt's or Zechariah's home after leaving the mosque during menstruation. — Al-Kalbī says Zechariah took her to his home with her sister; ʿIkrima says her aunt's house. Bursawī names the aunt Eshaa and says Mary washed in winter. Accounts place Gabriel's arrival while she washed, after dressing, or after entering the house; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the detailed report as unsupported by the wording. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الكلبي); THALABI(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q03/p2 In an eastern room or east of the prayer chamber, behind a screen. — Al-Kalbī specifies an eastern room after her washing; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says some locate her east of the prayer chamber. Asad reports Ibn Kathīr's suggestion of a Temple chamber dedicated to Mary by her mother (3:35–37). [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Ibn Kathir)]

## 19:17/q04 What does 'Our Spirit' refer to here?  [رُوحَنَا · referent]
turns on: The antecedent of 'appeared,' the messenger's later speech, and whether 4:171's 'spirit from Him' identifies this sent spirit.
- 19:17/q04/p1 Gabriel, the angel sent to Mary. — Many authorities identify him; al-Zajjāj and al-Qurṭubī cite the ensuing human appearance; Rāzī cites the messenger's later words and 26:193–94. Ibn Kathīr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite the revelation angel named elsewhere as Gabriel, Holy Spirit, faithful Spirit and noble messenger (2:97, 16:102, 26:193, 81:19). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains collective angelic speech in 3:45–47 as one speaking for the group; Asad calls him the angel of revelation. [40 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والسدي وابن جريج وابن منبه); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك وقتادة وابن جريج ووهب بن منبه والسدي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirciler); STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭs, Z,Z); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI(أبو مسلم)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 19:17/q04/p2 Jesus' spirit, or the spirit from which Jesus was created. — Al-Zajjāj invokes 4:171; al-Māwardī and Abū Muslim speak of the creating spirit, while Ubayy's account says Jesus' soul belonged to those pledged in Adam's time and entered Mary through her mouth. Some explain the pronoun of 'appeared' as an angel even if the spirit means Jesus. Ibn Kathīr calls Ubayy's report strange, objectionable and seemingly Israelite; al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject the split referents because the pronoun refers to the sent spirit. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج والماوردي); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs,Ubayy ibn Kaʿb); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)-; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q04/p3 A light cast on Mary as a special divine gift. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says Jesus, the Spirit of God, emerged from this light's radiance and effects. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 19:17/q04/p4 An angel without specifying Gabriel. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers an angel from God's sending and possessive attribution; Abū Ṣāliḥ calls him an angel who breathed into Mary's garment; Asad describes an angel or spiritual medium of inspiration. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 19:17/q04/p5 A distinct angel whose proper name is Rawḥannā or Rūḥunnā. — Al-Naqqāsh reports a reading with doubled nūn; Ibn ʿAṭiyya had seen no other witness for it and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the interpretation of an angel other than Gabriel. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش); SAMIN-DURR(النقاش); ALUSI-FULL(النقاش); TABATABAI-]

## 19:17/q05 Why is Gabriel called God's Spirit?  [رُوحَنَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'spirit' highlights life-giving revelation, Gabriel's spiritual nature, or affectionate attribution; these explanations can coexist.
- 19:17/q05/p1 He brings revelation through which religion and human souls gain life. — Al-Naḥḥās compares Jesus' title; Ibn al-Anbārī calls Gabriel the bearer of revelation and relates spirit to breath and joy. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī, Bursawī and Study Quran connect spiritual life with his revelation. Asad notes rūḥ can mean inspiration or its angelic medium. [12 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 19:17/q05/p2 He is spiritual or created from spirit, with God's possessive marking honor. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī describe his spiritual nature and honor; al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress the possessive honor. Bursawī says spirit literally describes Gabriel's abstract reality and figuratively his imagined form, invoking life in the Samaritan's calf from his trace; Asad reports Rāzī's view of angels as purely spiritual. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض الكبار); ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 19:17/q05/p3 It is an affectionate metaphor of divine closeness and honor. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Bursawī compare calling a beloved person 'my soul'; Rāzī includes God's affectionate attribution. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 19:17/q06 How is 'Our Spirit' read?  [رُوحَنَا · readings]
turns on: The rāʾ vowel and possible nūn doubling, with different interpretations of the resulting name.
- 19:17/q06/p1 Rūḥanā, with ḍamma on rāʾ, the majority reading. — Al-Samīn says it names that by which people live. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 19:17/q06/p2 Rawḥanā, with fatḥa on rāʾ. — Abū Ḥaywa, and also Sahl in some sources, transmit it. Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī connect it to relief, spiritual life, nearness to God or the promised spirit in 56:88–89. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو حيوة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو نهيك); RAZI-FULL(أبو حيوة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة وسهل); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيوة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة وسهل)]
- 19:17/q06/p3 A reading with doubled nūn, treating the word as an angel's name. — Al-Naqqāsh is the reported transmitter; al-Suyūṭī reports Abū Ḥaywa for a doubled letter and Ibn Mihrān's interpretation as Gabriel's proper name. Ibn ʿAṭiyya has not found al-Naqqāsh's reading elsewhere; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the distinct-angel interpretation. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش); SAMIN-DURR(النقاش); ITQAN(أبو حيوة,ابن مهران); ALUSI-FULL(النقاش); TABATABAI-]

## 19:17/q07 What does the Spirit's 'appearing' to Mary mean?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ لَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tamaththala describes a change of the angel's body or only how he became perceptible to Mary.
- 19:17/q07/p1 He assumed or presented the appearance of a human to Mary while remaining an angel or spirit. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī explain tamaththala as taking a complete human likeness; al-Ālūsī derives it from mithāl. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an assumed simile. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues the word means presentation to an observer without change of species; the Qur'anic context says Gabriel remained angelic, and this differs from an image with no real thing behind it. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:17/q07/p2 Gabriel really took a human-sized bodily form, with size change explicable by bodily or spiritual constitution. — Rāzī says a bodily angel may have few essential and many additional parts, or a spiritual angel may assume large or small bodies. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that this presupposes a real transformation absent from the verse. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 19:17/q08 How is the human figure's form described?  [بَشَرًۭا سَوِيًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether sawiyyan describes only sound human proportions or a more specific reported likeness: a handsome youth, Yusuf, or Adam.
- 19:17/q08/p1 A complete, sound, well-proportioned human form. — Al-Suddī and Wahb give a balanced form; al-Ṭabarī says a properly proportioned man from humankind. Al-Dāmghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī compare God's fashioning the human form (32:9; 82:7). Ibn ʿĀshūr calls bashar a simile for a complete human. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL(السدي,وهب بن منبه); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirciler); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z)]
- 19:17/q08/p2 A handsome, smooth-faced or beardless young man, often described with curly hair. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās describe a fair or smooth face, tightly curled hair, and in Ibn ʿAbbās's report the first moustache. Other reports call him radiant and blemish-free; details vary between beardless, smooth-cheeked, and just beginning a moustache. [11 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z)]
- 19:17/q08/p3 He looked like a familiar sanctuary servant named Yusuf. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls Yusuf a peer of Mary's; Rāzī reports him as a contemporary; al-Ālūsī calls him a relative and says the familiar form would put her at ease. Rāzī says the text cannot decide between this and the handsome youth report. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:17/q08/p4 He appeared in the form of Adam. — This is Qatāda's report through Yaḥyā ibn Sallām. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة)]

## 19:17/q09 How does 'a sound human' function grammatically?  [بَشَرًۭا سَوِيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:17/q09/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial expression describing how the spirit appeared. — Al-Samīn specifies that the ḥāl relates to the subject of tamaththala and that an indefinite noun can be ḥāl because sawiyyan describes it; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls bashar a circumstantial simile. [4 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:17/q10 Why did the Spirit appear in human form?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ بَشَرًۭا سَوِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the form served communication, a test of chastity, conception through desire, or the life-giving mission; al-Ālūsī disputes the desire claim from Mary's response.
- 19:17/q10/p1 To put Mary at ease so she could hear and speak with him without fear. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī and others say an angel's true form would frighten her; al-Qurṭubī and Asad say she could not bear or see it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that a human speaker is a fitting form for a messenger, comparing worldly life appearing as a woman. [12 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z)]
- 19:17/q10/p2 To test and display Mary's chastity and piety. — Al-Zamakhsharī says her immediate refuge from the beautiful figure proves chastity; Iṣlāḥī says the unexpected angelic encounter tested an exceptionally pure character. Al-Ālūsī invokes her immediate refuge against an arousal account. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:17/q10/p3 To awaken desire, leading to conception through Mary's bodily response. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports a suggestion that his speech reassured Mary and perhaps aroused seminal fluid; al-Qāshānī says the handsome shape acted through imagination like a wet dream. Al-Ālūsī calls this indecent and inconsistent with Mary's immediate refuge and chastity. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 19:17/q10/p4 The complete human form suited his mission to breathe life producing a human child. — Bursawī joins this to Mary's ability to hear God's words comfortably. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 19:17/q11 What manner of conception is reported?  [فَأَرْسَلْنَآ رُوحَنَا فَتَمَثَّلَ · reports]
turns on: Whether conception followed a breath through clothing, entry of Jesus' spirit through Mary's mouth, or an extraordinary bodily process induced or supported by the Spirit.
- 19:17/q11/p1 Gabriel breathed into the opening, sleeve, neckline, or robe of Mary's garment, and the breath reached her womb. — Ibn Jurayj mentions the opening and sleeve; Umayya ibn Abī al-Ṣalt's poem describes the neckline and a well-formed child born alone. Abū Ṣāliḥ says the breath entered her vulva, while Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says it reached where God willed; reports differ on the garment opening. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أمية بن أبي الصلت,ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,سعيد بن جبير)]
- 19:17/q11/p2 Jesus' spirit entered Mary's mouth after appearing and speaking as a man. — Al-Zajjāj reports entrance through her mouth without further detail; Ubayy ibn Kaʿb identifies the entering spirit with Jesus. Ibn Kathīr judges Ubayy's version strange, objectionable and seemingly Israelite. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب)]
- 19:17/q11/p3 The human appearance stirred Mary's own seminal emission, enabling conception. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports this as a possibility. Al-Qāshānī compares a wet dream: imagined form passes through faculties into nature; revelation and true dreams quiet bodily faculties, while images from the heart and holy spirits affect the body. Al-Ālūsī rejects the associated arousal proposal as contrary to Mary's chastity. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 19:17/q11/p4 A Holy Spirit aided extraordinary self-conception through Mary's own bodily faculties. — Al-Qāshānī compares male semen to rennet and female semen to milk, each possessing contracting and receiving power in different degrees. He says Mary's masculine warmth made right-kidney semen hotter; a receptive retaining womb let right and left semen act as male and female. The Holy Spirit could alter temperament beyond measurable proportion. [5 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:17/q12 How long did Mary's pregnancy last?  [رُوحَنَا · reports]
turns on: The differing reports' stated duration, including the one-episode and eight-month miracle accounts.
- 19:17/q12/p1 Nine months. — Al-Kalbī and Abū al-Qāsim al-Ṣaymarī give this duration. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو القاسم الصيمري,الكلبي)]
- 19:17/q12/p2 Conception and delivery occurred in one episode. — This is an alternative reported from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 19:17/q12/p3 One day. — Al-Māwardī records this as another view. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 19:17/q12/p4 Eight months. — Al-Māwardī adds that Jesus alone survived birth at eight months as a sign. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 19:17/q13 Is assimilation read in 'fa-tamaththala lahā'?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ لَهَا · readings]
turns on: Which sound Ruways transmission is followed; both assimilation and clear articulation are reported.
- 19:17/q13/p1 Ruways assimilates the adjacent sounds in this phrase in one sound transmission. — Al-Dānī and Ibn al-Faḥḥām transmit the assimilation here and in wa-li-tuṣnaʿa ʿalayya; al-Tadhkira and al-Mabhaj list both. Ibn al-Jazarī places this among fourteen optional Ruways assimilation sites across several sūras. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني وابن الفحام,الباقون عن رويس)]
- 19:17/q13/p2 Ruways also has a sound transmission with clear pronunciation here. — Other transmitters preserve clear pronunciation across the listed passages; Ibn al-Jazarī accepts both transmissions. [1 notes: NASHR(الباقون عن رويس)]

## 19:17/q14 Could Mary have been a prophetess?  [فَأَرْسَلْنَآ إِلَيْهَا رُوحَنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether an angelic mission and conversation establish prophethood, despite the lack of explicit designation and the usual male-prophet tradition.
- 19:17/q14/p1 Yes; the divine mission and angel's conversation suggest prophethood. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers it; Abū Ḥayyān treats the wording as apparent evidence. Ibn Ḥazm and Rūzbihān add that named Qur'anic figures are generally prophets (12:109). [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ibn Ḥazm and Rūzbihān al-Baqlī); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 19:17/q14/p2 No; an angel may speak in human form to a person who is not a prophet. — Abū Ḥayyān compares Gabriel appearing as Diḥya and in the report about Islam and faith. Most authorities say the Qur'an never explicitly calls Mary a prophet and tradition generally holds prophets male; they rank her instead among the most pious women (12:109). [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(most Muslim authorities); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 19:17/q15 Can an angel of immense size appear as a human-sized body?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ بَشَرًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether manifestation changes Gabriel's actual bodily dimensions or presents an unchanged angel through human perception.
- 19:17/q15/p1 Yes; essential bodily parts may be few, or a spiritual being may assume bodies of different sizes. — Rāzī answers the objection that losing parts destroys Gabriel and overlapping them is impossible by distinguishing essential from additional bodily parts, or spiritual from bodily existence. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q15/p2 The objection fails because the verse describes an appearance to Mary, not bodily transformation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects Rāzī's physical answer for presupposing a transformation; Gabriel remained an angel perceived in human shape. He compares Satan appearing as Surāqa at Badr (8:48), other reported appearances, and dream figures without independent visible bodies. [7 notes: TABATABAI]
- 19:17/q15/p3 A huge body cannot become human-sized without destructive loss or impossible overlapping parts. — This objection is recorded by Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī as an argument put to the exegetes. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون في التفسير الكبير وغيره); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]

## 19:17/q16 Does angelic human appearance undermine certainty that a person seen twice is the same?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ بَشَرًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether recognition rests directly on sensation or on tested inference from sensory images, and whether a possible impersonation defeats ordinary certainty.
- 19:17/q16/p1 It could: an angel, jinn, or duplicate might impersonate the known person. — Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī record the example of Zayd seen on two days; the objection says sensory recognition alone would invite skepticism. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون في التفسير الكبير وغيره); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q16/p2 The possibility is already present if God can create an exact duplicate or naturalists' rare conjunctions occur. — Rāzī says the objection is not specific to angels, since a freely acting Creator or planetary conjunction could yield the same apparent duplicate. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects an added reply that such duplicates are rare because its rarity could not be established. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 19:17/q16/p3 Sensation supplies an image; reason and tested experience judge the external person's identity. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites shifts of apparent size, direction, motion and the sun's size, fear-produced images and mirages. The sensed image is certain as an experience, while external identification requires thought and evidence; this does not abolish trust in the senses. He rejects the unproven 'rare duplicate' reply. [5 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 19:17/q17 Does human manifestation imply that angels could appear as insects or other animals?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ بَشَرًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether one reported human manifestation entails animal forms, and whether revelation establishes their nonoccurrence.
- 19:17/q17/p1 Yes, as an alleged absurd consequence of allowing human form. — Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī record this third objection. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون في التفسير الكبير وغيره); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q17/p2 Reason allows the possibility, but reliable revelation establishes that such cases do not occur. — Rāzī answers the objection by separating intellectual possibility from divinely reported actuality. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q17/p3 Human appearance does not entail animal appearance, and bare possibility is harmless. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the appeal to revelation because he finds no reliable scriptural proof excluding animal forms. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 19:17/q18 Does angelic impersonation undermine mass-transmitted reports?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ بَشَرًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether revelation or tested inference can secure the identity of figures known through sensory reports.
- 19:17/q18/p1 It could, since the person at Badr might be an impersonator rather than Muhammad. — Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī record this fourth objection. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون في التفسير الكبير وغيره); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q18/p2 Reliable revelation rules out appearances that would unsettle transmitted certainty. — Rāzī gives this answer, alongside his answer about insect form; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that revelation itself comes through hearing and would face the same uncertainty. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 19:17/q18/p3 Ordinary confidence rests on reason and tested experience interpreting sensory images. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī applies his account of perception to transmitted reports and rejects circular reliance on revelation; appearances of Satan as Surāqa at Badr and other figures show that a real being can present a familiar image. [5 notes: TABATABAI]

## 19:17/q19 What does Mary's response to the visitor show?  [فَتَمَثَّلَ لَهَا بَشَرًۭا · interpretation]
- 19:17/q19/p1 Her immediate appeal for refuge displays exceptional chastity and piety. — Al-Zamakhsharī treats the handsome visitor as a test; Iṣlāḥī says the unexpected divine encounter showed unmatched character. Al-Ālūsī uses the next verse's refuge to reject the proposal that she was aroused. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:17/q20 What practice was associated with Mary's screen?  [حِجَابًۭا · reports]
- 19:17/q20/p1 Christians use screens at altars and ritual sites because Mary screened herself. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this explanation to ʿUmar. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 19:17/q21 How was Mary's eastern withdrawal linked to prayer direction or symbolism?  [فَٱتَّخَذَتْ · links]
turns on: Whether east has a historical prayer association or a symbolic relation to Jesus; al-Ālūsī doubts the latter.
- 19:17/q21/p1 Reports connect Christian eastward prayer to Mary's eastern retreat, while her eastward direction may have been incidental. — Al-Ālūsī also records a report that Christians faced Jerusalem during Jesus' life; Bursawī reports al-Ḥasan's contrast of Christians facing east and Jews west because the Torah meeting occurred on the mountain's western side (28:44). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن)]
- 19:17/q21/p2 The physical sunrise in the east prefigured Jesus as spiritual light. — Al-Ālūsī records this proposed symbolism but calls it far-fetched. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 19:17/q22 How does this story connect to the surrounding sūra and prior sūra?  [* · links]
- 19:17/q22/p1 The sūras join accounts of astonishing births and events; John's birth prepares for Jesus' birth. — Abū Ḥayyān compares al-Kahf's Cave, Moses and Khiḍr, and Dhū al-Qarnayn with John's birth to old and barren parents and Jesus' birth without a father (18:9, 60, 83). Iṣlāḥī says John was Jesus' predecessor and relative, making Christians reconsider deifying one of two consecutive prophets in the same family. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:17/q23 Where was Sūrat Maryam revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 19:17/q23/p1 The sūra is Meccan, with a reported Medinan exception for its prostration verse. — Abū Ḥayyān says it is Meccan like the prior sūra; Muqātil places the prostration verse in Medina after migration to Abyssinia. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:17/q24 What is the veil in the inward reading?  [حِجَابًۭا · inward]
- 19:17/q24/p1 The sanctuary of holiness, separated from the soul's world by the chest and the limits of material perception. — Al-Qāshānī says the Holy Spirit could be sent only after Mary rose through detachment into the holy world. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:17/q25 How does the inward reading explain the Spirit's effect on Mary's body?  [رُوحَنَا فَتَمَثَّلَ بَشَرًۭا · inward]
- 19:17/q25/p1 Holy Spirit and imagined human form act through the heart, animal and natural souls to comfort and alter Mary's bodily powers. — Al-Qāshānī likens revelation to true dreams with quiet bodily faculties; images of the rational soul and holy spirits pass into the body, stirring desire and conception. He adds that Spirit's support can change temperament beyond proportion. [5 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:17/q26 What did Jesus' extraordinary birth lead some followers to claim?  [* · theology]
- 19:17/q26/p1 Some followers deified Jesus despite his call to worship God alone. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the extraordinary miracle prompted this misdirection. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 19:17/q27 How is Muhammad's mission contrasted with disputes over Jesus?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:17/q27/p1 After centuries of dispute, God chose a final messenger who was fully human and shared ordinary human needs. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 25:7 and presents this as following the disputes over Jesus' nature. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 19:17/q28 What is the great sign of Muhammad's prophethood in this comparison?  [* · theology]
- 19:17/q28/p1 The Qur'an, a clear Arabic book the Arabs could not match. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ frames her study around this sign. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 19:17/q29 Did the Muʿtazilites deny miracles other than the Qur'an?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether declining to use unseen reports in later debate amounts to denying the miracles themselves.
- 19:17/q29/p1 No; they affirmed other miracles as proofs but did not rely in debate on reports unavailable to later audiences. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ explicitly rejects the claim of wholesale denial. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 19:17/q29/p2 They denied all miracles other than the Qur'an. — This is the claim Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects. [0 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]

## 19:17/q30 How does Islam relate to earlier revelations in this account?  [* · theology]
- 19:17/q30/p1 It confirms and safeguards their truth and completes the prophetic message as humanity matures in responsibility. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects this to Muhammad's final mission. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 19:17/q31 What action preceded Mary's taking a screen?  [* · meaning]
- 19:17/q31/p1 Mary withdrew from her people. — Al-Ṭabarī explains intabadhat in the preceding verse as form VIII of n-b-dh, 'to cast aside.' [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
