# 91:14: 24 questions

## 91:14/q01 What did they deny in 'they denied him'?  [فَكَذَّبُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether denial names rejection of the threatened punishment, the camel's divine status, Ṣāliḥ generally, or the act itself; al-Naḥḥās disputes reversing fa's temporal order.
- 91:14/q01/p1 They rejected Ṣāliḥ's warning that harming the camel would bring punishment. — Ṭabarī identifies the threatened drinking-day penalty; al-Zajjāj cites 7:73. Al-Wāḥidī says the accusative 'God's she-camel' signals warning; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the imperative implies a threat, explicit in 7:73, and distinguishes this later denial from an earlier rejection. Islahi says they dismissed the last warning as intimidation. [23 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:14/q01/p2 They denied Ṣāliḥ's divine report or claim about God's she-camel and its allotted water. — Ibn Qutayba identifies the claim that it was God's camel. Al-Farrāʾ and Ṭabarī say the people accepted alternating drinking days without true belief; al-Naḥḥās rejects that claim as unsupported and contradicted by a report. Al-Ālūsī prefers the interpretation that Ṣāliḥ relayed God's command. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI+; NAHHAS-]
- 91:14/q01/p3 They rejected Ṣāliḥ himself and his message or prophethood. — Ibn Kathīr adds that the camel came from rock as a sign and proof. Kuran Yolu says the disaster followed persistent denial of prophethood and evil, beyond the slaughter alone; Corpus Coranicum notes that kadhdhaba repeats verse 11 and may reflect Syriac kaddeb. [10 notes: BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM; QUSHAYRI]
- 91:14/q01/p4 Their hamstringing itself amounted to denial. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn Qutayba allow the action to precede or constitute denial; Ṭabarī says linked cause and act can be expressed in either order. Al-Naḥḥās objects that fa marks a subsequent event, and the warning followed by slaughter makes reversal unnecessary. [8 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); BURHAN; NAHHAS-]

## 91:14/q02 Why does denial precede hamstringing, and how do the clauses connect?  [فَكَذَّبُوهُ فَعَقَرُوهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether fa marks event order, rhetorical sequence, or the answer to the earlier 'when' clause.
- 91:14/q02/p1 Denial preceded and prompted the hamstringing; fa gives sequence. — Al-Naḥḥās insists fa places the slaughter after denial. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says denial caused the act. Al-Yamānī's natural ordering, quoted by al-Suyūṭī, puts rejection before slaughter. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads a second denial after the warning, while allowing a summary followed by detail. [9 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA; ITQAN; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS+]
- 91:14/q02/p2 The acts were concurrent or reversed in reality: hamstringing was itself denial. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ṭabarī allow ordering of closely linked cause and act either way; Ibn Qutayba expressly reads hamstringing first. Al-Naḥḥās argues against this because fa signals succession. [8 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); BURHAN; NAHHAS-]
- 91:14/q02/p3 'They denied him' is complete; 'they hamstrung her' answers the earlier clause about the most wretched one rising. — Al-Farrāʾ proposes this alternative; Ṭabarī and the grammarians he reports connect the second verb with 91:12. Al-Naḥḥās also accepts the completed first clause. [4 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); NAHHAS]
- 91:14/q02/p4 Fa can introduce details after a general statement, with intervening events. — Al-Biqāʿī calls it explanatory. Ibn ʿĀshūr says textual sequence need not reproduce the event order, comparing 2:36 and 7:4. [3 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:14/q03 Had Thamūd ever believed Ṣāliḥ before this denial?  [فَكَذَّبُوهُ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported early adherence occurred.
- 91:14/q03/p1 They remained unbelievers throughout. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call this the majority view. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(جمهور المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور,الجمهور وقيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 91:14/q03/p2 They followed Ṣāliḥ as Muslims for a time, then denied him and killed the camel. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this from a group; Abū Ḥayyān preserves it as an alternative to the majority. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وقيل,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 91:14/q04 What does 'hamstrung her' mean?  [فَعَقَرُوهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb's direct sense is the disabling cut or the resulting slaughter.
- 91:14/q04/p1 It means cutting a camel's legs or flesh so that blood flows and it falls for slaughter; killing follows by association. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī describe cutting into flesh and bloodshed; al-Ṭabrisī compares damage at an animal's root to a basin's base. Elmalılı specifies legs and tendons. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes wounding forelegs so the camel kneels for breast slaughter; Islahi says killing is the resultant, not primary, sense. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:14/q04/p2 It means slaughtering or killing the she-camel. — Al-Ālūsī gives slaughter or killing as the sense; Study Quran reports commentators who render it slaughter, with 7:77–78. Asad refers to his note on 7:91 for his rendering without restating it here. [4 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ASAD-NOTES]

## 91:14/q05 Who hamstrung the camel, and why is the act attributed to all?  [فَعَقَرُوهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular culprit is the direct actor with collective consent or two men acted directly.
- 91:14/q05/p1 One direct killer acted with the people's approval or conspiracy, so all are charged. — Ṭabarī says they agreed to deny water and consented to slaughter despite the alternating days. Qatāda reports pledges from young and old, men and women, before Qudār acted. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān compare 54:29 or 7:77. Qutb adds failure to stop him; Islahi says dissenting reformers were spared. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the plural also follows Thamūd's collective grammar. [29 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); TABRISI; RAZI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR(قتادة); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 91:14/q05/p2 Two men performed the killing. — Al-Farrāʾ reports two perpetrators; al-Qurṭubī explains that Arabic can use the singular superlative 'most wretched' for two. [4 notes: RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)]

## 91:14/q06 What names and descriptions are reported for the killer and accomplice?  [فَعَقَرُوهَا · reports]
turns on: The reports preserve different names or descriptions and a literary comparison.
- 91:14/q06/p1 The killer was Qudār or Qidār, a rebellious Thamūd leader. — Al-Suhaylī gives Qudār ibn Sālif and names his mother Qādira. Islahi calls Qidār autocratic; an Arabic couplet and al-Afwah's poetry make him a proverbial corrupt figure beside Qayl of ʿĀd. [5 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(al-Afwah al-Awdī)]
- 91:14/q06/p2 Muṣda ibn Wihrāwa ibn Jahm was his accomplice. — Al-Suhaylī supplies the name in Bursevī's report. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي)]
- 91:14/q06/p3 A Prophetic report calls the camel's hamstringers the most wretched of earlier generations. — Study Quran reports the Prophet's designation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 91:14/q06/p4 Aḥmar is named as culprit in a likely authentic poem, though elsewhere linked with ʿĀd. — Corpus Coranicum says this may show overlapping tribal names or traditions; the poem has the calf's curse, not a divine warner or explicit command. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:14/q07 What happened to the camel's calf after the slaughter?  [فَعَقَرُوهَا · reports]
- 91:14/q07/p1 It fled up a mountain and cried three times that its mother had been killed; the people could not seize it. — Muqātil says the people asked Ṣāliḥ what to do. Al-Ḥasan says it escaped to a mountain cleft and God struck their hearts. [3 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن)]

## 91:14/q08 What warning, respite, and destruction followed the slaughter?  [فَعَقَرُوهَا فَدَمْدَمَ · reports]
turns on: The narrative reports specify different moments of the episode.
- 91:14/q08/p1 Ṣāliḥ gave three days marked by yellow, red, then black faces; punishment came on the fourth. — Muqātil links the three days to the calf's three cries. The people prepared graves, embalmed themselves, and first mistook the delay for mercy. Islahi also reports three days to repent, which increased arrogance. [2 notes: MUQATIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:14/q08/p2 Gabriel blocked the sun, cried, and their souls left as houses collapsed over their graves. — Muqātil places this on the fourth day and connects the ruined settlement to 11:68; Ibn al-Jawzī repeats the grave and collapsed-house account. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]
- 91:14/q08/p3 The camel was a demanded sign and a test with an assigned drinking turn. — Islahi cites 54:27–29 and says Ṣāliḥ gave it after their demand. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:14/q09 How is fa-damdama read?  [فَدَمْدَمَ · readings]
turns on: The manuscripts and cited readers differ in consonants and wording; al-Jishumī restricts recitation to the established form.
- 91:14/q09/p1 The established widespread reading is fa-damdama. — Al-Jishumī says only this form may be recited despite Ibn al-Zubayr's variant; Abū Ḥayyān labels it the majority reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وابن الزبير); JISHUMI(ابن زبير)+]
- 91:14/q09/p2 Ibn al-Zubayr reads fa-dahdama or a related hāʾ form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says a codex has hāʾ between the dāls. Al-Qurṭubī calls the forms dialects, comparing imtaqa and intaqa; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say its meaning matches the common reading. Abū Ḥayyān connects it with repeated covering. [10 notes: JISHUMI(ابن زبير); IBNATIYYA(ابن الزبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن الزبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الزبير); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور وابن الزبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الزبير); SAMIN-DURR(ابن الزبير); ALUSI(ابن الزبير); ALUSI-FULL]
- 91:14/q09/p3 Some codices read fa-dammara. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the codices and mentions it among manuscript variants. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 91:14/q09/p4 Ibn Masʿūd's codex has fa-dammāhā ʿalayhim. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the reading and notes it again among variants. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 91:14/q10 What does damdama mean?  [فَدَمْدَمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is derived from covering, crushing, shaking, anger or sound, and how reduplication intensifies it.
- 91:14/q10/p1 It means punishment enclosing, covering, or overwhelming them. — Al-Zajjāj derives the sense from covering a grave or coating a fat camel; repetition strengthens it. Al-Rāzī compares coating every side and the doubled kabkabū. Al-Ālūsī analyzes the doubled form as faʿfal. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports grave-covering as the majority view but himself favors the sound of an angry cry. [37 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI(الزجاج,الواحدي); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,الواحدي); QURTUBI(بعض اللغويين); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Müfessirîn); IBNASHUR(أكثر المفسرين); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:14/q10/p2 It means destroying or annihilating them utterly. — ʿAṭāʾ, Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Muʾarrij give destruction or complete extermination; al-Muʾarrij specifies that nothing is left. Al-Qāmūs gives total punishment or grinding a people. Ibn Khālawayh glosses it 'destroyed.' [43 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(غيره); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; MAWARDI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي,ابن عباس,المؤرج); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(المدرج,عطاء ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء ومقاتل); TABRISI(ابن الأعرابي,عطاء، مقاتل,مؤرج، ابن الأعرابي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المؤرج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس,الصحاح,المؤرج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,المؤرج); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الصحاح,مؤرج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الصحاح,القاموس,مؤرج); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Kāmûs,Müfessirîn,Sıhâh-ı Cevherî); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:14/q10/p3 It means shaking or quaking the earth or a structure. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn al-Aʿrābī give the earthquake sense; al-Biqāʿī quotes al-Rāzī on shaking a building until it topples. Al-Wāḥidī calls this the view of most commentators. [8 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين); RAZI(ثعلب); RAZI-FULL(ثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BIQAI(الرازي); BIQAI-FULL(الرازي)]
- 91:14/q10/p4 It means leveling, crushing, or covering them with earth. — Al-Ṣiḥāḥ defines pressing an object to earth and crushing it; al-Qushayrī compares burial. Ibn ʿĀshūr says those who take this sense derive it from covering a grave. - 91:14/q10/p5 It means divine anger, or the angry cry that destroyed them. — Ibn al-Anbārī also uses the word for speech that agitates someone. Qutb says the sound of the word pictures terror. ## 91:14/q11 What form or sound effect does damdama have?  [فَدَمْدَمَ · grammar]
turns on: The notes distinguish morphological repetition from sound symbolism.
- 91:14/q11/p1 It is a reduplicated quadriliteral that intensifies repeated covering or punishment. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Rāzī compare doubled forms such as kabkabū. Al-Ālūsī calls the pattern faʿfal; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it faʿlala. Elmalılı derives it from damma, while al-Jishumī compares ṣarṣar. [11 notes: JISHUMI; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); RAZI(الزجاج); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 91:14/q11/p2 Its sound evokes the violence and terror of the destruction. — Qutb says the word itself pictures divine wrath's terrifying scene; al-Samīn lists sound imitation of a collapse among its glosses. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 91:14/q12 What does 'upon them' and 'their Lord' add?  [عَلَيْهِمْ رَبُّهُم · rhetoric]
- 91:14/q12/p1 'Upon them' conveys punishment descending with encompassing severity. — Al-Biqāʿī emphasizes its force and encirclement. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 91:14/q12/p2 'Their Lord' recalls the benefactor they denied, heightening the withdrawal of favor and wrath. — Al-Biqāʿī makes this contrast within the verse. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 91:14/q12/p3 Naming the Lord resolves suspense created by the earlier indirect mā in the oath series. — Corpus Coranicum says the creator emerges enigmatically earlier and is named explicitly here. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:14/q13 What does 'because of their sin' specify?  [بِذَنۢبِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The first explains the historical cause; the second explains why that cause is stated.
- 91:14/q13/p1 The bāʾ marks cause: the punishment came for their own denial, disbelief, and killing of the camel. — Al-Samīn gives the grammatical point. Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī list denial and slaughter; al-Biqāʿī stresses shared wrongdoing; Kuran Yolu says slaughter alone does not account for the judgment. - 91:14/q13/p2 It explicitly warns every sinner that sin has consequences. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls Thamūd's fate a grave warning; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī say stating the cause even after fa universalizes the warning. [7 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

- 91:14/q14/p5 God made later nations equal to Thamūd. — Al-Māwardī reports this further possibility without an argument. [1 notes: MAWARDI]

## 91:14/q15 How does the verse's wording connect destruction to creation earlier in the sūrah?  [فَسَوَّىٰهَا · links]
- 91:14/q15/p1 Sawwā in verse 14 echoes the fashioning of the soul in verse 7. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it complete paronomasia. Corpus Coranicum reads the double use as sarcastic, linking moral obligation in creation to judgment; Study Quran says God destroys as readily as He creates and punishment continues the soul's fashioning. Elmalılı calls leveling a reversal of fashioning. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]
- 91:14/q15/p2 The leveling echoes the sun and the objects sworn by earlier. — Al-Biqāʿī compares the leveled people with the sun in 91:1 and the subsequent oath objects. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 91:14/q16 What does the Thamūd episode do in the sūrah's argument and oath?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes identify either an implied oath answer and threat or the story's broader moral and historical function.
- 91:14/q16/p1 It warns Quraysh that rejecting Muhammad risks destruction like Thamūd's rejection of Ṣāliḥ. — Al-Zamakhsharī makes this the oath's answer and treats 'he succeeds' as an aside. Ibn ʿĀshūr also makes Thamūd's destruction the implied answer, its lām omitted after a long oath as in al-Burūj; he says the warning addressed arrogant Quraysh and recalls their sword punishment. Islahi says Quraysh leaders' reported plot against Muhammad sharpens the warning. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:14/q16/p2 It exemplifies humanity's choice between good and evil and the destruction of a rebellious community. — Corpus Coranicum calls it the oldest genuine Quranic punishment legend and suggests it may mirror the preacher's rejection. Kuran Yolu links it to the moral choice and to al-Aʿrāf and Hūd. Islahi says historical examples move ordinary hearers alongside cosmic and inward proofs. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:14/q16/p3 Thamūd alone suffices because its history was familiar to Quraysh. — Islahi quotes Farāhī that the allusion evoked inherited settlements and traditions; he cites Arab poetry about Qidār and says the rebellion and fate were proverbial. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(al-Afwah al-Awdī)]

## 91:14/q17 What does the verse teach about collective responsibility and divine judgment?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish social accountability, justice, and the camel's role as a sign.
- 91:14/q17/p1 A community shares worldly punishment when it collectively approves or fails to restrain wrongdoing. — Qutb calls this a principle of social accountability and says neglect of mutual counsel is culpable; he preserves individual judgment in the afterlife. Islahi likewise separates collective worldly punishment from individual final judgment and says reformers or dissenters are spared. Al-Biqāʿī includes consenting supporters in the simultaneous punishment. [7 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 91:14/q17/p2 God's destruction of the guilty is just. — Elmalılı argues that creation, restoration, and recompense belong to God, so annihilation does not harm Him or wrong them. Islahi says a nation receives time and clear warning before collective destruction. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:14/q17/p3 The punishment answers deliberate harm to a divine sign after warning. — Islahi says the camel tested the nation; further delay risked murder of the Messenger, a crime bringing national destruction. Kuran Yolu stresses persistent rejection beyond the slaughter. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 91:14/q18 How is this Thamūd account related to other accounts and traditions?  [* · links]
- 91:14/q18/p1 The Qur'anic allusion reworks an earlier camel story around a divine messenger, command, and ethical rejection. — Corpus Coranicum compares Umayya's likely authentic poem, which has cultic disrespect and a calf's curse but no divine warner; the Quran omits a lame survivor's aftermath because it does not serve the general lesson. It places Q 91 alongside 89, 85, and 105 as early narrative texts, with fuller Thamūd material later in Q 54 and shorter references in 69 and 51. [6 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:14/q18/p2 Other Quranic passages describe the catastrophe as a cry, earthquake, or humiliating thunderbolt. — Elmalılı cites 15:73, 15:83, 23:41, 7:78, 7:91, 29:37, and 41:17; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the cry in 15:73. [3 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 91:14/q18/p3 The repeated kadhdhaba foregrounds rejection of the messenger as the immediate cause. — Corpus Coranicum connects verse 14 with verse 11 and Syriac kaddeb, directing readers also to 95:7, 92:16, and 73:11. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:14/q19 How may verse 14 be divided in verse counting?  [* · sciences]
- 91:14/q19/p1 A verse break after ʿaqarūhā fits the offense and punishment divide and the rhyme. — Corpus Coranicum attributes this to the Ḥimṣ, Mecca b and Medina I traditions, noting that Ḥimṣ lacks a break after sawwāhā where the rhyme calls for one. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:14/q20 What virtue report is attached to reciting this sūrah?  [* · sciences]
- 91:14/q20/p1 A report equates its recitation with giving charity over everything lit by the sun and moon. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports the saying from the Prophet without a grading in this note. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 91:14/q21 What inward analogy is drawn from the camel?  [* · inward]
- 91:14/q21/p1 The camel represents the soul as a mount; its water represents remembrance and worship. — Al-Biqāʿī says neglecting these gifts resembles slaughtering the camel. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 91:14/q22 How does the camel warning supply the context for this verse?  [* · links]
- 91:14/q22/p1 The earlier command implies preserving the camel and its drinking right, with threatened punishment for harming her. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies omitted imperatives such as 'preserve,' 'leave,' or 'beware' and points to other sūra accounts of the camel. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 91:14/q23 How does the following clause connect after fa-sawwāhā?  [* · readings]
- 91:14/q23/p1 Wāw begins a new sentence; fāʾ instead links the clauses in sequence. — Ibn Khālawayh says the fear in 'he does not fear its consequence' is not the people's act, so wāw starts anew, while fāʾ presents a continued order. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 91:14/q24 How is Thamūd's transgression in the preceding verse read and understood?  [* · readings]
- 91:14/q24/p1 Ṭughyān means exceeding the bounds in corruption; some read bi-ṭughwā with ḍamma as a feminine verbal noun. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives the lexical sense and reports al-Ḥasan and Ḥammād ibn Maslama's ḍamma reading, comparing feminine verbal nouns rajʿā and ḥusnā. [2 notes: TABRISI(الحسن، حماد بن مسلمة)]
