# 37:55: 13 questions

## 37:55/q01 How is فَٱطَّلَعَ read?  [فَٱطَّلَعَ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vocalization and voice of the verb, and the form of the preceding question.
- 37:55/q01/p1 The usual reading has a connecting alif and a doubled, open ṭāʾ in faṭṭalaʿa, following the usual muṭṭaliʿūn. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to the majority and al-Fārisī to all readers except a reported Abū ʿAmr transmission; Abū al-Ḥasan, as cited by al-Fārisī, says the doubled form is commoner Arabic than aṭlaʿa. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(القراء كلهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن)+]
- 37:55/q01/p2 A reported Abū ʿAmr reading pronounces an initial hamza, still ṭāʾ, and kasra on the lām, alongside a differently vocalized preceding muṭṭaliʿūn. — Ibn Mujāhid also specifies fatḥa on the ʿayn. Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes it to Ḥusayn's transmission from Abū ʿAmr and Abū al-Barahsam, and cites al-Zajjāj that it fits the preceding form with kasra on lām; al-Fārisī also reports the exceptional sequence. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو في رواية حسين وأبو البرهسم)]
- 37:55/q01/p3 Some read fa-uṭṭuliʿa as a passive past verb. — Al-Ālūsī names Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Muḥayṣin, ʿAmmār ibn Abī ʿAmmār and Abū Sirāj, while also recording Ḥusayn al-Jaʿfī's transmission of Abū ʿAmr's preceding form with still ṭāʾ and fatḥa on nūn. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن عباس وابن محيصن وعمار بن أبي عمار وأبو سراج)]
- 37:55/q01/p4 A further sequence has a light first word with kasra on nūn, followed by the passive past verb. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this to Abū al-Barahsim and, through Khalaf, ʿAmmār ibn Abī ʿAmmār. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو البرهسم وعمار ابن أبي عمار)]
- 37:55/q01/p5 A reported Ibn ʿAbbās reading gives the preceding question an object suffix: 'Will you show me?'. — Al-Ṭabarī transmits this through al-Suddī; the variant concerns the question immediately before this verse. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 37:55/q02 What action does فَٱطَّلَعَ describe?  [فَٱطَّلَعَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verbal form denotes the speaker's looking, someone showing him, or coming/arriving in the proposed passive reading.
- 37:55/q02/p1 The speaker looked out toward the people of Hell and into the Fire. — Al-Ṭabarī says he looked into Hell; al-Bayḍāwī says he looked toward them; al-Ālūsī identifies them as the people of Hell. Al-Fārisī reports Abū al-Ḥasan that iftaʿala is commoner than aṭlaʿa, though both are Arabic. Al-Zajjāj, cited by al-Qurṭubī, and al-Ālūsī treat ṭalaʿa, aṭlaʿa, and iṭṭalaʿa as equivalent in this intransitive sense. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 37:55/q02/p2 In the causative construction, the interlocutors show the companion to the speaker so that he can look. — Al-Fārisī illustrates ṭalaʿa versus aṭlaʿahu with 'Zayd looked/ascended and someone made him do so.' Al-Ālūsī says aṭlaʿa can be transitive and reads Abū ʿAmr's muṭṭaliʿūn as 'those who will show': their agreeing to look enables his view. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; ALUSI-FULL]
- 37:55/q02/p3 Ṭalaʿa or aṭlaʿa may mean 'came' or 'arrived' in the proposed passive construction. — The author of al-Lawāmiḥ, reported by al-Ālūsī, suggests an omitted verbal noun or prepositional phrase for the passive reading; Abū Ḥayyān objects that a passive subject cannot be omitted. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب اللوامح)]

## 37:55/q03 Is فَٱطَّلَعَ a present response or a past event?  [فَٱطَّلَعَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb answers the preceding question in the subjunctive or narrates the next event in the past.
- 37:55/q03/p1 It can be a subjunctive present, 'so I may look,' in response to the preceding question. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this parse, and al-Qurṭubī repeats both of his alternatives. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)]
- 37:55/q03/p2 It can be a past verb, 'so he looked.' — Al-Naḥḥās equates aṭlaʿa and iṭṭalaʿa in this parse; al-Qurṭubī transmits it. The passive past is separately transmitted among the readings. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البرهسم وعمار ابن أبي عمار,أبو عمرو وابن عباس وابن محيصن وعمار بن أبي عمار وأبو سراج)]

## 37:55/q04 What is understood between the question and his looking?  [فَٱطَّلَعَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which intervening speech and actions the condensed narrative leaves unexpressed.
- 37:55/q04/p1 The interlocutors answered yes before he looked. — Al-Ṭabarī says their reply is omitted because context supplies it. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 37:55/q04/p2 Their agreement and looking precede the speaker's own look, a sequence compressed into the fa-clause. — Al-Ālūsī calls the question a courteous, emphatic request and says the fa-clause explains omitted wording: they look and then he looks. Ibn ʿĀshūr understands the prior question's missing object from this verse as looking at the people of Hell; he says speaker and companions looked, although only the speaker's view is narrated. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 37:55/q05 Why does the verse narrate one man's looking?  [فَٱطَّلَعَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the singular serves the saved man's personal speech or marks his prior identification of the companion.
- 37:55/q05/p1 The singular keeps attention on the saved man's rebuke and self-congratulation, letting his words instruct the reader. — Al-Biqāʿī says a plural would wrongly suggest everyone spoke. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 37:55/q05/p2 His view alone is stated because he looked first and identified the companion for the others. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says speaker and companions all looked in the compressed scene. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 37:55/q06 How could the believer look into Hell?  [فَٱطَّلَعَ · reports]
- 37:55/q06/p1 Paradise has openings or windows through which its inhabitants can look into the Fire. — Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by al-Baghawī, says this believer used one. Kaʿb al-Aḥbār, transmitted by Qatāda and reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, says the view of an enemy increases gratitude. Al-Rummānī from Abū ʿAlī, reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, says seeing punishment brings comfort and pleasure as admonition and retribution. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(كعب الأحبار); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الرماني عن أبي علي)]

## 37:55/q07 Who is the person he saw?  [فَرَءَاهُ · referent]
- 37:55/q07/p1 He saw his former companion in Hell, a worldly companion who did not believe. — Muqātil calls him the believer's brother and says he was chained to his satanic companion, who almost led him astray. Al-Bayḍāwī identifies the seen man as the speaker's companion; Ibn al-Jawzī calls him unbelieving. The Study Quran calls him the worldly companion. [14 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(خليد العصري); TAB-FULL(قتادة); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 37:55/q08 What was the companion's condition, and how was he recognized?  [فَرَءَاهُ · reports]
turns on: The reports supply different details of his appearance and surroundings rather than explicitly disputing each other.
- 37:55/q08/p1 Fire had changed his appearance so much that God had to identify him to the believer. — Khulayid al-ʿAṣrī, reported by ʿAbd al-Razzāq and al-Ṭabarī, says skulls were boiling or visible there. Muṭarrif ibn ʿAbd Allāh likewise reports the change, as do Ibn ʿAṭiyya's paired report, Ibn al-Jawzī's report attributed with a variant spelling to Khālid/Khulayid, and Qatāda's report of unnamed scholars in al-Qurṭubī. Muqātil adds a black face, blue eyes, and chains to a satanic companion. [7 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(خليد العصري); TAB-FULL(خليد العصري,مطرف بن عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مطرف بن عبد الله وخليد العصري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(خليد العصري); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 37:55/q08/p2 The companion stood in Hell's center with thorns surrounding him. — Ibn Masʿūd, as reported by al-Naḥḥās, supplies the thorns; al-Qurṭubī repeats the detail and cites him. [2 notes: NAHHAS(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 37:55/q09 How does فَرَءَاهُ connect grammatically?  [فَرَءَاهُ · grammar]
- 37:55/q09/p1 It is coordinated with فَٱطَّلَعَ. — Al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī gives this syntactic connection. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 37:55/q10 What does سَوَآءِ mean here?  [سَوَآءِ · meaning]
- 37:55/q10/p1 It means the middle or center of Hell. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, Ibn Masʿūd, al-Farrāʾ, al-Rāghib and many authors give this gloss. Al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explain that the center is equally distant from the sides; al-Biqāʿī likens it to a circle's center. Abū ʿUbayda, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar's 'my middle/back gave out'; al-Suyūṭī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cite Arabic poetry, including Balʿāʾ ibn Qays's middle of the head. Ibn Qutayba contrasts the long, open-initial form here with short siwā meaning 'other than', witnessed in Dhu al-Rumma's usage; al-Suyūṭī distinguishes 'equal' in 2:6 and 'complete' in 41:10, rejects al-Burhān's proposed Qur'anic 'other' sense, and finds al-Kalbī's exceptive interpretation of 18:18 preferable to that example but remote because siwā is not used unannexed. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ derives the sense from equal endpoints and its figurative uses in 3:64 and 2:6; she notes al-Aṣmaʿī, Ibn al-Sikkīt and Ibn al-Anbārī discuss the word among antonyms. Al-Rāghib, as she reports, notes adjectival and adverbial uses. [37 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ذو الرمة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(عبد الله بن مسعود); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قول غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(عيسى بن عمر); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,الأصمعي، وابن السكيت، وابن الأنباري,الراغب,الفراء)]

## 37:55/q11 What does ٱلْجَحِيمِ denote?  [ٱلْجَحِيمِ · meaning]
- 37:55/q11/p1 It is a mass of piled-up fire embers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this lexical description. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 37:55/q12 What lesson does this encounter convey?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The reports draw different lessons from the same encounter without stating a dispute.
- 37:55/q12/p1 The saved believer owes his escape from the companion's fate to God's grace and guidance. — Al-Sulamī reports that seeing one's likenesses in Hell shows that deeds alone did not save him, only his Lord's grace. The Study Quran says he realizes he would have shared his worldly companion's fate had he followed him; it cites 30:16 and 34:38. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN]
- 37:55/q12/p2 The dialogue contrasts belief and responsible life rewarded in Paradise with denial and lack of ultimate responsibility ending in Hell. — Kur'an Yolu calls the dialogue representative of Paradise's people and relates it to 37:41 and 37:44. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:55/q13 What inward distinction does the act of seeing suggest?  [* · inward]
- 37:55/q13/p1 There are two kinds of seeing: the elect's, marked by modesty and abiding life, and the lowly person's, marked by extinction and ruin. — Al-Sulamī reports this saying from al-Qāsim. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]
