# 91:8: 19 questions

## 91:8/q01 What does alhamaha mean?  [فَأَلْهَمَهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether ilhām gives moral knowledge and choice, implants dispositions, or grants effective guidance and abandonment; sources cite its root, 90:10, 91:9–10, and decree reports.
- 91:8/q01/p1 God taught, showed, or made the soul know and distinguish its two moral paths. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās explain it as making known; Abū Muslim equates ilhām and iltihām and gives informing, suggesting, and teaching as related senses. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ālūsī compare 90:10. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its specialized sense is knowledge arising without instruction, experience, or thought, including instinct and rational acquisition; the root verb is obsolete and pre-Islamic poetic evidence is absent. [85 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سفيان,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة وسفيان والضحاك); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس,الكلبي عن أبي صالح عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الكلبي عن أبي صالح عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الفراء,قتادة,مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء,قتادة,مجاهد); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الزمخشري,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ITQAN; ALUSI(ابن زيد وقتادة,مجاهد وابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 91:8/q01/p2 God placed or bound wickedness and piety in the soul itself. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr uses 'bound/imposed'; Ibn Zayd says 'placed within'; al-Wāḥidī argues that inspiration differs from teaching because it casts something into the heart and fits the root sense of swallowing. Ibn al-Jawzī defines ilhām as placement in the soul. Al-Qurṭubī calls the explanations close in meaning. - 91:8/q01/p3 God grants success in piety to one soul and abandons another to wickedness. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī prefer this; al-Wāḥidī says the root sense of insertion favors it. Al-Baghawī says this means God creates piety in believers and evil in unbelievers; al-Rāzī adduces causal regress and involuntary thoughts that lead to desire and action. - 91:8/q01/p4 God endowed each soul with both moral capacities and the ability to acquire or choose either path. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Zamakhsharī stress human acquisition; 91:9–10 names humans as agents of purification and corruption. Biqāʿī says revelation and messengers clarify the paths and establish proof while destiny remains hidden. Quṭb says revelation and outside influences awaken latent dispositions. Corpus Coranicum says the wording leaves determination versus choice open. - 91:8/q01/p5 God's inspiration supplies innate practical cognition, from instinct to judgments about one's own acts. — Ibn ʿĀshūr includes an infant's search for nourishment, avoidance of harm, and first rational acquisitions; without such capacities law and accountability are unintelligible. Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies concrete practical images and judgments beyond general action categories; he compares innate religion in 30:30. Elmalılı limits unaided reason about long-term outcomes. [15 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:8/q01/p6 Inspiration is directed only to good; sin is shown for avoidance, not commanded. — Bursevī attributes this to some elders, who reason that God never commands evil. Elmalılı likewise separates showing evil as harmful from commanding evil and cites the prohibition of sin and command to piety. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار); ELMALILI]

## 91:8/q02 How does fa-alhamaha connect grammatically to the preceding oath?  [فَأَلْهَمَهَا · grammar]
turns on: The antecedent of mā, whether it is a relative naming the Maker, a verbal noun naming the making, or a figurative cosmic order, controls the subject and coordination of alhamahā.
- 91:8/q02/p1 It is coordinated with the soul's fashioning, with fa marking inspiration as its consequence or sequence. — Abū Muslim links the fa to 'the soul and what fashioned it.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls inspiration the soul's completion; Bursevī allows causation or sequence after its faculties. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its subject can refer to the act of formation; the masculine verb treats taswiyah as grammatically nonfeminine or follows relative mā. Al-Ālūsī says the sequence may be customary, and development of faculties makes the interval unobjectionable. [18 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR+; ALUSI(الزمخشري,الطيبي,قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 91:8/q02/p2 It is an independent verbal clause following the relative mā, whose subject is the divine Maker. — Al-Zamakhsharī objects that verbal-noun mā leaves alhamahā coordinated to a noun without a subject; Elmalılı prefers the relative construction as syntactically clearer and naming the Maker. Al-Ālūsī answers that context supplies the subject even with verbal-noun mā, and verb–noun coordination is possible. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الإمام,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 91:8/q02/p3 The preceding mā can be a verbal noun, 'fashioning'; an implicit divine subject still licenses 'then inspired it.' — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, al-Mubarrad and Qatāda are cited for verbal-noun mā across the three oaths. ʿAbd al-Jabbār favors it to avoid an oath by created things preceding an oath by God. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī defend recovery of the subject from context; al-Ṭībī objects that the cadence requires parallel independent clauses. The immediate-sequence objection is answered by defining fashioning as adjusting faculties. [21 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(الطيبي,الفراء والزجاج والمبرد وقتادة,القاضي عبد الجبار,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي عبد الجبار)+; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 91:8/q02/p4 Mā can refer figuratively to the wise cosmic order behind creation, to which inspiration is ascribed metaphorically. — Al-Ālūsī reports and dismisses this as far-fetched; the proposal encompasses the sky, earth, and soul. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-]

## 91:8/q03 What does the pronoun ha in alhamaha refer to?  [فَأَلْهَمَهَا · referent]
- 91:8/q03/p1 It refers to the soul in the preceding verse. — Al-Suyūṭī notes that hā here is a feminine pronoun; Ibn ʿĀshūr discusses the preceding soul's developing faculties. [5 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:8/q04 Whose soul is meant?  [فَأَلْهَمَهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the following purification and corruption apply to all morally responsible souls.
- 91:8/q04/p1 Human souls generally, with moral accountability extending to jinn. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects restriction to Adam because the following purification and corruption language is general; he notes Qurʾānic jinn accountability. Study Quran says every soul has primordial moral knowledge. [3 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 91:8/q04/p2 Adam's soul specifically. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this narrower reading only while arguing against it. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 91:8/q05 What are fujur and taqwa?  [فُجُورَهَا تَقْوَىٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the words denote acts and reverence, their final outcomes, underlying motives, or a specialized inward application; a philological proposal contests the source of fujūr's moral sense.
- 91:8/q05/p1 They are wickedness or evil and goodness or reverent obedience. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses good and evil; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others gloss disobedience and obedience, inward and outward. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says fujūr tears religion's veil through prohibition, while taqwā shields from feared transgression. Biqāʿī describes desire breaking law versus fear restraining it. Elmalılı includes adultery, lying, indecency, and sin; taqwā is protective obedience. Corpus Coranicum derives fujūr from fajara and compares 75:5, 83:7, 82:14, 80:42, with the concrete 'breaking' sense at 82:3 and 17:90. [34 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الضحاك بن مزاحم); TAB(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سفيان); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI(ابن عباس برواية الوالبي,العوفي عن ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); QURTUBI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الرواية); CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 91:8/q05/p2 They mean misery and happiness, the outcomes toward which a soul inclines. — Mujāhid supplies this gloss; another report describes God showing the soul its misery. [4 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 91:8/q05/p3 They are fear and hope that motivate wrongdoing or reverence. — Al-Māwardī offers this further possibility, locating the terms in the motives that impel conduct. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 91:8/q05/p4 Fujur may be exertion in livelihood; taqwa, a heart at rest in trust for provision. — Al-Qāsim is reported by al-Sulamī to apply the pair to earning and trust in God's promised provision. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]
- 91:8/q05/p5 Fujur's moral sense may reflect Syriac-Aramaic pagrānā, 'physical, carnal.' — Ahrens and Jeffery propose an influence from early Christian anthropology where the cognate was sinful; Corpus Coranicum also notes a possible native 'breaking' derivation. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Ahrens und Jeffery)]

## 91:8/q06 Why is fujur named before taqwa?  [فُجُورَهَا تَقْوَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources explain the order through moral pedagogy, rhyme, or the audience's circumstances.
- 91:8/q06/p1 Recognizing and removing evil precedes cultivating good. — Al-Ālūsī says vice must be recognized for avoidance and cleansing precedes adornment; Elmalılı frames it as preventing harm before obtaining benefit and as warning against sin. [5 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BURSEVI]
- 91:8/q06/p2 The order serves the rhyme or cadence. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī transmit this explanation. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 91:8/q06/p3 The Meccan audience's deeds were mostly transgression, while pious Muslims were few. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the sequence to the historical audience. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:8/q07 Why are both traits attached to the soul by possessive suffixes?  [فُجُورَهَا تَقْوَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the suffixes carry a point about innate disposition and accountability or primarily support the cadence.
- 91:8/q07/p1 The suffixes signal the soul's preparedness and responsibility for either path. — Al-Ālūsī reports the prior disposition explanation. Biqāʿī says God creates and decrees capacity and choice, yet acts are attributed to the soul because proof is established while destiny remains hidden. [5 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 91:8/q07/p2 The suffixes serve the verse's rhyme. — Al-Ālūsī transmits this alternative. [2 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]

## 91:8/q08 What other rhetorical choices shape the paired wording?  [فُجُورَهَا تَقْوَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes discuss either the ellipsis between the pair or the grammatical force of the paired nouns.
- 91:8/q08/p1 The pairing uses ellipsis to make each named quality evoke its opposite. — Biqāʿī identifies iḥtibāk: vice implies omitted stillness or restraint, while piety implies the omitted absence of fear. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 91:8/q08/p2 The nouns name the moral states or sinful acts as objects of inspiration. — Al-Qurṭubī calls both verbal nouns functioning as direct objects; Elmalılı argues taqwā is a result noun, so fujūr names sinful acts rather than merely committing them, with prohibition and command implicit. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 91:8/q12 How does this verse relate to the surrounding oath and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The links emphasize either the oath's progression to moral accountability or parallels for the two paths.
- 91:8/q12/p1 It completes the movement from created cosmos to the morally responsible soul and the contrasting outcomes in 91:9–10. — Al-Rāzī says the prior oaths established God's rule over celestial and terrestrial bodies before voluntary acts; Biqāʿī says the oath supports success through guidance and loss through desire. Corpus Coranicum sees paired cosmic phenomena narrowing to humanity, a break after verse 4, and the Creator's enigmatic mā resolved at 91:14; it compares the shift to 81:1–14. Al-Ālūsī links the prior sūra's people of right and left; Ibn ʿĀshūr prepares the oath's answer with purification and loss. [22 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:8/q12/p2 It elaborates the two paths shown in 90:10 and innate guidance elsewhere. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī, Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, Quṭb and Study Quran compare al-Balad 90:10; Biqāʿī also cites 76:3 and balances 37:96. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites innate religion at 30:30, and Study Quran cites the primordial covenant at 7:172 and self-witness at 75:14–15, 81:14, 82:4–5. [15 notes: FARRA; RAZI; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 91:8/q13 How does the verse's moral insight guide conduct?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paired categories encompass practical discernment generally or exclude morally permissible acts.
- 91:8/q13/p1 The soul should recognize harmful vice, avoid it, cultivate piety, and answer for its choices. — Biqāʿī contrasts humans knowingly pursuing harm with animals avoiding visible harm. Elmalılı says immediate pleasure can obscure long-term and others' interests; reason, revelation, experience, conscience, and consultation help, while repeated evil can seal the heart (2:27). He cites reports to consult the heart and leave doubt. Iṣlāḥī says awareness itself witnesses accountability and preserving moral balance, gratitude, and alertness lead to success. [18 notes: BIQAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:8/q13/p2 Inspiration concerns only sin and piety because permissible acts already belong to the soul's nature. — Bursevī reports this as an explanation of why the verse names only the two moral categories. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 91:8/q14 What inward readings are given to the soul's inspiration?  [* · inward]
turns on: The reports use distinct symbolic schemes for the moral and cosmic imagery.
- 91:8/q14/p1 Angelic prompting and intellect disclose piety and sin. — Al-Qāshānī names angelic prompting and material intellect's recognition of piety's good and sin's evil. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 91:8/q14/p2 The soul's two qualities can be read as livelihood effort and trusting repose. — Al-Qāsim contrasts exertion in provision with the heart's stillness in trust of God's promise. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]
- 91:8/q14/p3 The surrounding oath images map onto divine reality, possible being, intellect, body, and spiritual longing. — Al-Ālūsī reports an esoteric mapping from sun as divine essence and brightness as Muhammadan reality through night as veiling existence, heaven as intellect, earth as body, and camel/drink as longing/remembrance or reflection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل)]

## 91:8/q15 What is said about the oath's literary form?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources explain the form through genre history or the rhetorical progression of the oath.
- 91:8/q15/p1 The cumulative oath adapts a likely pre-Islamic soothsayer expression to creation and moral antithesis. — Corpus Coranicum calls the genre link probable but under-researched, compares cumulative oaths at 93:1–2 and 100:1–5, and says verses 5–7 reuse the form for biblical creation ideas before the contrast in 91:9–10. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:8/q15/p2 The oath moves from sensible cosmic signs toward God's majesty and human accountability. — Al-Imām answers ʿAbd al-Jabbār: the sun has four signs of grandeur before God is named with three attributes; al-Rāzī says divine oaths impress gratitude and obedience. Iṣlāḥī says the oath's suppressed complement can contain all its evident implications, including destruction of rebels. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:8/q16 How is the soul taught what conduct to pursue or avoid?  [فَأَلْهَمَهَا · interpretation]
- 91:8/q16/p1 God clarifies the difference between evil and piety and makes known which actions to perform or avoid. — Qatāda says the two are made distinct; al-Kalbī says the soul learns what it should and should not do. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); THALABI(الكلبي)]

## 91:8/q17 Does inspiration follow each soul's destined end?  [فَأَلْهَمَهَا · theology]
- 91:8/q17/p1 Those destined for felicity receive piety, and those destined for misery receive wickedness. — Al-Qāsim states the individual assignment in terms of the two final destinies. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]

## 91:8/q18 How is responding to an urging expressed in Arabic?  [فَأَلْهَمَهَا · grammar]
- 91:8/q18/p1 The form inbaʿatha expresses response to baʿatha, an urging to act. — Bursevī illustrates the pattern with a person being urged toward a matter and responding, mentioning the two paths in 90:10. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 91:8/q19 How is the following purification verse used to explain this inspiration?  [* · links]
- 91:8/q19/p1 Purification in 91:9 can be God's act of rectifying the soul and enabling it to obey. — Al-Rāzī attributes this explanation of the next verse to Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, ʿAṭāʾ, ʿIkrima, Muqātil, and al-Kalbī. [1 notes: RAZI(سعيد بن جبير وعطاء وعكرمة ومقاتل والكلبي)]
