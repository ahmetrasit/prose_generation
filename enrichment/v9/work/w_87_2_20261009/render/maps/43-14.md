# 43:14: 9 questions

## 43:14/q01 What return to the Lord does the declaration describe?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the return is the final resurrection, an act of prayer, safe homecoming, or an inward return; the Study Quran explicitly offers prayer and the Hereafter as alternatives.
- 43:14/q01/p1 People return to God after death, for resurrection and the Hereafter. — Al-Zajjaj and al-Tabataba'i call it an affirmation of resurrection; al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Nasafi and Ibn Ashur specify resurrection, while Ibn Ashur adds reckoning and recompense. Ibn Kathir says death precedes the greater journey; al-Biqa'i says it ends worldly return. Elmalılı relates all changes and journeys to God's final destination and cites 2:156. The Study Quran offers this reading alongside prayer and cites 7:125. [17 notes: STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; MUQATIL+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; THALABI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 43:14/q01/p2 People turn to their Lord in prayer. — The Study Quran gives this as an alternative to return in the Hereafter, citing 7:125. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 43:14/q01/p3 The declaration also alludes to a traveler's safe return home and to family. — Ibn Ashur argues that the God who restores the dead to life can bring a traveler home safely. The Study Quran says a successful return from dangerous travel depends wholly on God. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:14/q01/p4 Inwardly, the subdued self returns to God after spiritual struggle. — Bursevi reports that mastery of the commanding self is a greater divine gift than transport: struggle renders it obedient, calling for gratitude. He says the self returns after this subduing and cautions against restricting God's gifts to food, drink and transport. [2 notes: BURSEVI(قال بعضهم); BURSEVI+]
- 43:14/q01/p5 Inwardly, spirits return by divine attraction to their original realm of lordship. — Bursevi reports another explanation: return reverses first creation, with the soul as mount and the heart as vessel; it cites 21:104. [1 notes: BURSEVI(قال بعضهم)]

## 43:14/q02 How is this declaration joined to the preceding words?  [وَإِنَّآ · grammar]
- 43:14/q02/p1 It is coordinated with the preceding speech. — Al-Nahhas identifies the connection as coordination. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 43:14/q03 How does the wording convey certainty of return?  [وَإِنَّآ لَمُنقَلِبُونَ · rhetoric]
- 43:14/q03/p1 The declaration is emphatic and makes the return definite. — Al-Biqa'i says emphasis answers an audience that denies resurrection and keeps riders mindful of their Benefactor. The Study Quran says the active participle munqalibūn presents an inescapable return toward God at every moment, willingly or unwillingly. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 43:14/q04 Why is return to God recalled while riding?  [وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously connect riding with sudden death, the analogy between journeys, the bier, continuing divine favor, and safe homecoming; al-Baydawi and al-Biqa'i explicitly mark alternatives.
- 43:14/q04/p1 Travel's danger can bring sudden death, so riders should remember their certain meeting with God. — Al-Zamakhshari names falls, runaway animals and shipwreck; al-Razi links ships and animals to possible death; al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi add drowning, stumbling and fatal accidents, urging readiness through reform, remembrance and speech. The Study Quran adds that setting out unprepared is dangerous and a safe return depends on God. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BURSEVI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 43:14/q04/p2 An earthly journey and its provisions point toward the greater journey to God. — Al-Baydawi says riding entails movement and the greatest journey is return to God. Ibn Kathir compares provisions for travel with piety and clothing with piety, citing 2:197 and 7:26; the Study Quran also cites 2:197. Al-Biqa'i describes life through time as travel on a ship or mount, often forgotten. Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalılı draw conduct from this analogy; Elmalılı says every change of state can prompt it. [9 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 43:14/q04/p3 The rider recalls the funeral bier, the final worldly conveyance. — Al-Zamakhshari reports this as another interpretation; al-Tabrisi, al-Nasafi and Bursevi likewise identify the bier as life's last mount. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI+; TABRISI+]
- 43:14/q04/p4 The phrase recalls God's gifts before departure and after return as well as during travel. — Al-Biqa'i offers this alternative because riders were told to remember the blessing while traveling; repose before and after travel is also within God's continuing favor. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 43:14/q04/p5 It also entrusts the traveler's safe return home to the God who raises the dead. — Ibn Ashur draws the allusion from God's power to restore life; the Study Quran stresses dependence on God for a successful return from travel. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 43:14/q05 What should remembering this return change in a rider's conduct?  [وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: These are complementary duties drawn from the same reminder: preparation for death, lawful and reflective travel, and avoidance of heedless pleasure.
- 43:14/q05/p1 Prepare for death and meeting God through remembrance, reflection and reform. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi and Bursevi connect falls or shipwreck to urgent reform; Bursevi says each breath may be the last. Ibn Atiyya urges repeated recollection of resurrection. Abu Hayyan calls for readiness in heart and speech. Al-Nasafi adds awareness that no one escapes God's decree. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 43:14/q05/p2 Use a journey for reflection and lawful purposes. — Al-Nasafi contrasts reflection with pleasure seeking. Bursevi names pilgrimage, maintaining kinship and seeking knowledge; Alusi and Elmalılı likewise require a licit aim and link that aim to the greater return. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 43:14/q05/p3 Avoid pleasure journeys marked by intoxication, music and heedlessness. — Al-Zamakhshari says such riders remember and obey Satan instead of preparing for death; he relates a ruler who drank through a month-long journey and became sober only at its destination, unaware of the trip. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 43:14/q06 What should a rider say on mounting, and is saying it aloud obligatory?  [وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ · law]
- 43:14/q06/p1 Remember the formula on mounting, especially for travel; saying it aloud is not obligatory, and add travel prayers. — Al-Qurtubi distinguishes remembrance from a requirement to utter it aloud and recommends a prayer for God's company, protection from hardship and a bad return. He explains that its phrase al-jawr ba'd al-kawr means affairs scattering after they were ordered. Qatada applies the verse to ships and livestock and cites the blessed-landing prayer of 23:29; al-Tabari's Qatada also cites 11:41 for ships. Al-Nasafi reports the ship prayer from 11:41. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 43:14/q07 What mounting and travel practices are transmitted?  [وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ · reports]
turns on: The reports vary by transmitter and sequence; Abu Majliz's rider is named Hasan in one transmission and Husayn in another, and the repeated formulas and their counts differ.
- 43:14/q07/p1 Ali mounted with God's name, praised and magnified Him, recited the verses, sought forgiveness, and said the Prophet did likewise. — Ibn Hibban, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqa'i, al-Durr and al-Alusi transmit versions through Ali ibn Rabi'a and Ali. Ali says the Prophet marvelled at or delighted in a servant who knows only God forgives sins. Some versions specify praise or magnification three times and laughter. Al-Qurtubi names Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi and Ibn Khuwayzimindad as transmitters; Ibn Kathir notes variant routes and al-Tirmidhi's hasan sahih grading; al-Biqa'i also cites hasan sahih, while al-Alusi calls al-Tirmidhi's grading authentic. [9 notes: IBNHIBBAN(علي بن ربيعة الأسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن ربيعة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي); ALUSI-FULL(علي)]
- 43:14/q07/p2 A report describes the Prophet saying the basmala at the stirrup, praising God after mounting, reciting through this verse, then saying three takbirs and three tahlils. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi and Elmalılı give this sequence, with slight variation in the description of the initial praise. Al-Tha'labi says no grading. Al-Qurtubi cites a concise version attributed to Ali and adds a supplication taught for disembarking from ship or animal; al-Nasafi adds the Qur'anic ship prayer of 11:41. [5 notes: THALABI(علي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); NASAFI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Resûl-i Ekrem)]
- 43:14/q07/p3 Ibn Umar reports the Prophet's travel formula followed by prayers for piety, good deeds, an easy journey and care for family. — Ibn Kathir names Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and al-Tirmidhi, with routes from Abu al-Zubayr. Al-Biqa'i adds Ahmad to the cited collections. Al-Tirmidhi grades his route hasan gharib. Al-Alusi gives a shorter version with praise, glorification and magnification three times before reciting the verses, citing Muslim, al-Tirmidhi, Abu Dawud and al-Darimi without grading. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر); TIRMIDHI(النبي)]
- 43:14/q07/p4 Qatada distinguishes the words for boarding ships and camels and cites a blessed-landing prayer. — Al-Tabari reports basmala on ships, the glorification formula on camels, and the prayer of 23:29 when disembarking, also citing 11:41. Al-Tha'labi says the verse teaches recitation on ships or livestock and cites 23:29. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة)]
- 43:14/q07/p5 Tawus thanked God for the ride as His favor and grace, then recited the glorification verses. — Al-Tabari and al-Durr transmit the same practice, varying between the wording favor and grace and gift and bounty. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(طاوس); DURR-FULL(طاوس)]
- 43:14/q07/p6 Ibn Abbas reports the Prophet's repeated takbir, praise and glorification, a tahlil, and laughter while mounted. — Al-Biqa'i specifies three of each of takbir, praise and glorification, one tahlil, leaning back and God's smiling response to a rider doing likewise. Al-Durr gives three takbirs, a declaration of unity after settling, and laughter; the abbreviated details vary. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 43:14/q07/p7 Abu Majliz says a rider identified as al-Hasan ibn Ali first praised God for Islam, Muhammad and the best community, then recited the verses. — Al-Tabari says Sufyan identifies the rider as al-Hasan ibn Ali; this name differs from al-Durr's Husayn. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو مجلز)]
- 43:14/q07/p8 Abu Majliz says al-Husayn ibn Ali taught the rider that same praise before reciting the verses. — Al-Durr names Husayn rather than the Hasan named in al-Tabari's version; both name Islam, Muhammad and the community as objects of praise. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو مجلز)]
- 43:14/q07/p9 Alqama said the basmala before mounting, praised God once seated, and recited the riding supplication when the animal rose. — Mujahid transmits this from Ibrahim about Alqama ibn Qays. [1 notes: MUJAHID(إبراهيم)]

## 43:14/q08 What is reported about neglecting the riding remembrance?  [وَإِنَّآ إِلَىٰ رَبِّنَا لَمُنقَلِبُونَ · reports]
turns on: The notes give two different illustrations of heedlessness, a prophetic warning about omission and an anecdote about intoxication.
- 43:14/q08/p1 Satan urges an omitting rider to sing or, failing that, to indulge fantasies and waste the ride. — Ibn Atiyya reports this warning from the Prophet. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي)]
- 43:14/q08/p2 A ruler reportedly drank for a month-long journey and arrived unaware of the trip. — Al-Zamakhshari offers the story as an example of heedless riding. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 43:14/q09 How does this declaration relate to the surah's larger argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes provide either a specific polemical link through resurrection or a general claim of whole-surah coherence.
- 43:14/q09/p1 Its affirmation of return answers the audience's denial of resurrection within the surah's challenge to Makkan disbelief. — Al-Biqa'i says the emphatic wording addresses resurrection deniers. The Study Quran describes the surah as answering Makkan idolaters' objections and forms of disbelief. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:14/q09/p2 It belongs to a surah whose every part serves one coherent central theme. — Islahi says the whole of Surat al-Zukhruf forms a harmonious unit from beginning to end, without specifying a closer link for this verse. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
