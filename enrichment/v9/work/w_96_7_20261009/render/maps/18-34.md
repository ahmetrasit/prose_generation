# 18:34: 20 questions

## 18:34/q01 How is ثمر read here and at 18:42?  [ثَمَرٌۭ · readings]
turns on: Vowel reports for thāʾ and mīm, their transmission by named readers, and whether the two occurrences match.
- 18:34/q01/p1 Both thāʾ and mīm have ḍamma: thumur. — Most Hijazi and Iraqi readers per Ṭabarī; lists include Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, Kisāʾī; Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid are also named. Ṭabarī prefers it by reader consensus. Some sources note Yaʿqūb or Ruways varies between occurrences. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Ḥajjāj threatened this reading and al-Aʿmash defied him; Qurtubī reports an inverse account of the threat. Ibn Khālawayh presents the rounded reading as the general reading in the Cave passage, while naming ʿĀṣim and Abū ʿAmr as exceptions. - 18:34/q01/p3 Thāʾ has ḍamma and mīm is still: thumr. — Generally attributed to Abū ʿAmr, also al-Aʿmash and Abū Rajāʾ; some transmissions of Abū ʿAmr instead match Nāfiʿ. - 18:34/q01/p5 The two occurrences can differ in vocalization. — One Yaʿqūb report reads the first with fatḥa and the second with ḍamma; Abū Ḥayyān gives Ruways's transmission with ḍamma here and fatḥa at bi-thamarihi. Lists of readers vary. [3 notes: JISHUMI(يعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رويس عن يعقوب); NASHR(أبو جعفر وعاصم وروح)]

## 18:34/q02 What does ثمر denote?  [ثَمَرٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the context of the gardens controls the word, or Arabic usage and its readings extend it to property, principal assets, or separate produce.
- 18:34/q02/p1 Abundant wealth or property of many kinds. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda include gold, silver, livestock, fruit, and other assets; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes money, gardens and fields and derives the metaphor from assets fruiting, citing al-Nābigha. Abū ʿAmr cites a poem attributed to al-Ḥārith ibn Kalda for wealth and children. Bursevī cites Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥasan's broad definition of māl. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this via al-Ṣiḥāḥ and al-Qāmūs; Ālūsī rejects mere produce as ill fitting. Some take wealth additional to the gardens, supporting expenditure on cultivation. Ālūsī cites Ubayy's explanatory codex phrase, 'We gave him abundant wealth.' [52 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(بعض أهل اللغة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء عن مجاهد,قتادة); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو,الآخرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); TABRISI(أبو عمرو,قتادة وابن عباس,قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الفراء); RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء,ابن عباس وقتادة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما,بشير بن عبيد,قتادة رضي الله عنه); BURSEVI(محمد بن الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(أبي,ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); QIRAAT-ER; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 18:34/q02/p2 Gold and silver in particular. — Mujāhid repeatedly specifies precious metal/cash; some place it alongside the two gardens. A few sources call this a metaphor for wealth that increases; Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī says garden fruit fits better in context. [21 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي الفارسي,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(مجاهد رضي الله عنه); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN]
- 18:34/q02/p3 Fruit or produce of the two gardens. — Ṭabarī prefers it from 18:32 and reader consensus; Zajjāj, Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAlī point to the preceding gardens or expenditure. Al-Layth defines tree borne fruit. Al-Kāshifī includes grapes, dates and other fruits; Iṣlāḥī reads harvest time as a moment of intoxication by plenty. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says mention of fruit implies future yields from roots. The word is a collective/plural with several derivations from thamrah and thimār, including the light form. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ubayy's codex as saying abundant fruit or wealth. Some readings distinguish stock from fruit. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); FARISI-HUJJA(بعض أهل اللغة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(أبو يزيد المدني)]

- 18:34/q02/p6 Fruit owned independently of the garden land or trees. — Ibn ʿAbbās says he owned fruit without the trees or land, adding to the total wealth; Ṭabrisī reports it as an alternative to palms bearing fruit. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 18:34/q02/p7 The open and rounded forms can share the same sense. — Māwardī reports semantic equivalence and then gold/silver, varied wealth and productive principal as glosses; Ibn al-Anbārī allows thumar to signify either fruit or amassed wealth. Abū Rajāʾ's variant is said by Ālūsī to retain the meaning. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش)]

Bashīr's thamar includes children and slaves while thumur is fruit, reversing the common assignment. ## 18:34/q03 What Meccan reading transmission background is recorded?  [ثَمَرٌۭ · readings]
turns on: The transmitters' relationships and how widely Meccans adopted their readings.
- 18:34/q03/p1 Ibn Kathīr's Meccan reading followed Mujāhid, who received it through Ibn ʿAbbās from Ubayy. — Ibn Mujāhid says Ibn Kathīr never differed from Mujāhid; Ḥumayd ibn Qays also read with Mujāhid without differing, and Meccans remained united on Ibn Kathīr. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حميد بن قيس)]
- 18:34/q03/p2 Ibn Muḥayṣin had Arabic expertise and independent choice but lacked Ibn Kathīr's Meccan consensus. — Ibn Mujāhid says he studied with Darbās, Ibn ʿAbbās's freedman, as did Ibn Kathīr; he also studied with Mujāhid, who praised his Arabic ability. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID(مجاهد)]

## 18:34/q04 Why is ownership singular after the mention of two men?  [وَكَانَ لَهُۥ · grammar]
- 18:34/q04/p1 Only one of the two men possessed this fruit or wealth. — Al-Akhfash says mention of both in the parable's setup suffices for the later singular; al-Naḥḥās reports his explanation. [2 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(الأخفش)]

## 18:34/q05 Who is the companion addressed by the garden owner?  [لِصَٰحِبِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether ṣāḥib and a claim of more nafar exclude kinship, or whether a brother can be called a companion and nafar denotes servants.
- 18:34/q05/p1 The believer is his brother or fellow companion. — Muqātil and several exegetes say they were brothers; Damghānī notes ṣāḥib can denote brother. Māwardī says the believer spent for the afterlife while the unbeliever spent for worldly standing. Ālūsī says companion does not preclude brotherhood; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls him the paired man of the parable. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:34/q05/p2 The wording suggests they were companions, not brothers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this inference from 'more men,' but counters that nafar can be servants. Abū Ḥayyān infers non-brotherhood from the companion wording and notes that sharing a clan would weaken the inference. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:34/q06 What does يحاوره mean?  [يُحَاوِرُهُۥ · meaning]
- 18:34/q06/p1 They exchange and return speech in reciprocal conversation or debate. — Several derive muḥāwara from ḥāra, to return; Rāzī, Samīn and others compare 84:14–15, and Samīn quotes Imruʾ al-Qays. Jishumī and Ṭabrisī say speech without reply is not dialogue. The term itself does not settle the dispute's subject. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 18:34/q07 Whose state does وهو يحاوره describe?  [وَهُوَ يُحَاوِرُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the circumstantial pronoun relates to the speaking subject or to his companion, the object.
- 18:34/q07/p1 It describes the wealthy speaker while he argues with his companion. — Abū Ḥayyān takes his apparent disputation as denial of resurrection and associationism. Samīn says the circumstantial clause clarifies actual dialogue because speech alone need not be reciprocal. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:34/q07/p2 It may describe the Muslim companion as he admonishes the owner. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this alternative: the believer urges faith in God and resurrection. Samīn says the clause grammatically permits attachment to subject or object. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 18:34/q08 What were the men discussing?  [وَهُوَ يُحَاوِرُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The text reports reciprocal speech without quoting the preceding exchange; sources reconstruct its topic from the parable and its continuation.
- 18:34/q08/p1 They disputed faith and unbelief, including resurrection and gratitude. — Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī list this as a topic. Abū Ḥayyān names resurrection and associationism; Ibn ʿĀshūr infers the poorer man had already urged faith and righteous conduct from the response at 18:37. Iṣlāḥī says he urged gratitude and warned of wrath; the owner answered arrogantly. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL]
- 18:34/q08/p2 They disputed worldly pursuit against pursuit of the afterlife. — Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī report this alternative; narratives say the believer invested his inheritance in the hereafter and was mocked. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; MUQATIL; JISHUMI]

## 18:34/q09 What is the effect of the form أنا in the boast?  [أَنَا۠ · readings]
turns on: Whether the final alif is delivered briefly or prolonged.
- 18:34/q09/p1 Short ana gives the self-reference a clipped delivery. — The Qirāʾāt note describes the short form without madd. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:34/q09/p2 Prolonging the final alif mirrors the speaker's self-aggrandizement. — The Qirāʾāt note describes madd as an iconic extension of self-reference. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 18:34/q10 What does the claim of more wealth mean?  [أَكْثَرُ مِنكَ مَالًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: How literally to take the comparative against the narratives of the believer's poverty and equal inheritance.
- 18:34/q10/p1 The garden owner claims greater property than his companion. — The contrast follows his abundant gardens and other assets; Ṭabarī calls the companion poorer. Muqātil adds his fine clothes and mount against the believer's worn state. Al-Kashshāf says extra gold and silver enabled him to develop land, and Biqāʿī says it enabled labor and tools. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; MUQATIL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:34/q10/p2 The comparison appears literal and implies the companion had some wealth and followers. — Abū Ḥayyān uses the comparative text against historical accounts of a destitute petitioner. Bursevī and Ālūsī point to the tension with reports that the brothers inherited equal shares and the believer gave his away; Bursevī leaves it unresolved. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 18:34/q11 How are مالا and نفرا construed?  [مَالًۭا نَفَرًۭا · grammar]
- 18:34/q11/p1 Both are accusative specifications of the comparative degree. — Zajjāj and Ālūsī label both accusative specifications; Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls nafaran specification of the comparison. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:34/q12 What does أعز express in the boast?  [وَأَعَزُّ · meaning]
- 18:34/q12/p1 Greater strength, honor and social standing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links strength and honor to numerous courageous kin. Others summarize it as a stronger retinue, clan or greater status. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 18:34/q13 What is omitted after أعز, and how does the comparative work?  [أَكْثَرُ مِنكَ وَأَعَزُّ · grammar]
- 18:34/q13/p1 The second comparative inherits the standard منك from the first. — Burhān says omitted comparison standards are often inferred when afʿal is a predicate; it compares 87:17. The same source outlines construct, definite and unmarked-plus-min patterns and conditions for definite, plural and collective nouns after afʿal, citing 63:8, 26:111, 18:103, 6:123 and 11:27. [5 notes: BURHAN]

## 18:34/q14 Who are the owner's نفر?  [نَفَرًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether social power is counted by dependents, male offspring, or the wider kin group; later references to offspring favor the first two for some authors.
- 18:34/q14/p1 His retainers, servants and attendants. — Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās identify servants; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this reading permits the two men to be brothers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers servants and children by the later comparison at 18:39. Some combine attendants with children. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 18:34/q14/p2 His children, especially sons. — Muqātil adduces the later mention of wealth and offspring (18:39); Ibn ʿĀshūr cites verse 40. Al-Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī explain that sons accompany their father unlike daughters. Some pair sons with servants and helpers. [18 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 18:34/q14/p3 His clan, kinsmen and fighting supporters. — Abū Muslim and Abū Sulaymān take clan; Rāzī says they defend and accompany him. Ṭabrisī says the group accompanies him on errands; Ibn Qutayba's examples present many brothers as protection and support for the back. Some sources combine clan and general retinue. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان); RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(آخرون); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر)]
- 18:34/q14/p4 A small group of men, conventionally three to ten. — Ibn Fāris gives three to ten, comparing qawm and rahṭ as collectives without a singular of the same form. Qurtubī says fewer than ten, and Bursevī specifies male helpers and an upper limit of ten. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن فارس); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 18:34/q15 What background is reported for the two men and their garden?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the parable is a specific historical incident, a differently located tale, or a general figure; names, sums and settings vary across reports.
- 18:34/q15/p1 They were brothers who inherited wealth; the believer spent for God and the unbeliever built worldly prosperity. — Muqātil has the impoverished believer seek generosity and describe lending his inheritance to God for himself and his children; the unbeliever mocks him. Māwardī contrasts afterlife spending with status seeking. Qurtubī recounts charity, mosque building, the believer seeking work and being expelled after resurrection was denied; another account details heavenly land, house, wife and servants. Jishumī connects the story with 37:50. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL]
- 18:34/q15/p2 They were Meccan Makhzūmī brothers, Abū Salama and al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Asad. — Al-Kalbī says each inherited four thousand dinars and the believer spent his share for God; Qurtubī cites 37:51. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 18:34/q15/p3 They were Israelite brothers, the believer Judah or Tamlīkha and unbeliever Qarṭūsh. — Qurtubī attributes Judah to Ibn ʿAbbās and Tamlīkha/Qarṭūsh to Muqātil; the names vary in the reports. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل)]
- 18:34/q15/p4 They were partners with eight thousand dinars. — ʿAṭāʾ supplies the partnership and sum. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 18:34/q15/p5 The gardens were at Lake Tinnīs, which flooded overnight. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports that one brother sold his share, spent in obedience and was mocked before the lake flooded. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 18:34/q15/p6 The wealthy owner is Fatrus or Qutrus. — Thaʿlabī calls him Fatrus and depicts his tour, boast, doubt in the Hour and hope of a better return; Nasafī calls him Qutrus. These are name variants in the accounts. [2 notes: THALABI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 18:34/q15/p7 A fishing test and an angelic vision expose the difference between worldly luck and final fate. — Qurtubī says the unbeliever caught many fish invoking an idol while the believer caught none invoking God; an angel protested, and Gabriel showed each man's abode, so both judged earthly fortune irrelevant. God took the believer and ruined the unbeliever, who was seen in Hell as in 37:51–52. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:34/q15/p8 The parable may be a general example for this community rather than an account of particular men. — Māwardī reports it as an exhortation to renounce worldly life and desire the hereafter; Qurtubī says the context argues against this. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]
- 18:34/q15/p9 The pair can stand for the Prophet and Meccans, believers and disbelievers, or ʿUyayna's party and Salmān's. — Qurtubī reports these further identifications alongside the link with 37:51; they are separate generalized applications grouped here as variants rather than an agreed historical identification. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 18:34/q16 How does this verse fit the surrounding parable and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources compare the boast with adjacent admonition, a later eschatological dialogue, and the parable's destruction.
- 18:34/q16/p1 The boast answers the earlier call to remain with believers and exposes the arrogance of rich opponents. — Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya compare ʿUyayna and al-Aqraʿ asking the Prophet to dismiss Salmān, Khabbāb and Ṣuhayb as inferior; Qurtubī links the parable to patience with believers at 18:28. Abū Ḥayyān likens the pride to Quraysh leaders; Iṣlāḥī sees Quraysh's outward and inward arrogance. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:34/q16/p2 The worldly exchange here has an afterlife counterpart in 37:51–61. — Qurtubī says the latter describes the brothers' afterlife situation, with the believer recalling his companion in Hell; Jishumī also connects the inherited-wealth account with 37:50. [5 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 18:34/q16/p3 The garden's collapse later demonstrates the error of trusting wealth and followers. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the companion refutes the claim verbally and ruin demonstrates it. Iṣlāḥī contrasts the owner's assumption of indestructibility with Dhū al-Qarnayn's gratitude and trust in God's promise. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:34/q17 What does the owner's boast teach about wealth and status?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The outward moral of worldly pretension and an inward symbolic reading of its images.
- 18:34/q17/p1 Worldly wealth and strong followers can feed arrogance and are no proof of lasting divine favor. — Qatāda calls them the wicked person's ambition; Qushayrī says the unbeliever failed to thank his Provider and regretted ruin. Iṣlāḥī says opponents treat prosperity as proof of belief and expect a better hereafter. Study Quran compares 18:46, 9:55, 9:85, 23:55–56, 3:10, 3:116, 9:69, 26:88 and 34:35, arguing wealth and children cannot shield punishment. Ṭabāṭabāʾī has the later ruin refute self-sufficiency. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:34/q17/p2 In an inward reading, the gardens and fruit represent worldly desire and cravings. — Al-Nīsābūrī maps wealth and status to blameworthy traits, imagined better return to delusion about God, and turning hands in regret to wasted life and good potential. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(النيسابوري)]

## 18:34/q18 What details about the gardens before the boast are supplied?  [* · links]
turns on: The immediately preceding garden description and whether its word for 'both' is singular in form or genuinely dual.
- 18:34/q18/p1 The two gardens bore full produce, with palms, crops and flowing water. — Qurtubī describes palms encircling them, grapes and crops, and a river through the middle; ḥifāf means an encircling edge. Jishumī says the gardens contained other trees too. Ṭabrisī describes the later calamity erasing their green growth. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI]
- 18:34/q18/p2 The preceding كلتا is grammatically singular in form while denoting two gardens. — Qurtubī cites singular ātat and Jarīr's singular predicate and prefers the Basran analysis. The Basrans reject al-Farrāʾ's dual derivation because oblique case with an overt noun would require yāʾ; poetic shortening is necessity. Sībawayh treats alif as feminine and tāʾ as replacing wāw; al-Jurmī reverses what is root and added. Qurtubī compares naḥnu as singular form for multiple referents. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أهل البصرة,سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 18:34/q18/p3 The preceding كلتا is a dual derived from كل. — Al-Farrāʾ says lām is lightened and dual alif added; it is always annexed and has no standalone singular. Basrans and Qurtubī dispute the analysis from case and agreement. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل البصرة)-]

## 18:34/q19 What ancillary examples of wording and authority appear in the collected excerpts?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These excerpts concern separate cited examples and are not direct interpretations of the verse.
- 18:34/q19/p1 Sentence conjunction depends on the strength of the connection. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī says adjective/noun or emphasis form such a unity that conjunction would coordinate a thing with itself; distinct sentences sharing syntactic role are conjoined; wholly unrelated sentences are not. Omitting conjunction may indicate maximal connection or complete separation. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 18:34/q19/p2 Fronting an object challenges the target's fitness for the action. — Al-Jurjānī's example a-Zaydan taḍrib questions whether anyone would dare strike Zayd. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 18:34/q19/p3 Several lexical and proverbial explanations address support, relief and hidden danger. — Ibn Qutayba glosses taṣfarr as lacking affection, makfūfa as folded and supported by Shammākh, and prophetic nafas as relief through the Anṣār. He transmits ʿUmar's wind from God's rūḥ, Abū Bakr's 'handful' image for numbers small before God, and competing small-cave versus water-source senses of al-Ghuwayr, one attributed to Bihays. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبو بكر,بعضهم,عمر)]
- 18:34/q19/p4 Public consultation is necessary for a ruler's pledge. — Ibn Qutayba reports ʿUmar saying a private pledge establishes neither man as ruler and could leave both liable to be killed; authority requires the gathering's assent. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(عمر)]
- 18:34/q19/p5 The Qurʾān itself proves the Prophet's mission by its miraculous composition. — Al-Bāqillānī says God made the Prophet's heart repository of revelation but could withhold it, and the Qurʾān establishes its divine source. Other scriptures need another sign because their composition is not miraculous, though their unseen reports may be; Moses knew speech directly, reciters know by inference. [5 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 18:34/q19/p6 ʿUthmān defended his rule against suspicious faultfinders. — Al-Bāqillānī reports ʿUthmān describing public flatterers who concealed hostility and followed first callers, arguing that they accepted ʿUmar despite greater complaints because he restrained them, and asserting the imam's claim to act. [3 notes: BAQILLANI(عثمان بن عفان)]

## 18:34/q20 What other historical reports are present among the collected excerpts?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate historical excerpts included in the source notes rather than positions on the garden parable.
- 18:34/q20/p1 Moses, Aaron and the Night Journey are described in distinct reports. — Al-Itqān reports Moses as tall, dark and curly-haired and al-Thaʿlabī gives his lifespan as 120. Aaron's kinship is disputed between full, maternal or paternal brother; he is described as taller and eloquent, born a year earlier and dying first. A Night Journey report places him in the fifth heaven with half-white beard; Ibn Miskawayh glosses his Hebrew name as 'beloved.' [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن مسكويه,الثعلبي,الكرماني)]
- 18:34/q20/p2 David's lineage, appearance and reign are reported. — Al-Itqān lists his ancestry through Jesse, Judah and Jacob; al-Tirmidhī/Kaʿb describe his devotion, appearance, voice and prophethood with kingship; al-Nawawī reports 100 years of life, 40 of rule and twelve sons. [3 notes: ITQAN(النووي,كعب)]
- 18:34/q20/p3 Solomon's qualities and reign are reported. — Kaʿb describes his appearance, devotion, humility and early consultation by his father. Ibn ʿAbbās names him and Dhū al-Qarnayn among believing world kings, against Nimrod and Nebuchadnezzar. Historians give accession at thirteen, Temple building four years later and death at fifty-three. [3 notes: ITQAN(أهل التاريخ,ابن عباس,كعب)]
- 18:34/q20/p4 Job's lineage is uncertain, with different reports. — Ibn Isḥāq says only his father Abyad is secure; Ibn Jarīr traces him through Esau to Isaac; Ibn ʿAsākir makes his mother Lot's daughter and his father Abraham's believer, placing him before Moses. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن إسحاق,ابن جرير,ابن عساكر)]
- 18:34/q20/p5 Accounts of early conversions and teaching in Yathrib are excerpted. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports ʿUmar hearing Ṭā Hā after learning his sister and her husband converted, then accepting Islam. After al-ʿAqaba the Prophet sent Muṣʿab to Yathrib to teach with Asʿad, and Usayd arrived armed to confront them at Saʿd's request. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
