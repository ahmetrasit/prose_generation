# 10:24: 35 questions

## 10:24/q01 Does innama restrict this comparison?  [إِنَّمَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether other parables preclude restriction.
- 10:24/q01/p1 It restricts the likeness to worldly life's swift ending. — Biqa'i and Ibn Ashur say the form overturns an expectation of permanence. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 10:24/q01/p2 It is not restrictive here. — Abu Hayyan cites the Quran's other parables of worldly life. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 10:24/q02 What is the likeness in the parable?  [مَثَلُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا كَمَآءٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether water, plants, people or the full scene bears the comparison.
- 10:24/q02/p1 The whole rain-to-ruin sequence pictures worldly life. — Jurjani counts ten inseparable clauses; Baydawi, Nasafi, Tabatabai, Ibn Ashur, Itqan and Alusi call it composite. Ibn Ashur maps rain to childhood, growth to early life and ornament to peak benefit. [25 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 10:24/q02/p2 Worldly life resembles rain and its short-lived benefit. — Tabrisi and Abu Hayyan report this surface reading; Qurtubi parses the comparison as nominative. [5 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 10:24/q02/p3 Worldly life resembles the vegetation produced by rain. — Al-Jubba'i and Abu Muslim give this; Abu Hayyan allows an omitted plant noun. [4 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي، أبو مسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 10:24/q02/p4 Worldly life resembles the people whose lives are refreshed by rain. — Abu Hayyan says the owners' belief in control supports this; Tabrisī reports a life specified by these properties. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI]
- 10:24/q02/p5 The text requires an understood mithl before water. — Akhfash supplies mithl; Jurjani argues that calling worldly life water directly is senseless here, unlike simple identity-like similes. He cites 12:82 and prophetic comparisons. [8 notes: AKHFASH; JURJANI-ASRAR(النبي)]
- 10:24/q02/p6 Mathal may mean a familiar comparison or descriptive attribute. — Jishumi distinguishes mithl, counterpart; Alusi defines a parable as transferring a striking case. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 10:24/q03 What is the water sent from heaven?  [كَمَآءٍ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ مِنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Cloud, direction or reported heavenly origin.
- 10:24/q03/p1 Rain from clouds or the sky's direction. — Abu Hayyan gives both; Qurtubi treats anzalnahu as water's adjective; Samin attaches min al-sama to that verb, rejecting a circumstantial parse. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 10:24/q03/p2 Measured drops sent from the seventh heaven. — Abu Hurayra says each is measured; Ikrima likens a drop resting on cloud to a camel. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو هريرة,عكرمة)]

## 10:24/q04 How do water and plants mix?  [فَٱخْتَلَطَ بِهِۦ نَبَاتُ ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: Subject of mixing and force of ba.
- 10:24/q04/p1 Vegetation intertwines because of rain. — Ibn Atiyya and Zamakhshari take ba as causal; Alusi prefers it with Ibn Abbas's support. Ibn Ashur says fa signals quick growth; Islahi describes dense growth. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 10:24/q04/p2 Vegetation mingles with and absorbs rain. — Ata notes many colors; Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan describe interpenetration; Abu Hayyan makes ba accompaniment; Kuranyolu renders the absorption. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; JISHUMI]
- 10:24/q04/p3 Water mixes into earth and a new clause starts with plants. — Nafi and others pause after ikhtalata; Ibn Atiyya allows bihi to mean water or implied mixing. Abu Hayyan and al-Shaykh object to an obscure broken syntax. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة من القراء); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 10:24/q04/p4 The relation is adjacency rather than interpenetration. — Al-Kirmani proposes it; Abu Hayyan says actual interpenetration is possible. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكرماني)]
- 10:24/q04/p5 The verb may suggest varied, composed or extended growth. — Abu Hayyan rejects 'varied' as unsupported. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 10:24/q05 What does mimma ya'kulu modify?  [مِمَّا يَأْكُلُ · grammar]
turns on: Attachment of min.
- 10:24/q05/p1 It is circumstantial from the plants, with an omitted copula. — Abu al-Baqa's parse is judged clearer by Abu Hayyan and Samin; Alusi follows it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)+]
- 10:24/q05/p2 It attaches to ikhtalata. — Al-Hufi proposes this. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)]
- 10:24/q05/p3 Min marks a kind of vegetation. — Samin offers this. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 10:24/q06 Which plants are eaten by people and livestock?  [مِمَّا يَأْكُلُ ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمُ · referent]
- 10:24/q06/p1 People eat grains, legumes, fruits and vegetables; grazing animals eat grass and pasture. — Ibn Abbas lists kinds; Qurtubi adds straw and barley; Suddi identifies grazing animals. Ibn Ashur allegorizes lofty and low ambitions via 47:12. ## 10:24/q07 How does hatta idha function?  [حَتَّىٰٓ إِذَآ · grammar]
- 10:24/q07/p1 It marks the end of continuing growth; ata-ha amruna answers idha. — Ibn Atiyya extends the scene through owners' confidence; Abu Hayyan and Samin supply ongoing growth or mixing; Ibn Ashur says hatta compresses stages. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 10:24/q08 What is the earth's zukhruf?  [زُخْرُفَهَا · meaning]
turns on: Natural growth versus technological application.
- 10:24/q08/p1 Its complete beauty, greenery, flowers, gold-like ornament and colorful growth. — Ibn Qutayba traces gold to ornament; Mujahid cites 17:93; Zajjaj calls it full beauty; Wahidi refers to 6:112; Damghani, Tabrisī and Ibn Ashur explain the range. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 10:24/q08/p2 Illusory artificial adornment through technology. — Asad contrasts hostile mastery of nature with cooperation. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 10:24/q09 What image is conveyed by the earth's adornment?  [أَخَذَتِ ٱلْأَرْضُ زُخْرُفَهَا وَٱزَّيَّنَتْ · rhetoric]
- 10:24/q09/p1 The earth grows colorful plants as if a bride took fine clothes and jewelry. — Qatada says sprouting; Ibn Abbas names grains and fruit while others name plants or flowers; Ibn Atiyya cites 7:31, Ibn Ashur Bashshar's poem. Abu Hayyan sees repetition or purposeful personification; Alusi calls it bride metaphor. [8 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مروان بن الحكم وأبو جعفر والسبعة وشيبة ومجاهد والجمهور); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي وعبد الله وزيد بن علي والأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(أبي بن كعب وعبد الله وزيد بن علي والأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(عبدالله)]

- 10:24/q10/p5 An unchanged ifta'ala form is also possible. — Baydawi offers this. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 10:24/q11 What do the owners think they can do?  [وَظَنَّ أَهْلُهَآ أَنَّهُمْ قَٰدِرُونَ عَلَيْهَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Harvest or broad human mastery.
- 10:24/q11/p1 Harvest, gather and enjoy the crop that appears secure. — Ibn Abbas mentions cutting; Tabrisī says storing it; Abu Hayyan says healthy growth encourages confidence; Ibn Ashur calls control a metaphor. ## 10:24/q12 Does zanna mean supposition or certainty?  [وَظَنَّ · meaning]
turns on: The force of zann.
- 10:24/q12/p1 Ordinary judgment of a likely possibility. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan retain this; Abu Hayyan rejects certainty. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 10:24/q12/p2 Certainty of obtaining harvest. — Qurtubi gives this gloss. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 10:24/q13 What does the pronoun in alayha refer to?  [عَلَيْهَآ · referent]
turns on: Antecedent and ellipsis.
- 10:24/q13/p1 The earth, with its crop understood. — Wahidi distinguishes grammatical earth and intended plants; Abu Hayyan and Alusi prefer earth with ellipsis and reject yield and adornment as weak. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 10:24/q13/p2 The plants or crop. — Samin says produce can avoid ellipsis. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 10:24/q13/p3 The adornment. — Tha'labi, Baghawi, Qurtubi and Alusi list it. [4 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 10:24/q14 What is the arriving divine command?  [أَتَىٰهَآ أَمْرُنَا · referent]
turns on: Decree or physical event.
- 10:24/q14/p1 God's decree or order to destroy, also called punishment. — Ibn Abbas calls it punishment; Ibn Ashur says decree and creation; Islahi applies it to punishment. ## 10:24/q15 Why use ata for the command?  [أَتَىٰهَآ · rhetoric]
- 10:24/q15/p1 Ata suits an unseen arrival, while ja'a suits a visible one. — Burhan draws this distinction. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 10:24/q18 What does hasidan mean?  [حَصِيدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Literal crop loss versus rhetorical vehicle.
- 10:24/q18/p1 Cut down, uprooted, dried or gone, as if harvested. — Abu Ubayda says uprooted from root, Ibn Abbas nothing remains, Mawrdi gone or dry, Ibn Atiyya includes blight. Rummani and Ibn Abi al-Isba note its vivid metaphor for ruin. [19 notes: MAJAZ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RUMMANI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع); STUDYQURAN(R); BIQAI-FULL]
- 10:24/q18/p2 The ruined earth is an explicit simile or direct metaphor. — Alusi favors explicit simile and allows metaphor; he finds Sakkaki strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(السكّاكي)-]
- 10:24/q18/p3 Harvest elaborates an implied metaphor for the adorned earth. — Sakkaki holds this; Alusi rejects it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السكّاكي); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 10:24/q19 Why is hasidan singular and masculine?  [حَصِيدًۭا · grammar]
- 10:24/q19/p1 Its active form has passive force and can describe collective plants without feminine marking. — Majaz compares hasaid; Qurtubi and Samin explain fa'il with maful sense. [6 notes: MAJAZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 10:24/q20 What does lam taghn mean?  [لَّمْ تَغْنَ · meaning]
turns on: Dwelling, flourishing or mere continuing, and land versus crop.
- 10:24/q20/p1 As though the land had not been inhabited. — Ibn Qutayba and Zajjaj derive it from dwelling and maghani; Tabari cites al-Nabigha, Mawrdi Labid; Bint al-Shati cites Muhalhil and Labid, links dwelling to sufficiency and notes ruined peoples at 7:78 and 7:92. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الليث); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأثير,الراغب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 10:24/q20/p2 As though it had never flourished or prospered. — Qatada repeats this; Suddi makes the former flourish the adornment; Ibn Atiyya glosses thriving. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 10:24/q20/p3 As though the land or crop had not stood or existed. — Ali b Isa says not stood; al-Layth says vanished, Ibn Abbas not existed; Wahidi includes both land and produce; Asad calls total disappearance an idiom. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الليث); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 10:24/q20/p4 Its omitted subject is specifically the crop. — Baydawi and Nasafi supply it; Alusi prefers earth pronouns with understood crop; Abu Hayyan chooses crop for masculine reading. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 10:24/q24 What are the detailed signs?  [كَذَٰلِكَ نُفَصِّلُ ٱلْءَايَٰتِ · referent]

## 10:24/q30 What lessons are drawn from the parable?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Individual loss, communal punishment, wealth use or spiritual growth.
- 10:24/q30/p1 Worldly wealth and pleasure disappear at their peak, so attachment and confidence deceive. — Rummani says perishable things are insignificant; Qatada says the world abandons its lover; Wahidi names death and disaster. [58 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); RUMMANI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 10:24/q30/p2 God's punishment can seize proud communities at their strongest. — Ibn Ashur compares 6:44; Islahi says resources accelerate a morally bankrupt nation's fall. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 10:24/q30/p3 Use only needed wealth and give zakat. — Nasafi compares excessive water with hoarded wealth, likens zakat to a bridge maintained by prayer and quotes a Prophetic bridge saying; Itqan echoes the measured-water analogy. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ITQAN]
- 10:24/q30/p4 People's spiritual soil can yield virtue or corruption. — Nasafi contrasts piety, generosity and love with shirk, greed and harm. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 10:24/q32 How do rhetorical works use this verse and related examples?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Simile, metaphor, ellipsis and related figures.
- 10:24/q32/p1 It is an extended sensory image for worldly transience. — Baqillani cites it among similes; Jurjani insists on linked clauses; Burhan and Itqan classify sensory and rational comparison. The Imam bars sensory-to-rational comparison, others allow it via a stars-and-Sunnas poem; Ibn Abi al-Isba analyzes harvested crop as an intellectual shared feature. [18 notes: BAQILLANI; JURJANI-ASRAR; BURHAN(الإمام,غيره); ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع,الإمام); SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 10:24/q32/p2 Concision, omission and metaphor are separate rhetorical arts. — Baqillani lists ten arts and cites 12:82, 47:21, 13:31, 2:179, 63:4, 10:23 and 35:43. [4 notes: BAQILLANI(بعض أهل الأدب والكلام)]
- 10:24/q32/p3 The Pleiades-as-sash image is defensible. — Critics object or substitute Jauza; defenders describe rising, setting or mid-sky crossing. Baqillani compares several poets and rejects exclusive superiority. [5 notes: BAQILLANI(أبو عمرو,بعضهم,قوم); BAQILLANI+]
- 10:24/q32/p4 Deletion removes an evidenced element, whereas an essential subject remains implicit. — Burhan distinguishes hadhf and idmar; Ibn Maymun and Ibn Jinni resist calling subjects deleted; al-Farisi cites the mixing construction; Ibn al-Da'i's divine application is rejected. [6 notes: BURHAN(ابن الضائع,ابن جنى,ابن ميمون,الفارسي)]
- 10:24/q32/p5 Related discussion concerns particles, word choice and parallel verses. — Burhan discusses ordinary aw as doubt, conditional and oath lam, ata versus ja'a, amal versus fi'l and river phrases in other suras. Tabrisī distinguishes command from supplication and a general summons. [8 notes: BURHAN; TABRISI]

## 10:24/q33 What positions on Quranic inimitability are reported?  [* · theology]
turns on: Intrinsic composition or external diversion.
- 10:24/q33/p1 The Quran's distinctive composition is intrinsically beyond matching. — Burhan cites 17:88 against diversion; Qadi Abu Bakr says diversion relocates the miracle. Bint al-Shati separates universal recognition from the elite challenge, cites Quraysh and 10:38, 11:13, 17:88. Samarrai invokes Moses' speech and even al-Kawthar. [18 notes: BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الباقلاني)+; BURHAN(النظام,فريق منهم)-]
- 10:24/q33/p2 God externally diverted the Arabs from matching it. — Al-Nazzam holds this; Burhan says the challenge would become empty and the miracle temporary. [1 notes: BURHAN(النظام); BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر)-]
- 10:24/q33/p3 Knowing its arrangement would make matching possible. — One group says so; Bint al-Shati rejects it. [1 notes: BURHAN(فريق منهم); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الباقلاني وفريق من المتكلمين)-]

- 10:24/q33/p5 Ibn al-Muqaffa allegedly challenged the Quran. — Burhan replies that his writing was only maxims. [1 notes: BURHAN(قوم)]

## 10:24/q34 What is the status of divine speech?  [* · theology]
turns on: Eternal attribute, temporal expression, both or neither.
- 10:24/q34/p1 It is created letters and sounds in another locus. — The Mu'tazila deny a real speech attribute; al-Jubba'i and Abu Hashim use a locus, Abu al-Hudhayl distinguishes 'Be', and al-Najjar distinguishes recitation from writing. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو الهذيل بن العلاف,الإمامية والخوارج والحشوية,الجبائي,الجبائي وأبو هاشم,الحسن بن محمد النجار,المعتزلة)]
- 10:24/q34/p2 Letter-and-sound speech is eternal in God. — Hashwiyya differ on human resemblance; Alusi warns of false attributions. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحشوية)]
- 10:24/q34/p3 An eternal capacity and temporal utterances both subsist in God. — Karramiyya distinguish capacity and utterance; another camp calls speech a temporal divine attribute. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكرامية)]
- 10:24/q34/p4 It is one simple meaning in God without letters. — Alusi reports this camp. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 10:24/q34/p5 It originated, but labels or its nature remain disputed. — Waqifiyya differ over eternal and created; others suspend judgment or say it is neither substance nor accident. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الواقفية,بعض القائلين بالحدوث)]
- 10:24/q34/p6 God cannot be described as speaking. — Alusi reports this minority. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المعترفين بالصانع)]
- 10:24/q34/p7 Eternal attribute and temporal ordered letters are both true in different circumstances. — Alusi accepts both apparently conflicting syllogisms contextually. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
