# 53:48: 14 questions

## 53:48/q01 How is وَأَنَّهُۥ read in this sequence?  [وَأَنَّهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the vowel makes each clause dependent on the report of earlier scriptures or an independent assertion.
- 53:48/q01/p1 As wa-anna, continuing dependent clauses. — The Study Quran notes that, except verse 46, verses 42–50 start with wa-anna and continue the report of Moses' and Abraham's scriptures in 36–41. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 53:48/q01/p2 As wa-inna, beginning independent affirmations. — The Study Quran reports this alternative, giving the sense 'truly' or 'verily' for each clause. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 53:48/q02 Why use هُوَ here?  [هُوَ · rhetoric]
- 53:48/q02/p1 It emphasizes that these acts are God's alone. — Abū Ḥayyān says people may claim power over laughter, weeping, life, death, enrichment, and provision; al-Zarkashī cites 2:258 and notes that the pronoun is absent for creating sexes (53:45), which no one claims. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN]

## 53:48/q03 What does أَغْنَىٰ mean?  [أَغْنَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether enrichment means material provision, freedom from need, contentment, increase, or God's own self-sufficiency.
- 53:48/q03/p1 God grants wealth or provision. — Mujāhid says wealth; al-Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī specify money; al-Ḍaḥḥāk specifies gold, silver, clothes, and homes. Abū Ṣāliḥ and others distinguish ordinary wealth from lasting assets under أقنى. Qatāda includes providing servants. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); THALABI(أبو الصلاح,الضحاك,مجاهد والحسن وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك والحسن وقتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة والحسن); TABRISI(أبو صالح,الحسن ومجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد ومجاهد وقتادة والحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد,مجاهد وقتادة); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,قتادة والضحاك); BURSEVI(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:48/q03/p2 God removes need or gives sufficiency. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls this kifāya, freedom from poverty. Al-Rāzī distinguishes absolute from partial freedom from need; sight, speech, nursing, food, and clothing remove particular needs. He applies the Prophet's instruction to give people enough not to ask on the feast day. Islahi distinguishes this from subsequent affluence. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:48/q03/p3 God makes some people rich while leaving others poor. — Zayd ibn Aslam and Ibn Zayd make the contrast about different people; al-Ṭabarī is reported to choose it. Ibn Kathīr finds it remote from wording. [7 notes: NAHHAS(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(ابن زيد); TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 53:48/q03/p4 God grants contentment. — Sufyān calls the first verb contentment; Biqāʿī says real wealth is a soul at peace with its allotted share, including Paradise's enrichment. Imam ʿAlī pairs livelihood and contentment. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(سفيان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سفيان); QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام)]
- 53:48/q03/p5 God is self-sufficient. — Sulaymān al-Taymī and al-Ḥaḍramī take God Himself as implicit recipient. Ibn Kathīr finds this remote from wording; al-Ālūsī finds the implied object awkward and potentially misleading. [6 notes: THALABI(سليمان التيمي); MAWARDI-FULL(سليمان التيمي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حضرمي); QURTUBI-FULL(سليمان التيمي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحضرمي بن لاحق); DURR-FULL(الحضرمي)]
- 53:48/q03/p6 God increases or abundantly provides. — Ibn Zayd contrasts increase with decrease under أقنى and cites extension and restriction of provision in 13:26 and 17:30. Ibn ʿAbbās also glosses it as increase or abundance. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:48/q03/p7 God provides livelihood. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk distinguishes livelihood under أغنى from wealth under أقنى. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك)]

## 53:48/q04 What does وَأَقْنَىٰ mean?  [وَأَقْنَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The q-n-y root, whether the hamza is privative, the implicit beneficiaries, and whether the pair describes parallel gifts or opposites.
- 53:48/q04/p1 God gives lasting possessions or capital kept beyond immediate need. — Ibn Qutayba derives it from qinya, what is acquired; al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, Abū Ṣāliḥ, Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn al-Aʿrābī, and others describe retained property or a reserve. Al-Layth distinguishes property kept from contentment; al-Wāḥidī cites poetry for retaining. Al-Jishumī cites qinya/qinyān, retaining rather than trading, attached shade, and enduring red. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites yaqna for acquiring wealth after poverty. Al-Jawharī's verb and causatives appear in Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say valuable enduring property merits special mention; the latter names gardens, livestock, and buildings. Ibn Kathīr says it need not be sold. Abū Zayd's traditional gift names distinguish goats (qinā), sheep (ghinā), and camels (minā). Bursevī cites Tāj al-maṣādir for capital. [60 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ(قيل); SULAMI(سفيان بن عيينة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو صالح,أبو عبيدة والمبرد وابن الأعرابي,ابن الأعرابي,الشاعر,الليث); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو صالح); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); TABRISI(أبو صالح); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو زيد,الجوهري,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو زيد,الجوهري,شاعر); SAMIN-DURR(الجوهري,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح وابن جرير وغيرهما); BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,قتادة والضحاك); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NAHHAS+]
- 53:48/q04/p2 God makes a person content or satisfied with what He gives. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil, and many others give this gloss; al-Wāḥidī calls it the majority explanation. Al-Layth and al-Mutalammis supply usage; Ibn ʿAbbās cites ʿAntara's aqni before Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq, as also recorded by al-Suyūṭī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ. Ibn ʿAṭiyya praises contentment as the best possession because wealth passes. Al-Rāghib treats satisfaction and obedience as acquired treasures; al-Bayḍāwī calls contentment lasting possession. Al-Wāḥidī allows the privative sense only if it means contentment with little. [46 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TUSTARI(ابن عيينة); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); SULAMI(سفيان بن عيينة); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(سفيان); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن زيد,الليث,مجاهد ومقاتل وعكرمة عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد ومقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو زيد,ابن عباس,الجوهري); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عنترة العبسي); ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الراغب); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب عليه السلام,قيل); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,الراغب,عنترة العبسي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد)]
- 53:48/q04/p3 God makes people poor. — Al-Akhfash holds the gloss, also attributed to Abū Zayd; Saʿdī al-Muftī prefers it because nearby clauses are antitheses. Al-Iṣfahānī, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī explain the hamza as privative, with al-Ālūsī comparing ashkā. Al-Ṭabarī is reported to prefer Ibn Zayd's rich/poor reading; al-Ālūsī also mentions poverty framed as contentment and patience. Al-Wāḥidī rejects bare decrease as linguistically unsupported; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says such glosses are not demanded by usage; Ibn Kathīr calls the pair remote from wording. [14 notes: THALABI(الأخفش); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأخفش); TABRISI(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد والأخفش); TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي)+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 53:48/q04/p4 God provides servants or people who serve. — Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, and Qatāda are among the reported transmitters; al-Suddī says a person becomes rich enough not to serve and can employ others. Some link servants to retained possessions. Reports vary between being made a servant and being given servants. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); THALABI(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك والحسن وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة والحسن); TABRISI(الحسن ومجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد ومجاهد وقتادة والحسن,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة)]
- 53:48/q04/p5 God makes creatures dependent on Him. — Sulaymān al-Taymī and al-Ḥaḍramī take the beneficiaries as all creatures, contrasted with God's own self-sufficiency. Ibn Kathīr finds it remote; al-Ālūsī objects to the implied objects. [6 notes: THALABI(سليمان التيمي); MAWARDI-FULL(سليمان التيمي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حضرمي); QURTUBI-FULL(سليمان التيمي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحضرمي بن لاحق); DURR-FULL(الحضرمي); IBNKATHIR-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الحضرمي)-]
- 53:48/q04/p6 God gives livestock as possessions. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk specifies camels, sheep, and cattle after gold and silver; Bursevī includes all livestock and contrasts clothes and homes under enrichment. [3 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI(الضحاك)]
- 53:48/q04/p7 God reduces provision or gives less. — Ibn Zayd says first verb means increase and second decrease, citing expansion and restriction of provision in 13:26 or 17:30. Mujāhid has a transmitted gloss of granting and withholding. Al-Wāḥidī rejects bare decrease without linguistic basis, unless contentment with little is intended. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد)-]
- 53:48/q04/p8 God gives offspring. — Ibn Kaysān's gloss is repeated by al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī; al-Qurṭubī says it returns to an earlier view. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن كيسان); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كيسان)]
- 53:48/q04/p9 God gives a female singer together with capital assets. — Abū al-Ṣalāḥ specifies a qayna and principal property alongside money. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو الصلاح)]
- 53:48/q04/p10 God gives enduring earnings that remain after one's death. — Al-Māwardī adds this as a possible extension of preserving wealth. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 53:48/q04/p11 God gives settled or stable circumstances. — Al-Qurṭubī reports an Arab usage in which aqnā gives what settles a person. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 53:48/q04/p12 God gives a richer state above basic sufficiency. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes need-removing gifts from what exceeds them; he suggests qāf's articulation above ghayn fits the higher state. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:48/q04/p13 A reported Mujāhid gloss is printed as mūn. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits the printed wording without resolving its meaning. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 53:48/q05 What forms and related uses illuminate أقنى?  [وَأَقْنَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Which attested derivative and semantic development applies to the verse.
- 53:48/q05/p1 It is causative from qinya, acquisition or retention. — Al-Jishumī discusses qinya, qinyān, qanā, iqtanā, the related qanāt with an alif from wāw and plural qanawāt, and al-maqniʾa with or without hamza. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes causative and doubled forms; al-Jawharī reports qaniya/yaqnā and transitivity governed by vowels. Al-Samīn says the alif of aqnā derives from yāʾ because of qinya and cites a line; al-Ālūsī gives aqnāhu and qannāhu. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says aqnā is unique in form and root in the Qurʾān, while a related wāw-root appears as qinwān in 6:99. Ibn al-Athīr explains iqtnāh in a hadith as taking a servant as chosen favorite. [12 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجوهري); SAMIN-DURR(الجوهري); ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأثير)]
- 53:48/q05/p2 The verb can also mean to grant contentment. — Al-Layth and Tāj al-maṣādir record this sense alongside retained wealth; al-Rāghib figures contentment as an acquired treasure. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب)]

## 53:48/q06 Why are the objects of the two verbs unstated?  [أَغْنَىٰ وَأَقْنَىٰ · grammar]
- 53:48/q06/p1 The clause focuses on attributing the actions to God. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn compare the following verbs; al-Ālūsī rejects specifying God and creatures as implicit objects because it sounds awkward and could imply something wrong. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الحضرمي)]

## 53:48/q07 Why mention lasting property after enrichment?  [أَغْنَىٰ وَأَقْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second clause singles out enduring assets or adds gifts beyond sufficiency.
- 53:48/q07/p1 It singles out a valuable form of wealth included in the general gift. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls enduring property the noblest wealth; al-Ālūsī names gardens, livestock, and buildings; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes a particular after the general as honoring it. Ibn Kathīr says lasting wealth completes the favor. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح وابن جرير وغيرهما); BURSEVI]
- 53:48/q07/p2 It names a surplus beyond what removes need. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes need-removing gifts from further wealth and suggests qāf articulated above ghayn mirrors the higher state. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:48/q08 Which nearby words have other reported readings?  [* · readings]
- 53:48/q08/p1 Some readers assimilate ʿĀd al-ūlā, omit tanwīn, and do not distinctly pronounce hamza. — Al-Jishumī names Abū Jaʿfar, Nāfiʿ in a transmission, Abū ʿAmr, and Yaʿqūb. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع في رواية وأبو عمرو ويعقوب)]
- 53:48/q08/p2 Abū ʿAmr uses opening rather than imāla on listed non-final words. — Ibn al-Jazarī says this applies on routes inclining verse endings; for Mūsā he follows the usual between-vowels reading where imāla is transmitted. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو)]

## 53:48/q09 Does exclusive divine causation allow intermediate causes?  [* · theology]
- 53:48/q09/p1 Yes; natural and non-natural intermediaries remain subject to God. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists human joy, organs of laughter, angels, and other causes as intermediaries without independent power. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 53:48/q10 What inward sense is given to enrichment and possession?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inner gift is obedience, divine nearness, or stable illumination of the heart.
- 53:48/q10/p1 God enriches through nearness to Himself and impoverishes through separation. — Al-Junayd's formulation is reported by al-Sulamī and Bursevī. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); BURSEVI(الجنيد)]
- 53:48/q10/p2 God enriches through obedience and makes poor through disobedience. — Al-Tustarī contrasts outward worldly provision with this inward sense. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 53:48/q10/p3 God grants and preserves light in a sound, steady heart. — Bursevī reports that an unstable heart can lose the divine effusion to the commanding ego's darkness. The Prophet's prayer asks God to keep his heart firm in religion; Bursevī compares cultivating the spirit with keeping a vicious, barren doglike ego. [3 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,بعضهم)]

## 53:48/q11 What ruling is drawn about keeping property?  [* · law]
- 53:48/q11/p1 Useful wealth may be acquired; keeping a dog without a useful purpose is forbidden. — Bursevī cites uses such as guarding crops or livestock. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:48/q12 What does the verse teach about prosperity and dependence?  [* · interpretation]
- 53:48/q12/p1 Joy, grief, life, death, provision, children, prosperity, and poverty depend on God. — Islahi urges turning to Him at all times; Kuran Yolu reads 53:43–49 as examples of all events under His will and power. Islahi says wealth withheld by rich people hoping for Paradise comes from God, not Sirius, which He also rules; the passage addresses those who assume Abrahamic or Mosaic descent secures Paradise. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:48/q13 How does the verse fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which part of the surrounding argument is being traced: scriptural testimony, paired divine acts, structural parallel, or moral address.
- 53:48/q13/p1 It continues truths attributed to Moses' and Abraham's scriptures. — The Study Quran says most clauses in verses 42–50 begin wa-anna and that the section turns from reflection on creation to perennial truths in those scriptures. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 53:48/q13/p2 Its placement maintains paired assertions of God's power over creation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says provision could have followed creation as in 26:78–79 and 30:40, but is delayed so the clauses pair God's power over both creations. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:48/q13/p3 It occupies the same structural place as the statement that human effort will be seen. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 53:40. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:48/q13/p4 It answers misplaced confidence in lineage and wealth. — Islahi contrasts descent from Abraham and Moses with their teachings and sacrifices; he notes the rich withhold spending while dreaming of Paradise although their wealth comes from God rather than Sirius (53:33–34). [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:48/q14 What does the nearby 'second genesis' refer to?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the second creation is future bodily return or the ennobling of human life.
- 53:48/q14/p1 Resurrection. — The Study Quran says most commentators use creation from a drop to demonstrate resurrection, citing 75:37–40 and parallels in 16:4, 18:37, 22:5, 35:11, 36:77, 40:67, 76:2, and 80:19. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 53:48/q14/p2 Breathing the Spirit into a human. — The Study Quran reports al-Rāzī: the Spirit ennobles humans and distinguishes them from other animals, citing 15:29 and 38:72. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Al-Rāzī)]
