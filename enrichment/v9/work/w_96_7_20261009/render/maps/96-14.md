# 96:14: 15 questions

## 96:14/q01 Who is the person asked whether he knows God sees?  [أَلَمْ يَعْلَم · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun and changing addresses are tied to the Abu Jahl incident, the Prophet and opponent in turn, or the general audience.
- 96:14/q01/p1 The forbidding man is Abu Jahl. — Al-Tabari and others identify the opponent who forbade Muhammad's prayer; al-Farra explicitly makes this question about Abu Jahl. Al-Alusi reports Ibn Atiyya's claim of agreement that the servant is the Messenger and the forbidder Abu Jahl. Ibn Ashur says the prior prohibition supplies the omitted referent. [26 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI(ابن عطية,الجمهور,الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:14/q01/p2 The question can address Muhammad about Abu Jahl, or address Abu Jahl himself. — Al-Mawardi gives both possible interlocutors. Al-Alusi reports a judge-like alternation between the unbeliever and Prophet. Al-Baydawi reports a shift to the unbeliever in the second address. [10 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قيل); ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:14/q01/p3 It may refer to either the praying servant, who knows God sees, or the forbider, who fails to heed that knowledge. — Elmalili says either pronoun assignment still threatens the prohibiter. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 96:14/q01/p4 The address extends to any capable listener, or to humanity. — Al-Alusi cites 96:8 for the humanity reading. Al-Razi's general reading of al-insan and Bint al-Shati's discussion extend the scene beyond its particular occasion. [4 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي)]

## 96:14/q02 How does 'know' take the following bāʾ phrase?  [يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition follows a certainty sense of yaʿlam or is an added bāʾ before its object.
- 96:14/q02/p1 Know means be certain, accounting for the bāʾ. — Ibn Ashur expressly treats yaʿlam as carrying certainty. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:14/q02/p2 The bāʾ is an added preposition on an object that the verb can take directly. — Al-Zarkashi's Burhan compares 2:195 and 19:25, and cites 22:15, 38:33, and 21:31 among parallels. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 96:14/q03 How does the final question complete the preceding arāʾayta clauses and conditions?  [أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: The object requirements of arāʾayta, whether the closing interrogative is an apodosis or a fresh sentence, and whether an interrogative apodosis needs fāʾ.
- 96:14/q03/p1 It is the expressed answer to the later conditional, with the earlier answer omitted or supplied by it. — Al-Zamakhshari allows a question to answer a conditional without fāʾ, using 6:47 and other interrogative examples; al-Radi approves. The forbidden person and conditional supply arāʾayta's objects, with the second arāʾayta emphatic. Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi likewise supply the earlier answer from the later condition. [17 notes: KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-; ALUSI(أبو حيان,الدماميني)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)-]
- 96:14/q03/p2 The final question is an independent rebuke; conditional answers are omitted and indicated by it or by arāʾayta. — Al-Alusi distinguishes omission from grammatical tanāzuʿ: the clauses are semantically demanded, not syntactically shared. Ibn Ashur supplies an implicit answer that God knows and punishes; the final interrogative then begins afresh. Elmalili prefers this resumed reading. Al-Zarkashi reconciles Sibawayh's required object with Quranic cases by positing a conditional pointing to an omitted object and a fresh question. [13 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN(السهيلي,سيبويه); ELMALILI(Keşşâf)+]
- 96:14/q03/p3 The repeated arāʾayta clauses substitute for the first; the final question is their predicate or second object. — Al-Akhfash makes the second arāʾayta appositional to the first. Al-Tabari treats three repetitions as substitutions. Al-Qurtubi reports the same predicate analysis. Al-Biqaʿi insists the second object is an interrogative. Al-Alusi's version places both conditionals in the first question's second-object slot and lets the final verse answer the second. [9 notes: AKHFASH; TAB; QURTUBI(قيل); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 96:14/q03/p4 All three challenges have omitted completions, which the one concise final threat supplies. — Ibn Atiyya treats arāʾayta as an interrogative challenge without two cognitive objects. Its sequence gains one expansive but compressed conclusion; Abu Hayyan reports this reading. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 96:14/q03/p5 The first arāʾayta is visual, while the later two are intellectual and take conditional material as second objects. — Al-Alusi reports this as one parsing, with the last condition's explicit answer and the earlier omitted one; unjoining the final clause stresses the actual event and threat. [5 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:14/q03/p6 All three arāʾaytas are intellectual; the first condition fills an object's place and the last question is independently stated. — Al-Alusi reports a first relative clause as object one, a conditional or its omitted answer as object two, and a third question with omitted first object. The unjoined close sharpens the real threat. [8 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:14/q03/p7 The interrogative suspends arāʾayta's government, leaving its second object unspoken and directing attention to the warning. — Ibn Ashur treats the intellectual verb's objects as omitted for concision, identified by the servant and prohibition. Bint al-Shati cites 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23 and 53:33 for interrogative arāʾayta omitting its second object, and argues the repeated formula needs no inferred complement or hidden conditional answer. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI+]
- 96:14/q03/p8 A conditional can occupy the second object by the combined sense of its condition and interrogative consequence. — Al-Khafaji's reply, also attributed to al-Radi and al-Damamini, allows literal or semantic occupation of the slot. Al-Alusi rejects a categorical interrogative-only rule as conflicting with Sibawayh's reported preference. [3 notes: ALUSI(الخفاجي); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي); ALUSI+; ALUSI(أبو حيان)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-; BINTSHATI-]
- 96:14/q03/p9 A conditional cannot be the required second object; an interrogative or oath clause is needed. — Abu Hayyan raises the grammatical objection; Bint al-Shati cites his account of frequent Quranic interrogative objects. [3 notes: ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI; ALUSI(الخفاجي)-; ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)-]
- 96:14/q03/p10 An interrogative apodosis lacking fāʾ is impermissible outside poetic necessity. — Abu Hayyan invokes grammarians' rule for request-like clauses; al-Damamini questions hal yahliku at 6:47; Ibn Ashur says most grammarians, including Zamakhshari's Mufassal, require fāʾ. Bint al-Shati objects to the extra arāʾayta as well. [12 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(أبو حيان,الدماميني,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI+; BINTSHATI-]
- 96:14/q03/p11 The Quran also uses conditional questions followed by interrogatives without fāʾ. — Bint al-Shati cites 6:40, 6:46, 6:47, 28:71–72, 11:88, 41:52 and 10:50, asking readers to attend to Quranic usage beyond school grammar. [2 notes: BINTSHATI+]

## 96:14/q04 Why is this knowledge framed as an interrogative rebuke?  [أَلَمْ يَعْلَم · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give complementary explanations of the question's rebuking and astonishment effects.
- 96:14/q04/p1 The question confirms what the opponents themselves admit and exposes the contradiction of defying the Prophet under God's sight. — Al-Biqaʿi says the denier's conduct is irrational despite divine monitoring and control; Ibn Ashur calls the interrogative a rebuke for failing to protect himself. [7 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:14/q04/p2 The separated final clause makes each prior reproach forceful and the actual denial especially threatening. — Al-Alusi says the unjoined close signals the real occurrence of denial after a broadened hypothetical; three distinct reproaches concern forbidding prayer, claiming piety, then denying truth. [5 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:14/q04/p3 The unusual event is put as a question to evoke astonishment. — Ibn Ashur explains that an incredible known event elicits a request for confirmation and compares 88:1; al-Alusi says future wording vividly recalls the strange past scene. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI]
- 96:14/q04/p4 The neutral 'servant' and hypothetical guidance make a fair-minded concession before judging the forbider. — Al-Alusi says it avoids initially naming a chosen prophet, invites any discerning hearer, and gives the forbidder's claimed guidance and piety a hearing. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI(الجمهور)]

## 96:14/q05 Whom or what does God see here?  [ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the omitted object is understood from the prayer incident and the forbider's conduct, both sides' states, or all that exists.
- 96:14/q05/p1 God sees the forbidding man's acts, words and intended harm. — Al-Mawardi adds that God hears his words and prevents his intended harm to the Messenger. Ibn Kathir similarly joins hearing and sight. Al-Qurtubi connects sight with knowledge of Abu Jahl's conduct. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; NAHHAS; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:14/q05/p2 God sees the praying servant while the forbider tries to stop him. — Al-Mawardi presents this as another object of God's sight; one full-text note names Abu Jahl as the person praying as well as the forbider, a variant wording within that report. [3 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; MUQATIL]
- 96:14/q05/p3 God sees each person's guidance or error, and all their states. — Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi join divine observation to requital. Al-Baydawi considers both the forbider's supposed guidance and real denial; Qutb contrasts the denying opponent and guided worshipper. [15 notes: KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 96:14/q05/p4 The unexpressed object makes God's seeing encompass every existent. — Ibn Ashur explicitly says the omission generalizes the object; al-Biqaʿi calls divine sight and knowledge unrestricted over every visible and knowable thing. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 96:14/q06 What does divine seeing mean?  [ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'seeing' is an irreducible divine perception or interpreted through knowledge; the notes also discuss its unlimited extent.
- 96:14/q06/p1 God truly sees things; sight is not reduced to knowledge. — Al-Jishumi accepts literal divine seeing and rejects a Baghdadi claim that 'sees' only means 'knows'. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]
- 96:14/q06/p2 God's seeing is His perception or knowledge of deeds and perceptible things, without bodily organs or likeness to creatures. — Ibn Atiyya explicitly denies corporeal implication. Ibn Ashur defines it as knowledge relating to perceptible things. The Baghdadi 'means knows' view is reported by al-Jishumi, who rejects it. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI-]
- 96:14/q06/p3 God's sight and knowledge are unrestricted, grounding His power to punish wrongdoers and support the obedient. — Al-Biqaʿi says He knows every knowable and sees every visible thing. Al-Tabataba'i infers this from God's creating everything, a premise polytheists themselves acknowledge. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 96:14/q07 What warning or lesson does God's seeing convey?  [أَلَمْ يَعْلَم بِأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize requital, the immediate rebuke for forfeited guidance, the denial of practical knowledge, or vigilance.
- 96:14/q07/p1 God observes the wrongdoing and will punish or fully requite it; this deters sin and encourages obedience. — Al-Tabari, al-Baghawi and others address Abu Jahl's forbidden prayer. Al-Razi includes resurrection and atom-small acts that escape no divine knowledge; al-Tabrisi says obedience and avoidance of sin follow. Ibn Ashur calls seeing a metonym for punishment. Al-Biqaʿi pairs punishment of misguiders with aid to the obedient. [35 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:14/q07/p2 The verse is an immediate rebuke and threat to a man whose denial and turning away cost him guidance. — Al-Farra contrasts the guided praying servant and the denying forbider. Al-Alusi reports Isam al-Din's account of missed high rank, acquired harm and distance from God; he distances himself from that elaboration. Al-Razi's reported hypothetical asks what a sensible, wealthy man should have chosen. [16 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI(الإمام,عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,عصام الدين); ALUSI-]
- 96:14/q07/p3 The final question says Abu Jahl does not actually know or heed that God sees. — Al-Baghawi reads the following kalla as denying the knowledge presupposed in the rebuke. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 96:14/q07/p4 Knowing that God sees should produce vigilant self-observation on the way to direct witnessing. — Al-Qushayri says a person lacking vigilance cannot advance to witnessing. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 96:14/q08 How broadly does the warning apply to forbidding worship?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether a restriction targets worship itself or a separate wrong or protected interest, and how far the specific occasion generalizes.
- 96:14/q08/p1 It applies beyond Abu Jahl to anyone who obstructs prayer or other obedience, including distraction from worship. — Al-Razi extends the threat to everyone preventing obedience; al-Alusi extends it to verbal or practical prevention of other worship. Bint al-Shati says a specific occasion does not confine general wording. Kur'an Yolu prefers a universal application to tyrants obstructing religion's goodness, truth and justice. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 96:14/q08/p2 Preventing prayer in a usurped place or disliked time is outside this warning because the objection is to an accompanying wrong. — Al-Razi and al-Alusi distinguish forbidding the transgression from forbidding prayer itself. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:14/q08/p3 A master or husband may limit voluntary worship for a protected interest, with God's permission. — Al-Razi gives a slave's night prayer or voluntary fast and a wife's retreat as examples; the aim is welfare, not hostility to worship. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 96:14/q08/p4 One should avoid explicitly prohibiting a worshipper's prayer when possible. — Al-Alusi reports Ali's fear of this threat when people prayed before Eid: he stated what he saw the Prophet do. Abu Hanifa likewise answered Abu Yusuf with the proper formula and prostration rather than an explicit ban. [4 notes: ALUSI(أبو حنيفة,علي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,علي)]

## 96:14/q09 What happened when the opponent tried to stop the Prophet praying?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted accounts differ on names, manner of prevention and the prayer scene; chronology is used to test the Abu Talib story.
- 96:14/q09/p1 Abu Jahl threatened to trample the Prophet but recoiled from a trench of fire, terror and wings. — Abu Hurayra's report includes his oath by al-Lat and al-Uzza; the Prophet said angels would seize him limb by limb. Al-Baghawi's version is uncertain whether the revelation clause belongs to Abu Hurayra's narration or elsewhere; al-Alusi says the passage through the sura was then revealed. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI(أبو هريرة)]
- 96:14/q09/p2 Abu Jahl came on the Prophet praying and forbade him; he also verbally repeated his prohibition. — Ibn Abbas ties the incident to the passage through the lying forelock. Al-Alusi says al-Tirmidhi authenticated the verbal warning, also transmitted by Ahmad and others; Qatada says the passage concerned the threatened trampling. [3 notes: SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI(ابن عباس)]
- 96:14/q09/p3 The forbider was Umayya ibn Khalaf, who stopped Salman from praying. — Al-Hasan supplies the names; al-Alusi deems it unlikely because Salman converted in Medina while the sura is Meccan, though he says the general warning would cover Umayya if true. [1 notes: KASHSHAF(الحسن); ALUSI(الحسن)-]
- 96:14/q09/p4 The prayer was Zuhr, also reported as the first congregational prayer with the Prophet, Abu Bakr and Ali. — Al-Alusi attributes these scene details to Abu Hayyan. [1 notes: ALUSI(أبو حيان)]
- 96:14/q09/p5 Abu Talib brought Ja'far to pray beside the Prophet and praised and protected him. — Al-Alusi reports Abu Hayyan's story, then questions it: the Mi'raj and obligatory prayer are dated after Abu Talib's death by Ibn Hazm, Ibn Faris and al-Suddi, at one year, fifteen months and seventeen months before Hijra respectively. A competing al-Zuhri chronology puts the Mi'raj five years after the mission, favored by Nawawi and Qurtubi, with objections noted. [1 notes: ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-]
- 96:14/q09/p6 The forbidding may have been physical prevention, revealing the tyrant's limited power. — Al-Alusi reports this explanation of the wording. [1 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]

## 96:14/q10 How does this verse close the sequence about forbidding prayer?  [* · links]
turns on: Who is the subject of the guidance hypothetical, whether its positive case is genuine or sarcastic, and how the successive challenges culminate here.
- 96:14/q10/p1 It closes three rising reproaches: forbidding prayer, claiming guidance while forbidding it, and actually denying and turning away. — Al-Alusi treats each as independently threatened; the majority instead treats the first conditional as sarcastic concession of the forbider's claimed guidance in idolatry and the second as his evident denial. Prayer's enacted example is stronger than speech, and forbidding it implies commanding its opposite. [16 notes: ALUSI(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 96:14/q10/p2 It contrasts a guided praying servant who calls to piety with the forbider who denies and turns away. — Al-Farra shifts the first condition's pronouns to the servant and the later verbs and knowing to the forbider; the omitted result is astonishment. The connective aw can divide or mean and. Abu Hayyan adds that prayer reforms the Prophet and calling to piety reforms others, both provoking Abu Jahl. [16 notes: QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); BURSEVI; ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUTB-ZILAL]
- 96:14/q10/p3 Both conditions concern the forbider: a supposedly good course is impossible, while his actual denial independently brings the threat. — Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi present his supposed idol-guidance versus rejection of truth; Bursevi says the first supposition broadens the case and the second independently triggers punishment. Al-Alusi reports the majority's sarcastic reading. [11 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الجمهور,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 96:14/q10/p4 The sequence laments a self-sufficient tyrant's missed guidance and earned punishment before this final threat. — Isam al-Din says 96:9 exposes tyranny, 96:11–12 lost benefit and 96:13 acquired harm; al-Alusi declines to adopt his elaboration. The Imam's reported alternative asks what a rational wealthy man should choose and laments higher ranks forfeited. [10 notes: ALUSI(الإمام,عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,عصام الدين); ALUSI-]
- 96:14/q10/p5 The warning completes the earlier challenge in one concise threat suited to each formulation. — Ibn Atiyya says the earlier questions have broad omitted completions and one close suffices; Abu Hayyan relays this. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 96:14/q10/p6 The sequence was placed after earlier revealed opening verses when public preaching and prayer later provoked Abu Jahl. — Bint al-Shati reports al-Razi's first solution that the rest of the sura was revealed later and directed to be placed at the start; she finds a later public-prayer setting plausible. She also reports his generic reading of humanity and leaves both possible. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي)]

## 96:14/q11 What other textual and transmission notes bear on this verse or its sura?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Different Mi'raj chronologies are reported; the other notes concern a sura virtue and an editorial cross-reference.
- 96:14/q11/p1 The Mi'raj and obligation of prayer are dated late enough to challenge the Abu Talib story. — Al-Alusi cites Ibn Hazm's one year, Ibn Faris's one year and three months, and al-Suddi's one year and five months before Hijra, all after Abu Talib's death. [1 notes: ALUSI]
- 96:14/q11/p2 Another Mi'raj chronology places it five years after the prophetic commission. — Al-Alusi reports Qadi Iyad's report from al-Zuhri, favored by Nawawi and Qurtubi, while noting objections. [1 notes: ALUSI(الزهري)]
- 96:14/q11/p3 A report promises the reciter the reward of reading all shorter suras. — Al-Baydawi Full attributes the promise to the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 96:14/q11/p4 A separate note directs readers to the treatment of the divine name Allah at 95:8. — Corpus Coranicum offers this cross-reference without an independent explanation here. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 96:14/q12 What inward meaning is drawn from God's sight?  [ٱللَّهَ يَرَىٰ · inward]
turns on: The two inward notes offer different complementary contemplative points.
- 96:14/q12/p1 Nothing is beyond God, while He is behind every beyond. — Al-Tustari states this as the verse's inward observation. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 96:14/q12/p2 Awareness that God sees calls for vigilance before direct witnessing. — Al-Qushayri makes vigilance a prerequisite to witnessing. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 96:14/q13 What does the earlier arāʾayta mean in leading to this question?  [* · grammar]
- 96:14/q13/p1 It means 'tell me': a request to see is used for a request to be informed. — Al-Alusi derives the usage from seeing's leading to knowledge and notes disagreement over whether it applies to visual and intellectual seeing alike. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 96:14/q14 What wrongdoing sets up the final rebuke?  [* · interpretation]
- 96:14/q14/p1 Abu Jahl denies God's Book, turns from belief and prevents the Prophet's prayer, an act of human tyranny. — Al-Qurtubi names denial and turning away; al-Alusi describes the passage from 96:9 as consequences of human tyranny and a threat to the prayer's obstructer. [2 notes: QURTUBI; ALUSI]

## 96:14/q15 How does calling the worshipper a servant sharpen the rebuke?  [* · rhetoric]
- 96:14/q15/p1 The indefinite 'servant' may be generic, or may magnify the Prophet and increase astonishment that he is forbidden. — Al-Alusi reports both functions of the indefinite expression. [1 notes: ALUSI]
