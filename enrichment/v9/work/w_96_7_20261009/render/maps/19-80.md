# 19:80: 12 questions

## 19:80/q01 What does God inheriting what the man says mean?  [وَنَرِثُهُۥ مَا يَقُولُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mā denotes real possessions, imagined Paradise goods, the man's entire legacy, his recorded words, his continuing speech, or the man himself; the occasion report and the prior promise to record his words are invoked in the discussion.
- 19:80/q01/p1 God takes the man's actual worldly wealth and children at death, ending his ownership of them. — Mujāhid identifies al-ʿĀṣ's wealth and children (MUJAHID, YAḤYĀ, ṬABARĪ, IBN KATHĪR, DURR); Qatāda says all he possesses (YAḤYĀ, ʿABD AL-RAZZĀQ, ṬABARĪ, IBN KATHĪR, DURR). Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and Ibn Zayd explain that destruction ends his ownership (WĀḤIDĪ, ṬABRISĪ, IBN AL-JAWZĪ); al-Zajjāj says it passes to others. Ibn Qutayba, Bayḍāwī, Bursawī and Ālūsī contrast real worldly goods with a baseless future boast; Ālūsī prefers this sense, reading mā as their substance. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls inheritance figurative dispossession, a threat of near death; his sons ʿAmr and Hishām became Muslims, bringing the Prophet good news and al-ʿĀṣ grief. Asad likewise calls it a metaphor for taking what had been vested in him. Al-Biqāʿī locates this at death; the Study Quran cites God as ultimate Inheritor (19:40, 15:23, 21:89, 28:58). Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān mention the possibility that even hoped-for worldly goods, if granted, are later removed, comparing 6:94; Ālūsī finds that imagined grant very remote in light of the occasion report. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the failed hope an inherited disappointment. [43 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وقتادة وابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI(الإرشاد,العيون); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Ṭs); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 19:80/q01/p2 God deprives him of the wealth and children he claimed for the Hereafter and assigns them to others. — Muqātil says the claimed reward goes to someone else; al-Farrāʾ chooses Paradise property for another. Al-Suddī and al-Kalbī specify gold, silver and silk for other Muslims (WĀḤIDĪ); Abū Suhayl says more deserving recipients (ABŪ ḤAYYĀN, ĀLŪSĪ). Ibn al-Jawzī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and says al-Farrāʾ selected it. Al-Kashshāf calls them the claimed goods given to someone deserving. Ālūsī says the interpretation presumes no procreation in Paradise. Wāḥidī adds that he also loses what he possessed here; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the hope disappointed. Māwardī presents this beside the worldly-wealth account. [17 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي والكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو سهيل,الكلبي); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سهل); TABATABAI(الأكثر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)+]
- 19:80/q01/p3 God inherits everything the man amassed and did in worldly life. — Ibn Zayd says nothing, small or great, accompanies him (ṬABARĪ and IBN KATHĪR); this extends the inherited object to his deeds as well as possessions. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)]
- 19:80/q01/p4 God preserves the man's utterance as a charge to be used against him at judgment. — Al-Baghawī says it is retained for requital; al-Naḥḥās compares scholars inheriting prophets through preserving their words (IBN ʿAṬIYYA, ABŪ ḤAYYĀN). Al-Kashshāf says the record exposes and shames him at the gathering. Ālūsī observes that the earlier 'We shall write' already expresses preservation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the man's surviving claim as a sin attached to him, with no worldly support left; he prefers this because the passage seriously rebuts the claim. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النحاس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 19:80/q01/p5 God ends the man's mocking speech when death overtakes him. — Al-Kashshāf says he speaks only while alive; Ālūsī says death reveals reality and ends further mockery. An objection that this assumes an unbeliever sincerely believed in resurrection is answered by saying mockery can continue until death. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:80/q01/p6 God inherits the man himself. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this concise gloss, without further explanation in the note. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 19:80/q02 How is mā in 'We inherit him what he says' construed?  [وَنَرِثُهُۥ مَا يَقُولُ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic relation of mā to the object pronoun, and whether an omitted noun is needed because literal words cannot be inherited.
- 19:80/q02/p1 Mā is an appositive of inclusion or clarification to the object pronoun. — Al-Samīn calls it an appositive of inclusion. Bursawī cites al-ʿUyūn for an appositional reading that accompanies his destruction and transfer of wealth and children. Ālūsī says it explains the pronoun; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an appositional clarification of the object pronoun, whose referent is actual property and children. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(العيون); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:80/q02/p2 Mā is the direct object, with the attached pronoun implying 'from him'. — Al-Samīn and Ālūsī present this syntactic alternative. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:80/q02/p3 An omitted noun before mā means the sense or named referent of his words. — Al-Samīn reports this because words themselves cannot be inherited. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:80/q03 What does 'what he says' refer to?  [مَا يَقُولُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the reported promise concerns goods in the next life or worldly goods, and whether the phrase includes the man's broader blasphemy and mockery.
- 19:80/q03/p1 His boast that he would receive wealth and children in the Hereafter. — Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabarī, al-Farrāʾ, al-Nasafī, the Study Quran and others identify the claim of future wealth and children; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the majority's reading as a sworn claim of Paradise goods after resurrection. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the short phrase compresses a lengthy boast and mockery and compares 3:183. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الأكثر); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Ṭs)]
- 19:80/q03/p2 His claim to receive wealth and children in worldly life. — Bursawī says only the worldly goods actually given can be inherited, because the future boast has no object. Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān entertain a hope for worldly wealth that could be granted and then removed; Ālūsī finds that suggestion remote given the occasion report. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's preferred account says he claimed disbelief would bring wealth and children and left that sinful claim behind. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI+]
- 19:80/q03/p3 His full blasphemy and mockery, compressed into a short phrase. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the concise reference to hostile words in 3:183. Al-Kashshāf and Ālūsī take the speech literally as continued mockery until death. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:80/q04 Why does 'he says' use a present verb?  [يَقُولُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the present form recalls an earlier claim or depicts ongoing mockery until death.
- 19:80/q04/p1 Its sense recalls what the man already said. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly says the present form has past meaning. Ālūsī relates the tense to what the man had received; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the expression as a compressed reference to his prior speech. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:80/q04/p2 It portrays his repeated speech while he lives, which death will stop. — Al-Kashshāf and Ālūsī say the man keeps mocking while alive; death exposes reality and silences him. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:80/q05 What variant wording is reported for 'We inherit what he says'?  [وَنَرِثُهُۥ مَا يَقُولُ · readings]
- 19:80/q05/p1 Ibn Masʿūd read it as God inheriting what the man has. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr and Ālūsī transmit this reading; Wāḥidī and Ālūsī use it to clarify the interpretation of actual possessions, and al-Durr connects it to his solitary arrival. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن مسعود); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وقتادة وابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 19:80/q06 What does coming to God alone mean?  [وَيَأْتِينَا فَرْدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether solitude stresses lost possessions and offspring or lost social and spiritual support; several sources include both.
- 19:80/q06/p1 He arrives at resurrection without his wealth, children, or other worldly possessions. — Muqātil, al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and others describe an empty-handed arrival. Ibn Zayd says nothing small or great accompanies him. Al-Kashshāf says he loses both his coveted goods and his wish; on the first interpretation everyone initially arrives this way before later outcomes diverge. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Kashshāf and the Study Quran cite 6:94; the Study Quran also cites 19:95 and says the goods had supplied worldly honor and security. Bursawī says he has no goods from this life, still less an added share; Bayḍāwī also allows that he has abandoned his claim. [33 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وقتادة وابن زيد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); TABATABAI(الأكثر); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Ṭs)]
- 19:80/q06/p2 He arrives without a defender, helper, clan, or any useful support. — Māwardī offers this as an alternative to lacking wealth and children. Jishumī says a person deserving punishment has no helpful thing; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān stress humiliation and lack of supporters. Al-Qurṭubī mentions the clan; al-Biqāʿī adds lost honor and strength. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says only the preserved statement and its consequence accompany him. Asad says he must rely solely on God's grace and mercy, citing 6:94 and 19:95. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 19:80/q07 What is the grammatical role of 'alone'?  [فَرْدًۭا · grammar]
- 19:80/q07/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative describing his state on arrival. — Al-Naḥḥās identifies the circumstantial construction. Ālūsī says it holds under all interpretations: future-oriented when wealth and children are transferred, otherwise simply his arrival alone. [2 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:80/q08 Who is the man to whom the pronouns refer?  [وَنَرِثُهُۥ يَقُولُ وَيَأْتِينَا · referent]
turns on: The reports identify al-ʿĀṣ; Ṭabāṭabāʾī also records al-Walīd as an alternative.
- 19:80/q08/p1 Al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil. — Mujāhid explicitly identifies him, as do the reports of Khabbāb's debt and al-Farrāʾ's explanation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this as one majority identification. [19 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(خباب بن الأرت); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(خباب بن الأرت); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,خباب,مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل,خباب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,خباب بن الأرت,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(خباب); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(الأكثر,خباب بن الأرت); BUKHARI(خباب)]
- 19:80/q08/p2 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this as an alternative identification within the majority reading, without further evidence in the note. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الأكثر)]

## 19:80/q09 What happened to prompt the passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the event was Khabbāb's individual unpaid work or companions collecting debts, and whether mockery of resurrection fits a passage that answers the claim seriously.
- 19:80/q09/p1 Khabbāb sought payment from al-ʿĀṣ, who demanded that he renounce Muhammad and mocked repayment after resurrection from future wealth and children. — Yaḥyā says Khabbāb told the Prophet before revelation; ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr and Bukhārī give the same core report. Al-Qurṭubī says Muslim and Bukhārī transmitted it; Ibn Kathīr notes several canonical routes. Al-Biqāʿī's Bukhārī version specifies Khabbāb forged a sword; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Kalbī and Muqātil's detail that the blacksmith made jewelry, with al-ʿĀṣ claiming Khabbāb had no greater Paradise entitlement. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites similar reports from al-Ṭabarānī, Saʿīd ibn Manṣūr through al-Ḥasan and an unnamed Companion, and Ibn ʿAbbās, without grading them. Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that a disbeliever's mockery of resurrection does not fit the passage's serious rebuttal. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(خباب بن الأرت); ABDURRAZZAQ(خباب بن الأرت); TAB-FULL(خباب); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل,خباب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); BIQAI-FULL(خباب); TABATABAI(خباب بن الأرت); BUKHARI(خباب); TABATABAI-]
- 19:80/q09/p2 Companions sought debts from al-ʿĀṣ, who mocked their promised Paradise and said he too would have wealth, children, and a book there. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this account in Ṭabarī; Ibn Kathīr transmits it by al-ʿAwfī. It differs from the Khabbāb account in the claim of a book and the companions' collective debt collection. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's contextual objection to mockery also bears on this account. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI-]

## 19:80/q10 Does Paradise include procreation?  [مَا يَقُولُ · theology]
turns on: The force and wording of the conditional Tirmidhī report, the reliability of the other report, and whether the no-child reports exclude all procreation or only ordinary reproduction.
- 19:80/q10/p1 Its people have no offspring there. — Ālūsī reports Mujāhid, Ṭāwūs and Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī denying procreation, citing reports that Paradise's people have no offspring and a long report from Laqīṭ ibn ʿĀmir. Ibn Mandah says only a denier or ignorant person rejects the Laqīṭ report. A report from Abū Dharr al-ʿUqaylī likewise says no child. Al-Safārīnī says most scholars favor no procreation and supplies ten arguments, though Ālūsī says many are debatable. The side answers opposing reports as excluding only ordinary worldly reproduction; al-Bayhaqī weakens the latter opposing report. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر العقيلي,ابن منده,مجاهد وطاوس وإبراهيم النخعي); ALUSI-FULL(السفاريني)+]
- 19:80/q10/p2 A believer who wishes for a child in Paradise can have one, with rapid conception, birth and growth. — Abū Saʿīd's Tirmidhī report says all stages occur in an hour; Tirmidhī calls it gharīb. Abū Nuʿaym transmits another report that a child is possible as soon as wished. Al-Safārīnī calls the Tirmidhī chain the best procreation report but notes inconsistent wording; Ālūsī says even a ḥasan conditional report need not mean the condition occurs and finds reconciliation weak. Al-Bayhaqī calls the latter report weak. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,السفاريني); ALUSI-FULL(السفاريني)-]

## 19:80/q11 What judgment does the verse make on the boast?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:80/q11/p1 The lie is recorded for added punishment, and the boaster loses every claimed advantage at judgment. — Ṭabarī says he had neither knowledge of the unseen nor a covenant through faith and obedience: disbelief and the false boast bring increased punishment. Islāḥī says disbelief and arrogance draw escalating punishment, followed by empty-handed arrival. Bukhārī's report says the passage rejects the boast and foretells punishment and solitary return. Ibn al-ʿĀshūr calls the dispossession a threat of destruction; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the preserved sinful statement alone accompanies him to its consequence. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BUKHARI(خباب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:80/q12 What inward lesson is drawn from his solitary return?  [* · inward]
- 19:80/q12/p1 Attachment to fortune, offspring, salvation and status breeds conceit and leaves a person unsupported; one must break that conceit. — Bursawī describes deluded people who boast of worldly fortune and future rank while mocking renunciants; attachment distances them from God. He cites al-Kamāl al-Khujandī's couplet that breaking the idol of conceit in the lovers' faith is better than a hundred acts of worship. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكمال الخجندي)]
