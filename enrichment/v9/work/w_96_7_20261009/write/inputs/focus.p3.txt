# 96:7/q11 Who is addressed by the rebuke kalla, and how does it connect to this verse? [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether kalla points forward to a rebuked human opponent or continues the address to the Prophet; its value as rebuke or affirmation also matters.
- 96:7/q11/p1 Kalla rebukes human ingratitude and the later opponent who bars prayer.
  reasons: Tabari says it rebukes receiving God's gifts with disbelief. Baydawi connects it to rejection of favor; Kuranyolu calls it a negative restraint against arrogance and turning from truth. Ibn Ashur says an initial rebuke can point forward to the man forbidding prayer, comparing an initial particle in Imru al-Qays.
  holders (5 notes): TAB; BAYDAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: TAB:96:7/r1 BAYDAWI-FULL:96:4-8/r4 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:6/r3 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p441/r7 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r1
- 96:7/q11/p2 The Prophet remains the addressee: he is warned to continue the command to read.
  reasons: Elmalili prefers this because it preserves continuity with the opening; the warning about humanity and Abu Jahl then follows. He reports an alternative taking the warning as first addressed to Abu Jahl but says it disconnects the opening.

# 96:7/q12 What contrast does Ibn Masud draw when citing these verses? [* · reports]
- 96:7/q12/p1 The scholar and worldly seeker are both insatiable, but knowledge brings divine approval while worldly pursuit deepens transgression.
  reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Kathir and Durr transmit the comparison and connect it with 35:28 on learned fear of God. Ibn Kathir also gives a version raised to the Prophet that states the two are insatiable; the repeated Durr entries occur under several verses.

# 96:7/q13 What warnings about future prosperity are associated with this verse? [* · reports]
turns on: One set warns about a community's future wealth; another illustrates the recurring human appetite behind that warning.
- 96:7/q13/p1 The Prophet warned that abundance and competition for worldly goods could destroy his community as they destroyed earlier peoples.
  reasons: Burhan reports fear of worldly adornment and cites 6:32. Biqai transmits forecasts of conquest, regular rich meals and worldly competition; some listeners judged former days better for worship, but the Prophet corrected them that their present poverty was better. Biqai also applies it to the community receiving earth's treasures.
  holders (5 notes): BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BURHAN:v2p140/r5 BIQAI:96:7/r5 BIQAI:96:7/r6 BIQAI-FULL:v8p483/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p483/r2
- 96:7/q13/p2 Reports about an unfilled appetite illustrate how satisfaction invites further desire.
  reasons: Biqai cites the saying that only dust fills a human belly and the last person to leave Hell, who repeatedly asks for more after promising not to; the versions vary in emphasis on the final abode and successive gifts.
  holders (4 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:96:7/r3 BIQAI:96:7/r4 BIQAI-FULL:v8p482/r10 BIQAI-FULL:v8p482/r11

# 96:7/q14 What response to the warning should a person cultivate? [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources emphasize either grateful awareness of need or active restraint of the conduct that perceived power produces.
- 96:7/q14/p1 Recognize dependence on God, thank Him for gifts, and restrain pride and desire.
  reasons: Qushayri says seeing one's need instead of admiring oneself prevents transgression; Yahya ibn Muadh recommends asceticism and hunger to cut desire. Qutb says wealth should bring recognition and gratitude. Kuranyolu stresses that God owns and may remove every resource and that humans also need society. Ibn Kathir says God will question how wealth was acquired and spent; Elmalili points to inevitable return to the Lord.
  holders (11 notes): QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء، يحيى بن معاذ); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR

- 96:7/q14/p2 Religious or rational restraint must check the powerful person's contempt and abuse.

# 96:7/q15 How does this warning relate to knowledge at the sura's beginning? [* · links]
turns on: Whether knowledge in the opening functions chiefly as the praised alternative to riches or as another gift that can tempt self-importance.
- 96:7/q15/p1 The opening praises knowledge, while the ending condemns wealth and urges learning and religion over worldly riches.
  reasons: Razi names 96:4-5 and 96:6 as the contrast. Tabari and Qutb also recall that the transgressor received knowledge and other favors from God, making disbelief ingratitude.
  holders (5 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; TAB; QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: RAZI:96:7/r6 RAZI-FULL:v32p19#3/r6 TAB:96:6/r1 TAB:96:7/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:96:6/r2
- 96:7/q15/p2 Acquired knowledge can itself become a ground for arrogant self-sufficiency.
  reasons: Bint al-Shati reads the movement from bestowed knowledge to the human's pride in knowledge and status; her warning invokes one's place as a creature. Qashani also warns against claiming independent perfection after receiving knowledge.
  holders (3 notes): BINTSHATI; QASHANI
  notes: BINTSHATI:v2p24/r4 BINTSHATI:v2p25/r2 QASHANI:96:3-8/r3
sources: BINTSHATI = ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419; QASHANI = ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736; QUTB-ZILAL = Sayyid Quṭb, d. 1386; RAZI = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606; RAZI-FULL = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606; TAB = al-Ṭabarī, d. 310

# 96:7/q16 What inward lesson is drawn from perceived self-sufficiency? [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inner danger is the veil of riches and desire or the self's claim to perfection through knowledge.
- 96:7/q16/p1 Seeing oneself as rich veils one's need and breeds tyranny; remembering poverty before God counters it.
  reasons: Ibn Ata says wealth harms one whose proper state is poverty and makes him insolent toward the Enricher; Yahya ibn Muadh recommends asceticism and hunger against sinful desire. Qushayri says the person who witnesses need instead of admiring self does not transgress.
  holders (4 notes): SULAMI(ابن عطاء، يحيى بن معاذ); QUSHAYRI
  notes: SULAMI:v2p407#2/r1 SULAMI:v2p407#2/r2 SULAMI:v2p407#2/r3 QUSHAYRI:v3p436/r9
- 96:7/q16/p2 God's gift of knowledge must not be taken as independent spiritual perfection.
  reasons: Qashani applies the passage to the Prophet before firm establishment in abiding, warning against selfhood and the claim of independent perfection.
  holders (1 notes): QASHANI
  notes: QASHANI:96:3-8/r3
sources: QASHANI = ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736; QUSHAYRI = al-Qushayrī, d. 465; SULAMI = al-Sulamī, d. 412
