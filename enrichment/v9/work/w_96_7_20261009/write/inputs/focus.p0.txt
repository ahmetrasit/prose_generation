# 96:7/q01 What does an do in this clause? [أَن · grammar]
- 96:7/q01/p1 It introduces the cause of transgression: because he sees himself self-sufficient.
  reasons: Al-Akhfash, as reported by Wahidi and Razi, supplies an omitted causal lam; Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur likewise attach the clause to yatgha. Corpus Coranicum calls it explanatory and causal and compares 43:5. Biqai says the emphatic formulation meets people's denial of their own tyranny.

# 96:7/q02 Who does the pronoun in ra'ahu denote, and what kind of seeing permits this construction? [رَّءَاهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ra'a is restricted here to mental judgment or may also be visual perception; the latter changes istaghna from second object to circumstantial clause.
- 96:7/q02/p1 The human sees or judges himself inwardly; cognitive ra'a takes the coreferential pronoun and istaghna as its second object.
  reasons: Farra distinguishes two-complement cognition verbs from ordinary one-object verbs, for which Arabic uses nafs, citing forms such as azunnuni and ra'aytuni; he calls the self word omitted. Tabari, Nahhas, Farisi, Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Ibn Ashur give the subject-and-predicate or two-object analysis. Kashshaf and Nasafi exclude physical sight on their grammatical account. Ibn Atiyya calls it perception close to knowledge. Ibn Ashur cites 17:62; Corpus Coranicum notes Tabari's parallel. Farra notes that forms like adimtuni are not standard, while Alusi and Ibn Ashur mention faqada and adima as exceptional parallels.

# 96:7/q03 How is ra'ahu read? [رَّءَاهُ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted consonantal length and the scope of imala; the short form's status turns on its transmission and whether rare Arabic deletion justifies it.
- 96:7/q03/p1 The full form retains the alif, with open vowels rather than imala.
  reasons: Ibn Mujahid assigns open vowels to Nafi and Hafs; Farisi says neither inclines the ra. Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur say most readers retain the alif; Qurtubi prefers lengthening. Nashr says al-Zaynabi alone transmitted Qunbul's full form, while Ibn Mujahid may have refused it as an error according to the report there.

- 96:7/q03/p4 A transmitted short form omits the alif after hamza.
  reasons: Ibn Mujahid calls Qunbul's form erroneous; Farisi argues rare deletion in an irregular imperfect, Ru'bah's wasani, and metathesized poetic man ra do not license it, citing 12:31 and a jussive poetic form. Ibn Atiyya calls it lightening or deletion of the weak final radical. Qurtubi also names Mujahid and Humayd. Abu Hayyan and Samin defend a transmitted form with Arabic parallels, including al-Ajjaj's wasani and law tara ahl Makka, even if non-analogical. Nashr says most Qunbul transmitters, including Ibn Mujahid and Ibn Shanabudh, gave the short form, while critics disputed Ibn Mujahid's report and cited al-Khuza'i; Nashr answers that suspected mistakes need not be passed on. Ibn Ashur rejects dismissing a form explicitly ascribed to Qunbul but says no one else reports it. Alusi relays al-Bahr's defense. Qiraat-ER says it changes prosody but not root meaning.
