# 96:7/q07 How does the transgression appear in conduct? [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes apply the general transgression either to outward luxury, unbelief and abuse, Quraysh's rejection, or the later act of forbidding prayer.
- 96:7/q07/p1 It appears as arrogance, disbelief, ingratitude and overstepping toward God and people.
  reasons: Tabari says gifts are met with disbelief; Ibn Kathir calls it exultation and boasting. Ibn Ashur traces contempt and abuse to feeling protected by servants, weapons and allies. Qutb lists rebellion and lack of gratitude; Kuranyolu adds boasting, disobedience and a claim to control one's fate.

# 96:7/q08 What happened when Abu Jahl threatened the Prophet at prayer? [* · reports]
- 96:7/q08/p1 He threatened to trample the praying Prophet, retreated before a fiery barrier and angels, and was spared their seizure.
  reasons: Abu Hurayra reports a trench of fire, terror and wings; the Prophet said angels would have seized or torn him apart. Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Kathir note uncertainty whether the verse's revelation was named within Abu Hurayra's report. Ibn Kathir names Ahmad, Muslim, Nasa'i and Ibn Abi Hatim as transmitters through Mu'tamir. Kuranyolu treats the event as occasioning 96:6-19; Zajjaj and Elmalili also connect Abu Jahl's threat to this passage, with Elmalili naming Bukhari and Tirmidhi for Ibn Abbas's version.

# 96:7/q09 What does the report about Mecca's mountains say? [* · reports]
- 96:7/q09/p1 Abu Jahl asked for mountains of gold, or gold and silver, to make his people rich; Gabriel offered it with a warning, and the Prophet declined.
  reasons: Qurtubi's Ibn Abbas via Abu Salih version says mountains of gold, polytheists gaining wealth, transgressing and abandoning their religion; Alusi's wording adds silver and Quraysh. The threatened punishment is likened to the people of the Table if they disbelieved afterward. Both say the Prophet refused to spare them, knowing they would not accept in Qurtubi's account. No grading is supplied by Alusi.

# 96:7/q10 How do these verses relate to the first revelation and Abu Jahl's later opposition? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the Abu Jahl incident dates the whole sura or only later material, and where its direct application begins.
- 96:7/q10/p1 The first five verses came earlier; later verses about Abu Jahl were joined to them.
  reasons: Qurtubi compares the commanded placement with 2:281 appended to earlier al-Baqara verses. Elmalili reports later revelation on Abu Jahl's threat. Ibn Ashur reports the objection that obligatory prayer began only at the Night Journey, motivating an interval and later attachment.
  holders (3 notes): QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: QURTUBI-FULL:96:6-7/r2 ELMALILI:96:1-8#3/r9 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p442/r5
- 96:7/q10/p2 The whole sura may have been revealed when Abu Jahl forbade prayer.
  reasons: Qurtubi records this alternative, which would put the whole sura after the first revelations. Ibn Ashur reports that some consequently deny this sura was first revelation.
  holders (2 notes): QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: QURTUBI-FULL:96
