# 22:12: 25 questions

## 22:12/q01 Who is the person invoking besides God?  [يَدْعُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the surrounding apostasy describes the caller specifically, and whether invocation of idols excludes a Jewish referent.
- 22:12/q01/p1 A worshipper who turns back to unbelief after a trial and then invokes idols. — Al-Tabari connects this with the precarious worshipper's trial and apostasy in 22:11; Ibn Zayd calls it disbelief after belief. Al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi and Bursawi describe the same return to unbelief; Ibn Ashur makes this clause circumstantial to انقلب in 22:11. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 22:12/q01/p2 An idol worshipper or polytheist. — Muqatil and Yahya identify the caller through idol worship; al-Razi prefers a polytheist reading because the objects called upon are idols. Al-Alusi likewise says the idol reading makes a Jewish referent unlikely, although Jews took rabbis and monks as lords. [4 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 22:12/q01/p3 Polytheists who attached themselves to the Messenger hypocritically. — Al-Razi offers this as the likely setting for the passage. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 22:12/q01/p4 People whose profession of faith lacks full commitment and who credit other forces with power over destiny. — Asad includes real or imagined forces in the failure of commitment. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 22:12/q01/p5 A Jew. — Al-Razi finds this unlikely because of idol worship; al-Alusi agrees even while noting that Jews took religious authorities as lords. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 22:12/q02 What does يدعو mean here?  [يَدْعُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes the worshipful act, a plea for aid, or the act of naming or addressing.
- 22:12/q02/p1 To worship or serve the object. — Ibn Atiyya gives worship as one sense; al-Alusi prefers supplication as worship in this context. The many explanations of worship if served and abandonment if unserved use this sense. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 22:12/q02/p2 To invoke, plead with or seek help from the object, especially in distress. — Ibn Atiyya includes distress invocation; Ibn Kathir specifies help, victory and provision. Al-Alusi describes calling on a lifeless thing to rescue one from affliction. Islahi includes praying, pleading, beseeching, and seeking help or mercy; Antara's line illustrates calling or addressing someone. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI(عنترة)]
- 22:12/q02/p3 To call or name a thing by a name. — Al-Tabrisi offers this alternative and cites a verse of Ibn Ahmar in which something is called by a name. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 22:12/q03 What is invoked besides God?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ مَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the object is identified concretely as an idol, extended to leaders, or generalized to perceived powers.
- 22:12/q03/p1 Idols, stones or other inert false gods. — Al-Farra names idols; al-Wahidi specifies stones. Al-Zajjaj says the idol neither hears nor sees. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi stress inertness; the Study Quran cites 6:71, 10:18, 10:106, 20:89, 21:66, 25:55 and 26:73 for this common reading. Al-Razi prefers idols and thereby disfavors a Jewish referent. [12 notes: FARRA; YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 22:12/q03/p2 Idols or human leaders whom the worshipper follows. — Al-Jishumi reports both objects as the possible destination of the worshipper's turn; the leaders are associated with nearer harm than benefit. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 22:12/q03/p3 Real or imagined forces assigned decisive influence over destiny. — Asad applies the verse to forces to which the wavering believer grants divine qualities. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 22:12/q04 What does invoking something besides God imply?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · theology]
- 22:12/q04/p1 It wrongly transfers worship, trust or control over harm and benefit away from God. — Al-Biqa'i says all besides God are beneath the possessor of every perfection; worship is owed through chosen action rather than compelled natural behavior. He also contrasts this reliance with the exclusive plea for divine help in 1:5. Islahi says only God governs harm and benefit and all reliance belongs to Him; Asad calls assigning decisive influence to other forces a grant of divine qualities. Al-Tabataba'i says the idol lacks awareness and will. Al-Jishumi draws the practical conclusion that one must turn to God alone in every circumstance, since benefit and harm ultimately come from Him. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; JISHUMI]

## 22:12/q05 Why is harm mentioned before benefit?  [لَا يَضُرُّهُۥ وَمَا لَا يَنفَعُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 22:12/q05/p1 The order reflects the wavering person's wish to escape perceived harm from Islam and imagined anger from idols. — Ibn Ashur says the ensuing wording mocks the mistake of seeking relief from powerless idols. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:12/q06 Why repeat ما before the denial of benefit?  [مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُۥ وَمَا لَا يَنفَعُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 22:12/q06/p1 The repetition reinforces the object's inertness and treats the negations of harm and benefit separately. — Al-Biqa'i details both directions: it cannot cause harm or block benefit, and cannot provide benefit or avert harm; blame need not require the absence of both qualities together. Bursawi and al-Alusi say the repeated ma points to an inanimate object. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:12/q07 In what sense can the invoked object neither harm nor benefit?  [مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُۥ وَمَا لَا يَنفَعُهُۥ · interpretation]
- 22:12/q07/p1 The object has no power to punish neglect or reward service, nor to remove distress or grant advantage. — Muqatil and al-Tabari locate ineffective punishment in this world and ineffective reward in the Hereafter. Al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Nasafi and Bursawi explain disobedience versus obedience or abandonment versus worship. Al-Zajjaj notes lack of hearing and sight; Ibn Ashur says seeking relief or advantage from it is mistaken. Al-Biqa'i adds that abandoning it cannot prevent God's beneficence and worship cannot secure its aid. Al-Tabataba'i grounds impotence in lack of awareness and will. Al-Suddi distinguishes the idol's impotence from harm arriving in the afterlife. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; QASHANI]

## 22:12/q08 What does ذلك point back to, and what does its wording emphasize?  [ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ · rhetoric]
- 22:12/q08/p1 It points to the invocation, emphatically identifying that act as the distant error. — Ibn Ashur identifies the antecedent as invocation and says the demonstrative, separating pronoun and restriction work as in 22:11. Asad gives the literal wording as 'this, this is the far-away straying' and refers to his explanation at 14:18. Samarra'i counts huwa 13 times in al-Hajj versus 7 in Luqman, including 8 versus 3 uses as a separating pronoun, to account for its strong stylistic presence here. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 22:12/q09 What does the distant error mean?  [ٱلضَّلَٰلُ ٱلْبَعِيدُ · meaning]
- 22:12/q09/p1 An extreme, long or far departure from truth and guidance, pictured as being lost far into a wilderness. — Al-Farra calls it long error; al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi express distance from God's path, truth or guidance. Al-Baydawi, Bursawi and al-Alusi compare it to a traveler astray in a wilderness. Ibn Ashur says idolatry is an extreme degree of error because its objects have no utility; al-Biqa'i calls reliance on them gross folly. Al-Qashani explains the distance of the error through attachment to the invoked object, which veils the caller from the Real. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QASHANI]

## 22:12/q10 How does the clause about invocation connect to the preceding account?  [يَدْعُوا۟ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is syntactically dependent on the earlier turning back or starts an explanatory or additional sentence.
- 22:12/q10/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing the person who turned back. — Ibn Ashur attaches it to the subject of انقلب in 22:11. Al-Alusi reports this analysis with the preceding material treated as parenthetical. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 22:12/q10/p2 It resumes as a new sentence exposing a further fault of the apostate. — Bursawi describes it as resuming the account of the apostate polytheist's great loss; al-Alusi lists a new sentence exposing another fault. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:12/q10/p3 It explains the scale of the preceding loss. — Al-Alusi gives this as a further possible connection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:12/q11 How can 22:12 deny harm and benefit while 22:13 says harm is nearer?  [مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُۥ وَمَا لَا يَنفَعُهُۥ · links]
- 22:12/q11/p1 The idol itself has no power, but worshipping it harms its devotee, especially through loss in the afterlife. — Kuran Yolu gives this explanation. Al-Tabataba'i attributes the harm and loss to the worshipper's own act, not the unaware idol; al-Suddi places the harm in the afterlife; Ibn Kathir says worldly harm is nearer than any benefit and next-world harm is certain. Kuran Yolu also reports unspecified linguistic explanations for the apparent difference. Al-Qashani locates the nearer harm in the caller's own invocation and attachment: stopping with the object veils the caller from the Real. [6 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bir yorum,müfessirler); TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QASHANI]

## 22:12/q12 What inward lessons are drawn from reliance on other than God?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · inward]
turns on: Which aspect of exclusive dependence on God is foregrounded.
- 22:12/q12/p1 Reliance on anything besides the Real is reliance on harm; reliance on God is reliance on the source of everything. — Ibn Ata gives the contrast as an inward application of the verse. Al-Qashani adds that calling on or stopping with something besides God veils one from the Real; this is why such attachment is distant error and its harm is nearer than its benefit. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QASHANI]
- 22:12/q12/p2 God's decree of humiliation cannot be reversed by another, and the garment of Truth never changes. — Al-Sulami reports this from al-Sayyari. [1 notes: SULAMI(السياري)]

## 22:12/q13 How do other verses and the surrounding passage illuminate this error?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw different connections: parallel idol texts, the appeal in 1:5, continuing worship after apostasy, and the passage's contrast with the righteous.
- 22:12/q13/p1 Other passages expose the impotence of false objects of worship and the danger of seeking refuge with them. — Islahi invokes 22:73: such beings cannot create a fly or recover what it steals, and escape from God's trial ends in Hell. Samarra'i links this passage's denunciation to 22:13, 22:31, 22:71 and 22:73. The Study Quran supplies parallels at 6:71, 10:18, 10:106, 20:89, 21:66, 25:55 and 26:73. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 22:12/q13/p2 The appeal to other beings reverses the exclusive request for God's help. — Al-Biqa'i expressly compares the caller's reliance with 1:5. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:12/q13/p3 After turning from God, people still seek an object of worship and supplication, even one useless or harmful. — Kuran Yolu states this as the passage's human pattern. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 22:12/q13/p4 The passage contrasts the misguided and righteous through a garden parable and their respective afterlife outcomes. — The Study Quran's introduction frames the passage this way. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 22:12/q14 How is the adjacent phrase يدعو لمن ضره construed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The function of the lam and whether the following words form a new clause or complete يدعو's object.
- 22:12/q14/p1 The lam is a postponed emphatic or oath particle, and an implicit object is supplied for يدعو. — Al-Tha'labi transmits the displacement analysis from al-Farra and al-Zajjaj. Al-Tabrisi reports al-Zajjaj's postponed emphatic lam and a version specifying an emphatic oath lam with an implicit pronoun. [3 notes: THALABI(الفراء والزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج)]
- 22:12/q14/p2 The lam is an extra particle. — Al-Tha'labi attributes this to some grammarians and cites Ibn Masud's reading without the lam. [1 notes: THALABI(بعض النحاة)]
- 22:12/q14/p3 يدعو is circumstantial with an implied object. — Al-Tabrisi reports this among al-Zajjaj's analyses. [1 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج)]
- 22:12/q14/p4 ذلك functions as a relative pronoun whose clause supplies the object of يدعو. — Al-Tabrisi transmits this as an analysis of al-Zajjaj; al-Jishumi reports al-Zajjaj making 'that' the object with relative force. [2 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج); JISHUMI(الزجاج)]
- 22:12/q14/p5 The phrase begins a new subject-predicate clause, or is treated as the object of يدعو. — Al-Jishumi gives the new-clause analysis and also an alternative in which 'that' is nominative with 'a wretched protector' its predicate. Al-Tabrisi records both new-clause and object analyses. [2 notes: JISHUMI(الزجاج); TABRISI]

## 22:12/q15 What variant reading is cited for the adjacent phrase?  [* · readings]
- 22:12/q15/p1 Ibn Masud's reading omits the lam in يدعو لمن. — Al-Tha'labi cites the omission in support of the view that the lam is extra. [1 notes: THALABI(بعض النحاة)]

## 22:12/q16 What does حرف in the preceding verse mean?  [* · meaning]
- 22:12/q16/p1 The long edge or side. — Al-Nahhas reports al-Farra's gloss. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء)]

## 22:12/q17 What does عشير in the following verse mean?  [* · meaning]
- 22:12/q17/p1 A companion, spouse or one who associates with another. — Al-Jishumi gives these related senses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 22:12/q18 What governs the order of meanings and words in composition?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether composition is explained from the speaker's prior grasp of meanings or from isolated words and the listener's sequence of reception.
- 22:12/q18/p1 The composing speaker arranges understood meanings first, and verbal signs follow that thought. — Al-Jurjani rejects isolated-word thinking: without meaning there is nothing to think about, and thinking of words apart from meaning is implausible. The listener's hearing words before understanding does not establish the order of the speaker's composition. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 22:12/q18/p2 The order of a listener's reception or isolated wording determines compositional thought. — Al-Jurjani rejects this inference because analysis must consider the composing speaker rather than the listener. [0 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL-]

## 22:12/q19 Must a speaker know formal grammar to compose correctly?  [* · grammar]
- 22:12/q19/p1 No; intuitive grasp of the relevant distinctions is enough. — Al-Jurjani compares a Bedouin's linguistic competence with early scholars' knowledge of theological truths before later technical terms. A Bedouin's question 'what did he do?' when a muezzin said accusative Muhammadan shows recognition that the utterance lacked a predicate. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 22:12/q20 How should reports of grammatical errors in the codex be understood?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported lahn denotes an impermissible grammatical error or a permitted dialect or reading choice, and how reliable the Uthman report is.
- 22:12/q20/p1 The codex contains actual uncorrected grammatical errors made by scribes. — A report from Aisha says scribes erred in the forms at 20:63, 4:162 and 5:69; Said ibn Jubayr called the form al-muqimin al-salah a scribe's grammatical error. Al-Alusi calls Aisha's report sound on the conditions of the two Sahihs but rejects a literal error conclusion. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,عائشة); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:12/q20/p2 The reported forms concern dialect or reading choices, not an impermissible Qur'anic error. — Al-Alusi calls the Uthman error report weak, disrupted and disconnected; collective failure to correct such errors would be implausible. He interprets Uthman's remark about correction by tongues as later review of dialectal forms, Aisha's words as a less suitable dialect chosen among seven readings, and Said's lahn as possibly language or reading; he cites another reading and grammatical analyses. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,عبد الله بن عبد الأعلى)]

## 22:12/q21 What makes the Qur'an miraculous?  [* · theology]
- 22:12/q21/p1 The whole Qur'an, including its shortest surah, is miraculous through its form and contents. — Al-Alusi names order, eloquence, unseen reports, rational accord and subtle meaning; some aspects can be hidden while others suffice. On the no-contradiction account, ordinary fine sermons or poetry and short surahs do not negate the miracle of the complete Qur'an. Other scholars cite its awe, growing love on repetition, lasting presence and God's promise of preservation. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:12/q22 Why does 2:196 explicitly call ten complete?  [* · rhetoric]
- 22:12/q22/p1 To prevent the mistaken inference that only seven are due on returning. — Al-Alusi notes the apparent obviousness of the wording but explains its clarifying function. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:12/q23 What comparison closes al-Alusi's broader discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
- 22:12/q23/p1 Successive manifestations are compared to stars, each setting star giving way to others. — Al-Alusi quotes the line without attribution. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:12/q24 Where and when was Surat al-Hajj revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Which passages are assigned to the Makkan or Madinan period.
- 22:12/q24/p1 It is Madinan except for Makkan verses 52–55. — The Study Quran attributes this dating to IK. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 22:12/q24/p2 It is Makkan except for Madinan verses 19–22. — The Study Quran attributes this dating to Q. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 22:12/q24/p3 It contains both Makkan and Madinan verses, without the specified split. — The Study Quran reports this as the view of most authorities. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 22:12/q25 What distinctive circumstances are reported for Surat al-Hajj?  [* · sciences]
- 22:12/q25/p1 It came in contrasting settings and contains several contrasting kinds of verses. — The Study Quran cites a tradition that it came at night and by day, on journeys and at home, in war and peace, and includes abrogating and abrogated as well as clear and allegorical verses. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]
