# 102:1: 27 questions

## 102:1/q01 What does أَلْهَىٰكُمُ mean?  [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb is rendered by its ordinary effect, distraction, or by the alluring manner of that diversion.
- 102:1/q01/p1 It occupied, distracted, or made you forget what matters. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī derive it from turning away or forgetting; Ibn Fāris calls anything occupying one lāhī. Imruʾ al-Qays's line illustrates 'occupied her' (Qurṭubī). Al-Ālūsī traces lahw from heedlessness to any diversion, conventionally pleasurable play; al-Rāghib defines it by neglect of what matters. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes Qurʾānic examples of person, wealth, children, trade, hopes and speech (63:9); play is paired separately with lahw (6:32, 29:64). [41 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,بعض المفسرين); RAZI(الزبير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(امرؤ القيس); QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]
- 102:1/q01/p2 It allures or deceives people. — Islāḥī uses the sense of enticement for the verb. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 102:1/q02 What did rivalry distract people from?  [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the unspoken object is read broadly or supplied specifically from the audience and context.
- 102:1/q02/p1 Obedience, worship, remembrance of God, and preparation for the Hereafter. — Muqātil says remembrance of the afterlife; al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī and others say obeying God and escaping His anger. Rāzī lists duties, recommended works, knowledge, reflection, and contemplation. Al-Biqāʿī adds death and saving acts; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies hearing Qurʾānic evidence and accepting the Prophet's call. 63:9 is used for the warning (Jishumī); al-Qāriʿah and the scales ground it for al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı. Zayd b. Aslam transmits in a mursal report a prophetic expansion that rivalry distracts from obedience. - 102:1/q02/p2 The omitted object allows every important concern neglected by diversion. — Rāzī says an absolute construction intensifies blame; al-Biqāʿī says omission magnifies the importance of what is neglected, and Elmalılı notes both distraction's object and accumulation's kind are unstated. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BAYDAWI]
- 102:1/q02/p3 In its immediate address, it diverted polytheist chiefs from Qurʾānic guidance and fair acceptance of Muhammad's call. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers the object from the wealthy chiefs' choice of luxury and their obstruction of others; past tense presents this distraction as already accomplished. ## 102:1/q03 Why is the object of أَلْهَىٰكُمُ unstated?  [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether ellipsis deliberately leaves all neglected duties open or expects a definite object to be inferred.
- 102:1/q03/p1 Its omission broadens and intensifies the rebuke. — Rāzī says an absolute construction lets the mind supply every neglected object: remembrance, duties, recommended acts, knowledge and reflection. Al-Bayḍāwī calls it grandeur and emphasis; al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı say it magnifies the neglected religious concern. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 102:1/q03/p2 The object is self-evident from the addressees: Qurʾān, reflection, and accepting its truth. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the omission to polytheist leaders who preferred comfort to Muhammad's call; the past tense marks the diversion as accomplished. [2 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 102:1/q05 What does ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ mean here?  [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether form VI names mutual boasting, acquisitive increase beyond boasting, or a possible Greek-derived expression.
- 102:1/q05/p1 Mutual boasting and rivalry in having more people, possessions, or valued distinctions. — Ibn Qutayba names people and kin; al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī say each group claims numerical superiority. Al-Aʿshā's lines link numbers with honor (Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn ʿĀshūr). Rāzī equates takāthur with tafākhur at 57:20; Jishumī and Abū Muslim note form VI can express reciprocity, effort, or acquisition. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this majority gloss but challenges its equation with boasting. [34 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الأعشى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعشى); TABRISI; RAZI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(الأعشى); IBNASHUR-FULL(الأعشى); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الطبري,جمهرة المفسرين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-]
- 102:1/q05/p2 It is acquisitive striving for ever more, extending beyond competitive boasting. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the root is abundance or growth in quantity and 57:20 distinguishes takāthur from tafākhur; she prefers striving to monopolize worldly goods. Paret glosses it 'urge to have more.' Islāḥī notes greed outruns rising living standards. [6 notes: BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI+]
- 102:1/q05/p3 It may render Greek pleonexia, covetous desire for more. — Künstlinger proposes Arabic form VI renders the Greek comparative; Corpus Coranicum says a Greek New Testament setting, including Luke's warning against greed, is likelier than Peshitta wording if this connection holds. Andrae compares Ephrem's worldly person who always wants more. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Andrae)]

## 102:1/q06 What does the form of ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ express?  [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · grammar]
turns on: Which attested force of form VI is active in this context.
- 102:1/q06/p1 It expresses reciprocal boasting, each party claiming more. — Jishumī lists reciprocity among the possible senses of tafāʿala; Rāzī and Bursevī describe rival groups or individuals comparing wealth and followers. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 102:1/q06/p2 It can express determined effort to increase or acquisition of quantity, without reciprocal boasting. — Abū Muslim lists effort and an intransitive increase alongside reciprocity; Rāzī applies effort to the ambitious pursuit of wealth. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ grounds it in the root 'abundance' and distinguishes it from tafākhur at 57:20. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 102:1/q07 What kind of abundance or pursuit is meant by ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ?  [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · referent]

Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects restriction to money, numbers, or the dead; Qurṭubī likewise extends application to all such causes. ## 102:1/q08 What does the definite article in ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ identify?  [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether definiteness narrows the referent to a familiar evil or occasion, or the unsupplied object leaves accumulation open.
- 102:1/q08/p1 It identifies familiar blameworthy worldly competition. — Rāzī distinguishes this pursuit of passing pleasure from commendable rivalry in knowledge and virtue; Bursevī likewise names money, status, supporters, and kin. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 102:1/q08/p2 It points to the known tribal occasion of boasting in numbers. — Elmalılı takes the article as referring to the reported occasion of comparing groups. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 102:1/q08/p3 The verse still leaves the type of increase unrestricted. — Elmalılı says the unexpressed kind of abundance gives wide rhetorical scope; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ resists importing a restriction to particular goods or only blameworthy kinds. [2 notes: ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]

## 102:1/q09 Whom does the verse address?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the speech is initially to a historical audience, universal in application, or placed after resurrection.
- 102:1/q09/p1 Its immediate rebuke addresses Meccan polytheists, especially wealthy chiefs. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers this from severe threats at 102:3, 6 and their preference for luxury over the Prophet's call, which they also obstructed; he says this was not then a Muslim trait. Kur'an Yolu describes the arrogant Meccan elite. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 102:1/q09/p2 The warning includes all people distracted by worldly rivalry, whatever the initial occasion. — Al-Wāḥidī says every person so distracted until death is included; Qurṭubī prefers applying it to all cited groups and others. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses the unspecified object; Kur'an Yolu extends the Arab example to modern accumulation; Ibn ʿĀshūr says believers too must heed it. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI+; QURTUBI+]
- 102:1/q09/p3 It addresses unbelievers on Judgment Day after they have visited the graves. — Abū Muslim proposes this temporal address; al-Ālūsī calls it astonishing. [0 notes: ALUSI(أبو مسلم)-]

## 102:1/q11 How does the following grave visitation complete this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the next clause describes literal counting at graves, death in figurative language, or a visit that prompts reflection.
- 102:1/q11/p1 The rivals counted or named dead ancestors at the graves to extend a contest begun among the living. — Al-Kashshāf calls 'visiting' sarcastic; Abū Ḥayyān calls it a mocking depiction. Elmalılı stresses this misuse of graves is the source of rebuke; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows the endpoint to be the object of rivalry, even tombs. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(بنو سهم وبنو عبد مناف); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(unnamed view); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 102:1/q11/p2 The rivalry continued until death and burial; 'visiting graves' figuratively means dying. — Rāzī says the clause can mean they forgot the grave until reaching it. Al-Kashshāf cites two poetic examples of visiting graves for dying; al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn Kathīr, and others read the life wasted in worldly pursuit. Elmalılı says death certainly ends competition and the following rebuke favors this reading. Zayd b. Aslam's mursal prophetic report explains visiting the graves as the arrival of death. [25 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(ابن عباس والحسن); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ITQAN(زيد بن أسلم); ELMALILI+]
- 102:1/q11/p3 A literal visit may briefly soften hearts and end greed, but the rivals may instead boast at graves. — Rāzī reports a view that hardened hearts retain only a little religious feeling at graves, comparing scant gratitude at 7:10 or 2:83. Elmalılı says seeing death or graves can awaken receptive hearts, but not universally, and also records that the clause can attach to takāthur or ilhā. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 102:1/q11/p4 The grave is a temporary visit before return to one's true home. — ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz argues a visitor must return home, implying life after burial. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عمر بن عبد العزيز)]

## 102:1/q12 What does the verse teach about worldly rivalry and its consequences?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources emphasize either spiritual loss and judgment or its further social and material consequences.
- 102:1/q12/p1 Pursuit of passing gains makes people heedless of lasting felicity and accountability. — Rāzī ranks inner knowledge and virtue above bodily health and external wealth; he cites Abraham's prayer for wisdom at 26:83 and notes people sacrifice wealth to relieve bodily pain. Al-Qāshānī says sensory pleasure wastes innate purity and intellect. Al-Biqāʿī traces neglect to desire and love of ease; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says ingratitude meets questioning and punishment. Zayd b. Aslam's mursal prophetic report says rivalry keeps people from obedience until death arrives. [23 notes: RAZI; QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; ITQAN(زيد بن أسلم)]
- 102:1/q12/p2 Unchecked acquisition damages moral vision, happiness, social justice, and the environment. — Asad says exclusive pursuit erases moral limits, destabilizes persons and societies, harms the natural environment, and brings frustration and confusion in technological society. Kur'an Yolu adds injustice from personal to international life through economic, political, and communication power. Islāḥī says wealth desire remains insatiable despite changing family norms and rising living standards. [12 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 102:1/q13 Is every form of rivalry for increase blameworthy?  [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether context and the article restrict the condemned rivalry to worldly goods, or whether the verse's unrestricted wording should remain open.
- 102:1/q13/p1 No: rivalry in knowledge, obedience, virtue, and sincere good deeds is commendable. — Rāzī distinguishes genuine felicity from passing goods; 9:19's report of boasts about pilgrimage water, Kaʿba key, and fighting disbelief illustrates religious claims. 93:11 allows recounting God's favor to inspire others. Elmalılı cites commands to race in good at 2:148, 5:48, 83:26, while warning that sincerity and quality matter more than sheer quantity (57:23, 4:36, 5:100). Al-Biqāʿī reasons from an ordered universe and human injustice to a wise Creator's resurrection and judgment, corroborated by creation and revelation. [17 notes: RAZI(العباس وشيبة وعلي); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Râzî); BIQAI-FULL]
- 102:1/q13/p2 The verse's wording is not restricted to a predefined class of blameworthy increase. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects adding a limiting category to the unqualified noun; she still reads the address as covering people distracted by worldly adornments, including wealth and children. [2 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 102:1/q14 What is the Prophet's teaching about the wealth people call their own?  [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · reports]
turns on: The reports offer related admonitions: what wealth is retained, how desire persists, and what accompanies a person after death.
- 102:1/q14/p1 A person truly keeps only what is consumed, worn out, or given away for lasting reward; the rest is left behind. — The Prophet says this after reciting the sūra in reports from Ibn al-Shikhkhīr and Abū Hurayra; al-Ṭabarī treats it as evidence that wealth is one object of takāthur. Al-Āḥnaf says a coin becomes one's own when spent in gratitude or reward, and a poem contrasts keeping and spending. Elmalılı cites Tirmidhī's ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ grading; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it one fitting admonition, not an exhaustive gloss. [40 notes: TAB(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(النبي); THALABI(النبي); BAGHAWI(عبد الله بن الشخير); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبوه); IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(مطرف عن أبيه); QURTUBI-FULL(مطرف عن أبيه); IBNKATHIR(الأحنف بن قيس,الشاعر,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأحنف بن قيس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن الشخير); ELMALILI(Abdullah b. Şihhir,İbn Cerîr); IBNASHUR(عبد الله بن الشخير); IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن الشخير); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 102:1/q14/p2 Greed for wealth persists and asks for still more, until earth fills the human mouth. — Anas's report says one valley of gold leads to desire for two and God accepts repentance. Ibn Kathīr adds the sound report that old age leaves greed and hope intact; Ubayy's report of a saying once thought Qurʾān gives variants of two valleys and a third. [6 notes: QURTUBI(أنس بن مالك); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 102:1/q14/p3 At death family and wealth return while deeds remain with the person. — Anas reports the Prophet's triad of family, wealth, and deeds. [4 notes: BAGHAWI(أنس بن مالك); IBNKATHIR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 102:1/q15 What did Ubayy report about the saying on Adam's desire for wealth?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in valley count and in whether they describe prior treatment of the saying as Qurʾān or only its admonitory content.
- 102:1/q15/p1 People had treated the saying as Qurʾān until this sūra was revealed. — Versions mention one or two valleys of gold, desire for another, a belly filled only by earth, and divine acceptance of repentance. Ibn al-ʿArabī calls the report sound and neglected by commentators. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'we' could mean Muslims generally and does not fix a Medinan date. [19 notes: TAB(أبي بن كعب); TAB-FULL(أبي بن كعب); QURTUBI(ابن العربي,ثابت عن أنس عن أبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,ثابت عن أنس عن أبي); IBNKATHIR(أبي بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); DURR(أبي بن كعب); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ITQAN(أبي بن كعب); ALUSI(أبي بن كعب); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بريدة); ELMALILI(Übey b. - 102:1/q15/p2 The saying belongs to the broader prophetic warning against endless acquisition. — Anas's version asks for a second valley after a first, while Ubayy's variations ask for a third; Study Quran cites it as a parallel to takāthur. [3 notes: QURTUBI(أنس بن مالك); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]

## 102:1/q16 Does this sūra establish punishment in the grave?  [* · theology]
- 102:1/q16/p1 Yes: ʿAlī says uncertainty about grave punishment ended when this passage was revealed. — Naḥḥās takes the verse as indicating grave punishment; ʿAlī's report is transmitted by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Durr, al-Suyūṭī, and Ibn Kathīr. Ibn Kathīr cites al-Tirmidhī's gharīb judgment; al-Itqān and al-Ālūsī also use the report in dating the sūra. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(علي); IBNABIHATIM(علي); NAHHAS(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي); DURR(علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ITQAN(علي); SUYUTI-LUBAB(علي); ALUSI(علي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بريدة)]

## 102:1/q17 Was the sūra revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The rival Quraysh and Ansari occasion reports, Ubayy's wording, ʿAlī's grave-punishment report, and the fit of the sūra's threats to a Meccan audience.
- 102:1/q17/p1 It is Meccan. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he knows no disagreement; Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report exegetical consensus, while recording al-Bukhārī's contrary label. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers Mecca because the severe warning suits polytheists and the Quraysh occasion; he rejects Ubayy's 'we' as proof of Medina, since it could mean Muslims generally. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and Asad call it early Meccan. [25 notes: MAWARDI; JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; QURTUBI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(جميع المفسرين والبخاري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI(أبو حيان والجلال السيوطي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بريدة); IBNASHUR(ابن عباس والكلبي ومقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR+; ITQAN(ابن بريدة)-; ALUSI-]
- 102:1/q17/p2 It is Medinan. — Al-Ḥasan says Medina in Jishumī; al-Bukhārī is cited against the exegetes by Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān. Al-Suyūṭī and al-Ālūsī prefer Medina, using the Ansari occasion; al-Ālūsī adds Ubayy's saying and ʿAlī's grave-punishment report, arguing the latter is mentioned in Medinan material. Ibn ʿĀshūr contests Ubayy's dating inference. [12 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن); QURTUBI; ABUHAYYAN(جميع المفسرين والبخاري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ITQAN(علي); ALUSI(علي); IBNASHUR(السيوطي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو بريدة)+; ALUSI+; ITQAN(ابن بريدة)+; IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 102:1/q17/p3 Its place of revelation is disputed. — Al-Bayḍāwī states the dispute without choosing. [1 notes: BAYDAWI]

## 102:1/q18 What names are reported for the sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported companion title differs between al-Mughīra and al-Maqbara; codices also vary between two opening-word titles.
- 102:1/q18/p1 Al-Takāthur is the common title; some codices and al-Bukhārī call it Alhākum. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites most codices, commentaries, Tirmidhī, and old Qayrawan codices for the first title. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 102:1/q18/p2 Some Companions called it al-Mughīra. — Saʿīd b. Abī Hilāl transmits this name through Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن هلال); DURR(أصحاب رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن أبي هلال)]
- 102:1/q18/p3 Some Companions called it al-Maqbara, the Graveyard Sūra. — Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr both attribute this to Saʿīd b. Abī Hilāl, although other transmissions here give al-Mughīra. [5 notes: ALUSI(سعد بن أبي هلال); IBNASHUR(سعيد بن أبي هلال); IBNASHUR-FULL(سعيد بن أبي هلال); STUDYQURAN(some Companions)]

## 102:1/q19 How many verses does the sūra have and where is it placed in revelation order?  [* · sciences]
- 102:1/q19/p1 It has eight verses. — Al-Ālūsī says this is unanimous; several sūra headings agree. [10 notes: BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 102:1/q19/p2 On the Meccan chronology, it is sixteenth, after al-Kawthar and before al-Māʿūn. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both neighbors; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls sixteenth a common dating and names al-Kawthar; Study Quran places it after al-Kawthar. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]

## 102:1/q20 What merit or recitation practice is reported for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports describe different merits and circumstances; the weeping promise is explicitly graded weak.
- 102:1/q20/p1 Reciting it counts like reciting a thousand verses. — Reports from Ubayy, Ibn ʿUmar, and ʿUmar give the merit; Ibn al-Milq suggests its teaching of return to God is one of six Qurʾānic aims and matches one sixth of roughly 6,200 verses, but al-Ālūsī says the true basis lies beyond that calculation. [9 notes: JISHUMI(أبي); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(النبي); ALUSI(ابن عمر,عمر بن الخطاب,ناصر الدين بن الميلق); ALUSI-FULL(ناصر الدين بن الميلق)]
- 102:1/q20/p2 A report promises Paradise to whoever weeps at its recitation, or feigns tears. — Al-Durr records the invitation and repeats the promise at a second recitation; al-Bayhaqī weakens the report. [3 notes: DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 102:1/q20/p3 The Prophet recited the sūra in prayer or in a companion's hearing. — ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Shikhkhīr says he found the Prophet reciting it, including one version in prayer through its end. [2 notes: DURR(عبد الله بن الشخير); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن الشخير)]

## 102:1/q21 How does al-Takāthur connect with al-Qāriʿah and nearby sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: The proposed al-Qāriʿah link is either thematic in the canonical order or also treated as a direct sequential explanation; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes chronological succession.
- 102:1/q21/p1 It follows al-Qāriʿah's account of the Hour by naming the worldly rivalry that causes ruin and distracts from preparing for it. — Jishumī says the preceding chapter ended with disbelievers in Hell; al-Biqāʿī says this sūra identifies their cause of misery, and Ibn al-Zubayr says rivalry in kin and numbers diverts from al-Qāriʿah's terrors. Islāḥī pairs the two around the afterlife's scale of good deeds versus worldly treasure. [15 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(النيسابوري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 102:1/q21/p2 The link to al-Qāriʿah is thematic in muṣḥaf order, not a chronological succession. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Nīsābūrī's link but rejects chronological succession because Takāthur precedes Qāriʿah by thirteen sūras in revelation; she allows shared warning tone. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(النيسابوري)]
- 102:1/q21/p3 The following al-ʿAṣr recalls time wasted by striving for increase. — Study Quran says time is one of the blessings for which people will be questioned, connecting 102:1 and 102:8 with the following sūra. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 102:1/q21/p4 An extended sequence links accumulation to slander, mockery, and the pride associated with the Elephant. — Ibn al-Zubayr, as cited by al-Biqāʿī, connects 102:1, 104:1, and 105:1. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 102:1/q22 What is the proper lesson and practice of visiting graves in relation to this rebuke?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether literal visitation serves remembrance and reform or becomes the very boastful practice condemned by the passage.
- 102:1/q22/p1 A grave visit should awaken reflection on death and judgment, prayer to God, and resolve for good deeds. — Elmalılı cites the hadith that visiting graves reminds of the afterlife. He describes greeting the dead, praying to Allah for living and dead, reciting Qurʾān and dedicating reward, and reflecting on resurrection, scales and destinations (101:4–11); he compares a visit to reading a book and learning from the deceased's good example. Ibn ʿĀbidīn says benefit varies with visitors' insight. [10 notes: ELMALILI(İbn Âbidîn)]
- 102:1/q22/p2 Using graves to boast of ancestors defeats their lesson and is rebuked. — Elmalılı says the dead's deeds are theirs and ours ours (2:141), so competing over them is pointless. Love of the good should follow God and the Prophet (2:165, 3:31); a person rises with those loved, but worship and aid are sought from Allah, not the dead. He prefers reading the passage as rebuke rather than encouragement of literal visiting. [8 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]

## 102:1/q23 How does the opening warning return at the sūra's end?  [* · links]
- 102:1/q23/p1 Questioning over blessings exposes the cost of indulgence and calls for gratitude. — Al-Biqāʿī says even lawful pleasure is restrained by knowing the majestic King will question it, more so disliked or forbidden pleasure. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says ingratitude leads to loss and punishment; Study Quran says verses 3–8 promise certain knowledge of Hellfire. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 102:1/q23/p2 The warning can be illustrated through five ordinary blessings. — The Ibn ʿAbbās report in al-Thaʿlabī names a full stomach, cold drink, sleep, shade, and sound constitution after discussing wrongful accumulation, graves, and judgment. [1 notes: THALABI(النبي)]

## 102:1/q24 What does the sūra's basmala contribute to its theme?  [* · links]
- 102:1/q24/p1 It presents divine majesty, general provision and guidance, and completed mercy for those God loves. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this reading in both transmitted segments. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 102:1/q25 How does takāthur relate to God's nature?  [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · theology]
- 102:1/q25/p1 Increase and decrease do not apply to God, whom no number or place circumscribes. — Al-Qushayrī says God's holy name precedes time and cannot be spatially limited; the incapacity of number to encompass Him excludes numerical change. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 102:1/q26 What other uses of the l-h-w word family are noted?  [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · meaning]
turns on: These are distinct lexical uses of related forms, rather than competing glosses of the verse's verb.
- 102:1/q26/p1 The root is illustrated by stopping a conversation when thunder is heard. — Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī tell this of Ibn al-Zubayr; Rāzī names al-Zubayr and glosses it as abandoning conversation. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الزبير); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI(الزبير)]
- 102:1/q26/p2 Lahwa can denote a miller's handful or a gift by analogy. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports the noun and plural lahā; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ suggests the miller's occupied hand as an image of continuing diversion. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BINTSHATI]
- 102:1/q26/p3 Lahāt denotes a fleshy part by the throat or, alternatively, the back of the mouth. — Ibn al-Jawzī records the two anatomical glosses and plural lahā. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 102:1/q27 What unrelated lexical gloss is preserved with these notes?  [* · meaning]
