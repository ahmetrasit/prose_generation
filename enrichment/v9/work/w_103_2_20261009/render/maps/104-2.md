# 104:2: 22 questions

## 104:2/q01 Who is the one described by ٱلَّذِى?  [ٱلَّذِى · referent]
turns on: Whether named occasion reports identify the subject or the description applies to a recurring type; Quṭb doubts reports naming individuals.
- 104:2/q01/p1 The same slanderer and mocker condemned in the preceding verse, represented by each individual in that class. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the singular relative describes each individual; the later plural points to the group. Quṭb calls it a recurring type from the early mission. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls him the slanderer. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 104:2/q01/p2 A specific wealthy mocker associated with an early incident, identified in some reports as al-Walīd or al-Akhnas. — Muqātil connects him to 74:11–13, his Meccan and Taif gardens and seven sons; al-Ālūsī cites reports of al-Akhnas's fortune as four or ten thousand dinars. Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions reports that known Muslim defamers occasioned the warning. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNASHUR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL-]

## 104:2/q02 How is ٱلَّذِى connected grammatically to the preceding verse?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Case and whether definiteness allows an adjectival link to kull; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī challenge the adjective analysis.
- 104:2/q02/p1 It is a genitive appositive to kull or the preceding condemned person. — Al-Nahhās, al-Tabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give this parsing. Al-Samīn rejects treating it as an adjective or explanatory apposition because definiteness differs. Bursawī also denies adjective agreement. [15 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 104:2/q02/p2 It is accusative by an implied expression of censure or clarification. — Al-Naḥḥās says 'I mean'; al-Kashshāf and others call it accusative censure. [14 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 104:2/q02/p3 It is nominative with an implied subject or as a disjoined expression of blame. — Al-Naḥḥās supplies 'he is'; al-Samīn calls it disjunction; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī call it nominative dispraise. [7 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 104:2/q02/p4 It is a partitive appositive to kull. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as an alternative to full apposition. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 104:2/q02/p5 It is another adjective describing the same slanderer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the unlinked descriptions in 68:10–13. Al-Tabrisī mentions a codex variant; al-Jarbardī appeals to kull's definiteness and 50:21. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; TABRISI; ALUSI(الجاربردي); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-; BURSEVI-]

## 104:2/q03 What does the root of جَمَعَ convey?  [جَمَعَ · meaning]
- 104:2/q03/p1 It brings dispersed or varied things together, evoking massing, mingling, or assembly. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys eighteen simple-verb occurrences and notes no Qur'anic derivative of its doubled form; thirteen concern God gathering people, bones or humanity for Judgment. She cites prayer, the Day of Gathering, goods, troops, weapons, and 4:23. [6 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 104:2/q05 What counts as مَالًۭا here?  [مَالًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is being defined by general usage or the man's reported holdings.
- 104:2/q05/p1 Acquired resources for needs, with the familiar form varying by livelihood: camels, gardens and palms, or trade money. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Zuhayr on camels as blood money, Abū Hurayra and Abū Ṭalḥa on Ansārī gardens and palms, the Prophet's question to al-ʿAbbās on Meccan cash, and earlier discussion at 3:92. [8 notes: IBNASHUR(أبو طلحة,أبو هريرة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 104:2/q05/p2 The man’s possessions include gold, silver, lands, gardens, slaves, servants and livestock. — Muqātil's narrative lists the inventoried property, preparation to purchase servants and livestock, and gardens in Mecca and Taif bearing fruit in winter and summer. [3 notes: MUQATIL]

## 104:2/q06 Why is مَالًۭا indefinite?  [مَالًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indefiniteness enlarges the imagined amount or diminishes its worth.
- 104:2/q06/p1 It magnifies the amount or the wealth's corrupt, vile character. — Al-Rāzī gives the latter account; al-Ālūsī gives magnification and cites competing reports that al-Akhnas had four or ten thousand dinars. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 104:2/q06/p2 It belittles an individual's wealth as trivial before God or beside all worldly wealth. — Al-Rāzī invokes 18:46 and the whole world's wealth; al-Ālūsī calls it contemptible in God's sight; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it serves only brief bodily needs. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 104:2/q07 How is وَعَدَّدَهُۥ read?  [وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Regional reading consensus and the written two dāls versus reports of lightening and whether the light form is a noun or exceptional verb.
- 104:2/q07/p1 The established or majority reading doubles the dāl, ʿaddadahu. — Al-Ṭabarī says all regional readers agree and rejects a weak early alternative for opposing that consensus. Al-Biqāʿī says undoubled counting can mean only a few things; doubled form emphasizes repetition or abundance. Al-Bayḍāwī notes a separated doubling as support. [21 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي والحسن وابن يعمر); QURTUBI(الحسن ونصر بن عاصم وأبو العالية); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ونصر بن عاصم وأبو العالية); BAYDAWI(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي,قُرئ); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI+; TAB-FULL(قراء الأمصار)+; TAB+]
- 104:2/q07/p2 A minority reading lightens the dāl; it can be parsed as a noun for number or an unusual separated verb meaning counted. — Attributed variously to al-Ḥasan, al-Kalbī, Naṣr ibn ʿĀṣim, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Ibn Yaʿmar and al-Sulamī. ## 104:2/q08 What does وَعَدَّدَهُۥ mean?  [وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ʿaddadahu is intensive counting, provision from ʿuddah, increase in amount or kinds, or a coordinated noun for supporters; some explanations add heirs or boasting.
- 104:2/q08/p1 He counted and repeatedly inventoried his wealth, often guarding the amount from decrease. — Al-Akhfash derives it from ʿidda; al-Farrāʾ, al-Suddī and many others gloss tallying. Mujāhid specifies types; ʿIkrima counts enough for years. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says guarding the sum prevented charity. Al-Suddī pictures saying 'this is mine' by day and hiding it at night; Ibn Kathīr cites 70:18. Al-Ālūsī says love drove repetition; al-Iṣlāḥī adds business forecasts and investment calculations. Al-Dāmghānī gives ʿidda the sense of number or count and illustrates it with months, sleepers, possessions, and people. [64 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; TAB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI(السدي,عكرمة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,عكرمة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,عكرمة); RAZI(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور,الحسن والكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن والكلبي); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR(السدي وابن جرير); DURR(السدي); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن والكلبي,قيل); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(محمد بن إسحاق); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; DAMGHANI; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNATIYYA+; TAB-FULL(قراء الأمصار)+; TAB+]
- 104:2/q08/p2 He stored or prepared wealth as a reserve for calamities or future need. — Muqātil invokes provision and storage; al-Naẓm derives ʿaddadahu from ʿuddah. ## 104:2/q09 What is the force of counting the wealth?  [وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether attention falls on the hoarder's attachment and fear of loss or on the error of treating wealth as protection.
- 104:2/q09/p1 Repeated counting shows possessive love, obsessive calculation, and fear that the hoard will diminish. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he guards the sum and withholds charity; al-Suddī depicts daytime preoccupation and nighttime concealment; Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb depicts daylong piling and corpse-like sleep. Al-Ālūsī, Quṭb, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Iṣlāḥī stress pleasure or anxious calculation. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR(محمد بن كعب); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 104:2/q09/p2 Counting signals ignorance: wealth cannot protect against the calamities that divine wisdom uses to disperse it. — Al-Qāshānī and Bursawī connect counting with ignorance; Bursawī adds that accumulating reflects appetitive power. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 104:2/q10 What does this description condemn?  [ٱلَّذِى جَمَعَ مَالًۭا وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Which moral consequence of accumulation is foregrounded: unpaid duties, social arrogance, false security, or the warning's response.
- 104:2/q10/p1 Hoarding wealth, withholding its due and refusing charitable spending or obedience. — Al-Ṭabarī says he fails to spend in God's path or pay God's due. Al-Qurṭubī invokes 50:25 or 68:12 and 70:18; Ibn Kathīr invokes 70:18. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the stingy mock people pressing God's and others' rights and compares 9:79's mockery of poor donors. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Ṭabarī's reading. [20 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(السدي وابن جرير); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI(محمد بن إسحاق); STUDYQURAN]
- 104:2/q10/p2 Admiration of amassed wealth makes the slanderer imagine himself superior and belittle others. — Al-Rāzī and Bursawī explain why this vice is singled out. Al-Biqāʿī says a culture that boasts of profit calls it success, while the verse exposes his loss; Ibn al-Zubayr links his imagined completeness and faultfinding to 103:2. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links perceived self-sufficiency and tyranny to 96:6–7; Quṭb portrays a recurring contemptuous type. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 104:2/q10/p3 Trust in wealth to ward off death, judgment or hardship is delusion. — Quṭb says the hoarder thinks money secures life and evades God's decree; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says brief bodily needs and endless hope drive the illusion. Corpus Coranicum contrasts this hoarding with 90:6's showy spending, both seeking self-made immortality. Asad reads it as a safeguard. [6 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); CORPUSCORANICUM; QASHANI]
- 104:2/q10/p4 The obsession and ridicule reveal a degraded character whom the warning and punishment answer. — Quṭb says the warning defends believers and pairs arrogance with the crushing Fire, reaching the heart, enclosure and pillars; harsh sound and an awe-inspiring question intensify it. He sees the Qur'an strengthening believers and striking opponents. [6 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 104:2/q11 What inward lesson is drawn from collecting and counting wealth?  [ٱلَّذِى جَمَعَ مَالًۭا وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · inward]
turns on: The notes draw different spiritual lessons from wealth dependence and counting.
- 104:2/q11/p1 Wealth, status, kin or even obedience confer no true richness; relying on God alone does. — Al-Sulamī's anonymous sayings call the money-rich poor, status-rich contemptible, obedience-rich bankrupt and kin-rich lowly. Al-Qushayrī repeats dependence on wealth, rank and works as poverty or bankruptcy and says reliance on God gives nobility. [3 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI]
- 104:2/q11/p2 Accumulating, loving and hoarding wealth reveal spiritual ignorance, hypocrisy and unbelief. — An anonymous saying reported by al-Sulamī assigns these three diagnoses respectively; al-Tustarī says the miser makes wealth a servant of worldly life. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); TUSTARI]
- 104:2/q11/p3 Ignorance is the root vice, and entrenched immersion in it leads to punishment that overwhelms the heart. — Al-Qāshānī calls ignorance a vice of the royal faculty and says the immersed merit eternal punishment; he also says counting wealth falsely assumes it can withstand calamity. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 104:2/q12 What episode is reported behind the counting and condemnation?  [ٱلَّذِى جَمَعَ مَالًۭا وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · reports]
turns on: These are separate reported encounters; the notes do not say they describe the same event.
- 104:2/q12/p1 The man boasts that his wealth cannot be exhausted, is warned its Giver can take it, inventories it, then boasts to Muhammad; the sūra is revealed. — Muqātil reports the oath to distribute wealth through Quraysh, the warning of poverty, the inventory of gold, silver, land, gardens and slaves, and a boast that even as bread it would not run out. [4 notes: MUQATIL]
- 104:2/q12/p2 Al-Ḥasan rebukes a wealthy visitor who saves thousands for hardship rather than helping others. — Al-Kashshāf reports al-Ḥasan asking why the money ransoms no one and is given to no generous person; the visitor says it is for hardship, and al-Ḥasan predicts an ungrateful heir and an unforgiving Lord. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن)]

## 104:2/q13 How large was al-Akhnas's fortune in the reports?  [مَالًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports give different numbers without a stated criterion for choosing one.
- 104:2/q13/p1 Four thousand dinars. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī preserve this report as one of two amounts. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 104:2/q13/p2 Ten thousand dinars. — The same sources give this competing figure. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 104:2/q14 How does this verse connect to surrounding passages and Qur'anic themes?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes compare the verse with different neighboring and broader scriptural passages.
- 104:2/q14/p1 The wealth hoarder contrasts with al-ʿAṣr's call to truth, patience and recognition of human loss. — Al-Biqāʿī's Ibn al-Zubayr cites 103:2; al-Iṣlāḥī cites 103:3 and says hoarders taunt those who press duties. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 104:2/q14/p2 Other Qur'anic passages condemn hoarding, withholding and mockery of givers. — Al-Qurṭubī cites 50:25 or 68:12 and 70:18; Ibn Kathīr cites 70:18; Study Quran cites 9:34–35's branding of hoarded gold and silver. Al-Iṣlāḥī reads 9:79 as hypocrites mocking poor donors while giving nothing. [8 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(السدي وابن جرير); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 104:2/q14/p3 The sūra's woe and vice catalogue resemble other Qur'anic warnings and prophetic woe speeches. — Corpus Coranicum cites 107:4–7, 83:1–6, 83:10–11, 52:11–12, 51:60 and Micah 2:1–3, highlighting the wealth theme and rhetorical question in 83:1–6. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 104:2/q14/p4 The sūra condemns the hoarder in a Meccan nine-verse warning. — Quṭb identifies its place and length; Study Quran states its focus on torment for those who hoard in worldly life. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]

## 104:2/q15 Why render the perfect verbs as present actions?  [جَمَعَ وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · grammar]
- 104:2/q15/p1 Their perfect form is aspectual and describes a continuing type, warranting a German present tense. — Corpus Coranicum cites 96:1–2 for comparable aspectual use. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 104:2/q16 Why might a translation repeat the opening woe at this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 104:2/q16/p1 It treats the attitude in verses 2–3 as a separate blameworthy object beside the two kinds named in verse 1. — Asad explains his inserted repetition on this basis. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 104:2/q17 What is reported about the seven named readings and their preservation?  [* · sciences]
- 104:2/q17/p1 The seven named readings are sound choices of reciters within the ʿUthmānic codex tradition, not the Companions' seven aḥruf. — Ibn Kathīr transmits al-Qurṭubī citing al-Dāwūdī and Ibn Abī Ṣafra; reciters allowed one another's readings, choosing what seemed apt, and regional Muslims relied on soundly transmitted written documentation, fulfilling preservation. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(القرطبي)]

## 104:2/q18 Who was Abū Zayd named among the collectors of the Qur'an?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Anas's kinship and tribal claim and whether the named collector appears separately in al-Shaʿbī's list; Ibn Ḥajar finds an explicit report for Qays ibn al-Sakan.
- 104:2/q18/p1 Qays ibn al-Sakan of Banū ʿAdī ibn al-Najjār. — Ibn Ḥajar prefers the Ibn Abī Dāwūd report; Anas calls him his maternal uncle, says he died without descendants and Anas inherited him. Ibn Abī Dāwūd gives his lineage, ʿAqaba and Badr participation, and near-Prophetic death. [3 notes: ITQAN(أنس,ابن أبي داود); ITQAN(ابن حجر)+]
- 104:2/q18/p2 Saʿd ibn ʿUbayd ibn al-Nuʿmān of Banū ʿAmr ibn ʿAwf. — Al-ʿAskarī says Saʿd was the only Aws collector; Ibn Ḥabīb lists him as collecting in the Prophet's life. The identification is challenged because Anas was Khazrajī, called Abū Zayd an uncle, and al-Shaʿbī listed Saʿd separately. [3 notes: ITQAN(أبو أحمد العسكري,ابن حبيب); ITQAN-]
- 104:2/q18/p3 Qays ibn Abī Saʿṣaʿa, a Khazrajī also called Abū Zayd. — Ibn Ḥajar offers this possibility because it fits Anas's kin claim. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حجر)]
- 104:2/q18/p4 Saʿd ibn al-Mundhir ibn Aws ibn Zuhayr, another Khazrajī collector. — Ibn Ḥajar names him but finds no explicit evidence of the kunya Abū Zayd. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حجر)]
- 104:2/q18/p5 Thābit, Aws or Muʿādh. — Al-Suyūṭī lists these further proposed names without grounds. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 104:2/q19 When were Qur'anic verses and surahs divided and ordered?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the complete surah sequence was fixed by revelation or partly arranged during codex production; the notes distinguish verse placement from surah order.
- 104:2/q19/p1 Surahs and verse placement were established during the Prophet's lifetime. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports instructions to place a revealed verse in a named surah; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites known surah sizes, prophetic prayer recitations and marriage for memorized surahs. Al-Dānī says Gabriel showed placements, and Zayd reports arranging sheets before the Prophet, though al-Bayhaqī restricts this to verses. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,الداني,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,زيد بن ثابت)]
- 104:2/q19/p2 The Prophet fixed the order of surahs. — Al-Bāqillānī lists this possibility; al-Dānī attributes verse and surah placement to Gabriel. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو بكر الباقلاني,الداني)]
- 104:2/q19/p3 Some surah order was set in the Prophet's life, with remaining order fixed or preserved by Zayd and ʿUthmān. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits al-Bāqillānī's strong view of Zayd and ʿUthmān; Ibn ʿĀshūr finds long opening surahs and Mufaṣṣal already placed, the rest uncertain. Zayd and ʿUthmān sought to preserve known prophetic recitation. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو بكر الباقلاني,ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 104:2/q20 How was the Qur'an collected into sheets and a codex?  [* · sciences]
- 104:2/q20/p1 Abū Bakr collected separate sheets, which passed to ʿUmar and Ḥafṣa; ʿUthmān made a codex and sent copies. — Ibn ʿUmar gives the succession of sheets; a report through ʿUmāra ibn Ghaziyya says Zayd wrote on leather and palm stalks under Abū Bakr and joined them under ʿUmar. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the name 'sheets' and later provincial copies; al-Bukhārī's al-Fatḥ judges a single codex under Abū Bakr sounder. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عمر,زيد بن ثابت)]

## 104:2/q21 Why divide a book into surahs?  [* · rhetoric]
- 104:2/q21/p1 Division is more elegant and renews the reader's energy by marking progress. — Ibn ʿĀshūr transmits al-Zamakhsharī's analogy of a traveler passing a mile or league and cites 2:23. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]

## 104:2/q22 Who else may gather around and count the wealth?  [جَمَعَ وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · referent]
- 104:2/q22/p1 The slanderer's poorer companions may gather around him and count his wealth while aspiring to acquire their own through slander. — Elmalılı describes many in the humaza-lumaza circle as owning little, clustering around the wealthy man and counting his assets. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
