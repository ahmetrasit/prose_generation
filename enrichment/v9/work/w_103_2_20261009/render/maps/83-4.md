# 83:4: 21 questions

## 83:4/q01 How is ألا parsed?  [أَلَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the opening is an exhortative particle or a question formed with hamza and negative lā.
- 83:4/q01/p1 An interrogative hamza followed by negative lā. — Bursevī says an attention particle could be removed without changing the meaning, though the question may also urge thought. Ālūsī reads the hamza as denial and astonishment, not an introductory or attention particle. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 83:4/q01/p2 An exhortative particle urging them to consider. — Al-Samīn calls this the apparent reading and takes ẓann as certainty. Bursevī also allows an urging force while parsing hamza with negation. [2 notes: BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR+; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 83:4/q02 What force does the question have?  [أَلَا يَظُنُّ · rhetoric]
- 83:4/q02/p1 It rebukes and expresses astonishment that the cheaters behave without regard for resurrection and judgment. — Thalabī says they should know they will stand before the Lord. Qurṭubī says they act as though accountability will not occur. Ālūsī says the renewed question magnifies their fraud. Ibn ʿĀshūr says sincere Muslims ceased cheating while hypocrites persisted. Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī stress astonishment; Iṣlāḥī says human nature attests the great day. [8 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 83:4/q03 Who are أولئك?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the rebuked traders are identified generally, as believers or hypocrites in Medina, or as disbelievers.
- 83:4/q03/p1 The people who cheat others in measure and weight. — Muqātil identifies the defrauders; Ālūsī says the demonstrative highlights their defining vice. Ibn ʿĀshūr says their trait is the ground of the rebuke. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the warning also applies to the rest of the community. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 83:4/q03/p2 Believers in the Medinan community, possibly the Aws and Khazraj; the conduct persisted among hypocrites. — Rāzī offers believing Aws and Khazraj, whose existing belief the Prophet recalls. Ibn ʿAṭiyya takes the resurrection rebuke as supporting a Medinan setting involving believers. Ibn ʿĀshūr says sincere Muslims stopped the practice, whereas hypocrites did not. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 83:4/q03/p3 Disbelievers whose conduct evades the judgment their nature should make them fear. — Iṣlāḥī calls their attitude astonishing and says the great day is attested by human nature. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:4/q04 What does the distant demonstrative add?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · rhetoric]
- 83:4/q04/p1 It singles out the cheaters for blame and marks their moral distance. — Ālūsī says their vice sets them apart and their distance suggests remoteness from goodness and corruption. Ibn ʿĀshūr says their described trait supplies the reason for public blame. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the distance is from God's mercy. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 83:4/q05 What does يظن mean here?  [يَظُنُّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the context requires certainty, retains ordinary expectation sufficient for admonition, or permits a range keyed to evidence and the following particle.
- 83:4/q05/p1 To know or believe with certainty. — Thalabī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī state this sense. Rāzī says inferential knowledge may be called ẓann because its evidence makes one outcome more probable, unlike equal-sided doubt. Ibn Mālik restricts ẓann to certainty. Zarkashī says a kernel of certainty distinguishes it from ignorance; he cites 2:46, 69:20 and 75:28, and says praiseworthy ẓann is certain. He takes emphatic anna here and at 69:20 and 84:3 as a sign of certainty, contrasting light inna at 2:230 and 48:12 and ʿalima anna with ḥasibū allā at 8:66, 47:19 and 5:71. He says 9:118 is no counterexample because inna attaches to the verb, and explains 60:10 by taking ʿilm literally and īmān as verbal affirmation. Kashshāf reports the view but prefers an earlier interpretation not specified in these notes. [18 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(أبو الفتح,ابن مالك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 83:4/q05/p2 Its ordinary sense of expecting or supposing: even the possibility of resurrection should deter wrongdoing. — Rāzī says God's wisdom and mercy make even suspicion of resurrection enough to cause fear. Qurṭubī urges doubters to consider and investigate cautiously. Bayḍāwī and Kuran Yolu say belief, and even considering it possible, would restrain fraud. Abū Ḥayyān allows ordinary expectation. Abū al-Fatḥ and Zarkashī favor this sense because merely imagining the great resurrection should deter sin, still more certainty. Bursevī says weak belief heightens the warning though believers need certainty. Ālūsī keeps the ordinary sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines it as preponderant belief, citing 45:32. Biqāʿī says even suspicion should restrain, given decisive proofs. Other scholars allow ẓann to mean doubt, citing 60:10. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(أبو الفتح,غيره); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURHAN+]
- 83:4/q05/p3 A broad term whose force ranges from weak supposition to knowledge as its evidence strengthens. — Al-Rāghib says it rests on an indicator: strong evidence reaches knowledge, weak evidence leaves supposition. He says the strong sense takes emphatic or light an, while weak ẓann takes in with an imperfect; the former is praiseworthy and the latter blameworthy. Zarkashī gives the corresponding contextual and particle tests. [5 notes: BURHAN(الراغب)]

## 83:4/q06 How does the أنهم clause function syntactically?  [يَظُنُّ أَنَّهُم مَّبْعُوثُونَ · grammar]
- 83:4/q06/p1 The clause introduced by anna and its governed words fills both object positions of yaẓunnu. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the two-object analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 83:4/q07 What resurrection does مبعوثون announce?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · interpretation]
- 83:4/q07/p1 They will be raised for the great Day of gathering, reckoning, and recompense before God. — Muqātil identifies a tremendous Day; Zajjāj and Baghawī identify the Day of Resurrection. Sulamī says they return with their deeds. Thalabī adds that people stand before the Lord of the worlds. Abū Ḥayyān allows being raised on the Day or for its reckoning. Biqāʿī likens resurrection to a compelled awakening from sleep and says only God knows its full reality. Asad says wrongdoers are called to account before the Sustainer; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they are repaid. Qāshānī says inner virtues and vices become visible then, so they cannot be feigned or hidden. [11 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; SULAMI; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:4/q08 Why invoke resurrection against fraud?  [يَظُنُّ مَّبْعُوثُونَ · interpretation]
- 83:4/q08/p1 Awareness of judgment, even as a possibility, should stop fraud; continued sin behaves as disbelief in reckoning. — Zajjāj, reported also by Ibn al-Jawzī, says belief would prevent reducing measure and weight. Abū Ḥafṣ says a person who knows of judgment yet persists acts as if unbelieving. Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī and Kuran Yolu make the same deterrence point. Biqāʿī connects habitual fraud to feeling safe from punishment and denying accounting. Bursevī says even weak supposition should deter, though believers need certainty. Iṣlāḥī says the cheats demand fairness for themselves yet deny it to others. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(أبو حفص); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 83:4/q09 How wide is the warning against injustice?  [* · interpretation]
- 83:4/q09/p1 Even tiny fraud and injustice in any dealing matter, because God judges every wrong precisely. — Rāzī says judgment vindicates even a person wronged by a tiny amount, demonstrating precise divine knowledge and justice. Qurṭubī applies the threat to rulers: an Arab rebuked ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Marwān for taking Muslim property without measure. He extends the gravity to all dealings and acts. Abū Ḥayyān points to the rebuke, great Day, humbled standing, and Lordship wording. Ibn al-Zubayr, as reported by Biqāʿī, connects the record of every deed at 82:10 and exact weighing at 21:47. Bursevī mentions account for even a speck or mustard seed. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BURSEVI]

## 83:4/q10 What grounds are offered for resurrection and judgment?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the inference rests on the need for just recompense and human moral nature or on God's wisdom and mercy opposing neglect.
- 83:4/q10/p1 Moral recompense and divine justice require a final judgment of good and evil. — Rāzī says even deniers can infer resurrection from the need, or at least possibility, of recompense. Iṣlāḥī says a just God gave people a love of justice and a nature that distinguishes good from evil; righteous and wrongdoers therefore require different treatment. He points to his discussion of 75:14–15 and says deniers wish to evade this outcome. He also says the title Lord of the worlds argues for the Day's majesty and necessity. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:4/q10/p2 God's wisdom and mercy make posthumous neglect implausible and warrant at least fear of resurrection. — Rāzī argues that even without certainty one should suspect resurrection because God would not abandon people after death. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 83:4/q11 How is the following Day attached to being raised?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the later day expression is governed directly by 'raised' or apposed to 'great Day'.
- 83:4/q11/p1 The following 'day people stand' is an accusative time expression governed by 'raised'. — Ṭabrisī says it names the day on which they are resurrected. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 83:4/q11/p2 It is a substitute for the preceding 'great Day'. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Farrāʾ's analysis of yawm as a substitute for yawm ʿaẓīm. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)]

## 83:4/q12 What readings of the following day are reported?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · readings]
turns on: The case of the word for day: genitive dependence after li-yawm or a new nominative predication.
- 83:4/q12/p1 A transmitted reading puts 'day' in the genitive after 'for a day'. — Abū Ḥayyān reports it alongside al-Farrāʾ's appositional analysis. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)]
- 83:4/q12/p2 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads 'day' in the nominative, meaning 'that is a day'. — Abū Ḥayyān transmits this reading and its implied predication. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)]

## 83:4/q13 What does standing before the Lord mean?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether standing denotes judgment, bodily rising from graves, or exclusive worship and obedience.
- 83:4/q13/p1 Standing for judgment before God. — Rāzī calls this the soundest explanation; even trivial fraud then appears as a great accumulated wrong, and he cites 55:46. Asad says the wrongdoers face accountability before the Sustainer. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 83:4/q13/p2 Bodies rise from their graves when God restores souls to them. — Rāzī reports this as another explanation of standing. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 83:4/q13/p3 Standing in worship and obedience solely for God. — Rāzī reports Abū Muslim's explanation, comparing 'stand for God devoutly' at 2:238 and citing 82:19. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 83:4/q14 What do reports say about the length of standing?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different durations, and Ibn ʿAbbās specifies believers.
- 83:4/q14/p1 The people stand for three hundred worldly years without an order. — Rāzī transmits this as a Prophetic report. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي)]
- 83:4/q14/p2 They stand for forty years and then are addressed. — Rāzī transmits this from Ibn Masʿūd. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 83:4/q14/p3 For believers the standing lasts about the time needed to finish a prayer. — Rāzī transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās; it explicitly concerns believers. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 83:4/q15 What physical state is reported while people stand?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · reports]
- 83:4/q15/p1 Each person stands submerged in sweat up to half the ears. — Rāzī reports Ibn ʿUmar quoting the Prophet. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي)]

## 83:4/q16 What happened when Ibn ʿUmar recited the standing verse?  [مَّبْعُوثُونَ · reports]
- 83:4/q16/p1 He wept so intensely that he could not continue reciting. — Thalabī adds that he fell; Rāzī also reports the weeping and inability to continue. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عمر); RAZI-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 83:4/q17 Where are the opening verses placed in revelation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the passage or the whole sūrah is dated to Medina, Mecca, or both.
- 83:4/q17/p1 The opening warning has a Medinan setting, with one account placing the first four verses just after the Prophet arrived. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya infers a Medinan setting involving believers from the resurrection rebuke. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the address as to Muslims, distinguishing sincere Muslims from hypocrites. Asad cites traditions mentioned by Ṭabarī, Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr for the first four verses soon after arrival. Study Quran reports an account putting verses 5–28 in Medina and the last eight among the last Makkan revelations. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Bg, IK, Ṭ)]
- 83:4/q17/p2 The sūrah may be late Makkan, early Medinan, or combine both periods. — Study Quran states these alternatives without choosing among them. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 83:4/q18 How does this warning connect with earlier passages?  [* · links]
- 83:4/q18/p1 It develops al-Infiṭār's theme of moral accounting and the division of righteous and defiant people. — Iṣlāḥī says al-Infiṭār reasons from divine attributes shown in the human body, while this sūrah reasons from human moral nature. Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqāʿī, links fraud's warning to the record of every deed in 82:10 and the exact weighing of deeds in 21:47: small fraud is a major crime. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 83:4/q19 What inward application is drawn from the warning?  [* · inward]
turns on: The two inward readings apply the warning to different hidden realities: unveiled character at judgment or the self's relation to the spirit.
- 83:4/q19/p1 At judgment hidden virtues and vices become visible, exposing any feigned goodness. — Qāshānī says the failure to expect resurrection leaves people unprepared for this unveiling and its shame and consequence. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 83:4/q19/p2 The limited self shortchanges the spirit in essential monotheism, though formal monotheism is its due. — Bursevī says essential tawḥīd has no counterpart and should not be weighed; the self cannot fully provide it because of its limited capacity. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 83:4/q20 What does the surrounding rebuke particle كلا do?  [* · rhetoric]
- 83:4/q20/p1 It rebukes the cheats for their conduct. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly gives this function. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 83:4/q21 What force does the cited Qur'anic no-oath formula have?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether lā cancels the oath or denies the need to swear.
- 83:4/q21/p1 It denies God's need to swear and thereby confirms the message through its evident context. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares ordinary Arabic 'do not swear', 'I need no oath', and 'I need not urge you', which strengthen assurance or instruction. She says her study retains the early generations' exegetical service as a resource for later generations. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 83:4/q21/p2 The lā negates the oath itself. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this interpretation from some scholars and rejects it in favor of denying the need for an oath. [0 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(بعضهم)-]
