# 55:7: 11 questions

## 55:7/q01 How is السماء read and parsed?  [ٱلسَّمَآءَ · readings]
turns on: Whether السماء is an object governed by an implicit verb or a nominal subject, and which earlier clause supplies the syntactic parallel.
- 55:7/q01/p1 It is accusative, governed by an understood verb explained by رفعها. — Al-Nahhas calls this coordination with a like implied verb. Ibn Jinni, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, and Abu Ali attach it to the nearby يسجدان clause; al-Alusi finds attachment to the larger nominal clause mismatched, while other grammarians allow that contrast and al-Tibi relates it to the sun and moon clause. Al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, and al-Alusi identify an elided governor; al-Samin calls it ishtighal and cites 36:39 and Sibawayh's allowance without a resumptive pronoun, against al-Akhfash's requirement. Al-Farra says almost all read without such a pronoun. Ibn Jinni argues against Abu al-Hasan's ban on coordination of unlike case positions: an unspoken case position has no force, as omitted pronouns and this accusative reading show. Al-Burhan gives this as a case of an omitted governor explained by an expressed verb and as verbal matching after the trees' prostration, comparing 5:6. Elmalılı says the fronted object is resumed by a pronoun. [18 notes: NAHHAS; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو السمال); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(ابن جني); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); ELMALILI]
- 55:7/q01/p2 It is nominative as the subject of a full nominal clause coordinated with a preceding nominal clause. — Abu al-Sammal transmits this noncanonical reading. Ibn Jinni prefers it because the whole nominal sentence matches والنجم والشجر يسجدان; al-Tabrisi reports his preference. Al-Razi relates it to the opening nominal sentence. Al-Alusi relates it to 55:6. Al-Zamakhshari and al-Baydawi also note the reading. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السمال); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو السمال)+; TABRISI(ابن جني)+]

## 55:7/q02 What does God's raising of the sky mean?  [ٱلسَّمَآءَ رَفَعَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether raising describes physical location, exalted rank, celestial order, or an inward ascent; several authors allow more than one sense.
- 55:7/q02/p1 God created the sky physically elevated above earth, supporting or holding it aloft. — Muqatil and those reported by al-Wahidi and al-Bursevi specify five hundred years' distance; al-Wahidi and al-Zajjaj say God prevents its fall. Al-Bursevi reports its formation from smoke above earth's water and roof function. Al-Alusi says it was high from creation, not first low and then lifted. Ibn Ashur says raising entails creation and means making it high without pillars, like a tailor making a pocket wide. Al-Tabataba'i says either the upper direction was made high or celestial bodies were positioned above earth after separation (21:30). Elmalılı calls heaven the encompassing cosmos; Kur'an Yolu calls it cosmic space with galaxies and ordered bodies. Islahi cites 31:10 and 67:3–4 for the columnless, faultless sky and describes invisible gravitational columns. Elmalılı's Turkish rendering calls the heaven beautiful. [23 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وغيره); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:7/q02/p2 Its elevation also signifies rank: the sky is the source of divine decrees and commands and abode of revelatory angels. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Baydawi, and al-Bursevi associate its height and firmness with God's majesty and sovereignty. Al-Alusi admits rank while calling physical elevation primary. Ibn Ashur says the wording admits literal and figurative senses. Al-Tabataba'i allows spiritual elevation if angelic stations and sources of revelation are included; Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu likewise allow a spiritual sense. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:7/q02/p3 The sky is an ordered celestial realm with fixed stars, horizons, seasons and measured rain. — Al-Qushayri adds protection from disorder. Al-Biqa'i reads its ordered seasons and precipitation as a display of justice sustaining earth. Islahi describes a faultless balanced sky and its invisible gravitational supports; Kur'an Yolu describes galaxies and bodies moving in order. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:7/q02/p4 In an inward reading, the sky is intellect raised toward spirit and heart. — Al-Qashani pairs this with justice lowered to self and body and with the order of human virtues. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:7/q03 What does the raised sky show or accomplish?  [ٱلسَّمَآءَ رَفَعَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw different lessons from the same elevation: divine power, care for life, and the just order of creation.
- 55:7/q03/p1 It displays God's power, mercy, majesty and claim to reverence. — Al-Tabrisi calls it a sign of perfect power; al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi, and Abu Hayyan of majesty and sovereignty. Elmalılı sees the Rahman's creative mercy and His exaltedness above directions and place, prompting prostration. Kur'an Yolu says either literal or spiritual reading shows that only God deserves worship. Ibn Ashur says the wording draws attention to marvelous creation and invites reflection. [8 notes: TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:7/q03/p2 It sustains creatures' lives and shields them. — Ibn al-Jawzi says the sky and wind between it and earth support breathing and refresh creation; without wind creatures would die in distress. Al-Bursevi reports the sky serving as a roof, and al-Zajjaj and al-Wahidi say it is held from falling. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وغيره)]
- 55:7/q03/p3 Its measured celestial order sustains the earth and manifests justice. — Al-Biqa'i cites seasons and measured precipitation; Islahi points to the sky's flawless balance and invisible gravitational columns, citing 31:10 and 67:3–4. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:7/q04 What is the balance God set?  [ٱلْمِيزَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mizan names the virtue of justice, a concrete weighing device, a wider standard, revealed guidance, judgment, cosmic equilibrium, or an inward or eschatological measure. Some authors deliberately combine senses.
- 55:7/q04/p1 It is justice: giving each creature or rights-holder what is due and ordering the world. — Mujahid, al-Farra, Ibn Qutayba, al-Tabari, and many later authors give this gloss; al-Zajjaj and al-Wahidi explain that equalizing things is a kind of weighing. Al-Mawardi cites Hassan's verse about a balance invoked when affairs are confused. Al-Baghawi cites the following ban on transgression; al-Wahidi's author of al-Nazm cites 55:9. Ibn Atiyya calls the familiar scale one instance of the broader metaphor. Al-Razi, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Ashur, and others compare scripture and balance in 57:25. Al-Baydawi, al-Bursevi, and al-Alusi stress each recipient's due and each right fulfilled; al-Biqa'i says this orders both cosmic realms. Al-Alusi prefers this sense for its breadth despite the following command's fit with physical scales. Ibn Ashur says mizan is literally an instrument but metaphorically justice; he and the Study Quran note justice across creation and exchange. [37 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(قيل); NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد والسدي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,صاحب النظم,عطاء عن ابن عباس,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون ومنهم مجاهد والسدي واللغويون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والطبري والأكثرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî ve İbn Atıyye); STUDYQURAN(Most commentators); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 55:7/q04/p2 It is a physical instrument, or class of instruments, for weighing and measuring fairly. — Al-Hasan, Qatada, and al-Dahhak repeatedly hold this view; al-Baghawi says its basic sense is measurement. Al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, and Abu Hayyan extend it to measures, steelyards, and gauges. Al-Wahidi's language scholars call it a blessing without which many rights could not be secured. Al-Razi says people resent tiny losses and would quarrel without transparent measures. Al-Alusi says this reading fits the next command and the contrast with heaven's height; the device governs judgments and fair giving and taking. Al-Kashifi gives established scales as one interpretation of وضع. Ibn Atiyya treats a scale as a concrete instance of justice. [24 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(غيره); THALABI(الحسن والضحاك وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الحسن وقتادة والضحاك,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 55:7/q04/p3 It is a broad standard of measure or judgment, including both material quantities and abstract matters. — Al-Tabataba'i explicitly prefers this over justice alone or scales alone and includes belief, speech, deeds and weights, comparing 57:25. Asad follows al-Zamakhshari in extending measuring to concrete and abstract means and links 42:17 and 57:25. Abu Hayyan combines measuring instruments with God's just standard for taking and giving. Ibn Ashur says mizan and nearby verbs, prepositions and qist sustain both concrete and figurative senses, a mark of Qur'anic inimitability. Al-Kashshaf includes every instrument for determining quantity. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); TABATABAI+]
- 55:7/q04/p4 It is the Qur'an, the standard that explains what people need. — Al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl gives this view and says weighing fundamentally involves estimation. Al-Biqa'i calls the Qur'an the balance for knowledge, alongside the scale for sensible things. The Study Quran associates the reading with 57:25. [6 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسين بن الفضل); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 55:7/q04/p5 It is a universal equilibrium law governing cosmic and other relations. — Elmalılı emphasizes gravity as its most visible form, underlying scales and weights and also extending to chemical and spiritual relations as justice. Kur'an Yolu says the first occurrence denotes the universal law that lets cosmic processes work as an ordered system. Islahi describes the sky's balance through invisible gravitational supports. [3 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:7/q04/p6 It is judgment. — Al-Mawardi offers this as an alternative to the rights-preserving scale; al-Qurtubi also reports it among other interpretations. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 55:7/q04/p7 It is divine commands and prohibitions governing bodily limbs. — Al-Tustari expressly gives this as the inner meaning. The Study Quran also reports religious law as a possible identification, without specifying limbs. [2 notes: TUSTARI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 55:7/q04/p8 It is the scale that weighs deeds in the afterlife. — Al-Qurtubi reports this as one of the other interpretations. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 55:7/q04/p9 It signifies honoring the balance and harmony of the created order. — The Study Quran offers this extension of the balance image. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 55:7/q05 What does God do by setting the balance?  [وَوَضَعَ ٱلْمِيزَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether setting names the institution of justice, provision or inspiration of tools, or establishment of an all-encompassing measure.
- 55:7/q05/p1 He institutes and commands justice so rights are fulfilled and the world remains ordered. — Al-Tabari places justice among earthly creatures; al-Wahidi's author of al-Nazm reads 55:9 as the resulting command. Al-Baydawi, al-Bursevi and al-Alusi explain giving every deserving one their due; Ibn Ashur reads waḍʿ metaphorically as appointing justice and cites Abu Talha's designation of beloved property as parallel usage. Al-Bursevi links the institution of measures to Noah's time, asserting that weights, measures, and cubits did not exist earlier. Al-Qashani turns the ordering inward toward self and body. Muqatil says it requires people not to cheat in measures. [15 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:7/q05/p2 He provides measuring instruments so people can settle disputes and exchange goods fairly. — Al-Zajjaj says the scale settles disputes. Al-Wahidi says it secures rights and is a blessing and guidance; al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi place instruments on earth for transactions, judgments and fair giving and taking. Al-Razi describes quarrels otherwise caused by small perceived losses. Al-Kashifi gives two nuances: God established actual scales, or inspired humans to make them. Al-Bursevi dates the start of measures to Noah's time. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الحسن وقتادة والضحاك,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:7/q05/p3 He establishes a measure for all things, including cosmic order and human life. — Asad explicitly describes measure for all things; Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu describe a universal equilibrium law. Al-Biqa'i relates balance to ordered cosmic realms; the Study Quran takes the phrase to honor created harmony. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 55:7/q06 What variant readings occur in ووضع الميزان?  [وَوَضَعَ ٱلْمِيزَانَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the wording uses the common past verb, Abdullah's 'lowered,' or Ibrahim's verbal noun with a genitive balance.
- 55:7/q06/p1 The common wording has the past verb 'He set' with الميزان as its object. — Al-Samin contrasts this with Ibrahim's genitive verbal noun. Al-Farra and al-Tabari say setting and lowering are close in Arabic; Ibn Atiyya glosses وضع as establishing. [4 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 55:7/q06/p2 ʿAbd Allah's reading has وخفض الميزان, 'and He lowered the balance.' — Al-Farra and al-Tabari say lowering and setting are near in meaning. Al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, and al-Alusi also transmit it; Ibn Atiyya locates it in Ibn Mas'ud's codex. [5 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مصحف ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:7/q06/p3 Ibrahim reads ووضع with silent ض and genitive الميزان, making وضع a verbal noun dependent on رفع. — Al-Samin explains this as 'raising the balance's setting,' conferring elevated status on taking rights. Abu Hayyan also records the genitive verbal noun against the majority past-verb parsing. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(إبراهيم)]

## 55:7/q07 Why is heaven's raising paired with setting the balance?  [ٱلسَّمَآءَ رَفَعَهَا وَوَضَعَ ٱلْمِيزَانَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pairing chiefly marks cosmic and social correspondence, verbal opposition, or the sequence from revelation to justice.
- 55:7/q07/p1 The pairing joins celestial and earthly order and dignifies justice by associating it with heaven. — Al-Razi pairs raised heaven with the earth placed in 55:10. Ibn Ashur says the pairing presents justice as truth and virtue sent from the heavenly realm; he cites 10:5, 15:85, and 44:38–39 for justice in creation. Al-Biqa'i says ordered seasons and rain show a balance spanning both realms. Al-Alusi notes the physical scale would make a pointed contrast with heaven's raising. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:7/q07/p2 The opposite verbs 'raised' and 'set' form antithetical wordplay while وضع suggests appointing justice. — Ibn Ashur also notes an echo of scripture and balance being appointed together in 57:25 and cites Abu Talha's use of وضع for assigning property to a purpose. Al-Alusi says the concrete balance makes the vertical contrast especially immediate. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:7/q07/p3 The paired acts link revelation or knowledge with the justice that orders human dealings. — Al-Razi moves from knowledge and the Qur'an as highest knowledge to justice and its tool, comparing 57:25. Abu Hayyan says the balance follows the Qur'an so people may learn and obey. Al-Biqa'i names Qur'an the balance of knowledge and scales the balance of sensible things. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:7/q08 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the local link is the paired signs, the moral commands following balance, or the passage's enumerative form.
- 55:7/q08/p1 It continues a sequence of paired heavenly and earthly signs before the earth is mentioned explicitly. — Al-Zamakhshari, as reported by al-Samin, pairs sun and moon as celestial, najm and tree as earthly, then heaven and earth; he says conjunctions resume where related clauses join and celestial order resembles submission. Al-Razi contrasts celestial signs before earthly ones and pairs raised heaven with placed earth at 55:10. Ibn Ashur says the passage moves from sun/moon and stars/trees to heaven/earth and resumes a verb predicated of its subject like the opening at 55:1–2. Al-Tabari's earlier debate over najm concerns low vegetation versus a star: Ibn Abbas, Sa'id, al-Suddi, and Sufyan favor plants; Mujahid and Qatada favor a star. Al-Tabari prefers plants, which pair with trunked trees; Ibn Abbas describes spreading growth, Sa'id cites ground-spreading couch grass, and Mujahid a sky star. Muqatil says low plants and trunked trees prostrate through their moving shadows. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد,مجاهد); MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 55:7/q08/p2 The following prohibitions and command to weigh equitably explain balance as a demand for justice in human dealings. — Al-Baghawi cites the ban on transgression; al-Wahidi's author of al-Nazm cites 55:9. Muqatil applies it to cheating in measures. Al-Alusi observes the next command suits a physical scale, while preferring justice as the broader sense. The Study Quran says the three repetitions of balance make justice fundamental in creation and transactions. Islahi says the first signs show God cherishes justice and rejects violations. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:7/q08/p3 The opening's enumeration of gifts explains the shifts in conjunction and the fronted sky. — Al-Zamakhshari, reported by al-Samin, says initial conjunctions are omitted to rebuke through enumeration, then return for connected pairs. Ibn Ashur says fronting heaven draws attention to reflecting on its creation. Elmalılı says the object is first withheld then resumed by the pronoun to emphasize raising. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 55:7/q09 What other passages or sayings illuminate this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the cross-reference illuminates the justice of balance or the creation and order of heaven.
- 55:7/q09/p1 Scripture and balance in 57:25 illuminate justice and measured conduct here. — Al-Razi, Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Ashur, al-Tabataba'i, Asad, and the Study Quran invoke 57:25; Asad also cites 42:17. Al-Razi and Abu Hayyan relate the order of knowledge or Qur'an followed by balance. Al-Biqa'i distinguishes the Qur'an's intellectual balance from the material scale. Ibn Ashur sees an echo of God's appointing both scripture and balance. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 55:7/q09/p2 Other creation verses elucidate the sky's elevation and ordered balance. — Al-Tabataba'i cites 21:30 for separation of celestial bodies. Islahi cites 31:10 and 67:3–4 for a sky without visible pillars or defects. Ibn Ashur cites 10:5, 15:85, and 44:38–39 for justice in creation. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:7/q10 What is reported about justice upholding heaven and earth?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported justice governs all creation, its inhabitants, or only human judgments; no grading is supplied in these notes.
- 55:7/q10/p1 A saying attributed to the Prophet states that heaven and earth stand by justice, encompassing the created order. — Al-Baydawi and al-Bursevi cite the saying; the Study Quran reports it without grading. Al-Alusi prefers its scope to include the whole created order, describing God's justice as giving each thing its created form and due. Ibn Ashur alludes to the same maxim when relating justice to creation. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Prophet); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 55:7/q10/p2 The saying concerns the inhabitants of heaven and earth. — Al-Alusi reports this limiting interpretation but prefers the whole created order. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:7/q10/p3 The saying concerns adjudication between people. — Al-Alusi reports and rejects this restriction, taking it instead as God's justice in creating and apportioning everything. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 55:7/q11 What does the concrete imagery of weighing illustrate about figurative expression?  [ٱلْمِيزَانَ · rhetoric]
- 55:7/q11/p1 A concrete image can convey an associated quality or effect through its characteristic use and context. — Al-Jurjani's staff examples show that a weak staff may suggest a gentle shepherd while a firm one suggests effective herd control; both can indicate good care by different routes. He excludes a different line about a firm staff and aloofness from flirtation from that shepherding point. 'Finger' can signify a skilled shepherd's effect because delicate work depends on fingers, but ordinary usage does not call house traces its fingers or make 'finger' a general word for effect. Likewise 'hand' can denote a benefit through its characteristic action; bare 'acquired a blessing' loses the image. The notes provide these examples of contextual figurative inference, without expressly applying them to mizan. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
