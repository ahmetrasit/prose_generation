# 76:2: 24 questions

## 76:2/q01 Why does the verse say 'Indeed We created'?  [إِنَّا خَلَقْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Which divine act and audience require emphasis?
- 76:2/q01/p1 The emphatic divine subject answers the opening question and stresses God's sole creation against denial. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says idolaters act as deniers by worshipping what did not create. Sāmarrāʾī says the opening question elicits assent; initial creation, reproduction, testing and judgment require emphasis, unlike ordinary parental generation (45:24; 34:7). The same divine subject creates, tests and equips people. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:2/q01/p2 The emphatic attribution of guidance echoes this creation formula. — Sāmarrāʾī says guidance, enabling worship (51:56), may deserve even greater emphasis; the revelation statement adds three emphasis markers because Quraysh disputed revelation more than creation (31:25; 39:38). [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:2/q01/p3 The contrast with 'We created them' later reflects the opening's contested creation and testing. — Sāmarrāʾī raises the wording contrast, then explains that the opening includes initial creation and a disputed purpose; ordinary reproduction is accepted. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:2/q02 Who is 'the human' created from a mixed drop?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the generic noun can include Adam when creation here is from a drop.
- 76:2/q02/p1 Adam's descendants, or humankind generically as reproduced from a drop. — Al-Māwardī calls this the commentators' consensus; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ṭabāṭabāʾī reason that Adam was not made from semen. Al-Biqāʿī takes a generic term narrowed here to descendants. Bursawī prefers species in both verses, invoking resurrection. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 76:2/q02/p2 Adam is included, either with all humans or through a nonhuman drop of origin. — Al-Biqāʿī generalizes the noun to Adam and descendants. Elmalılı says clay verses (3:59; 23:12; 30:20; 35:11; 40:67; 6:2; 55:14) do not exclude a nonhuman nuṭfa for Adam. Al-Nasafī and Study Quran report Adam or both as options. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 76:2/q02/p3 The first verse may mean Adam, while this verse means his descendants. — Bursawī reports and declines this reading; Sāmarrāʾī says it displays continued generation and divine power. Al-Nasafī reports an Adam reading linked to his time before recognition. [4 notes: BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 76:2/q03 Why is 'the human' repeated instead of a pronoun?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether definite repetition identifies the same referent or highlights a changed one.
- 76:2/q03/p1 The explicit noun magnifies creation and emphasizes humanity as its subject. — Bursawī says it reinforces the statement; Ālūsī says it stresses the material; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it records God's creation and governance; Suyūṭī identifies explicit repetition as magnification. Burhān connects repetition with restoration of creation and human creation's greatness. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ITQAN; BURHAN]
- 76:2/q03/p2 Repetition can mark different referents, Adam first and descendants second. — Bahāʾ al-Dīn cites this and other definite noun examples against a rigid identity rule; Burhān adduces scriptural and sovereignty counterexamples. Suyūṭī says some apparent counterexamples use generic definites and do not overturn the rule. [3 notes: ITQAN(الشيخ بهاء الدين); BURHAN]

## 76:2/q04 What does nuṭfah denote?  [نُّطْفَةٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names the man's emission or the joined generative material.
- 76:2/q04/p1 A small quantity of liquid, especially reproductive fluid. — Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, Qurṭubī and others trace it to liquid in a vessel; Ibn Rawāḥa's waterskin verse illustrates it, and Thaʿlabī relates its root to dripping. Ṭabrisī notes that it can also mean a river, citing ʿAlī on Nahrawān, and poetry illustrates a drop in a basin. Raghib calls it pure water; Elmalılı cites 75:37 and a Muslim report that generation is from part of semen. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; TABRISI(أمير المؤمنين,شاعر غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن رواحة); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 76:2/q04/p2 Here it is the man's semen, later mingled with the woman's contribution. — Al-Māwardī gives this alternative to calling the joined fluids the drop; Jishumī calls its primary referent male fluid. Asad supplies the unstated female ovum (86:6–7). [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 76:2/q04/p3 Here it is the already combined male and female reproductive fluids. — Al-Māwardī gives al-Suddī's view; Thaʿlabī calls both fluids nuṭfah. Elmalılı questions this timing: after fusion the product is an embryo or clot. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ELMALILI-]

## 76:2/q05 What does amshāj mean in Arabic usage?  [أَمْشَاجٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the gloss describes general mixing or specifically varied color.
- 76:2/q05/p1 Mixed or mixtures; mashj, mashaj and mashīj are cited as related forms. — Abū ʿUbayda, Farrāʾ, lexicographers and commentators define the root as mixing. Ruʾbah's mixed blood, Abū Dhuʾayb's mixed adjective, al-Shammākh's mixture, and a Hudhalī arrow furnish poetic evidence; Ibn al-Aʿrābī supplies variant singular forms. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes variant witnesses to an interlaced-image line. [36 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(رؤبة بن العجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة والفراء,أهل اللغة والمفسرون,ابن الأعرابي,الشمّاخ,هذلي); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(الشماخ); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن السكيت وغيره); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأعرابي,رؤبة والهذلي والشماخ); SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأعرابي,الشيخ,الهذلي,رؤبة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأعرابي,ابن عباس,المبرد); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ITQAN]
- 76:2/q05/p2 Its root can also describe a mixture of two colors. — Yamān and Ibn al-Aʿrābī supply this usage; Suyūṭī glosses the word as mixed in color. [4 notes: THALABI(يمان); BIQAI-FULL(يماني); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأعرابي); ITQAN]

## 76:2/q06 What are the mixtures in the drop?  [نُّطْفَةٍ أَمْشَاجٍۢ · interpretation]

- 76:2/q06/p6 The constituents are bodily humors or temperaments. — Language scholars list heat, cold, dryness and moisture; another list is blood, phlegm and yellow and black bile, with Elmalılı also naming lymph. - 76:2/q06/p8 It is a stringlike substance emitted after urination from which a child develops. — Two Ibn ʿAbbās reports give this distinctive account. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 76:2/q06/p9 Some soil from each newborn's future burial place is mingled into the drop. — Bursawī transmits this report from the Prophet. [1 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله)]

## 76:2/q07 How can amshāj describe singular nuṭfah?  [نُّطْفَةٍ أَمْشَاجٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether afʿāl can itself be singular, or plural agreement reflects the parts of a single drop.
- 76:2/q07/p1 Amshāj is plural in form; the single drop contains many mixed components, or an omitted 'possessing mixtures' is understood. — Akhfash, Ibn Qutayba and many grammarians give singulars mashj or mashīj; Wāḥidī likens a composite pot, garment and land. Abū Ḥayyān cites Sibawayh that afʿāl is not singular. Sāmin explains each component can count as a drop. Wāḥidī and Rāzī supply an omitted construct; Arabic examples of generic singular with plural adjective include 55:76. [32 notes: AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأعرابي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المعاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء وابن السكيت والمبرد); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ELMALILI+; KASHSHAF-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-]
- 76:2/q07/p2 Amshāj is a lexical singular or singular adjective with a plural-looking form. — Zamakhsharī compares barmah aʿshār and burd akyās; Nasafī follows. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this, saying Sibawayh did not explicitly disallow it. Sāmin defends Zamakhsharī as describing agreement through parts; Abū Ḥayyān and the shaykh object that afʿāl is not a singular pattern. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI(Sâhib-Keşşâf); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 76:2/q07/p3 Both singular and plural analyses can operate, with different images of mixture. — Elmalılı says singular emphasizes one composite, plural multiple composite elements; Sāmarrāʾī compares other Arabic nouns that can function in either number. [3 notes: ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:2/q08 Why use the plural-looking amshāj?  [أَمْشَاجٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 76:2/q08/p1 Its form stresses the many blended elements within the single drop. — Sāmarrāʾī compares pluralized descriptors for extensive distribution, such as sabāsib and qifār; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the description intensifying; Elmalılı distinguishes a single blend from blends within blends. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء وابن السكيت والمبرد); ELMALILI]

## 76:2/q09 What determines the child's resemblance and bodily contributions?  [نُّطْفَةٍ أَمْشَاجٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether precedence or predominance explains resemblance, and how each report assigns material and colors.
- 76:2/q09/p1 Whichever parent's fluid precedes or prevails determines resemblance; their contributions to the body differ. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās and others assign bone, nerves, strength or nails to the man, flesh, blood, hair or fat to the woman; ʿIkrima also says skin from the man. The fluids are described as thick white and thin yellow. Mujāhid says paternal or maternal uncles are resembled; a Prophetic report through Anas says precedence or dominance decides. A report through Abū Ayyūb asks about the child's sex. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس والحسن وعكرمة ومجاهد والربيع); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة عن أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والكلبي ومقاتل وعكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن وعكرمة ومجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أيوب الأنصاري,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(عكرمة)]
- 76:2/q09/p2 The accounts give differing color assignments for each parent's fluid. — Mujāhid variously describes male white/red and female green/red or green/yellow; another report has male white and female green/yellow. These details vary across transmitters. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 76:2/q10 What is meant by 'We test him'?  [نَّبْتَلِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the trial is prenatal transformation or later accountable life, and which forms of trial are meant.
- 76:2/q10/p1 God tests the person through later moral duties, commands, prohibitions, and deeds. — Ṭabarī links sound nature and intellect to worship (51:56); Ṭabrisī says duties expose obedience for recompense. Ibn Kathīr cites 67:2; Elmalılı adds hardship, reward, punishment and 75:40. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī treat later accountability as intended; Ibn ʿĀshūr places it after rational maturity. Al-Biqāʿī says this establishes proof though God already knows. [41 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI-]
- 76:2/q10/p2 God tests people with good and evil, calling for gratitude in ease and patience in hardship. — Al-Kalbī and al-Ḥasan identify the test's contrasting circumstances; Ibn ʿAbbās joins transformation through stages to testing by good and evil. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الكلبي والحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 76:2/q10/p3 Testing names the embryo's passage through successive states. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Zamakhsharī take transfer in the womb as a figurative trial; Bayḍāwī and Sāmin repeat it. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this because hearing and sight follow the test in the text, whereas they enable legal duty. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 76:2/q10/p4 Testing means bringing the human into existence and worldly life. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān attach this sense to creation as a simultaneous circumstance. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 76:2/q10/p5 The verb can mean assigning responsibility or choosing, as well as testing. — Māwardī offers taklīf and religious commands; Jishumī says ibtilāʾ can mean choosing and explains God's action by analogy with a tester so known conduct becomes manifest. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 76:2/q11 How does God's testing relate to His prior knowledge?  [نَّبْتَلِيهِ · theology]
- 76:2/q11/p1 Testing manifests what God already knows and establishes the basis for accountability. — Jishumī says God acts as a tester without acquiring knowledge; Ṭabrisī says obedience and disobedience become evident for recompense. Biqāʿī says the test establishes proof in human terms; Bursawī distinguishes general foreknowledge from manifested acceptance and rejection. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 76:2/q12 How does nَbtalīhi connect grammatically to the surrounding clauses?  [نَّبْتَلِيهِ فَجَعَلْنَٰهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether testing temporally follows the senses, is intended at creation, or describes creation itself.
- 76:2/q12/p1 It is a prospective circumstantial clause: God created the human intending a later test. — Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Nasafī and others connect it to creation; Sāmin says it can qualify creator or human, and legal accountability makes it prospective. Sāmarrāʾī cites 37:112, 48:27 and 90:4 for future states; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is advanced for emphasis, while guidance follows in 76:3. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:2/q12/p2 It is a purpose clause with an omitted lām: creation is so that God may test. — Rāzī compares ordinary expressions and 74:6; Sāmarrāʾī compares 67:2 and says the unprefixed form admits both purpose and circumstance. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:2/q12/p3 The clauses are understood in reverse temporal order: God made him hearing and seeing in order to test him. — Farrāʾ and Ibn Qutayba reason that senses or completed creation precede testing; Naḥḥās argues the faʾ instead orders events and no purposive lām occurs. Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, Sāmin and Ālūsī call inversion forced or unnecessary; Ṭabāṭabāʾī also rejects using it to rescue a duty reading. [10 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة,الفراء ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); TAB-FULL-; NAHHAS-; KASHSHAF-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)-; TABATABAI-]
- 76:2/q12/p4 The ordinary order stands: creation is for testing, then hearing and sight equip the person. — The composer cited by Wāḥidī attaches test to initial creation, with senses supplied afterward; Ṭabarī says trial needs sound nature and intact mind even without hearing or sight. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reads two coordinated complete clauses; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the result order favors a prenatal trial. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 76:2/q12/p5 It may begin a new sentence: 'We will test him.' — Study Quran reports this alternative. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 76:2/q12/p6 With embryonic transformation, the circumstantial clause is simultaneous with creation. — Abū Ḥayyān and Sāmin contrast this with the prospective legal-duty reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس)]

## 76:2/q13 Why use the form nabtalīhi rather than a lighter verb for testing?  [نَّبْتَلِيهِ · rhetoric]
- 76:2/q13/p1 The iftaʿala form intensifies the test and suits this sura's fuller account. — Sāmarrāʾī compares ṣabara/iṣṭabara and kasaba/iktasaba, with 3:154 and 33:11; he cautions that more letters do not always intensify, citing ḥadhar versus ḥādhir. He contrasts al-Mulk's yablūkum (67:2): its forgiving close and briefer treatment of test, unlike this sura's senses, guidance, deeds and outcomes, suit the lighter form. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:2/q14 What does 'so We made him hearing and seeing' mean?  [فَجَعَلْنَٰهُ سَمِيعًۢا بَصِيرًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the words describe bodily faculties alone or the understanding and response they make possible.
- 76:2/q14/p1 God endowed the human with the physical faculties of hearing and sight. — Qushayrī and Nasafī state the grant; Damghānī cites auditory usage at 3:193 and visual usage at 12:93 and 50:22. Ibn al-Jawzī defines hearing as perception of sounds and baṣīr as opposite of blind. Muqātil locates the gift after lifeless fetal stages. [9 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; DAMGHANI; MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 76:2/q14/p2 Hearing and sight also imply intellect, understanding and discernment. — Māwardī contrasts animals; Rāzī reports understanding, obedience and knowledge as interpretations and cites 19:42. Abū Ḥayyān calls the faculties the noblest means of apprehension; Ibn ʿĀshūr says humans perceive and discriminate more than animals. Elmalılı says a human must truly perceive duties. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 76:2/q14/p3 The phrase can mean being able to hear and see guidance. — Qurṭubī records this more specific interpretation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 76:2/q15 What further Arabic senses of hearing and seeing are noted?  [سَمِيعًۢا بَصِيرًا · meaning]
turns on: The distinction between direct sensory meaning and other attested root usages.
- 76:2/q15/p1 The normal senses are perceiving sounds and seeing with the eyes. — Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes samāʿ as perception from samʿ as faculty; baṣīr is opposite blind, and baṣura/abṣara can mean seeing. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 76:2/q15/p2 The roots have extended senses of knowledge, proof and other homonyms. — Ibn al-Jawzī says baṣar/baṣīr can denote knowledge; baṣīra can mean a blood drop, shield or proof; a poem uses baṣīr for dog. With different vocalization samʿ can mean good renown or a wolf-hyena offspring, and samāʿ can be an imperative 'listen.' [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 76:2/q16 How do hearing and sight serve the human test?  [سَمِيعًۢا بَصِيرًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether senses are necessary instruments of accountability or themselves the tested gift.
- 76:2/q16/p1 They equip humans to receive revelation, perceive created signs, judge, and obey or disobey. — Bayḍāwī associates auditory revelation and visible signs with the faʾ. Biqāʿī details outward and inward senses, avoiding harm and seeking benefit; Ibn ʿĀshūr assigns hearing to law and sight to proofs. Bursawī says intellect is implicit as their instrument. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran note both obedience and disobedience are possible (7:179; 7:198; 10:43). [22 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 76:2/q16/p2 The senses themselves are the means or part of the test. — Ibn Qutayba's reordered reading tests through the senses; Wāḥidī reports grammarians who understand testing by making one hear and see. Ṭabarī objects that trial remains possible without either faculty, because sound nature and mind are decisive; the senses call for gratitude. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض النحويين); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); STUDYQURAN(IK); TAB-FULL-]

## 76:2/q17 Why is hearing mentioned before sight?  [سَمِيعًۢا بَصِيرًا · rhetoric]
- 76:2/q17/p1 Hearing is especially important for receiving address, law and revelation. — Biqāʿī says audible signs are clearer; Sāmarrāʾī says a blind person can receive a message more easily than a deaf person. A reported proof notes no prophet was deaf while Jacob became blind; Sāmarrāʾī limits superiority to receiving revelation, not every respect. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قالوا); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]

## 76:2/q18 Why use these adjectival forms and join them without a conjunction?  [سَمِيعًۢا بَصِيرًا · rhetoric]
turns on: How the adjective pattern and coordination convey the gift.
- 76:2/q18/p1 The intensive forms mark the fullness of the favor and human powers. — Bursawī identifies 'seeing' as the second object of 'made'; Ibn ʿĀshūr associates the forms with heightened human discrimination. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 76:2/q18/p2 Samīʿ is suited to praise, while sammāʿ would evoke blame. — Sāmarrāʾī raises the choice and says Qurʾānic samīʿ praises God or people, whereas sammāʿ describes blameworthy listeners (5:41–42; 9:47). [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:2/q18/p3 Omitting 'and' makes both faculties attributes of the same human. — Sāmarrāʾī says a conjunction might suggest separate groups, one hearing and another seeing. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:2/q19 What does the sequence of creation, trial and senses teach about humanity?  [* · interpretation]
- 76:2/q19/p1 Humble origin and intricate formation display God's power, wisdom and human dependence. — Ibn Kathīr stresses prior insignificance; Biqāʿī describes bones, joints, organs, digestion, circulation and three darknesses to argue for the unseen Maker, contrasting people's admiration of wall designs. Elmalılı rejects autonomous nature and stresses staged cultivation and eventual return; Study Quran emphasizes lowly origin (86:6–7). [15 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 76:2/q19/p2 Creation aims at accountable worship, and duties distinguish humanity. — Ṭabarī cites 51:56; Elmalılı invokes 67:2 and 75:40. Ibn ʿĀshūr says religious law underlies civilization and leads into guidance; Sāmarrāʾī lists the complete sequence of agents, faculties, choices and outcomes. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:2/q19/p3 The verse invites gratitude for faculties and warns against ingratitude. — Ṭabarī says hearing and sight recall favor; Qatāda says the origin story tests recognition of God's rights; Ishlāḥī says awareness of good and evil makes gratitude the test. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 76:2/q20 How does this verse relate to nearby verses and other Quranic passages?  [* · links]
turns on: How creation here supplies the argument and narrative progression of the surrounding text.
- 76:2/q20/p1 It answers 76:1's question of prior nonexistence and leads to 76:3's guidance and outcomes. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the answer to suspense; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening question expects assent and denies actual prior human existence, though material existed. Sāmarrāʾī maps the whole sura from origin to afterlife. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:2/q20/p2 It parallels creation and resurrection passages, especially Qiyāmah and Muʾminūn. — Qurṭubī links clay in 23:12; Qatāda parallels 23:14. Biqāʿī says the passage counters Qiyāmah's proud deniers (75:3,31–38); Ishlāḥī pairs the suras and contrasts arguments from the reproaching soul (75:2) with creation and recompense. Elmalılı adds 71:14 and 75:14. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 76:2/q20/p3 The sura moves from creation and guidance to reward, punishment and the Prophet's patience. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī outlines this sequence and the closing command to worship. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 76:2/q20/p4 Hearing and sight precede guidance because they convey its evidence and rulings. — Sāmarrāʾī says the sensory means normally bring information to the mind before guidance in 76:3. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:2/q21 How does the verse admit an inward reading?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the verse is read of spiritual components, divine perception or preexistent knowledge.
- 76:2/q21/p1 The mixed human contains contending spiritual powers under divine aid. — Abū ʿUthmān al-Maghribī counts nine: hearing, sight and tongue tempt; self, desire and enemy or Satan oppose faith; intellect, spirit and heart or angel support it. God's aid lets intellect govern and subdue passion; 2:193 and 8:39 are cited. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان المغربي); THALABI(أبو عثمان المغربي); BURSEVI(أبو عثمان المغربي)]
- 76:2/q21/p2 Male and female mixing signifies receptive potential and active power. — The Najmī taʾwīlāt assigns these to holy effusions. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 76:2/q21/p3 God becomes the servant's hearing and sight in inward perception. — The Najmī taʾwīlāt says nothing audible or visible escapes perception through Him. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 76:2/q21/p4 The human's unseen and visible forms are eternally known to God. — The Najmī taʾwīlāt says divine names manifest in both forms; it denies a time outside divine remembrance, and Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq restates the question. Ālūsī rejects an eternal human species and says an archetype defense applies equally to all things (36:12); Umar and Ibn Masʿūd's responses support prior nonexistence. [4 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,جعفر الصادق); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود,عمر بن الخطاب)-]

## 76:2/q22 What concrete creation reports are connected with the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The different reported durations and persons in creation narratives.
- 76:2/q22/p1 The embryo receives the spirit after three forty-day phases. — Bursawī gives drop at forty days, clot at eighty, spirit at 120; Ibn Masʿūd's report has forty days each as drop and clot before an angel writes sex, destiny, provision, effects and lifespan. [2 notes: BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 76:2/q22/p2 Adam underwent three forty-year clay phases before the spirit. — A report through al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās lists clay, dark mud and baked or molded clay; Ālūsī also transmits Adam lying between Mecca and Taif for forty years and a tradition that the earlier 'time' has unknown length. [4 notes: BURSEVI(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة)]
- 76:2/q22/p3 Abū Bakr wished creation had ended before testing. — Elmalılı reports Rāzī's account as an expression of awareness of unfinished human perfection and future duties. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Hazret-i Sıddîk)]

## 76:2/q23 What does the verse's divine attribution of testing imply about evil?  [نَّبْتَلِيهِ · theology]
- 76:2/q23/p1 God may attribute an ordeal to Himself, while Quranic phrasing treats evil with care. — Sāmarrāʾī says testing Moses' people (20:85) and humanity (67:2; 76:2) is an ordeal, not evil. He notes passive phrasing for harm (17:83; 72:10; 4:72), general benefit in punishing unbelievers (13:32), and divine determination of good and evil against dualism; the Quran does not say God adorned evil deeds. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:2/q24 What is said about the sura's place and literary form?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports of revelation place and assessment of literary flow.
- 76:2/q24/p1 Its place of revelation is disputed between wholly Medinan, partly Medinan, and wholly Meccan. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes Ahl al-Bayt and many Sunni Medinan reports, a final-nine-verses Meccan variant, and a wholly Meccan view. Ishlāḥī notes Medinan muṣḥaf labels but argues the subject confirms a wholly Meccan sura. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some maṣāḥif); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 76:2/q24/p2 The opening exemplifies smooth Qurʾānic flow, and a segment can scan as poetry without poetic intent. — Suyūṭī calls this insijām, reporting badīʿ authorities' point that flowing prose can scan metrically; he identifies the opening with al-munsariḥ meter. [3 notes: ITQAN(أهل البديع)]
