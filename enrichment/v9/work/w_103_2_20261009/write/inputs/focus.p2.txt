# 103:2/q07 What does khusr mean, and what is lost? [خُسْرٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether loss is read as a lexical diminution, unbelief, punishment, squandered life, spiritual deprivation, bodily decline or loss across both worlds.
- 103:2/q07/p1 Loss, ruin, diminishment, or destruction of one's capital.
  reasons: Abu Ubayda, Ibn Qutayba, Tabari, Suddi, Ibn Shajara, Akhfash and others give these overlapping lexical glosses; Ibn al-Jawzi says khusr and khusran are equivalent. Wahidi likens it to lost capital. Razi and Raghib note that loss may be predicated of a person or a transaction; Kashshaf compares the nominal forms. Bagawi and Ibn al-Jawzi identify the self as the greatest capital. Ibn Qutayba glosses loss as deficiency and says the excepted believers are not diminished. Ibn al-Jawzi says khusrān is conventionally a reduction of one's capital. Al-Damghani's sense list also includes decrease, without supplying an example in this extract. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats loss as the opposite of trading profit and compares an unprofitable trade in 2:16. Ibn Atiyya includes a bad condition among its senses.

- 103:2/q07/p2 Misguidance and disbelief that persist until death without faith.
  reasons: Mujahid glosses loss as misguidance; Wahidi reports Muqatil's worldly explanation, and Razi links the particular-unbeliever interpretation to misguidance and belief as profit. Muqatil says Abu Lahab remains in error until Fire. Al-Damghani applies the sense 'misguidance' directly to humanity here and cites 4:119; Ibn al-Jawzi reports the same gloss from the exegetes, also with 4:119.

- 103:2/q07/p3 Punishment for sins, especially Fire and forfeited home, family or property in Paradise.
  reasons: Farra, Mawardi and Qurtubi cite punishment, with Qurtubi comparing 65:9; Nahhas calls entry into Fire the greatest loss. Wahidi adds the forfeited Paradise station. Hasan adds void deeds; Jishumi sees lasting punishment as unsurpassable. Tabrisi and Qur'an 39:15 are used for loss of self and family at Judgment. Al-Damghani lists punishment as another sense of khusr, although this extract ends before its examples.

- 103:2/q07/p5 The lost capacity to serve God and attain spiritual nearness.
  reasons: Razi says deprivation of serving the Lord is the central loss, beside which Paradise and Hell are secondary, citing 51:56. Qushayri distinguishes deeds whose loss is evident at the end from present loss of states: contraction after expansion, veiling after nearness and resort to concessions after a higher path. Qashani and Study Quran describe losing innate guidance or attachment to God.

- 103:2/q07/p6 Decline of bodily strength with age, while believers' earlier deeds keep being recorded.

- 103:2/q07/p7 Loss in both worldly life and the Hereafter, culminating in loss of dignity and the soul.
  reasons: Study Quran cites 35:39, 22:11 and 39:15, noting that a person may lose what is dear without material poverty. Qutb makes earthly profit negligible beside God's pleasure, Paradise and human dignity; Abu Hayyan calls losing both worlds the greatest loss. Ibn Atiyya says this is clearest in a disbeliever and calls it manifest loss.

- 103:2/q07/p8 An adverse afterlife outcome rather than a verdict on worldly fortune.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says trade loss metaphorically names the bad end awaiting one who expects bliss; the warning concerns the future, especially the eternal afterlife, not worldly turns of fortune (3:196–197).
