# 103:2/q08 Why does ordinary human life tend toward loss? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ · interpretation]
turns on: How finite life and human choices produce loss or enduring gain.
- 103:2/q08/p1 Life and time are finite capital; misuse or failure to earn lasting good spends them irretrievably.
  reasons: Bagawi calls self and lifetime the greatest capital. Razi says sin, permissible activity and even incomplete worship miss enduring gain; Elmalili compares every breath to an entrusted loan and cites 25:62, 53:39 and 52:21. Poems about each passing day, his elegy and the image of death consuming life sharpen the point. Tabatabai calls earthly life a test (13:26; 21:35) in which belief and deeds determine the afterlife (53:39–41; 30:44; 41:46). Islahi stresses rapidly consumed life. Bint al-Shati's broad al-insan usage is distinguished from al-nas and jinn contexts. Baydawi and Alusi say people spend life in pursuits that yield no afterlife benefit; Alusi adds possible harm at resurrection. Biqai stresses attachment to the passing present.

- 103:2/q08/p2 Human desire, distraction and frailty draw people away from lasting good.
  reasons: Sulami mentions neglect of God's rights, following desires and lack of fear. Razi says the afterlife's rewards are hidden whereas sensory appetites and anger draw people toward the visible world; Elmalili reports this. Biqai cites human injustice and ignorance (33:72) and notes that only a few are preserved from these defects. Qutb applies the loss to communities without righteous authority. Biqai describes loss as driven by whims, personal aims and delusion about what passes away; Ibn al-Zubayr connects innate injustice and ignorance in 33:72.

- 103:2/q08/p3 A person can turn life's expense into profit by using the present for faith, obedience and the afterlife.
  reasons: Abu Hayyan contrasts trading away the afterlife for this world with buying it through this world. # 103:2/q09 Who escapes loss, and to what extent? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether exemption requires all four traits and whether a believer's sinful or incomplete action still entails a degree of loss.
- 103:2/q09/p1 The complete exception requires faith, righteous deeds, and mutual counsel in truth and patience.
  reasons: Muhammad ibn Kaʿb says belief alone is insufficient and lists the successive conditions. Muqatil says those who do these things remain in the Gardens; Elmalili calls the four complete faith; Qutb calls them the single successful path in every age and the basis of a community. Al-Shafiʿi and others regard the surah as sufficient guidance. Tabari and Hasan compare another connected exception with this one. Ibn Qutayba says those who believe, do good and counsel truth and patience are not diminished.
  holders (13 notes): MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Mehmet Âkif Ersoy); STUDYQURAN; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL+
  notes: MUQATIL:103:1-3/r7 TAB-FULL:v24p520/r5 IBNABIHATIM:v10p3462/r3 ELMALILI:103:1-3#4/r21 ELMALILI:103:1-3#6/r9 ELMALILI:103:1-3/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:103:1/r3 QUTB-ZILAL:103:1/r45 QUTB-ZILAL:103:2/r2 QUTB-ZILAL:103:2/r44 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:103:1/r19 STUDYQURAN:103:intro/r4 IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL:v1p204#2/r5
- 103:2/q09/p2 Believers who do righteous deeds are exempted from the loss.
  reasons: Mujahid identifies the excepted believers; Muqatil and Thalabi also name belief and deeds. Razi says believers gain enduring happiness with their self and time. Ibn Ashur says good believers avoid loss when they do no opposite deed or repent; al-salihat includes commanded good and avoidance of sin. Islahi says deeds show faith has taken root, cites 4:136, and explains their outward, inward, worldly, religious, individual and collective scope through Farahi. Ibn Atiyya says believers' temporary hardship is forgiven beside lasting success; Ibrāhīm says their youthful deeds continue to be credited in decline.

- 103:2/q09/p3 Sinful believers incur a lesser, possibly temporary loss, though faith ultimately saves them.
  reasons: Biqai distinguishes denial from losses proportionate to disobedience. Elmalili rejects the Murji'a claim that faith without action entails no loss; sinful believers whose evil outweighs good may experience Hell until cleansed. Ibn Ashur distinguishes eternal loss through denial from graded lesser losses and cites 11:114 on forgiveness of minor sins. Samarrai says mutual counsel prevents loss of further reward. Alusi says one outside the exception need not face eternal Fire: an unpardoned sinner may enter Fire, while a pardoned one may lose higher ranks. He calls other, unspecified replies offered by al-Maturidi strained.
