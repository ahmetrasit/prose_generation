# 103:2/q04 What wording and vowel readings of this verse are transmitted? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ خُسْرٍ · readings]
turns on: Which transmitted wording or vocalization is used, and whether an explanatory addition belongs to canonical recitation.
- 103:2/q04/p1 The received codex reads the present wording, with a still sīn in khusr.
  reasons: Thalabi prefers the general reciters and codices; Qurtubi says Ali's explanatory addition is not Qur'an for recitation under the Uthmanic codex. Jishumi, Abu Hayyan and Samin attest the still sīn as the majority form. Qurtubi explicitly prefers the community's codices and says wording opposed to the Uthmanic codex is not Qur'an for recitation.

# 103:2/q05 What is the force of inna and la-fī? [إِنَّ لَفِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the phrase depicts an eventual outcome or a continuing condition.
- 103:2/q05/p1 Inna emphasizes the assertion and fī portrays the human as immersed and surrounded by loss.
  reasons: Razi assigns emphasis to inna and describes loss encompassing a person from every side. Razi adds that the emphatic lam works with inna; Ibn ʿĀshūr says figurative containment is stronger than simply calling someone a loser.

# 103:2/q06 What does the indefinite khusr convey? [خُسْرٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indefiniteness signals magnitude, a distinct or unrestricted category, or relative smallness.
- 103:2/q06/p1 It magnifies a loss whose full extent is known only to God.
  reasons: Razi prefers this because sins oppose God and immense blessings; Baydawi, Bursevi and others also intensify the loss. Bint al-Shati cites Razi's awe explanation, but rejects treating intensity as its sole force. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates magnification to the threatening context of the oath.
