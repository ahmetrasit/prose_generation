# 103:2/q13 How is this sūra situated in revelation? [* · sciences]
turns on: The differing reported settings, with most named authorities favoring Mecca.
- 103:2/q13/p1 It is Meccan, probably after Sūrah 94.
  reasons: Abu Hayyan reports Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr and the majority as Meccan; Ibn Kathir and Qutb affirm it. Tabatabai favors Meccan despite allowing either; Asad and Study Quran place it shortly after al-Sharh.

- 103:2/q13/p2 It is Medinan.
  reasons: Abu Hayyan attributes this view to Mujahid, Qatada and Muqatil; Tabatabai allows the possibility but favors Meccan.

# 103:2/q14 What reports describe the sūra's recitation or reception? [* · reports]
turns on: Different reports concern distinct acts of recitation and reception.
- 103:2/q14/p1 Two Companions recited it to one another before parting and then exchanged greetings.
  reasons: Reported by Tabarani through Abu Madina in Ibn Kathir and repeated by Elmalili, Qutb and Study Quran; Qutb takes the practice as renewing commitment to its guidance. Durr attributes the Companion practice to Abu Mulayka al-Darimi.
  holders (6 notes): IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن حصن أبو مدينة); ELMALILI(Ebû Medîne); QUTB-ZILAL(الرجلان من أصحاب رسول الله); STUDYQURAN; DURR(أبو مليكة الدارمي)

- 103:2/q14/p2 The Prophet interpreted the sūra's phrases by naming Abu Jahl and four Companions.
  reasons: Thalabi transmits Ubayy's recitation to the Prophet and question for interpretation; its reported reply identifies loss with Abu Jahl and belief, deeds, truth and patience with Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali. # 103:2/q17 What historical era does the oath and warning evoke? [* · interpretation]
- 103:2/q17/p1 The era of Muhammad's mission opens a transformation from widespread ignorance and loss toward guidance.
  reasons: Elmalili describes religious disorder and injustice before the mission, the Qur'an's first iqra revelation on the Night of Power, and the upright religion calling people to reform, Paradise and divine approval. He links this transition to al-Bayyina and reads the oath before the warning as meaningful. Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasizes people living when Islam appeared, especially those reached by its call worldwide.
  holders (5 notes): ELMALILI; IBNASHUR

# 103:2/q21 How does this verse illustrate Ibn Ashur's account of Qur'anic style? [* · rhetoric]
- 103:2/q21/p1 Its broad declaration is precisely qualified by the following exception for believers.
  reasons: Ibn Ashur cites 103:2–3 as an example of carefully delimited Qur'anic propositions, unlike loose generalities that may overlook rare cases. He places this in a wider catalogue of Qur'anic innovations: a preserved recited book distinct from poetry and oratory, systematic topics, vivid narratives, reshaped reported speech, and adapted narrative names. Those other items describe his surrounding stylistic account rather than further claims about this verse.
