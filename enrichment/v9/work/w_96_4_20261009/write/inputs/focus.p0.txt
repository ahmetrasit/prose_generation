# 96:4/q01 How does 'who' attach to the surrounding sentence? [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative clause qualifies an earlier relative or serves in the new explanatory sentence.
- 96:4/q01/p1 It modifies the earlier 'who' as an adjective.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās takes this relative clause as a description of the first alladhī.
  holders (1 notes): NAHHAS
  notes: NAHHAS:v5p261/r8
- 96:4/q01/p2 The teaching clause is an explanatory predication in the sentence about 'your Lord'.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says rabbuka is the subject, with either this clause or 'taught humanity what it did not know' as predicate; al-Samīn says the paired teaching clauses admit the same analyses as the paired creation clauses.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR
  notes: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p439/r2 SAMIN-DURR:96:4-5/r1
sources: IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; NAHHAS = Abū Jaʿfar al-Naḥḥās, d. 338; SAMIN-DURR = al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756

# 96:4/q02 What does God teach here? [عَلَّمَ بِٱلْقَلَمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pen refers to ordinary writing, heavenly scripture, or a wider kind of knowledge; the expanded reading explicitly supplies 'writing'.
- 96:4/q02/p1 God teaches human beings writing and the knowledge it records through a pen.
  reasons: Muqātil also joins writing to teaching the Qur'an. Al-Kalbī allows 'writing or the written book'; Abū Isḥāq specifies humans writing. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī, and others give the straightforward script or handwriting gloss. Al-Jishumī prefers it for preservation and instruction. Ibn al-Zubayr's expanded wording supplies 'writing'; al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī link it to divine generosity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾi allows teaching reading or writing.

# 96:4/q03 Why are the objects of 'taught' unstated? [عَلَّمَ · grammar]
- 96:4/q03/p1 They are omitted so the statement centers God's act of teaching; context supplies writers and writing.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says both objects of ʿallama are omitted for this focus. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies 'writers' or 'people to write' and notes the Arabs' regard for literacy. Ṣāḥib al-naẓm supplies humanity and its unknown knowledge from verse 5; repetition emphasizes teaching.
  holders (4 notes): ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم)
  notes: ABUHAYYAN:96:4/r7 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p507/r10 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p440/r1 WAHIDI-BASIT:v24p170#4/r1
sources: ABUHAYYAN = Abū Ḥayyān, d. 745; ABUHAYYAN-FULL = Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; WAHIDI-BASIT = al-Wāḥidī, d. 468

# 96:4/q04 What does the bāʾ in 'by the pen' express? [بِٱلْقَلَمِ · grammar]
- 96:4/q04/p1 It marks the pen as the instrument or means of teaching and writing.
  reasons: Al-Burhān defines instrumental bāʾ through writing with a pen and compares bism Allāh. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it causal while still treating the pen as means, and allows a circumstantial or fresh statement.
  holders (2 notes): BURHAN; TABATABAI
  notes: BURHAN:v4p255/r1 TABATABAI:96:1-7/r10
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; TABATABAI = Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402

# 96:4/q05 What does 'pen' denote here? [ٱلْقَلَمِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the pen is the earthly writing tool or a heavenly medium.
- 96:4/q05/p1 The ordinary writing instrument, specifically a trimmed reed nib and, by extension, writing tools.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the smooth reed, slit tip, ink, and paper, and recalls the pens in 3:44. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares the oath in 68:1. Kur'an Yolu extends its functional sense to modern tools of information exchange.
  holders (7 notes): BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: BAGHAWI:96:4/r1 BAGHAWI-FULL:v8p479#2/r1 IBNASHUR:96:4/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p440/r5 BINTSHATI:v2p22/r3 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:1/r7 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:4-5:anmerkung/r8
- 96:4/q05/p2 A heavenly pen or the written speech of God on the Preserved Tablet.
  reasons: Abū ʿAlī says angels read and transmit the Tablet's writing. Neuwirth invokes 85:22, 68:1, and 80:13–16; Corpus Coranicum allows that but says later plural uses at 31:27 and 3:44 also support earthly pens. Al-Qāshānī inwardly reads the pen as the supreme first spirit.
