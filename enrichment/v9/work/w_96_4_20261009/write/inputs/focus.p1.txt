# 96:4/q07 Is 'writing' part of a variant reading of the verse? [عَلَّمَ بِٱلْقَلَمِ · readings]
turns on: Whether an addition differing from the codex consonants counts as a reading or only explains the received text.
- 96:4/q07/p1 A reading attributed to Ibn al-Zubayr explicitly adds 'writing' after 'taught'.
  reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Ālūsī cite it to make the writing gloss explicit; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ gives a similar attribution to Ibn ʿAbbās. Corpus Coranicum calls it a likely later clarification.

# 96:4/q09 What does this teaching show about knowledge and writing? [* · interpretation]
- 96:4/q09/p1 Writing is a divine favor that preserves and communicates knowledge and sustains religious and worldly life.
  reasons: Qatāda says religion and livelihood cannot stand without it. Al-Zamakhsharī lists sciences, wisdom, former peoples' accounts, sayings, and revealed books; al-Bayḍāwī stresses distant communication. Al-Rāzī calls the pen a hunter of knowledge and likens its far silent reach to Zechariah's quiet prayer (19:3 or 3:38); Solomon's ifrit contrasts passing speech with writing that binds it. Ibn Kathīr distinguishes mental, oral, and written knowledge: writing entails the other two; an athar urges binding knowledge in writing. Al-Bursevī urges writers to consider what they will see at Judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds transmission across generations, oral teaching, and discovery. Qutb calls the pen humanity's deepest teaching tool, whose full value was not yet apparent.
