# 96:4/q20 Which passages followed the opening revelation? [* · sciences]
- 96:4/q20/p1 Nūn and the Pen, al-Muddaththir, and al-Duha are named as later revelations.
  reasons: ʿĀʾisha gives these in the aftermath of Iqra; one Mujāhid transmission specifically says Nūn and the Pen followed. Ibn Shaddād connects al-Duha to a pause and reassurance.
  holders (6 notes): TAB(عائشة، عبد الله بن شداد، مجاهد)

# 96:4/q22 What other reports identify Idris and his family? [* · reports]
- 96:4/q22/p1 Idris is called Khanuq, Noah's grandfather, a prophet and messenger, and the first pen writer.
  reasons: Wahb calls Khanuq Syriac; another derivation links Idrīs to extensive study of scriptures. A weak al-Mustadrak report says he was raised to the sixth heaven after seeing earthly injustice, linked to 19:57; Ibn Qutayba gives age 350 and Ibn ʿAbbās says a thousand years separated him from Noah.

# 96:4/q23 What ancillary genealogical claims appear with the account of early writers? [* · reports]
- 96:4/q23/p1 The cited material gives variant names, ancestry, birth intervals, and lifespans for Abraham, Ishmael, and Isaac.
  reasons: Al-Jawālīqī treats Ibrahim as a non-Arabic name with variant forms and a seven-reader form Ibrāhām; another claim gives Syriac 'merciful father', while al-Kirmānī derives it from intense looking. The genealogy runs from Azar/Tarah through Nahor, Sharukh, Raghu, Falikh, Abir, Shalikh, Arfakhshadh, and Shem to Noah. Al-Wāqidī dates Abraham's birth two thousand years after Adam. Abū Hurayra gives circumcision at 120 and death at 200; al-Nawawī reports 175. Ishmael is eldest, his name has a final-nūn variant, and Isaac is said to be born fourteen years later, live 180 years, with a Hebrew name meaning laughter.
  holders (12 notes): ITQAN(أبو علي بن مسكويه، أبو هريرة، الجواليقي، الكرماني، النووي وغيره، الواقدي)

# 96:4/q24 What adjacent examples of letters and grammatical particles do sources give? [* · grammar]
turns on: The sources give separate analogies about letters and bāʾ while explaining language associated with pen and writing.
- 96:4/q24/p1 Opening letters and the oath by them honor the alphabet, whose abbreviations follow Arab speech habits.
  reasons: Ibn Qutayba says letters build revealed books, divine names and attributes, speech, and worship; shorthand substitutes related words or nearby letters, as in مدهته for مدحته and جدث/جدف.
  holders (2 notes): IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL
  notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL:v1p183/r1 IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL:v1p183/r4
- 96:4/q24/p2 A prepositional phrase with bāʾ implies a verb, and bāʾ has several grammatical functions.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī compares bi-l-qalam with 'I wrote with the pen', oath bi-llāh, and travel 'in God's name'; omitting the verb widens implication. Sībawayh contrasts the bāʾ's kasra with the kāf's fatḥa. Al-Rāzī lists original or extra bāʾ, adhesion, partitive according to al-Shāfiʿī, strengthening negation (41:46), transitivity (2:17), and meaning 'in', supported by poetry and 46:9.

# 96:4/q25 How should the title 'Most Generous' be understood or used? [* · rhetoric]
- 96:4/q25/p1 God's unrestricted superlative generosity is unique, an intensive attribute without self-interest.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī defines karam as fitting benefit without ulterior motive, links it to teaching by pen, and reports scholars criticizing the unrestricted human honorific in correspondence, especially for Jews and Christians; he urges reverence and gratitude. Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Akram is an intensifier, not comparison.
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:96:4/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p180#3/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p181/r6 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p439/r6
- 96:4/q25/p2 The second 'Read' introduces assurance that answers Muhammad's excuse.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports analysis taking it as a new command, with 'Your Lord is most generous' as a circumstantial clause rather than mere emphasis.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI-FULL
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p180#3/r2
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; ALUSI-FULL = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

  reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ defines knowledge against ignorance and refers to her discussion of 102:3; Ibn Kathīr calls these first verses God's first mercy and blessing to people.
