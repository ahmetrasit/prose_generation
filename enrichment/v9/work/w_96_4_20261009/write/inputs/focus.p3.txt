# 96:4/q16 Who first developed or transmitted writing among peoples? [* · reports]
turns on: The accounts differ over a first divinely taught writer and later human transmission of Arabic scripts.
- 96:4/q16/p1 Writing was taught by God and was known to Adam or other prophets.
  reasons: Al-Burhān cites this verse and 68:1, and Ibn Fāris includes letters among Adam's divinely taught names. Kaʿb's account gives Adam Arabic and Syriac clay books; reports also name Idrīs as inventor of sand writing, Tahmurth of Persian, and Joseph of papyrus.
  holders (3 notes): BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن فارس); BURSEVI(كعب الأحبار)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p377/r2 ITQAN:v4p442/r5 BURSEVI:v10p364/r1
- 96:4/q16/p2 Ishmael first established Arabic writing.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās gives this report; al-Burhān says accounts of origins are numerous and conflicting.
  holders (1 notes): BURHAN(ابن عباس)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p377/r1
- 96:4/q16/p3 Arabic script reached the Hijaz through al-Hira and al-Anbar, with Harb ibn Umayya bringing it to Mecca.
  reasons: Al-Kalbī and al-Haytham trace Harb through Aslam ibn Asdara or Sidra to inventor Marāmir ibn Murra. Al-Ālūsī says writing was ancient among non-Arabs but comparatively recent in Arabia; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds the earlier Himyarite Musnad. One poem boasts of learning to write.
  holders (6 notes): ALUSI(الكلبي والهيثم بن عدي، غير واحد); ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي، الهيثم بن عدي); IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:96:5/r24 ALUSI:96:5/r25 ALUSI:96:5/r26 ALUSI-FULL:v30p181/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p181/r4 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p440/r3
- 96:4/q16/p4 Himyarites used a separate, unjoined Musnad script; writing systems have many branches.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī says Musnad was prestigious and restricted to authorized learners. He allows a count of twelve scripts only as root families, because derivatives are nearly innumerable.
  holders (3 notes): ALUSI(بعضهم، قيل); ALUSI-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:96:5/r27 ALUSI:96:5/r28 ALUSI-FULL:v30p181/r5
- 96:4/q16/p5 Early Arab and Muslim writing had established letter names, grammar, and orthographic conventions.
  reasons: Al-Burhān answers claims of ancient ignorance by tracing letter names to Adam. He says Abū al-Aswad revived grammar and al-Khalīl prosody, while codex spelling of wāw, yāʾ, and hamza shows early orthographic knowledge. Al-Farrāʾ prefers codex spelling where Arabic usage supports it; Mālik requires preserving first codex orthography even for extra unpronounced wāw or alif.
  holders (5 notes): BURHAN(الفراء، مالك)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p377/r3 BURHAN:v1p377/r4 BURHAN:v1p377/r5 BURHAN:v1p377/r6 BURHAN:v1p377/r7

# 96:4/q17 What happened when the opening revelation came to Muhammad? [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in whether the delivery includes a written silk sheet or the opening and washing of his chest.
- 96:4/q17/p1 At Hira after true dreams and retreat, Gabriel repeatedly ordered him to recite, embraced him three times, and delivered the opening verses.
  reasons: ʿĀʾisha's account has Khadīja supply provisions; Muhammad answered that he could not read and returned trembling. Al-Wāḥidī notes transmission in Bukhārī and Muslim; Ibn Kathīr cites both Ṣaḥīḥs through al-Zuhrī; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the retention shows immediate recitation and Khadīja's consultation of Waraqa confirms it. Ibn Shaddād likewise reports the squeezing. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the words remained fixed in his heart.

# 96:4/q19 Was this the first revelation, and how much of the sura came first? [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports variously call the first unit the sura or specify its first five verses; verse counts also differ.
- 96:4/q19/p1 The opening five verses through 96:5 were the first Qur'anic revelation at Hira; the rest came later.
  reasons: ʿĀʾisha and several transmitters identify Iqra as first; Ibn ʿAbbās and scholars quoted by al-Wāḥidī and al-Durr specify five verses. Ibn ʿĀshūr dates it to Ramadan 17, year 40 after the Elephant; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites al-Ṭabarī and biography reports stopping at verse 5 and Abū Ḥayyān's rejection of claims for al-Muddaththir or al-Fātiḥa. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the next subject starts at verse 6.
