# 68:2: 19 questions

## 68:2/q01 What is the Lord's favor in this verse?  [بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ · meaning]
turns on: The sources identify the favor as mercy, protection, prophethood, broader personal gifts, or inward knowledge; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also distinguishes the form niʿma from naʿīm.
- 68:2/q01/p1 God's mercy. — Muqātil and al-Māwardī gloss niʿma as mercy; al-Wāḥidī transmits Muqātil's gloss alongside the Meccan accusation. [4 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 68:2/q01/p2 The gift of prophethood, often together with faith. — ʿAṭāʾ and Ibn ʿAbbās specify faith and prophethood; al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī and others say prophethood makes madness untenable. Bayḍāwī adds sound judgment; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers prophethood as the decisive proof over general virtues. [13 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(عطاء وابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI+]
- 68:2/q01/p3 The Lord's protection. — Reported as an alternative gloss by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī. [2 notes: THALABI(قائل غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 68:2/q01/p4 God's gifts of reason, character, wisdom, courage, and fitness for the mission. — Al-Naḥḥās points to intellect and understanding; al-Ṭabrisī names full intellect, prophethood and wisdom. Al-Rāzī, al-Muntakhab, al-Biqāʿī, al-Elmalılı and the reported alternative in al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī add eloquence, conduct, freedom from defects, nurturing, the Qur'an and worldly and afterlife felicity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds prophethood more decisive. [9 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ناصر الدين); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI-]
- 68:2/q01/p5 Insight into what the Soul and Intellect inscribe and into hidden destinies. — Al-Qāshānī interprets the favor as unveiled insight into destiny and realities; Najm al-Dīn says the Prophet is not veiled from the eternal past or future. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(نجم الدين)]
- 68:2/q01/p6 In Qur'anic usage, niʿma denotes worldly blessings, while naʿīm denotes afterlife bliss. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says her survey in al-Takāthur, citing 102:8, found this distinction between the two forms. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:2/q02 How does 'by your Lord's favor' fit the sentence?  [بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ · grammar]
turns on: The disagreement is over what governs the first bāʾ phrase and whether a circumstantial attachment would restrict the denial to the Prophet's favored state.
- 68:2/q02/p1 It gives the cause or means of the negation: madness is absent through God's favor. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq attach it to the negation; the comparison is attributing intelligence or freedom from poverty to God's blessing. Al-Ṭabrisī estimates an omitted removal of madness; Ibn al-Ḥājib attaches it to the verbal sense of mā, favored by Ibn Hishām, though Ibn ʿĀshūr objects that grammarians generally do not let a particle govern an adverbial. Naṣir al-Dīn calls the bāʾ causal. Al-Samīn attaches it to the conveyed sentence meaning. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(صاحب المنتخب); ALUSI-FULL(ناصر الدين); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الحاجب,ابن هشام); ELMALILI(Zemahşerî); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 68:2/q02/p2 It is a circumstantial phrase attached to the negated predicate majnūn or its pronoun. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the extra predicate bāʾ does not block majnūn from governing an earlier qualifier, just as an affirmed predicate can; the sense is 'not mad while favored.' Al-Qurṭubī gives it an accusative circumstantial role. Al-Nasafī refers to an omitted expression with accusative position governed by majnūn; Elmalılı also reports a stable state from anta. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn object that the scope of negation could imply only madness-with-favor is denied; Naṣir al-Dīn and al-Ālūsī raise the same restriction, and Bayḍāwī questions its sense. Al-Khafājī illustrates the scope with a line about a campfire at night. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي); IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ELMALILI(Zemahşerî); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(ناصر الدين)-]
- 68:2/q02/p3 It is a circumstantial phrase governed by the sense of negation, qualifying the Prophet while favored. — Bayḍāwī treats the meaning of negation as its governor; al-Bursawī describes an omitted state qualifying the pronoun in majnūn. Al-Ālūsī says the first bāʾ marks association and the second is emphatic; Naṣir al-Dīn prefers the unrestricted denial while the Prophet has sound judgment, prophecy and courage. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ناصر الدين)+]
- 68:2/q02/p4 It is a parenthetical praise, 'praise be to God,' interrupting subject and predicate. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī report this idiomatic aside; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares 'you are, praise be to God, virtuous,' and al-Samīn notes that Ibn ʿAṭiyya leaves the bāʾ's governor unexplained. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers the ordinary Arabic aside and rejects a merely semantic alternative. [12 notes: THALABI(قائل غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ELMALILI(İbn Atıyye); BINTSHATI+; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)-]
- 68:2/q02/p5 An omitted conjunction gives 'you are not mad, and the favor belongs to your Lord.' — Compared to 'Glory be to You, O God, and praise be to You.' Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī cite Labīd and al-Nābigha for understood words in coordinated phrases; al-Samīn says Labīd's line illustrates meaning rather than a syntactic parse. Abū Ḥayyān describes this as a semantic gloss, and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects it as no parsing. [10 notes: THALABI(النابغة,قائل غير مسمى,لبيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,لبيد والنابغة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)-]
- 68:2/q02/p6 The bāʾ introduces an intervening oath by the Lord's favor. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī observe that bāʾ as well as wāw marks oaths. The Shaykh, Abū Ḥayyān and Najm al-Dīn prefer this to a restricted circumstantial denial; the oath's answer may be unstated or inferred from the surrounding clause. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو حيان,نجم الدين); ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 68:2/q02/p7 A parenthetical clause has the bāʾ attached to an omitted predicate: 'that is by your Lord's favor.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this because it follows Arabic usage, citing 11:41 and al-Faḍl ibn ʿAbbās al-Lāhibī's verse using bi-niʿmat Allāh. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الفضل بن عباس اللهبي); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 68:2/q03 What does 'your Lord' add to the wording?  [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 68:2/q03/p1 It honors the Prophet and promises God's continued favor and elevation. — Al-Ālūsī says calling God his Lord signals that God will complete His favor and raise him to unmatched heights. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 68:2/q04 How are mā, anta, and bi-majnūn parsed?  [مَآ أَنتَ بِمَجْنُونٍۢ · grammar]
- 68:2/q04/p1 Mā has anta as its subject and majnūn as its predicate; the predicate bāʾ affects its form. — Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī give subject and predicate; the grammarians cited by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ call bāʾ extra, assigning surface genitive to majnūn while its underlying predicate position remains nominative or accusative in their differing descriptions. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 68:2/q05 What force does the bāʾ before majnūn carry?  [بِمَجْنُونٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the grammatical label 'extra' adequately describes the bāʾ's necessary semantic effect, and whether that effect is emphasis or categorical denial.
- 68:2/q05/p1 It intensifies the negation. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī say the added bāʾ reinforces denial. Ibn Hishām counts such added bāʾs as meaningful emphasis. Ibn ʿĀshūr counts it with the oath and nominal negative clause as three reinforcements against 68:51. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts this bāʾ with the emphatic lām of the disbelievers' assertion at the sūra's end. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(النحويون من المفسرين); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI-]
- 68:2/q05/p2 It makes the denial categorical; the particle has a necessary semantic role. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects 'extra' as implying dispensability. Her survey finds bāʾ on explicit single-word predicates of mā and laysa, usually a categorical denial; in negative questions it yields insistent affirmation. She cites many Qur'anic examples, including 27:81, 8:51, 2:74, 75:40 and 39:36. She says grammarians assign the bāʾ to wording rather than meaning. [7 notes: BINTSHATI(النحويون من المفسرين); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI+]

## 68:2/q06 What does majnūn mean and how is it formed?  [بِمَجْنُونٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical sense is shared; the derivational issue concerns attested Arabic usage versus a regular formation.
- 68:2/q06/p1 A person deprived of reason, whose mind is covered. — Al-Naḥḥās relates the root to night, fetus, grave, and shield covering something; a line by ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa uses majnī for a shield. Al-Jishumī says junūn is lost reason from concealment; al-Bursawī calls it a barrier between soul and intellect and allows jinn striking, heart injury, or separation of soul and intellect. [4 notes: NAHHAS(عمر بن أبي ربيعة); JISHUMI; BURSEVI]
- 68:2/q06/p2 Its attested form majnūn follows Arabic usage although regular derivation would give majn. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the idiom ajanna fa-huwa majnūn and contrasts the regular majn; al-Mubarrad favors a regular derivation from janna. [2 notes: NAHHAS; NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد)+]

## 68:2/q07 What does the denial of madness establish about the Prophet?  [بِمَجْنُونٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes move from a direct denial of insanity to what that denial proves about character, mission, or success.
- 68:2/q07/p1 The charge is false, and God has given the Prophet reason, virtue, and sound character. — Al-Naḥḥās calls him the wisest of his time; al-Rāzī points to eloquence, intellect, conduct, virtue and lack of defects, and says 38:86 rules out affected character because pretense eventually fails. Al-Biqāʿī recalls forty years of trusted conduct and the Qur'an's coherence, citing 17:88 and 4:82. Al-Muntakhab says favor removes a blameworthy trait and gives praiseworthy traits; the Study Quran links 68:4's character to the opposite of possession. [18 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:2/q07/p2 The denial vindicates his messengership and the divine guidance he brings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the polytheists called him mad to avoid admitting his mission, so denying madness restores that claim. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says prophethood itself excludes the mental disorder alleged to invalidate guidance. Ibn Kathīr contrasts their ignorance with the manifest truth he brought; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the opening affirms his mission against those calling the miracle ancient tales. [7 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 68:2/q07/p3 The phrase also denies failure or ill fortune. — Al-Māwardī reports al-Kalbī's nonliteral gloss of the Prophet as not unsuccessful. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي)]

## 68:2/q08 What accusation or event prompted this assurance?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports vary between a general Quraysh charge, an account after Ḥirāʾ, named opponents, and an insult against ʿAlī; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects restricting general wording by reported occasions.
- 68:2/q08/p1 Meccan or Quraysh opponents called Muhammad mad, sometimes alleging demonic possession. — 15:6 preserves their taunt that the recipient of the Reminder is mad. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī specify a devil or demon; Ibn Jurayj says the charge preceded revelation of this assurance. Al-Kashshāf, al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı attribute it to envy and hostility; Kur'an Yolu explains that Meccans linked poets, seers and magicians to jinn. Asad says the taunt persisted for years; Iṣlāḥī says his certainty about punishment and compassionate preaching prompted charges of hallucination and magic. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ ties the early charge to his call to abandon ancestral idols. [25 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(عطاء وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:2/q08/p2 After the first revelation at Ḥirāʾ, Khadīja consulted Waraqa; Quraysh later called him mad. — Ibn ʿAbbās's account has Gabriel teach ablution and prayer, Khadīja search for the Prophet and Waraqa promise support before dying. Al-Bursawī retells it. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites hadith and sīra in which Khadīja reassures him using his virtues and Waraqa predicts denial, harm, fighting and exile. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; BINTSHATI(خديجة,ورقة بن نوفل)]
- 68:2/q08/p3 Much of the sūra concerned al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra and Abū Jahl. — Abū Ḥayyān names them; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says reports naming either do not limit the verse's general wording without contextual evidence, though occasions provide clues. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BINTSHATI-]
- 68:2/q08/p4 Quraysh insulted ʿAlī after the Prophet honored him; the passage contrasts slanderers with the guided. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk's report identifies ʿAlī among the guided and his Quraysh detractors among those astray. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الضحاك)]

## 68:2/q09 How does this verse answer the preceding oath and its objects?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes agree the oath leads to the denial but vary over its objects and why writing or scripture is apt.
- 68:2/q09/p1 It is the answer to the oath in 68:1, denying madness and introducing reward and great character. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı and al-Ālūsī identify the oath's answer. Muqātil as reported by al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī name the whale or Nūn, pen, and deeds or writing; al-Rāzī continues the sworn statements through reward and character. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:2/q09/p2 The divinely created pen and angelic writing of decrees frame the denial. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this follows if Nūn is the fish; Abū Ḥayyān also reports the cosmic-fish reading, whose pen records divine decrees and whose writers are angels. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 68:2/q09/p3 The familiar pen and human writing frame the denial. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbās if Nūn is an inkpot; Abū Ḥayyān reports a name interpretation and cites Ibn ʿAbbās. He also notes that mā can be relative or a verbal noun, the writer pronoun can indicate angels or all writers through the written book, and al-Zamakhsharī lets pen stand for its users, their pages or writing. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 68:2/q09/p4 Writing is a fitting oath because it underlies knowledge and worldly and otherworldly affairs. — Abū Ḥayyān calls the pen a universal divine blessing and companion to speech. Elmalılı says the ensuing denial, favor, understanding, character and reward identify the praised side of the pen's significance. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 68:2/q09/p5 The pen and writing signify the universal Intellect, Soul, forms and destined states. — Al-Qāshānī reads Nūn as universal Soul, Pen as universal Intellect, and intermediate intellects and holy spirits as metaphorical scribes while God truly writes. Al-Bursawī says these encompass existence and divine determination, fitting the sworn declaration. [3 notes: BURSEVI(القاشاني)]
- 68:2/q09/p6 The oath by earlier scriptures and the Qur'an confirms that his teaching continues the prophets' message. — Iṣlāḥī says prophets from Adam to Jesus taught the same truth and history confirms it. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 68:2/q10 How is the verse linked to the accusation in 15:6?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether textual correspondence is treated as thematic exegesis or as a temporally sequenced reply, especially for oath-formula lā.
- 68:2/q10/p1 It directly answers the taunt that the recipient of the Reminder is mad. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and the Study Quran cite 15:6, sometimes 15:7. Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, via Ibn Hishām, uses the pair to illustrate one Qur'anic passage explaining another. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(عطاء وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 68:2/q10/p2 The two passages cannot be treated as a chronological reply within an oath formula. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues al-Qalam was revealed much earlier than al-Ḥijr and that transmitted readings join lā to uqsimu; her objection specifically concerns making oath-formula lā answer earlier speech. Al-Itqān reports the contrary possibility that lā denies a preceding rejection of resurrection across sūras. [1 notes: ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]

## 68:2/q11 How does the opening denial relate to the end of al-Qalam?  [* · links]
- 68:2/q11/p1 The opening refutes the closing accusation that Muhammad is mad. — Al-Biqāʿī, al-Suyūṭī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and al-Sāmarrāʾī cite 68:51. Al-Biqāʿī says the denial comes first to honor and comfort him, with praise of character intervening; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the closing supports him. Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes opening emphatic bāʾ answers the closing emphatic lām. [11 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 68:2/q12 Why does al-Ṭūr add 'soothsayer' to its parallel denial?  [* · links]
- 68:2/q12/p1 Al-Ṭūr lists broader accusations and themes, whereas al-Qalam answers madness alone. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites accusations of madness, poetry and fabrication at 52:29–33, the jinn-listening or seer context of 52:38, and magic at 52:15. Al-Ṭūr's oath in 52:1–6 is longer than al-Qalam's brief pen oath, fitting the added charge; al-Qalam closes at 68:51 with madness alone. Al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr note 52:29 as a parallel. [9 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 68:2/q13 How does the preceding sūra al-Mulk prepare this denial?  [* · links]
- 68:2/q13/p1 Al-Mulk's proofs of divine power, knowledge and Qur'anic truth make madness untenable. — Abū Ḥayyān says the earlier account of the blessed and wretched answers claims of poetry, magic and madness. Al-Biqāʿī says 67:29 leaves guidance versus error open before evidence decides for the Prophet; al-Mulk's signs, including 67:3, 67:5 and 67:14, exceed ordinary reason. Ibn al-Zubayr emphasizes the inexhaustible proof in seven layered heavens, glossing ṭibāqan as closely matching layers like a sandal sole; the report carries no grading. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 68:2/q14 What is reported about when this verse was revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The accounts differ over which passage was first: the opening of al-ʿAlaq or 87:1.
- 68:2/q14/p1 It belongs to the sūra's first five verses among the earliest revelation after Ḥirāʾ. — Al-Rāzī gives Ibn ʿAbbās's Ḥirāʾ narrative with 96:1, then says God swore against Quraysh's charge in the first five verses. Al-Bursawī repeats the narrative and early-five-verse claim. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ dates the dispute to the stage when only the first verses of al-ʿAlaq were revealed and takes Nūn as signaling miraculous expression. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 68:2/q14/p2 It was the second revelation, after 'Glorify the name of your Lord Most High.' — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Rāzī and al-Bursawī, naming 87:1 as first. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]

## 68:2/q15 How does the assurance affect the Prophet and later believers?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The reported extension to later followers broadens the immediate reassurance to Muhammad.
- 68:2/q15/p1 It consoles and steadies the Prophet against hostile accusations. — Al-Qushayrī says God removes distress; al-Biqāʿī says the great oath comforts him and the enhanced denial honors him. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the following verses as support; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Khadīja's and Waraqa's assurances prepared him for the burden of the mission. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 68:2/q15/p2 The passage applies to followers whose moral judgments rest on belief in God and the afterlife. — Asad explicitly broadens its reach beyond the original accusation. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 68:2/q16 What rhetorical pattern do these verse endings illustrate?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The rhetorical works use the passage to exemplify different sound and composition figures.
- 68:2/q16/p1 Balanced ornament, including tarsīʿ with tajnīs, related figures, and close sounding endings. — Al-Bāqillānī lists Qur'anic parallels including 68:3, 7:201, 100:7, 52:1–2 and 79:3–4, then Imruʾ al-Qays, Abū Nuwās and Ibn al-Rūmī for tarsīʿ. He cites al-Khansāʾ for muḍāraʿa, sayings attributed to al-Manṣūr, ʿUmar ibn Dharr, Bashshār and Arabs for takāfuʾ near antithesis, and Imruʾ al-Qays for taʿaṭṭuf. Al-Burhān says Qur'anic endings use identical or phonetically close letters, citing al-Qamar and al-Fātiḥa. [6 notes: BAQILLANI; BURHAN]
- 68:2/q16/p2 The adjacent endings exemplify iltizām, committing to extra letters before the rhyme. — Al-Itqān defines luzūm mā lā yalzam as an unforced commitment to one or more letters before the rhyme and says 68:2–3 commit to two. Nearby it reports Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ's twin-meter verse device and other theorists' extension of tashrīʿ to prose in which a complete clause gains a second rhyming clause. [3 notes: ITQAN(آخرون,ابن أبي الإصبع)]

## 68:2/q17 What does this verse illustrate about reading Qur'anic passages together?  [* · links]
turns on: How far textual parallels may be treated as direct answers despite independent contexts and chronology.
- 68:2/q17/p1 One passage can clarify another, as this denial answers the accusation elsewhere. — Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, cited through Ibn Hishām, says the Qur'an is like one sūra and pairs 15:6 with 68:2. Ibn ʿĀshūr counts passage-to-passage clarification, specification, qualification and implication as internal exegesis. Al-Itqān reports that even an oath's reply may cross sūras. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 68:2/q17/p2 Cross-passage clarification is valid, but treating the entire Qur'an as one sūra is too broad. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says some verses stand independently and analogous passages need not share a meaning. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ warns that chronology prevents making the oath lā in al-Qalam a direct reply to al-Ḥijr. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 68:2/q18 What supporting disciplines and reports does Ibn ʿĀshūr associate with interpreting such passages?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The disagreement concerns whether a discipline is a prerequisite source of tafsīr or merely aids fuller argument.
- 68:2/q18/p1 Uṣūl al-fiqh supplies language methods and organized rules for deriving legal meanings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it explains implication, contrary implication and other methods grammarians omit; al-Ghazālī counts it among Qur'anic and legal sciences. He says it also systematizes inference from commands, prohibitions and general wording. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:2/q18/p2 Kalām is a prerequisite because one must establish that God speaks or know what can be ascribed to Him. — ʿAbd al-Ḥakīm gives the first reason; al-Ālūsī, deriving from al-Jurjānī, gives the second and cites 20:5. Ibn ʿĀshūr finds al-Ālūsī's rationale closer but rejects both as requirements for tafsīr: God speaking was established before kalām, and extended argument does not make a discipline a source of exegesis. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الآلوسي,عبد الحكيم); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 68:2/q18/p3 Fiqh can enrich exegesis but is derived from it and is not a prerequisite source. — Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects al-Suyūṭī's inclusion of fiqh as a tafsīr source; it helps elaborate legal meanings, etiquette and inference. He quotes Bayḍāwī's demand for broad religious, Arabic and literary learning. Tafsīr remains a principal science while borrowing detail, as ʿAbd al-Ḥakīm explains. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:2/q18/p4 Prophetic and Companion verse explanations are themselves tafsīr. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes these reports from external disciplines on which tafsīr draws. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:2/q19 How does the later phrase about al-maftūn describe who is mad?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether al-maftūn names an affliction, an afflicted person, or a group containing the madman.
- 68:2/q19/p1 Al-maftūn is a verbal noun meaning trial or temptation. — Al-Ṭabrisī compares maʿqūl and maḥṣūl used for a thing's rationality or outcome. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 68:2/q19/p2 Al-maftūn is a passive participle, 'the afflicted one,' with an extra bāʾ. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports that the clause depends on 'they will see.' [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 68:2/q19/p3 The bāʾ means 'in': which of the Muslims or disbelievers contains the madman. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports al-Farrāʾ and cites a rajaz line using an extra bāʾ in 'we hope for relief.' [1 notes: TABRISI(الفراء)]
