# 68:1: 30 questions

## 68:1/q01 What does نٓ designate?  [نٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether ن is the written letter name, an independently inflected noun, an abbreviation or an allusive symbol; grammatical case, script form, reported authorities and context are invoked.
- 68:1/q01/p1 The isolated alphabet letter nūn, like other sūra initials. — Al-Mubarrad, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Qushayrī and others appeal to its written single-letter form, sukūn and lack of noun inflection. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the written sign from the pronounced letter name. Abū Ḥayyān calls case assignment conjectural; Elmalılı leaves its deeper import open. [39 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة وابن كيسان); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(ابن كيسان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القشيري); ALUSI-FULL(السلف); ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyan,Ebû Nasr Abdurrahîm Kuşeyrî,Sâhib-Keşşâf); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BINTSHATI(الراغب الأصفهاني)+; ELMALILI+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري أبو نصر عبد الرحيم)+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد)+]
- 68:1/q01/p2 A great fish supporting the earth or seven earths. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid reports put it below the lowest or seventh earth; movement shakes earth until mountains fix it. Wāḥidī cites Jonah's title Dhū al-Nūn at 21:87 for Arabic fish usage. Biqāʿī and Elmalılı offer symbolic or geological elaborations. [74 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قيل); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(أبو هريرة); THALABI(ابن عباس,مجاهد ومقاتل ومرة الهمداني وعطاء الخراساني والسدي والكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل والسدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس ومجاهد ومقاتل وعطاء والسدي والكلبي والواقدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس عن أبي ظبيان,ابن عباس ومجاهد ومقاتل والسدي والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد ومقاتل والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، مجاهد، السدي، ابن السائب، مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، مقاتل، السدي,بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد,مقاتل ومرة الهمداني وعطاء الخراساني والسدي والكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(قوم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); ITQAN(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(بعض مفسران); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas ve Mücâhid,ابن عباس,بعض المفسرين,مجاهد,مفسرون); BINTSHATI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل)-; RAZI-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)-; BINTSHATI-; ELMALILI-]
- 68:1/q01/p3 An inkwell. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan and Qatāda are repeatedly cited; a verse about streaming tears is offered as lexical evidence, but al-Ālūsī disputes its attestation. - 68:1/q01/p4 The final letter of the divine name al-Raḥmān, joined to alif-lām-rā and ḥā-mīm. — Tustarī and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās join the three openings into الرحمن; Ibn Qutayba defends letter abbreviations by Arab shorthand, while Rāzī objects that this licenses esoteric excess. [16 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); SULAMI(سهل); THALABI(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد,عكرمة عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); RAZI-FULL-]
- 68:1/q01/p5 A luminous tablet or the Preserved Tablet. — A report raised through Muʿāwiya ibn Qurra identifies a tablet of light; some versions pair it with a luminous pen that records until Resurrection. Ibn Kathīr calls a version mursal and gharīb. - 68:1/q01/p6 The name or opening title of this sūra. — Ibn Kaysān calls it its opening. Al-Qushayrī argues the indeclinable letter can serve as a sūra name; Ibn ʿAṭiyya takes it as a feminine proper name, while Bint al-Shāṭiʾ finds that explanation unhelpful. - 68:1/q01/p7 An opening letter standing for divine names beginning with nūn, such as Nūr, Nāṣir and Naṣīr. — Named interpreters vary the list; al-Qurazī instead takes the nūn to invoke God's naṣr, His support of believers, citing 30:47. - 68:1/q01/p9 A river in Paradise. — Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq is repeatedly cited; al-Bāqir's report says its water became sweet white ink for the Pen. - 68:1/q01/p10 Jonah's fish, recalled near the sūra's end. — Rāzī reports this fish identification; Elmalılı cites 68:48, 21:87 and 37:142 but objects that an explicit first reference would better say companion of the fish; Iṣlāḥī sees a deliberate allusion. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI(bazıları,مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI-]
- 68:1/q01/p11 The whale connected with Nimrod's bloodied arrow. — Rāzī reports this as a distinct identification. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 68:1/q01/p12 Ink itself. — Rāzī says angels use it; Ibn Qurṣa gives the gloss. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن قرصة)]
- 68:1/q01/p13 A sword or sword edge. — Biqāʿī sees sword and pen as power and knowledge; Elmalılı says Arabic permits this lexical sense but notes no transmitted identification here. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 68:1/q01/p14 A soul or universal Soul. — Māwardī proposes the soul whose fortune the pen decrees; Qāshānī makes it the Universal Soul receiving forms from Universal Intellect. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QASHANI]
- 68:1/q01/p15 A sign of pre-eternity or divine unity. — Jaʿfar's inward reading makes it pre-eternity and the origin of lights; Bursevī and other gnostics make it Essence, collective unity, or undifferentiated knowledge. [5 notes: SULAMI(جعفر); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,بعض العارفين)]
- 68:1/q01/p16 An oath object whose particular identity is unspecified. — Qatāda and Ibn Zayd say God swears by what He wills; Ibn ʿAbbās likens it to an oath by God's name. Māwardī suggests it signifies divine bringing-into-being if no report is established. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 68:1/q01/p17 A name of God. — Tustarī states this directly; a report from Ibn ʿAbbās calls it one of God's names. [2 notes: TUSTARI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 68:1/q01/p18 The Pen itself, the Prophet's name, his strength of heart, or a command to persist. — Al-Jāḥiẓ's Pen gloss, Ibn ʿAsākir's prophetic-name view, al-Kirmānī's imperative, and an anonymous heart-strength oath are separate minority suggestions. [4 notes: ITQAN(ابن عساكر,الكرماني)]
- 68:1/q01/p19 A Persian expression or a 'correct word'. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk supplies the Persian phrase; Biqāʿī lists the correct-word sense among proposed referents. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL]

## 68:1/q02 How is the final nūn of نٓ pronounced before وَٱلْقَلَمِ?  [نٓ وَٱلْقَلَمِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the isolated letter name is treated as paused or connected to wāw, and which reader or transmission route is followed.
- 68:1/q02/p1 It is pronounced clearly, as at a pause. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ṭabarī prefer clarity because letter names are spelled and treated as paused; al-Fārisī explains the isolated name as separate. Ibn al-Jazarī distinguishes transmission routes and says several readers have this as their sole form. ## 68:1/q03 What vowel or case is read on نٓ?  [نٓ · readings]
turns on: The transmissions and whether ن is treated as a paused letter, sūra name, or noun under an implied verb or oath particle.
- 68:1/q03/p1 The usual letter-name reading has final sukūn. — A named isolated letter is stopped even in connection; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this the mutawātir form. Sibawayh and Naḥḥās explain alphabet letters' lack of ordinary inflection. [15 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء,سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة وحفص عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السلف); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:1/q03/p2 It is read with kasra. — Some supply a deleted oath preposition; Abū Ḥātim, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn explain it as resolving adjacent sukūns. Al-Samīn rejects genitive after a deleted oath preposition as unusual here and requiring tanwīn. [15 notes: NAHHAS(أبو حاتم,ابن أبي إسحاق); THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن أبي إسحاق والحسن,بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، أبو رزين، قتادة، الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ونصر وابن أبي إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن أبي إسحاق والحسن وأبو السمال); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:1/q03/p3 It is read with fatḥa or accusative. — ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar's reading is explained by an omitted imperative 'mention/recite Nūn'; other explanations make it a light built form, an omitted oath preposition in a weak dialect, or an indeclinable feminine sūra name. Al-Samīn cites 38:84. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو حاتم,الأخفش سعيد,عيسى بن عمر); THALABI(عيسى بن عمر); JISHUMI(عيسى بن عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، أبو رزين، قتادة، الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن أبي إسحاق والحسن وأبو السمال); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 68:1/q03/p4 It is read with ḍamma. — Ibn al-Jawzī names al-Ḥasan, Abū ʿImrān and Abū Nuhayk; Qurṭubī names Hārūn and Ibn al-Sumayqiʿ, treating it as a built form. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن، أبو عمران، أبو نهيك); QURTUBI-FULL(هارون ومحمد ابن السميقع)]

## 68:1/q04 What is the linguistic range of the name nūn?  [نٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the question concerns attested nouns, a letter name or symbolic sound and shape.
- 68:1/q04/p1 Arabic nūn can name a fish, inkwell, letter, sword edge and other objects. — Wāḥidī cites Dhū al-Nūn at 21:87 for fish; the inkpot is supported by an anonymous poem but its authenticity is contested. Naḥḥās distinguishes plurals نونات for letter names and نينان or أنوان for fish. Biqāʿī and Elmalılı list further senses including sword and correct word. [14 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الشاعر); THALABI(الشاعر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، مقاتل، السدي,الضحاك، الحسن، قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(İbn Atıyye ve Râzî); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 68:1/q04/p2 Its named letter sound and shape suggest a range of manifested knowledge. — Biqāʿī relates tongue placement, nasalization, the repetition of its sound, tanwīn, light, growth and emergence to knowledge; Elmalılı notes the dotted cup shape and resonance. [25 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 68:1/q05 Why does the sūra open with نٓ?  [نٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Its relation to the sūra's argument, the status of disjoint letters, and whether phonetic, symbolic, or reported referents are intended.
- 68:1/q05/p1 The familiar letters challenge hearers to match the Qurʾān made from their own alphabet. — Abū Muslim and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connect the letter to inimitability; she stresses the early dispute over 'tales of the ancients' and the first revelation's Read and Pen. Elmalılı also speaks of challenge and testing. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI+; ELMALILI+]
- 68:1/q05/p2 It attracts attention to the revelation that follows. — Quṭrub ties this to Quraysh's command not to listen (41:26); others observe that letter openings commonly precede Qurʾānic content. [4 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي قطرب); BURHAN; BINTSHATI(قول)]
- 68:1/q05/p3 It honors alphabetic speech as the basis of revelation, names and human worship. — Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Qayyim emphasize the alphabet's role in divine books and human speech; an isolated letter's full force emerges within a word. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BINTSHATI(ابن القيم)]
- 68:1/q05/p4 Its full significance belongs to God among the ambiguous openings. — Abū Bakr and al-Shaʿbī call these letters the Qurʾān's secret; Bursevī and Elmalılı preserve ambiguity even while giving possible senses. [10 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الشعبي); STUDYQURAN]
- 68:1/q05/p5 It symbolizes encompassing knowledge and the manifestation of hidden things. — Biqāʿī derives this from articulation, shape, repeated nūn words and its partnership with Qāf and kāf in 'Be'; he makes the name and pen signal the sūra's unveiling of guidance. [46 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 68:1/q05/p6 Its letter and the sūra's nūn sounds fit its rhyme and vocabulary. — Zarkashī's proposal is cited by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, who objects that many endings lack the claimed pattern. [3 notes: BURHAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 68:1/q05/p7 It is an abbreviation or numerical sign with a hidden expansion. — Reports give divine names or an abjad calculation; Ibn Jinnī objects that altered initials could not be fixed divine names. - 68:1/q05/p8 It alludes to the cosmic fish as a sign of divine power. — Jishumī and Ṭabrisī contrast sea animals dying on land with land animals dying in water; Bursevī draws a lesson from the fish's burden. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); BURSEVI]

## 68:1/q06 What happened in reports about the primordial Pen and the fish?  [نٓ وَٱلْقَلَمِ · reports]
turns on: Different report chains, the order of creation, cosmological details, names and the status of literal versus interpretive readings.
- 68:1/q06/p1 God first created the Pen, ordered it to write destiny, then created the fish, spread earth upon it and steadied its movement with mountains. — Ibn ʿAbbās versions vary on whether water vapor forms heaven before or after the fish and whether the fish is above water or below the seventh earth; some explicitly recite 68:1–2. [8 notes: NAHHAS(أبو هريرة); THALABI(الرواة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الرواة,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(البغوي وجماعة من المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL]

Ibn ʿĀshūr relates the rows to cutting and quotes Abū Tammām. Samarāʾī contrasts saṭr's extended lines with kataba's possible single letter or word. [15 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو تمام); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 68:1/q12/p2 It can extend to doing or knowing. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported separately with both glosses; these are interpretive extensions beyond the usual lexical 'write'. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 68:1/q12/p3 Its imperfective form covers present and future writing. — Elmalılı says Turkish cannot fully carry this aspect and emphasizes that the Highest Pen's inscription continues. [4 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı)]

## 68:1/q13 How does مَا function with يَسْطُرُونَ?  [وَمَا يَسْطُرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā denotes the product, the act or the writers themselves.
- 68:1/q13/p1 It is a relative pronoun: 'what they write'. — The understood object pronoun can be omitted; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the object or product of writing is the target. Many allow this alongside the verbal noun. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:1/q13/p2 It is maṣdariyya: 'their writing' as an act. — Ṭabarī calls the resulting oath one by the book or writing; Ṭabrisī contrasts the oath by the written object. Rāzī and others accept both constructions. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 68:1/q13/p3 It means 'who'—the scribes themselves. — Thaʿlabī gives 'those who write them'; Ālūsī considers this forced compared with relative or verbal-noun mā. [1 notes: THALABI; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 68:1/q14 How is the plural subject of يَسْطُرُونَ supplied?  [يَسْطُرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the plural is supplied from human scribes, angelic scribes, many instruments or an honorific reference to one Pen.
- 68:1/q14/p1 The pen implies its rational owners or scribes by ellipsis or metonymy. — Zamakhsharī compares the understood possessor at 24:40; Elmalılı stresses that the sound masculine plural signals intelligent beings behind a singular tool. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers the unexpressed subject from the pen. [18 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı)]
- 68:1/q14/p2 It refers to many pens, with writing attributed to instruments as though they were agents. — Bayḍāwī and Biqāʿī describe personification of a genus; Ālūsī says tools stand for their users. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:1/q14/p3 It refers honorifically to the single primordial Pen. — Rāzī and Ālūsī report a plural of honor despite one heavenly Pen. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:1/q14/p4 It refers to angels known from the heavenly Pen and Tablet. — The cosmic-fish interpretation pairs the primordial Pen with angels, according to Ibn ʿAṭiyya's conditional distinction. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(İbn Atıyye)]

## 68:1/q15 What does the opening oath swear by and how is it joined?  [نٓ وَٱلْقَلَمِ وَمَا يَسْطُرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether Nūn is itself sworn by, whether the first wāw is oath or conjunction, and how coordination transfers oath force.
- 68:1/q15/p1 The wāw before the Pen begins or continues an oath, and the wāw before mā coordinates another oath object. — Naḥḥās says the first wāw coordinates with Nūn rather than beginning a new oath; Elmalılı says this depends on whether Nūn itself is an oath. Samarāʾī argues coordinated objects inherit the oath force, citing 79:1–5. [10 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(النحاة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 68:1/q15/p2 An omitted 'Lord of' yields an oath by the Lord of Nūn and Pen. — Naḥḥās and Jishumī transmit this ellipsis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI]
- 68:1/q15/p3 It swears by the pen's writers and their written lines, through metonymy. — Zamakhsharī and Rāzī make al-qalam stand for its users, making the plural pronoun intelligible. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 68:1/q15/p4 The whole oath honors the familiar letter, writing and the proof against the pagans. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers this, taking the wāw as an oath of exaltation; Iṣlāḥī says the oath substantiates the opening claims. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI+]

## 68:1/q16 What is the answer to the oath?  [* · grammar]
- 68:1/q16/p1 The negative assurance in 68:2 that Muhammad is not mad, with further assurance of reward. — Naḥḥās, Qushayrī, Jishumī and al-Samīn identify the next verse; Muqātil supplies the accusers Abū Jahl, ʿUtba and Shayba. Ibn ʿĀshūr points also to 68:51 and 81:22; Iṣlāḥī says the oath supports three opening claims. [10 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(عيسى بن عمر); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 68:1/q17 How does this opening answer its opponents?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The nature of the opponents' charge and whether the pen points to scripture itself or to writing more generally.
- 68:1/q17/p1 It refutes the charge of madness and vindicates Muhammad's revelation, character and reward. — Muqātil names Abū Jahl, ʿUtba and Shayba; al-Qalam compares the Prophet with corrupt Quraysh leadership. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ emphasizes the pagans' allegation of ancient tales; the closing verses return to the vindication. [25 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(ابن إسحاق,بعض المفسرين); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 68:1/q17/p2 The Qurʾān and its writing provide the evidence for prophethood. — Iṣlāḥī argues the recorded Qurʾān's elevated discourse and earlier scriptures testify; Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the pen and writing to the Book that caused the taunts. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Muj āhid)]

## 68:1/q18 How does the opening connect to other verses and sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the comparison uses muṣḥaf adjacency, revelation chronology, shared words or thematic parallels.
- 68:1/q18/p1 It follows al-Mulk's warning and final image of water, moving from divine power to knowledge and guidance. — Jishumī and Abū Ḥayyān connect denials and threats; Biqāʿī makes life-giving water in 67:30 a transition to reviving spirits with knowledge. Iṣlāḥī sees a pair with a sterner second member; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects that chronological revelation order does not support an original sequential context. [9 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI-]
- 68:1/q18/p2 It recalls the first revelation's teaching by the pen in 96:1–5. — Asad, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connect Read, letters, writing and prophethood; al-Qalam's early position strengthens the link. [13 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:1/q18/p3 It alludes ahead to Jonah as companion of the fish at 68:48. — Iṣlāḥī calls the surviving fish sense of Nūn a sūra-name allusion; Elmalılı accepts an allusion while rejecting it as primary. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI(bazıları,مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 68:1/q18/p4 It pairs with Qāf as knowledge and power. — Biqāʿī contrasts hidden fish or nūn knowledge with visible encircling Mount Qāf and kings' bodily power; both initials encompass the public Qurʾānic section. [14 notes: BIQAI-FULL(بعض السلف)]
- 68:1/q18/p5 Its short oath suits a shorter denial than al-Ṭūr's long oath. — Samarāʾī compares 52:1–6 with this verse and relates the extra denial of soothsaying in al-Ṭūr to the extended oath. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 68:1/q18/p6 The closure circles back to the opening vindication. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees 68:51 and the closing support for the Prophet returning to the opening denial of madness. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 68:1/q19 What is reported about this sūra's name, place, sequence and verses?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Transmission on chronology and count, and whether portions are distinguished from the sūra as a whole.
- 68:1/q19/p1 It is Meccan, has fifty-two verses, and is named Nūn, al-Qalam or Nūn wa-l-Qalam. — Jishumī, Bayḍāwī and Suyūṭī give the count; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes names in tafsirs, ḥadīth collections and a fifth-century Kufic codex. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ excepts 17–33 and 48–50 from the early Meccan material. [10 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:1/q19/p2 It was among the earliest revelations, often ranked second after al-ʿAlaq. — Thaʿlabī instead places al-Muddaththir before it and al-Ḍuḥā after; Jābir ibn Zayd ranks it before al-Muzzammil and al-Muddaththir, while Ibn ʿĀshūr favors ʿĀʾisha's al-Muddaththir report. Asad, Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and Study Quran call this the earliest disjoint-letter opening. [11 notes: THALABI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:1/q19/p3 A report promises reciters reward like people of excellent character. — Jishumī transmits a report from Ubayy without a grading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(النبي)]
- 68:1/q19/p4 Most of it concerns al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra and Abū Jahl. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr report this; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports the attribution. Separate ʿAlī reports assign the unguided accusers and guided status to him. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين); TABRISI(الضحاك بن مزاحم); TABATABAI(الضحاك)]
- 68:1/q19/p5 Counting the isolated letter as a verse depends on regional convention. — Zarkashī says Basrans count no disconnected opening as verses, Kufans count some, and Wāḥidī singles out Ṭā Hā. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 68:1/q20 What inward account does the verse give of existence and knowledge?  [* · inward]
turns on: The correspondence assigned to Nūn, Pen and inscription, and whether the lesson concerns cosmology, knowledge or spiritual conduct.
- 68:1/q20/p1 Nūn is Universal Soul, Pen is Universal Intellect, and their inscription produces forms and states of beings. — Qāshānī says intermediary intellects and holy spirits figuratively write, while God is the real writer; the oath honors the beginnings of divine ordination. [8 notes: QASHANI; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:1/q20/p2 Nūn is undifferentiated essential knowledge, Pen is detailed named knowledge, and writing unfolds the Book of Existence. — Bursevī cites al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya's seed and tree analogy and the hidden Book's letters, words, verses and sūras; divine knowledge is identical with Essence in his account. [6 notes: BURSEVI(الإمام علي,التأويلات النجمية,بعض العارفين,بعض الكبار)]
- 68:1/q20/p3 Nūn is pre-eternity and primordial light specially bestowed on Muhammad. — Jaʿfar says Muhammad's great character at 68:4 is the pre-eternal light singled out for him. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 68:1/q20/p4 The fish's burden teaches reward for bearing the divine trust. — Bursevī says the fish was sworn by and spared slaughter; likewise the believer who bears trust is spared separation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 68:1/q20/p5 Witnessing divine gentleness yields independence from creatures and praise in both gift and deprivation. — Qushayrī develops this ethical inward lesson. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 68:1/q21 How is the written sign نٓ represented and inflected?  [نٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the written single letter is a paused name or an independent symbol whose possible case is inferred.
- 68:1/q21/p1 The single written sign stands for the pronounced letter name nūn, which is read in a paused, uninflected form. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the exceptional use of one sign for the three-letter name, as in other openings; Zajjāj says sukūn only fits a letter name. Naḥḥās reports al-Farrāʾ's comparison with ثم or the plural نون; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes that a noun would be written in full. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)]
- 68:1/q21/p2 Independent opening letters can bear several possible syntactic cases without a fixed place. — Biqāʿī says an independent comprehensive letter can be parsed as nominative, accusative or genitive because no relation forces one case. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 68:1/q22 What claims are made about the Qurʾān's alphabetic composition?  [نٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether composition from ordinary letters is used to argue miraculous expression or the Qurʾān's createdness.
- 68:1/q22/p1 The fourteen distinct opening letters are half the Arabic alphabet. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ counts fourteen of the alphabet's letters across the openings. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 68:1/q22/p2 Because the Qurʾān is made of the familiar letters, the openings expose the audience's inability to match it. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects the counted letter set to the challenge of inimitability. [2 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 68:1/q22/p3 The letters show that the Qurʾān is composed of letters and is created. — Jishumī reports Abū Bakr al-Zubayrī's theological inference without choosing it. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو بكر الزبيري)]

## 68:1/q23 How are competing reports and explanations of نٓ assessed?  [نٓ · reports]
turns on: The authority of transmitted reports and the grammatical or lexical evidence for each gloss.
- 68:1/q23/p1 Only the alphabet-letter account is reliable among the proposed glosses. — Al-Baḥr rejects the other senses; Ālūsī questions how this squares with al-Ḥākim's authentication and al-Ḍiyāʾ's collection and points to contradictory Ibn ʿAbbās reports. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]
- 68:1/q23/p2 The inkwell gloss is linguistically uncertain. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Zamakhsharī's uncertainty over whether the term is linguistic or revealed, common or proper, and diptote; Ālūsī disputes its poetic witness. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 68:1/q23/p3 A criticism of al-Zamakhsharī's treatment is recorded without its details. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls an argument here erroneous but the note does not reproduce the argument. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]

## 68:1/q24 What parallels are proposed between alphabetic openings?  [نٓ · links]
turns on: Whether a letter's fit is local to its sūra or part of a broader contextual pattern.
- 68:1/q24/p1 Each opening letter is said to fit its own sūra's wording or theme. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports that Qāf's many qāf words span revelation, creation and resurrection; substituting qāf for nūn would spoil the fit. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 68:1/q24/p2 A contextual survey should test one rationale for all letter-opened sūras. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says her proposed pattern also fits al-Rūm's final defense against deniers and appeal for patience (30:58–60). [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 68:1/q24/p3 Al-ʿAnkabūt, al-Rūm and Nūn are exceptions to the usual opening-letter pattern. — Al-Suyūṭī refers the reason to Asrār al-Tanzīl. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 68:1/q25 How is lengthening handled before the final nūn's assimilation?  [نٓ · readings]
turns on: Whether assimilation changes the duration of the prolonged sound before the still consonant.
- 68:1/q25/p1 The madd has a required minimum before the still consonant, though specialists name its degree differently. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports full saturation, enabling the madd, and a rank below hamza lengthening as descriptions. [1 notes: NASHR(أهل الأداء)]
- 68:1/q25/p2 Assimilation takes longer madd than clear pronunciation. — Abū Ḥātim, al-Dānī and others reason that the sound continues through assimilation, comparing dābba and muḥyāy. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو حاتم السجستاني وأبو عمرو الداني)]
- 68:1/q25/p3 Clear pronunciation takes longer madd than assimilation. — Abū al-ʿIzz says the assimilated consonant gains strength from the next consonant and its vowel. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو العز)]
- 68:1/q25/p4 The two take equal madd. — Most readers say both contain adjacent still consonants, the operative cause. [1 notes: NASHR(جمهور القراء وأبو عمرو الداني)]
- 68:1/q25/p5 The same clear versus assimilated choice occurs in other initials. — Ibn al-Jazarī cites Ṭā-Sīn-Mīm in 26:1 and 28:1, where Ḥamza and Abū Jaʿfar pronounce the nūn clearly. [1 notes: NASHR(حمزة وأبو جعفر)]

## 68:1/q26 Are terms such as inkpot and pen exclusively Arabic?  [نٓ وَٱلْقَلَمِ · meaning]
- 68:1/q26/p1 Shared words belong to every language that uses them ordinarily; no language gains proven priority without decisive evidence. — Abū Maysara says the Qurʾān has words from every language. Ṭabarī cites inkwell, pen, paper, dirham and dinar as shared usage; he prefers describing them as Arabic-Ajamic or Ethiopic-Arabic and rejects exclusive origin claims without proof. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو ميسرة); TAB-FULL+]

## 68:1/q27 What examples show the effects of writing and decree?  [وَٱلْقَلَمِ وَمَا يَسْطُرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The different examples of beneficent decree, harmful human inscription, inferred divine knowledge and practical power.
- 68:1/q27/p1 Written decree can console one who grieves. — Thaʿlabī reports Ibn al-Zayyāt comforting a caliph by invoking divine favor and what was already written on the Tablet. [1 notes: THALABI(ابن الزيات)]
- 68:1/q27/p2 A written boycott documents a harmful human deed. — Biqāʿī recalls Quraysh's pact against Banū Hāshim and allies as an example of the writings included under the phrase. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 68:1/q27/p3 The pen's knowledge and its Lord are inferred from its rational writers. — Kuran Yolu recounts Elmalılı's sequence from writing to reason, the First Pen and the Lord of worlds. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı)]
- 68:1/q27/p4 Sword strength and pen knowledge govern worldly order. — Biqāʿī connects sword with Qāf and al-Ḥadīd's strength, and the pen with this sūra's disclosure of knowledge. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 68:1/q28 How is saṭr related to other written accounts?  [يَسْطُرُونَ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical relation of writing lines to the disputed noun for earlier accounts.
- 68:1/q28/p1 The verb means to write or inscribe. — Ibn Kathīr and al-Suyūṭī transmit Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Qatāda's straightforward gloss. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 68:1/q28/p2 Asāṭīr are written accounts of earlier people, not necessarily disordered false tales. — Ibn Qutayba rejects Abū ʿUbayda's singular usṭūra or isṭāra and his gloss of baseless tales without order. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة)]

## 68:1/q29 What observations about Qurʾānic script accompany the pen theme?  [* · sciences]
- 68:1/q29/p1 Some spelling choices depart from ordinary script conventions. — Zarkashī notes the wāw form of ولما in Hūd and Yūsuf versus fāʾ elsewhere and the separation of أن لا in ten places including al-Qalam; al-Marrākushī argues letter forms vary with meanings. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن العباس المراكشي)]

## 68:1/q30 What otherwise associated report concerns the later warning against bad character?  [* · reports]
- 68:1/q30/p1 A report excludes a hoarding miser, coarse brute and base gluttonous oppressor from Paradise. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits it from the Prophet and illustrates the traits by 70:15–16 and 3:159; the note concerns the later description in this sūra. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
