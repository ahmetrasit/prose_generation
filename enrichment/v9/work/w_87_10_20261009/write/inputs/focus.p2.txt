# 87:10/q11 How does this response relate to the preceding condition about useful reminder? [* · links]
turns on: How to read the preceding 'if': parenthesis without converse implication, omitted alternative, temporal 'when,' or an actual limit on useful reminding.
- 87:10/q11/p1 The condition does not restrict the command: all are invited, though only some benefit.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the condition parenthetical and says the outcome cannot be predicted; he cites 50:45 and says it challenges certainty of universal benefit, with Abū Jahl an example of known refusal. He adopts Ibn ʿAbbās's distinction between God's allies and enemies and rejects ellipsis. Ālūsī says a condition need not imply its converse. Rāzī says hidden fear requires general address. Study Quran also connects the preceding verse to benefit for those who fear.
  holders (8 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)+
  notes: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p284/r2 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p284/r3 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p284/r4 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p285/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p108/r5 RAZI:87:10/r3 RAZI-FULL:v31p131#2/r3 STUDYQURAN:87:10/r2
- 87:10/q11/p2 The command has an implicit second case: remind whether it benefits or does not.
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, al-Jurjānī, and al-Zahrāwī propose ellipsis; Ālūsī compares 16:81, which mentions clothing protecting from heat and omits cold. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās but calls the supplement unnecessary.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI-FULL(الفراء والنحاس والجرجاني والزهراوي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء)-
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p108/r4 | against: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p285/r2
- 87:10/q11/p3 The particle 'in' can mean 'when,' avoiding a restrictive condition.
  reasons: Ālūsī reports this Kūfan analysis with Quranic and hadith usage, including 3:139; other grammarians deny the sense and give alternative analyses of those examples.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI-FULL(الكوفيون)
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p108/r2
- 87:10/q11/p4 The benefit condition is real because the reminder is not futile and inclines the fearful toward truth.
  reasons: Tabāṭabāʾī explicitly treats benefit as a real condition; Iṣlāḥī reads the Prophet's task as reminding where useful.
  holders (2 notes): ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+
  notes: TABATABAI:87:1-19#4/r3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p271/r1

# 87:10/q12 How does this verse relate to the following contrast and the surah's message? [* · links]
turns on: The notes describe both the immediate contrast and its place in the wider surah.
- 87:10/q12/p1 It begins the contrast between the receptive and the wretched, whose responses lead to different fates.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts heeding with rejection and Fire. Quṭb says people diverge after the Prophet's reminder. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wretched counterpart is heedless in delusion and pleasure. Iṣlāḥī describes evaders who take worldly life as all and fear no Hereafter, then face Fire without life or death and wish vainly for death. Kuran Yolu cites 87:12. Corpus Coranicum says 87:10–11 contrast responses, 87:12–15 their fates in a chiasm paralleled by 92:5–11,14–21; the good outcome balances the Hell warning.
  holders (9 notes): IBNATIYYA-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p470/r4 QUTB-ZILAL:87:10/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p286/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p271/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p278/r6 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p279/r1 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:87:9/r5 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r5
- 87:10/q12/p2 It moves the messenger's divinely preserved revelation into public admonition within a surah about divine oneness and final recompense.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza addresses the messenger after praise, perhaps with close followers, and gives 87:6 public purpose; the warning implies fear, purification, invocation, and prayer, with 87:15 recalling 87:1. Quṭb describes the surah as a cosmic sanctuary of praise, good news of preservation and ease, and a message rooted in earlier scriptures about creation, revelation, and the afterlife; Biqāʿī adds gratitude for intellect and the faculties of heart, tongue, and limbs.
  holders (5 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL; BIQAI

# 87:10/q13 What are this surah's setting and recitational use? [* · sciences]
- 87:10/q13/p1 Al-Aʿlā is Meccan, has nineteen verses, and was recited by the Prophet at Eid and Friday prayers with al-Ghāshiya.
  reasons: Quṭb says the Prophet loved the surah and sometimes recited the two surahs on the same day.

# 87:10/q14 What anecdotes illustrate the choice between worldly goods and Paradise? [* · reports]
turns on: The anecdotes describe different people, bargains, and goods.
- 87:10/q14/p1 A hypocrite refused a Paradise date palm, while ʿUthmān gave an orchard to the petitioner.
  reasons: Bursawī connects this exchange to the verse's revelation.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p316/r5
- 87:10/q14/p2 One man chose eighty sheep over Paradise.
  reasons: Bursawī tells this as one of two related stories contrasting worldly goods and Paradise.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p316/r6
- 87:10/q14/p3 An old woman preferred Paradise to a hundred sheep for revealing Joseph's bones to Moses.
  reasons: Bursawī pairs her choice with the story of the man who took eighty sheep.
  holders (1 notes): BURSEVI
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p316/r6
sources: BURSEVI = İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127

# 87:10/q15 How are people's divergent responses related to divine knowledge and human disposition? [* · theology]
turns on: The notes emphasize divine foreknowledge or the hearer's disposition without explicitly arguing against each other.
- 87:10/q15/p1 God has always known who will accept or reject, but leaves each person's outcome unidentified as a trial and proof.
  reasons: Biqāʿī likens the groups to those who accept treatment and those who do not, and says God establishes proof through familiar means. He also links innate fear with those destined for felicity.
  holders (3 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:10/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p397/r9 BIQAI:87:10/r3
- 87:10/q15/p2 Rejection reflects the evader's own condition, while acceptance reflects preserved natural light.
  reasons: Iṣlāḥī says refusal shows no flaw in the message or preacher; people whose God-given light remains can recognize the prophet's call, while those whose light is extinguished await the Judgement trumpet. Quṭb says God deals with people according to their response.
  holders (3 notes): ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL
