# 87:10/q08 Does naming the fearful exclude other people from taking heed? [مَن يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse describes the strongest response or a boundary on who benefits.
- 87:10/q08/p1 Fear is singled out because it makes the response stronger, though hopeful people may also remember.
  reasons: Māwardī states both fear and hope can prompt remembrance; Qurṭubī transmits the same explanation from him. Ibn ʿĀshūr says believers differ by their degree of fear.

# 87:10/q09 Why does fear lead a person to heed the reminder? [مَن يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the notes emphasize reflective fear or an existing receptive disposition; they present compatible explanations.
- 87:10/q09/p1 Fear prompts reflection on the warning and its evidence, leading to recognition of truth and belief.
  reasons: Kashshāf contrasts the unreflective and unafraid. Abū Ḥayyān says one seeks escape from what one dreads; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a person considers possible harm and benefit lest the warning apply. Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī trace reflection to recognition and belief. Quṭb links a living heart's awe to knowledge of just accounting for good and evil; Biqāʿī says fear leads to every good and Paradise.

# 87:10/q10 Whom should the Prophet remind in light of this verse? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on a general invitation, avoiding fruitless pursuit, or tailoring its content.
- 87:10/q10/p1 He should address everyone, since the receptive are not known to him and the call itself fulfills his duty.
  reasons: Rāzī says only God sees inward fear; many opponents merely resist verbally, most people are hesitant, warnings of the greater Fire in 87:12–13 break hearts, and forfeiting much good to avoid little evil is itself evil. Qushayrī says the caller earns reward even where only the fearful benefit. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted object of 'remind' generalizes the audience; Quṭb says the Prophet fulfills his duty and God deals with each response. Corpus Coranicum, citing Robinson, says calls to the messenger can appeal generally, with him exemplary for believers.
  holders (12 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(القشيري); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: RAZI:87:10/r3 RAZI:87:10/r4 RAZI:87:10/r5 RAZI:87:10/r6 RAZI-FULL:v31p131#2/r3 RAZI-FULL:v31p131#2/r4 QURTUBI:87:10/r4 QURTUBI-FULL:87:10/r3 QUTB-ZILAL:87:10/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p284/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p284/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r7
- 87:10/q10/p2 He should remind where it is useful without pursuing or forcing the obdurate.
  reasons: Iṣlāḥī says the fearful heed and the wretched evade, so the Prophet is to leave the evaders to their fate; their rejection reflects their condition rather than a defect in the message or preacher.
