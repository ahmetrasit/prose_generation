# 87:10/q01 How is سَيَذَّكَّرُ formed? [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · grammar]
- 87:10/q01/p1 It is an assimilated form of sayatadhakkaru, with tāʾ assimilated or changed to dhāl.
  reasons: Jishumi gives the underlying sayatadhakkaru and assimilation; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the articulation points of tāʾ and dhāl are close. Biqāʿī takes the assimilation to suggest even subtle forms of reminder.

# 87:10/q02 What does the future form in سَيَذَّكَّرُ convey? [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the future marks a divine promise or the time needed for reflection.
- 87:10/q02/p1 It promises a certain future response by the fearful person.
  reasons: Rāzī compares God's assured promise in 87:6. Biqāʿī says the promise cannot fail and extends even to subtle reminders. Baghawī, Nasafī, and Bursawī render the response as soon forthcoming.
  holders (6 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BAGHAWI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI

- 87:10/q02/p2 It describes remembrance after time spent reflecting and deliberating.
  reasons: Rāzī presents this as the alternative to an assured promise; his fuller account also records both possibilities.

# 87:10/q03 Why is acquiring the reminder called remembering? [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · rhetoric]
- 87:10/q03/p1 Clear proofs make the knowledge seem previously present, though imitation and obstinacy have obscured it.
  reasons: Rāzī notes that remembering usually follows prior knowledge and forgetting; the clarity of the proofs explains this usage even for unbelievers.
  holders (2 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL

# 87:10/q04 What response does سَيَذَّكَّرُ describe? [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · interpretation]
- 87:10/q04/p1 The fearful person takes heed of the Quranic reminder, reflects on its truth, and accepts its guidance.
  reasons: Ṭabarī links this to Muhammad's commanded admonition and fear of punishment; Qatāda says no servant fears God without remembering Him, and contrasts disdain for the reminder and its people. Muqātil includes affirming God's oneness, forgiveness, and freedom from reckoning. Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī trace reflection to truth and belief; Nasafī says acceptance. Ibn Kathīr links it to meeting God and Muhammad's message. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says learned people and believers respond according to their guidance; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the verb as intransitive. Tabāṭabāʾī cites this verse against an interpretation of 87:14–16 that would conflict with the fearful person's remembrance.

# 87:10/q05 What does يَخْشَىٰ mean, and what is feared? [يَخْشَىٰ · meaning]
- 87:10/q05/p1 It means fearing God, His punishment, the Hereafter, or a bad end; fear occurs in degrees.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses khashya as fear, cites 20:44, and says believers differ by degree; he adds that the verb has no expressed object. Ṭabarī and Qushayrī specify God's punishment; Kashshāf and Nasafī mention a bad end; Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions the Hereafter. Study Quran distinguishes certainty, admitted possibility, and denial of return. Corpus Coranicum cites 79:26 and Andrae's parallel of fear of the judging God and watchfulness in contemporary Christian monastic piety.

# 87:10/q06 Who is the one described as fearing and heeding? [مَن يَخْشَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether man designates a class, a knowledge-defined subset, or an individual named by reports.
- 87:10/q06/p1 It denotes the general class of people disposed to fear God, with different degrees of receptivity.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr expressly rejects restriction to one person: man is generic and its singular form governs singular agreement; unexpressed fear lets the person weigh harm and benefit. Biqāʿī includes even innate fear and links it to felicity. Rāzī divides people into those certain of resurrection, undecided, and deniers; his broader reading includes believers and non-hostile doubters, who outnumber hostile deniers. Bayḍāwī includes the certain and hesitant; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān include scholars and believers in varying measure. Study Quran calls outright deniers the most wretched.
