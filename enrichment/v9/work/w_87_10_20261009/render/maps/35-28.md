# 35:28: 23 questions

## 35:28/q01 How do people and animals vary in color?  [وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ وَٱلدَّوَآبِّ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the analogy extends from visible color to inward states.
- 35:28/q01/p1 They have diverse colors, like the fruits and mountains mentioned before. — Muqatil, Tabari and Ibn Abbas list white, red, yellow and black; Qatada also lists green fruits and mountain tracks. Ibn Kathir distinguishes very dark Berbers and Ethiopians, fair Slavs and Romans, Arabs and Indians, and spotted animals; Ibn Ashur notes geographic and Arab classifications. Ibn Kathir's report about God's unfading dye names red, yellow and white but has mursal and mawquf forms. Thalabi transmits al-Farra's earlier description of gharabib as crow-black. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن جريج,ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK); THALABI(الفراء)]
- 35:28/q01/p2 Visible color variation also suggests people's differing inward knowledge and reverence. — Mawardi, Ibn Jurayj, Elmalili and Qurtubi connect varied colors with varying fear; Study Quran reports Q on inward states. Alusi judges Ibn Jurayj's analogy weak. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Q); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 35:28/q02 What does ٱلدَّوَآبِّ denote here?  [ٱلدَّوَآبِّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the broad lexical sense or narrower customary use governs.
- 35:28/q02/p1 Moving land creatures broadly, with livestock named separately for clarity. — Ibn Kathir says everything moving on legs; Biqai says anything creeping on earth, though customary usage narrowed to riding animals. Ibn Atiyya says the broad term includes people and livestock, separately named for emphasis. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 35:28/q02/p2 Beasts distinct from humans and livestock, especially riding animals. — Majaz excludes people and camels here despite broad usage in 11:6; Damghani likewise excludes humans and livestock. Bursevi names horses, mules and donkeys; Kuranyolu follows Razi's riding-animal sense. [4 notes: MAJAZ; DAMGHANI; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 35:28/q03 What are ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ?  [ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · meaning]
- 35:28/q03/p1 Livestock such as camels, cattle, sheep and goats, not horses, mules or donkeys. — Tabrisi and Tabatabai list camels, sheep and cattle; Bursevi adds goats and the exclusions; Kuranyolu glosses meat animals. [4 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 35:28/q04 Can أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ mean kinds as well as colors?  [أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether alwan here is limited to hue or includes wider kinds.
- 35:28/q04/p1 It can include types and sorts as well as visible colors. — Islahi explicitly extends alwan in this part of the verse; Kuranyolu says people, plants, mountains and animals differ in many traits. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:28/q04/p2 It denotes visible hues of people and animals. — The color lists name red, white, black and yellow; Asad and Kuranyolu associate natural beauty and varied hues with creative power. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 35:28/q05 How does مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ work grammatically?  [مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: What licenses masculine singular agreement.
- 35:28/q05/p1 An omitted singular kind, creature or some is described as varied; the pronoun returns to it. — Nahhas and Zajjaj supply created things; Ibn Ayyash, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash and Bursevi supply an implicit ma or some. Sam in parses the preposed phrase as predicate and cites poetry for participle government. Tabrisi prefers an omitted kind over an understood subject; Ibn Atiyya specifies livestock. Sahib al-Kashf prefers this nominative analysis for its clear transition; Ibn Ashur says fronting licenses the indefinite subject. [15 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI(محمد بن عياش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+; TABRISI+]
- 35:28/q05/p2 The singular masculine reflects the preceding min or the included humans. — Al-Muarrij and others point to min or its collective man; Razi invokes masculine human inclusion as more elevated and apt. [5 notes: THALABI(المؤرخ); JISHUMI(المؤرج); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المؤرج); RAZI-FULL]

## 35:28/q06 Why are these color clauses nominal and compact?  [وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ وَٱلدَّوَآبِّ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ مُخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: The accounts explain different aspects of the wording.
- 35:28/q06/p1 Their nominal form presents fixed or continuing variation, unlike recurring water and fruit changes. — Ibn Ashur contrasts fixed created colors with repeated emergence; Irshad says fruit variation alone follows the seeing question. Al-Bahr instead calls what is seen especially obvious, which Alusi notes conflicts with Irshad's view of its obviousness. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم,البحر)]
- 35:28/q06/p2 The fronted partitive min concisely says some in each group differ, covering the whole range. — Ibn Ashur says the clause matches mountain-streak syntax and min is partitive; Majaz distributes the description across the three groups. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; MAJAZ]

## 35:28/q07 Why are people, beasts and livestock ordered thus?  [ٱلنَّاسِ وَٱلدَّوَآبِّ وَٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · rhetoric]
- 35:28/q07/p1 Humans lead as the noblest animal kind; beasts precede livestock because of live benefit or the horse's higher rank. — Razi sets animals after plants and minerals and offers both reasons for beast before livestock. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 35:28/q08 How is ٱلدَّوَآبِّ pronounced?  [ٱلدَّوَآبِّ · readings]
turns on: The doubled consonant and its pronunciation.
- 35:28/q08/p1 Most readers double the bāʾ. — Abu Hayyan contrasts this form with Zuhri's lightened variant. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 35:28/q08/p2 Al-Zuhri lightens the bāʾ to avoid two adjacent vowelless consonants. — Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Sam in and Alusi compare forms of al-dallin, with moving or deleting a consonant and sometimes hamza. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزهري); ALUSI-FULL(الزهري)]

## 35:28/q09 Is أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ also read with feminine agreement?  [أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ · readings]
- 35:28/q09/p1 Ibn al-Sumayfi reads alwanaha with a feminine suffix. — Sam in and Alusi transmit the variant, with slightly different spelling of the reader's name. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن السميفع); ALUSI-FULL(ابن السميقع)]

## 35:28/q10 Does كَذَٰلِكَ close the comparison or begin the next thought?  [كَذَٰلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether likewise modifies the prior variation, predicates an omitted summary, or governs the next clause.
- 35:28/q10/p1 It closes the prior comparison with fruit and mountain colors; the scholars' sentence begins anew. — Ibn Qutayba, Yahya, Thalabi and Baghawi place the break here; Nahhas, Bursevi and Alusi construe the kaf as modifying an omitted verbal noun. Sam in cites al-Dani's complete stop and objects that innama cannot govern backward; Abu Hayyan agrees. Ibn Atiyya acknowledges this pause. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السمين); SAMIN-DURR+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 35:28/q10/p2 It predicates an omitted subject, meaning such is the situation or variation, then leads into the next thought. — Alusi reports the parsing; Ibn Ashur prefers this new result and cites 18:91. Tabatabai says it summarizes prior colors; al-Khafaji says neither the objection from innama nor the stop defeats it. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 35:28/q10/p3 It attaches forward as the cause: observing diverse creation makes scholars fear God. — Ibn Atiyya reports many exegetes; Sam in and Abu Hayyan reject backward government by innama and cite the stop; Tabatabai finds it remote in wording and meaning; Alusi calls Ibn Jurayj's related analogy weak. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); TABATABAI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(السمين)-]

## 35:28/q11 What does natural variety prove?  [* · interpretation]
- 35:28/q11/p1 It demonstrates one Creator's power, will and choice, calling people to reflect on the signs. — Qushayri infers a majestic Creator from diversity within kinds; Razi surveys plants, minerals and animals; Qurtubi says a choosing maker; Biqai contrasts one earth's homogeneous materials and varied qualities against natural causation. Ibn Atiyya says the argument challenges Quraysh; Ibn Kathir cites 30:22 on colors and languages; Kuranyolu describes harmony and the possibility that scientific research moves doubters to acknowledge divine power. [12 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 35:28/q12 What kind of fear is يَخْشَى?  [يَخْشَى · meaning]
turns on: Whether its distinctive feature is reverence or sheer intensity.
- 35:28/q12/p1 Reverent awe rooted in awareness of God's greatness, distinct from ordinary fear of harm. — Ibn Ata says reverence surpasses ordinary fear; Qushayri contrasts rahba's flight with khashya's restraint and relates faith, knowledge and gnosis to degrees of fear (3:175). Burhan contrasts khashya from greatness with khawf from weakness, citing 13:21 and 16:50. Bursevi distinguishes sin-based fear from prophetic awareness of majesty. Kuranyolu emphasizes concern over failing God's due; Samarrai compares battle danger; Tabatabai adds inward awe and outward humility. [13 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI; BURHAN; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI]
- 35:28/q12/p2 The strongest degree of fear. — Samarrai reports this alternative definition. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قسم)]

## 35:28/q13 Who are ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟?  [ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether learned status is defined by knowledge, fear and deeds, or specifically beneficial heart knowledge.
- 35:28/q13/p1 Those who know God, His power, attributes and acts, and thus fear Him. — Ibn Abbas repeatedly singles out omnipotence, might and sovereignty; Kashshaf adds justice, oneness and possible or impossible attributes, Abu Hayyan necessary attributes. Alusi excludes mere grammar; Ibn Ashur includes religious law but excludes unrelated disciplines alone. Yahya identifies believers; Abu Hayyan includes the Messenger and like people. Asad stresses unseen reality; Kuranyolu deep recognition of signs. Ibn Ashur says followers of scholars may share a derived fear. - 35:28/q13/p2 True scholars are known by fear and obedient deeds, not reports, titles or legal facts alone. — Mujahid, Shabi, Masruq and Rabi make fear the test; Ibn Masud contrasts it with abundant narration and Malik calls it a heart-light. Sadiq requires deeds to match words; Ibn Abbas names avoidance of shirk, lawful limits and reckoning; Said ibn Jubayr says fear blocks sin. The three-class report separates knowledge of God, law and fear; Hasan describes faith's desires and renunciations; Ali joins hope, warning, understanding and worship. - 35:28/q13/p3 Beneficial knowledge resides in the heart; tongue knowledge alone can be evidence against its possessor. — A Prophetic report divides heart and tongue knowledge; Malik's light and Ahmad ibn Salih's explanation fit the distinction. ## 35:28/q14 How are knowledge and fear related?  [إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fear is expressed as knowledge's consequence or its sufficient sign.
- 35:28/q14/p1 Knowledge causes fear, and fuller knowledge produces fuller fear. — Muqatil, Salih, Razi, Kashshaf and later exegetes state the proportion; Aisha's report says the Prophet knew God best and feared Him most after people shunned a dispensation; another says people with his knowledge would weep more. Alusi transmits Moses' question on wisdom and fear and a sound Prophetic report. Ibn Atiyya prefers knowledge as the head and cause of fear; he links fearful remembrance to 87:10. Bursevi urges knowledge of God. [30 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(صالح أبي الخليل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي ﷺ); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(صالح أبو الخليل); BURSEVI(الإرشاد,النبي); ALUSI-FULL(النبي,عطاء عن موسى); ELMALILI(Resûlullah); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو محمد بن أبي زيد); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(حديث شريف); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 35:28/q14/p2 Fear is called the head or sufficient measure of knowledge or wisdom. — Qatada, Ibn Masud, Masruq and Hudhayfa use sufficiency language, contrasting fear with self-delusion or false security; Psalms and prophetic sayings call fear the head of wisdom. Ibn Atiyya regards fear as the head of knowledge as homiletic and prefers knowledge as its cause; Ibn Abbas also calls asceticism sufficient knowledge. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(عبد الله بن مسعود); THALABI(مسروق); MAWARDI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض المفسرين,قائل غير مسمى,مسروق); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,حذيفة,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مسروق); IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 35:28/q15 What conduct does reverent fear produce?  [يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · inward]
turns on: The notes describe complementary inward discipline and visible practice.
- 35:28/q15/p1 It produces obedience, restraint, humility, compassion and scrupulous worship and speech. — Tabari connects knowledge of punishment with obedience; S ulami reports avoiding breaches of respect, overfamiliar supplication and improper service, while al-Wasiti traces veneration to awe and effacement. Qushayri mentions fear of falling short and bad manners. Ibn Abbas depicts hearts broken by majesty before righteous action; Ibn Masud's Quran bearer worships, grieves and avoids harshness. Umar's maxims commend guarded speech, truth, caution and consulting the God-fearing. Bursevi warns that unapplied learning leaves ignorance and prays for sincere fear. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; SULAMI(الحارث,النصرآباذي,الواسطي,جعفر); QUSHAYRI(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود,عمر بن الخطاب); BURSEVI]
- 35:28/q15/p2 Outward deeds must verify inward knowledge and speech. — Sadiq's definition, repeated via Ali ibn al-Husayn, denies the name scholar where deeds contradict words; Ibn Ashur says legal understanding prompts restraint or repentance after a lapse. [4 notes: TABRISI(الصادق); TABATABAI(أبو حمزة عن علي بن الحسين عليه السلام,الصادق عليه السلام); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:28/q16 What does إِنَّمَا restrict, and why this word order?  [إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which element is restricted and whether the restriction is absolute or contextual.
- 35:28/q16/p1 The delayed scholars are restricted as the true fearers; reversing the order would emphasize God as the sole object feared. — Muqatil and Kalbi describe object fronting. Jurjani's two striking examples show that restriction falls on the delayed constituent, not one before illa; he cites 33:39 for the other sense. Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur concur, with Alusi citing 9:18. Qushayri makes innama emphatic and restrictive; Alusi rejects denial of restriction and reads an implicit rebuke of ignorant unbelievers. [15 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); JURJANI-DALAIL; QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 35:28/q16/p2 The restriction is contextual or relative: ignorant polytheists lack fear, while believers' degrees of fear vary. — Ibn Atiyya gives contextual examples from Antarah and divine oneness; Ibn Ashur notes believers of the time count as learned and non-scholars can follow scholars. Abu Hayyan records Zamakhshari's stricter view and Ibn Atiyya's qualification. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:28/q16/p3 If scholars were fronted for focus, the sense would be that they fear none but God. — Jurjani rejects this as the actual point, though he finds a related sense in 33:39. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 35:28/q17 What readings make God or the scholars the subject?  [ٱللَّهَ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · readings]
turns on: The transmission and case endings, then the sense assigned to khashya.
- 35:28/q17/p1 The common reading makes God the accusative object and scholars the nominative subject. — Jishumi, Sam in, Abu Hayyan and Biqai record it; Thalabi calls it correct. Jishumi rejects the rival as nonstandard, possibly a hypothetical gloss. Ibn al-Jazari and al-Daraqutni reject Abu Hanifa's attribution to a fabricated collection. Suyuti discusses unreliable anomaly chains, the need for Arabic, script and transmission, mutawatir and mashhur classes, and standard reading books; Burhan notes anomalous parsing's difficulty. Nashr also catalogs an unrelated vowel-transition class. [19 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; NASHR(أبو العلاء الواسطي,أبو حنيفة); ITQAN(ابن الجزري,الدارقطني وجماعة,خارجة); BURHAN; THALABI(عمر بن عبد العزيز)+]
- 35:28/q17/p2 A reported reverse-case reading makes God the subject and scholars the object, with khashya figuratively meaning honoring, revering or knowing. — Attributed to Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, Abu Hanifa, Abu Haywa and Ibn Sirin; Sam in compares 2:124. Zamakhshari likens it to revering an imposing man, Razi to magnification, Burhan defends figurative awe. Biqai invokes mutual honor; al-Qayati cites a powerless but revered beloved. Jishumi and Thalabi also transmit 'knows.' Abu Hayyan doubts its attribution; Alusi records an independent Abu Haywa citation. [16 notes: THALABI(عمر بن عبد العزيز); JISHUMI(عمر بن عبد العزيز وأبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري,عمر بن عبد العزيز وأبو حنيفة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو حنيفة وابن عبد العزيز وابن سيرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(عمر بن عبد العزيز وأبو حنيفة وأبو حيوة); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(محمد بن القاياتي); BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة وعمر بن عبد العزيز وابن سيرين); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,عمر بن عبد العزيز وأبو حنيفة,قيل); JISHUMI-; NASHR(أبو حنيفة)-; ITQAN(الدارقطني وجماعة)-]
- 35:28/q17/p3 In the reverse-case reading, khashya can mean choosing: God selects the scholars. — Ibn Umar gives this explanation; Alusi cites a poem whose khashitu my cousins means selected, and Jishumi and Thalabi also mention it. [5 notes: BURSEVI(عبد الله بن عمر); ALUSI-FULL(الشاعر,قيل); THALABI(عمر بن عبد العزيز); JISHUMI(عمر بن عبد العزيز وأبو حنيفة); JISHUMI-]
- 35:28/q17/p4 A passive reading yields that scholars revere God. — Nasafi attributes the passive form to Abu Hanifa, Ibn Abd al-Aziz and Ibn Sirin; this differs from reports of simple case reversal. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL(أبو حنيفة وابن عبد العزيز وابن سيرين)]

## 35:28/q18 Can إِنَّمَا be parsed differently?  [إِنَّمَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ma is a relative pronoun or a blocking particle.
- 35:28/q18/p1 Ma may be relative, with scholars its predicate and an implicit referent in yakhsha. — Burhan notes ma can collectively denote rational persons and cites 4:3. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 35:28/q19 Was this verse revealed about a particular person?  [إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · reports]
- 35:28/q19/p1 Reports say it was revealed about Abu Bakr when his fear became visibly apparent. — Ata al-Khurasani's raised report adds the Prophet's conversation with him and the maxim that greater knowledge brings greater fear; Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit the shorter account. [3 notes: THALABI(عطاء الخراساني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق)]

## 35:28/q20 How does knowledge rank beside worship and worldly pursuit?  [ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The rank of knowledge and the condition of acting on it.
- 35:28/q20/p1 Knowledge raises scholars above worshippers and worldly seekers because it produces piety and beneficial teaching. — Razi argues from 49:13 that honor tracks piety, then knowledge, though failure to act challenges one's claim. Mawardi calls God-fearers masters. Qurtubi cites a mursal report through Makhul; Durr reports the Prophet's comparisons and prayers by creatures for teachers; Study Quran summarizes Paradise, angelic prayers and prophetic inheritance. Ibn Masud contrasts insatiable seekers of knowledge and wealth by approval versus transgression (96:6–7); Elmalili calls knowledge the highest rank. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(عبد الله); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,رسول الله); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(R,ḥadīth)]
- 35:28/q20/p2 Knowledge must become worship and practice to benefit its possessor; worldly use of religion brings trial. — Study Quran cites Baydawi on following obligatory worship, and Elmalili links practice with 35:10. Kab warns of learning without action and seeking worldly gain; Abu al-Darda transmits a raised version without a grade. Bursevi's couplet warns that reading without doing leaves ignorance. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,كعب); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); BURSEVI]

## 35:28/q21 Why does the verse end عَزِيزٌ غَفُورٌ?  [عَزِيزٌ غَفُورٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether forgiveness here is chiefly the paired theological motive or also a rhetorical completion.
- 35:28/q21/p1 Might calls for fear because God can punish; forgiveness gives pardon, repentance and hope. — Muqatil, Wahidi and Baghawi specify dominion and believers' sins; Razi pairs retribution and hope. Qurtubi adds reward; Baydawi contrasts persistent tyrants and repentant people. Biqai says nothing God wills is difficult and He erases sins; Alusi says pardon belongs to one capable of punishment. Elmalili warns against complacency; Tabatabai adds trust and longing. Ibn Ashur says divine independence need not discourage converts because repentance is accepted and scholars are promised forgiveness. [17 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 35:28/q21/p2 Ghafur is also a rhetorical completion after Aziz. — Alusi reports this view and a parallel verse by al-Ghanawi. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 35:28/q22 How does the verse fit the surrounding passage and surah?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace immediate links or a larger argumentative span.
- 35:28/q22/p1 Visible signs lead to knowledgeable fear, recalling 35:18 and leading into the Book and righteous action. — Nasafi notes the turn after 35:27; Baydawi says divine acts demonstrate power. Alusi, Bursevi, Elmalili and Tabatabai recall 35:18's receptive fearful people; Ibn Ashur links them to recitation in 35:29. Asad and Study Quran summarize creation, resurrection, revelation and oneness. Islahi reads 19–28 as contrasts between receptive living hearts and deprived ones, with 23–26 a parenthesis; rain nurtures growth as Quran nurtures good and exposes evil. [19 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:28/q22/p2 The broader discourse confronts opponents who demand prompt punishment instead of heeding the messenger. — Islahi says the demand harasses the Prophet and evades monotheistic warning; the messenger delivers truth and God decides when to punish. Rejection after warning brings destruction; Muhammad's universal mission includes People of the Book backing Quraysh. The following passage answers objections, and natural opposites serve a divine purpose. [9 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:28/q23 What related reports apply this verse's fear and mercy?  [* · reports]
turns on: The ancillary reports apply fear to mercy, recitation and learning.
- 35:28/q23/p1 The Prophet's tears for a dying child show divinely placed mercy and allow weeping without discontent. — Ibn Hibban gives Usama ibn Zayd's account of Zaynab's daughter; another version echoes the 'only ... from His servants' construction. [3 notes: IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 35:28/q23/p2 A Quran reciter's fear should sound in the voice and lead to reflection, humility and obedience. — Ibn Kathir calls Jabir's connected chain weak because Abdullah ibn Jafar al-Madini and his teacher are weak; he disapproves entertaining musical modes. Hudhayfa's warning condemns song, chant and lament that fail to pass throats; another report includes appointing singers for performance over learning, with two further traits omitted. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عليم)]
- 35:28/q23/p3 Knowledge of divine greatness guards against vanity and deepens remembrance. — Alusi says knowledge can invite pride unless checked by greatness and cites Junayd on satisfaction following rooted recognition. Kuranyolu stresses nature study and a Muslim duty to pursue knowledge. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
