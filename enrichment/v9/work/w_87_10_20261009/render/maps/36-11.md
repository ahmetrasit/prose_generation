# 36:11: 27 questions

## 36:11/q01 Whose warning is restricted by 'you only warn,' and in what sense?  [إِنَّمَا تُنذِرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'only' restricts the act of warning, its benefit, a stage of the mission, the subject warned about, or God's foreknowledge of responders.
- 36:11/q01/p1 The restriction concerns effective warning: followers of the Reminder benefit and attain faith or salvation, while hardened opponents do not. — Yahya says recipients accept and heed; Tabari contrasts sealed polytheists and private neglect by hypocrites; Wahidi says warning the astray is as though they were unwarned; Kashshaf and Baydawi define its intended aim as faith; Ibn Atiyya and Kur'an Yolu deny restriction of the Prophet's audience; Tabrisi says all have already received warning. Biqai says the Prophet should not grieve over those God knows to be hardened (2:2); Qashani grounds responsiveness in luminous innate preparedness; Islahi calls for open hearts, reasoning beyond sense, and counsel. [29 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 36:11/q01/p2 The Prophet must warn everyone, since he cannot know in advance who will respond, though only responders benefit. — Razi says the command is general and its effect selective; he separately argues that the Prophet cannot know whom he will guide. Elmalılı says warning everyone protects those who respond even when most do not. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 36:11/q01/p3 Warning began generally; after full delivery and stubborn mockery, only followers remained receptive. — Razi names mockery, turning away, and arrogance after complete delivery. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 36:11/q01/p4 The warning addresses everyone about basic beliefs but followers specifically about practical duties. — Razi names prayer and almsgiving as duties for those already following; Samarrai says believers also receive reminders of their duties, citing the Prophet's kin and those fearing resurrection as audiences. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 36:11/q01/p5 Those warned are existing believers, and warning alerts them to later lapses. — Alusi offers this to avoid treating warning already responsive believers as redundant. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:11/q01/p6 The beneficiaries are those God knows will follow, the smaller group not subject to the decree. — Alusi reports this as another reading of the restriction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:11/q02 What is 'the Reminder' here?  [ٱلذِّكْرَ · referent]
turns on: Whether dhikr designates the already mentioned Qur'an, admonition generally, or the signs and proofs by which one remembers God.
- 36:11/q02/p1 The Reminder is the Qur'an. — Yahya compares 35:18; Razi appeals to the definite article and the Wise Qur'an of 36:2; Study Quran compares 36:69. The Qur'an is followed through listening, recitation, reflection, affirmation, and practice in various sources; Kur'an Yolu calls this the majority view. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(IK, Q, R, Ṭ)]
- 36:11/q02/p2 The Reminder is admonition or exhortation. — Kashshaf offers exhortation; Abu Hayyan attributes admonition to Qatada; Alusi treats it as an alternative and relates the past verb to certain future following; Biqai includes every reminder that recalls God. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 36:11/q02/p3 The Reminder may be the Qur'an's signs or demonstrative proofs that complete innate nature. — Razi gives both alternatives and says each reading makes the warned learned people who fear God; Kur'an Yolu reports him. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]

## 36:11/q03 What does following the Reminder involve?  [ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلذِّكْرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'following' is described by outward response and practice or by the inward preparedness that makes response possible.
- 36:11/q03/p1 It involves receptive faith, reflection or listening, and acting on what is received. — Zajjaj and Wahidi mention listening; Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and others stress action; Baydawi combines reflection and practice; Alusi does likewise; Elmalılı adds serious recitation and heeding admonition; Tabatabai says affirming and inclining as verses are recited; Razi calls following faith and connects it to forgiveness (35:28; 34:4); Asad notes the literal sense 'who is following.' [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 36:11/q03/p2 It is the response of people whose pure, luminous innate readiness allows guidance and original knowledge. — Qashani connects receptivity to innate monotheism; Islahi says they open their hearts and reason with sight, hearing, mind, and heart before direct proof. [2 notes: QASHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:11/q04 Why is 'followed' in the past form?  [ٱتَّبَعَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the past marks certain future response or already completed adherence.
- 36:11/q04/p1 Its past form expresses certainty that following will occur. — Alusi applies this to the admonition reading; Tabatabai likewise says the occurrence is certain. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 36:11/q04/p2 It describes believers who already followed, so later warning concerns future lapses. — Alusi reports this to avoid a redundant warning. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:11/q05 Why is the one feared called 'the Merciful'?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the name chiefly balances hope against fear or shows how divine kindness alone moves the receptive.
- 36:11/q05/p1 The name joins fear with hope: mercy must not make one complacent, and punishment must not cause despair. — Razi contrasts divine names that evoke awe and affection and says withdrawing repeated gifts is fearful; Baydawi and Bursevi note divine vengeance or overpowering power despite mercy; Alusi cites 15:49-50; Elmalılı cites 15:49; Tabatabai says this balance sustains worship; Islahi says graciousness requires reward for piety and punishment for wrongdoing at the appointed Day. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:11/q05/p2 Even reminders of God's kindness are enough to move the truly fearful. — Biqai sees a shift from majesty to mercy and describes their fear as intense. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:11/q06 What is feared when one fears the Merciful?  [وَخَشِىَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fear is explained by punishment or by God's majesty and possible withdrawal of favor; several sources include both.
- 36:11/q06/p1 The person fears God's punishment, including the Fire, while holding hope in His mercy. — Qatada is reported by Ibn Abi Hatim, Mawardi, Qurtubi and Durr; Muqatil, Nasafi, Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalılı also speak of punishment. Alusi cites 15:49-50; Bursevi cites 70:28. [10 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 36:11/q06/p2 The person reveres God's majesty and fears losing His favor or blessings. — Razi says the withdrawal of repeated gifts would be grievous; Abu Hayyan mentions loss of blessings; Biqai includes lost favor and God's majesty; Bursevi says uninterrupted mercy makes its loss frightening. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 36:11/q07 What does fearing 'in the unseen' mean?  [بِٱلْغَيْبِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether al-ghayb concerns absence of human observers, the not-yet-visible divine realities or punishment, the hidden heart, concealed deeds, or mystical witnessing.
- 36:11/q07/p1 It means fearing or worshipping God privately, sincerely, when other people do not see. — Yahya stresses sincere inner faith; Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq say only God sees; Tabari and Jishumi contrast hypocrites, while Alusi contrasts ostentation. Ibn Kathir adds awareness that God sees and knows one's deeds; Biqai calls it fear without a human observer to impress; Study Quran compares 50:33. Ibn Jawzi points to 21:49 without restating the interpretation. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭs); TABATABAI-]
- 36:11/q07/p2 It means fearing God or His unseen punishment before death or resurrection makes realities visible. — Razi says evidence supports fear before sight, when fear still serves a purpose; Baydawi and Bursevi say punishment is feared before arrival; Alusi reports readings where the punishment or the fearing person is unseen; Tabatabai prefers fear behind the veil before death or resurrection to fear in human seclusion; Biqai rejects demanding a sign that removes the veil; Islahi calls faith before direct visual proof valuable. [13 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭs); TABATABAI+]
- 36:11/q07/p3 It means fearing unseen matters of the afterlife, especially resurrection and the Fire, and perhaps divine unity. — Jishumi and Tabrisi report afterlife matters; Qatada's punishment and Fire belong to this reading; Razi calls resurrection realities common and reports divine unity as another included unseen matter; Biqai emphasizes unseen powers and resurrection. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 36:11/q07/p4 It means inward fear in the hidden heart. — Alusi reports a grammatical construal making al-ghayb the heart, with the preposition attached to 'feared'; Elmalılı says only God knows this heart and distinguishes it from outward display. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 36:11/q07/p5 It means concealing one's evil deeds from other people. — Mawardi reports this from al-Suddi. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي)]
- 36:11/q07/p6 It means inwardly witnessing the disastrous end of disbelief and the sweetness of faith. — Bursevi reports this mystical interpretation from al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya, adding the rank of gnosis. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 36:11/q08 What is the purpose and timing of 'give him good news'?  [فَبَشِّرْهُ · interpretation]
- 36:11/q08/p1 Glad tidings follow beneficial warning as the second part of the Messenger's mission. — Razi calls it the second part of his task; Alusi reports Abu Hayyan that the conjunction orders good news after following and fearing, proving the warning benefited the person; Elmalılı says the mission's wisdom includes generous tidings as well as warning; Islahi says promise a great reward to those ready for counsel. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:11/q09 Why is the recipient of good news singular?  [فَبَشِّرْهُ · grammar]
- 36:11/q09/p1 The singular pronoun agrees with the singular form of 'whoever' and refers to the person who both follows and fears. — Ibn Atiyya explains agreement with man; Bursevi identifies the two qualifications; Biqai adds that the singular signals how few such people are in this comprehensive, heart-like surah. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 36:11/q10 What does the promised forgiveness cover or signify?  [بِمَغْفِرَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether forgiveness is explicated as pardon of sins or as inward removal of veils; both may be understood as vast.
- 36:11/q10/p1 It pardons the believer's sins, possibly including grave, repeated, past or future sins. — Yahya, Tabari, Zajjaj, Mawardi, Wahidi, Qurtubi and others specify sin; Biqai says grave and repeated sins do not cancel genuine fear when one repeatedly repents; Alusi reports past sins or possible future lapses; Razi and Elmalılı take indefinite form or nunation to magnify its extent; Ibn Kathir compares 67:12. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; THALABI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 36:11/q10/p2 It is a vast spiritual veiling that removes the soul's traces or sins that veil divine realities. — Razi says the indefinite form implies forgiveness on every side, no trace of the lower self, and lights of the purified spirit; Qashani speaks of veils over God's acts, attributes, and Essence. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QASHANI]

## 36:11/q11 What is the 'noble reward'?  [وَأَجْرٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ajr karim is identified by its destination, purity, abundance, honor, or an inward account of divine gardens; several descriptions can coexist.
- 36:11/q11/p1 It is Paradise, with its beautiful, abundant, lasting and incomparable gifts. — Yahya calls it noble recompense; Ibn Atiyya adds praise and honor; Alusi reports Qatada that its greatest delight is seeing God; Bursevi calls its gifts incomparable; Biqai describes an immense pleasant continuous abode without discomfort; Tabatabai says indefinite wording makes it immeasurable; Ibn Kathir compares 67:12. Nahhas leaves the competing gloss to God. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 36:11/q11/p2 Karim means a good or beautiful reward. — Nahhas reports Dahhak from Ibn Abbas; Jishumi and Tabrisi add that it is free of impurities. [3 notes: NAHHAS(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI; TABRISI]
- 36:11/q11/p3 It is an abundant or generous recompense. — Mawardi presents abundance as one interpretation; Razi calls the reward characterized by generosity and points to earlier explanations of noble provision; Ibn Kathir says great, abundant and beautiful; Elmalılı says it lacks deficiency. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 36:11/q11/p4 It is a reward accompanied by praise and honor, given for obedience. — Mawardi presents honor as another interpretation and calls it the reward of obedience; Ibn Atiyya says the worker receives it with praise and honor; Elmalılı says it is without favor or deficiency. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 36:11/q11/p5 It is the gardens of the Real's acts, attributes and Essence. — Qashani gives this inward account of the reward. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 36:11/q12 How do the two promised outcomes match following and fear?  [ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلذِّكْرَ وَخَشِىَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ بِمَغْفِرَةٍۢ وَأَجْرٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · interpretation]
- 36:11/q12/p1 Following or faith brings forgiveness, while fear and righteous deeds bring reward. — Razi explicitly pairs following with faith and forgiveness, and fear with works and reward, citing 35:28 and 34:4; Bursevi says reflection and admonition lead to faith and forgiveness, while fear leads to good deeds and reward, citing 56:24. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 36:11/q13 Are denial and following the Reminder created by God or performed by people?  [ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلذِّكْرَ · theology]
- 36:11/q13/p1 They are human acts. — Jishumi rejects the Mujbira claim that God creates those acts. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 36:11/q14 What inward conduct expresses sincere fear and worship?  [وَخَشِىَ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ بِٱلْغَيْبِ · inward]
turns on: Tustari describes practical secret worship and fairness; Qashani describes an inward passage from veiling to presence.
- 36:11/q14/p1 Secret worship produces certainty; truthful speech raises the heart; fairness directs every limb to God and makes generosity toward people essential. — Tustari says fairness places heaven and earth in one's balance; concern over tomorrow's provision divides the heart's concern, while demanding fairness for oneself fails fairness. [3 notes: TUSTARI]
- 36:11/q14/p2 The person remembers and fears God through His veiled majesty, then follows a path from absence to presence and sight. — Qashani describes the progression as seeing by the light of what was formerly absent. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 36:11/q15 What is the highest station of those who remember God?  [ٱلذِّكْرَ · inward]
- 36:11/q15/p1 They forget awareness of their own remembrance while witnessing the Remembered and guard their time from returning to self-awareness. — Sulami reports this from al-Husayn. [1 notes: SULAMI(الحسين)]

## 36:11/q16 What do the reports of John and Jesus illustrate about the heart's attention to God?  [بِٱلْغَيْبِ · reports]
turns on: The two reports emphasize different degrees of undivided heart attention.
- 36:11/q16/p1 John's body could be beside Jesus while his heart remained fixed at the Throne. — Tustari reports Sahl's account in which Jesus reproaches John after he struck a woman. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 36:11/q16/p2 John said a moment of resting his heart in Gabriel would make him doubt that he truly knew God. — Tustari reports John's statement. [1 notes: TUSTARI(يحيى)]

## 36:11/q17 How does this verse fit the preceding warning and the sura's opening?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources connect the verse either to the immediately preceding failed warning, to the Abu Jahl episode, or to the sura's opening argument for prophethood.
- 36:11/q17/p1 It contrasts the unresponsive with those who benefit from warning, relieving the Prophet of grief over hardened hearers. — Kashshaf reconciles 36:10's failure of warning with its effective goal here; Alusi says the preceding opponents seemed unmoved; Study Quran compares 35:8; Islahi says leave nonlisteners to God, who records their deeds; Biqai cites 2:2 for the guided God-fearing. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 36:11/q17/p2 The passage is connected with Abu Jahl and al-'Alaq's account of one who forbids a servant from prayer. — Muqatil connects it to 96:9-10. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 36:11/q17/p3 Verses 1-12 establish Muhammad's prophethood through the wise Qur'an, God's favor to a people astray without guidance. — Islahi says arrogance, inherited traditions and future desires hinder reflection on the strongest proof of the messenger and his straight path. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:11/q18 How does the following verse's resurrection imagery extend this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether revival in the linked discussion is literal resurrection or also the spiritual revival of hardened hearts.
- 36:11/q18/p1 God will raise the dead, and may also revive disbelievers' dead hearts through guidance. — Ibn Kathir compares 57:17; Bursevi reports al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya's shower of divine turning and closeness that revives hardened hearts. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 36:11/q18/p2 The following verse speaks of literal revival with sensation and movement. — Bursevi defines revival this way, while noting the Prophet also called affluent people and unjust rulers dead. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 36:11/q19 Who performs the revival mentioned in the following verse?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the plural divine pronoun permits angelic participation or emphasizes exclusive divine action.
- 36:11/q19/p1 God alone revives the dead; the plural 'We' expresses majesty and divine attributes. — Bursevi challenges the suggestion that angels share in revival because the emphatic pronoun denotes God alone; al-Bahr says the repeated pronoun adds emphasis and highlights recompense with honor or retribution. [2 notes: BURSEVI(البحر)]
- 36:11/q19/p2 Angels may share in reviving the dead. — Bursevi reports this view only to challenge it with the emphatic 'We alone.' [0 notes: BURSEVI-]

## 36:11/q20 What does the following verse mean by recording what people send ahead and their traces?  [* · links]
- 36:11/q20/p1 It records the good and evil deeds already done and lasting good legacies. — Bursevi lists teaching knowledge, writing books, endowing works, and building mosques, hospices or bridges; Sa'di's couplet says one does not die while a good legacy remains. [3 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي)]

## 36:11/q21 Who writes people's deeds in the following verse, and why is writing mentioned after revival?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 'We write' refers directly to divine preservation or to recording angels acting by divine command.
- 36:11/q21/p1 God preserves and establishes the deeds in the Preserved Tablet. — Bursevi gives this as one sense of 'We write' and says writing follows revival in the wording because its purpose is resurrection. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 36:11/q21/p2 Noble recording angels write at God's command, so the act is attributed to Him as warning. — Bursevi gives this alternative and likewise explains the order by writing's purpose in resurrection. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 36:11/q22 What can 'dead' mean in the Prophet's adjacent report?  [* · reports]
- 36:11/q22/p1 The dead can mean an affluent person or unjust ruler whose company kills the heart. — Bursevi reports a prophetic saying that repeated sin, excessive company and talk with women, arguing with a fool, and keeping company with the 'dead' are four heart-killing things. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 36:11/q23 How is a mosque's neighbor defined in the adjacent excursus?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether neighborhood is measured by distance in houses or audibility of the call.
- 36:11/q23/p1 Anyone within forty houses in each direction. — Bursevi reports this from some authorities. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 36:11/q23/p2 Anyone who hears the call to prayer. — Bursevi reports this as another view. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 36:11/q24 Which mosque should a worshipper choose when two are near?  [* · law]
- 36:11/q24/p1 Choose the older mosque, or if equal the nearer; a scholar may increase attendance at the smaller congregation. — Bursevi says the older mosque has greater sanctity and permits others to choose freely. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 36:11/q25 What is the status and reward of congregational mosque prayer?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the congregation's strongly commended status entails legal obligation or confirmed sunna; reported reward variants differ between twenty-five and twenty-seven.
- 36:11/q25/p1 Mosque congregation is preferable for obligatory prayers and tarawih and yields more reward than home congregation. — Bursevi reports a prophetic reward of twenty-five times that of praying at home or market; another wording says twenty-seven, matched to seventeen obligatory and ten regular prayers. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 36:11/q25/p2 Congregational prayer is obligatory. — Bursevi reports this as the view of most scholars; he cites the Prophet's contemplated order to burn absentees' houses as evidence for strong enforcement. [2 notes: BURSEVI(أكثر العلماء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 36:11/q25/p3 Congregational prayer is a confirmed sunna. — Bursevi reports this from some scholars and argues that the house-burning report could concern abandonment of an obligation or a confirmed sunna; he says the Prophet would not intend a sinful act. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعضهم)]

## 36:11/q26 What excuses permit missing mosque congregation?  [* · law]
- 36:11/q26/p1 Illness, disabling injury, inability to walk, blindness, bad weather, severe darkness, or fear of oppression can excuse absence. — Bursevi specifies illness warranting tayammum, paralysis, rain, mud, cold, and fear of rulers or other oppressors; a report promises complete light on Judgment Day to those who walk to mosques in darkness, so he treats only severe darkness as an excuse. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 36:11/q27 What purposes can repetition serve in Qur'anic speech?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether discussing the purposes of repetition or its technical distinction from verbal emphasis.
- 36:11/q27/p1 Repetition confirms, draws attention, removes suspicion, refreshes earlier points, or magnifies and overawes. — Itqan says repetition can be stronger than emphasis and cites 25:50 for remembrance; repeated 'O my people' at 40:38-39 raises attention; 16:119, 16:110, 2:89, 3:188 and Joseph's dream at 12:4 show refreshing; 69:1, 101:1 and 56:27 show magnification. [4 notes: ITQAN]
- 36:11/q27/p2 Repetition is a figure distinct from formal emphasis, especially when repeated wording is separated from its first occurrence. — Itqan says emphasis need not repeat wording, repetition may only emphasize meaning, and verbal emphasis cannot be separated from what it emphasizes; examples include 2:223 and 3:42. [2 notes: ITQAN]
