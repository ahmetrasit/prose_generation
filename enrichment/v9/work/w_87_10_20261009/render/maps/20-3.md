# 20:3: 17 questions

## 20:3/q01 How is 'except as a reminder' construed with the preceding clause?  [إِلَّا تَذْكِرَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether illa is disconnected or connected, what governs tadhkiratan, and whether a reminder can substitute for suffering or coexist with another purpose clause.
- 20:3/q01/p1 The exception is disconnected: the Quran was sent as a reminder, rather than to cause the Prophet hardship. — Baydawi, Nasafi, al-Zamakhshari as reported by Abu Hayyan and al-Samin, al-Samin, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi and Tabatabai read illa as 'but'; Biqai cites a poetic exception qualifying praise. Study Quran allows 'however' as a turn from verse 2. Baydawi and al-Zamakhshari reject substitution because reminder and misery differ in kind. Al-Samin disputes the Shaykh's grammatical explanation of that difference: al-Zamakhshari means al-Farisi's semantic objection. Bursevi makes the accusative a purpose of revelation under a disconnected exception and says two purposes require substitution or coordination. [10 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:3/q01/p2 An omitted verb of sending or reminding governs the accusative reminder. — Al-Farra and al-Mubarrad supply 'We sent it down'; al-Mubarrad compares ellipsis in 92:19-20. The Kufan grammarians, Ibn Atiyya, Jishumi, Tabrisi and al-Samin also supply a verb; al-Samin gives 'but We reminded' or 'you remind by it.' Tabrisi prefers this analysis and calls the exception disconnected. Alusi reports 'rather, We reminded you with a reminder.' Majaz gives this as one reordered construal. [10 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,المبرد); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(المبرد); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI+]
- 20:3/q01/p3 Tadhkiratan is an accusative of purpose or a cognate verbal noun governed by the revelation. — Nahhas prefers a verbal noun or purpose object; Tabrisi calls it a purpose object, and al-Nahhas as reported by Qurtubi says the Quran was sent to remind, not burden. Al-Zamakhshari allows a purpose object alongside li-tashqa because the first purpose lacks the conditions for a bare accusative while the second meets them; Alusi says the grammatical relations differ and multiple purpose clauses need no coordination. Al-Samin argues multiple purposes are possible. Tabatabai calls tadhkira an active verbal noun and the purpose of sending. Baydawi reports an alternative purpose attached to Quran through an understood adjective. Alusi also reports a purpose object if li-tashqa describes Quran, but this requires an omitted relative construction and unusual definite article. Objections by Abu al-Baqa and Baydawi disallow two direct purposes; Alusi answers that no causal link is required, or that reminding entails toil. Ibn al-Munir objects that this makes hardship a cause of revelation and conflicts with consolation in 18:6; Alusi rejects the supposed conflict. [14 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR+; BAYDAWI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)-]
- 20:3/q01/p4 Tadhkiratan substitutes for the syntactic position of li-tashqa or its verb. — Basran grammarians, Abu Ishaq, Ibn Atiyya, al-Akhfash, al-Zajjaj and Burhan give the substitute analysis: the Quran was sent only as a reminder. Nahhas finds it remote; Abu Ali says reminder is not a kind of suffering; al-Zamakhshari objects that the accusatives differ in kind; Abu Hayyan says li-tashqa has no case position to replace; Alusi says exception-based substitution requires one kind. Al-Samin reports the Shaykh's distinction between the underlying accusative of tadhkiratan and the apparent accusative after the preposition drops, but questions how this affects substitution. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); BURHAN; NAHHAS-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:3/q01/p5 Tadhkiratan is a circumstantial accusative: the Quran or addressee is a reminder or one reminding. — Baydawi and Nasafi report a circumstantial reading; al-Samin calls the verbal noun circumstantial, 'except as one reminding.' Alusi permits the state of the addressee or Quran with the verbal noun understood adjectivally or intensively. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:3/q01/p6 Tadhkiratan is an appositive to al-Quran, which is itself a reminder. — Al-Hawfi and Abu al-Baqa as reported by Abu Hayyan allow apposition to Quran; al-Samin and Alusi likewise report al-Hawfi's analysis. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي وأبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي)]
- 20:3/q01/p7 The wording is reordered: the Quran was sent as a reminder to the fearful and not to make the Prophet suffer. — Majaz allows fronting and postponement with an understood pronoun, alternatively treating reminder as separate from the hardship clause. Al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl, reported by Thalabi and Qurtubi, makes reminder precede the denial of suffering and treats tanzilan as apposition to tadhkira. [3 notes: MAJAZ; THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل)]
- 20:3/q01/p8 The negated hardship clause is repeated and the affirmative exception cannot be apposition to it. — Some anonymous grammarians cited by Tabari reject apposition to the negated li-tashqa because the exception is affirmative and explain the wording as repetition. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 20:3/q02 What does tadhkiratan mean here?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether reminder is described generally, by its warning or restraint, or as recall of innate knowledge.
- 20:3/q02/p1 It is a reminder or admonition conveyed by the Quran. — Maqatil, Yahya, Tabari, Ibn Zayd, Wahidi, Baghawi and others apply the term to the revelation. Study Quran notes that Reminder is also a name for the Quran, citing 3:58, 21:2, 21:7, 21:10, 21:24 and 38:1. Qatadah, quoted by Tabari and Durr, and Ibn Kathir call scriptures and messengers a mercy that enables recollection and benefit, including instruction in lawful and forbidden matters. Jishumi says the word is exposing a person to reminder or explanation. Tabrisi notes that tadhkira is a verbal noun like tadhkir. Elmalili says Quran reading and tafsir aim at such reminding and prays for Quranic light in hearts. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(المبرد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:3/q02/p2 It is a warning to whoever fears God. — Mawardi offers warning; Kuranyolu says the Quran guides, reminds and warns those conscious of responsibility. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:3/q02/p3 It is a rebuke that restrains the fearful from sins. — Mawardi offers this alternative to warning. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:3/q02/p4 It recalls truths already present in human nature. — Islahi says Quranic arguments appeal to the inner and outer worlds, reason, familiar history and ancestral teachings; people whose nature remains sound are awakened by these reminders. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:3/q03 How does the reminder benefit its hearers?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the benefit is described as practical guidance and belief or as differentiated spiritual effects.
- 20:3/q03/p1 It lets receptive listeners remember, believe, act and benefit from guidance. — Tabari and Ibn Kathir say revelation sets out lawful and forbidden matters. Jishumi says tadhkira requires reflection. Tabatabai says latent fear awakens on hearing truth, producing belief and mindfulness. Islahi says sound nature responds while corrupted nature remains unmoved, and that inner and outer evidence, reason and history support the reminder. Bursevi describes reminder and proclamation as universal but benefit as selective, citing 25:1. Tabatabai calls deliberate rejection 'forgetting' because it has the same result as failure to attend. Kuranyolu says guidance and warning address human responsibility. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:3/q03/p2 It gives admonition to the fearful, mercy to sinners, and intimacy to lovers. — Ja'far, reported by Sulami, distinguishes these three effects. Qushayri says understanding brings future relief while spiritual arrival brings immediate intimacy. Ibn Ata says Muhammad, as leader of the fearful, finds repose in the beloved's words. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); QUSHAYRI]

## 20:3/q04 How does the phrase li-man yakhsha attach grammatically?  [لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase depends directly on the verbal noun or on an omitted adjective.
- 20:3/q04/p1 It attaches to tadhkiratan, with the lam reinforcing the verbal noun's governance. — Al-Samin gives this direct attachment; Tabrisi treats the prepositional phrase as an adjective for reminder, and Alusi also permits attachment to tadhkira. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:3/q04/p2 It modifies an omitted adjective describing tadhkiratan. — Al-Samin and Alusi give this alternative; Tabrisi calls the prepositional phrase adjectival for reminder. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:3/q05 Who is 'one who fears'?  [لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether fear names current receptivity, eventual response known to God, the Prophet as foremost recipient, or scholars specifically.
- 20:3/q05/p1 God-fearing people who receive and respond to the reminder. — Maqatil, Yahya, Tabari, Ibn Zayd, Wahidi, Baghawi and Tabrisi identify the beneficiary generally. Study Quran equates them with the God-revering muttaqin, citing 2:2. Kuranyolu describes people conscious of their human responsibility. Yahya contrasts disbelievers who do not accept reminder. [9 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:3/q05/p2 Those disposed to fear whose tender or sound hearts respond when warned. — Baydawi describes tender hearts and also reports future fear; Nasafi gives present or eventual fear. Alusi stresses gentle temperament. Tabatabai says latent fear emerges upon hearing truth. Islahi ties piety to uncorrupted human nature; those who corrupt it remain unmoved. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:3/q05/p3 Those whom God knows will come to fear when warned. — Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Alusi offer eventual fear known to God as the criterion. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:3/q05/p4 Muhammad is included among the fearful, and is foremost among them. — Ibn Ata addresses him as their leader who can find relief from fear in the beloved's book; al-Kabir says the Messenger is foremost in fearing and receiving the reminder. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(الكبير)]
- 20:3/q05/p5 They are scholars whose knowledge needs protection from vanity and pride. — Alusi reports this identification with 35:28 as proof; remembrance of God's greatness checks scholars' faults. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:3/q06 Why are those who fear singled out if the Quran addresses everyone?  [لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:3/q06/p1 They alone gain benefit from the universal reminder. — Yahya says disbelievers do not accept it. Jishumi says all are reminded but reflection is needed. Bursevi cites 25:1 for warning to all worlds while benefit is particular; Alusi says nonbeneficiaries are treated as nonexistent. Islahi contrasts sound and corrupted human nature. Kuranyolu says the message guides those who accept responsibility. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:3/q07 What fear does yakhsha imply, and what follows from it?  [يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:3/q07/p1 Reverent fear of God motivates faith, righteous action and attention to truth. — Ibn Atiyya says khashya includes faith and righteous deeds because it moves one to them; Abu Hayyan agrees. Tabatabai says fear of the consequences of heedlessness and worldly desire jolts the soul toward truth. Mawardi distinguishes ordinary fear of apparent causes from reverent fear of hidden causes. Islahi says God values intellectually acknowledged belief over the sensory proof demanded by those responsive only to force. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:3/q08 What unspoken object does 'fears' have?  [يَخْشَىٰ · grammar]
- 20:3/q08/p1 The omitted object is their Lord, feared unseen. — Islahi supplies this from parallel Quranic wording and cites 2:2. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:3/q09 How do verses 2 and 3 console the Prophet about the Quran's burden?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the hardship denied is grief over disbelief, strain in worship, or the inevitable work of proclamation.
- 20:3/q09/p1 His duty is to convey the reminder; he need not exhaust himself over others' rejection. — Razi cites 18:6 and 10:65: each person's belief concerns that person. Qurtubi cites 18:6 and says the Prophet need not guarantee belief. Tabatabai says he cannot compel acceptance. Islahi says reminding rather than coercion is his task; response belongs to the hearer. Kuranyolu portrays a demoralized Prophet facing idol worshippers. Ibn Ashur says the ending echoes the opening and closes the debate: completed delivery comforts him while the heedless remain astray until truth becomes clear. Maqatil says revelation was not meant to make Muhammad suffer. [7 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:3/q09/p2 The Quran is for reminder, so the Prophet should not harm himself through excessive worship or hardship. — Tabrisi says he should not toil so intensely; Qurtubi associates this reading with the Prophet's swollen feet and Gabriel's advice that his body has rights, framing the mission as an easy upright religion. Kuranyolu says the message does not extinguish life's joy. Elmalili says recitation and tafsir aim at reminder, not imposed hardship. [5 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(جبريل); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:3/q09/p3 The toil of delivery, argument and conflict can itself serve the Quran's purpose as reminder. — Al-Zamakhshari, reported by Abu Hayyan and al-Samin, describes burdens of preaching and confrontation. Alusi says the Prophet has already borne enough such strain; he answers the objection that hardship and reminder lack a causal link by saying no such link is needed or reminder entails toil. Ibn al-Munir objects that this makes hardship the reason for revelation and conflicts with the consolation of 18:6; Alusi says the intended sense removes that conflict. An objection invokes a hadith that reward is proportional to effort, suggesting suffering might have been described as increasing reward. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)-]
- 20:3/q09/p4 The Quran brings success and happiness; unbelief is the true misery. — Qurtubi reports this answer to Abu Jahl and al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, who called Muhammad wretched for leaving his fathers' religion. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جهل والنضر بن الحارث)]

## 20:3/q10 What occasion of revelation is reported for the passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported circumstance explains the denial of hardship; Tabatabai questions how a prayer narrative fits the surrounding context.
- 20:3/q10/p1 The Prophet and companions tied ropes across their chests for night prayer, thinking it obligatory. — Mujahid reports this through Thalabi. Tabatabai finds the narrative's fit with the surrounding verses unclear. [1 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); TABATABAI-]
- 20:3/q10/p2 The Prophet prayed at night until his feet swelled, and Gabriel urged him to spare his body. — Qurtubi reports this as an alternative occasion; Gabriel says the body has rights. Tabatabai questions the fit of the narrative with the context. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جبريل); TABATABAI-]
- 20:3/q10/p3 Abu Jahl and al-Nadr ibn al-Harith called the Prophet wretched after he left his fathers' religion. — Qurtubi reports their charge and the verse's reply that Islam and the Quran bring happiness while unbelief is misery. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جهل والنضر بن الحارث)]

## 20:3/q11 How does this reminder connect to the preceding surah and the broader Quran?  [* · links]
- 20:3/q11/p1 It fulfills the promise of pious believers at the close of the preceding surah. — Biqai cites 19:96: enough believers will arise to match the Quran's miraculous stature and endurance. He says the Quran gathers and surpasses earlier scripture in laws, counsel, spiritual insight, knowledge, manners, histories and the interests of both worlds; the community's size reflects its book's greatness. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:3/q12 What inward meanings are given to the reminder and its recipients?  [تَذْكِرَةًۭ لِّمَن يَخْشَىٰ · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward reminder concerns primordial union or states of insight, mercy and intimacy in its hearers.
- 20:3/q12/p1 It recalls the soul's former union and stirs longing to regain it. — Alusi reports one reading in which souls recall the days of union before embodiment and are promised return; another says recollection produces longing, tears and effort toward reunion. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:3/q12/p2 It gives spiritual intimacy and insight according to the hearer's state. — Ibn Ata says the Prophet, as leader of the fearful, finds repose in the beloved's book and words. Ja'far distinguishes admonition for the fearful, mercy for sinners and intimacy for lovers. Qushayri contrasts understanding and future relief with spiritual arrival and immediate intimacy. Qashani says the Prophet's discipline has made him perfected and perfecting, while hard hearts explain resistance to guidance. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]

## 20:3/q13 How is the following word tanzilan parsed in relation to this verse?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Its relation to tadhkiratan, anzalna or yakhsha, and the nominative variant.
- 20:3/q13/p1 It is apposition to tadhkiratan. — Al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl's reordered reading, reported by Thalabi, makes tanzilan apposition to the reminder. Al-Zamakhshari also offers apposition among several alternatives; Abu Hayyan says the verbal nouns do not naturally support it. [2 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 20:3/q13/p2 It is governed by an omitted verb or anzalna, serves emphasis, or is an object of yakhsha. — Al-Zamakhshari reports these alternatives via Abu Hayyan. Abu Hayyan calls the parses strained because some lose restriction and others are distant. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 20:3/q13/p3 It is a passive verbal noun used circumstantially for the pronoun referring to the Quran. — Tabatabai reads it as a divine revelation reminding the fearful. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:3/q13/p4 A nominative reading, tanzilun, has an implicit huwa and is detached from yakhsha. — Abu Hayyan reports the reading of Ibn Abi Ablah. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة)]

## 20:3/q14 Who speaks in the preceding 'We sent down' clause?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural is God's speech or a quoted utterance by the descending angels.
- 20:3/q14/p1 God is the apparent speaker. — Abu Hayyan rejects al-Zamakhshari's suggestion that the plural may quote Gabriel and the descending angels. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)+]
- 20:3/q14/p2 Gabriel and the descending angels may be speaking in quotation. — Al-Zamakhshari proposes this possibility; Abu Hayyan says God is the apparent speaker. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]

## 20:3/q15 What do the preceding words li-tashqa and shaqa mean and how are they parsed?  [* · grammar]
- 20:3/q15/p1 The lam expresses the purpose of suffering, which the sentence negates. — Some grammarians call it a negative lam or lam al-juhud; Ibn Kaysan calls it a genitive lam. All yield that the Quran was not revealed to make Muhammad suffer. Qurtubi says shaqa can be pronounced long or short, derives from a waw root and means hardship and toil; a poet illustrates suffering amid ease and happiness amid ignorance. Al-Raghib distinguishes otherworldly from worldly misery, and worldly psychological, bodily and external misery; he reports that all misery is toil but not all toil is misery. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الحسن بن كيسان,الشاعر,بعض النحويين); TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 20:3/q16 How does the surah's opening form frame this passage?  [* · links]
- 20:3/q16/p1 Ta Ha functions as introductory disconnected letters like other surah openings. — Majaz states the shared introductory function. [1 notes: MAJAZ]
- 20:3/q16/p2 The opening uses linked ending letters comparable to the opening of al-Tur. — Rummani cites the two openings as examples of identical ending letters. [1 notes: RUMMANI]

## 20:3/q17 What contrast do verses 2 and 3 make between hardship and reminder?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:3/q17/p1 The Quran was sent as a reminder for those who fear, not to make Muhammad suffer. — Tabari reports the reading with the reminder clause placed before the denial of suffering. Baghawi gives this as an alternative parsing. Study Quran says it follows when verse 3 is read continuously with verse 2. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); STUDYQURAN]
