# 80:3: 25 questions

## 80:3/q01 What does 'what makes you know' mean?  [وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ · meaning]
- 80:3/q01/p1 What could make you know the man's state or outcome? — Al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi gloss it as knowledge of the man's condition. Ibn ʿAshur analyzes interrogative mā plus causative adrā and compares 69:3 and 6:109. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:3/q02 What does the interrogative formula do rhetorically?  [وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ · rhetoric]
- 80:3/q02/p1 It alerts the hearer to an overlooked matter, first stated generally and then explained or made more awesome. — Ibn ʿAshur gives 101:3 for the general-to-detailed movement; he quotes al-Raghib that mā adrāka is followed by an explanation, and distinguishes explicit explanation here and in 97:2–3 from heightened awe in 101:10 and 69:3. The two sets of Ibn ʿAshur notes repeat these points. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الراغب)]

## 80:3/q03 How does this clause relate syntactically to the preceding action?  [وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ · grammar]
- 80:3/q03/p1 It is a circumstantial clause qualifying the subject of the preceding frowning and turning away. — Ibn ʿAshur calls the clause and its continuation circumstantial; Tabatabaʾi explicitly attaches it to the subject of 'frowned and turned away.' [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 80:3/q04 Does 'perhaps he may purify himself' complete the verb of knowing or begin independently?  [يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُۥ يَزَّكَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether laʿalla blocks the verb's government, an object is supplied by the following clause, or the object is omitted before a new sentence.
- 80:3/q04/p1 It supplies what the Prophet could not know: that the man might purify himself. — Al-Alusi calls this the apparent attachment of the clause to the knowing verb as its object and identifies the pronoun with the blind man. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 80:3/q04/p2 Laʿalla suspends the knowing verb's object governance, making the following clause independent. — Al-Samin and Abu ʿAli al-Farisi, as reported by Ibn ʿAshur, liken hope to interrogation because both request; Ibn ʿAshur says causative yudrīka then retains one object rather than three. The later parallel note describes suspension of its two governed objects. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي)]
- 80:3/q04/p3 Pause after the question, leaving the man's affair or outcome unspoken; 'perhaps' resumes as a new statement. — Al-Samin reports a pause at yadrī and a fresh question about who can inform the Prophet. Bursevi recommends a pause because the next clause is not the question's object. Al-Alusi reports an omitted object, the man's affair and final state, followed by an explanatory resumption. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI+]

## 80:3/q05 What force does 'perhaps' have here?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether laʿalla is read as hope, an interrogative, or assurance in divine speech, and whose perspective supplies the hope.
- 80:3/q05/p1 It conveys a real prospect or hope, whether felt by the blind man or viewed from the Prophet's standpoint. — Al-Alusi says either person's hope is enough to forbid neglect, especially given the seeker's receptiveness. Bursevi says the Prophet's standpoint makes even hoped-for benefit sufficient; al-Alusi says the phrasing promises some purification without requiring perfection. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI]
- 80:3/q05/p2 It can be interrogative, asking whether purification or remembrance is hoped for. — Al-Samin says both hope and interrogation express a request and permit suspension of the knowing verb. Al-Itqan cites its use with verbs of knowing or perception. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN]
- 80:3/q05/p3 In speech of the mighty, it can express certainty despite the form of hope. — Bursevi sets this beside the alternative of hope from the Prophet's perspective, while affirming that God knows the outcome. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 80:3/q06 What knowledge of the man's future does the verse deny?  [وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُۥ يَزَّكَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 80:3/q06/p1 The Prophet could not know the man's inner state or whether instruction would purify him; only God knows hearts. — Al-Kashshaf, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Nasafi, Bursevi, and al-Alusi say the possible gain was hidden and foreknowledge would have prevented neglect. Al-Biqaʿi extends the point to all human attempts to discern hearts. Al-Kashshaf also applies uncertainty to the alternative disbeliever reading. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:3/q07 Who does the pronoun in 'perhaps he' refer to?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent available in context and whether the Prophet's hope for a pagan convert can be what the clause describes.
- 80:3/q07/p1 The blind man, Ibn Umm Maktum. — Al-Kashshaf and al-Razi connect his prospect to learning, release from ignorance or sin, and useful admonition. Al-Qurtubi calls this primary; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi follow it. Al-Alusi objects that a disbeliever has not been mentioned and a singular pronoun ill suits the group addressed, though one report has only one pagan. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القول الأول); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 80:3/q07/p2 The disbeliever whom the Prophet hoped to bring to Islam. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, and al-Baydawi report that hope for the pagan's conversion explains the turn from the blind man, though success was unknown. Abu Hayyan calls this reading unworthy of Qur'anic interpretation. Al-Alusi rejects the unmentioned singular antecedent, while acknowledging a report of one person. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القول الثاني); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 80:3/q08 What purification might 'he purify himself' describe?  [يَزَّكَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tazakkī here is conversion, learning and further moral cleansing for an existing believer, a specific form of instruction or worship, or purity already attained.
- 80:3/q08/p1 He may shed sin or ignorance and grow in goodness through the Prophet's teaching, the Quran, reflection, and good deeds. — Al-Farra, al-Tabari, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Qushayri, al-Wahidi, al-Jishumi, al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, Ibn Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Tabrisi, al-Biqaʿi, Bursevi, and al-Alusi give forms of this explanation. Al-Kalbi stresses upright conduct; Ibn ʿAtiyya adds growth in blessing and benefit from faith. Ibn Kathir adds inward purification and deterrence from forbidden acts. Tabatabaʾi makes good action follow reflection and awakening. Ibn ʿAshur stresses deeper faith, deeds, legal virtues and character after initial belief, citing the Prophet's saying about companions retaining their spiritual state and being greeted by angels. Al-Biqaʿi says even slight growth counts. Al-Wahidi prefers this reading because Ibn Umm Maktum had already believed; Bursevi adds that he knew religious essentials. [27 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكلبي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القول الأول); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(السهيلي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 80:3/q08/p2 He may come to Islam or believe. — Muqatil adds prayer and correcting what Quranic reminder exposed; Ibn Zayd identifies purification with becoming Muslim, while ʿAtaʾ and the reported Muqatil gloss say belief. Al-Wahidi rejects Islam as the intended purification because Ibn Umm Maktum was already a believer; Bursevi likewise notes his existing Islam and religious basics. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد وعطاء)-; BURSEVI(السهيلي)-]
- 80:3/q08/p3 He may worship by doing good deeds. — Al-Mawardi reports Ibn ʿIsa's specific explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى)]
- 80:3/q08/p4 He may memorize the Quran recited to him. — Al-Mawardi reports al-Dahhak's specific explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 80:3/q08/p5 He may gain understanding of religion. — Al-Mawardi reports Ibn Shajara's specific explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة)]
- 80:3/q08/p6 He is already purified from sins and receptive to admonition. — Al-Alusi reports this further view as an implication about the person alluded to. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 80:3/q09 How is the form yazzakkā derived?  [يَزَّكَّىٰٓ · grammar]
- 80:3/q09/p1 It comes from yatazakkā, with tāʾ changed to and assimilated into zāy. — Al-Nahhas, al-Jishumi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Nasafi, Bursevi, and Ibn ʿAshur give the derivation. Al-Jishumi and Ibn ʿAshur compare yadhdhakkaru from yatadhakkaru; Ibn ʿAshur explains the nearby articulation points enable assimilation. [7 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:3/q10 What does the reflexive form tazakkī convey?  [يَزَّكَّىٰٓ · meaning]
- 80:3/q10/p1 Purification takes effect in the person himself. — Ibn ʿAshur calls it the reflexive result of purification and points to his discussion at 79:18; al-Jishumi identifies tazkiyah as purification. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 80:3/q11 Does the hoped-for purification require complete purity?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ يَزَّكَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the paired possibilities describe any degree of purification or distinguish complete cleansing from lesser benefit through remembrance.
- 80:3/q11/p1 No; the phrase can mean some further purification, even slight improvement. — Al-Alusi says uncertain wording gives the basic sense without requiring perfection; al-Biqaʿi explicitly allows a slight degree of added goodness. Ibn ʿAshur describes growth beyond existing faith. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:3/q11/p2 It can mean total cleansing, with the following remembrance offering benefit if complete cleansing does not occur. — Al-Alusi reports this interpretation of the two alternatives; he calls a further distinction between obligatory and optional learning weak. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 80:3/q12 Why does the passage shift from third person to direct address?  [وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shift primarily intensifies blame, restores gracious attention, or mirrors the incident's movement.
- 80:3/q12/p1 It heightens the reproach by turning to confront the person after reporting his act. — Bursevi compares a complainant confronting an offender; al-Alusi reports that image as censure supported by proof. Tabatabaʾi says direct address intensifies accountability after third-person distancing. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 80:3/q12/p2 It softens or consoles the Prophet: the frown is first veiled, then direct address restores closeness. — Al-Tabrisi says God does not begin with 'you frowned.' Al-Alusi calls the turn attention after estrangement; Tabatabaʾi reports that third person implies someone else acted and direct address restores intimacy. [3 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 80:3/q12/p3 Its sequence mirrors the Prophet's first turning away and then addressing the man. — Bursevi reports al-Suhayli's reading of the narrative movement, which also consoles the Prophet. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي)]

## 80:3/q13 Why is the visitor called blind in this passage?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:3/q13/p1 The description shows his excuse for interrupting and calls for compassion toward him and similarly weak people. — Al-Alusi says he could not see that the Prophet was occupied; he extends the consideration beyond this particular blind man. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:3/q14 Who is reproached in the opening passage?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the narrated occasion identifies the unnamed subject despite al-Murtada's appeal to the text's silence and prophetic character.
- 80:3/q14/p1 The Prophet, who turned from Ibn Umm Maktum while addressing potential converts. — The reported incident identifies his frown and turn; al-Tabrisi, Bursevi, al-Alusi and Tabatabaʾi discuss the manner of the rebuke on this assumption. The reports vary in how explicitly they name the Prophet's interlocutors. [10 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)+]
- 80:3/q14/p2 The text does not establish that the Prophet is its subject. — Al-Murtada, reported and rejected by al-Tabrisi, argues that the Prophet is unnamed and that frowning at a believing seeker or favoring the rich over the poor conflicts with his noble character. [0 notes: TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)-]

## 80:3/q15 What happened when Ibn Umm Maktum came for teaching?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the Prophet's audience is described as several named leaders or one prospective convert, with variant names for that individual.
- 80:3/q15/p1 He interrupted an appeal to Meccan or Quraysh leaders; the Prophet turned back to them or frowned, fearing elite disdain for poor or disabled followers. — Al-Jishumi reports al-Hasan and others. Al-Tabrisi and the al-Majmaʿ report quoted by Tabatabaʾi name ʿUtba, Abu Jahl, al-ʿAbbas, Ubayy and Umayya ibn Khalaf and identify the man also as ʿAbdullah ibn Shurayh ibn Malik ibn Rabiʿa al-Fihri; they mention repeated requests for instruction and fear that leaders would scorn followers who were blind or enslaved. Islahi stresses fear of offending Quraysh vanity and neglect of a worthier student. [5 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل); TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:3/q15/p2 The Prophet was hoping to convert a particular pagan, named al-Walid or Umayya, whose conversion might bring many others. — Bursevi reports al-Suhayli's alternative identifications and his explanation that Ibn Umm Maktum was already Muslim and knew essential religion whereas the pagan did not yet believe. [2 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي)]

## 80:3/q16 What happened to Ibn Umm Maktum after the revelation?  [* · reports]
- 80:3/q16/p1 The Prophet honored him, welcomed him as the man for whom God reproached him, and asked about his needs. — Al-Jishumi and al-Tabrisi report the greeting and concern; al-Farra more generally reports careful and honorable treatment. Al-Tabrisi says he was twice appointed deputy in Medina; al-Farra says he was appointed to lead prayer. [3 notes: FARRA; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:3/q16/p2 Anas later saw him at al-Qadisiyya in armor, carrying a black banner. — Al-Jishumi and al-Tabrisi transmit Anas's report; the latter calls it a black standard. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أنس); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك)]
- 80:3/q16/p3 The episode gave him an enduring great reputation, unlike Pharaoh, who neither purified himself nor gained from a reminder. — Al-Biqaʿi draws this comparison between the two responses and their outcomes. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 80:3/q17 What does the saying about concealing revelation indicate about this passage?  [* · reports]
- 80:3/q17/p1 If the Messenger had concealed any revelation, he would have concealed this reproach. — Al-Jishumi reports Ibn Zayd's saying. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن زيد)]

## 80:3/q18 What teaching priority follows from the possible purification of the seeker?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ يَزَّكَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 80:3/q18/p1 Attend to receptive seekers, even when their benefit is only hoped for; wealth or rank cannot replace willingness to learn. — Bursevi says outwardly preferring the rich hurt the poor seeker despite a hoped-for larger conversion benefit, so the better course was missed. Al-Alusi says reasonable hope sufficed to forbid turning away. Islahi says spiritual purification is the messengers' mission and that questions or attentive listening both merit attention; those seeking pampering do not advance that mission. [6 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 80:3/q18/p2 The possible purification of the blind man indirectly suggests that the disbelievers being addressed would not purify themselves. — Al-Alusi likens it to telling a poor learner that another, capable listener might understand. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:3/q19 How does this episode serve the sura's wider message?  [* · links]
turns on: How the episode is related to the sura's themes and to al-Naziʿat.
- 80:3/q19/p1 It opens a rebuke of favoring privileged people over poor believers, followed by human dependence, ingratitude, resurrection, recompense and warning. — Tabatabaʾi traces that sequence through the sura. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 80:3/q19/p2 It shows the warning mission purifying receptive hearts; creation and food then show God's power and the folly of rejection. — Al-Biqaʿi links warning those who fear, citing 79:45, to resurrection, human creation and sustenance. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:3/q19/p3 It shares al-Naziʿat's central theme, discourse style and reasoning, while changing the order of topics. — Islahi describes the two suras as a pair and notes this sura's distinctive sequence; the note mentions 79:45. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:3/q20 Where was this sura revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 80:3/q20/p1 It is Meccan. — Tabatabaʾi calls this unquestionable. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 80:3/q21 What saying about disparaging people accompanies this discussion?  [* · reports]
- 80:3/q21/p1 The Prophet said that Aisha's remark about a woman's shortness would contaminate the sea; belittling a person is backbiting. — Ibn ʿAtiyya transmits the exchange between Aisha and the Prophet. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 80:3/q22 How is the earlier causal expression attached to 'frowned and turned away'?  [* · grammar]
- 80:3/q22/p1 Its causal lām is elided; it grammatically modifies the first verb for Kufans, the second for Basrans, or both for al-Farra, while causally explaining both. — Al-Alusi reports all three attachments and says their meaning agrees. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 80:3/q23 What variant of the earlier verb 'frowned' is reported?  [* · readings]
- 80:3/q23/p1 Zayd ibn Ali doubles the bāʾ in ʿabasa for intensification. — Al-Alusi says this doubling is emphatic rather than causative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]

## 80:3/q24 How is the earlier interrogative hamza read?  [* · readings]
- 80:3/q24/p1 Some read one hamza, while others articulate two; both express repudiating interrogation. — Al-Alusi names al-Hasan, Abu ʿImran al-Jawni and ʿIsa for one hamza, and unnamed other readers for two. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وأبو عمران الجوني وعيسى وبعض القراء)]

## 80:3/q25 Where is the earlier pause at 'turned away' placed?  [* · grammar]
- 80:3/q25/p1 A pause is made at tawallā, then an elliptical exception gives 'except that the blind man came' before the rebuke. — Al-Alusi supplies the proposed pause and ellipsis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
