# 74:23: 10 questions

## 74:23/q01 What does thumma indicate in this sequence?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the successive expressions describe delays between acts or a rhetorical rise within one period of deliberation.
- 74:23/q01/p1 It marks a delay or successive stages after his thinking and plotting. — Al-Nasafi says the earlier 'he looked' is coordinated with 'he thought and plotted,' the curse is parenthetical, and repeated thumma marks delays. Al-Tabataba'i describes deliberation, looking, frowning, turning away, pride, then speech; Asad gives the same unfolding search for arguments. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 74:23/q01/p2 It raises the description in rank rather than marking elapsed time. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the successive acts are coordinated with 'devised' and all happened during his thinking and planning. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:23/q02 What does 'turned away' mean, and from what did he turn?  [أَدْبَرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether idbār describes rejection of faith or its messenger, bodily departure, or abandonment of his search for a description.
- 74:23/q02/p1 He turned from truth, faith, the Qur'an, or the call to believe. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Māwardī's reported explanation, al-Wāḥidī citing Muqātil, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī's reported view, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Burūsawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and the Study Quran describe rejection of faith or truth; al-Ṭabarī specifies God's revelation, Ibn Kathīr submission to the Qur'an, and Elmalılı a move from faith to unbelief. Al-Kashshāf places the rejection before his charge. Al-Biqāʿī says reflection had led toward recognition of the Qur'an's soundness. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Pharaoh's turning away in 79:22. Abū Ḥayyān reports departure from truth as one alternative. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 74:23/q02/p2 He physically turned or left, going to his family in some accounts. — Al-Jishumī explains idbār as moving rearward, the opposite of facing forward, and contrasts tadabbur, considering an outcome. Al-Māwardī's alternative says he left his place and spoke arrogantly. Al-Wāḥidī says he went to his family as a denier; al-Rāzī says he left the others for his family; al-Qurṭubī also names his family. Abū Ḥayyān reports a physical turn as an alternative. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 74:23/q02/p3 He turned away from the Messenger. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī give the Messenger as an alternative object to truth; both pair this with arrogant refusal to follow him. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:23/q02/p4 He abandoned his attempt to devise a description of the Qur'an in despair. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as a possible metaphor, tying the abandoned effort to his devising in 74:18, and says he then turned to arrogant disdain. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:23/q03 What does his growing arrogant consist in?  [وَٱسْتَكْبَرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the arrogance chiefly names refusal of revelation, pride in his own standing, or arrogant speech.
- 74:23/q03/p1 He considered himself above accepting faith, truth, obedience, or following the Messenger. — Al-Naḥḥās contrasts his refusal to believe with his willingness to bow to powerless stones. Al-Māwardī reports arrogance toward obedience; al-Wāḥidī says he held himself above faith; several sources link it to an invitation to believe. Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī describe refusal to believe. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī specify refusal to follow the Messenger. Al-Nasafī and the Study Quran describe rejecting or disdaining truth; the Study Quran explains an implied ʿan ('from') after istakbara and also gives scorn for the Prophet. Al-Biqāʿī says he rejected truth despite his need to acknowledge it. Al-Tabataba'i treats pride as an inward state made evident by his words. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, JJ)]
- 74:23/q03/p2 He acted proudly about his own standing and speech. — Al-Nasafī gives this as an alternative to arrogant rejection of truth. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 74:23/q03/p3 He spoke arrogantly after leaving his place. — This is al-Māwardī's alternative account of the outward act. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 74:23/q04 Who is the man described here?  [* · referent]
- 74:23/q04/p1 He is al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra al-Makhzūmī. — Al-Jishumī reports, according to Ibn ʿĀmir, that he was wealthy, had sons, and was called al-Waḥīd among his people. Ibn Kathīr calls him a Quraysh leader. Other notes explicitly identify al-Walīd in the scene. [10 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 74:23/q05 At whom was his preceding scowl directed?  [* · links]
turns on: Whom the earlier facial gesture addressed.
- 74:23/q05/p1 At the Prophet when he called him. — Al-Māwardī proposes this possibility; Abū Ḥayyān also reports that the facial expression might have been toward the Prophet. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 74:23/q05/p2 At those who believed in and supported the Prophet. — Al-Māwardī gives this as an alternative target of the scowl. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 74:23/q06 What did the preceding frown and scowl show as he turned away?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the expression reflects exhausted options, contrived denial, consideration of proofs, or recognition resisted through envy and pride.
- 74:23/q06/p1 They were a changed, rejecting expression. — Al-Māwardī reports al-Suddī's gloss of busūr as 'changed' and cites a line attributed to Tubbaʿ illustrating altered, rejecting manner. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي وتوبة)]
- 74:23/q06/p2 He had run out of options for describing the Qur'an. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this possible reading of his frowning and scowling. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 74:23/q06/p3 His strained gestures exposed a fabricated or staged judgment. — Abū Ḥayyān calls them satirical signs of fabrication: a truthful judgment would bring visible joy without strained, delayed deliberation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the succession satirizes his contortions while inventing a description for the Qur'an. Iṣlāḥī says he assessed listeners' moods and frowned to convey a hopeless judgment before muttering as he turned away. Asad traces his search for disproof and arguments before frowning. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 74:23/q06/p4 He had considered the Qur'an's proofs before frowning. — Abū Ḥayyān records this as another view, alongside his own account of satirical fabrication. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 74:23/q06/p5 He recognized a sound judgment or Muhammad's superior rank, then frowned from envy and turned away in pride. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes this conditional on reading the curse as praise of his first judgment; al-Biqāʿī likewise says reflection had led him toward belief in the Qur'an's soundness before he refused to acknowledge it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 74:23/q07 How do these acts connect with the preceding gestures and the following words?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the conjunctions are explained through the narrated order and immediate speech, or through the causal relation between pride and turning.
- 74:23/q07/p1 The account moves from deliberation and visible gestures to turning away, inward pride, and then speech. — Al-Tabataba'i gives this order and says the inward states become apparent in his words; the following fāʾ links the words to those attitudes. Al-Rāzī says fāʾ marks immediate speech after turning in pride. Abū Ḥayyān also says fāʾ signals immediate utterance after the charge formed. Al-Nasafī treats the curse as parenthetical within the action sequence. Al-Māwardī distinguishes scowling after conversation from scowling before it. Asad and Iṣlāḥī give detailed sequences ending in the turn and utterance; al-Burūsawī says he turned away, refused to follow, and then spoke. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:23/q07/p2 Arrogance motivated his turning away. — Abū Ḥayyān says the paired acts are causally related even though the wording names turning away first; he also observes that frowning and scowling are near-synonyms. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 74:23/q08 What charge did he voice after turning away?  [* · links]
turns on: How the dismissal of the Qur'an in the ensuing speech is characterized.
- 74:23/q08/p1 He called the Qur'an magic, learned from another human being. — Muqātil adds that he called it human speech. Al-Ṭabarī likewise says he claimed magic taken from another person. Al-Jishumī's story says he ruled out calling Muhammad a soothsayer, poet, or madman, chose 'magician,' and claimed the Qur'an divided families. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he called it magic after turning in pride. Al-Kashshāf places the charge after rejecting truth. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 74:23/q08/p2 He dismissed the Qur'an as the Prophet's stirring eloquence. — Iṣlāḥī says the Quraysh leader delivered this excuse disdainfully, ignored the warning, and mistook the Qur'an's statements for eloquence although he would soon meet their realities. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:23/q09 What wider warning does his conduct carry?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer a general application to later unbelievers and a warning about the truth of what he dismissed.
- 74:23/q09/p1 Others can repeat al-Walīd's arrogant unbelief toward the Qur'an. — Kur'an Yolu says verses 18–25 condemn people who adopt the same unbelieving posture. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:23/q09/p2 His disdain made him ignore the Qur'an's warning, whose realities he would soon encounter. — Iṣlāḥī says he misread its message as a prophet's stirring eloquence, despite the realities it announced. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:23/q10 Which other verses are used to illuminate this turn and pride?  [* · links]
turns on: Which Qur'anic parallel each source invokes.
- 74:23/q10/p1 Qur'an 96:6–7 is a comparison for his arrogance. — Asad directs readers to these verses for comparison. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 74:23/q10/p2 Pharaoh's turning away in 79:22 is a comparison for rejecting belief. — Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes Pharaoh's description to explain this possible sense of adbara. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
