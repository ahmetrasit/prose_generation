# 27:7: 29 questions

## 27:7/q01 What governs the opening إذ?  [إِذْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the story opens under an implied imperative or is syntactically attached to the preceding divine attribute, and whether the latter would limit that attribute to a time.
- 27:7/q01/p1 An omitted command to remember governs إذ, introducing Moses' story. — Al-Zajjāj calls it accusative and paraphrases recall Moses' story; al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī address Muḥammad. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn, and Ibn ʿĀshūr also allow the adjacent ʿalīm analysis. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 2:30 and takes it as a general time expression, not a temporal adverb. Al-Biqāʿī says the governing verb is omitted because obvious. Al-Ālūsī prefers this analysis as a recitation of revealed history confirming what precedes. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزجاج والزمخشري وغيرهما); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:7/q01/p2 إذ is attached to the preceding divine attribute ʿalīm: God knew at the time of Moses' speech. — Al-Ṭabarī takes this temporal attachment. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī allow it. Abū Ḥayyān objects that a temporal complement would restrict the divine attribute; al-Samīn likewise finds it weak. Al-Kashf answers, reported by al-Ālūsī, that the scene exemplifies God's knowledge and wisdom generally and does not restrict them. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 27:7/q01/p3 An omitted command to know, inferred from a preceding attribute, governs إذ. — Al-Samīn reports this possible analysis from ʿalīm or ḥalīm but objects that it ties the attribute to this circumstance. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]

## 27:7/q02 Whom does Moses call his family?  [لِأَهْلِهِۦٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether ahl and the plural address designate only the wife or other companions, especially children, as well.
- 27:7/q02/p1 His wife, Shuʿayb's daughter, is the family member addressed; some accounts say she alone accompanied him. — Muqātil and al-Zajjāj identify his wife; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya name Shuʿayb's daughter. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that ahl euphemistically means wife and cites the hadith والله ما علمت على أهلي إلا خيرا; he nevertheless says two young sons accompanied them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites al-Qaṣaṣ 28:23, 25, 27, and allows other companions. [15 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الحديث); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 27:7/q02/p2 His wife and other companions or children were with him. — Al-Jishumī reports children with her; al-Nasafī names wife and companions; Bursevī describes wife and those with her. Abū Ḥayyān reports a son born during the stay with Shuʿayb, Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies two young sons, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows others. Elmâlılı takes the plural as evidence of more than one addressee. [7 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 27:7/q03 Why does Moses address his family in the plural?  [سَـَٔاتِيكُم لَّعَلَّكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural is a manner of addressing one wife or counts other hearers.
- 27:7/q03/p1 One wife can be addressed as a group or honorifically. — Al-Jishumī and al-Kashshāf report the wife-alone explanation. Al-Ṭabrisī says she gave Moses familiarity and safety in desolation; Abū Ḥayyān mentions an honorific plural, and al-Ālūsī the wider sense of household and followers. Al-Biqāʿī says the masculine plural discreetly protects mention of his wife. Al-Bayḍāwī notes that ahl can euphemistically denote a wife; al-Majmaʿ, cited by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, treats her as company in loneliness. [9 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 27:7/q03/p2 The plural reflects multiple actual hearers accompanying his wife. — Al-Jishumī reports her children; al-Nasafī says wife and companions, Abū Ḥayyān reports one son, and Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies two young sons. Elmâlılı reads the plural as showing more than one family member. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:7/q04 What circumstances brought Moses to the fire?  [إِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ نَارًۭا · reports]
turns on: The first answer recounts the journey's practical circumstances; the second emphasizes its prophetic outcome.
- 27:7/q04/p1 Traveling from Midian toward Egypt, Moses lost the road on a cold dark night; his fire-making failed, so he sought directions and warmth. — Al-Ṭabarī says his flint failed; Ibn ʿAbbās says he had completed his term. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds rain, lost fire, and failed fire-sticks. Bursevī reports his wife in labor and a fire by the Mount; al-Ālūsī specifies Wadi Ṭuwā, and Elmâlılı says all means had failed. Al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Iṣlāḥī and Kur'an Yolu give variants of the same journey. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 27:7/q04/p2 Moses found the fire at the place where divine calling and prophecy awaited him. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim presents the lost road as the setting for divine honor, prophecy and speech. Ibn Kathīr places the fire by the Mount and says Moses returned with momentous news and great light; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the utterance immediately prepares for the call in 27:8. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:7/q05 What does آنست mean here?  [ءَانَسْتُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb merely states sensory perception or also conveys reassurance and familiarity.
- 27:7/q05/p1 Moses saw or perceived a fire. — Abū ʿUbayda links ins to what is visible; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a line of Ḥassān, and al-Qurṭubī a line of al-Ḥārith ibn Ḥilliza. Al-Rāzī prefers seeing because Arabic does not distinguish perceived with my eye from saw with my eye. Bursevī's Tāj relates the root to appearance and the departure of estrangement; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes subtle sensory noticing by sight or hearing. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(الحارث بن حلزة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(التاج); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); RAZI-FULL+]
- 27:7/q05/p2 Moses sensed or encountered the fire in a reassuring, familiar way. — Qatāda, al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Jishumī stress perception with familiarity or calm; al-Rāzī reports coming upon the fire and feeling familiarity. Al-Biqāʿī says the sight dispelled loneliness and fear. Al-Majmaʿ, reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, offers this as an alternative gloss. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); MAJAZ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(آخرون); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان)]

## 27:7/q06 What was the fire Moses perceived?  [نَارًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the apparent fire is identified as divine light or only described as it appeared to Moses.
- 27:7/q06/p1 It appeared to be fire but was divine light. — Muqātil calls it the light of the Lord of Majesty, seen Friday night on the mountain's right in the Holy Land; Bursevī repeats him. Yaḥyā ibn Sallām cites 20:10 and al-Suddī's light gloss. Ibn ʿAbbās says it looked like fire but was God's light; Kur'an Yolu says commentators explain it so. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it otherworldly light visible only to Moses. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 27:7/q06/p2 He saw a distant, blazing fire by the Mount. — Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn Kathīr describe a distant fire; Ibn Kathīr calls it flaring. Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Elmâlılı put it by the Mount in a cold, dark setting. These descriptions report its appearance without resolving its underlying nature. [9 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 27:7/q07 Who could see the fire, and why is the statement emphatic?  [إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ نَارًۭا · interpretation]
- 27:7/q07/p1 The fire was shown only to Moses; the emphatic statement may answer his wife's doubt. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers that otherworldly light was visible only to Moses, and that inna suggests his wife could not see it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that, had the others seen it, Moses would have pointed to the already known fire instead of introducing it indefinitely. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 27:7/q08 To what does the following جاءها refer?  [نَارًۭا · referent]
- 27:7/q08/p1 Its pronoun refers to the fire Moses saw. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies the antecedent of the following verse's pronoun. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 27:7/q09 What does the future prefix in سآتيكم convey?  [سَـَٔاتِيكُم · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether sīn chiefly strengthens the assurance or marks distance and expected delay; several sources combine them.
- 27:7/q09/p1 It assures his family that he will return with something, even if delayed. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and al-Bayḍāwī treat sīn as strengthening a promise. Al-Ālūsī prefers this over a mere distance reading and rejects a simple short-wait implication. Al-Biqāʿī says the firm promise suits the sūra's concern with knowledge; al-Sāmarrāʾī says it conveys certainty and empowerment relative to al-Qaṣaṣ. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:7/q09/p2 It allows for the fire's distance or the time needed to reach it. — Al-Zamakhsharī suggests a long distance; al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī mention distance or delay. Al-Ālūsī allows reassurance despite distance but prefers the stronger promise analysis. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:7/q10 What news did Moses hope to bring?  [بِخَبَرٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether khabar names directions, information about the fire's location, or a lexical quality of reliable information.
- 27:7/q10/p1 Directions or information about the road they had lost. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Muqātil, and numerous authors say the family was off the route. Yaḥyā ibn Sallām connects 20:10 to finding guides. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī envisage a person near the fire who could give directions; al-Biqāʿī says news might guide them through darkness. Study Quran attributes the road interpretation to IK and R. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IK, R)]
- 27:7/q10/p2 Information about the fire or the place where it appeared. — Ibn Shajara identifies knowledge about the fire; Ibn ʿĀshūr says news of the place from which it appeared and suggests hospitality from a household there. Al-Ṭabarī observes that the suffix in minhā refers to the fire, establishing the source of the news without specifying its subject. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:7/q10/p3 Truthful or certain information, as illustrated by the hoopoe's report from Sheba. — Ibn al-Jawzī's Nuzha records this second sense and cites the hoopoe's report as parallel usage. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 27:7/q11 Why is news mentioned before a firebrand?  [بِخَبَرٍ أَوْ ءَاتِيكُم بِشِهَابٍۢ قَبَسٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited notes give a spiritual ordering and a practical or thematic ordering.
- 27:7/q11/p1 News comes first as the higher, spiritual or otherworldly aim, before the worldly benefit of warmth. — Elmâlılı gives this ordering and treats the party's two needs as practical and spiritual. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 27:7/q11/p2 News takes priority because guidance through the lost road would solve their immediate plight. — Al-Biqāʿī observes that the broad word news can include route guidance and suits the sūra's concern with knowledge; the sources describing their lost way supply the practical setting. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 27:7/q12 What does أو imply about Moses' two hoped-for returns?  [أَوْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the travelers needed only one remedy, needed both, or simply hoped that at least one would be available.
- 27:7/q12/p1 He expected at least one of directions or fire, even if he could not get both. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī connect this with trust that God would not combine two deprivations. Al-Nasafī says the options do not promise both. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī call them uncertain hopes; al-Samīn says aw offers ordinary alternatives of different kinds. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the repeated verb marks the two returns. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 27:7/q12/p2 One outcome would suffice: a guide to continue, or fire to warm them if they had to remain. — Abū Ḥayyān says these are conditional alternatives and not simultaneous needs; al-Ālūsī reports the same view. Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī and Bursevī describe fire as the fallback if no guide is found. [5 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 27:7/q12/p3 He needed both outcomes, but said or to avoid claiming certainty about getting both. — Al-Ālūsī counters the sufficiency view with the reported newborn, bitter cold, pitch darkness, lost road and scattered livestock; careful truthful speech explains the alternative. Elmâlılı also calls directions and warmth two needs. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 27:7/q13 How can Moses promise a return here while speaking tentatively in parallel accounts?  [سَـَٔاتِيكُم لَّعَلَّكُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether the difference is ordinary variation between confidence and hope or a deliberate fit to each sūra's fearful or confident atmosphere.
- 27:7/q13/p1 Strong hope can be spoken as a promise while still admitting possible failure; the accounts need not conflict. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī compare 20:10 or 28:29. Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes that ordinary speech can move between resolve and qualification as circumstances change. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:7/q13/p2 The firm Naml wording suits strength and honor, while the hopeful Qasas wording suits Moses' fear there. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says fear pervades al-Qaṣaṣ from before Moses' birth, whereas al-Naml is an atmosphere of certainty, empowerment and honor. He notes the Naml promise is firmer even though bringing a flaming brand is harder than bringing an ember. The narrative scope also differs: Qasas gives the long life account and Naml a brief episode. [12 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:7/q14 Why is آتيكم repeated before the second possibility?  [سَـَٔاتِيكُم ءَاتِيكُم · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the repetition principally separates the alternatives or contributes to a confident stylistic pattern.
- 27:7/q14/p1 It presents news and fire as two distinct possible returns. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains the repeated verb by the distinct objects of the two returns; al-Sāmarrāʾī observes that al-Qaṣaṣ uses it only once. [2 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:7/q14/p2 The repetition adds resolve and confidence and fits al-Naml's frequent use of the verb for coming. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Qaṣaṣ's fearful atmosphere and counts twelve uses of coming in al-Naml against six in al-Qaṣaṣ, citing 27:18, 21, 28, 37–40, 54–55, 87 and 28:30, 46, 49, 71–72. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:7/q15 What does شهاب mean?  [بِشِهَابٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is defined by flame, any brightness, a meteor as the original referent, or a concrete burning stick.
- 27:7/q15/p1 A bright flame or extended column of firelight. — Al-Layth gives a blazing flame and plurals shuhub and shuhbān. Al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Majmaʿ describe column-like or extended light. Abū ʿUbayda glosses fire; the poems cited by al-Māwardī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Qurṭubī portray luminous flame, spearhead, meteor trail or a brand that burns out. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites lexicons and says a flaming shihāb gives more light and warmth than an ember. - 27:7/q15/p2 Any white or luminous object, including star, fire, spearhead or burning stick, can be a shihāb. — Al-Zajjāj offers the broad definition; Abū ʿAlī is unsure whether it is transmitted or inferred. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī include stars and burning sticks; al-Māwardī notes a streaking star, and al-Fārisī cites a bright spear and meteor poetry. - 27:7/q15/p4 A fire-bearing stick, with an ember or flame at one end and none at the other. — Ibn al-Sikkīt and Abū al-Haytham describe the burning stick; al-Baghawī and Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā specify the one burning end. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this image is evoked here; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a burning ember. ## 27:7/q16 What does قبس mean and why is it paired with شهاب?  [قَبَسٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: The first is the concrete object sought here; the second is a cited extension of the same root.
- 27:7/q16/p1 Fire or a flame taken from another fire, especially a live ember or brand to kindle or carry warmth. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Naḥḥās, al-Māwardī, al-Jishumī and many others define taken fire. Yaḥyā ibn Sallām compares the glowing ember of 28:29; al-Baghawī describes a brand burning at one end, al-Biqāʿī a dry branch carrying established fire. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares qabḍ, and al-Qurṭubī derives the noun from aqbasa. Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss is supported by lines of Ṭarafa; Abū Zubayd and other spearhead poetry illustrate the flame. The qualifying word distinguishes a kindled or taken flame from one not used as a brand, including a meteor. [34 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(الشاعر); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); MAWARDI-FULL(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:7/q16/p2 The word also extends metaphorically to acquiring illuminating knowledge or guidance. — Al-Māwardī, al-Jishumī and Bursevī explain iqtibās as borrowing knowledge as one takes light from fire; Bursevī cites the idiom for taking both fire and knowledge. Al-Sāmarrāʾī gives qabasa al-ʿilma. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:7/q17 How is شهاب قبس read and parsed?  [بِشِهَابٍۢ قَبَسٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The reading's tanwīn and whether qabas is adjective, apposition, noun for a distinct firebrand, verbal noun, or an allegedly self-annexed synonymous name. Al-Naḥḥās rejects only the last iḍāfa explanation.
- 27:7/q17/p1 With tanwīn on shihāb, qabas stands separately as an adjective or apposition. — The Kūfan readers ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are repeatedly named; later lists include Yaʿqūb, sometimes except Zayd, and Khalaf. Al-Zajjāj, Abū ʿAlī and al-Fārisī favor adjectival agreement if qabas means kindled or taken; al-Ṭabarī allows apposition when flame and taken fire are identical. Al-Mahdawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and others allow both parses. Al-Qurṭubī mentions an uncanonical accusative as apposition or circumstance; al-Akhfash accepts both constructional possibilities. - 27:7/q17/p2 Without tanwīn, the words form an iḍāfa, a flame of a firebrand or taken fire. — Al-Ṭabarī attributes this to Medinan and Basran readers, while later lists give the majority and other seven readers; note source lists vary. Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim prefer it, as does Abū al-Ḥasan because it is common, comparing brick house and gold bracelet. Al-Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain genus-to-kind or material annexation; al-Ṭabarī and al-Zamakhsharī distinguish flame from taken fire. Al-Farrāʾ instead defends different names for one thing with dār al-ākhira, 12:109, ḥabbat al-khaḍrāʾ and laylat al-qamrāʾ; al-Naḥḥās rejects literal self-annexation. Ibn Khālawayh also suggests omitted min; Abū ʿAlī allows qabas as a verbal noun, comparing ḥalab and badā, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares jalb. Both readings are correct and close in sense for al-Ṭabarī. ## 27:7/q18 How is the first-person yāʾ of إني read?  [إِنِّىٓ · readings]
- 27:7/q18/p1 The yāʾ is opened with fatḥa, yielding inniya without a change in meaning. — Ibn al-Jazarī names the two Medinan readers, Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr; Qirāʾāt ER notes the suffix-vowel variant. Ibn Mujāhid counts 27 possessive yāʾs in the sūra, six disputed, and names the first four locations in the cited excerpt. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID; NASHR(المدنيان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ)]

## 27:7/q19 What does لعلّكم تصطلون mean?  [لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَصْطَلُونَ · meaning]
- 27:7/q19/p1 So that, or in hope that, you may warm yourselves by the fire against the cold. — The gloss is shared from Yaḥyā ibn Sallām through al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. Al-Wāḥidī equates ṣalā and iṣṭalā with warming by fire; al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī say ṣilāʾ can denote a great fire. Al-Ālūsī says ṣalāʾ may mean approaching fire for heat or the fire itself, with vocalization distinguishing the senses; Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the iftiʿāl form as an effort to get warm. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,عمرو بن ميمون,محمد بن اسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 27:7/q19/p2 They might find relief from the cold. — ʿAmr ibn Maymūn phrases the purpose as relief from cold; this is a nuance of the warming gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عمرو بن ميمون)]

## 27:7/q20 What is the morphology of تصطلون?  [تَصْطَلُونَ · grammar]
- 27:7/q20/p1 Its ṭāʾ replaces the iftiʿāl tāʾ near the emphatic ṣād. — Al-Naḥḥās says the tāʾ was replaced to harmonize with ṣād; al-Qurṭubī and al-Samīn concur. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives iṣṭilāʾ from burning or roasting by fire and says the form conveys seeking warmth. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:7/q21 What season and immediate need does the warmth phrase imply?  [لَّعَلَّكُمْ تَصْطَلُونَ · reports]
- 27:7/q21/p1 It was winter or a severely cold night, and the family needed warmth. — Qatāda says winter; al-Kalbī says the depth of winter. Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda say cold struck the travelers. Al-Baghawī calls it severe winter; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī and others describe cold weather and failed fire-making. An anonymous verse quoted by al-Qurṭubī calls fire the fruit of winter. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(شاعر مجهول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 27:7/q22 What does the report say happened after Moses reached the calling place?  [مُوسَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The notes report different subsequent events rather than an explicit argument between them.
- 27:7/q22/p1 Moses left his family there for forty years before returning to carry out God's command. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits this through Jaʿfar ibn Abī al-Mughīra. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(جعفر بن أبي المغيرة)]
- 27:7/q22/p2 He was honored with divine speech and prophecy and returned with momentous news and great light. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim frames the lost route as preparation for honor, prophecy and speech. Ibn Kathīr says Moses' prediction was fulfilled by the news and light he brought back. Al-Zamakhsharī says Moses did not yet know he would receive both worldly honor and honor in the afterlife. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 27:7/q23 How does this account differ in wording from the Moses accounts in Ṭā Hā and al-Qaṣaṣ?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the notes simply catalogue the textual differences or explain them through the two sūras' differing narrative scope and mood.
- 27:7/q23/p1 The parallel accounts vary in detail and expression while recounting the same encounter. — Al-Wāḥidī and Asad refer readers to Ṭā Hā 20:9–10; Kur'an Yolu also names al-Aʿrāf and al-Qaṣaṣ. Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes that al-Qaṣaṣ adds the mountain-side location and the command to wait, whereas al-Naml is concise and repeats ātiikum. Al-Qaṣaṣ names a jadhwa ember; al-Naml a bright shihāb qabas. Al-Ṭāhā narrative and the wording of 28:29 supply comparison points; Study Quran also mentions Exodus 3:1–6. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 27:7/q23/p2 Each wording fits its sūra's scope and atmosphere: concise, confident al-Naml versus detailed, fearful al-Qaṣaṣ. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī ties the added side-of-Mount and stay details to al-Qaṣaṣ's extended story from before birth to Pharaoh's drowning, and the brighter flame and repeated coming to al-Naml's strength. He also pairs specific idkhāl with specific shihāb qabas and broader sulūk with broader jadhwa, and says each expression accurately conveys its context. [16 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:7/q24 Why use the brighter شهاب قبس rather than the ember of al-Qaṣaṣ?  [بِشِهَابٍۢ قَبَسٍۢ · links]
- 27:7/q24/p1 A blazing flame gives more warmth and illumination and suits the resolve of al-Naml's scene. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says a jadhwa might be a spent piece of wood or a stick with fire but no flame; the shihāb is harder to bring and signals courage and strength. He says wording that Moses will take a flame from the fire suggests purposeful action, and links the contrast to al-Qaṣaṣ's fear and al-Naml's confidence. [9 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:7/q25 What does this episode contribute to the sūra's presentation of revelation and prophecy?  [* · links]
turns on: These sources emphasize different implications of the same episode: divine wisdom, prophetic consolation, divine training, sura pattern, or expressive density.
- 27:7/q25/p1 Moses' encounter displays God's knowledge and wisdom and parallels Muḥammad's receipt of revelation. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī treat it as another sign of divine knowledge; al-Biqāʿī says the ordered stories demonstrate perfect wisdom and knowledge and counter claims that the Qur'an is human or exhaustible. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the transition from revelation's praise to unseen prophetic history and the parallel to divine speech at 27:9; he calls it a form of inimitability and an admonitory model. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the five stories realizing the sūra's opening promise and warning, with the first three emphasizing promise. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 27:7/q25/p2 The story consoles and instructs the Prophet and confronts opponents through an earlier prophet's similar mission. — Ibn Kathīr traces God's choosing Moses, giving signs and sending him to Pharaoh, whose elite rejected them. Iṣlāḥī says revelation is God's favor, Moses' experience reassures Muḥammad, and his story addresses rivals, especially Israelites who professed Moses while opposing Muḥammad. He describes nine signs, Pharaoh's recognized but rejected divine message, and destruction; he says earlier prophetic stories offer conclusive truth to opponents. [10 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 27:7/q25/p3 Prophets are selected and trained by God through life events, rather than attaining the office by study or personal effort. — Iṣlāḥī contrasts prophethood with learned crafts; chosen people and their audiences need time to adjust. He cites Joseph's prison, Moses tending Jethro's goats in Midian, and a report of David moving from flock-keeping to kingship as examples of preparation through trials. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(David (sws))]
- 27:7/q25/p4 Every sūra opening with ṭā places a detailed Moses account near its beginning. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts this pattern with sūras beginning with other disconnected letters. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 27:7/q25/p5 Its concision suggests profound inspirations exceeding ordinary exposition. — Elmâlılı observes that the terse wording carries more than ordinary commentary can contain. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 27:7/q26 What legal inference is drawn from differing Moses narratives?  [* · law]
- 27:7/q26/p1 The different wording supports transmitting hadith by meaning and a valid marriage without an explicit I marry you formula. — Al-Nasafī argues from variant wording for the same story, comparing 28:29. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 27:7/q27 How can Moses' words be given in Arabic if he did not speak Arabic?  [إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ نَارًۭا · interpretation]
- 27:7/q27/p1 The Qur'anic Arabic renders an equivalent expression in Moses' own language. — Al-Ālūsī says the quotation represents the original utterance's meaning. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:7/q28 What inward meanings are given to Moses, his family and the fire?  [* · inward]
turns on: The sources offer different symbolic mappings of the same fire scene.
- 27:7/q28/p1 Moses is the heart, his family the soul and senses, and the fire the active intellect from which the heart seeks knowledge of the path to God. — Al-Qāshānī reads the command to wait as suspending sensory activity; tending animal powers caused loss of the path. The alternative luminous flame from intellect frees the soul and senses from bodily attachment and stirs longing for divine gardens and love's journey to the breast. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 27:7/q28/p2 Knowing of fire, seeing it, and being consumed by it mirror theoretical knowledge, direct vision, and transformation by Truth. — Study Quran reports this three-stage Sufi reading. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Sufi commentators)]

## 27:7/q29 What other observations are made about the scene's fire and warming?  [نَارًۭا تَصْطَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: These are separate ancillary observations, one ethical and one dialectal.
- 27:7/q29/p1 Some say warming by fire hardens the heart and report no instance of the Prophet doing so. — Bursevī transmits this as a view without furnishing a report of the Prophet warming himself. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 27:7/q29/p2 The Kūfans call the fire-stick al-majhūla. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports al-Ṭabarī's note on Kūfan terminology for the fire-stick. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أهل الكوفة)]
