# 42:13: 47 questions

## 42:13/q01 What does “He prescribed” mean?  [شَرَعَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it means clarifying a path, instituting a law, choosing, or imposing.
- 42:13/q01/p1 He clarified and laid out a manifest religious path. — Muqatil, Qushayri, Ibn al-Jawzi, Nasafi, Tabarsi and Ibn Ashur connect the verb to a clear road; Qurtubi adds access to water, splitting hide, and entering an undertaking. Ibn Ashur calls legislation a metaphor from path making. [16 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:13/q01/p2 He instituted the religion as a law. — Mawardi reports this; Baghawi combines explanation with establishing law. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 42:13/q01/p3 He chose it for you. — Al-Kalbi, reported by Mawardi. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 42:13/q01/p4 He made it obligatory. — Mawardi's fourth gloss. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 42:13/q02 What is the shift from third-person to first-person divine speech?  [شَرَعَ أَوْحَيْنَآ وَصَّيْنَا · rhetoric]
- 42:13/q02/p1 Iltifāt and majestic plural honor Muhammad and his revelation. — Razi raises the apparent shift as a difficulty; Ibn Ashur calls it iltifāt; Bursevi, Alusi and Tabatabai stress majestic speech. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 42:13/q03 How many verses begin with shīn according to al-Kisāʾī?  [شَرَعَ · sciences]
- 42:13/q03/p1 Four, including this verse. — Burhan lists Month of Ramadan, God bears witness, grateful for His favor, and He prescribed for you. [1 notes: BURHAN(الكسائي)]

## 42:13/q04 What is the force of min in “of the religion”?  [مِّنَ ٱلدِّينِ · grammar]
turns on: Redundant versus partitive.
- 42:13/q04/p1 Min is extra: the phrase means “the religion.” — Muqatil, Mawardi and Damghani gloss it away; Damghani compares 24:30 and 12:101. [3 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI]
- 42:13/q04/p2 Min is partitive and al-din generically covers earlier revealed religions. — Ibn Ashur's grammatical account. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:13/q05 What common religion did the prophets receive?  [مِّنَ ٱلدِّينِ مَا وَصَّىٰ بِهِۦ نُوحًۭا · interpretation]

Razi lists angels, books, messengers, judgment and virtue; Ibn al-Arabi lists truth, trust, kinship and bans on murder, adultery and cruelty. Ibn Ashur adds social necessities; Islahi traces monotheism even to Adam. Ibn Abbas adds that what was addressed to Muhammad is sufficient for him. - 42:13/q05/p2 The common injunction includes lawful and unlawful rulings. — Qatada repeatedly gives halal and haram; Ibn Atiyya says every mission includes rulings. Jishumi's Qatada wording reverses lawful and unlawful from the other reports. Qushayri and Kuran Yolu allow shared rules. [15 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(قتادة,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:13/q05/p3 Its core is the injunction to establish religion and avoid division. — Zajjaj, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Ashur and Kuran Yolu read the imperative as the shared commission. - 42:13/q05/p4 All revealed religion is true and must be upheld during its binding period. — Tabatabai says each age practices its own current ruling; abrogation reveals an end rather than falsehood. Elmalili also says each community upholds its own law. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(جمع)]
- 42:13/q05/p5 The ordained religion is reverence for Muhammad. — An unnamed interpretation in Sulami. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 42:13/q06 How do shared principles relate to differing laws and abrogation?  [مِّنَ ٱلدِّينِ · law]
turns on: Timeless core versus whole binding revelation in each age.
- 42:13/q06/p1 Enduring fundamentals are shared; detailed rites and branches differ. — Qushayri, Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Kathir, Alusi, Asad and Kuran Yolu cite 5:48. Alusi says prayer, alms, fasting and pilgrimage in Ibn al-Arabi's list must be generic; Razi rejects an inference that Muhammad first followed Noah's law. [20 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(جمع)-]
- 42:13/q06/p2 Every law is true for its appointed period; current law must be practiced. — Tabatabai says abrogation reveals a term's end, not falsehood; he rejects restricting the command to three fundamentals. Elmalili says each community upholds its own law. [12 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI(جمع)]

## 42:13/q07 What does “religion” itself mean here?  [ٱلدِّينِ · meaning]
- 42:13/q07/p1 What people practice and are recompensed for. — Biqai gives this lexical explanation. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 42:13/q08 Why al-ladhī for Muhammad and mā for prior prophets?  [مَا وَصَّىٰ وَٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ · rhetoric]
turns on: A known versus generally known law, or avoidance of repetition.
- 42:13/q08/p1 Al-ladhī marks a known, detailed law; mā suits earlier laws known only generally. — Ibn Ashur explains the relative forms through 4:58, 50:23 and poetry; Samarrai agrees. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري وجماعة,سيبويه); SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 42:13/q08/p2 Variation avoids repeating the same relative form three times. — Ibn Ashur mentions but prefers the semantic distinction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:13/q09 How do the clauses about the five prophets connect?  [مَا وَصَّىٰ بِهِۦ نُوحًۭا وَٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ وَمَا وَصَّيْنَا بِهِۦٓ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰٓ · grammar]
- 42:13/q09/p1 The relative clauses coordinate as objects of “He prescribed.” — Nahhas and Ibn Atiyya parse them this way; Ibn Ashur says the Quran adds elaboration; Tabatabai reads each named prophet as receiving a law. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:13/q09/p2 An omitted “He prescribed” is supplied before later clauses. — Muqatil notes fronting; Ibn al-Jawzi reports Zajjaj, and Jishumi reports this construction. [3 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]

## 42:13/q10 What does the simile with Noah's charge imply?  [مَا وَصَّىٰ بِهِۦ نُوحًۭا · grammar]
- 42:13/q10/p1 Islam resembles Noah's charge through an implicit comparison expressed as identity. — Ibn Ashur says the compressed comparison is ordinary, citing Waraqa ibn Nawfal's phrase about revelation to Jesus. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:13/q11 Was Muhammad initially following Noah's law?  [مَا وَصَّىٰ بِهِۦ نُوحًۭا · law]
- 42:13/q11/p1 The verse concerns what all listed prophets agreed on, without proving prior adherence to Noah's law. — Razi argues from the other names in the list. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 42:13/q11/p2 Some infer that he initially followed Noah's law. — Razi reports and rejects this: the verse also lists other prophets and concerns the law shared by all. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 42:13/q12 What does “enjoined” convey?  [وَصَّىٰ · meaning]
- 42:13/q12/p1 A command accompanied by admonition, reinforcement and special care. — Wahidi glosses command; Jishumi, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Tabatabai stress emphatic urging. Al-Raghib adds a comparison with continuous vegetation. [7 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 42:13/q13 Why say “revealed” for Muhammad and “enjoined” for the others?  [وَصَّىٰ أَوْحَيْنَآ · rhetoric]
turns on: Honorific emphasis versus distinction between temporary and final law.
- 42:13/q13/p1 It emphasizes Muhammad's messengership and honors his revelation. — Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi and Kuran Yolu say the shift answers deniers; majestic We and the relative pronoun imply special care. Tabatabai reads the plural as majesty. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 42:13/q13/p2 Waṣṣā is used for religious and moral matters, awṣā for material matters. — Samarrai contrasts this verse with inheritance usages. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 42:13/q13/p3 Earlier laws were temporary trusts until Muhammad's final revelation. — Ibn Ashur compares them to a trust awaiting its owner and notes the echo of 42:3. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:13/q14 What did Noah first bring?  [نُوحًۭا · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in their lists of relatives and description of prior duties.
- 42:13/q14/p1 The first revealed law, with bans on marriage to mothers, daughters and sisters. — Tustari, al-Hakam and later reports give these bans; variants omit a relative. Ibn al-Arabi contrasts Adam, first prophet but without formal duties, with Noah's duties and manners. Qurtubi cites a sound intercession report naming Noah first messenger to earth. - 42:13/q15/p1 He is the earliest messenger or lawgiver and shows the religion's antiquity. — Biqai says this rebuts its being called new; Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Tabatabai stress its ancient foundation. ## 42:13/q16 Why these five prophets?  [نُوحًۭا إِلَيْكَ إِبْرَٰهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Prophetic rank and lawgiving, or ties to the audience.
- 42:13/q16/p1 They are principal law-bearing messengers of firm resolve, renowned and widely followed. — Razi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Tabatabai and Kuran Yolu give this; 33:7 and 46:35 are compared. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı İslâm âlimleri)]

[10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 42:13/q17 Why is Muhammad placed after Noah before the intervening three?  [نُوحًۭا وَٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Continuity of first and last, or Muhammad's priority.
- 42:13/q17/p1 The order pairs first and last and frames continuous revelation. — Ibn Ashur compares 4:163 and 33:7 and says the intervening laws follow; Islahi and Kuran Yolu agree. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 42:13/q17/p2 Muhammad is advanced for honor and privilege. — Biqai, Bursevi and Tabatabai say so; Ibn Ashur reports Kashshaf's related view on 33:7. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]

## 42:13/q18 What does “what We revealed to you” designate?  [وَٱلَّذِىٓ أَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَيْكَ · referent]
turns on: Quran as a whole, revelations to date, or the opening of this sura.
- 42:13/q18/p1 The Qur’an and its Islamic rulings. — Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Ashur identify the Qur’an; Tabatabai cites other revelation verses. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 42:13/q18/p2 All revelations, or what had descended by this verse. — Alusi allows the broad reading, with 16:123 and 18:110; Kuran Yolu reports both scopes. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 42:13/q18/p3 The sura's opening and 42:7. — Alusi reports this narrower option. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 42:13/q18/p4 Muhammad learned Noah's prior charge through revelation. — Asad's inference from the wording. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 42:13/q19 How does the wording mark divine choice and Muhammad's address?  [ٱللَّهُ يَجْتَبِىٓ إِلَيْكَ · rhetoric]
- 42:13/q19/p1 The choice is God's alone; “you” directly addresses Muhammad; “whomever He wills” follows an established pattern. — Ibn Ashur takes fronting of Allah as exclusivity; Kuran Yolu says Muhammad is unnamed because addressed directly; Razi compares 29:21. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL]

## 42:13/q20 What reading is recorded for Abraham's name?  [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ · readings]
- 42:13/q20/p1 The canonical reading keeps accusative Ibrāhīma. — Qiraat-ER records this form. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(canonical)]

## 42:13/q22 Who is commanded to establish religion and avoid division?  [أَنْ أَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلدِّينَ وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ فِيهِ · referent]
turns on: All prophetic communities or Muhammad's immediate community.
- 42:13/q22/p1 All named prophets and their peoples, including Muslims. — Tabari and Zajjaj read a shared charge; Ibn Ashur says conveyance implies communities; Elmalili and Samarrai extend the command through generations. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد من الأجلة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler)]
- 42:13/q22/p2 Muhammad's community is immediately addressed. — Alusi prefers this as the community that divided; Sheikh al-Islam uses it against one parse; Bursevi says the address extends through them. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 42:13/q23 Does “establish religion” explain all prophetic charges or only the last?  [أَنْ أَقِيمُوا۟ ٱلدِّينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase reaches all coordinated clauses or the immediately preceding charge.
- 42:13/q23/p1 It explains the charge to all the named prophets. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives this alternative to the narrower parse; Zajjaj and Ibn Ashur also extend it across the list. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ZAJJAJ; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 42:13/q23/p2 It explains only the immediately preceding charge to Abraham, Moses and Jesus. — Ibn al-Jawzi reports this as a possible construction. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

- 42:13/q24/p2 Affirm God's oneness and worship Him sincerely. — Muqatil and Ibn al-Jawzi gloss tawhid; Ibn Atiyya says exclusive worship; Qurtubi and Biqai add books, messengers and final recompense. - 42:13/q24/p3 Practice sincere obedience, good character and disciplined spiritual states. — Tustari and Sahl give the spiritual charge; Elmalili says knowledge alone needs training of feeling and religious education. [5 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); ELMALILI]
- 42:13/q24/p4 Call others to religion and strive for it. — Mawardi reports calling and adds striving against opponents; Tabarsi also includes invitation. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 42:13/q25 What division is forbidden?  [وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ فِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Disagreement and sects, rejection of messengers, polytheism, or failure to act together.
- 42:13/q25/p1 Disagreement, enmity and sectarian splits in the one religion. — Abu al-Aliya says be brothers; Muqatil says prophets confirm one another; Jishumi rejects mutual takfir, imitation and sects and calls for evidence. Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Ashur warn against disabling shared religion. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية,مقاتل); JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية,مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL(علي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 42:13/q25/p2 Do not divide over fundamentals, though legal branches may differ. — Qushayri, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi cite 5:48; Ibn Ashur cites the Prophet's saying that juristic understanding is a divine good. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 42:13/q25/p3 Do not affirm some messengers and reject others. — Nahhas and Abu Hayyan say this preserves prophetic unity; Alusi applies it to fundamentals. [3 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 42:13/q25/p4 Do not split worship among many gods. — Razi prefers this, citing 12:39 and 21:25; Kuran Yolu reports it. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); RAZI-FULL+]
- 42:13/q25/p5 Do not split the labor of practicing religion. — Ibn Ashur says members must help one another establish it. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:13/q26 Why preserve unity?  [وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ فِيهِ · interpretation]
- 42:13/q26/p1 Unity strengthens and secures; division causes enmity, ruin and defeat. — Qatada and Ali contrast security or mercy with ruin or torment. Razi cites mutual aid and 8:46; Biqai, Bursevi and Elmalili use the flock and bundled sticks; Ibn Ashur warns of factional plots. ## 42:13/q27 Does the command against division forbid legal analogy?  [وَلَا تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ فِيهِ · law]
- 42:13/q27/p1 Opponents of qiyās say it does because analogical schools perpetuate disputes. — Razi reports their argument. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(نفاة القياس)]

## 42:13/q28 Why the fuller form tatafarraqū?  [تَتَفَرَّقُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 42:13/q28/p1 It makes an emphatic and enduring warning across prophetic communities. — Biqai notices the added ta; Samarrai contrasts the shorter form in 3:103 for one community with this lasting covenant. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 42:13/q29 What is the sense of “was grave upon”?  [كَبُرَ عَلَى · meaning]
- 42:13/q29/p1 The call felt heavy and hard to accept. — Ibn Ashur explains weight as a metaphor and the role of ʿalā; Tabatabai says they were reluctant. Jishumi notes the new clause and rejects a proposed raising of the past verb. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 42:13/q30 Why was the call hard for them?  [كَبُرَ عَلَى ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Ancestral loyalty, status, form of revelation, or message.
- 42:13/q30/p1 It challenged inherited gods, leadership and desires. — Jishumi cites ancestors and threatened leadership; Ibn Ashur cites 38:5 and 34:7 for one God and resurrection; Kuran Yolu adds leaders' interests. [6 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); QASHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 42:13/q30/p2 They resented an ordinary messenger and demanded a different form of revelation. — Razi notes injured pride; Ibn Ashur cites 17:94, 43:31, 17:93, 25:21 and 2:118; Kuran Yolu mentions wealth, a whole book and signs. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 42:13/q31 How does this sentence connect to the next verse?  [كَبُرَ عَلَى ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ مَا تَدْعُوهُمْ إِلَيْهِ · links]
- 42:13/q31/p1 It explains rejection between legislation and the next account of division. — Ibn Ashur calls it an interruption; the present verb marks continuing invitation, and the indefinite what portrays the call as strange to rejecters. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 42:13/q32 What grammatical patterns are cited alongside kabura?  [كَبُرَ · grammar]
- 42:13/q32/p1 Stative verbs have varied verbal noun and adjective patterns across size, force, moral and mental qualities. — Sibawayh compares kabura and ʿaẓuma with qualities such as beauty, strength, rank, knowledge and geminated roots; kasra may be lighter with doubling. [17 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 42:13/q33 Who are the polytheists?  [ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · referent]
- 42:13/q33/p1 The Meccan idolaters, especially Quraysh. — Ibn al-Jawzi and Islahi identify them. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 42:13/q33/p2 The challenge reaches Arabs, Jews, Christians and others called to Islam. — Ibn Atiyya includes these audiences and connects the following division to early Jews and Christians. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 42:13/q34 What call was hard for the polytheists?  [مَا تَدْعُوهُمْ إِلَيْهِ · referent]

- 42:13/q34/p1 Worship God alone and abandon idols. — Muqatil cites 38:5; Qatada says Satan opposed the testimony but God would make it prevail. Tabatabai says the whole religion of unity is meant. - 42:13/q34/p2 Recognize Muhammad's messengership and revelation. — Mawardi calls this more repellent; Razi notes pride; Tabarsi and Alusi report resentment at his selection. - 42:13/q34/p3 Establish the whole religion in unity and obey Muhammad's commands. — Kashshaf and Razi stress establishment; Jishumi prefers commands; Alusi reports broader scope. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; JISHUMI(قيل)+]

## 42:13/q35 What do the two pronouns “to Him/it” refer to?  [إِلَيْهِ · referent]
turns on: God versus religion or the call.
- 42:13/q35/p1 The religion of divine unity or prophetic call. — Baydawi, Bursevi and Tabatabai give this; Alusi reports it and critiques Zamakhshari's surface link with unity. [14 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,بعضهم); TABATABAI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Beyzâvî ve Hâzin); STUDYQURAN]
- 42:13/q35/p2 God in both clauses. — Alusi prefers this; Tabatabai reports it but favors the contextual reading. Study Quran notes uncertainty. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 42:13/q36 What does divine choosing answer here?  [ٱللَّهُ يَجْتَبِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يَشَآءُ · links]
turns on: Consolation, rebuttal of status claims, or sufficiency without the arrogant.
- 42:13/q36/p1 It consoles the Prophet that some will respond under God's will. — Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Islahi make this point; Razi speaks of the community's mercy and honor; Ibn Ashur links resistance to distance from guidance. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 42:13/q36/p2 It rebuts social-rank objections to God's choice of a messenger. — Tabarsi, Razi and Ibn Ashur say God alone selects; Ibn Ashur reads fronting of Allah as exclusivity. [4 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 42:13/q36/p3 God has no need of arrogant unbelievers' faith. — Tabatabai compares angelic worship at 41:38. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 42:13/q37 How is selection and return read inwardly?  [ٱللَّهُ يَجْتَبِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يَشَآءُ وَيَهْدِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يُنِيبُ · inward]
- 42:13/q37/p1 Prior attraction draws elect souls; seekers progress through discipline and illumination. — Qashani contrasts elect and seekers, and veiling under prior wretchedness; Bursevi's Najmi report and Sufi master describe stages from law to witnessing. Al-Kashifi says to leave all else for the Real. [10 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الكاشفي)]

## 42:13/q38 What does the mention of God's names imply for His unity?  [ٱللَّهُ · theology]
- 42:13/q38/p1 The many beautiful names accompany one divine essence worthy of worship. — Elmalili states this in explaining monotheism. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 42:13/q39 What does yajtibī mean?  [يَجْتَبِىٓ · meaning]
turns on: Choosing, gathering near, or accepting.
- 42:13/q39/p1 Choose, gather and draw near to God or His religion. — Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, Jishumi and others gloss selection; Razi and al-Raghib cite water collection and revenue gathering. Bursevi says gathering suits unity. ## 42:13/q40 Whom does God choose?  [يَجْتَبِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: Believers, messengers, or inward elect.
- 42:13/q40/p1 Those He wills for faith, monotheism, obedience and nearness. — Tabari says for guardianship and love; al-Kalbi distinguishes born Muslims and converts; Kashshaf and Nasafi stress enabling help. Ibn Kathir says guidance goes to those meriting it. - 42:13/q40/p2 Prophets chosen for their mission independently of human status. — Jishumi allows ilā as li; Tabarsi says the chosen can bear the charge; Razi and Ibn Ashur rebut worldly rank objections. ## 42:13/q41 How does God guide whoever turns?  [وَيَهْدِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يُنِيبُ · theology]

- 42:13/q41/p1 He directs, enables and gives gentle aid toward truth and obedience. — Tabari stresses repentance and obedience; Jishumi reports luṭf; Baydawi and Bursevi add indication and enabling. ## 42:13/q42 How does divine help relate to human turning?  [وَيَهْدِىٓ إِلَيْهِ مَن يُنِيبُ · theology]

- 42:13/q42/p1 God enables and assists those who turn toward obedience. — Zamakhshari speaks of those benefiting from grace and kindness; Abu Hayyan identifies this as Mu'tazilite doctrine. - 42:13/q42/p2 Election and guidance are distinct graces within the people of religion. — Alusi prefers both pronouns to God and distinguishes chosen and guided groups. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 42:13/q43 What does yunīb mean?  [يُنِيبُ · meaning]
- 42:13/q43/p1 Return to God in repentance, sincere devotion and obedience. — Nahhas, Jishumi and Ibn Atiyya explain return from sin or unbelief; Bursevi says sound repentance yields ināba. [7 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 42:13/q45 How were Qur’anic sūras arranged?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Explicit prophetic order or Companion ijtihad grounded in his practice.
- 42:13/q45/p1 Companions arranged them through ijtihad from what they heard of the Prophet. — Gharnati reports Malik and most scholars; Uthman's codex was adopted, and Companions knew occasions and placement. He says both accounts yield certainty and order must observe coherence. [10 notes: GHARNATI(مالك)]
- 42:13/q45/p2 The Prophet explicitly instructed their order. — Gharnati reports a second group; explicit command would settle objection. Verse order within each sura is agreed prophetically fixed. [9 notes: GHARNATI(طائفة من العلماء,مالك)]

## 42:13/q46 What does a report that the Qur’an descended with tafkhīm mean?  [* · readings]

- 42:13/q46/p4 A forceful impact on polytheists is intended. — Jamal al-Qurra calls this far-fetched since revelation also has mercy. [1 notes: ITQAN(جمال القراء)]
- 42:13/q46/p5 The Qur’an should be revered. — A reported answer. [1 notes: ITQAN]
