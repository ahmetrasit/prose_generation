# 2:24: 43 questions

## 2:24/q01 What action do the challengers fail to do?  [تَفْعَلُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 2:24/q01/p1 Produce a surah comparable to the Qur'an, even with their helpers; they failed before and will fail thereafter. — Muqatil notes the fronted wording; al-Tabari includes testing and helpers, and Ibn Jurayj says summon witnesses to judge the match. Al-Tabrisi and Ibn Ashur include allies and consultation. Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi and Qatada stress past failure and future inability; Elmalılı extends it to Resurrection. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من المفسرين); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:24/q02 What makes the unanswered challenge evidence for revelation and prophethood?  [فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا۟ وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا۟ · theology]
turns on: Whether the inability lies in the Qur'an's qualities or a divine diversion of the challengers.
- 2:24/q02/p1 The Qur'an itself surpasses opponents' ability, especially in its eloquent composition and other excellences. — Al-Rummani: strong motives would produce an answer if possible, and universal challenge makes silence decisive; sound harmony, clarity, and proof contribute. Al-Tabrisi uses rational inference. Al-Razi cites hostility, Muhammad's prudent refusal to risk disgrace, and no answer through his era. Ibn Kathir lists language, knowledge, true unseen reports and just rulings. Ibn Ashur stresses eloquence, style, wisdom, full-surah composition, continued transmission, and Arab and non-Arab access through testimony and learning; he rejects style alone, noncontradiction, or unseen reports alone as sufficient. Elmalılı treats the challenge as an experimentally checkable effect. Al-Walid and Bedouin reactions illustrate perceived eloquence. [58 notes: RUMMANI; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(الوليد بن المغيرة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عبيدة,التفتازاني,السكاكي,السيد,القاضي عياض,الوليد بن المغيرة,شرف الدين الطيبي,كثير من العلماء); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(محمد عبده); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهرة أهل العلم والتحقيق)+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 2:24/q02/p2 God diverted challengers' desire or ability to answer (ṣarfah), itself an extraordinary sign. — Al-Rummani accepts it as one aspect beyond customary patterns. Ibn Ashur says a minority holds it; reports differ whether al-Ashari, al-Nazzam, al-Murtada, al-Isfarayini, Ibn Hazm, or many Mu'tazilites are counted. Ibn Ashur rejects the theory. [5 notes: RUMMANI(بعض أهل العلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(طائفة قليلة); IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 2:24/q03 What does the future prediction add to the proof?  [وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا۟ · theology]
- 2:24/q03/p1 It is a fulfilled prophecy of failure and thus knowledge of the unseen, strengthening the proof of prophethood. — Al-Rummani compares Badr, Roman recovery, ascendancy, and other predictions. Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan say it provokes efforts and makes failure more striking. Al-Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi and Alusi say an answer would have been transmitted. Razi and Baydawi say a false claimant would not risk so testable a prediction. Ibn Ashur says it holds across centuries; Elmalılı counts 1,344 years. Abu Hayyan distinguishes Musaylima and al-Mutanabbi's claims from a true counterchallenge. [28 notes: RUMMANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كيسان); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:24/q04 Why use conditional إن despite certainty of failure?  [فَإِن · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the condition adopts the opponents' outlook or expresses ironic certainty.
- 2:24/q04/p1 It speaks from the opponents' pre-reflection confidence or uncertainty, inviting a fair test while exposing their presumption. — Al-Zamakhshari, Razi, Nasafi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai, Burhan, Alusi, and Ibn Ashur say eloquent opponents expected a chance to succeed. Ibn Ashur says the apparent openness encourages attempt, exhausts excuses and alternates conciliation and threat. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:24/q04/p2 It is a taunt or ironic condition from one certain of victory. — Al-Zamakhshari and Razi compare a strong man saying 'if you defeat me'; Abu Hayyan, Baydawi and Alusi include mockery. Study Quran says a divine conditional need not imply uncertainty. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 2:24/q05 How do إن and لم govern the first verb?  [فَإِن لَّمْ تَفْعَلُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether two particles with distinct meanings can compete for one verb.
- 2:24/q05/p1 إن conditions the whole negative clause; لم makes the imperfect jussive and past in sense. — Zajjaj, Wahidi, Tabrisi, Baydawi and Ibn Ashur distinguish their functions. Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi say إن can precede past-like لم because it is non-governing in written form. Akhfash and Ibn Atiyya analogize لم to negative لا dropping an ending; others analogize it to conditional إن. Wahidi says conditional clauses' length favors deletion. Alusi leaves the exact syntactic locus unresolved; Samin makes the whole clause occupy the conditional's jussive place. Ibn Ashur rejects competing government here because meanings differ. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الأخفش سعيد,غيره); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:24/q05/p2 إن and لم compete to govern the same verb. — Ibn al-Alj allows particle competition; Ibn Hisham objects that particles do not denote events, and Alusi and Ibn Ashur object that these particles differ in polarity. Ibn Ashur notes Abu Ali al-Farisi allows competition when semantic demands agree and cites a rajaz example. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إبن العلج); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,الراجز); ALUSI-FULL(ابن هشام)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العلج)-]

## 2:24/q06 Why say 'do it' instead of repeating the challenged act?  [تَفْعَلُوا۟ تَفْعَلُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the challenge's object is grammatically omitted or the general verb is used absolutely.
- 2:24/q06/p1 The broad verb concisely stands for bringing a matching surah and its required conditions. — Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Burhan, Biqai, Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Samin invoke brevity. Biqai says denial of the broader act covers the specific one. Ibn Ashur includes witnesses. Abu Hayyan and the Sheikh object that 'bring' with omitted object could be just as concise. Samarai says omission leaves formal scope unrestricted. [19 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); BURHAN; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 2:24/q06/p2 The verb denotes simply performing the action; no grammatical object need be restored. — Al-Zarkashi says transitive verbs can be used without an intended object when occurrence alone matters, comparing eat and drink; he also discusses how conditions and plurals compress alternatives and how fronting works. [6 notes: BURHAN(ابن الأثير)]

## 2:24/q07 What does لن express, and how long does its negation last?  [وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether emphasis and duration are inherent in the particle or supplied by context.
- 2:24/q07/p1 It negates the future emphatically; any permanence here rests on the prediction or context, not the particle alone. — Zamakhshari, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Suyuti and Abu Hayyan describe emphasis. Abu Hayyan says its overt future marking strengthens it; Burhan cites 19:26, 2:95, 20:91, 22:73 against inherent perpetuity. Alusi rejects fixed long or short duration; Samin denies inherent permanence. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(سيبويه، إحدى الروايتين عن الخليل); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه وإحدى الروايتين عن الخليل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:24/q07/p2 It expresses an unbounded or permanent future negation, literally or by emphasis. — Zamakhshari cites 22:73 and this challenge. Ibn Ashur argues that unbounded future denial normally spans all future times and disputes grammarians who deny such force. [3 notes: ITQAN(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; BURHAN-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:24/q07/p3 It denies an expected or near event without extending the negation indefinitely. — Ibn al-Zamalakani says لن denies something expected or near, whereas لا denies the doubtful; Abu Hayyan calls this nearly the reverse of Zamakhshari. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن خطيب زملكاني); ITQAN(ابن الزملكاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:24/q08 What is the grammatical origin of لن?  [لَن · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle is primitive or derives from contracted or altered لا.
- 2:24/q08/p1 It is a simple, independent particle of future negation. — Wahidi cites object fronting before لن against a contracted relative particle; Sibawayh's objection is reported by Nahhas and Tabrisi. Abu Hayyan and Samin reject compound derivations. Ibn Ashur, however, calls Khalil's contraction good. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه); NAHHAS(سيبويه); TABRISI(سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL(سيبويه والخليل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الخليل)]
- 2:24/q08/p2 It contracts لا أن. — Attributed to al-Khalil; he answers the object-fronting objection by compound particles' changed behavior, comparing هلا. Tabrisi gives phonetic contraction; Alusi reports dropped hamza and alif, with a weak poetic proof. Ibn Ashur approves it. [11 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الخليل); KASHSHAF-FULL(الخليل); RAZI-FULL(الخليل); TABRISI(الخليل); BAYDAWI-FULL(سيبويه والخليل); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الخليل); IBNASHUR-FULL(الخليل)+; NAHHAS(سيبويه)-; WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه)-; TABRISI(سيبويه)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الخليل)-]
- 2:24/q08/p3 It derives from لا with its alif changed to nūn. — Attributed to al-Farra; Alusi rejects the unmotivated alteration. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الخليل)-; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)-]

## 2:24/q09 What case does لن govern here?  [لَن تَفْعَلُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether cited vowelless verbs show dialectal jussive government or poetic license.
- 2:24/q09/p1 Normally it makes the imperfect subjunctive, here shown by dropping the plural nūn. — Zajjaj relates the subjunctive to أن and a nounlike clause; Nahhas compares secondary verb cases to noun cases. Wahidi, Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi concur. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:24/q09/p2 Some Arab dialects use لن with a jussive imperfect. — Abu Ubayda, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Ibn al-Tarawa cite poetry, especially al-Nabigha, and an Ibn Umar dream report. Samin says poetic sukūn explains the evidence instead. [5 notes: NAHHAS(أبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الطراوة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة)-]

## 2:24/q10 How is ولن تفعلوا related syntactically to the condition?  [وَلَن تَفْعَلُوا۟ · grammar]
- 2:24/q10/p1 It is a parenthesis without syntactic position between the conditional clause and its answer. — Wahidi cites 18:30; Jishumi cites 3:30. Abu Hayyan says لن marks the insertion as distinct from a circumstantial clause. Nasafi says it ends the uncertainty suggested by إن. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:24/q11 What is the conditional answer and why does it take ف?  [فَٱتَّقُوا۟ · grammar]
- 2:24/q11/p1 فاتقوا النار is the apodosis, with ف linking an independent imperative to its condition. — Nahhas identifies the answer. Wahidi contrasts the first coordinating ف with the consequence-marking second ف and says a self-contained imperative needs it. Samin, Abu Hayyan and Alusi agree; Alusi notes a suggested implicit answer to the objection that avoiding Hell is always required. [7 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:24/q12 What does اتقوا mean here, and how is its form explained?  [ٱتَّقُوا۟ · meaning]
- 2:24/q12/p1 Guard yourselves against reaching the Fire, especially by believing, obeying, and abandoning denial after proof. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi and Ibn Ashur connect the command to proof and abandonment of obstinacy; Qurtubi adds obedience. Al-Harali calls it ending the separation between God and Messenger. Nahhas and Qurtubi report Tamim and Asad's فتقوا; Sibawayh cites تقى يتقي, and Samin adduces poetry for تقوه and تق الله. [34 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NAHHAS(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:24/q13 How are النار and التي parsed, and what forms does التي have?  [ٱلنَّارَ ٱلَّتِى · grammar]
- 2:24/q13/p1 النار is the imperative's direct object; التي is its feminine definite relative adjective. — Nahhas, Qurtubi and Samin agree. Qurtubi says its article cannot be dropped, lists dual and plural forms, diminutive اللتيا, poetic vocative يا التي, and idiom اللتيا والتي. Nahhas reports Basran and Kufan accounts of its feminine tāʾ; Samin cites poetic اللت and للت. [11 notes: NAHHAS(الكوفيون); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:24/q14 Why is the Fire definite and described by a relative clause?  [ٱلنَّارَ ٱلَّتِى وَقُودُهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether definiteness points chiefly to prior mention or the Fire's distinctive description.
- 2:24/q14/p1 It recalls a fire already known, especially the earlier description in al-Tahrim 66:6. — Zamakhshari, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Burhan contrast a Meccan indefinite mention with this Medinan definite one; some also allow knowledge from the People of the Book or Prophet. Alusi notes that al-Tahrim's priority is disputed. Biqai says the definite report presents divine knowledge as universally established. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:24/q14/p2 It identifies a distinctive Fire fueled directly by people and stones. — Zamakhshari and Razi distinguish it from ordinary fires that require initial kindling; Nasafi calls the fuel distinctive, while Abu Hayyan denies that the relative description proves multiple fires. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:24/q15 What does وقود mean here?  [وَقُودُهَا · meaning]
turns on: The initial vowel and whether a noun for fuel can also serve as a verbal noun.
- 2:24/q15/p1 Fuel or firewood, the material kindling and feeding the Fire. — Most distinguish fatḥa as fuel and ḍamma as burning; Akhfash, Zajjaj, Ibn Jinni, Sibawayh, Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Ashur allow overlap or exceptional fatḥa verbal noun. Ibn Ashur says the instrument pattern supports fuel; Samin cites rare fuʿūl verbal nouns, with al-Kisai adding وزوع. Al-Harali says fuel mediates fire's action. Elmalılı includes coal, grass, tinder and rags. [38 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الكسائي والأخفش سعيد); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي والأخفش,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL(سيبويه); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:24/q15/p2 It can denote the action of burning or kindling, especially with initial ḍamma. — Akhfash, Ibn Qutayba, Tabari, Zajjaj and later grammarians distinguish the verbal noun; Jishumi compares fiʿl vocalizations. Ibn Jinni adduces وقدت النار وقودا, ولوعا and القبول; his side discussions of وضوء, ساكوت, الضارورة and possessive-yāʾ patterns illustrate irregular formation. Some allow either vowel for both senses. [21 notes: MAJAZ; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو بكر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت); JISHUMI(ابن الخليل); KASHSHAF-FULL(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:24/q16 Which readings of وقودها are reported?  [وَقُودُهَا · readings]
turns on: Transmission and whether the vowel denotes fuel or burning.
- 2:24/q16/p1 The prevalent reading has fatḥa on the wāw, matching the fuel sense. — Jishumi calls it general; Ibn Jawzi, Nahhas and Samin prefer it because people and stones are fuel. Ibn Ashur also requires fuel in context. Ibn Atiyya says Talha excepts al-Buruj; Durr says Mujahid excepts 85:5. Some construe it as verbal noun with ellipsis or intensive identification; Jishumi rejects its recitation, while Alusi says it can be fuel noun. - 2:24/q16/p3 Ubayd ibn Umayr reads وقيدها, also meaning fuel. — Qurtubi records the qaf form; Abu Hayyan identifies a faʿīl pattern; Jishumi rejects it for lack of broad transmission. ## 2:24/q17 How is وقودها الناس والحجارة parsed?  [وَقُودُهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ · grammar]
- 2:24/q17/p1 It is a nominal relative clause: وقودها is subject, الناس predicate, and الحجارة coordinated with الناس. — Nahhas, Qurtubi and Samin specify the resumptive pronoun. Baydawi says if وقود is a verbal noun an omitted possessive or burning is needed; Ibn Jinni, Abu Hayyan and Alusi give ellipsis or emphatic metaphor for the ḍamma reading. [7 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وطلحة وأبو حياة وعيسى بن عمر الهمداني); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:24/q18 Who are the people named as fuel?  [ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
- 2:24/q18/p1 Those destined for Hell, particularly disbelievers or sinful people; the general noun is contextually restricted. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan say grammatical generality does not mean every person. Ibn Ashur treats it as the unbelieving class, possibly specified by prior scriptural covenant; Bursevi says the sinful. Tabatabai says deeds kindle each person's punishment; Asad takes human beings as deviant human actions. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 2:24/q19 Why mention people before stones?  [ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ · rhetoric]
- 2:24/q19/p1 People feel pain and are more frightening to see burn; their bodies may also ignite more readily. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite sentience, flesh, fat and skin; Ibn Ashur says the pairing indirectly threatens worshippers alongside idols. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:24/q20 How is الحجارة formed and used in Arabic?  [ٱلْحِجَارَةُ · meaning]
- 2:24/q20/p1 It is a plural of حجر, often judged irregular beside أحجار. — Wahidi, Jishumi and Ibn Ashur call the plural nonanalogical; Baydawi compares جمالة. Abu Hayyan and Samin say its feminine ending marks collective plurality. Ibn Malik calls it a collective noun, with أحجار the paucity plural; Abu al-Haytham says final tāʾ eases pausing after a long vowel. Al-Harali defines stone by compact earth and water. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو الهيثم,سيبويه); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:24/q21 Which stones fuel the Fire?  [ٱلْحِجَارَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the noun denotes a special material, idols, stones as a kind, treasure, or otherwise unknown fuel; the arguments appeal to reports, 21:98, and the threat's intensity.
- 2:24/q21/p1 Sulfur stones, often described as black, intensely hot, foul, smoky, adhesive, large and hard. — Ibn Masud reports their divine creation as God willed; some place it in the lowest heaven at creation, others in Hell or beneath the second earth. ## 2:24/q32 What inward meaning is given to Fire and its fuel?  [ٱلنَّارَ ٱلَّتِى وَقُودُهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ · inward]
