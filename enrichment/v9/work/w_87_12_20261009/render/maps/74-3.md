# 74:3: 16 questions

## 74:3/q01 What does “your Lord” convey here?  [رَبَّكَ · referent]
turns on: Which implication of the possessive expression the commentator draws.
- 74:3/q01/p1 God is the Prophet’s master, owner, and governor of his affairs. — Al-Qurṭubī explains the possessive Lordship as mastery, ownership, and governance, in the setting of denying God a consort or child. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 74:3/q01/p2 God is the one who uniquely nurtures the Prophet. — Al-Biqāʿī uses this special care to ground the charge to rise diligently and proclaim Him alone greatest in speech and belief. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]
- 74:3/q01/p3 The wording subtly suggests that God's oneness was then particular to the Prophet. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies this as an implication of calling Him 'your Lord'. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 74:3/q02 What does placing “your Lord” before the verb do?  [رَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fronting entails exclusive restriction or supplies emphasis.
- 74:3/q02/p1 It singles out God alone for magnification. — Al-Zamakhsharī is reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn; Abū Ḥayyān notes that this follows al-Zamakhsharī's account of fronting. Al-Samīn says some grammarians infer exclusivity. Al-Naḥḥās identifies ربك as the accusative object of كبّر. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī, al-Nasafī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Iṣlāḥī apply the restriction to belief, speech, and the rejection of idols or other claims to greatness. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it restriction of individuals, especially against idols. [13 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 74:3/q02/p2 It emphasizes the object. — Al-Samīn reports emphasis as another grammatical account of the fronted objects. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 74:3/q03 How does the wāw connect this command to its context?  [وَرَبَّكَ · grammar]
- 74:3/q03/p1 It coordinates magnifying the Lord with the preceding order to rise and warn. — Ibn ʿĀshūr points to 74:2 as the coordinated command. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 74:3/q04 What is the force of the fāʾ in “fa-kabbir”?  [فَكَبِّرْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle answers an understood condition, marks the result of rising, or is syntactically additional.
- 74:3/q04/p1 It answers an implicit general condition: whatever occurs, keep magnifying God. — Al-Zajjāj initially describes a conditional response. Al-Kashshāf, as reported by al-Rāzī, and al-Zamakhsharī as reported by al-Samīn and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī make the implied condition unrestricted. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Abū Ḥayyān give this reading. Al-Ālūsī says a delayed apodosis particle allows the object before it; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fronted object prepares the construction and compares ففيهما فجاهد concerning parents. Al-Samīn's Shaykh compares زيدا فاضرب, understood as تنبه فاضرب زيدا, and points to the debate at 2:40. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 74:3/q04/p2 It marks consequence or sequence: rise, then magnify God as the first aim of rising. — Al-Zajjāj, reported by al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī, applies the same consequential force to the surrounding commands. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Būrsawī relate the sequence to knowing the Creator before declaring His transcendence; al-Bayḍāwī says the audience already acknowledged the Creator. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI+]
- 74:3/q04/p3 The fāʾ is extra; the command simply means “magnify your Lord.” — Abū al-Fatḥ al-Mawṣilī, reported by al-Rāzī, and Ibn Jinnī, reported by al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr, compare زيدا فاضرب, meaning 'strike Zayd'. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو الفتح الموصلي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جني); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني)]
- 74:3/q04/p4 It is inserted because the speaker imagines a condition. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative to account for the object of the following verb preceding the fāʾ. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 74:3/q05 What does the command to magnify the Lord require?  [فَكَبِّرْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether magnification is explained as broad monotheistic exaltation, an uttered formula, prayer's opening, unlimited transcendence, or its effect on the caller's regard for others.
- 74:3/q05/p1 Exalt God in conviction, speech, worship, and action, declaring His sole greatness and freedom from partners and unworthy attributes. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī say to describe Him as exalted and greatest; Abū Mālik in al-Durr and Ibn Kathīr gloss it as exalting Him. Al-Naḥḥās specifies worship of Him alone; al-Ḥasan in al-Sulamī says He needs no preaching and guides whom He wills. Al-Kalbī in al-Rāzī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Būrsawī and al-Biqāʿī deny the idolaters' descriptions, association, division, invoking others, and preoccupation with others. Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes worship and spreading revealed law; Ibn al-ʿArabī in al-Qurṭubī includes sanctification, abandoning idols, and attributing every act and blessing to Him. Al-Qurṭubī denies consort or child. Al-Rāzī denies partners, opposites, equals and likeness to contingent things, citing 16:2; another gloss excludes idle talk and frivolity. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the takbīr form as metaphorically assigning the root quality of greatness by superior qualities, and includes perfection, no offspring, inward conviction, speech, and instruction. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Elmalılı include belief, deed, speech, and action; Iṣlāḥī and Kur'an Yolu call this foundational monotheism. [30 notes: SULAMI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; MUQATIL+; NAHHAS+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 74:3/q05/p2 Utter “Allāhu akbar” to proclaim that God is greater than every great thing. — Muqātil as reported by al-Rāzī gives the formula; al-Nasafī says to say it whenever something else confronts one. Al-Qurṭubī says religious convention made it the magnification formula for the call to prayer, prayer, remembrance, and sacrifice, with ritual offerings free from association. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its comparison is unrestricted and explains its use at prayer's opening. The Prophet's reply at Uḥud to Abū Sufyān's 'Exalt Hubal' was 'God is higher and more majestic,' in al-Qurṭubī. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 74:3/q05/p3 Say the takbīr that opens prayer. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī report this narrower explanation. Al-Rāzī allows voluntary prayer even before obligatory prayer existed. Ibn al-ʿArabī applies magnification to the Prophetic prayer beginning with takbīr and ending with taslīm. Al-Qurṭubī refers to al-Baqara for the transmitted prayer formula. Al-Būrsawī argues that prayer's bodily forms suggest limitation, so its takbīr declares God free from all determination. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect this verse to the clothing purification command; the latter calls it the opening takbīr. [15 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو هريرة,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 74:3/q05/p4 Recognize a greatness beyond every created measure and even beyond descriptions of God. — Al-Jurayrī in al-Sulamī says His greatness cannot be encompassed. Al-Qushayrī exalts Him beyond demands, connections, separation, causes, and created things. Al-Būrsawī says His majesty is intrinsic and independent of magnifiers, and cites the Prophet's Miʿrāj confession that he cannot encompass God's praise. The Imams of the Prophet's family, in al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, say He is too great to be described even by descriptions used of Him. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes tasbīḥ's denial of deficient attributes such as death, incapacity, and ignorance from takbīr's transcendence of positive and negative descriptions, since all concepts are bounded; he says this monotheism surpasses other revealed traditions. [8 notes: SULAMI(الجريري); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); TABATABAI(أئمة أهل البيت); BURSEVI+; QUSHAYRI+; TABATABAI+]
- 74:3/q05/p5 Let God alone loom large, so other powers and concerns become small. — Muqātil specifies not magnifying Mecca's unbelievers in one's mind. Al-Nasafī says no one else should loom large; al-Qāshānī says witnessing divine grandeur makes all else insignificant in heart and eye. Elmalılı says all else is small and low. Al-Ālūsī applies this to warning fearlessly because all creatures are under God's control. [6 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; MUQATIL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QASHANI+]

## 74:3/q06 Why is magnifying God placed among the early commands to warn?  [وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ · links]
turns on: Whether the placement chiefly establishes the warning's first doctrine, strengthens its messenger, or joins warning to worship and purification.
- 74:3/q06/p1 The warning must begin with God's transcendence and monotheism. — Al-Rāzī says knowing and declaring transcendence precedes other calls, citing 16:2. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Būrsawī connect the sequence to first knowing the Creator, then denying unworthy descriptions. Al-Ālūsī says the ordering emphasizes takbīr and alludes indirectly, 'I mean you, neighbor,' to first knowing God and then denying association. Iṣlāḥī says proclaiming sole greatness in pagan Arabia provoked society but every prophet had to do so because tawḥīd is the foundation. Al-Jishumī says the command to warn starts with the Prophet, then reaches people, after al-Muzzammil's prayer commands. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 74:3/q06/p2 Magnifying the sender sustains a caller facing the burdens of warning. — Al-Biqāʿī says warning meets resistance and the caller may neglect the command or demand pay; help comes from magnifying the sender and refusing wages. Al-Ālūsī says God's control over creatures lets the Prophet warn without fear. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 74:3/q06/p3 Its place beside purification signals worship and preparation for the mission. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says purification follows magnification because it is prescribed for prayer. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the verses as establishing prayer alongside preaching; Iṣlāḥī summarizes verses 1–10 as preparation for warning the Hereafter, exalting God, purification of heart and from polytheism, and perseverance, with God's call to spread. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TABATABAI+]

## 74:3/q07 What event brought down the opening commands that include this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported trigger is followed; al-Durr explicitly marks the Quraysh report weak.
- 74:3/q07/p1 After the retreat at Ḥirāʾ and a pause in revelation, the Prophet saw the angel between heaven and earth, feared, asked to be covered, and received the opening verses. — Jābir's accounts appear in ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī; variants say he heard a call in the valley, the angel was seated on a throne, he requested cold water, and the cited span ends at 74:3, 74:4, 74:5, 74:6, or 74:7. Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn Kathīr cite Muslim; al-Qurṭubī also cites Tirmidhī, who calls it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ, and Bukhārī for the version including 74:6. Al-Thaʿlabī likewise reports the encounter and says the revelation through 'leave the idols' preceded the obligation of prayer. Al-Zuhrī in ʿAbd al-Razzāq describes the earlier opening of al-ʿAlaq, distress after the pause, and Gabriel's reassurance at mountain peaks. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الزهري,جابر); THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-ASBAB(جابر); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جابر بن عبد الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); DURR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); TABATABAI(جابر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله)+; THALABI+]
- 74:3/q07/p2 After Quraysh chose to call Muhammad's message transmitted magic, he grieved and covered his head, and verses 1–7 descended. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr. Al-Durr explicitly grades this report weak; Quraysh had debated labels including sorcerer, soothsayer, and poet. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 74:3/q08 Was al-Muddaththir's opening the first revelation or the first after a pause?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether Jābir's 'first' means absolutely first or first after the interruption, as Ibn Kathīr argues.
- 74:3/q08/p1 The opening of al-ʿAlaq came first; these verses were the first revelation after the pause. — Al-Zuhrī in ʿAbd al-Razzāq says al-ʿAlaq opened revelation. Ibn Kathīr calls Muslim's pause account the preserved version and uses it to reconcile Jābir's accounts. Al-Durr says the next revelation, al-Muddaththir's opening, began a vigorous continuous flow. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الزهري); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); DURR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله)+]
- 74:3/q08/p2 Al-Muddaththir was the first revelation, before Iqraʾ. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Jābir's explicit identification in a second account and cites Bukhārī's version. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله)]

## 74:3/q09 What happened when this specific command was revealed?  [فَكَبِّرْ · reports]
- 74:3/q09/p1 The Prophet said “Allāhu akbar”; Khadīja also magnified God or rejoiced and recognized revelation. — Muqātil, al-Qushayrī, al-Nasafī, al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and reports in al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī give variants: some mention Khadīja saying takbīr, some only her joy, and al-Bayḍāwī says the Prophet himself knew. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Būrsawī say Satan would not command takbīr; no grading is supplied in al-Bayḍāwī. ## 74:3/q10 Was this verse revealed in response to a question about beginning prayer?  [فَكَبِّرْ · reports]
turns on: The early date of the sūra against Abū Hurayra's later conversion and the timing of prayer.
- 74:3/q10/p1 Yes; believers asked how to begin prayer, and the verse directed them to begin with takbīr. — Abū Hurayra is named by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Durr, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī; al-Qurṭubī reports the Prophet's version. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies Ibn Mardawayh as a transmitter. Al-Ālūsī says the account requires interpretation if authentic because prayer had not yet existed at revelation. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI-]
- 74:3/q10/p2 The report's chronology is doubtful. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that Abū Hurayra entered Islam long after the Hijra while this sūra belongs to the mission's beginning; he asks where Abū Hurayra or the Companions were then. Al-Ālūsī's qualification is that prayer had not yet existed at revelation if the report is authentic. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 74:3/q11 What was the status of prayer when this verse was revealed?  [فَكَبِّرْ · sciences]
turns on: Whether 'prayer' in the chronological reports means any early prayer, obligatory prayer, or the later five daily prayers.
- 74:3/q11/p1 The obligation or formal prescription of prayer came after these verses. — Al-Thaʿlabī says the opening through 'leave the idols' preceded prayer's obligation. Al-Rāzī says obligatory prayer was not yet instituted; al-Būrsawī says prayer was not yet established. Maʿmar via al-Zuhrī in Muslim, as cited by Ibn ʿĀshūr, says the event preceded prayer's prescription. Al-Ālūsī notes the same chronological problem for Abū Hurayra's prayer report. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNASHUR-FULL(معمر عن الزهري); THALABI+]
- 74:3/q11/p2 Prayer in some form existed early, even before the five daily prayers took their present form. — Al-Rāzī allows the Prophet's voluntary prayers before obligatory prayer. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers an early prayer from the Prophet praying in the Sacred Mosque and construes 'before prayer was prescribed' as distinct from the five daily prayers. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says prayer in principle existed at the mission's start, citing 96:10 and 73:20; he says the five prayers took their present form at the Night Journey, initially ten units and later increased by seven. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(معمر عن الزهري); IBNASHUR-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 74:3/q11/p3 These commands signal or establish prayer, with takbīr followed by purification. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the juxtaposition with 74:4 as a possible signal of prayer's institution and places its prescription after this sūra. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads 74:3 as opening takbīr and 74:4 literally as cleansing clothing for prayer, so both establish prayer alongside preaching; he mentions 2:187. Ibn ʿĀshūr says purification follows magnification because it is required for prayer. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TABATABAI+]

## 74:3/q12 How is takbīr performed in reciting the Qurʾān's final sūras?  [فَكَبِّرْ · readings]
turns on: The transmitter, the point at which takbīr starts and ends, whether it is connected, and whether tahlīl is added.
- 74:3/q12/p1 In al-Bazzī's transmission it begins at al-Ḍuḥā and continues through the Qurʾān's end. — Al-Rawḍa gives the formula Allāhu akbar and says it is disconnected at the end of al-Nās. Al-Saʿīdī instead starts from the end of al-Ḍuḥā, gives al-Bazzī's formula with added tahlīl, and describes pauses at sūra ends followed by connection to the basmala. Ibn Kathīr, Ibn Muʾmin, and al-Tajrīd are cited for continuing until completion; the author says a separated takbīr after the final sūra is impossible. Al-Shāṭibī's statement is read by the author as limiting an end-of-Nās takbīr to those starting at al-Ḍuḥā; he also allows that it concerns joining takbīr with al-Fātiḥa and al-Baqara's opening. [4 notes: NASHR(صاحب الروضة); NASHR+]
- 74:3/q12/p2 In the al-Zaynabī transmission from Qunbul it begins at al-Sharḥ. — Al-Rawḍa gives Allāhu akbar as the wording and describes a disconnected takbīr at the end of al-Nās. Al-Saʿīdī gives Qunbul's formula as Allāhu akbar while distinguishing al-Bazzī's tahlīl addition. [2 notes: NASHR(صاحب الروضة)]
- 74:3/q12/p3 For Ibn Hāshim it extends from the start of al-Ḍuḥā only to the start of Qul aʿūdhu. — Ibn al-Jazarī corrects al-Hudhalī's imprecise description, which had said to the end of Qul aʿūdhu; this is a correction to the reported end point. [1 notes: NASHR+]

## 74:3/q13 What authority and prayer practice are reported for the recitational takbīr?  [فَكَبِّرْ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted authority of the practice and whether the formula needs differentiation during prayer.
- 74:3/q13/p1 Leaving the recitational takbīr abandons a Prophetic sunnah. — Al-Dānī transmits al-Shāfiʿī's statement from al-Bazzī, without a grading. Ibn al-Jazarī conjectures that al-Sakhāwī's unnamed scholar was Ibn Mujāhid, from a chain through al-Bazzī, Muḍar, Ḥāmid, and al-Ḥasan ibn Muḥammad. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني)]
- 74:3/q13/p2 Takbīr was practiced in prayer at Mecca, with tahlīl added to distinguish it from the bowing takbīr. — Qunbul reports Ibn al-Muqri hearing Ibn al-Shahīd al-Ḥajabī, or another of the Ḥajaba, make takbīr behind the Maqām during Ramadan. Al-Naysābūrī recommends tahlīl plus takbīr in prayer in Ibn Kathīr's reading to avoid confusion with bowing, citing Meccan jurists and reciters including al-Shāfiʿī, Sufyān, Ibn Jurayj, and Ibn Kathīr. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو الحسن علي بن أحمد النيسابوري,قنبل)]

## 74:3/q14 What verbal pattern is illustrated by this verse?  [وَرَبَّكَ فَكَبِّرْ · rhetoric]
- 74:3/q14/p1 It reads the same forward and backward, a pattern called mustawī. — Al-Burhān describes the mustawī pattern for a word or sequence and illustrates it with Qurʾānic phrases including 21:33. Al-Itqān says the Qurʾān's only examples are كل في فلك and وربك فكبر. [2 notes: BURHAN+; ITQAN+]

## 74:3/q15 How does this sūra's charge relate to al-Muzzammil?  [* · links]
- 74:3/q15/p1 The two sūras are counterparts in address, mission, and perseverance. — Al-Jishumī says al-Muzzammil commands prayer and the next sūra commands warning, beginning with the Prophet then people. Ibn al-Zubayr in al-Biqāʿī notes that both openings honor and address the Prophet, with night standing in 73:2 and warning in 74:2; he compares patience under people's speech in 73:10 with patience for the Lord in 74:7. Iṣlāḥī says the names share a meaning and the central themes are essentially alike. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI+]

## 74:3/q16 What is reported of God's presence during prayer?  [فَكَبِّرْ · reports]
- 74:3/q16/p1 God's face corresponds to the praying servant's face. — Al-Būrsawī cites this as a sound report while explaining that prayer's physical forms can suggest limitation and thus call for takbīr. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]
