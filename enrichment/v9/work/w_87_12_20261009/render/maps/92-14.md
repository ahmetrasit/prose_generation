# 92:14: 22 questions

## 92:14/q01 What does 'I warned you' mean?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ · meaning]
- 92:14/q01/p1 I warned and frightened you with punishment. — Al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi explicitly gloss warning as frightening; al-Qurtubi specifies punishment. Al-Wahidi says the audience was made afraid of the blazing fire. [5 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; WAHIDI-QT]

## 92:14/q02 Who is addressed by 'you'?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether a specific Meccan or occasion based audience limits the wording's general address; al-Qaffal and al-Razi say it does not.
- 92:14/q02/p1 The people of Mecca, specifically Quraysh. — Muqatil names Mecca; al-Baghawi does likewise. Islahi identifies Quraysh, stressing that revelation guides without forcing acceptance and that persistent deniers face the Fire. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:14/q02/p2 People generally are warned. — Al-Tabari urges people to avoid disobedience and disbelief in this life so they do not enter Hell in the next. Al-Qaffal says the sūra's particular occasion does not limit its general message; al-Razi cites the general human striving of 92:4 and this warning. Kur'an Yolu says God warns people through revelation and prophets. [7 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 92:14/q03 Who speaks the warning?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the first-person warning is direct speech or speech to be conveyed through an implied command.
- 92:14/q03/p1 God directly addresses the audience. — Ibn Atiyya offers direct divine address as one possibility. Tabatabai says the shift to first person identifies God as the ultimate warner, while His messenger delivers the message. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 92:14/q03/p2 God implicitly commands Muhammad to tell the audience this warning. — Ibn Atiyya presents an implied command to Muhammad as the alternative to direct address. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 92:14/q04 What time does 'I warned' express here?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the past morphology is literal completed speech or a way to make a present warning immediate.
- 92:14/q04/p1 It refers to an already completed warning. — Ibn Ashur permits literal past speech. Al-Samarra'i says the past frames one completed warning of one thing, the blazing Fire, unlike continuing warnings with present verbs in 41:13 and 78:40. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 92:14/q04/p2 Its past form conveys a present warning. — Ibn Ashur says past time is brought close to present time, comparing the conventional call that prayer has begun; the warning follows the divine undertaking to guide at 92:12. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:14/q05 How does the opening 'fa' connect this warning to what precedes?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ · links]
turns on: The preceding passage used as the warning's immediate basis, and whether the past warning is literal or present in force.
- 92:14/q05/p1 The warning follows from God's undertaking to guide. — Al-Alusi says God guides by warning and intensifies guidance (92:12). Tabatabai also connects the warning to divine guidance. Qutb adds that God has clarified guidance and owns both abodes; Kur'an Yolu says He shows the path and warns through revelation and prophets. Ibn Ashur's present-warning reading also branches from 92:12. [8 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:14/q05/p2 It details a preceding general warning. — Ibn Ashur ties the literal past warning to 92:8–11: fa introduces a detailed warning after that general one, like an explanatory fa; he also allows simple continuation of discourse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:14/q05/p3 It turns God's established dominion, knowledge, and power into a softened personal warning. — Al-Biqa'i says the first-person singular brings understanding near and distances apprehension after the earlier account of divine dominion, knowledge, and power. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 92:14/q06 Why is the warning phrased in the first person singular?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shift chiefly affects the hearer's reception or makes the ultimate speaker explicit.
- 92:14/q06/p1 It softens and brings the warning close to the hearer. — Al-Biqa'i says the singular voice brings understanding near and distances apprehension. [1 notes: BIQAI]
- 92:14/q06/p2 It identifies God as the ultimate warner although the messenger conveys His message. — Tabatabai links the shift to the divine source of the warning and its delivery through the messenger. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 92:14/q07 Why does this warning name the Fire alone?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ نَارًۭا · rhetoric]
- 92:14/q07/p1 Warning concerns danger, so Paradise as good tidings lies outside this passage's purpose. — Al-Samarra'i says al-Layl has warning without glad tidings and therefore names the Fire alone. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:14/q08 What is the fire being warned of?  [نَارًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the description covers all Hell or singles out a tier; al-Razi invokes 70:15–16 against the tier reading. Al-Qashani reads the fire inwardly across all existence.
- 92:14/q08/p1 Hell as a whole, the blazing Fire of the afterlife. — Al-Tabari explicitly names Hell. Al-Razi rejects restriction to one tier because the phrase can describe all Hell and 70:15–16 calls it laza, stripping skin; he also allows that the description grammatically could apply to a fire, but says the other verse speaks of Hell. Al-Qadi's alternative takes it as all Hell and reads the next verse's restriction as the most wretched being most entitled to it. Tabatabai also identifies Hell via 70:15. Corpus Coranicum compares 70:15 and refers to its note on nar at 111:3; Study Quran compares Hell's crushing Fire at 104:4–5. [13 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; RAZI(القاضي); TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; BAGHAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 92:14/q08/p2 One particular blazing tier of Hell. — Al-Qadi offers this to allow sinners other than the most wretched to enter other tiers, citing 4:145. Al-Razi calls it weak: the description can cover all Hell and 70:15–16 calls Hell laza. [2 notes: RAZI(القاضي); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 92:14/q08/p3 The great fire pervading all levels of existence. — Al-Qashani's inward reading locates it in veiling, divine wrath, and punishment by effects. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 92:14/q09 Why is 'fire' indefinite?  [نَارًۭا · rhetoric]
- 92:14/q09/p1 The indefinite form magnifies the fire's terror. — Ibn Ashur says the indefinite nar intensifies the threat. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:14/q10 What does 'talazza' say the Fire does?  [تَلَظَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes the fire's physical blazing and glow or personifies it as angry.
- 92:14/q10/p1 It blazes, flares, glows, or burns intensely. — Mujahid variously says flaring, burning brightly, kindled, and glowing; Muqatil says catching fire. Qatada says a blaze or glow. Ali's poem, transmitted by al-Mawardi, compares a glowing fire with salt in a mixture and agate in shade. Al-Wahidi and al-Razi link the verb to laziyat al-nar and Hell's name Laza; al-Jishumi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai, and Bint al-Shati also note that root or name, with 70:15 cited by the last two. Al-Biqa'i says it blazes effortlessly, even without effort from keepers or kindler; Elmalili says it grows fiercer. The remaining sources give close variants of blazing, glowing, flaring, and burning. [39 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); NAHHAS(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI(مجاهد,مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(علي,مجاهد,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 92:14/q10/p2 It seethes or fumes with anger. — Al-Kalbi gives this gloss as reported by al-Mawardi and the fuller version. [2 notes: MAWARDI(الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي)]

## 92:14/q11 What is the grammatical form of 'talazza'?  [تَلَظَّىٰ · grammar]
- 92:14/q11/p1 A feminine imperfect verb contracted from tatalaẓẓā by loss of one initial tāʾ. — Al-Farra and al-Tabari say a past form would be talazzat; al-Tabari calls the present verb nominative. Al-Nahhas, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, al-Alusi, Elmalili, and Ibn Ashur give the underlying two-tāʾ form; al-Tabari also calls it a future form. Al-Wahidi gives the related past verb talaẓẓat and Ibn Ashur derives it from laẓiyat al-nār. Al-Itqan states a general omission rule: where either element can be dropped, the second normally is; its examples involve an understood second predicate or genitive. [15 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN]

## 92:14/q12 How is 'talazza' read?  [تَلَظَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether the original two tāʾs are reduced by deletion, assimilated as a geminate, or retained separately; phonetic difficulty and transmitted routes are discussed.
- 92:14/q12/p1 With one light tāʾ. — Most of the Seven or the majority read the one-tāʾ form; Ibn Atiyya says it is ungeminated. Al-Farisi says Qunbul through al-Nabbal and other readers lighten it; the form is explained by deletion of one initial tāʾ. [8 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(البزي عن ابن كثير); THALABI(عبيد بن عمير); JISHUMI(عبيد بن عمير); IBNATIYYA(جمهور السبعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور السبعة); ABUHAYYAN(ابن الزبير وزيد بن علي وطلحة وسفيان بن عيينة وعبيد بن عمير والبزي والجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI]
- 92:14/q12/p2 With an assimilated, doubled tāʾ. — Al-Bazzi transmits it from Ibn Kathir; Ibn Atiyya also attributes this form to Ubayd ibn Umayr and describes assimilation into the doubled tāʾ. Al-Jishumi offers assimilation of the prefix tāʾ or of tanwīn as explanations. Al-Farisi calls it phonetically harder than talqaf because a consonant precedes the tāʾ and the assimilated tāʾ is silent rather than a glide; he compares la tanajaw (58:9), takhataf, and talqaf (7:117). Al-Samin compares the awkward meeting of silent letters to 24:15. Al-Nashr includes this among 31 future verbs with possible doubled prefixes, citing routes and examples including 20:69 and 49:13. Qiraat-ER says the geminate tt matches semantic intensity. [11 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(البزي عن ابن كثير); JISHUMI(ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA(البزي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(البزي عن ابن كثير وعبيد بن عمير); ABUHAYYAN(ابن الزبير وزيد بن علي وطلحة وسفيان بن عيينة وعبيد بن عمير والبزي والجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(البزي); QIRAAT-ER]
- 92:14/q12/p3 With two separately pronounced tāʾs, tatalaẓẓā. — Al-Farra saw it in Abd Allah's codex. Al-Nahhas reports Ibn Uyayna with unassimilated tāʾs, though some transmitters assimilate them. Reports name Ubayd ibn Umayr, Abu al-Zubayr, Ibn al-Zubayr, Zayd ibn Ali, Talha, Sufyan ibn Uyayna, and Yahya ibn Yamar in varying combinations; al-Suyuti calls Ubayd's chain sound. This preserves the original form. [17 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; THALABI(عبيد بن عمير); JISHUMI(عبيد بن عمير); KASHSHAF(أبو الزبير); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو الزبير); IBNATIYYA(عبيد بن عمير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(البزي عن ابن كثير وعبيد بن عمير); QURTUBI(عبيد بن عمير ويحيى بن يعمر وطلحة بن مصرف); QURTUBI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير، يحيى بن يعمر، طلحة بن مصرف); ABUHAYYAN(ابن الزبير وزيد بن علي وطلحة وسفيان بن عيينة وعبيد بن عمير والبزي والجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن الزبير، سفيان، زيد بن علي، طلحة); DURR(عبيد بن عمير); DURR-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); ALUSI(ابن الزبير وزيد بن علي وطلحة وسفيان بن عيينة وعبيد بن عمير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الزبير)]

## 92:14/q13 How is the tanwīn pronounced before the doubled tāʾ?  [نَارًۭا تَلَظَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: Grammar permits kasra to avoid adjacent quiescent letters, but Ibn al-Jazari insists that this recitation lacks transmitted precedent.
- 92:14/q13/p1 Give the tanwīn kasra to avoid two silent letters. — Al-Nahhas says the tanwīn should be vowelled before the next consonant. Abu al-Baqa describes kasra with doubled tāʾ; al-Samin calls it rare but grammatically regular, comparing 2:267. Al-Ja'bari permits kasra among ten such cases. Ibn al-Jazari says no earlier reading authority transmits kasra in nāran talazzā: recitation depends on transmission, not grammatical allowance. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); NASHR(الجعبري); NASHR-]
- 92:14/q13/p2 Retain the tanwīn's sukūn. — Al-Ja'bari reports that most readers retain sukūn in the relevant ten cases. Ibn al-Jazari objects to the proposed kasra here for lack of earlier transmission from reading authorities. [2 notes: NASHR(الجعبري); NASHR+]

## 92:14/q14 What does the shortened or assimilated sound of 'talazza' suggest?  [تَلَظَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 92:14/q14/p1 It suggests the Fire's extreme intensity, even at its least, without effort from its keepers. — Al-Biqa'i infers from omission of the tāʾ and assimilation that even the least afterlife fire is severe; its kindler or keepers need make no effort, so the warning is a fortiori. Qiraat-ER says assimilating the Form V prefix produces a geminate matching the meaning's intensity. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 92:14/q15 What conduct does this warning urge?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:14/q15/p1 Avoid disobedience and disbelief, heed guidance, and seek protection from Hell. — Al-Tabari urges avoidance in this life lest one burn in Hell in the next. Al-Baghawi says the wretched denies the Messenger and turns from faith. Islahi says Quraysh benefit by accepting guidance and persistent deniers face fire, while generous spenders are protected. Kur'an Yolu says God warns against following the wrong path while showing the right one. [9 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; DURR(قتادة)]

## 92:14/q16 How does the Fire warning fit the sūra's contrasts?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace either the stanza's structural reversal or the moral identity of the warned entrant.
- 92:14/q16/p1 It makes the earlier threat concrete and begins a reversed, mirrored final contrast. — Corpus Coranicum says 92:14 resolves the enigmatic threat of 92:10 as blazing Hell; the final stanza reverses the earlier saved/damned order, with ittaqā in 92:5 linking to atqā in 92:17. The earlier behavior lists precede consequences, while the last warning and promise precede conduct. It compares this disclosure pattern with 104:4–7 and 102:3–8, and describes two antitheses spanning 92:5–13 and 92:14–21. Kur'an Yolu says 92:17–20 follows the warning with giving for God, purification, and protection from fire. [6 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:14/q16/p2 The warning identifies the wretched miser as destined for Hell. — Al-Tha'labi identifies the palm owner who rejected reward with the self-sufficient miser, denier of recompense, and most wretched entrant. Al-Baghawi says the wretched denies the Messenger and turns from faith. [2 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 92:14/q17 What occasion or historical figures are associated with this warning?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported figures instantiate the sūra's contrasting giver and miser; both accounts allow a wider application.
- 92:14/q17/p1 Abu Bakr's spending and Umayya ibn Khalaf's miserliness and disbelief. — Al-Qaffal reports this account of the sūra's revelation but says its message is general. Kur'an Yolu reports other exegetes' attribution of 92:5–19 to Abu Bakr and likewise says a specific occasion does not limit its message or rulings; al-Razi cites 92:4 and 92:14 for general scope. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 92:14/q17/p2 Abu al-Dahdah and the palm owner who refused the Prophet's offer. — Ata reports that a palm dropped fruit into a neighbor's yard; its owner refused a promised palm in Paradise, Abu al-Dahdah bought and donated the palm, and the Prophet transferred it to the neighbor. Al-Tha'labi makes the owner the miser and most wretched entrant. Al-Qurtubi reports the same alternate identification, mapping ease to Paradise, hardship to Hell, and death to loss of wealth. [3 notes: THALABI(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 92:14/q18 What reports illustrate the impact or delivery of the Fire warning?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe two different events: a public prophetic warning and a later reciter's reaction.
- 92:14/q18/p1 The Prophet repeatedly warned of the Fire loudly enough to be heard in the market. — Al-Nu'man ibn Bashir reports that the Prophet's cloak slipped from his shoulder to his feet as he warned. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR(النعمان بن بشير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النعمان بن بشير)]
- 92:14/q18/p2 Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz wept while reciting the warning and could not continue. — Malik reports that Umar moved to another sūra in prayer. [2 notes: THALABI(مالك عن عمر بن عبد العزيز); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)]

## 92:14/q19 How is the least punishment in Hell described in reports cited here?  [* · reports]
turns on: The two transmitted versions differ in the footwear image and added perception of the sufferer.
- 92:14/q19/p1 Two live coals under the feet make the person's brain boil. — Al-Nu'man ibn Bashir reports the Prophet's description; Ibn Kathir cites al-Bukhari. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR(النعمان بن بشير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النعمان بن بشير)]
- 92:14/q19/p2 Fiery sandals and straps make the brain boil like a pot. — The Muslim version adds that this least-punished person imagines no one suffers more. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR(النعمان بن بشير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النعمان بن بشير)]

## 92:14/q20 What virtue is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 92:14/q20/p1 Its reciter receives until contentment, is spared hardship, and finds ease made easy. — Al-Kashshaf transmits this promise from the Messenger. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:14/q21 What is noted about the Qur'anic history of the warning root?  [فَأَنذَرْتُكُمْ · meaning]
- 92:14/q21/p1 This is probably the earliest Qur'anic occurrence of andhara and the n-dh-r root. — Corpus Coranicum points to further early Meccan examples at 74:36. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:14/q22 Why is this warning brief rather than extended and emphatic?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:14/q22/p1 Al-Layl gives one warning in one verse, while al-Naba repeats and extends its warning. — Al-Samarra'i contrasts this verse with 78:4, 78:21–26, and 78:40, where the repeated extended warning warrants emphasis. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
