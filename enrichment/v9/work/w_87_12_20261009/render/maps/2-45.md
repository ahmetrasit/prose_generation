# 2:45: 24 questions

## 2:45/q01 Who is addressed by the command?  [وَٱسْتَعِينُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the surrounding Israelite discourse continues, shifts, or states a general command.
- 2:45/q01/p1 The Israelites or People of the Book are addressed; the instruction can also apply to others. — Al-Wahidi and Razi prefer continuity with the surrounding Israelite address; Ibn Ashur cites the conjunction and 2:43. Tabarsi and Ibn Kathir extend its instruction generally. Razi says Jews knew the shared essence of patience and prayer despite differing ritual forms. Ibn Ashur says the passage also indirectly praises Muslims. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر أهل العلم); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(الرماني وغيره); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-ASBAB(أكثر أهل العلم)+]
- 2:45/q01/p2 The address turns to Muslims or believers in Muhammad. — Al-Jubba'i and Abu Ali propose a change of audience; Razi's reported argument is that rejecters of the religion's prayer would not be addressed with it. Al-Wahidi, Razi, and Ibn Ashur favor the contextual Israelite reading over this shift. [9 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(بعضهم); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(قوم,معترض); WAHIDI-ASBAB(أكثر أهل العلم)-; RAZI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 2:45/q01/p3 All morally responsible people are addressed. — Tabarsi prefers this because nothing restricts the wording, while conceding that the preceding and following context concerns the People of the Book. [1 notes: TABRISI+]

## 2:45/q02 For what are patience and prayer sought as help?  [وَٱسْتَعِينُوا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: The command leaves its object unstated; sources supply the preceding duties, ultimate reward, present needs, or a particular obstacle.
- 2:45/q02/p1 For obeying God's commands and abandoning sin, including the preceding covenant duties and accepting Muhammad. — Tabari ties this to the Book's covenant, losing leadership, and worldly attachment; Zajjaj, Wahidi, Razi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur, and Kur'an Yolu explain resistance to status, bribes, habits, and inherited error. Jishumi lists prayer, alms, prohibitions, covenant and the messengers' promise as possible omitted objects. Ghazali's account in Ibn Ashur sets religious motive against appetite. [26 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الغزالي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 2:45/q02/p2 For attaining the Hereafter, God's pleasure, mercy, or reward. — Muqatil details the five prayer times; the Durr version adds recitation, postures, takbir, tashahhud and blessings. Abu al-Aliya calls both acts obedience, Ibn Jurayj two aids to mercy. The Muslim-address interpretation seeks Paradise. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن جريج); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,مقاتل بن حيان); SULAMI(سهل بن عبد الله); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن جرير,مقاتل بن حيان); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,قتادة,مقاتل بن حبان)]
- 2:45/q02/p3 For distress, calamity, needs, and the good of this life and the next. — Baghawi, Kashshaf, Baydawi and others apply the command to affliction; Tabatabai says human strength fails before some tasks and God supplies the true aid. Elmalili emphasizes trust that changing hardship will pass. Some explain patience as awaiting relief. [19 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 2:45/q02/p4 For establishing religion, certainty, devotion, and spiritual realities. — Sahl names steadfast certainty; Ibn Ata the depths of reality; Abu Uthman time free for devotion. Qushayri and Qashani construe aid as maintaining divine presence and service amid overwhelming unveilings. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,ابن عطاء,بعضهم,سهل بن عبد الله); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 2:45/q02/p5 Specifically to avoid taking bribes for altering religion. — Jishumi reports a reading that addresses Jews taking money from followers and directs them to seek relief instead. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:45/q03 How does the wording of 'seek help through patience' work?  [وَٱسْتَعِينُوا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic scope of the imperative and the unspoken object.
- 2:45/q03/p1 It is an imperative; the prepositional phrase expresses an instrument or cause, and the object of help is omitted for generality. — Al-Samin derives استعينوا from استعونوا with a seeking sīn and notes direct or bā-mediated government, with bā also able to mean accompaniment; Nahhas parses الصبر as genitive. Akhfash discusses coordinated predicates, distinguishing wāw from aw with 9:62, 62:11, 28:73, 4:112 and several poems. [11 notes: NAHHAS; AKHFASH(ابن أحمر,البرجمي); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 2:45/q03/p2 The command joins the earlier imperatives despite intervening clauses. — Al-Samin treats it as coordinated with the preceding commands. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:45/q04 What does الصبر mean here?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣabr names its broad discipline, a particular practice or duty, or an inward state.
- 2:45/q04/p1 Broad patient restraint: enduring hardship, doing duties, and resisting forbidden desires. — Tabari rejects confinement to fasting; Nahhas and Qurtubi prefer obedience and abstention, citing 39:10 and unrestricted صابر. Wahidi includes harm, calamities and fighting enemies. Ibn Ashur and Ghazali explain discipline of appetites for truth; Elmalili says fasting trains rather than exhausts this sense; Tabatabai calls fasting an application. Umar rates abstention from prohibitions above patience under calamity; Sa'id ibn Jubayr adds entrusting affliction and hoping for reward even if inwardly anguished. [42 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو بكر حبش بن الورد البغدادي,بعضهم,سعيد بن جبير,عمر بن الخطاب); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,سعيد بن جبير,عمر بن الخطاب); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الغزالي,النبي); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Gazzâlî,Râgıb el-İsfahânî); STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:45/q04/p2 Fasting specifically. — Mujahid and others identify it with fasting; Ramadan is the month of patience because it restrains food, drink, and intercourse. Reports call fasting half of patience and a shield from fire; Razi says it weakens worldliness and illuminates the heart with prayer. Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi pair fasting's curb on greed with prayer's humility. Tabari explicitly rejects exclusivity; Elmalili and Tabatabai treat fasting as a way of practicing broad patience. [36 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(مجاهد); TUSTARI; IBNABIHATIM(النبي ﷺ,بعضهم,مجاهد,محمد بن طلحة الأسدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(أئمتنا عليهم السلام); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبي الحسن,الصادق); TAB-FULL-]
- 2:45/q04/p3 Persevering in the prescribed obligations, especially prayer. — Muqatil relates it to fulfilling duties while preserving all five prayer times. Baghawi offers patience concerning prayer, supported by 20:132. Alusi considers this prayer-specific construal especially remote. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مقاتل بن حبان); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:45/q04/p4 Refraining from sins or desires specifically. — Qatada and Baghawi stress leaving disobedience; Nahhas notes the unrestricted descriptor includes this with obedience. Some apply it to surrendering leadership or status. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:45/q04/p5 Enduring calamity, pain, or harm with trust in God. — Kashshaf pairs endurance with turning to prayer; Sa'id says the sufferer acknowledges God's decree and hopes for reward. Qashani aims at contentment. Elmalili insists on active trust rather than passive surrender; the prophetic saying locates full patience at the first shock. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:45/q04/p6 Prayer itself. — Some interlocutors propose this, but Abu Bakr Habash rejects it because the verse names patience and prayer as separate items. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(أبو بكر حبش بن الورد البغدادي)-]
- 2:45/q04/p7 Waiting for relief while relying on God. — Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi offer this application; Yahya ibn al-Yaman describes contentment with one's allotment and not wishing for a different state. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن اليمان); BURSEVI(يحيى بن اليمان); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:45/q04/p8 Inwardly, withdrawing from everything other than God and bearing divine presence. — Sulami's Iraqi source defines patience away from all but God; Qushayri contrasts leaving familiar comforts with abiding in the unseen presence, and the Najmiyya reading resists egoic desire. Harali extends patience even to lawful desires. [4 notes: SULAMI(بعض العراقيين); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 2:45/q04/p9 Supplication, alongside restraint from base desire and hardship. — Harali allows this sense and says its completion through fasting, prayer and turning to God enlightens the heart and attains righteousness, comparing 20:132. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:45/q05 What is the basic lexical sense of ṣabr?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the lexical focus is restraint or sustained firmness.
- 2:45/q05/p1 Holding back or restraining oneself or another. — Ibn Qutayba, Tabari, Mawardi, Wahidi citing Abu Ubayd, Tabarsi and Qurtubi use the fasting person, bereaved person, held killer, bound target animal maṣbūra, and imposed oath; Antara's verse illustrates steadfastness. Abu Hayyan notes the verb normally takes an object, often omitted. Jishumi adds the intensive ṣabūr. [21 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد); JISHUMI; TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(عنترة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:45/q05/p2 Steadfastness in a matter. — Abu Bakr Habash chooses this definition after rejecting the assertion that ṣabr is prayer and finding a generic 'enduring a thing' unsatisfactory. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو بكر حبش بن الورد البغدادي,بعضهم)]

## 2:45/q06 What special value or scope is assigned to patience?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ · interpretation]
- 2:45/q06/p1 Patience underlies faith and moral success and receives exceptional reward. — Qurtubi and Bursevi contrast tenfold deeds, multiplied charity, and reward without measure for patience (6:160, 2:261, 39:10, 42:43); some tie this to fasting and the divine report that God rewards it. Ali likens patience to faith's head; Umar and Maymun extol it. Ibn Ashur says faith, virtues, courage and learning require it, citing 103:1–3, 32:24, 7:137, 2:153, a saying attributed to Ali and Zufar's verse. Ghazali counts over seventy Qur'anic mentions and describes human patience as unique between instinct-driven animals and perfected angels. Hafiz's ruby couplet stresses its cost. [17 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعضهم,علي); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب,غير مسمى,ميمون بن مهران); BURSEVI(يحيى بن اليمان); IBNASHUR-FULL(الغزالي,زفر بن الحارث الكلابي,علي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Gazzâlî)]
- 2:45/q06/p2 Patience is a means of receiving God's gift of patience and great good. — The Durr reports God grants patience to one seeking it, with no better gift, while Umar calls it the best life and Maymun says great good requires it. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب,غير مسمى,ميمون بن مهران)]

## 2:45/q07 What does الصلاة mean here?  [وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether الصلاة names the ritual, the Arabic sense of supplication, or inward communion.
- 2:45/q07/p1 Ritual prayer, including prescribed and, on one account, voluntary forms. — Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur make it the ritual act; Mujahid in Abu Hayyan includes obligatory, sunnah and optional prayers. Baydawi catalogs purification, covering, spending, qibla, bodily worship, intention, recitation, testimony, and abstention. Muqatil and Durr stress guarding times and postures. Alusi considers the alternative supplication reading strained. [7 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(مقاتل بن حبان)]
- 2:45/q07/p2 Supplication or earnest asking God for relief. — Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Nasafi align it with steadfastness and remembrance in 8:45; Abu Hayyan says the construction may imply an omitted term. Baydawi allows it; Alusi calls it strained. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:45/q07/p3 In inward interpretation, connection with gnosis or presence before God. — Tustari calls prayer connection to gnosis and says true connection removes complaint and the need to ask; Qushayri makes it abiding in the unseen presence, Qashani reception of divine attributes, and Najmiyya standing at the unseen threshold. [5 notes: TUSTARI; QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 2:45/q08 How do patience and prayer provide help?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: What causal or spiritual work the two practices perform.
- 2:45/q08/p1 Prayer's recitation, remembrance, and discipline turn one toward God and the Hereafter and restrain wrong, while patience curbs desire. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Jishumi, Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and others cite 29:45 and describe recitation of promise and warning, reflection, admonition, humility, and resistance to leadership and greed. Wahidi says fasting curbs greed while prayer curbs arrogance. Baydawi, Alusi, Elmalili and Kur'an Yolu add purification, lifelong relation to God, trust, repair of hardship and moral resolve. Study Quran contrasts deferred gratification with prayer's retreat from worldly occupation. [36 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أئمتنا عليهم السلام); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:45/q08/p2 Their combination joins the strongest inward act with the strongest bodily act. — Jishumi and Tabarsi present patience as the great act of the heart and prayer as the great act of the limbs; Ibn Ashur makes them patience and gratitude. Sulami and Qushayri describe patience's inward withdrawal paired with prayer's presence. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الغزالي); SULAMI(بعض العراقيين); QUSHAYRI]
- 2:45/q08/p3 True help is from God; the practices dispose the person to receive it. — Tabatabai says tasks exceed individual strength, patience shrinks calamity, and prayer awakens faith in God's support. Qashani denies independent human power; Sulami reports that believers achieve both only with God's help. [4 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QASHANI; TABATABAI]

## 2:45/q09 Why is prayer singled out after patience?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The function of the sequence and prayer's separate mention.
- 2:45/q09/p1 Prayer is specified for its special importance although worship broadly falls under patience. — Wahidi's language scholars compare the singled-out fruit, palm, pomegranate and Gabriel and Michael (55:68; 2:98); Nahhas notes the coordination. Qurtubi calls it an honor; Abu Hayyan Islam's pillar and mark distinguishing Muslims; Biqai says prayer turns to God while patience diminishes self. [5 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:45/q09/p2 Patience comes first because it removes impediments before prayer supplies benefit. — Razi says fasting removes the undesirable before prayer brings the desirable; Abu Hayyan reports this but prefers the context of habits, bribes, concealment and status. Alusi adds prayer requires patience and preventing harm precedes gaining benefit. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 2:45/q09/p3 Prayer follows because it brings divine provision and eases the demand to forgo income from concealment. — Biqai citing Harali links the sequence to 20:132, which commands persistent family prayer and says God provides. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 2:45/q09/p4 The sequence reflects its audience and differs from Luqman's instruction. — Biqai says 2:45 speaks to worldly engrossed unbelievers, while Luqman's addressee is a believing ascetic. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:45/q10 How is the coordination of patience and prayer construed?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the two terms remain coordinate or patience is directed particularly at prayer.
- 2:45/q10/p1 The wāw coordinates two distinct means. — Abu Bakr Habash uses their separate naming to reject equating ṣabr with ṣalāt; Nahhas compares the pairing of Gabriel and Michael in 2:98. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو بكر حبش بن الورد البغدادي); NAHHAS]
- 2:45/q10/p2 It can mean patience concerning or upon prayer together with prayer. — Baghawi compares 20:132; Abu Hayyan reports a reading of the conjunction as 'upon' and an omitted term; Alusi calls patience specifically in prayer remote. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:45/q11 To what does the feminine pronoun in وَإِنَّهَا refer?  [وَإِنَّهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether grammar favors a nearby expressed noun, an implied action, both coordinated nouns, the wider discourse, or a contextually inferred matter.
- 2:45/q11/p1 Prayer alone. — Akhfash, Abu Hayyan, al-Tabari as relayed by Ibn Kathir, and Alusi favor the nearer feminine noun. Others stress prayer's prominence, universal obligation, superiority, or inclusion of many kinds of patience; 9:34 and 62:11 offer analogies, though al-Samin questions 62:11 because it uses aw. Qurtubi adds that prayer engages all limbs and desires, making it heavier than fasting. Samarrai ties the return to prayer to the prior command in 2:43. Tabari rejects an unsupported hidden referent and maintains the apparent sense. [33 notes: AKHFASH; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المؤرج,قيل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(المؤرج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(أكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، الحسن، مجاهد، الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مؤرج); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BURHAN(قيل); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN(commentators); TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 2:45/q11/p2 The act of seeking help through the two practices. — Al-Husayn ibn al-Fadl and others derive the verbal noun from استعينوا; Jishumi calls it the most apparent general antecedent, Tabarsi adduces a poetic adjective with implied abstract noun, and Alusi rebuts the claim that seeking help cannot be burdensome. Bursevi chooses it. [20 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسين بن الفضل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(محمد بن القاسم النحوي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(البجلي); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(قيل); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:45/q11/p3 Each of patience and prayer distributively, or both together under a singular form. — Al-Akhfash's distributive reading invokes Mary and her son as one sign (23:50); Baghawi adds the two gardens (18:33), and poets use singular predicates. Others explain dominance, ellipsis, or inclusion of the first item in the second by 9:62. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin object that wāw normally requires dual agreement, unlike aw; al-Samin rejects this joint reading. [25 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(الشاعر,حسان); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,الشاعر); JISHUMI(الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 2:45/q11/p4 The whole preceding set of commands and prohibitions or admonition. — Kashshaf traces it back to 'Remember My favor'; Razi and Baydawi allow it, and Ibn Ashur prefers this widest, most inclusive referent while allowing all three main possibilities. Study Quran notes day and night attention. [9 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(commentators); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:45/q11/p5 Worship or self-discipline implied by patience and prayer. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi propose worship; Jishumi adds self-discipline and 'what came before.' Al-Samin lists worship as an understood antecedent. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); JISHUMI(القاضي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:45/q11/p6 Responding to Muhammad. — Mawardi reports it, al-Asamm and al-Akhfash are named by later sources; Ibn Atiyya says the text gives no evidence, Jishumi says it is neither named nor uniquely implied, and Alusi considers preceding commands closer. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); JISHUMI(الأصم)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)-; TABRISI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:45/q11/p7 The Kaaba or changed qibla implied by prayer. — Ibn Abbas and Muqatil are reported for this; Ibn Atiyya calls it weaker than responding to Muhammad, and Alusi calls it still less likely than the broad prior-command referent. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس، مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مقاتل بن حبان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل)-; TABRISI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:45/q11/p8 An implicit deed or offense. — Abu Muslim's implicit 'this deed' or 'this offense' appears in Jishumi and Tabarsi; Tabarsi rejects omitted antecedents as contextually unmentioned. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); TABRISI-]

## 2:45/q12 What grammatical form is 'وَإِنَّهَا لَكَبِيرَةٌ'?  [وَإِنَّهَا لَكَبِيرَةٌ · grammar]
- 2:45/q12/p1 Inna takes the pronoun as its noun and kabīra as predicate; the emphatic lām is attached to the predicate. — Nahhas and Qurtubi note possible masculine forms outside Qur'anic recitation; Tabarsi says the lām moves to the predicate to distinguish it from inna, unlike other sisters of inna. Razi shows an understood noun can receive an implicit pronoun, citing 16:61. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL]
- 2:45/q12/p2 A singular predicate may serve one or both coordinated nouns by attraction, ellipsis, or distribution. — Akhfash distinguishes wāw, normally joint, from aw, selecting one and usually the nearer; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin contest use of aw parallels for wāw. Poems of Ibn Ahmar, Burjumi, Hassan and anonymous poets, plus 9:34, 9:62, 23:50 and 62:11, furnish examples. [14 notes: AKHFASH(ابن أحمر,البرجمي); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(الشاعر,حسان); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:45/q12/p3 The exception has an implicit negative: the act is not light except to the humble. — Al-Samin attaches على الخاشعين to كبيرة; Abu Hayyan calls the exception complete. Zarkashi says illa can follow positive wording with negative meaning or a statement made true by the exception. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN]

## 2:45/q13 What does لَكَبِيرَةٌ mean?  [لَكَبِيرَةٌ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective describes general difficulty, a particular historical demand, or the greatness of prayer.
- 2:45/q13/p1 Heavy, arduous, or psychologically difficult. — Tabari, Dahhak, Hasan, Wahidi, Jishumi, Zamakhshari and others explain physical size or weight as a metaphor for difficulty, comparing 42:13; Ibn Ashur also cites 2:143 and 6:35. Abu Hayyan distinguishes the sense from size and age; Harali notes magnitude or rank, outward or inward, while Biqai reads prayer's greatness as divine orientation. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن، الضحاك); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن، الضحاك); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:45/q13/p2 For the Jews and hypocrites, the concrete burden was the qibla change from Jerusalem to the Kaaba. — Muqatil names Jaddi ibn Akhtab and Sa'id ibn Amr the poet among the Jews; other versions mention Jews and hypocrites. Believing humble people did not resist it. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); DURR-FULL(مقاتل بن حبان)]
- 2:45/q13/p3 For polytheists, Muhammad's summons to prayer and faith felt like a great demand. — Ibn Zayd reports their words; Zajjaj and Wahidi say prayer joined to faith in the Prophet weighed on unbelievers. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد)]

## 2:45/q14 Why is it light for the humble, and heavy for others?  [لَكَبِيرَةٌ ٱلْخَٰشِعِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ease comes from expected reward, trained disposition, divine help, or communion, and whether ease threatens the basis for merit.
- 2:45/q14/p1 Belief in reward, judgment, and benefit makes the effort bearable. — Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi, Tabarsi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Ibn Ashur and others compare bitter medicine accepted for its cure; hypocrites and showoffs expect no benefit. Razi rejects the inference that deeper bodily and inward engagement makes it harder for the humble. Ibn Kathir says they know they will meet and return to God. The Prophet called prayer the coolness or delight of his eyes even while praying until his feet swelled. [22 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم,معترض); TABRISI(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:45/q14/p2 Humility and habituation make service congenial and release attachment to status. — Tabarsi says the humble have habituated themselves to prayer and can lose rank to gain faith; Ibn Ashur says a disciplined soul rests in God's command. Elmalili says practice becomes pleasure. Qurtubi's spiritual teachers invoke eternal election, while Ibn Kathir quotes divine easing. [7 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(كثير)]
- 2:45/q14/p3 Intimate communion or divine self-disclosure eclipses the experienced burden. — Qushayri's transmitted saying links manifestation and humility; he distinguishes toil in ongoing obedience from union and nearness through witnessing and says God eases overpowering unveilings so service resumes. Sulami's authorities speak of bowed heart, secret and limbs; Qashani of tender hearts receiving subtle lights; Bursevi says absorption in converse with God makes hardship unfelt. [10 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,بعض العراقيين); QUSHAYRI(النبي); QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 2:45/q14/p4 The objection is that the humble should bear more, since they engage body and inner faculties more fully. — Razi records the argument from engaged body, heart, hearing and sight and responses to warnings and promises; he answers that difficulty concerns those without confidence in reward or punishment. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(معترض); RAZI-FULL-]
- 2:45/q14/p5 A further objection asks how an unburdened act can merit reward. — Jishumi raises this difficulty without an explicit answer in the note. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:45/q16 What is the meaning and range of khushūʿ?  [ٱلْخَٰشِعِينَ · meaning]
turns on: How its inward, bodily, vocal and visual senses relate, especially in comparison with khuḍūʿ.
- 2:45/q16/p1 Humility, lowering, quietness, stillness, or submission. — Tabari cites humbled mountains in a poem; Wahidi traces still voices (20:108), collapsed wall, barren land (41:39), drooping hump and lowered eyes, with al-Nabigha's poetry. Zajjaj likens it to a deserted house; Qurtubi adds low land, obscure town and quiet voices; Ibn Ashur's al-Nabigha image is a worn trough rim. Jishumi reports Ibn Durayd's synonyms and dialectal sense of bowing. Biqai's Harali includes still limbs and thoughts. [29 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن دريد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,النابغة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(جعفر بن عبلة الحارثي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:45/q16/p2 Khushūʿ reaches voice and sight as well as the body; khuḍūʿ chiefly denotes bodily submission. — Mawardi, Tabarsi, al-Layth and al-Samin draw this distinction; Tabarsi adds softened or lowered gaze, voice and bowed head. Abu Hayyan cites the Kaaba as a low mound on water. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الليث); SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:45/q16/p3 Khushūʿ belongs to the heart, whereas khuḍūʿ applies to the limbs. — Tabatabai states the reverse distinction from sources that make khushūʿ broader; Qurtubi and Qatada likewise place the praiseworthy state in the heart with natural outward signs. [3 notes: TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 2:45/q16/p4 Khushūʿ is bodily humility, while khuḍūʿ is softness and compliance attributable to the heart. — Baydawi reverses the customary body/heart allocation; Kashshaf defines khuḍūʿ through softened speech. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 2:45/q17 What conduct counts as genuine humility?  [ٱلْخَٰشِعِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether an outward pose expresses an inward state or is staged for people.
- 2:45/q17/p1 Inward fear and justice issue naturally in composure, kindness, equal treatment, and attentive prayer. — Qurtubi cites Nakha'i on treating high and low equally in right, Ali on a humble heart, gentleness and not turning during prayer, and Sahl on every hair responding, with 39:23. Bursevi repeats these; the early generations tried to conceal outward signs. Ibn Ashur says true humility is a disciplined readiness to accept God's command and rejects restricting it to reverence while already standing in prayer (23:1–2). [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,سهل بن عبد الله,علي بن أبي طالب); BURSEVI(سهل بن عبد الله,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:45/q17/p2 Affected outward signs to gain esteem are false humility. — Al-A'mash rebukes Sufyan for reducing it to coarse clothing, food, or bowed head; Umar tells a youth to raise his head and strikes a theatrical mourner in another report. Qurtubi calls such display hypocrisy; Bursevi cites Umar's loud speech, brisk walk and forceful strike as compatible with true asceticism. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش,الحسن,عمر بن الخطاب); BURSEVI(عمر,قيل)]

## 2:45/q19 How does the command connect with its context and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How the verse's command and prayer focus relate to the immediate discourse and parallel passages.
- 2:45/q19/p1 It remedies the difficult prior Israelite commands and renews the earlier order to pray. — Tabari links endurance and glorification at 20:130; Ibn Ashur links 2:43 and explains that confidence in the addressees encourages compliance. - 2:45/q20/p2 The description distinguishes the humble from people who lack faith in recompense. — Kashshaf and Bursevi describe hypocrites and ostentatious worshippers as experiencing only difficulty without hope of reward or fear of punishment. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 2:45/q21 What is meant by divine patience in the associated reports?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ · theology]
- 2:45/q21/p1 God's ṣabr means forbearance and delay of deserved punishment. — Qurtubi cites the Bukhari report through Abu Musa: people attribute a child to God yet He pardons and provides; scholars including Ibn Furak say this attribute appears in hadith, not Qur'an, and al-Sabur is an emphatic name. ## 2:45/q22 What inward or allegorical readings are attached to the verse?  [* · inward]

- 2:45/q22/p1 Patience leaves all besides God; prayer abides in His unseen presence. — Sulami's Iraqis, Qushayri, Qashani and Najmiyya describe ego restraint, proper standing, divine manifestations, and continuing service when awe threatens to absorb the servant; Qushayri says easing the unveiling restores ordinary duties. ## 2:45/q23 What pressures make this command necessary?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources name personal attachment and public wrongdoing as different pressures.
- 2:45/q23/p1 Attachment to worldly comfort or status makes obedience difficult. — Biqai reports that the Prophet asked the grieving Umar whether he was content for others to have this world while they had the Hereafter; this illustrates the preceding demand to surrender worldly attachment. - 2:45/q23/p2 Public moral decline and promotion of falsehood bring suffering and divine anger. — Elmalili says this is a fundamental source of human suffering, though God's mercy remains universal. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 2:45/q24 What does practicing prayer effectively require?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:45/q24/p1 Enduring its duties, distraction, sincerity, attention, and reverence. — Kashshaf says seek help by joining patience to prayer and bearing its hardships and etiquette; Biqai says prayer's greatness demands leaving self-interest and accepting the lowliness of servitude before God. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
