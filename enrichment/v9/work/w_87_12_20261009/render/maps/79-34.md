# 79:34: 12 questions

## 79:34/q01 How does the opening fa connect this event to what precedes?  [فَإِذَا · links]
turns on: Whether the fāʾ emphasizes sequence after worldly provision or the necessary consequence of divine creation and governance.
- 79:34/q01/p1 It moves from earthly provision to the afterlife, when worldly comforts end and judgment arrives. — Al-Ālūsī takes the fāʾ as sequence, supported by the preceding word for provision. Bursevī says the event follows temporary worldly provision. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the earth's creation and provision in 79:30 and 79:33. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:34/q01/p2 The resurrection follows necessarily from the creation and governance just described. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly reads the opening fāʾ as this connection. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 79:34/q02 What does the past-tense verb after idhā imply?  [فَإِذَا جَآءَتِ · grammar]
- 79:34/q02/p1 It speaks of a future event in the past form to stress that its occurrence is certain. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says idhā shifts the past-tense verb to future reference while the past form reinforces certainty. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:34/q03 What does saying the calamity 'comes' convey?  [جَآءَتِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give two rhetorical features of 'coming': the hardship associated with jāʾa and the image of a timed event arriving.
- 79:34/q03/p1 Jāʾa suits the arrival of a grave and difficult event. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the calamity with 19:88–90, 17:81 and 80:33–34; he distinguishes the report of the Overwhelming arriving in 88:1 from the event itself arriving here. His comparison of atā amr Allāh and jāʾa amr Allāh uses 16:1, 40:78, 12:110, 6:34, 29:53 and 16:26: jāʾa marks the harder occurrence involving judgment and loss. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 79:34/q03/p2 Its occurrence is pictured as a traveler arriving when the appointed time is reached. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the coming of a timed event metaphorical. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:34/q04 What answers the condition introduced by idhā?  [فَإِذَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the apodosis is present later in the passage or omitted, and, if omitted, which sense is supplied.
- 79:34/q04/p1 The following account of the transgressor and his fate supplies the answer. — Al-Ṭabrisī points to 'whoever transgresses' and what follows. Al-Samīn cites fa-ammā man ṭaghā and an analogy concerning Banū Tamīm. Al-Rāzī proposes the statement that Hell is the abode as the apodosis of a compound condition: when the calamity comes, whoever comes as a transgressor has Hell as home. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; RAZI-FULL]
- 79:34/q04/p2 The answer is omitted but understood as the separation of people into the blessed and wretched and their respective destinations. — Al-Wāḥidī supplies entry into Hell and Paradise, inferred from the following destinations; al-Biqāʿī supplies division into the wretched and blessed. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:34/q04/p3 An omitted answer says 'the matter is so.' — Al-Samīn reports this as an alternative to taking the following fa-ammā clause as the answer. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 79:34/q04/p4 An omitted response says that Hell is his abode. — Al-Samīn attributes this supplied response to al-Zamakhsharī, describing it as an omitted oath response. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 79:34/q04/p5 An omitted answer says 'they saw.' — Al-Samīn lists this among other proposals. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 79:34/q04/p6 An omitted answer says 'they knew.' — Al-Samīn lists this among other proposals. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 79:34/q04/p7 The answer itself governs idhā, and its meaning is expressed by 'the day when the human remembers.' — Al-Samīn reports Abū al-Baqāʾ's syntactic analysis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]
- 79:34/q04/p8 The missing response affirms that the event must occur. — Study Quran reports this understanding of the omitted conditional response. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 79:34/q05 What does ṭāmmah mean, and what explains that sense?  [ٱلطَّآمَّةُ · meaning]
- 79:34/q05/p1 An overpowering, all-encompassing calamity that rises above, covers, or eclipses other events. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others explain its magnitude and power over other terrors; al-Ṭabarī calls them lesser horrors. Al-Naḥḥās says Arabs call anything momentous a ṭāmmah. Ibn ʿĪsā, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Majmaʿ describe covering, height and irresistibility; al-Jishumī adds a calamity that cannot be repelled. Al-Mubarrad and al-Thaʿlabī cite a horse exhausting its effort in a run; al-Mubarrad also cites water filling a river. Al-Layth cites filling a well with earth, a flood burying a cistern and rising to cover things; al-Qaffāl draws the sense of burial and rising above. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Samīn cite the proverb of water or a valley overwhelming villages. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran derive the image from overflowing water, with al-Rāghib's overflowing sea cited by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the word denotes an event rare in its class. Al-Biqāʿī glosses the phrase as the supreme calamity. Bursevī says it rises above and overwhelms. Al-Wāḥidī cites al-Farrāʾ's explanation that Resurrection overwhelms everything. [34 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الليث,المبرد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القفال,الليث,المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); BINTSHATI(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:34/q06 Which Arabic verbal forms of the root are reported?  [ٱلطَّآمَّةُ · grammar]
- 79:34/q06/p1 Both ṭumm and ṭamm are used in Arabic. — Al-Farrāʾ states that both forms are Arabic; al-Wāḥidī transmits his observation. [2 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)]

## 79:34/q07 Which event is the Great Overwhelming Calamity?  [ٱلطَّآمَّةُ ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the name denotes the whole Resurrection or Hour, a particular blast, a later stage of judgment or admission to the abodes, or an inward manifestation.
- 79:34/q07/p1 The Resurrection or Hour, overwhelming all earlier disasters; some take it to encompass the whole succession of terrors through final judgment. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls it one of Resurrection's names, magnified by God as warning; al-Rabīʿ and al-Kalbī call it the Hour, beyond which nothing remains. Al-Rāzī's unnamed group cites its fire, terrifying assembly and extraordinary signs. Ibn Kathīr cites 54:46, where the Hour is more grievous. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes all horrors until the groups reach their abodes and lists al-ṣākhkha, al-qāriʿa and al-rājifa as other Qurʾānic descriptions; al-Itqān also lists al-ghāshiya and al-ḥāqqa. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports that al-Zamakhsharī appears to favor Resurrection among three readings and says ṭāmmah occurs nowhere else in the Qurʾān; she cites al-Rāghib's lexical support for it. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Jishumī, al-Kur'an Yolu and others relate the name to its surpassing every horror. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās transmit Ibn ʿAbbās's divine warning. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī also report the second blast as an alternative; al-Muqātil calls it the final blast from Jerusalem, namely Resurrection. [32 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكلبي); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(others); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)+]
- 79:34/q07/p2 The second trumpet blast, which raises the dead and brings the resurrection assembly. — Al-Muqātil specifies a final blast from Jerusalem. Al-Zajjāj says the cry overwhelms all and ushers in resurrection, judgment, punishment, torment and mercy. Al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿAbbās and others identify the second blast; al-Rāzī says all creatures then gather, and Study Quran cites 39:68 for the gathering of humans and jinn. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates this alternative to the single blast of 79:13–14. Al-Thaʿlabī's and other accounts distinguish it from the eventual sorting of the two groups. [23 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس عن الضحاك والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الراغب,الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 79:34/q07/p3 The moment when the people of Paradise and Hell are led to their respective abodes. — Al-Qāsim ibn al-Walīd gives this account, also attributed to Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan. Al-Māwardī cites Ibn ʿĪsā for the lexical sense separately. Ibn ʿĀshūr's broader account reaches this endpoint; al-Mālīk ibn Maʿwal places the event at the moment each group goes to its destination. Al-Ṭabarī's al-Qāsim and al-Qurṭubī's al-Qāsim al-Hamdānī vary in naming but give the same event. Ibn al-Jawzī calls it a procession and al-Rāzī a dispatch. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(القاسم بن الوليد); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(القاسم بن الوليد); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(آخرون,مالك بن معول); QURTUBI-FULL(القاسم بن الوليد الهمداني); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(القاسم بن الوليد الهمذاني); ALUSI-FULL(القاسم بن الوليد الهمداني); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الراغب,الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 79:34/q07/p4 The decisive moment when Hell's people are called, commanded, handed over, presented to Mālik, or driven into the Fire. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports a call to Hell's people to enter. Sufyān says they are handed to the guards. ʿAmr ibn Qays says the order to take someone to the Fire arrives. Mujāhid specifies presentation to Mālik, Hell's guardian, in al-Durr; al-Ālūsī distinguishes ʿAmr's driving into the Fire from Mujāhid's presentation to Mālik. These details vary in the reports. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان); DURR-FULL(عمرو بن قيس الكندي,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(عمرو بن قيس الكندي)]
- 79:34/q07/p5 The reading of each person's record at the time described by the following verses. — Al-Rāzī reports this possibility among views placing the catastrophe in the subsequent scene and cites 17:13. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]
- 79:34/q07/p6 A dense, enveloping event. — Al-Ṭabrisī transmits this alternative description without further identification. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 79:34/q07/p7 The first blast, if ṭāmmah names the earlier of the two blasts. — Bursevī presents this as a conditional way to explain the order of this sūra's ṭāmmah and the next sūra's ṣākhkhah. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 79:34/q07/p8 The light of divine unity manifesting and effacing everything. — This is al-Qāshānī's inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:34/q08 Why is the event called al-kubrā, 'the greatest'?  [ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the adjective chiefly intensifies the terror, distinguishes worldly disasters, or compares it with every other calamity.
- 79:34/q08/p1 It intensifies the severity and terror of the calamity. — Al-Ālūsī reports reinforcement of severity as one explanation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the adjective intensifies the accompanying horrors; Bursevī stresses greatness rather than old age. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 79:34/q08/p2 It distinguishes this calamity from familiar worldly disasters. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as an explanation of the adjective. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:34/q08/p3 It marks this calamity as greater than every other calamity. — Al-Ālūsī reports the comparative explanation. Al-Ṭabarī says it swallows lesser terrors; al-Rabīʿ calls it more dreadful and bitter than any other, while al-Biqāʿī calls it supreme. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 79:34/q09 How does this event fit the sūra's account of resurrection and final fates?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is the developing resurrection scene, its contrasting outcomes, or the order of calamity names across sūras.
- 79:34/q09/p1 It advances the sequence from resurrection's first convulsion and blast through judgment to the two groups' final abodes. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it with 79:6 and 79:13, and says warning and glad tidings follow the proof of resurrection because judgment follows resurrection; preparation for lasting reward is wiser than transient pleasure. Al-Biqāʿī sees the sūra's resurrection and gathering, its oaths and final fates as the climax after preceding sūras' exposition. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:34/q09/p2 It introduces the contrast between the rebellious and the God-fearing, or between disbelievers' and believers' outcomes. — Iṣlāḥī describes the following passage as punishment for the rebellious and glad tidings for those who fear God; Study Quran says verses 34–41 contrast disbelievers and believers. Al-Jishumī says the verses show Resurrection's tremendous condition and many terrors. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI]
- 79:34/q09/p3 The ordering of ṭāmmah here and ṣākhkhah in the next sūra fits either an earlier first blast or two descriptions of the second blast. — Bursevī says a first-blast ṭāmmah precedes the deafening second blast, while, if ṭāmmah is already the second, both names fit that event. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 79:34/q10 What warning or lesson does the verse introduce?  [* · interpretation]
- 79:34/q10/p1 Resurrection is certain and terrifying; people should prepare for judgment and lasting reward rather than cling to fleeting worldly pleasures. — Al-Jishumī says the verses demonstrate Resurrection's magnitude and many terrors. Ibn ʿĀshūr says warning and glad tidings follow the proof of resurrection since judgment follows it. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās, says God magnifies this name to warn His servants. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس)]

## 79:34/q11 Does this phrase give the sūra a title?  [ٱلطَّآمَّةُ · sciences]
- 79:34/q11/p1 Yes; al-Ṭāmmah is one title, alongside the title from the opening word and al-Sāhirah from the gathering plain. — Study Quran lists these names of the sūra. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 79:34/q12 What poetic language is cited for the surrounding account of return after death?  [* · meaning]
- 79:34/q12/p1 A poem attributed to Shāhir of Hamadhān uses al-sāhirah and al-ḥāfirah while describing return after death. — Al-Muqātil transmits the poem, set at Yarmūk, as lexical poetic evidence; the two terms belong to the surrounding resurrection passage. [1 notes: MUQATIL(شاهر همذان)]
