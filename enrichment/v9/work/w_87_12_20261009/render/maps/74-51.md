# 74:51: 12 questions

## 74:51/q01 What does فَرَّتْ say happened, and who fled?  [فَرَّتْ · meaning]
- 74:51/q01/p1 The wild asses fled. — Al-Wāḥidī explicitly identifies the fleeing ones as the asses; al-Qurṭubī glosses farrat as 'fled.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the preceding ḥumur as wild asses and mustanfira as fleeing. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 74:51/q02 How does the clause فَرَّتْ مِن قَسْوَرَةٍ relate grammatically to the asses?  [فَرَّتْ مِن قَسْوَرَةٍۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether farrat specifies the asses adjectivally or gives their state circumstantially.
- 74:51/q02/p1 It is a circumstantial clause, with qad understood. — Al-Nasafī supplies an understood qad. Abū al-Baqāʾ, as reported by al-Samīn, permits this analysis. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]
- 74:51/q02/p2 It is an adjective describing the asses, alongside mustanfira. — Abū al-Baqāʾ, as reported by al-Samīn, gives this as the alternative to a circumstantial clause. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

Al-Māwardī cites al-Farazdaq; Abū Ḥayyān cites a poetic simile. Al-Ālūsī reports the majority of lexicographers for lion; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes Ibn Sīda's qaswar/qaswara pair. ## 74:51/q04 What is the form and derivation of قَسْوَرَةٍۭ?  [قَسْوَرَةٍۭ · grammar]
turns on: The notes give a root and pattern analysis and a separate poetic alteration, without presenting them as competing derivations.
- 74:51/q04/p1 It is on the faʿūla pattern from qasr, overpowering or compelling. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Ālūsī explicitly name the pattern. Ibn Qutayba proposes the derivation tentatively; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī's lexicographers, al-Jishumī, al-Rāzī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī's Ibn ʿArafa, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, and al-Biqāʿī relate it to domination, commonly because a lion subdues beasts. Al-Bursawī compares ḥaydara in form and sense. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة، وابن الأعرابي، والمبرد); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عرفة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 74:51/q04/p2 Poetic qaswar is a form of qaswara adapted for rhyme. — Ibn Khālawayh says Imruʾ al-Qays drops the final tāʾ marbūṭa and adds alif in a cited verse for rhyme. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 74:51/q05 Is قَسْوَرَةٍۭ an Arabic or Ethiopic word for lion?  [قَسْوَرَةٍۭ · meaning]
turns on: The reported language equivalents conflict: some Ibn ʿAbbās reports call it Arabic or Ethiopic lion, while ʿIkrima disputes the Ethiopic gloss and another Ibn ʿAbbās report disputes Arabic lion usage.
- 74:51/q05/p1 It names a lion in Arabic and also in Ethiopic. — Ibn ʿAbbās's multilingual reports in al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī, and al-Ālūsī list Arabic lion and Ethiopic qaswara, with varying Persian and Nabataean equivalents. Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests Ibn ʿAbbās's divergent statements can be verbal because he denied lion as the original Arabic sense. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 74:51/q05/p2 Qaswara is the Ethiopic term for lion. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports in Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Suyūṭī's Itqān, al-Bursawī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr; al-Burhān lists it as an Ethiopian Qurʾanic word, and Ibn al-Subkī lists it among words from another language. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Ibn ʿAbbās denied it as original Arabic lion usage. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس في رواية سليمان بن قتة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن السبكي,ابن عباس)]
- 74:51/q05/p3 The Ethiopic word for lion is ʿanbasa, not qaswara. — ʿIkrima replies to the Ethiopic lion claim this way in al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī; al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī repeat the disagreement. [4 notes: FARRA(عكرمة); TAB-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة); RAZI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 74:51/q05/p4 Lion is not a known Arabic sense here; the Arabic sense is a band of men. — In Abū Jamra's report Ibn ʿAbbās denies knowing lion as Arab usage; al-Qurṭubī, al-Samīn, and al-Suyūṭī transmit the objection. Ibn ʿĀshūr considers it alongside the Ethiopic lion reports. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 74:51/q06 How should the neighbouring word mustanfira be read in light of فَرَّتْ?  [فَرَّتْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the form says the asses flee themselves or are made to flee; farrat and the Bedouin usage are cited for kasra.
- 74:51/q06/p1 With kasra on fāʾ, describing asses that flee themselves. — Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī prefers this because farrat says the asses fled. Abū Sawwār/Abū Sarār al-Ghanawī (called Abū Sarar al-ʿAtawī by Abū Ḥayyān) confirms the fleeing sense in his exchange with Muḥammad ibn Sallām; Ibn Khālawayh, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī recount variants of it. [6 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(أعرابي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو سوار الغنوي); RAZI-FULL(أبو سوار الغنوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن سلام); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سرار الغنوي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)+]
- 74:51/q06/p2 With fatḥa on fāʾ, describing asses driven into flight. — Al-Ālūsī reports Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, and al-Mufaḍḍal from ʿĀṣim with fatḥa; his Bedouin report distinguishes this passive or driven sense from the kasra form. Ibn Khālawayh's Bedouin anecdote begins with a reader's fatḥa. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو سرار الغنوي,نافع وابن عامر والمفضل عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(أعرابي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي)-]

## 74:51/q08 What human conduct does this flight portray?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:51/q08/p1 The disbelievers recoil from the Qurʾan, the Prophet's recitation, and the reminder as wild asses flee danger. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies the polytheists turning from the reminder. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Rāzī compares their flight from Muḥammad or his recitation to flight from a lion. Al-Baghawī specifies hearing the Prophet recite; al-Nasafī specifies hearing the reminder. Ibn Kathīr says flight from truth; al-Bursawī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise identify rejection of admonition. Iṣlāḥī adds that the Prophet should continue reminding, while benefit goes to those made receptive by God's laws. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة وعطاء والكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:51/q09 What force does the wild-ass comparison add?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The image can emphasize folly, vivid speed, fear, or the fate of fugitives; Ibn ʿĀshūr makes the fear nuance depend on the lion gloss.
- 74:51/q09/p1 It censures the disbelievers' folly and lack of sense. — Al-Kashshāf compares them to the donkey carrying books at 62:5 and emphasizes the absurdly frantic flight. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says donkeys are notably ignorant; al-Rāzī reports al-Kashshāf's point; al-Ālūsī repeats 62:5. Kur'an Yolu calls the response thoughtless, foolish, coarse, and indecorous. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:51/q09/p2 It makes their recoil vivid through the exceptional panic and speed of wild asses. — Al-Kashshāf calls their startled stampede exceptionally swift, a standard Arab comparison for camels' speed and flight from hunters at water. Abū Ḥayyān says wild asses are skittish and light in flight. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the image a sensory picture of an abstract rejection and cites Labīd, Ṭarafa, al-Ḥārith, al-Ḥajjāj and his son, and Dhū al-Rumma as poetic precedents. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:51/q09/p3 It conveys terror at the Qurʾan's warnings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says that on the lion reading the simile newly combines rejection with fear of the Qurʾan's threats. Iṣlāḥī mentions the lion's roar and the disbelievers' evasive terror. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:51/q09/p4 Their flight leads them into Hell's snares or the hunters' grasp. — Al-Biqāʿī uses the lion and hunters in the image: fleeing one terror leaves them caught in the other danger. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 74:51/q10 Why is قَسْوَرَةٍۭ chosen in this simile?  [قَسْوَرَةٍۭ · rhetoric]
- 74:51/q10/p1 It accommodates both proposed similes and preserves the verse ending. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the word suits either the familiar hunter pursuit or the lion image and maintains the cadence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:51/q11 How does this image connect to the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 74:51/q11/p1 It follows the declaration that the Qurʾan is a reminder and expresses amazement at persistent rejection. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the reminder for humankind at 74:31. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī places it after threat and promise and before the demand for individual revelation: they should believe but recoil from manifest truth. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 74:51/q12 What reward is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Muddaththir?  [* · sciences]
- 74:51/q12/p1 Ten good deeds are promised for each person in Mecca who believed or disbelieved in Muhammad. — Al-Kashshāf attributes the report to the Prophet and gives no grading. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله)]
