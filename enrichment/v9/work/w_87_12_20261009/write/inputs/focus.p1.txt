# 87:12/q07 What numerical comparison between earthly fire and Hellfire is reported? [ٱلنَّارَ ٱلْكُبْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted numbers differ; Ālūsī proposes that seventy could be an expression of abundance.
- 87:12/q07/p1 Earthly fire is one seventieth of Hellfire.
  reasons: Bayḍāwī quotes the Prophet; Bursawī repeats the comparison; Ālūsī cites Abū Hurayra's marfūʿ report and notes that seventy may express abundance because another version gives one hundred. Al-Biqāʿī instead describes the afterlife fire as seventy degrees greater than the intermediate realm's and worldly fires.

# 87:12/q08 What fate in the Fire is described with this verse and the next? [يَصْلَى ٱلنَّارَ · interpretation]
turns on: The Prophetic report distinguishes permanent inhabitants from those God later removes.
- 87:12/q08/p1 Those destined to remain suffer without death, relief, or a life worth calling life.
  reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī says there is neither death for relief nor beneficial life. Ibn Kathīr reports the Prophet's distinction and connects enduring torment to 43:77 and 35:36, which say its inhabitants are not killed and punishment is not eased. Iṣlāḥī says evaders who deny the Hereafter face eternal fire and will wish vainly for death; Kur'an Yolu says continued existence amid horrific suffering is no positive life. Ibn Kathīr adds that the person remains conscious of painful punishment.
  holders (7 notes): THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR

- 87:12/q08/p2 Those whom God removes from Hell become charcoal, emerge in groups, and sprout by Paradise's rivers.
  reasons: Ibn Kathīr reports the Prophet's distinction between these people and those destined to remain in Hell.

# 87:12/q09 How does the fire warning connect with the surrounding verses? [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize different links: contrasting responses and fates, reciprocal ellipsis, or clarification of the earlier label.
- 87:12/q09/p1 It gives the bad outcome of evading the reminder, balanced by the good outcome promised to those who heed it.
  reasons: Qutb says the Prophet's duty is to remind, after which people diverge by their reception. Iṣlāḥī says the fearful heed the warning while the obdurate evade it; the messenger should not force them. Corpus Coranicum sees verses 10–11's responses matched with fates in verses 12–15, a chiasm comparable to 92:5–11 and 92:14–21, with the warning balanced by the promise in 87:14–15.
  holders (7 notes): QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:10/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r5 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p271/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p278/r6 IBNKATHIR:87:11/r1 IBNKATHIR:87:13/r1
- 87:12/q09/p2 The passage uses paired omissions: the causes and outcomes of the two groups imply their unspoken opposites.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says fear and happiness imply the other's omitted hardness and misery; the misery named as cause implies omitted happiness. Al-Malawī says Quranic concision pairs cause and consequence across passages, likening judgment, decree, and ordainment to designing a waterwheel, constructing it, and measuring its water. Al-Biqāʿī also pairs the fire with its omitted opposite and later purification with its omitted opposite; purification, remembrance, and prayer form a therapy sequence, reprise 87:1's removal of defects and acquisition of perfections, and link prayer with escape from the fire.

# 87:12/q10 How does this warning fit the surah's broader message? [* · links]
- 87:12/q10/p1 It makes revelation's public admonition part of a surah about divine praise, the messenger's mission, and afterlife recompense.
  reasons: Qutb describes the cosmos praising God and the Prophet receiving good news of preservation and ease; he names oneness in creation, revelation, and afterlife recompense as foundations rooted in earlier scriptures (87:18–19), alongside the ease of the message, messenger, and community. Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza addresses the messenger again: after praising God, perhaps with close followers, he speaks to a wider audience, making the revelation of verse 6 public in purpose.
