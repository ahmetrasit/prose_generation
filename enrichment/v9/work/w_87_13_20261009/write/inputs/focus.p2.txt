# 87:13/q08 Which other Qurʾānic passages illuminate this verse? [* · links]
turns on: The cited passages serve different connections: repeated descriptions of Hell, a reckoning of deaths and lives, or continuation of the warning in the next sūrah.
- 87:13/q08/p1 Other passages repeat or support the denial of death and lasting punishment.
  reasons: Ibn Kathīr cites 43:77, where Hell's people ask Mālik for death and are told they will remain, and 35:36, where they are neither killed nor relieved. Al-Rāzī also cites 35:36. Corpus Coranicum calls it a Qurʾānic topos, identifying the exact wording at 20:74 and related formulations at 35:36, 43:77, and 69:27; Study Quran adds 74:28–29. Asad relates 74:28–29 to the condemned's avoidance of reminders.
  holders (10 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES
  notes: RAZI:87:13/r1 RAZI-FULL:v31p133#2/r1 IBNKATHIR:87:11/r1 IBNKATHIR:87:12/r6 IBNKATHIR:87:12/r7 IBNKATHIR:87:13/r1 IBNKATHIR-FULL:v8p380#2/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:13:anmerkung/r1 STUDYQURAN:87:12/r3 ASAD-NOTES:87:9#n8/r1
- 87:13/q08/p2 The two deaths and two lives of 40:11 explain why resurrected disbelievers have no further death or life in Hell.
  reasons: Elmalılı applies 40:11 after resurrection for punishment.
  holders (1 notes): ELMALILI

  reasons: Iṣlāḥī identifies the great torment of 88:21–26 with this passage's great Fire.

# 87:13/q09 How does this warning fit the sūrah's surrounding argument? [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace the warning's role at different scales: the immediate contrast of responses, the stanza's shift to public proclamation, or the sūrah's larger themes.
- 87:13/q09/p1 It is the fate of those who evade the Prophet's reminder, contrasted with the good who heed it.
  reasons: Quṭb says the Prophet reminds and responses diverge. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wretched favor worldly life. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet should remind without forcing the obdurate, whose rejection reflects their own condition; evaders deny the Hereafter and wish for death in vain. Corpus Coranicum says verses 10–11 contrast responses, expanded by the warning in 12–13 and the good's fate in 14–15; the passage forms a chiasm like Q 92:5–11 and 14–21. Asad connects the fate to keeping away from divine reminders in 74:28–29. Elmalılı compares their fate to pasture reduced to black refuse.
  holders (9 notes): QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:10/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p272/r4 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p271/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p278/r6 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p279/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r5 ASAD-NOTES:87:9#n8/r1 ELMALILI:87:7-13/r17
- 87:13/q09/p2 The second stanza turns the Prophet's received revelation into a public admonition.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum says the Prophet first praises God, perhaps with close followers, then addresses a wider audience; this makes verse 6's revelation public in purpose.
  holders (1 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:9-15:kommentar/r1
- 87:13/q09/p3 The sūrah frames the warning within God's oneness, revelation, recompense, and the Prophet's divinely eased mission.
  reasons: Quṭb says creation becomes a sanctuary of praise, God preserves and eases the Prophet's mission, and these themes are rooted in earlier scriptures at 87:18–19.
  holders (2 notes): QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r3 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r4

  reasons: Quṭb notes that the two sūrahs were sometimes recited on the same day.
