# 87:13/q04 Why deny life after denying death? [وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the added denial mainly excludes pleasant survival, implies no escape, or is explained as verbal antithesis.
- 87:13/q04/p1 It prevents survival in the Fire from sounding like comfortable life or an honor; their life has no benefit or relief from pain.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says immortality could otherwise sound like an extraordinary honor, while failure to purify, believe, and pray explains absence of beneficial life. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the second denial prevents imagining a mild, painless, nonfatal burning. Al-Itqān describes denying a thing when its defining benefit is absent; al-Burhān applies this to wholesome life.
  holders (6 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN; BURHAN
  notes: BIQAI:87:13/r2 BIQAI-FULL:v8p400/r2 IBNASHUR:87:13/r4 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p286/r4 ITQAN:v3p208#3/r1 BURHAN:v3p395/r2
- 87:13/q04/p2 Denying life can also mean denying escape from the Fire.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this metonym: fire normally causes death, while life entails not dying. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says both death and a transition to blessed life would end punishment.
  holders (3 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:13/r5 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p286/r5 TABATABAI:87:14-16/r9
- 87:13/q04/p3 The paired words form an antithesis.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the opposition of dying and living and rejects treating it merely as a standard double denial that establishes a middle quality, comparing 24:35.
  holders (3 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:13/r7 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p286/r5 IBNASHUR:87:13/r6

# 87:13/q05 Whom does the verse's denial of death apply to? [لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the warning is delimited by permanence in Hell, by the highest degree of wretchedness, or by the preceding description of those who reject the reminder.
- 87:13/q05/p1 It applies to permanent inhabitants of Hell, especially disbelievers; sinful believers who enter the Fire can die and later leave through intercession.
  reasons: Abū Saʿīd's Prophetic report in Muslim draws the distinction; al-Farrāʾ calls the believers' place a lesser fire. Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı cite the report. Elmalılı relates disbelievers' continuing punishment after resurrection to the two deaths and two lives of 40:11.

# 87:13/q06 What do the reports say happens to sinful monotheists in the Fire and after intercession? [لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in how they describe the rescued people's revival and where it occurs; the Abū Saʿīd versions specify charcoal, intercession, and Paradise's rivers, while Abū Hurayra describes growth in the open and souls re-entering bodies.
- 87:13/q06/p1 They are burned and made dead like charcoal; intercessors bring them out, and they grow again by Paradise's rivers like flood-carried seed.
  reasons: Abū Saʿīd transmits the Prophet's distinction from permanent inhabitants. Versions say God first mercies them, that they emerge in groups, and that water or the river of life restores them; one compares revival to a tree changing color. Aḥmad and Muslim transmit versions, including Aḥmad's route through al-Jarīrī; Ibn Kathīr notes Muslim's routes through Bishr ibn al-Mufaḍḍal and Shuʿba. Al-Qurṭubī also cites Muslim and al-Farrāʾ's “lesser fire.”

# 87:13/q07 How is the reported death of sinful believers in Hell understood and timed? [لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the report's death separates soul and body, signifies torpor or sleep, when death occurs, and whether punishment can persist during death.
- 87:13/q07/p1 They truly die after suffering punishment proportionate to their sins, with souls leaving their bodies, and remain dead until intercession; delayed Paradise and confinement continue their penalty.
  reasons: Ibn Rajab takes the Prophet's emphatic verbal noun as evidence for literal death. Al-Ālūsī prefers this account, likening it to beating followed by imprisonment; their confinement is not an unpunished life. He also says death may finish punishment or spare them the sight of the Fire. Al-Bazzār's Abū Hurayra report of souls returning supports real separation.
  holders (9 notes): ALUSI(ابن رجب، النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة، ابن رجب); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+

- 87:13/q07/p2 Their death is figurative, like torpor, stillness, or lifelessness; they remain alive in the Fire.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the claim that sinners are dead but interprets it metaphorically to retain the verse's denial of death.
