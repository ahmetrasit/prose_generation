# 19:13: 21 questions

## 19:13/q01 What does ḥanānan min ladunnā mean?  [وَحَنَانًۭا مِّن لَّدُنَّا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gift is divine tenderness to Yahya, mercy to his parents, tenderness placed in Yahya, love, honor, blessing, or a distinct bond; the phrase 'from Us' and Arabic usage guide the readings.
- 19:13/q01/p1 God's mercy or tender compassion toward Yahya, bestowed from His presence. — Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Qatāda, al-Ḥasan, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and many later commentators give mercy; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Rabīʿ say only God can give it. Mujāhid specifically has the Lord's tenderness toward Yahya. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī and others cite Arabic poetry for mercy. Al-Zajjāj includes affection; Ibn Kathīr compares a camel's tenderness for its young. Al-Qurṭubī prefers tenderness while leaving its direction open. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the divine gift exceeds ordinary human measure; al-Asad calls it special. Al-Bāqir's report has God answer Yahya's invocation, 'Here I am, John.' [69 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن وقتادة والكلبي,مجاهد); MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,عكرمة,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، رواية علي بن أبي طلحة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، قتادة، الحسن,الضحاك,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,جمهور المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وعكرمة وقتادة والضحاك وأبو عبيدة والفراء,مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,شاعر,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الربيع,مجاهد); ITQAN(unknown); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك وعكرمة والفراء وأبو عبيدة,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,الراغب,بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو عبيدة والفراء,أهل التأويل); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 19:13/q01/p2 The gift is mercy to Zakariyyā or both parents through the grant of Yahya. — Al-Farrāʾ reads an accusative of cause and makes the child and answered prayer mercy for the parents; al-Wāḥidī says this requires an ellipsis. Qatāda's report names Zakariyyā. Al-Rāzī adds that the response purifies him from being a rejected supplicant. Ibn al-Anbārī says God acted mercifully toward Yahya's parents. [12 notes: FARRA; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABATABAI-]
- 19:13/q01/p3 God placed mercy, tenderness, or a gentle heart in Yahya for his parents, contemporaries, and other people. — Al-Naḥḥās's Ibn ʿAbbās report says it helped Yahya save people from unbelief and evil. Al-Māwardī adds softening hearts for a prompt response; Abū ʿAlī and al-Wāḥidī connect it to preaching obedience. Al-Rāzī says purity protects mercy from excusing wrongdoing; he and al-Bursawī cite 24:2. Al-Kāshshāf includes parents and others. [24 notes: NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABATABAI-]
- 19:13/q01/p4 Ḥanān is love or loving affection. — ʿIkrima, Ibn Zayd, ʿAwf and Saʿīd al-Juhanī use love; al-Qurṭubī cites spouses' affection in poetry. Al-Wāḥidī records Ibn ʿAbbās tentatively calling it God's affection for His servant. Al-Ālūsī specifies Yahya was made beloved by people, comparing 20:39; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also reports popular love as one reading. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,عكرمة); THALABI(عوف); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، رواية عكرمة); JISHUMI(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة وابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,عكرمة); DURR-FULL(سعيد الجهني); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد وعكرمة); TABATABAI(بعضهم); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أهل التأويل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI-]
- 19:13/q01/p5 It means exaltation, honor, or reverence from God. — ʿAṭāʾ b. Abī Rabāḥ gives exaltation. Al-Rāzī cites Waraqa's threat to honor Bilāl if killed as comparable wording. Al-Biqāʿī includes reverence in the divine endowment; Ibn al-Aʿrābī also records a sacred or exalted use. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء بن أبي رباح); RAZI-FULL(عطاء بن رباح); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:13/q01/p6 It means blessing or provision. — Ibn Jubayr is reported with blessing; Ibn al-Aʿrābī records provision and blessing among the senses, while al-Biqāʿī includes them in the direct gift. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:13/q01/p7 It is a special divine attraction and bond between Yahya and his Lord. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this because 'from Us' places the gift beyond ordinary causes; he sets aside readings as popular affection or Yahya's compassion for people. Al-Wāsiṭī says the divine tenderness brought Yahya intimacy and expansiveness. [2 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); TABATABAI+]
- 19:13/q01/p8 It describes Yahya's living, vibrant heart and soul. — Iṣlāḥī says Yahya's Gospel statements still move people and objects to reports that Ibn ʿAbbās could not understand this common Arabic word. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:13/q01/p9 Its meaning was unknown to Ibn ʿAbbās in some reports. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ transmit his non-answer, sometimes through Saʿīd b. Jubayr; al-Suyūṭī also lists it among four words Ibn ʿAbbās said he did not know. Iṣlāḥī contests this attribution. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 19:13/q02 What do the root and Arabic forms of ḥanān convey?  [وَحَنَانًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the relevant Arabic form is a verbal noun, conventional expression, dialectal form, or dual.
- 19:13/q02/p1 The root moves from longing or yearning to tenderness, compassion, and mercy. — Al-Ṭabarī calls ḥanān a verbal noun and cites taḥannan and ḥannah in poetry. Al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn Qutayba as transmitted by Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī compare a she-camel's longing for its young; al-Rāzī also recalls the Prophet's moaning palm trunk. Al-Majāz cites Imruʾ al-Qays and al-Ḥuṭayʾa; al-Zajjāj gives two poetry witnesses; al-Ḥuṭayʾa's request to ʿUmar is cited by al-Jishumī. [21 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(شاعر غير مسمى); ZAJJAJ(قال أيضا,قال الشاعر); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الحطيئة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(شاعر); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 19:13/q02/p2 Ḥanānik may be a conventional expression or dialectal form, or a dual expressing repeated compassion. — Al-Majāz, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ record disagreement on dual versus independent or dialectal form. Ṭarafa's line and Imruʾ al-Qays's ḥanānaka are cited. Abū ʿUbayda says the dual is common; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes it as compassion upon compassion. [20 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(امرؤ القيس,طرفة بن العبد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(طرفة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الطبري)]
- 19:13/q02/p3 Ḥanān is rare in the Qur'an despite the abundance of mercy vocabulary. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says this form and root occur only here, while forms of r-ḥ-m are frequent; she mentions 18:81. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 19:13/q03 How is ḥanānan construed grammatically?  [وَحَنَانًۭا · grammar]
turns on: What omitted or preceding term governs ḥanānan and its case.
- 19:13/q03/p1 It is an accusative coordinated with the earlier gift of judgment; 'We gave' governs it. — Al-Jishumī also allows its association with the gift of the Book; Ibn Kathīr says wisdom, affection, and purity are jointly given. Al-Bursawī says indefiniteness magnifies the gift; al-Ālūsī says it amplifies the endowment. [9 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:13/q03/p2 It is a causal accusative: God did this as mercy for the parents. — Al-Farrāʾ ties the accusative to the gift of the child. Al-Wāḥidī says the reading needs an ellipsis about the child and answered prayer; al-Ālūsī lists a causal object among the possible parses. [3 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:13/q03/p3 It is an accusative verbal noun in a supplication with an omitted verb. — Abū al-Baqāʾ compares suqyan and raʿyan; al-Ālūsī also lists a verbal-noun parse. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:13/q03/p4 It may be a nominative predicate with an omitted subject. — Al-Samīn cites the expression in 12:18; Sibawayh's quoted poem supplies a nominative use of ḥanān. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه)]
- 19:13/q03/p5 It can attach to the description 'as a child' or be coordinated with ṣabiyyan. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī record this alternative. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:13/q04 What does min ladunnā add?  [مِّن لَّدُنَّا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase merely names the giver, intensifies the gift, or identifies its special character.
- 19:13/q04/p1 The tenderness is directly and specially bestowed by God. — Al-Nasafī glosses it 'from God'; al-Daḥḥāk and al-Rabīʿ say none else grants this mercy. Al-Biqāʿī says Yahya received it without teaching or experience; the Study Quran and Asad call it a special divine gift. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); JISHUMI(الضحاك); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 19:13/q04/p2 It qualifies and magnifies the gift of ḥanān. — Al-Samīn says it qualifies ḥanān. Al-Ālūsī construes it with an omitted adjective, marking both its innate greatness and its special divine source. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:13/q04/p3 It points to a bond beyond ordinary causes between Yahya and God. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses the phrase to prefer this reading over people's affection and Yahya's compassion. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 19:13/q05 May Ḥannān be used as a name or attribute of God?  [وَحَنَانًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether figurative merciful usage suffices or a reliable naming report is required.
- 19:13/q05/p1 It may be applied to God as compassionate and merciful, with figurative language. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Samīn liken the expression to calling Him Raḥīm; al-Rāzī reports permissive usage. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a divine attribute. Ibn al-Aʿrābī distinguishes doubled-n Ḥannān as Merciful from light-n ḥanān as tenderness; Ibn Kathīr transmits a report of a man in Hell invoking 'O Ḥannān, O Mannān' for a thousand years. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:13/q05/p2 It should not be established as a divine name. — Al-Rāzī reports an objection from the root's original associations and the absence of a reliable report naming God so. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(منهم)]

## 19:13/q06 What does zakāh mean here?  [وَزَكَوٰةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether zakāh refers to moral purity, deeds, blessing and growth, charitable gift or giving, purification of followers, divine praise, or spiritual nurture.
- 19:13/q06/p1 Purity or purification of Yahya from sin, impurity, or evil. — Muqātil says God made him righteous and purified him of sins; al-Ṭabarī includes bodily obedience. Al-Tustarī instead specifies purification from people's suspicions. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī say God is the purifying subject; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 79:18 for purification of the soul. Iṣlāḥī includes inward and outward cleanliness. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that basic beneficial growth better fits the context. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; SULAMI(سهل); THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن فارس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABATABAI-]
- 19:13/q06/p2 Righteous and pure deeds, obedience, and sincerity. — Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Ibn Jurayj give righteous work; Yaḥyā b. Sallām connects it to the purified believers rewarded in 20:75–76. Ibn ʿAbbās gives obedience and sincerity; al-Biqāʿī focuses on pure intention appearing in word and deed. Study Quran includes both freedom from sin and righteous action. [26 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,الضحاك,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,قتادة); JISHUMI(قتادة، الضحاك، ابن جريج); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة والضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك وقتادة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك وابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك وقتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 19:13/q06/p3 A blessing or increase and growth in goodness. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Anbārī offer growth or increase; Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with blessing. Al-Qurṭubī says Yahya becomes a blessing through guiding people; al-Ālūsī says he teaches good. Ibn ʿAṭiyya combines growth with purification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī instead anchors the word in sound growth of the spirit. [12 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 19:13/q06/p4 A charitable gift God made of Yahya to his parents, or charity for them. — Al-Kalbī and Ibn Qutayba frame the child as God's charity to the parents; al-Thaʿlabī's wording also speaks of charity Yahya's father gave the parents. Al-Farrāʾ links both mercy and purification to them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues this misses the context's basic sense of growth. - 19:13/q06/p5 Charitable giving by or through Yahya to other people. — Al-Kāshshāf takes it as Yahya's kindness and almsgiving; al-Bursawī says God enabled him to give alms, while al-Ālūsī lists alms to people alongside other charitable readings. - 19:13/q06/p6 Yahya's call purified those who accepted him. — Al-Ḥasan gives this interpretation; al-Bayḍāwī also records Yahya being enabled to affirm people. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); JISHUMI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL]

- 19:13/q06/p9 Tenderness or compassion. — Al-Burhān says zakāh usually denotes wealth but in Yahya's description it denotes tenderness or compassion. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:13/q07 How does zakāh fit the syntax?  [وَزَكَوٰةًۭ · grammar]
- 19:13/q07/p1 It is accusative and coordinated with the preceding gifts. — Al-Ṭabarī relates it to wisdom; al-Jishumī joins it to ḥanān and contrasts taqiyyan as kāna's predicate. Ibn Kathīr also coordinates zakāh with ḥanān. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 19:13/q08 Why is Yahya's purity attributed to God?  [وَزَكَوٰةًۭ · theology]
turns on: Whether divine attribution means creation of acts or gracious guidance and enabling.
- 19:13/q08/p1 God creates the human acts that constitute his purity. — Al-Rāzī says calling purity God's gift supports this doctrine and that reducing it to enabling grace departs from the apparent wording. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 19:13/q08/p2 God's guidance and لطف enabled Yahya's purity, especially in childhood; he could also accept that guidance. — Al-Jishumī states both formulations, and al-Ṭabrisī says divine لطف guided him and enabled obedience. Al-Qushayrī joins acquired piety with piety given by direct grace. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL-]

## 19:13/q09 What does 'he was taqiyyan' say about Yahya?  [وَكَانَ تَقِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taqiyyan names general devotion, Muslim submission, or filial duty.
- 19:13/q09/p1 He was devout, God-fearing, obedient, and avoided forbidden acts. — Al-Ṭabarī includes performing duties and hastening to obey; al-Rāzī says it means neither violating prohibitions nor neglecting commands and calls it highest praise. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies an intensive form and says kāna establishes a settled trait. Al-Tustarī defines turning from all else toward God; al-Qushayrī sees both acquired and gifted piety. Iṣlāḥī points to John's simple life, public call to repentance, and death for God's Book; Study Quran glosses taqwā as reverence. [27 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); SULAMI(سهل); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:13/q09/p2 He was Muslim and sincere. — Muqātil glosses taqī as Muslim; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī add sincerity and obedience. [4 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 19:13/q09/p3 He was dutiful toward his parents. — Muqātil is cited for this narrower explanation; Ibn Kathīr and Kur'an Yolu include respect and dutifulness among his pious acts. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 19:13/q10 How complete was Yahya's freedom from sin?  [وَزَكَوٰةًۭ تَقِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the piety is explained by the verse's general moral description or by reports of never even intending sin.
- 19:13/q10/p1 He neither committed nor intended sin; reports extend this to minor and major acts, shirk, and desire for women. — Al-Ḥasan's Prophetic report exempts Yahya from the usual committing or intending of sin. Ibn ʿAbbās says he was purified and did not sin; another report says he did not associate anyone with God. Qatāda says he never desired a woman. A report through ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr says all children of Adam come to Judgment with sin except Yahya; al-Qurṭubī gives no grading. Ibn ʿUyayna also says Yahya neither disobeyed nor entertained disobedience. ## 19:13/q17 How should a difficult Qur'anic word such as ḥanān be investigated?  [وَحَنَانًۭا · sciences]
