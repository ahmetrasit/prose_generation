# 19:15: 25 questions

## 19:15/q01 What does the salām upon Yahya mean?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether salām is read as protective safety, a greeting that itself honors Yahya, or a commanded greeting.
- 19:15/q01/p1 It is safety and protection for Yahya. — Al-Tabari and others gloss it as safety (Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi); al-Kalbi calls it safety from God (Wahidi); Wahidi-Wajiz and Ibn Kathir apply it throughout the named stages. Al-Tabatabai distinguishes salām as a fitting, unfeared condition from amn, mere absence of feared harm. Jishumi includes safety from sin and later punishment among the glosses. [29 notes: TAB-FULL; SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي,الطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ,Ṭ, R)]
- 19:15/q01/p2 It is a greeting of honor and praise, with a prayer for safety. — Ibn Atiyya finds an ordinary greeting nobler than safety inferred from innocence; Qurtubi calls that good. Ibn Ashur says God's greeting contains praise or prayer and compares 36:58 and Jesus's greeting. A report from Ibn Abbas says God greeted Yahya as he was born. Tabatabai describes salām as positive well-being, not just absence of harm. [17 notes: THALABI(الحلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي عن ابن عباس,عطاء عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI; TABRISI(عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,عطاء); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); DURR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)+]
- 19:15/q01/p3 It is a greeting commanded for the community to give Yahya. — Jishumi lists this alongside divine peace, angelic greetings, and safety as an interpretation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:15/q02 Who gives Yahya the greeting of peace?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · referent]
turns on: The implied giver of an unassigned greeting; Razi says an angelic greeting still has divine authority.
- 19:15/q02/p1 God gives it. — Al-Halabi, Ata, Ibn Abbas and Dur al-Manthur say it is God's greeting; Abu Hayyan favors God because the preceding context says God gave Yahya wisdom. Ibn Ashur likewise attaches it to God's gifts; al-Islahi says the angels convey God's favor. [13 notes: THALABI(الحلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي عن ابن عباس,عطاء عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI; TABRISI(عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,عطاء); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); DURR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 19:15/q02/p2 Angels give it by God's command. — Jishumi lists angelic greetings; al-Islahi says angels greeted John with peace and blessings despite people's treatment of him. Razi says angelic greeting has the same honor as God's, since angels act only by His command; Abu Hayyan reports Razi's allowance. [4 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:15/q02/p3 The community is commanded to greet Yahya. — Jishumi records this as an alternative interpretation of salām. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:15/q03 How is the indefinite salām parsed grammatically?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ · grammar]
- 19:15/q03/p1 Salām is a nominative indefinite subject in a prayer. — Al-Nahhas says an indefinite subject is apt in a prayer, and Arabic salām ʿalayka equals salām Allāh ʿalayka. Abu Ishaq says this common noun may begin a sentence because its indefinite use approaches a definite sense. [2 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)]

## 19:15/q04 Why is salām indefinite here?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form conveys scope and grandeur, distinguishes the two speakers, or reflects Yahya's death.
- 19:15/q04/p1 Its indefiniteness conveys great, broad, comprehensive peace. — Tabatabai says the indefinite form magnifies the positive well-being expressed by salām; Samarrai contrasts it with definite al-salām, which he calls specific or restricted. [2 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 19:15/q04/p2 It marks God's greeting of Yahya as different from Jesus's definite self-greeting. — Samarrai links the forms to who speaks. Burhan says Jesus's definite al-salām evokes God's name al-Salām, invoking what that name denotes. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BURHAN]
- 19:15/q04/p3 It reflects the fact that Yahya was killed and his body lacked full bodily peace. — Biqai contrasts the following account of Jesus at 19:33. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 19:15/q05 What effect does the nominal sentence have?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · rhetoric]
- 19:15/q05/p1 It makes the peace stable and enduring. — Bursevi applies its durability through the frightening transitions. Alusi says the sentence continues Yahya's gifts and closes the preceding account. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:15/q06 Why are birth, death, and resurrection singled out?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدَ وَيَوْمَ يَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ يُبْعَثُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain the selection by human vulnerability, the scope of the three life phases, or gift and reward.
- 19:15/q06/p1 They are the loneliest and most frightening human transitions, so peace there is special honor. — Ibn Uyayna says birth leaves the former place, death brings unfamiliar people, and resurrection an unseen gathering (Baghawi); Ibn Abi Uyayna likewise notes unfamiliar states (Bursevi). Ibn Naftawayh calls them first views of this world, the next, and Paradise and Hell. Biqai reasons that safety at the hardest moments implies safety elsewhere. Al-Rida compares Jesus's proclamation at the same stages. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT(سفيان بن عيينة); BAGHAWI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عيينة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سفيان بن عيينة); RAZI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة,عبد الله بن نفطوية); TABRISI(سفيان بن عيينة); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عيينة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن أبي عيينة); TABATABAI(علي موسى الرضا)]
- 19:15/q06/p2 They encompass the successive worlds or phases of Yahya's existence. — Ibn Ashur identifies entry into worldly life, departure, and entry into the next; Tabatabai specifies earthly safety, bliss in the intermediate realm, and true life without fatigue. Jishumi says the three-stage blessing implies blessedness throughout life. [3 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:15/q06/p3 Peace at birth is a gift; peace at death and resurrection may be reward. — Razi says Yahya had earned nothing at birth; Jishumi reports the same distinction, treating the later peace as reward. [2 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL]

## 19:15/q07 What protection or benefit does peace give at each stage?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدَ وَيَوْمَ يَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ يُبْعَثُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the benefits are specified as escape from Satan and eschatological terrors, lifelong religious integrity, positive well-being, or martyr status. Jishumi rejects a literal Satanic blow because Satan can only whisper.
- 19:15/q07/p1 At birth it guards against Satan, at death against grave trials, and at resurrection against terror or punishment. — Tabari says Satan harms other children; Baydawi and Nasafi similarly specify Satan, grave punishment, and Fire or the Great Terror. Bursevi adds death's terror and Hell. Study Quran transmits protection from Satan, exemption from grave trials, and avoidance of resurrection dread or punishment. Jishumi records these as one set of readings. Al-Tabari's continuation reported by Razi names grave and resurrection punishments. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ,Ṭ, R); JISHUMI-]
- 19:15/q07/p2 It protects Yahya from disobedience or doctrinal error in life and trials in the next life. — Ibn Tahir extends protection through death and contrasts Jesus's self-praise; al-Wasiti infers lifelong obedience from safety at life's two ends. Qushayri names deviation, doctrinal error and afterlife trial; Biqai names religious harm at birth and every fear afterward. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:15/q07/p3 It gives fitting safety or bliss in each of three distinct worlds. — Tabatabai explains the stages as earthly safety, intermediate bliss, and unwearied true life; his lexical explanation of salām is a positively fitting, unfeared state. Al-Islahi describes God's favor through all phases despite others' treatment. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:15/q07/p4 Yahya will be raised among the martyrs. — Jishumi lists this among readings of the final stage; other notes connect the alive wording with martyrdom. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:15/q08 Can Satan physically strike Yahya at birth?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدَ · theology]
turns on: Whether Satan's action can include a physical strike rather than only whispering.
- 19:15/q08/p1 Yes, birth protection is described as exemption from Satan's customary harm or sting. — Tabari says other human children suffer Satan's harm; Bursevi calls it a sting. Baydawi and Nasafi also name Satanic harm at birth. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; JISHUMI-]
- 19:15/q08/p2 No; Satan can only whisper, as scripture says. — Jishumi rejects the claim that Satan struck Yahya at birth on this scriptural ground. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:15/q09 What does yawm mean in these phrases?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدَ وَيَوْمَ يَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ يُبْعَثُ · meaning]
- 19:15/q09/p1 It means the time or occasion of an event, including night. — Muqatil points to 9:36 and 19:33 for yawm as 'when'; Damghani gives birth, death, resurrection, travel, residence and harvest as occasions. Ibn al-Anbari and Ibn al-Jawzi say nights are included since birth or death may occur then; Ibn al-Jawzi compares 5:3. Wahidi extends peace to the following periods; Ibn Ashur calls each a time of transition. Al-Suddi specifies resurrection as the Day of Resurrection. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:15/q10 Why does the verse say 'he dies' in the present tense?  [وَيَوْمَ يَمُوتُ · rhetoric]
- 19:15/q10/p1 The present tense vividly evokes Yahya's future death. — Ibn Ashur says the Quran only summarizes his killing. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:15/q11 Can a murdered Yahya be described as dying?  [وَيَوْمَ يَمُوتُ · interpretation]
- 19:15/q11/p1 Yes; the death wording includes a murdered person. — Alusi relates it to a report that an Israelite prostitute killed John. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:15/q12 Why add 'alive' after 'he is raised'?  [وَيَوْمَ يُبْعَثُ حَيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥayyan reinforces resurrection, specifies bodily life, or evokes the life of martyrs.
- 19:15/q12/p1 It emphasizes that Yahya really will be raised alive. — Jishumi and Tabrisi explicitly call it emphasis; Alusi lists emphasis as one possibility. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:15/q12/p2 It indicates that resurrection is bodily rather than merely spiritual. — Alusi records this as another possible force of the word. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:15/q12/p3 It alludes to Yahya's martyrdom or being raised among martyrs who are alive. — Tabrisi, Razi via Ibn Naftawayh, Abu Hayyan via Razi, and Tabatabai compare the living martyrs of 3:169; Alusi also lists the martyr reading. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(عبد الله بن نفطوية); TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)]

## 19:15/q13 What did Jesus and Yahya reportedly say when they met?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · reports]
- 19:15/q13/p1 They asked one another for prayer or forgiveness; Jesus cited God's greeting of Yahya over Jesus's self-greeting. — Al-Hasan versions vary: some say each named the other better; Ibn al-Jawzi's Saʿid ibn Jubayr variant says God praised Yahya while Jesus praised himself. Qurtubi and others call them maternal cousins. Yahya-Sallam says Jesus recognized the honor of Yahya's divine greeting. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن); THALABI(الحسن عن قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن البصري,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 19:15/q14 Does the comparison of Yahya's and Jesus's greetings establish either one's higher rank?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · theology]
turns on: Whether a divine greeting outranks a commanded self-greeting, or whether Jesus's self-greeting itself signals greater authority.
- 19:15/q14/p1 It favors Yahya, because God greeted or praised him while Jesus greeted or praised himself. — Al-Hasan's encounter report puts this inference in Jesus's mouth; its variants substitute blessing or praise for greeting. Yahya-Sallam notes Jesus recognized the distinction. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن); THALABI(الحسن عن قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن البصري,سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); JISHUMI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:15/q14/p2 It favors Jesus, whose confident self-greeting implies a higher rank. — Ibn Atiyya reports his father's account of some scholars' inference; Qurtubi transmits it and says Ibn Atiyya allowed each side a case. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء)]
- 19:15/q14/p3 The greetings cannot decide their relative rank. — Jishumi says God's own greeting and a commanded greeting do not prove different rank; he calls the mutual-superiority report unsound unless it reflects mistaken self-estimation. Razi says infallible Jesus spoke at God's command, so his self-greeting equals God's greeting of Yahya. [2 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL]

## 19:15/q15 Was Jesus a messenger specifically to Yahya?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · theology]
turns on: Whether confirming another prophet entails being that prophet's addressee.
- 19:15/q15/p1 Yes; Yahya confirmed Jesus, and the two lived together while Yahya was three years older. — Al-Asamm cites Yahya's confirmation of a word from God. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم); JISHUMI-]
- 19:15/q15/p2 That does not follow from Yahya's confirmation of Jesus. — Jishumi compares Muhammad's confirmation of earlier prophets, which does not make those prophets messengers to us; both Jesus and Yahya were prophets, and God knows their exact relation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:15/q16 How does this greeting relate to the later greeting spoken by Jesus?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · links]
turns on: The later passage repeats the three times but changes speaker and wording; sources assign different significance to that change.
- 19:15/q16/p1 They echo the same three stages, while Jesus declares peace upon himself. — Yahya-Sallam links 19:15 to 19:30–31 and 19:33, where Jesus calls himself God's servant and prophet, blessed wherever he is. Al-Rida compares the greetings at the frightening stages. Ibn Ashur compares Jesus's later greeting with God's greeting of Yahya. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(علي موسى الرضا)]
- 19:15/q16/p2 The change in birth, death, and resurrection wording places Jesus's peace during his lifetime. — Tabatabai reads the altered wording at 19:33 in this way. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 19:15/q16/p3 Jesus's form also carries his own praise and the invocation of God's name al-Salām. — Ibn Tahir says Jesus's greeting praises himself, sweet in knowledge and subtle in kindness; Burhan explains the definite al-salām as invoking the divine name and its meaning. Samarrai contrasts Jesus's definite self-speech with God's indefinite greeting of Yahya. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); BURHAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:15/q17 What special honor does this greeting add to Yahya's portrayal?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · interpretation]
- 19:15/q17/p1 It marks Yahya as uniquely blessed and honored across his life. — Jishumi says the blessed state runs from birth to death; Razi says other prophets, including Noah and Abraham, lack an explicit birth-day salutation. Tabatabai lists his unique name, childhood wisdom, leadership, chastity, prophethood, righteousness, election and guidance (3:39, 3:45, 21:90), while noting Jesus shared some traits but was born later. Kuranyolu counts wisdom, compassion, purity, piety, respect for parents, freedom from tyranny, and obedience. Ibn Ashur says the address lets Muslims recognize Yahya's honor. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 19:15/q18 How does the peace sentence complete the preceding description of Yahya?  [* · links]
- 19:15/q18/p1 It crowns his obedience, humility, and divine gifts. — Tabari describes Yahya as dutiful to parents, quick in obedience, humble, submissive, and never disobedient to God or parents. Alusi says the nominal sentence continues the gifts and closes the account. Ibn Ashur links the greeting to God's grant of wisdom in 19:12. Tabatabai and Kuranyolu list the virtues that the greeting completes. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 19:15/q19 What do reports say about Yahya's freedom from sin?  [وَسَلَٰمٌ عَلَيْهِ · reports]
turns on: Whether broad reports of Yahya's sole exemption are read literally or limited in light of other infallible figures.
- 19:15/q19/p1 Yahya alone among Adam's children reached judgment without sin or even intent to sin. — A Prophetic report in Tabari and Durr says every other person reaches Judgment with a sin. Durr also reports that others erred or intended sin while Yahya did neither. Biqai's Abu Hurayra version says Yahya alone might not face punishment, calling him noble, chaste, prophetic and righteous; the Prophet indicated an extremely small remembrance. Haythami says Hajjaj ibn Sulayman in that chain was trusted by Ibn Hibban and others but weakened by Abu Zurʿa and others; the remaining transmitters are trustworthy. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي); BIQAI-FULL(أبو هريرة,الهيثمي); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 19:15/q19/p2 Broad sinlessness wording must be restricted to infallible prophets and Imams. — Tabatabai attributes the apparent excess of Sunni-route reports to narrators' paraphrase. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 19:15/q20 What is reported about Yahya and Jesus before their births?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدَ · reports]
- 19:15/q20/p1 They were maternal cousins conceived together, and Yahya's mother saw her fetus bow to Mary's. — Malik says the bow honored Jesus, who later raised the dead and healed; he contrasts Yahya's ascetic fear of God. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(مالك)]

## 19:15/q21 What is reported about Yahya's ascetic life and martyrdom?  [* · reports]
- 19:15/q21/p1 He lived austerely from fear of sin and was a martyr. — Malik says Yahya ate plants and wept until tears wore a track in his face, contrasting Jesus's healing and raising of the dead. A Prophetic report calls him martyr son of martyr, dressed in wool and eating plants. Tabatabai's reports add grass, palm fiber, and tears marking his face. Alusi cites a report that an Israelite prostitute killed him. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,مالك); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:15/q21/p2 The sky wept only for Yahya and Husayn, and their killers were illegitimate sons. — Qurra gives the sky report; a second report from al-Sadiq adds the killers' parentage. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قرة)]
- 19:15/q21/p3 God said seventy thousand were killed on Yahya's account and 140,000 through the Prophet's grandson. — Tabatabai transmits this as an Ibn Abbas report addressed to Muhammad. [1 notes: TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]

## 19:15/q22 What inward reading is given to Yahya's three stages?  [يَوْمَ وُلِدَ وَيَوْمَ يَمُوتُ وَيَوْمَ يُبْعَثُ · inward]
- 19:15/q22/p1 Birth is emergence from nature, death is annihilation of natural demands in God, and resurrection is subsistence after annihilation. — Bursevi reports this from al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya and begins a related allegory: Zakariyya is the human spirit, his wife the body, and Yahya the heart open to divine effusion despite the spirit's doubt after long bodily attachment. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 19:15/q23 Where was this surah revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 19:15/q23/p1 It is Meccan, though Muqatil excepts the prostration verse as Medinan. — Abu Hayyan says Muqatil dates that verse after the migration to Abyssinia. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:15/q24 How does the surah connect its prophetic stories and the preceding surah?  [* · links]
- 19:15/q24/p1 Both surahs recount astonishing acts of God. — Abu Hayyan contrasts the Cave, Moses and Khidr, and Dhul-Qarnayn (18:9, 18:60, 18:83) with Yahya's birth to an old man and barren woman and Jesus's birth without a father. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 19:15/q24/p2 The surah pairs divinely gifted children or relatives with prophets' withdrawal and separation. — Study Quran points to the accounts of Zachariah, Mary, Abraham and Moses. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 19:15/q24/p3 Zakariyya's story introduces Mary's and undercuts treating Jesus's miraculous birth as proof of divinity. — Islahi reads the older parents' miraculous child as preparation for the subsequent account. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:15/q25 What larger argument does the surah make through these stories?  [* · links]
- 19:15/q25/p1 It recalls prophetic monotheism and warns that later followers abandoned prayer, zakāh, and truth. — Islahi says prophets in Adam's, Noah's and Abraham's lines preached monotheism, prayer and zakāh, while followers embraced polytheism, innovations and desires. He says worldly success gives the adversaries only respite until the truth is conveyed and their excuse ends. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:15/q25/p2 It urges Muhammad to endure hostility with glad tidings of success. — Islahi connects the earlier stories to the Prophet's present opposition. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:15/q25/p3 It exposes People of the Book who secretly backed Quraysh and rejects divinizing Jesus. — Islahi says Mary's and Jesus's prominence addresses Jewish and Christian support for Quraysh; Zakariyya's story counters Christian claims from Jesus's miraculous birth. Biqai says the stages of child and parent show both depend on need, unlike independent God; he cites 3:39. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
