# 44:56: 20 questions

## 44:56/q01 What does ‘therein’ refer to?  [فِيهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the location is strictly the gardens or the broader afterlife, and whether the broader reading strains the construction.
- 44:56/q01/p1 The gardens of Paradise. — Bursevî and al-Ālūsī identify the antecedent as the gardens; al-Ālūsī's disconnected analysis consequently locates the first death outside them. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 44:56/q01/p2 The afterlife, whose first state is death. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows this as a connected exception. Al-Ālūsī reports it but objects that it fragments the construction and needs figurative usage. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)-]

## 44:56/q02 How is the clause ‘they do not taste’ attached grammatically?  [لَا يَذُوقُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier pronoun or clause supplies the circumstance, or whether this sentence stands independently.
- 44:56/q02/p1 It is a circumstantial clause from the pronoun in ‘secure’ (āminīn). — Al-Samīn gives this antecedent for the circumstantial clause. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 44:56/q02/p2 It is a second or third circumstantial clause from the object of ‘We paired them’ (zawwajnāhum). — Al-Samīn gives this alternative and also records ways of attaching the preceding āminīn to yadʿūn, an earlier circumstantial clause, or an independent clause. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 44:56/q02/p3 The clause may be independent. — Al-Ālūsī leaves both independence and circumstantial attachment open. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 44:56/q02/p4 The clause may be circumstantial, with its precise attachment left open. — Al-Ālūsī mentions the circumstantial possibility without choosing an antecedent; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an added circumstantial clause promising lasting bliss. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:56/q03 How is ‘they taste’ read?  [يَذُوقُونَ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted verbal form and whether an explicit object ‘taste’ is added.
- 44:56/q03/p1 The passive form, ‘they are made to taste.’ — Al-Samīn attributes lā yudhāqūn to ʿAmr b. ʿUbayd; al-Ālūsī attributes the passive reading to ʿUbayd b. ʿUmayr. The reported transmitters differ. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(عمرو بن عبيد); ALUSI-FULL(عبيد ابن عمير)]
- 44:56/q03/p2 A reading adds ‘the taste of death.’ — Al-Ālūsī attributes this expanded wording to ʿAbd Allāh. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 44:56/q04 What does tasting death mean here?  [يَذُوقُونَ ٱلْمَوْتَ · meaning]
- 44:56/q04/p1 It figuratively describes undergoing death and its painful causes, as though tasting unpleasant food. — Scholars cited by al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī say death is an accident and cannot literally be tasted; al-Ṭabrisī compares it to disliked food. Al-Rāzī says ‘taste’ can mean experience, as with knowledge or remembered death. Bursevî distinguishes mawt, death as a genus, from mawta, one occurrence: denial of even one taste excludes all death. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 44:56/q05 What life does the denial of death promise the people of Paradise?  [لَا يَذُوقُونَ فِيهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ · interpretation]
- 44:56/q05/p1 They live in Paradise permanently and never die there. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevî, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Asad and the Study Quran state the assurance. Al-Rāzī says it follows the enumeration of pleasures; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say it secures enjoyment from death's interruption, though people in Hell also do not simply die. Bursevî lists no aging, sleep, fear, day or night, darkness, heat, cold or exit; Iṣlāḥī adds unwithheld fruit and safety from fear. Al-Biqāʿī says even death's onset is absent and complete bliss follows resurrection. Al-Samīn's Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains that only worldly death occurred; Ibn Kathīr calls the exception a reinforcement of this negation. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Z)]

## 44:56/q06 How does the exception of the first death work?  [إِلَّا ٱلْمَوْتَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an exception from a future denial can name a past worldly death; the attested sense of illā; whether the exception must be connected; and whether the dying are already in Paradise by anticipation.
- 44:56/q06/p1 It is disconnected or means ‘but’: they already tasted worldly death, with none in Paradise. — Al-Naḥḥās says the accusative exception is outside the preceding category. Al-Qurṭubī cites Sībawayh's poetry for disconnected exception; al-Samīn, Ibn Kathīr, al-Zarkashī, Bursevî and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī give this analysis. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī phrase it as ‘but they already tasted it.’ Al-Dāmghānī calls it a resumptive exception and cites 92:19, 88:23, 95:6, 72:28 and 34:23. The objectors cited by Ibn Qutayba compare excluding yesterday's gift from today's giving; al-Ālūsī says the first death happened before Paradise, and prefers connection unless evidence requires disconnection. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that this does not explain the word ‘first.’ [16 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض); DAMGHANI; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى); TABATABAI; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الطاعنون)-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 44:56/q06/p2 Illā means ‘other than’ or ‘apart from’: no death beyond the one already undergone. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn's Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ālūsī and Asad invoke the exception at 4:22; Ibn al-Jawzī also mentions 11:107, and Asad compares 37:58–59. Al-Wāḥidī transmits al-Farrāʾ's account of an exception adding rather than subtracting. Al-Ṭabrisī prefers an adjectival or substitute construction meaning ghayr, since ghayr is an adverb but illā a particle and the first death has passed. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers apposition for the past death and denial of barzakh or Garden death, but says this analysis alone does not explain ‘first.’ Al-Ṭabarī objects that ‘other than’ would imply a further death; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿAṭiyya reject his objection as the meaning is coherent. Al-Ālūsī says excluding the past first death from future deaths removes the paradox. [21 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية والطبري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الطبري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,ابن عطية); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; TAB-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 44:56/q06/p3 Illā has the sense ‘after’: they taste no death after the first. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this from a closeness of meanings and says Arabic may substitute illā for baʿda; he uses 4:22 and analogies of rajāʾ meaning fear in Abū Dhuʾayb and zannū meaning knowledge in another poet. Al-Thaʿlabī compares ‘no food today except what I ate yesterday’; al-Baghawī allows ‘after’ as well as ‘apart from.’ Al-Samīn reports the majority reject this because the proposed sense of illā is unattested; al-Ālūsī says most commentators do not recognize it. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو ذؤيب,الشاعر); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية والطبري); SAMIN-DURR(الطبري); ALUSI-FULL(الطبري); TAB-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)-]
- 44:56/q06/p4 The exception is attached to an impossible future repetition of a past death, emphatically denying any death. — Al-Zamakhsharī's explanation is quoted by al-Rāzī, al-Samīn and al-Zarkashī; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevî, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr also use the impossible condition. Al-Samīn calls it negation by an impossible proof and cites al-Nābigha's sword-notches as an impossible flaw. Al-Zarkashī compares the camel passing through a needle's eye, which has no actual endpoint; he notes a poet's still stronger emaciation hyperbole but says that gives no license to match the Qurʾān. Al-Ālūsī compares the offer of water ‘only embers’ and 4:22; Ibn ʿĀshūr recalls 44:35. Al-Ālūsī prefers the interpretation; al-Biqāʿī says even death's first onset is denied. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,النابغة); BURHAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 44:56/q06/p5 The exception is connected because a believer already approaches or sees Paradise at worldly death. — Ibn Qutayba says the blessed encounter comfort in differing degrees, including spirit and fragrance, an opened gate and martyrs' spirits in green birds; a quoted poet uses taʿallaqa for eating, and Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib is depicted flying with two wings among angels. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī likewise say the dying see their dwellings, angels, provision or blessings and so are associated with Paradise. Al-Samīn speaks of vision of its promise or certainty of its bliss; al-Zarkashī treats this as one of three explanations, with ‘tasting’ figurative. Al-Biqāʿī extends its causes into life and posthumous enjoyment. Al-Ālūsī reports it as a connected reading. Ibn Qutayba's Kitāb Gharīb directs the reader to his Mushkil treatment. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(القتبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 44:56/q06/p6 The exception is connected because death is the first state of the afterlife. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives this option. Al-Ālūsī reports it but argues that it fragments the sentence and calls for figurative language. Al-Ālūsī also notes the objection that a past worldly death cannot be tasted later in Paradise unless a negative exception affirms the excluded item there; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī raises both the apparent implication of dying in Paradise and the past-versus-future problem. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير مسمى)-; TABATABAI-]

## 44:56/q07 Which death is ‘the first death’?  [ٱلْمَوْتَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether ‘first’ identifies the one worldly death or includes a second transition associated with barzakh; the inward reading gives death a different referent.
- 44:56/q07/p1 The death at the end of worldly life. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bursevî, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad, Iṣlāḥī and the Study Quran locate it in this world; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī explain ‘first’ as prior to abiding life, citing 44:35. Kur'an Yolu prefers this because the passage contrasts mortal worldly life with eternity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the worldly death is past, although he also posits a second transition from barzakh. Al-Elmâlı says no later death occurs in barzakh or the Hereafter, despite the disbelievers' words at 40:11. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Z); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 44:56/q07/p2 ‘First deaths’ can encompass the two deaths already past, in the world and in the intermediate realm. — Kur'an Yolu offers this possibility in light of 40:11 but prefers the single worldly death; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī separately says there are two transitions, into barzakh and from barzakh to the afterlife, which raises the question why the second is omitted. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 44:56/q07/p3 In an inward reading, the first death is bodily nature. — Al-Qāshānī adds that voluntary annihilation of acts, attributes and Essence is not thereby excluded: each such death is a purer and more delightful life in its own garden. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 44:56/q08 Does this verse rule out repeated worldly lives or death after the first?  [ٱلْمَوْتَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · theology]
turns on: How 40:11's two deaths relate to the single ‘first death’ named here and whether a second transition belongs to the same sequence.
- 44:56/q08/p1 People die once at the end of worldly life and do not repeatedly die and return; the verse rejects reincarnation. — Kur'an Yolu says this follows whichever reading of prior deaths is chosen and cites 2:28. Al-Elmâlı compares the disbelievers' two deaths at 40:11 and denies further death for the God-fearing even in barzakh. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 44:56/q08/p2 There are two deaths between worldly life and the Hour, through entry to and departure from barzakh. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 40:11 and asks why the verse mentions only the first; his preferred grammatical account excludes the prior worldly death while denying death in barzakh and the Garden. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 44:56/q09 How can a believer be connected to Paradise before resurrection?  [فِيهَا ٱلْمَوْتَةَ ٱلْأُولَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ‘Paradise’ before resurrection names promised sights and early provision or spiritual enjoyment already present in life.
- 44:56/q09/p1 At death or afterward, believers see or receive signs of their future Paradise. — Ibn Qutayba gives differing degrees: spirit and fragrance, an opened gate, martyrs in green birds, and Jaʿfar flying with angels; the poet he cites uses taʿallaqa for eating. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī mention their dwellings, mercy's angels, provision or Paradise's comforts. Al-Biqāʿī cites sound reports of posthumous seeing and martyrs' food, an unnamed fighter at Uḥud who smelled Paradise beyond the mountain before dying, and says complete bliss comes after resurrection. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(القتبي); BIQAI-FULL(غير مسمى في المقتطف)]
- 44:56/q09/p2 Believers can already enjoy a figurative Paradise through faith, virtue, and God's care. — Al-Rāzī says the soul's knowledge, obedience and love of God are true Paradise even in worldly life; he cites a saying attributed to the Prophet that prophets move between abodes rather than die. Al-Biqāʿī describes present Paradise through causes and decree, quotes reports of the fruits of Paradise for a believer visiting a brother and the gardens of Paradise in which passersby may graze, and cites Ibn Burjān's ‘small paradise’ of God's care, nearness, regard, remembrance and companionship in worship. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي عليه السلام); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 44:56/q10 What do reports say about the end of death and the life of Paradise?  [لَا يَذُوقُونَ فِيهَا ٱلْمَوْتَ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different promised features and events; no source presents them as competing accounts.
- 44:56/q10/p1 Death is brought as a speckled ram and slaughtered between Paradise and Hell; both groups are told there is no more death. — Ibn Kathīr says this report occurs in both Ṣaḥīḥ collections. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 44:56/q10/p2 Paradise's people retain health, life, ease and youth without illness, death, hardship or aging. — Ibn Kathīr attributes this prophetic report to Abū Saʿīd and Abū Hurayra and says Muslim transmits it. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 44:56/q10/p3 Whoever fears God enters Paradise and lives without death, worn clothing or fading youth. — Ibn Kathīr reports this from Abū Hurayra from the Prophet. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 44:56/q10/p4 Paradise's people do not sleep, because sleep is death's sibling. — Ibn Kathīr gives Jābir's prophetic report; for one route al-Bazzār says only al-Thawrī narrated from Ibn al-Munkadir and only al-Firyābī from al-Thawrī, while Ibn Kathīr notes another route. Ibn Kathīr says Iraqis and Abū Isḥāq call one transmitter Abū Muslim al-Agharr, while Medinans call him Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Agharr. Al-Ālūsī calls the Jābir report sound in chain. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جابر بن عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله)]
- 44:56/q10/p5 An unattributed saying lists ten things absent from Paradise: aging, sleep, death, fear, night, day, darkness, heat, cold and departure. — Bursevî transmits the saying and gives these features in his description of eternal Paradise. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 44:56/q11 What does ‘He protected them from the punishment of Hell’ mean?  [وَوَقَىٰهُمْ عَذَابَ ٱلْجَحِيمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the Fire is read as afterlife punishment or as an inward image of deprivation from God.
- 44:56/q11/p1 God guards the blessed from Hell's punishment and harm. — Muqātil, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevî, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad state the protection; Bursevî and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī define wiqāya as warding off harm. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī call it divine favor. Ibn Kathīr says the blessed gain what they seek and escape what they fear; al-Biqāʿī calls it recompense for piety. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it cause for gratitude and urges thanks when seeing hardship. Iṣlāḥī calls freedom from death and Hell the supreme triumph. The Study Quran relates it to mercy preceding wrath, cites 52:18, and quotes the Prophet's comparison: God is more tender to His servants than a mother protecting her child from fire. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(The Prophet)]
- 44:56/q11/p2 In an inward reading, the Fire signifies deprivation through distance from God and abandonment. — Bursevî gives this allusion. Al-Qāshānī says God protects them from the hell of deprivation caused by any residue, and still more from the hell of bodily nature. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 44:56/q12 How is ‘and He protected them’ read?  [وَوَقَىٰهُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the qāf is doubled for emphasis.
- 44:56/q12/p1 The qāf is undoubled: wa-waqāhum. — Al-Samīn attributes this reading to the majority. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 44:56/q12/p2 The qāf is doubled: wa-waqqāhum, intensifying protection. — Al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī report the form; Abū Ḥayya or Abū Ḥaywah is named by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say doubling strengthens the sense rather than making it transitive, since the verb already takes two objects. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيوة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة)]

## 44:56/q13 Whom does the pronoun in ‘He protected them’ resume?  [وَوَقَىٰهُمْ · grammar]
- 44:56/q13/p1 It resumes the people in ‘We paired them’ through a shift from God's first-person speech to third person. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the antecedent at 44:54 and the change of grammatical person. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:56/q14 Why is protection from Hell mentioned after Paradise's blessings?  [وَوَقَىٰهُمْ عَذَابَ ٱلْجَحِيمِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the sequence is understood as reversing the temporal order of salvation or as naming two kinds of hardship.
- 44:56/q14/p1 Though logically prior to attaining Paradise, the wording mentions protection afterward while indicating God protected them from the outset. — Al-Rāzī explicitly raises and answers the ordering problem. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 44:56/q14/p2 The clauses complete a twofold removal of hardship: death changes abode or state, while Hell changes joy to misery. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains the sequence through the two hardships excluded. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 44:56/q15 Does protection from Hell imply that sinful believers can never leave it?  [وَوَقَىٰهُمْ عَذَابَ ٱلْجَحِيمِ · theology]
turns on: Whether ‘protected’ rules out any prior punishment for everyone ultimately saved, or can describe pardon, final rescue, or protection from a particular torment.
- 44:56/q15/p1 Yes: those counted among the saved are protected from the Fire, so a sinful believer cannot leave Hell. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this Muʿtazilī inference from the verse; he rejects it. [1 notes: TABRISI(المعتزلة); TABRISI-]
- 44:56/q15/p2 No: the protection can concern those never deserving entry, those pardoned, everlasting protection, or immunity from unbelievers' kind of torment. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives these four ways to limit the inference. Al-Biqāʿī says some sinners enter Hell proportionate to sins, become charred, leave after intercession and revive in the water of life; permanent denizens neither die nor live. He cites Abū Saʿīd through al-Dārimī, Anas through Abū Yaʿlā, Abū Saʿīd's report of believers crossing the Bridge and rescuing brothers, and accounts of regrowth in the river at Paradise's gate. One Jābir report is graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ by al-Tirmidhī. Al-Ghāfir al-Fārisī explains ḍabāʾir as groups; al-Biqāʿī explains thaʿāyir as an asparagus-like plant. [10 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,الغافر الفارسي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 44:56/q16 How are Paradise's rewards and protection a divine favor?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether a deserved or obligatory reward can still be described as God's favor.
- 44:56/q16/p1 The rewards come by God's generosity, not as a debt owed to creatures. — Al-Rāzī's school takes the enumerated rewards this way. Al-Rāzī argues that if reward were obligatory on God, omission would imply folly and denial of divinity, so it could not properly be called favor. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī call protection a favor; Asad and Iṣlāḥī call freedom from Fire the supreme triumph. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 44:56/q16/p2 People may earn most rewards through deeds, yet they are favor because God graciously prescribed the duties leading to them. — Al-Qāḍī compares this to giving someone money to acquire an estate. Al-Rāzī rejects it on the ground that a compulsory reward cannot be a favor. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-]

## 44:56/q17 How does this promise connect with the fate of disbelievers and the passage's argument?  [* · links]
- 44:56/q17/p1 It contrasts the secure, lasting bliss of the reverent with disbelievers' lasting torment and their denial of resurrection. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī say people in Hell also do not die, but undergo deathlike anguish or repeated deaths from torment; the Study Quran cites 14:18. Al-Biqāʿī says the garden wording may suggest sinners taste death outside it. Ibn ʿĀshūr says death ends earthly enjoyment while deathlessness threatens the associators with endless punishment. The Study Quran contrasts the promise with the disbelievers' claim at 44:35 that they taste only one death. Iṣlāḥī says disbelief in afterlife drove Quraysh's heedlessness; the passage supplies intellectual, natural and historical arguments for reward and punishment before describing both fates and God's just differentiation. ## 44:56/q20 How may the following word faḍlan be parsed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether the following word is read or construed accusatively as manner or nominatively as a new predication.
- 44:56/q20/p1 Accusative faḍlan: God did this graciously. — Al-Farrāʾ gives this explanation of the following word. [1 notes: FARRA]
- 44:56/q20/p2 Nominative faḍlun: ‘That is a favor from your Lord.’ — Al-Farrāʾ also allows this construction. [1 notes: FARRA]
