# 8:24: 25 questions

## 8:24/q01 Who is addressed as believers?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
- 8:24/q01/p1 Those who already profess and affirm faith. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi say this is undisputed; Biqa'i distinguishes profession by the tongue, and Qashani describes belief in the unseen. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI]

## 8:24/q02 Why repeat the address by faith?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 8:24/q02/p1 It stirs professing believers to make their faith real through wholehearted obedience. — Al-Alusi says naming faith reminds them what impels obedience; Biqa'i says they must respond eagerly. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 8:24/q03 What does responding to God and the Messenger mean?  [ٱسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the derived form adds a request for response or is synonymous with ajāba.
- 8:24/q03/p1 Answer, obey, and comply with God's and the Messenger's summons. — Majaz cites Ka'b ibn Sa'd's poem and both direct and lām constructions; Zajjaj and Jishumi cite a poet's lam yastajibhu; Ibn Atiyya notes istajāba normally takes lām while ajāba usually does not. Ibn Abbas glosses it as obedience, and Biqa'i applies this to every call. [29 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة والزجاج,ابن عباس,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة والزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيد والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(البخاري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 8:24/q03/p2 It can mean seeking an answer. — Jishumi reports this alternate morphology, while the cited poetic use favors simply answering. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 8:24/q04 How broad is the command to respond?  [ٱسْتَجِيبُوا۟ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the condition narrows the calls covered, and how the prayer incident bears on the command.
- 8:24/q04/p1 Every valid call of the Messenger calls for obedience, including an immediate summons. — Mawardi links response wording to the summons. Ibn Jawzi transmits Ibn Abbas's 'everything'; Razi argues the imperative and subsequent threat make it obligatory, with Ubayy's rebuke as practical report evidence. Biqa'i says the condition does not restrict the command; Ibn Ashur treats 'what gives life' as explanatory and cites the prayer incident. Kuran Yolu extends following the Prophet after his death to Quran and Sunna. [23 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(أكثر الفقهاء)+]
- 8:24/q04/p2 The verse primarily commands obedience to guidance, while the Prophet also applied it to a literal call. — Tabari says Ubay's prayer incident does not confine the verse to prayer, and Ibn Ashur cites 3:172 and 3:104 for broader guidance. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 8:24/q05 Who was praying when the Prophet called and cited this verse?  [ٱسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ · reports]
turns on: Whether the narrated worshipper is Ubayy, Abu Saʿid, or Hudhayfa, and whether these are separate similar incidents.
- 8:24/q05/p1 Ubayy ibn Kaʿb delayed his answer during prayer; the Prophet cited the verse. — Abu Hurayra gives versions in which Ubayy turns or hastens but waits to finish, then promises to answer promptly even in prayer. In continuations the Prophet teaches him al-Fatiha as the unparalleled seven oft-repeated verses; Biqa'i notes Tirmidhi's hasan sahih grading. Ibn Ashur says Ibn Atiyya's Malik version is the same report as Tirmidhi's. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); THALABI(أبو هريرة,رسول الله); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو هريرة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عطية)]
- 8:24/q05/p2 Abu Saʿid ibn al-Muʿalla finished his prayer before answering; the Prophet cited the verse. — Bukhari's version adds that the Prophet taught him al-Fatiha as the greatest sura and seven oft-repeated. Ibn Atiyya cites Bukhari and Muslim as naming him rather than Ubayy; Ibn Ashur and others preserve both similar incidents. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو سعيد بن المعلى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(البخاري ومسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد بن المعلى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد بن المعلى); BIQAI-FULL(أبو سعيد ابن المعلى); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو سعيد بن المعلى); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Saîd el-Muallâ)]
- 8:24/q05/p3 A similar incident is reported with Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman at the Battle of the Trench. — Ibn Atiyya reports it; Ibn Ashur says multiple cases underline the special force of the Messenger's call. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 8:24/q06 What does the prayer incident imply for interrupting prayer?  [ٱسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the call is a Prophetic privilege, a necessary interruption, or an act within prayer.
- 8:24/q06/p1 An urgent summons permits interrupting prayer, including comparable danger to others. — Kashshaf, Baydawi and Alusi give an urgent-matter reading; Alusi says Ubayy's report alone does not establish that prayer remained intact. Awza'i's child-at-the-well case exemplifies necessity. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الأوزاعي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Evzâî,İmam Şâfiî ve Evzâî)]
- 8:24/q06/p2 Answering the Prophet is required and need not invalidate prayer. — Shafi'i says a required utterance or act in prayer does not invalidate it because answering him is itself obedience. Baydawi says the apparent hadith favors this. Qurtubi and Kuran Yolu extend the logic to Awza'i's example of warning a child near a well. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأوزاعي,الشافعي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Evzâî,İmam Şâfiî ve Evzâî); BAYDAWI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 8:24/q06/p3 This duty to answer during prayer is specific to the Prophet. — Kashshaf reports the privilege view; Biqa'i insists the Prophet's distinct authority and discusses when an act is exclusive, citing 33:50. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]
- 8:24/q06/p4 Answering is not obligatory and does invalidate prayer. — Al-Ruyani's transmitted view, reported by Alusi. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الروياني)]

## 8:24/q07 What wider model of prophetic obedience is drawn from the command?  [ٱسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ · law]
turns on: How the immediate summons extends to prophetic conduct, precedent, and later followers.
- 8:24/q07/p1 The Prophet's calls and acts should be followed as sources of rulings and divine love. — Biqa'i says his protected conveyance makes his acts indicators of rulings; Abu Bakr and Ibn Umar imitated even mount turns, dye and sandals; the Yemen consecration, harvest command, pollination case and jihad/debt distinction illustrate following him. He cites 3:31 for divine love and 33:50 for explicit exclusivity. [13 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي رضي الله عنه)]
- 8:24/q07/p2 Following him after his death means following the Quran and Sunna in life. — Kuran Yolu distinguishes attending the Prophet in his lifetime from obeying his transmitted guidance later. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 8:24/q08 Why is the caller singular after naming God and the Messenger?  [لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ دَعَاكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 8:24/q08/p1 God's call comes through the Messenger, so the two named authorities issue one call. — Kashshaf says naming both reinforces obedience; Baydawi points to the single caller just mentioned; Nasafi compares the preceding verse; Elmalili says the Messenger's call is not distinct from God's. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 8:24/q09 How does the lām in the life-giving call work?  [لِمَا يُحْيِيكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Which verb governs the prepositional phrase.
- 8:24/q09/p1 The lām means 'to' and connects the response with its goal. — Tha'labi and Jishumi state the equivalence to ilā; Abu Hayyan notes poetry; Qurtubi allows this attachment, and Tabataba'i calls it common usage. [5 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 8:24/q09/p2 The lām attaches to 'calls,' whose construction takes a lām. — Abu Hayyan distinguishes the two prepositions' functions and cites poetry; Qurtubi says limā yuḥyīkum attaches to the response, giving the alternative parse. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 8:24/q10 Why say lām rather than ilā?  [لِمَا يُحْيِيكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 8:24/q10/p1 It conveys the nearness of obedience's benefit. — Biqa'i links the choice to life through faith and knowledge rather than the heedlessness of 8:22. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 8:24/q11 What is the life-giving call?  [لِمَا يُحْيِيكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Which life is meant: faith, revelation, battle, martyrdom, knowledge, worldly welfare, or lasting life; and whether the wording restricts the answer.
- 8:24/q11/p1 Jihad or battle restores the community's strength, protects its life, and leads to reward. — Muqatil and Ibn Ishaq recall honor after humiliation and strength after weakness; Wahidi/Farra say abandonment emboldens enemies; Kashshaf cites 2:179, and Razi adds martyrdom and 29:64. Ibn Qutayba says jihad revives religion. ## 8:24/q12 How does the verse use 'life'?  [يُحْيِيكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'life' denotes spiritual fulfillment, bodily preservation, or everlasting afterlife.
- 8:24/q12/p1 Life is metaphorical for the knowledge, faith, and fulfilled human purpose that animate an otherwise dead heart. — Ibn Ashur distinguishes bodily life, which supports perception and movement, from analogical life that completes purpose; he also notes preservation of literal life in 2:179 and 5:32. Al-Alusi calls the causal naming metaphor or metonymy. Tabataba'i makes true life subtler than bodily awareness. [19 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد والجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Jesus)]
- 8:24/q12/p2 Life is the enduring reality of the Hereafter. — Tabataba'i contrasts 29:64 with worldly play and describes faith's light and the Holy Spirit as degrees of life. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الجرجاني); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); TABATABAI]
- 8:24/q12/p3 Life includes bodily survival, health, and worldly well-being. — Ibn Ashur compares retribution and saving a life; Kuran Yolu emphasizes religious practice, health, social safety, and psychological resilience. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 8:24/q13 What is the inflection of yuḥyīkum?  [يُحْيِيكُمْ · grammar]
- 8:24/q13/p1 The final yāʾ bears an unpronounced nominative ḍamma because it is heavy; assimilation is impermissible. — Nahhas and Qurtubi give the same grammatical account. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 8:24/q14 What does ḥawl mean here?  [يَحُولُ · meaning]
- 8:24/q14/p1 It means to intervene, separate, or change a state. — Raghib, cited by Alusi, gives change and separation; Tabari says interposing between two things is placing a barrier; Tabataba'i defines ḥaylūla as interposition. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 8:24/q15 What does 'heart' denote?  [قَلْبِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'heart' names the cognitive faculty or what it holds.
- 8:24/q15/p1 The inward faculty of cognition, reason, feeling, and intention. — Wahidi and Qurtubi cite 50:37 for heart as intellect; Razi says loss of intellect ends legal responsibility. Tabataba'i says qalb names the organ and, in Quranic use, the soul acting through inner faculties; Kuran Yolu includes consciousness, reason, and feelings. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 8:24/q15/p2 The heart's contents, including hopes and desires. — Ibn al-Anbari lists wishes for survival, victory, conquest; Qadarites gloss the intended content of heart. Asad cites Raghib on the gap between desire and action. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(القائلون بالقدر); ASAD-NOTES(Raghib)]

## 8:24/q16 How does God come between a person and the heart?  [أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَحُولُ بَيْنَ ٱلْمَرْءِ وَقَلْبِهِۦ · interpretation]

Razi argues causes of knowledge and motives cannot regress through human intentions; Tabataba'i grounds sovereignty in God's ownership of every created faculty, citing 13:41, 64:1, 50:16, 40:16 and 82:19. ## 8:24/q25 What cautions about Quranic expression and interpretation accompany readings of this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Different cited cautions concern syntax, meter, figurative language, and the practice of commentary.
- 8:24/q25/p1 Grammatical relations, rather than mere word placement, determine the composed meaning. — Sibawayh's examples distinguish an appositive الرجل with هذا from circumstantial منطلقا and say a pronoun is not modified by an overt noun. Jurjani rejects the silk-weaving analogy and uses Abu Tammam's ink and venom image to show that changing the parse changes the image without moving words. He urges clarity and fidelity to knowledge; he describes eloquence as ranked composition culminating in inimitability. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH; JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 8:24/q25/p2 Interpretation should preserve earlier insight while adding grounded understanding. — Ibn Ashur describes the Quran as protected guidance and lasting miracle, the Companions' clarification, and his aim to explain law and moral insights, judge between schools, and refine earlier work after hesitation about the burden. Bint al-Shati reports Jurjani's warning against unqualified speculation, his loyalty to knowledge, and his case for Quranic composition's eloquence. [10 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(عبد القاهر الجرجاني)]
- 8:24/q25/p3 Incidental rhythm does not make Quranic speech poetry. — Baqillani says poetry requires intention; a chance rajaz line is insufficient, some demand four rhyming lines, and Quranic fragments vary in rhyme. Arabic meter also requires matched length and syllable pattern; if the Quran were familiar poetry, poets could have imitated it. [4 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 8:24/q25/p4 Images and word choices carry their contextual force, rather than being only rhyme fillers. — Jurjani discusses a thick-lipped black camel image, claws gripping prey as a figure for domination, and Hutay'a's neglected guest. He reports the 'hoof for foot' rhyme explanation but argues the hoof may also evoke a guest's rough journey and forceful driving. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(قالوا)]
