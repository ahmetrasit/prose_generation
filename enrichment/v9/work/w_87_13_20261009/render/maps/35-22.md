# 35:22: 19 questions

## 35:22/q01 Who or what are the living and the dead?  [ٱلْأَحْيَآءُ ٱلْأَمْوَٰتُ · referent]
turns on: Whether life and death primarily name faith and disbelief, knowledge and ignorance, broader spiritual states, Sufi realization, or the image's literal sensory contrast.
- 35:22/q01/p1 The living are believers, and the dead are disbelievers, whose hearts and actions lack the life of faith. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tabari, Qatada, and others explain that believers know, see, act, and respond while disbelievers do not benefit from revelation. Yahya, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Ashur, al-Tabatabai, and the Study Quran cite 6:122; al-Wahidi's Ibn Abbas cites 16:21; Ibn Kathir adds 11:24, and the Study Quran adds 39:22. Ibn Abbas in al-Tabari maps the wider images to obedience and disobedience; in al-Nahhas he maps them to belief, error, Paradise, and Fire. Al-Razi and Abu Hayyan say the dead heard clear signs without benefit; Ibn Ashur distinguishes good action and receptivity from their loss. Al-Biqai notes variation among living people and among the dead in decay and hidden fate. Al-Bursevi adds outward remembrance and inward reflection. Al-Alusi's al-Tibi reads the pair anaphorically as those accepting the mission and those persisting arrogantly. Al-Thaʿalibi's assignment of believer and disbeliever to only blind and sighted is rejected by al-Alusi. [48 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة,قول غير مسمى); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرازي); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin genel kanaati,Râzî,Taberî); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(الثعالبي)-]
- 35:22/q01/p2 The living are learned or intelligent people, and the dead are ignorant or unreasoning people. — Ibn Qutayba gives intelligence versus ignorance; al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi, al-Bursevi, and al-Alusi report scholars versus the ignorant. Al-Bursevi cites a couplet calling an ignorant person a dead person in a shroud. Kuranyolu reports both learned/ignorant and reasoning/unreasoning formulations. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قول غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler)]
- 35:22/q01/p3 The contrast distinguishes spiritual life and death generally: a living heart or soul possesses meaningful wisdom and receptivity, beyond mere bodily existence. — Asad calls both states spiritual. Al-Bursevi says animals also possess bodily life, whereas wisdom and knowledge of realities give lasting life; Saadi's lines distinguish an outward form from a living soul. Al-Qushayri contrasts those close to and witnessing God with the veiled and heedless, and quotes poetry on God's lovers and opponents. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI(منشدون); BURSEVI(سعدي); ASAD-NOTES]
- 35:22/q01/p4 The truly living are Sufi knowers who attain life through voluntary death and receive knowledge from the Ever-Living. — Al-Bursevi reports masters describing annihilation of acts, attributes, and self in God; a saying about Abu Bakr as a walking dead man illustrates the idiom. Abu Yazid, as recounted by Ibrahim al-Harawi, contrasts his group's inspired knowledge from the Ever-Living with ordinary knowledge taken from the dead. [3 notes: BURSEVI(أبو يزيد,النبي,بعض الكبار)]
- 35:22/q01/p5 In the image's literal sense, the living possess sensory powers and the dead have lost them; their states cannot be equated. — Al-Bursevi explains the bodily contrast before applying it to the unbeliever's idle outward conduct and false inward state. Al-Qushayri stresses that the paired states are unlike. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]

## 35:22/q02 Why is the living and dead contrast stronger than the earlier comparisons?  [وَمَا يَسْتَوِى ٱلْأَحْيَآءُ وَلَا ٱلْأَمْوَٰتُ · rhetoric]
- 35:22/q02/p1 The dead lack the useful perception that even a blind living person retains, so the last comparison intensifies the difference. — Al-Razi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Biqai, al-Alusi's Imam, and Kuranyolu argue that a blind person shares some cognition with a sighted one, whereas the dead differ from the living in perception or very reality. Al-Razi and al-Samin tie this to the repeated verb; Kuranyolu says the repeated 'are not equal' supports the reading of verse 19. Al-Bursevi calls the image stronger and notes its plural forms. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرازي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]

## 35:22/q03 Why are the living and dead plural here?  [ٱلْأَحْيَآءُ ٱلْأَمْوَٰتُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural itself stresses the universal disparity or varies the class expression for referents and cadence.
- 35:22/q03/p1 The plural underscores an absolute difference between every living person and every dead person. — Al-Razi and al-Samin say no dead individual equals any living individual in perception, whether comparing kinds or individuals; al-Bursevi links the plurals with the stronger contrast. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 35:22/q03/p2 The plural marks multiple referents, while earlier singulars express generic classes or preserve cadence. — Al-Alusi says blind and sighted are singular for generic class and verse ending. Ibn Ashur says singular and plural can both denote classes with individuals, unlike light, shade, and heat; plural here varies the expression. Al-Biqai notes that living people differ in speech, intelligence, temperament, and manner, with scarcely any two identical. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 35:22/q04 Why do the living precede the dead?  [ٱلْأَحْيَآءُ ٱلْأَمْوَٰتُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the sequence reflects life's prominence, the temporal movement from life to death, or a general principle about ordering unequal terms.
- 35:22/q04/p1 Life comes first because it manifests the difference, honors the superior state, or places believers before those who later persisted in disbelief. — Al-Biqai says life makes the difference visible. Ibn al-Saʾigh, reported in al-Itqan, counts putting living before dead among honorific orderings, alongside men before women, free before enslaved, and hearing before sight; he invokes al-Naqqash via Ibn Atiyya. Al-Razi and al-Alusi describe believers accepting the message before the dead persist in rejection; al-Alusi cites the next hearing clause. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن الصائغ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 35:22/q04/p2 Death follows life because the intended dead previously had life, as the grave image confirms. — An unnamed explanation in al-Alusi contrasts the earlier pairs, which put nonexistence first, with this pair's order. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 35:22/q04/p3 Placing an inferior item first need not lower the superior item's rank. — Al-Alusi reports this alternative account of order and illustrates precedence despite lesser rank with fire above smoke and horsemen's turbans above dust. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 35:22/q05 Why is يستوي repeated for the living and the dead?  [وَمَا يَسْتَوِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether repetition principally intensifies the contrast or resumes and completes a sequence interrupted by earlier clauses.
- 35:22/q05/p1 Repeating the verb emphasizes the final and strongest contrast. — Al-Razi, al-Samin, al-Biqai, al-Baydawi, and Kuranyolu associate repetition with the larger gap between living and dead and the blind and sighted. Al-Tibi, reported by al-Alusi, says omission of emphatic la in the blind/sighted pair prepares for this renewed opening. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 35:22/q05/p2 Repeating the formula restores the comparison after a long interruption and supplies omitted verbs in the preceding clauses. — Al-Tabatabai compares repeated questions in 9:7–8. Ibn Ashur says the explicit verb here also fills the ellipsis in the two preceding clauses. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:22/q06 What is the force of لا in this and the preceding comparisons?  [وَلَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether la is emphatic surplus or an operative negation requiring reconstruction of the paired comparisons.
- 35:22/q06/p1 It is extra or emphatic, reinforcing the denial that the paired opposites are equal. — Al-Akhfash in al-Nahhas treats it as extra; al-Nasafi and al-Tabatabai say repetition strengthens negation. Al-Razi and Kuranyolu distinguish stronger direct opposites in later pairs. Al-Samin says blind and sighted may alternate in one person, unlike the other pairs. Al-Tibi in al-Alusi says it does not compare kinds of darkness with each other. Al-Alusi says the emphatic account makes Ibn Atiyya's proposed deletion unnecessary. [10 notes: NAHHAS(الأخفش سعيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قول غير مسمى); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,الطيبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 35:22/q06/p2 It is an ordinary negation denying equality between the two groups, with implied repetition or omitted counterparts in the series. — Ibn Jawzi reports a view taking la as negation. Ibn Atiyya, reported by al-Alusi, posits a corresponding omitted la or term before the paired noun; Ibn Ashur reconstructs omitted clauses in the two middle comparisons. Al-Alusi reports that later la avoids reading the whole set as one combined group, and another explanation says the first pair needs no la because its contrast is readily understood. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قول غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:22/q07 What does God making someone hear mean?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُسْمِعُ مَن يَشَآءُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether hearing here denotes audible perception itself or the further understanding and acceptance that benefits the hearer.
- 35:22/q07/p1 God grants receptive hearing: understanding, guidance, admonition, and a response to faith. — Muqatil calls the object faith; Yahya, al-Wahidi's al-Suddi, al-Durr's al-Suddi, and al-Biqai call it guidance. Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, and others add taking heed, accepting proof, and submission. Ibn Jawzi says understanding; al-Khafaji in al-Alusi says attentive acceptance and reflection. Ibn Ashur connects this to a prepared heart and the restriction of useful warning in 35:18. Al-Islahi glosses the causative as urging and propelling to listen and accept; al-Tabatabai speaks of reviving a receptive believer, citing 6:122; the Study Quran cites 30:52–53. [25 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي وغيره); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 35:22/q07/p2 The causative can mean producing an audible sound that a listener can perceive, or enabling perception of that sound. — Al-Jishumi defines ismaʿ as making an audible, perceptible sound and samʿ as perception or the ear itself. Al-Tabrisi gives the same account of ismaʿ. Al-Alusi says God enables perception of sounds; al-Tabrisi specifies that literal hearing by unbelievers is compatible with their failure to benefit. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:22/q08 Whom does God choose to make hear, and on what basis?  [مَن يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: Whether the notes specify divine choice alone, prior protection for Paradise, or a human readiness preserved under God's wisdom.
- 35:22/q08/p1 God freely grants guidance and effective hearing to whomever He wills; identical outward access to the message does not determine response. — Ibn Atiyya returns hearing to God's will. Al-Biqai argues the same call and displayed miracles reach people alike, so natural capacity alone cannot explain different responses; selective hearing explains why warning benefits those God wills. Al-Nasafi says God knows who will enter Islam, while the Prophet does not. Al-Tabatabai says God is the source of guidance and the Prophet its instrument. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 35:22/q08/p2 Those God makes hear are His allies, created for Paradise and protected from disbelief. — Al-Wahidi reports this specification from Ibn Abbas. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 35:22/q08/p3 God grants the urge to understand to those who preserve their natural capacities; neglect makes others spiritually dead. — Al-Islahi ascribes the distribution to divine wisdom and says only God can revive those who neglected their natural abilities. Al-Tabatabai similarly describes the believer's inner readiness for revival, citing 6:122. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 35:22/q09 Whom can the Prophet not make hear, and what does that inability mean?  [وَمَآ أَنتَ بِمُسْمِعٍۢ مَّن فِى ٱلْقُبُورِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause chiefly describes unreceptive disbelievers or distinguishes God's power to confer hearing from the Prophet's own ability.
- 35:22/q09/p1 He cannot make stubborn disbelievers benefit from or accept the message, just as the buried dead do not answer a summons. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tabari, Qatada, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, and others explain the grave simile as failure to heed scripture, proofs, and warning. Qatada says the unbeliever neither hears nor benefits from what is heard; Ibn Abi Hatim adds that earlier communities had messengers, proofs and scriptures and that deniers faced worldly punishment and Fire (35:24–26). Al-Baydawi and al-Bursevi say the expression extends the metaphor and discourages hope of conversion. Al-Biqai stresses that even extraordinary signs bring no benefit. Ibn Ashur says these are people God did not will to hear, and al-Islahi describes the Prophet's worry over his people's indifference. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS(الحسن); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي، الحسن، الجحدري); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وعيسى الثقفي وعمرو بن ميمون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî,İbn Atıyye)]
- 35:22/q09/p2 God may make even the physically dead hear, but the Prophet cannot do so by himself; the analogy also relieves him of responsibility for conversion. — Al-Razi offers this as one reading, then alternatively says God alone could make even deaf rocks hear. Al-Biqai says the nominal wording denies the Prophet independent power. Al-Bursevi links 28:56 and 3:128 to the distinction between God's station and the Prophet's, warning against confusions like calling Ezra or Jesus God's son. Al-Tabatabai calls the Prophet an instrument of guidance. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 35:22/q10 What do the graves add to the comparison?  [مَن فِى ٱلْقُبُورِ · rhetoric]
- 35:22/q10/p1 The graves make spiritual deadness vivid: burial prevents ordinary hearing and response, and earth itself blocks the sound. — Ibn Atiyya calls the inability familiar to everyone. Ibn Ashur says mentioning graves adds the earth's sound barrier, absent from simply saying 'the dead.' Al-Bursevi defines qabr as where a dead person is laid and qabartu as placing someone there; he says the image extends the comparison. Al-Biqai allows physical and metaphorical graves. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:22/q11 Can the dead literally hear, and how does this verse bear on the Badr report?  [بِمُسْمِعٍۢ مَّن فِى ٱلْقُبُورِ · theology]
turns on: Whether the Badr address involved actual hearing by the slain, exceptional restoration or disembodied hearing, or only a reproach directed at living unbelievers.
- 35:22/q11/p1 Ordinary dead people do not hear the living, but God may restore spirits or grant an exceptional hearing, including at Badr. — Ibn Atiyya says faithful spirits are reported in trees near the Throne or lamps, unbelieving spirits in Sijjin, and spirits may sometimes approach graves and hear; he cites footsteps and Badr. He prefers Umar and his son Abdullah's report that the Prophet said the Badr dead heard no less than the living, explaining that God restored their spirits to rebuke them. Al-Bursevi likewise suggests revival to humiliate them. Al-Alusi distinguishes ordinary effective hearing from exceptional hearing and compares divine attribution of the Prophet's throwing in 8:17. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر وابنه عبد الله)+]
- 35:22/q11/p2 The Badr dead heard the Prophet, and spirits may hear more clearly than embodied people because the sensory veil is removed. — Al-Bursevi draws this inference from the report's 'no less than the living' wording, after recounting the Prophet's address and the dead men's inability to answer. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 35:22/q11/p3 The dead did not hear at Badr; the Prophet's address reproached the living unbelievers, as this verse indicates. — Ibn Atiyya reports Aisha's interpretation and says he prefers the opposing report from Umar and his son. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر وابنه عبد الله)-]

## 35:22/q12 What is reported to have happened when the Prophet addressed the slain at Badr?  [مَّن فِى ٱلْقُبُورِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the Prophet's words at the well were heard by the slain or addressed rhetorically to the living.
- 35:22/q12/p1 He addressed slain enemies and said they heard him no less than the living, although they could not answer. — Ibn Abbas in al-Durr reports named slain enemies, a question about their hearing, and revelation of this verse as a likeness for disbelievers, with 30:52 mentioned. Al-Bursevi reports the Prophet asking the well's dead whether God's promise proved true and saying they heard but could not reply. Ibn Atiyya cites Umar and Abdullah's report and prefers it. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر وابنه عبد الله)+]
- 35:22/q12/p2 His words were a reproach to living unbelievers; the slain did not hear him. — Ibn Atiyya reports Aisha's account, which invokes this verse. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر وابنه عبد الله)-]

## 35:22/q13 How is بِمُسْمِعٍ مَّن read and construed?  [بِمُسْمِعٍۢ مَّن · readings]
turns on: Whether the active participle is independent and nunated or enters an idafa with min.
- 35:22/q13/p1 The common reading has tanwin on musmiʿ; min is accusative in syntactic position. — Al-Jishumi identifies this as the general reading. Abu Hayyan says the majority retain tanwin. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 35:22/q13/p2 A reading omits tanwin and annexes musmiʿ to min, making min genitive. — Al-Thaʿlabi and al-Jishumi attribute it to Ashhab al-Uqayli; Ibn Atiyya to al-Hasan ibn Abi al-Hasan; Ibn Jawzi also names al-Sulami and al-Jahdari; Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi name Ashhab and al-Hasan. Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi say the omission eases pronunciation; al-Qurtubi adds Isa al-Thaqafi and Amr ibn Maymun. [8 notes: NAHHAS(الحسن); THALABI(أشهب العقيلي); JISHUMI(أشهب العقيلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي، الحسن، الجحدري); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وعيسى الثقفي وعمرو بن ميمون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأشهب والحسن)]

## 35:22/q14 What does the emphatic form of the Prophet's negated ability convey?  [وَمَآ أَنتَ بِمُسْمِعٍۢ · grammar]
- 35:22/q14/p1 The nominal construction and extra ba emphasize that the Prophet has no independent power to produce receptive hearing. — Al-Biqai locates the emphasis in the nominal expression and reserves enabling power for God. Al-Alusi calls the ba extra in the grave metaphor. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:22/q15 How do the surrounding contrasts lead into this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: How the commentators describe the sequence's organizing function: mapped opposites and consequences, or repeated examples of different human responses.
- 35:22/q15/p1 The sequence contrasts believers and disbelievers, faith and unbelief, and their consequences, culminating in life and death. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Samin, Ibn Ashur, al-Tabatabai, Kuranyolu, and Study Quran connect the pairs of sight, light, shade, and life with believers, and blindness, darkness, heat, and death with unbelievers. Ibn Kathir adds believers' gardens and disbelievers' burning wind, boiling water, and smoky shade (56:43–44); Kuranyolu gives ease versus hardship and fire. Ibn Ashur sees alternating comparisons of people and states, with a parallel sequence in 13:16. Al-Nasafi says conjunctions pair even with even and odd with odd. Al-Alusi traces the analogies back to the two seas of 35:12. Al-Nahhas transmits Ibn Abbas's assignments and notes that harur, unlike one proposed distinction from samum, may occur by day or night because its intensive form means harmful heat. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin genel kanaati,Râzî); STUDYQURAN; NAHHAS+]
- 35:22/q15/p2 The passage also repeats explicit comparisons as a rhetorical pattern, moving through nature to show unlike human responses. — Al-Burhan notes the explicit comparisons unlike examples that leave subjects unnamed, and elsewhere calls the paired contrasts repeated comparisons. Al-Itqan similarly cites 35:19–22 as repeated examples. Al-Islahi compares Qur'anic rain received by fertile land but wasted on barren land and says God created natural opposites for the universe's benefit while giving respite to virtuous and vicious inclinations. [7 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:22/q16 How does the hearing clause connect to the preceding warning and the Prophet's task?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُسْمِعُ مَن يَشَآءُ وَمَآ أَنتَ بِمُسْمِعٍۢ مَّن فِى ٱلْقُبُورِ · links]
- 35:22/q16/p1 It explains why warning benefits only receptive people and consoles the Prophet: he must deliver the message, while God enables its acceptance. — Al-Biqai and Ibn Ashur relate selective hearing to 35:18. Ibn Ashur says the hearing clauses answer the prior comparison in matching order and excuse the Prophet's duty to preach to both groups. Al-Razi offers consolation as a reading: even deaf rocks would hear only through God. Al-Nasafi says the Prophet strains over people whose fate is hidden from him. Al-Islahi says the Prophet worried his people's indifference meant he had failed, though he must address the living and leave the dead to God; he says truth reaches wrongdoers conclusively while righteous people refine capacities. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 35:22/q17 What further theological point do the verse's contrasts support?  [* · theology]
turns on: Which larger doctrinal implication is drawn from the contrast: divine sovereignty and exclusion of partners, or resurrection.
- 35:22/q17/p1 They display God's free agency and exclude partners from His power and wise judgment. — Al-Biqai says unlike created states reveal God's unrestricted agency and that nothing resembles Him. Al-Islahi places the passage against idolatry and intercession: mercy and punishment are God's, al-Aziz means invincible dominance and al-Hakim wise action, and a needed co-creator or one able to sway divine justice would negate those attributes. He says the Prophet should leave stubborn idolaters' case to God. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:22/q17/p2 The verse is evidence for resurrection. — Al-Biqai states this inference without further proof in the note. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 35:22/q18 What textual or revelation context is noted for this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 35:22/q18/p1 The verse is in a Meccan context among other Meccan verses. — Al-Tabatabai states this placement. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 35:22/q18/p2 One quotation wrongly combines an opening from 27:80 with the ending of 35:22. — Al-Tabatabai identifies the mismatch with the Qur'anic wording. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 35:22/q19 How is the verb associated with الأحياء read?  [ٱلْأَحْيَآءُ · readings]
turns on: Masculine versus feminine agreement with the plural noun in the reading.
- 35:22/q19/p1 The majority use masculine agreement with الأحياء. — Abu Hayyan reports the majority reading and links the larger order to a move from believer and unbeliever to their light and final dwelling. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 35:22/q19/p2 Zadan transmits feminine agreement from al-Kisaʾi. — Abu Hayyan reports this variant beside the majority form. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
