# 14:17: 22 questions

## 14:17/q01 What is the drink that the condemned gulps?  [يَتَجَرَّعُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether ṣadīd is specified simply as wound pus or by the reported origin of the blood and pus.
- 14:17/q01/p1 It is the foul pus drink, ṣadīd. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls ṣadīd liquid pus from a wound; the drink is repeatedly described as bitter, hot, foul, or repulsive. [5 notes: TABATABAI; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 14:17/q01/p2 It is blood and pus from the private parts of prostitutes in the Fire. — Al-Ṣādiq gives this identification in the report preserved by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 14:17/q02 What does يتجرعه convey about drinking?  [يَتَجَرَّعُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: The force of the derived verbal form: exertion and gradual sipping, equivalence to the simple verb, or response to being made to gulp.
- 14:17/q02/p1 He reluctantly forces down the drink in successive small gulps. — Muqātil stresses difficulty. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī and others cite bitterness and heat; al-Ṭabrisī adds stench and compulsion. Al-Ṭabarī says thirst makes him swallow despite repulsion. Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as compelled drinking, as reported by al-Wāḥidī. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī add thirst and heat; Ibn Kathīr mentions an angel striking with an iron mallet and 22:21. Al-Rāzī stresses continuous gulp by gulp drinking. Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān identify effort or gradual action as possible senses of the form. Ibn ʿAbbās says they dislike the drink, as al-Thaʿlabī reports. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 14:17/q02/p2 The form can equal the simple verb جرع, to gulp. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn list this alongside effort and gradual action; al-Samīn compares ʿadawtu and taʿaddaytuhu. Al-Ālūsī also reports equivalence to the simple verb. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:17/q02/p3 The form may express the response or result of making someone gulp. — Al-Samīn gives it as the result of جرعته; al-Ālūsī reports a passive-response analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:17/q03 How is يتجرعه connected syntactically to the preceding words?  [يَتَجَرَّعُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause describes the water, the drinker while being served, or begins a fresh explanation.
- 14:17/q03/p1 It modifies the preceding ماء, describing the water. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and Abū al-Baqāʾ as reported by al-Ālūsī allow this adjective analysis. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 14:17/q03/p2 It is a circumstantial clause from the pronoun in يسقى. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Ālūsī allow this analysis. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,أبو حيان)]
- 14:17/q03/p3 It begins an independent sentence answering an implied question about the drink. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and Abū al-Baqāʾ allow a new sentence. Al-Bursawī supplies the implied question; al-Ālūsī finds this analysis strongest. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 14:17/q04 What does يسيغه mean?  [يُسِيغُهُۥ · meaning]
- 14:17/q04/p1 It means letting the drink pass smoothly down the throat. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī give this sense; al-Wāḥidī cites al-Farrāʾ's poem. Al-Rāzī distinguishes sāgha, passing down, from asāghahu, making it go down. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī include willing acceptance; al-Ālūsī says its negation denies ease and acceptance. Al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss it as getting it down. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 14:17/q05 Does ولا يكاد يسيغه allow the drink to go down?  [وَلَا يَكَادُ يُسِيغُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: How negated kāda works here, and whether other passages and reports require that some liquid actually enter the stomach.
- 14:17/q05/p1 He cannot swallow it or even nearly do so. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī compare 24:40, where seeing does not occur; al-Baghawī calls kāda an emphatic addition. Al-Zajjāj and Ibn ʿAbbās deny getting the drink down. Al-Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Nasafī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr say failure to approach swallowing entails failure to swallow; al-Rāzī reports this alternative. Al-Wāḥidī calls the redundant reading less strong; Abū Ḥayyān permits eventual drinking if the cited report is sound. Al-Wāḥidī also points back to his discussion of kāda in 2:20 without restating its analysis. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,المفسرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 14:17/q05/p2 He eventually swallows it, but only after delay and great difficulty. — The idiom mā kidtu aqūmu and 2:71 show eventual action for al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī. Al-Wāḥidī cites 22:20, where the drink melts stomach contents, and repeated sipping as evidence some enters; al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī use the same verse. Al-Baghawī reports that it boils inside him; Abū Ḥayyān makes this conditional on the soundness of the report that he then drinks. Al-Wāḥidī prefers the analysis that keeps kāda meaningful. Ibn Kathīr cites the drink's bad color, taste, smell, and unbearable heat or cold; Study Quran reports choking. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); QURTUBI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 14:17/q05/p3 He swallows some of the drink but cannot swallow all of it. — Ibn al-Anbārī's reconciliation, reported by al-Wāḥidī, restricts the negation to the whole drink; al-Rāzī gives the same reconciliation. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL]
- 14:17/q05/p4 The denied swallowing is willing acceptance or relish, though liquid may reach his stomach. — Al-Wāḥidī says the unbeliever sips in disgust and cannot drink readily in one gulp. Al-Rāzī similarly distinguishes reaching the stomach from true willing swallowing. Al-Ālūsī says sugh includes acceptance. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:17/q05/p5 The phrase describes the drinker's attempt to reconcile himself to suffering. — This is Asad's interpretive gloss. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 14:17/q06 How are the forms and clauses of يسيغه parsed?  [وَلَا يَكَادُ يُسِيغُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: These are distinct grammatical points about verbal form and circumstantial attachment.
- 14:17/q06/p1 The verb يسيغه uses the causative form أساغ, which can mean making a drink pass. — Al-Ālūsī says the yāʾ has ḍamma because both sāgha al-sharāb and asāghahu ghayruhu occur, the latter being more eloquent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:17/q06/p2 The clause لا يكاد is circumstantial of the drinker, the drink, or both. — Al-Ālūsī lists all three grammatical attachments. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:17/q07 What does the report say the drink does to its recipient?  [يَتَجَرَّعُهُۥ يُسِيغُهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The extent of the reported effects, especially the extra valley of pus in al-Qummī's version.
- 14:17/q07/p1 As it nears, it burns his face and scalp; once drunk, it cuts his bowels and exits below. — Abū Umāma transmits the Prophetic account. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī and others connect it with 47:15 and 18:29; al-Thaʿlabī also cites 22:20. Al-Ḥākim is said by al-Ālūsī to grade it authentic, and Abū Nuʿaym is said by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī to authenticate it. Some notices stop before the exit detail. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(أبو أمامة); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو أمامة عن رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أمامة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(أبو أمامة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو أمامة عن النبي); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 14:17/q07/p2 One version adds that torn bowels lie beneath his feet and a valley of pus flows from him. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this expansion from Tafsīr al-Qummī. [1 notes: TABATABAI(تفسير القمي)]

## 14:17/q08 What is meant by الموت coming to the condemned?  [ٱلْمَوْتُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether الموت stands for omitted death pangs, for metaphorical deathlike suffering in Hell, or for worldly calamities despite the Hell context.
- 14:17/q08/p1 The causes, pains, or prolonged agonies of death afflict him without death occurring. — Al-Wāḥidī reports an ellipsis of agony and distress because actual death cannot arrive while he lives. Al-Ālūsī allows figurative expression or an omitted noun. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and others describe death's causes and throes; Ibn ʿAbbās says each torment would kill if God allowed it. Al-Qurṭubī reports God creating deathlike pain or prolonging death pangs. [26 notes: FARRA(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عيسى); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Bḍ)]
- 14:17/q08/p2 Death is a figurative name for deathlike misery and afflictions. — Al-Akhfash applies this to Hell's calamities in al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī. Abū Bakr, reported by al-Wāḥidī, calls the breathing sufferer figuratively dead; a report calls poverty the greatest death, and al-Wāḥidī cites a poet contrasting a rested dead person with a miserable living one. Al-Burhān distinguishes figurative death under terror from literal death. Al-Ālūsī reports a specifically worldly-afflictions version. [8 notes: THALABI(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,الحديث); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش)]
- 14:17/q08/p3 It names severe worldly calamities of disbelievers. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this form of al-Akhfash's reading to him; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī object that the passage describes the Hereafter and Hell. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش)-]

## 14:17/q09 What does من كل مكان describe?  [مِن كُلِّ مَكَانٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'place' is a bodily location, an external direction, a kind of lethal ordeal, or an encompassing time and place.
- 14:17/q09/p1 Pain reaches every part of the body, down to hairs, veins, toes, organs, and joints. — Ibrāhīm al-Taymī repeatedly specifies beneath or to the ends of every hair. Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported for every hair; al-Thawrī specifies every vein; Maymūn names bones, veins, sinews, and nerves; Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb names organs and joints; al-Ḍaḥḥāk adds the big toe. Al-Wāḥidī says most commentators take bodily parts. Al-Ṭabrisī includes inside and outside. [35 notes: FARRA(إبراهيم التيمي); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); THALABI(إبراهيم التيمي,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الثوري); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(إبراهيم التيمي,ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سفيان الثوري,عطاء عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,الضحاك,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,محمد بن كعب,ميمون بن مهران); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); STUDYQURAN(R, Bḍ)]
- 14:17/q09/p2 Death's causes surround him from every direction, conventionally all six sides. — Ibn ʿAbbās lists front, rear, sides, above and below; al-Farrāʾ records four horizontal directions. Al-Qurṭubī cites 39:16's canopies of fire. Al-Damghānī says atā can mean descend. Al-Kashshāf and later sources offer this alongside bodily parts; Abū Ḥayyān calls the six-direction sense apparent. [25 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 14:17/q09/p3 Every kind of torment that could kill him confronts him. — Ibn ʿAbbās says any one punishment would kill if God so decreed. Al-Qurṭubī specifies bites, stings, fire, chains, and boiling water; al-Nasafī and al-Bursawī similarly say each part of the suffering would be fatal. Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb describes successive death experiences on seeing, approaching, and drinking the beverage. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,محمد بن كعب); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 14:17/q09/p4 The experience pervades every limb, place, and time. — Al-Qushayrī extends the expression across bodily, spatial, and temporal dimensions. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 14:17/q10 Why does he remain not dead amid deathlike suffering?  [وَمَا هُوَ بِمَيِّتٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes either explain the purpose and effect of survival or describe the soul's suspended state.
- 14:17/q10/p1 He remains alive so he cannot gain the relief of death and his punishment continues. — Al-Ṭabarī says he neither dies and rests nor lives with ease. Al-Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān stress prolonged agony. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Qurṭubī cite 35:36; al-Ṭaḥlabī and al-Qurṭubī connect the trapped soul with 20:74, al-Baghawī with 87:13. Ibn Kathīr says this enables perpetual punishment; al-Biqāʿī says God keeps him alive. Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb's repeated 'deaths' are experiences short of ending life; al-Qurṭubī rejects literal dying. [26 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك,قيل,محمد بن كعب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(R, Bḍ)]
- 14:17/q10/p2 His soul is held at his throat, unable either to leave or return. — Mujāhid and Ibn Jurayj give this mechanism: leaving would bring death, while returning would restore bodily ease or life. Al-Ṭabarī incorporates it in his explanation. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(ابن جريج); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 14:17/q11 What does ميت imply about the person's state?  [بِمَيِّتٍۢ · grammar]
- 14:17/q11/p1 It describes a present state, while related forms may denote someone who will die. — Al-Farrāʾ contrasts miyyit as an existing condition with māʾit or mayyit for future death, comparing sayyid/sāʾid and karīm/kārim. [1 notes: FARRA]

## 14:17/q12 How is ميت read here?  [بِمَيِّتٍۢ · readings]
- 14:17/q12/p1 All readers geminate it because death has not yet become an accomplished state. — Ibn al-Jazarī notes that contexts where the death has already happened differ. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 14:17/q13 What does من ورائه mean here?  [وَمِن وَرَآئِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the spatial word locates punishment ahead of the person or sequences a further punishment after the present ordeal.
- 14:17/q13/p1 Ahead of the condemned person, in his still unseen future. — Ibn Qutayba explains that unseen things can be described as behind or before; al-Ṭabarī cites a poem, a Basran explains the relation to the present, a Kufan says it is especially common of passing times, and another grammarian treats وراء as an antonym. Al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās placing it before the sinner on Judgment Day. Al-Qurṭubī cites poems and 18:79, and reports explanations based on concealment or pursuit; al-Azharī and Abū ʿUbayda connect both directions to concealment, and Ibn al-Anbārī says Hell is hidden. Al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Bursawī explicitly take it as ahead. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي أهل البصرة,بعض نحويي أهل الكوفة,بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش,الأزهري,النحاس,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 14:17/q13/p2 After the drink or punishment just described, a further punishment follows. — Al-Zajjāj glosses waraʾ as after; al-Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī's placement after the pus drink and says Abū Isḥāq and Abū Bakr prefer it. Ibn al-Sāʾib and al-Ṭabrisī likewise place it after that ordeal; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees a repeated image of further punishment. Al-Farrāʾ and Abū ʿUbayda, as reported by al-Qurṭubī for 2:91, give 'other than what preceded' and 'what comes after.' [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)+]

## 14:17/q14 To what does the pronoun in ورائه refer?  [وَرَآئِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent of the singular pronoun: كل جبار or the punishment already described.
- 14:17/q14/p1 It refers to the tyrant or condemned person. — Al-Samīn calls this the clearest antecedent, كل جبار; Abū Ḥayyān contrasts it with the punishment antecedent. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 14:17/q14/p2 It refers to the preceding punishment. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows this; Abū Ḥayyān reports it, and al-Ālūsī attributes it to al-Kalbī. Al-Samīn considers the tyrant antecedent clearer. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 14:17/q15 What is the عذاب غليظ that awaits?  [عَذَابٌ غَلِيظٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase specifies unending residence, successive escalation, confined breath, or severe accumulated torments generally.
- 14:17/q15/p1 It is continuing, severe punishment, with further pain after the present ordeal. — Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe another punishment; al-Wāḥidī's thick image means accumulated, multiplied pains and cites 2:7. Al-Qurṭubī takes it as strong, uninterrupted pain and cites 9:123. Al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz and Ibn Kathīr say it becomes more severe; Ibn Kathīr lists cycles of Zaqqum, boiling drink, and return to Hell with multiple Qur'anic passages. Al-Biqāʿī allows after the ordeal or on Judgment Day. Ibrāhīm al-Taymī is reported by Ibn al-Jawzī as placing it after abiding in Hell. [11 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(إبراهيم التيمي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 14:17/q15/p2 It is eternal residence in Hell, without an end to punishment. — Al-Māwardī, al-Qushayrī, al-Ṭabrisī and reports in al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī identify abiding in Fire; al-Rāzī reports endless duration. Ibrāhīm al-Taymī in al-Durr glosses it as eternal duration. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي)]
- 14:17/q15/p3 Each future moment or turn brings harsher punishment than the last. — Al-Rāzī reports this contrast with mere endless duration. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Kāshifī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī emphasize escalation that prevents habituation or expectation of ease. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:17/q15/p4 It is breath being cut off and confined in bodies. — Al-Fuḍayl ibn ʿIyāḍ gives this gloss in several sources; al-Rāzī attributes it to al-Mufaḍḍal. Al-Bursawī says this explains why crucifixion is among the severest punishments. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الفضيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفضيل بن عياض); RAZI-FULL(المفضل); QURTUBI-FULL(فضيل بن عياض); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(الفضيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفضيل); DURR-FULL(فضيل بن عياض); BURSEVI(الفضيل); ALUSI-FULL(الفضيل)]

## 14:17/q16 What further event does Muqātil report after prolonged suffering?  [وَمِن وَرَآئِهِۦ عَذَابٌ غَلِيظٌۭ · reports]

## 14:17/q17 How does this scene fit the passage about messengers and their opponents?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the verses continue the account of messengers and tyrants or resume with the Meccan drought request.
- 14:17/q17/p1 It describes the afterlife fate of arrogant opponents after God grants the messengers victory. — İṣlāḥī reads verses 5–17 as messengers enduring hardship and finally prevailing, encouraging the Prophet and companions to persist and give thanks; his section addresses Muslims despite general wording. Kur'an Yolu says opponents tried to force prophets back to their religion, the prophets sought help, God destroyed the tyrants, and Hell awaited them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and other summaries locate the punishment in the Hereafter. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 14:17/q17/p2 The passage may turn to Meccans who sought rain during famine and were promised Hell's pus instead. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports this alternative to continuity with the messengers' story. Al-Ālūsī attributes it to al-Zamakhsharī and, on the proposal, favors taking the initial wāw as resumption because its earlier antecedent is distant and seeking victory need not mean requesting rain. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 14:17/q18 What lesson does the verse's punishment scene contribute to the sūra?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw either a moral lesson from the passage or summarize the force of its punishment imagery.
- 14:17/q18/p1 It warns that ingratitude and rebellion end in ruin and severe chastisement, while the faithful should prepare for the Hereafter. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts a few easy days with preparation for what follows and compares disbelievers' works to ash in a storm, ruining them in both worlds. İṣlāḥī calls it punishment of the rebellious. Study Quran connects ingratitude for blessings to loss in the Hereafter and identifies chastisement here and in the next world as a sūra theme. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:17/q18/p2 It portrays suffering that approaches or exceeds death yet never releases the condemned. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī's two treatments summarize the pus drink, choking, death from all directions, survival, and further torment. Study Quran reports that death would otherwise end the pains. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(R, Bḍ)]

## 14:17/q19 How is the cited Banu ʿAjl death poem explained?  [ٱلْمَوْتُ · links]
turns on: The cited lines shift from the killed man and the revenge taunt to the sword's personified death image.
- 14:17/q19/p1 The speaker depicts felling a mighty man and taunts Banu ʿAjl's false claim of blood revenge. — Al-Marzūqī explains that Banu ʿAjl owed revenge to Banu Māzin but killed an uninvolved Yuhsab guest; the poet says the death blow struck the man himself, not his intended target. Al-wajba al-ʿuẓmā means death, with 22:36 cited for falling sacrificial camels; a fallen trunk recalls 69:7 and makes stature vivid. Al-fātik surprises or kills treacherously, with a hadith calling faith restraint and al-Duraydī relating resolve. Al-Marzūqī reads zaʿamtum as casting doubt on their right, revenge, or both, preferring both. He gives the object of aqūlu in the next hemistich, omitted objects of zaʿamtum with 28:62, and explains plural mafāriq, khar/khurūr, suhuq, fa-abid, and the two senses of malhab. Ajl al-qātilīn uses part for whole and emphatic repetition, with alternative case parsing. Awmada and wamada both mean gleam; mināyā al-mawt is a corrupted variant of thanāyā al-mawt. [14 notes: HAMASA]
- 14:17/q19/p2 A second line likens an unsheathed sword's gleam to death showing its teeth. — Al-Marzūqī calls this more eloquent than the earlier image: death smiles at its expected victim as the sword leaves the sheath, confident of striking. The gleam can be described as the teeth of death. [3 notes: HAMASA]

## 14:17/q20 How is the other cited sword poem's image of approaching death explained?  [ٱلْمَوْتُ مِن كُلِّ مَكَانٍۢ · links]
turns on: The two recensions and the figurative relation of eye, heart, sword, and death.
- 14:17/q20/p1 The sword's movement makes death seem to grin at a worthy opponent, certain of striking him. — Al-Marzūqī says the image of death arriving from every lookout depicts the sword and its wielder's force. The opponent's bone signals comparable courage; attributing the grin to molars is figurative, and al-ḍawāḥik are visible laughing teeth. Al-akhlāq means smooth and al-bātik cutting. The expansive expression min ḥaddi akhlāq describes drawing the sword from its sheath. [5 notes: HAMASA]
- 14:17/q20/p2 On one recension the eye is the heart's lookout; sleep and wakefulness explain their different roles. — The eye watches for the sword to be drawn and corrects what the heart dislikes. Al-Marzūqī answers that the heart stays alert during sleep while the eye guards it in wakefulness. In ilā salla, ilā can mean 'with' or mark the endpoint after which action belongs to the heart. [3 notes: HAMASA]
- 14:17/q20/p3 The alternate recension has enemy footmen appear and prompt the drawing of the sword. — Al-Marzūqī prefers it as the more reliable wording. Al-ʿadī are foot soldiers ahead of cavalry, a collective like al-kalib and al-dāʾin. [2 notes: HAMASA; HAMASA+]

## 14:17/q21 How is 'death comes but he is not dead' related to another Qur'anic paradox?  [وَيَأْتِيهِ ٱلْمَوْتُ مِن كُلِّ مَكَانٍۢ وَمَا هُوَ بِمَيِّتٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 14:17/q21/p1 The apparent contradiction dissolves when each statement is considered in its own respect. — Al-Zarkashī compares 22:2, 'you see people drunk though they are not': drunkenness is figurative with respect to Judgment's terrors and literal with respect to wine; similarly, deathlike pain comes without literal death. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 14:17/q22 What limited relief for other people in Hell is mentioned alongside this punishment?  [* · reports]

[1 notes: BURSEVI]
