# 7:24: 27 questions

## 7:24/q01 Who is commanded to descend?  [ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the serpent or Iblis is included, whether descendants can be addressed before existing, and how the plural relates to dual commands elsewhere.
- 7:24/q01/p1 Adam, Eve, and Iblis are addressed. — Al-Tabataba'i says this is the apparent address. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi say the command includes Iblis; al-Kur'an Yolu notes that enmity cannot remain in Paradise, the abode of peace. Ibn Kathir treats Adam and Iblis as the principal opponents, with Eve following Adam; he cites 20:123. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi say the repeated command binds Iblis to human earthly life. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(كثير من السلف); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:24/q01/p2 Adam, Eve, Iblis, and the serpent are addressed. — Al-Suddi's report adds that the serpent was cursed, lost its legs, and was made to crawl and live on dust before descent. Ibn Atiyya attributes this identification to Abu Salih, al-Suddi, al-Tabari and others. Al-Alusi notes that objections and replies appear in the discussion of al-Baqara. Ibn Kathir makes the serpent's inclusion conditional on the report's authenticity. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو صالح والسدي والطبري وغيرهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:24/q01/p3 Adam and Eve with Adam's descendants, together with Iblis or his descendants, are addressed. — Al-Farra and al-Alusi allow those not yet existent to be addressed; the Study Quran cites 2:36 and 20:123. Ibn Atiyya objects that the descendants did not yet exist and that a conditional command to descend would have no direct effect on them, unlike prayer or fasting. Al-Baydawi reports the alternative of Adam, Eve and their descendants. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 7:24/q01/p4 Adam and Eve alone are addressed; the plural can encompass their descendants as humanity's source. — Al-Mawardi reports their joint descent despite the plural imperative. Al-Nasafi says Iblis had descended earlier, or alternatively had returned to heaven before a joint descent. Al-Alusi cites the dual command in 2:38. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:24/q01/p5 Adam, Eve, and the serpent are addressed. — Abu Salih and al-Mawardi take the three as sufficient for the plural. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو صالح); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح)]
- 7:24/q01/p6 Adam, Eve, and the whispering temptation are addressed. — Al-Mawardi reports this from al-Hasan. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 7:24/q02 Why is the command to descend given here again, and what descent does it mark?  [قَالَ ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the repeated plural command represents one joint descent or a summary of separate speeches.
- 7:24/q02/p1 The command includes Iblis in a shared earthly descent and signals his continuing association with humanity. — Al-Baydawi says the speech is reported in separate parts; al-Alusi says repeating the command with Iblis shows his lasting involvement with humankind. Study Quran distinguishes this collective descent from Iblis's earlier order to leave the Garden in 7:13: this is banishment from the celestial realm. Al-Nasafi alternatively supposes Iblis ascended again before all descended. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(كثير من السلف); STUDYQURAN]
- 7:24/q02/p2 Separate commands were given and summarized together. — Al-Alusi compares the collective address to the messengers in 23:51. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:24/q03 How did God address Adam after the transgression?  [قَالَ · reports]
turns on: Whether Moses' special status excludes direct divine speech to Adam, and whether Eve was included in the literal call.
- 7:24/q03/p1 The call came through revelation rather than direct speech. — The majority cited by Abu Hayyan appeal to Moses' distinction as God's interlocutor and the intercession report; 2:253 can be reconciled by restricting Moses' distinctive speech to earthly life. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 7:24/q03/p2 Adam was addressed directly by God. — Abu Hayyan cites Ibn Abi Khaythama's history calling Adam a prophet to whom God spoke. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:24/q03/p3 The literal call was to Adam alone. — A reported view observes that no report says God spoke to Eve; a person may be addressed by name, kind, or description even when this is unstated. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 7:24/q04 How is God's earlier reproach and warning connected to the descent?  [قَالَ ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · links]
turns on: Whether an intervening incident occurred; al-Alusi judges its report unsound.
- 7:24/q04/p1 The reproach follows Adam's act immediately and recalls the warning against Satan. — Al-Alusi finds no intervening event in the apparent sequence. Abu Hayyan says the quoted question depends on an omitted verb of saying and reproaches their oversight; the earlier warning is 20:117, the covenant Adam literally forgot. Ibn Abbas says Satan's refusal to prostrate and threat to mislead the descendants already displayed his enmity. Abu Hayyan explains the shift from a near to a distant demonstrative for the tree as anticipating separation and expulsion. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:24/q04/p2 A report places bodily waste and Adam's attempt to relieve himself before descent. — Al-Ghazali's reported account makes the forbidden tree the sole cause of waste in Paradise. Al-Alusi calls it unsound and maintains the immediate sequence. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الغزالي)-]

## 7:24/q05 What variant wording is reported for the preceding divine reproach?  [قَالَ · readings]
- 7:24/q05/p1 Ubayy's reading asks whether both had been forbidden that tree and told so. — Abu Hayyan attributes the dual wording to Ubayy. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي)]

## 7:24/q06 Where did Adam and Eve land?  [ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: The reports give incompatible sites or levels of precision; Ibn Kathir doubts the reliability and usefulness of all such location reports.
- 7:24/q06/p1 Adam landed in India and Eve in Jeddah. — Al-Hasan also places Iblis at Dast Maysan near Basra and the serpent in Isfahan. Ibn Atiyya reports derivations of Mina, Arafat, and Jam from Adam's longing for, recognition of, and reunion with Eve. Ibn Kathir says these landing place accounts are Israelite reports of uncertain reliability; had the locations mattered, God or His Messenger would have specified them. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رواية); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q06/p2 Adam landed at al-Safa and Eve at al-Marwa. — Ibn Umar transmits these paired sites. Ibn Kathir questions the reliability and relevance of landing place reports generally. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q06/p3 Adam landed at Dahna in India. — One Ibn Abbas report locates Dahna in India; Ibn Kathir questions landing place reports generally. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q06/p4 Adam landed at Dahna between Mecca and al-Taif. — A different Ibn Abbas report gives this location; Ibn Kathir questions landing place reports generally. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q06/p5 Adam landed on Mount Wasim in India and Eve in Jeddah. — Al-Mawardi also places the serpent in Isfahan; Ibn Kathir questions landing place reports generally. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q06/p6 Adam landed in India, with no more precise site given. — Al-Suddi adds that he carried leaves from Paradise; from them arose India's fragrant trees. Ibn Kathir questions landing place reports generally. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]

## 7:24/q07 Where did Iblis land?  [ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted sites; Ibn Kathir treats location reports as uncertain Israelite material.
- 7:24/q07/p1 He landed in the Maysan or Basra area. — Al-Hasan specifies Dast Maysan near Basra; Ibn Atiyya reports Maysan or Basra and quotes Ibn Umar saying Iblis spread his rich carpet there. Ibn Kathir questions the landing place reports generally. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رواية); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q07/p2 He landed at al-Ubulla. — Al-Mawardi reports this as one possibility; Ibn Kathir questions such reports generally. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q07/p3 He landed at al-Madar. — Al-Mawardi reports this second possibility; Ibn Kathir questions such reports generally. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 7:24/q07/p4 He landed in Egypt, where he bred. — Ibn Atiyya includes Egypt among reported possibilities; Ibn Kathir questions such reports generally. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(رواية); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]

## 7:24/q10 What happened to Adam when he descended and began earthly life?  [ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: These are distinct transmitted details of Adam's descent, not exclusive accounts.
- 7:24/q10/p1 Adam descended bowed, learned labor, and had to cultivate and prepare his own food. — Raja ibn Abi Salama describes Adam's hands on his knees and head bowed, contrasting Iblis's interlocked fingers and raised head. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report ironworking and farming, followed by plowing, watering, harvesting, threshing, winnowing, kneading, baking and cooking. Abu Hayyan reports Adam's explanation that he thought no creature would swear falsely by God; God sends him to earn subsistence by labor. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رجاء بن أبي سلمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رواية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:24/q10/p2 Adam carried Paradise leaves whose fragrance generated India's perfume trees. — Al-Suddi says Adam retained the leaves out of grief over leaving Paradise. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي)]
- 7:24/q10/p3 Adam brought seeds from Paradise, but Iblis's touch made some seeds useless. — Al-Nasri ibn Yahya transmits the seed report. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النسري بن يحيى)]

## 7:24/q11 What befell Eve and the serpent after the transgression?  [ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · reports]
- 7:24/q11/p1 Eve was assigned menstrual bleeding and painful pregnancy and childbirth; the serpent lost its legs and had to crawl. — Ibn Atiyya reports that the bleeding tree foreshadowed Eve's monthly bleeding and that her lament passed to her descendants; another report says the serpent once had camel-like legs. Al-Suddi says God cursed it, removed its legs, and made it live on dust. Muhammad ibn Qays reports Adam blaming Eve, Eve the serpent, and the serpent Iblis, followed by these punishments and Iblis's curse. Ibn Atiyya says he abbreviates such reports because the verse's wording does not require them. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رواية); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن قيس)]

## 7:24/q12 What do reports say about Iblis's earthly haunts and conduct?  [ٱهْبِطُوا۟ · reports]
- 7:24/q12/p1 Bathhouses are his dwelling and markets his gathering place; he uses forbidden food, drink, amusements and desires. — Salih, client of al-Tu'ma, cites some books: flutes are his amusement, food without God's name his food, intoxicants his drink, lusts his messengers, and women his snares. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(صالح مولى التؤمة)]

## 7:24/q13 What does the enmity describe, and between whom?  [بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The range of the mutual pronouns and whether the serpent or human descendants are included.
- 7:24/q13/p1 Adam or humanity and Iblis are opposed to each other. — Muqatil says Iblis is enemy to Adam and Eve and they to him. Al-Nasafi takes the hostility as reciprocal. Ibn Kathir cites 20:123 for the central Adam–Iblis conflict. Bursevi says Iblis resents Adam's succession and his own lost leadership; his hostility is innate like a scorpion's sting or wolf's theft, while humans must oppose their father's enemy. Al-Tabataba'i sees antagonism in their different natures; Islahi says Satan seeks to mislead Adam's offspring while they can defeat his designs and regain Paradise. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:24/q13/p2 The hostility extends to the descendants of Adam and Iblis and also includes conflicts among humans. — Al-Biqa'i names both sets of offspring and interhuman enmity. Kur'an Yolu includes human–Satan conflict, quarrels between spouses, and conflicts among descendants. Al-Alusi says if Adam and Eve alone are addressed, their descendants can be included, or the spouses may oppress one another under Satan's misguidance. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:24/q13/p3 It also includes human hostility toward snakes. — Ibn Atiyya says the hostility to snakes is well known and cites the Prophet's statement, transmitted by Qatada, that Muslims have not made peace with snakes since their fight. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 7:24/q14 How does the enmity clause work grammatically?  [بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is dependent as a circumstance or independent as a new statement.
- 7:24/q14/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing their state on descent. — Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi parse it as a hal; al-Alusi allows simultaneous or prospective circumstance. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:24/q14/p2 It is a fresh sentence answering what their descent will be like. — Al-Alusi reports this grammatical analysis from some grammarians. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:24/q15 Why is the singular ʿaduww used for plural enemies?  [عَدُوٌّۭ · grammar]
- 7:24/q15/p1 The singular noun can have plural reference in Arabic. — Ibn Atiyya compares qawm aduww and qawm sadiq, and cites an anonymous poem using sadiq for a plural group. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 7:24/q16 What do reports about snakes imply for people?  [عَدُوٌّۭ · law]
- 7:24/q16/p1 Snakes should not be spared out of fear of their revenge; a persistent house snake may be killed after warning. — Ibn Atiyya reports Aisha's curse on one who leaves a snake for fear of revenge and Ibn Umar's assertion that one who leaves snakes alone is outside the community. A Prophetic report says Medina's jinn include Muslims, so warn a house snake three times; if it stays, kill it as an unbeliever. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة,عبد الله بن عمر)]

## 7:24/q17 What does the earth as their mustaqarr mean?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether mustaqarr is a place or action, and whether its scope includes burial; Ibn Ashur and al-Alusi give explicit objections to narrower or temporal readings.
- 7:24/q17/p1 A place of earthly dwelling and stability during life. — Al-Tabari calls the earth a spread-out resting place; Abu al-Aliya and al-Rabi link it to the earth made a bed in 2:22. Al-Biqa'i says the whole earth, including plains. Al-Mawardi and al-Nasafi allow a place noun, while Ibn Ashur identifies a mimetic verbal noun denoting remaining and cites 6:67 and 6:98; al-Alusi also allows the act of settling. Study Quran refers to 6:98. Ibn Ashur objects to graves because enjoyment follows and jinn or devils are not buried in earth. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية عن الربيع); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:24/q17/p2 The graves beneath the earth are the place of settlement. — Reports attribute this to Ibn Abbas, with Ibn Masud and al-Suddi agreeing in one transmission. Ibn Ashur rejects the burial reading because the clause also promises enjoyment and because jinn and devils are not buried in the earth. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-]
- 7:24/q17/p3 Settlement covers life on the surface and residence beneath it after death. — Al-Tabari and Ibn Atiyya insist on the general scope and cite 77:25–26, earth containing the living and dead. Ibn Abbas is also reported to say both above and beneath the earth. Al-Alusi says grave dwelling can count as being in earth. Al-Durr's Jewish scholar adds the womb and final abode, Paradise or Hell, to the stages of human settlement. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(حبر تيما); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 7:24/q17/p4 Mustaqarr can describe the act or state of settling, rather than a physical place. — Al-Mawardi contrasts the verbal act with Abu Salih's place gloss. Al-Nasafi says stability or place of stability; al-Alusi says settling or a place. Ibn Ashur's mimetic verbal noun denotes remaining or staying. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:24/q17/p5 It may signify dominion established on earth and permission to use it. — Al-Alusi offers this meaning but calls it less apparent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:24/q17/p6 It could mean a period of settlement. — Al-Alusi rejects this because a temporal sense would repeat the subsequent 'until a time.' [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:24/q18 What is the earthly matāʿ promised here?  [وَمَتَٰعٌ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the promise of enjoyment includes everyone addressed or is directed especially to Iblis.
- 7:24/q18/p1 Temporary worldly enjoyment, benefits, and sustenance for use. — Al-Mawardi calls it worldly goods and benefits; al-Nasafi livelihood; Ibn Atiyya enjoyment and acquisition of benefit, mainly during life though a grave stay might somehow count. Ibn Ashur says the noun can denote passing pleasures or the useful things themselves and cites 4:102. Study Quran contrasts it with lasting reward or punishment of the Hereafter, citing 16:80, 2:36, 3:14, 4:77, 9:38, 10:70, 13:26, 20:131, 28:60, 40:39 and 42:36. [10 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]
- 7:24/q18/p2 The temporary enjoyment is directed specifically to Iblis, although earthly dwelling is general. — Al-Qushayri distinguishes the scope of the two promises. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 7:24/q19 How is the phrase 'on earth' attached grammatically?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّۭ وَمَتَٰعٌ · grammar]
turns on: The attachment of 'on earth' and how the second nominal clause joins the first.
- 7:24/q19/p1 The earth clause is coordinated with the preceding enmity clause. — Ibn Ashur treats the statement about settlement as joined to the statement that some are enemies to others. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:24/q19/p2 The prepositional phrase attaches to enjoyment, to both enjoyment and settlement by attraction, or to an omitted adjective of enjoyment. — Al-Alusi reports these alternative syntactic attachments without selecting one. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:24/q20 How long does earthly settlement and enjoyment last?  [إِلَىٰ حِينٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the addressees are viewed individually or collectively, and whether hin supplies a precise endpoint or only an indefinite interval.
- 7:24/q20/p1 Until each being's appointed lifespan ends in death. — Al-Suddi glosses provision as sustenance until death; Ibn Abbas through Ikrima glosses the term as life. Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursevi, and Ibn Ashur likewise tie it to individual life; Ibn Ashur says this divinely determined term ends both settled existence and enjoyment, and is neither favor nor punishment. Ibn Kathir says terms are decreed and recorded in the first book; Muqatil gives Iblis a special term until the first trumpet. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس عن عكرمة,السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:24/q20/p2 Collectively the term extends until the Hour or the world's end, while individual enjoyment ends at death. — Ibn Atiyya explicitly distinguishes collective from individual duration; al-Biqa'i says lifespans expire and then the world ends. Al-Tabari and al-Mawardi identify the world's end, and Ibn Abbas names the Day of Resurrection. Al-Alusi reports death or resurrection as endpoints and allows 'for you' to refer collectively to humankind. Muqatil distinguishes ordinary lifetimes from Iblis's first-trumpet term. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:24/q20/p3 The term lasts until arrival at Paradise or Hell. — Ibn Abbas gives the final destinations as the end of provision. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 7:24/q20/p4 Hin denotes an indefinite period, long or short, whose exact limit is not specified by the word itself. — Al-Tabari cites a poem about 'a time, no time'; al-Mawardi says the period can be long or short, usually suggesting long duration, and cites a poem. Ikrima calls it an unapprehended endpoint. Ibn Atiyya calls the time unspecified in Arabic usage; Ibn Ashur says its duration varies by kind and individual. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:24/q21 What happened when Adam's earthly life ended?  [إِلَىٰ حِينٍۢ · reports]
- 7:24/q21/p1 Angels washed, shrouded, and buried Adam in Serendib and taught his descendants burial practice. — Thabit al-Bunani reports that near death Eve circled the gathered angels and Adam told her to leave them, saying his fate came through her; the angels also perfumed him. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL(ثابت البنانى)]

## 7:24/q22 Why were Adam and Eve sent to earth after repentance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How accepted repentance relates to the continued descent: punishment, planned trial, moral development, or providential spiritual gain.
- 7:24/q22/p1 Earth was appointed as the arena of a trial against Satan; Paradise becomes the reward for success. — Islahi says God's acceptance of repentance did not end the divinely planned contest; he cites the Adam sequence in 2:30–39. Kur'an Yolu cites 2:37 for accepted repentance and says earth became the place of testing. Al-Biqa'i calls earth an abode of struggle and urges constant vigilance, guided by reason and revelation, even after repentance. Thalabi describes living, dying and rising there. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL; THALABI]
- 7:24/q22/p2 The fall enabled freely chosen moral action, repentance and growth. — Kur'an Yolu says the first innocence was instinctive and lacked moral value; the tree's prohibition introduced choice, followed by wrongdoing, recognition, pardon and the capacity to choose goodness consciously. Study Quran says the fall manifests divine forgiveness and human humility and repentance, yielding a perfection otherwise unavailable. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:24/q22/p3 The descent providentially enabled earthly vicegerency and nearness to God through exile. — Study Quran attributes this merciful interpretation to some Sufis and quotes Ibn Ata Allah: disobedience that breeds humility and need is better than obedience that breeds pride. Al-Qushayri says Adam lost his honored abode but retained virtue, citing God's choosing him in 20:122. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(Aj,Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh); QUSHAYRI]
- 7:24/q22/p4 Adam and Eve's sin caused their expulsion and shaped humanity's fate. — Kur'an Yolu cites 2:36, 2:38 and 20:123, observes that the Torah narrates additional hardships absent from the Quran, and still describes repentance as accepted in 2:37. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:24/q23 What does the descent teach about Adam's and Iblis's standing?  [* · theology]
turns on: How Adam's lapse relates to his preserved rank and prophethood, in contrast with Iblis's irreversible fall.
- 7:24/q23/p1 Adam lost his honored place but retained virtue and received mercy; Iblis fell in state and rank without recovery. — Al-Qushayri cites God's later choosing Adam in 20:122 and contrasts Adam's change of abode with Iblis's fall into curse. Bursevi, reporting al-Qushayri, says Adam's loss of garments and ease after one sin warns those committing many sins; a Hafiz couplet expresses longing for reunion while destiny binds the soul to separation. Bursevi adds that divine decree governs prophets and saints. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الحافظ,القشيري)]
- 7:24/q23/p2 Adam's grave sin preceded his prophethood. — Al-Razi says this resolves the theological difficulty raised by the account, which he had discussed in al-Baqara. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 7:24/q24 What inward lessons are drawn from Adam's descent?  [* · inward]
turns on: The distinct symbolic lessons Bursevi draws from loss, knowledge and surrender.
- 7:24/q24/p1 Outward descent can be followed by spiritual ascent through love, loss and unveiling. — Bursevi says Adam ate from the tree of love, endured separation, and was promised discovery after loss. He warns against the tree of mere knowledge without direct witnessing: its owner remains veiled from reality, while God may complete a seeker's journey in the postmortem isthmus. [2 notes: BURSEVI]
- 7:24/q24/p2 The seeker should relinquish self-directed planning and submit to divine decree. — Bursevi reads prayer's standing as primordial surrender, bowing as submission to eternal ordering, and prostration as effacement of both; he also says decree governs prophets and saints. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:24/q25 What are the conditions and protections of earthly life?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:24/q25/p1 Earthly life alternates hardship and ease, good and evil, life and death, victory and loss, with means of protection from harm. — Al-Qushayri says God conceals people from visible causes of harm and equips them with benefits to ward off many harms. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 7:24/q26 Do this verse and 6:38 show that animals share human religious obligations?  [* · theology]
- 7:24/q26/p1 No; that inference from the combined verses is strained. — Al-Burhan rejects the argument that animals bear the same religious obligations as humans, despite its appeal to 7:24 and 6:38. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 7:24/q27 How does this verse connect with the surrounding Quranic account?  [* · links]
- 7:24/q27/p1 It continues the Adam narrative and parallels the descent account in al-Baqara and 20:123. — Al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan refer readers to prior treatment in al-Baqara; Abu Hayyan names 2:36. Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Kathir cite 20:123 for Adam–Iblis enmity. Kur'an Yolu links 2:36, 2:38 and 20:123; Study Quran places 7:24 within verses 11–27 on creation, temptation and expulsion. Ibn Atiyya says he abbreviated extra reports because the present wording does not demand them. [8 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
