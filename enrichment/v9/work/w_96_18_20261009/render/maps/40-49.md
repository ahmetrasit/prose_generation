# 40:49: 20 questions

## 40:49/q01 Who are the people in the Fire who make this request?  [ٱلَّذِينَ فِى ٱلنَّارِ · referent]
- 40:49/q01/p1 The inhabitants of Hell generally, including the unbelieving nations, the weak and powerful, and both followers and those followed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says all the people in the Fire. Al-Biqāʿī includes followers and those followed; Bursawī and al-Ālūsī include the weak and arrogant after their options fail. Al-Qurṭubī calls them unbelieving nations. Ibn ʿĀshūr excludes a restriction to Pharaoh’s family: the next scene has guardians and plural messengers, whereas Pharaoh had only Moses (40:49–50). [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:49/q02 How is the plural ٱلَّذِينَ formed and inflected?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the plural retains the singular relative pronoun’s built form or follows sound masculine plural inflection.
- 40:49/q02/p1 It remains built like its singular alladhī, with a joined nūn. — Al-Akhfash, reported by al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī, compares joining nūn to alladhī with the indeclinable compound number khamsata ʿashar; al-Qurṭubī reports Arab usage retaining the singular’s indeclinability. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش)]
- 40:49/q02/p2 It is declined as the sound masculine plural alladhūn. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this alternative, and al-Qurṭubī reports that some Arabs use it. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 40:49/q03 What reading is preferred for ٱلنَّارِ here?  [ٱلنَّارِ · readings]
- 40:49/q03/p1 Imāla is analogically preferable here even for readers who otherwise use fatḥ. — Ibn al-Jazarī argues that assimilation leaves a kasra after the alif. [1 notes: NASHR+]

## 40:49/q04 What prompts the inmates to make this plea now?  [وَقَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ فِى ٱلنَّارِ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously emphasize unbearable torment or the failure of all previous avenues.
- 40:49/q04/p1 The severity of their punishment becomes unbearable and drives them to seek relief. — Muqātil and al-Baghawī place the plea after punishment intensifies; al-Ṭabarī calls it severe distress. Al-Ṭabrisī says they cannot endure it, despite knowing punishment never ends or lessens. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 40:49/q04/p2 Their arguments and other options have failed, leaving them to turn to the guardians. — Bursawī says both weak and arrogant have tasted punishment and exhausted their options; al-Ālūsī says their arguments fail and options narrow. Ibn Kathīr ties their despair to God’s command at 23:108 to remain despised and silent. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 40:49/q05 Who or what are the keepers of Hell?  [لِخَزَنَةِ جَهَنَّمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the angelic guardians also carry an allegorical sense.
- 40:49/q05/p1 They are angels charged with guarding Hell and administering its punishment. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr give khazana as the plural of khāzin, with alternatives khuzzān and khazan; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a keeper of a place’s contents, and their charge over Hell’s fire, fuel, inmates and affairs. Al-Rāghib, reported by Bursawī, traces khazn to storing and then keeping anything, even a secret. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī, and Asad identify the keepers as angels or angelic forces watching or administering torment. [9 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 40:49/q05/p2 They may also allegorize sinners’ belated awakening of conscience. — Asad offers this as a possible allegorical sense of the angelic forces that watch over sinners’ suffering. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 40:49/q06 Why explicitly say جَهَنَّمَ instead of using a pronoun?  [لِخَزَنَةِ جَهَنَّمَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the explicit name substitutes for a pronoun to evoke dread, specifies a particular location, or is required by the meaning of what the guardians guard.
- 40:49/q06/p1 Naming Jahannam heightens dread and the threat. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī explain the explicit name as intensifying horror; al-Ālūsī adds that Jahannam is more specific than the Fire. Al-Burhān compares repeated naming to invoking the Caliph’s authority. Al-Biqāʿī says it evokes the layer and grimacing guards, exposing the inmates’ misery and disordered judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as al-Zamakhsharī’s account. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 40:49/q06/p2 The name identifies the inmates’ location in Jahannam, perhaps its deepest level. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives identification of their location as an alternative. Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī entertain the deepest-pit sense and the special standing of its angels; al-Ālūsī also reports that these angels may be assigned to the worst inmates. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:49/q06/p3 The phrase specifies the guardians of what Hell contains, so no pronoun has been replaced. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says guardians guard Hell’s contents rather than the fire itself; he cites the pronominal construction at 67:6–8 and calls his analysis clearer than al-Zamakhsharī’s. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 40:49/q07 What does جَهَنَّمَ designate here?  [جَهَنَّمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether Jahannam names Hell generally or a specific deepest level in this context.
- 40:49/q07/p1 It is the name of God’s kindled fire, Hell. — Bursawī calls it the name of God’s kindled fire; Ibn ʿĀshūr speaks of Hell and what it contains. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:49/q07/p2 It may designate the deepest pit or level of Hell. — Al-Bayḍāwī suggests the deepest level; al-Nasafī cites the expression of a deep well, and al-Ālūsī reports the same possible location. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 40:49/q08 Why ask the keepers to petition God?  [لِخَزَنَةِ جَهَنَّمَ ٱدْعُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 40:49/q08/p1 The inmates seek angelic mediation because their own access to God or other protectors has failed and they hope the keepers are heard. — Ibn Kathīr cites despair after 23:108; al-Biqāʿī says they feel distant from God and unfit to address Him, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they despair of their own prayers. Al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr report or infer hope that these angels are nearer God and more likely to be answered; al-Ālūsī adds their assignment to the worst inmates as a possibility. Study Quran says worldly protectors fail in the Hereafter (6:27–28; 14:44). [9 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:49/q09 What do the inmates ask the keepers to do?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ · interpretation]
- 40:49/q09/p1 They ask the keepers to pray to God or intercede for a temporary easing of punishment. — Al-Dāmghānī explains duʿāʾ as asking or petitioning, citing 7:134, 40:60 and 7:55–56; Bursawī calls it intercession. Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Study Quran describe the same request. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:49/q10 Why do they call God رَبَّكُمْ?  [رَبَّكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 40:49/q10/p1 Calling God the guardians’ Lord encourages them to pray, on the assumption that their prayer will be answered. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this form of address seeks to move the guardians to petition their own Lord; the inmates believe the guardians have a better chance of being heard. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:49/q11 Why is يُخَفِّفْ jussive after the command?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ يُخَفِّفْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the jussive is licensed as an imperative’s result or by an elided lām of command.
- 40:49/q11/p1 It is a jussive response or result of the imperative without fāʾ. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say a response with fāʾ would be subjunctive but Arabic commonly omits fāʾ; they cite Imruʾ al-Qays’s qifā nabki as eloquent usage. Some grammarians reported by Ibn ʿĀshūr construe it as the request’s result, showing the inmates’ confidence that the prayer will be heard; he notes the pattern after commands to say and similar requests and refers to 14:31. [6 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر); IBNASHUR-FULL(من أهل العربية)]
- 40:49/q11/p2 It reflects an omitted lām of command. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this as a frequent construction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:49/q12 What reduction of punishment do they seek?  [يُخَفِّفْ مِّنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ · interpretation]
- 40:49/q12/p1 They ask for only a limited easing of punishment, not its permanent removal. — Al-Dāmghānī says takhfīf means reducing punishment. Al-Samīn reads the partitive min as some punishment, for some day rather than all punishment on every day. Al-Biqāʿī and Bursawī say only part is to be relieved; Bursawī says a slight, short request reflects their knowledge that greater relief is impossible. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a day of respite would help more than merely reducing the fire’s intensity. [6 notes: DAMGHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:49/q13 What duration does يَوْمًۭا describe?  [يَوْمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the measure is a worldly day or a figurative amount understood in the hereafter.
- 40:49/q13/p1 A measure equal to one day of this world. — Muqātil understands an omitted phrase, one day from the days of this world. Al-Ṭabarī says the afterlife has no night by which to measure a day and mentions 2:96. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī likewise give a worldly day’s measure. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:49/q13/p2 A short amount of time or portion of punishment, rather than a literal measured day. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows an amount of time; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls day figurative for a little time and treats min al-ʿadhāb as explaining that amount. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī understands a portion of punishment, with day reckoned according to the nature of their realm. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 40:49/q14 How do يَوْمًۭا and مِّنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ attach to يُخَفِّفْ?  [يُخَفِّفْ يَوْمًۭا مِّنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether day marks duration or is the object, and whether min is partitive, explanatory, or extra.
- 40:49/q14/p1 Day is an adverb of duration and min al-ʿadhāb is the partitive object of the verb. — Al-Samīn prefers this apparent parse: some of the punishment is eased for some day, without specifying one day or all days. Al-Ālūsī allows partitive min; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the phrase may attach directly to the verb. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 40:49/q14/p2 Day is an adverb, while the object of the verb is omitted and min al-ʿadhāb explains or supplies it. — Al-Samīn reports this parse and al-Akhfash’s view that min is extra, making al-ʿadhāb the object. Bursawī makes min al-ʿadhāb clarify the omitted object; al-Ālūsī gives the explanatory reading of min. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:49/q14/p3 Day is the direct object, meaning a day’s punishment or its amount, with an omitted noun or explanatory min al-ʿadhāb. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives an omitted genitive as an alternative. Al-Samīn reports this parse but calls it strained with min al-ʿadhāb, and an emphatic-adjective treatment even weaker. Al-Ālūsī explains an omitted noun such as the torment of a day. Ibn ʿĀshūr says yukhafif can mean diminish and govern day directly, or an omitted ʿadhāb may supply its object. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 40:49/q15 How do the keepers answer the plea?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ · reports]
- 40:49/q15/p1 They refuse to pray for the inmates and tell them to pray for themselves. — Ibn al-Jawzī says the keepers will not pray for them. Abū Ḥayyān takes their reply as either mockery or a challenge, since they dare not make the request. Al-Qurazī’s report has the keepers recall that messengers brought clear proofs and then tell the inmates to pray; Abū al-Dardāʾ’s report also ends with the angels directing prayer and its futility. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,محمد بن كعب القرظي); NAHHAS(أبو الدرداء)]

## 40:49/q16 What sequence of events do reports add around this plea?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports supply different preceding details and different wording of the keepers’ reply.
- 40:49/q16/p1 Hunger, painful food and scalding drink precede the appeal to the angels, who tell the inmates to pray to no avail. — Abū al-Dardāʾ’s report, cited by al-Naḥḥās and by al-Qurṭubī from al-Tirmidhī and others, describes hunger, unsatisfying thorny and choking food, and boiling drink that scalds faces and cuts bowels before the plea. The two notes vary in detail, but recount the same sequence. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو الدرداء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 40:49/q16/p2 The inmates beg for a day’s relief, and the keepers recall the messengers’ proofs before directing them to pray. — Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Qurazī as saying he was told this sequence, including that unbelievers’ prayers prove futile. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 40:49/q17 Can the inmates’ prayer or repentance now avert punishment?  [* · theology]
- 40:49/q17/p1 Their late pleas cannot avert or alleviate the punishment. — Ibn al-Jawzī says the disbelievers’ prayer is futile. Al-Ṭabrisī says they know necessarily that punishment never ends or lessens but still hope for relief. Study Quran says belated affirmation and supplication are too late, citing 14:44, 32:12 and 6:27; even a return would lead them to repeat forbidden conduct (6:28). Kuranyolu says after judgment no one can rescue another and even angels refuse. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 40:49/q18 How does this appeal relate to the preceding exchange between followers and leaders?  [* · links]
- 40:49/q18/p1 After leaders prove unable to relieve their followers, both groups are in the Fire and turn to the keepers. — Al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī include followers and leaders, weak and arrogant, among the petitioners. Kuranyolu, citing Rāzī, says those who followed powerful deniers had asked their leaders to remove even part of the fire; the leaders could save nobody, and the followers may have known this, exposing their helplessness. Study Quran likewise says worldly protectors cannot help them (6:27–28; 14:44). [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:49/q19 What practical warning does this scene convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 40:49/q19/p1 Seek salvation before Judgment by rejecting dominant misleaders and following the messenger’s clear guidance. — Kuranyolu says people should freely choose belief and conduct rather than follow wealthy, influential immoral deniers; after judgment no one can save another except by God’s will, even angels refuse pleas. It urges resistance to inner desires and dominant misleaders in this life and adherence to the messenger’s pure way. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 40:49/q20 How does this scene fit the sūra’s warning and Moses–Pharaoh narrative?  [* · links]
- 40:49/q20/p1 The judgment scene extends the warning that rejecting God’s messenger ends in ruin despite worldly power, while the Moses–Pharaoh narrative encourages steadfastness. — Iṣlāḥī says Moses and Pharaoh’s story supports the earlier claim about destroyed rejecters, warns Quraysh, and urges Muhammad and his companions to endure until victory; the believer in Pharaoh’s family is a lesson for hesitant sympathizers. Study Quran says the Hereafter warning in verses 10–20 prepares the extended narrative centered on that believer (40:28, 40:45). [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
