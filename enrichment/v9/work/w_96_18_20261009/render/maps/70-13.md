# 70:13: 12 questions

## 70:13/q01 Who or what is his faṣīla?  [فَصِيلَتِهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether faṣīla denotes a specific clan subdivision, close kin generally, the whole tribe, paternal ancestors, or the mother who nurtured him; al-Bursawī uses the prior mention of sons, and al-Qurṭubī appeals to common usage.
- 70:13/q01/p1 His close clan or branch of the tribe, linked to him by a nearer line of descent. — Muqātil, Ibn Zayd, and others call it the clan that supports him; Abū ʿUbayda, al-Mubarrad, al-Layth, al-Majāz, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, ʿIkrima, and al-Majmaʿ specify a subdivision beneath the tribe. Al-Majāz sets it within a hierarchy descending from shaʿb. Ruʾba's faṣāʾil, cited by al-Wāḥidī, supports the lineage usage. Abū ʿUbayd says al-ʿAbbās was called the Prophet's faṣīla; al-Rāzī explains that an uncle stands in a father's place. Al-Biqāʿī calls them the closest remaining relatives, with the strongest claim on him. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares Banū Hāshim for the Prophet and says the Arabic use for a wife does not fit here because ṣāḥiba has already named her. Al-Ālūsī prefers the clan from whom a person separated over al-Rāghib's reverse formulation. Al-Qurṭubī says the general clan sense is the more common usage in speech. [47 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ابن زيد,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس,أبو عبيد,ابن عباس,الليث,رؤبة,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,المبرد,مجاهد وابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة,مجاهد والسدي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(المجمع); ALUSI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 70:13/q01/p2 His nearest relatives or family, without a narrower tribal rank being specified. — Al-Baghawī reports this as an alternative to his clan gloss; al-Samīn likewise reports it as another explanation and cites 49:13. Al-Nasafī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress kin who shelter him; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a comparison to close forefathers. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(غيره); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 70:13/q01/p3 His tribe or broad people to whom he belongs. — Qatāda, Mujāhid, Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb, and al-Suddī give the tribe gloss. Ibn Kathīr transmits the joint Mujāhid–al-Suddī wording as tribe and clan; Elmalılı gives people, tribe, clan, or community, emphasizing those who raised and sheltered him. Study Quran juxtaposes tribe with the narrower mother gloss. [8 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد,محمد بن كعب); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ; IK, Q)]
- 70:13/q01/p4 His closest forefathers or ancestors. — Al-Farrāʾ, Thaʿlab, and later transmitters identify the closest paternal ancestors from whom lineage is traced. Al-Bursawī includes children in the word's possible range but says the preceding mention of children in 70:11 makes nearest forebears and clan apt here. Al-Qurṭubī gives this as the narrower possible construction in an endowment or bequest, while preferring the more common general clan sense. [9 notes: FARRA; QURTUBI-FULL(ثعلب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثعلب); SAMIN-DURR(ثعلب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ثعلب); TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 70:13/q01/p5 His mother, who nursed or raised him. — Maʿmar says he heard the nursing-mother gloss. Mālik's gloss describes the mother who raised him; al-Qurṭubī says al-Māwardī transmitted it through Ashhab, and Ibn Kathīr also gives it through Ashhab. Study Quran mentions mother as the specific alternative to tribe. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(معمر); MAWARDI-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك عن أشهب); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ; IK, Q)]

## 70:13/q02 Why is this kin group called faṣīla?  [فَصِيلَتِهِ · meaning]
turns on: The notes give either branching from a lineage or a separated bodily piece as the image, and al-Ālūsī disputes which side is described as separating.
- 70:13/q02/p1 The name evokes separation from the kin or ancestral stock from which a person descends. — Al-Jishumī derives it from cutting and a subgroup separated from the tribe by an ancestor. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ālūsī describe the group from which the person branched or separated. Al-Rāzī compares a child's separation from parents and cites the Prophet's statement that Fāṭima is a part of him. Al-Bursawī says a child and parent can each be described as separated from the other, allowing application to forebears or children. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a more specific shared forefather and another gloss of kin separated from him. Al-Ālūsī favors separation from the clan over al-Rāghib's formulation that the clan separated from the person. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(المجمع,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 70:13/q02/p2 Kin are called faṣīla by analogy to a piece separated from a body. — Abū al-ʿAbbās and al-Mubarrad describe a faṣīla as a piece of the body and the kin group as part of the person; al-Qurṭubī applies the analogy to a man's ʿitra. Al-Bursawī gives this as the original sense and extends it to forebears and children. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس); QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); BURSEVI]

## 70:13/q03 What are the plural forms of faṣīla?  [فَصِيلَتِهِ · grammar]
- 70:13/q03/p1 Its plurals are faṣāʾil, faṣl, and faṣalān. — Al-Naḥḥās lists the three forms. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 70:13/q04 What does it mean that his faṣīla takes him in?  [ٱلَّتِى تُـْٔوِيهِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether taking him in describes inclusion through kinship or actual shelter and protection when threatened; several sources give both.
- 70:13/q04/p1 His kin gather, embrace, or include him by their bond of lineage. — Al-Māwardī reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk's explanation of where one turns by lineage. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī explain affiliation through kinship; al-Bursawī cites 12:69 for drawing near or gathering. Elmalılı emphasizes a community that raised and embraced him. [18 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 70:13/q04/p2 His kin give him refuge, protection, and support in fear or hardship. — Al-Māwardī offers seeking refuge in fear as an alternative; al-Qurṭubī says they gather and protect him if afraid. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī name hardship or calamity. Al-Biqāʿī adds that this protection makes them especially dear and gives them the greatest claim on him. [20 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 70:13/q05 How is تُـْٔوِيهِ read or eased?  [تُـْٔوِيهِ · readings]
turns on: The easing of the hamza, whether its substitute is assimilated, and the vowel or replacement of the final pronoun; authorities cite transmission, the rasm, analogy, and ease of speech.
- 70:13/q05/p1 Its hamza is replaced with a matching glide, without assimilation. — Al-Kāfī and al-Tabṣira prefer this for tuʾwīhi and ruʾyan; al-Tabṣira calls it established practice. Al-Taysīr presents this and assimilation equally, followed by al-Shāṭibī. The Qirāʾāt note describes Ḥamza's easing as loss of the glottal stop and lengthening, without a change in meaning. Ibn al-Jazarī favors clear articulation over assimilation in the comparison with ruʾyā as more analogical and the practice of most reciters; he says deletion solely from the rasm is impermissible. He also says readers agree on replacing ruʾyā's quiescent hamza with wāw after ḍamma. [5 notes: NASHR(صاحب التيسير); QIRAAT-ER(hmz); NASHR(صاحب الكافي وصاحب التبصرة)+]
- 70:13/q05/p2 Its hamza is replaced and the resulting glide assimilated. — Al-Tadhkira and al-Dānī favor this treatment for tuʾwīhi and ruʾyan, citing explicit transmission from Ḥamza and agreement with the rasm; al-ʿUnwān gives only this form. Al-Taysīr gives it equal standing with replacement alone, followed by al-Shāṭibī. As a comparison, al-Hudhalī, al-Dānī, and others allow Abū Jaʿfar's assimilation of wāw into the following yāʾ in ruʾyā; Ibn Shurayḥ transmits that but considers it weak. Ibn al-Jazarī favors clear articulation there despite assimilation's agreement with the rasm. [3 notes: NASHR(الهذلي,صاحب التيسير); NASHR(صاحب التذكرة والداني)+; NASHR-]
- 70:13/q05/p3 It is read without hamza. — Al-Nasafī reports this reading from Yazīd. The Qirāʾāt note also describes Ḥamza's easing as removing the glottal stop and lengthening the vowel, without changing the meaning; the two accounts differ in their level of phonetic detail. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(يزيد); QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]
- 70:13/q05/p4 The pronoun hāʾ has its original ḍamma. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn report al-Zuhrī's nominative or ḍamma hāʾ in both tuʾwīhi and tunjīhi; al-Samīn calls it the original vowel. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهري); SAMIN-DURR(الزهري)]
- 70:13/q05/p5 Al-Sūsī leaves the hāʾ unreplaced. — Al-Samīn reports that replacing it would make the form heavier, whereas substitution is intended to ease pronunciation. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(السوسي عن أبي عمرو)]

## 70:13/q06 What does the criminal wish to do with his sheltering kin?  [وَفَصِيلَتِهِ ٱلَّتِى تُـْٔوِيهِ · interpretation]
- 70:13/q06/p1 He would offer even his sheltering kin, with his other loved ones and all earth's people, as ransom for himself, but it would not save him. — Al-Ṭabrisī defines iftidāʾ as escaping harm by giving something in one's place. Al-Jishumī specifies offering them to the Fire. Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn Kathīr say even everything, including wealth or a dearest child, is unacceptable as ransom. Al-Qushayrī stresses the criminal's longing to give his dearest intimates; Qatāda attributes it to the severity of the Day. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī names sons beloved above all, a comforting wife, a supportive brother, sheltering clan, and then all rational beings on earth. Study Quran relates the futile ransom to 39:47, 3:91, 13:18, 2:48, 2:123, 10:54, and 57:15. Islahi describes cosmic upheaval, lost social bonds, and Hell seizing those who evaded the call and hoarded wealth. Kuranyolu emphasizes psychological terror; al-Sāmarrāʾī says the list leaves no one but the person seeking ransom. [19 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(أنوار التنزيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 70:13/q07 What happens to family bonds in this scene?  [وَفَصِيلَتِهِ ٱلَّتِى تُـْٔوِيهِ · interpretation]
- 70:13/q07/p1 Kin remain recognizable and dear, yet fear makes the criminal willing to sacrifice them for himself. — Al-Qushayrī says people recognize relatives but their hearts do not soften toward each other. Al-Biqāʿī notes the sheltering clan's strongest claim on him and dearness, sharpening the shock of the offer. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees an escalation beyond intimates not asking after one another; Anwār al-Tanzīl, quoted by al-Sāmarrāʾī, says the criminal is so absorbed in himself that he wishes to sacrifice his closest instead of caring for them. Islahi says the scene depicts the collapse of social bonds. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(أنوار التنزيل)]

## 70:13/q08 What does the order of relatives in the ransom list convey?  [وَفَصِيلَتِهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order is understood as an outward widening from dearest kin or as an escalation to increasingly precious relatives; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's two notes use both descriptions.
- 70:13/q08/p1 The offer begins with those dearest to him and expands outward to the clan and everyone on earth. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the sequence begins with children because 70:10 already mentioned close kin, then expands to more distant people; 70:19–21's account of anxiety explains the desperate starting point. The close-first order shows punishment beyond imagination and no room to bargain. His list names children, wife, brother, clan, and all earth's people, leaving only the ransomer. In comparison with 80:34–37, he says flight moves from more distant relatives toward the closest, while the ransom offer moves outward from the dearest. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes sons as beloved above all and the subsequent wife, brother, close kin and all people; Qatāda says he offers dearest loved ones and then progressively nearer relatives because of the Day's severity. [9 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 70:13/q08/p2 The list escalates to increasingly precious relatives and then everyone on earth. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the passage intensifies the preceding statement that intimates do not ask after one another by making the criminal willing to offer ever more precious kin before everyone on earth. His separate description calls the sons beloved above all, so the notes give both formulations of the progression. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 70:13/q09 Why are the parents absent from the ransom list?  [وَفَصِيلَتِهِ · rhetoric]
- 70:13/q09/p1 Their commanded honor and high standing make offering them to Hell impermissible, though one might flee them. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī gives this explanation in two discussions: God commanded kindness and honor toward parents; he distinguishes fleeing parents in the other passage from ransoming oneself by consigning them to punishment here. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 70:13/q10 How does this scene's order relate to the flight scene in ʿAbasa?  [* · links]
- 70:13/q10/p1 Flight in ʿAbasa runs from more distant kin toward spouse and children; ransom here starts with the dearest and widens outward. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 70:11–14 with 80:34–37: al-Maʿārij lists children, spouse, brother, clan, then everyone on earth, while ʿAbasa lists brother, parents, spouse, children. He explains the differing order through the differing acts of fleeing and offering people as ransom. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 70:13/q11 Who is the criminal whose ransom wish is described?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether al-mujrim is restricted primarily to unbelievers or covers sinners generally; Abū Ḥayyān allows an extension to punished sinful believers.
- 70:13/q11/p1 Primarily an unbeliever, though the description can include a sinful believer under punishment. — Abū Ḥayyān says al-mujrim primarily denotes a disbeliever but may extend to a punished sinful believer. Kuranyolu reports exegetes' alternatives of unbelievers or sinners more generally. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 70:13/q11/p2 Anyone implicated in crime or sin, not only a disbeliever. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly broadens the term; Kuranyolu reports the general sinner reading alongside the unbeliever reading. Study Quran calls them the guilty or those who work evil in 39:47. [3 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN]

## 70:13/q12 What is the inward reading of this ransom scene?  [* · inward]
- 70:13/q12/p1 The lower-self-stained soul would offer its inner faculties to escape separation and veiling, but its lost readiness and time make that futile. — Al-Bursawī maps the children to the heart's offspring and qualities, the companion to the self, the brother to the secret, the clan to followers, and everyone on earth to spiritual and bodily powers. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
