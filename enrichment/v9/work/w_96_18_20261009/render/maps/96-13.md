# 96:13: 17 questions

## 96:13/q01 How is أَرَءَيْتَ read?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: The notes distinguish elision, facilitation, and vowel lengthening in pronunciation.
- 96:13/q01/p1 The medial hamza is dropped in the canonical reading among the Seven. — QIRAAT-ER says the omission eases pronunciation without changing meaning. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 96:13/q01/p2 The hamza is facilitated with a brief pause. — QIRAAT-ER says this retains a trace of the hamza without full articulation. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 96:13/q01/p3 The canonical madd lengthens the ā before the hamza. — QIRAAT-ER says the lengthening gives the question extra rhythmic weight. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 96:13/q02 What does أَرَءَيْتَ do here?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether seeing is an attention-getting challenge, intellectual apprehension, or initially literal sight.
- 96:13/q02/p1 It prompts consideration or an answer and conveys rebuke, warning, or astonishment rather than seeking information. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an interrogative challenge without the two objects of cognitive vision. Al-Ālūsī explains 'tell me' from sight leading to knowledge and notes two grammatical accounts. Elmalılı describes admonition and amazement. Al-Rāghib compares it to akhbirnī. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ points to Meccan parallels at 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23, 53:33 and questions in al-Wāqiʿa at 56:58, 63, 68, 71, as well as 10:59, 26:75, 35:40, 39:38, 53:19, 46:4. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; ALUSI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الراغب)]
- 96:13/q02/p2 The seeing is inward or intellectual. — Al-Ālūsī reports this for the opening and third questions, and notes grammarians' two analyses. [2 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 96:13/q02/p3 The first seeing is visual, while the later questions are intellectual. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the visual first use needs no second object, whereas the later conditional clauses occupy second-object positions. [4 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]

## 96:13/q03 Whom does أَرَءَيْتَ address?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun sequence and whether the discourse questions an observer, the forbider, or both disputants in turn.
- 96:13/q03/p1 The Prophet is addressed throughout. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers the apparent address to the Messenger on the pronoun sequence; al-Baḥr also favors him. Al-Rāzī says the proofs are plain to sound reason. Elmalılı says the opening address clearly reaches the Prophet and extends to others. [11 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(البحر); ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN+]
- 96:13/q03/p2 The unbelieving forbider is addressed. — Al-Rāzī asks whether Muhammad, if lying, would need the forbider's intervention when God sees him. Al-Bayḍāwī compares a judge addressing opposing litigants. Elmalılı asks whether the servant should obey the forbider and abandon prayer. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 96:13/q03/p3 The addresses alternate between the unbeliever and the Prophet, like a judge questioning litigants. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the second question faces the unbeliever and the third the Prophet; 'or' then divides like 'and.' Elmalılı finds such turn-taking more meaningful. [4 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); ELMALILI+]
- 96:13/q03/p4 The address is general, to any understanding person or humanity. — Al-Ālūsī compares the address to humanity at 96:8; he also reports that some exclude both immediate parties on contextual grounds. Elmalılı allows all three questions to address humanity. [5 notes: ALUSI(البحر); ELMALILI]

## 96:13/q04 Who denies and turns away?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronouns name the historical forbider, express a hypothetical about the worshipper, or extend the warning to all such actors.
- 96:13/q04/p1 The forbider of prayer, Abū Jahl, is the denier who turns away. — Muqātil contrasts the Prophet alone with Abū Jahl's company. Qatāda names him; Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies him with the forbider. Ibn ʿĀshūr says context sends these pronouns back to the man in 96:9–10. The Qatāda report identifies Muhammad as guided, Abū Jahl as denier, his council as his clan and zabāniya as police. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI(الفراء); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:13/q04/p2 Hypothetically the praying servant or Muhammad could be the denier, if he obeyed the forbider or had lied. — Al-Rāzī poses the forbider's supposition that Muhammad is lying or turning away and asks why divine sight would not suffice. Elmalılı asks whether the servant's denial and abandonment of prayer would be good. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 96:13/q04/p3 The wording reaches every tyrant who prevents prayer or obstructs religion. — Al-Ālūsī says the Abū Jahl occasion does not limit the threat. Kur'an Yolu prefers all tyrants in every age who obstruct religion's social work of goodness, truth, and justice. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says a specific occasion allows general wording. [4 notes: ALUSI; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 96:13/q05 What does he deny and turn away from?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: The unexpressed object of 'denies' and the sphere of 'turns away': God, the Book, the Messenger, truth generally, or the worshipper's guidance.
- 96:13/q05/p1 He denies God and turns from obedience to Him. — Al-Naḥḥās adds denial of God's Messenger and prevention of prayer. Al-Jishumī links denial of God and His Messenger, turning away, and preventing worship with punishment and care to follow truth. [4 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 96:13/q05/p2 He denies the Qur'an or God's Book and turns away from faith. — Muqātil says Abū Jahl rejects the Qur'an. Al-Ṭabarī says he denies the truth Muhammad brought and does not believe. Al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī specify faith or belief. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 96:13/q05/p3 He denies the Messenger and refuses to accept him. — Al-Māwardī gives this as a further possibility. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the unexpressed object of denial as the preceding praying servant and predicts Abū Jahl's denial when the Prophet calls him to Islam. [6 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:13/q05/p4 He denies the truth or religion and turns from right guidance or the reminder. — Al-Qushayrī names religion and guidance; al-Wāḥidī names Islam and the reminder. Al-Kashshāf names true religion; al-Ṭabrisī says he refuses even to listen. Muḥammad Asad attributes the turning to arrogance that prevents facing truth. Corpus Coranicum compares the paired verbs at 92:16, 79:21–22 and 75:32, also noting 95:7, 73:11 and 88:23. [20 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الجمهور); ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 96:13/q05/p5 He denies that the praying servant is rightly guided and turns from accepting piety or prayer's truth. — Al-Biqāʿī grounds guidance in service to the universally acknowledged Lord; desire drives the denial. He says turning forcibly redirects an original disposition toward piety and damages inward and outward conduct. Al-Ālūsī calls the actual premise denial of prayer's truth. [5 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI]

## 96:13/q06 How do the repeated أَرَءَيْتَ clauses work grammatically?  [أَرَءَيْتَ إِن · grammar]
turns on: Whether arayta governs two objects, whether later occurrences repeat or govern independently, and whether the rhetorical question needs supplied syntax.
- 96:13/q06/p1 The later questions substitute for or repeat the first; the conditionals and the statement that God sees complete the ellipsis. — Al-Akhfash calls the second clause appositional and 96:14 its predicate. Al-Ṭabarī treats the three questions as substitutions about the one forbidding, denying, and turning. Al-Qurṭubī reports the later questions standing for the first. Al-Zamakhsharī says the repetition is emphatic, with the first relative and conditional as objects and one conditional's answer supplied by the other. Al-Bayḍāwī treats the condition as second object and later answer as supply. [25 notes: AKHFASH; TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-; BINTSHATI-]
- 96:13/q06/p2 The first question takes the relative clause as object; the later first objects are omitted pronouns referring back, with the final question about God's seeing as the third question's second object. — Abū Ḥayyān insists a second object must be interrogative and cites 53:33, 53:35, 19:77–78 and 56:58–59; the omitted pronouns can refer to forbider or servant according to antecedent. He rejects syntactic competition because clauses cannot be pronominalized. Al-Ālūsī likewise distinguishes semantic ellipsis from tanāzuʿ, illustrating with a report about Zayd. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls verse 13 a fresh threat. [21 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN+; ALUSI(الرضي)-]
- 96:13/q06/p3 The first question is visual and later questions intellectual; their conditionals fill second-object positions, while the final unjoined question marks actual occurrence and threat. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative and says the earlier supposition broadens the case and borrows its answer from the later one. [5 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 96:13/q06/p4 All three questions concern intellectual seeing, but the third independently opposes the first condition. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the first conditional has an omitted answer, the second repeats the first question, and the third states the threatening response; the final clause is unjoined to intensify rebuke and mark truth of the second condition. [5 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 96:13/q06/p5 The interrogative challenge needs no second object, explicit or inferred. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes challenge from knowledge-based vision. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says repeated arayta invites reflection on the warning, citing multiple Qur'anic questions; verse 14 itself is the focal lesson. She cautions against imposing school grammar when Qur'anic style differs. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; BINTSHATI]

## 96:13/q07 How does the condition in إِن connect to the next question?  [إِن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the apodosis is implicit, expressed as the following question without fa, or replaced by an independent warning; grammarians dispute the required fa.
- 96:13/q07/p1 Its answer is omitted; the question that God sees indicates the omitted response. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the closing question supplies broad completions and one threat suffices. Al-Ālūsī's principal parsing infers both omitted answers from 96:14, though he notes a view using arayta as cue. Al-Biqāʿī's iḥtibāk infers being astray from earlier guidance and divine sight from its later mention, rather than tanāzuʿ. Islāhī says suppressed apodosis conveys unspeakable severity in classical Arabic. Elmalılı prefers omission and independent resumption. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI(Keşşâf)+]
- 96:13/q07/p2 The answer is indicated by the repeated 'Have you seen?' itself. — Al-Ālūsī records the cue as prospective reference to the statement still to come. [3 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]
- 96:13/q07/p3 The question 'Does he not know that God sees?' directly answers the condition without fa. — Al-Zamakhsharī uses 6:47 for this construction; al-Raḍī explicitly permits an interrogative answer without fa and compares al-Anʿām. Al-Khafājī says conditional and interrogative can jointly fill object and answer slots, directly or by implication; al-Raḍī and al-Damāmīnī connect this with Sībawayh's preferred analysis. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 6:40, 6:46–47, 28:71–72, 11:88, 41:52 and 10:50 as other such Qur'anic questions. [14 notes: KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الخفاجي,الرضي,الزمخشري); IBNASHUR(صاحب الكشاف); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR(الرضي)+; ALUSI(أبو حيان,الدماميني)-; IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)-]
- 96:13/q07/p4 The final question is an independent resumption; an implicit answer says God knows and will punish the denier. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the majority's required fa, including al-Zamakhsharī in al-Mufaṣṣal. Elmalılı prefers this to the Kashshāf's expressed consequences. Al-Bursevī says the second condition stands separately because denial and turning occur independently and bring a threat. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Keşşâf)+]
- 96:13/q07/p5 Verse 14 gives the warning itself, without a hidden conditional answer. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Qur'anic interrogative patterns need not be forced into inferred school-grammar positions; she offers multiple conditional-interrogative examples without fa. [3 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 96:13/q08 Whose actions do the conditional pronouns describe?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding guidance clause shares the forbider as subject or shifts to the worshipper; the denial clause consistently points back to the forbider in these analyses.
- 96:13/q08/p1 The verbs of denial and turning, as well as knowing in verse 14, describe the prohibiter. — Al-Ālūsī's principal parsing carries the unstated subjects of the surrounding verbs back to the forbider. Al-Farrāʾ instead assigns the earlier 'was' and 'commanded' to the praying servant while keeping denial and turning for the prohibiter. Ibn ʿĀshūr says context directs the pronouns to different antecedents. [10 notes: ALUSI(الفراء); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل)]
- 96:13/q08/p2 Under the alternative address, the pronouns in both conditional clauses refer to the forbider. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this with the view that addresses the unbeliever. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN(قيل)]

## 96:13/q09 What contrast do the linked questions make?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conditions compare worshipper and forbider, test the forbider's professed state, or mark an escalation of rebuke.
- 96:13/q09/p1 They marvel that the guided, pious worshipper is forbidden by a denier who turns away. — Al-Farrāʾ presents the conditional form as an astonishing juxtaposition. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī describe the worshipper's prayer and call to piety against the opponent's denial. Abū Ḥayyān adds that prayer reforms the Prophet and enjoining piety reforms others, both troubling Abū Jahl. The omitted conclusion can be 'How astonishing this is'; aw can function as waw. Al-Bayḍāwī says prayer alone is an active call, or the ban may cover other matters, contrasting self-perfection in worship with perfecting others through invitation. [27 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء,قيل)]
- 96:13/q09/p2 The conditions test the forbider's own supposed guidance against his actual denial, with ironic concession to his claim of piety. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī ask whether idol worship could be guidance or piety in the forbider's view; God sees both guidance and error. The majority, as reported by al-Ālūsī, say forbidding God's worship and commanding idols makes the supposed guidance impossible, so the second condition repeats and reverses the taunt. Al-Nasafī says he indeed denies the truth. Al-Biqāʿī describes a state opposed to guidance. [17 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI; ALUSI(الجمهور)]
- 96:13/q09/p3 The three questions escalate from condemning a ban on prayer, to condemning it even under claimed guidance, to condemning the man's actual denial and turning away. — Al-Ālūsī says the separated clauses intensify censure and warning and make each point independent; the future form evokes a past strange scene. Al-Bursevī says the second condition independently carries the threat. Qutb says denial and turning add a worse offense to the original prohibition. [11 notes: BIQAI; ALUSI; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 96:13/q09/p4 It asks whether the wealthy sensible opponent should seek guidance and command piety rather than disbelieve and prohibit worship. — The Imam, reported by al-Ālūsī, sees lament over abandoning high ranks for low ones. Ibn al-Anbārī asks whether the prohibiter is right. [2 notes: ALUSI(الإمام); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]
- 96:13/q09/p5 The questions mark successive losses: tyranny from self-sufficiency, forfeited guidance and piety, then deserved punishment and distance from God. — ʿIṣām al-Dīn gives this sequence, with verse 14 a severe threat. Al-Ālūsī calls the elaboration far-fetched and leaves the intention to God. [5 notes: ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-]

## 96:13/q10 What warning does denial and turning away carry?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize either divine surveillance and repayment or the moral absurdity of opposing clear guidance.
- 96:13/q10/p1 God sees the forbider and will requite or punish him for denial, turning away, and obstructing worship. — Al-Jishumī connects threatened punishment to following truth. Al-Zamakhsharī says God sees and repays both parties; if Abū Jahl persists, God seizes his forelock and drags him to Hell. Ibn Kathīr adds God hears. Al-Qāshānī says God sees either state. Al-Rāzī argues evident proofs and sound reason should deter stubborn obstruction. Islāhī cites 92:15–16 on the wretched entering Hell; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the threat. [14 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:13/q10/p2 The question exposes the strangeness and moral evil of opposing evident guidance. — Al-Biqāʿī says denial against clear evidence is plainly evil and follows desire, while turning away damages disposition and conduct. Al-Ṭabarī concludes the prohibiting denier must know God sees him. Al-Rāzī calls the conduct stubborn. [8 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; TAB; TAB-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 96:13/q11 What occasion involving Abū Jahl is reported for this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the narrated encounter and detail of angelic intervention.
- 96:13/q11/p1 Abū Jahl forbade or threatened to trample Muhammad while he prayed; the passage was revealed concerning this confrontation. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports Abū Jahl's boast of his council and says angels would seize him if summoned. Qatāda identifies Muhammad as guided and Abū Jahl as denier, with his council his clan. Al-Suyūṭī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's report through the lying forelock. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the sequence suggests public preaching and prayer, while allowing general application. [10 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(ابن عباس); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI]
- 96:13/q11/p2 Abū Jahl approached to trample the Prophet but recoiled from a trench of fire, terror, and wings. — Abū Hurayra's report says the Prophet declared that angels would seize him limb by limb. Al-Baghawī notes uncertainty whether the revelation reference belongs to Abū Hurayra's narration or another source. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 96:13/q12 When and how was this passage placed in the sura?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the passage was later occasioned by Abū Jahl and inserted after the opening, or continues the sura's generic description of humanity.
- 96:13/q12/p1 The remainder of the sura came later about Abū Jahl, and the Prophet was directed to place these verses after the opening. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this as al-Rāzī's first solution; she finds both his accounts possible and takes the sequence as following public preaching and prayer. [2 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 96:13/q12/p2 The generic human in the opening continues into the warnings about transgression and forbidding worship. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's second solution: despite creation and blessings, people forget their return, transgress, and forbid worship when they should follow guidance and enjoin piety; she considers it possible. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي)]

## 96:13/q13 What theological point is drawn from these acts?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · theology]
- 96:13/q13/p1 Denial and turning away are acts of human beings, not acts created by God. — Al-Jishumī draws this from the threat directed to the disbeliever. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 96:13/q14 How broadly does the ban on forbidding prayer apply?  [* · law]
- 96:13/q14/p1 The warning reaches everyone who prohibits prayer itself, but an objection to prayer in a usurped place or disliked time can concern that accompanying circumstance. — Al-Ālūsī distinguishes prayer as such from a concomitant quality, even though the occasion concerns Abū Jahl. [1 notes: ALUSI]

## 96:13/q15 Which other verses are linked to the paired acts of denial and turning?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰٓ · links]
- 96:13/q15/p1 The pair recurs at 92:16, 79:21–22, and 75:32; 92:15–16 also supplies the denier's fate in Hell. — Corpus Coranicum notes the recurring verbs and related discussions at 95:7, 73:11, and 88:23. Islāhī cites 92:15–16 for the wretched person's fate. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 96:13/q16 What recitation merit is reported for the sura?  [* · sciences]
- 96:13/q16/p1 A reciter receives reward as though he recited the entire set of shorter suras. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this as a prophetic report. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 96:13/q17 Can a conditional clause be the second object of أَرَءَيْتَ?  [أَرَءَيْتَ إِن · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second object must itself be interrogative or oath-like, or may be supplied by a conditional and its question together.
- 96:13/q17/p1 No: its second object must be an interrogative or oath clause. — Abū Ḥayyān and other grammarians give this objection; al-Irshād includes an oath-like clause among the permitted forms. [3 notes: ALUSI(أبو حيان)]
- 96:13/q17/p2 Yes: the conditional and following question can together occupy or indicate that second-object position. — Al-Khafājī replies to the objection in al-Ālūsī's account; al-Raḍī and al-Damāmīnī cite Sībawayh's preferred analysis against the restriction. [4 notes: ALUSI(أبو حيان,الخفاجي,الرضي)]
