# 7:194: 13 questions

## 7:194/q01 Who are those invoked besides God?  [ٱلَّذِينَ تَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address fixes its referent to physical idols, to angel worshippers, or to every object of worship; al-Wāḥidī invokes 7:195 and historical worship against the angel reading.
- 7:194/q01/p1 The immediate referents are the idols worshipped by the polytheists. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, the exegetes reported by al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and others identify idols. Al-Wāḥidī prefers this because the contemporary polytheists worshipped idols and 7:195 describes beings without functional limbs. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿAbda ibn al-Ṭabīb for the relative phrasing. Iṣlāḥī says statues represented angels, jinn, and stars; al-Bursawī calls them idols named gods. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 7:194/q01/p2 The referents are angels worshipped by a group of Khuzaʿa. — ʿAṭāʾ reports Ibn ʿAbbās identifying angels in al-Wāḥidī; Muqātil specifies Khuzaʿa in al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Abū Ḥayyān. Al-Baghawī prefers idols; al-Wāḥidī objects from contemporary worship and the limbs in 7:195. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 7:194/q01/p3 The wording reaches any being or object worshipped apart from God, including saints and images. — Al-Qāshānī includes humans and other beings; Asad includes living or dead saints, idols, fetishes, and physical or mental images; Kur'an Yolu extends the point to polytheists throughout history. Iṣlāḥī explains that Arab statues represented angels, jinn, and stars. [4 notes: QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 7:194/q02 What does تَدْعُونَ mean here?  [تَدْعُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the first use of calling denotes worship, the designation 'gods,' or an actual request; al-Biqāʿī explains why invocation can stand for worship.
- 7:194/q02/p1 It means worshipping those beings. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, and al-Ālūsī give this sense. Al-Biqāʿī says invocation accompanies worship, or that inability to answer invocation proves unfitness for worship. Al-Ālūsī says the object required by this interpretation is omitted. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:194/q02/p2 It means naming or calling them gods. — Al-Wāḥidī links this to 7:191; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Nasafī give the designation. Al-Ālūsī says the implied object 'gods' is omitted. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:194/q02/p3 It is invocation or a request addressed to them. — Al-Ālūsī lists invoking as a possible sense; al-Biqāʿī ties such calling to worship. Al-Jishumī defines duʿāʾ generally as requesting an act through an imperative, prohibition, or statement such as 'May God forgive him.' [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 7:194/q03 What does مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ mean?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · meaning]
- 7:194/q03/p1 It means other than God. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī state the gloss directly. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:194/q04 In what sense are they عِبَادٌ أَمْثَالُكُمْ?  [عِبَادٌ أَمْثَالُكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'servants like you' states literal shared creation and ownership, grants a sarcastic hypothetical equality, invokes human shape, or stresses shared lack of independent power; 7:195 tests how far the likeness extends.
- 7:194/q04/p1 They are created, owned, and governed by God, as their worshippers are. — Al-Ṭabarī calls them their Lord's property; al-Kalbī and al-Ḥasan stress ownership or creation; al-Akhfash, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī stress subjection, with al-Ṭabrisī deriving servitude from subduing and citing 26:22. Qutrub and al-Tabrīzī say 'created.' Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Shawkānī combine these traits. Ibn ʿĀshūr traces ʿabd from a person owned to every creature, citing 2:178 and 19:93, and prefers metaphorical concord in calling the idols servants. Ibn Jinnī cites 55:6 and 17:44 for created things' glorification; Ibn Kathīr adds that humans hear, see, and act while idols cannot. Abū Ḥayyān insists the next verse's missing organs cannot cancel likeness in creation or ownership. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,الكلبي,قطرب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI(الأخفش,الحسن,الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 7:194/q04/p2 Even if the idols were alive and rational, they would still merely be fellow servants with no right to worship. — Al-Zamakhsharī reads a sarcastic concession, followed by 7:195 denying that idols even possess human capacities; al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān report it. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī connect the supposition with carved human figures. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Kashshāf. Abū Ḥayyān rejects only the claim that the following question cancels all likeness, since likeness in being created remains. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 7:194/q04/p3 The comparison rebukes worshippers by setting their admitted weakness beside the idols' still greater helplessness. — Al-Biqāʿī says humans surpass idols in life and reason, while a deity could not be like its worshipper. Al-Ālūsī says people admit their own weakness yet attribute power to visibly weaker idols. Elmalılı calls the objects enslaved, senseless structures and says the worshippers themselves resemble them; he cautions against taking human slaves as the intended referent. Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr detail the idols' lack of human faculties. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:194/q04/p4 They are called servants because makers shaped them like humans. — Al-Ḥaddādī gives the human shape as the reason for the term; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī mention carved human form in their hypothetical interpretation. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الحدادي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:194/q04/p5 Every invoked being shares human inability to act independently of God. — Al-Qāshānī includes human and nonhuman objects; al-Ālūsī follows that reading. Asad includes saints and images. The condition of dependence on divine enabling explains the challenge for al-Qāshānī. [3 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 7:194/q05 Why do the pronouns and plurals treat the idols as rational persons?  [ٱلَّذِينَ عِبَادٌ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the human grammar directly mirrors the idolaters' claims or follows established Arabic usage for idols.
- 7:194/q05/p1 The wording follows worshippers' belief that idols understand, discern, and can harm or benefit. — Ibn al-Anbārī uses the worshippers' claims to license الذين, masculine plurals, and عباد; al-Rāzī says their claim of benefit and harm makes the verse address idols as intelligent; al-Qurṭubī gives the same explanation. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر بن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 7:194/q05/p2 Arabic customarily used rational plural forms for idols, so the verse follows that usage. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the usual human sense of ʿabd from the Arabs' established rational plurals for idols. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:194/q06 How is إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ... عِبَادٌ أَمْثَالُكُمْ read and parsed?  [إِنَّ عِبَادٌ أَمْثَالُكُمْ · readings]
turns on: The consonants and case of إن and عباد/أمثالكم, whether light إن can govern as a negative or emphatic particle, and whether affirmation of likeness can coexist with a denial of human capacities.
- 7:194/q06/p1 The common reading has emphatic إِنَّ and nominative عِبَادٌ, affirming that the invoked are servants like humans. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority reading; al-Samīn makes the relative pronoun the subject of إن and عباد its predicate. Ibn Jinnī contrasts it with Saʿīd's reading and says created things glorify God, citing 55:6 and 17:44. Abū Ḥayyān argues that the affirmative cannot be contradicted by a second divine report. [4 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:194/q06/p2 Saʿīd ibn Jubayr reads light إن and accusative عبادا أمثالكم; negative إن works like Ḥijāzī ما and denies human likeness. — Ibn Jinnī calls the negative government's licensing weak; Ibn ʿAṭiyya records Sībawayh's preference for nominative, al-Mubarrad's allowance of accusative, and al-Kisāʾī's claimed restriction to إلا, citing 67:2. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report support from al-Kisāʾī, most Kufans except al-Farrāʾ, Ibn al-Sarrāj, al-Fārisī, and Ibn Jinnī, against al-Farrāʾ and most Basrans; reports on Sībawayh and al-Mubarrad vary. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Biqāʿī defend attestations in prose and poetry; al-Samīn cites a line with مستوليا. Al-Itqān gives negative إن in nominal and verbal clauses and cites 9:32, 58:2, 10:18, 4:117, 86:4, while noting dispute over إلا or لما. Critics say the reading opposes the muṣḥaf, preferred grammar, and affirmative meaning; al-Naḥḥās and Abū Jaʿfar give these objections, also citing 35:40. Abū Ḥayyān replies that it comes from a notable Successor and Rabīʿa spelling could omit the accusative alif; al-Samīn rejects the spelling and grammatical objections. Abū Ḥayyān himself rejects the negative interpretation on semantic grounds; al-Samīn, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ālūsī answer that likeness can be affirmed in creation but denied in capacities, while al-Zamakhsharī makes the affirmation concessive. Idols are stone or wood, and less rational than humans, in Ibn Jinnī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and al-Qurṭubī's accounts. [34 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; KASHSHAF-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العباس المبرد,الكسائي,سعيد بن جبير,سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير,المفسرون,النحاس); SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفتح ابن جني,الشيخ,سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(الزمخشري,سعيد بن جبير); ITQAN(الكسائي والمبرد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جني,سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:194/q06/p3 The light إن remains emphatic and operative; an unusual construction makes the accusatives its predicate or both its nominal parts. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this parsing, citing ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa's verse and grammarians who allow accusative predicates of إن and its sisters; opponents reparse the poetry. Al-Samīn's Shaykh cites إن حراسنا أسدا, the reading of 11:111, and a poetic line interpreted with ترى رواجعا. Al-Samīn says both light إن operating and double accusative government are contested, and al-Ālūsī calls the analysis rare and weak. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جماعة من النحاة,عمر بن أبي ربيعة); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:194/q06/p4 With light إن and accusatives, an omitted verb governs the accusative and preserves the affirmative sense. — Abū Ḥayyān offers this as an opponents' answer, comparing poetry about wishing youth's days back; al-Samīn inventories it as a third parsing. Al-Ālūsī reports an ungoverned إن with an omitted predicate. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:194/q06/p5 In a variant, light إن has accusative عباد as a circumstance and nominative أمثالكم as predicate. — Abū Ḥayyān places the circumstance on an omitted pronoun in the relative clause. Al-Samīn says إن is ungoverned, الذين its subject, and أمثالكم its predicate, meaning created and owned like humans; he calls an alternative accusative relative pronoun weak because light إن rarely governs. Al-Ālūsī also transmits the form. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:194/q06/p6 Another variant has heavy إن with accusative عباد as a circumstance and nominative أمثالكم as predicate. — Abū al-Baqāʾ transmits it through al-Samīn, who compares the preceding parse; al-Ālūsī reports a heavy anna form. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:194/q06/p7 إن is interrogative with an omitted hamza, asking whether the idols are servants like humans. — The Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm says 64:6 and 26:22 show questions with unexpressed hamza; the resulting denial belittles idols, which lack reason, hearing, and sight, before testing their power. He rejects treating it as a plain assertion. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم)]

## 7:194/q07 What are the worshippers challenged to ask the invoked beings to do?  [فَٱدْعُوهُمْ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: What action the second 'call' seeks: practical aid, reward for worship, any observable response, following the callers, or a demonstration of divine capacity.
- 7:194/q07/p1 Call on them for benefit, rescue, or removal of harm and see whether they answer. — The people of meaning reported by al-Wāḥidī say benefit and removal of harm are impossible; al-Ṭabrisī specifies urgent needs. Al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī give benefit or harm; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers a plea for aid and victory, citing al-Māzinī's verse on rescuers summoned without asking which battle or place. Ibn al-Jawzī includes reward. Elmalılı says unanswered prayers make seeking help futile. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 7:194/q07/p2 Worship them and test whether they reward, requite, save, intercede, or support you. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Qurṭubī; al-Wāḥidī's al-Wajīz adds benefit. The conditional claim can be expectation of benefit, reward, intercession, or support. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:194/q07/p3 Summon them and see whether they can answer at all, in any matter. — Muqātil and al-Baghawī cast the response as proof of divinity. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a test of the prior description; Ibn al-Jawzī glosses 'let them answer.' Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ālūsī demand an observable reply. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 35:14; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the challenge metonymically shows both idols' inability to respond and worshippers' inability to produce such an exchange. Al-Biqāʿī adds any benefit or a comparable surah; Iṣlāḥī casts it as Quraysh summoning their gods for rescue, parallel to 2:23. [13 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:194/q07/p4 Invite them to follow you, then let them respond. — An anonymous opinion in al-Qurṭubī supplies the omitted 'to follow you' and conditions response on the claim that they are gods. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:194/q07/p5 Ask them to produce what God has not enabled them to provide. — Al-Qāshānī makes the failure a test of attributed independent efficacy; al-Biqāʿī specifies answering, benefiting, or producing a comparable surah. [2 notes: QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:194/q08 Why command the idols to respond when they cannot?  [فَٱدْعُوهُمْ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لَكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 7:194/q08/p1 It is a taunting test that exposes inability to answer and thus unfitness for worship. — Al-Wāḥidī's people of meaning call the lām an imperative of incapacitation; al-Ṭabrisī compares 'bring your proof' in 27:64. Al-Rāzī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Study Quran call it a challenge or mockery. Al-Ṭabarī reasons that one unable to hear and answer cannot harm or benefit, whereas one who does can reward, punish, or help. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that addressing absent idols through hearers requires the hearers to transmit the command, which the idols cannot even receive. Al-Qāshānī tests alleged independent efficacy. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:194/q09 How does the lām in فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ work?  [فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The notes distinguish the lām's commanding function from its reduced pronunciation.
- 7:194/q09/p1 It is the imperative lām issuing a third-person command to the absent idols. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this is the regular form for commanding an absent party; through the hearers, the command itself displays the idols' inability to hear. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī identify its rhetorical use as an incapacitating challenge. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI]
- 7:194/q09/p2 Its original vowel is kasra, which is dropped for ease. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes the loss of kasra to its heaviness. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:194/q10 What claim does إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ put to the test?  [إِن كُنتُمْ صَٰدِقِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the challenged claim is the deities' status, particular promised benefits, or independent causal power.
- 7:194/q10/p1 That the invoked beings are gods entitled to worship. — Muqātil, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Qurṭubī's anonymous ellipsis link truthfulness to divinity. Ibn ʿĀshūr says failure to obtain a response leaves worshippers unable to establish that claim. Al-Burhān says language of God's 'partners' reports the idolaters' claim rather than affirming it, citing 2:165. [9 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 7:194/q10/p2 That the idols can benefit, harm, reward, intercede, or do what worshippers cannot. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī lists benefit, reward, intercession, and support; al-Qurṭubī speaks of benefit from worship. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say the claim is that the idols can act where their worshippers cannot. Iṣlāḥī says rescue of Quraysh would vindicate their claim. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:194/q10/p3 That the alleged partners have any independent efficacy. — Al-Qāshānī and al-Ālūsī challenge worshippers to find any matter in which calling them produces an effect beyond what God enables. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:194/q11 What theological conclusion follows from their failure to answer?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the conclusion focuses on idols' unfitness for worship or extends incapacity and dependence on God's decree to all creatures.
- 7:194/q11/p1 Created, powerless objects cannot deserve worship; benefit and harm belong to God. — Al-Ṭabarī calls their worship shirk and unbelief and reasons from inability to hear or answer to inability to help. Al-Rāzī says three conclusive proofs lead a rational person to the powerful, knowing, living, wise God, and compares Abraham's rebuke in 19:42; Abū Ḥayyān makes the same comparison. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the objects lack the knowledge and power presupposed by worship, linking 7:191–193. Iṣlāḥī says people receive God's favors yet thank powerless partners. Kur'an Yolu says the objects are unconscious, ignorant, cannot guide or hear, and that there is no deity besides God; al-Sāmarrāʾī lists their inability to create, help, hear, see, or move against God's hearing and knowledge, citing 7:191, 7:198, 37:95. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:194/q11/p2 All created beings, including humans, can affect nothing independently of God's decree. — Al-Qāshānī applies the verse to every invoked human or nonhuman and cites the Prophet's counsel to Ibn ʿAbbās: ask and seek help only from God; no community can benefit or harm except as God wrote, for the pens have been lifted and pages dried. Al-Ālūsī repeats the point that every invoked being shares incapacity. [5 notes: QASHANI(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام); ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:194/q12 How does this verse connect with its context and other challenges?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is the immediately preceding argument, a change of addressee, or an analogous Qurʾanic challenge.
- 7:194/q12/p1 It continues the preceding proof that idols cannot follow a call, guide, benefit, or harm. — Abū Ḥayyān says the sentence confirms the preceding proof. Al-Ālūsī links it to 7:193. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives two contextual readings: following an earlier address to polytheists, it starts a direct address with emphasis and a third-to-second-person shift to counter denial of servitude; if the earlier address was to the Prophet and believers, it independently turns to polytheists. On another reading it explains why idols cannot follow guidance: they are created servants. Al-Sāmarrāʾī ties the Aʿrāf passage to 7:191 and 7:198. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 7:194/q12/p2 It parallels other scriptural challenges and rebukes to false gods. — Al-Ṭabrisī compares 'bring your proof if truthful' at 27:64; al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān compare Abraham's rebuke at 19:42; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite nonresponse in 35:14; Iṣlāḥī compares the challenge in 2:23. Al-Burhān compares the wording about those called in al-Naḥl and al-Aʿrāf, reporting 'besides God' versus 'besides Him.' ## 7:194/q13 What scene involving the sun and moon is reported for this challenge?  [فَٱدْعُوهُمْ · reports]
- 7:194/q13/p1 At the Resurrection, the sun and moon and their worshippers will be brought before God, and the worshippers told to call on them. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr; al-Durr also transmits Saʿīd's report and includes 'if you are truthful.'
