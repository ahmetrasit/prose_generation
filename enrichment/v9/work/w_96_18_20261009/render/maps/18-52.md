# 18:52: 15 questions

## 18:52/q01 How is the day introduced and connected to the preceding passage?  [وَيَوْمَ · grammar]
- 18:52/q01/p1 An implicit command to remember governs yawm; the scene continues the earlier account. — Al-Samīn supplies udhkur. Al-Rāzī relates it to the earlier command concerning the angels. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it either to the command in 18:50 or, by implication, to 18:51: those denied a role in creation cannot help at judgment. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:52/q02 Who delivers the command on Judgment Day?  [يَقُولُ · referent]
turns on: Whether God's rebuke is direct or conveyed by angels.
- 18:52/q02/p1 God addresses the unbelievers, possibly through an intermediary. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify God as speaker; al-Ālūsī allows an intermediary. Bursawī prefers God speaking personally: divine reproach is plausible after His address to Iblis, and he invokes God's appearance in forms according to people's beliefs. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI+]
- 18:52/q02/p2 Angels deliver the command. — Bursawī reports this view, then prefers God Himself as speaker. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم); BURSEVI-]

## 18:52/q03 How is يقول read, and what supports each reading?  [يَقُولُ · readings]
turns on: Person of the verb, continuity with preceding first-person speech, and agreement with My partners.
- 18:52/q03/p1 Yaqūlu, He says, with third-person yāʾ. — Reported as the majority reading by Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, al-Thaʿlabī and later commentators. Ibn Khālawayh says it narrates God's command through the Prophet. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī find it apt with My partners rather than Our partners; al-Ṭabrisī prefers it. Al-Samīn relates it to God's name just before; al-Biqāʿī reads the shift as distancing false partners from God's presence, with 18:48 in view. [17 notes: IBNMUJAHID; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة); THALABI(حمزة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة ويحيى والأعمش وحمزة); TABRISI(حمزة); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI+]
- 18:52/q03/p2 Naqūlu, We say, with first-person nūn. — Ibn Mujāhid and al-Fārisī assign it to Ḥamza alone; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also names Ṭalḥa, Yaḥyā and al-Aʿmash, al-Qurṭubī names Yaḥyā and ʿĪsā, and al-Ālūsī adds Ibn Abī Laylā and Ibn Muqsam. Al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī cite the preceding first-person statement; al-Rāzī recalls God's earlier speech to the angels; al-Samīn cites mā ashhadtuhum; al-Biqāʿī notes continuity and majesty. Ibn Khālawayh and Qirāʾāt-ER say it makes God speak directly. [23 notes: IBNMUJAHID; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة); THALABI(حمزة); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(حمزة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة ويحيى والأعمش وحمزة); TABRISI(حمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة); BIQAI-FULL(حمزة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; TABRISI-]

## 18:52/q04 What is the purpose of commanding them to call?  [نَادُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the challenged response is aid and intercession or an affirmation of divinity.
- 18:52/q04/p1 They are challenged to seek rescue or intercession from their supposed partners. — Al-Naḥḥās allows repayment for worship as well as rescue. Ibn Kathīr calls it public rebuke and cites 6:94; al-Rāzī compares 40:47, Bursawī 14:21, and Study Quran 6:22, 16:27 and 28:62. Al-Ālūsī says their desperate attempt and its failure mock their delusion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they hope for aid and for the idols to come or answer labbaykum. [21 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 18:52/q04/p2 They are challenged to ask whether the beings are gods, but no affirmation comes. — Muqātil states they cannot answer as gods; al-Dāmghānī cites 2:68 for the interrogative force. [2 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI]

## 18:52/q05 Who are the claimed partners?  [شُرَكَآءِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ زَعَمْتُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is restricted to idols or jinn, or includes righteous beings falsely worshipped.
- 18:52/q05/p1 Any beings or things worshipped as divine partners, especially idols. — Al-Naḥḥās includes partners in divinity and worship; al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī allow everything worshipped besides God. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage broadens from Satan and jinn to common idols. Asad calls them beings imagined to share divinity and cites 6:22. Al-Wāḥidī says the claim was made in worldly life. [11 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 18:52/q05/p2 The jinn worshipped by the idolaters. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī identify them as jinn; al-Rāzī adds that the worshippers ask for intercession and aid. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 18:52/q05/p3 Iblis and his descendants. — Al-Ālūsī explains that obeying their whisperings made them allies, while also allowing the broader class of worshipped things. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:52/q05/p4 Honored angels, Jesus, Mary, ʿUzayr or other saintly figures whom sinners worshipped. — Al-Kashshāf names angels, ʿUzayr, Jesus and Mary. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran use this identification to explain a gulf from Hell to their exalted abode; Asad prefers a gulf to saintly people who never claimed divinity. Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects to putting all partners in Hell because prophets, angels and saints are included. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الثعالبي,بعضهم); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R, Z); ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Razi)+]

## 18:52/q06 Why are they called My partners, and what does claimed imply?  [شُرَكَآءِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ زَعَمْتُمْ · rhetoric]
- 18:52/q06/p1 The attribution quotes the polytheists' false claim to rebuke and shame them. — Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī and others explicitly deny divine endorsement. Al-Biqāʿī calls the command sarcastic; Ibn ʿĀshūr says putting partners before claimed sharpens the mockery. Al-Wāḥidī specifies the worldly claim; Abū Ḥayyān supplies the omitted objects of zaʿamtum as them to be My partners. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says zaʿm generally reports doubtful or false speech, at best assigning responsibility to its speaker, as in Sībawayh's attribution to al-Khalīl. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:52/q07 How is شركائي pronounced?  [شُرَكَآءِىَ · readings]
turns on: Length and articulation of the possessive ending.
- 18:52/q07/p1 The majority articulate or soften the hamza with the possessive yāʾ and a long form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes full and softened hamza; Abū Ḥayyān says both forms are possessive. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 18:52/q07/p2 Ibn Kathīr and Meccan readers use a shortened or opened final yāʾ form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes opening the final yāʾ; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report the short possessive form. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:52/q08 Is the summons and lack of response literal or figurative?  [فَدَعَوْهُمْ فَلَمْ يَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the narrated call occurs as an event and whether response means speech or effective aid.
- 18:52/q08/p1 They literally call, and the alleged partners neither answer in the needed sense nor help. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers the plain literal scene; Abū Ḥayyān calls it apparent. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the command exposes their falsehood and the immediate past-tense sequence makes failure seem instant. Ibn Kathīr compares 28:64, 46:5–6 and 19:81–82 on false gods becoming enemies; al-Biqāʿī says they disregard their worshippers. [13 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:52/q08/p2 Their recognition that lifeless objects are powerless is depicted as calling without response. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān present this as a possible figure, although each gives the literal account priority. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 18:52/q08/p3 Failure to respond means failure to rescue or benefit them, and does not exclude a formal spoken reply. — Bursawī cites 28:63 and draws a moral: obedience after death to the order to call cannot help; obedience mattered before death. Study Quran also allows abandonment or disavowal. [2 notes: BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 18:52/q09 What does بينَهُم refer to or denote?  [بَيْنَهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether bayn denotes a location between two groups or their former connection.
- 18:52/q09/p1 The interval between idolaters and their alleged partners. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent referent. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī report it; the valley or barrier readings commonly presuppose it. Al-Qurṭubī cites 10:28. Al-Bursawī prefers an intervening barrier that blocks intercession even if both parties share the Fire. [13 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن الأعرابي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك وابن عباس وابن الأعرابي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(أنس); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI+]
- 18:52/q09/p2 The separation of guided believers from misguided unbelievers. — Nawf and ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr speak of guidance and error; al-Bikālī distinguishes confessors of God's oneness from everyone else. Al-Wāḥidī questions derivation of mawbiq as barrier, not the identity of these parties. Ibn Kathīr cites 30:14, 30:43, 36:59 and 10:28–30. The two reported group descriptions are variants of this divide. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(البكالي,نوف البكالي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبدالله بن عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); BIQAI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر,عمرو البكالي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:52/q09/p3 The bond or solidarity that connected worshippers and worshipped in worldly life. — Al-Farrāʾ takes bayn as connection, corroborated by the nominative reading of 6:94; al-Sīrāfī agrees in al-Ālūsī. Their worldly solidarity becomes ruin in the next life. Al-Wāḥidī describes baynahum as an object referring to the polytheists; Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this overall reading. Al-Biqāʿī cites 10:28, 7:38, 16:86 and 29:25 for affection turning to rupture. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء والسيرافي); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 18:52/q10 What is the mawbiq made between them?  [مَّوْبِقًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Lexical derivation, whether the division is physical or relational, and whether the claimed partners can include honored beings outside Hell.
- 18:52/q10/p1 Destruction, destructive punishment, or a place or cause of ruin. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk repeatedly gloss destruction; al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq make it the torment God places between them. Ibn Qutayba cites 42:34 for sins destroying a person. Al-Māwardī cites poetry with abaq and mawbiq; al-Akhfash compares the pattern of mawʿid, al-Zajjāj reports wabiqu/yabiqu and prefers the first, and al-Kisāʾī gives two vowelings. Al-Samīn favors a verbal noun; al-Bayḍāwī allows place or verbal noun; al-Qāmūs includes place of ruin. Ibn Kathīr prefers contextual destruction while allowing a valley or barrier; al-Biqāʿī ties the root to forceful, enduring separation. [42 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة، الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,الضحاك); JISHUMI(الكسائي,ثعلب); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء والضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد ونوف البكالي وعكرمة وعطاء والضحاك وأبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, Q, R, Ṭ); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; ZAJJAJ+; TABATABAI-]
- 18:52/q10/p2 A valley in Hell, sometimes specified as deep and flowing with blood and pus. — Mujāhid, Anas and Ibn ʿUmar are repeatedly cited; Anas' version gives pus and blood, and Ibn ʿUmar's distinguishes guidance from error. Al-Kashshāf calls it a shared place of ruin; Kaʿb names Mawbiq among four punishing valleys with Ghaliz, Atham and Ghayy. Al-Itqān also lists Ghayy, Atham, Mawbiq, Saʿīr, Wayl, Sāʾil and Suḥāq as Hell valleys. Al-Thaʿlabī adds boiling fluid as a reported gloss. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects identifying the place as Hell because honored beings may be among the supposed partners. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس، مجاهد، عبد الله بن عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو وأنس بن مالك ومجاهد); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أنس بن مالك ومجاهد); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك وابن عباس وابن الأعرابي,مجاهد ونوف البكالي وعكرمة وعطاء والضحاك وأبو عبيدة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر وأنس ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك,قتادة); DURR-FULL(أنس,كعب,مجاهد); ITQAN(أنس بن مالك,كعب); BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, Q, R, Ṭ); TABATABAI-]
- 18:52/q10/p3 A deep valley separating people of guidance from people of error. — Qatāda transmits from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr through Nawf or ʿAmr al-Bakkālī; another wording separates Muslims from all others on Resurrection Day. Al-Ṭabrisī also attributes the ravine to Mujāhid and Qatāda. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(نوف البكالي); TABRISI(مجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عبدالله بن عمرو); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); BIQAI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر,عمرو البكالي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:52/q10/p4 A river of fire with great dark snakes on its banks. — ʿIkrima's report makes the snakes as large as pack animals or mules; when they move toward the condemned, people flee into the Fire. Al-Itqān distinguishes this river gloss from valley reports. [6 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد ونوف البكالي وعكرمة وعطاء والضحاك وأبو عبيدة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ITQAN(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]

Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran envisage Hell's sinners far from angels, Jesus or other honored figures in the highest gardens; al-Thaʿālibī calls it a distant barzakh. Bursawī prefers a barrier blocking intercession even where both sides enter Fire. Al-Wāḥidī objects that he knows no derivation for barrier. - 18:52/q10/p6 Enmity between the worshippers and the worshipped, itself ruinous. — Al-Ḥasan is repeatedly cited; al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī cite poetry on excessive love and destructive hatred, and al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī compare ʿUmar's warning. Al-Biqāʿī cites 29:25 and other passages for worldly affection becoming mutual hostility. [17 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL(عمر رضي الله عنه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري); BIQAI-FULL(الحسن البصري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ, Z)]
- 18:52/q10/p7 An appointed meeting, gathering, or assembly. — Abū ʿUbayda gives an appointment or appointed destruction; al-Rabīʿ gives a gathering place. Ibn ʿAṭiyya regards the meeting-place gloss as weak. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الربيع); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد ونوف البكالي وعكرمة وعطاء والضحاك وأبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الربيع بن أنس); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

Al-Jishumī relates it to a hadith on detention at the bridge because of sins. [6 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عرفة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الربيع بن أنس); ALUSI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,بعضهم)]
- 18:52/q10/p9 Their worldly connection itself becomes their destruction in the afterlife. — Al-Farrāʾ's explanation takes bayn as connection; al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Bursawī cite the variant nominative reading of 6:94. Ibn ʿAṭiyya judges this the strongest account and parses the connection as an object of made. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); BURSEVI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء والسيرافي); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:52/q10/p10 The supposed relationship between idolaters and partners is annihilated, leaving a gulf. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers destruction of the relationship rather than either party; the distant call signals separation and he cites 6:94 and 10:28. This also explains why honored angels and prophets need not be placed in Hell. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:52/q10/p11 A divide of unreality between sinners and imagined divine beings. — Asad presents this as one possible interpretation, while finding the separation from real saintly figures more probable. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Razi)-]

## 18:52/q11 How does جعلنا govern موبقا and بينهم?  [وَجَعَلْنَا بَيْنَهُم مَّوْبِقًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The sense and transitivity of jaʿalnā and whether bayn denotes location or connection.
- 18:52/q11/p1 Jaʿalnā means made or turned and takes two objects; mawbiq and baynahum serve as object and complement. — Al-Samīn allows mawbiq as first object with preposed baynahum second; al-Ālūsī likewise if the verb means turned. Abū Ḥayyān says an adverbial baynahum is second object, whereas al-Farrāʾ's connection sense makes it first. Al-Wāḥidī reports Abū Isḥāq's adverbial bayn and verbal-noun mawbiq. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:52/q11/p2 Jaʿalnā means created or placed and takes one object, mawbiq; baynahum is attached or circumstantial. — Al-Samīn allows the phrase to attach to or describe mawbiq; al-Ālūsī says it is fronted for the verse ending when jaʿalnā means created. ## 18:52/q12 What does the scene teach and how does it connect to related verses?  [* · links]
- 18:52/q12/p1 The powerless partners expose false divinity and leave worshippers bereft on Judgment Day. — Al-Qushayrī says idols neither benefit, harm nor help, ending the delusion of nearness. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that inability to benefit disproves divinity and brings despair; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls this the third reminder, exposing the broken bond. Ibn Kathīr cites 28:64, 46:5–6 and 19:81–82; al-Biqāʿī cites 10:28, 7:38, 16:86 and 29:25 for mutual hostility; Study Quran adds 6:22, 16:27, 28:62, 6:94 and 28:63. Al-Iṣlāḥī sees the deprivation pictured by a pit. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 18:52/q13 Why is the lām in لهم open before an attached pronoun?  [لَهُمْ · grammar]
- 18:52/q13/p1 The pronoun-attached prepositional lām is opened because it cannot be confused with nominative emphasis lām. — Sībawayh contrasts the ordinary kasra, used to distinguish a preposition from emphasis lām as in inna hādhā la-ʿalā, with the open form before a pronoun. He compares attached-object forms such as aʿṭaytukumūhu with overt objects but says they are not identical; Yūnus reports aʿṭaytukumhu and aʿṭaytukumhā by overt-noun analogy, while the usual pronominal form is better known. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس)]

## 18:52/q14 What nearby lexical and textual excursuses accompany this verse in the notes?  [* · meaning]

- 18:52/q14/p4 Al-Biqāʿī's extended root permutations relate mawbiq to enclosure, gathering, permanence, concealment and ruin. — For q-w-b he traces circular holes, eggs, hatching, shells and calamities; qāb may mean approaching, fleeing or splitting, with qāba qawsayn as a bow measure.
