# 40:50: 29 questions

## 40:50/q01 Who speaks in this exchange, and whom do they address?  [قَالُوٓا۟ قَالُوا۟ · referent]
- 40:50/q01/p1 The keepers of Hell address its condemned inhabitants, who answer them. — Muqātil identifies the messengers as coming from the inmates' own people. Al-Kashshāf identifies the keepers as attendants charged with torment. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that the plural messengers and mention of guardians point to Hell's people generally, rather than only Pharaoh's family, who had Moses alone. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:50/q02 When do the keepers reply?  [قَالُوٓا۟ · reports]
- 40:50/q02/p1 They answer after a delay. — Al-Nasafī calls it a long interval; Bursevī likewise notes a delayed answer. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 40:50/q03 How does the opening أَوَلَمْ attach to the preceding speech?  [أَوَلَمْ · grammar]
turns on: Which implicit response supplies the coordinate of the waw.
- 40:50/q03/p1 The interrogative hamza introduces a question whose waw coordinates it with an understood prior thought: 'Did you not take notice of this?' — Bursevī supplies the omitted sense and identifies the hamza as interrogative. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 40:50/q03/p2 The waw joins the keepers' words to the inmates' request by prompting the reply the inmates ought to have given. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this ʿaṭf al-talqīn and compares 2:124. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:50/q04 Why do the keepers ask whether messengers brought proofs?  [أَوَلَمْ تَكُ تَأْتِيكُمْ رُسُلُكُم بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ · interpretation]
- 40:50/q04/p1 They compel an admission of established guidance and rebuke the inmates for rejecting it and wasting the time when prayer could help. — Al-Kashshāf says the question forces proof and recalls neglected conditions of answered prayer. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī call it rebuke; al-Bayḍāwī adds admission of evidence. Al-Ṭabrisī says they stubbornly resisted proofs of unity and prophethood. Al-Biqāʿī says the question exposes why they are unworthy of a hearing: they failed to use reason. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say the proofs warned of the evil end of disbelief and sins and established guilt; al-Ālūsī compares 39:71. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it recalls everlasting punishment and their blameworthy choice. The Study Quran compares 6:130, 39:71, and 67:8–9. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:50/q05 What is the implicit subject of تَكُ?  [تَكُ · grammar]
- 40:50/q05/p1 The omitted subject of kāna is the story or occasion, explained by the following clause about messengers. — Al-Ṭabrisī supplies al-qiṣṣa; al-Nasafī says the story or occasion. [2 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL]

## 40:50/q06 What does the added verb تَكُ contribute?  [تَكُ · rhetoric]
- 40:50/q06/p1 It marks the coming of messengers as an established fact, while لم makes the question refer to past time. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the added verb kāna and the temporal role of lam. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:50/q07 What is conveyed by the omitted nūn of تَكُ?  [تَكُ · rhetoric]
- 40:50/q07/p1 It evokes the messengers' gradual ascent from immediate sensory proofs to reason, remembrance, and certainty. — Al-Zarkashī gives this explanation in al-Burhān; al-Sāmarrāʾī reports it, saying the movement is from ignorance through accessible sensory signs to certainty. Al-Biqāʿī also observes the omission of the nūn, without assigning this explanation to it. [2 notes: BURHAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الزركشي)]

## 40:50/q08 What does the form تَأْتِيكُمْ suggest about the messengers' coming?  [تَأْتِيكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 40:50/q08/p1 Their coming in worldly life was repeated or continuous. — Al-Biqāʿī attributes successive coming to the imperfect tense and also notes the omitted nūn of تك. Bursevī describes the coming as continual; al-Ālūsī says they came repeatedly. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 40:50/q09 What is the force of رُسُلُكُم, 'your messengers'?  [رُسُلُكُم · referent]
- 40:50/q09/p1 The messengers were human beings from among the addressed people's own kind, which gave them reason to listen. — Muqātil says they came from among the people themselves. Al-Biqāʿī says people are more receptive to those of their own kind. [2 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:50/q10 How is the sīn of رُسُلُكُم read?  [رُسُلُكُم · readings]
turns on: The competing transmissions for Abū ʿAmr and the usual reading of the other readers.
- 40:50/q10/p1 It is lightened with sukūn, yielding ruslukum. — Ibn Mujāhid reports Abū ʿAmr's lightening in plurals attached to a two-letter pronoun, with parallels رسلنا and رسلك; one report attributes the light reading of رسلك to him. Qiraat-Er says this compresses the sound without changing referent or syntax. [2 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER]
- 40:50/q10/p2 It is read heavy, with the sīn vocalized. — Ibn Mujāhid reports another transmission from Abū ʿAmr reading رسلك heavily, as the other readers generally do. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو)]

## 40:50/q11 What are the بَيِّنَٰت brought by the messengers?  [ٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which content of the messengers' clear evidence is being specified; the notes give compatible specifications rather than a stated polemic.
- 40:50/q11/p1 Clear signs warning of the evil end of disbelief and sin. — Bursevī says these warnings establish guilt; al-Ālūsī says the messengers repeatedly brought them in worldly life and compares 39:71. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:50/q11/p2 Unmistakable proofs together with explicit calls to follow guidance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr includes the explicit calls. Al-Biqāʿī describes the proofs as clearer than anything else. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:50/q11/p3 Miracles. — Al-Nasafī gives this gloss. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 40:50/q11/p4 Clear proofs of God's unity and the messengers' prophethood. — Al-Ṭabrisī specifies both matters against which the sinners resisted. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 40:50/q12 What do the inmates acknowledge by answering بَلَىٰ?  [قَالُوا۟ بَلَىٰ · interpretation]
- 40:50/q12/p1 They concede that messengers and their proofs came, despite their own rejection of the messengers. — Al-Ṭabrisī adds that they denied the messengers' prophethood. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī compare the explicit confession in Sūrat al-Mulk, 67:9. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the bayyināt proofs and calls to guidance. Al-Nasafī says the admission precedes the keepers' mockery. [10 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:50/q13 Where may one stop at بَلَىٰ?  [بَلَىٰ · readings]
- 40:50/q13/p1 Stopping at بلى is preferred because it completes the answer without syntactic dependence on what follows; some also allow beginning there. — Al-Zarkashī reports this preference of many readers and linguists for ten occurrences. [1 notes: BURHAN+]

## 40:50/q14 How does the fa in فَٱدْعُوا۟ follow the admission?  [فَٱدْعُوا۟ · grammar]
- 40:50/q14/p1 It makes the order to pray a consequence of their admission that they rejected the messengers' proofs. — Al-Ālūsī calls the fa explanatory with an ellipsis: since they admit it, they must pray themselves. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sequence follows naturally under either analysis of the preceding waw. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:50/q15 What do the keepers mean by فَٱدْعُوا۟?  [فَٱدْعُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imperative directs them to pray for themselves, exposes their former false objects, invites a curse, or dismisses prayer entirely.
- 40:50/q15/p1 Pray for yourselves: the keepers decline to intercede, and the instruction rebukes or mocks the inmates without promising an answer. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, and classical commentators reported by Asad explain the refusal to pray for them. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī call the command mocking; al-Ṭabrisī reports contemptuous dismissal. Al-Kashshāf, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Bayḍāwī stress despair rather than hope; al-Kashshāf reasons that if a close angel's prayer is unheard, theirs will be worse. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it permission or equal treatment and cites the saying that one who chooses its cool must bear its heat. Al-Biqāʿī allows prayer now to God, human messengers, angels, or others; Muqātil and Ibn Kathīr say it changes nothing. [16 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators)]
- 40:50/q15/p2 Invoke the false objects and values once treated as divine, to see whether they can help. — Asad prefers this reading and cites the next clause: those prayers were deluded in earthly life, whereas the delusions are gone on Judgment Day. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES+]
- 40:50/q15/p3 Pray for woe and destruction. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this alternative. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]
- 40:50/q15/p4 Stop praying, because no one will hear you. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this as an alternative to the command to pray now. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:50/q16 Why do the keepers refuse to pray for the inmates?  [فَٱدْعُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether refusal is explained by the inmates' conduct, lack of divine permission, or the settled impossibility of relief.
- 40:50/q16/p1 The inmates' own rejection of guidance makes them unworthy of intercession. — Al-Ālūsī says the fa grounds the refusal in their conduct and rejects a bare lack-of-permission explanation for missing this connection. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they chose to turn away from the messengers and are left to manage their own affairs. Bursevī calls it implausible that the keepers would pray for them. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:50/q16/p2 The keepers have no permission to intercede for such sinners. — Al-Kashshāf says angels can intercede only for a nonevildoer, with permission, before final judgment; al-Ṭabrisī says the guards have not received permission; al-Bayḍāwī says they dare not pray without it. Al-Ālūsī rejects this explanation because it fails to account for the fa and implies that a change in permission could have changed their case. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 40:50/q16/p3 They know the punishment will not be reduced and their prayer cannot bring release. — Al-Baghawī explicitly gives the impossibility of reduction. Ibn Kathīr says they refuse help or a request for release because prayer will change nothing. Al-Wāḥidī describes their refusal; the Study Quran says even belated affirmation cannot avert or alleviate punishment and compares 14:44, 32:12, and 6:27. [4 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:50/q17 Who says the closing statement about futile prayer?  [وَمَا دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ إِلَّا فِى ضَلَٰلٍ · referent]
turns on: Whether the closing clause continues the dialogue or is a divine aside; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes a parenthetic waw for the latter.
- 40:50/q17/p1 The keepers continue speaking to the inmates. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this view. Abū Ḥayyān calls it the apparent reading; al-Nasafī calls it a continuation of their taunt. Al-Ālūsī attributes the sentence to the keepers. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows it as their concluding warning. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:50/q17/p2 God reports the conclusion to Muhammad as a parenthetical statement. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī report this alternative. Ibn ʿĀshūr permits it and takes the waw as parenthetic. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:50/q18 Whose دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ is meant by the genitive construction?  [دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the genitive names the supplicants or the beneficiaries of the supplication.
- 40:50/q18/p1 The disbelievers' own prayer. — Bursevī gives this as one grammatical reading of the annexation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 40:50/q18/p2 The prayer of others on the disbelievers' behalf. — Bursevī gives this as the other grammatical reading of the same annexation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 40:50/q19 How is دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ written in the received script?  [دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ · readings]
- 40:50/q19/p1 Its final hamza after alif is written on waw without disagreement. — Ibn al-Jazarī in al-Nashr lists the Ghāfir occurrence among eight such written forms, alongside examples including شركاء in al-Anʿām and نشاء in Hūd. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 40:50/q20 What marks the people called ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ here?  [ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · interpretation]
- 40:50/q20/p1 They veil their faculties of reason from reason's light supported by sound transmission. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this characterization of the disbelievers. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:50/q21 What does ضَلَٰلٍ mean in the closing statement?  [ضَلَٰلٍ · meaning]
- 40:50/q21/p1 Loss or going astray, originally missing one's road. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the usage in 32:10. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:50/q22 What does it mean that disbelievers' prayer is فِى ضَلَٰلٍ?  [وَمَا دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ إِلَّا فِى ضَلَٰلٍ · interpretation]
- 40:50/q22/p1 Their pleas bring no benefit, acceptance, answer, or relief from punishment. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Nasafī call the prayer fruitless; al-Bayḍāwī says the clause extinguishes hope. Al-Biqāʿī compares the futility of their worldly deeds: the Hereafter harvests what this life plants. Bursevī says the prayer is made at the wrong time. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the denial generic across times and places, whether spoken by angels or God. The Study Quran says belated affirmation cannot alleviate punishment, with parallel pleas at 14:44, 32:12, and 6:27. [12 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:50/q23 Which prayers does the closing statement have in view?  [دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the generic wording applies to the present afterlife plea, every disbeliever's prayer, or their former idolatrous invocations.
- 40:50/q23/p1 The condemned people's pleas on Judgment Day. — Bursevī says they pray at the wrong time. Al-Ālūsī expressly limits the verse's denial to their prayer on Judgment Day, while allowing worldly requests to be granted. Al-Bayḍāwī and the Study Quran connect it to despair of relief from the present punishment. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:50/q23/p2 Disbelievers' prayers generally, across times and places. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that the generic annexed noun gives universal scope, regardless of speaker. The majority reported by Bursevī use this verse against saying a disbeliever's prayer is answered. Al-Ālūsī restricts this inference to Judgment Day. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 40:50/q23/p3 Invocations directed to idols or false objects in worldly life, which prove deluded or unanswered. — Al-Suddī says idols cannot answer anyone in this life or the next; Qatāda says idols have no answered prayer. Another view in al-Thaʿlabī says idols are invoked now but deny their worshippers in the next, since they never demanded worship or claimed divinity. Asad says earthly delusions have vanished by Judgment Day. Bursevī cites Saʿdī's poem: an idol fails its devotee for years, but God grants the need when the desperate man collapses at the temple. [5 notes: THALABI(السدي,قتادة); BURSEVI(الشيخ سعدي); ASAD-NOTES]

## 40:50/q24 Can a disbeliever's worldly prayer be answered?  [دُعَٰٓؤُا۟ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ إِلَّا فِى ضَلَٰلٍ · theology]
turns on: Whether a worldly grant counts as an answered prayer and whether the verse's denial extends beyond Judgment Day.
- 40:50/q24/p1 It is not accepted as an answered prayer, even if a requested rescue occurs. — The majority reported by Bursevī cite this verse, arguing that disbelief entails not knowing God or invalidating belief by describing Him wrongly. Ibn ʿĀshūr says rescues in 6:63–64 and 10:22–23 expose ingratitude, not honorable acceptance; a rescue may have been decreed already or coincide with righteous people's prayers. He cites the Muslim report from Abū Hurayra in which the Prophet questions how a traveler nourished by unlawful food and drink could have his raised-hands prayer answered. [6 notes: BURSEVI(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم؛ أبو هريرة)]
- 40:50/q24/p2 A wronged disbeliever's prayer can be answered. — Some reported by Bursevī interpret kufr here as ingratitude and cite God's granting Satan's request for respite. Bursevī says this permissive ruling is followed and himself adduces Satan's granted request. [2 notes: BURSEVI; BURSEVI(بعضهم)+]
- 40:50/q24/p3 Worldly requests may be granted; this verse only settles the fate of prayers on Judgment Day. — Al-Ālūsī says some infer from the verse that no disbeliever's prayer is answered and that disbelievers cannot join the rain prayer, but he limits its scope to the Day of Judgment and leaves open what one calls a worldly grant. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 40:50/q25 Why are these future exchanges narrated in past tense?  [قَالُوٓا۟ قَالُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 40:50/q25/p1 Past tense conveys certainty that the future events will occur. — Abū Ḥayyān supplies this explanation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 40:50/q26 Why did the condemned turn to the keepers for help?  [* · interpretation]
- 40:50/q26/p1 They sought someone who might intercede or lessen the fire, though no creature could free them. — Al-Kashshāf suggests the worst rebels are in this fire and deliberately approach assigned angels, whose closeness to God might make their prayers more likely to be heard. Kuranyolu says the followers of powerful leaders had already asked those leaders to remove some fire; the leaders could save no one, and the question itself exposed their helplessness. Al-Kashshāf identifies the keepers as attendants charged with torment. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 40:50/q27 How does the surrounding wording heighten the scene?  [* · rhetoric]
- 40:50/q27/p1 Naming Jahannam in the preceding verse rather than using a pronoun intensifies terror. — Al-Kashshāf also reports a possible derivation from جهنام, a deep well, and notes that al-Nābigha was called Jahannām for deep knowledge of poetry. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 40:50/q28 How does this exchange fit the sūra's Moses–Pharaoh account?  [* · links]
- 40:50/q28/p1 It carries forward the warning that rejecting a messenger ends in ruin and encourages the Prophet and believers to endure. — Iṣlāḥī says the Moses–Pharaoh narrative warns Quraysh, consoles Muhammad and his companions amid trials, and presents a believer in Pharaoh's family as a lesson for Quraysh and hesitant sympathizers. He adds that the story supports the prior claim that worldly grandeur cannot save a people rejecting their messenger. The Study Quran says verses 10–20 warn of the disbelievers' Hereafter before the extended Moses–Pharaoh account focused on that believer, comparing 40:28 and 40:45. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 40:50/q29 What lesson does the exchange give about seeking salvation?  [* · interpretation]
- 40:50/q29/p1 People must heed guidance and choose rightly before judgment; then neither leaders nor angels can rescue them. — Kuranyolu says one must resist desires and powerful immoral deniers, freely choose belief and conduct, heed the messenger's clear truths, and seek salvation in this life; after judgment no one helps except by God's will. It says followers of powerful people enter Hell with them and discover their leaders' helplessness. The Study Quran says belated affirmation and supplication cannot avert punishment, citing 14:44, 32:12, and 6:27, and adds that even if returned they would repeat forbidden conduct, citing 6:28. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
