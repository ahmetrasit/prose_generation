# 96:17: 12 questions

## 96:17/q01 Who is challenged to call for help?  [فَلْيَدْعُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the command is restricted to the historical prohibitor or generalized from him.
- 96:17/q01/p1 Abū Jahl is the challenged man. — Al-Māwardī identifies him directly. Al-Ṭabarī and later exegetes identify him from his ban on Muḥammad's prayer and boast of the valley's largest council; Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima and Abū Hurayra transmit this setting. [25 notes: MAWARDI; TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-ASBAB(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI(أبو جهل); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(أبو هريرة,قتادة,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(المفسرون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Rivayete göre)]
- 96:17/q01/p2 The challenge also addresses the archetypal arrogant person who trusts a faction. — Quṭb speaks of the arrogant man, Bint al-Shāṭiʾ of the boastful person, and Iṣlāḥī of the arrogant. This extends the specific story into a general pattern; Asad's preferred symbolic reading likewise applies to those imagining self-sufficiency. [5 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES+]

## 96:17/q02 What does the command dare him to do, and when?  [فَلْيَدْعُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command addresses Abū Jahl's earthly boast in sequence or is read after the seizure and angelic summons in the hereafter.
- 96:17/q02/p1 He is dared now to summon his human supporters against Muḥammad or to rescue himself; God's angels will overpower or seize them. — Al-Ṭabarī links his boast to God's counter-summons in 96:18. Al-Wāḥidī cites the threatened valley full of cavalry and men. Ibn ʿAbbās says angels would seize him immediately if he called. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call the command taunting; Ibn ʿĀshūr says neither he nor his hearers dared act. [38 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Rivayete göre)]
- 96:17/q02/p2 After God seizes his forelock and calls the guards in the afterlife, he may call his assembly to try to stop them. — Al-Rāzī reports this as an alternative ordering of the clauses; the assembly's call comes after the divine seizure and summons. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 96:17/q03 What rhetorical force does the imperative and its fāʾ carry?  [فَلْيَدْعُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes vary in whether the taunt stresses his failure to act, the impotence of his group, or the ironic claim of noble defenders.
- 96:17/q03/p1 It is a taunting challenge that exposes his inability to summon effective help. — Abū Ḥayyān says God will not enable him and angels would seize him openly; al-Ālūsī quotes this and Ibn ʿĀshūr says 96:19's kallā confirms he will not carry it out. Al-Rāzī says the fāʾ marks a miracle because Abū Jahl dared not accept the challenge; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise calls the unaccepted public challenge a Qurʾānic miracle. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Rivayete göre)]
- 96:17/q03/p2 It sarcastically calls on his allegedly generous or noble defenders. — Al-Rāzī's alternative derivation of nādī from a gathering of generosity makes the invitation pointedly sarcastic. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 96:17/q04 How is the command read in the reported variant?  [فَلْيَدْعُ نَادِيَهُۥ · readings]
- 96:17/q04/p1 The reading attributed to Ibn Masʿūd says to call to his council and changes the following response to 'then I will call'. — Al-Farrāʾ reports ʿAbd Allāh's reading with ilā before nādiyah and fa-sa-adʿū for the answering call; Ibn ʿAṭiyya independently reports Ibn Masʿūd's 'to his council'. [2 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 96:17/q05 Who or what is his nādī?  [نَادِيَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term denotes the present assembly and its people, a specifically named social body or companion, or a symbolic inward resource; the lexical notes also distinguish consultation from generosity associations.
- 96:17/q05/p1 It is the people of his gathering, his companions, supporters, clan or wider faction whom he expects to help. — Al-Farrāʾ says Arabs name a man's group by council, assembly, gathering or witness, and cites a poet. Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn Hishām and others distinguish the venue from its people: the place bears the name while they meet there. Ibn Hishām cites 29:29, 19:73, 12:82 and verses by ʿUbayd, Salama and al-Kumayt. Jarīr, Zuhayr and an unnamed poet supply council and station parallels; Abū Ḥayyān observes Jarīr's adjectives describe attendees, not masonry. Muʾarrij gives Quraysh usage; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes a daytime gathering, unlike night sāmir (23:67). Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 68:21, 40:32, 29:29 and 19:73 for related root uses. Elmalılı compares an assembly, congress or parliament and proposes Turkish kurultay. Al-Iṣlāḥī says a linked communal group or party. The cited poetic wording and attribution vary, including Jarīr versus Dhū al-Rumma. [123 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNHISHAM(ابن هشام); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TUSTARI; TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF(جرير,زهير); KASHSHAF-FULL(جرير,زهير); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(زهير,زهير والأعرابية); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI(آخر,جرير,زهير); QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر,جرير,زهير); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(جرير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أعرابية,جرير,زهير,مؤرج); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(أبو هريرة,قتادة,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI(جرير أو ذو الرمة,زهير); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 96:17/q05/p2 Specifically, his supporters are Banū Makhzūm. — Muqātil names the clan behind Abū Jahl's gathering. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 96:17/q05/p3 It is an individual companion or fellow sitter. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this as an alternative to the gathered council or its people. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 96:17/q05/p4 Historically it is Mecca's pagan elders' council, Dār al-Nadwah. — Asad reports this as the classical historical identification; al-Rāzī invokes Dār al-Nadwah as evidence of consultation usage. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Quṣayy founded its council house by the Sacred Mosque for affairs, contracts and puberty ceremonies; he traces later purchases and mosque incorporation, while Bursawī notes its later Hanafi gathering hall. [7 notes: ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); RAZI(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:17/q05/p5 In the deeper general reading, his 'council' is the arrogance that makes humans imagine they are self-sufficient. — Asad prefers this from the language of 96:6–7 over the historical council reading. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES+]
- 96:17/q05/p6 The word can evoke a gathering marked by generosity or hospitality. — Al-Māwardī associates the place with generosity and hospitality; al-Rāzī reports a generosity derivation and says it makes Abū Jahl's appeal to supposed noble defenders sarcastic. [4 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 96:17/q06 How does a gathering place become what he can call?  [نَادِيَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted 'people' is supplied, the place is used metonymically, or nādī itself denotes the present people.
- 96:17/q06/p1 An omitted head noun, 'people', is understood: he calls the people of his council. — Ibn Qutayba says Arabic omits a possessive head and promotes its dependent; al-Naḥḥās names this genitive-head omission. Al-Ṭabrisī, Bursawī and others apply it here because a place cannot answer a summons; 12:82's 'ask the town' is analogous. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:17/q06/p2 The place is named figuratively for its occupants, so the council itself stands for its people. — Al-Samīn offers this beside ellipsis and compares 12:82; al-Burhān and al-Itqān describe naming occupants by location. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 12:82 and poetic majlis, maqām and majmaʿ parallels; al-Ālūsī gives figurative attribution or naming inhabitants by place. [9 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 96:17/q06/p3 The noun can already denote the assembled people directly. — Abū ʿUbayda as cited by al-Rāzī says a vacant place is not called nādī; Ibn ʿĀshūr also says it names the people gathered there. Ibn Qutayba mentions a second pattern where one verb belongs to one of two things and another verb is understood for the other. [4 notes: RAZI(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); IBNASHUR; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 96:17/q07 Why is the gathering called 'his'?  [نَادِيَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 96:17/q07/p1 It is attached to Abū Jahl because he is its chief and its members gather around him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites an Arab woman's praise of a man as master of his council and support for those in need; his boast treats followers as proof of leadership. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(أعرابية)]

## 96:17/q08 What did the reports say prompted this challenge?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the threat, the visual barrier or angelic intervention, the order of boast and recitation, and whether a scene or grading is preserved.
- 96:17/q08/p1 Abū Jahl barred the praying Prophet, boasted of the valley's largest gathering, and the verses answered his boast. — Ibn ʿAbbās places the confrontation at the Maqām and says angels would seize him if he summoned his group; al-Tirmidhī's grading is given as ḥasan gharīb ṣaḥīḥ by al-Qurṭubī and ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ by Ibn Kathīr and al-Suyūṭī. Reports vary over whether the Prophet's rebuke is preserved, whether the boasts follow recitation, and whether his threat is explicit. Al-Tirmidhī and al-Nasāʾī are named by Ibn ʿĀshūr. [40 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس,عكرمة); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI(أبو جهل); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو جهل); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Tirmizî); QUTB-ZILAL(الروايات الصحيحة); IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(المفسرون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Rivayete göre)]
- 96:17/q08/p2 Abū Jahl threatened to trample the Prophet's neck or back while he prayed; angels would seize him openly if he did. — Ibn ʿAbbās locates the prayer at the Kaʿba or Maqām; wording varies between stepping on the neck and trampling. Ibn Kathīr adds that challengers of the Prophet face loss. Qatāda reports the threat as the occasion and calls the zabāniya police in Arabic; Mujāhid calls them angels. [28 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي); TAB(ابن عباس); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(أبو جهل,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة,مجاهد)]
- 96:17/q08/p3 Abū Jahl threatened to kill Muḥammad for praying at the Maqām; dark battalions appeared between them and would have seized him if he moved. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr transmit Ibn ʿAbbās's report. The Prophet says the space between them darkened or that he saw ranks; variants specify the prayer place and whether the revealed sequence included 96:1. [6 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس)]
- 96:17/q08/p4 When Abū Jahl moved toward the praying Prophet, he recoiled from a trench of fire, terror and wings; angels would have torn him limb from limb. — Abū Hurayra transmits the scene; al-Ṭabarī notes uncertainty over attribution of the quoted revelation. Ibn Kathīr says Aḥmad, Muslim, al-Nasāʾī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmit via Muʿtamir and notes the narrator's uncertainty whether the revelation context belonged to Abū Hurayra's account. [7 notes: TAB(أبو هريرة); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 96:17/q08/p5 Abū Jahl threatened to summon cavalry or fill the valley with mounted and foot supporters. — Al-Thaʿlabī's Ibn ʿAbbās report says the Prophet warned the guards would take him openly; al-Jishumī's other account says angels would seize him limb by limb if he approached. Al-Wāḥidī cites the valley full of horses and men. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 96:17/q08/p6 After hearing the forelock threat, Abū Jahl boasted of his people, feared the zabāniya and said he saw a threatening horseman. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this account at both verse entries; its order and horseman differ from the fiery-trench and dark-ranks reports. [2 notes: QURTUBI]
- 96:17/q08/p7 Abū Jahl vowed to trample Muḥammad as he prostrated; after the sūra was recited, he said the sky's horizon was blocked from him. — Al-ʿAbbās transmits this version and adds that Muḥammad prostrated at the sūra's end. [2 notes: DURR(العباس بن عبد المطلب)]
- 96:17/q08/p8 Abū Jahl threw camel entrails onto the Prophet's back during prostration. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies him as the man forbidding prayer in this report. [2 notes: DURR(ابن عباس)]

## 96:17/q09 When is the threatened seizure or defeat realized?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the angels' seizure is the immediate counter to his call, a Badr fulfillment, or an afterlife punishment.
- 96:17/q09/p1 If he summoned his group or approached, the angels would seize him immediately, visibly. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly gives this conditional explanation, and the Prophet's warning in the trample reports is similar. Ibn Kathīr cites al-Tirmidhī's ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ grading of the assembly report; al-Qurṭubī gives ḥasan gharīb ṣaḥīḥ. [14 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QUTB-ZILAL(ابن عباس)]
- 96:17/q09/p2 The threat foretells his humiliating dragging in this world at Badr. — Al-Rāzī reports the Badr explanation; Iṣlāḥī says Badr later demonstrated the Quraysh's humiliation by God's guards. [2 notes: RAZI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:17/q09/p3 The angels drag him into Hell in the afterlife. — Al-Rāzī reports this distinct explanation; he separately reports a reversed sequence in which he then calls his assembly. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 96:17/q10 How does this challenge connect to the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The connection may stress the counter-summons of 96:18 or the broader self-sufficiency theme of 96:6–7 and denial in 96:19.
- 96:17/q10/p1 His call to a human council is met by God's call to the zabāniya, exposing the council's helplessness. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī and Ibn Kathīr relate 96:17 to 96:18. Muqātil describes the zabāniya as Hell's keepers with feet in the lowest earth and heads in the sky; al-Nasafī derives their name from pushing and calls them punishment angels; Qatāda glosses the word as police. Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿIkrima gloss the prior forelock seizure as 'we will take him'. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,عكرمة); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Tirmizî); BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 96:17/q10/p2 The challenge follows the general warning that imagined self-sufficiency leads to transgression and is then canceled by 96:19's kallā. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads 96:6–7 as introducing the specific prohibitor through this challenge and says kallā denies that he can summon his council. Asad appeals to 96:6–7 for his preferred symbolic reading. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 96:17/q11 What broader lesson is drawn from this challenge?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources differ between a lesson directly drawn from calling the council and a broader contrast of worldly pursuit with knowledge.
- 96:17/q11/p1 Worldly status, clan and companions can embolden arrogance but cannot protect the transgressor against God. — Al-Biqāʿī says disbelievers rely on community for pride and fear becoming followers; Quṭb and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ describe the same illusion of family protection. Al-Jishumī says the summoned followers can only witness shared calamity. Iṣlāḥī points to Badr as demonstration. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:17/q11/p2 The desire for worldly gain is insatiable and leads to transgression, unlike knowledge seeking, which wins God's approval. — Ibn Masʿūd connects the sura's claim about human transgression in 96:6–7 with 35:28; the note is transmitted here alongside the verse's material. [1 notes: DURR(ابن مسعود)]

## 96:17/q12 What virtue is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 96:17/q12/p1 The reciter receives a reward as though they had recited all the shorter sūras. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this report without a grading in the supplied note. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
