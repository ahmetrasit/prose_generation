# 69:30: 12 questions

## 69:30/q01 Who receives the order to seize him?  [خُذُوهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the addressee is the infernal guard collective, each keeper summoning helpers, or an angel in al-Qashani's inward reading.
- 69:30/q01/p1 God addresses the keepers of Hell or angels assigned to the condemned. — Muqatil, Thaʿlabi, al-Wahidi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Bursevi, al-Alusi, al-Tabatabaʾi, and Study Quran identify the infernal keepers or angels. Ibn ʿAtiyya also allows an angel to give the order by God's command. Ibn ʿAshur calls them angels assigned to drive the judged to their destination. Al-Biqaʿi and al-Bursevi name the zabaniya; al-Biqaʿi adds that the condemned had scorned them. Jishumi reports this as one possibility and also reports each keeper gathering tormenting angels. Elmalili says the command is phrased as though addressed to the man's useless wealth and power, while its actual recipients are the keepers. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 69:30/q01/p2 Each keeper is called to gather tormenting angels. — Jishumi gives this as an alternative to addressing the keepers collectively. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 69:30/q01/p3 The order reaches the angel governing the world of generation and corruption. — Al-Qashani reads the speaker as a voice of sovereign majesty and the angel's work as binding the man in forms corresponding to his soul's dispositions. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:30/q02 Who is the one seized?  [خُذُوهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is identified by the surrounding left-hand account or by reports naming Abu Jahl.
- 69:30/q02/p1 The condemned man whose record came in his left hand and whose wealth and power failed him. — Al-Bursevi identifies the disobedient recipient of the left-hand record; Elmalili identifies the speaker lamenting his useless wealth and power; Kur'an Yolu places the command after his account. Al-Baydawi and Asad call him the condemned person. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:30/q02/p2 Abu Jahl is the particular person intended. — Ibn ʿAtiyya reports Ibn Jarir saying the passage was revealed concerning Abu Jahl; al-Durr reports Ibn Jurayj saying he was told the seized and shackled person is Abu Jahl. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جرير); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج)]

## 69:30/q03 What does taking him involve?  [خُذُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the seizure describes bodily custody or the inward condition of the soul.
- 69:30/q03/p1 Seize him physically, gripping him and taking him toward Hell. — Ibn ʿAshur glosses taking as gripping by hand. Ibn Kathir says the keepers forcibly remove him from the gathering and take him to Hell to immerse him in it. Abu Hayyan includes roasting him in the great Fire; Kur'an Yolu also describes transfer to Hell and attachment to the criminals' long chain. Asad states the basic command to seize the condemned man. [7 notes: THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:30/q03/p2 Seizing him is an inward binding in forms matching his soul's dispositions. — Al-Qashani says the angel governing generation and corruption restricts the man's volition and imprisons him in nature. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:30/q04 What is reported to happen when the command to seize him is given?  [خُذُوهُ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in number, in whether they speak of angels or tormentors, and in what they do.
- 69:30/q04/p1 One hundred thousand angels rush upon him and bind his hand or hands to his neck. — Al-Tustari adds that one angel could grasp the earth with its mountains and seas, and says he is then cast into Hell. Al-Wahidi, al-Razi, and al-Qurtubi also give the hundred-thousand number and binding. [4 notes: TUSTARI; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 69:30/q04/p2 Seventy thousand angels rush to seize or shackle him. — Ibn Kathir reports al-Minhal ibn ʿAmr saying one angel can throw seventy thousand into the Fire; he also reports al-Fudayl ibn ʿIyad saying every angel wants to place the collar on the man. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الفضيل بن عياض,المنهال بن عمرو)]
- 69:30/q04/p3 Four hundred thousand rush him, crushing everything in their way. — Ibn Kathir cites a report in Ibn Abi al-Dunya's al-Ahwal: when the man asks why, they say his Lord and everything else are angry with him. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن أبي الدنيا)]
- 69:30/q04/p4 One hundred thousand tormentors tear one inmate apart, after which he is recreated. — Al-Thaʿlabi reports this account about a person in Hell; the note calls them tormentors and does not identify them as the angels in the seizure reports. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 69:30/q05 How is the spoken command introduced?  [خُذُوهُ · grammar]
- 69:30/q05/p1 An unspoken verb of saying introduces God's order. — Al-Samin understands an omitted qala, as with 'eat' in 69:24. Al-Alusi supplies 'He says' before God's address to the keepers. Al-Tabatabaʾi supplies 'it is said to the angels.' Ibn ʿAshur supplies 'it is said' and treats the resulting speech as circumstantial with the criminal's wish in 69:25. Elmalili calls the commands an implied utterance, phrased as though directed to the wealth and power the man had invoked. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 69:30/q06 How may the pronominal ha be pronounced after a long vowel?  [خُذُوهُ فَغُلُّوهُ · readings]
- 69:30/q06/p1 Its following waw or ya may be shortened or fully realized in connected speech. — Sibawayh prefers dropping the post-ha vowel letter after a preceding long vowel, yet accepts full realization as Arabic; he also mentions 7:176 and 12:20 as evidence of the usage. The note states the rule generally for pronominal ha. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH+]

## 69:30/q07 What does shackling him mean here?  [فَغُلُّوهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the shackle denotes a bodily restraint, allegorical consequences of deeds, representative imagery for unseen realities, or an inward restraint of the soul.
- 69:30/q07/p1 Put him in a physical shackle, chiefly binding his hands to his neck. — Muqatil, al-Tustari, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, and Kur'an Yolu specify hand or hands joined to the neck. Jishumi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, and Ibn ʿAshur describe a neck collar or shackle; al-Bursevi specifies an iron neck ring that joins hand to neck and limits head movement. Al-Biqaʿi further has the feet tied behind the back, up toward the front of the head; al-Tabatabaʾi says the ghull binds hand, foot, and neck. Al-Qurtubi and al-Alusi gloss it as fettering with shackles or chains. Kur'an Yolu adds the criminals' long chain, and Study Quran points to 36:8 for shackles placed on disbelievers and made to course through their bodies. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 69:30/q07/p2 The shackles are allegorical for the sinner's deeds, inclinations, and despair. — Asad explicitly calls the shackles allegorical and refers to his notes on 13:5, 34:33, and 36:8. Kur'an Yolu reports Razi reading similar imagery as a metaphor for deeds, inclinations, and the general despair of the next world, mentioning 14:49. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 69:30/q07/p3 The shackles are representative imagery that conveys an unseen afterlife reality to worldly hearers. — Kur'an Yolu says God explains realities of the unseen afterlife in terms accessible to people in this world. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:30/q07/p4 The binding is an inward imprisonment in nature and restriction of volition. — Al-Qashani says the angel binds the man in forms corresponding to the dispositions of his soul. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:30/q08 How is the verb ghulluhu formed?  [فَغُلُّوهُ · grammar]
- 69:30/q08/p1 It is a noun-derived imperative attested in the triliteral form. — Ibn ʿAshur says the expected doubled form may have been shortened for ease. Al-Tabatabaʾi identifies the imperative as derived from ghull, a restraint binding hand, foot, and neck. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 69:30/q09 What sequence do fa and the repeated thumma convey?  [فَغُلُّوهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether thumma principally intensifies the pictured punishments or links reconstructed commands; both analyses give fa immediate force.
- 69:30/q09/p1 Shackling follows seizure immediately; the later thumma marks escalating terror, not a pause between punishments. — Ibn ʿAshur says fa signals speed; Elmalili and al-Bursevi say the hands are bound without delay. Al-Kashshaf and al-Bursevi say the repeated thumma escalates from shackling to roasting to passage through the chain, rather than marking elapsed time; al-Bursevi says the threat calls for no pause. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:30/q09/p2 Thumma connects successive implicit orders to wardens, while fa marks immediate execution. — Ibn al-Shaykh, reported by al-Bursevi, posits omitted successive commands so thumma and fa need not coordinate the same clause. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ)]

## 69:30/q10 How does the command contribute to the passage's cadence and order?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: For the following command's fronting, whether verse-ending cadence or special emphasis explains its order.
- 69:30/q10/p1 The command is an example of a balanced cadence followed by a longer third clause. — Al-Burhan contrasts the longer third clause here and in 69:31 with a longer second clause in 'Your companion has neither strayed nor erred.' [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 69:30/q10/p2 The following Fire command fronts 'the Fire' to preserve aligned verse endings. — Al-Burhan says 'roast him in the Fire' would convey the sense but lose the joined cadence. It adds that verse-ending alignment can motivate multiple frontings; the strongest competing reason governs, and equal reasons leave either order available. [2 notes: BURHAN]
- 69:30/q10/p3 The following Fire command fronts 'the Fire' for emphasis or exclusivity. — Al-Burhan reports this as an explanation given by some. [1 notes: BURHAN(بعضهم)]

## 69:30/q11 What practical ruling is drawn from neck collars?  [فَغُلُّوهُ · law]
turns on: Whether resemblance to the punishment of Hell makes the collar objectionable even when it prevents escape.
- 69:30/q11/p1 Putting a neck collar on a slave is disliked because it is a punishment of Hell's people. — Al-Bursevi gives the likeness to the infernal punishment as the reason. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 69:30/q11/p2 A collar is not disliked when needed to prevent flight. — A jurist reported by al-Bursevi says collars were customary in his time when flight was feared; he distinguishes them from shackling and says restraining rebels is Muslim practice. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الفقيه)]

## 69:30/q12 How does this command connect to the preceding account?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize different triggers or narrative links: the books and warning, testimony of limbs, failed wealth and power, or scorn of the guards.
- 69:30/q12/p1 After the two groups are described by their books, the command warns sinners of prepared punishment. — Jishumi states this link to the earlier presentation of the two groups. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 69:30/q12/p2 The man's limbs testify to his association of partners with God, prompting the order. — Al-Baghawi reports this from Muqatil. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 69:30/q12/p3 The command turns on the left-hand recipient's failed wealth and power. — Al-Bursevi identifies him by his left-hand record; Elmalili says he has just lamented his useless wealth and power and hears the command as though they were addressed; Kur'an Yolu places it after his account. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:30/q12/p4 The condemned man is handed to guards he had previously scorned. — Al-Biqaʿi says he had scorned the zabaniya when they were mentioned. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
