# 74:31: 43 questions

## 74:31/q01 Who are the companions of the Fire?  [أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلنَّارِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase denotes literal angelic guards, an elliptical construction, or allegorical human faculties.
- 74:31/q01/p1 The angels appointed to guard and administer Hell, rather than its inmates or human jailers. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and others connect them to the nineteen over Saqar in 74:30; Bursevī contrasts the normal sense in 59:20; Elmalılı names Mālik as chief. Al-Wāḥidī reports ḥaddād as jailer; Islahi distinguishes prison guards from inmates. [33 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر بن الأنباري); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن فارس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)-]
- 74:31/q01/p2 The phrase elliptically means the keepers of the people of the Fire. — Kashf al-asrār supplies an omitted genitive; Bursevī rejects it because angels guard the Fire itself. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); BURSEVI-]
- 74:31/q01/p3 The angelic powers of human perception and conception, whose misuse brings afterlife suffering. — Asad links this allegorical reading to the preceding 'over it' and to moral discrimination and ascent. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:31/q02 Why are the Fire's keepers angels?  [إِلَّا مَلَٰٓئِكَةًۭ · interpretation]
- 74:31/q02/p1 Angels cannot be overpowered by the condemned, and their different nature prevents pity. — Al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, and others stress their strength, obedience, and lack of kinship. Al-Rāzī contrasts compassionate human messengers and answers that God can preserve angels from fire pain; al-Biqāʿī cites the angel's cry destroying Thamūd; 66:6 describes them as stern. [36 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(رجل من المشركين); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العوام,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:31/q02/p2 Their appointment shows pure servants manage Hell as they manage the universe. — Islahi contrasts angels with jinn or devils, emphasizing exact obedience. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:31/q03 What does their number count?  [عِدَّتَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether nineteen names individual chiefs, thousands, ranks, or an unspecified kind of count.
- 74:31/q03/p1 Nineteen individual angels, commonly understood as chiefs with many other angelic attendants. — Al-Ālūsī cites the majority and a report of seventy thousand reins each carried by seventy thousand angels; al-Nasafī notes Mālik as chief. The report of Abū al-ʿAwwām contrasts nineteen with nineteen thousand. [10 notes: MUJAHID(أبو العوام,رجل من بني تميم); DURR-FULL(أبو العوام); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); STUDYQURAN(Prophet,Q, Ṭ)]
- 74:31/q03/p2 Nineteen thousand keepers. — A Banū Tamīm man argues from the statement that their number is a trial; Abū al-ʿAwwām affirms the answer in Mujāhid's report. [1 notes: MUJAHID(رجل من بني تميم)]
- 74:31/q03/p3 Nineteen groups or ranks of angels. — Al-Ālūsī reports an omitted classifier but considers it weak against the transmitted report and the mockery of a small number. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 74:31/q03/p4 The number may leave open whether it counts individuals or kinds. — Elmalılı says the uncertainty itself tests unbelievers. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 74:31/q04 Why exactly nineteen keepers?  [عِدَّتَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the number's wisdom is inaccessible or can be derived from arithmetic, Hell's organization, or human faculties.
- 74:31/q04/p1 Its precise wisdom belongs to God's free choice and remains unknown to creatures. — Al-Rāzī compares every finite body's determinate number and every event's determinate time; al-Ālūsī prefers consigning this ambiguous matter to God and criticizes speculative rationales. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the count was chosen for an undisclosed wisdom. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 74:31/q04/p2 Nineteen combines nine, the largest single digit, and ten, the smallest greater number. — Al-Māwardī offers the possible wisdom; al-Ālūsī reports this symbolic explanation without endorsing it. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); ALUSI-FULL(قائلون)]
- 74:31/q04/p3 The nineteen divide into six leading, six driving, six striking, and Mālik as chief. — Al-Nasafī reports this functional scheme. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL(قيل)]
- 74:31/q04/p4 Nineteen corresponds to nineteen levels of Saqar or nineteen kinds of torment. — Al-Nasafī transmits both alternatives. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(قيل)]
- 74:31/q04/p5 The keepers correspond to stabilizing mountains; Hell's original number was one hundred ninety. — Al-Nasafī reports this further explanation. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL(قيل)]
- 74:31/q04/p6 Nineteen is derived from schemes of levels and categories of sinners. — Al-Ālūsī records six unbeliever classes with three overseers each plus one for sinful Muslims, or five levels leaving two for major and minor sinners or men and women; the Fire was first prepared for disbelievers. He also reports twelve animal faculties and seven natural powers, and twenty-four hours less five prayer hours, judging the philosophical scheme weak. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قائلون); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 74:31/q04/p7 The number symbolizes causes of human corruption in an inward reading. — Bursevī's Najmī source maps nine causes of soul corruption and a hell of distance, expulsion, curse and veiling; seven prostrating limbs plus animal nature, or anger and desire, complete nineteen. Nine signifies domination and loss of mercy. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 74:31/q05 What makes the number a fitna for unbelievers?  [فِتْنَةًۭ لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fitna is epistemic testing through a surprising count, punitive consequence, empowered few, or a misread allegory.
- 74:31/q05/p1 The unexpectedly small number exposes unbelievers' mockery, denial, and assumption they can overpower the keepers. — Sources mention sneering at nineteen rather than twenty, its seeming inability to punish all jinn and humans, denial of resurrection, and reluctance to accept divine power. Al-Biqāʿī says the obscurity tests dispositions; Islahi compares mockery of the Night Journey and zaqqūm in 17:60; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others gloss fitna as test. [58 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(الأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي,الكعبي,المعتزلة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 74:31/q05/p2 Fitna means punishment in the Hereafter. — Al-Qurṭubī compares 51:13–14; Study Quran reports the same alternative. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 74:31/q05/p3 The number is a demanding obligation through which God demonstrates that few empowered angels suffice. — Al-Jubbāʾī contrasts nineteen empowered angels with one hundred thousand strong angels; al-Aṣamm says their strength can equal a greater host; al-Naḥḥās urges recognition of God's power. [4 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI(الأصم); RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)]
- 74:31/q05/p4 The number is an allegory whose literalization misleads deniers. — Asad says the literal count distracts from the allegory; Islahi says eschatological similes describe realities known fully only to God, invoking 3:7. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Those who deny the truth); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:31/q05/p5 The trial is the disclosure of the count, not the creation of the guardians. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguish announcing the number from making angels; Ibn ʿĀshūr places the trial in its mention in 74:30. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:31/q06 How is 'we made their number only a trial' constructed?  [وَمَا جَعَلْنَا عِدَّتَهُمْ إِلَّا فِتْنَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fitna names a cause through metonymy or the condition imposed on unbelievers.
- 74:31/q06/p1 Fitna is jaʿalnā's second object and denotes the cause or occasion of a trial, with effect named for cause. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn supply an omitted 'cause'; Bursevī says the number cannot literally equal the unbelievers' temptation; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī take the number's announcement as the effective making. Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's substitution as unwarranted. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 74:31/q06/p2 Fitna is the condition of trial occurring to unbelievers; the restricted object names that outcome. — Ibn ʿĀshūr analyzes the emptied exception in jaʿalnā's second-object slot and says the implicit point is the disclosure's trial. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:31/q07 What incident prompted the answer that the guards are angels?  [وَمَا جَعَلْنَآ أَصْحَٰبَ ٱلنَّارِ إِلَّا مَلَٰٓئِكَةًۭ · reports]
turns on: Which reported taunt and speaker elicited the verse.
- 74:31/q07/p1 Abū Jahl mocked the nineteen and challenged Quraysh to overpower the guards; a strongman offered to take seventeen while others took two. — Accounts vary the strongman's name among Abū al-Ashadd, Abū al-Ashaddayn, Abū al-Asad and al-Ḥārith ibn Kalda; some report a ten-to-one challenge, a cowhide feat, or a boast of pushing ten right and nine left. Ibn Kathīr discusses divergent wrestler identifications; Ibn Isḥāq reports later avoidance of public recitation. [38 notes: IBNHISHAM(أبو جهل,ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(رجل من المشركين); THALABI(مقاتل); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وقتادة، والضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو جهل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الأشدين,أبو جهل,ابن إسحاق,السهيلي); BIQAI-FULL(أبو الأشد بن أسيد بن كلدة الجمحي,أبو جهل); DURR-FULL(أبو الأشدين الجمحي,ابن عباس,السدي); BURSEVI(أبو الأسود ابن أسيد بن كلدة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو الأشد بن أسيد الجمحي,ابن عباس وجابر بن زيد,السدي,قيل); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(narratives); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Abū Jahl)]
- 74:31/q07/p2 The verse answered an unnamed boast that even the least strong could enter Hell and then reach Paradise. — Al-Baghawī transmits this alternative preceding boast. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 74:31/q08 What happened when Jews asked about Hell's keepers?  [لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · reports]
- 74:31/q08/p1 They asked the Prophet or Companions the count; the Prophet indicated nineteen with his fingers and they affirmed it. — Jābir's versions vary between ten then nine and ten, ten, then nine; al-Barāʾ's version says revelation followed a Companion's deferral. Ibn al-ʿArabī calls Jābir's report authentic but notes the apparent Medinan setting; Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ālūsī say Jews could meet Meccans or travel, and Ibn al-ʿArabī offers that Ansar had not heard the verse or did not know nineteen named keepers. [13 notes: BIQAI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله); BURSEVI(عين المعاني); ALUSI-FULL(البراء,جابر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي,جابر بن عبد الله)]

## 74:31/q09 Why do scripture recipients become certain?  [لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether certainty arises from a matching scriptural number, divine power, or an allegorical continuity.
- 74:31/q09/p1 The Qur'anic nineteen matches their Torah or Gospel, confirming the report, Muhammad's prophethood, and revelation's divine origin. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and many commentators transmit the scriptural count. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī stress that Muhammad had not read their books; al-Rāzī adds that hearing the Messenger removes doubts about textual alteration, while proclaiming a mockable detail shows sincerity. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Islahi note certainty need not lead every informed person to faith. [54 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري، الزجاج,ابن عباس,الفراء,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وقتادة، والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وغيرهم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg, IK, Q, Ṭ)]
- 74:31/q09/p2 The count demonstrates God's power to empower one being as many. — Jishumī reports this as another account of what becomes certain. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 74:31/q09/p3 They recognize an allegory and the Qur'an's rational continuity with earlier revelation. — Asad says understanding the symbolism convinces earlier revelation recipients. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:31/q10 Who are 'those given the Book' here?  [ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the referent is all scriptural communities, only Jews, believing recipients, or inward intellectual types.
- 74:31/q10/p1 Jews and Christians, including believing scripturalists. — Al-Ālūsī calls this most likely; al-Qurṭubī cites believers such as ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām; Ibn ʿAbbās specifically understands believing recipients, while al-Qurṭubī also reports all scripturalists. Islahi describes sincere recipients who stood beside believers. [5 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:31/q10/p2 Jews specifically, including those whose certainty fails to become faith. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues Jewish scholars held the knowledge and were in contact with Mecca; 2:146 describes concealed truth. He says knowledge without action becomes evidence against them. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:31/q10/p3 People endowed with discriminating intellect, or imitators who received scripture. — Qāshānī's inward reading contrasts intellectual certainty, direct unveiling, and unverified imitators. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 74:31/q11 How does li-yastaqīna relate to 'we made'?  [لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic governor of the purpose clause and whether its lām denotes intention or consequence.
- 74:31/q11/p1 Its lām expresses purpose and attaches to jaʿalnā, coordinating the following faith and doubt clauses. — Al-Naḥḥās calls the lām originally prepositional, with repetition optional; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr say it does not depend on fitna; al-Intiṣāf allows its reach across the exception; al-Rāzī also supplies an omitted 'and'. Bursevī reads the sin as seeking certainty; Ibn ʿĀshūr says istīqān intensifies certainty. [11 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الانتصاف,البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:31/q11/p2 It depends on an omitted 'we did this' rather than directly on jaʿalnā. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this alternative. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]
- 74:31/q11/p3 The lām expresses consequence rather than an intended purpose. — Islahi takes the trial's beneficial results as consequences for sincere readers. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:31/q12 How do believers increase in faith?  [وَيَزْدَادَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِيمَٰنًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether faith grows by added assent and corroboration, its fruits or quality, or inward unveiling.
- 74:31/q12/p1 They affirm the newly revealed report of nineteen and gain confidence when scripture readers corroborate it. — Sources variously describe additional confirmation, assent to new revelation, witness to Muhammad's truthfulness, stronger recognition of God's wisdom and power, and more secure conviction after weighing objections. Al-Rāzī distinguishes increase in faith's fruits from essence; Ibn ʿĀshūr holds assent's quality fixed and its objects cumulative. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:31/q12/p2 Faith grows through unveiling and direct witnessing beyond intellectual certainty. — Qāshānī contrasts discriminating intellect, verified realization, and imitation. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 74:31/q13 What does the promise of no doubt add?  [وَلَا يَرْتَابَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ · rhetoric]
- 74:31/q13/p1 It reinforces certainty and growing faith, ensuring stable conviction about the report and prophethood. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Ṭabarī focus on the number; Jishumī adds God's power and prophethood. Al-Rāzī notes forgotten premises can reopen doubt; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Ālūsī describe settled minds despite apparent counterevidence. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts knowledge without action among some scripture readers; al-Biqāʿī contrasts deliberate doubt. [24 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:31/q13/p2 The repetition and naming of both groups prevent an ambiguous pronoun and emphasize the two distinct responses. — Al-Ālūsī says a pronoun might refer only to believers and notes the active participle's connotation of enduring faith; al-Biqāʿī says God's passive giving of scripture needs no named giver. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 74:31/q14 Who have disease in their hearts?  [ٱلَّذِينَ فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the Meccan verse predicts later hypocrisy, describes contemporary doubt or opposition, or points to Jewish envy.
- 74:31/q14/p1 Hypocrites, especially those later appearing in Medina, with doubt mingled in their hearts. — Many explain the Meccan verse as foretelling Medinan hypocrisy; al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī defend predictive revelation as miraculous. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues hypocritical motives could already exist in Mecca, invoking 29:10–11 and 22:11. Al-Rāzī also reports a broad unbeliever identification. [34 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(أكثر المفسرين); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي، مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل)-; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسين بن الفضل)-; JISHUMI-; RAZI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل البجلي)-]
- 74:31/q14/p2 Doubters, opponents, or wavering Meccans, not later hypocrites. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl invokes the Meccan setting and absence of Meccan hypocrisy; Ibn ʿĀshūr names al-Akhnas and al-Walīd and defines disease as ill intent. Muqātil instead names doubting Jews of Medina and treats the verse as Medinan. Al-Rāzī distinguishes most doubting Meccans from those sure the Prophet was false; Asad calls them half-hearted. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسين بن الفضل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسين بن الفضل,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل البجلي); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q); JISHUMI+]
- 74:31/q14/p3 Jealous Jews whose envy of Ishmaelites and Muhammad produces mockery. — Islahi says disease here is envy, while sincere scripturalists respond differently; Muqātil names doubting Medinan Jews. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL]
- 74:31/q14/p4 Heart disease may denote inward vices such as ignorance, cowardice and miserliness. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāghib's broad range but rejects extending punitive Quranic heart disease to every such trait; she limits it to hypocrisy, doubt, rancor, temptation and lust. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]

## 74:31/q15 Who are the unbelievers named beside the diseased-hearted?  [وَٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether these unbelievers are Meccan polytheists or denying scripture communities.
- 74:31/q15/p1 Meccan Arab polytheists or those openly fixed in denial. — Al-Wāḥidī prefers Muqātil's Arab identification since scripture readers would not deny their own book; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Biqāʿī stress settled denial. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء، الكلبي)+]
- 74:31/q15/p2 Jews and Christians. — Al-Wāḥidī reports ʿAṭāʾ and al-Kalbī then rejects this against his Arab-polytheist reading; al-Qurṭubī reports it as the majority's view. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء، الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 74:31/q16 What does the lām of 'so that they say' express?  [وَلِيَقُولَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether their speech is a divine aim, an incidental consequence, or a prediction.
- 74:31/q16/p1 An eventual or unintended outcome, not the intended purpose of their objectionable speech. — The Muʿtazila, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others compare 28:8; al-Ālūsī distinguishes intended guidance from incidental misguidance and allows both clauses as consequence; Bursevī resolves the problem of God intending mockery this way. [10 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 74:31/q16/p2 A real divine purpose, since God can intend their response. — Al-Rāzī says his theological position allows this; al-Ṭabrisī reports a purpose by which questioning and reflection might lead to faith. Al-Ālūsī says divine acts can be explained by purposes. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(المحققون)]
- 74:31/q16/p3 A prediction of what these people will say. — Jishumī gives this beside the consequential reading. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 74:31/q17 How is mādhā arāda parsed?  [مَاذَآ أَرَادَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mādhā is a single object or mā plus a relative predicate.
- 74:31/q17/p1 Mādhā is one interrogative unit serving as the object of arāda. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Ālūsī report this analysis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:31/q17/p2 Mā is interrogative subject and dhā is a relative predicate meaning 'that which'. — Al-Naḥḥās reports dhā as 'which'; al-Ālūsī supplies an omitted relative pronoun; Ibn ʿĀshūr derives this use from the demonstrative. [3 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:31/q18 What is meant by 'this example'?  [بِهَٰذَا مَثَلًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether mathal means a plain account, mockingly named extraordinary number, or genuine allegory.
- 74:31/q18/p1 It means the account or description of the nineteen keepers. — Al-Wāḥidī observes there is no explicit parable as in 2:26 and cites 13:35 and 48:29 for mathal as account; al-Layth cites 13:35, and Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 47:15. - 74:31/q18/p2 They call the strange count a parable or proverb because it seems astonishing or symbolically incomplete. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī say nineteen rather than twenty appears defective to them; al-Rāzī says they suspect another sense is intended; al-Biqāʿī reports both astonishment and a nonliteral suspicion. - 74:31/q18/p3 It is an allegory of eschatological realities rather than a literal numerical claim. — Asad says those who literalize the count miss its purpose and compares 2:26. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:31/q19 What do the questioners intend by asking what God wanted?  [مَاذَآ أَرَادَ ٱللَّهُ بِهَٰذَا مَثَلًۭا · interpretation]

## 74:31/q22 How does God guide and misguide through this example?  [يُضِلُّ ٱللَّهُ مَن يَشَآءُ وَيَهْدِى مَن يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: Whether God creates the response, reveals a chosen response through a test, withholds aid, judges it, or manifests primordial disposition.
- 74:31/q22/p1 God guides receptive believers and leaves mocking deniers astray according to His will and knowledge. — Al-Ṭabarī says God abandons some from truth and enables others; al-Rāzī says the verse supports his companions' doctrine and counters Muʿtazilī alternatives. ## 74:31/q23 What are God's junūd?  [جُنُودَ رَبِّكَ · meaning]

- 74:31/q23/p2 Specifically the angelic helpers behind the nineteen keepers. — ʿAṭāʾ and later sources say each chief has innumerable attendants; al-Ālūsī reports a hadith of the Fire's many reins and angels. - 74:31/q35/p1 Aṣḥāb is a plural of ṣāḥib with an augment deleted. — Al-Naḥḥās says afʿāl does not usually pluralize faʿil or faʿl without that deletion. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 74:31/q36 What is the lexical history of malak?  [مَلَٰٓئِكَةًۭ · meaning]
- 74:31/q36/p1 Malak originally means messenger and then became specific to a class of accountable beings. — Jishumī cites poetry for the messenger and angel usages and notes the beings' particular form. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 74:31/q37 What further responses does mention of the nineteen elicit?  [عِدَّتَهُمْ · interpretation]
- 74:31/q37/p1 Mockers treat the report as a riddle; truth seekers benefit from its wisdom, while arrogant readers reject it. — Elmalılı contrasts receptive believers with diseased deniers; Islahi says mockers presume to know everything, and Asad reports speculation about why Muhammad chose this number. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:31/q38 How do the four stated responses reinforce one another?  [لِيَسْتَيْقِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَيَزْدَادَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِيمَٰنًۭا وَلَا يَرْتَابَ · rhetoric]
- 74:31/q38/p1 The sequence magnifies certainty, growth of faith, and freedom from doubt before contrasting the doubters. — Al-Rāzī enumerates four effects of the ambiguous passage; al-Zamakhsharī says stated certainty and denied doubt emphasize believers' composure; al-Zarkashī cites the order of scripturalists and believers as progression and the repeated phrase as magnification and awe. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BURHAN]

## 74:31/q39 What can govern 'for those who disbelieve'?  [لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · grammar]
- 74:31/q39/p1 It may be governed jointly by jaʿalnā and fitna. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says corrupt interpretation turned the count into a trial for unbelievers, while others could defer to God and reflect. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:31/q40 How does this wording recall an earlier question about an example?  [مَاذَآ أَرَادَ ٱللَّهُ بِهَٰذَا مَثَلًۭا · links]
- 74:31/q40/p1 It recalls the similar question in 2:26. — Abū Ḥayyān directs the reader to his earlier grammatical analysis of that phrase. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 74:31/q41 What doctrine is argued from making the count a trial?  [فِتْنَةًۭ لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ · theology]
- 74:31/q41/p1 The passage supports the view that God can will misguidance. — Al-Rāzī reports its use by those who argue that God may intend error for unbelievers. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بأن الله قد يريد الإضلال)]

## 74:31/q42 What further identification is given for God's hosts?  [جُنُودَ رَبِّكَ · referent]
- 74:31/q42/p1 The term can mean angels generally or specifically the keepers of Hell. — Ibn al-Jawzī records both senses in his lexical survey. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 74:31/q43 What does Qāshānī take the final pronoun to resume?  [وَمَا هِىَ · referent]
- 74:31/q43/p1 It resumes Saqar from the earlier promise to roast the sinner there. — Qāshānī says the final warning continues that earlier description. [1 notes: QASHANI]
