# 52:13: 15 questions

## 52:13/q01 How is يومَ connected grammatically to the surrounding verses?  [يَوْمَ · grammar]
turns on: The proposed governor or appositional antecedent: the day in 52:9 or 52:11, an implied saying, speech in 52:14, دافع, or لواقع; Makkī disputes the last verb's governance.
- 52:13/q01/p1 It is an apposition to the preceding يومئذ, the day of woe for the deniers in 52:11. — Naḥḥās, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī identify يومئذ as the antecedent; Rāzī relates it to the day promised in 52:11. Samīn allows this alongside another antecedent. Elmalılı says only that it replaces the preceding day expression. [8 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 52:13/q01/p2 It is an apposition to يوم تمور, the day the sky heaves in 52:9. — Bayḍāwī, Samīn and Bursevī give the day when the sky heaves as antecedent; Ālūsī cites 52:9, and Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the apposition inclusive. Elmalılı leaves his preceding day unspecified. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 52:13/q01/p3 It is a time adverb for an understood saying that introduces the next verse's speech. — Bayḍāwī and Samīn posit an implied saying before هذه النار; Bursevī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı likewise attach the time to the quoted speech, with Elmalılı saying it explains the woe. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 52:13/q01/p4 It is governed by the following statement in 52:14: on that day they are told this is the fire they denied. — Rāzī regards this as the plainest construction and contrasts it with apposition to the earlier day. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 52:13/q01/p5 It is accusative under دافع in the preceding verse. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this parse from al-Ḥawfī. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي)]
- 52:13/q01/p6 It depends on لواقع, with ما له من دافع interrupting its governor and complement parenthetically. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this parse. He also reports Makkī's assertion that واقع cannot govern يوم, without an argument for that restriction. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي)-]

## 52:13/q02 What does يُدَعُّونَ mean here?  [يُدَعُّونَ · meaning]
turns on: The forceful-pushing sense of the doubled verb, the light reading from calling, or Māwardī's proposed cursing by the wardens.
- 52:13/q02/p1 The deniers are shoved or driven harshly and forcefully toward or into Hell, with coercion and humiliation. — Mujāhid and Qatāda describe forced driving; Ibn ʿAbbās specifies their necks, while Mujāhid also says from behind. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk stresses coercion. Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Zayd, Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī compare 107:2, the pushing away of an orphan; Majāz cites Arab usage daʿa fī qafāhu and a light orphan verb, citing 2:107. Al-Layth cites a famine poem about pushing an orphan; Māwardī cites rajaz. Ibn al-Aʿrābī explains الدفر as pushing. Qatāda's version can emphasize violent rousing or hurrying. Samīn relates the common form to دفع and specifies pushing in the chest. Bursevī evokes shoving a stumbling person. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers harsh, rebuking coercion over mere deprivation of an orphan's right, since no right is being denied here. ʿIṣlāḥī adds hatred and worldly mockery; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes frightened people falling back and angels shoving them forward. Qāshānī adds dragging. The common form is doubled; Jishumī compares ṣakka yaṣukku ṣakkan. [43 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والضحاك والسدي وابن زيد,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الليث,عامة المفسرين وقتادة,مجاهد وابن الأعرابي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، الشعبي، محمد بن كعب، الضحاك، السدي، الثوري); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 52:13/q02/p2 They are called or summoned to the Fire. — Abū Rajāʾ and other named readers use a light form from دعاء; Qurtubī says the keepers then point out the denied Fire. Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn say they are told to come or enter; Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, Rāzī and Study Quran report calling, with the noun explained as a circumstantial expression or great calling. [9 notes: JISHUMI(أبو رجاء العطاردي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو رجاء العطاردي); RAZI-FULL(قارئ); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو رجاء العطاردي وابن السميقع); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وأبو رجاء والسلمي وزيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(علي والسلمي وأبو رجاء وزيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي والسلمي أبو رجاء); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 52:13/q02/p3 Hell's wardens call down curses on the condemned. — Māwardī offers this as a third possible sense after forceful pushing and driving. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 52:13/q03 Does دعّ derive from a call to a stumbling person?  [يُدَعُّونَ دَعًّا · meaning]
turns on: Whether al-Rāghib's call to a stumbling person explains the verse's forceful pushing.
- 52:13/q03/p1 Yes; its root began as the call دع دع to a stumbling person. — Samīn attributes this derivation to al-Rāghib but judges it remote from this verse's sense. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الراغب)-]
- 52:13/q03/p2 That derivation is remote from the sense here. — Samīn objects to al-Rāghib's proposed origin; his own explanation derives the common forceful form from دفع. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب)+]

## 52:13/q04 How is يُدَعُّونَ دَعًّا read?  [يُدَعُّونَ دَعًّا · readings]
turns on: Doubling versus lightening the verb's ʿayn changes its derivation from pushing to calling; reports also discuss the cognate noun's form and grammar.
- 52:13/q04/p1 The common reading doubles the ʿayn, giving the forceful-pushing sense. — Jishumī calls it the majority reading and compares ṣakka yaṣukku ṣakkan. Samīn describes open dāl and doubled ʿayn. ʿIṣlāḥī treats the established reading in the present muṣḥaf as authoritative for interpreting verses and words, arguing that alternative readings lose eloquence, purpose and wisdom. [4 notes: JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); SAMIN-DURR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 52:13/q04/p2 A light reading takes the verb from calling or summoning, and some accounts read دعاء as its accusative circumstantial expression. — Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya name Abū Rajāʾ; Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies still dāl and open ʿayn. Qurtubī adds Ibn al-Sumayqa. Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn add ʿAlī, al-Sulamī and Zayd ibn ʿAlī. Ālūsī names Zayd, al-Sulamī and Abū Rajāʾ and calls the noun circumstantial. Rāzī explicitly reports دعاء as accusative circumstantial; Bayḍāwī and Study Quran explain the resulting summons, the latter as a great calling. [9 notes: JISHUMI(أبو رجاء العطاردي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو رجاء العطاردي); RAZI-FULL(قارئ); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو رجاء العطاردي وابن السميقع); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وأبو رجاء والسلمي وزيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(علي والسلمي وأبو رجاء وزيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي والسلمي أبو رجاء); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 52:13/q05 What happens as the deniers are brought to Hell?  [يُدَعُّونَ إِلَىٰ نَارِ جَهَنَّمَ · interpretation]
- 52:13/q05/p1 Hell's keepers bind the deniers and force them into the Fire, commonly face-first and prodded from behind. — Muqātil says this follows judgment: hands shackled to necks, forelocks tied to feet behind their backs, then a face-first push and the announcement of the Fire they denied. Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī transmit Muqātil; Baghawī, Qurtubī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Nasafī, Bursevī and Ālūsī describe the same binding and thrusting. Baghawī, Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī add prodding from behind; Nasafī and Bursevī allow face-first or backward entry. Wāḥidī also reports the general exegetes' face-first account and Qatāda's violent hurrying. Study Quran adds dragging on faces, shackles, boiling liquid and cold murky fluid, citing 54:48, 40:71–72, 36:8, 40:70 and 38:57. ʿIṣlāḥī places the driving after the deniers' worldly pranks and before the rebuke for denial. [15 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة المفسرين وقتادة,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 52:13/q06 What does Hell signify in the inward reading?  [نَارِ جَهَنَّمَ · inward]
- 52:13/q06/p1 Hell is deprivation and pain deep in rebellious nature amid chains of attachment and bodily disposition. — Qāshānī gives this inward account while also glossing the verb as violent driving and dragging. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 52:13/q07 What does the image of being pushed toward Hell convey?  [يُدَعُّونَ إِلَىٰ نَارِ جَهَنَّمَ · rhetoric]
- 52:13/q07/p1 It depicts terror and humiliation: keepers cast them from a distance, and frightened deniers are forced forward. — Rāzī says Hell is so terrifying that its keepers do not approach it but cast inmates from afar. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that terrified people fall back while assigned angels shove them onward. Biqāʿī says God appoints the agents of harsh driving and personifies Hell as a grimacing, loathing, coarse, roaring layer that receives them. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 52:13/q08 What does the repeated verbal noun دَعًّا add?  [دَعًّا · rhetoric]
- 52:13/q08/p1 It confirms and intensifies the shove, presenting it as emphatically real and great in force. — Rāzī says the noun makes the push undeniable, comparing insistence that a weak run or trivial blow barely deserves its name. Biqāʿī says repetition of the root confirms and heightens the scene. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the indefinite noun magnifies the act. ʿIṣlāḥī says the repeated verbal form increases the force and hatred. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:13/q09 How is the next verse's statement ‘This is the Fire’ supplied here?  [يَوْمَ يُدَعُّونَ إِلَىٰ نَارِ جَهَنَّمَ دَعًّا · grammar]
- 52:13/q09/p1 An omitted saying introduces the guardians' words: ‘This is the Fire.’ — Majāz expands the compressed wording as fa-yuqālu hādhihi al-nār. Samīn treats هذه النار as a nominal clause dependent on an implied saying verb and attributes the speech to the guardians. Rāzī makes the following declaration govern the day; Muqātil and ʿIṣlāḥī place the announcement after the shove. [5 notes: MAJAZ; SAMIN-DURR; RAZI-FULL; MUQATIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:13/q10 How can pushing toward Hell here relate to being dragged through fire in 54:48?  [يُدَعُّونَ إِلَىٰ نَارِ جَهَنَّمَ · links]
turns on: Whether 54:48 describes another fire, another angel's task, dragging by a chain from outside, or a later action inside the same Hell.
- 52:13/q10/p1 The two descriptions appear to conflict: here they are pushed toward Hell, whereas 54:48 says they are dragged through fire. — Rāzī poses the difficulty before reporting four possible reconciliations. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 52:13/q10/p2 They are dragged through one fire, then cast from a distance into another, hotter Hell. — Rāzī cites 40:71–72 for dragging through boiling liquid before kindling in fire. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 52:13/q10/p3 Different angels handle different stages: one pushes them toward fire and another drags them within it. — Rāzī distinguishes the tasks or stages of the angels. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 52:13/q10/p4 Chains allow them to be dragged inside while the angel pulling them remains outside. — Rāzī proposes this as a third reconciliation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 52:13/q10/p5 Angels push them in to humiliate them, then enter and drag them through Hell. — Rāzī offers this as a fourth possibility. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 52:13/q11 What interpretive principles does ʿIṣlāḥī bring to this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 52:13/q11/p1 He relies on the established muṣḥaf reading, Arab usage, discourse context and Qurʾānic parallels, aiming to protect readers from hostile misinterpretation. — ʿIṣlāḥī says his exegesis counters Orientalist, Jewish and extremist Shiite mischief; attentive readers will be protected. He takes the current muṣḥaf's mutawātir reading as the basis of verse and word interpretation, and says Arab usage, context and Qurʾānic parallels remove doubt. He argues that using other readings sacrifices eloquence, purpose and wisdom. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:13/q12 How does ʿIṣlāḥī understand the Qurʾān's arrangement and sūrah coherence?  [* · links]
- 52:13/q12/p1 God and His Prophet set an order grounded in religious and divine-law wisdom; linked sūrahs and internally coherent verses serve central themes. — ʿIṣlāḥī contrasts the order with historical ordering of the Torah and Gospel. He says every Qurʾānic group is linked, each sūrah closely follows its predecessor, changing placement creates a gap, and removing a verse can damage its thematic sequence. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 52:13/q13 When was Sūrah al-Ṭūr revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notes offer both broad Makkan dating and a more specific sequence after al-Sajdah without arguing that they conflict.
- 52:13/q13/p1 It is Makkan, probably from the latter half of the Makkan period. — Study Quran reports commentators' agreement on Makkan provenance and its likely later dating. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 52:13/q13/p2 Some place its revelation immediately after Sūrah 32, al-Sajdah. — Study Quran reports this chronology from some commentators, citing 32:1. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 52:13/q14 Why is the sūrah called al-Ṭūr?  [* · sciences]
- 52:13/q14/p1 Its title comes from the Mount in its first verse, usually understood as Mount Sinai. — Study Quran says most readers take al-Ṭūr as Sinai wherever it appears, citing 52:1, 2:63, 2:93, 19:52, 20:80, 28:29 and 28:46; it notes the fuller name in 23:20 and 95:2. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 52:13/q15 How does this verse fit al-Ṭūr's opening and the preceding sūrahs?  [* · links]
- 52:13/q15/p1 It forms part of the opening warning of promised punishment and Judgment, developing the preceding sūrahs' proof of resurrection and the deniers' fate. — ʿIṣlāḥī places al-Ṭūr third after Qāf and al-Dhāriyāt, which prove resurrection and judgment by intellectual, historical, inward and outward evidence while presenting reward and punishment; he cites 51:5–6. He says 52:1–16 warns obstinate Quraysh through historical and universal facts, asserts punishment cannot be hastened or averted, and depicts Judgment. Study Quran links the opening half to 51:59–60 and the promises in 51:5 and 51:22; it describes the sequence from oaths and punishment to the reverent's reward, responses to charges against the Prophet, challenges to deniers, and closing counsel of patience and praise. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
