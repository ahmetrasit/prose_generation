# 7:195: 34 questions

## 7:195/q01 Whose limbs and senses are challenged?  [أَلَهُمْ أَرْجُلٌۭ أَمْ لَهُمْ أَيْدٍۢ أَمْ لَهُمْ أَعْيُنٌۭ أَمْ لَهُمْ ءَاذَانٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the referent is the statue, its represented leader, or the function of sculpted limbs.
- 7:195/q01/p1 The questions address the worshipped idols or images. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī, and later readers describe inert idols. Ibn ʿĀshūr names human-shaped Hubal, Dhū al-Kaffayn, Kaʿb, and Suwāʿ. Islahi distinguishes statues from their imagined divine referents and cites the Psalms' parallel. Al-Biqāʿī says any capacity they have comes from their maker. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:195/q01/p2 Here they concern the worshipped leaders, distinct from the idols in the earlier passage. — Elmalılı rejects the claim that the two passages merely repeat each other; he takes the first as images and this one as leaders. [1 notes: ELMALILI+; ELMALILI(Keşşâf gibi بعض müfessîrin)-]
- 7:195/q01/p3 Sculpted limbs may exist, but their functions do not operate independently. — Qāshānī says God moves all organs. Abū Ḥayyān allows the negation to target functions even if limbs are carved. Al-Ālūsī's inward explanation similarly makes God the mover. [3 notes: QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:195/q02 What do the four questions prove about worship of idols?  [أَلَهُمْ أَرْجُلٌۭ أَمْ لَهُمْ أَيْدٍۢ أَمْ لَهُمْ أَعْيُنٌۭ أَمْ لَهُمْ ءَاذَانٌۭ · interpretation]
- 7:195/q02/p1 Their ineffective motion and perception make them unable to benefit, answer, or defend worshippers; humans surpass them, so worship is irrational. — Al-Ṭabarī lists travel, defense, seeing hidden matters, and hearing unknown matters as routes to benefit. Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī specify interests, harm, discernment, and prayers. Rāzī stresses capacities over organs. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Zahrawī stress worshippers' superiority. Ṭabrisī says even worshipping a living created being is blameworthy. Al-Bursawī adds life and a mover. Islahi compares flies and animals taking offerings; Kur'an Yolu says idols cannot guide or hear. [51 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزهراوي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:195/q02/p2 Each specific denial strengthens the general proof that idols are inert and cannot answer. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the details force assent to the broad conclusion. Al-Ālūsī says lacking even one faculty defeats the claim. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the questions to the preceding summons. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:195/q03 How is the hāʾ in أَلَهُمْ read?  [أَلَهُمْ · readings]
- 7:195/q03/p1 Abū Jaʿfar gives the hāʾ ḍamma; other readers give it kasra. — Al-Baghawī reports Abū Jaʿfar's ḍamma here and in al-Qaṣaṣ and al-Dukhān, with kasra for the remaining readers. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر)]

## 7:195/q04 Why specify walking with the legs?  [أَرْجُلٌۭ يَمْشُونَ بِهَآ · rhetoric]
- 7:195/q04/p1 The characteristic action is the target: apparent legs that cannot walk lack effective function. — Al-Bursawī says response requires life, a mover, and active and perceptive faculties. Al-Ālūsī says a limb never performing its defining action is not a real functional limb. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines walking as repeated movement between places. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:195/q05 What does يَبْطِشُونَ mean?  [يَبْطِشُونَ بِهَآ · meaning]
- 7:195/q05/p1 It means seizing, grasping, or striking forcefully, including assault and defense. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Jishumī cite 85:12. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī stress forceful taking; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies harming by hand. Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī apply it to defending worshippers. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:195/q06 How is يَبْطِشُونَ read?  [يَبْطِشُونَ · readings]
turns on: The verb has two attested Arabic imperfect vowels and differing reader attributions.
- 7:195/q06/p1 The common reading has kasra on ṭāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān also name Nāfiʿ, al-Ḥasan, and al-Aʿraj. Abū ʿAlī calls this more eloquent; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Qiraat-ER recognize both vowel patterns. [10 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع والحسن والأعرج); TABRISI(أبو جعفر,أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن والأعرج ونافع); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(الباقون); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(vw)]
- 7:195/q06/p2 Abū Jaʿfar reads ḍamma on ṭāʾ, also reported from Shayba and Nāfiʿ. — Reports extend the form to al-Qaṣaṣ and al-Dukhān. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī call it dialectal. Qiraat-ER says it is a valid Form I pattern, perhaps heavier in sound. [14 notes: NAHHAS(أبو جعفر وشيبة); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأبو جعفر وشيبة); TABRISI(أبو جعفر,أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر وشيبة ونافع); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(أبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 7:195/q07 What grammatical forms do the limb nouns have?  [أَرْجُلٌۭ أَيْدٍۢ ءَاذَانٌۭ · grammar]
- 7:195/q07/p1 Hand, foot, and ear are feminine, and yad restores a root yāʾ in its diminutive. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī give feminine diminutive marking and yadiyyah with doubled yāʾ. Al-Naḥḥās also reports dialectal ayd. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو جعفر وشيبة); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:195/q08 What does عَيْن denote here and in related Arabic usage?  [أَعْيُنٌۭ · meaning]
- 7:195/q08/p1 Here it is the physical seeing eye; the noun also has transferred senses. — Ibn al-Jawzī calls the seeing eye primary, citing 90:8; he lists a person's self, springs, visible water, tribal notables, deliberate action, service while watched, direct meeting, and the evil eye. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 7:195/q09 Why mention eyes and ears?  [أَعْيُنٌۭ يُبْصِرُونَ بِهَآ ءَاذَانٌۭ يَسْمَعُونَ بِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stress lies on outward faculties or inward perception.
- 7:195/q09/p1 They would let an ally recognize danger and hear appeals, but idols cannot use them. — Al-Ṭabarī describes seeing what worshippers miss and hearing what they cannot; al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī name harms, benefits, prayers, and pleading. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees both as aids to rescue. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:195/q09/p2 Physical sight alone does not yield true inward perception. — Al-Qushayrī says idolaters saw images while hearts were veiled; seeing spiritual masters means inward unveiling in proportion to faith and reverence. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 7:195/q10 How does أَمْ work in these questions?  [أَمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a genuine choice is sought or successive rhetorical denials are made.
- 7:195/q10/p1 It is disconnected, shifting to a fresh denial as bal plus interrogative hamza. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes alternative am and says the difference is semantic, not grammatical form. Abū Ḥayyān says transition does not cancel the prior clause. Al-Suyūṭī says repudiative hamza functions like negation. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī hear a new rebuke; Ibn ʿĀshūr also permits an aw sense after a nonliteral question. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:195/q10/p2 In a genuine choice question, am coordinates alternatives and asks for specification. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares aw and cites 10:59; Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts this with the present verse. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:195/q11 Why are the faculties separately named and ordered this way?  [أَلَهُمْ أَرْجُلٌۭ أَمْ لَهُمْ أَيْدٍۢ أَمْ لَهُمْ أَعْيُنٌۭ أَمْ لَهُمْ ءَاذَانٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether order prioritizes ascending worth, the mechanics of rescue, or immediate force of rebuke.
- 7:195/q11/p1 Detailed, condemnatory questions make the incapacity unmistakable. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says dividing a broad claim into specifics strengthens proof; Abū Ḥayyān adds denial and amazement. Ibn ʿĀshūr says fronting the predicate stresses lack of possession, citing Ḥassān, and notes human-shaped idols. Al-Ālūsī says every clause repeats rebuke. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:195/q11/p2 The sequence ascends from lower to nobler or more useful faculties, hearing highest. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān describe ascent; al-Biqāʿī moves from feared approach to assault to sight and hearing distant threats. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:195/q11/p3 The order follows rescue: feet come to help, hands fight, and eyes and ears guide action. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the unusual eyes-before-ears order versus 2:7. Al-Bursawī and Shaykh al-Islām contrast one's own movement with grasping others; hands pair with legs. Al-Ālūsī says immobility is more conspicuous and eyes are more familiar. [6 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:195/q11/p4 Denying the highest attribute first could intensify the blame. — Abū al-Fatḥ al-Qushayrī objects that denial of the highest does not entail denial of lower faculties; al-Burhān answers by defending ascent. [1 notes: BURHAN(الشيخ أبو الفتح القشيري); BURHAN-]
- 7:195/q11/p5 Ascending denial progressively removes all likeness after createdness is admitted. — Al-Burhān replies that removing weaker then stronger shared properties is subtler. Al-Suyūṭī also lists verse-end sound and restriction as ordering considerations, without specifying this verse. [2 notes: ITQAN; BURHAN+; BURHAN(الشيخ أبو الفتح القشيري)-]
- 7:195/q11/p6 The sequence can be read as descending from the most important capacity. — Abū Ḥayyān says the questions move from the most important capacity downward. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 7:195/q12 Why omit tongues from the organ list?  [أَرْجُلٌۭ أَيْدٍۢ أَعْيُنٌۭ ءَاذَانٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 7:195/q12/p1 Rescuers act at once without asking why help is needed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the four organs serve movement, knowledge, and defense; speech is unnecessary for the invoked rescue. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:195/q13 How does this verse develop the preceding description of idols?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the earlier likeness was actual, hypothetical, or referred to different objects.
- 7:195/q13/p1 It confirms inability to answer or help and intensifies the prior challenge. — Al-Baghawī, al-Wāḥidī, Rāzī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect it to 7:192–194. Al-Wāḥidī reports the Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm's point that human powers show idols are not truly equals. Islahi distinguishes conceived beings from statues; Samarrai surveys 7:191–198. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 7:195/q13/p2 The questions cancel a merely supposed equality. — Al-Ālūsī reports that a hypothetical likeness is withdrawn; the commentators Elmalılı reports distinguish this test of worshipworthiness from an earlier reproach. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Keşşâf gibi بعض müfessîrin)]
- 7:195/q13/p3 Likeness in being created or owned remains, although functional likeness is denied. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's total cancellation: otherwise one divine report would conflict with another. Al-Ālūsī also allows actual likeness with idol inferiority. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 7:195/q13/p4 This passage concerns leaders while the previous one addressed images. — Elmalılı rejects the repetition explanation. [1 notes: ELMALILI+; ELMALILI(Keşşâf gibi بعض müfessîrin)-]

## 7:195/q14 Does denying idols' organs imply God has bodily organs?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether divinity requires bodily organs or simply power and perception.
- 7:195/q14/p1 No; the comparison concerns human superiority to inert idols, while God has perfect abilities without organs. — Rāzī rejects an anthropomorphist inference. Abū Ḥayyān repeats the reply. Al-Ālūsī says inability, not an anatomical condition of divinity, is at issue. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(المشبهة)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-]
- 7:195/q14/p2 Some anthropomorphists infer that God must have organs if their absence disproves idols' divinity. — Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān report the inference; Rāzī and al-Ālūsī argue against it. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(المشبهة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:195/q15 Can created things have effects granted by God?  [* · theology]
- 7:195/q15/p1 God may give things effective power that acts by His permission. — Al-Ālūsī supports this and rejects calling it near disbelief; al-Biqāʿī says all capacity comes from a maker; Qāshānī says God moves organs. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 7:195/q16 Who are شُرَكَاءَكُمْ?  [شُرَكَآءَكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the alleged partners are cult objects or human and jinn opponents.
- 7:195/q16/p1 The idols or beings worshipped and associated with God. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Wāḥidī. Al-Naḥḥās adds that people assigned them a share of wealth; al-Baghawī calls them idols; Asad refers to 6:22. Islahi distinguishes imagined beings from carved representations. [9 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:195/q16/p2 Human and jinn partners. — Qāshānī's inward explanation identifies them this way. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 7:195/q17 Why call the idols the worshippers' partners?  [شُرَكَآءَكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 7:195/q17/p1 The possessive reflects the worshippers' own naming and assignment of them as divine partners. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān explain this naming; al-Naḥḥās adds the assigned share of wealth. [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:195/q17/p2 Human-like reference accommodates an audience treating idols as deities. — Al-Burhān explains man in place of normal nonrational mā and says it clarifies the target for hearers. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 7:195/q18 What does ٱدْعُوا۟ شُرَكَآءَكُمْ command?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ شُرَكَآءَكُمْ · interpretation]
- 7:195/q18/p1 Summon the alleged partners to help against the Prophet. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Nasafī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Asad describe enlisting them. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says their action should then show if they are gods. [14 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:195/q19 How is قُلِ read before ٱدْعُوا۟?  [قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ · readings]
- 7:195/q19/p1 Liaison allows kasra or ḍamma. — Qiraat-ER says ḍamma preserves the imperative's original vowel, while kasra adapts to hamzat al-waṣl. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vhr)]

## 7:195/q20 What does كِيدُونِ mean?  [ثُمَّ كِيدُونِ · meaning]
- 7:195/q20/p1 Plot against me with every possible stratagem. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it trickery; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines kayd as harm disguised as harmless, referring to 7:183. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn Kathīr emphasize all available effort. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:195/q21 Who is addressed by كِيدُونِ?  [كِيدُونِ · referent]
turns on: Who counts as the worshippers' partners in the joint plot.
- 7:195/q21/p1 The idolaters and their alleged partners together. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and others include both. Al-Biqāʿī notes the many opponents against one Prophet; Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the result to divine victory. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:195/q21/p2 The opponents include human and jinn partners. — Qāshānī's inward reading presents these as challengers under God's protection. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 7:195/q22 Why challenge them to plot?  [قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ شُرَكَآءَكُمْ ثُمَّ كِيدُونِ فَلَا تُنظِرُونِ · interpretation]
- 7:195/q22/p1 Their failure exposes impotence and the Prophet's confidence in God's protection. — Al-Ṭabarī says God guards him while idols cannot guard theirs. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī stress fearlessness; al-Bursawī adds a Persian couplet. Ibn ʿĀshūr grants opponents allies, stealth, and speed so failure is decisive and hints their idols will be destroyed. Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 21:105 and Muhammad's unusual declaration of righteousness. The Study Quran says their scheme cannot thwart God's mission. [23 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:195/q23 What prompted the challenge?  [قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ شُرَكَآءَكُمْ ثُمَّ كِيدُونِ · reports]
- 7:195/q23/p1 The idolaters threatened the Prophet with harm from their gods. — Al-Ḥasan is reported by al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Rāzī. - 7:195/q24/p1 Do not delay or give me respite; act at once. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Rāzī give this gloss. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares 2:280. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the challengers should prove any claimed power immediately. ## 7:195/q25 How are the final yāʾs of كِيدُونِ and تُنظِرُونِ read?  [كِيدُونِ تُنظِرُونِ · readings]

- 7:195/q25/p1 Many readers omit the first-person yāʾ of kaydūn in continuation and pause. — Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī are repeatedly listed; Warsh, Qālūn, and al-Musayyibī also omit it. - 7:195/q25/p2 Abū ʿAmr and some others retain kaydūnī's yāʾ in continuation only. — Nāfiʿ in named transmissions, Abū Jaʿfar, and al-Dājūnī from Hishām are also reported. Al-Samīn corrects Abū Ḥayyān: Abū ʿAmr always drops it at pause. Qiraat-ER notes full continuous yāʾ against a pausal sukūn. [19 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو ونافع عن ابن جماز وإسماعيل بن جعفر وابن عامر); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو ونافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وأبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو ونافع); TABRISI(هشام ويعقوب وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وإسماعيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو ونافع); RAZI-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو وهشام); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(أبو عمرو وأبو جعفر والداجوني عن هشام,أبو عمرو وأبو جعفر ويعقوب,هشام); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(Yz); NASAFI-FULL(يعقوب وأبو عمرو)]
- 7:195/q25/p3 Hishām and Yaʿqūb are reported retaining kaydūnī's yāʾ in both contexts. — Ibn al-Jazarī prefers the majority Hishām report over a continuation-only report, instructing that al-Taysīr be read accordingly. Al-Ḥulwānī, Ibn Suwār, Ibn Fāris, and Qunbul through Ibn Shanabūdh are also reported; 11:55 retains yāʾ universally. - 7:195/q25/p4 Most omit the yāʾ of tunẓirūn; Yaʿqūb retains it in continuation and pause. — Al-Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn al-Jazarī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr give Yaʿqūb's form. Al-Jishumī adds eased pronunciation; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls deletion eloquent. Samarrai contrasts concise and extended contexts, excepting verse endings and vocatives. ## 7:195/q26 Why is the yāʾ shorter here than in Hūd?  [كِيدُونِ تُنظِرُونِ · links]
- 7:195/q26/p1 Al-Aʿrāf's concise challenge suits short forms; Hūd's longer confrontation suits pronounced yāʾ. — Samarrai says Hūd is more intense and repeats first-person yāʾ; al-Aʿrāf uses it once in 7:196. He notes the general link between written yāʾ and expanded context, with verse-end and vocative exceptions. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 7:195/q26/p2 Al-Aʿrāf stresses swift punishment, while Hūd has fuller accounts and respite. — Samarrai compares ʿĀd (7:72; 11:58–60), Ṣāliḥ (7:78; 11:66–67), and Noah (7:63–64; 11:32–33). Al-Aʿrāf places thumma before fa, like 7:95's respite then sudden seizure; Hūd reverses. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 7:195/q27 How do ثُمَّ and فَ order the challenge?  [ثُمَّ فَلَا · grammar]
- 7:195/q27/p1 Thumma introduces plotting after summoning; fa gives the immediate no-respite command. — Samarrai contrasts Hūd's reversed order and relates fa to immediacy and thumma to interval; 7:95 offers a parallel. Elsewhere he notes thumma works in commands and reports and can order statements without later events, citing 44:48, 2:199, 10:46, 11:3, 11:52, and poetry. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 7:195/q28 What prophetic challenges echo this verse?  [قُلِ ٱدْعُوا۟ شُرَكَآءَكُمْ ثُمَّ كِيدُونِ فَلَا تُنظِرُونِ · links]
- 7:195/q28/p1 Hūd and Noah issue analogous challenges; God's challenge to deniers also echoes the scheme motif. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān cite Hūd's response after claims that gods harmed him (11:54–55). The Study Quran adds Noah (10:71), 77:39, 35:44, and 7:182–183. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes other prophets' challenges. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:195/q29 What inward response follows recognizing the true protector?  [* · inward]
- 7:195/q29/p1 Reliance on God cuts hope in creatures and brings contentment with His care. — Al-Qushayrī says God alone guides, answers, meets need, and controls harm; when He withholds, contentment in the innermost self exceeds gifts to the heart. Al-Bursawī's Persian couplet voices fearlessness even against both worlds. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]
- 7:195/q29/p2 Inner sight surpasses mere physical looking. — Al-Qushayrī says idolaters' hearts were veiled despite seeing idols; spiritual sight of masters is an unveiling proportionate to reverence and faith. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 7:195/q29/p3 Faith and hypocrisy can coexist, with the prevailing element determining a heart's fate. — Salman distinguishes covered, inverted, mixed, and bare hearts; the believer's heart holds a lamp. Faith is a watered plant, hypocrisy a pus-fed sore; whichever prevails consumes its host. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سلمان الفارسي)]

## 7:195/q30 What associated Arabic examples illuminate ellipsis?  [* · rhetoric]
- 7:195/q30/p1 Arabic may leave an alternative, verb, noun, or participle unspoken when context supplies it. — Ibn Qutayba cites an unnamed poet omitting the second alternative after ahum hamamtuhu; Abū Dhuʾayb implies am ghayy; another line implies muqbilan after raʾatnī bi-ḥablayhā; Dhū al-Rumma implies aqbala before al-layl; the Umm Salim line implies sabīl; al-Namir's aynamā implies dhahab. Samarrai describes contextual omissions of letters, verbs, nouns, and parallel features. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبو ذؤيب,النمر بن تولب,ذو الرمة); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 7:195/q31 What associated examples illustrate rhetorical implication and poetic comparison?  [* · rhetoric]
- 7:195/q31/p1 Context selects one implication where a noun form can suggest several. — Al-Jurjānī explains indefinite shar in shar aharra dhā nāb; arajul atāka am imraʾah asks gender, whereas arajul atāka am rajulān asks number. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 7:195/q31/p2 Conventional signs and lexical use govern a poem's meaning. — Al-Jurjānī says tears signify grief and rejects reading dry eyes as reunion's joy: jumūd is a defect, like a rainless year or milkless camel. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 7:195/q31/p3 Indefiniteness may restrict a benefit to a future part rather than every case. — Al-Jurjānī says desire targets what is absent; 2:179's ḥayāh and 16:69's healing name partial benefits. Retribution saves the intended victim rather than the deterred murderer. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 7:195/q31/p4 Imitation and eloquence require purposeful arrangement of meanings, not recitation or meter alone. — Al-Jurjānī says repeating Imruʾ al-Qays makes no poet; a Qurʾanic match needs meanings requiring its sequence, and meter is not eloquence. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 7:195/q31/p5 Poetic parallels compare distinct images and paradoxes. — Al-Jurjānī lists gifts across Najd and lowlands, enticement and refusal, desire versus glory, veiled sunlight, eyes wounding hearts, safety as disease, perfection hastening decline, fewer visits preserving love, modest benefaction, and calamity striking the best. [9 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(غير مسمى)]
- 7:195/q31/p6 Denying a necessary concomitant can deny its subject. — Al-Burhān applies this to idols' eyes and ears. Ibn al-Munīr separately defends Jarīr's use of 'before' to negate evil, against al-Aṣmaʿī, Khalaf, and Ibn Rashīq, citing 18:109 and 13:2. [2 notes: BURHAN; BURHAN(ابن المنير)+]

## 7:195/q32 What associated reports describe units and modes of revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 7:195/q32/p1 Reports recommend learning five verses at a time and say Gabriel conveyed five. — Al-Suyūṭī cites Abū Saʿīd, ʿUmar, and Abū al-ʿĀliya. A weak ʿAlī report adds memory protection except al-Anʿām. If sound, the report describes memorization in groups, not an exclusive revelation pattern. [4 notes: ITQAN(أبو العالية,أبو سعيد الخدري,عمر)]
- 7:195/q32/p2 Revelation may mean manifested recitation or God's teaching Gabriel; delivery has two modes. — Al-Iṣfahānī says Sunnis agree divine speech is revealed but differ over meaning. The Prophet may rise toward angelic form or Gabriel descend in human form; the former is harder. [2 notes: ITQAN(الأصفهاني)]

## 7:195/q33 What adjacent reading and grammar notes accompany this verse?  [* · readings]
- 7:195/q33/p1 A light negative inna reading makes ʿibād accusative, but its construction is disputed. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports it as like Ḥijāzī mā yet says it is unattested; al-Ālūsī says the reading reinforces idol incapacity and lack of likeness. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:195/q33/p2 Other reading discussions mention ayh and extra yāʾ locations. — Ibn al-Jazarī introduces alif deletion in ayh and two distinctive Qunbul yāʾs before the excerpts end; al-Mubhij uniquely reports Abū Nashīt affirming a yāʾ against other transmitters, and eight extra yāʾs including thumma kaydūn are listed. [4 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو وأبو جعفر ويعقوب,أبو نشيط عن أبي جعفر,قنبل)]

## 7:195/q34 How does the verse fit the sūra's closing warning?  [* · links]
- 7:195/q34/p1 The final section renews warnings against denial of God's signs and idolatry. — The Study Quran describes the final section this way. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
