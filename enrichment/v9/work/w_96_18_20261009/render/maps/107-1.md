# 107:1: 21 questions

## 107:1/q01 How is arāʾayta read?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the past-tense hamza may be deleted or eased, and whether the resulting sound is grammatically mobile.
- 107:1/q01/p1 The second hamza is retained and fully pronounced. — Al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq, al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī prefer retention: the past verb rītu is unattested, whereas deletion is regular in the imperfect. The initial interrogative can ease deletion, and a cited poetic line has rayta. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls full hamzation the majority reading; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ agrees. Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Zajjāj's analysis. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو ونافع); IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(الجمهور); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 107:1/q01/p2 The second hamza is omitted: arayta. — Al-Akhfash calls both hamzated and unhamzated forms dialectal, with frequent use favoring omission. Al-Kisāʾī, Nāfiʿ and others are reported; al-Ālūsī explains the form by analogy with the imperfect and ease after the interrogative, preferring this to the poetry analogy, while rejecting a weaker past-tense analogy. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Zajjāj and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cite a poetic parallel but disfavor the form. Qirāʾāt ER calls it a softening without a semantic change. [19 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(نافع وغيره); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو ونافع); RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI(قرئ); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); ALUSI(الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي,بعضهم); IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(شاعر غير مسمى); QIRAAT-ER; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; KASHSHAF-; KASHSHAF-FULL-; BINTSHATI(بعضهم)-]
- 107:1/q01/p3 The hamza is eased between full articulation and omission or replaced by a lengthened vowel. — Al-Naḥḥās reports Sībawayh's mobile intermediate hamza and cites the a-an raʾat verse and 2:6: a still hamza beside a still yāʾ would yield adjacent sukūns. Nāfiʿ and Egyptian Warsh transmitters use an alif, according to Ibn ʿĀshūr. Qirāʾāt ER describes madd and tashīl. [8 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); IBNASHUR; QIRAAT-ER; NASHR]
- 107:1/q01/p4 The eased hamza is made still and the yāʾ given kasra. — Al-Naḥḥās rejects this as an impossible Arabic form: the still yāʾ and still hamza would meet, and Sībawayh's intermediate pronunciation remains mobile. [0 notes: NAHHAS-]

## 107:1/q02 Is an address kāf added to arāʾayta?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the transmitted form includes an address marker after the verb.
- 107:1/q02/p1 The received form has no extra address kāf. — Al-Farrāʾ says adding or omitting kāf leaves the meaning unchanged; al-Naḥḥās calls the standard form clear. [2 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); NAHHAS]
- 107:1/q02/p2 A reading adds an emphatic second-person kāf: arāʾaytaka. — Attributed to ʿAbd Allāh or Ibn Masʿūd by al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and others, with 17:62 cited as a parallel. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say the kāf supports the 'tell me' sense because it does not attach to literal seeing; al-Ālūsī contests an absolute ban after figurative seeing. [15 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); NAHHAS(عبد الله بن مسعود); KASHSHAF(ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); RAZI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI(قرئ); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 107:1/q03 What does the opening question do?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the interrogation seeks recognition, expresses wonder, invokes sight, or asks for an account; several sources combine these functions.
- 107:1/q03/p1 It draws the listener to identify or recognize the denier through the following conduct. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī paraphrase 'do you know who?' and identify the orphan-pusher in 107:2. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it recalls anyone with the traits; al-Ālūsī says believers need to recognize and avoid them. Al-Rāghib, via Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, says every Qurʾānic araʾayta alerts the hearer; Muḥammad ʿAbduh says it flags something obscure. The Corpus Coranicum compares 96:9–13 and 53:33–34. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI(الراغب,محمد عبده); CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 107:1/q03/p2 It expresses astonishment and rebuke at denial and its surprising moral consequences. — Al-Rāzī compares asking why someone exposed himself to danger. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Elmalılı, Iṣlāḥī and Kur'an Yolu stress wonder; Ibn ʿĀshūr says common denial creates suspense until the shocking orphan abuse. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says asking what seems obvious sharpens attention; the ordinary information question becomes figurative when the asker knows. Al-Qushayrī notes the rebuke for mistreating orphans. [18 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 107:1/q03/p3 It asks whether the denier has been seen, prompting visual recall. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Ḥawfī permit literal seeing without ellipsis. Ibn ʿĀshūr portrays notorious deniers and deeds as visibly present. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows literal vision or recognition. [8 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN(الحوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي); IBNASHUR; TABATABAI]
- 107:1/q03/p4 It functions as 'tell me' or 'inform me.' — Al-Rāghib is cited for this gloss; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī connect it to a two-object construction, often with an unspoken second object. Ibn Khurūf notes jurists' hypothetical use with a following suspended question. [14 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي,الزمخشري); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي); BINTSHATI(الراغب); BURHAN(ابن خروف)]

## 107:1/q04 Who is addressed by arāʾayta?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · referent]
turns on: Whether direct address is specific to the Prophet or extends to every capable hearer.
- 107:1/q04/p1 The Prophet Muhammad is directly addressed. — Al-Biqāʿī calls him the community's leader and the one who fully grasps the matter; Elmalılı and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī extend the address through him to listeners. Al-Rāzī reports this option; Bursevî has the Prophet asked. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 107:1/q04/p2 Any rational or suitable listener is addressed. — Al-Rāzī asks why one would risk eternal punishment after clear proofs or trade lasting good for fleeting gain. Ibn ʿAṭiyya asks listeners to recall acquaintances; al-Ālūsī permits anyone fit; Qutb summons anyone able to see. Corpus Coranicum suggests the address may even reach the worshippers later criticized in verses 4–5. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 107:1/q05 How does arāʾayta govern the sentence?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · grammar]
turns on: The verb's sense determines whether it needs one object, two objects with an ellipsis, or a later syntactic completion.
- 107:1/q05/p1 It has one object, the relative phrase, as a verb of visual seeing or knowing. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Ḥawfī say literal seeing needs no ellipsis. Al-Samīn gives one object to visual perception; al-Ālūsī allows knowing with one object. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifically prefers physical vision. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows sight or recognition. Al-Ālūsī resists the rule that figurative vision must derive from physical sight. [11 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN(الحوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي); IBNASHUR; TABATABAI]
- 107:1/q05/p2 As 'inform me,' it takes two objects; the omitted second asks whether the denier deserves punishment or is right or wrong. — Al-Ḥawfī and al-Biqāʿī supply deserved punishment after the proofs; al-Qurṭubī supplies 'is he right or mistaken?' Abū Ḥayyān says this omitted clause is a second object rather than the answer to the question. The addressee kāf supports the 'inform me' analysis for Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn. [13 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي)]
- 107:1/q05/p3 As 'inform me,' it takes two objects; the omitted second asks who the denier is. — Al-Zamakhsharī supplies 'who is he?'; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report it, while Abū Ḥayyān says it is a second object rather than an independent answer. Al-Ālūsī allows this ellipsis. [6 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي)]
- 107:1/q05/p4 An alternative coordinates fa-dhālika with the denier and lets later text indicate an omitted answer. — Al-Zamakhsharī proposes accusative coordination and treats later woe to the worshippers as an indication, with a generic singular yielding plural reference. Abū Ḥayyān rejects the parse: the demonstrative points to the same denier, nominative subject is simpler, a second object is confused with an answer, and plural substitution is forced. Al-Kashf, cited by al-Ālūsī, objects to the pronoun and worshippers substitution. [3 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI(قيل)-]
- 107:1/q05/p5 An interrogative hamza is put before niʿma or biʾsa. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects this: these are performative expressions, whereas an interrogative must take a declarative statement. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 107:1/q06 Who is 'the one who denies'?  [ٱلَّذِى يُكَذِّبُ · referent]
turns on: Which occasion reports identify the denier, and whether a particular occasion restricts the general wording.
- 107:1/q06/p1 The wording applies generally to anyone who denies judgment or displays the traits. — Al-Rāzī favors the general denier of resurrection; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the relative is generic as most exegetes hold, and a named occasion would not restrict it. Elmalılı and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ likewise say the ruling follows the general wording. Study Quran says an initial individual leaves the accusation general; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī applies it to praying hypocrites who profess belief but act in denial. [10 notes: RAZI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 107:1/q06/p2 Al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil al-Sahmī is the named person. — Muqātil, al-Kalbī, Ibn al-Sāʾib and Abū Ṣāliḥ from Ibn ʿAbbās are cited. Al-Rāzī says he joined denial of Judgment to vile conduct; Elmalılı says he denied resurrection. Variant spelling is al-ʿĀṣī. Some say the report covers him with Hubayra, or only the Meccan half of the sūra. [23 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل والكلبي); MAWARDI(الكلبي ومقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي، مقاتل); WAHIDI-ASBAB(مقاتل والكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT(العاص بن وائل); BAGHAWI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب,هبة الله المفسر); RAZI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(هبة الله المفسر الضرير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(Mukātil); IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Muqātil and al-Kalbī); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور)]
- 107:1/q06/p3 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra is the named person. — Attributed to al-Suddī, Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān, Ibn Kaysān and others. [17 notes: THALABI(السدي ومقاتل بن حيان وابن كيسان); MAWARDI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي وابن حيان); WAHIDI-QT(الوليد بن المغيرة); BAGHAWI(السدي، مقاتل بن حيان، ابن كيسان); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي ومقاتل بن حيان وابن كيسان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(Süddî); IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(بعضهم)]
- 107:1/q06/p4 Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥarb is the named person. — Ibn Jurayj reports that he slaughtered a camel weekly, or two weekly, and struck an orphan asking for meat or food with a stick; the verses followed. The counts vary among transmitters. Ibn ʿĀshūr places this before his Islam. [15 notes: MAWARDI(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-QT(أبو سفيان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); RAZI(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL(ابن جريج); QURTUBI(ابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Ibn Jurayj)]
- 107:1/q06/p5 Abū Jahl is the named person. — Al-Māwardī's report says he guarded a destitute orphan's property, shoved the child when asked for it, then yielded when Muhammad came; al-Rāzī and Elmalılı add his fear of two spears he imagined beside the Prophet and Quraysh's mockery. Al-Qummī's tafsīr names Abū Jahl with Quraysh unbelievers. [11 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); BAGHAWI(الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR]

Ibn ʿĀshūr links this report to a Medinan date. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(İbni Abbas); IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(ابن عباس)]
- 107:1/q06/p8 Two hypocrites are meant. — Abū Ḥayyān reports two hypocrites as one identification, with no names supplied. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 107:1/q06/p9 Abū Lahab is the contextual referent. — Iṣlāḥī says the relative lacks an expressed antecedent but context points to Abū Lahab, a wealthy, stingy guardian of the House treasury who used it as personal property; Sūrat Lahab depicts his fate. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 107:1/q06/p10 Hubayra ibn Abī Wahb al-Makhzūmī is named. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports him as another identification; Muqātil's report names him alongside al-ʿĀṣ and calls him the Prophet's aunt Umm Hāniʾ's husband. [2 notes: THALABI; MUQATIL]
- 107:1/q06/p11 A known, identifiable person is indicated without settling which individual. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the next verse supports a definite known-person reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the demonstrative later singles the person out for inspection or points to someone obvious. [3 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR]

## 107:1/q07 What does yukadhdhibu express?  [يُكَذِّبُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the form is treated as the ordinary denial verb or as emphasizing repeated conduct.
- 107:1/q07/p1 It means denying or disbelieving the object named after bi. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts the wider Arabic kadhib, which includes false appearances, mistaken expectations, sensory error, false dreams or hopes and self-delusion; kadhdhaba bi is explicit denial. Qurʾānic denial commonly targets God, signs, messengers, resurrection and warning. Al-Biqāʿī stresses an active rejection of whoever conveys the matter; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the imperfect verb conveys repeated conduct. [5 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 107:1/q07/p2 The imperfect depicts repeated, continuing denial. — Ibn ʿĀshūr groups 'denies,' 'pushes' and 'urges' as ongoing behavior rather than isolated acts. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 107:1/q08 Is the preposition bi retained before al-dīn?  [بِٱلدِّينِ · readings]
turns on: Whether early parallels with kadhdhaba support the received preposition; al-Ṭabarī says it makes no semantic difference.
- 107:1/q08/p1 The received reading includes bi. — Al-Ṭabarī calls its bāʾ an added particle that does not alter the meaning. Corpus Coranicum favors it from early Meccan parallels in which dīn or yawm al-dīn are objects of belief or denial (95:7, 83:11, 82:9, 74:46, 70:26). [3 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL(عبد الله); CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 107:1/q08/p2 A reading attributed to ʿAbd Allāh omits bi. — Al-Ṭabarī reports the reading and says the sense stays the same because the received bāʾ is additional; Corpus Coranicum argues for the received preposition from early Qurʾānic usage. [2 notes: TAB(عبد الله); TAB-FULL(عبد الله); CORPUSCORANICUM-]

## 107:1/q09 What does al-dīn mean here?  [ٱلدِّينِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the immediate context and early Qurʾānic parallels point to eschatological reckoning, or the wider senses of religious belief, law, and scripture.
- 107:1/q09/p1 Reckoning, accounting, and resurrection judgment. — Muqātil, Ibn Jurayj, Mujāhid, ʿIkrima and others gloss it as reckoning; al-Tustarī places the accounting on the day of judgment. Ibn Kathīr includes resurrection. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others link accounting closely to recompense. Corpus Coranicum favors this early eschatological sense, comparing dīn and yawm al-dīn in 82:9, 95:7 and other passages, and notes an Aramaic dīnā connection. [35 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB(ابن جريج); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); MAWARDI(عكرمة ومجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة، مجاهد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وعكرمة); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI(أكثر المفسرين); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الطبري); CORPUSCORANICUM(Birkeland); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM+; RAZI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين)+; RAZI+]
- 107:1/q09/p2 Recompense: reward for good and punishment for evil. — Al-Māwardī presents this as a third meaning beside reckoning and God's judgment; al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ālūsī and others include reward and punishment. Al-Rāzī prefers it with reckoning because resurrection belief can motivate good even in a non-Muslim. Elmalılı cites 95:7 and 99:7–8; Iṣlāḥī cites 1:3; al-Ālūsī quotes the maxim that one is repaid as one acts. [37 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI(أكثر المفسرين); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الطبري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين)+; RAZI+]
- 107:1/q09/p3 God's judgment, command, ordinance, or law. — Ibn ʿAbbās is repeatedly reported for this gloss. Ibn al-Jawzī compares the king's law in 12:76. Kur'an Yolu says God's judgment and afterlife judgment entail one another. Al-Ālūsī lists God's command. [13 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 107:1/q09/p4 Religion or Islam, including religious belief and law. — Al-Rāzī reports denial of God, prophethood, resurrection or revealed law as included, but elsewhere favors recompense; he argues unqualified dīn in Qurʾānic usage denotes Islam (3:19), since false doctrine follows passion rather than submission. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers Islam, citing 3:19, 39:3, 5:3 and 3:85; the standard professions and rites alone do not establish it. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran allow this sense. Corpus Coranicum reports Birkeland and Nöldeke/Schwally for it but rejects it for this early passage, even while noting later 109:6 and a probably authentic Umayya poem already use the sense. [22 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(unnamed view); ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI+; CORPUSCORANICUM(Birkeland)-]
- 107:1/q09/p5 The Qurʾān is the dīn denied. — Ibn al-Jawzī records this as an eleventh lexical sense, citing this verse; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also list it. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 107:1/q09/p6 Dīn names the objective validity of religion and moral law. — Asad gives this formulation. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ derives the Arabic word from obedience and submission: madīn is a subjected servant and dayyān can be subduer, judge or ruler. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI]
- 107:1/q09/p7 The word also has other Arabic and Qurʾānic senses, without making each the sense of this verse. — Jishumī lists professed way, obedience and custom and calls recompense the root. Ibn al-Jawzī cites obedience (9:29), custom (49:17), creed/community (98:5), legal penalties (24:2), and even number of sacred months (9:36). Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes fourteen Qurʾānic instances of yawm al-dīn and denial of dīn in 82:9, 95:7 and here. Corpus Coranicum traces a later religious sense to Middle Persian dēn yet notes Umayya's earlier poem, with development beyond a purely linguistic shift. [13 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن قتيبة); BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 107:1/q10 Where was the sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports assign Mecca, Medina, or a split revelation, partly using the later hypocrite and ostentation material to date the sūra.
- 107:1/q10/p1 It was revealed in Mecca. — Reported by ʿAṭāʾ, Jābir, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima, Qatāda and ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr in different sources; most commentators are said to take this view. Ibn ʿAṭiyya thought it undisputed in his knowledge, despite al-Thaʿlabī's Medinan report. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls it early Meccan; Asad likewise places it in the early mission. [25 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL(عطاء وجابر); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة); BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(عطاء وجابر وابن عباس); BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR; DURR(ابن عباس وعبد الله بن الزبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس وعبد الله بن الزبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور); IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:1/q10/p2 It was revealed in Medina. — Reported from Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Wāqidī and al-Thaʿlabī. Qutb also leans toward a wholly Medinan setting because public hypocrisy and ostentation fit it; Study Quran says political advantage from hypocrisy could make an entirely Medinan date plausible. [17 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); JISHUMI(الضحاك، الواقدي); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(الثعلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة); QURTUBI(عطاء وجابر وابن عباس); BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور); IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:1/q10/p3 The first part was Meccan and the later part Medinan. — Muqātil puts the division after mention of the needy; Hibat Allāh says the Meccan half concerned al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil and the Medinan half ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy. Other reports divide verses 1–3 from 4–7. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this more apparent; Qutb calls the mixed account likelier though he also leans to Medina overall; Study Quran reports it. [11 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(هبة الله المفسر); ABUHAYYAN(هبة الله المفسر الضرير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور); TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR+; QUTB-ZILAL+]

## 107:1/q11 How many verses are counted in the sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Which regional verse-counting tradition receives seven rather than six; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes conflicting attributions.
- 107:1/q11/p1 Seven verses. — Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Qutb give seven. Al-Ālūsī calls it the Iraqi count, but Ibn ʿĀshūr reports ʿAlī al-Nūrī al-Ṣafāqusī assigning seven to Ḥimṣ instead. [13 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة); BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(عطاء وجابر وابن عباس); BAYDAWI; NASAFI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:1/q11/p2 Six verses. — Bursevî allows six or seven. Al-Ālūsī assigns six to non-Iraqi traditions; Ibn ʿĀshūr says most counters give six and notes a contrary assignment of six to Iraqi Basran and Kufan authorities. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR]

## 107:1/q12 What names are given to this sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Which element of the sūra supplies its title.
- 107:1/q12/p1 Al-Māʿūn. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this common title derives from the word unique to this sūra; Asad says the title comes from its final verse. [9 notes: IBNASHUR; ASAD-NOTES; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور)]
- 107:1/q12/p2 Araʾayta or Araʾayta alladhī. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites commentaries, an early Qayrawān muṣḥaf, al-Bukhārī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Ālūsī also record it. [12 notes: IBNASHUR(ابن عطية); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري,الطبري,الفخر الرازي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور)]

- 107:1/q12/p4 Al-Takdhīb. — Al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī give it; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Khafājī and Saʿdī glosses. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور); IBNASHUR]
- 107:1/q12/p5 Al-Yatīm. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes this title to al-Biqāʿī. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 107:1/q13 Where is this sūra placed in revelation order?  [* · sciences]
- 107:1/q13/p1 Seventeenth, after al-Takāthur and before al-Kāfirūn. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this is the Meccan sequence; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls it the commonly reported seventeenth, following al-Takāthur. Asad says probably after sūra 102. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 107:1/q14 What virtue is reported for reciting the sūra?  [* · reports]
- 107:1/q14/p1 Forgiveness is promised to a reciter who pays zakāt. — Jishumī reports a Prophetic saying through Ubayy, and al-Zamakhsharī reports it. The notes give no grading. [2 notes: JISHUMI(النبي); KASHSHAF(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 107:1/q15 How is its opening related to poetic meter or formulaic questions?  [* · rhetoric]
- 107:1/q15/p1 The opening can scan in al-khafīf, but Qurʾānic metrical coincidence remains distinct from poetry. — Al-Burhān records the claim and treats coincident lines, hemistichs or sequences separately from actual poetry. Al-Bāqillānī quotes Abū Nuwās recasting the al-Māʿūn lines in al-khafīf and changing wording to fa-dhālika alladhī to suit his poem. [3 notes: BURHAN(قالوا); BAQILLANI(أبو نواس)]
- 107:1/q15/p2 Its opening is one of the Qurʾānic interrogative starts. — Abū Shāma, cited in al-Itqān, compares six interrogative sūra openings including ʿamma yatasaʾalūn, hal atā, and alam nashraḥ. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو شامة)]

## 107:1/q16 What does the basmala evoke in this commentary?  [* · inward]
- 107:1/q16/p1 Its hearing nourishes and heals souls, while naming divine perfection and mercy. — Al-Qushayrī says it nourishes lovers' spirits, lights finders' secrets, heals repentant hearts, tests the poor and remedies the needy. Al-Biqāʿī praises divine perfection, mercy to good and bad, and special grace to close servants in communion. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:1/q17 How is the denier characterized in faith?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the term identifies outright unbelief, practical hypocrisy, or an inward state manifested in conduct.
- 107:1/q17/p1 The denier is an unbeliever. — Al-Ḥasan gives this gloss in reports by Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Suyūṭī. Al-Ṭabarī explains that denial of God's reward and punishment leads to disobedience of commands and prohibitions. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); TAB; TAB-FULL]
- 107:1/q17/p2 The traits can expose practical denial within people who profess Islam. — Ibn al-Zubayr, cited by al-Biqāʿī, says orphan neglect, poor feeding, prayer neglect, display and withheld help may mark professing Muslims. He cites ungraded sayings about four marks of complete hypocrisy and a fornicator lacking belief while acting. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says praying people who display belief may practically deny requital; Study Quran says hypocrisy is not limited to Muslims alone. [10 notes: BIQAI(الإمام أبو جعفر ابن الزبير,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 107:1/q17/p3 The denier is inwardly veiled and ignorant. — Al-Qāshānī reads ignorance and veiling of the rational faculty as the root of all vices and obscuration of the disgraceful consequence. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 107:1/q18 Why does denial of recompense lead to bad conduct?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes link belief in final accountability to motives for social and religious duties and infer inner denial from visible behavior.
- 107:1/q18/p1 Without expected reward or punishment, selfish desire and vice lose their restraint. — Al-Wāḥidī's linguistic authorities say denial removes incentives for good and restraints on evil. Al-Rāzī says unrestrained vice points more strongly to denial of resurrection than denial of Islam, since someone outside Islam might still do good if convinced of resurrection. Al-Biqāʿī calls requital the culmination of divine wisdom; Ibn ʿĀshūr says belief creates habitual moral restraint without external enforcement. Iṣlāḥī says a denier spends only for himself or display, while Final Day belief prompts generosity. [35 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); RAZI(أكثر المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI(الإمام أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:1/q18/p2 Its outward signs include abuse of the orphan, neglect of the poor, neglected prayer, display and withheld aid. — Al-Qushayrī focuses on abuse of orphans. Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān connect denial to harshly pushing the orphan and failing to urge feeding the poor; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr include prayer and aid. Al-Ālūsī calls orphan abuse the identifying sign and later vices an intensification, comparing the rebuke in 5:91. Corpus Coranicum maps religious omissions in 1 and 4–6 against social omissions in 2–3 and 7. [25 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI(الإمام أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 107:1/q18/p3 The denier's behavior discloses a concealed inward state. — Al-Biqāʿī says a hidden denier can be recognized by signs even when trying to conceal unbelief; Iṣlāḥī says the description of Abū Lahab's denial identifies his inner self. Qutb says verbal professions and rites cannot alone prove faith. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI]

## 107:1/q19 What standard of faith and worship does the passage teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether verbal profession and rites demonstrate faith without the social conduct that the verse and sūra demand.
- 107:1/q19/p1 Faith and worship must become sincere care and righteous conduct. — Qutb says religion is an integrated life of sincere worship, social duties, cooperation and care; verbal Islam, prayer and rites alone do not prove faith, because settled faith moves to action. He distinguishes the surah's divine measure from juristic boundaries for transactions. The passage surprises conventional expectations of faith and discloses the creed's mercy. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says people often assume profession and worship suffice; Study Quran and Corpus Coranicum connect belief and ritual with daily conduct. Iṣlāḥī says the House was built for exclusive worship and care for orphans and the poor. [17 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:1/q19/p2 Believers are warned to shun traits that resemble denial of judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī says even professing Muslims can carry these traits and cites earlier sūras and Prophetic sayings; al-Ālūsī says the warning intensifies because denial lies behind every sin. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the surah makes resurrection denial ugly by its moral consequences. [12 notes: BIQAI(الإمام أبو جعفر ابن الزبير,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 107:1/q20 How does this sūra connect with nearby and earlier sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: How adjacent and earlier sūras supply context for the warning about denial and conduct.
- 107:1/q20/p1 It follows God's favors and commands to Quraysh in sūras 105–106 by showing their failure of gratitude, worship and care. — Jishumī and Abū Ḥayyān say Quraysh's security and provision precede this rebuke. Al-Biqāʿī connects 106:3–4's call to worship with belief in recompense and God's wise treatment. Al-Ālūsī notes the contrast between God's feeding of the hungry and neglect here. Corpus Coranicum says future accountability complements 106's past favors. Elmalılı invokes need, satiety, fear and safety. [10 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 107:1/q20/p2 It continues warnings in earlier sūras about ignorance, greed, loss and false security in wealth. — Ibn al-Zubayr, cited by al-Biqāʿī, links 100:6, 103:2 and 102:3 or 104:3 with accumulation, slander, mockery and pride in the Elephant (102:1, 104:1, 105:1). Corpus Coranicum compares social solidarity in 92:5–9 with the lack of eschatological awareness in 104:1–3; Iṣlāḥī also cites al-Layl's contrast of generosity and greed. [6 notes: BIQAI(الإمام أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 107:1/q20/p3 Together with al-Kawthar, it judges the custodians of the House and anticipates their replacement. — Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh custodians failed the House's purposes of one-God worship and care for orphans and the poor; 108 then settles their fate and passes the trust to worthy people. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 107:1/q20/p4 It parallels seven-verse al-Anfāl in count and in returning its ending to its opening. — Al-Biqāʿī says al-Anfāl expands the same lesson and cites 8:1. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:1/q21 How do the opening and later verses fit together?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the later verses extend one person's portrait or support an alternative grammatical coordination.
- 107:1/q21/p1 The orphan-pusher identified after the opening is the same denier; later vices deepen or mirror the warning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ joins denial to pushing the orphan as one person's attributes; the demonstrative builds suspense and fixes attention, whereas plain wording would simply coordinate the acts. Al-Kashshāf says the warning in verses 1–3 reveals weak faith and the prayer warning follows. Al-Ālūsī says later vices press whether such conduct is good or evil; Corpus Coranicum gives a cross correspondence of religious and social omissions. [8 notes: KASHSHAF; IBNASHUR; ALUSI; CORPUSCORANICUM; TABATABAI]
- 107:1/q21/p2 A proposed syntax joins fa-dhālika to the denier and lets the later praying group supply an answer. — Al-Zamakhsharī proposes generic singular giving a plural sense and an omitted answer indicated by 107:4. Abū Ḥayyān objects to accusative coordination, a needless demonstrative of the same person, and forced substitution by al-muṣallīn; al-Kashf, reported by al-Ālūsī, also objects to the pronoun reference. [3 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI(قيل)-]
