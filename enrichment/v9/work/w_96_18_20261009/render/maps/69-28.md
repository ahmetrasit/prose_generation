# 69:28: 17 questions

## 69:28/q01 Is mā a negation or a self-reproaching question?  [مَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is a negative particle or an interrogative object of aghnā; both convey denial of benefit.
- 69:28/q01/p1 Mā negates any benefit from his wealth. — Naḥḥās calls it a non-parsing negator. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī say the object of aghnā is omitted; Biqāʿī says this makes the denial cover every possible benefit. Abū Ḥayyān describes regret. Ālūsī treats this as the primary parsing, with the first mā negative and the second mā relative. [13 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 69:28/q01/p2 Mā asks what benefit his wealth gave him, as a rhetorical question of denial and self-reproach. — Naḥḥās makes mā an accusative object of aghnā. Bayḍāwī also parses it as interrogative object. Rāzī, Samīn, and Abū Ḥayyān call the question reproachful; Biqāʿī says he reproaches himself for imagining wealth could save him from reckoning. Ālūsī says followers enter this reading only by implication. [11 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 69:28/q02 What does aghnā mean here?  [أَغْنَىٰ · meaning]
- 69:28/q02/p1 The Form IV perfect aghnā means availed or sufficed. — Qiraat-ER identifies the standard form as Form IV perfect. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 69:28/q03 What is the grammatical role of the nūn before ʿannī's first-person yāʾ?  [عَنِّى · grammar]
- 69:28/q03/p1 It is nūn al-wiqāya before the first-person yāʾ. — Itqān says this nūn occurs after verbs and particles, with ladun, and with min or ʿan, citing this and other verses. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 69:28/q04 What wordplay occurs in aghnā ʿannī?  [أَغْنَىٰ عَنِّى · rhetoric]
- 69:28/q04/p1 Aghnā ʿannī contains near graphic paronomasia. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares it with a cited expression about pride deceiving its possessor. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:28/q05 What does 'my wealth' encompass?  [مَالِيَهْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the relative construction and possessive wording extend māliya to followers, or the apparent wording confines it to money.
- 69:28/q05/p1 It includes worldly property and comparable assets such as followers. — Bayḍāwī includes property and followers. Bursawī reports a relative reading in which the possessive lām broadens the scope to wealth and followers. Ālūsī's primary parsing makes the relative mā in māliya the subject of aghnā, with 'was mine' as its relative clause; he prefers this sense because it more readily includes followers. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI-]
- 69:28/q05/p2 It refers directly to his worldly money; followers enter, if at all, only by implication. — Bursawī calls money alone the apparent Quranic wording, says it distracted and harmed him, and rejects the majority generalization to followers. Ālūsī says the alternative interrogative construction yields 'my wealth,' with followers only implicit. Nasafī describes wealth amassed in worldly life. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI+]

## 69:28/q06 How is the possessive yāʾ formed beneath the final hāʾ?  [مَالِيَهْ · grammar]
- 69:28/q06/p1 The underlying ending is the first-person possessive -iya, whose final yāʾ can be vowelled or quiescent. — Elmalılı gives the underlying forms ending in -iya. Sāmarrāʾī compares kitābī and kitābiyah in 69:19. Qiraat-ER says -ya remains the possessive suffix even when the pausal hāʾ is omitted. [3 notes: ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; QIRAAT-ER]

## 69:28/q07 What is the final hāʾ of māliyah and why is it there?  [مَالِيَهْ · grammar]
- 69:28/q07/p1 It is hāʾ al-sakt, a pause consonant preserving the ending or its vowel. — Burhān says it marks a pause after a built ending, preserves its vowel, is normally dropped in connection, and is supported by codex spellings. Itqān identifies it at verse endings, including sulṭāniyah and mā hiyah, and at pauses on plural verse endings. Elmalılı says it follows fatḥa, is always read with sukūn, and likewise occurs in kitābiyah, ḥisābiyah, and sulṭāniyah. Qiraat-ER says it marks the possessive suffix at pause. [5 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ELMALILI; QIRAAT-ER]

## 69:28/q08 Is the final hāʾ of māliyah retained in connected recitation?  [مَالِيَهْ · readings]
turns on: How a pausal hāʾ is handled during liaison, and which readers transmit each treatment; Abū Ḥayyān contests a claim that retaining it is erroneous.
- 69:28/q08/p1 Most readers retain the hāʾ in connection as well as at a stop. — Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿAmr, and Ibn ʿĀmir, and says all retain it at pause; his listed parallels include 6:90, 69:29, and 101:10, while two other al-Ḥāqqa forms retain it in both settings. Bayḍāwī contrasts Ḥamza with other readers. Abū Ḥayyān calls retention the majority reading for book, reckoning, wealth, power, and mā hiyah. Ibn al-Jazarī likewise says the others retain it. Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zuhrawī's claim that connected retention is an error because the reading is mass-transmitted. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر,الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BURHAN(حمزة); NASHR(حمزة، ويعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 69:28/q08/p2 Some readers omit the hāʾ in connection, while retaining it at a stop. — Ibn Mujāhid says Ḥamza omits it in the listed forms, whereas al-Kisāʾī omits it only in lam yatasannah and iqtadih and retains it in the other forms. Jishumī and Ibn al-Jazarī name Ḥamza and Yaʿqūb for māliyah, sulṭāniyah, and al-Qāriʿah's mā hiyah; Bayḍāwī names Ḥamza for both al-Ḥāqqa endings. Ibn ʿĀshūr names Ḥamza and Khalaf for māliya and sulṭāniya. Abū Ḥayyān also names Ibn Abī Isḥāq and al-Aʿmash in connected speech and says Ḥamza omits it in selected forms. Burhān says Ḥamza omits it in three words but retains it at a stop. Qiraat-ER notes that without it māliya runs into halaka. [9 notes: IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي,حمزة); JISHUMI(حمزة ويعقوب); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(حمزة); NASHR(حمزة، ويعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة وخلف); QIRAAT-ER]
- 69:28/q08/p3 Ibn Muḥayṣin deletes the hāʾ and makes the yāʾ quiescent. — Abū Ḥayyān distinguishes this reading from those that delete the hāʾ only in connected speech. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 69:28/q09 How are the adjacent hāʾ sounds in māliyah halaka pronounced?  [مَالِيَهْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the first hāʾ keeps its pausal status with a slight stop or is treated as original in uninterrupted connection.
- 69:28/q09/p1 The two hāʾs are kept clear, often with a light pause on the first. — Ibn al-Jazarī says most reciters use iẓhār because the first is hāʾ al-sakt. Makkī read with iẓhār and calls it correct despite his analogy favoring assimilation. Abū Shāma says iẓhār requires a slight pause, since unpaused connection requires assimilation or vocalization; al-Sakhāwī prefers stopping and, if connecting, iẓhār because the first hāʾ is notionally paused. Ibn al-Jazarī follows al-Dānī's light pause with taḥqīq. Qiraat-ER calls the standard reading a saktah before halaka. Burhān explains pausal intent for retained hāʾs in kitābiyah and ḥisābiyah. [7 notes: NASHR(أبو الحسن السخاوي,أبو شامة,الجمهور,مكي); QIRAAT-ER; BURHAN(حمزة); NASHR+]
- 69:28/q09/p2 The final hāʾ assimilates into the initial hāʾ of halaka in continuous recitation. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports reciters who assimilate and permits an inferred idghām in māliyah halak by analogy with qāla rajul/rajulān and qāla rabb, when it conflicts with no explicit transmission, consensus, or principle. Makkī argues that naql at kitābiyah innī treats the hāʾ as original and so calls for assimilation here, although his practice is iẓhār. Abū Shāma says true connection requires assimilation or vocalization. Ibn al-Jazarī follows al-Dānī's assimilation with ilqāʾ because the hāʾ is treated as original. Qiraat-ER describes merging the two hāʾs. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو شامة,الجمهور,مكي); QIRAAT-ER; NASHR+]

## 69:28/q10 May variant readings be combined across this connected passage?  [مَالِيَهْ · readings]
turns on: The degree of connection between words or stretches of discourse, and whether switching is an error, forbidden, or merely disliked.
- 69:28/q10/p1 Mixing readings is an error. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports al-Sakhāwī's unqualified judgment. [1 notes: NASHR(السخاوي)]
- 69:28/q10/p2 One should keep one of the seven readings while discourse is connected; switching after it ends is allowed. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports al-Nawawī, who adds that using one reading for the whole session is preferable. He says Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ gives the same sense. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو إسحاق الجعبري,النووي)]
- 69:28/q10/p3 Combining readings within a word or connected words is forbidden; otherwise it is disliked. — Ibn al-Jazarī attributes this distinction to al-Jaʿbarī. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو إسحاق الجعبري)]

## 69:28/q11 What does the hāʾ ending contribute to the sound of the scene?  [مَالِيَهْ · rhetoric]
- 69:28/q11/p1 It evokes exhausted sighing, hardship, and pain through the long judgment-day ordeal. — Sāmarrāʾī relates the repeated hāʾ endings to waiting for intercession and the scene's psychological weight, citing 69:19, 69:26, 70:4, 74:9, and 76:10 across the two notes. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 69:28/q12 What did the speaker's wealth fail to do for him?  [* · interpretation]
- 69:28/q12/p1 His worldly wealth gave him no protection or benefit against the punishment of the next life. — Muqātil specifies the Fire; Ṭabarī, Baghawī, and Bursawī say no part of God's punishment was warded off; Māwardī says abundant worldly wealth failed in the afterlife. Thaʿlabī reports the majority's paired explanation that his wealth failed and his argument vanished. Nasafī notes that the omitted object is 'anything.' Ibn Kathīr adds that worldly standing also failed and he has no helper or rescuer. Elmalılı says wealth may have aided others while its owner kept regret and punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a wealthy, powerful unbeliever whose followers grieve at their model's ruin. Tabatabaʾi says he once relied on wealth and power for happiness, safety, and control, but now sees wealth and children fail and regret itself is useless. Kuranyolu says even wealth accumulated without regard to lawful and unlawful cannot avert punishment. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:28/q13 How had the speaker's use of wealth contributed to his loss?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which misuse of wealth the explanation emphasizes: neglected duties, spiritual distraction and pride, or unlawful accumulation.
- 69:28/q13/p1 He withheld God's due, neglected kinship, and used wealth to exalt himself. — Tustarī says his abundant wealth could not help because he paid none of God's due and failed to maintain kinship. Biqāʿī adds that he used it to exalt himself above God's servants. [2 notes: TUSTARI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 69:28/q13/p2 The pursuit and adornment of wealth distracted him from the afterlife and truth, and harmed him. — Māwardī says worldly adornment and wealth distracted him from the afterlife. Bursawī says his money harmed rather than helped, citing 92:11 and 111:2. Tabatabaʾi says he labored for wealth and power as means of happiness, protection, and control while turning from God and truth. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 69:28/q13/p3 He accumulated wealth without regard to lawful and unlawful means. — Kuranyolu links this conduct with wealth's inability to avert punishment. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:28/q14 Which worldly figures does this speaker evoke?  [* · referent]
turns on: Which named Quraysh leaders are taken as implicit examples of the wealthy doomed speaker.
- 69:28/q14/p1 The wording alludes to wealthy Quraysh leaders such as al-Walīd. — Bursawī says the next mention of lost power exposes their worldly status. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 69:28/q14/p2 It implicitly targets leaders such as Abū Jahl and Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Ibn ʿĀshūr depicts a wealthy, powerful disbelieving leader and says his followers grieve on seeing their model's ruin; he mentions 73:11. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:28/q15 What later story illustrates the line?  [* · reports]
- 69:28/q15/p1 Fannā Khusraw died repeating that his wealth and authority had failed him. — Elmalılı reports that he went mad soon after boasting that he had conquered fate; the report echoes this verse and 69:29. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 69:28/q16 What broader lesson about worldly supports does this lament teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 69:28/q16/p1 Wealth and other worldly supports pass or cannot help on Judgment Day; belief and deeds endure. — Kuranyolu names property, sovereignty, status, and power, leaving only good or bad beliefs and deeds. Study Quran connects 18:46's wealth and children with enduring righteous deeds. Sāmarrāʾī says unbelief is broader than denial and cites 3:10, 14:21, 11:101, 53:26, and 8:54 for wealth, children, followers, authority, gods, and intercessors failing; an instance of disbelief is called denial. Tabatabaʾi says wealth, children, and power collapse and present regret brings no help. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI]

## 69:28/q17 How does the lament connect with its surrounding and related verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which contextual relation is highlighted: solitary arrival or the sequence contrasting the blessed and the wretched.
- 69:28/q17/p1 It fits the condemned person's solitary arrival before God. — Rāzī compares this loneliness with 19:80, 'and he comes to Us alone.' [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 69:28/q17/p2 It contrasts the blessed person's joy and good life with the wretched person's grief before punishment. — Rāzī says the blessed are described eating and drinking before the wretched person's restraints and food are described. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
