# 96:18/q01 How is 'We will summon' read? [سَنَدْعُ · readings]
turns on: The verb's agent and voice, and the wording of the preceding challenge.
- 96:18/q01/p1 The common reading has active first-person plural sanadʿu: God summons the zabāniya.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān calls this the majority reading; al-Samīn calls the plural majestic. Al-Farrāʾ and the codex discussions cite its consonantal spelling.
  holders (4 notes): ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; FARRA

- 96:18/q01/p2 Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads the verb passively, sayudʿā or sutdaʿā: the zabāniya are summoned.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī specify nominative al-zabāniya; al-Rāzī reports the passive form.

# 96:18/q02 What is the grammatical mood of sanadʿu? [سَنَدْعُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding imperative governs God's answering verb.
- 96:18/q02/p1 It is indicative: no subjunctive or jussive governor precedes it.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī rejects the proposed jussive response to the command as weak; he and his full version note the independent clause.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-
  notes: ALUSI:96:18/r7 ALUSI-FULL:v30p188/r5 | against: ALUSI:96:18/r8
- 96:18/q02/p2 It is jussive as the answer to the taunting command to summon the council.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the challenged man's summons is answered by God's counter-summons, and the future prefix strengthens the response.
  holders (2 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR:96:18/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p452/r1
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; ALUSI-FULL = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

# 96:18/q03 Why does the muṣḥaf write sanadʿu without its final wāw? [سَنَدْعُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the spelling reflects pronunciation, economy, rhetorical force, or the pause form.
- 96:18/q03/p1 The spelling follows loss of wāw in connected pronunciation before a quiescent consonant.
  reasons: Al-Farrāʾ cites yadʿu al-insān and three poetic parallels; Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Khālawayh as quoted by Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain the meeting of two vowelless letters. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes it is no verse-end pause.
  holders (8 notes): FARRA; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن خالويه); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: FARRA:v1p91/r1 ABUHAYYAN:96:18/r8 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p511/r18 BURSEVI:v10p367/r3 ALUSI:96:18/r7 ALUSI-FULL:v30p188/r5 IBNASHUR:96:19/r2 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p452/r4
- 96:18/q03/p2 The wāw is omitted for abbreviation, lightness, or conformity to the preceding fal-yadʿu.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says abbreviation; Bursawī and al-Ālūsī mention parallelism. Biqāʿī observes all ʿUthmānic codices omit it and sees no phonetic necessity.
  holders (6 notes): IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL
  notes: IBNATIYYA:96:18/r8 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p503/r5 BURSEVI:v10p367/r2 BIQAI:96:18/r3 ALUSI:96:18/r7 ALUSI-FULL:v30p188/r5
- 96:18/q03/p3 The omission suggests swift, easy divine action, an immediate response, and violent seizure.
  reasons: Al-Zarkashī compares 54:50; al-Marrākushī generalizes omitted letters as speed and force, and al-Suyūṭī applies it to the guards. The severe end of the threat is left unstated.
  holders (3 notes): BURHAN; ITQAN(المراكشي)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p397/r3 ITQAN:v4p446/r2 ITQAN:v4p447/r3
- 96:18/q03/p4 The omission emphasizes that angels have no independent power; strength belongs to God.
  reasons: Biqāʿī presents this tentatively as one explanation of the ʿUthmānic spelling.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:96:18/r4 BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r5
- 96:18/q03/p5 The spelling evokes an imperative, making the summons and response inevitable.
  reasons: Biqāʿī links the prefixed sīn with certainty; Bursawī likewise suggests inevitability as one possibility.
  holders (3 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI

  holders (2 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL

# 96:18/q04 How is the omitted wāw treated when recitation stops? [سَنَدْعُ · readings]
turns on: Whether a stop follows written spelling, underlying form, or is discouraged.
- 96:18/q04/p1 Readers normally pause according to the written form, without restoring wāw.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī says all readers follow the rasm in four forms with omitted wāw before a still letter; his discussion of plural mīm uses sanadʿu as a comparable connected spelling. Al-Suyūṭī lists exceptional stop spellings.
  holders (4 notes): NASHR(جميع القراء); ITQAN; BURHAN(يعقوب)
  notes: NASHR:v1p456/r4 NASHR:v2p140/r3 ITQAN:v1p239/r2 BURHAN:v4p103/r5
- 96:18/q04/p2 A route from Yaʿqūb pauses with the underlying wāw.
  reasons: Al-Dānī transmitted it from two teachers; Ibn al-Jazarī says he read the individual route. Al-Zarkashī uses Yaʿqūb's pause as evidence for the underlying wāw.
  holders (2 notes): NASHR(يعقوب عن أبي الفتح وأبي الحسن); BURHAN
  notes: NASHR:v2p140/r6 BURHAN:v2p497/r5
- 96:18/q04/p3 Makkī and others discourage pausing on these forms.
  reasons: They feared violating either rasm or underlying form; Ibn al-Jazarī rejects the restriction because emergency stops and sound transmitted readings are permissible. He likewise rejects Makkī's analogous prohibition at hāʾum as error.
  holders (1 notes): NASHR(مكي وغيره); NASHR-
  notes: NASHR:v2p140/r4 | against: NASHR:v2p140/r5 NASHR:v1p456/r3
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; ITQAN = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911; NASHR = Ibn al-Jazarī, d. 833

# 96:18/q05 What does the future prefix in sanadʿu convey? [سَنَدْعُ · rhetoric]
- 96:18/q05/p1 It announces a certain divine promise to avenge and help the Prophet, even after a delay.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī says future sīn does not imply doubt; he cites the Prophet's assurance, 'I will help you, even after a time.' Biqāʿī also sees certainty and establishment in sīn.

# 96:18/q06 What singular or number analysis is proposed for zabāniya? [ٱلزَّبَانِيَةَ · grammar]

- 96:18/q06/p1 Its singular is zabniya or zubniya, with closely varied vowels and spelling.
  reasons: Abū ʿUbayda and al-Mubarrad compare ʿifriya; al-Bayḍāwī and Biqāʿī also explain a nisba form with tāʾ replacing yāʾ. Some call a rebellious human or jinn by this form.
