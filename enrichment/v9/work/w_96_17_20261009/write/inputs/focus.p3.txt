# 96:17/q11 What broader lesson is drawn from this challenge? [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources differ between a lesson directly drawn from calling the council and a broader contrast of worldly pursuit with knowledge.
- 96:17/q11/p1 Worldly status, clan and companions can embolden arrogance but cannot protect the transgressor against God.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says disbelievers rely on community for pride and fear becoming followers; Quṭb and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ describe the same illusion of family protection. Al-Jishumī says the summoned followers can only witness shared calamity. Iṣlāḥī points to Badr as demonstration.
  holders (8 notes): JISHUMI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7482/r2 BIQAI:96:17/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p487/r14 QUTB-ZILAL:96:17/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p32/r4 BINTSHATI:v2p33/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:15-18:kommentar/r4 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p391/r1
- 96:17/q11/p2 The desire for worldly gain is insatiable and leads to transgression, unlike knowledge seeking, which wins God's approval.
  reasons: Ibn Masʿūd connects the sura's claim about human transgression in 96:6–7 with 35:28; the note is transmitted here alongside the verse's material.
