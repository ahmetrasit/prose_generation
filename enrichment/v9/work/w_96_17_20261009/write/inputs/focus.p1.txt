# 96:17/q06 How does a gathering place become what he can call? [نَادِيَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted 'people' is supplied, the place is used metonymically, or nādī itself denotes the present people.
- 96:17/q06/p1 An omitted head noun, 'people', is understood: he calls the people of his council.
  reasons: Ibn Qutayba says Arabic omits a possessive head and promotes its dependent; al-Naḥḥās names this genitive-head omission. Al-Ṭabrisī, Bursawī and others apply it here because a place cannot answer a summons; 12:82's 'ask the town' is analogous.
  holders (8 notes): IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL
  notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL:v1p133/r1 IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL:v1p133/r17 NAHHAS:v5p261/r20 TABRISI:96:2/r14 SAMIN-DURR:96:17/r1 BURSEVI:v10p366#2/r12 ALUSI:96:17/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p187#3/r1
- 96:17/q06/p2 The place is named figuratively for its occupants, so the council itself stands for its people.
  reasons: Al-Samīn offers this beside ellipsis and compares 12:82; al-Burhān and al-Itqān describe naming occupants by location. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 12:82 and poetic majlis, maqām and majmaʿ parallels; al-Ālūsī gives figurative attribution or naming inhabitants by place.
  holders (9 notes): SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI
  notes: SAMIN-DURR:96:17/r1 BURHAN:v2p279/r13 ITQAN:v3p101/r5 ALUSI:96:17/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p187#3/r1 IBNASHUR:96:17/r7 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p451/r1 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p451/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p32/r1
- 96:17/q06/p3 The noun can already denote the assembled people directly.
  reasons: Abū ʿUbayda as cited by al-Rāzī says a vacant place is not called nādī; Ibn ʿĀshūr also says it names the people gathered there. Ibn Qutayba mentions a second pattern where one verb belongs to one of two things and another verb is understood for the other.
