# 19:3: 24 questions

## 19:3/q01 How is إذ نادى construed?  [إِذْ نَادَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: The governor may be mercy or mention; an alternative makes the time an appositive to Zakariyyā.
- 19:3/q01/p1 A temporal accusative adverb, with no governor specified. — Al-Naḥḥās identifies its time and case. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 19:3/q01/p2 It is governed by raḥma, God's mercy. — Al-Nasafī gives this parse; Abū al-Baqāʾ permits it alongside dhikr. Al-Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give it too. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي وأبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:3/q01/p3 It is governed by dhikr, the mention. — Al-Ḥawfī gives dhikr; Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Ālūsī allow it. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي وأبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الحوفي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:3/q01/p4 It is an appositive of inclusion to Zakariyyā. — Al-Samīn says the time contains the event and compares 19:16; al-Ālūsī repeats the comparison. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:3/q02 What does نادى or نداء mean here?  [نَادَىٰ نِدَآءً · meaning]
turns on: Whether the ordinary vocal sense or the contextual sense of supplication governs the word.
- 19:3/q02/p1 Supplication and earnest request to God. — Al-Ḍamghānī cites 21:89, 21:83 and 21:87; Ibn al-Jawzī cites Noah and Job in 21:76 and 21:83. Al-Ṭabrisī defines prayer as requesting action and response as its counterpart. Al-Qurṭubī adds longing. [7 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 19:3/q02/p2 Ordinarily a raised, projected call to a distant addressee. — Ibn al-Jawzī lists yā, ayā, hayā, ayy and interrogative alif, citing poetry, Dhū al-Rummah and Sībawayh. Al-Rāghib allows any sound or sign as well. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the ordinary vocal sense. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI]
- 19:3/q02/p3 In broader usage, nidāʾ can mean revelation. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as a seventh sense without applying it here. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 19:3/q03 What does the address ربّه suggest?  [رَبَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 19:3/q03/p1 Naming him Lord without a vocative particle signals extreme closeness. — Al-Biqāʿī reads the bare address as intimate proximity. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 19:3/q04 What is the syntax of نداءً خفياً?  [نِدَآءً خَفِيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:3/q04/p1 Nidāʾan is accusative, and khafiyyan is its adjective. — Al-Naḥḥās calls it an emphatic verbal noun; al-Jishumī calls it a direct object. [2 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI]

## 19:3/q05 What does خفياً mean?  [خَفِيًّۭا · meaning]
- 19:3/q05/p1 Concealed, secret or hidden. — Al-Akhfash derives it from ikhfāʾ. Al-Wāḥidī compares adjective forms khāfin and khafī with sāmiʿ and samīʿ. Al-Ḍamghānī cites 7:55 and 20:7. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes khafiyah, concealment, from khīfah, fear, in 7:205. [9 notes: AKHFASH; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:3/q06 How can a نداء be خفياً?  [نَادَىٰ نِدَآءً خَفِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether secrecy concerns audibility, secluded setting, prayer, or inward state.
- 19:3/q06/p1 It may be spoken aloud where no one else can hear. — Al-Ālūsī says secrecy need not require a low voice. Ṭabāṭabāʾī applies this to normally audible nidāʾ and notes Zechariah later left the prayer niche to address his people. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:3/q06/p2 He raised his voice as far as possible, but age made it faint. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes the intention of calling from its weak audible result. Al-Ālūsī cites a saying that old voices fade and hearing becomes intermittent. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 19:3/q06/p3 He prayed within ritual prayer, so his supplication was quiet. — Al-Rāzī infers the setting from the angels' answer while he stood praying in the sanctuary. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 19:3/q06/p4 Hiddenness can figure inward sincerity rather than volume. — Al-Ālūsī offers this figure; al-Kashf says literal concealment is also intended, chiefly for sincerity. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)]
- 19:3/q06/p5 He kept the prayer within himself. — Ibn ʿAbbās says Zechariah concealed the prayer within himself. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 19:3/q06/p6 He felt distant because of sins and poor condition and called like one fearing punishment. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this explanation from some interpreters. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم)]

## 19:3/q07 Why was Zechariah's call hidden?  [نِدَآءً خَفِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The sources attribute the hiddenness to motive, audience, age, time or devotional posture.
- 19:3/q07/p1 To guard sincerity and avoid ostentation. — Al-Ḥasan, Ibn Jurayj, Mujāhid and others explain it as freedom from show. Qatāda says God hears the quiet voice and knows the pure heart. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī say loud and quiet are equal before God; privacy is purer. Al-Wāḥidī cites 7:55 and near-whispered Muslim prayers. Al-Bayḍāwī adds humility; al-Kashf says sincerity was chief even if secrecy was literal. [33 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج,الحسن); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IK); KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 19:3/q07/p2 To avoid mockery, criticism or embarrassment about seeking a child in old age. — Muqātil says people would mock the old man; Ibn al-Anbārī reports al-Kalbī and Muqātil's fear of criticism. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds blame if a worldly request went unanswered. Al-Qurṭubī calls this clearest; al-Nasafī gives ages seventy-five or eighty. [14 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري عن الكلبي ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الماوردي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 19:3/q07/p3 To keep his relatives from hearing. — Abū Sulaymān says cousins might resent a child and think he opposed their succession. Al-Burūsawī calls them hostile; al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī mention feared relatives. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 19:3/q07/p4 Because frail old age made his voice weak. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī give this explanation; al-Ālūsī says 120 years fits it best. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 19:3/q07/p5 Because he prayed privately at night. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī place him in the prayer chamber at night; Ibn ʿAṭiyya suggests midnight. Ibn Kathīr's early report says companions slept. [6 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:3/q07/p6 A low voice expresses humility, weakness, surrender and intimacy. — Al-Rāzī contrasts loud strength with the weakness proper to petition and reliance on grace. Al-Biqāʿī sees a lover's intimate address, heard by God however secret. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 19:3/q08 Where and when was the call made?  [نَادَىٰ نِدَآءً خَفِيًّۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in their details of sanctuary, companions, offerings and retreat.
- 19:3/q08/p1 In the sanctuary or prayer chamber at night. — Al-Thaʿlabī adds offerings brought near and no witness but God; al-Baghawī says night. Al-Qurṭubī cites the angels' call in 3:39. Al-Burūsawī says an offering was made in Jerusalem. [4 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 19:3/q08/p2 At night after others slept. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī suggest midnight. Ibn Kathīr transmits an early report: companions slept, Zechariah softly repeated 'Lord,' and God answered three times. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:3/q08/p3 Probably during retreat at the Temple. — Iṣlāḥī suggests iʿtikāf there. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

turns on: Different transmitted estimates of his age; al-Ālūsī links the highest to vocal frailty.

## 19:3/q10 What inward reading is given to the hidden call?  [نِدَآءً خَفِيًّۭا · inward]
turns on: The reports differ on self-concealment, silence before majesty and subsumption in remembrance.
- 19:3/q10/p1 He hid it even from himself, showing it only to God. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says this excludes spiritual fluctuation and leaves the call with the One who can answer and grant it. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 19:3/q10/p2 Divine majesty can silence heart and tongue within remembrance. — An unnamed report in al-Sulamī describes a call hidden within and away from remembrance when majesty overwhelms a person. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 19:3/q10/p3 True remembrance subsumes the rememberer. — Al-Sulamī reports this further saying. [1 notes: SULAMI(قيل)]

## 19:3/q11 What etiquette does the quiet prayer teach?  [نِدَآءً خَفِيًّۭا · interpretation]
- 19:3/q11/p1 Humble private supplication is preferable, more sincere and more likely answered. — ʿUqba rates one private prayer above seventy public ones. Al-Ḥasan cites 7:55 and Muslim near-whispers; Qatāda says God hears a quiet voice. Reports from Saʿd and the Prophet commend hidden remembrance and sufficient provision. Al-Qurṭubī cites the teaching that the One called is not deaf. Al-Rāzī cites 7:55 and dependence on grace; Iṣlāḥī says privacy removes pretension. Qushayrī compares Abraham's intimate address after his initial call. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عقبة بن عبد الغافر); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); JISHUMI; TABRISI(الحديث); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 19:3/q11/p2 Begin supplication by recalling God's blessings and pleading humbly. — Al-Jishumī recommends this opening etiquette. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:3/q12 What does this imply for qunūt and congregants' amen?  [نِدَآءً خَفِيًّۭا · law]
turns on: Whether Zechariah's hidden prayer or the Prophet's audible qunūt controls the ritual volume.
- 19:3/q12/p1 The imam's qunūt and followers' amens should be silent. — Yūnus reports al-Ḥasan's inference from Zechariah. Ibn al-ʿArabī says Mālik held quiet qunūt. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(يونس بن عبيد)+]
- 19:3/q12/p2 The imam's qunūt should be audible. — Ibn al-ʿArabī reports al-Shāfiʿī's view and prefers it because the Prophet prayed qunūt aloud. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]

## 19:3/q13 What did Zechariah request?  [نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُۥ · interpretation]
- 19:3/q13/p1 A child and worthy successor. — Al-Suddī points to 19:4–6; Ṭabāṭabāʾī links his prayer to Mary's worship and provision in 3:37–41. Kur'an Yolu says he feared relatives would not lead the community and sought a virtuous, capable, just heir from his and Jacob's line. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MUQATIL]

## 19:3/q14 How does the following speech relate to نادى?  [نَادَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 19:3/q14/p1 The subsequent قال directly explains a completed call. — Al-Burhān contrasts Noah's story, where nādā means 'about to call' and an explanatory fāʾ follows; no fāʾ is needed here. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:3/q15 How is the opening mention of mercy and Zechariah parsed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The proposals change the omitted subject, verb form, object assignments and inflection.
- 19:3/q15/p1 An omitted demonstrative yields 'this is the mention'; ʿabd is accusative after mercy and Zakariyyā explains it. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this and compares 9:1 and 24:1. A Kufan grammarian also supplies a demonstrative with reordered wording. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(نحوي كوفي); TAB-FULL+]
- 19:3/q15/p2 A supplied 'among what is recited to you' introduces the passage. — A Basran grammarian gives this construction and makes ʿabd the object of raḥma. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(نحوي بصري)]
- 19:3/q15/p3 Dhikr is governed by the opening letters. — The Kufan grammarian offers this alternative. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(نحوي كوفي)]
- 19:3/q15/p4 The account is God's mercy to Zechariah. — Ibn Kathīr recalls discussion of the disconnected letters at the start of al-Baqara. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 19:3/q15/p5 Intensified past dhakkara takes accusative raḥmata and an omitted first object. — Al-Ālūsī reports al-Ḥasan and Ibn Yaʿmar: mercy is the second object, ʿabdahu the object of mercy. Abū al-Fatḥ proposes the Quran, the letters as Quran, or God as subject, with people omitted as object. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو الفتح,الحسن وابن يعمر)]
- 19:3/q15/p6 Other dhakkara object assignments and a figurative parse are possible. — Al-Ālūsī reports mercy as second object and servant first, or mercy first and servant second; another makes God metaphorically cause mercy to remember His servant. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:3/q15/p7 Light past dhakara makes mercy the object and servant the subject. — Al-Kalbī's reading is reported by al-Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 19:3/q15/p8 An imperative commands remembrance of mercy toward Zechariah. — Al-Dānī reports Abū Yaʿmar's reading; people may be reminded directly or through that mercy. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو يعمر)]

## 19:3/q16 How is Zakariyyā read or inflected?  [* · readings]
turns on: The notes distinguish forms of the name and a construction changing its case.
- 19:3/q16/p1 His name has long and short readings plus other reported forms. — Ibn Kathīr calls two forms well known. Al-Ālūsī lists five foreign-name forms: long and short in the Seven, two Zakari forms and Zakar like qalam. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:3/q16/p2 His name is read in an accusative construction. — Ibn Kathīr reports this from Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(يحيى بن يعمر)]

## 19:3/q17 What is reported about Zechariah's identity and work?  [* · reports]
- 19:3/q17/p1 An Israelite prophet of a prophetic line, associated with revelation in Jerusalem. — Ibn Kathīr calls him a great Israelite prophet. Ibn ʿAbbās says son of Dān, father of Yaḥyā, and a prophetic descendant who wrote revelation in Jerusalem. Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists prophet, recipient of revelation, servant, righteous, elect and guided, citing 6:85 and 6:87. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 19:3/q17/p2 The last prophet of the Children of Israel. — Al-Ālūsī reports Ibn Masʿūd's statement. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 19:3/q17/p3 A carpenter. — Ibn Kathīr cites Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī; al-Ālūsī cites a sound-graded report from Abū Hurayra. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 19:3/q18 How does the account serve the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes relate it to the sūra's moral pattern, Christian claims, the previous sūra, or its imagery.
- 19:3/q18/p1 It exemplifies the sūra's warning and glad tidings through blessed prophets, erring descendants and repentant believers. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 19:97 and 19:58; the prophets receive friendship, prophethood, truthfulness and sincerity. Successors abandon devotion, but repentant believers return. Rewards and punishments answer denials of resurrection, divine offspring and idolatry. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 19:3/q18/p2 It introduces Mary and rebuts the claim that Jesus' miraculous birth proves divinity. — Iṣlāḥī says the prophet lines taught monotheism, prayer and zakāh but followers turned to polytheism, lost rites and followed desire. He says the Prophet is urged to persevere against hostile opponents who misread temporary prosperity. Christians and Jews secretly aided Quraysh; exposing them undermines that support. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:3/q18/p3 The sūra continues the previous sūra's astonishing stories. — Abū Ḥayyān connects the Cave, Moses and Khiḍr, and Dhū al-Qarnayn in 18:9, 18:60 and 18:83 with Yaḥyā's birth to aged, barren parents and Jesus' fatherless birth. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 19:3/q18/p4 The night framing echoes the secret whisper. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the hidden prayer evokes the concealment of night. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:3/q19 Where was the sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 19:3/q19/p1 It is Meccan, with a reported exception for its prostration verse. — Abū Ḥayyān says it is Meccan like the previous sūra; Muqātil excepts the prostration verse, placing it in Medina after the Abyssinian migration. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:3/q20 How should wording with multiple senses be interpreted?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The plain, legal and customary senses have different priorities; equal conflicting senses require indicators.
- 19:3/q20/p1 A unique sense is clear; otherwise retain the plain sense unless evidence displaces it. — Al-Māwardī says unambiguous wording reveals God's intent. Another class bears two or more senses; a hidden sense requires evidence against the plain one. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 19:3/q20/p2 Legal usage outranks linguistic and customary usage; custom outranks linguistic usage. — Al-Māwardī grounds legal priority in transfer and binding force, customary priority in familiar usage. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL+]
- 19:3/q20/p3 When equal senses conflict, jurists use indicators to infer intent. — Al-Māwardī uses qurūʾ, purity or menstruation, as an incompatible pair. Each mujtahid's indicator-based conclusion is God's intended meaning for that jurist, even if another differs. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 19:3/q21 What makes a distant simile effective?  [* · rhetoric]
- 19:3/q21/p1 A hidden likeness appears when the relevant feature is selected from unlike objects. — Al-Jurjānī says difference and discovery delight. Ibn al-Muʿtazz likens lightning's motion to a Quran reader's opening and closing, ignoring other traits. Jarīr first pities ʿAdī's bird-and-needle image, then envies the completed pen drawing ink image. Al-Khalīl's miserly hand is another example. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(الخليل)]

## 19:3/q22 May ذات express God's essence?  [* · theology]
turns on: The phrase fī dhāt Allāh is read as either essence or religious cause.
- 19:3/q22/p1 No; nafs names reality directly, while ذات in the cited report concerns God's religion or law. — Al-Suhaylī relates nafs to nafāsa and nafīs. He rejects essence as the sense of the Abraham report's fī dhāt Allāh. [2 notes: BURHAN(السهيلي); BURHAN(السهيلي)+]
- 19:3/q22/p2 Yes; ذات can be equated with nafs, essence and existence. — Al-Burhān reports this as most people's view, citing the Abraham report. [1 notes: BURHAN(أكثر الناس); BURHAN(السهيلي)-]

## 19:3/q23 What evidence is given for Quranic inimitability?  [* · theology]
- 19:3/q23/p1 Opponents used war and accusations rather than produce a rival sūra, showing inability. — Al-Itqān says imitation would have been easier than war, captivity and lost property. Ibn ʿAbbās reports al-Walīd praising the Quran's sweetness, splendor, fruitfulness and force before Abū Jahl pressed him to call it transmitted sorcery. Al-Jāḥiẓ says the challenge came at the Arabs' peak in poetry, oratory, language and numbers. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس,الجاحظ)]

## 19:3/q24 What does ḥaḍara mean in Arabic?  [* · meaning]
- 19:3/q24/p1 Being present or existing, the opposite of absence, without necessarily coming to a place. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī gives this distinction. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
