# 29:29: 22 questions

## 29:29/q01 How is the opening أئنكم read?  [أَئِنَّكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the first corresponding clause asserts or asks, and how the interrogative hamza is realized; Abū ʿUbayd objects to two successive interrogatives and al-Naḥḥās answers him.
- 29:29/q01/p1 Both corresponding clauses are interrogative, with two articulated hamzas. — Ibn Mujāhid assigns this to Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī; al-Naḥḥās calls it the Kufan reading. Jishumī also names ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī. Al-Naḥḥās rejects Abū ʿUbayd's objection that two interrogatives are too close: one question can follow another and his analogy in this sūra is different. Ibn ʿĀshūr says all readers give the corresponding question two hamzas. Ibn al-Jazarī says Ḥamza's easing at pause is Arabic and transmitted, though full pronunciation is also transmitted; Sufyān says every part of his recitation has precedent. He catalogues still and moving hamzas and their vocalic environments, including permanent and pause-induced stillness and medial positions. [14 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو وعاصم عن أبي بكر وحمزة والكسائي); NAHHAS(قراءة الكوفيين); JISHUMI(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر وحمزة والكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNASHUR-FULL(جميع القراء); NASHR(حمران بن أعين وطلحة بن مصرف وجعفر بن محمد والأعمش وسلام بن سليمان وهشام بن عمار وأبو سليمان عن قالون,سفيان الثوري); NAHHAS(أبو عبيد)-]
- 29:29/q01/p2 The first corresponding clause is declarative and the second interrogative, with two hamzas in the latter. — Ibn Mujāhid reports Ḥafṣ and Ibn ʿĀmir with two hamzas; Ibn ʿĀmir's transmitters differ on lengthening, and a Hishām report has lengthening like ʿāʾidhan. Abū ʿAlī reports Ibn ʿĀmir and Ibn Dhakwān with length, and Ḥafṣ matching Nāfiʿ in the first occurrence. Jishumī explains the first hamza as eased and second as original. Al-Nasafī associates the opening two hamzas with Syrian readers and Ḥafṣ and the canonical codex. [8 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم وابن عامر,هشام عن ابن عامر); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر وابن ذكوان,حفص عن عاصم); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وحفص عن عاصم ويعقوب,ابن عامر وحفص); NASAFI-FULL(شامي وحفص); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 29:29/q01/p3 The first corresponding clause is declarative and the second interrogative, with one lengthened hamza. — Jishumī assigns this realization to Abū Jaʿfar, Qālūn from Nāfiʿ, and Zayd from Yaʿqūb; Ibn Mujāhid and Abū ʿAlī also report a lengthened form from Nāfiʿ. Al-Naḥḥās says Nāfiʿ's first clause follows the written form and is an emphatic assertion; Muḥammad ibn Yazīd compares an emphatic poetic reply to a question of love. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(نافع); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر وقالون عن نافع وزيد عن يعقوب,أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وحفص عن عاصم ويعقوب); NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد,نافع)]
- 29:29/q01/p4 The first corresponding clause is declarative and the second interrogative, with one unlengthened hamza. — Ibn Mujāhid says another Nāfiʿ report follows Ibn Kathīr; Abū ʿAlī says Ibn Kathīr has no length and Nāfiʿ has both reports. Jishumī adds Yaʿqūb. Al-Nasafī lists the Makki, Nāfiʿ other than Qālūn, Sahl, and Yaʿqūb other than Zayd, with a kasra yāʾ after the shortened hamza. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID(نافع); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وحفص عن عاصم ويعقوب,ابن كثير ونافع ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(عمرو ومكي ونافع غير قالون وسهل ويعقوب غير زيد)]
- 29:29/q01/p5 Both corresponding clauses are interrogative, with a single eased or lengthened hamza. — Ibn Mujāhid and Abū ʿAlī distinguish Abū ʿAmr from readers who sound two hamzas. Al-Naḥḥās says he lightens the hamza; Jishumī calls it one lengthened hamza. Al-Nasafī gives the shortened hamza followed by kasra yāʾ to ʿAmr. Ibn Khālawayh allows a softened second hamza. The qirāʾāt note says the hamza realization does not alter meaning. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو وعاصم عن أبي بكر وحمزة والكسائي); NAHHAS(قراءة الكوفيين); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو وعاصم في رواية أبي بكر وحمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL(عمرو ومكي ونافع غير قالون وسهل ويعقوب غير زيد); QIRAAT-ER]
- 29:29/q01/p6 The Kufis other than Ḥafṣ lengthen the hamza and follow it with kasra yāʾ. — Al-Nasafī gives this further realization. Ibn al-Jazarī points readers back to earlier discussions of the variant in إنكم لتأتون and related passages. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(كوفي غير حفص); NASHR]

## 29:29/q02 What does the question form do in Lot's accusation?  [أَئِنَّكُمْ لَتَأْتُونَ ٱلرِّجَالَ وَتَقْطَعُونَ ٱلسَّبِيلَ وَتَأْتُونَ فِى نَادِيكُمُ ٱلْمُنكَرَ · rhetoric]
- 29:29/q02/p1 It asserts and rebukes conduct too outrageous to accept. — Al-Zajjāj calls the form interrogative but its force assertion and rebuke. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says emphatic lām and nūn mark a question that no sensible listener would credit or accept. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the denunciatory question covers the whole list, repeats the emphatic particle, and expands the general charge of indecency with coordinated offenses. Al-Biqāʿī says repeating the rebuke underscores male intercourse and adds further grounds for blame as Lot continues admonition. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:29/q03 What conduct does approaching men denote?  [لَتَأْتُونَ ٱلرِّجَالَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether other accounts of aggressive approaches restrict the charge to force, or whether the wording about desire includes all male intercourse.
- 29:29/q03/p1 Men have sexual intercourse with men; the accusation covers the act whether consensual or coerced. — Yaḥyā specifies intercourse in the rear; al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī gloss the charge as male intercourse. Al-Ṭabarī pairs it with assault on strangers, citing 6:19 and 7:81. Ibn Kathīr calls the act unprecedented and adds their denial of their messenger. Rāzī says they abandon women and the customary life-preserving route. Study Quran cites 7:81, 26:165–166, and 27:54–55 for desire and says consent does not limit the charge; it reports commentators who say no earlier people committed it so widely. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 29:29/q03/p2 The accusation is specifically about forcibly approaching men. — Study Quran reports this view from some commentators on the strength of aggressive approaches in 11:77, 11:79, 15:67, and 15:71; its own explanation includes desire whether relations were consensual or not. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators); STUDYQURAN-]

## 29:29/q04 What does cutting off the road mean here?  [وَتَقْطَعُونَ ٱلسَّبِيلَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sabīl is an actual travel route obstructed through assault, robbery, or notoriety, or a figurative route to offspring; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes context for the latter.
- 29:29/q04/p1 They intercepted, sexually assaulted, and terrorized travelers, making the route unusable. — Yaḥyā says the targets were outsiders, not one another, and mentions seizure; Ibn Zayd and al-Farrāʾ specify passersby. Ibn Shajara says fear of their sexual crimes itself deterred travel. Reports from Muqātil describe stone and clod attacks from their assembly; al-Wāḥidī relates sling stones and a report of pebble lots by which a hit traveler was claimed, after which Ibn ʿAbbās says travelers avoided the road. Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī report seizure, theft, rape, a three-dirham charge, and a judge; al-Ṭabrisī specifies selection by lot. Al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and others include murder and robbery within attacks; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they sat along roads choosing targets and that the sexual aim made inherently corrupt attacks worse. Al-Qurṭubī allows this crime to coexist with robbery and abandoning women. [33 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد,الفراء,النبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شجرة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 29:29/q04/p2 They robbed, killed, or intimidated road users as highway bandits. — Ibn Zayd is reported as calling this robbery; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes a group who say it was widespread. Al-Nasafī specifies murder and property seizure. Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Qurṭubī combine robbery with other potential harms. Ibn al-Jawzī glosses qaṭʿ as waylaying or intimidating. Al-Ṭabrisī distinguishes this from the elaborate traveler-assault report. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 29:29/q04/p3 They cut off procreation, the natural way of offspring, by turning from women to men. — Al-Zajjāj reports the path of offspring; Wahb says they made do with men. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say the chosen intercourse cannot produce offspring; al-Bursawī contrasts abandoning the procreative field. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this as the contextual metaphor of turning from women and marriage, and says the context contradicts the literal-road reports. Asad reports al-Ḥasan via al-Baghawī and al-Zamakhsharī as 'the way of nature' and says Rāzī adopts that sense exclusively. Study Quran also reports the offspring sense. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ(التفسير); MAWARDI-FULL(وهب); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Baghawi and Zamakhshari (on Al-Hasan's authority),Razi); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); TABATABAI+]
- 29:29/q04/p4 Their notorious conduct deterred travel and ruined the trade route even without direct interception. — Ibn Shajara says fear of their vile conduct kept travelers away; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports an account based on their reputation. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī mention ruined trade or scandalous stories. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:29/q04/p5 The phrase itself is interpreted as sodomy. — Al-Dāmghānī reports this as an alternative to terrorizing travelers. [2 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 29:29/q05 What does sabīl denote in this expression?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ · meaning]
- 29:29/q05/p1 A customary, easily traversed road, whose cutting can stop travel or involve robbing travelers. — Al-Bursawī gives the lexical description and two usages of cutting the road. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 29:29/q06 What does nādī mean?  [نَادِيكُمُ · meaning]
- 29:29/q06/p1 A council, gathering, or meeting place, properly called that while people are assembled. — Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss the plural as assemblies; Ibn Qutayba and al-Wāḥidī define the place, the latter referring to 19:73. Abū ʿAlī equates nādī and nadī and cites a line of Kathīr on assembly. Al-Baghawī adds nadā and muntadā. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī say singular nādī is generic for their many meeting places. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress that the place is so called while its people are present; Abū Ḥayyān calls its later use figurative. Kashf al-Asrār says it is for evening sociability and its plural is andiya, while Ibn ʿĀshūr derives it from nadw, daytime assembly, distinguishes nighttime sāmir, and adds conversation and consultation. [23 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,القاسم بن محمد); FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 29:29/q07 What does taʾtūn mean in the phrase about wrongdoing?  [وَتَأْتُونَ · meaning]
- 29:29/q07/p1 It means to do or commit an act. — Al-Dāmghānī cites this phrase and 27:54; Ibn al-Jawzī reports it as a sixth sense of atā. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 29:29/q08 What does munkar mean?  [ٱلْمُنكَرَ · meaning]
- 29:29/q08/p1 Reprehensible or obscene speech and action, condemned by reason, law, or good conduct. — Ibn Qutayba says it collects obscenities in speech and deed without settling the disputed specific act. Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī repeat him. Abū Ḥayyān adds condemnation by reason, religious law, or character; al-Biqāʿī includes even slight impropriety. Al-Rāghib, cited by al-Bursawī, says sound reason judges it vile or, where reason hesitates, sacred law does. Al-Qurṭubī notes disagreement about the act. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب)]

## 29:29/q09 What wrongdoing did they commit in their gathering?  [وَتَأْتُونَ فِى نَادِيكُمُ ٱلْمُنكَرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which particular act the general term munkar singles out: a prophetic report and al-Ṭabarī support pelting; reports from Mujāhid support open sexual conduct; others give flatulence or wider vice lists, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeals to context.
- 29:29/q09/p1 They had sexual intercourse with men openly in their assemblies. — Mujāhid is repeatedly transmitted, sometimes with Manṣūr; Qatāda and Ibn Zayd agree. Ibn ʿAbbās says the practice became entrenched. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, and Abū Ḥayyān add that others watched. Yaḥyā says the indecency occurred in the assembly. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says sexual obscenities or their preliminaries occurred there and prefers the specific sexual reading over a broad vice list. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they discussed, prepared, and initiated it there, with flirtation and pebbles for choosing a target. Al-Rāzī calls public display an added wrong. [27 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,بعض أهل التأويل,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاسم بن محمد,مجاهد ومنصور); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(منصور ومجاهد والقاسم بن محمد وقتادة بن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد ومنصور والقاسم بن محمد وقتادة وابن زيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 29:29/q09/p2 They pelted passersby or strangers with pebbles and mocked or mistreated them. — Muqātil identifies stone throwing. The report from Umm Hāniʾ attributes pelting and mockery to the Prophet; al-Ṭabarī prefers this on that report, and ʿIkrima and al-Suddī independently mention pelting. Al-Tirmidhī grades the report ḥasan, known through Ḥātim ibn Abī Ṣaghīra from Simāk; al-Ḥākim authenticates it, and al-Ṭayālisī records it. Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās call the act khadhf. Al-Bayḍāwī gives a version specifying small stones or pellets; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī acknowledges the report yet says context favors sexual conduct. Al-Thaʿlabī transmits a prophetic warning that flicking stones does not catch game or harm an enemy but can blind an eye or break a tooth. ## 29:29/q10 Why was doing wrong in a public gathering especially grave?  [فِى نَادِيكُمُ ٱلْمُنكَرَ · interpretation]
- 29:29/q10/p1 Public display compounded the act by removing shame and spreading it. — Jishumī says open lewdness is more grotesque; Rāzī says display adds to the act's ugliness. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts their shamelessness with ordinary people's care to avoid even slight impropriety in company. Ibn ʿĀshūr says their preparations and open practice abandoned concealment and spread the vice. [4 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 29:29/q11 What conduct should replace evil in assemblies?  [فِى نَادِيكُمُ ٱلْمُنكَرَ · interpretation]
- 29:29/q11/p1 People should gather for remembrance and nearness to God, admonish one another, and forbid wrongdoing. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq say socializing around mockery, idle amusement, or forbidden acts should yield to what brings one near God. Al-Junayd calls any gathering for something other than remembrance evil. Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir says failure to enjoin right and forbid wrong is the evil; al-Qayyim singles out neglect of elders' respect. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say some of these acts occur among sinners of Muhammad's community, making mutual admonition obligatory. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,الجنيد,القيم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 29:29/q12 What ruling follows for male same-sex intercourse?  [لَتَأْتُونَ ٱلرِّجَالَ · law]
- 29:29/q12/p1 It is prohibited; Rāzī argues for a prescribed punishment, specifically stoning. — Kur'an Yolu says natural reproduction and sexual life rest on male-female union, Lot taught marriage to women as the sole lawful sexual relationship for men, and the relevant passages unequivocally prohibit homosexuality. Rāzī argues that sharing the label 'abomination' with zinā in 17:32 carries its deterrent by analogy, then argues for stoning from the rain of stones on Lot's people. He says sex with women serves preservation of human life, making the deviation's ugliness clear. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 29:29/q13 What legal instruction is associated with pebble flicking?  [ٱلْمُنكَرَ · law]
- 29:29/q13/p1 The Prophet prohibited khadhf, small-stone flicking. — Jābir's report cites this verse; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says al-Durr transmits it through Ibn Mardawayh. The Prophet's warning in al-Thaʿlabī says it is ineffective for hunting or combat but can blind an eye or break a tooth. Ibn ʿUmar sharply reproached someone who equivocated about the report and threw pebbles at his face. [4 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(جابر)]

## 29:29/q14 What does the people's demand for punishment challenge?  [ٱئْتِنَا بِعَذَابِ ٱللَّهِ إِن كُنتَ مِنَ ٱلصَّٰدِقِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Lot had explicitly warned of punishment or his rebuke implied a judgment of vileness, liability to punishment, or prophetic authority.
- 29:29/q14/p1 They challenge Lot to bring the divine punishment he warned would come. — Muqātil specifies punishment in this world; Abū al-ʿĀliya paraphrases 'if what you say is true.' Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, and others say he warned them first; al-Thaʿlabī refers to 29:30 and says punishment was coming. Ibn ʿĀshūr says warning is an expected part of prophetic preaching although omitted earlier, and the imperative declares Lot powerless. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says another story passage attests his warning. Al-Ālūsī gives this as one reading. Al-Biqāʿī says invoking God's greatest name intensifies insolence and 'if truthful' attacks Lot's authority. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 29:29/q14/p2 They challenge Lot's implied assertion that their conduct merits punishment. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives this as one possible target of their reply; al-Ālūsī says the reproachful question implied that punishment was deserved. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:29/q14/p3 They challenge Lot's judgment that their conduct is vile. — Al-Bayḍāwī offers this target; al-Ālūsī names the censured conduct's reprehensibility. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:29/q14/p4 They challenge the prophethood and warning authority implied by his rebuke. — Al-Bayḍāwī names prophethood; al-Biqāʿī says 'if among the truthful' challenges his authority and tries to provoke him. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:29/q15 Why did they demand punishment instead of answering Lot?  [فَمَا كَانَ جَوَابَ قَوْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّآ أَن قَالُوا۟ ٱئْتِنَا بِعَذَابِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 29:29/q15/p1 Their denial, obstinacy, mockery, and conviction that no punishment would come drove the taunt. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, and al-Ṭabrisī call it mockery. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say they believed Lot false and punishment impossible; al-Qurṭubī says the wording implies settled disbelief beyond mere stubborn challenge. Rāzī says they first denied divine punishment and later threatened expulsion after continued admonition. Ibn Kathīr connects the taunt to Lot's prayer in 29:30. Al-Biqāʿī calls it ignorance, obstinacy, and provocation; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it declares Lot powerless. Study Quran compares the challenges of ʿĀd to Hūd and Thamūd to Ṣāliḥ in 46:22, 7:70, and 7:77. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 29:29/q16 How can this be their only answer when other verses give an expulsion answer?  [فَمَا كَانَ جَوَابَ قَوْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّآ أَن قَالُوا۟ · links]
turns on: Whether the two replies occurred at different times, among different speakers, in public versus private discourse, or are exclusive relative to the hoped-for response.
- 29:29/q16/p1 The punishment challenge came at the first admonition; expulsion followed repeated rebukes. — Rāzī reads 27:56 as a later threat. Al-Irshād says the answers in 7:39 and 27:56 belong to the final dispute. Abū Ḥayyān and Abū al-Suʿūd, reported by al-Ālūsī, use early versus later stages; al-Ālūsī notes an objection that this lacks transmitted evidence and the defense that early mockery and later retaliation fit the progression. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 29:29/q16/p2 This was their answer to Lot; expulsion was said among themselves during deliberation. — Al-Ālūsī reports this distinction with 7:82 and 27:56. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:29/q16/p3 The different answers came from different groups, chiefs and others. — Al-Ālūsī reports this reconciliation with 7:82 and 27:56. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:29/q16/p4 Each exclusive formulation is relative to the response Lot hoped to receive. — Al-Ālūsī says some eminent commentators appear to choose this reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:29/q17 What happened after the people's challenge?  [ٱئْتِنَا بِعَذَابِ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
- 29:29/q17/p1 Lot prayed for help and God sent angels to punish his people. — Al-Ṭabrisī says Lot prayed against the sinful corrupters. Ibn Kathīr ties his prayer to 29:30; al-Biqāʿī says he addressed God as benefactor against a powerful people beyond his own strength. Al-Qurṭubī says the angels first visited Abraham to announce Lot's victory, referring to Hūd. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:29/q18 What theological claim is drawn from these acts?  [لَتَأْتُونَ ٱلرِّجَالَ · theology]
- 29:29/q18/p1 The people originated their sexual immoralities themselves, contrary to the claim that God creates human acts. — Jishumī directs the point against the Mujabbira doctrine. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 29:29/q19 Why is Lot's call to worship God absent from this account?  [* · links]
- 29:29/q19/p1 Abraham's monotheistic preaching was already known among Lot's people, so Lot stressed their distinctive offenses. — Abū Ḥayyān says Lot belonged to Abraham's people and compares later Abraham and Shuʿayb, who had to call to worship God. Al-Ālūsī says earlier worshippers had passed away before Abraham and Shuʿayb, referring to 29:16 and 29:36. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:29/q20 How does this account relate to the accounts in Hūd and earlier sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the note explains the difference in level of detail or simply refers to earlier discussion.
- 29:29/q20/p1 Al-ʿAnkabūt details the offenses that Hūd summarizes as evil deeds. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 29:28–29 with 11:78; the longer account suits this sūra. He also says Lot's greater distress, the people's demand and his prayer make the messengers' arrival feel prolonged compared with 11:77, fitting the use of an, perhaps for emphasis. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 29:29/q20/p2 This is a reprise whose detailed reading and story have been explained in earlier passages. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya refers back for a-innakum; al-Qurṭubī points to al-Aʿrāf and Hūd. Ibn al-Jazarī points back for إنكم لتأتون, Abraham's messengers, لننجينه وإنا منجوك, and سيء. Kur'an Yolu points to earlier treatments in al-Aʿrāf, Hūd, and al-Ḥijr. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASHR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 29:29/q21 Why do Lot's people answer differently from Abraham's people?  [فَمَا كَانَ جَوَابَ قَوْمِهِۦٓ · links]
- 29:29/q21/p1 Abraham attacked idols held sacred, prompting a death threat; Lot condemned acts his people did not treat as religious duty, prompting a challenge. — Rāzī compares 29:24. Ibn ʿĀshūr also parallels 29:24, describing the demand for punishment as a challenge. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 29:29/q22 Can two interrogatives follow one another in this construction?  [أَئِنَّكُمْ · grammar]
- 29:29/q22/p1 Yes; a question may follow another question. — Al-Naḥḥās rejects Abū ʿUbayd's objection to the double interrogative reading and says the example from this sūra used in the objection has a different construction. [1 notes: NAHHAS+]
