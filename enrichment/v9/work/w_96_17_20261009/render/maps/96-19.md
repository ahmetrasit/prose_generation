# 96:19: 24 questions

## 96:19/q01 What does kallā do here?  [كَلَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether kallā negates Abū Jahl's confidence or serves as an alert.
- 96:19/q01/p1 It rebukes and negates the forbidding man's claim or expectation. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and later commentators apply it to Abū Jahl's ban or boast. Al-Rāzī adds that his assembly cannot help and he cannot end prayer. Al-Biqāʿī denies the threat, reform and protection of the gathering. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to 96:17; Būrsawī calls the pause after it absolute; Corpus Coranicum notes recurrent kallā at 96:6–8 and 15. [32 notes: TAB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 96:19/q01/p2 It can alert the hearer with the force of 'indeed' or 'lo'. — Al-Rāzī offers equivalence to alā. [1 notes: RAZI]

## 96:19/q02 Whom does 'him' designate?  [تُطِعْهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether to identify the preceding denier specifically as Abū Jahl.
- 96:19/q02/p1 Abū Jahl, who forbade the Prophet's prayer. — Abū Hurayra is cited by al-Māwardī. Other sources treat him as the historical opponent. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; MAWARDI(أبو هريرة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو هريرة); THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; IBNATIYYA; QURTUBI; BIQAI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(المفسرون); STUDYQURAN]
- 96:19/q02/p2 The unnamed disbeliever who turned away in the prior context. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the preceding pronouns apparently point to this figure, while reporting the conventional Abū Jahl identification. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(المفسرون)]

## 96:19/q03 What does 'do not obey him' require?  [لَا تُطِعْهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether obedience is literal compliance, answering his speech, or caution induced by fear.
- 96:19/q03/p1 Keep praying and worshipping despite his ban and threats. — Muqātil and most commentators specify refusal to abandon prayer. Al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī cite 68:8. Ibn Kathīr says the Prophet may pray anywhere under divine protection; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies the Sacred Mosque; Elmalılı adds continued recitation. Al-Biqāʿī includes devotion generally. [46 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 96:19/q03/p2 Do not accept his words or opinion if he comes with accommodation. — Al-Māwardī offers this extension of obedience. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 96:19/q03/p3 Do not answer his words or retaliate for his actions. — Al-Māwardī uses atiʿ for answering, citing the Prophet's prayer that God not answer a traveler asking for rain to stop lest people perish. [4 notes: MAWARDI(النبي); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 96:19/q03/p4 Do not let fear of him make the Prophet abandon prayer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls obedience a metaphor for fear-induced caution; Abū Jahl cannot harm him. The closing command refers back to 96:9–10. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:19/q04 What does sujūd mean in its root usage?  [وَٱسْجُدْ · meaning]
- 96:19/q04/p1 Lowering and bending, extended to humility and obedience. — Ibn Qutayba cites a camel lowering itself, a bending palm, and a forehead on earth. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites the angels before Adam and Joseph's family at 12:100 as submission. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BINTSHATI]

## 96:19/q05 What action does 'prostrate' command here?  [وَٱسْجُدْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sujūd names prayer, its posture, submission generally, or recitation prostration.
- 96:19/q05/p1 Continue prayer and worship, with prostration naming prayer. — Muqātil glosses prayer to God. Al-Rāzī attributes this to most exegetes and adds action and preaching with trust in God's support. Abū Ḥayyān calls prostration prayer's finest posture; al-Biqāʿī includes worship generally; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the next command confirms prayer. [32 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(أكثر أهل التأويل); RAZI-FULL(أكثر أهل التأويل); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 96:19/q05/p2 Perform bodily prostration within prayer. — Al-Rāzī reports this from others; Ibn al-ʿArabī calls it apparent from the prayer context. Elmalılı prefers the literal act because 'draw near' already covers general worship. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ allows the specific act within broader humility, citing 48:29, 39:9, 17:107 and 19:58. [10 notes: RAZI(آخرون); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI; ELMALILI+]
- 96:19/q05/p3 Submit and humble oneself generally. — Al-Rāzī reports this from some. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Islam reserved worshipful prostration for the Creator while the language of full humility remains. [5 notes: RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BINTSHATI]
- 96:19/q05/p4 Perform prostration on reciting this passage. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports it as one of four obligatory recitation prostrations. Ibn al-ʿArabī mentions this possibility but calls prayer the contextual sense. [2 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); QURTUBI(ابن العربي)-]

## 96:19/q06 Why is prayer expressed through prostration?  [وَٱسْجُدْ · rhetoric]
- 96:19/q06/p1 Prostration is prayer's noblest and most humble posture, so naming it emphasizes prayer and nearness. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it the finest posture; al-Biqāʿī the noblest component. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees reinforcement of the prayer command. Elmalılı says naming it separately stresses its importance because 'draw near' spans other worship. Islahi calls it prayer's greatest component. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 96:19/q07 What kind of prayer or prostration lay behind this early scene?  [وَٱسْجُدْ · reports]
- 96:19/q07/p1 Prostration was known before Islam, including among idol worshippers. — Muqātil says idolaters began with prostration before bowing and standing, whereas God later changed the Prophet's order to standing, bowing, then prostrating. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Abraham and Ishmael's sanctuary (2:125; 22:26) and pre-Islamic idol prostration. Neuwirth, following Uri Rubin, sees pre-Islamic ritual in the command. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth; Uri Rubin)]
- 96:19/q07/p2 The Prophet practiced a public, still unregulated prayer involving prostration before the five daily prayers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it was distinct from the later obligation, visible enough to provoke Abū Jahl; he places the Night Journey and five-prayer obligation a year before Hijra. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR+]
- 96:19/q07/p3 Before the Night Journey he prayed once before sunset and once before sunrise. — Al-Ḥarbī gives the two times and Yaḥyā b. Sallām concurs. Ibn ʿĀshūr's full account identifies them as ʿAṣr and Fajr. [4 notes: IBNASHUR(الحربي,يحيى بن سلام); IBNASHUR-FULL(الحربي,يحيى بن سلام)]
- 96:19/q07/p4 The prayer may have consisted of glorification and prostration. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī states this tentatively. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 96:19/q08 Is supplication during prostration legally required?  [وَٱسْجُدْ · law]
turns on: Whether the exhortation establishes obligation or encouragement.
- 96:19/q08/p1 It is strongly urged because likely to be answered. — Abū Hurayra's report urges abundance; al-Qurṭubī cites a sound hadith contrasting bowing's glorification with prostration's supplication. These reports state encouragement without an explicit ruling on obligation. [4 notes: BAGHAWI(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 96:19/q08/p2 It is obligatory. — Al-ʿIzz b. ʿAbd al-Salām's opinion is described by al-Ālūsī as unusual. [2 notes: ALUSI(العز بن عبد السلام); ALUSI-FULL(العز بن عبد السلام)]

## 96:19/q09 Is prostration the best pillar of prayer?  [وَٱسْجُدْ · law]
- 96:19/q09/p1 Several scholars hold it to be the best pillar. — Al-Ālūsī grounds this in reports of nearness and Thawbān's report that each prostration raises a degree and erases a sin. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد)]

## 96:19/q10 What is the status of recitation prostration at this verse?  [وَٱسْجُدْ · law]
turns on: Whether established recitation prostration also has obligatory status.
- 96:19/q10/p1 A prostration of recitation is recognized here. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya's group recognize it. Sound reports from Abū Hurayra in Muslim and both Ṣaḥīḥ collections attest the Prophet prostrating at Iqraʾ and al-Inshiqāq (84:1 or 84:21). Mālik prostrated privately. Al-Qurṭubī prefers the sound report despite Ibn al-ʿArabī's contextual prayer reading. Study Quran counts it among fifteen prostration verses. [18 notes: MAWARDI; IBNATIYYA(جماعة من أهل العلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة من أهل العلم); QURTUBI(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن العربي,مالك); ABUHAYYAN(النبي,مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI(علي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI+]
- 96:19/q10/p2 It is one of four obligatory or decisive recitation prostrations. — Imam al-Ṣādiq and ʿAlī list al-Sajda, Fuṣṣilat/Ḥā-Mīm, al-Najm and al-ʿAlaq; the rest are recommended per the former. Ibn Wahb calls this decisive. Ibn al-ʿArabī says that if ʿAlī's report is authentic, its logic requires the second Ḥajj prostration (22:77) despite bowing there. [10 notes: TABRISI(عبد الله بن سنان عن أبي عبد الله); QURTUBI(ابن العربي,ابن وهب,علي بن أبي طالب); QURTUBI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب,مالك); ABUHAYYAN(علي بن أبي طالب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(عبد الله بن سنان عن أبي عبد الله,قيل)]

## 96:19/q11 Who is told to draw near, and to what?  [وَٱقْتَرِب · referent]
turns on: Whether the second imperative continues addressing Muhammad or turns to Abū Jahl.
- 96:19/q11/p1 The Prophet is told to draw near to God. — Al-Māwardī gives this as his view; Ibn al-Jawzī ascribes it to the majority. Al-Jishumī prefers it because the sūra addresses the Prophet. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; JISHUMI+]
- 96:19/q11/p2 Abū Jahl is challenged to approach the Fire or praying Prophet and see his destruction. — Zayd b. Aslam directs only the second command to Abū Jahl. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's group and Ibn al-Jawzī call it a threat. Al-Rāzī adds seeing the zabāniya seize him and rage at prayer (48:29), or sarcastically putting his foot on the Prophet. Al-Jishumī argues the sūra addresses the Prophet. [16 notes: IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI(زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA(جماعة من أهل العلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن وهب عن جماعة من أهل العلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي,زيد بن أسلم); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(زيد بن أسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); DURR(زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); JISHUMI-]

## 96:19/q12 How is nearness in 'draw near' attained or understood?  [وَٱقْتَرِب · interpretation]
turns on: Whether nearness is sought rank, direct closeness, reward, supplication, or general worship.
- 96:19/q12/p1 Approach God through obedience, worship and prostration. — Muqātil and al-Zajjāj specify obedience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds good deeds. Al-Rāzī speaks of nearness in rank and contrasts obedient closeness with Satan's distance (2:186). Al-Biqāʿī adds inward love through every worship. Ibn ʿĀshūr says prayer pleases God. [39 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 96:19/q12/p2 Prostration itself is the servant's nearest state to God. — Mujāhid cites this command; many reports make the same point. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects Abū Ḥayyān's 'seek closeness through devotion' for direct nearness. Al-Qurṭubī and a poem explain how humility before majesty brings honor. [15 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI(أبو هريرة,الشاعر,علماؤنا); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,الشاعر,علماؤنا); DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(الرضا); BINTSHATI+]
- 96:19/q12/p3 Draw near to God's reward. — Al-Ṭabrisī adopts this goal and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports it as another interpretation. [2 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 96:19/q12/p4 Draw near through supplication while prostrating. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this means; a sound report urges supplication then because it is likely to be answered. [4 notes: QURTUBI(النبي,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل)]
- 96:19/q12/p5 Join in worship more generally. — Corpus Coranicum calls the second imperative less concrete, comparing sūra endings that call to worship, praise or proclamation (108:2; 93:11; 52:48–49; 56:74,96; 69:52). [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 96:19/q13 What force does the form iqtirāb add?  [وَٱقْتَرِب · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form stresses effort to gain nearness or direct nearness achieved in prostration.
- 96:19/q13/p1 The iftiʿāl form conveys exertion and seeking closeness through prayer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr derives this from the morphology. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects Abū Ḥayyān's similar devotional reading. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-]
- 96:19/q13/p2 It conveys direct nearness, the worshipper's highest aspiration. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the ending breaks the proud man's vanity and counters imagined independence; 92:13 recalls God's ownership of both lives. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]

## 96:19/q14 What does prostration bring, and what practice follows from its nearness?  [وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب · interpretation]
- 96:19/q14/p1 The servant is nearest to God while prostrating, so abundant supplication is urged and likely answered. — Mujāhid and many Prophetic reports give the closest-state formula; Abū Hurayra adds frequent supplication, reported sound by Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says prayers then are likely answered. Al-Qurṭubī contrasts glorification in bowing with supplication in prostration. Būrsawī parses mā as verbal noun, aqrab as subject with omitted predicate, and yakūn as complete in the hadith. [35 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); BAGHAWI(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); KASHSHAF(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); TABRISI(عبد الله بن مسعود عن النبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة); RAZI(النبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(أبو هريرة,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI(الحديث); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI; QASHANI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI; DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الرضا,عبد الله بن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 96:19/q14/p2 Frequent prostration raises ranks and removes sins. — Thawbān transmits this as sound; al-Rāzī says repeated prayer raises rank until even minor sins become repugnant. Elmalılı cites a qudsī report in which voluntary worship draws the servant near until God guides hearing, sight and heart. [4 notes: ALUSI(ثوبان); ALUSI-FULL(ثوبان); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 96:19/q14/p3 Prostration's abasement is supreme servitude and a source of honor and nearness. — Al-Qurṭubī's scholars say abasement before divine majesty brings nearness to Paradise; a poem says dignity lies in humility. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls prostration prayer's climax; Islahi calls it life's objective and refuge under challenge. [6 notes: QURTUBI(الشاعر,علماؤنا); QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر,علماؤنا); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 96:19/q15 What inward approach do these commands describe?  [وَٱسْجُدْ وَٱقْتَرِب · inward]
- 96:19/q15/p1 Prostration is annihilation of acts, attributes and essence, ending in abiding in God. — Al-Qāshānī reads attentive monotheistic prayer and the Prophet's refuge formula as three stages. Al-Ghazālī, cited by al-Biqāʿī, reads it as ascent through witnessing God's acts, attributes and essence until God alone praises Himself. The refuge prayer names pardon from punishment, pleasure from anger, and God from God. [5 notes: QASHANI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI(الإمام الغزالي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(الغزالي)]
- 96:19/q15/p2 Bodily servanthood is joined to inner witnessing of Lordship. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ speaks of entry to Lordship's court after release from servanthood's court. Al-Qushayrī keeps the self on servanthood's ground while the heart witnesses Lordship, or body prostrates as the secret draws near. Al-Ḥusayn says limbs display worship while one approaches only when drawn near, citing 56:88. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الحسين); QUSHAYRI]
- 96:19/q15/p3 Nearness involves continual ascent through spiritual stations and gratitude. — Al-Biqāʿī says earlier stations appear deficient beside later ones; the Prophet sought forgiveness seventy times a day and ceased self-observation. ʿĀʾisha's question about his weeping despite forgiven sins elicited his wish to be grateful; 14:7 links thanks with increase. [8 notes: BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة رضي الله عنها); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة)]
- 96:19/q15/p4 The elect witness divine qualities beyond common people's limited capacity. — Al-Wāsiṭī says the elect see no other attributes; commoners' inner secrets are too weak and distant from truth's source. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 96:19/q15/p5 Permission to prostrate signals that God has already drawn the servant near. — Dhū al-Nūn urges an inward approach in light of the report that prostration is the nearest state. [1 notes: SULAMI(ذو النون)]

## 96:19/q16 What did Abū Jahl's confrontation with the praying Prophet involve?  [* · reports]
- 96:19/q16/p1 He threatened to trample the Prophet, recoiled before a fiery trench and wings, and was warned of angels' seizure. — Abū Hurayra reports the threat, retreat, awe, trench and wings; the Prophet said angels would tear him apart. Al-Ṭabarī's short note flags uncertain attribution of the quoted revelation. Qatāda ties revelation to the threat; the zabāniya would snatch him if he acted. Būrsawī says summoning his council would reveal torment angels. [6 notes: TAB(أبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 96:19/q17 What general lesson follows from the final commands?  [* · interpretation]
- 96:19/q17/p1 One must obey God rather than a creature who commands disobedience, and persevere in worship. — Al-Jishumī states the maxim. Sayyid Quṭb extends the warning to all believers who pray and call to God against aggressors, leaving the tyrant to the zabāniya. Study Quran extends it to deniers of religion or prayer. Islahi adds forbearance, perseverance and divine help through prayer. [6 notes: JISHUMI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 96:19/q18 How does the ending complete the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
- 96:19/q18/p1 It counters the tyrant's worldly authority and returns to the opening call to worship. — Corpus Coranicum says the imperatives turn from the accused to the Messenger, summarize the sūra, contrast human and divine authority, and echo 96:1,3. Neuwirth likens the close to a psalmic hallelujah (Psalm 149:9). Al-Biqāʿī says the ending returns to the beginning. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says nearness defeats the illusion of independence; Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the forbidding man introduced after human transgression. [8 notes: BIQAI; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:19/q18/p2 Prayer, recitation and devotion shield from distraction and sustain spiritual state. — Al-Biqāʿī says required recitation and devotion veil distractions and increase purity; he cites Abraham's scroll advising attention to one's affairs, tongue and time. [3 notes: BIQAI(إبراهيم عليه الصلاة والسلام); BIQAI-FULL]

## 96:19/q19 When and in how many stages was the sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether internal tone and cohesion support one episode or an interval after the opening five verses.
- 96:19/q19/p1 The opening five verses came first and the rest followed after a pause. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Gabriel remained visible and non-Qur'anic revelation continued; he finds a two-year pause unlikely, treats its exact duration as unimportant, and puts the rest after a short interval when prayer and news of revelation had spread. Consecutive recitation normally reflects consecutive revelation. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:19/q19/p2 The pause lasted two years. — Al-Suhaylī reports this duration; Ibn ʿĀshūr considers it unlikely. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(السهيلي)]
- 96:19/q19/p3 The sūra is a coherent single revelation unit. — Islahi finds the first five verses as severe in tone as the rest, no indication of two episodes, and uses tone, addressee and style to assess period. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 96:19/q21 What other reports are attached to this sūra or its recitation?  [* · reports]
- 96:19/q21/p1 Eighty-four verses were revealed about Abū Jahl, 104 about al-Walīd and 32 about al-Naḍr. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by al-Māwardī. [2 notes: MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 96:19/q21/p2 At revelation the writing implements and then Muʿādh and the Prophet prostrated. — A report through Mālik, Rabīʿa and Nāfiʿ from Ibn ʿUmar says the tablet, pen and inkwell bowed and prayed for blessed remembrance, removal of burdens and forgiveness when Muʿādh was told to write the sūra. ## 96:19/q23 How are the attached religious and tribal group nouns used and derived?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether naṣārā rests on unattested naṣrī or attested naṣrāna.
- 96:19/q23/p1 Majūs and Yahūd are tribal nouns, with feminine gender prevailing. — Sībawayh cites Imruʾ al-Qays's 'fire of Majūs'; a man called Majūs is diptote like ʿAmmān. Al-Yahūd and al-Majūs resemble nisba adjectives with deleted yāʾ, whereas bare majūs is indefinite. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 96:19/q23/p2 Naṣārā is an indefinite plural of naṣrān and naṣrāna. — Sībawayh likens it to nadāmā from nadmān, says prose usually prefers nisba forms, and adduces poetry with indefinite naṣārā and feminine naṣrāna. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 96:19/q23/p3 Naṣārā derives from naṣrī and naṣrān, with yāʾ replaced by alif. — Al-Khalīl compares nadmān/nadāmā and mihri/mahārā; Sībawayh objects that naṣrī is unattested. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); SIBAWAYH-]
- 96:19/q23/p4 Naṣārā more analogically derives from attested feminine naṣrāna. — Sībawayh prefers the analogy of ashʿath/ashāʿith and cites Abū al-Akhzar al-Ḥammānī's verse using naṣrāna. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH; SIBAWAYH+]

## 96:19/q24 How are the attached tribal plurals in wāw-nūn or maqtawīn explained?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether maqtawīn preserves an original form or follows a plural lacking an independent singular.
- 96:19/q24/p1 Tribal wāw-nūn plurals are analogical additions beside broken plurals, not fully productive. — Al-Khalīl cites al-Ashʿarūn and al-Aʿjamūn and treats maqtawī/maqtawīn like al-Ashʿarī/al-Ashʿarīn. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 96:19/q24/p2 Maqtawīn retains the original form, parallel to maqātawa. — Sībawayh reports Abū al-Khaṭṭāb heard the form from Arabs though not all know it. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 96:19/q24/p3 Maqtawīn follows madharwīn because no independent singular is used. — Sībawayh offers this as an alternative. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
