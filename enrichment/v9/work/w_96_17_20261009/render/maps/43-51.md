# 43:51: 25 questions

## 43:51/q01 Who is Pharaoh here?  [فِرْعَوْنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh is a named man or a royal title.
- 43:51/q01/p1 Al-Walid ibn Musab, a Copt. — Muqatil calls him Coptic; Itqan supplies the name and kunyas Abu al-Abbas, Abu al-Walid, and Abu Murra. [2 notes: MUQATIL; ITQAN]
- 43:51/q01/p2 Merneptah II. — Ibn Ashur identifies the Pharaoh of this narrative thus. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:51/q01/p3 A Persian from Istakhr. — Itqan transmits Mujahid through Ibn Abi Hatim. [1 notes: ITQAN(مجاهد)]
- 43:51/q01/p4 A title for each Egyptian ruler. — Itqan reports this explanation. [1 notes: ITQAN(قائلون)]

## 43:51/q02 Whom did Pharaoh address?  [فِى قَوْمِهِۦ يَٰقَوْمِ · referent]
- 43:51/q02/p1 Egyptians or Copts, initially perhaps their nobles. — Muqatil and Bursevi call them Copts; Tabari says Egyptians. Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, and Alusi envisage an address to notables that spread. Nasafi makes the people the call's setting; Biqai sees an appeal to shared identity and strength. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 43:51/q03 Why did Pharaoh proclaim this?  [وَنَادَىٰ فِرْعَوْنُ فِى قَوْمِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is threatened belief in Moses or assertion of royal obedience.
- 43:51/q03/p1 He feared the signs and lifted punishment would move his people to Moses. — Nahhas and Qurtubi give the aftermath of punishment; Tabrisi stresses Moses' growing influence. Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, and Islahi describe threatened allegiance. Biqai says he suppressed suspicion that he had converted; Ibn Ashur says he concealed his plea for relief and made the reprieve seem accidental, recalling the broken pledge in 7:135. [12 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مالك); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 43:51/q03/p2 He reasserted a superior claim to obedience. — Nahhas says fear exposed his inability; Biqai says he cast Israel's departure as rebellion and excused his broken pledge. [2 notes: NAHHAS; BIQAI-FULL]

## 43:51/q04 Did Pharaoh call personally or by herald?  [وَنَادَىٰ فِرْعَوْنُ فِى قَوْمِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether he spoke himself or commanded a public announcer.
- 43:51/q04/p1 He spoke or raised his voice in an assembly, perhaps to nobles. — Abu Malik glosses the call as speech. Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, and Alusi envisage notables, while other sources leave personal address possible. Alusi cites a poetic construction making the call established among them; Tabatabai places Pharaoh among his people. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو مالك); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن جريج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 43:51/q04/p2 He ordered heralds to proclaim it on his behalf. — Ibn Jurayj reports delegation. Kashshaf and Nasafi compare an emir credited with an ordered act; Alusi places criers in public spaces. Ibn Ashur calls this rational metaphor, cites 12:70 and 26:53–55, and quotes al-Hariri on formal heralding. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:51/q05 What does 'he said' add after 'he called'?  [وَنَادَىٰ قَالَ · grammar]
- 43:51/q05/p1 It explains the content of the call, answering 'What did he say?' — Tabatabai supplies the implied question; Ibn Ashur says the speech shares the call's rational attribution if heralds spoke. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:51/q06 What place or extent is Miṣr here?  [مِصْرَ · referent]
turns on: The reports locate Miṣr at a city or give different regional boundaries.
- 43:51/q06/p1 The Nile country from Alexandria to Aswan. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Fatḥ al-Raḥmān as quoted by Bursevi, and Alusi give this span; Bursevi cites al-Qamus identifying Aswan as an Upper Egyptian town. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(القاموس,فتح الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:51/q06/p2 An area forty by forty farsakhs. — Muqatil, al-Naqqash through Mawardi and Qurtubi, and Bursevi give this measure. [4 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش); BURSEVI]
- 43:51/q06/p3 Alexandria. — Mujahid and an alternative reported by Qurtubi identify it. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش)]
- 43:51/q06/p4 The familiar city of Egypt. — Zajjaj prefers the city sense and notes Miṣr's masculine grammar despite usual feminine 'city'. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ+]
- 43:51/q06/p5 The country or wider territory. — Zajjaj reports this but finds it unlikely because balad usually contains many major cities; Alusi speaks of ruling the territory. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ZAJJAJ-]
- 43:51/q06/p6 From Alexandria to the Syrian border. — Al-Kashifi is quoted by Bursevi. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 43:51/q07 What is the name Miṣr derived from?  [مِصْرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the derivation is a builder, urbanization, or separation.
- 43:51/q07/p1 Its builder Miṣr. — Bursevi quotes Rawḍat al-Akhbār calling him son of Ham son of Noah, and al-Qamus calling him son of Noah. [2 notes: BURSEVI(القاموس,روضة الأخبار)]
- 43:51/q07/p2 The place becoming urbanized. — Bursevi reports al-Qamus's alternative. [1 notes: BURSEVI(القاموس)]
- 43:51/q07/p3 Cutting off: buildings separated city from open land. — Bursevi reports this alternative. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 43:51/q08 Why is Miṣr declined or left without tanwīn?  [مِصْرَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether construed as a feminine place name or a country name.
- 43:51/q08/p1 It is a feminine proper place name normally without tanwīn. — Nahhas gives the Basran derivation from a masculine word. Kufans compare Hind but say rarity and feminine parallels such as balda favor no tanwīn. Biqai says the proper-name form suits Pharaoh's grand claim. [3 notes: NAHHAS; BIQAI-FULL]
- 43:51/q08/p2 If a country name, it can be fully declined. — Zajjaj reports Basran permission. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 43:51/q09 What is Pharaoh claiming by the kingdom of Egypt?  [لِى مُلْكُ مِصْرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the boast is framed as political authority, wealth, or a threatened ruler's appeal.
- 43:51/q09/p1 Exclusive government, resources, and a right to obedience. — Tabrisi says he could control Egypt; Alusi specifies administration, not land alone. Biqai makes any challenge illegitimate and Israel's departure rebellion. Tabari and Ibn Atiyya say Pharaoh uses power and riches as an argument against Moses, with Tabari citing 28:72. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:51/q09/p2 Prosperity compared with Moses' poverty. — Muqatil asks who else has such gardens and rivers; Qatada reports these resources. Ibn Abbas stresses wealth versus poverty, Nasafi youth and strength versus weakness, and Bint al-Shati the river as worldly boast. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 43:51/q09/p3 Royal possession asserted as Moses' miracles erode loyalty. — Islahi compares Quraysh's objection about the Quran's recipient in 43:31–35. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 43:51/q10 What are the rivers?  [هَٰذِهِ ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · referent]
turns on: Whether literal Nile waterways or figurative commanders, wealth, or horses.
- 43:51/q10/p1 The Nile and its major branches or canals. — Wahidi cites commentators; Mawardi allows the Nile itself. Thalabi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, and Alusi usually list four: King's or Alexandria canal, Tulun, Damietta, and Tanis/Tinnis. Alusi says Ibn Tulun may have restored an older canal; Ibn Ashur calls each a river-sized branch of one Nile. Qatada mentions Pharaoh's gardens and rivers. [17 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; TAB-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 43:51/q10/p2 Commanders and chiefs under Pharaoh's banner. — Al-Dahhak is reported by Mawardi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, and Alusi; Abu Hayyan and Alusi call it far-fetched or esoteric. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 43:51/q10/p3 Conspicuous wealth distributed to followers. — Mawardi proposes it; Qurtubi says this enticed followers; Abu Hayyan reports it. Abu Hayyan and Alusi reject the figurative sense. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 43:51/q10/p4 Horses, because a horse can be called a river. — Abu Hayyan reports but rejects this with other esoteric readings; Alusi likewise rejects it. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 43:51/q10/p5 Water outlets feeding city and gardens. — Ibn Ashur speaks of dams and reservoirs controlled by Pharaoh, who could cut water supply, and compares Joseph's offer of full measure in 12:59. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:51/q10/p6 The Nile together with other rivers. — Tabrisi gives this wider identification. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 43:51/q11 What geography is given for the Nile waterways?  [ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · reports]
turns on: The notes report different geographical details.
- 43:51/q11/p1 The Nile divides into 360 channels. — Bursevi quotes Kashf al-Asrar. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]
- 43:51/q11/p2 Tinnis was an island near Damietta famed for textiles. — Bursevi and his quoted al-Qamus give these details. [2 notes: BURSEVI(القاموس)]
- 43:51/q11/p3 The Nile is exceptionally long, with a remote southern source. — Kharidat al-Ajaib via Bursevi gives two months each in Muslim lands, unbelieving lands, and wilderness, then four in ruined land; its source lies at the Mountain of the Moon beyond the equator and the Sea of Darkness or Black Sea. [2 notes: BURSEVI(خريدة العجائب)]
- 43:51/q11/p4 The Indus or Mehran alone compares with the Nile. — Bursevi gives this comparison. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:51/q12 What does 'these' indicate, and why is 'rivers' definite?  [هَٰذِهِ ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · referent]
turns on: The demonstrative depends on the assumed capital; the article has a separate possessive explanation.
- 43:51/q12/p1 At Memphis, the visible Delta branches, Damietta and Rosetta. — Ibn Ashur locates them near Cairo and suggests Tanis was a branch name after a later submerged city. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:51/q12/p2 At Thebes, familiar local Nile channels. — Ibn Ashur makes this conditional on the capital near present-day Abu. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:51/q12/p3 The definite article substitutes for a possessive phrase. — Bursevi takes the phrase as his Nile channels. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:51/q13 What does 'flow beneath me' mean?  [تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'beneath' indicates power or a physical position at palace, throne, garden, or person.
- 43:51/q13/p1 The streams are under his command or possession. — Ata stresses ownership, Hasan command. Tabarsi, Wahidi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi, and Asad explain authority. Ibn Ashur uses subjugation, cites 66:10 and 19:24; his irrigation reading compares 12:59. Biqai says he directed canals. Qurtubi adds immediate authority and a report that the Nile stopped when he held its reins. [17 notes: THALABI(الحسن,عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن وعطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 43:51/q13/p2 Physically beneath Pharaoh's palace or elevated palace garden. — Muqatil, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and many others report the palace. Bursevi quotes al-Kashifi on four streams through a garden; Tabatabai mentions an elevated garden containing the palace. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 43:51/q13/p3 Before him through his gardens and orchards. — Tabari, Qatada, Thalabi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, and Alusi give this position. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 43:51/q13/p4 Beneath a raised throne. — Mawardi and Qurtubi report it; Abu Hayyan describes diverted Nile channels under a great throne; Alusi says it could overlook the streams. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:51/q13/p5 Around Pharaoh. — Thalabi reports Ibn Abbas; Wahidi reports al-Kalbi. [2 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 43:51/q13/p6 Below Pharaoh or through his realm. — Muqatil and Mawardi give a general below; Ibn Ashur has the Nile course from Aswan to sea under his rule. [3 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:51/q14 How is 'and these rivers flow' parsed?  [وَهَٰذِهِ ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ تَجْرِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether waw coordinates nouns, introduces a circumstance, or coordinates predicate clauses.
- 43:51/q14/p1 The rivers coordinate with the kingdom; 'flow' is circumstantial. — Nahhas allows accusative ḥāl; Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, Alusi, and Tabatabai give this construction. [11 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 43:51/q14/p2 The waw begins a circumstantial sentence: 'these' subject, 'flow' predicate. — Nahhas allows nominative predicate. Samin makes 'rivers' adjective or apposition and the clause circumstantial of lī. Other grammarians report the same alternative. [10 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 43:51/q14/p3 The clause 'these flow' coordinates with laysa's predicate. — Alusi reports this third analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 43:51/q16 What are they asked to see?  [أَفَلَا تُبْصِرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The implied object may be comparative power, material benefit, exclusive rule, or sight itself.
- 43:51/q16/p1 His wealth, greatness, and rule against Moses' weakness. — Mawardi, Qurtubi, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Baydawi, and Abu Hayyan make this contrast; Ibn Abbas adds poverty. Samin supplies dominion and grandeur as object; Tabatabai says it repeats the ownership question. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); TABATABAI]
- 43:51/q16/p2 His ability to benefit and spend on them, unlike Moses. — Mawardi and Qurtubi report this. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 43:51/q16/p3 His claim that no one should rival him. — Biqai calls this inner perception the appeal of a weakened man. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 43:51/q16/p4 The matter just mentioned, or sight and insight themselves. — Alusi allows an implied object or an intransitive verb. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 43:51/q17 What is the rhetorical force of 'do you not see'?  [أَفَلَا تُبْصِرُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question stands alone as affirmation or connects to the next one.
- 43:51/q17/p1 A negative question demands assent to his boast. — Ibn Ashur calls it affirmative prompting. Burhan and Itqan call the kingdom question boast or self-praise; Bursevi says pride. Muqatil glosses 'why do you not?' and Ibn Atiyya reports an exhortation. [7 notes: MUQATIL; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BIQAI-FULL]
- 43:51/q17/p2 It opens an alternative completed by the next 'or am I better?' — Zajjaj reports Sībawayh and al-Khalil's paired questions; Ibn Atiyya explains the balancing conjunction. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه والخليل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه)]

## 43:51/q18 What readings of 'do you see' are reported?  [تُبْصِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: The person and nūn vowel differ.
- 43:51/q18/p1 The common second-person plural addresses Pharaoh's audience. — Samin supplies dominion and grandeur as the omitted object. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 43:51/q18/p2 Isa reads kasra on the nūn with an implied yāʾ object. — Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Alusi report it; Samin writes tabṣirūnī. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى); SAMIN-DURR(عيسى); ALUSI-FULL(عيسى)]
- 43:51/q18/p3 The verb reads third-person yabṣirūn. — Abu Hayyan names Mahdi ibn al-Sufayr; Samin and Alusi vary it as Fahd ibn al-Saqr or al-Qaṣr. Samin sees a shift from address or reference to Moses' people. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مهدي بن الصفير); SAMIN-DURR(فهد بن الصقر); ALUSI-FULL(فهد بن القصر)]

## 43:51/q19 How does the next 'am I better' relate to this question?  [أَفَلَا تُبْصِرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether am connects, disconnects, means bal, receives a pause, or is redundant.
- 43:51/q19/p1 Am joins a balanced alternative: 'do you not see, or see I am better?' — Zajjaj cites Sībawayh and al-Khalil; Ibn Atiyya notes lā remains negative; Burhan acknowledges the connected parsing. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه والخليل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); BURHAN(الفراء وقوم من أهل المعاني)]
- 43:51/q19/p2 Am means bal, 'rather'. — Abu Ubayda, al-Suddi, Thalabi's majority, and a group cited by Ibn Atiyya. [4 notes: MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 43:51/q19/p3 Am is disjunctive and starts a new question. — Burhan calls this familiar and favored by the negative first question; al-Wahidi compares 52:42. Abu al-Baqa, endorsed by Zarkashi, says no prior interrogative hamza permits coordination. Burhan also reports Ibn Tahir's question to Ibn al-Rammak about Sībawayh. [5 notes: BURHAN(ابن طاهر,الواحدي); BURHAN(أبو البقاء)+]
- 43:51/q19/p4 One pauses at am and supplies a question before 'I am better'. — Thalabi and Burhan cite al-Farra and other grammarians; Ibn Atiyya reports Mujahid's pause and an omitted 'do you see or not?' [5 notes: THALABI(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس,فرقة); BURHAN(الفراء وقوم من أهل المعاني)]
- 43:51/q19/p5 Am is redundant, giving 'do you not see that I am better?' — Burhan cites Abu Zayd. [1 notes: BURHAN(أبو زيد)]

## 43:51/q20 How does Pharaoh's challenge continue after this verse?  [أَفَلَا تُبْصِرُونَ · links]
turns on: Which part of the continuing challenge is described.
- 43:51/q20/p1 He belittles Moses' social standing and speech. — Thalabi explains lowly, weak, and a stutter or tongue knot. Tabari and Ibn Atiyya say the wealth contrast implies a true God would not leave Moses poor. [3 notes: THALABI; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 43:51/q20/p2 He demands gold bracelets or accompanying angels for Moses. — Thalabi reports Mujahid that bracelets and a necklace marked leadership; angels would proceed as witnesses. Abu Amr treats asāwira/asāwir/asāwīr as dialectal plurals. Hasan, Yaqub, Abu Hatim, and Hafs read aswira; Ubay asāwir; Ibn Masud asāwīr; common asāwira is plural of plural. [4 notes: THALABI(أبو عمرو بن العلاء,الحسن ويعقوب وأبو حاتم وحفص,مجاهد)]
- 43:51/q20/p3 The next challenge has variant wording or a pause. — Ibn Atiyya reports ammā anā khayrun and Mujahid's pause; Qatada says Ubayy's codex adds 'or this one?' [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس,قتادة)]

## 43:51/q21 What does his boast show about worldly power and divinity?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether prosperity, wonders, or persuasion substantiate Pharaoh's claim.
- 43:51/q21/p1 His riches were a divine lure, and boasting of Egypt exposes his false divine pretension. — Tabari says he credited God's gifts to himself and misled others. Kashshaf and Alusi point to the gap between Egyptian rule and divinity. Ibn al-Faraj ibn al-Jawzi says he did not make the Nile flow. Study Quran says God alone sends water and removes prosperity, citing 6:6, 2:266, 15:3–4, 16:54–55, 22:48, 23:54–56, 44:25–28, 68:17–20. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الفرج بن الجوزي); STUDYQURAN]
- 43:51/q21/p2 God gives rule to virtuous and wicked alike. — Aisha says Pharaoh ruled Egypt for four hundred years. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 43:51/q21/p3 Extraordinary wonders can occur through a false claimant to divinity. — Qurtubi quotes al-Qushayri: a miracle is unnecessary to distinguish God from a false god. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)]
- 43:51/q21/p4 Unsophisticated common people could be persuaded by this boast. — Ibn Ashur explains its effect. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:51/q22 What later reactions to the boast were reported?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which anecdote or literary response is given.

## 43:51/q23 How is Pharaoh's speech linked to this sura and nearby verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link concerns Quraysh's status argument, repeated denial, or common prophetic teaching.
- 43:51/q23/p1 His status argument parallels Quraysh's preference for a wealthy recipient of revelation. — Abu Hayyan, Elmalili, Tabatabai, Islahi, and Study Quran compare the two; Tabatabai cites 43:31 and drowning, Islahi 43:31–35, and Elmalili says the story consoles the Prophet and has distinctive subtleties. Islahi sees a harmonious sura; Kuran Yolu calls the account a historical lesson in the Prophet's tawhid mission. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:51/q23/p2 The account follows mockery of signs and covenant breach after relief. — Ibn Ashur compares construction with 43:47 and betrayal with 7:135. Kuran Yolu says distress revives buried belief, which fades on relief. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:51/q23/p3 Moses' struggle affirms every prophet's shared call to tawhid. — Islahi compares Moses and Jesus and Pharaoh's arrogance with Quraysh; Kuran Yolu says all prophets share this message. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 43:51/q23/p4 Bint al-Shati explains Quraysh's resistance despite recognizing the Quran's force. — She says Arabic speakers knew its inimitability; leaders warned visitors of magic and divided families, yet some opponents submitted after hearing it. She reports Umar setting out armed against Muhammad before being redirected. She notes threatened ancestral religion and Meccan privileges, the Quran as Muhammad's sign, his known honesty, and private recognition that it was neither poetry, magic, nor soothsaying. [9 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
