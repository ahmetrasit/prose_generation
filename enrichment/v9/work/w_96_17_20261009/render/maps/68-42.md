# 68:42: 18 questions

## 68:42/q01 When is the day described?   [يَوْمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the summons requires a new legal obligation after death; others read it as rebuke.
- 68:42/q01/p1 The Day of Resurrection, especially its first or most severe hour. — Al-Tabari, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id, al-Dahhak, Ibn Kathir, and the majority identify the final gathering; some describe the first hour or transition from earthly life. The preceding promise of Paradise also points there (Ibn Kathir). [51 notes: MUQATIL(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,عكرمة,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء عن ابن عباس,عطاء عن ابن عباس,مجاهد عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,ابن عباس والحسن ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة والضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس عن العوفي,ابن عباس عن علي بن أبي طلحة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); ELMALILI(مجاهِد); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء,مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ibn ‘Abbās,Mujāhid); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(مفسرlerin çoğunluğu); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 68:42/q01/p2 The final day of earthly life or death, when the angels or Hereafter become apparent. — Abu Muslim argues that Resurrection has no ritual obligation; al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi describe death and witnessing, while al-Jishumi places the call when angels appear. Razi cites 25:22 and 56:83; Elmalılı cites 25:22 and 6:158. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ELMALILI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; ELMALILI-]
- 68:42/q01/p3 Old age, illness, or bodily incapacity in earthly life. — Al-Mawardi and Abu Muslim describe the loss of capacity before death; al-Qurtubi pictures a weakened patient summoned to prayer but unable to rise. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ELMALILI(أبو مسلم); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 68:42/q02 What governs the accusative time word yawm?  [يَوْمَ · grammar]
turns on: The unstated governing word and whether this clause continues the earlier challenge.
- 68:42/q02/p1 It depends on the preceding challenge to bring alleged partners. — The partners are challenged to appear on that day to aid or intercede; al-Qurtubi says this forbids a pause at ṣādiqīn. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI+]
- 68:42/q02/p2 It depends on an implied imperative, 'remember' or 'mention.' — This starts a new sentence depicting the day's horrors; al-Qurtubi says a pause before yawm is then possible. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI+; TABATABAI+]
- 68:42/q02/p3 It depends on a suppressed event or predicate. — Al-Zamakhshari and Razi say suppression heightens the threat; al-Samin also gives an omitted predicate. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 68:42/q02/p4 It attaches to the later humbled-eyes or engulfing-shame clause. — Abu al-Baqa allows khāshiʿa but al-Samin finds it remote; Alusi and Elmalılı mention khāshiʿa or tarhaquhum. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 68:42/q03 How is yukshafu read?  [يُكْشَفُ · readings]
turns on: The verbal subject and voice; the communal reading versus transmitted variants.
- 68:42/q03/p1 Passive third-person masculine yukshafu is the communal or majority reading. — Al-Nahhas privileges the communal reading over soundly transmitted solitary variants; Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Asim attest the raised yāʾ. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); DURR-FULL(عاصم); NAHHAS+]
- 68:42/q03/p2 Active first-person plural nakshifu, 'We uncover.' — Reported from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Masud, Abu Mijlaz, Ibn Ya'mur, al-Dahhak and Ibn Hurmuz; it makes God the agent. [10 notes: NAHHAS(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأخفش وابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود، أبو مجلز، ابن يعمر، الضحاك); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس وعبد الله); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 68:42/q03/p3 Active feminine takshifu: the Hour, Resurrection, or crisis uncovers. — Reported from Ibn Abbas, Ubayy, Hasan and others; al-Alusi finds the personification strained and its syntax problematic. [16 notes: NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس,الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب، ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس، الحسن، أبو العالية); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Q); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 68:42/q03/p4 Passive feminine tukshafu with an understood feminine subject. — Ibn Abbas is reported so; al-Samin says an implicit 'severity' is needed, while al-Alusi raises agreement objections. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 68:42/q03/p5 Active masculine yakshifu with God as subject. — Reports attribute this to Ibn Masud, Ibn Abi Abla, Asim al-Jahdari and Abu al-Jawza; the verb has fatḥa on yāʾ and kasra on shīn. [7 notes: NAHHAS(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي عبلة، عاصم الجحدري، أبو الجوزاء); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مسعود وابن أبي عبلة); DURR-FULL(ابن منده); ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:42/q03/p6 A form from akshafa denotes entering a state of exposure. — Reported with tāʾ ḍamma and shīn kasra, or an intransitive yāʾ form; lexicographers compare the exposed lip of an akshaf man. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:42/q03/p7 Ibn Kathir is reported to read the alif of sāq with hamza. — Ibn Khalawayh says all other readers agree on the alif form. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(ابن كثير)]

## 68:42/q04 What grammatical role and lexical range do the words about the sāq have?  [يُكْشَفُ عَن سَاقٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the words are parsed literally, as a passive construction, or as a figurative phrase.
- 68:42/q04/p1 In the passive reading, ʿan sāq functions as the deputy subject. — Al-Samin and Bursevi state this iʿrāb; al-Alusi objects to some feminine alternatives because of gender agreement and preposition deletion. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 68:42/q04/p2 Sāq is feminine and literally names a supporting leg, then a plant stem or trunk. — Al-Nahhas notes its feminine diminutive takes tāʾ; Jishumi describes the animal limb supporting the body and a tree's trunk; Ibn al-Jawzi and Elmalılı give the literal and extended senses. [4 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ELMALILI]
- 68:42/q04/p3 Kashf means removing a covering. — Jishumi gives the lexical sense; al-Rabi and Elmalılı apply it to lifting a veil. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TAB-FULL(الربيع); ELMALILI(Rebî‘ b. Enes)]

## 68:42/q06 What image gives the idiom its force?  [يُكْشَفُ عَن سَاقٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Different accounts of the concrete scene behind the established figurative usage.
- 68:42/q06/p1 Someone facing hard work bares the shin or gathers up clothing to exert effort. — Ibn Qutayba, Thalabi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Ubayda, Qurtubi and others cite Duraid, Hudhali, war and drought verse. Jishumi notes repeated use made the image proverbial; Elmalılı includes readiness and urgency. - 68:42/q06/p2 Panicked people, especially modest women fleeing defeat, lift their garments and expose their legs. — Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Alusi, Bursevi, Ibn Ashur and Islahi explain this scene; Ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat's poem and Anas's Uhud account illustrate exposed legs under urgent conditions. ## 68:42/q07 What does the indefinite sāq contribute?  [سَاقٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indefiniteness marks an extraordinary crisis, disallows a specified limb, or exalts a revealed foundation.
- 68:42/q07/p1 It magnifies an unfamiliar, unprecedented terror. — Zamakhshari compares the unknown terror of 54:6; Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi and Elmalılı stress awe or magnitude. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Zemahşerî)]
- 68:42/q07/p2 It is not possessively specified, weakening identification with a familiar divine limb. — Zamakhshari, Razi, Nasafi and Elmalılı use the indefinite form against a corporeal referent; Razi asks how an uncovered leg would identify God. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(المشبهة); ELMALILI(Müşebbihe)]
- 68:42/q07/p3 On the foundation reading, it conveys exaltation. — Bursevi and Alusi mention this alternative, while Alusi notes that a parabolic image need not be parsed word by word. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 68:42/q08 How do commentators treat a bodily reading of God's sāq?  [سَاقٍۢ · theology]
turns on: The force of divine possessive reports, corporeality proofs, Arabic figurative usage, and treatment of ambiguous attributes.
- 68:42/q08/p1 A literal divine bodily limb is rejected; the expression is read figuratively or its modality left to God. — Al-Mawardi and Qurtubi deny parts or cover; Razi argues bodies are finite, originated and contingent; Zamakhshari and Nasafi cite rhetoric and the indefinite noun. Sa'id ibn Jubayr objects forcefully. Elmalılı invokes 42:11, while saying the reports' actual mode remains unknown. Razi insists figurative interpretation needs decisive proof, warning from 2:25 and 22:77 against unrestricted metaphor. [26 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(المشبهة); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ELMALILI(Müşebbihe,Sa‘îd b. Cübeyr); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI+]
- 68:42/q08/p2 The wording and transmitted reports are accepted as an ambiguous divine attribute or imaginal manifestation. — Some exegetes and the Salaf leave the report among ambiguous texts; esoteric Sufis take the visual manifestation in an imaginal mode. Abu Ubayda, quoted by Zamakhshari, portrays Muqatil and Jahm as opposing extremes. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(المشبهة); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ALUSI-FULL(أرباب الباطن من الصوفية,ابن مسعود,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(فريق من المفسرين); ELMALILI-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 68:42/q09 What sequence do the reports place around the disclosure?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted scene and its variants, plus the reliability assigned to particular transmissions.
- 68:42/q09/p1 After a long gathering, communities follow their claimed objects of worship; monotheists await and recognize God at the sign, then sincere believers prostrate while hypocrites fail. — Ibn Masud reports forty years standing; Abu Hurayra describes settled wrongs, likenesses of Ezra and Jesus, and light and bridge crossing; Abu Sa'id compares vision to sun and moon. Some versions have an initial unrecognized manifestation. Al-Qurtubi says the central report is established in Sahih Muslim, Ibn Kathir in both Sahihs. Variants add Paradise, Fire, intercession, and different speeds across the bridge. [44 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود); ZAJJAJ(ابن مسعود); IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي سعيد الخدري); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(أبو موسى,الفناري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد,ابن مسعود); ELMALILI(أبو سعيد,ابن مسعود); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,عبد الله بن مسعود); STUDYQURAN(ḥadīth)]
- 68:42/q09/p2 The unveiling is a great light at which people fall in prostration. — Abu Musa transmits this wording; al-Bayhaqi grades the report weak and Ibn Kathir notes an unidentified transmitter. - 68:42/q09/p3 The disclosure is the lifting of a veil. — Al-Rabi reports this; the Abu Musa scene says the monotheists recognize God when the veil lifts. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع); DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(أبو موسى)]
- 68:42/q09/p4 The reports differ in detail and are assessed differently. — Ibn Atiyya notes additions and omissions. Abu Bardah swears to the Abu Musa transmission, and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz praises it; al-Qurtubi cites Sahih Muslim. Ibn Kathir and Suyuti note an anonymous transmitter in the light report, and Ibn Ashur dismisses other weak reports; Daruqutni and Hakim call the Ibn Masud report sound. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز); BURSEVI(عمر بن عبد العزيز); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي سعيد الخدري); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Dârekutnî ve Hâkim); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:42/q10 Who are summoned to prostrate?  [وَيُدْعَوْنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun targets hypocrites specifically or includes unbelievers and the broader gathering.
- 68:42/q10/p1 Hypocrites among Muslims are the specific people exposed by the test. — Zajjaj and Ibn Jawzi name them; Ibn Ashur argues they are raised among Muslims and distinguishes them from earlier polytheists by the later statement that they were called while safe. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL(عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 68:42/q10/p2 Disbelievers and hypocrites alike are addressed. — Bukhari-type reports contrast sincere believers with show worshippers; Baghawi, Wahidi, Bursevi and others explicitly include both unbelievers and hypocrites. [14 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود,جميع المفسرين,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 68:42/q10/p3 The gathered creatures are summoned; true believers succeed, others fail. — Wahidi reports an exegetical consensus that creatures prostrate once but unbelievers and hypocrites cannot afterward; reports distinguish willing worship from ostentation. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(جميع المفسرين); MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(ḥadīth)]

## 68:42/q11 Who issues or conveys the summons?  [وَيُدْعَوْنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the passive leaves a divine, angelic, human, visual, or inward caller implied.
- 68:42/q11/p1 God calls them, directly or through an angel. — Al-Alusi says the apparent caller is God or an angel; Ibn Ashur suggests angels assigned to the gathering, citing 54:6–8. Kur'an Yolu says God commissions people. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:42/q11/p2 Other people are inspired to call them. — Ibn Ashur compares people calling one another to seek intercession. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:42/q11/p3 The sight of believers prostrating itself summons them to imitate. — Al-Alusi gives this alternative, and Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan explain that they want to follow but are unable. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 68:42/q11/p4 The realm of spirits calls them to inward submission. — Qashani says dark dispositions and bodily veils prevent receiving divine lights. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 68:42/q12 Why are they called to prostrate?  [وَيُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the scene is a post-resurrection test and rebuke, a legal command, an attempt at rescue, or worldly prayer.
- 68:42/q12/p1 The summons is rebuke and humiliation for refusing prostration while able in earthly life. — Al-Nahhas, Jishumi, Razi, Nasafi, Biqai, Bursevi and others deny a new legal duty in the afterlife. Their failed attempt heightens regret; Qatada says believers' prostration humiliates hypocrites. Verse 43 recalls the earlier safe summons. Ibn Atiyya reports other scholars who likewise call it rebuke. [24 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(النقاش); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء)]
- 68:42/q12/p2 It is a binding command, in the Resurrection or in worldly life. — Al-Mawardi offers both temporal settings; Biqai says legal responsibility ends only once both groups reach their abodes. Abu Muslim objects that there is no ritual duty after death. Al-Fanari gives a distinct case: people on the Heights with equal scales complete a remaining afterlife duty, tipping their balance toward Paradise. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الفناري); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 68:42/q12/p3 They seek prostration as supplication and submission to escape the gathering's terror. — Tabrisi says fear drives them to prostrate though it no longer benefits; Ibn Ashur treats the act as plea for rescue; Asad describes voluntary, glad humility. [4 notes: TABRISI(قول آخر); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:42/q12/p4 They want to imitate believers whom they see prostrating. — Ibn Atiyya grounds this in the absence of legal obligation in the afterlife; Abu Hayyan reports the same explanation. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 68:42/q12/p5 The call concerns the appointed prayers during earthly life. — Ka'b swears the verses were revealed about five obligatory prayers; Baydawi describes prayer times on a death reading; Study Quran reads the prostration in both verses as worldly prayer. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(كعب الحبر); BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:42/q12/p6 The call spiritually invites submission and reception of divine light. — Qashani describes a realm-of-spirits summons, blocked by dark bodily habits. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 68:42/q13 Why can they not prostrate?  [فَلَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether inability is bodily punishment, removed capacity, missed opportunity, or an exposed inner disposition.
- 68:42/q13/p1 Their backs or limbs become a single rigid bone, plate, horn, or skewer, so they cannot bend and may fall backward. — Ibn Masud, Ibn Abbas, Muqatil and Prophetic reports give these physical images; Biqai calls the hadith authentic. Variations describe one vertebra, cattle horns, spits, and a flat board; Wahidi says the failure follows neglect or ostentation in life. [56 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود); ZAJJAJ(ابن مسعود); IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); NAHHAS; THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود,جميع المفسرين,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(المشبهة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي سعيد الخدري); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود,الفناري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ELMALILI(أبو سعيد,ابن مسعود); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,عبد الله بن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN(ḥadīth)]
- 68:42/q13/p2 God removes their effective capacity at that moment. — Razi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Ibn Ashur state that ability is withheld as punishment, intensifying grief; Biqai emphasizes their still wanting to submit. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:42/q13/p3 The useful time for prostration and deeds has passed. — Al-Nahhas, Ibn Bahr, Tabrisi and Baydawi say an afterlife prostration cannot make up earthly neglect; on the death reading the patient cannot rise. [5 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI(قول آخر); BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 68:42/q13/p4 Their ingrained arrogance or dark inner dispositions prevent inward submission. — Tabatabai says arrogance became fixed and hidden reality is exposed, citing 86:9; Qashani blames bodily veils that destroyed original readiness. [2 notes: TABATABAI; QASHANI]
- 68:42/q13/p5 They lacked sincere worldly worship and are exposed by the test. — Abu Sa'id contrasts willing worship with fear or show; Wahidi says unbelievers had never prostrated; Biqai says people rise in the state in which they died. [4 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي سعيد الخدري)]

## 68:42/q14 What theological inference is drawn from their inability?  [فَلَا يَسْتَطِيعُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether later inability proves earlier capacity and whether an impossible assignment is admissible.
- 68:42/q14/p1 Their later inability implies that disbelievers possessed worldly capacity to believe. — Al-Jubbai argues that specifying inability in the Hereafter implies its absence was not true in worldly life. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)]
- 68:42/q14/p2 That inference fails; capacity accompanies the act rather than preceding it. — Ibn Atiyya invokes Ash'ari doctrine; Razi replies using divine foreknowledge, and Abu Hayyan denies the temporal inference. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأشعري); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)-]
- 68:42/q14/p3 A task beyond one's capacity may be assigned, though no intrinsically irrational task can. — Bursevi reports Ash'ari permission and Mu'tazili prohibition, and says no one permits ordering the annihilation of the eternal. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الأشاعرة)]

## 68:42/q15 How does the verse connect with the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The transition from the partners challenge, the contrast with verse 43, and the sūra's wider symbolism.
- 68:42/q15/p1 It brings the challenge to alleged partners into Judgment, where their claim to help or intercede fails. — Zajjaj, Wahidi, Razi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai and Islahi connect the preceding challenge and claimed felicity to exposure at the gathering; Elmalılı reads warning for criminals and glad tidings for believers. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 68:42/q15/p2 The contrast with being called while safe explains why they cannot respond now. — Biqai finds iḥtibāk: inability here implies former ability, and former safety implies present unsafety. Study Quran distinguishes Judgment's prostration from the worldly call in verse 43. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ZAJJAJ]
- 68:42/q15/p3 The unveiling distinguishes outward prostration from inward devotion. — Biqai says visible prostration expresses submission and the devotion within; Elmalılı links it to the manifestation of truth and judgment. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 68:42/q15/p4 The imagery gives a symbolic echo of the sūra's opening nūn. — Elmalılı makes this comparison. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 68:42/q16 What inward reading is given?  [* · inward]
- 68:42/q16/p1 The terrifying unveiled ordeal and spiritual summons reveal how mercy, justice, and prior inward habits determine response. — Ja'far says divine care spares a believer the Bridge and Judgment terrors, while one met by justice has a stone-like back; one saint's meeting another unveils hardships. Qashani describes bodily habits removed and dark veils blocking the realm-of-spirits summons. [4 notes: SULAMI(جعفر); QASHANI]

## 68:42/q17 How do broader linguistic notes illuminate this figurative expression?  [يُكْشَفُ عَن سَاقٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: How general Arabic rhetoric and grammar bear on reading the verse's compact image.
- 68:42/q17/p1 Arabic transfers words by association, resemblance, and context, and figures can be stronger than direct wording. — Ibn Qutayba offers rain called sky, vegetation anwa', earth or clouds 'laughing,' and hardship as 'sweat'; Jurjani separates lexical transfer from agency attribution, describes representation, sensory simile and degrees of metaphor; Zarkashi compares apt 'wing of humility' with dubious 'water of blame' and cites war uncovering a shin. Razi says metaphor needs proof before displacing a literal scriptural sense. [36 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الأعشى,الشاعر,رؤية بن العجاج); JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-DALAIL(المتعلقون بأمر اللفظ); BURHAN(الإمام فخر الدين,بعضهم); RAZI-FULL]
- 68:42/q17/p2 The Qur'an's language requires careful eloquence, without treating any element as meaningless surplus. — Baqqillani contrasts its inimitability with Imru al-Qays and ordinary saj', and rejects proposed weaknesses of Qur'anic variation; Kashshaf demands semantic and rhetorical expertise; Zarkashi and Suyuti describe the caution and skills needed for ambiguous passages. [32 notes: BAQILLANI(الأصمعي,الذين يتعصبون له); KASHSHAF-FULL; BURHAN(أبو سليمان,المحققون,داود الظاهري); ITQAN(العلماء,غيره); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو سليمان الخطابي)]
- 68:42/q17/p3 Arabic vocative particles can mark distance, emphasis, or direct presence and are sometimes omitted. — Sibawayh's five forms and the rare poetic omission of ya show that expression depends on context; his examples include lamentation and imploring. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 68:42/q18 What wider transmission and eschatological context accompanies the reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: How the sources frame the transmitted verse scene within broader reports.
- 68:42/q18/p1 The judgment account is set after the final trials, resurrection, and the sorting of worshippers, then proceeds through intercession and the bridge. — Ibn Masud narrates Dajjal, Jesus, Gog and Magog, the cold wind, trumpet, bodily regrowth, false worshippers' fate, graded crossing and intercession; Ka'b describes Hell's terror. These accounts cite 21:96, 30:48, 18:100, 37:24, 17:79, 74:42 and 74:46. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,كعب)]
- 68:42/q18/p2 The Qur'anic codex was standardized after reading disputes under Uthman. — Al-Tabari's background reports describe Syrian-Iraqi disputes, Hafsah's sheets, provincial copies, erasure of other codices, and Qur'anic materials before compilation. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(أنس بن مالك,الزهري,عثمان بن عفان)]
