# 19:74: 15 questions

## 19:74/q01 What force does 'how many' have here?  [وَكَمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether كم reports abundance or poses a question.
- 19:74/q01/p1 It expresses the abundance of destroyed peoples. — Al-Samīn says كم must be fronted; al-Ālūsī prefers the exclamatory or informative sense. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it an exclamatory report. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 19:74/q01/p2 It may be interrogative. — Al-Ālūsī also reports this reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:74/q02 How do 'how many' and 'of a generation' work syntactically?  [وَكَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا مِّن قَرْنٍ · grammar]
- 19:74/q02/p1 كم is the object of أهلكنا, and من قرن specifies it. — Al-Nasafī calls من قرن an accusative modifier, noting that قرنا without من would be clearer. Al-Samīn says كم is obligatorily fronted. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:74/q03 What is a qarn?  [قَرْنٍ · meaning]
- 19:74/q03/p1 A people, nation, or civilization of one era relative to successors. — Al-Kāshifī says a group gathered at one time. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī relate the term to the projecting or preceding horn. Asad cites 19:98. [12 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:74/q04 How long is a qarn said to last?  [قَرْنٍ · meaning]
turns on: The reported estimates differ.
- 19:74/q04/p1 One hundred years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this estimate; al-Ālūsī notes disagreement. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:74/q04/p2 Eighty years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this estimate; al-Ālūsī notes disagreement. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:74/q04/p3 Seventy years. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this estimate; al-Ālūsī notes disagreement. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:74/q05 Who are the people compared with the former generations?  [قَبْلَهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun denotes the unbelievers generally or al-Naḍr's group.
- 19:74/q05/p1 The boastful unbelievers, especially Quraysh or Mecca's people. — Muqātil names Mecca's people; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Arab polytheists and an intervening address to the Prophet. Al-Biqāʿī says they saw the earlier peoples' homes and traces. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:74/q05/p2 Al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith and his associates. — Al-Ṭabrisī says they groomed their hair, wore silk, and boasted of their appearance. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 19:74/q06 What does 'they were better' modify?  [هُمْ أَحْسَنُ · grammar]
turns on: The attachment of the clause and whether كم can be qualified.
- 19:74/q06/p1 It is an accusative adjective of كم. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū al-Baqāʾ say أحسن alone would be accusative. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn object that كم cannot take an adjective. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 19:74/q06/p2 It modifies قرن with plural agreement by collective sense. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite the Basran rule on كم. Al-Samīn calls the adjective genitive and cites 54:44 and 36:32; al-Ālūsī says singular agreement is possible. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 19:74/q06/p3 The clause is circumstantial. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this parse. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 19:74/q07 What does athāth denote?  [أَثَٰثًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is general property, household goods, a particular kind of good, or appearance.
- 19:74/q07/p1 Household furnishings or possessions, often decorative. — Mujāhid includes adornment; al-Layth speaks of kinds of household goods. Al-Aṣamm includes furniture and clothes. Ibn ʿĀshūr emphasizes decoration. Al-Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues from Quranic use, especially 16:80, for abundant furnishings as distinct from wider matāʿ. Al-Wāḥidī adduces thick plants and dense hair, with a poem, for the root. [41 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الحسن,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث,مجاهد); JISHUMI(ابن عباس,الأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,الأزهري,الفراء)]
- 19:74/q07/p2 Property or wealth broadly, including livestock and slaves in one report. — Abū Zayd includes livestock, slaves and household goods. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also includes livestock. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī and Qatāda give wealth. Asad stresses abundance; al-Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes the breadth as usual usage. [23 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد,ابن عباس والسدي,قتادة); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,الفراء)]
- 19:74/q07/p3 Clothing or things worn and spread out. — Muqātil and al-Ḥasan say clothing; al-Harawī restricts it to worn and spread things. [6 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الهروي)]
- 19:74/q07/p4 Rugs, carpets, or fine bedding. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says rugs and carpets; al-Kashshāf fine bedding; al-Nasafī new carpets. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 19:74/q07/p5 New or fine furnishings, contrasted with worn khirthī. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī cite al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī al-Ṭūsī's poem about worn goods. Al-Bayḍāwī allows the restriction. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن بن علي الطوسي)]
- 19:74/q07/p6 Appearance or bearing. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with this gloss; the paired Muqātil gloss is clothing. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 19:74/q07/p7 Unseen or useful goods paired with visible ornaments or blessings. — Al-Māwardī reports several paired explanations: new clothing and blessings, unseen and visible possessions, wealth and appearance, useful goods and ornaments. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 19:74/q08 Does athāth have a singular?  [أَثَٰثًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether athātha is its singular.
- 19:74/q08/p1 It is a collective without a singular of the same form. — Al-Farrāʾ compares matāʿ and gives alternate plural forms ātha and athat. Some lexicographers say it denotes a large quantity. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabarī, al-Jishumī and al-Bint al-Shāṭiʾ transmit the view. [10 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء)]
- 19:74/q08/p2 It has singular athātha. — Al-Aḥmar compares ḥamām/ḥamāma and saḥāb; Abū Zayd and al-Aṣamm also provide a singular. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(الأحمر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد); JISHUMI(الأصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(خلف الأحمر); TABRISI]

## 19:74/q09 What does the final word mean?  [وَرِءْيًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The proposed roots of sight, watering, adornment, judgment, drink or display.
- 19:74/q09/p1 Visible appearance, beauty, form, dress or display of goods. — Mujāhid says what people see. Ibn ʿAbbās specifies color and beauty; al-Ḥasan forms. Al-Ṭabarī cites ʿAlqama's poem for displayed goods, explaining ṣawwān as a clothes chest. Al-Jawharī and Abū ʿUbayda cite poetry for conspicuous dress. Al-Fārisī distinguishes the thing seen from raʾy and ruʾya and compares 3:13. Al-Bint al-Shāṭiʾ includes witnessed or imagined scenes and rereads the disputed poem accordingly. [42 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,مجاهد); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); JISHUMI(ابن عباس,الأصم وأبو علي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الجوهري); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الجمهور,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن البصري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,الطبري); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 19:74/q09/p2 Watered luxuriance, freshness, or fullness visible in appearance. — Yaḥyā links rain with crops and livestock. Al-Naḥḥās describes skin replete with blessing. Abū ʿAlī treats rayy as verbal noun or passive participle. Mundhir reports a view that their lands were well irrigated and abundant. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL(منذر بن سعيد عن بعض أهل العلم); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي,الزهري وأبو جعفر وشيبة وطلحة وأيوب وابن سعدان وابن ذكوان وقالون); SAMIN-DURR(قالون وابن ذكوان); ALUSI-FULL(الزهري وأبو جعفر وشيبة وطلحة وأيوب وابن سعدان وابن ذكوان وقالون); IBNASHUR-FULL(قالون عن نافع، وابن ذكوان عن ابن عامر)]
- 19:74/q09/p3 Fine attire or gathered adornments under the zāy reading. — Al-Farrāʾ cites zayyaytu al-jāriya. Ibn Jinnī derives ziyy from zawaytu, citing the Prophet's 'earth gathered' expression and al-Aʿshā's poetry. Al-Qurṭubī repeats the expression; al-Ālūsī cites a poem on attractive furnishings. [14 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; THALABI(أبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وأبي بن كعب وسعيد بن جبير والأعسم المكي ويزيد البربري); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، ابن جبير، يزيد البربري، الأعسم المكي); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس وابن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير ويزيد البربري والأعصم المكي,الثقافي)]
- 19:74/q09/p4 Wealth, standing and residence. — Al-Ḥasan is reported to say wealth; Qatāda adds standing and residence. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة)]
- 19:74/q09/p5 Good judgment concerning property. — Al-Ṭabarī reports this derivation from rawiya for unhamzated riyyan. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 19:74/q09/p6 Drink or satiation. — The Ibn al-Azraq exchange and al-Itqān cite a poem; al-Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the sense is outside the seven canonical readings, lacks Arabic support, and the poem is better read as appearance. [2 notes: ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 19:74/q09/p7 Mutual display of beauty. — Al-Yazīdī's riyāʾ is derived from showing off to one another. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(اليزيدي); SAMIN-DURR(اليزيدي); ALUSI-FULL(اليزيدي)]

## 19:74/q10 How is the final word read?  [وَرِءْيًۭا · readings]
turns on: The consonants, placement of hamza and derivation differ.
- 19:74/q10/p1 Riʾyan with hamza before yāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī; Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir vary. Kufans and Abū ʿAmr preserve raʾaytu. Al-Ṭabarī prefers the sight derivation by consensus of interpreters. Ibn al-Jazarī explains omission of hamza's image in the script by adjacent y-shaped letters, rejecting explanation from kasra alone. Al-Samīn says Abū ʿAmr avoids confusion with rayy. [26 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID; NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أشهب عن نافع,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ورش عن نافع وابن ذكوان عن ابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الطبري); QIRAAT-ER; BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر القراء); TAB-FULL+]
- 19:74/q10/p2 Riyyan with doubled yāʾ and no hamza. — Medinan readers and transmissions of Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir use it. Hamza may become yāʾ after kasra and assimilate, or rayy may mean watered prosperity. Al-Naḥḥās favors agreement with unhamzated endings; al-Ṭabarī accepts it with the sight meaning. The second canonical reading keeps that root and sense. [30 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID; NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر وأبو جعفر ونافع غير ورش); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأهل المدينة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المدينة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزهري وأبو جعفر وشيبة وطلحة وأيوب وابن سعدان وابن ذكوان وقالون); SAMIN-DURR(قالون وابن ذكوان); ALUSI-FULL(الزهري وأبو جعفر وشيبة وطلحة وأيوب وابن سعدان وابن ذكوان وقالون); IBNASHUR-FULL(قالون عن نافع، وابن ذكوان عن ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER; NAHHAS+]
- 19:74/q10/p3 A light single-yāʾ form without hamza. — Ṭalḥa and a report from Ibn ʿAbbās transmit it. Ibn Jinnī proposes dropping a yāʾ or moving hamza's vowel before deleting it. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī defend it; al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī suspect error or weakness. Al-Rāzī reports deletion of hamza from ريئا after shifting its vowel to the preceding yāʾ. [12 notes: NAHHAS; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وطلحة); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي,بعض النحويين); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس فيما روى عنه طلحة)+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)+; SAMIN-DURR+; NAHHAS-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف)-]
- 19:74/q10/p4 Riyʾan with yāʾ before final hamza. — Abū Bakr via ʿĀṣim and Ḥumayd transmit it. Al-Zajjāj deems it linguistically possible though unreported to him. Kathīr's rāʾanī and another poem witness inversion; Sībawayh attests rāʾ. Al-Samīn doubts metathesis but allows derivation from rāʾahu yarāhu. - 19:74/q10/p5 Ziyyan or zayyan with zāy. — Ubayy, Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Yazīd al-Barbarī, al-Aʿṣam and others are named. It means appearance, dress or gathered adornment. Al-Ṭabarī rejects its departure from the reciters' reading but accepts the interpretation. - 19:74/q10/p6 Riyāʾ from mutual display. — Al-Yazīdī transmits it; al-Samīn says its first hamza is eased to yāʾ. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(اليزيدي); SAMIN-DURR(اليزيدي); ALUSI-FULL(اليزيدي)]

## 19:74/q11 How is Hamza's hamza treated at pause?  [وَرِءْيًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether transmission, analogy, script or possible confusion with rayy governs the pause.
- 19:74/q11/p1 Replace hamza with yāʾ and articulate both yāʾs clearly. — Al-Kāfī and al-Tabṣira prefer this; al-Tabṣira calls it established. Ibn al-Jazarī prefers it as analogical and common. For related ruʾyā readers agree on wāw substitution after ḍamma. [6 notes: NASHR(الهذلي,صاحب التيسير); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); NASHR(صاحب الكافي وصاحب التبصرة)+]
- 19:74/q11/p2 Replace hamza with yāʾ and assimilate. — Al-Tadhkira and al-Dānī prefer explicit transmission from Ḥamza and agreement with the script; al-ʿUnwān gives only this. Al-Taysīr and al-Shāṭibī give both. Al-Hudhalī and others allow related Abū Jaʿfar assimilation in ruʾyā, while Ibn Shurayḥ weakens it. [6 notes: NASHR(الهذلي,صاحب التيسير); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); NASHR(صاحب التذكرة والداني)+]
- 19:74/q11/p3 Realize hamza fully to prevent a meaning change. — Al-Tadhkira proposes this; Ibn al-Jazarī rejects it as against transmitted performance. [0 notes: NASHR-]
- 19:74/q11/p4 Delete hamza and stop on one light yāʾ. — Al-Fārisī reports this from the script; Ibn al-Jazarī deems it invalid and impermissible. [0 notes: NASHR(الفارسي)-]

## 19:74/q12 What happened to the earlier peoples and their advantages?  [وَكَمْ أَهْلَكْنَا قَبْلَهُم مِّن قَرْنٍ هُمْ أَحْسَنُ أَثَٰثًۭا وَرِءْيًۭا · interpretation]
- 19:74/q12/p1 Many earlier peoples had better goods and looks, yet God destroyed them and their resources. — Muqātil says wealth and bodies were destroyed; al-Ṭabarī says property was destroyed and appearance changed; Qatāda adds standing and residence before disfigurement. Al-Biqāʿī says unprefixed 'before' spans all earlier generations and their visible traces. Ibn ʿĀshūr frames an address to the Prophet. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 19:74/q13 What lesson or warning follows from their destruction?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:74/q13/p1 Wealth, beauty and status neither establish divine favor nor protect deniers from punishment. — Al-Rāzī also rejects the inference that God's beloved cannot suffer grief. Al-Jishumī extends the lesson to leadership at Resurrection. Al-Qurṭubī allows punishment here or later. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts short worldly life with eternity. Al-Kāshifī's couplet gives the lesson. Kuranyolu names ʿĀd and Thamūd, their cultivation and thriving countries. Study Quran cites destruction parallels at 6:6, 7:4, 17:17, 19:98, 21:11, 22:45, 28:58, 38:3, 44:37, 47:13 and 50:36. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 19:74/q14 How does the verse answer the previous boast and connect to other verses?  [* · links]
- 19:74/q14/p1 It rebuts the 19:73 boast of superior station and company by recalling peoples with greater worldly advantages. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Pharaoh's Egyptian kingdom and rivers in 43:51–55, then his drowning. Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān connect 6:6 and 16:80. Study Quran cites further destruction parallels; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the threat interrupts before the reply. [7 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 19:74/q15 What inward lesson is drawn?  [* · inward]
- 19:74/q15/p1 Seekers humble and discipline themselves; love of wealth, status and desire ruined earlier deniers. — Al-Bursawī transmits the Najmī taʾwīl contrasting disciplined seekers with indulgent deniers. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
