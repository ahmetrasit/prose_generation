# 70:15: 14 questions

## 70:15/q01 What does kallā mean here, and how does it divide the speech?  [كَلَّآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kallā denies the ransom hope or affirms the following statement, and where the pause falls.
- 70:15/q01/p1 It rebukes the criminal's hope of ransom: no ransom will save him, and the Fire is then announced. — Al-Zajjāj calls it a rebuke and warning that none return. Muqātil, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran stress that ransom cannot rescue him. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the new report about a layer of Hell magnifies its horror; al-Baghawī likewise starts a new statement. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wish may be unspoken or voiced, cites 78:40 and 4:42, and calls the next sentence an explanatory resumption. Al-Bursawī adds that sin has darkened the criminal's condition; the authorities and the Sajāwandī stop sign favor a full pause at kallā. Al-Jishumī reports a derivation from kadhā lā, shortened and doubled for emphasis. Al-Qurṭubī also permits the denial, with the preceding sentence closing at yunjīhi. Al-Iṣlāḥī sets the wish against collapsing family and tribal bonds. [22 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(القرطبي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI(المحققون)+]
- 70:15/q01/p2 It means 'indeed' or 'truly,' introducing an affirmation about the Fire. — Al-Jishumī reports 'indeed' as an alternative. Al-Qurṭubī permits this sense; al-Bursawī's report of him places the close at yunjīhi and begins the next sentence with kallā. Study Quran reports the rendering 'Truly it is a churning fire.' Al-Bursawī says the authorities and the Sajāwandī stop sign favor the rebuke instead. [4 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(القرطبي); STUDYQURAN(Q); BURSEVI(المحققون)-]

## 70:15/q02 What does the pronoun in innahā refer to?  [إِنَّهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the antecedent is an implied Fire, the visible sight of it, an implicit matter, or an indefinite item explained afterward; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn dispute what can explain the indefinite pronoun.
- 70:15/q02/p1 It refers to Hell or the Fire, understood from the preceding punishment. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī infer the Fire from the prior mention of punishment; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise identify the Fire or Hell. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it burns, strips extremities and pursues criminals, so ransom cannot turn it aside. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 70:15/q02/p2 It is a pronoun of the matter or story, explained by the following statement. — Al-Rāzī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report or hold this analysis. Al-Samīn says a pronoun of shaʾn or qiṣṣa can be explained by the full clause laẓā nazzāʿa. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 70:15/q02/p3 It is an indefinite pronoun whose meaning is supplied by a following word. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn give this alternative. Al-Samīn replies to the Shaykh's objection that a single word, either laẓā or nazzāʿa, must explain it; he rejects explaining this kind of pronoun by the whole clause, which would make it the story-pronoun analysis. Abū Ḥayyān challenges al-Zamakhsharī's view because a single word does not explain it here. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 70:15/q02/p4 It points to the sight of Hell that the criminal sees before him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the visible scene before the criminal as the pronoun's referent. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:15/q02/p5 It points to the people, as its predicate explains. — Al-Nasafī gives this alternative alongside the Fire and the implicit matter pronoun. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 70:15/q03 How are innahā, laẓā and the following nazzāʿa construed?  [إِنَّهَا لَظَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: The role of the initial pronoun and whether the nominative nazzāʿa is another predicate, an omitted-pronoun predicate, a new subject, or the predicate of laẓā.
- 70:15/q03/p1 Laẓā is the nominative predicate of inna, with nazzāʿa a second predicate. — Al-Jishumī and al-Samīn state this analysis; al-Ālūsī explicitly calls laẓā inna's predicate. Sībawayh cites 70:15–16 in his discussion of raised predicates, and Abū Ḥayyān notes variation of analysis on the majority nominative reading. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 70:15/q03/p2 Laẓā is inna's predicate and nazzāʿa is the predicate of an omitted hiya. — Al-Samīn gives this as another way to construe the nominative nazzāʿa when the pronoun refers to the Fire. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 70:15/q03/p3 Laẓā is inna's predicate, while nominative nazzāʿa begins a new subject construction. — Al-Akhfash expressly gives this alternative nominative analysis. [1 notes: AKHFASH]
- 70:15/q03/p4 Laẓā is an apposition to the pronoun and nazzāʿa is inna's predicate. — Al-Samīn gives this alternative if the pronoun refers to the Fire. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 70:15/q03/p5 Laẓā is a subject and nazzāʿa its predicate; their clause serves as inna's predicate. — Al-Jishumī reports a support pronoun with laẓā as subject. Al-Samīn gives the clause as predicate of inna when the pronoun is of the story or indefinite type, though he later restricts whole-clause explanation to a story or matter pronoun. [3 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 70:15/q04 Why is the pronoun feminine?  [إِنَّهَا · grammar]
- 70:15/q04/p1 It agrees with laẓā, whose final alif marks it as feminine. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the following descriptions add further predicates of it. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 70:15/q05 What does Laẓā denote here?  [لَظَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term names all of Hell, a particular level, or flame itself; the level reports specify second, eighth, or no number.
- 70:15/q05/p1 It is a proper name of Hell or the Fire as a whole. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī citing al-Farrāʾ, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran identify it as a name for Hell or Fire. Several connect the name to blazing or intense heat; al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī cite the blazing fire of 92:14. Al-Māwardī presents the naming as possible, and Abū Ḥayyān and others also allow a level-specific reading. [21 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 70:15/q05/p2 It denotes a pure or clear flame, emphasizing the intensity of the burning. — Al-Layth, as quoted by al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī, defines a pure or clear flame. Al-Zamakhsharī and those reported by Ibn al-Jawzī allow the ordinary flame sense instead of a proper name. Al-Bursawī calls it pure and smokeless; al-Ālūsī calls the whole fire a flame by hyperbole. Asad describes the punishment as a raging flame. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الليث); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 70:15/q05/p3 It is the second level of Hell. — Al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī report this as another view; al-Baghawī says it is named for its blazing. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī also allow the second level. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 70:15/q05/p4 It is a layer of Hell, without a specified number. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the report about this layer magnifies its horror. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 70:15/q05/p5 It is the eighth level of Hell. — Al-Māwardī reports this from al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك)]

## 70:15/q06 From what is the name Laẓā derived?  [لَظَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name comes from blazing or from a proposed older form referring to lasting punishment.
- 70:15/q06/p1 It comes from words for a fire's blazing, ignition or intense burning. — Al-Wāḥidī gives laẓiyat al-nāru talazzā laẓan and talaẓẓat talaẓẓiyan, and cites 92:14. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī derive the name from blazing; Ibn al-Anbārī, quoted by Ibn al-Jawzī, cites talaẓẓā and a verse about unrelenting fire. Al-Layth, quoted by al-Rāzī, gives laẓat, talaẓẓā and talaẓẓat. Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes iltizāʾ, ignition, from talazzī, blazing, and cites 92:14; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī say the name was transferred from flame. Al-Bursawī says the flame is smokeless, while Study Quran describes churning flames. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(الليث); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 70:15/q06/p2 It comes from an original laẓẓ, suggesting the punishment's duration, with one doubled consonant replaced by long alif. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as another proposed etymology. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 70:15/q07 Why does Laẓā lack tanwīn?  [لَظَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the missing tanwīn follows from feminine proper-name status, pause-like pronunciation, or a generic-name transfer.
- 70:15/q07/p1 It is a definite feminine proper name and so is indeclinable or diptote. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī reporting al-Farrāʾ, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give proper-name status as the reason; most explicitly add feminine gender. Al-Rāzī says definiteness makes it diptote. [8 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 70:15/q07/p2 Connected speech is treated as though the reciter paused on the word. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative explanation for the absent tanwīn. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 70:15/q07/p3 It is treated as a generic proper name transferred from a form with the definite article. — Al-Ālūsī compares saḥar used for a particular dawn. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 70:15/q08 Where is the pause after the declaration 'innahā laẓā'?  [إِنَّهَا لَظَىٰ · grammar]
- 70:15/q08/p1 A complete and good pause falls after Laẓā. — Al-Naḥḥās explicitly judges the stop after innahā laẓā complete and good. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 70:15/q09 Why use the word Laẓā in this wording?  [لَظَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 70:15/q09/p1 It is a rarer synonym favored by Qur'anic diction, often to preserve a sura's rhyme. — Al-Itqān lists laẓā alongside ḍīzā, al-ḥuṭama, saqar and hāwiya as examples. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 70:15/q10 What fate does the declaration about Laẓā announce for the criminal?  [لَظَىٰ · interpretation]
- 70:15/q10/p1 A fierce Fire awaits the criminal, burning and stripping him; ransom cannot divert the punishment. — Muqātil says the blaze stretches out and has power over them. Ibn Kathīr and Elmalılı stress intense, engulfing heat; Asad calls it a raging flame, and Kur'an Yolu calls verses 15–16 a warning of severe punishment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the blaze is prepared for the criminal and strips his scalp, referring to 70:17. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that it tears away extremities and pursues criminals. Al-Iṣlāḥī places it amid cosmic upheaval, broken social bonds and the doomed offer of family or tribe as ransom, with Hell seizing those who turned away and hoarded wealth. [8 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 70:15/q11 What does shawā in the following description of the Fire mean?  [* · links]
- 70:15/q11/p1 For humans it refers to the hands, feet and head, or limbs generally. — Abū ʿUbayda gives these human parts; he reports hearing a Medinan say iqshaʿarrat shawātī, 'my limbs bristled.' He says a horse's shawā means its legs, as in ʿablu al-shawā, and excludes its head because refined face and slender cheeks are prized. He also records Abū ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAlāʾ correcting al-Aʿshā's shawātahu to sarātahu. [3 notes: MAJAZ]

## 70:15/q12 What does the preceding word al-mawaddah convey in this context?  [* · links]
turns on: The two senses of al-mawaddah reported by al-Ṭabrisī.
- 70:15/q12/p1 It can mean wishing for something. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this as one of two senses. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 70:15/q12/p2 It can mean loving something. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this as the other sense. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 70:15/q13 How is the Fire's calling in the surrounding passage understood?  [* · rhetoric]
- 70:15/q13/p1 The Fire's 'calling' is figurative. — Al-Itqān classifies this as a metaphor in which one side is literal and the other figurative; it also compares the picture of Hell as the sinners' mother, a refuge and return like a human mother sheltering her child. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 70:15/q14 How does the passage's ending fit its sequence?  [* · rhetoric]
- 70:15/q14/p1 The sequence through 'it calls whoever turned away and turned back' exemplifies its fawāṣil. — Al-Burhān gives this as another example of the passage's verse-ending pattern. [1 notes: BURHAN]
