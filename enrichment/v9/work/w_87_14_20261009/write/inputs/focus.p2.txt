# 87:14/q06 Who is the successful person in this verse? [مَن · referent]
turns on: Whether man is read as a general description or linked by particular reports to ʿUthmān or Abū Bakr.
- 87:14/q06/p1 Any person who purifies himself and responds to God's reminder.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies this person with the one who fears and remembers in 87:9–10; Corpus Coranicum says the messenger exemplifies a general appeal to believers. Other commentators describe a general pattern of belief, worship and charitable action.

# 87:14/q07 How does purification lead into remembrance and prayer? [تَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sequence expresses a spiritual progression, the timing of charity and prayer, or their combined effect.
- 87:14/q07/p1 Purification is the foundation; remembrance brings knowledge of God and prayer expresses obedience.
  reasons: Rāzī, Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr order the stages as clearing false belief, knowing or recalling God's names and perfections with fear and hope, then bodily service. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a pure soul accepts guidance and cites 29:45; the Study Quran adds heart, tongue and action.
  holders (9 notes): RAZI; JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الإمام); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN
  notes: RAZI:87:15/r2 RAZI:87:15/r3 JISHUMI:v10p7380/r14 BURSEVI:v10p317/r10 IBNASHUR:87:14/r11 IBNASHUR:87:15/r4 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p287/r6 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p288/r3 STUDYQURAN:87:14/r9
- 87:14/q07/p2 The sequence can describe almsgiving before prayer, especially fiṭra before Eid prayer.
  reasons: Ibn Masʿūd and Abū al-Aḥwaṣ apply the order to giving before prayer; Ibn ʿUmar checks charity before leaving for Eid. Rāzī objects that the Qurʾān usually names prayer before alms; Biqāʿī and others maintain that fiṭra precedes Eid prayer in practice. Versions specify remembrance as Eid takbīrs or remembrance on the way to the prayer ground.

# 87:14/q08 What does the verse teach about the path to success? [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes vary in whether they emphasize faith and outward practice, inward discipline, or detachment from worldly desire.
- 87:14/q08/p1 Success follows purification, remembrance and prayer, with faith and obedient deeds.
  reasons: Muqātil names testimony, alms and five prayers; Jishumī combines obligations with avoidance of grave sins and says the actions are human acts. Abū Ḥayyān joins worship and charity. Qutb gives success in this life and the next; Islahi ties it to heeding the reminder and God's pleasure.
  holders (9 notes): MUQATIL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN
  notes: MUQATIL:87:14-19/r1 JISHUMI:v10p7380/r14 ABUHAYYAN:87:15/r2 QUTB-ZILAL:87:14/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:87:15/r3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p271/r2 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p279/r4 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:87:9/r7 STUDYQURAN:87:14/r9
- 87:14/q08/p2 The verse calls for fear of God privately and publicly and inward freedom from appetite and fixation.
  reasons: Sahl and Tustarī stress fear in secret and public; al-Jurayrī names appetites, whim and unruly temperament. Qushayrī includes sins, faults and fixation on creatures; Qāshānī speaks of cleansing the self's qualities and bodily darkness after spiritual readiness.
  holders (5 notes): TUSTARI; SULAMI(الجريري، سهل); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI
  notes: TUSTARI:87:14/r1 SULAMI:v2p390#3/r1 SULAMI:v2p390#3/r2 QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r21 QASHANI:87:13-15/r2
- 87:14/q08/p3 Purification includes detachment from worldly attachments and spending for God's sake.
  reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects this with the contrast in 87:16; spending frees attachment to wealth and the earth, while ablution symbolizes removing traces of bodily acts.
  holders (2 notes): TABATABAI
  notes: TABATABAI:87:14-16/r10 TABATABAI:87:14-16/r11
