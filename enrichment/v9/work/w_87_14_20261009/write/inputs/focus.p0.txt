# 87:14/q01 What does aflaha promise? [أَفْلَحَ · meaning]
- 87:14/q01/p1 Attaining one's desired good and lasting felicity, including salvation and Paradise.
  reasons: Al-Zajjāj and Ibn Jawzī explain enduring life and success; Wāḥidī specifies lasting residence in Paradise. Al-Ālūsī and Bursawī pair escape from feared harm with attainment of hoped-for good. Jishumī calls falāḥ attainment of one's goal; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes both victory and benefit, citing 2:5. Biqāʿī says every desired good; Quṭb distinguishes worldly sweetness of remembrance from afterlife rescue and bliss.

# 87:14/q02 Why use qad and the perfect tense for success? [قَدْ أَفْلَحَ · rhetoric]
- 87:14/q02/p1 They present the promised success as certain and announce good news after the warning.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 23:1 and 91:9 and says the wording encourages the wicked hearers to join those who fear God. Bursawī says qad prepares listeners for the good outcome and answers an implied question about the rememberer.

# 87:14/q03 What does the form tazakkā convey? [تَزَكَّىٰ · grammar]
- 87:14/q03/p1 It conveys deliberate effort to become pure and cultivate the self.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr derives striving from the tafaʿʿala form, compares the purification and corruption of 91:9–10, and says the process begins with monotheism and includes good works and almsgiving. Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes cultivating the self toward good.

# 87:14/q04 Why name the purifier where the rememberer might be expected? [مَن تَزَكَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:14/q04/p1 The wording describes the rememberer by the prior trait of purification.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī says the expected expression would identify the one who remembers; naming the purifier instead characterizes that person by the state that precedes remembrance.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI
  notes: ALUSI:87:15/r22
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270

# 87:14/q05 What purification does tazakkā describe? [تَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether purification is understood as faith, moral and ritual cleansing, growth in virtue, charity generally, or the specifically later fiṭra rite; arguments invoke word usage, context, cross-references, reports, and chronology.
- 87:14/q05/p1 Leaving idolatry or unbelief through faith and professing God's oneness.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, ʿAṭāʾ and others identify confession of no deity but God; the report through Jābir adds rejection of idols and affirming Muhammad's mission. Rāzī prefers this: the omitted object comes from the preceding warning, and cleansing unbelief is purification's fullest instance. Ibn Jawzī and Thaʿlabī appeal to the sūra's Meccan setting.

- 87:14/q05/p2 Cleansing the self broadly from sins, corrupt traits, and worldly attachment while obeying God.
  reasons: Ṭabarī joins avoidance of disbelief and sin to commands and obligations. Kashshāf and Bayḍāwī include sins with unbelief; Ibn Kathīr mentions base traits and following revelation. Biqāʿī extends cleansing to belief, character, speech, acts and wealth. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses worldly attachment and uses ablution as an illustration. Elmalılı includes heart, body, sincerity, ritual washing and charity; Qutb says every stain. Qatāda explicitly allows purification of wealth or of character.
