# 20:76: 19 questions

## 20:76/q01 What are the Gardens of Eden?  [جَنَّٰتُ عَدْنٍۢ · meaning]
- 20:76/q01/p1 They are gardens of permanent residence, the lofty ranks promised to believers. — Al-Ṭabarī says there is no departure or end; al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr call them everlasting; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī gloss ʿadn as abiding residence. Al-Jishumī includes rivers beneath trees and buildings; al-Biqāʿī says the dwelling conditions are already prepared. Ibn Jurayj identifies the promised high ranks with Eden; al-Rāzī and Asad describe them as believers' lofty stations. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:76/q02 How is 'Gardens of Eden' parsed with the preceding 'high ranks'?  [جَنَّٰتُ عَدْنٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the following circumstantial khālidīn has a grammatical governor.
- 20:76/q02/p1 It is an apposition to the preceding high ranks or degrees. — Al-Ṭabarī calls jannāt nominative by apposition to al-darajāt (20:75; also mentions 9:72). Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū al-Baqāʾ argue that an implied 'they are gardens' leaves the circumstantial khālidīn without a governor, so the pause should not fall at al-darajāt al-ʿulā. Al-Ṭabrisī says the preceding adverbial predicate of ulāʾika raises the degrees and governs what follows. Ibn Kathīr cites 20:75; al-Burūsawī also makes the river clause circumstantial to the gardens. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)+; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)+; TABRISI+]
- 20:76/q02/p2 It is an explanatory substitute for the high ranks. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow this alongside apposition; Abū al-Baqāʾ says this parse lets an implied abiding or the demonstrative's meaning govern khālidīn. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:76/q02/p3 It is the predicate of an omitted subject, 'they are gardens.' — Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū al-Baqāʾ identify this possible parse only to reject it: khālidīn would have no governing element. [0 notes: TABRISI-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]

## 20:76/q03 What do the rivers beneath the gardens refer to?  [تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · interpretation]
- 20:76/q03/p1 Rivers run below the gardens and their trees, dwellings, chambers, couches, or grounds. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies trees and cites 2:25; al-Burūsawī says dwellings or trees. Al-Jishumī adds buildings. Al-Qurṭubī specifies rivers of wine, honey, milk, and water under chambers and couches. Al-Biqāʿī adds grounds and says a river flows wherever desired. Muqātil, al-Nasafī, and Asad describe flowing waters beneath the gardens without a more specific location. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:76/q04 What is the grammatical role of the river clause?  [تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · grammar]
- 20:76/q04/p1 It is circumstantial to the gardens. — Al-Burūsawī and al-Ālūsī both parse the flowing-river clause as a ḥāl modifying the gardens. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:76/q05 What does abiding in the gardens mean?  [خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا · interpretation]
- 20:76/q05/p1 Their inhabitants remain there forever, without death, departure, or a fixed endpoint. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā explicitly deny death; Yaḥyā and al-Ṭabarī deny departure, and al-Ṭabarī says there is no defined endpoint, mentioning 2:162. Al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, al-Nasafī and Asad affirm enduring residence. [9 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:76/q06 How is 'abiding therein' parsed and governed?  [خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا · grammar]
- 20:76/q06/p1 It is circumstantial to the people promised the gardens, governed by the demonstrative's sense or by their established abiding there. — Al-Bayḍāwī names the demonstrative's meaning or the notion of abiding as governor. Al-Burūsawī relates the phrase to those for whom the reward is established or indicated. Al-Ālūsī specifies the pronoun in lahum and either the sense of establishment or ulāʾika as governor; he reports Abū al-Baqāʾ calling it a prospective state. Al-Samīn also reports the implied abiding or demonstrative meaning. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 20:76/q07 What does 'that' point to, and why use a distant demonstrative?  [وَذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
- 20:76/q07/p1 It points to the described lasting triumph and bliss; its distance expresses exaltation. — Al-Ālūsī says the referent is near in discourse but is elevated by the distant form. Al-Biqāʿī supplies the omitted antecedent as 'that immense lasting bliss'; al-Burūsawī refers it to the reward just described. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:76/q08 What does 'reward' mean here compared with 'wage'?  [جَزَآءُ · meaning]
- 20:76/q08/p1 Jazāʾ is recompense for good or bad, with or without contract; ajr is beneficial payment under a contract or its analogue. — Al-Burūsawī states this distinction while identifying the recompense here as the gardens already described. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:76/q09 What does purifying oneself mean here?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase specifies confession of faith, deeds, removal of sin, or the word's broader sense of growth; several sources combine faith and deeds.
- 20:76/q09/p1 Cleansing oneself of unbelief and sins through faith, obedience, and righteous conduct. — Al-Thaʿlabī offers righteous or cleansed of disbelief and sins; al-Jishumī says deeds bring reward. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Burūsawī, and al-Ālūsī name disbelief and sins, often adding faith and obedience. Ibn Kathīr adds rejection of evil and shirk, sole worship of God, and belief in messengers' reports and commands. Al-Biqāʿī supplies faith and good works; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says true belief and righteous action constitute sound growth. Islāḥī adds ideological, practical, and moral purification through Qur'anic guidance; Asad calls it attaining purity. [13 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:76/q09/p2 It means believing in God or professing 'There is no god but God,' thereby leaving unbelief or associationism. — Yaḥyā gives belief alone. Al-Kalbī adds giving the alms of one's soul to the profession; other reports from al-Kalbī, Ibn ʿAbbās through al-ʿAṭāʾ, al-Zamakhsharī's Ibn ʿAbbās report, and al-Rāzī give the profession. Al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz and al-Nasafī explicitly make it a departure from shirk. Al-Ālūsī uses Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss to support inclusion of a major sinner in the promise. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:76/q09/p3 It means performing righteous deeds or choosing and carrying out pure obedience. — Qatādah says righteous deeds. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says obeying God and choosing the purest acts, with the form suggesting acquisition. Al-Ṭabrisī reports an intention to obey followed by action. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل)]
- 20:76/q09/p4 It means cleansing the self of sins by obedience and avoiding disobedience. — Al-Wāḥidī al-Basīṭ adds seeking goodness and virtue; al-Baghawī and al-Zamakhsharī speak of removing sins or their stains. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 20:76/q09/p5 The root means growth in goodness; tazakkī is seeking growth, or, in another gloss, purification. — Al-Jishumī compares the increase to wealth growing through zakāt. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes sound growth as a life of true belief and righteous action. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI]

## 20:76/q10 How do deeds and divine mercy relate to this reward?  [جَزَآءُ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether the verse makes deeds a condition of its promise, and how that relates to God's mercy.
- 20:76/q10/p1 The promised reward is tied to purification through faith and good deeds. — Al-Jishumī says the verse shows reward comes through deeds. Al-Biqāʿī, al-Burūsawī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Asad, and Islāḥī connect lasting recompense to faith and righteous practice. Islāḥī rejects the theologians' claim that deeds do not matter and compares Jewish false hopes in al-Baqarah, while affirming that Paradise's key remains with God. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 20:76/q10/p2 People enter Paradise by God's mercy, while their deeds determine their ranks there. — Al-Wāḥidī transmits this from the Prophet without grading the report. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي)]
- 20:76/q10/p3 Deeds are unimportant to entry into Paradise. — Islāḥī attributes this wishful claim to theologians and rejects it, saying the Qur'an makes no such promise and that the key remains with God. [0 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 20:76/q11 Do sinful believers have a share in Paradise or lesser ranks?  [جَنَّٰتُ عَدْنٍۢ جَزَآءُ مَن تَزَكَّىٰ · theology]
turns on: The scope of 'criminal' and the conditional threat in 20:74, the place of forgiveness, and whether major sinners remain believers eligible for the promise.
- 20:76/q11/p1 Sinful believers whom God pardons may receive lesser ranks or gardens, and a major sinner can remain within the promise. — Al-Rāzī infers lesser ranks from the highest ranks being assigned to believers with faith and deeds (20:75), and attributes sinners' admission to grace and mercy. Sunnis argue that 'criminal' in the preceding threat often means unbeliever, citing those questioned about Saqar and mockers of believers (74:41, 74:46, 83:29); forgiveness limits the threat, and the conditional need not be universal. Even granting opposing premises, a major sinner remains a believer who has done good works, against the Muʿtazilite intermediate station; accountable people are believers or unbelievers. Even if the highest promise excludes sinners, lesser ranks remain. Al-Ālūsī cites Ibn ʿAbbās's shahāda gloss in support. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أهل السنة,ابن عباس)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:76/q11/p2 A major sinner is a criminal under the preceding threat and is therefore certainly assigned Hell. — The Muʿtazilites derive 'criminal' from a root associated with cutting fruit, later used for reprehensible acts; they apply the threat to every such offender. Sunnis challenge the term's scope, possible forgiveness, and the universal reading of the conditional. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL(أهل السنة,ابن عباس)-]

## 20:76/q12 Whose speech includes this promise and the preceding warning?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the register and parallel tellings mark a divine insertion, or the verses continue the magicians' address to Pharaoh.
- 20:76/q12/p1 They are the believing magicians' words to Pharaoh. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports a group reading the verses as the sorcerers' admonition explaining their choice; al-Nasafī reports the same. Al-Bayḍāwī allows this reading. Al-Ālūsī prefers it against al-Nīsābūrī's view. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects it because parallel accounts do not similarly narrate this speech. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+; NASAFI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 20:76/q12/p2 They are God's own speech inserted into the story. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports that God addresses Muhammad to expose Pharaoh's evil, the sorcerers' virtue, and the story's warning. Al-Nasafī prefers direct divine speech; al-Bayḍāwī allows it. Islāḥī says the afterlife register suits God's majesty, differs from the surrounding speech, and recalls the insertion at 20:53–59. Ibn ʿĀshūr observes the interruption before the exodus and rejects the magicians' speech from parallel accounts. Al-Ālūsī reports the divine reading as contrasting Pharaoh's evil and magicians' good but prefers the other. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; NASAFI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 20:76/q13 If the magicians spoke of these gardens, how did they know about them?  [جَنَّٰتُ عَدْنٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether their knowledge came through other believers or direct inspiration.
- 20:76/q13/p1 They may have heard the descriptions from Moses or Israelites in Egypt, including believers of Pharaoh's family. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as a possible explanation conditional on the magicians being the speakers. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:76/q13/p2 God may have inspired the magicians with this knowledge when they believed. — Al-Qurṭubī allows this alongside their having heard the descriptions. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:76/q14 What is reported about the magicians' fate after their belief?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh's threatened punishment is established as having happened.
- 20:76/q14/p1 Their execution by Pharaoh is not established by the Qur'an or sound reports. — Al-Burūsawī reports this caution about whether Pharaoh carried out his threatened punishment. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 20:76/q14/p2 They became martyrs by evening after beginning the day as magicians or unbelievers. — Al-Burūsawī cites Ibn ʿAbbās for 'magicians in the morning, martyrs by evening' and Bahr al-ʿUlūm for 'unbelievers in the morning, righteous martyrs by evening'; the opening descriptions vary. He also cites the Mathnawī's story that they honored Moses by inviting him to cast first and treats cited reports as support for their martyrdom and Pharaoh's crucifixion. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عباس,المثنوي,بحر العلوم)]

## 20:76/q15 What reports describe the recipients of Eden and the highest ranks?  [جَنَّٰتُ عَدْنٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different recipients and scenes, without a stated dispute.
- 20:76/q15/p1 People raised in Islam and good deeds without sin receive a banner and enter Eden after recognizing their description. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās through Saʿīd ibn Jubayr: they are summoned at Resurrection, recognize the description, receive a banner, and follow it into the Gardens of Eden. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]
- 20:76/q15/p2 The highest ranks appear from below like a bright star; Abū Bakr and ʿUmar are among their people. — Al-Burūsawī cites a hadith, and al-Ālūsī cites Abū Saʿīd reporting the Prophet; neither note gives a grading. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد؛ رسول الله)]

## 20:76/q16 How does this promise function in the story and sūra?  [* · links]
- 20:76/q16/p1 It answers Pharaoh's threats and promises by setting eternal reward against the criminal's fate. — Al-Ālūsī says it makes the preceding conditional explicit and that the criminal's fate comes first to hasten an answer about the threatened punishment's severity and duration (20:71). Ṭabāṭabāʾī says faith and works oppose the criminal's fate and answer Pharaoh's threat and promise (20:71–72); the high ranks answer his promised closeness and eternal gardens answer his payment (7:113–14). Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the interruption an admonition and confirmation of the believers' words. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:76/q16/p2 The promise consoles the Companions for persecution at the sūra's revelation. — Al-Biqāʿī applies the promise to their suffering. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:76/q17 Where else are Eden and its rivers explained or mentioned?  [جَنَّٰتُ عَدْنٍۢ تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · links]
- 20:76/q17/p1 These descriptions are explained or repeated in other Qur'anic passages. — Yaḥyā says the phrase was explained in Sūrat Maryam and the rivers in another passage. Ibn ʿĀshūr refers back to his treatment at 9:72. The Study Quran lists other mentions of Eden at 13:23, 16:31, 18:31, 35:33, and 98:8. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 20:76/q18 What spiritual conduct does the promise invite?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · inward]
- 20:76/q18/p1 Choose God, remove blameworthy and satanic traits, cultivate spiritual and divine qualities, and give wealth and life for the highest opening. — Al-Burūsawī gives this inward application of purification. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:76/q19 What danger does Islāḥī see in rejecting Qur'anic guidance on purification?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:76/q19/p1 Pursuing religious purity without the Qur'an's guidance leads to extremism. — Islāḥī says that guidance addresses human pitfalls and concessions and links ideological, practical, and moral purification to the reward. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
