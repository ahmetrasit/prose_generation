# 20:64: 17 questions

## 20:64/q01 Who gives this exhortation?  [فَأَجْمِعُوا۟ وَقَدْ أَفْلَحَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the closing clause continues the contestants' speech or is an inserted divine judgment, and whether Pharaoh or the sorcerers address the group.
- 20:64/q01/p1 The magicians exhort one another, including the closing claim. — Al-Qushayrī and Asad present mutual urging; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call this the apparent reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the magicians appealing to group zeal; the call answers their private dispute. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QUSHAYRI(السحرة); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:64/q01/p2 Pharaoh addresses the magicians. — Muqātil and Abū ʿAlī explicitly attribute the command to Pharaoh; Wahb says Pharaoh gathered the people and ordered the sorcerers to enter in ranks. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī report this attribution for the closing words. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:64/q01/p3 God inserts the closing success clause as an aside. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls it a divine aside; al-Samīn objects that the surface sequence makes it the contestants' encouragement to their own group. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 20:64/q02 How is فَأَجْمِعُوا۟ read?  [فَأَجْمِعُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted hamza and mīm vowels, and whether 20:60 supports repetition of 'gather' or favors a new command to resolve.
- 20:64/q02/p1 Most readers use a cut hamza and kasra on mīm, fa-ajmiʿū, from ajmaʿa. — Ibn Mujāhid, Abū ʿAlī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Nashr and Ibn ʿĀshūr report the majority reading. Al-Qaṭʿī and Hārūn also transmit it from Abū ʿAmr. It commonly means firm resolve; some allow gathering too. Abū al-Ḥasan says the cut form is more widely read; Abū ʿAlī allows dialectal Form I/Form IV alternation. [17 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو عن القطعي، عبيد، هارون,الباقون); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن,الباقون); THALABI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER(Vr); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]
- 20:64/q02/p2 Abū ʿAmr and other transmitted readers use a joined hamza and fatḥa on mīm, fajmaʿū, from jamaʿa. — Abū ʿAmr is the standard attribution; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī add al-Zuhrī, Ibn Muḥayṣin, Yaʿqūb in one route, and Abū Ḥātim. The wording فجمع كيده at 20:60 is cited for it, though Muḥammad ibn Yazīd argues the earlier gathering makes the distinct sense 'resolve' preferable. Al-Akhfash prefers this form for collecting. ## 20:64/q03 What does the command فَأَجْمِعُوا۟ mean?  [فَأَجْمِعُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the command evokes firm joint resolve or the collecting of available stratagems; some authorities allow both verbs to overlap.
- 20:64/q03/p1 Settle the plan decisively and agree on it together. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and others say unanimity prevents division and failure. Al-Farrāʾ illustrates firm resolution with 'I resolved to travel'; poetic mujmaʿ means a settled affair. Al-Rāghib describes ijmāʿ as collecting after deliberation. Al-Ālūsī cites Abū al-Haytham: making an affair one after wavering. Ibn Hishām associates ajmaʿa with abstract matters; ʿUmdat al-Ḥuffāẓ says this is usual but reversible. Al-Muḥkam and Abū ʿAlī permit overlap of the two forms. [37 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون,الشاعر,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,شاعر); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الهيثم,ابن هشام,المحكم,عمدة الحفاظ); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); TABATABAI(الراغب); ASAD-NOTES; QIRAAT-ER(Vr); BAGHAWI-FULL+]
- 20:64/q03/p2 Collect, marshal and bring forward every stratagem and instrument of magic. — Ibn Qutayba says marshal the plots; Muqātil and Yaḥyā connect them with magic. Al-Zajjāj and al-Farrāʾ say leave no trick unused. 20:60's فجمع كيده is cited for the connected reading; al-Nahhās accepts this despite the previous gathering. Al-Aṣmaʿī distinguishes bringing things from places from forming a single whole; al-Farrāʾ distinguishes gathering people and money. Abū Dhuʾayb's verse about gathered booty and a Hudhalī line support collection; al-Thaʿlabī allows the cut form this meaning too. [40 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو عمرو); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,الشاعر); THALABI(أبو عمرو,الباقون); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو,الأخفش,الزجاج,الفراء,الهذلي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو); TABRISI(الشاعر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ذؤيب,الثعلبي,النحاس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الأصمعي,الفراء,المحكم); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER(Vr)]

## 20:64/q04 What is the magicians' كَيْدَكُمْ?  [كَيْدَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the plot is described by its magical means or its larger public purpose.
- 20:64/q04/p1 Their sorcery, tricks, and means for defeating Moses and Aaron. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā identify it as magic; al-Thaʿlabī adds deceit and expertise, al-Nasafī its instruments, and Ibn Kathīr says they would cast all at once to dazzle eyes and overwhelm the prophets. [9 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:64/q04/p2 A coordinated public deception to make Moses' sign seem like ordinary magic and protect the regime's way of life. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they planned to match or exceed the sign so its distinctive force vanished before a perplexed public; 26:38–40 depicts assembly and calls to attend. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ describes charges of sorcery and expulsion as political incitement tied to ancestral custom and Pharaoh's rule. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:64/q05 How is كَيْدَكُمْ governed grammatically?  [كَيْدَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the object is directly governed, follows a new phrase, or depends on an unspoken ʿalā.
- 20:64/q05/p1 It is the verb's direct object. — Al-Samīn explicitly parses kaydakum as a direct object and dismisses an elided preposition. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 20:64/q05/p2 The resolve command may be complete, with كيدكم beginning a new phrase. — Abū al-Ḥasan, as reported by al-Ṭabrisī, allows understanding 'agree on it' before a new phrase about their plot; he also allows dialect variation between the forms. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو الحسن)]
- 20:64/q05/p3 It follows an omitted preposition ʿalā. — Al-Samīn reports this alternative only to reject it. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]

## 20:64/q06 Why are they urged to act together now?  [فَأَجْمِعُوا۟ كَيْدَكُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether the immediate spur is internal disagreement or the preceding political appeal; both can operate together.
- 20:64/q06/p1 Their preceding private dispute threatened unity after Moses' warning, so they must agree and act as one. — Abū ʿAlī says this is the magicians' consultation, distinct from Pharaoh's gathering at 20:60. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties gathering divergent views to their earlier dispute. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says Moses' 20:61 warning shook some magicians: they had no proof of their gods' divinity or intercession, and some hesitated to face him; nazwā means private speech and tanāzuʿ dispute. Al-Iṣlāḥī says professional rivalry threatened defeat; the rulers pressed for collective action. Al-Ālūsī makes the fāʾ explanatory of the preceding argument. [16 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; TABRISI(أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QASHANI]
- 20:64/q06/p2 They appeal to fear for their land and inherited order, rallying the public against Moses. — Al-Bursevī says they think Moses and Aaron intend expulsion. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes zeal for ancestral morality. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the expulsion accusation threatened homes and property, recast idolatrous custom as national survival, and ignored the visible oppression of Israelites and Moses' proof. Abū Ḥayyān reports interpretations of their 'best way' as kingdom or the notables, and one reading as 'people of your way,' linked to Moses' demand at 20:47. [11 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:64/q07 How is ثُمَّ ٱئْتُوا۟ read?  [ثُمَّ ٱئْتُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether the hamza is pronounced or replaced and whether thumma's mīm can take kasra.
- 20:64/q07/p1 The usual reading opens the mīm of thumma and pronounces the hamza in iʾtū. — Ibn Mujāhid says the remaining readers read this way; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority form. [2 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير عن النبال وغيره، الباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 20:64/q07/p2 Ibn Kathīr is transmitted with open mīm and a following quiescent yāʾ replacing the hamza in the spelling. — Ibn Mujāhid prefers this correctly transmitted form through Shibl. Abū ʿAlī says Ibn Kathīr meant to follow the written form with the replacement yāʾ. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير من طريق شبل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير عن شبل، الحسن بن محمد عن شبل، محبوب عن إسماعيل المكي)+]
- 20:64/q07/p3 A Shibl route gives kasra on thumma's mīm with hamza eased to yāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid, Abū ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya call the kasra erroneous because thumma's mīm must be open. Al-Lawāmiʿ explains kasra by the collision of two silent letters, parallel to fatḥa in the usual form. Al-Qurṭubī reports a yāʾ and kasra form and explains hamza replacement. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(شبل بن عباد، وابن كثير في رواية شبل عنه); ALUSI-FULL(شبل بن عباد وابن كثير,صاحب اللوامح); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير عن خلف عن عبيد عن شبل)-; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير من طريق شبل)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(شبل عن ابن كثير)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو على)-]

- 20:64/q08/p1 Hārūn al-Qāriʾ reports a Banu Tamim dialectal form, but the excerpt gives no form. — Al-Naḥḥās mentions the dialect at this point without specifying its shape or interpretation. [1 notes: NAHHAS(هارون القارئ)]

## 20:64/q09 What does صَفًّا describe?  [صَفًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣaff names their formation, their collected group, or their destination.
- 20:64/q09/p1 They should approach in orderly ranks or rows. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAbbās and many later exegetes describe aligned ranks; the magicians are not to intermingle. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the singular ṣaff is a generic collective for many rows; compares 61:4 and angelic ranks at 78:38 and 89:22. Al-Bursevī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ define ṣaff as a straight alignment of people or trees. [33 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI(السحرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,مقاتل والكلبي وابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، أكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:64/q09/p2 They should come all together as a single gathered group. — Yaḥyā, Ibn Qutayba, Muqātil and al-Kalbī express this sense. Al-Dāmghānī compares the assembled presentation of people at 18:48; Ibn al-Jawzī also treats ṣaff as a crowd. Asad takes literal 'one line' to mean acting in unison. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI(مقاتل والكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والكلبي وابن عباس); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل والكلبي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل والكلبي); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:64/q09/p3 Ṣaff is the festival gathering place or prayer ground to which they are told to come. — Abū ʿUbayda/Abū ʿUbayd cites Arab speech, 'I could not come to the ṣaff today,' for the prayer place. Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq mention the festival ground. Al-Baghawī identifies the appointed place. Al-Ālūsī finds the general explanation remote. [14 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة,بعضهم); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو عبيدة); THALABI(أبو عبيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); TABRISI(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:64/q09/p4 Ṣaff may be the proper name of a specific place at the festival site. — Al-Ālūsī proposes this while finding Abū ʿUbayda's generic prayer-ground sense remote; it would be an object of 'come'. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:64/q10 What grammatical role does صَفًّا have?  [صَفًّۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ṣaffan qualifies the subject, names what is approached, or contains an ellipsis.
- 20:64/q10/p1 It is an accusative circumstance describing the approaching magicians. — Abū Isḥāq, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the ḥāl analysis. Al-Zajjāj makes the singular verbal noun serve circumstantially, meaning 'while arrayed'; al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr note the verbal noun may function like a participle, active or passive. [12 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:64/q10/p2 It is the direct object of 'come,' naming the group or place reached. — Al-Naḥḥās offers the object analysis; Abū ʿUbayda names the prayer ground. Al-Samīn also allows an object meaning a ranked group and a verbal noun used as a name. Al-Ālūsī's proper place proposal requires this parse. [7 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:64/q10/p3 It abbreviates 'people possessing a row or formation' (dhawī ṣaff). — Al-Samīn records the omitted-annexed-word analysis as another way to construe ṣaffan. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:64/q11 Why should they approach in a row?  [ٱئْتُوا۟ صَفًّۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the stated purpose is public intimidation, disciplined cooperation, or both.
- 20:64/q11/p1 A coordinated formation looks imposing, frightens observers and Moses, and makes the magic more convincing. — Al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq and later exegetes connect orderly ranks with stronger appearance and effort. Ibn Kathīr adds synchronized casting to dazzle the eyes. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes rulers' lionskins and warriors' leopard skins as parallels for intimidating display; Ibn Maʿd Yakrib's verse illustrates the latter. Al-Biqāʿī says the lines match each other in the contest. The meeting place was spacious, with Moses and the magicians at opposite edges before they moved toward the center, according to al-Irshād and al-Ālūsī. [23 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، أكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن معد يكرب); TABATABAI]
- 20:64/q11/p2 Appearing together expresses and enforces unity of purpose. — Yaḥyā and Asad stress togetherness; al-Biqāʿī says division weakens the sorcerers, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ links a single formation to ending hesitation and acting as one force. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QUSHAYRI(السحرة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:64/q12 What do reports say about the magicians' number and deployment?  [ٱئْتُوا۟ صَفًّۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports give incompatible totals, and al-Ālūsī questions their reliability.
- 20:64/q12/p1 They were seventy thousand. — Al-Bayḍāwī says 70,000 advanced together carrying ropes and staffs; al-Ālūsī lists 70,000. Al-Bursevī gives seventy lines of one thousand each. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:64/q12/p2 They were twenty-five thousand in twenty-five rows of one thousand. — Al-Ḥasan gives this exact arrangement. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن)]
- 20:64/q12/p3 They were seventy-two. — Abū Ḥayyān calls 72 the smallest transmitted total and says each bore staffs and ropes; al-Ālūsī also lists 72. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:64/q12/p4 They were nine hundred. — Al-Ālūsī lists 900 and notes one account divides them among Persians, Romans and Alexandrians. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:64/q12/p5 They were fifteen thousand. — Al-Ālūsī includes this reported figure. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:64/q12/p6 They were thirty-some thousand. — Al-Ālūsī includes this reported range. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:64/q12/p7 They were nine hundred thousand. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the largest reported total. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:64/q12/p8 They were numerous, but the precise reports are unreliable. — Ibn ʿĀshūr only states all Egypt's magicians were summoned in great numbers. Al-Ālūsī says the varied reports lack clear reliability and distrusts exaggerated totals. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:64/q13 What does أَفْلَحَ mean here?  [أَفْلَحَ · meaning]
- 20:64/q13/p1 To succeed, win, or attain the intended goal. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies defeating the opponent; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Bursevī gloss attainment of one's aim; al-Bayḍāwī says obtaining what one seeks. The other glosses say the victor prospers or wins. ## 20:64/q14 What does ٱسْتَعْلَىٰ mean here?  [ٱسْتَعْلَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the derived verb indicates achieved dominance, intensified rising, or pursuit of superiority.
- 20:64/q14/p1 To rise above the opponent by prevailing or overcoming. — Al-Layth permits the expression for overcoming or rising above a thing; al-Zajjāj glosses victory. Al-Ālūsī cites al-Ṣiḥāḥ for the derived form functioning like simple ʿalā; Ibn ʿĀshūr instead sees sīn and tāʾ intensifying the sense, as in istaʾkhara. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الكلبي,قتادة); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الليث); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:64/q14/p2 To seek supremacy and exert effort to gain it, even before actual victory. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this sense; al-Ālūsī argues it makes the exhortation stronger because even those striving can hope for success. Al-Rāghib says seeking superiority may be blameworthy or noble; al-Bursevī allows victory or social elevation. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]

## 20:64/q15 Who is meant by مَنِ ٱسْتَعْلَىٰ?  [مَنِ ٱسْتَعْلَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the maxim applies to either party, to the magicians as a group or individuals, or is God's statement about the prophets.
- 20:64/q15/p1 Whichever side defeats the other in the contest. — Al-Ṭabarī says whoever overcomes the opponent; al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr treat the maxim as applying to the day's winner. Ibn Kathīr says the magicians expected Pharaoh's reward, while Moses would gain authority if he won. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); TABRISI(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:64/q15/p2 The magicians collectively, if their combined effort prevails. — Al-Irshād and al-Ālūsī allow all of them together, reflecting their boast. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:64/q15/p3 Whichever individual magician prevails, urging each to try his utmost. — Al-Irshād and al-Ālūsī offer this individual reading; the promised prize gives each an incentive. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:64/q15/p4 Moses and Aaron, in God's closing declaration. — Al-Rāghib records the possibility that these are God's words about the prophets, without a magician's exhortation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]

## 20:64/q16 What success do the contestants expect?  [وَقَدْ أَفْلَحَ ٱلْيَوْمَ مَنِ ٱسْتَعْلَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish the contestants' hoped-for worldly prize from later moral and inward lessons.
- 20:64/q16/p1 Victory brings Pharaoh's promised payment, favor and status; defeat threatens their position. — Ibn Kathīr contrasts the sorcerers' expected lavish reward with the authority Moses would gain. Al-Irshād and al-Ālūsī cite Pharaoh's promised pay and closeness, with al-Ālūsī referring to 26:42 and 26:44. Al-Iṣlāḥī calls it a decisive day. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the magicians sought worldly success because, though believing in another life, they did not think the contest affected it. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 20:64/q16/p2 The contest illustrates the failure of forbidden worldly pursuits to give spiritual elevation. — Al-Bursevī says magic used for wealth instead of God or the hereafter is blameworthy and leaves only toil, citing 35:10. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 20:64/q16/p3 A caller to truth must first dismantle false beliefs and doubts with proof. — Al-Qāshānī says imagination precedes intellect and certainty, so falsehood must be refuted before truth can settle; he applies this to the surrounding confrontation. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:64/q17 How does the closing clause function in the speech?  [وَقَدْ أَفْلَحَ ٱلْيَوْمَ مَنِ ٱسْتَعْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether its rhetorical role is a parenthetic spur or a summation of the plan.
- 20:64/q17/p1 It is a parenthetic maxim encouraging the contestants. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī call the clause parenthetical; al-Samīn sees the contestants encouraging their group. Al-Ālūsī says it can exhort every magician to exert maximum effort. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:64/q17/p2 It sums up the plot's immediate objective: victory in today's gathering. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls the sentence a summation; the same-day reference ties their consultation to the appointed contest. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
