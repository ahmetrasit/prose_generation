# 87:10: 15 questions

## 87:10/q01 How is سَيَذَّكَّرُ formed?  [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · grammar]
- 87:10/q01/p1 It is an assimilated form of sayatadhakkaru, with tāʾ assimilated or changed to dhāl. — Jishumi gives the underlying sayatadhakkaru and assimilation; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the articulation points of tāʾ and dhāl are close. Biqāʿī takes the assimilation to suggest even subtle forms of reminder. [3 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI]

## 87:10/q02 What does the future form in سَيَذَّكَّرُ convey?  [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the future marks a divine promise or the time needed for reflection.
- 87:10/q02/p1 It promises a certain future response by the fearful person. — Rāzī compares God's assured promise in 87:6. Biqāʿī says the promise cannot fail and extends even to subtle reminders. Baghawī, Nasafī, and Bursawī render the response as soon forthcoming. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BAGHAWI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 87:10/q02/p2 It describes remembrance after time spent reflecting and deliberating. — Rāzī presents this as the alternative to an assured promise; his fuller account also records both possibilities. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 87:10/q03 Why is acquiring the reminder called remembering?  [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · rhetoric]
- 87:10/q03/p1 Clear proofs make the knowledge seem previously present, though imitation and obstinacy have obscured it. — Rāzī notes that remembering usually follows prior knowledge and forgetting; the clarity of the proofs explains this usage even for unbelievers. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 87:10/q04 What response does سَيَذَّكَّرُ describe?  [سَيَذَّكَّرُ · interpretation]
- 87:10/q04/p1 The fearful person takes heed of the Quranic reminder, reflects on its truth, and accepts its guidance. — Ṭabarī links this to Muhammad's commanded admonition and fear of punishment; Qatāda says no servant fears God without remembering Him, and contrasts disdain for the reminder and its people. Muqātil includes affirming God's oneness, forgiveness, and freedom from reckoning. Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī trace reflection to truth and belief; Nasafī says acceptance. Ibn Kathīr links it to meeting God and Muhammad's message. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says learned people and believers respond according to their guidance; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the verb as intransitive. Tabāṭabāʾī cites this verse against an interpretation of 87:14–16 that would conflict with the fearful person's remembrance. [40 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 87:10/q05 What does يَخْشَىٰ mean, and what is feared?  [يَخْشَىٰ · meaning]
- 87:10/q05/p1 It means fearing God, His punishment, the Hereafter, or a bad end; fear occurs in degrees. — Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses khashya as fear, cites 20:44, and says believers differ by degree; he adds that the verb has no expressed object. Ṭabarī and Qushayrī specify God's punishment; Kashshāf and Nasafī mention a bad end; Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions the Hereafter. Study Quran distinguishes certainty, admitted possibility, and denial of return. Corpus Coranicum cites 79:26 and Andrae's parallel of fear of the judging God and watchfulness in contemporary Christian monastic piety. [22 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]

## 87:10/q06 Who is the one described as fearing and heeding?  [مَن يَخْشَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether man designates a class, a knowledge-defined subset, or an individual named by reports.
- 87:10/q06/p1 It denotes the general class of people disposed to fear God, with different degrees of receptivity. — Ibn ʿĀshūr expressly rejects restriction to one person: man is generic and its singular form governs singular agreement; unexpressed fear lets the person weigh harm and benefit. Biqāʿī includes even innate fear and links it to felicity. Rāzī divides people into those certain of resurrection, undecided, and deniers; his broader reading includes believers and non-hostile doubters, who outnumber hostile deniers. Bayḍāwī includes the certain and hesitant; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān include scholars and believers in varying measure. Study Quran calls outright deniers the most wretched. [18 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(صاحب التفسير الكبير); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:10/q06/p2 It means those whose knowledge of God and certainty of resurrection make them fearful. — Rāzī's narrower reading attributes fear to knowledge of God's power, knowledge, and wisdom, citing 35:28, which links fear to knowledgeable servants. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:10/q06/p3 The person is ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān. — ʿAṭāʾ is reported by Wāḥidī as identifying the person who takes heed; separate reports name ʿUthmān as the occasion of revelation. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:10/q06/p4 The person is Ibn Umm Maktūm. — Al-Farrāʾ and reports through Ibn ʿAbbās identify him in the revelation account; Rāzī also records this occasion. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس)]
- 87:10/q06/p5 The person is Abū Bakr. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn Waqād identifies the fearful one as Abū Bakr, as reported by Naḥḥās. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الحسين بن وقاد)]

## 87:10/q07 What occasion of revelation is reported for this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports identify different people, and one supplies the date-palm incident.
- 87:10/q07/p1 It was revealed about Ibn Umm Maktūm. — Wāḥidī attributes this to al-Farrāʾ; Qurṭubī gives Abū Ṣāliḥ's report from Ibn ʿAbbās; Rāzī records the alternative report. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس)]
- 87:10/q07/p2 It was revealed about ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān. — Rāzī records this report. Bursawī gives the accompanying account: a hypocrite refused to exchange a date palm for one in Paradise, so ʿUthmān gave the petitioner his own orchard and the verse was revealed. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 87:10/q08 Does naming the fearful exclude other people from taking heed?  [مَن يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse describes the strongest response or a boundary on who benefits.
- 87:10/q08/p1 Fear is singled out because it makes the response stronger, though hopeful people may also remember. — Māwardī states both fear and hope can prompt remembrance; Qurṭubī transmits the same explanation from him. Ibn ʿĀshūr says believers differ by their degree of fear. [6 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:10/q08/p2 Those who fear are the people who benefit from the reminder, while the wretched evade it. — Abū Ḥayyān and Study Quran describe benefit as limited to the fearful; Kuran Yolu points to the evasive wretched and Hell in 87:12. Qatāda contrasts the one who remembers with one who shuns the reminder out of disdain and hatred. Tabāṭabāʾī says reminder benefits the fearful by inclining them to truth. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI]

## 87:10/q09 Why does fear lead a person to heed the reminder?  [مَن يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the notes emphasize reflective fear or an existing receptive disposition; they present compatible explanations.
- 87:10/q09/p1 Fear prompts reflection on the warning and its evidence, leading to recognition of truth and belief. — Kashshāf contrasts the unreflective and unafraid. Abū Ḥayyān says one seeks escape from what one dreads; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a person considers possible harm and benefit lest the warning apply. Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and Ālūsī trace reflection to recognition and belief. Quṭb links a living heart's awe to knowledge of just accounting for good and evil; Biqāʿī says fear leads to every good and Paradise. [15 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 87:10/q09/p2 A tender heart or preserved natural light is receptive to the reminder. — Qāshānī names tender hearts, sound natures, and receptivity to the reminder's light and purity. Iṣlāḥī says people who preserve nature's God-given light recognize the prophet's call or awaken when prompted; where it is extinguished, only the Judgement trumpet awakens them. [2 notes: QASHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:10/q10 Whom should the Prophet remind in light of this verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on a general invitation, avoiding fruitless pursuit, or tailoring its content.
- 87:10/q10/p1 He should address everyone, since the receptive are not known to him and the call itself fulfills his duty. — Rāzī says only God sees inward fear; many opponents merely resist verbally, most people are hesitant, warnings of the greater Fire in 87:12–13 break hearts, and forfeiting much good to avoid little evil is itself evil. Qushayrī says the caller earns reward even where only the fearful benefit. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted object of 'remind' generalizes the audience; Quṭb says the Prophet fulfills his duty and God deals with each response. Corpus Coranicum, citing Robinson, says calls to the messenger can appeal generally, with him exemplary for believers. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(القشيري); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:10/q10/p2 He should remind where it is useful without pursuing or forcing the obdurate. — Iṣlāḥī says the fearful heed and the wretched evade, so the Prophet is to leave the evaders to their fate; their rejection reflects their condition rather than a defect in the message or preacher. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:10/q10/p3 He should adapt the reminder to what matters to each hearer. — ʿIṣām al-Dīn says unbelievers should be called to faith before subsidiary duties, while a sinful believer may need reminding about prayer rather than faith. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين)]

## 87:10/q11 How does this response relate to the preceding condition about useful reminder?  [* · links]
turns on: How to read the preceding 'if': parenthesis without converse implication, omitted alternative, temporal 'when,' or an actual limit on useful reminding.
- 87:10/q11/p1 The condition does not restrict the command: all are invited, though only some benefit. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the condition parenthetical and says the outcome cannot be predicted; he cites 50:45 and says it challenges certainty of universal benefit, with Abū Jahl an example of known refusal. He adopts Ibn ʿAbbās's distinction between God's allies and enemies and rejects ellipsis. Ālūsī says a condition need not imply its converse. Rāzī says hidden fear requires general address. Study Quran also connects the preceding verse to benefit for those who fear. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)+]
- 87:10/q11/p2 The command has an implicit second case: remind whether it benefits or does not. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, al-Jurjānī, and al-Zahrāwī propose ellipsis; Ālūsī compares 16:81, which mentions clothing protecting from heat and omits cold. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās but calls the supplement unnecessary. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الفراء والنحاس والجرجاني والزهراوي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء)-]
- 87:10/q11/p3 The particle 'in' can mean 'when,' avoiding a restrictive condition. — Ālūsī reports this Kūfan analysis with Quranic and hadith usage, including 3:139; other grammarians deny the sense and give alternative analyses of those examples. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكوفيون)]
- 87:10/q11/p4 The benefit condition is real because the reminder is not futile and inclines the fearful toward truth. — Tabāṭabāʾī explicitly treats benefit as a real condition; Iṣlāḥī reads the Prophet's task as reminding where useful. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]

## 87:10/q12 How does this verse relate to the following contrast and the surah's message?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes describe both the immediate contrast and its place in the wider surah.
- 87:10/q12/p1 It begins the contrast between the receptive and the wretched, whose responses lead to different fates. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts heeding with rejection and Fire. Quṭb says people diverge after the Prophet's reminder. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wretched counterpart is heedless in delusion and pleasure. Iṣlāḥī describes evaders who take worldly life as all and fear no Hereafter, then face Fire without life or death and wish vainly for death. Kuran Yolu cites 87:12. Corpus Coranicum says 87:10–11 contrast responses, 87:12–15 their fates in a chiasm paralleled by 92:5–11,14–21; the good outcome balances the Hell warning. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:10/q12/p2 It moves the messenger's divinely preserved revelation into public admonition within a surah about divine oneness and final recompense. — Corpus Coranicum says the second stanza addresses the messenger after praise, perhaps with close followers, and gives 87:6 public purpose; the warning implies fear, purification, invocation, and prayer, with 87:15 recalling 87:1. Quṭb describes the surah as a cosmic sanctuary of praise, good news of preservation and ease, and a message rooted in earlier scriptures about creation, revelation, and the afterlife; Biqāʿī adds gratitude for intellect and the faculties of heart, tongue, and limbs. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL; BIQAI]

## 87:10/q13 What are this surah's setting and recitational use?  [* · sciences]
- 87:10/q13/p1 Al-Aʿlā is Meccan, has nineteen verses, and was recited by the Prophet at Eid and Friday prayers with al-Ghāshiya. — Quṭb says the Prophet loved the surah and sometimes recited the two surahs on the same day. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:10/q14 What anecdotes illustrate the choice between worldly goods and Paradise?  [* · reports]
turns on: The anecdotes describe different people, bargains, and goods.
- 87:10/q14/p1 A hypocrite refused a Paradise date palm, while ʿUthmān gave an orchard to the petitioner. — Bursawī connects this exchange to the verse's revelation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 87:10/q14/p2 One man chose eighty sheep over Paradise. — Bursawī tells this as one of two related stories contrasting worldly goods and Paradise. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 87:10/q14/p3 An old woman preferred Paradise to a hundred sheep for revealing Joseph's bones to Moses. — Bursawī pairs her choice with the story of the man who took eighty sheep. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 87:10/q15 How are people's divergent responses related to divine knowledge and human disposition?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes emphasize divine foreknowledge or the hearer's disposition without explicitly arguing against each other.
- 87:10/q15/p1 God has always known who will accept or reject, but leaves each person's outcome unidentified as a trial and proof. — Biqāʿī likens the groups to those who accept treatment and those who do not, and says God establishes proof through familiar means. He also links innate fear with those destined for felicity. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:10/q15/p2 Rejection reflects the evader's own condition, while acceptance reflects preserved natural light. — Iṣlāḥī says refusal shows no flaw in the message or preacher; people whose God-given light remains can recognize the prophet's call, while those whose light is extinguished await the Judgement trumpet. Quṭb says God deals with people according to their response. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL]
