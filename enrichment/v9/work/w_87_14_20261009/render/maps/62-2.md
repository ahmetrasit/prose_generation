# 62:2: 34 questions

## 62:2/q01 To whom does 'He' refer?  [هُوَ · referent]
- 62:2/q01/p1 God alone. — Al-Tabari relates the pronoun to God's name; al-Biqa'i stresses exclusivity. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 62:2/q02 What does sending the Messenger express here?  [بَعَثَ · interpretation]
- 62:2/q02/p1 God raised and sent a messenger as a favor, guidance, and an expression of His sovereignty and will. — Al-Biqa'i describes sending from the hidden presence with legal commands and prohibitions; Qatada calls it mercy and guidance; Elmalili likens new knowledge among ignorant Arabs to life from death; Islahi connects God's sovereignty in 62:1 with communicating His directives; Kur'an Yolu says prophethood and revelation depend on God's will. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 62:2/q03 Who are the unlettered people named here, and what describes them?  [ٱلْأُمِّيِّۦنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the label names an ethnic first audience, an absence of scripture, a literacy condition, Meccan origin, or membership in the believing community; al-Tabataba'i and Ibn Atiyya cite context against narrow alternatives.
- 62:2/q03/p1 The Arabs, generally lacking scripture and widespread reading and writing. — Muqatil, Qatada, Mujahid, al-Tabari and many later commentators identify the Arabs. Ibn Zayd and Ibn Abbas stress that no book was sent to them; Qatada says there was no scribe, while al-Mufaddal allows that a few wrote. Al-Qurtubi reports Ibn Abbas includes literate Arabs because they were not People of Scripture. Al-Biqa'i says they remained near the natural state of birth. Ibn Kathir cites 3:20; al-Tabataba'i says few could read or write. Islahi identifies them particularly as Ishmaelites and explains that Jews used the designation disparagingly but the Qur'an dignifies it; Study Quran reports al-Tabari's specification of idolatrous Arabs. Kur'an Yolu points to 2:78, 3:20, and 3:75. [38 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,المفضل,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 62:2/q03/p2 People unable to write, or unable to read and write, whatever their identity. — Abu Ubayda and al-Tabataba'i define the word through literacy; al-Zajjaj and al-Razi describe the original human condition before learning; the Prophet's hadith says this community neither writes nor calculates and counts months by days. Ibrahim's narrower definition permits reading but excludes writing. Al-Qurtubi gives Quraysh as an example of nonwriters; al-Alusi reports a definition limited to inability to write. [14 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم,بعضهم); ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 62:2/q03/p3 The people of Mecca or Quraysh specifically. — Al-Mawardi's reported view says Quraysh learned writing only late from al-Hira; al-Tabrisi relates the interpretation to Mecca as Mother of Cities. Ibn Atiyya rejects restriction to Quraysh because the verse concerns all Arabs; al-Tabataba'i rejects Meccans as unsuitable to a Medinan sura and its pronoun reference. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 62:2/q03/p4 Anyone without a revealed book, including non-Jews or other peoples outside the scriptural communities. — Study Quran attributes the broad reading to al-Razi and cites 3:20; Kur'an Yolu lists the community without its own revealed book. Al-Tabataba'i objects that defining the group as non-Jews or other People of the Book would wrongly constrain the Prophet's mission, citing 3:75. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(R); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 62:2/q03/p5 Those who affirm Muhammad and follow his example. — Al-Tustari says affiliation to Muhammad requires emulation and denies membership to one who does not emulate him. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 62:2/q03/p6 Specifically the Ishmaelites who had already accepted faith. — Islahi reads the otherwise general wording by its closing reference to their previous error; he also takes the title to distinguish Ishmaelites. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 62:2/q04 What is the derivation of 'ummī'?  [ٱلْأُمِّيِّۦنَ · meaning]
turns on: Which noun the adjective is derived from and whether a Meccan derivation would wrongly restrict its scope.
- 62:2/q04/p1 It relates to one's mother and the original condition at birth, before learned literacy. — Ibn Atiyya compares a child in the womb; al-Alusi prefers this best-known derivation and includes lack of calculation; Elmalili prefers it in context and adduces the hadith that the community does not write or calculate. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+]
- 62:2/q04/p2 It relates to ummah, the community or ordinary human disposition before instruction. — Ibn Atiyya gives the original human disposition; al-Alusi reports the Arab nation derivation; al-Nasafi derives it from the Arab nation, which did not read or write as other nations did; Elmalili lists this possibility. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 62:2/q04/p3 It relates to Umm al-Qurā, Mecca. — Al-Alusi and Elmalili list this derivation; Ibn Atiyya considers it weak because it would restrict the verse to Quraysh. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 62:2/q05 How did writing spread among the Arabs?  [ٱلْأُمِّيِّۦنَ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different initial locations and populations.
- 62:2/q05/p1 Writing began for Arabs at al-Ta'if, whose people learned from al-Hira, ultimately from al-Anbar. — Al-Zajjaj reports the Ta'if to Hira to Anbar chain; al-Nasafi and al-Alusi repeat it, al-Alusi saying Hira's people were from Anbar. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ(أهل الطائف); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 62:2/q05/p2 Some Quraysh learned writing from al-Hira late in the pre-Islamic age. — Al-Mawardi includes this in the report that confines the unlettered to Quraysh. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 62:2/q06 How does the following verse's 'others' attach to the people and teaching named here?  [ٱلْأُمِّيِّۦنَ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'others' is coordinated with the earlier genitive group or with the object of 'teaches'.
- 62:2/q06/p1 It is genitive, attached to 'the unlettered' with an implied 'among'. — Al-Tha'labi supplies an understood fī, yielding 'among other unlettered people'. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 62:2/q06/p2 It is accusative, attached to the object pronoun in 'teaches them'. — Al-Tha'labi says the Prophet thereby teaches other believers who follow his religion. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 62:2/q07 How is 'al-ummiyyīn' read?  [ٱلْأُمِّيِّۦنَ · readings]
turns on: Whether omission of the nisba yāʾ is admitted as a variant.
- 62:2/q07/p1 It is read with the ordinary nisba yāʾ; no disagreement in the sura is reported. — Ibn Mujahid states that there is no reading disagreement throughout Surat al-Jumu'a. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID]
- 62:2/q07/p2 A variant omits the nisba yāʾ, yielding al-amīn or a shortened al-ummiyyīn. — Al-Razi reports al-amīn and al-Alusi reports omission of the nisba yāʾ. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 62:2/q08 Who is the Messenger, and in what sense is he 'from them'?  [رَسُولًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether membership expressed by min also conveys the characteristic of unletteredness or only common people and origin.
- 62:2/q08/p1 He is Muhammad, a human Arab of their own people, lineage, and language. — Ibn Abbas and al-Tabrisi cite 9:128; al-Razi also cites 23:32. Al-Baghawi says shared language makes the proof compelling. Al-Qurtubi reports kinship across Arab tribes except Taghlib, which Ibn Ishaq excludes from his ancestry on account of Christianity. Al-Wahidi praises his lineage, standing, character, and parents. Islahi says his belonging prevents detractors from attributing Arab guidance to a foreign people. [22 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 62:2/q08/p2 He is from them in the further sense of sharing their unlettered condition. — Al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, al-Biqa'i, Bursevi, and a group reported by al-Alusi stress shared unletteredness. Al-Alusi notes that partitive min alone establishes membership of the group, not necessarily this property; the group prefers the common distinguishing quality. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(جمع)+]
- 62:2/q08/p3 Min is partitive: he belongs to their group, without necessarily proving that he is unlettered. — Al-Alusi explicitly parses min as partitive and says membership by genus need not transfer the group's literacy description to him. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 62:2/q09 Why is an unlettered Messenger from these people a sign or blessing?  [رَسُولًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 62:2/q09/p1 His unletteredness fulfilled earlier predictions, aided acceptance through resemblance, and excluded learning his revelation from written books; his knowledge proves his mission. — Al-Mawardi gives the three grounds; al-Qurtubi says each proves the miracle. Al-Wahidi, al-Razi, al-Tabrisi, and Ibn al-Jawzi repeat them, with al-Tabrisi adducing accurate history of past nations. Al-Qushayri sees God granting knowledge without study, citing 4:113 and 42:52; Bursevi quotes Isaiah's prediction, al-Kashifi's Persian couplets (last corrupt), and the Burda on learning in an unlettered orphan; al-Alusi cites that poem too. Al-Biqa'i says reform by one of their own is remarkable; al-Baydawi and al-Biqa'i add his recitation despite no study. Al-Wahidi describes revelation surpassing Arab eloquence despite their failure to imitate it. [24 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي,شعيا عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 62:2/q10 Does being sent among the unlettered limit his mission to them?  [فِى ٱلْأُمِّيِّۦنَ رَسُولًۭا مِّنْهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether naming the initial Arab recipients implies exclusivity despite verses explicitly addressing all humanity.
- 62:2/q10/p1 No; the Arab or Ishmaelite first audience is compatible with a universal mission. — Al-Razi cites 34:28 and says naming a group does not exclude others, comparing 29:48. Ibn Kathir says the favor was emphatic for first recipients and cites 43:44, 26:214, 7:158, 6:19, 11:17. Al-Biqa'i appeals to explicit texts and decisive proofs. Bursevi says a supposed restriction is overridden by the explicit universal statement. Al-Tabataba'i describes an Arabic first phase followed by addresses to Jews, Christians, Magians, and rulers; he says Abraham's prayer in 2:129 embraces Ishmael's descendants beyond Mecca. Ibn Ashur affirms all nations, and Kur'an Yolu includes later people. Islahi says the Prophet completed the specific Ishmaelite mission while his followers carry the universal mission as witnesses until Judgment. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 62:2/q10/p2 Specifying the unlettered means the Prophet was sent only to the Arabs. — Al-Razi reports the People of the Book's inference; he objects that mention of one group is not exclusion of all others. Bursevi expressly refutes the claim. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أهل الكتاب); RAZI-FULL-; BURSEVI-]

## 62:2/q11 Why say 'from them' rather than 'from themselves'?  [مِّنْهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 62:2/q11/p1 Minhum suits the wider group of Quraysh and other Arabs, believers and unbelievers; min anfusihim suggests closer intimacy. — Al-Samarra'i contrasts the intimacy and distinction of min anfusihim with simple group membership in minhum, using 3:164; context can still add honor or closeness to minhum. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 62:2/q12 How is the Messenger's reciting described grammatically and lexically?  [يَتْلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: The notes address both the construction's grammatical role and the manner conveyed by the verb.
- 62:2/q12/p1 Yatlū alayhim is a circumstantial description of the Messenger, coordinated with the subsequent tasks. — Al-Nahhas calls it an accusative circumstantial phrase modifying rasūl; al-Tabataba'i calls the phrase a description, while al-Alusi identifies the coordination of later verbs with it. [4 notes: NAHHAS; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 62:2/q12/p2 Tilāwa means sustained, successive recitation, a narrower act than broadly voicing letters. — Al-Biqa'i says one elevated passage follows another repeatedly; Bursevi likens it to study or regular litanies and contrasts wider qirā'a. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 62:2/q13 What are the signs he recites, and what does their recitation achieve?  [ءَايَٰتِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ayat points chiefly to the Qur'an as recited text or to its functions as proof and legal clarification.
- 62:2/q13/p1 The signs are the Qur'an, conveying revelation, rulings, proof, and guidance. — Muqatil, al-Mawardi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi and al-Dahhak identify the Qur'an; al-Tabrisi includes lawful and forbidden matters, proofs and rulings. Al-Wahidi says its proofs answer falsehood and dispel doubt and disbelief. Kur'an Yolu stresses conveying revelation as received. Bursevi and al-Biqa'i stress the miracle of an unlettered reciter. [11 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 62:2/q13/p2 They are manifest proofs of his prophethood and the signs distinguishing truth and falsehood or setting out law. — Al-Razi permits both explicit evidence of mission and legal signs; al-Wahidi speaks of proof overcoming falsehood. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 62:2/q14 What does the Messenger's purification mean?  [وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether tazkiya names inner and moral cleansing, the call and practices that cause it, or collection of alms.
- 62:2/q14/p1 He removes idolatry, disbelief, corrupt beliefs, sins, and bad conduct, cultivating faith and virtue. — Ibn Abbas specifies pure hearts through faith; Ibn Jurayj and Muqatil specify disbelief and sins; Ibn Atiyya adds growth in good. Al-Razi adds vile speech and deeds; al-Nasafi adds jahiliyya's vices; al-Biqa'i adds base morals and deviant beliefs and his loving gaze. Al-Tabataba'i traces tazkiya to wholesome growth and describes habituated virtue perfecting humanity; Elmalili adds spiritual flourishing and clearer minds. Islahi connects cleansing to divine purity in 62:1; Kur'an Yolu describes spiritual and ethical education. [21 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج ومقاتل,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج، مقاتل,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Ṭs); ASAD-NOTES]
- 62:2/q14/p2 He calls people to obedience and the means of purity, so that following them reforms them. — Al-Nahhas says obedience is what renders them purified; al-Razi reports calling to what makes them pious; al-Tabrisi includes such a call alongside cleansing; Ibn Zayd says the Book and righteous deeds are means. [4 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 62:2/q14/p3 He takes their zakat or alms. — Al-Mawardi attributes the interpretation to al-Suddi as zakat of their deeds; al-Qurtubi transmits it as taking alms. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي)]

## 62:2/q15 How does purification reach later believers, and who ultimately purifies?  [وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · inward]
- 62:2/q15/p1 The Prophet's traces and his inheritors extend purification and teaching beyond his Companions, in proportion to believers' receptivity. — Al-Biqa'i says longing strengthens adherence to Qur'an and Sunna and deepens practice. Bursevi says perfected inheritors teach later believers the Qur'an's realities after the Prophet taught the Companions directly. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 62:2/q15/p2 God is the true purifier, manifested through the Perfect Human and the Messenger's action. — Bursevi invokes 4:49 and 4:80 to connect divine agency with the Perfect Human as manifestation of divine attributes. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 62:2/q16 Why does purification come before teaching in this wording?  [وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The order of the Prophet's actual education versus the ranking of qualities requested in Abraham's prayer.
- 62:2/q16/p1 Moral formation precedes true knowledge in educating believers, and each named benefit is a distinct divine favor. — Al-Tabataba'i says ethical formation comes first in the Prophet's education. Al-Biqa'i says its contextual priority counters affection for disbelievers. Al-Irshad, reported by Bursevi, and al-Alusi distinguish recitation's theoretical improvement from purification's practical perfection, even though teaching follows recitation in actual occurrence; the wording displays separate bounties. [4 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 62:2/q16/p2 In Abraham's prayer knowledge comes before purification because knowledge ranks earlier and higher in what is requested. — Al-Tabataba'i distinguishes the order in 2:129, which requests realization of the qualities, from this verse's description of actual formation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 62:2/q17 What does 'the Book' taught by the Messenger mean?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kitab denotes revelation as text and rulings, the skill of writing, knowledge gained through it, or law as an institution.
- 62:2/q17/p1 The Qur'an or revealed scripture, including its wording, commands, prohibitions, explanation, and admonition. — Al-Tabari describes divine commands, prohibitions and ordinances; Muqatil includes lawful and unlawful admonition. Al-Tabataba'i calls teaching explanation of verse wording and difficult passages. Al-Biqa'i describes revelation containing religious and worldly good. Ibn Ishaq, as reported by al-Mawardi, treats knowledge of good and evil through the Book as leading to action. Bursevi says it can include the wording, and Elmalili includes Qur'anic instruction. Al-Alusi and al-Tabataba'i say repeating 'signs' and 'Book' marks distinct blessings. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن إسحاق,الحسن); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 62:2/q17/p2 Writing with a pen and literacy taught to the Arabs. — Ibn Abbas says writing spread through religion's command to record matters; al-Qurtubi, al-Mawardi and Bursevi transmit him; Elmalili includes literacy in teaching. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ELMALILI]
- 62:2/q17/p3 Knowledge of good and evil, directing people to do one and avoid the other. — Al-Mawardi and Bursevi attribute this interpretation to Muhammad ibn Ishaq. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); BURSEVI(ابن إسحاق)]
- 62:2/q17/p4 Divine law or sharīʿa, whose implementation requires authority. — Islahi identifies al-kitab as law and connects enforcement with power and authority; Elmalili includes the rulings to be applied. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 62:2/q18 What is the wisdom taught alongside the Book?  [ٱلْحِكْمَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether wisdom is identified with Sunna, legal understanding, meanings and practice within revelation, sound discernment, or a wider body of inspired knowledge.
- 62:2/q18/p1 The Prophet's Sunna and teaching. — Al-Tabari gives this gloss and reports Qatada; al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi report al-Hasan. Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi, al-Nasafi, Bursevi and Study Quran also give it; Elmalili includes Sunna and hadith. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 62:2/q18/p2 Religious law, obligations, or jurisprudential understanding. — Al-Tabrisi and al-Baydawi identify law; al-Razi reports obligations; Malik, transmitted by al-Mawardi, al-Qurtubi and Bursevi, identifies understanding jurisprudence. Al-Nasafi offers understanding religion; al-Alusi reports rulings and laws in Prophetic reports, while Islahi relates wise law to welfare in this life and the next. [10 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(مالك بن أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن أنس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(مالك); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 62:2/q18/p3 The Qur'an's inner meanings and the right way to understand and practice its verses. — Al-Razi reports meanings within explicit verses and proper adherence; Bursevi reports wording and meaning and describes the Qur'an's realities. Al-Tabataba'i calls it true knowledge within the Qur'an; al-Biqa'i joins understanding to practice so each matter is placed well, in contrast with Israel's deviation in 62:5. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 62:2/q18/p4 Understanding, admonition, and sound judgment that yields beneficial action. — Al-A'mash, reported by al-Mawardi and Bursevi, says understanding and admonition. Al-Tabataba'i defines wisdom as judgment free of falsehood and harm; he links it with revelation and the wise Qur'an at 4:113, 36:2, 2:129, 3:164 and with 2:269. Al-Biqa'i joins mastered practice to transmitted and rational knowledge; Kur'an Yolu refers to 2:269. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأعمش); BURSEVI(الأعمش); TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 62:2/q18/p5 All divinely intended or inspired knowledge of religion, worldly life, and the Hereafter, including rational and transmitted disciplines. — Al-Tabrisi includes Qur'an, Sunna and applicable knowledge of what to do or avoid; al-Baydawi offers principles from transmitted and rational learning. Al-Alusi takes Book and wisdom together as a metaphor for all such disciplines; Bursevi includes law and refined spiritual practice, Elmalili knowledge needed to apply rulings, and Study Quran inspired knowledge of both worlds. [6 notes: TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]

## 62:2/q19 What does teaching the Book and wisdom accomplish and whom can it reach?  [وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ · interpretation]
- 62:2/q19/p1 It forms the first and later believers in revelation and practice and is itself a sign of the Prophet's mission. — Ibn Zayd extends teaching to later generations until the Hour, citing 9:100 and 59:10, and contrasts many foremost early people with fewer later ones via 56:11, 56:13, 56:27, 56:39. Bursevi says the Prophet taught Companions directly and later believers through perfected inheritors; he, al-Biqa'i, and al-Baydawi say this teaching alone suffices as a miracle. Ibn Kathir says the complete law meets needs of this life and the next, resolves doubts, gathers earlier messengers' excellences and grants Muhammad unique distinctions. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 62:2/q20 How should the repeated titles 'signs', 'Book', and 'wisdom' be understood?  [ءَايَٰتِهِۦ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ · rhetoric]
- 62:2/q20/p1 Each title brings out a distinct benefit of the same revelation and so marks a separate blessing. — Al-Alusi makes this explicit; al-Tabataba'i reports the proposal for signs and Book. Bursevi's report from al-Irshad likewise treats recitation, purification, and teaching as distinct bounties deserving thanks. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI(الإرشاد)]

## 62:2/q21 What is the force and syntax of 'wa-in kānū ... la-fī'?  [وَإِن كَانُوا۟ مِن قَبْلُ لَفِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The notes supply complementary particle parsing and clause placement.
- 62:2/q21/p1 In is a lightened emphatic inna; the lām marks the affirmative predicate and distinguishes it from negative or conditional in. — Al-Tabrisi calls the separating lām necessary; al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi identify the lighter particle; Bursevi excludes both conditional and negative readings; al-Alusi distinguishes conditional in; Ibn Ashur adds that the lightened particle governs neither noun nor predicate and kāna fills its expected place; al-Tabataba'i concurs. [7 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 62:2/q21/p2 The clause is parenthetical within the Messenger's enumerated tasks. — Ibn Ashur places the parenthesis either between coordinated elements or between the pronouns and the comitative object. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 62:2/q22 What does their former manifest error consist of?  [مِن قَبْلُ لَفِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether manifest error is specified as idolatry and jahiliyya or expressed more generally as distance from truth.
- 62:2/q22/p1 Before the Prophet's mission they were plainly astray through idolatry, unbelief, ignorance, and corrupt conduct. — Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Dahhak, Bursevi and others specify polytheism and idols. Al-Qurtubi says 'before' means before the Messenger or his sending. Al-Tabrisi calls it departure from truth and religion; al-Biqa'i says they abandoned Ishmael's religion and cites scorn for seekers like Zayd ibn Amr. Al-Nasafi says no error was greater; Elmalili adds bewilderment. Ibn Kathir says most people, not only Arabs, had strayed: Arabs altered Abraham's religion, scriptural communities altered their books, with a tiny remnant following Jesus. Al-Tabataba'i sees the clause as praise of divine favor after glorification. [20 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 62:2/q22/p2 They were far from truth in a broad sense, a state needing the Messenger's guidance. — Al-Qurtubi gives the general expression; al-Tabrisi names departure from truth and religion. Islahi recalls long ignorance as a contrast with later honor. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 62:2/q23 Does the description of former error include every Arab without exception?  [وَإِن كَانُوا۟ مِن قَبْلُ لَفِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance in monotheism removes the named individuals from a judgment that may also concern revealed law.
- 62:2/q23/p1 It describes the people predominantly; a few monotheists were exceptions. — Sa'di al-Mufti names Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Zayd ibn Amr, and Quss ibn Sa'ida and reports the Prophet's saying each would rise as a community alone; al-Alusi likewise names Waraqa. [2 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI-]
- 62:2/q23/p2 Even monotheist exceptions remained astray regarding revealed laws and rulings, so the description applies in that respect. — Bursevi accepts their guidance in divine oneness but rejects Sa'di's exemption as to law; he also says 93:7 does not mean the Prophet committed idolatry or sins. [2 notes: BURSEVI; BURSEVI+]

## 62:2/q24 Why mention their prior error and emphasize it here?  [وَإِن كَانُوا۟ مِن قَبْلُ لَفِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 62:2/q24/p1 It magnifies the divine favor and need for guidance, and rebuts the idea that such people taught the Prophet his revelation. — Ibn Atiyya makes the before-and-after contrast; al-Baydawi and Bursevi link it to need and the absence of a human teacher. Al-Biqa'i says the emphasis answers those who thought they followed sound religion and warns of later straying; the visible error heightens the miracle and urgency. Al-Alusi says it dispels a human-instruction hypothesis, and Islahi urges gratitude for ascent from long ignorance and dishonor. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 62:2/q25 How does this verse fulfill Abraham's prayer?  [بَعَثَ يَتْلُوا۟ عَلَيْهِمْ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ · links]
- 62:2/q25/p1 Muhammad's mission among Ishmael's descendants answers Abraham's prayer for a messenger who recites, teaches, and purifies. — Ibn Kathir cites 2:129 and places the sending after a long prophetic break; al-Tabataba'i says Abraham and Ishmael's prayer embraces Ishmaelite descendants beyond Mecca. Islahi identifies the same tasks, sees Muhammad as fulfilling Abraham's prayer and Jesus' prediction, and refers to the discussion of the Prophet's attributes in al-Baqara. Ibn al-Jawzi points to 2:129 for the preceding teaching; Kur'an Yolu also compares 2:129, 2:151, and 3:164; Study Quran lists those recurrent task verses and relates the teaching to 3:81, 4:54, 5:110, and 4:113. [15 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 62:2/q26 How does the verse fit the surrounding sūras and opening glorification?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace distinct links to the opening divine names, the previous sūra, and the rest of al-Jumu'a.
- 62:2/q26/p1 The praise of the Messenger follows the opening account of God's attributes and turns the passage from divine oneness to prophethood and favor. — Al-Razi notes the turn after 62:1; al-Tabataba'i says glorification grounds the mission's power to guide and perfect, and that the first three verses frame divine favor. Islahi says the Messenger manifests the Sovereign's directives and divine purity; al-Samarra'i notes that the tasbih opening is followed by sending the Messenger. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 62:2/q26/p2 It answers the previous sūra's account of Jesus and his followers by recalling prediction of Muhammad and Abraham's prayer for the Ishmaelites. — Abu Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr, reported by al-Biqa'i, says praise of this community counters a possible impression from 61:14 that Jesus' followers are superior; Islahi identifies Jesus' prediction and Abraham's prayer and says the Ishmaelites must value this favor despite Jewish opposition. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 62:2/q26/p3 The sūra repeatedly centers the human recipients of the Messenger's mission and those who come later. — Al-Samarra'i says this is why al-Jumu'a repeats mā. Islahi says the whole sura stresses the favor to Arabia's unlettered people, whom Jews envy; a later incident and the Muslims' weaknesses showed that many undervalued it. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 62:2/q27 What response does the favor of this mission call for?  [* · interpretation]
- 62:2/q27/p1 Recipients should recognize, honor, and preserve God's favor of guidance and carry it to others. — Ibn Atiyya says the verse enumerates favors to the Arabs; Islahi says Ishmaelites should resist Jewish schemes, honor the favor, encourage others, and serve as vanguard witnesses to humanity. He says the formerly disparaged people were entrusted with guiding the world, while the closing error clause reminds them what they escaped. Kur'an Yolu says revelation and prophethood are bestowed by divine will. Al-Biqa'i reads purification of stubborn people as evidence of divine power, contrasting Israelite refusal and showing power to purify all rational beings. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 62:2/q28 Which specific conversions illustrate the Messenger's purification and guidance?  [وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ · reports]
turns on: Different narrated cases of guidance.
- 62:2/q28/p1 Umayr ibn Wahb converted after plotting with Safwan to kill the Prophet, then brought the fleeing Safwan back after Mecca's conquest; Safwan converted too. — Al-Biqa'i recounts the plot after Badr, the Prophet's guidance of Umayr, and Umayr's later standing as a leading Companion. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 62:2/q28/p2 Al-Tufayl ibn Amir al-Dawsi received a luminous sign, then called his family and people, all of whom accepted Islam. — Al-Biqa'i relates the light, the call, and the response. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 62:2/q29 What understanding of wisdom and error follows from the Qur'an's transformation of human outlook?  [ٱلْحِكْمَةَ ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · interpretation]
- 62:2/q29/p1 Revelation corrects materialist assumptions about life and causation, teaches true beliefs and virtuous action through innate nature, and gives enduring life. — Al-Tabataba'i contrasts materialist pursuit of transient goods with religious preparation for the afterlife, noting professing believers can live inconsistently (2:148, 2:17). He grounds true belief and action in fitrah (30:30) and defines truth as conformity to reality with right guidance (9:33, 61:9, 10:35). Qur'anic teaching explains existence and human nature, replacing the view that this world is all there is (45:24, 29:64); it ascribes creation, command and judgment to God rather than night and day (34:33, 7:54, 12:40), denies independent human will and guidance (81:29, 28:56), strength (2:165), or honor from wealth and allies (4:139, 63:8), and says martyrs live (2:154). He calls these teachings wisdom used in calling and mutual counsel (16:125, 103:2-3, 2:269), a new life (8:24, 6:122). [19 notes: TABATABAI]

## 62:2/q30 Is close study of Qur'anic meanings a forbidden kind of interpretation?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether teaching the Book and wisdom includes inquiry into Qur'anic meanings.
- 62:2/q30/p1 No; prophetic teaching of the Qur'an's meanings warrants study beyond bare recitation. — Al-Tabataba'i rejects the assertion that exegesis is merely recitation and close study is prohibited. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]
- 62:2/q30/p2 Exegesis is only recitation and close study of meaning is forbidden interpretation. — Al-Tabataba'i reports and rejects this claim. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 62:2/q31 What inward effect can the appearance of God's beauty or majesty have on a servant?  [* · inward]
- 62:2/q31/p1 Beauty gathers thoughts around divine generosity; majesty consumes the self in God and effaces concern for this world and the next. — Al-Qushayri distinguishes the heart's response to beauty from the secret heart's response to majesty. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 62:2/q32 What divine qualities underlie the Messenger's commission?  [* · theology]
- 62:2/q32/p1 God knows and governs all things without doubt, bodily organs, tools, reflection, or consultation. — Al-Wahidi cites 6:59 for knowledge of seen, unseen, public and secret things; he says God knows without thinking, sees without eyes, creates without tools, speaks without tongue or sound, wills without resolve, and decrees without counsel. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 62:2/q33 What ancillary textual or scholarly notes accompany discussion of this verse?  [* · reports]
- 62:2/q33/p1 Some commentators direct the reader to parallel explanations of the same prophetic tasks and expressions elsewhere. — Al-Wahidi points to his earlier al-Baqara treatment; Abu Hayyan points to 3:164; Ibn al-Jawzi points to 2:78 for the unlettered and 2:129 for teaching; Islahi points to his al-Baqara account against Hadith rejecters; Kur'an Yolu points to 7:157-158 for the unlettered Prophet and 2:269 for wisdom. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 62:2/q33/p2 Al-Wahidi composed his commentary to address meanings, parsing, obscure points, and variant readings at scholars' urging amid neglect of learning. — He says a few genuine scholars remain virtuous and learning remains honorable despite fashionable pretense and neglect. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 62:2/q34 What minor phonetic and poetic reading matters are reported?  [* · readings]
