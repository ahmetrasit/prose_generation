# 79:18: 13 questions

## 79:18/q01 What does “هل لك” convey in Moses’ address?  [هَل لَّكَ · meaning]
- 79:18/q01/p1 It asks whether Pharaoh has a desire, need, or inclination toward the offered purification, in an established idiom of invitation. — Al-Biqāʿī and al-Bursevī gloss the desire or need; al-Elmalılı adds intention. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a concise fixed invitation, like asking a guest to stay, and cites Kaʿb’s هل لك فيما and Bujayr’s هل لك في التي. Al-Rāzī compares هل لك في. Al-Samīn gives هل لك في الخير as an idiom for رغبة. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the question as asking inclination. Ibn Kathīr frames the invitation as accepting a way that purifies Pharaoh. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(بجير,كعب); TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 79:18/q02 How is “هل لك إلى أن تزكى” grammatically understood?  [هَل لَّكَ إِلَىٰٓ أَن تَزَكَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the unusual إلى is explained by an omitted noun, an implicit verb of inviting, an expanded sense of the preposition, or figurative usage.
- 79:18/q02/p1 An implicit nominal such as need or desire supplies the omitted subject of the question. — Sībawayh says هل لك في ذلك, like لا عليك and من له في ذلك, omits understood حاجة through frequent use. Al-Fārisī supplies حاجة أو إربة; al-Wāḥidī gives the same and cites an anonymous poetic line. Al-Rāzī reports al-Wāḥidī and cites هل لكم فيها إلي. Al-Samīn describes لك as predicate of an omitted subject, with إلى أن attached to it, and cites the same poetic pattern. Al-Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr also identify an implicit subject, Ibn ʿĀshūr suggesting رغبة or ميل; al-Elmalılı similarly notes implied subject. Al-Ālūsī locates إلى with the omitted predicate and cites poetry, while recording others' omitted رغبة. [13 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARISI-HUJJA(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:18/q02/p2 The implicit noun can specifically be a way or access to purification. — Al-Rāzī allows an omitted سبيل, as does al-Samīn's paraphrase هل لك سبيل إلى التزكية; Abū Ḥayyān lists a way alongside desire and need. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 79:18/q02/p3 The unusual “إلى” reflects an implied invitation, so the construction includes the force of ‘I call you to.’ — Abū al-Baqāʾ explains إلى through an intended أدعوك; al-Samīn says this does not change parsing. Al-Burhān treats it as تضمين and says the prepositional phrase is governed by لك. Al-Itqān explains verbal تضمين as one verb carrying another's meaning and unusual preposition; it favors expanding the verb over the preposition because verb examples are more numerous, and applies أدعوك here. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn Jinnī's shared-meaning account via al-Ṭībī, comparing رفث إلى at 2:187; al-Ālūsī allows attachment to the utterance's meaning or an implicit expression. [10 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني); ITQAN(المحققون)+]
- 79:18/q02/p4 “إلى” is used with the sense of the more usual “في.” — Al-Burhān reports this explanation alongside the implicit-call reading. Al-Itqān explicitly glosses إلى as في here, ‘are you willing in this matter?’ Its discussion says some linguists and grammarians favor expanding the preposition's sense. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(المحققون)]
- 79:18/q02/p5 The preposition signals a figurative invitation rather than a syntactic inclusion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this alternative to Ibn Jinnī's تضمين; al-Bursevī also allows هل لك as figurative for ‘shall I attract and invite you?’ Al-Itqān says تضمين is figurative because a word cannot literally carry both meanings simultaneously. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ITQAN]
- 79:18/q02/p6 The object of “إلى” is elided and understood as desire, need, or a way toward something. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly proposes an omitted object of إلى and cites an unnamed poet; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also says its object is elided, yielding inclination to purification. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 79:18/q03 Why does Moses frame the offer as a question?  [هَل لَّكَ · rhetoric]
- 79:18/q03/p1 The question offers purification gently and tactfully, drawing Pharaoh toward acceptance. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it an invitation any reasonable person would accept. Al-Bursevī says the tactful, unthreatening offer could draw Pharaoh from obstinacy, avoiding accusations of polytheism, ignorance, or disbelief. Al-Ālūsī says it lowers Pharaoh from arrogance. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ ties its tact to 20:44. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes an encouraging offer. Al-Elmalılı says beginning with desire recognizes inclination and intention as prerequisites for voluntary acts. [10 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 79:18/q04 What purification does “تزكى” invite Pharaoh to seek?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether purification is the initial move to faith, its declaration, good conduct, comprehensive moral growth, repentance from tyranny, inward self-effacement, or charity; the charity gloss is disputed in Pharaoh's unbelieving context.
- 79:18/q04/p1 To leave unbelief or shirk and embrace faith, Islam, and God's oneness. — Muqātil includes repairing corruption and accepting God's oneness. Al-Ṭabarī says pure belief; Ibn Zayd links this sense throughout the Qur'an to Islam, citing 20:76 and 80:3, 7. Al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī cite 91:9 and 18:74 for purification from shirk, al-Fārisī also 80:7. Al-Māwardī transmits Qatāda. Al-Baghawī says both readings indicate escape from shirk, Islam, and uprightness. Al-Rāzī says the invitation includes monotheism and religious law. Others mention rebellion, tyranny, obedience, or freeing Israel as extensions of the change. Ibn Kathīr glosses the requested purification as submitting and obeying. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(ابن زيد); FARISI-HUJJA; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز ويعقوب، والآخرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 79:18/q04/p2 To cleanse deficiencies and vices and acquire virtues: a full moral purification that includes faith. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says restricting the sense to conversion or the declaration is too narrow. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Biqāʿī agree; al-Biqāʿī specifies outward and inward vices, with faith and freeing Israel as the lowest beginning. Al-Bursevī adds the opacity of human and natural conditions. Al-Elmalılı adds growth in Islamic qualities; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ adds growth of the soul's virtues. Al-Rāzī speaks of every defect and what is unfitting. [10 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 79:18/q04/p3 To profess that there is no deity but God. — Al-Ṭabarī reports ʿIkrima. Al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās, and also Muqātil's related formulation of affirming divine oneness. Al-Baghawī reports Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Qurṭubī transmits it through al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Qushayrī reports a promise attached to the declaration: an imperishable kingdom, ageless youth, four hundred years of comfort, then paradise. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the formula is contained in a broader purification. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI]
- 79:18/q04/p4 To do good. — Al-Māwardī and al-Wāḥidī both report this gloss from al-Kalbī. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 79:18/q04/p5 To repent of tyranny and return to God. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this reading, with ensuing faith-driven fear motivating obedience and restraining sin. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 79:18/q04/p6 In an inward reading, to annihilate selfhood. — Al-Qāshānī says guidance to fear is knowledge of the unity of the Essence, which softens and dissolves selfhood. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 79:18/q04/p7 To give alms. — Abū ʿAmr's explanation derives this from assimilated تتزكى. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Khālawayh reject applying it here because Moses first called an unbeliever to belief, not charity; Ibn Khālawayh cites the wording زكية in 18:74. Al-Qurṭubī reports Abū ʿAmr's preference for the light reading and belief sense. [0 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عمرو)-; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو)-]

## 79:18/q05 What lexical sense and form underlie “تزكى”?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · meaning]
- 79:18/q05/p1 It draws on purity and growth in goodness; the reflexive form conveys seeking or working to become pure. — Al-Jishumī says zakāh is growth in good, zākī grows in good, and tazakkī seeks that status. Al-Rāzī takes zakī as free of defects, citing 18:74 and 91:9. Ibn ʿĀshūr says zakāt is increase, conventionally inward good without a contextual clue, citing 91:9–10; he says the reflexive form implies self-training. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says zakā is growth in goodness and blessing. Al-Elmalılı joins cleansing and flourishing. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 79:18/q06 How is “تزكى” read and formed?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · readings]
turns on: How the original two tāʾs of تتزكى are reduced, which readers transmit each form, and whether the stronger form carries a distinct sense.
- 79:18/q06/p1 With a doubled zāy, assimilating a tāʾ from original تتزكى. — Reports assign it variously to Nāfiʿ and Ibn Kathīr; the Hijaz readers and Yaʿqūb; Abū Jaʿfar, Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, and Yaʿqūb; and in variant transmissions Abū ʿAmr. Al-Ṭabarī's summary differs, naming Medinans generally as doubled. Al-Fārisī cites proximity of tāʾ and zāy; al-Samīn compares assimilated تصدى at 80:6; Qirāʾāt ER says the cluster contracts while preserving root ز ك و and Form V. Al-Biqāʿī hears more complete purification in the heavier form. Abū ʿAmr's charity gloss for it is contested in the interpretation question. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عمرو); NAHHAS(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع,عباس عن أبي عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير ويعقوب,أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز ويعقوب، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير، نافع، أبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو,نافع); BAYDAWI-FULL(الحجازيان ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(حجازى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحرميان وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن كثير); BIQAI-FULL(الحجازيان ويعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(الحرميان); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو جعفر ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER(Assim); NASHR(المدنيان وابن كثير ويعقوب)]
- 79:18/q06/p2 With a light zāy, dropping one tāʾ from original تتزكى. — Reports assign it to the other readers, with different accounts of Abū ʿAmr; al-Naḥḥās expressly assigns a light reading to Medinans and Abū ʿAmr, while al-Ṭabarī reports Kufans and Basrans generally light. Al-Fārisī finds deletion and lightening more natural; al-Ṭabarī calls it more eloquent. Al-Baghawī and al-Naḥḥās say both readings share the sense of purification. Al-Samīn says grammarians dispute which of two initial letters is omitted and compares 97:4. Al-Biqāʿī says lightening suggests an easier, lesser first degree. Al-Elmalılı describes the underlying تتزكّى, though calls it an imperfect in tafʿīl. [22 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الحجاز ويعقوب، والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الباقون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحرميان وأبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو جعفر ويعقوب); NASHR(الباقون); FARISI-HUJJA+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو)+; TAB-FULL+]

## 79:18/q07 What does the invitation imply about choice and divine decree?  [هَل لَّكَ إِلَىٰٓ أَن تَزَكَّىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether the offered choice implies Pharaoh's independent power to accept, in light of the reported decree and al-Rāzī's rejection of the Muʿtazilite inference.
- 79:18/q07/p1 Its wording presents purification as something Pharaoh may intend and choose. — Al-Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilites' argument that if God caused Pharaoh's response, Moses' appeal would turn against him. Al-Elmalılı says asking first about desire shows inclination and intention precede voluntary action. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL-]
- 79:18/q07/p2 God's hidden decree prevented Pharaoh's acceptance despite Moses' gentle counsel. — Al-Qushayrī says the decree turned Pharaoh's heart away and planted refusal. Al-Rāzī rejects the Muʿtazilite inference by referring to an answer he says he already gave, without restating it here. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL]

## 79:18/q08 What rule for calling people to religion is drawn from the wording?  [هَل لَّكَ · interpretation]
- 79:18/q08/p1 A caller should begin with kindness, gentle speech, and restraint. — Al-Jishumī says this is an obligation. Al-Rāzī links Moses' invitation to 20:44 and the instruction to Muhammad in 3:159, requiring avoidance of harshness. Study Quran says Moses was told to invite Pharaoh peacefully to follow revelation first, as the Prophet invited Quraysh. Al-Bursevī says tact can draw a resistant hearer from obstinacy. [4 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:18/q09 How does purification lead into guidance and fear in the next verse?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · links]
- 79:18/q09/p1 Cleansing precedes adornment; knowledge of God through guidance then produces fear. — Al-Bursevī invokes 35:28 and makes fear the aim of knowing God. Al-Ālūsī similarly says purification comes first as cleansing before adornment. Al-Qāshānī's inward account makes knowledge of the unity of the Essence dissolve selfhood. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports fear from faith motivating obedience and restraining sin. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; QASHANI; TABATABAI]

## 79:18/q10 How does this address relate to Moses' instruction in Ṭā Hā?  [* · links]
- 79:18/q10/p1 It enacts and explains the command in 20:44 to speak gently to Pharaoh. — Al-Rāzī cites 20:44 alongside 3:159. Al-Bursevī says the unthreatening offer spells out what purification and guidance entail. Al-Ālūsī says it draws Pharaoh down from arrogance. Al-Elmalılı says these verses unpack the gentle address and condense Pharaoh's tyranny into his boast. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses offers guidance. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also cites 20:42–44 and its hope Pharaoh may remember or fear. Study Quran compares Muhammad's peaceful invitation to Quraysh. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:18/q11 What report explains Moses' concern that Pharaoh would refuse?  [* · reports]
- 79:18/q11/p1 Moses asked why he should go if Pharaoh would not accept; God ordered him on, saying twelve thousand angels seek knowledge of destiny without attaining it. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes the report to Sakhr ibn Juwayriyya; al-Bursevī reports the same episode without that attribution in the note. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(صخر بن جويرية); BURSEVI]

## 79:18/q12 How does the Pharaoh episode serve the sūra's wider argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw either the sūra's resurrection warning through Pharaoh's failure or the proof of divine lordship and fair warning through a messenger.
- 79:18/q12/p1 Pharaoh's failed worldly power supports the sūra's warning about final fate and resurrection. — Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra establishes resurrection and gathering after the preceding sūras' fuller exposition; its oaths bring the matter to a climax. He says Pharaoh's reputation and projects failed to save him, citing 28:38 and 40:37, and that God reassured Moses against Pharaoh's power. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:18/q12/p2 It warns resurrection-denying polytheists, consoles the Prophet, and establishes God's lordship through Moses' authorized mission. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Pharaoh's destruction proves Moses' divine authorization, which entails God's lordship over people against the claim of lesser lords. Study Quran notes the wider rule that no community is punished before a messenger brings guidance, citing 28:59, 26:208, 6:131, 10:13, and 15:4. [3 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:18/q13 What grammatical analogies involving “هل” and neighboring forms appear in the cited discussions?  [هَل · grammar]
turns on: The cited grammar chapter offers distinct analogies rather than one analysis of the verse; its explicit disagreement concerns whether من قدام is indefinite or definite and feminine.
- 79:18/q13/p1 Particle-like or ambiguous adverbial forms are restricted in declension and can have fixed vocalization. — Sībawayh lists أين، متى، كيف، حيث، إذ، إذا، قبل and بعد as ambiguous, non-fully-declinable adverbs, akin to interjections or non-nouns. قبل and بعد cannot freely stand like nouns and resemble particles across times; قبل، بعد and حيث take final ḍamma, though some analogize حيث to أين. He notes لدن's limited jussive behavior, its likeness to قط, and قط/حسب for ‘only’; لد abbreviates لدن, restored before pronouns, and resembles عن. Where two unvowelled consonants meet, the latter gets a vowel, but stays still after a vowel, as with هل، بل، أجل، نعم and جير. He also notes ḍamma following ḍamma in a form like رد يا فتى. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 79:18/q13/p2 Several comparison and place expressions shift between adverbial, nominal, adjectival, and elliptical uses. — Al-Khalīl says مع takes accusative since معا is independent and pronoun constructions are adverbial. He treats من عل as more declinable; he and Sībawayh contrast أول and حكم with invariant الذي and من. Al-Khalīl reads أول in مذ عام أول as adjective with من عامك omitted, and also as a noun; أول من أمس and بعد غد omit comparisons. Sībawayh says ابدأ به أول omits من كذا, obligatory in عام أول. Al-Khalīl records rare adverbial مذ عام أول. Sībawayh makes أسفل an adverb in زيد أسفل منك, comparing 8:42, and comparative أفعل in جاء من أسفل, comparing 33:10. Al-Khalīl says دون، فوق، تحت، قبل، بعد accept complements and nominal uses; poetry attests من تحت، من عل، من أمامه and من دون. A poetic أولا can be adjective or adverb; al-Jaʿdī exemplifies accusative أماما and خلفا by analogy with يمنة and شأمة. [12 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 79:18/q13/p3 من أمام and related bare place terms are indefinite unless attached to a definite noun. — Al-Khalīl prefers this on Arab usage; Sībawayh cites ʿAlawiyyīn and Tamīmī forms من قديديمة and من وريئة, comparing صباحا and other time adverbs. Yūnus instead treats من قدام as definite and feminine diptote; Sībawayh says none of his Arab informants used that analysis. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH; SIBAWAYH(الخليل)+]
- 79:18/q13/p4 Yūnus treats من قدام as definite and feminine, hence diptote. — Sībawayh reports this to reject it against the Arab usage he consulted. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس); SIBAWAYH(الخليل)-]
- 79:18/q13/p5 When used as a personal name, these forms change shape like لو، هل، بل، ليت and ذا. — Sībawayh uses هل among the examples of forms adapted on naming. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 79:18/q13/p6 Poetic necessity can make معكم rhyme like هل. — Sībawayh cites al-Rāʿī's verse. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 79:18/q13/p7 هل is also analyzed in the summons هلم, whose derivation and dialectal inflection are disputed. — Al-Itqān says هلم is an invitation. One derivation combines hā with lammamtu ‘I gathered or set right’ after dropping alif; another parses hal umm, ‘will you approach?’, linked to هل لك إلى. Hijaz leaves هلم unchanged in dual/plural, while Tamīm inflects it. [4 notes: ITQAN]
- 79:18/q13/p8 General rhetorical and syntactic examples show expressions used beyond their literal function. — Al-Itqān lists prepositions, commands, prohibitions, questions, wishes, hopes and calls as rhetorical forms. Al-Burhān records two other disputed analyses: for accompaniment waw, some make waw govern its object, while the preferred account lets the preceding verb govern through waw; for a verb joined to a verbal noun, Kūfans allow it, while the preferred account uses conjunction and implicit أن. [3 notes: ITQAN; BURHAN+]
