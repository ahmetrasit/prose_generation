# 22:5: 65 questions

## 22:5/q01 Who is addressed by 'O people'?   [ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the general wording primarily addresses Meccan deniers or all people.
- 22:5/q01/p1 The unbelievers or polytheists of Mecca, especially resurrection deniers. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās via Wāḥidī, and others identify the Meccan audience; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they are the primary target, while the wording remains general. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:5/q01/p2 The wording addresses people generally, while deniers are especially in view. — Ibn al-Jawzī preserves the general wording; Biqāʿī allows both scopes and associates the broad address with prevalence of denial. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:5/q02 Why frame the doubt with 'if'?  [إِن كُنتُمْ فِى رَيْبٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the condition depicts entrenched doubt, weak doubt, or accommodates a mixed audience.
- 22:5/q02/p1 It poses actual doubt hypothetically so the proof can dispel or rebuke it. — Bursevī says the evidence uproots doubt; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 43:5; Ālūsī says the condition ascribes doubt to deniers without strengthening their case. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:5/q03 What does rayb mean here?  [رَيْبٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it is plain doubt or doubt colored by disturbance, suspicion, or fear.
- 22:5/q03/p1 Doubt about resurrection. — Yaḥyā, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Ibn ʿAbbās use the ordinary gloss; Qurṭubī also records accusation and need in other contexts. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNATIYYA-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,القرطبي)]
- 22:5/q03/p2 A troubling, ugly doubt, with possible fear or suspicion. — Ṭabrisī calls it an ugly doubt; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues rayb and shakk are not synonyms, citing Qurʾānic distribution, lexicographers, and poetry; Ibn Fāris adds fear. [6 notes: TABRISI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأثير,ابن فارس,الراغب,الطبري)]

## 22:5/q04 What aspect of resurrection is doubted?  [مِّنَ ٱلْبَعْثِ · interpretation]
turns on: Possibility or actual occurrence.
- 22:5/q04/p1 Whether reviving the dead is possible and within divine power. — Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī emphasize possibility because the following creation proofs show capacity; Ālūsī cites 22:7 for occurrence. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 22:5/q04/p2 Whether resurrection will in fact occur. — Bursevī notes this alternative; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it return to God; Islāḥī explains idolaters' reluctance to face accountability. [3 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 22:5/q05 How is البعث read?  [ٱلْبَعْثِ · readings]
turns on: Whether ʿayn is quiescent or open, and whether vowel alternation is a general guttural rule or only attested usage.
- 22:5/q05/p1 The usual reading has quiescent ʿayn. — Basrans treat open forms as lexical dialect variants; Kufans permit lightening around gutturals, a generalization contested by Abū Isḥāq and al-Samīn. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ(البصريون); NAHHAS(الكوفيون); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 22:5/q05/p2 Al-Ḥasan reads with open ʿayn. — Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and later commentators compare other guttural-middle words; Basrans call it dialectal, Kufans a lightening pattern. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن بن أبي الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ZAJJAJ]

## 22:5/q06 How does 'We created you' answer the condition?  [فَإِنَّا خَلَقْنَٰكُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause itself is the answer or evidence for an omitted answer.
- 22:5/q06/p1 Creation is evidence for an implicit command to consider one's origin and recognize resurrection. — Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr note that creation preceded the hearers' doubt, so the literal act of creation cannot be a consequent event; Ālūsī permits the clause to occupy the answer's place. [3 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:5/q06/p2 The creation clause directly presents the proof that resolves the conditional doubt. — Ṭabarī and others present the staged first creation as the immediate argument; the one who originated life can restore it. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 22:5/q07 How were humans created from dust?  [تُرَابٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether dust refers to Adam's origin or to the food-chain origin of every human.
- 22:5/q07/p1 It refers to Adam, ancestor of humankind, with descendants included through him. — Zajjāj, Ṭabarī and most commentators name Adam; Rāzī cites 3:59 and 20:55; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes Eve and later reproductive descent; Ālūsī prefers this. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(commentators); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 22:5/q07/p2 Each person derives from earth through nourishment, which becomes reproductive fluid and bodily matter. — Rāzī traces semen and menstrual blood through food to plants, earth and water; Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān give the same route; Study Quran includes animals and milk. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 22:5/q07/p3 Dust signals humanity's lowly biological kinship with earthy substances. — Asad cites 3:59 and 23:12; Muqātil recalls that humans began as nothing. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; MUQATIL]

## 22:5/q08 What is a nutfah here?  [نُّطْفَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the fluid is semen, Adam's own semen, or a fertilized ovum; its base water sense varies.
- 22:5/q08/p1 Semen from which Adam's descendants develop, literally a drop or quantity of water. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī and Ālūsī discuss dripping, flowing, or pouring roots; Ṭabrisī includes male and female fluid; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says water may be little or much. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي,البيضاوي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 22:5/q08/p2 Adam's own semen is the intended drop. — Al-Naqqāsh is quoted by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān; Ālūsī calls this remote. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:5/q08/p3 It may name the sperm or the fertilized ovum produced from it. — Kur'an Yolu uses embryonic context; Elmalılı speaks more precisely of the seed within semen. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]

## 22:5/q09 What is an ʿalaqah?  [عَلَقَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word describes congealed blood or an attached early embryo.
- 22:5/q09/p1 A piece of fresh, thick, congealed or clotted blood. — Abū ʿUbayd emphasizes intense redness; Wāḥidī cites al-Qaṭāmī; al-Samīn cites an Arabic saying about battlefield blood; commentators describe the preceding fluid thickening. [26 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS(أبو عبيد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 22:5/q09/p2 It denotes a moist, clinging or attached early stage. — Ibn al-Jawzī derives it from adhesion; Islāḥī allows a living leech-like referent; Kur'an Yolu prefers a fertilized ovum attached to the uterine wall. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 22:5/q10 What is a muḍghah?  [مُّضْغَةٍۢ · meaning]
- 22:5/q10/p1 A small piece of flesh about the size of a mouthful to chew. — Ibn Qutayba compares names for scooped or eaten quantities; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains its morphology; a hadith calls the heart a muḍghah. [25 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 22:5/q11 What distinguishes formed from unformed?  [مُّخَلَّقَةٍۢ غَيْرِ مُخَلَّقَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the contrast concerns form, completeness, ensoulment, miscarriage, term birth, or the primordial covenant.
- 22:5/q11/p1 One embryo is visibly shaped, complete or smooth; the other is not yet or imperfectly shaped. — Qatāda's complete/incomplete gloss is widespread. Zajjāj, Rāzī, Zamakhsharī, and Nasafī allow deficiencies in limbs and faculties; Rāzī prefers this because the address concerns future humans. [44 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(آخرون,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن عباس وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو العباس,ابن الأعرابي,قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والشعبي وأبو العالية); TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(القفال,قتادة والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشعبي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Ibn 'Abbas and Qatadah); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Katâde,İbn Abbas); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 22:5/q11/p2 The formed pregnancy lives or reaches birth; the unformed one miscarries before completion. — Mujāhid sometimes describes formed and unformed miscarriages; Ibn Masʿūd's angel report has an unformed drop expelled as blood. Ṭabarī prefers expulsion before completed shaping and ensoulment. [53 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,بعضهم,عامر,عبد الله,مجاهد); THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الضحاك,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي,الكلبي,عبد الله بن مسعود,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والشعبي وأبو العالية); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,السدي,الفراء وابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الشاعر,الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والشعبي وأبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عامر الشعبي,عبد الله,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,السدي,الشعبي,عكرمة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة والشعبي وأبي العالية وعكرمة); STUDYQURAN(commentators (R, Ṭ)); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); TAB-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 22:5/q11/p3 The formed receives a spirit; the unformed lacks ensoulment. — Wāḥidī prefers this explanation, and Ibn ʿAbbās links it to life and the widow's waiting period; Bursevī reports an inward version in which unformed is an image without soul. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(التأويلات); ASAD-NOTES(Tabari); WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 22:5/q11/p4 The formed has the primordial covenant; the unformed does not. — Ibn ʿAbbās in ʿAṭāʾ's transmission and a report from Abū Jaʿfar identify formed particles in Adam's loins under the covenant; the latter includes expelled semen and pre-ensoulment miscarriage among unformed. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر)]
- 22:5/q11/p5 Every developmental stage is created and may count as mukhallaqah. — Ibn al-ʿArabī reasons from the root; Qurṭubī prefers repeated creation and cites 23:14, against a sense restricted to an imaged fetus. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 22:5/q12 How are the adjectives 'formed and unformed' construed or read?  [مُّخَلَّقَةٍۢ غَيْرِ مُخَلَّقَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Whether they are genitive adjectives or accusative circumstantial expressions.
- 22:5/q12/p1 The common genitive forms modify muḍghah. — Bursevī and Ālūsī make the attachment explicit; al-Samīn identifies the general reading. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 22:5/q12/p2 Accusative forms are read as circumstantial or detached qualifiers. — Al-Kisāʾī permits them; Ibn Abī ʿAblah reads both accusative; Sībawayh allows a circumstantial qualifier even after an indefinite noun, though rare. [7 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:5/q13 What do reports say about fetal stages and angelic decrees?  [نُّطْفَةٍۢ عَلَقَةٍۢ مُّضْغَةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the timing of shaping and angelic writing.
- 22:5/q13/p1 A drop, clot, and morsel each last forty days, followed by ensoulment and decrees. — Ibn Masʿūd's Prophetic report lists provision, life, deeds, and fortune; Qurṭubī derives four months and links it to the widow's waiting period. Ibn Kathīr transmits Ṣaḥīḥayn versions. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 22:5/q13/p2 An angel shapes or records the embryo after roughly forty to forty-five nights. — Abū Dharr and Ḥudhayfa versions mention sex and fortune or bodily features; Ibn Kathīr cites Muslim. Bursevī says an early reference to form means its decree, with visible form later. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو ذر); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك,حذيفة بن أسيد الغفاري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(غير مسمى); BURSEVI]
- 22:5/q13/p3 A settled drop is classified as formed or expelled, and the formed child's particulars are copied from the Mother Book. — Ibn Masʿūd and related reports list sex, provision, lifespan, deeds, fortune, and sometimes burial place; Ṭabāṭabāʾ reconciles changeable and unchangeable tablets. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله); THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(عبدالله); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر)]

## 22:5/q14 What does 'so that We may make clear to you' disclose?  [لِّنُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether its object is creation, power, resurrection, guidance, or intentionally left open.
- 22:5/q14/p1 The successive stages and manner of creation in wombs. — Yaḥyā, Ibn Qutayba, Wāḥidī's Ibn Muslim, and Qatāda focus on explaining the stages of human creation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports that incomplete embryos and miscarriages make womb stages clear. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 22:5/q14/p2 God's power, wisdom, and choice in creating varied forms. — Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī say the stages display divine power; Biqāʿī argues variation defeats nature as a sufficient cause; Rāzī and Elmalılı add God's free choice. [17 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول آخر); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:5/q14/p3 The truth or possibility of resurrection. — The author of al-Naẓm and Bursevī read the stages as disclosure of resurrection; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the clause parenthetical; Kur'an Yolu prefers gradual development as the fit with the passage. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 22:5/q14/p4 What people should do, avoid, and practice in worship. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this gloss; Zajjāj connects human creation with guidance and right conduct. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ZAJJAJ]
- 22:5/q14/p5 The object is omitted to suggest knowledge and signs beyond enumeration. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, and Ālūsī emphasize breadth; Ālūsī prefers this over restricting the object to resurrection. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 22:5/q15 How is لنبين read?  [لِّنُبَيِّنَ لَكُمْ · readings]
turns on: First-person nūn or third-person yāʾ.
- 22:5/q15/p1 The common reading uses nūn: 'so that We make clear.' — Rāzī and later grammarians contrast this with third-person variants. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 22:5/q15/p2 Ibn Abī ʿAblah and others read with yāʾ: 'so that He make clear.' — Ibn al-Jawzī also names Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī; Ālūsī calls it a shift in grammatical person. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمران الجوني وابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة)]

## 22:5/q16 How is ونقر read and connected syntactically?  [وَنُقِرُّ · readings]
turns on: Indicative independent clause or accusative purpose; variant yāʾ and pouring-root forms.
- 22:5/q16/p1 The majority read indicative nuqirru, a new statement about retaining embryos. — Sībawayh, Farrāʾ, Zajjāj and others separate it from the purpose clause; Zajjāj argues humans were created for guidance, not simply retention; Kashf prefers coordination with 'We created.' [22 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI(عاصم وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,صاحب النظم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور,الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL(المفضل); BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,العامة); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:5/q16/p2 An accusative/subjunctive reading coordinates retention, sometimes delivery, with 'to make clear.' — Transmitted from ʿĀṣim or Yaʿqūb; Zamakhsharī takes retention through birth and accountability as a second purpose; Abū al-Baqāʾ proposes an outcome lām, challenged by al-Samīn. [22 notes: NAHHAS(أبو حاتم,عاصم); THALABI(عاصم وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المفضل عن عاصم); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,يعقوب وعاصم في رواية); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,ابن جبارة,الزمخشري,عاصم,يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(المفضل وأبو حاتم); ZAJJAJ-; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)-; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الحاجب,الزجاج)-]
- 22:5/q16/p3 Third-person variants use يقر or يُقر, with indicative or accusative endings. — Ibn Masʿūd, Abū Rajāʾ, Abū al-Jawzāʾ and Abū Isḥāq al-Sabīʿī are named; some yāʾ variants also govern يخرجكم. ## 22:5/q28 What does knowing nothing after knowledge mean?  [لِكَيْلَا يَعْلَمَ مِنۢ بَعْدِ عِلْمٍۢ شَيْـًۭٔا · interpretation]

## 22:5/q30 What inward meanings are given to bodily decline and earth's revival?  [أَرْذَلِ ٱلْعُمُرِ ٱلْأَرْضَ هَامِدَةًۭ · inward]
