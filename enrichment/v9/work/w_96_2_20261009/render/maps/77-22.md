# 77:22: 5 questions

## 77:22/q01 What is the known appointed measure?  [قَدَرٍۢ مَّعْلُومٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the measure names birth or the whole period to birth, a specified number of months, formation of the embryo, or the phases within gestation; the notes also differ on whether its length varies.
- 77:22/q01/p1 The divinely appointed span of pregnancy, ending at birth. — Mawardi, Wahidi-Wajiz, Baghawi, Qurtubi's unnamed explanation, Abu Hayyan, and Elmalili call it the time of birth; Wahidi-Basit gives both the duration and its endpoint, while Ibn al-Jawzi and Tabatabai call it the duration of pregnancy. Baydawi says God appoints the period until birth. Kashshaf, Nasafi, Biqai, and Alusi specify nine months, less, or more; Nasafi says God decrees it, and Biqai says God alone knows it. Ibn Atiyya says the term differs by individual, is known to God, and cannot be known by people in advance; Razi likewise says only God knows the womb's term until birth and cites 31:34. Ibn Ashur explains that qadar with an open dal is a fixed, specified measure, here a span of time. Islahi says the secure womb holds the fluid until its appointed time so its potential develops before God brings it out. Kur'an Yolu identifies the fluid as sperm and the secure place as the womb, citing 75:37. [19 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 77:22/q01/p2 Nine months. — Muqatil specifies nine months as the known term. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 77:22/q01/p3 Six or nine months, after which God determines the outcome. — Ibn Kathir gives these two specified lengths and says God then determines the outcome. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 77:22/q01/p4 The term lasts until the embryo is formed. — Qurtubi reports this explanation from Mujahid. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 77:22/q01/p5 The phases of gestation. — The Study Quran refers to the gestational phases described at 22:5 and 23:14. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 77:22/q02 What develops during and at the end of the appointed period?  [إِلَىٰ قَدَرٍۢ مَّعْلُومٍۢ · interpretation]
- 77:22/q02/p1 God develops the child in the womb, determines its traits and outcome, and brings it forth at the appointed time. — Wahidi-Basit reports al-Kalbi that God fashions traits such as height or shortness and male or female. Ibn Kathir says God determines the outcome after the set period. Ibn Ashur says the fa of the next verse makes apportionment follow from keeping the embryo until gestation ends and its stages produce infants. Islahi says the secure womb lets the fluid's destined potential develop and God brings it out when time expires. Kur'an Yolu says God forms humans from simple semen, develops them through womb stages, grants material and spiritual abilities, and makes them Earth's most perfect beings. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 77:22/q03 How are the following verse's light and doubled readings understood?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the doubled form is simply equivalent to the light form or permits Abu Ali's analysis as 'the estimators'; al-Farra says 77:23 does not decide for the light reading.
- 77:22/q03/p1 The light and doubled readings have the same meaning. — Wahidi-Basit reports al-Farra that Arab usage permits both, with 86:17 mentioned; therefore an argument from 77:23 for the light reading is not decisive. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)]
- 77:22/q03/p2 The doubled form can mean 'the estimators.' — Wahidi-Basit reports Abu Ali's analysis: extra letters can be dropped as in the Arabic forms he cites; he refers to 15:22. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]

## 77:22/q04 How does human creation support resurrection and judgment?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give different grounds: power to repeat creation, the wisdom of creation and provision together with moral awareness, or lordship and the obligation to recompense.
- 77:22/q04/p1 God's first creation of humans shows His power to recreate them, making denial of resurrection baseless. — Islahi says physical structure and creation stages display God's power, providence, and wisdom, so resurrection is certain and its deniers doomed; creation from mud and an insignificant drop makes recreation possible even after bones decay. Islahi also describes the Qur'an's argument from fluid as one of God's power, wisdom, and creativity. Kur'an Yolu calls human origins and development a striking proof of creative power and says the creator of a perfect human from simple water can resurrect that person after death. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 77:22/q04/p2 The wisdom of human creation, the system sustaining humans, and innate moral awareness require a Day of Judgment. — Islahi says the signs of power, wisdom, and creativity in making man entail judgment, as does the elaborate provision system that sustains him; people's innate knowledge of good and evil is further evidence. His overview says these arguments make judgment certain, beyond merely showing it possible from creation out of fluid. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 77:22/q04/p3 Creation and governance prove God's sole lordship, which entails human submission and a Day of Decision for recompense. — Tabatabai says creatures owe submission and obligation to their sole Lord; these duties require recompense. He describes the passage as proving sole lordship and the Day of Decision. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 77:22/q05 How does the creation passage fit the surrounding sura?  [* · links]
- 77:22/q05/p1 It uses human creation as evidence for the Day of Decision within the sura's sequence of signs and warnings. — Islahi says the passage turns from historical evidence to human creation as evidence for resurrection and judgment; after signs within humans and in the external world, the ensuing passage makes unseen punishment vivid to deniers. Tabatabai says this stretch proves sole lordship and the Day of Decision, recalls punishment for deniers and blessings for the pious, and reproaches arrogance toward worship and revelation. Kur'an Yolu says people respond to God's gifts with ingratitude and disobedience, despite the recurring warning 'Woe on that day to the deniers' since 77:18. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
