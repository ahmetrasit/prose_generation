# 36:79: 20 questions

## 36:79/q01 Why is the Prophet commanded to say this answer?  [قُلْ · rhetoric]
- 36:79/q01/p1 The command makes the Prophet rebuke the challenger and direct him to reason from his own first creation. — Al-Biqāʿī says listeners await a reply and the address is to one able to grasp the dispute; Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say it shames the denier for forgetting his natural constitution; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says remembering creation from a sperm drop would answer the objection. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 36:79/q02 Why does the answer explicitly say 'revives them'?  [يُحْيِيهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the full verbal reply makes the denied act explicit, follows the question's clause form, or deliberately changes its form.
- 36:79/q02/p1 Repeating the verb makes the denied resurrection explicit and forestalls evasion. — Al-Burhān contrasts other question-answer passages where the answer omits what the question supplies (23:84–85, 6:12, 10:31); al-Biqāʿī says explicit repetition prevents evasive argument. Al-Burhān also explains that a short answer such as 'Zayd' binds it to the question and restricts the answer, whereas 'Zayd came' can be an independent sentence. [3 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 36:79/q02/p2 The verbal answer matches the verbal question, with an omitted verb understood in shorter answers. — Ibn Mālik says answers normally match the question's clause type and analyzes a short 'Zayd' answer as a verbal clause with an omitted verb; al-Itqān cites 5:4 and 36:79 as verbal answers and 43:9 in its discussion. [2 notes: BURHAN(ابن مالك); ITQAN]
- 36:79/q02/p3 The answer deliberately uses a different expression from the question for a rhetorical purpose. — Ibn al-Muraḥḥal compares the answer about the lunar phases in 2:189; al-Burhān calls this analysis questionable. [1 notes: BURHAN(شهاب الدين بن المرحل)]

## 36:79/q03 Does life inhabit bones, so that the bones themselves can be revived?  [يُحْيِيهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether life requires felt sensation and whether 'revives them' applies literally to bones or to their owner or restored bodily form.
- 36:79/q03/p1 Life enters bones, and the plain sense says that the bones themselves are revived. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent sense. Al-Ālūsī says the verse appears to support it and attributes it to the Shāfiʿī position, also Mālik and Aḥmad; Ibn Zuhr finds Galen inconsistent about bone sensation and argues bones sense slowly because animal spirit prevents their decay during life. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إبن زهر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 36:79/q03/p2 Bones do not themselves have life; reviving them means restoring fresh, moist bones in a living, perceptive body, or reviving their owner. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the fresh-body interpretation. Al-Ālūsī reports jurists' and philosophers' argument that life entails sensation but cutting bones causes no pain; he reports both the owner and moist-form readings, yet says these depart from the apparent sense. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:79/q04 Are a dead animal's bones impure?  [يُحْيِيهَا · law]
turns on: Whether bones have life and whether carrion impurity follows death itself or moisture and flowing blood.
- 36:79/q04/p1 They are impure because bones have life and therefore undergo death. — Al-Biqāʿī draws the ruling from life entering bones. Al-Ālūsī says the verse appears to support the Shāfiʿī ruling, attributed also to Mālik and Aḥmad. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 36:79/q04/p2 They remain pure after death. — Al-Ālūsī reports one argument that bones never had life, and another that even living bones remain pure because carrion impurity comes from moisture and flowing blood, which bones lack. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:79/q05 Who is the one said to originate and revive the bones?  [ٱلَّذِىٓ أَنشَأَهَآ · referent]
- 36:79/q05/p1 God, who created the bones initially, is the one who will revive them. — Muqātil says He first created them from nothing; al-Ṭabarī says they did not exist before; al-Baghawī says 'created' explains 'brought into being.' Al-Wāḥidī cites Ibn ʿAbbās glossing first creation as initial origination. Al-Nasafī glosses anshaʾahā as 'created them.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the Prophet describing origination without a prior model. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 36:79/q06 How does initial creation prove resurrection?  [أَنشَأَهَآ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍۢ · theology]
- 36:79/q06/p1 The power that first brought people or bones into life can restore them after death. — Al-Māwardī contrasts creating from nothing with restoring from existing matter; Abū Isḥāq and al-Durr's ʿIkrima report call origination more remarkable than recreation; al-Bayḍāwī says divine capacity is unchanged and matter remains receptive; al-Ālūsī says essential, eternal divine power cannot cease and cites al-Fārābī's syllogism. Ibn ʿAṭiyya recalls a speaking disputant's formation from sperm, itself life after nonexistence. Bursawī argues from dust, semen, organs, speech and intellect, and rebuts the claim that what no longer exists cannot exist again. Al-Islāḥī says first creation was no burden. Al-Rummānī and al-Burhān praise the exchange as exceptionally forceful; al-Biqāʿī and Bursawī call it proof of bodily resurrection. Al-Burhān and al-Itqān compare the following fire from green wood, which changes properties; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a third proof, and Bursawī says fire from watery wood shows power to join opposites and restore dried matter. [21 notes: RUMMANI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الفارابي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:79/q07 How are bodies restored, and what makes the resurrected body the same person?  [يُحْيِيهَا أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether surviving bodily matter is a tailbone or dispersed original parts, and whether the original particles first cease to exist.
- 36:79/q07/p1 God gathers each person's original, dispersed parts, restores life or the soul, and raises the same body. — Al-Baḥr al-ʿulūm says original particles are gathered and souls restored. Bursawī says numerical identity persists through individuating accidents whether original parts were annihilated and recreated or merely scattered and recomposed. A Prophetic report in al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says souls remain in their stations while earthly remains are preserved, reassembled after resurrection rain, and reunited with their souls. The al-Ḥudhayfa report transmitted by Aḥmad, confirmed by ʿUqba ibn ʿAmr, describes God reassembling a man whose ashes were scattered and forgiving his fear of God. Al-Bayḍāwī mentions restored qualities or their equivalents. [5 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(حذيفة); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 36:79/q07/p2 Restoration proceeds from the remaining tailbone. — Al-Qurṭubī names the remaining tailbone ʿajm al-dhanab and notes the spelling ʿajb with bāʾ. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 36:79/q07/p3 The evidence does not decide whether particles are annihilated and recreated or dispersed and recomposed. — Qāḍī ʿAḍud al-Dīn leaves both accounts open for want of proof; Bursawī says 28:88 can describe either annihilation or dispersal and loss of function, and says bodily identity can be retained on either account. [3 notes: BURSEVI(القاضي عضد الدين)]

## 36:79/q08 What does 'the first time' add to the reply?  [أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 36:79/q08/p1 It emphasizes initial creation and implicitly contrasts the later creation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 'the first time' reinforces origination. Al-Biqāʿī calls the pairing badīʿ al-iḥtibāk: first revival points to second revival, second origination to first origination, and 'first time' implies a second time. Al-Nasafī glosses it as initial creation. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 36:79/q09 What does khalq mean here?  [خَلْقٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khalq denotes the created object, the act or mode of creation, or both.
- 36:79/q09/p1 It means created beings or things. — Al-Nasafī and al-Islāḥī explicitly gloss it as every creature; al-Baghawī says every created thing. Al-Kūrānyolu also accepts this sense alongside the act of creating. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAGHAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:79/q09/p2 It means every kind or act of creation and making. — Al-Biqāʿī includes every possible making or measuring, initially or again; al-Kashshāf ranges from minute to immense forms. Abū Ḥayyān says every manner, and Elmalılı includes material and immaterial creation with or without means or precedent. Al-Kūrānyolu accepts this sense alongside creatures. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:79/q09/p3 The expression encompasses both created beings and acts of creation. — Al-Kūrānyolu says both senses apply; its author consequently speaks of detailed knowledge of creatures and every material or immaterial way of creating, with or without instruments or models, first or later. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 36:79/q10 What does God's knowledge of every creation include?  [وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ خَلْقٍ عَلِيمٌ · interpretation]
- 36:79/q10/p1 It includes every creature and process of origination, death, dispersal, reassembly, and return, down to each person's fragments and qualities. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās as cited by al-Wāḥidī, and Ibn al-Jawzī include first and second creation; al-Ṭabarī adds death and says nothing is hidden. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī include how to initiate and restore. Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān include every kind and scale. Al-Bayḍāwī details origins, divisions, locations and identification of fragments, and restoring qualities or equivalents; al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and al-Kūrānyolu detail scattered parts, their arrangement, features or powers. Yaḥyā and al-Islāḥī invoke 67:14, and al-Islāḥī adds 50:4 about what earth consumes. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God never forgets what bones were before or after death. [25 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 36:79/q11 How does God's knowledge answer the objection about dispersed remains?  [وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ خَلْقٍ عَلِيمٌ · theology]
- 36:79/q11/p1 God distinguishes every person's parts, even where bodies mingle, and knows how to reunite each body. — Bursawī reports a learned authority naming intermingled parts and dry bones as the two problems; he specifies parts eaten by animals or incorporated into another human body, then answers the identification problem with God's complete knowledge. Al-Bayḍāwī specifies the locations and identifying marks of fragments; al-Nasafī and al-Biqāʿī mention land and sea. Al-Islāḥī cites 50:4 that what earth consumes is recorded; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God does not forget. [9 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الأفاضل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 36:79/q12 How does the closing clause function in the sentence?  [وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ خَلْقٍ عَلِيمٌ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the knowledge clause independently confirms the proof or is syntactically coordinated with the relative clause.
- 36:79/q12/p1 It confirms the preceding resurrection argument as a separate assertion. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as one syntactic possibility; he says the nominal sentence indicates continuous divine knowledge, unlike discrete acts of creation. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:79/q12/p2 It is joined to the relative clause about the one who originated the bones. — Al-Ālūsī presents this as the other syntactic possibility and says nominal form signals knowledge's continuity. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:79/q13 Who challenged the Prophet with the decayed bone, and what happened?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted reports differ over the challenger's identity and the extent of the revealed passage; Ibn Kathīr tests one identity against the sūra's Meccan setting.
- 36:79/q13/p1 Ubayy ibn Khalaf brought or scattered a decayed bone, challenged resurrection, and the reply was revealed. — ʿUrwa says public denial preceded the challenge, Ubayy scattered crumbled bone in the wind, and the Prophet was hurt. Yaḥyā, al-Durr and Study Quran report the Prophet's warning that Ubayy would be revived and cast into the Fire; al-Durr's reports vary over whether the Prophet also says God will kill him. Ibn Kathīr cites Mujāhid, ʿIkrima, ʿUrwa, al-Suddī and Qatāda, while al-Durr adds Abū Mālik; reports vary between the revelation of this verse, the passage from 36:77, and the final verses of Yāsīn. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites al-ʿAyyāshī from al-Ṣādiq and al-Durr's many routes. One al-Durr report calls him Ubayy al-Jumaḥī. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(يحيى); IBNABIHATIM(عروة بن الزبير); BURHAN(أبي بن خلف); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن عباس,عروة بن الزبير,عكرمة,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، وعكرمة، وعروة بن الزبير، والسدي، وقتادة); ITQAN(أبي بن خلف); TABATABAI(أبي بن خلف,ابن عباس وعروة بن الزبير وقتادة والسدي وعكرمة); STUDYQURAN(IK, JJ, Q, W)]
- 36:79/q13/p2 Al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil was the challenger with the crumbled bone. — Al-Māwardī cites Ibn ʿAbbās and says the Prophet promised revival, death, resurrection and Hell. Ibn Kathīr cites Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, locating the bone at al-Baṭḥāʾ; he says Ibn Jarīr transmits the account through Saʿīd without naming Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this alternative. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس وعروة بن الزبير وقتادة والسدي وعكرمة)]
- 36:79/q13/p3 Abū Jahl was the man who crumbled the bone and asked who could revive it. — Al-Durr transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 36:79/q13/p4 ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy was named as the visitor in one report. — Ibn Kathīr calls al-ʿAwfī's report munkar: Yāsīn is Meccan whereas Ibn Ubayy was in Medina. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī mentions the alternative Ibn ʿAbbās attribution. [2 notes: TABATABAI(ابن عباس وعروة بن الزبير وقتادة والسدي وعكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(العوفي عن ابن عباس)-]
- 36:79/q13/p5 An unnamed polytheist brought a crumbled old bone, challenged revival, and the passage through the sūra's end was revealed. — Al-Jishumī records the anonymous report. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 36:79/q14 What was the Prophet's answer about the challenger's fate?  [* · reports]
- 36:79/q14/p1 God would revive or resurrect the challenger and put him in the Fire. — Yaḥyā's report names Ubayy; al-Māwardī's Ibn ʿAbbās report names al-ʿĀṣ; Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr transmit both forms, with some adding that God would first kill him or kill him again; the Kūrānyolu report says God will kill, resurrect and cast him into Hell without naming him. Study Quran reports the Fire warning. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(يحيى); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûl-i Ekrem); STUDYQURAN(IK, JJ, Q, W)]

## 36:79/q15 What objection does this answer address, and how strong is its argument?  [* · interpretation]
- 36:79/q15/p1 It rebuts denial of bodily resurrection based on the apparent improbability of reviving decayed remains. — Bursawī says most deniers offer no proof, citing 32:10, 23:82 and 36:78, and he notes the objections from nonexistence, scattered intermingled parts and bodily dryness. Study Quran lists comparable objections at 13:5, 17:49, 17:98, 23:35, 23:82–83, 27:67, 37:16–17, 37:53, 50:3, 56:47–48 and 79:11. Al-Kūrānyolu says the denier ignores his own creation and arrogantly judges the Creator and Prophet; it reports al-Rāzī's view that the general condemnation of denial matters more than the particular occasion. Al-Rummānī and al-Burhān call the exchange exceptionally persuasive. [8 notes: BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); RUMMANI; BURHAN]

## 36:79/q16 What can anā in the preceding challenge mean?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the preceding interrogative asks about manner or source; Ibn Qutayba says the senses overlap.
- 36:79/q16/p1 It can mean 'how.' — Ibn Qutayba compares 2:259's question of how God revives a town and 2:223's instruction to approach a field as one wishes; he says the two senses are close enough to interpret through each other, citing al-Kumayt's line with both anā and min ayna. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 36:79/q16/p2 It can mean 'from where.' — Ibn Qutayba compares 9:30 and 6:101 and says the senses can be interpreted through each other; al-Kumayt's verse uses both anā and min ayna. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 36:79/q17 How does this answer connect with the following fire-from-green-wood proof?  [* · links]
- 36:79/q17/p1 The next proof reinforces resurrection by showing God brings fire from green, water-filled wood despite contrary properties. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a third proof after reflection on first creation; al-Burhān and al-Itqān say changing properties makes first creation and return comparable; Bursawī argues that people accept this union of apparent opposites and should accept restoring dry bones. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI]

## 36:79/q18 How does this verse fit the sūra and other Qur'anic passages?  [* · links]
- 36:79/q18/p1 It completes the sūra's resurrection argument and echoes other verses about divine knowledge and revival. — Al-Biqāʿī connects 36:51–53, 55, 59 and 64–65 to bodily and spiritual resurrection, gathering, judgment and afterlife. Al-Islāḥī says the closing section resumes the opening themes of monotheism and Hereafter and the Prophet's ability to awaken the morally living rather than dead-hearted. Yaḥyā and al-Islāḥī cite 67:14 about the Creator's knowledge; al-Islāḥī adds 50:4 about what earth consumes. Study Quran notes the similar passage at 17:51. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; YAHYA-SALLAM; STUDYQURAN]

## 36:79/q19 What does the written yāʾ in 'revives them' suggest in an inward reading?  [يُحْيِيهَا · inward]
- 36:79/q19/p1 Its retention with the object pronoun suggests inner life is clearer to knowledge and stronger in perception than outward life. — Al-Burhān contrasts the omitted yāʾ of standalone yaḥyā with its retention here; more generally he links omitted yāʾ with absence from perception and with submission to God in the station of excellence. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 36:79/q20 What do reports on the continuation say about God's creative command and will?  [* · theology]
turns on: The reports address different aspects of divine creative action: command and will.
- 36:79/q20/p1 God's creative command is an effective act rather than audible speech, and an eternal command would imply a second deity. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī quotes Nahj al-Balāgha on the effective character and non-eternity of the command. [1 notes: TABATABAI(نهج البلاغة)]
- 36:79/q20/p2 God's will is His bringing something into existence, an attribute of action rather than an inward state. — Ṣafwān's report from Abū al-Ḥasan contrasts human intention followed by action with God's immediate causing; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says many Imams' reports describe divine will as an attribute of action. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو الحسن عليه السلام)]
