# 18:54: 18 questions

## 18:54/q01 What does ṣarrafnā say God did with the Qur'anic examples?  [صَرَّفْنَا · meaning]
- 18:54/q01/p1 He varied, repeated, and explained them in multiple forms. — Muqātil says the Qur'an describes likenesses on diverse matters. Rāzī says taṣrīf signals repetition. Bursevī mentions varied forms and arrangements, including the two gardens, worldly life, and calls to faith. Alūsī specifies multiple forms of expression for people's benefit; Biqāʿī adds different meanings and eloquent styles. [7 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 18:54/q02 How is qurʾān read here?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانِ · readings]
- 18:54/q02/p1 Ḥamza's canonical reading simplifies its hamza. — Qiraat-ER attributes this to dialectal pronunciation and states no semantic difference. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 18:54/q03 Why does this verse place 'in this Qur'an' before 'for people'?  [فِى هَٰذَا ٱلْقُرْءَانِ لِلنَّاسِ · rhetoric]
- 18:54/q03/p1 The order foregrounds the Qur'an and fits al-Kahf's opening with the Book before its human stories. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts 17:89: here the context commends Qur'anic truth rather than human desires. Samarrai-ASRAR says al-Isrāʾ first discusses people and divine favors (17:83), whereas al-Kahf opens with the Qur'an (18:1; also 17:1, 17:9). Samarrai-ASRAR-MUH and LAMASAT-HALAQAT cite 18:1–2 and the subsequent human stories. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 18:54/q04 Who are 'the people' given these examples?  [لِلنَّاسِ · referent]
- 18:54/q04/p1 The wording includes all future recipients, with the polytheists as its immediate audience. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this general wording shaped by a particular context and compares 17:89 and 18:56. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:54/q05 How does min kulli mathalin attach to ṣarrafnā?  [مِن كُلِّ مَثَلٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether min is partitive with an implicit object or grammatically extra.
- 18:54/q05/p1 Min is partitive; an omitted object means 'a parable from every kind' or examples from each needed category. — Samīn al-Ḥalabī supplies an omitted noun qualified by the phrase. Alūsī supplies omitted mathalan or makes the phrase's content the object. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:54/q05/p2 Min is extra, leaving kulli mathalin as the direct object. — Samīn attributes this to al-Akhfash and the Kufans; Alūsī reports al-Akhfash's analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش)]

## 18:54/q06 What kinds of mathal are meant?  [مِن كُلِّ مَثَلٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether mathal denotes the broad range of needed instruction or particular kinds of stories, proofs, and warnings.
- 18:54/q06/p1 The full range of examples and lessons people need for guidance. — Ṭabarī includes example, admonition, and proof; Zajjāj, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Baghawī say every needed kind. Muqātil says diverse matters; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes warnings and hopes. Alūsī says useful categories, not literally every imaginable parable, and allows familiar parables or striking qualities attractive to the soul. Asad translates mathal as 'lesson' as at 17:89. Study Quran cites 17:89, 30:58, 39:27. KURANYOLU spans mosquito to heavens, worldly transience, good and evil. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:54/q06/p2 Instructive histories of earlier peoples warn of their fate. — Māwardī gives this as one alternative, associated with reading human contentiousness as obstinacy. Qurṭubī repeats it; al-Kāshifī in Bursevī says past stories supply lessons. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 18:54/q06/p3 Proofs of God's lordship or power explain divine truth. — Māwardī calls this exposition and associates it with disputation as argumentation. Qurṭubī repeats it; al-Kāshifī says such proofs deepen insight. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 18:54/q06/p4 Parables convey facts about the afterlife and unseen world as warnings. — Islāḥī specifies otherwise unseen realities. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:54/q07 Why were the examples varied for people?  [صَرَّفْنَا لِلنَّاسِ مِن كُلِّ مَثَلٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give a general instructional purpose and a specific polemical purpose in the surrounding Meccan dispute.
- 18:54/q07/p1 To prompt remembrance, reflection, guidance, repentance, and acceptance of faith. — Ṭabarī adds abandoning idolatry; Baghawī taking warning; Jishumī reflection and, from repeated examples, religious reasoning over blind imitation. Qushayrī says clear arguments were available though some went astray. Ibn Kathīr says they prevent leaving guidance; Biqāʿī says eloquent proof should win broad acceptance; Alūsī says acceptance of faith was intended though refused. Study Quran calls it moral and religious instruction; Islāḥī calls its diverse presentation merciful, though opponents ask for torment. KURANYOLU says reflection and truth meet stubbornness, denial, selfishness, and self-interest. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:54/q07/p2 Their repetition answers and exposes the wealthy Meccan unbelievers' boast. — Rāzī says these opponents nevertheless persist in futile disputation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 18:54/q08 Who is al-insān in this statement?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether a general human description is narrowed by the ensuing unbelievers' dispute or by reported occasions naming individuals.
- 18:54/q08/p1 Humankind generally, including believers. — Baghawī explicitly prefers this over named persons and unbelievers. Ibn ʿAṭiyya applies named occasion reports generally. Abū Ḥayyān and Alūsī cite the Prophet's use of the verse for ʿAlī; Alūsī calls the report sound while noting an unresolved difficulty. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the species is broader than al-nās and rejects limiting it to unbelievers as in 19:66; the blame in 18:56 is more specific. Ṭabarī, Muqātil, Jishumī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, and others speak generally. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+]
- 18:54/q08/p2 Unbelievers generally, disputing God, prophets, and revelation. — Yaḥyā says the disbeliever argues about God. Zajjāj invokes the false disputes of 18:56; Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī and Study Quran relay that context. Ibn Zayd gives objections that messengers are ordinary people or revelation impossible (23:33, 23:24, 6:7, 15:14–15). Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly rejects limiting this verse to unbelievers, treating 18:56 as the specific charge. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(author والزجاج); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Bḍ); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 18:54/q08/p3 The contentious Quraysh or Meccans. — Islāḥī says the generic expression conveys aversion to Quraysh. Al-Kalbī in Jishumī identifies the people as Meccans. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI(الكلبي)]
- 18:54/q08/p4 Al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith. — Ibn ʿAbbās links his dispute to the Qur'an; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Alūsī also report his name among proposed occasions. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(النضر بن الحارث); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(author والزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن السائب); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:54/q08/p5 Ubayy ibn Khalaf. — Al-Kalbī and Ibn al-Sāʾib are named among transmitters. Ibn al-Sāʾib says Ubayy challenged resurrection with a decayed bone. Some sources give the fuller name Ubayy ibn Khalaf al-Jumaḥī. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(أبي بن خلف); JISHUMI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); QURTUBI-FULL(author والزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن السائب); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 18:54/q08/p6 Ibn al-Zabʿarī. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Alūsī report this alternative name; the spelling varies slightly in their notes. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن السائب); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 18:54/q09 What does jadal mean here?  [جَدَلًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether jadal here inherently means false wrangling, neutral disputation, or obstinate refusal.
- 18:54/q09/p1 Disputation, especially contentious or false argument against truth. — Jishumī gives the root sense of firmness and ajdal for a strong falcon. Bursevī and Alūsī describe twisting against an opponent, while acknowledging argument need not intrinsically be false (Bursevī cites 16:125). Ṭabarī's Ibn Zayd and al-Durr specify rejection of prophets. Kashshāf calls it contention and false wrangling and cites 16:4; Nasafī says quarrelling. Ibn Kathīr, Biqāʿī, Study Quran and others link it to opposing truth after clear proof; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it contentious disputation. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Bḍ); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 18:54/q09/p2 Obstinacy. — Māwardī associates this reading with the examples as warnings from earlier peoples. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:54/q09/p3 Argumentation or verbal contention, not necessarily false in itself. — Māwardī associates argumentation with examples as proofs of lordship. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it verbal contention. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 18:56 specifies false disputation, whereas this verse describes a wider human trait; Abraham's debate in 11:74–75 can be praised. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:54/q10 How does akthara shayʾin compare human argument?  [أَكْثَرَ شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the superlative is distributive comparison, comparison among rational kinds, or intensification without an intended ranking.
- 18:54/q10/p1 Humans exceed each other being capable of dispute considered one by one. — Kashshāf, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn treat singular shayʾ as collective or distributive; Samīn says it means each disputant, and Abū Ḥayyān cites 36:77. Rāzī and Alūsī also read a comparison with disputants. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:54/q10/p2 Humans argue more than other rational kinds, including angels and jinn. — Zajjāj notes Iblīs, angels, and jinn also dispute; Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān relay the comparison. Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise names angels and jinn. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects a literal comparison with jinn or devils; their ability to debate is unestablished, while angelic dialogue can be praiseworthy (2:30). [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 18:54/q10/p3 The superlative intensifies humanity's abundance of argument rather than literally ranking species. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says indefinite comprehensive singular shayʾ makes the form sound; earthly nonhuman kinds cannot debate, and jinn or devils are not intended comparands. He rejects that literal ranking. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:54/q11 What is the grammatical role of jadalan?  [جَدَلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether jadalan directly specifies the superlative relation or requires reconstructing an underlying subject or omitted comparand.
- 18:54/q11/p1 It is an accusative specification (tamyīz) of the relation. — Jishumī also identifies akthar as kāna's predicate; Rāzī, Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Alūsī call jadalan a specification. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it specifies that human argument is abundant. Kashshāf compares the construction with 'then he is a clear disputant' (16:4). [9 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:54/q11/p2 It is derived from an underlying subject: 'the human's disputation was the greatest thing.' — Samīn presents this possible analysis but says it is difficult. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:54/q11/p3 An omitted 'human's disputation' is the comparand, with jadalan specifying it. — Abū al-Baqāʾ proposes this estimate. Samīn rejects it because it requires attributing disputation to disputation itself by a further metaphor, like 'poet's poetry.' [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 18:54/q12 Why are humans exceptionally argumentative?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ أَكْثَرَ شَىْءٍۢ جَدَلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer different accounts of the human trait: self-attachment, a volatile range of capacities, and microcosmic diversity.
- 18:54/q12/p1 Natural attachment to oneself sustains argument after clear proof. — Biqāʿī attributes the trait to self-attachment; KURANYOLU names stubbornness, denial, selfishness and self-interest in resistance to revelation. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:54/q12/p2 Human nature spans angelic heights and animal depths. — Alūsī gives this volatile range as a reason for unusually great dispute. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:54/q12/p3 Humans manifest diverse divine names and contain the macrocosm as a microcosm. — Alūsī gives this further explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:54/q13 What does the verse teach about the value of argument?  [جَدَلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish argument's moral status by its object and purpose; one transmitted report treats its prevalence as a sign of straying.
- 18:54/q13/p1 Argument against truth and resistance to divine guidance are blameworthy, but debate in defense of truth can be praiseworthy. — Qushayrī permits debate against God's enemies but calls disputing with God resistance to decree and shirk; the fortunate believer acts rather than argues. Ibn ʿĀshūr says discipline restrains blameworthy debate and cites Abraham on Lot's people (11:74–75) as praised debate. Bursevī says argument need not be false and cites 16:125. Rāzī reports that prophets' religious debate is implied because disputation has two sides, against reliance on imitation. Jishumī says varied examples validate reasoning over blind imitation. Abū Ḥayyān observes that the Qur'an often mentions humanity blamefully. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:54/q13/p2 Disputation can signal straying after guidance. — Bursevī cites a prophetic report through Abū Umāma, from Abū al-Layth: a people are given disputation only after they stray from guidance. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو أمامة)]

## 18:54/q14 What incident is reported when the Prophet applied this verse?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ جَدَلًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on whether the sleeper was ʿAlī at home with Fāṭima or an unnamed man in the mosque.
- 18:54/q14/p1 He urged ʿAlī and Fāṭima to pray at night; ʿAlī invoked God's control of their souls; the Prophet left, reciting the verse and, in many accounts, striking his thigh. — Yaḥyā's Ḥasan report says a household member was sleeping; its wording varies in identifying the sleeper. ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Thaʿlabī, Māwardī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr, Abū Ḥayyān, Alūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr transmit or discuss the ʿAlī–Fāṭima account. Māwardī separately reports ʿAlī's reply. Ibn Kathīr says both Ṣaḥīḥ collections record it; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Alūsī call it sound. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ʿAlī should have welcomed the wake-up and reproach. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(علي بن حسين); THALABI(الحسن بن علي عن أبيه); MAWARDI-FULL(علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(author والزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(علي); IBNASHUR-FULL(علي)]
- 18:54/q14/p2 He nudged a sleeping man in the mosque to pray; the man invoked what God decreed, and the Prophet cited the verse. — Yaḥyā transmits this second version from al-Ḥasan without naming the man. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن)]

## 18:54/q15 How is the Prophet's reaction to ʿAlī's reply understood?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ جَدَلًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the Prophet's gesture and recitation rebuked ʿAlī's defense or accompanied acceptance of his excuse.
- 18:54/q15/p1 His thigh strike expressed amazement at ʿAlī's quick answer and refusal of exhortation as an excuse. — Al-Nawawī gives this explanation; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise says ʿAlī should have accepted the wake-up and its reproach. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(النووي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:54/q15/p2 He accepted ʿAlī's excuse. — Alūsī reports this alternate reading of the Prophet's reaction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(النووي)]

## 18:54/q16 What inward obstacle does disputation create?  [جَدَلًۭا · inward]
- 18:54/q16/p1 It obstructs action, obedience, and communion by trapping people in disputes over religion. — The Najmī inward reading in Bursevī lists prophecy, scripture, law, ambiguous verses, abrogation, interpretation, revelation occasions, readings, and scripture's createdness as subjects that divert disputants. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 18:54/q17 What does the verse imply about human agency in obeying God?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ جَدَلًۭا · theology]
- 18:54/q17/p1 People may be left without help toward obedience in an intermediate state called takhliya. — Qushayrī says they are neither simply capable nor helpless: if they willed what was commanded, it would be withheld. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 18:54/q18 How does this verse connect with other Qur'anic passages and its sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace different links: prior parables, nearby disputes, an accountability pattern, and vocabulary in adjacent discussion.
- 18:54/q18/p1 Its examples recall related verses and earlier examples in al-Kahf. — Yaḥyā, Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī compare 17:89; Wāḥidī refers to two passages in Banī Isrāʾīl, and Ibn al-Jawzī to 17:41. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees 18:32 and 18:45 continued here, with praise of guidance recalling 18:27 and 18:29. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:54/q18/p2 Its close on disputation fits al-Kahf's surrounding dialogues and prepares the ensuing charge against unbelievers. — Samarrai's three works cite the garden companions (18:34, 18:37), Moses and the righteous servant, the youths' dispute (18:22), and false argument in 18:56; al-Isrāʾ lacks comparable debate language. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the closing implies the audience disputed the Qur'an and prepares 18:56. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the next six verses threaten punishment after reminders. Islāḥī says opponents ask for torment rather than accept mercy. [8 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:54/q18/p3 The sūra illustrates a pattern linking the judgment record and the worldly book of religious rulings. — Al-Itqān identifies this Qur'anic pattern in al-Kahf and al-Isrāʾ as grounding accountability. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 18:54/q18/p4 Adjacent commentary explains the vocabulary of appointment and refuting truth. — Majāz glosses mawbiq as an appointed meeting place or time, citing poetry; Abū Ḥayyān reports Thaʿlab's gloss of adḥaḍa al-ḥaqq as rendering truth ineffectual from slipping in mud, and two poems about a camel slipping. These are notes on neighboring expressions. [3 notes: MAJAZ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثعلب)]
