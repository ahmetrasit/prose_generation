# 39:6: 28 questions

## 39:6/q01 Who is the single soul from which humanity was created?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'one soul' identifies the first person, the human kind, or an inward principle.
- 39:6/q01/p1 Adam, the common origin of humankind. — Muqatil and many others name Adam; al-Tabari cites 2:21 and 4:1, Ibn Kathir notes diverse languages and colors from one origin, and Study Quran cites 4:1, 6:98, 7:189, and 31:28. Al-Razi calls the common origin proof of a freely choosing Creator; Biqa'i says Adam is the source in which descendants were determined. Asad refers to 4:1. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(IK); BIQAI-FULL+]
- 39:6/q01/p2 The human species or a soul of the same human kind. — Ibn Atiyya allows species alongside Adam; Tabataba'i identifies Adam but takes his mate as a woman of the same kind; Islahi likewise reads the mate as from the same species. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:6/q01/p3 The universal rational self, the true Adam. — Qashani reads its mate as the animal self and visible forms as descending from the Preserved Tablet and hidden world. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 39:6/q01/p4 The spirit. — Bursevi's inward reading makes the heart its mate, analogously to Eve from Adam's rib. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 39:6/q02 How was the mate made from the single soul?  [ثُمَّ جَعَلَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'from it' means physical derivation, shared kind, or an inward relation.
- 39:6/q02/p1 Eve was made from Adam's rib or body. — Al-Tabari and Qatada report a rib; al-Mawardi transmits al-Dahhak's rib on Adam's back; Ibn Atiyya prefers the short-rib account on the hadith that bending a woman made from a rib breaks it. Alusi specifies the lowest left rib, part or whole, with replacement; Baydawi calls this an exceptional sign. Qurtubi notes the purpose of procreation. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 39:6/q02/p2 Eve was made from Adam's remaining clay. — Ibn Atiyya reports this and says the rib account is sounder; Tabrisi records it alongside the rib alternative. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 39:6/q02/p3 The mate is from Adam's substance or human kind. — Ibn Bahr glosses 'from her' as 'like her'; Bursevi allows Adam's kind as an alternative to his short rib; Tabataba'i and Islahi emphasize sameness of human species. Study Quran cites mate-making as a gift inspiring awe and gratitude, with 16:72, 30:21, 35:11, 42:11, and 78:8. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:6/q02/p4 The mate is the animal self from the universal rational self. — Qashani identifies the rational self as the true Adam. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 39:6/q02/p5 The mate is the heart made from the spirit. — Bursevi presents this as an inward analogy to Eve's creation from Adam's rib. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 39:6/q03 What sequence does 'then He made from it its mate' express?  [ثُمَّ جَعَلَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the sequence is chronological, orders narration or foreknowledge, or ranks two signs.
- 39:6/q03/p1 It orders the report or discourse, though Eve was created before Adam's descendants. — Al-Tabari, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Farra via Ibn al-Jawzi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Suyuti cite normal Arabic reporting later-mentioned earlier events; Ibn Atiyya adduces Abu Nuwas's father and grandfather verse, while Tabrisi cites poetry. Suyuti rejects redundant thumma and says an omitted clause explains 9:118; Ibn Hisham says discourse order does not explain delay. Tabataba'i calls the sequence rhetorical. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو النواس,فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,غيره); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; TABATABAI]
- 39:6/q03/p2 It is temporal: Adam's descendants were extracted as particles before Eve was created. — Al-Tabari cites a Prophetic report of all future souls from Adam's back and Companion reports, preferring this account; others report it. Tabrisi rejects it as weak; Alusi accepts it only on sound Prophetic or equivalent transmission. Abu Hayyan says the sequence is temporal on this account; Nasafi and Baydawi mention particles. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL+; TABRISI-]
- 39:6/q03/p3 It is temporal because the single soul was first alone and its mate was made afterward. — Al-Tabari, Tabrisi, Razi, Baydawi, and Samin attach the clause to the understood description 'alone'; Tabari adduces Arabic construction and a rajaz gloss of كوحته as overcoming. Alusi permits coordination with the description of one or with 'your creation.' Zajjaj says Eve was made before the rest of humanity. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(الراجز); ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:6/q03/p4 It marks the higher rank of Eve's exceptional creation as a sign, rather than elapsed time. — Zamakhshari via Abu Hayyan and Samin treats Eve's creation from Adam's body as more striking. Samin records preference; Baydawi and Alusi connect the construction to ordinary reproduction versus Eve's exceptional origin. Ibn Ashur contrasts this with 7:189, where blessings govern order. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 39:6/q03/p5 The sequence concerns events fixed in divine foreknowledge. — Ibn Atiyya reports that the certain future can be narrated as already settled; he clarifies that humanity actually emerged from Adam after Eve. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 39:6/q03/p6 It marks Eve's creation after Adam's division, both in time and rank. — Biqa'i says 'made' marks Adam as her cause and material, excluding partnership; Adam was created as the source of descendants. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:6/q04 Why does this verse say 'created you' rather than another creation verb?  [خَلَقَكُم · rhetoric]
- 39:6/q04/p1 The verb suits this surah's context and repeated vocabulary. — Samarrai compares al-Anam's أنشأكم with خلقكم in al-Nisa, al-Araf, and al-Zumar; each choice fits its setting. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 39:6/q05 Why are humans addressed or singled out in this passage?  [خَلَقَكُم يَخْلُقُكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second-person address singles out humans or includes livestock.
- 39:6/q05/p1 Human creation is an especially remarkable and relevant proof of God's power and care. — Razi calls humanity the noblest animal kind before livestock. Baydawi calls the lower-world proof nearer and remarkable; Alusi points to reason and the divine trust and quotes the human as a small body containing a greater world. Ibn Ashur notes a shift from third-person to direct address; Burhan rejects extending address to livestock as needless strain. Kuranyolu compares its wonder with the universe. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURHAN+]
- 39:6/q05/p2 The rational plural address also includes livestock's creation in wombs. — Tabataba'i takes the womb passage to explain both humans and cattle, with rational address extending to animals; Baydawi allows singling out the intended human audience. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 39:6/q05/p3 The womb clause concerns Adam and Eve's descendants, rather than the first couple. — Samarrai says the first couple's creation has already been described; Kuranyolu sees an allusion to both humanity's origin and each person's womb development, citing 4:1. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:6/q05/p4 Arabic usage of human terms ranges from Adam and descendants to named individuals. — Damghani's broader lexical inventory lists Adam, descendants, Hisham or al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, Qaraz, Abu Jahl, al-Nadr, Barsisa, Budayl, al-Akhnas, Usayd, Kilda, and Uqba ibn al-Walid; the note does not assign these names to this verse. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 39:6/q06 What does 'sent down for you' mean for the livestock?  [وَأَنزَلَ لَكُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether descent is literal, an act of creation or provision, or is attributed through divine decree or descending causes.
- 39:6/q06/p1 God created or made the animals available to people. — Muqatil compares 7:26 and 57:25; Hasan via Mawardi and Nasafi says made for people; Ibn Jawzi lists livestock alongside provision and iron as 'created' in 10:59, 39:6, and 57:25; Sa'id ibn Jubayr glosses it 'created.' Samarrai states this sense. Islahi says the wording redirects gratitude from immediate causes to God, citing 57:25. [11 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 39:6/q06/p2 The animals were created in Paradise or heaven and actually brought down. — Mawardi attributes this to Ibn Isa; Ibn Atiyya and Jubbai report it, the latter citing a report that sheep and camels are Paradise animals. Qurtubi compares iron with Adam in 57:25; Samin allows literal descent, and Samarrai links livestock provisions with Adam and Eve's arrival. Alusi says the account lacks sound basis. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:6/q06/p3 God's decree or apportionment descended from heaven. — Ibn Atiyya calls benefactions and rain descending conventionally; Razi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, and Alusi connect the phrase to the Preserved Tablet and heavenly divine decisions. Alusi describes appointed angels and emergence after concealment. Qurtubi says the command to create descends from heaven. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:6/q06/p4 Rain or other descending causes support fodder, livelihood, and so the livestock. — Ibn Atiyya traces rain to plants to fattening animals and introduces rajaz; Tabarsi and Qurtubi compare clothing in 7:26. Razi mentions soil and water; Baydawi adds starlight; Abu Hayyan cites poems about camel humps in clouds and surprising prepositions; Samin cites poetic rain imagery. Alusi reports this metonymy but dismisses it as unconvincing. [16 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:6/q06/p5 The animals were provided or bestowed as sustenance and lawful human use. — Tabrisi, Qurtubi, and Study Quran describe provision; the latter specifies carrying burdens and slaughter (6:142). Ibn Jawzi lists bounty as a sense of inzāl in 42:27. Asad contrasts lawful gift here with baseless taboos in 6:143–144 and stresses dependence on God. [6 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 39:6/q06/p6 Their appearance in the visible world is a descent from unseen divine treasuries. — Tabataba'i frames descent as emergence from God's unseen treasuries. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 39:6/q06/p7 The wording highlights the powerful animals' subjection to human use. — Biqa'i reasons that livestock are stronger than people, making their domestication a sign of divine power. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:6/q06/p8 Inzāl can literally mean bringing down from a height. — Ibn Jawzi supplies related Arabic uses for hardship, war encounter, a guest (with poetry), and pilgrimage (with poetry). He also lists revelation or speech at 6:93 and actual rain at 42:28 as distinct uses; these are lexical parallels rather than separate livestock origins. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 39:6/q07 How is 'anzala lakum' pronounced in the reported recitation?  [وَأَنزَلَ لَكُم · readings]
turns on: Which sound transmission from Ruways is followed.
- 39:6/q07/p1 Ruways may assimilate the contact in anzala lakum. — Nashr reports Tahir ibn Ghalbun and Ibn al-Fahham transmitting assimilation at both locations, with al-Mabhaj and al-Kifaya; it lists this among fourteen optional passages. [2 notes: NASHR(طاهر بن غلبون وابن الفحام)]
- 39:6/q07/p2 Ruways may pronounce it clearly. — Nashr says other transmitters show clear pronunciation in all the listed passages and that both forms are sound. [1 notes: NASHR(الباقون عن رويس)]

## 39:6/q08 What does 'livestock' denote here?  [ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · meaning]
- 39:6/q08/p1 Camels, cattle, sheep, and goats. — The four familiar domestic species yield the eight male and female members. Bursevi says anʿām originally names a camel herd without a singular of that form; Ibn al-Shaykh excludes horses, mules, and donkeys. Biqa'i notes Bactrian camels and buffaloes are unmentioned. [26 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:6/q09 What are the eight pairs?  [ثَمَٰنِيَةَ أَزْوَٰجٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pairs are domestic animals or inward animal qualities.
- 39:6/q09/p1 Male and female members of sheep, goats, cattle, and camels. — Muqatil calls them four males and four females; Ibn Qutayba and others cite the Anam listing (6:143–144); Ibn Kathir describes offspring. Zajjaj, Razi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Baḥr al-Ulum, and Islahi clarify that zawj can name one member or a pair; Razi and Abu Hayyan cite 75:39, Baḥr 51:49. Bursevi notes meat, hides, hair, and wool as reasons these four are named; Biqa'i contrasts independent male and female livestock with Eve from Adam. [39 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ,بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:6/q09/p2 Eight animal traits within people. — The Najmiyya inward reading lists eating, drinking, excretion, urination, desire, greed, rapacity, and anger; it reduces them to desire drawing benefit and anger repelling harm. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 39:6/q10 Why are the livestock placed after human creation?  [وَأَنزَلَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ · rhetoric]
- 39:6/q10/p1 They provide livelihood and show God's care and power. — Razi says livestock rank next after humans among animals and both develop in wombs; Burhan sees providence, reproduction, and livelihood as the point. Bursevi lists useful products. Asad calls them sustenance. Samarrai says they were provisions when Adam and Eve descended, and compares the order with al-Nahl 16:3–5. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:6/q11 How is the livestock clause arranged grammatically and rhetorically?  [وَأَنزَلَ لَكُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَنْعَٰمِ ثَمَٰنِيَةَ أَزْوَٰجٍۢ · grammar]
- 39:6/q11/p1 It is coordinated with 'created you'; the adverbial phrase precedes its explicit object for emphasis. — Samin identifies coordination with خلقكم. Alusi says fronting the adverbial phrase foregrounds it and builds anticipation for 'eight pairs.' [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:6/q12 How is 'He creates you' pronounced and parsed?  [يَخْلُقُكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the issue is transmitted assimilation or clause independence.
- 39:6/q12/p1 The qaf assimilates into the following kaf. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan transmit this reading from Isa ibn Umar and Talha ibn Musarrif throughout the Qur'an. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر وطلحة بن مصرف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى وطلحة)]
- 39:6/q12/p2 The clause is independent, without an omitted subject. — Samin rejects taking it as a predicate of an elided subject; this is syntactic, not a different pronunciation. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 39:6/q13 How is 'mothers' formed and read?  [أُمَّهَٰتِكُمْ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowels of the initial syllable and mim.
- 39:6/q13/p1 The majority pronounce initial hamza with damma and mim with fatha. — Ibn Atiyya says the hamza-damma is the majority dialect; Razi, Qurtubi, and Ibn Ashur give the fuller vowel pair in connected reading and pause. Bursevi explains ummahat as umm with added ha, as ahraqa from araqa. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI]
- 39:6/q13/p2 Hamza reads both hamza and mim with kasra. — Razi, Qurtubi, and Ibn Ashur transmit this; Ibn Ashur explains vowel assimilation in connected reading. Qiraat-ER says the initial vowel quality does not change the root, meaning, or syntax. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة); QIRAAT-ER]
- 39:6/q13/p3 Al-Kisai has a mixed initial and mim vowel reading. — Razi and Qurtubi give kasra on hamza and fatha on mim; Ibn Ashur records mim kasra while hamza stays open in connected reading. The notes differ in their exact assignment, preserved here without adjudication. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الكسائي)]
- 39:6/q13/p4 Yahya ibn Waththab reads initial hamza with kasra. — Ibn Atiyya calls it a dialectal alternative to the majority's damma. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب)]

## 39:6/q14 What does 'creation after creation' describe?  [خَلْقًۭا مِّنۢ بَعْدِ خَلْقٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the successive creations are fetal stages or a sequence beginning before the womb and extending beyond it.
- 39:6/q14/p1 Successive embryonic stages within the womb. — Early authorities list drop, clot, lump, bones, flesh, hair, and spirit with variants: Mujahid continues until complete formation; Suddi, Muqatil, Zajjaj, and Ibn Kathir add breathing the spirit; Qatada adds hair; Ibn Kathir adds sinews and veins. Tabari prefers this because the verse explicitly locates creation in the womb and cites 23:13; Razi cites 23:12–14; Baghawi 71:14; Study Quran prefers three gestational stages (23:13–14; 22:5). Ibn Ashur supplies ten stages from a five-millimeter nutfa and 33-day alaqa through fetal size, organs, hair, and circulation in month nine; Kuranyolu describes fertilization, implantation, muscles, vessels, and nerves. Sources differ in detail, not in locating the development in the womb. [49 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي,الضحاك,عكرمة,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة والسدي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة والسدي); TABRISI(قتادة، مجاهد، السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,عكرمة ومجاهد والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN+; TAB-FULL+]
- 39:6/q14/p2 Womb creation follows an earlier creation in Adam's back or fathers' loins. — Ibn Zayd, reported across commentaries, makes the womb stage follow Adam's back; Ibn Atiyya adds fathers' backs. Tabari rejects this for the womb-stage reading because of the verse's explicit location. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); TAB-FULL-]
- 39:6/q14/p3 Creation spans the father's loins, the mother's womb, and life after birth. — Mawardi and Qurtubi present this broader three-stage possibility. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]
- 39:6/q14/p4 Unity unfolds into multiplicity through names, qualities, and acts. — Qashani's inward reading situates staged creation through bodily, vegetative, and animal levels. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 39:6/q15 What governs the accusative 'khalqan' and its following phrase?  [خَلْقًۭا مِّنۢ بَعْدِ خَلْقٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the following phrase qualifies the verbal noun or the verb.
- 39:6/q15/p1 It is an absolute object of the creation verb, specifying successive kinds. — Jishumi gives خلقكم as governor; Samin identifies يخلق and says من بعد خلق can specify its kind or attach to the verb if خلقا only emphasizes. Ibn Ashur treats it as an absolute object specified by 'after creation.' [3 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:6/q15/p2 'After creation' attaches to the verb while 'khalqan' supplies emphasis. — Samin sets out this alternative syntactic analysis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 39:6/q16 What are the three darknesses?  [ظُلُمَٰتٍۢ ثَلَٰثٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Which three enclosing places, developmental states, or inward principles the phrase counts.
- 39:6/q16/p1 The mother's abdomen, womb, and placenta or fetal membrane. — This is the dominant transmitted set from Ibn Abbas, Ikrima, Mujahid, Qatada, Suddi, Ibn Zayd, Dahhak and others. Tabari describes nested layers; Dahhak calls the afterbirth السلى. Qurtubi calls it sounder; Ibn Ashur says the membrane protects the fetus and gives it an independent nutrient cycle. Kuranyolu specifies abdominal and uterine walls; Islahi a cavity enclosing the womb. ## 39:6/q17 How does 'in three darknesses' attach syntactically?  [فِى ظُلُمَٰتٍۢ ثَلَٰثٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical governor of the second 'in' phrase.
- 39:6/q17/p1 It modifies the creation verb. — Ibn Ashur says it modifies 'He creates you.' [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:6/q17/p2 It attaches to the preceding non-emphatic creation noun. — Samin argues the emphatic verbal noun cannot govern it and that the identical preposition already attached to the verb blocks another attachment; he reports Abu al-Baqa's rejected attachment to the emphatic noun. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]
- 39:6/q17/p3 It is an inclusion appositive of 'in your mothers' wombs.' — Samin permits repeating the preposition despite the intervening source phrase, since the material shares a governor. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 39:6/q18 What do the hidden womb stages demonstrate?  [يَخْلُقُكُمْ فِى بُطُونِ أُمَّهَٰتِكُمْ فِى ظُلُمَٰتٍۢ ثَلَٰثٍۢ · theology]
- 39:6/q18/p1 God's power, knowledge, and skill reach concealed places. — Jishumi says instrument-dependent perception could not work in a womb; Razi refers to 3:6; Ibn Ashur, Islahi, and Kuranyolu say hidden development shows comprehensive power and knowledge. Nahhas says the darkness does not prevent sun or moon as it prevents creatures. Qushayri says the origin should prevent self-admiration. [8 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:6/q18/p2 The bodily stages invite love and remembrance of their giver. — Qushayri distinguishes organs bearing knowledge, power, and life from sense organs; souls and hearts love their benefactor and should remember God. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 39:6/q19 What does 'that is God, your Lord' point back to?  [ذَٰلِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ رَبُّكُمْ · referent]
turns on: How far back the demonstrative gathers the proofs.
- 39:6/q19/p1 The Creator identified by the preceding acts. — Zajjaj, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Ashur, and Tabataba'i connect the demonstrative to the creator and governor of humans, livestock, and developmental stages. Ibn Ashur says it summarizes proof, uses God's proper name for clarity, and invokes 13:16. Ibn Ashur says the demonstrative singles out that bearer of attributes, comparing 2:5. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:6/q19/p2 All acts described since the surah's beginning identify Him. — Biqa'i extends the demonstrative to creation's preceding testimony, with all creatures attesting by speech or state; Ibn Ashur also relates the conclusion to the universe's creation and control. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:6/q20 How is 'that is God, your Lord' parsed?  [ذَٰلِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ رَبُّكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Which nominative supplies the predicate.
- 39:6/q20/p1 'That' is subject, 'God' predicate, and 'your Lord' adjective or apposition. — Samin gives this analysis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 39:6/q20/p2 'That' is subject, 'God' explanatory apposition, and 'your Lord' predicate or substitute. — Tabrisi gives this analysis; Samin allows the appositive and predicate arrangement. [2 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 39:6/q21 How is 'to Him belongs sovereignty' parsed?  [لَهُ ٱلْمُلْكُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is circumstantial, independent, or predicative.
- 39:6/q21/p1 It is an independent clause or another predicate. — Samin allows independence, a successive predicate, or a predicate after God and Lord; he also parses the preposition as predicate and 'sovereignty' as subject. Tabataba'i treats both following clauses as further predicates of 'that is God.' [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 39:6/q21/p2 It is circumstantial. — Tabrisi says sovereignty can be raised by the adverb in a circumstantial construction; alternatively it is a second predicate. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 39:6/q22 What does God's exclusive lordship and sovereignty establish?  [رَبُّكُمْ لَهُ ٱلْمُلْكُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ · theology]
turns on: The argument from creation and ownership to exclusive divinity, and its implications.
- 39:6/q22/p1 God alone owns, governs, provides, and deserves worship. — Muqatil, Qushayri, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabataba'i, and Islahi tie creation and provision to sole lordship. Tabataba'i argues pagans acknowledge one Creator but multiply lords, overlooking governance as part of creation; worship follows the managing lord through fear, hope, and gratitude. Bursevi says dominion has no partner in either life; Biqa'i says nurturer need not be sovereign, so the claim completes the proof. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:6/q22/p2 Another god would either conflict with God or lack power, so divinity must be one. — Razi cites 21:22 and argues that rival power creates impossible conflict, while powerlessness is deficiency. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 39:6/q22/p3 The Creator is beyond body, parts, and place. — Razi says the verse identifies God through wondrous acts, not organs, establishing transcendence. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 39:6/q22/p4 God alone has real or absolute existence. — Qashani says other objects of worship have no independent existence; Bursevi describes essential unity and the true goal. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 39:6/q22/p5 God has no need of human worship or benefit from it. — Razi reasons from necessary existence and self-sufficiency: eternal need would demand eternal creation, new need would be deficiency, and all the world's disbelief cannot harm the Creator. Nasafi says 39:7 clarifies God's independence. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 39:6/q23 How is 'there is no deity but Him' parsed?  [لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it supplies a new predicate, an independent assertion, or a circumstance.
- 39:6/q23/p1 It is independent or a successive predicate. — Samin gives both possibilities; Tabataba'i says the two ensuing clauses predicate further of 'that is God.' [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 39:6/q23/p2 It is circumstantial, expressing God's unique oneness. — Tabrisi permits this or another predicate. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 39:6/q24 What does the final question ask or rebuke?  [فَأَنَّىٰ تُصْرَفُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stress lies on the rebuke itself or the passive portrayal of diversion.
- 39:6/q24/p1 How can people turn from the only Creator and Lord to idols or other powerless objects of worship? — Tabari connects 10:32 and reports Suddi's address to polytheists; Zajjaj compares فأنى تؤفكون and Tabrisi 6:95. Qurtubi and many later commentators invoke the prior proof. Study Quran relates the false sons and daughters treated as intermediaries in 39:3 and the preceding realities in 39:5–6. Asad says literally 'how are you turned away' from truth; Durr cites 43:87. - 39:6/q24/p2 Its passive form suggests people surrender to diversion despite innate reason and clear proof. — Biqa'i says their conduct is overpowered by pursuit of ruin despite nature and reason; Islahi explicitly describes yielding their reins to a devil. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:6/q25 What is the grammatical force of 'how' in the final question?  [فَأَنَّىٰ تُصْرَفُونَ · grammar]
- 39:6/q25/p1 It is an adverbial or verbal-noun accusative meaning 'how.' — Tabrisi supplies the parsing. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 39:6/q26 How does the verse relate to neighboring verses and other creation passages?  [* · links]

- 39:6/q26/p1 It adds human and livestock creation to the surrounding proofs of divine oneness and governance. — Ibn Ashur treats the human-creation clause as circumstantial or a renewed proof, comparing 7:189; Tabataba'i links both verses' creation and governance. - 39:6/q26/p3 The verse's gifts should move people toward gratitude and worship. — Asad contrasts the livestock of 6:143–144 with pre-Islamic taboos; Study Quran cites paired human beings in 16:72, 30:21, 35:11, 42:11, and 78:8. Qushayri frames God as Creator, Provider, guide, and teacher of unity and limits. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; QUSHAYRI]

## 39:6/q27 How is the Qur'an's descent described in related reports on inzāl?  [وَأَنزَلَ · reports]
turns on: The stages and timing of revelation in the broader material on descent.
- 39:6/q27/p1 It descended whole on the Night of Power, then gradually. — Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn Abbas report a whole descent to Gabriel or the House of Might followed by measured delivery; Ibn Abbas cites 2:185 and 97:1. Abu Shama explains رسلا and star settings. Ibn Kathir reports consensus on a whole descent to the House of Might. Ibn Hajar prefers this as sound, connecting Ramadan review to each year's revelation. - 39:6/q27/p2 The needed annual portion descended each Night of Power. — Razi considers annual descent from the Preserved Tablet and remains undecided; Ibn Kathir attributes this possibility to Muqatil ibn Hayyan, and Halimi and Mawardi adopt it. - 39:6/q27/p3 Revelation began on the Night of Power and continued piecemeal. — Sha'bi gives this third account. [1 notes: ITQAN(الشعبي)]
- 39:6/q27/p4 Keepers transferred the whole Qur'an to Gabriel across twenty nights, and he delivered it across twenty years. — Mawardi's fourth account is called strange by Ibn Hajar; the ITQAN note records it as rejected. [0 notes: ITQAN(الماوردي)-]
- 39:6/q27/p5 The debt verse was the last Qur'anic verse connected with the Throne. — Ibn Shihab supplies this separate report concerning 2:282. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن شهاب)]

## 39:6/q28 What related terms and reports illuminate the call to turn toward God?  [* · interpretation]

- 39:6/q28/p1 Returning and gratitude answer divine benefaction; denial repays blessing with disbelief. — Jishumi defines ināba as returning, gives anāba/yunību, and contrasts denial with grateful recognition, glorification, and obedience.
