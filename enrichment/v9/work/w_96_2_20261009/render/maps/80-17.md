# 80:17: 10 questions

## 80:17/q01 What does قُتِلَ mean here?  [قُتِلَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether قُتِلَ retains its conventional imprecatory force, names punishment or destruction, or predicts an event; Ibn ʿAṭiyya disputes the exact gloss 'cursed'.
- 80:17/q01/p1 An imprecation against the human: may he be killed, ruined, or cursed. — Muqātil, Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, Abū Mālik and others gloss it as a curse. Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān call killing the gravest worldly calamity; Bursawī says spiritual destruction is harsher. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the expression began as a call for death, but divine imprecation conveys contempt and threat; compare passive قُتِلَ at 74:19. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 74:19–20, 51:10, and 85:4. Study Quran gives 'may man perish' and reports 'may man be cursed.' [36 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(جار الله); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري في الكشاف,الضحاك عن ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة وأبو مالك,صاحب الكشاف); TABATABAI(الكشاف); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; STUDYQURAN(IK and Q); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد)-]
- 80:17/q01/p2 It means the person is punished. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī transmit this alongside the curse gloss. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 80:17/q01/p3 It means the person is destroyed. — Al-Naḥḥās reasons that one who is killed has been destroyed; he also reports the curse gloss. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 80:17/q01/p4 It describes man's self-destruction. — Asad points to his note on sūra 74 for this rendering. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 80:17/q01/p5 It predicts the later killing of disbelievers, using the past tense for certainty. — Al-Ālūsī reports and rejects this proposal tied to the later fighting verse. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 80:17/q02 What force does the curse formula have?  [قُتِلَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether wonder belongs to the curse formula itself or only to the following exclamation; the passive follows from whether a real killer is intended.
- 80:17/q02/p1 It expresses blame, wrath, contempt, and threat, without a literal divine request. — Ibn Qutayba says such formulas blame rather than ask for actual harm. Rāzī says familiar Arab speech assigns severe punishment to grave vice despite God neither lacking power nor knowledge. Ibn al-Shaykh in Bursawī likewise denies divine inability or ignorance; Ibn ʿĀshūr says people pray to God, so God's own imprecation is disparagement and threat. Al-Biqāʿī calls the declarative form stronger than an imperative; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others stress its severe, compressed censure. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,جار الله); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:17/q02/p2 Arab imprecations can convey astonishment, and sometimes praise, through their conventional usage. — Ibn Qutayba names fine speech, poetry, argument and marksmanship; Imruʾ al-Qays's archer poem and Kaʿb b. Saʿd's هوت أمه marvel at extraordinary performance. Al-Māwardī uses this convention to explain wonder at ingratitude. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects applying that sense to this curse, since the following exclamation supplies wonder. Al-Ālūsī rejects as fabricated a different couplet attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays about desiring summer in winter. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(امرؤ القيس,كعب بن سعد الغنوي); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 80:17/q02/p3 The passive omits an actual killer because the verb is an imprecation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the common Qurʾanic passive usage at 74:19. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:17/q03 Who is ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether generic ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ denotes all humankind, its prevalent unbelieving members, the earlier audience, or a particular opponent; authors appeal to the following account of human origins and competing occasion reports.
- 80:17/q03/p1 The disbeliever or disbelievers generally, especially those denying resurrection. — Mujāhid repeatedly says Qurʾanic 'the human was killed' refers to an unbeliever; Sufyān had heard the same. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the form generic but intent specific. Jishumī prefers unbelievers because condemning believers this way is impossible. Abū Ḥayyān says general reference survives a specific occasion. Ibn ʿĀshūr says conventional generic usage targets prevalent polytheists, supported by 80:18–22 and widespread historical idol worship or creator denial. Kur'an Yolu specifies pagan opponents who disputed the Prophet and resurrection; Study Quran's IK specifies deniers without evidence. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(سفيان,مجاهد); NAHHAS(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(جملة المفسرين,مجاهد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK); IBNASHUR-FULL+; JISHUMI+]
- 80:17/q03/p2 Humanity as a class, including the self-satisfied person just described; the judgment may describe the prevalent tendency rather than every individual. — Abū Muslim says humanity generally. Rāzī prefers generality because the cause of reproach and the account of origins at 80:19 and 80:21 are general and yield greater benefit. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī allow both the prior self-satisfied person and the species; al-Ālūsī favors the general reading in view of the reported occasion. Ibn ʿĀshūr says generic definiteness may literally include all or conventionally describe most, though some people believe. Mujāhid in Jishumī says no person fulfills every divine duty. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the human tendency to follow desire and forget lordship. [12 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,مجاهد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 80:17/q03/p3 ʿUtba ibn Abī Lahab. — Muqātil, al-Kalbī, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima and many later sources identify him; the occasion report tells of his apostasy and death by a lion after the Prophet's prayer. Ibn ʿĀshūr observes that this story is separate from Ibn Umm Maktūm's visit and makes this a new opening. [22 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج والكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bir rivayete göre); STUDYQURAN(Some)]
- 80:17/q03/p4 Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is cited for this identification. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that Umayya was present when Ibn Umm Maktūm approached. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض المفسرين,قيل)]
- 80:17/q03/p5 The people the Prophet was addressing when Ibn Umm Maktūm approached. — Jishumī and Rāzī report this link to the earlier scene. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]
- 80:17/q03/p6 The wealthy person who looks down on the poor. — Rāzī reports this as an alternative target, tying the reproach to pride based on wealth. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]
- 80:17/q03/p7 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this among individual identifications. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 80:17/q04 What happened in the reported occasion concerning ʿUtba?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · reports]
- 80:17/q04/p1 After accepting Islam, ʿUtba renounced it or the Star revelation; the Prophet prayed against him, and a lion killed him on a journey to Syria. — Muqātil and later transmitters say reconciliation with Abū Lahab involved money or trade; reports vary between rejecting the Star, its Lord, that sūra alone, or the Qurʾān. The Prophet's wording is given as a prayer for a beast or 'one of God's dogs'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also records a wording asking whether ʿUtba feared such a beast. Al-Qurṭubī names al-Ghadīra and the companions' protective measures; al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī report his father's recognition that Muḥammad's prayer came true. Study Quran links the apostasy to sūra 53. [14 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); BIQAI-FULL(أبو لهب,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); STUDYQURAN(Some)]

## 80:17/q05 How is مَآ أَكْفَرَهُۥ construed?  [مَآ أَكْفَرَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: The function of mā: exclamatory indefinite subject, interrogative pronoun, or another object-related construal; the exclamation is defended as more eloquent, while the question is transmitted as a tafsīr reading.
- 80:17/q05/p1 An exclamation: how extreme his disbelief or ingratitude is. — Al-Ṭabarī and others marvel at disbelief despite God's blessings; al-Zajjāj compares 2:175. Al-Qurṭubī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Jurayj. Al-Suyūṭī and al-Burhān say exclamatory mā is an indefinite nominative subject with the following clause its predicate; they list 2:175 as another Qurʾanic instance and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's reading of 82:6 as a possible third. Abū Ḥayyān says the apparent form conveys wonder from a human perspective. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that amazement may concern both unbelief and its degree. [43 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH(بعضهم); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,ابن جريج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الكشاف); TABATABAI(قيل)+]
- 80:17/q05/p2 A reproachful question: what made him disbelieve or could justify his doing so? — Al-Farrāʾ prefers this as transmitted in tafsīr; al-Naḥḥās prefers it in light of God's signs and blessings. Al-Suddī, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, Muqātil and al-Kalbī are also cited. Al-Qurṭubī says mā is interrogative in the sense of ayy. Ibn Kathīr connects the question to denial of resurrection. Al-Ālūsī rejects the reading with the implied answer 'nothing justifies it'; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ finds exclamation more eloquent. [19 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي ويحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء,مقاتل، الكلبي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BURSEVI; FARRA+; NAHHAS+; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 80:17/q05/p3 It says how accursed the person is. — Al-Māwardī reports Qatāda's third gloss, repeated in Ibn Kathīr. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 80:17/q05/p4 It says the person is accursed for what he disbelieves, or asks what mankind disbelieves. — Study Quran records these alternative construals from Q; the first takes mā as what is disbelieved, and the second as the object of an inquiry. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 80:17/q06 What does the unbelief or ingratitude in أَكْفَرَهُۥ encompass?  [أَكْفَرَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether kufr is specifically failure to thank for gifts or the wider concealment and denial of divine truth; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ appeals to the following account of governance.
- 80:17/q06/p1 Ingratitude for God's abundant favors. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, Bursawī, Elmalılı and others relate it to blessings despite human failure to thank; the subsequent verses enumerate divine care. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ reports this as al-Kashshāf's reading but prefers a broader sense. [13 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الكشاف); TABATABAI-]
- 80:17/q06/p2 Concealing manifest truth generally, including denying God's lordship and neglecting worship. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ prefers this because the surrounding account of creation, measured life, guidance, death, burial, resurrection, and food describes divine governance, not only favors. The person knows he lacks independent control. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 80:17/q06/p3 Disbelief in God and His revelation, especially resurrection, as well as ingratitude toward Him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says its object, 'toward God,' is omitted as obvious; the unbelief denies oneness, bodily resurrection, the Messenger and revelation, and persists despite warnings. Study Quran says both ingratitude and disbelief, comparing 2:85–86. Al-Biqāʿī includes associating partners and resisting the Qurʾān. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 80:17/q07 Why does the verse censure the human so severely?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize complementary faults: ingratitude amid divine care, proud and ungrounded denial, and inward ignorance.
- 80:17/q07/p1 He receives God's creation, guidance, care, and provisions yet responds with pride, neglect, and ingratitude. — Rāzī contrasts Quraysh leaders' pride with origin as a lowly drop and end as a corpse; creation proves both Creator and resurrection (80:19, 80:21), and the following account spans life's beginning, middle and end. Al-Kashshāf traces gifts from birth through life's stages. Al-Biqāʿī says people forget their Lord and admire what He fashioned; Bursawī reports the failure to know oneself and Maker. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ describes comprehensive governance and food as evidence of subtle mercy (14:34). [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 80:17/q07/p2 He denies resurrection and truth without evidence, from improbability, ignorance, and stubbornness. — Ibn Kathīr calls the rejection mere improbability and ignorance. Study Quran's IK says deniers lack knowledge and find the truth far-fetched. Al-Biqāʿī names denial of resurrection, shirk, and resistance to the Qurʾān; the human creation and food cycle show divine power. Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses persistence despite repeated reminders and warnings. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 80:17/q07/p3 Ignorance of guidance and complacency obstruct goodness and gnosis. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ links ignorance in seeking guidance and complacency in the Lord's promise to blocked paths of good; al-Wāsiṭī treats ignorance of gnosis as arising from ignorance itself. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي)]

## 80:17/q08 How can divine speech express astonishment here?  [مَآ أَكْفَرَهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether astonishment necessarily requires an unknown cause, and whether an exclamation about divine action is assigned to God or to its human hearers.
- 80:17/q08/p1 It elicits wonder in hearers; God knows its cause, so no divine ignorance or helplessness is implied. — Al-Burhān prefers wonder directed at the audience and says God's knowledge of what merits wonder is called wonder; the learned explain divine exclamation, prayer and hope from the hearer's perspective, citing Sībawayh on 20:44. Abū Ḥayyān locates amazement in the human viewpoint. Bursawī says real surprise requires ignorance of a cause; Ibn al-Shaykh says the idiom conveys wrath. Al-Ālūsī calls it figurative for God. Rāzī says the familiar Arab form expresses punishment and vice. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(المحققون); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(جار الله); BURHAN+]
- 80:17/q08/p2 Magnifying God in exclamatory language is permissible because it accords with belief in His greatness. — Al-Burhān reports the counterview and its anonymous poem that God brings what is distant near; some scholars say astonishment magnifies a matter without implying a creator of that matter, and its cause can be hidden from us. [2 notes: BURHAN(بعض المحققين,قيل)]
- 80:17/q08/p3 Exclamation about God is objectionable if it suggests something made Him great, despite His eternal attributes. — Al-Burhān reports this view and says the dispute concerns whether true astonishment requires an unknown cause; he lists this verse with 2:175 and a reading of 82:6 among Qurʾanic exclamations. [3 notes: BURHAN(قيل)]

## 80:17/q09 How does this verse connect with the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the report about ʿUtba marks a fresh occasion or the human rebuke continues the earlier encounter and warning.
- 80:17/q09/p1 It follows the honored scribes and reminder by turning to denounce those who reject them, then invokes creation and resurrection as proof. — Al-Biqāʿī links the sūra's warning to receptive hearts, human creation and food as demonstrations of power (cf. 79:45); refusal of the exalted reminder makes rejection astonishing. Rāzī connects the prior leaders' pride with the lowly origin and final death of the human. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes the self-sufficient man of 80:5 in the generic designation. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:17/q09/p2 The verses can continue the preceding passage as one context. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ allows continuity even while noting that other commentators claim a separate occasion. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 80:17/q09/p3 The sentence is a new opening, connected thematically but with a separate ʿUtba occasion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Ibn ʿAbbās's ʿUtba story is unrelated to the visit of Ibn Umm Maktūm in 80:1–2; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ reports the separate-occasion view. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]

## 80:17/q10 What effect does the verse's compact form have?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes discuss different expressive features: concentrated censure, measured sound, and the authenticity of a purported poetic comparison.
- 80:17/q10/p1 Its brevity, paired curse and exclamation, intensifies anger and comprehensive blame. — Al-Kashshāf calls the phrasing exceptionally severe and compressed; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr similarly connect concision with wrath and censure. Al-Ālūsī reports the Imam's pairing of the worst worldly punishment with the worst moral offense and his claim the wording was previously unheard. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,جار الله); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:17/q10/p2 Its measure and sound balance fit the sense; the clause follows a shortened ramal meter. — Al-Burhān cites it as wording balanced with meaning. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the first, truncated form of ramal. [3 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 80:17/q10/p3 An alleged pre-Islamic poetic parallel is inauthentic. — Al-Ālūsī says a couplet attributed to Imruʾ al-Qays about wishing for summer in winter was fabricated by a later poet. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
