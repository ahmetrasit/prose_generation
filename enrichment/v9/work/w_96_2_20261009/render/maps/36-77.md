# 36:77: 17 questions

## 36:77/q01 Why is the alif of يَرَ absent in writing?  [يَرَ · readings]
turns on: The reason for the written alif's deletion.
- 36:77/q01/p1 The written alif is shortened because matching letters meet. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects the competing explanation that it was removed to prevent two sukūns; he also rejects attributing tarāy to Hishām, comparing 53:18. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 36:77/q01/p2 It was omitted to avoid two sukūns. — Ibn al-Jazarī explicitly argues against this proposed explanation. [0 notes: NASHR-]

## 36:77/q02 How does the rāʾ of يَرَ behave in imāla?  [يَرَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the alif after rāʾ was originally removed by jussive grammar or only in connected pronunciation, and whether the Hishām attribution is sound.
- 36:77/q02/p1 An originally jussive deletion of the following alif prevents imāla here; imāla can survive only a connecting deletion. — Ibn al-Jazarī distinguishes awalam yara al-insān from cases in which an alif follows rāʾ but is deleted only in connection; he cites 26:61 and 6:77. Rāʾ attracts imāla because of its strong, trilled articulation. [2 notes: NASHR]
- 36:77/q02/p2 A tarāy reading is attributed to Hishām. — Ibn al-Jazarī calls the attribution unsound. [0 notes: NASHR-]

## 36:77/q03 What does يَرَ mean here?  [يَرَ · meaning]
- 36:77/q03/p1 It means to know with the certainty of direct sight. — Al-Biqāʿī calls the knowledge as evident as eyesight; Bursevī calls it inward or intellectual seeing; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says certain knowledge. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 36:77/q04 What does the opening question أَوَلَمْ convey?  [أَوَلَمْ · rhetoric]
- 36:77/q04/p1 Its hamza rebukes and expresses amazement, while its wāw joins an implied clause. — Bursevī combines denial and wonder with an omitted coordinate; al-Ālūsī compares the earlier question in 36:71 and says the question can repeat and strengthen that rebuke by pointing to people's clearest knowledge, their own creation. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:77/q05 Who is ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ in this verse?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]

Al-Kashshāf's report places Abū Jahl, al-ʿĀṣ and al-Walīd with him. Ibn al-Jawzī says most exegetes follow this; al-Ālūsī relays Abū Ḥayyān's preference and the report that the Prophet killed Ubayy at Uḥud. ## 36:77/q06 Why say ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ instead of using a pronoun?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the general noun chiefly universalizes the rebuke or voices disgust at the Quraysh.
- 36:77/q06/p1 The noun exposes a trait of human nature, extending the rebuke beyond the immediate opponent. — Al-Irshād, cited by Bursevī, says a pronoun would obscure that denial concerns human beings as such; al-Ālūsī likewise emphasizes human nature; Ibn Kathīr and Bursevī apply the general wording to every denier, and al-Biqāʿī includes the original opponent within the species. [5 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 36:77/q06/p2 The broad noun expresses disgust at the Quraysh. — Iṣlāḥī connects the choice to the conduct of the Quraysh under discussion. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:77/q07 What encounter is reported as the verse's occasion?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ خَصِيمٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The reports vary over the challenger's identity, and some vary over who accompanied him, the Prophet's wording, and the span of revelation.
- 36:77/q07/p1 Ubayy ibn Khalaf crumbled a decayed bone before the Prophet and challenged its resurrection. — Muqātil sets it among Meccan leaders hearing the resurrection claim; al-Kashshāf names Abū Jahl, al-ʿĀṣ and al-Walīd in the group; Bursevī names a Quraysh group. The Prophet affirmed resurrection and, in many accounts, warned Ubayy that he would be raised and enter Hell. Some reports say the closing verses of Yā Sīn were revealed. Al-Ālūsī adds the report that the Prophet killed Ubayy at Uḥud. [10 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(أبي بن خلف); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة والجمهور); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد، وعكرمة، وعروة بن الزبير، والسدي، وقتادة); DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(أبي بن خلف); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مردويه)]
- 36:77/q07/p2 Al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil crushed a bone and challenged the Prophet about resurrection. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr reports differ in wording; Ibn Kathīr says one chain through Saʿīd omits Ibn ʿAbbās and places the bone at al-Baṭḥā. The Prophet answered that God would raise him and put him in Hell; reports say this verse or the closing verses of Yā Sīn followed. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس)]
- 36:77/q07/p3 Umayya ibn Khalaf crumbled a bone and disputed resurrection with the Prophet. — Al-Ḥasan's report in al-Wāḥidī says the verse was revealed in response. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن)]
- 36:77/q07/p4 ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy made a similar bone challenge. — Al-ʿAwfī's Ibn ʿAbbās report says a similar incident happened to him; al-Durr gives the Prophet's answer of resurrection and Hell. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr reject this identification because Yā Sīn is Meccan whereas Ibn Ubayy was in Medina; al-Ālūsī repeats the objection. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(العوفي عن ابن عباس)-]
- 36:77/q07/p5 Abū Jahl was the bone challenger. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmits this variant from Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Ālūsī notes another Ibn ʿAbbās report. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس)]
- 36:77/q07/p6 An unnamed polytheist or man brought a decayed bone, challenged resurrection, and received the Prophet's answer. — Al-Jishumī says the passage from this verse to the sūra's end was revealed. Al-Qurṭubī reports the Prophet's promise to resurrect the questioner and admit him to Hell before this verse was revealed. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 36:77/q08 What does نُّطْفَةٍۢ mean?  [نُّطْفَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term here is restricted to semen or can include the fertilized cell.
- 36:77/q08/p1 A small drop of water, especially a man's semen. — Al-Qurṭubī relates it to natafa, 'to drip'; Bursevī calls it clear water and semen; al-Nasafī says the fluid passes through an impure channel. Al-Biqāʿī stresses how tiny and seemingly useless it is. Study Quran equates it with sperm or base fluid and cites 16:4, 18:37, 22:5, 35:11, 40:67, 53:46, 75:37, 76:2, 80:19, 32:8 and 77:20. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:77/q08/p2 In Qurʾānic usage it may also denote a fertilized cell or zygote. — Kur'an Yolu gives this as an alternative to male semen. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 36:77/q09 What lies between creation from the drop and the mature human?  [خَلَقْنَٰهُ مِن نُّطْفَةٍۢ فَإِذَا هُوَ · interpretation]
- 36:77/q09/p1 The verse compresses stages of formation, birth and endowment with human faculties. — Abū Ḥayyān says stages are elided and points to 23:13. Bursevī reports the hadith of forty days each as drop, clot and morsel, after which an angel writes lifespan, provision and final fortune; he then describes human form, nourishment, intellect, senses, heart, spirit, motion and speech. Al-Qushayrī stresses assembled limbs and reason; al-Biqāʿī contrasts the father's soil and mother's flesh and bones. Study Quran points to 32:7–11, 22:5 and 23:14 on human creation and gestation. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 36:77/q09/p2 A reported divine saying emphasizes God's complete provision through life, followed by late regret at death. — Ibn Kathīr cites Aḥmad and Ibn Mājah for the saying: God creates, proportions and clothes Adam's child from a tiny origin, but when death reaches the throat the person asks too late to give charity. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بسر بن جحاش)]

## 36:77/q10 How does فَإِذَا هُوَ introduce the later state?  [فَإِذَا هُوَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the sudden clause foregrounds hostile disputation or the created capacity for articulate argument.
- 36:77/q10/p1 It introduces a sudden nominal sentence of persistent disputation after unstated intervening stages. — Al-Samarqandī, cited by Bursevī, says the sense of suddenness is implicit and can construe the dispute itself or the encounter with it, comparing 30:36. Abū Ḥayyān says the stages are elided and cites 23:13. Al-Ālūsī says the nominal form marks persistence, while suddenness heightens wonder. [3 notes: BURSEVI(السمرقندي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:77/q10/p2 The clause coordinates with 'created him' and depicts the sudden outcome of creation: an eloquent, discerning person. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative, with mubīn taken transitively and suddenness attached to the creative transformation. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 36:77/q11 What does خَصِيمٌۭ مُّبِينٌۭ describe?  [خَصِيمٌۭ مُّبِينٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase chiefly marks hostile, open opposition or the striking new human capacity for articulate reasoning.
- 36:77/q11/p1 A conspicuous, contentious opponent who states his false case openly. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī gloss mubīn as clear in dispute or able to lay out an argument. Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī make the opponent's quarrel with the Prophet or God over resurrection explicit. Bursevī allows either that his quarrel is clear or that he makes his argument apparent; al-Ālūsī takes mubīn transitively, revealing inner thoughts. Al-Ālūsī says the nominal sentence indicates continuing dispute. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:77/q11/p2 A mature person is articulate, discerning and able to formulate and express an argument. — Al-Zamakhsharī contrasts insignificant fluid with articulate agency and compares 43:18; al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān explain the capacity to express what is within. Al-Ālūsī reports this as an alternative reading in which mubīn is transitive. Asad cites Rāzī's treatment at 16:4 of khasīm as a high manifestation of rational speech; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes first creation itself yields an articulate disputant. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Razi); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 36:77/q12 What does the human's change from drop to disputant teach about conduct?  [نُّطْفَةٍۢ خَصِيمٌۭ مُّبِينٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the change is foregrounded as a rebuke of arrogant denial or a reminder of the gift of human capacities.
- 36:77/q12/p1 It rebukes denial of resurrection and the ingratitude and arrogance of using God-given powers against Him. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī say reflection on one's lowly origin should end the dispute. Al-Zamakhsharī stresses unbelief, baseness and the contradiction of arguing that lifeless bones cannot live when the disputant arose from lifeless matter; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān agree. Al-Qushayrī compares a trained archer turning on his teacher; al-Biqāʿī cites that poem, and Bursevī adds poetry about a pupil and a puppy biting its keeper. Al-Bayḍāwī calls denial repayment of an immense gift with disobedience. Iṣlāḥī links favors with gratitude and accountability. Kur'an Yolu says the human arrogantly judges Creator and Prophet by reason alone; Study Quran says he becomes his own adversary by ignoring his origin. Ibn Kathīr cites Aḥmad and Ibn Mājah's divine saying about late regret for charity at death. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بسر بن جحاش); BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 36:77/q12/p2 The transformation also recalls God's favor in giving existence and the human ability to reason and dispute. — Al-Māwardī describes the move from nonexistence to disputation as a favor; Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as an alternative. Al-Qushayrī lists proportioned limbs, reason and discrimination; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī describe the resulting articulate faculty. Asad relays Rāzī's account of it as the highest manifestation of rational speech. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]

## 36:77/q13 How does first creation from a drop support resurrection?  [خَلَقْنَٰهُ مِن نُّطْفَةٍۢ · theology]
- 36:77/q13/p1 The Creator who first brought a human from a tiny, lifeless origin can restore life after death. — Al-Māwardī argues from first creation from nothing. Al-Qushayrī says revival is no harder than first creation and cites fire from green branches and the equivalence of creating a likeness; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls first creation itself revival after nonexistence. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān stress the contradiction in denying life for old bones despite one's lifeless origin. Al-Bayḍāwī calls restoration easier than first creation. Ibn Kathīr links the weak drop to 77:20–22 and 76:2; Study Quran cites creation texts 16:4, 18:37, 22:5, 35:11, 40:67, 53:46, 75:37, 76:2, 80:19, 32:8 and 77:20. Kur'an Yolu adds that Makkan pagans accepted God as cosmic Creator. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 36:77/q14 Why did the Makkan polytheists mock resurrection?  [* · interpretation]
- 36:77/q14/p1 They resisted prophethood and resurrection to preserve established worship of other gods and sway their community. — Kur'an Yolu says mockery served to influence those around them. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 36:77/q15 What inward life and death can this creation image suggest?  [خَلَقْنَٰهُ مِن نُّطْفَةٍۢ · inward]
- 36:77/q15/p1 God gives believing hearts life by gnosis, while desire and tyranny deaden unbelieving souls. — Al-Qushayrī parallels this with God's reviving souls after death and forming humans from sperm and birds from eggs. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 36:77/q16 How does this verse connect to its context in Yā Sīn?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the contextual force is the renewed proof from human creation or consolation following the opponents' speech.
- 36:77/q16/p1 It resumes and sharpens the proof of resurrection after earlier signs of God's power and provision. — Al-Biqāʿī points from change and encompassing knowledge at 36:68 to a clearer proof here. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī contrast the previous refutation of shirk from people's possessions with this proof from themselves. Al-Ālūsī links the two questions at 36:71 through inversion: God provided goods for gratitude, yet people disbelieve; He made people from lowly fluid for humility, yet they argue. Iṣlāḥī connects 36:71 and 36:73 to gratitude, monotheism and final accountability, and says the closing section returns to the sūra's opening themes. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:77/q16/p2 It continues consolation and assurance to the Prophet amid his opponents' words. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls the reminder a second consolation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī tentatively says the renewed resurrection argument may explain the preceding reference to their speech. Iṣlāḥī says 36:76 continues the assurance as Quraysh insult the Prophet and mock resurrection. Al-Ālūsī rejects construing this verse itself as consolation parallel to 36:76. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 36:77/q17 What virtues of Yā Sīn are reported?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The two reports promise different benefits.
- 36:77/q17/p1 Sincere recitation brings forgiveness and a reward like reciting the Qurʾān twenty-two times. — Al-Zamakhsharī attributes this to a report from the Prophet that also calls Yā Sīn the Qurʾān's heart. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 36:77/q17/p2 Yā Sīn intercedes for its reciter and brings forgiveness to its listener. — Al-Zamakhsharī transmits this second Prophetic report without a grading. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
