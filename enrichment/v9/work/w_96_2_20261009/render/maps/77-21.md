# 77:21: 9 questions

## 77:21/q01 What does the pronoun in فَجَعَلْنَٰهُ refer to?  [فَجَعَلْنَٰهُ · referent]
- 77:21/q01/p1 The reproductive water or fluid from which a child forms. — Muqātil calls it water; al-Ṭabarī calls it weak fluid; al-Nasafī explicitly identifies the pronoun's referent as water; Ibn Kathīr speaks of male and female fluid gathered in the womb; al-Biqāʿī calls it a drop; Kur'an Yolu calls the despised water sperm and cites 75:37. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 77:21/q02 What is the قَرَارٍۢ in which the fluid is placed?  [قَرَارٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether Ibn ʿAṭiyya's alternative expression names the womb specifically or the woman's abdomen more broadly; the notes do not argue between them.
- 77:21/q02/p1 A place of rest and settlement, identified as the womb. — Mujāhid identifies the place as the womb, as reported directly and by al-Ṭabarī, who also mentions 23:13. Al-Ṭabarī describes a stable place in which the weak fluid settles. Wahb b. Munabbih says the womb protects the fetus from heat and cold. Al-Rāzī says formative material remains established there, unlike material that does not form a child, and mentions 23:13. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines qarār as a place of settling and remaining. Iṣlāḥī says qarār can mean rest or a place of rest and that the womb was appointed for safety and successive development. Ibn Kathīr specifies a place designed to hold what is deposited; al-Biqāʿī says development proceeds there through successive stages. The Study Quran reports IK and Ṭ for the womb. [25 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK and Ṭ)]
- 77:21/q02/p2 A woman's abdomen. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this as an alternative expression to the womb. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 77:21/q03 What does مَّكِينٍ convey about the resting place?  [مَّكِينٍ · meaning]
- 77:21/q03/p1 It is firmly established and secure, protecting what is placed in it. — Al-Māwardī reports al-Kalbī's explanation: the fluid neither flows back out nor spreads through the body. He reports Wahb b. Munabbih that the fetus is shielded from heat and cold. Al-Ṭabarī calls the place stable; al-Rāzī says formative material remains established there. Ibn Kathīr says it holds and protects its contents; al-Biqāʿī mentions protection from air and other harms. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the adjective from the root for settled security. Iṣlāḥī specifies protection from hazards, dangers, and undue intervention. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 77:21/q04 How does the description مَّكِينٍ work rhetorically?  [مَّكِينٍ · rhetoric]
- 77:21/q04/p1 Calling the abstract resting place firmly established is a rational metaphor. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that what is firmly established is the estimate within the resting place, namely the womb. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 77:21/q05 What happens to the fluid in this secure place?  [فَجَعَلْنَٰهُ قَرَارٍۢ مَّكِينٍ · interpretation]
- 77:21/q05/p1 God holds and protects reproductive fluid in the womb while it develops through stages into a human being. — Al-Ṭabarī says weak fluid settles there. Al-Māwardī reports al-Kalbī that it does not flow back out or spread through the body, and Wahb that the fetus is shielded from heat and cold. Al-Rāzī distinguishes child-forming material from material that does not form a child. Ibn Kathīr says male and female fluid are gathered there; al-Biqāʿī mentions protection from air and other harms through successive stages. Iṣlāḥī stresses the fluid's remarkable staged development. Kur'an Yolu says God develops simple semen in the womb and endows the resulting human with material and spiritual abilities, making humanity Earth's most perfect beings. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 77:21/q06 What does human creation here establish?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw different conclusions from creation: God's creative attributes and sole lordship, the possibility of recreation, and the necessity of a judgment that answers divine wisdom, governance, and moral obligation.
- 77:21/q06/p1 Resurrection is possible because God already created humans from humble fluid and can recreate them after death. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the stages of creation support resurrection because overwhelming divine power cannot be thwarted; the following praise reinforces this. Iṣlāḥī says the initial creation from mud and a drop of fluid answers deniers who ask how decayed bones can be recreated: what is already evident makes recreation possible. He also cites God's power, wisdom, creativity, the provision system, and innate moral awareness among the passage's arguments for judgment. Kur'an Yolu says human origin and development display creative power, make denial of resurrection baseless, and show that the creator of a perfect human from simple water can raise that human after death. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 77:21/q06/p2 The Day of Judgment and recompense must come. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reasons that creation and governance of human development prove sole lordship; lordship entails submission and obligation, and obligation requires recompense on a Day of Decision. Iṣlāḥī says God's power, wisdom, and creativity in human formation require judgment; the elaborate system sustaining humanity and innate awareness of good and evil supply further grounds. He summarizes these as arguments that judgment is required by God's wisdom and provision, and certain rather than merely conceivable. [6 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 77:21/q06/p3 Creation displays God's power, providence, wisdom, and creativity. — Iṣlāḥī points to human physical structure and stages of creation as signs of power, providence, and wisdom. Kur'an Yolu calls human origins and development a striking example of creative power and describes the gift of material and spiritual abilities. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 77:21/q06/p4 The governance of human creation proves God's sole lordship. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī presents creation as proof of sole lordship; he says that lordship entails creatures' submission and obligation and, ultimately, recompense. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 77:21/q07 What response to God's gifts does the passage call for or expose?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe both the obligation implied by lordship and humanity's failure to meet it.
- 77:21/q07/p1 Creatures owe submission and obedience under God's lordship. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says lordship entails submission and obligation, which in turn require recompense on the Day of Decision; his account of the passage says it reproaches arrogance toward worship and revelation. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 77:21/q07/p2 People show ingratitude and disobey despite the priceless gifts of creation. — Kur'an Yolu connects this response to the surah's repeated warning, already present in 77:18: 'Woe on that day to the deniers.' [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 77:21/q08 Which other creation account is this verse linked with?  [* · links]
- 77:21/q08/p1 It parallels 32:8 and the account of human creation in Sūrat al-Muʾminūn. — Al-Wāḥidī compares God's making offspring from an extract of lowly water in 32:8 and notes that the matter is also discussed in Sūrat al-Muʾminūn. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī mention 23:13 alongside the womb, while Kur'an Yolu mentions 75:37 alongside the identification of the despised water as sperm. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 77:21/q09 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources focus on different parts of the sequence: the turn to creation, its warning and praise, and the pairing of womb with earth.
- 77:21/q09/p1 It turns from historical evidence to human creation as evidence for resurrection and the Day of Judgment. — Iṣlāḥī explicitly places the turn after the historical evidence. His description of the preceding argument also refers to signs within human beings and in the external world, followed by a vivid depiction of unseen punishment for deniers. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 77:21/q09/p2 It belongs to a sequence proving lordship and the Day of Decision while warning deniers. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the passage recalls punishment for deniers and blessings for the pious and reproaches arrogance toward worship and revelation. Kur'an Yolu relates human ingratitude to the warning repeated since 77:18. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the following praise in 77:23 reinforces the argument from God's power. [3 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 77:21/q09/p3 The womb and earth together show people enclosed in God's power before and after birth, through life and death. — Elmalılı links the need for a dwelling before birth and after death to the womb and the earth. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
