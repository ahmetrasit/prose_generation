# 96:2/q11 What legal implication is taken from the verse's blood and earth imagery? [عَلَقٍ · law]
turns on: The notes make different legal uses of the shared term and of material transformation.
- 96:2/q11/p1 A word shared by blood and sticky earth can carry both senses at once.
  reasons: Biqai cites al-Shafi'i's use of this verse as evidence for applying a homonym to both meanings simultaneously.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI(الشافعي); BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:96:2/r6 BIQAI-FULL:v8p479/r7
- 96:2/q11/p2 Blood and earth in their original states are inedible, but transformation can make their products lawful.
  reasons: Biqai says blood transformed into flesh and earth mixed with water transformed into dates or grain become lawful; reddish semen has not thereby transformed. He compares changes of legal status in marriage and other matters and says eating blood conveys its qualities.

# 96:2/q13 How does the verse connect reading and human creation? [خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · links]
turns on: Whether creation establishes the purpose of reading, a progression to knowledge, or the Prophet's newly enabled recitation.
- 96:2/q13/p1 The human was created for Quranic reading and understanding.
  reasons: Alusi reports Zamakhshari's reading of the creation clause after the reading command, compared with al-Rahman 55:1-3.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)
  notes: ALUSI:96:2/r3 ALUSI-FULL:v30p180/r2
- 96:2/q13/p2 Creation, reading and teaching form a movement from human origins to knowledge and revealed guidance.
  reasons: Razi connects the lowly clot with knowledge through 96:4-5 and the rational proof of lordship with transmitted revelation. Baydawi similarly moves from rational to transmitted knowledge. Ibn Kathir says writing preserves knowledge, distinguishes mental, spoken and written knowledge, cites aphorisms on writing and acting on knowledge, and says Adam's teaching distinguished him from angels. Bint al-Shati reads creation, pen and teaching together.

# 96:2/q14 What was first revealed, and how much of this sura came first? [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether 'first sura' means the opening five verses or the complete chapter, and the competing priority claims.
- 96:2/q14/p1 The opening five verses of al-ʿAlaq, ending at 96:5, were the first revelation; the remainder came later.
  reasons: Tabari preserves Aisha, Ubayd b. Umayr, Abu Raja, Ata and Mujahid, the latter adding Nun and the Pen afterward. Wahidi reports some scholars locating the five at Hira. Baghawi and Thalabi cite most commentators. Ibn Kathir calls these the first mercy. Burhan says Bukhari's Aisha report was authenticated by al-Hakim and Muslim extends to 96:5. Durr cites Ibn Abbas. Ibn Ashur bases the conclusion on Aisha, Abu Musa and most exegetes and dates it Ramadan 17, year 40 after the Elephant. Bint al-Shati says Tabari and biography stop at verse five and cites Razi for later completion amid opposition; she reports Abu Hayyan judging Muddaththir and Fatiha first claims unsound.

# 96:2/q15 What happened when the opening verses were delivered at Hira? [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in whether they describe repeated embraces, a written silk sheet, or chest opening and weighing.
- 96:2/q15/p1 After true dreams and seclusion, Gabriel repeatedly commanded reading, pressed Muhammad three times, and delivered the opening verses.
