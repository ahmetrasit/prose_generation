# 96:2/q16 What happened after Muhammad returned from the first revelation? [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the note concerns immediate reassurance and Waraqa or the subsequent pause and revelations.
- 96:2/q16/p1 Khadija reassured him through his virtues, and Waraqa identified Gabriel as Moses' angel and predicted opposition.
  reasons: Tabari, Baghawi and Ibn Kathir list kinship, truthful speech, support for the needy, hospitality and help in hardship; Waraqa promised support if still alive. Ibn Shaddad adds the later pause and al-Duha reassurance. Bint al-Shati's Aisha account says Muhammad saw Gabriel across the sky. Baghawi notes no grading for its report.

# 96:2/q18 How does this verse connect with neighboring suras and the rest of al-ʿAlaq? [* · links]
turns on: The sources link the verse to earlier suras through human origin and destiny, prophetic rank, or the reported chest opening.
- 96:2/q18/p1 Al-Tin's best human form is followed by al-ʿAlaq's account of human material origin and later destiny.
  reasons: Alusi links 95:4 to the material origin and afterlife. Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqai, connects creation in the best form and decline in 95:4-5 with divergent destinies under prior will, citing 32:13; God's wise judgment in 95:7-8 concerns the community despite address to the Prophet, as 39:65 does. The Qur'an's guidance explains beginnings and ends, and sūra order is based on arrangement, not revelation chronology.
  holders (7 notes): ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير)

- 96:2/q18/p2 The preceding suras show the Prophet's highest rank, while this sura also portrays a person who forbids prayer.
  reasons: Ibn al-Zubayr compares promised satisfaction and expanded breast, 93:5 and 94:1, with Moses' prayer at 20:25; he contrasts the blocker of prayer in 96:9 with the command to disobey him in 96:19.

# 96:2/q19 What wider Quranic themes are anticipated by the opening passage? [* · links]
turns on: Whether the opening is related to wider creation and accountability passages or to letters, writing and the Quranic challenge.
- 96:2/q19/p1 Its reading command and creation, pen and teaching themes preview the Quran's guidance and human accountability.
  reasons: Itqan calls the opening a title of the Qur'an because it names reading in God's name, legal and religious knowledge, and divine teaching. Bint al-Shati connects creation to later origin, death and resurrection passages; Corpus Coranicum contrasts early Meccan womb-development texts (86:5-7, 80:18-19, 75:38, 77:20, 70:39) with later Adam-earth accounts (55:14, 18:37, 22:5, 23:12-14, 32:7-9, 40:67).
  holders (5 notes): ITQAN; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: ITQAN:v3p284/r3 BINTSHATI:v2p19/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p19/r3 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:1-2:anmerkung/r10 CORPUSCORANICUM:96:1-2:anmerkung/r11
- 96:2/q19/p2 Reading and the pen connect to the disjointed nun and the Quran's later challenge to opponents.
  reasons: Bint al-Shati says the isolated nun in 68:1 is a letter whose sound gains significance in words; the Prophet had not read earlier scripture (29:48), yet opponents resisted a human messenger. She says nine early Meccan suras opened with letters amid accusations of folklore, poetry, soothsaying and magic; the Quran moved from eloquence to explicit challenge and recalled punished peoples.
