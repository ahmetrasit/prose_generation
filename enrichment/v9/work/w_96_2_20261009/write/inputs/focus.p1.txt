# 96:2/q05 Why is 'ʿalaq' plural or collective? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ عَلَقٍ · grammar]
- 96:2/q05/p1 The generic 'human' has a collective sense, so plural 'ʿalaq' matches all humans although each begins from one ʿalaqa.
  reasons: Farra, Tabari and others compare singular/plural pairs such as shajara/shajar and qasaba/qasab. Kashshaf and Razi cite 103:2. Samīn says each person comes from a clot. Ibn Ashur calls alaq a collective noun. Farra, Wahidi, Ibn Jawzi, Bursevi and Alusi add that it fits the verse endings. Elmalili reports Baydawi's grammatical account.

# 96:2/q07 Why is the human singled out after creation in general? [خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether specification chiefly marks human honor, a persuasive proof, explanatory magnification, or an inward account of prophethood.
- 96:2/q07/p1 Humans are noble or honored, and revelation addresses them.
  reasons: Kashshaf, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Bursevi cite the addressee of revelation and rank among earthly creatures; Abu Hayyan notes Zamakhshari's suggestion concerning angels. Qurtubi says explicit naming honors humanity. Bint al-Shati adds reading, learning and responsibility; Baydawi relates it to worship.

- 96:2/q07/p2 Human formation is an especially wondrous and accessible proof of the Creator.
  reasons: Ibn Atiyya points to intellect, perception, ligaments and bones. Tabrisi calls the move from all creatures to humanity specification after generality, comparing general unseen belief and the afterlife and Labid's line on blame and slowness. Biqai calls humanity the highest animal, a summary of the cosmos; Baydawi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur say its constitution establishes worship or divine oneness. Ibn Ashur cites 51:21 because each can reflect on oneself. Bursevi calls creation the earliest blessing and evidence of existence, knowledge and wisdom.
