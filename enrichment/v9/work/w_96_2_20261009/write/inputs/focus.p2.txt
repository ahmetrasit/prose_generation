# 96:2/q08 Why mention the clot stage of human origin? [مِنْ عَلَقٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stage is cited for its lowliness, audience familiarity, place in formation, life-bearing quality, or a prophetic allusion.
- 96:2/q08/p1 The low beginning and mature, rational or knowledgeable human reveal God's favor, power and wise direction.
  reasons: Mawardi contrasts a lowly clot with upright reason and discernment; Jishumi urges gratitude and worship. Qurtubi, Baydawi, Razi, Bursevi, Alusi and Tabatabai stress the ascent to human perfection, knowledge and writing; Razi cites 96:4-5 and calls knowledge humanity's noblest trait. Asad reads simplicity against spiritual and intellectual potential. Ibn Ashur speaks of hidden capacities and earthly sovereignty. Ibn Kathir repeatedly says the passage calls attention to humanity's beginning in a clinging clot.

# 96:2/q09 What divine claim does human creation establish? [خَلَقَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ · theology]
turns on: Which inference the notes draw from creation: exclusive lordship, a knowing cause, divine attributes, the ability to teach, or resurrection.
- 96:2/q09/p1 God alone creates and so alone merits divinity and worship.
  reasons: Razi's theologians say creation distinguishes God's essence and capacity to originate defines divinity, using Pharaoh and Moses in 26:23-26; idols are carved by humans. Razi invokes 43:87 and 16:17: acknowledged creator and noncreator cannot be equal. Abu Hayyan notes Arabs called idols lords, so the Creator attribute excludes them. Ibn Ashur sees a reproach to polytheists for missing proof within themselves.

  notes: RAZI:96:2/r1 RAZI:96:2/r4 RAZI:96:2/r7 RAZI-FULL:v32p16/r2 RAZI-FULL:v32p16/r3 BIQAI:96:2/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p479/r4 IBNASHUR:96:2/r5 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p438/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p17/r5 BINTSHATI:v2p18/r1 TABATABAI:96:1-7/r4
- 96:2/q09/p2 Ordered development establishes a knowing, choosing Lord rather than self-sufficient natural causation.
  reasons: Razi says a created cause needs a cause, while an eternal cause either necessitates its effects or acts by choice; ordered beneficial changes imply knowledge and choice, with 16:17. He recounts Abu Hanifa advising Zufar to present opponents' arguments before his own, as an example of gradual persuasion. Biqai says God employs causes in the world yet could create directly.

- 96:2/q09/p3 The created human supplies rational proof of divine power, wisdom, knowledge and mercy.
  reasons: Razi links the clot to divine attributes and the pen to transmitted rulings, so knowing lordship grounds prophethood. Baydawi calls knowing God the first obligation; Baydawi and Ibn Ashur see the opening as proofs of existence, oneness, acts and perfection. Kuranyolu says understanding Creator and creation supports human development.
