# 87:8: 14 questions

## 87:8/q01 What does God's facilitation of the Prophet involve?  [وَنُيَسِّرُكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether facilitation names the Prophet's readiness for a course or the breadth of affairs God makes easy for him.
- 87:8/q01/p1 God prepares and enables the Prophet to carry out an easy course. — Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taysīr as making a task easy for its doer and says the surface wording is a metaphor for empowering the Prophet; his readiness extends to preserving the Qurʾān, conveying the law and gaining good outcomes. Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise stress readiness and capacity; Biqāʿī includes contemplation and an inclination to ease. Corpus Coranicum compares Q 92:7 and 92:10. [15 notes: JISHUMI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:8/q01/p2 God makes all of the Prophet's affairs easy and grants success in what he undertakes. — ʿAṭāʾ extends the promise to all Muhammad's affairs; Shawkānī applies it to every religious or worldly undertaking. Al-Rāzī points to the unmatched transformation of an orphan among an ignorant people into a universal guide. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]

## 87:8/q02 Why is the Prophet himself the object of 'We ease'?  [نُيَسِّرُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the direct object describes a prepared person figuratively or an inverted expression for easing the way itself.
- 87:8/q02/p1 The wording makes the Prophet fit for the easy way until ease is a settled capacity or disposition. — Rāzī says the agent is made ready for the act and cites the Prophetic saying and Q 92:7; Bayḍāwī says this wording suits coordination with 'We shall make you recite.' Biqāʿī says the Prophet comes to love ease; Bursawī and Ālūsī say mastery becomes a settled trait; Elmali̇lı says success becomes ingrained. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the surface reading as metaphorical preparation, cites the ordinary object construction and Moses' prayer at Q 20:26, and says the lām marks the goal. Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares Moses' pledge at Q 7:105. [18 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 87:8/q02/p2 The construction may invert the ordinary expression: God makes the easy way easy for the Prophet. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as an alternative that keeps the verb literal; he says inversion strongly identifies the facilitated person with the facilitated way, comparing an Arabic camel-and-trough expression, a verse of al-ʿAjjāj and Q 28:76. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:8/q03 What does the divine plural in 'We ease' convey?  [نُيَسِّرُكَ · rhetoric]
- 87:8/q03/p1 It magnifies the gift by magnifying its giver. — Rāzī compares other divine first-person plurals, citing Q 97:1, 12:2, 15:9 and 108:1 across the two notes. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 87:8/q04 What does the lām in 'for the easy way' express?  [لِلْيُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:8/q04/p1 It marks the purpose or end for which the Prophet is equipped, or his readiness to receive it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the benefited person or purpose normally follows the verb with a lām, citing Moses' prayer at Q 20:26; here it marks the way of ease and its acceptance. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:8/q05 What is the form of al-yusrā, and what noun does it qualify?  [الْيُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:8/q05/p1 It is a feminine elative adjective, with an omitted noun most likely meaning the religious law or way. — Ṭabarī calls it the feminine built from yusr. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the feminine of al-aysar and says its fuʿlā form intensifies ease; the omitted head can be feminine singular or a plural construed as feminine, and context favors the law the Messenger conveys. Iṣlāḥī likewise identifies an adjective with an implicit noun. [8 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:8/q06 What is the easy way promised in al-yusrā?  [الْيُسْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'the easy way' names conduct, the law, revelation, Paradise, the whole mission, worldly and afterlife outcomes, inner ease or the transition out of hardship.
- 87:8/q06/p1 It is the easy, tolerant, upright religious law. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk contrasts an easy religion with hardship; other sources call it the tolerant Ḥanīfī law. Ibn Kathīr adds a straight, just law without deviation or distress. Biqāʿī says what God establishes is easiest and best suited to its time. Ibn ʿĀshūr favors the omitted 'law' because delivering it defines the Messenger's office. Study Quran cites Q 2:185 and 94:5–6. [39 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود والضحاك); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 87:8/q06/p2 It is goodness or good deeds, including the work leading to Paradise. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as goodness and enablement to do good; Muqātil and others specify the work of Paradise. Rāzī says good works lead to ease; Ibn Kathīr includes good words. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this broadens the earlier promise beyond preserving revelation. [31 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود,مقاتل، ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(مقاتل وابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 87:8/q06/p3 It is ease in receiving, retaining, understanding, teaching and practicing revelation. — The contextual reading ties this to 87:6–7: God's knowledge of the Prophet's spoken recitation to Gabriel and private rehearsal answers his fear of forgetting. Zamakhsharī, Rāzī and others take memorization as the easiest path; Ṭabrisī includes practice and Ālūsī includes comprehension. [29 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود والضحاك); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 87:8/q06/p4 It is Paradise itself, the greatest or ultimate ease. — Ibn Masʿūd names Paradise and explains that God eases the deeds leading there. Al-Jubbāʾī calls Paradise the greatest ease and says God eases the Prophet's entry. Study Quran extends the eased entry to believers; Asad calls it ultimate ease. [11 notes: MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود); TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود والضحاك); DURR(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 87:8/q06/p5 It covers every part of religion: revelation, knowledge, teaching, practice, guidance and guiding others. — Bursawī and Ālūsī describe sustained success across these domains and the Prophet's and others' perfection; Ālūsī calls this the main or preferred broad sense. Elmali̇lı adds outward and inward revelation, tawḥīd, sincerity, knowledge and rightly ordered effort. Qāshānī describes intellectual and practical perfection, wisdom and power. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verse promises both an easy charge and capacity to fulfill it. [9 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 87:8/q06/p6 It is good in this world and the next, including victory, high standing and elevation in Paradise. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya lists victory, success, the message's rising rank and elevation in Paradise; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report the same view. Ālūsī says adding religious affairs makes it broad. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes worldly and afterlife good among the enabled outcomes. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:8/q06/p7 It is aid for the difficult work of preaching and patience. — Abū Muslim prefers divine support that steadies the Prophet; he connects this with the preceding recitation promise. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God prepares him to choose the easiest preaching path and bear its difficulties; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verse reassures the initially anxious Messenger. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; TABRISI(أبو مسلم)+]
- 87:8/q06/p8 It is the present condition of ease, including ease of mind and peace of spirit. — Naḥḥās glosses the phrase as a state or condition; Asad's note specifies inward tranquillity. [2 notes: NAHHAS; ASAD-NOTES]
- 87:8/q06/p9 It is an easier replacement for a verse. — Muqātil explains the easing through replacement of one verse by an easier one. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 87:8/q06/p10 It is relief from present hardship that will emerge with time. — Iṣlāḥī says present hardships train and discipline the Prophet for later favor under a law of gradual development, comparing 87:3–5. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:8/q07 How does this clause connect syntactically with the preceding verses?  [وَنُيَسِّرُكَ · grammar]
- 87:8/q07/p1 It coordinates with 'We shall make you recite' in 87:6; the knowledge statement in 87:7 is parenthetical. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this analysis. Samīn says it remains within the promise; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its result broadens preservation into enabling good. Bayḍāwī says the direct-object wording suits the coordination. [19 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]

## 87:8/q08 How does the promise lead into the Prophet's mission and the next command?  [* · links]
- 87:8/q08/p1 Divine preparation for revelation and religion enables the Prophet to remind and guide others. — Ālūsī says this broad reading makes the fāʾ of 87:9 especially apt; Ibn ʿĀshūr says God equips the anxious Messenger to bear revelation and the hard work of guiding the community. Baghawī's contextual reading moves from recitation to memory and teaching. Biqāʿī includes reflection and every gentle course. [5 notes: BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR]

## 87:8/q09 What does this promise teach about ease in the Prophet, religion and community?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes develop distinct consequences for personal conduct, religious duties and the community's place in creation.
- 87:8/q09/p1 Ease and gentleness shape the Prophet's character and his practice of the message. — Quṭb says the divine gift shapes speech, steps, work, thought and dealings, turning the great trust into a loved, joyful practice; his reports describe the Prophet's easy choices, habits, instruction, naming and reconciliation. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites his preference for the easier permissible choice and instruction to facilitate. Qurʾān 21:107 calls him mercy and 7:157 says he lifts burdens. [7 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q09/p2 The message is easy and duties are governed by human capacity and necessity. — Quṭb cites Q 54:17 on memorization, 22:78 and 5:6 against hardship, 2:286 on capacity, and 6:119 on necessity; he says creed is simple and obligations coherent. Reports urge doing what one can, avoiding prohibitions, and making religion easy without overburdening oneself. [6 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q09/p3 The easy Prophet and message form a balanced, merciful community in harmony with an ordered, easy cosmos. — Quṭb says creation emerges and proceeds toward its end with ease; celestial bodies, living beings and events move in measured harmony. The one God creates the concord of universe, messenger, message and community; the sūrah places praise throughout the cosmos and presents ease as the community's character. [7 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:8/q10 Which reports illustrate the Prophet's ease and teaching about it?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different sayings, daily habits and episodes offered as illustrations of the promise.
- 87:8/q10/p1 He chose the easier of two choices and was gentle and cheerful at home. — Quṭb transmits ʿĀʾisha's description; Ibn ʿĀshūr also cites the choice of the easier option. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(الرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p2 His clothing, food and sleeping arrangements followed what was available without fuss. — Ibn al-Qayyim, as quoted by Quṭb, reports varied clothing and turban styles; pleasant available food without criticizing what he declined, including a lizard eaten in his presence; and sleep on a bed, mat, ground, couch or black cloth. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(ابن قيم الجوزية)]
- 87:8/q10/p3 He taught that religion is easy and that excessive rigor defeats its practitioner. — Quṭb cites al-Bukhārī. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p4 He warned against self-imposed strictness, citing earlier people who were burdened by it. — Quṭb cites Abū Dāwūd. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p5 He likened overburdening oneself to a traveler who neither crosses the land nor saves the mount. — Quṭb cites al-Bukhārī. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p6 He instructed people to make matters easy rather than difficult. — Quṭb cites both Ṣaḥīḥ collections; Ibn ʿĀshūr quotes his instruction that the companions were sent to facilitate. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p7 He prayed for mercy on one who is gracious in selling, buying and collecting payment. — Quṭb cites al-Bukhārī. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p8 He described a believer as gentle and easygoing. — Quṭb cites al-Bayhaqī. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p9 He said a believer forms relationships and is welcomed by others. — Quṭb cites al-Dāraquṭnī. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p10 He said God most hates the contentious, quarrelsome person. — Quṭb cites both Ṣaḥīḥ collections. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p11 He changed harsh names to gentler ones. — Saʿīd b. al-Musayyib reports from his father that the Prophet changed Ḥazn to Sahl; the father refused and Saʿīd said hardness remained among them (al-Bukhārī). Ibn ʿUmar reports changing ʿĀṣiya to Jamīla (Muslim). [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(ابن عمر,سعيد بن المسيب)]
- 87:8/q10/p12 He called a cheerful greeting of one's brother an act of kindness. — Quṭb cites al-Tirmidhī. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p13 He calmed his companions and reconciled an insulting Bedouin through a further gift. — Quṭb recounts a private gift followed by public reconciliation; the Prophet compared his method to calmly retrieving a frightened runaway camel and warned that killing the man would have sent him to Hell. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p14 He instructed people to perform commands as able and avoid prohibitions, with necessity as an exception. — Quṭb gives no collection or grading and cites Q 6:119. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:8/q10/p15 He said each person is facilitated for what they were created for. — Rāzī, Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr invoke this saying; Ibn ʿĀshūr pairs it with the two outcomes in Q 92:7 and 92:10. [6 notes: RAZI(محمد); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:8/q11 How does this promise fit the sūrah and related passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the verse to different contexts: the immediate recitation promise, other consolatory passages, the sūrah's wider themes and the intervening knowledge clause.
- 87:8/q11/p1 It consoles the Prophet as the sūrah moves from revelation's preservation to aid for his mission. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the promise to 87:6 and says the Creator who proportioned and guided also equipped the Prophet for revelation and easy law; a later reference says the promise of easing difficulties continues 87:8. Iṣlāḥī says the preceding sūrah warns stubborn deniers, whereas this sūrah addresses the Prophet and begins a series easing preaching fears; verses 6–8 promise maturing, preserved revelation with a divine exception and relief from known hardships. Quṭb describes the sūrah as good news of preservation and ease. [9 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:8/q11/p2 The ease here recalls the facilitated outcomes of Q 92 and the consoling ease after hardship in Q 94. — Corpus Coranicum compares Q 92:7 and 92:10, where the blessed are eased toward ease and the damned toward hardship, and Q 94:1, 94:5–6, where consolation likewise addresses the Prophet. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:8/q11/p3 The sūrah's easy message belongs with its themes of divine oneness, revelation and final recompense. — Quṭb calls al-Aʿlā Meccan with nineteen verses and reports that the Prophet loved it and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes on one day; he cites 87:18–19 on earlier scriptures. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:8/q11/p4 God's public and hidden knowledge reinforces the preservation promise near this verse. — Al-Durr transmits Qatāda's report that the Prophet forgot nothing except what God willed and that divine knowledge includes inner whispering; Saʿīd b. Jubayr says it includes what a person conceals inwardly. [2 notes: DURR(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة)]

## 87:8/q12 What reading of al-yusrā is recorded?  [الْيُسْرَىٰ · readings]
- 87:8/q12/p1 A ten-reading variant vocalizes it yusurā with a fuller vowel texture. — Qiraat-Er says the root and the phrase's function stay the same. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 87:8/q13 How is an agent made ready to perform an act?  [نُيَسِّرُكَ · theology]
- 87:8/q13/p1 An act occurs when divine facilitation tips a possible action toward performance. — Rāzī says possibility leaves doing and refraining equal until the active side is preferred; the preponderance is facilitation. He cites the Prophetic saying that each person is facilitated for what they were created for and Q 92:7. Jishumī says facilitation can come through ability or preparation and notes yasar may be named optimistically or because right-hand work is easy. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI(محمد); RAZI-FULL]

## 87:8/q14 Why is the promised way already called easy?  [الْيُسْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:8/q14/p1 Its ease is already established; God protects the Prophet from obstacles to receiving and following it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the description points to an intrinsically easy charge and to the Prophet's divinely given capacity to fulfill it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
