# 54:17: 14 questions

## 54:17/q01 How is the word ٱلْقُرْءَانَ read?  [ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · readings]
- 54:17/q01/p1 The hamza may be eased or dropped, yielding qurāna. — QIRAAT-ER attributes this hmz form to tashīl and the Hijazi tendency to lighten or elide hamza. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]

## 54:17/q02 What does God's making the Qur'an easy involve?  [يَسَّرْنَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ease is located chiefly in recitation, memorization, comprehensible admonition, or preparation for the Qur'an's teaching purpose; some sources join these benefits. Al-Alusi favors admonition from context, while al-Islahi rejects restricting it to memorization or elementary Arabic comprehension.
- 54:17/q02/p1 God enabled human tongues to recite and speak the Qur'an, which otherwise they could not bear to utter. — Mujahid glosses ease as recitation in MUJAHID, IBNKATHIR, DURR and ALUSI; al-Suddi specifies tongues. Muqatil, al-Farra, Ibn Qutayba, Tustari and reports from Ibn Abbas, Anas and Hasan say creatures could not speak God's speech without divine easing; Muqatil adds recitation in every circumstance, Ibn Qutayba also listening. Al-Mawardi calls accessibility to speakers of every language miraculous. Ibn Kathir includes revelation in seven ahruf. Al-Alusi says the Anas report, even if authentic, need not interpret this verse and favors admonition in context. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,مجاهد); BURSEVI(الحسن عن النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 54:17/q02/p2 God made the Qur'an easy to memorize completely and retain by heart. — Al-Farra and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, cited by many sources, contrast other revealed books: none is recited entirely from memory as the Qur'an is. Al-Zajjaj notes Torah and Gospel readers generally look at a written copy. Al-Qurtubi reports that Israelites, except Moses, Aaron, Joshua and Ezra, read Torah from a copy and marveled when Ezra rewrote it after a fire. Al-Qurtubi says God helps seekers and makes preservation nearly innate. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan cite its arrangement, sound or pleasant wording, lack of filler, and noble true meanings that engage hearts; al-Baydawi cites concision and pleasing wording, al-Alusi adds eloquence and lack of obscurity. Al-Islahi accepts memorization as a correct inference but rejects limiting taysir to it. [25 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ(قيل); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(people generally)]
- 54:17/q02/p3 God made the Qur'an clear and understandable so people can reflect, learn, take warning and follow its guidance. — Ibn Zayd glosses yassarna as 'made clear'; al-Tabari and al-Nahhas stress explanation and detail. Muqatil in al-Mawardi says its knowledge and derivation of meaning are eased. Al-Wahidi cites intelligible Arabic and 26:195; al-Tabrisi cites clear expression, proofs, sound rulings and true meanings, and explains that recalling proofs opens knowledge. Al-Zamakhshari in BURHAN describes interwoven admonition, promise and threat. Al-Biqai says Arabic familiar to its hearers, examples, eloquence, and stories make warning accessible; unsurpassed eloquence together with clarity is miraculous, and difficult matters reward elevated scholars (58:11). Ibn Kathir cites 38:29 and 19:97. Ibn Ashur sees guidance for varying capacities and a rebuke to those who turn away, linked to the preservation promise in 15:9. Al-Islahi specifies Quraysh Arabic, gradual revelation (17:106), early short teachings then elaboration (11:1), varied presentations (7:58), and complementary sura groups and pairs (39:23; 15:87). He rejects the claim that rudimentary Arabic automatically yields full understanding. Al-Tabatabai says simple and deep readers each grasp according to capacity. [40 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(ابن زيد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشرى); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); ALUSI-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 54:17/q02/p4 God fitted or prepared the Qur'an for the work of remembrance, as one equips a mount or a person for a task. — Al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi cite preparing or bridling a camel or horse; al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin cite poetry using mayssiran for a bridled horse. Al-Islahi says taysir means fitting and equipping for a purpose; his cited poem by Mudarris ibn Rub'i describes equipping a leader for a campaign. He broadens this to the Qur'an's full means of educating Quraysh. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Muḍarris ibn Rub‘ī)]
- 54:17/q02/p5 The ease includes recitation on the tongue and memorization, understanding and knowledge in different hearts. — Al-Qushayri says different groups share these gifts and are all Qur'an people and God's elect; al-Biqai repeats him. The Study Quran calls this mercy to tongue, mind and heart. Ibn Kathir joins accessible wording and meaning; al-Biqai also includes honor for those who attend to it. Al-Islahi's early concise verses aided memorization, understanding, practice and transmission. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:17/q02/p6 Yassarnā means simply 'We made easy' or 'facilitated'. — Al-Zajjaj supplies the direct gloss; Mujahid in al-Tabari and al-Nahhas says 'made manageable/easy'; al-Damghani gives yusr and taysir as facilitation, also used for an easy outcome in provision. Al-Elmalili says it may mean readily granted for reflection. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); NAHHAS(مجاهد); DAMGHANI; ELMALILI]

## 54:17/q03 What does لِلذِّكْرِ mean here?  [لِلذِّكْرِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether dhikr chiefly names admonitory reflection, memorization and recitation, or the broader education and reawakening that the Qur'an supplies.
- 54:17/q03/p1 It means taking heed, reflecting on warning and guidance, and keeping the lesson in mind. — Muqatil in al-Wahidi specifies reflection on Qur'anic content; Ibn Abbas in his Ata transmission says ease is for anyone willing to heed. Abu Hayyan points to admonition, promise and threat. Ibn Ashur distinguishes useful mental recollection from spoken mention; Asad cites Raghib's definition of a thing present in the mind and combines understanding with remembering, also at 54:22, 32 and 40. Al-Islahi expands the scope to informing, counseling, instructing, warning and communicating proof. Al-Tabrisi says remembrance of evidence is required for knowledge. Al-Biqai says reminders recall what was known and forgotten, including reflection on God's acts in the visible and unseen worlds and His names and attributes; humans contain traces of those acts. Al-Qashani connects admonition to the one path of truth and shared prophetic foundations. [23 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس في رواية عطاء,مقاتل); JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشرى); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî); STUDYQURAN(Aj); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 54:17/q03/p2 It means memorizing and reciting the Qur'an so its teachings stay present. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr connects this with the unique complete memorization of the Qur'an among scriptures. Ibn Atiyya says memorization keeps admonitions, knowledge and guidance in mind; Abu Hayyan similarly joins retention to later recollection. Al-Wahidi distinguishes the recitation interpretation from reflection. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(people generally); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 54:17/q03/p3 It means a broad work of educating and awakening knowledge already latent in people. — Al-Islahi says it includes educating, instructing, informing, warning, counseling and conclusively establishing truth; the Qur'an revives neglected innate treasures rather than importing alien knowledge. Al-Wasiti says the remembering heart is taught by the Qur'an's spirit. Al-Biqai describes recollection of former knowledge that one cannot independently summon. [4 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 54:17/q04 How does the lām in لِلذِّكْرِ work?  [لِلذِّكْرِ · grammar]
- 54:17/q04/p1 It marks remembrance as the beneficiary of God's making easy, with a relation like transitivity. — Ibn Ashur attaches the lām to yassarnā as a non-fixed adverbial, not an ordinary purpose object. He prefers Ibn Malik's label ma'na al-ta'diya over Ibn Hisham's shibh al-tamlik: its noun resembles the first object of 'clothe' or 'give'. He compares 42:11, 19:5, 36:72, 87:8 and 92:7,10; here remembrance is personified as a beneficiary of guidance, and yassarnā furnishes the metaphor's image. He says the lām's basic causal meaning developed into several conventional uses. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن مالك)+]

## 54:17/q05 What is the form and derivation of مُّدَّكِرٍ?  [مُّدَّكِرٍ · grammar]
- 54:17/q05/p1 It is an iftiʿāl form from dhikr; consonants change and assimilate to produce doubled dāl. — Al-Jishumi gives an original mudhtakir with tāʾ changing to dāl before assimilation; al-Wahidi reports Abu Ishaq's explanation that voiced dāl better suits dhāl than voiceless tāʾ and compares muzdajir. Ibn Atiyya describes tāʾ becoming dhāl then assimilation; Bursevi describes dhāl assimilating into tāʾ and becoming doubled dāl. Elmalili gives an underlying muztakir and Ibn Ashur a form mudhtadhkir, explaining the close articulation points. Al-Wahidi notes frequent dāl forms iddakar and muddakir, comparing taqwā, taqiya and taqāt. Abu Ali cites Sibawayh for a dhāl form in al-dhikr, supported by Ibn Muqbil's verse. [8 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي الفارسي عن سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 54:17/q06 Which readings are reported for مُّدَّكِرٍ?  [مُّدَّكِرٍ · readings]
turns on: Whether the consonant is the standard doubled dāl or the reported dhāl form; Abu Hatim objects to the latter's assimilation rule.
- 54:17/q06/p1 The dāl form muddakir is the transmitted prophetic and common reading. — Ibn Atiyya reports Abu Hatim's sound chain from the Prophet. Bursevi reports Ibn Mas'ud being corrected from a dhāl form to dāl; al-Durr gives Ibn Mas'ud's dhāl recitation beside the Prophet's dāl recitation. Ibn Ashur says this verse's reading is specifically transmitted from the Prophet and points to 12:45. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم)+]
- 54:17/q06/p2 A dhāl form mudhakkir or muthakkir is reported from Ibn Mas'ud and Qatada. — Al-Durr says Ibn Mas'ud recited with dhāl while the Prophet recited with dāl; Bursevi says the Prophet corrected Ibn Mas'ud. Abu Hatim in Ibn Atiyya calls Qatada's dhāl assimilation poor because its proposed rule would change other words too. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حاتم)-; BURSEVI(ابن مسعود)-]

## 54:17/q07 Whom does فَهَلْ مِن مُّدَّكِرٍ call for, and to do what?  [فَهَلْ مِن مُّدَّكِرٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the invitation centers on taking warning, seeking knowledge or good, reciting and retaining scripture, leaving sin, or recovering inward remembrance.
- 54:17/q07/p1 Someone who remembers, reflects and takes admonition from the Qur'an and its warning. — Ibn Qutayba and many commentators treat it as a summons to take heed; Muqatil in al-Wahidi specifies recognizing the warning's truth and fearing it. Al-Wahidi distinguishes the reflection reading from the recitation reading. Ibn Zayd in Abu Hayyan glosses muddakir as one taking admonition; Ibn Abbas in al-Durr says 'anyone who will remember'. Al-Tabrisi urges considering the Qur'an; al-Biqai says the question asks people to recall what God made accessible. Al-Qashani invokes the single way of truth and shared foundations of prophetic laws. KURANYOLU includes taking a lesson or instruction; Study Quran links it to 54:15. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 54:17/q07/p2 A seeker of knowledge who will receive divine help to learn. — Matar al-Warraq's explanation appears in al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, al-Jishumi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqai and al-Durr; Ibn Kathir notes transmission by Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Jarir and al-Bukhari's definite suspended attribution. Qatada joins Matar in al-Jishumi and is also named by Ibn Kathir. Ibn Atiyya attributes a similar explanation to Mutarrif. Al-Nahhas says this is close to the first interpretation, whose apparent sense he favors. Al-Tabrisi says an eased path to knowledge gives strong reason to pursue learning. [10 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); THALABI(مطر الوراق); WAHIDI-BASIT(مطر الوراق); JISHUMI(قتادة ومطر الوراق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مطرف); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق وابن شوذب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مطر الوراق); BIQAI-FULL(مطر الوراق); DURR-FULL(مطر الوراق)]
- 54:17/q07/p3 A seeker of good who will be helped toward it. — Al-Nahhas reports 'good or knowledge' and favors the first apparent interpretation while calling this close. Abu Bakr al-Warraq and Ibn Shawdhab in al-Qurtubi join good and knowledge; Abu Hayyan and al-Durr attribute the good-seeker gloss to Qatada, and KURANYOLU reports it from Tabari. [5 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق وابن شوذب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 54:17/q07/p4 Someone who will recite, memorize, study and learn the Qur'an with God's help. — Al-Wahidi spells out recitation, study, learning and understanding on the recitation interpretation. Ibn Atiyya says memorization keeps guidance and knowledge present; al-Qurtubi says God helps those seeking to memorize but elsewhere glosses the question simply as reciting. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi give the memorization and divine-help reading; Ibn al-Jawzi urges recitation. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 54:17/q07/p5 Someone who will be deterred from sin and abandon it. — Muhammad ibn Ka'b gives this gloss in Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and al-Durr. Bursevi says a mindful person learns from the sign, fears God and leaves sin. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محمد بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب); BURSEVI]
- 54:17/q07/p6 Someone who recalls the pretemporal covenant with God. — Al-Qushayri reports a Sufi saying that God communicated through the Qur'an with some spirits before embodiment. Study Quran says either remembrance or admonition can evoke the pact of 7:172. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 54:17/q07/p7 Someone whose heart has been taught by the Qur'an's spirit and recalls what God already brought to them. — Al-Wasiti, as reported by al-Sulami, ties the capacity to remember to inward teaching of the heart. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 54:17/q08 What is the force of the closing question?  [فَهَلْ مِن مُّدَّكِرٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether hal mainly invites a willing hearer or rebukes an audience that has failed to take heed.
- 54:17/q08/p1 It is an urgent call inviting listeners to remember and take heed. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi and al-Tabrisi treat the question as an exhortation. Ibn Atiyya sees hope that a recipient accepts and follows guidance. Al-Wahidi says it urges recitation on one reading and reflection and fear on another. Al-Qurtubi says hal summons implanted understanding as evidence against listeners, with lām calling forth and hāʾ eliciting. Al-Islahi says it challenges stubborn hearers through sound proofs. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:17/q08/p2 It rebukes the audience and emphatically denies that anyone takes heed. — Bursevi and al-Alusi say no one could answer yes; al-Biqai says accessibility removes the excuse that warnings are too hard to grasp. Ibn Ashur describes the polytheists' self-deprivation through turning away. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 54:17/q09 What does the oath-marked wording contribute?  [وَلَقَدْ يَسَّرْنَا ٱلْقُرْءَانَ لِلذِّكْرِ · rhetoric]
- 54:17/q09/p1 The closing statement is marked as an oath that confirms the warning. — Bursevi identifies the closing statement as oath-marked. Al-Alusi says the oath after each of four stories confirms the preceding warning that reports came to the audience (54:4); each story deserved reflection but failed to elicit it. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 54:17/q10 Why does this refrain recur through the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the recurrence chiefly assigns a fresh admonition to each narrative, secures audience attention, or also carries symbolic numerical patterns.
- 54:17/q10/p1 Each preceding story supplies a fresh lesson, so the refrain renews admonition and challenges listeners to reflect on that case. — Al-Jishumi distinguishes the first call about the punished and saved from the second about Qur'anic admonitions. Al-Qurtubi says each account of peoples and messengers is itself a reminder; al-Zamakhshari in ITQAN and KURANYOLU says each report should awaken heedless listeners. The author of Arus al-Afrah says distinct preceding events make repetition meaningful. Bursevi names the four endings (54:17,22,32,40), and al-Alusi connects their warnings with 54:4. Elmalili says each story deters; Ibn Ashur distinguishes learning from ruined traces at 54:15 from hearing and following Qur'anic exhortation here. Al-Islahi says the refrain follows each prophetic account; Asad notes its sense repeats at 54:22,32,40. Al-Tabatabai says it states the warning's aim. [12 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; ITQAN(الزمخشري,صاحب عروس الأفراح); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî)]
- 54:17/q10/p2 Repetition draws attention, establishes comprehension and removes excuses. — Al-Qurtubi says the recurrence draws attention and clarifies the message. Al-Biqai says readily understood warnings remove the excuse of difficulty, and repeated prophets' accounts across suras together yield a straight path and clear light. Al-Islahi says it interrupts argument to challenge stubborn hearers. Bursevi says each story should have produced restraint, though none did. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]
- 54:17/q10/p3 The pattern of recurrence has further numeric and letter symbolism. — Al-Biqai counts the reminder four times and says the final line occurs separately twice, at the Noah opening and after Pharaoh. He compares the thirty-one repetitions of 55:13, then speculates about five consecutive suras and endings, reversed and assimilated letters, five senses as beginnings of knowledge, and two boundary letters assimilated into one verse. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 54:17/q11 How does this verse relate to Noah's story and the surrounding warnings?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the relevant link is the story's moral warning, the contrast of wrath and mercy, or the story's compact eloquence.
- 54:17/q11/p1 Noah's story and the earlier nations' punishments warn the audience to learn before the threatened fate arrives. — Al-Jishumi says these verses deter and frighten people lest earlier peoples' fate recur. Al-Qushayri says Noah's long and intense affliction ended with preservation of him and his children, from whom humanity descends; the story offers the faithful hope that enemies will fall and God's allies prevail, as with Moses and Pharaoh. Al-Islahi links it to 54:4: Quraysh should learn from nations that rejected messengers. He says surrounding creation, human nature and past peoples attest to punishment, while deniers demand to see it despite the Qur'an's complete warning. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:17/q11/p2 The positive ease refrain sets divine mercy beside the previous ominous refrain about wrath. — Study Quran contrasts the preceding 54:16 warning of wrath for rejecting God and signs with mercy through accessible revelation and truth. Al-Islahi calls the Qur'an a gracious advance warning for Quraysh. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:17/q11/p3 Noah's account itself demonstrates concise expression carrying the fullest meaning. — Al-Qushayri says the account has exceptional eloquence, few words and comprehensive meaning. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 54:17/q12 What obligations or effects follow from the Qur'an's ease?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give different practical consequences of ease: reflection and action, teaching, communal recitation, and the heart's response.
- 54:17/q12/p1 People must reflect on the accessible guidance, learn it and act on its clear teachings. — Al-Jishumi says the question establishes reflection as obligatory and human thought as an act. Al-Tabrisi says the easy path to knowledge gives strong reason to seek learning and recall its evidence. Al-Biqai says believers must accept revelation and act on clear rulings, while deep understanding of difficult matters is a gift to those God raises in knowledge (58:11). Ibn Ashur says accessibility encourages study and rebukes those who turn away. Al-Islahi argues that the Qur'an's teaching purpose requires thoughtful engagement, not treating ease as mere simple recitation or automatic grasp from rudimentary Arabic. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 54:17/q12/p2 Qur'an scholars have a duty to explain revelation to others. — Ibn Ashur invokes the explicit instruction of 16:44 and the covenant of 3:187, arguing this community is even more entitled to the duty. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 54:17/q12/p3 Recitation and study together bring tranquility, mercy, angelic presence and God's remembrance. — Ibn Ashur reports a hadith about people gathered in a mosque to recite and study God's Book. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الحديث)]
- 54:17/q12/p4 A receptive heart responds to the Qur'an with awe, tears and recognition of divine speech. — Al-Samarrai describes inimitability beyond description and says a closed heart does not yet grasp this experience. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 54:17/q13 What reports about valuing or describing the Qur'an are connected with this verse?  [* · reports]
- 54:17/q13/p1 Call a sura 'made easy' rather than 'light'. — Ibn Sirin corrected someone who called a sura light and grounded his wording in this verse; al-Durr and al-Alusi preserve the report. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(محمد بن سيرين); ALUSI-FULL(ابن سيرين)]
- 54:17/q13/p2 Those who learn the Qur'an and teach it are the best among people. — Study Quran reports this hadith. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 54:17/q13/p3 God will not burn in Hell one who has memorized the Qur'an. — Study Quran reports this hadith. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 54:17/q14 What inward reading is drawn from Noah's passage beside this verse?  [* · inward]
- 54:17/q14/p1 Noah represents the heart saved through spiritual qualities and divine care from the lower self, while the Flood figures gifts and knowledge. — Bursevi makes the commanding soul and human traits the heart's opponents; luminous qualities defeat animal darkness and carry it in a protective ship. Heavenly water represents compassion, mercy and generosity from the spirit world, and earthly springs knowledge and realities. Increased unveiling makes spirits long for annihilation, but providence carries them in protective ships. Prophets and saints are such ships; following them escapes error and misery and enters divine protection. [4 notes: BURSEVI]
