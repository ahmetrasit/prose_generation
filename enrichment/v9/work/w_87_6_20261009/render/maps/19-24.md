# 19:24: 24 questions

## 19:24/q01 Who called Mary from beneath her?  [فَنَادَىٰهَا مِن تَحْتِهَآ · referent]
turns on: Whether the newborn's immediate speech and Mary's later gesture identify the caller, or Gabriel called from a lower place; the readings alone do not settle it for all commentators.
- 19:24/q01/p1 The caller was Jesus, speaking as a newborn beneath Mary. — Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, Wahb, Saʿīd, Ubayy and others transmit this. Al-Ṭabarī cites the nearest antecedent (19:22) and Mary's later gesture (19:29); al-Rāzī adds that the infant was already below her, while an angel's presence at childbirth is implausible. Al-Ṭabrisī and Abū ʿAlī say his speech directly removes grief, loneliness and accusation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he spoke before she lifted him. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾi cites adjacent pronouns. ## 19:24/q02 How is مِن تَحْتِهَآ read and construed?  [مِن تَحْتِهَآ · readings]
turns on: The vowel and case of min/man and taḥtihā, and how strongly either reading fixes the identity of the caller.
- 19:24/q02/p1 With kasra, min is a preposition and taḥtihā is genitive: 'from beneath her.' — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Nashr and Ibn ʿĀshūr enumerate the readers, with some variations in lists. The subject of 'called' is implicit and can be Jesus or Gabriel, though Ibn ʿUyayna and ʿĀṣim associate this reading with Gabriel. Ibn Khalawayh treats the lower place as a locative container; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says min marks origin. - 19:24/q02/p2 With fatḥa, man is a relative pronoun and taḥtahā accusative: 'the one beneath her called.' — Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and Abū Bakr are prominent in reader lists, with later lists adding others. Many identify the person as Jesus; al-Farrāʾ and al-Baghawī allow Gabriel. Ibn Khalawayh calls the place an adverbial object; al-Naḥḥās says man is a noun and taḥtahā an adverb. ## 19:24/q03 What does 'beneath her' locate or refer to?  [تَحْتِهَآ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun points to Mary or the palm, and whether 'beneath' describes physical elevation, the womb, or a relative direction.
- 19:24/q03/p1 It locates the speaker below Mary, on lower ground, a slope or valley, or below her skirt if the speaker is Jesus. — Muqātil puts Mary on a rise; al-Kalbī and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās place Gabriel below a mound or at a mountain foot. Others place him beneath the palm. Jesus may be at or emerging from the womb. Ibn ʿAbbās defines above and below by the direction of one's gaze. Al-Ālūsī says Gabriel's lower stance may show reverence. [27 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، عمرو بن ميمون، الضحاك); THALABI(الحسن وأبو جعفر وشيبة ونافع وابن وثاب والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,بعض المتكلمين); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي,عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 19:24/q03/p2 The feminine pronoun refers to the palm tree, so the voice comes from beneath the palm. — Qatāda offers this antecedent; al-Samīn prefers Mary because it keeps the verse's two pronouns aligned. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī also allow the palm as antecedent. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، عمرو بن ميمون، الضحاك); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(عكرمة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ASAD-NOTES(Qatadah); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z)]
- 19:24/q03/p3 It can mean from Mary's womb or belly. — Al-Māwardī attributes a womb gloss to some theologians and calls it Coptic; al-Itqān attributes a belly gloss in Nabataean to Abū al-Qāsim. Jesus' speech upon emergence is the narrative implication. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتكلمين); ITQAN(أبو القاسم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; THALABI]
- 19:24/q03/p4 Below may mean nearby, in front of Mary, or lower by a relative reference point. — Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī compare the later stream beneath Mary; al-Rāzī invokes the battle verse 33:10 for relative position. Al-Kashshāf compares rivers flowing below in 2:25. The reading need not place Gabriel directly under Mary. [6 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Ṭs)]
- 19:24/q03/p5 It means below the ground. — Yaḥyā reports this distinct interpretation without a named caller. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 19:24/q04 Is there another reported wording for 'he called her'?  [فَنَادَىٰهَا · readings]
turns on: The reported replacement of the verb or explicit naming of its subject.
- 19:24/q04/p1 Zirr and ʿAlqama are reported to read 'he addressed her' (fa-khāṭabahā). — Abū Ḥayyān treats it as an explanation rather than a canonical reading because it departs from the agreed consonantal text. Al-Durr also gives a Zirr report worded 'he called her.' [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(زر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علقمة); RAZI-FULL(زر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زر وعلقمة); DURR-FULL(زر بن حبيش,علقمة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس؛ نوف)]
- 19:24/q04/p2 Ibn ʿAbbās's reported wording explicitly says an angel called Mary. — It is cited to support Gabriel as caller. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس)]

## 19:24/q05 What did the call accomplish?  [فَنَادَىٰهَا · interpretation]
turns on: The interpretation depends on whether the voice was Jesus' or Gabriel's.
- 19:24/q05/p1 Jesus' speech immediately comforted Mary and served as a sign that he could defend her. — Al-Zajjāj says it manifested his sign; Abū ʿAlī and al-Rāzī say it removed loneliness, grief and fear of accusation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it cleared suspicion. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it occurred before she lifted him; al-Ṭabarī connects it to her later pointing to him. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الحسن بن علي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:24/q05/p2 Gabriel's call reassured Mary by confirming God's purpose and prior good news. — Al-Rāzī says the angel reminded her of earlier good news; al-Qurṭubī says his call showed that this supernatural birth had a great divine purpose. Al-Qushayrī adds easing her loneliness. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:24/q06 Does the verse establish that Mary saw the caller?  [فَنَادَىٰهَا · interpretation]
- 19:24/q06/p1 The wording does not establish whether she saw him. — Al-Rāzī explicitly leaves this undecided. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 19:24/q07 Why was Mary told not to grieve?  [أَلَّا تَحْزَنِى · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of her distress the assurance chiefly answers.
- 19:24/q07/p1 She grieved over childbirth, likely accusation, and her isolation; the call and divine provision relieved those fears. — Muqātil recalls her wish for death. Al-Qurṭubī names giving birth; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses her chastity and respected family. Al-Nasafī names loneliness, food, drink and people's words. Al-Bursawī adds the dry place and defines grief as sorrow for lost benefit or present harm. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the stream made her condition an honor and reason for joy. [9 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 19:24/q07/p2 She grieved at a barren site without food, drink or washing water; the stream and dates supplied them. — Abū Ṣāliḥ's Ibn ʿAbbās report names all three lacks. Jishumī adds water for postpartum washing. The following instructions to eat and drink support the water interpretation. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(إبراهيم والضحاك وقتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:24/q07/p3 She grieved over bearing a child without a husband; the Jordan stream and dry palm's dates displayed God's power to create Jesus. — Muqātil is cited by Ibn al-Jawzī for this explanation. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 19:24/q08 How does أَلَّا تَحْزَنِى construe grammatically?  [أَلَّا تَحْزَنِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether an explains the words of the call or forms a subordinate verbal noun.
- 19:24/q08/p1 An is explanatory after the call, and lā is a prohibition: 'namely, do not grieve.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿĀshūr take an as introducing the call's content. Al-Samīn says the prohibitive verb is jussive with its nūn deleted. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 19:24/q08/p2 An may instead form a verbal noun after an omitted preposition, with lā negative and the verb subjunctive. — Al-Samīn gives accusative or genitive syntactic position for an, the latter from an omitted bi; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī also give the suppressed-preposition analysis. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:24/q09 Why does the message say 'your Lord'?  [رَبُّكِ · rhetoric]
- 19:24/q09/p1 The personal attribution honors and reassures Mary. — Al-Ālūsī says attaching 'Lord' to her dignifies her while God's provision explains the prohibition on grief. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:24/q10 What does سَرِيًّۭا mean here?  [سَرِيًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether Arabic usage, poetry and the eat-and-drink context establish a watercourse, or the word denotes a noble person; reports also disagree on whose nobility is meant.
- 19:24/q10/p1 A small river, stream, channel or spring of flowing water was supplied to Mary. — The majority and Arabic linguists give this sense. Labīd's verse and another poem use sarī for a watercourse; Ibn ʿAbbās cites a dog-drinking poem and another comparison of bounty to a stream. Some derive it from sarā, flowing. Reports call it Syriac, Nabataean, Coptic or Greek, while others call it Arabic; the meanings river, brook, channel and spring overlap. Prophetic glosses say stream, though Ibn Kathīr criticizes one Ibn ʿUmar chain and al-Ālūsī distinguishes sound suspended or companion reports from unsound raised ones. Eating and drinking, dates and water, support the sense. Some say a river of Jesus, a restored dry channel, or a channel from Jordan. ## 19:24/q11 What does 'beneath you' mean for the stream?  [تَحْتَكِ · referent]
turns on: Whether taḥt denotes location or control.
- 19:24/q11/p1 It is physically below, beside, at Mary's feet, or at a lower relative position. — Al-Kalbī locates water at her feet after a strike; Abū al-Ḥasan says lower in position need not mean directly aligned. Al-Rāzī replies to the objection that a stream must be beside her: 'below' can mark relative nearness even on level ground. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الحسن,الكلبي); JISHUMI(غير مسمى); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 19:24/q11/p2 The stream is under Mary's command and flows or stops as she directs. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī compare Pharaoh's rivers 'beneath me' in 43:51 as an expression of authority. Al-Ālūsī allows this but rejects it as departing from the apparent sense. [5 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض المفسرين); JISHUMI(غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 19:24/q12 How is 'God has made beneath you a sari' parsed?  [جَعَلَ تَحْتَكِ سَرِيًّۭا · grammar]
turns on: Which sense of jaʿala governs the number of objects.
- 19:24/q12/p1 If jaʿala means render, sarīyyan is the first object and taḥtaki the second. — Al-Samīn gives this two-object analysis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 19:24/q12/p2 If jaʿala means create, taḥtaki is syntactically extra rather than a second object. — Al-Samīn gives this alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:24/q13 How did the water come to Mary?  [قَدْ جَعَلَ سَرِيًّۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether 'has made' signals a new miracle, restoration of a dry channel, or notice of existing water; reports differ over whose strike caused it.
- 19:24/q13/p1 God caused water to flow in an existing dry riverbed, sometimes identified as a channel from the Jordan. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports a dried channel restored; al-Baghawī couples the renewed flow with the dry palm leafing, flowering and ripening. Other reports name a channel from Jordan; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the water and out-of-season dates as extraordinary. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,جمهور); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:24/q13/p2 Gabriel struck the ground with his foot, and a fresh spring or stream emerged. — Several reports attribute the strike to him, including one from Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī prefer a newly produced miraculous stream on the wording 'has made'. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī also give variants in which Jesus strikes. [11 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); JISHUMI(غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:24/q13/p3 Jesus struck the ground with his foot and a spring gushed out. — Al-Ṭabrisī cites the report; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the report from al-Bāqir favors Jesus. Accounts vary between foot and heel. [8 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); JISHUMI(غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(أبو جعفر); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 19:24/q13/p4 God directly produced the spring, without a reported human or angelic strike. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this as another account; prophetic reports speak of God bringing a river forth for Mary to drink, though Ibn Kathīr deems an Ibn ʿUmar chain very strange and weak. [4 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(النبي)]
- 19:24/q13/p5 The water was already flowing there, and God directed Mary to it. — Al-Rāzī reports this alternative; al-Ālūsī gives a version in which it already existed and God pointed it out. Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī prefer new miraculous water. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:24/q14 What signs or lesson does Mary's provision convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:24/q14/p1 The speaking infant, water and fruit are signs of God's power and Mary's innocence, consoling and honoring her. — Al-Kashshāf says the stream and dates show accusers that virgin birth belongs to divine action. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says life from the dry palm demonstrates life from what is dead. Al-Biqāʿī notes water in a waterless land; Ibn ʿĀshūr says food and water visibly honor and clear her, including before Joseph and witnesses. Islahi says her patient appeal to God was followed by mercy. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 19:24/q15 What is reported about Mary and Joseph before the birth?  [* · reports]
- 19:24/q15/p1 Mary fled with Joseph the carpenter after an accusation; Gabriel told him Jesus was from the Holy Spirit. — Al-Kalbī relates this account; Ibn ʿAṭiyya judges it weak. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)-]

## 19:24/q16 How long did Mary carry Jesus before delivery?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give different gestation intervals; al-Qurṭubī favors Ibn ʿAbbās's immediate-birth report.
- 19:24/q16/p1 Birth followed immediately. — Al-Qurṭubī says Ibn ʿAbbās's report is sounder and more apparent than the month-count reports. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 19:24/q16/p2 She carried him eight months. — ʿIkrima links this to the claim that an eight-month infant cannot survive, making Jesus' survival a special sign. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 19:24/q16/p3 She carried him nine months. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this without further evidence. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 19:24/q16/p4 She carried him six months. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this without further evidence. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:24/q17 What inward meaning is given to the call and stream?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the image is read as spiritual knowledge granted to Mary or a forecast of Jesus' status.
- 19:24/q17/p1 Gabriel calls from below Mary's heart station, while the stream is knowledge of nature and divine action granted to her. — Al-Qāshānī places sorrow in nature and takes the miraculous fetus formed from her semen alone as evidence of this knowledge and her selection. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 19:24/q17/p2 The promised noble one foreshadows Jesus' lordship and leadership from the womb. — Al-Bursawī transmits this from 'people of reality,' connecting it to his proclamation of prophethood in the cradle (19:30). [1 notes: BURSEVI(أرباب الحقيقة)]

## 19:24/q18 How does Sūrat Maryam relate to the preceding sūra?  [* · links]
- 19:24/q18/p1 Both recount astonishing divine stories. — Abū Ḥayyān pairs the Cave, Moses and Khiḍr, and Dhū al-Qarnayn in Sūrat al-Kahf with John born to aged, barren parents and Jesus born without a father; he cites 18:9, 18:60 and 18:83. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:24/q19 Where was Sūrat Maryam revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 19:24/q19/p1 It is Meccan, with a reported exception for its prostration verse. — Abū Ḥayyān says it is Meccan like the previous sūra; Muqātil says the prostration verse came in Medina after the migration to Abyssinia. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:24/q20 What is distinctive about the word سَرِيًّۭا in Qur'anic usage?  [سَرِيًّۭا · rhetoric]
- 19:24/q20/p1 This form occurs only once in the Qur'an, giving the water image a subtle, hidden quality. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts it with nahr and anhār, which she counts five times, and cites related night-travel forms in 89:4, 17:1, 11:81, 15:65, 20:77, 26:52 and 44:23. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 19:24/q21 What does the repeated فَ indicate in this sequence?  [فَنَادَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
- 19:24/q21/p1 It marks events following in sequence at their natural intervals. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the particle does not require instantaneous succession. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:24/q22 How can 'beneath you' be pronounced in Qays speech?  [تَحْتَكِ · readings]
- 19:24/q22/p1 Qays kashkasha replaces the feminine second-person kāf with shīn, yielding rabbish taḥtish. — Al-Burhān cites the verse for this dialectal form. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 19:24/q23 What is the plural of sarī?  [سَرِيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:24/q23/p1 For the noble-person sense, sarāh or sarwāʾ are irregular plurals, though asriyāʾ is expected; surāt is also cited. — Al-Samīn gives the irregular forms; Sāmarrāʾī cites an Arabic poetic line with surāt. Al-Itqān gives siryān as a plural on the pattern of khuṣyān. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:24/q24 What does Mary's earlier description of herself as nasy mean?  [أَلَّا تَحْزَنِى · links]
- 19:24/q24/p1 It means an insignificant thing easily forgotten. — Abū al-Ḥasan compares a sandal or whip; al-Shanfarā's poem depicts a small overlooked object searched for on the ground. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو الحسن,الشنفرى)]
