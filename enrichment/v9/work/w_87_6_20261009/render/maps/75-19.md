# 75:19: 17 questions

## 75:19/q01 What does “then” express here?  [ثُمَّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ثم denotes temporal succession, rhetorical rank, simple coordination, or the timing of the divine statement; Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes an inference from sequence to necessary delay.
- 75:19/q01/p1 It marks sequence with an interval: explanation follows reception, collection, and recitation. — Ibn al-Jawzī illustrates ordinary delayed succession with ʿAmr arriving after Zayd. Al-Biqāʿī ties the interval to the gravity of explanation and the time of need. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says explanation follows gathering and recitation; al-Bursawī says action may not be demanded before its needed clarification. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 75:19/q01/p2 It marks a higher rank of explanation over the guarantee of collecting and reciting, rather than an obligatory time lapse. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says ثم coordinates the whole clause, so it cannot prove that clarification always comes later. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also calls the ordering one of rank, even while saying explanation follows gathering and recitation. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 75:19/q01/p3 It can be equivalent to wāw, simply joining the clauses. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this application; al-Zarkashī reports it for comparable examples but maintains that ثم retains its original sense. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BURHAN(غير مسمى)-]
- 75:19/q01/p4 It introduces God's subsequent statement to Muhammad that explanation is His responsibility. — Al-Qaffāl compares a construction in al-Balad 90:13 and 90:17; al-Rāzī says this abandons the apparent meaning without evidence. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); RAZI-FULL-]

## 75:19/q02 What does “upon Us” convey?  [عَلَيْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the fronted expression merely promises divine responsibility or also restricts the source of clarification.
- 75:19/q02/p1 God undertakes and guarantees the explanation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says علينا has this force in both clauses; al-Biqāʿī extends the guarantee to wording, arrangement, and meaning, regardless of Gabriel's bell-like or ordinary delivery. Ibn Kathīr describes preservation, recitation, and explanation as three stages; Islahi includes collection and arrangement. Qatāda and a group describe God's undertaking as both explanation and preservation of the Qur'an for the Prophet. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وجماعة معه)]
- 75:19/q02/p2 Fronting “upon Us” restricts clarification of difficult passages to God. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares the preceding verse's same fronting. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:19/q03 Why is the divine undertaking plural?  [عَلَيْنَا · rhetoric]
- 75:19/q03/p1 The plural honors God and includes Gabriel's reciting and clarifying at God's command. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says God directs and the angel transmits. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:19/q04 What is the lexical sense of bayān?  [بَيَانَهُۥ · meaning]
- 75:19/q04/p1 It is making a meaning apparent and distinct to the mind, the opposite of concealment or obscurity. — Al-Jishumī emphasizes distinction from other meanings; al-Ṭabrisī contrasts bayān with hiding and obscuring. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 75:19/q05 What does the pronoun in “explain it” refer to?  [بَيَانَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Who is addressed in 75:16–19 and whether the intervening passage concerns Gabriel's revelation, human conscience, a person's record, or the Judgment.
- 75:19/q05/p1 It refers to the Qur'an or its recitation being revealed to Muhammad. — Elmalılı says the prophetic addressee and later mention of Qur'an settle the unexpressed antecedent; 97:1 also has an implied antecedent. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls قرآن a verbal noun for recitation, like غفران and فرقان, citing Ḥassān's elegy for ʿUthmān; Elmalılı allows either the verbal noun or the recited text. One report narrows the referent to what Gabriel had already recited. Al-Ālūsī calls the transmission that addresses the Prophet the authentic majority account, in contrast to al-Qaffāl's record reading; he prefers al-Zamakhsharī's Qur'anic account over the Day reading for its eloquence and subtle indications. [7 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال)+; ELMALILI+]
- 75:19/q05/p2 It can refer to a human being's inward knowledge or conscience. — Elmalılı notes that the antecedent is not explicit and makes this possible if the address is universal rather than specifically prophetic. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 75:19/q05/p3 It refers to the person's record and deeds at resurrection. — Al-Qaffāl links the address to 75:13–15 and 17:14; al-Bayḍāwī says the recipient hurries and stumbles from fear. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the evidence is gathered and demanded for acknowledgment. Al-Rāzī objects that this leaves the apparent prophetic sense without evidence; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the resemblance to 20:114 against it. Al-Balkhī and his followers prefer the record antecedent because 75:13 implies the book presented to the person; al-Qaffāl also appeals to 75:14. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL(القفال)-]
- 75:19/q05/p4 It refers to the Judgment Day whose occurrence and description are in question. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the view that the command forbids haste in asking when it comes; he calls it untenable. Al-Ālūsī reports that the pronoun can refer to the Day of Resurrection and the command can forbid asking its timing; he finds the reading seriously difficult. [4 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:19/q06 What does God promise by “explain it”?  [بَيَانَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether bayān means legal exposition, semantic clarification, clear wording and recitation, the Qur'an's own revelation, or the fulfillment of judgment and reward.
- 75:19/q06/p1 He will clarify the Qur'an's legal limits, rulings, lawful and forbidden matters. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, Qatāda, and others give the legal sense. Qatāda includes obedience, disobedience, and avoidance of the forbidden; Abū Ḥayyān adds general and specific wording. Al-Durr's Ibn ʿAbbās connects this with the command to follow the recitation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Qatāda and others extending the clarification to what is general and what is explained in detail, along with lawful and unlawful matters. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وعطية العوفي وقتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره)]
- 75:19/q06/p2 He will make the Qur'an clear on the Prophet's tongue or have him recite it to people. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported with both formulations; al-Māwardī adds Gabriel's teaching and the Prophet's subsequent recital. Ibn al-Jawzī says he listened, then recited after Gabriel left. The Ṣaḥīḥ reports cited by al-Ālūsī support this; al-Ālūsī favors recitation of the whole Qur'an because only some of it is ambiguous. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording becomes clear to hearers without a written copy. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe preservation and unchanged recital to people. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Qatāda and a group pairing explanation with God's preservation of the Qur'an for the Prophet. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وجماعة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وجماعة معه); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 75:19/q06/p3 He will clarify unclear or ambiguous Qur'anic meanings and their intended rulings after preservation or recitation. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī say the Prophet hurried to ask about difficult meanings. Al-Jishumī includes clear and ambiguous passages; al-Bursawī specifies the intended sense of general and ambiguous wording. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran say God inspires the Prophet with intended, legislated meaning. Al-Ṭabrisī's Qatāda places this after memorization; al-Ālūsī reports it but objects that bayān need not mean resolving ambiguity. Al-Ālūsī favors al-Zamakhsharī's account as fitting the Qur'an's eloquence and subtle indications when rejecting the Day reading. [11 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(IK); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 75:19/q06/p4 He will send it down as a clear Arabic Qur'an that itself contains clarification for people. — Al-Zajjāj adds that it has no crookedness; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī report the revelation and its public clarity. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]
- 75:19/q06/p5 He will fulfill the Qur'an's promises and threats through recompense in the afterlife. — Al-Ḥasan's version specifies repayment on Resurrection; al-Qurṭubī adds clarification of promises and threats as well as bringing them about. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 75:19/q06/p6 He will remind the Prophet of the Qur'an. — Al-Jishumī reports this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس)]
- 75:19/q06/p7 He will manifest separate Qur'anic meanings in the heart and self at the station of detail. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 75:19/q06/p8 He will explain a resurrected person's record and the punishment his deeds warrant. — Al-Qaffāl has God read and gather deeds, require confession, and then explain punishment, calling it a strong warning while acknowledging no supporting report. Al-Bayḍāwī describes requital after the person's fearful reading; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says explanation follows if the person denies the evidence. In al-Qaffāl's account God has the person acknowledge or consider the assembled, read record before explaining the deeds' consequences and punishment; the detailed reckoning is a severe threat. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:19/q06/p9 He will clarify the Judgment through the trumpet blast. — In the reported reading, God first gathers those destined for Judgment and reveals its description; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the reading. Al-Ālūsī reports the alternative in which God reveals the Day's conditions in the Qur'an and manifests the event at the trumpet blast; he finds the alternative seriously difficult. [2 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:19/q06/p10 He will explain the Qur'an's words, arrangement, and meanings through the Prophet and later scholars. — Al-Biqāʿī says Gabriel may deliver it as a bell-like sound or ordinary speech. Al-Zarkashī cites 16:44 for the Prophet and community scholars, while distinguishing sound explanation of intended meaning from apparent ambiguity to limited readers. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports many exegetes assigning the work of explanation to the Prophet. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من المتأولين)]

## 75:19/q07 Who conveys or supplies the promised explanation?  [عَلَيْنَا بَيَانَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse foregrounds direct divine clarification to Muhammad, explanation entrusted to the Prophet and scholars, or clarification supplied within the Qur'an.
- 75:19/q07/p1 God supplies it to the Prophet, who communicates it through his tongue. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports Gabriel's recitation followed by the Prophet's; al-Biqāʿī says it then reaches others by Muhammad. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is preserved in his heart and audible without a written copy. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Gabriel transmits under God's direction. Qatāda and a group, as reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, say God explains and preserves the Qur'an for the Prophet. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وجماعة معه)]
- 75:19/q07/p2 The Prophet is charged with explaining it, with scholars of his community also bearing its clarification. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the Prophet's responsibility; al-Zarkashī cites 16:44 and says the most reliable explanation discloses true intended meaning. Al-Biqāʿī includes the community's scholars. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports that many exegetes make the Prophet the one charged with explaining it. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير من المتأولين)]
- 75:19/q07/p3 Explanatory Qur'anic verses themselves supply its clarification. — Elmalılı says Qur'anic verses explain one another; Islahi identifies later explanatory verses; Muhammad ʿAbduh says proper reading makes the Qur'an its own best commentary. In the reported Day reading, God reveals the Day's conditions in the Qur'an before manifesting the event at the blast. [5 notes: ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Muhammad Abduh); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:19/q08 When may clarification follow the original address?  [ثُمَّ بَيَانَهُۥ · law]
turns on: Whether ثم establishes a time gap, whether bayān specifically means clarification of an ambiguous address, and whether obligation or explanation itself is postponed.
- 75:19/q08/p1 Clarification may come after the initial address, provided it arrives by the time it is needed for action. — The permissive inference is attributed to some jurists and al-Qāḍī Abū al-Ṭayyib; al-Bayḍāwī affirms it. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Bursawī prohibit delay beyond need, the latter because that would impose an impossible duty. Al-Biqāʿī joins delay to the time of need. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(من جوز تأخير البيان عن وقت الخطاب); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي أبو الطيب); IBNASHUR-FULL(بعض علمائنا); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 75:19/q08/p2 The wording delays when explanation becomes obligatory, not necessarily when clarification itself happens. — Al-Rāzī says obligation arises at need and answers the objection that the apparent wording would require delay even though its proponents do not require it. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 75:19/q08/p3 A general indication must accompany the address; detailed clarification alone may be delayed. — Abū al-Ḥusayn says the indication excludes the utterance's unwanted apparent sense. Al-Rāzī objects that ثم covers both general and detailed clarification. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو الحسين); RAZI-FULL-]
- 75:19/q08/p4 This verse cannot establish a rule that clarification is delayed after the address. — Abū al-Ḥusayn says proponents do not accept the apparent requirement of delay. Al-Ālūsī says bayān can mean making manifest without resolving ambiguous wording. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ثم marks rank and joins whole clauses, and meanings are clarified as their wording arrives. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو الحسين); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 75:19/q09 Why is God bound to clarify ambiguous revelation?  [عَلَيْنَا بَيَانَهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether the source of obligation is promise and grace or wisdom.
- 75:19/q09/p1 The duty follows from God's promise and grace. — Al-Rāzī states his school's ground for God's obligation to explain ambiguous wording. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 75:19/q09/p2 The duty follows from divine wisdom. — Al-Rāzī attributes this ground to the Muʿtazilites. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)]

## 75:19/q10 How are clear and ambiguous Qur'anic passages understood in connection with clarification?  [بَيَانَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether clarity is a property of wording and rulings, access to a single intended meaning, or the reader's ability to follow interlocking passages.
- 75:19/q10/p1 Muḥkam is firm through commands and prohibitions, especially clarification of lawful and unlawful matters; mutashābih can have several senses or need another passage. — Al-Zarkashī calls competing definitions close: the clear may bear one meaning; ambiguous wording may bear several or stand by reference to another passage. He says limited readers can mistake subtle interlocking meanings and Qur'anic ordering for obscurity, citing 6:141. [3 notes: BURHAN; BURHAN+]
- 75:19/q10/p2 Muḥkam is unrepealed or abrogating material, obligations and promise or threat, a single-sense text, immediately recognized meaning, or recurrent wording. — Al-Zarkashī reports the alternatives separately, citing 6:151 and 17:23 for one set and al-Ikhlāṣ and 42:11 for immediate clarity. [2 notes: BURHAN(قيل)]
- 75:19/q10/p3 Mutashābih can mean similar-looking wording with different senses, or Qur'anic parts resembling one another in truth, inimitability, promise, warning, and divine origin. — Al-Zarkashī compares garden fruit similar in appearance but different in taste (2:25). In the divine sense, similarity of Qur'anic parts is positive; blame falls on people who exploit seeming ambiguity for discord. [2 notes: BURHAN; BURHAN+]
- 75:19/q10/p4 Reported mutashābih categories include abrogated text, stories, parables, opening letters, hidden matters, and texts needing interpretation or another passage. — Al-Zarkashī includes the Hour, rain, death, and multiply interpretable wording, citing 11:37, 39:56, and 31:34. Other reports leave the hidden matters' knowledge with God. [2 notes: BURHAN(قيل)]
- 75:19/q10/p5 The Qur'an is not inherently obscure or enigmatic, although some passages need clarification. — Al-Ṭabrisī draws this conclusion from the passage. Al-Zarkashī distinguishes what appears ambiguous to limited readers from the intended divine category, and calls the most reliable explanation one that expresses the true intended meaning. [3 notes: TABRISI; BURHAN]

## 75:19/q11 What happened as the Prophet received revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether haste arose chiefly from fear of loss, inquiry into meaning, or the need for continuing guidance under pressure; the explanations can coexist.
- 75:19/q11/p1 He moved his tongue or lips in eagerness or fear of forgetting; God told him to listen to Gabriel and recite afterward. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd demonstrated the lip movement; al-Bukhārī and Muslim transmit the report by several routes. Ibn Kathīr cites al-Bukhārī's detail that the Prophet lowered his head when Gabriel came. Al-Durr says revelation strained him; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says fear of forgetting drove memorization. Farāhī adds fear of accountability for losing even a letter and responsibility to guide his people. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the fit of similar Ibn Jubayr reports with 75:36–39 is obscure. [11 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,البخاري ومسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس,بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 75:19/q11/p2 He hurried to memorize the words and to ask about difficult meanings during Gabriel's recitation. — Al-Kashshāf gives both motives; al-Rāzī relates reciting with Gabriel to 75:18 and questions about meaning to 75:19; Abū Ḥayyān says the command defers questions until recitation ends. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 75:19/q11/p3 He sought further revelation under the pressure of public mission and opponents' demands. — Islahi says only revelation guided his warning, strategy, future circumstances, and spiritual, moral, and intellectual needs. Opponents asked for a Qur'an all at once and obstructed the mission; its arrival renewed his heart. Qur'an and Hadith depict his looking heavenward during divinely wise pauses and eagerness when Gabriel recited. Farāhī adds both preservation and eagerness for guidance as motives. [12 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]

## 75:19/q12 What lesson does this promise give about haste and patient reception?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the exhortation is confined to the Prophet's reception of revelation or extends to every believer's study.
- 75:19/q12/p1 The Prophet should listen and trust God's timing, preservation, recitation, and explanation. — Al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr present stages of listening, retention, recital, and explanation. Islahi says the Prophet's motives were noble but gradual revelation served divine wisdom; he should accept each portion and trust that every task has its appointed time. Islahi compares 20:114–115. Al-Bayḍāwī says if haste is rebuked even in religion's foundation, it is blameworthy elsewhere. Al-Ālūsī reports the Imam's proposal that haste may have been expressly allowed before the prohibition, but finds that explanation problematic. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]
- 75:19/q12/p2 Every reader or listener should study the whole Qur'anic message patiently and seek understanding beyond fluent sound. — Asad extends the prophetic instruction to believers, linking 20:114; isolated verses invite hasty conclusions, and beautiful sound or mechanical fluency alone can miss the message. Muhammad ʿAbduh says the Qur'an properly read is its own best commentary. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Muhammad Abduh)]
- 75:19/q12/p3 Do not hasten to demand the Day of Resurrection's date, since that demand conflicts with wisdom. — In the alternative Day reading reported by al-Ālūsī, the command forbids opening one's mouth to demand its timing; God instead reveals its conditions and manifests it at the blast. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:19/q13 How is the promised explanation supplied through the Qur'an's later verses and arrangement?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe complementary means: later interpretive verses and their divinely guided placement.
- 75:19/q13/p1 Later explanatory verses clarify, specify, supplement, narrow, complete, replace, or abrogate earlier material at the proper time. — Islahi names explanatory verses that clarify an earlier directive, abrogate it, or complete it; the formula “thus does God explain” often marks them. Elmalılı lists confirmation, interpretation, alteration, replacement, and necessity. Farāhī adds specifying general directives, supplementary rulings, and narrowing after collection and arrangement; he says explanatory verses appear after their subject or at a sūrah's end for the whole theme and signal their function. [6 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]
- 75:19/q13/p2 The Prophet and Companions transmitted revelation in its assigned sūrah sequence, making its arranged explanation available. — Farāhī appeals to Qur'an and Hadith: the Prophet recited whole sūrahs in order, the Companions memorized and followed it, and he directed them to place each newly revealed verse in its assigned location. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]

## 75:19/q14 How does the revelation passage fit the surrounding sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether a passage addressed to the Prophet may interrupt resurrection discourse, whether it is linked by contrast and gathering imagery, or whether the whole passage must retain a Judgment referent.
- 75:19/q14/p1 Verses 16–19 briefly interrupt the resurrection discourse to instruct the Prophet, after which the prior theme resumes. — Al-Khaṭṭābī records an objection that this divides a discourse running from 75:14–15 to 75:20–21. Al-Itqān says the interruption occurs when revelation reaches 75:15, compares 20:114, and reports al-Rāzī's teacher who pauses to regain a pupil's attention. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says an interruption needs no surrounding contextual marker and resembles 20:114. [5 notes: KHATTABI; ITQAN(الفخر الرازي); TABATABAI]
- 75:19/q14/p2 It is integrated with the surrounding account of conscience or creates a meaningful contrast. — Elmalılı links the account of inner conscience to the start and end of the passage. Abū Ḥayyān contrasts the man who denies resurrection and signs with the Prophet eager to learn and present them. Al-Sāmarrāʾī links the sūra's gathering of bones and sun with moon to the earthly gathering of the Qur'an. Abū Ḥayyān, as reported by al-Ālūsī, contrasts a denier absorbed in sinful desires with someone preserving, studying, and presenting God's signs in hope of acceptance; the opposing portrayals illuminate one another. [4 notes: ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 75:19/q14/p3 A supposed lack of connection was alleged as evidence of alteration of the Qur'an. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Rāzī's account of an early extremist Rāfiḍī allegation; Abū Ḥayyān answers with the contrast between denier and Prophet. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرازي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 75:19/q14/p4 The verses continue the Judgment scene by describing a person's fearful reading of his record. — Al-Qaffāl cites surrounding self-witness and 17:14 but concedes no report supports the proposal. Al-Bayḍāwī says the reader stumbles; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this contextual objection by noting an interruption needs no sign. Al-Qaffāl connects the scene to 75:13–14; al-Balkhī and his followers prefer this account and construe the statement as circumstantial, with an implied saying at presentation of the record. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL(القفال)-]
- 75:19/q14/p5 The verses continue the Judgment theme as a prohibition on hastening to ask when it will come. — The reported account says God gathers those destined for Judgment and explains it at the trumpet blast; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects it. Al-Ālūsī reports that the Day reading makes the passage interrupt the threat to heighten terror; asking for the Day's date would violate wisdom. He finds the reading seriously difficult. [4 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:19/q15 How does the following kallā relate to this promise?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether kallā resumes with rebuke of haste, opens an emphatic assertion or attention call, or functions as a response or future marker; grammar and a tanwīn reading also bear on its classification.
- 75:19/q15/p1 It rebukes haste in recitation and restrains or denies the preceding conduct. — Ibn Qutayba gives general rebuke and restraint usages, citing 70:39, 74:52, 75:20, 104:4, 82:9, and 83:7, and specifically reads it after this promise as “stop hastening.” Sībawayh and most grammarians call it a simple deterrent particle and allow pause before following words. Thaʿlab instead analyzes a kāf of comparison plus lā with doubled lām. Al-Zamakhsharī permits tanwīn by analogy to salāsilā, while Abū Ḥayyān objects that kallā is not a noun. Ibn Hishām says rebuke is strained here after the long separation; some infer Meccan origin from its threatening force. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ITQAN(الزمخشري,ثعلب,سيبويه); ITQAN(أبو حيان,ابن هشام)-]
- 75:19/q15/p2 It means “indeed,” introducing the following assertion without rebuke. — The alternative allows pausing before kallā and beginning with it. Al-Kisāʾī gives this sense; Makkī treats kallā as a noun here, citing a tanwīn reading and possible roots denoting weariness or burden. Abū Ḥayyān rejects a noun analysis in his answer to the tanwīn analogy. [3 notes: ITQAN(الكسائي,مكي); ITQAN(أبو حيان)-]
- 75:19/q15/p3 It serves as the opening particle alā, calling attention to what follows. — Abū Ḥātim proposes this; Abū Ḥayyān says none preceded him and al-Zajjāj and others followed. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو حاتم)]
- 75:19/q15/p4 It answers like “yes.” — Al-Naḍr ibn Shumayl cites “By the moon” for this response use. [1 notes: ITQAN(النضر بن شميل)]
- 75:19/q15/p5 It carries the future sense “soon.” — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn Saʿdān offer this; Abū Ḥayyān records it. [1 notes: ITQAN(الفراء)]
- 75:19/q15/p6 Rebuke need not be present in every kallā occurrence, especially after this distant clause. — Ibn Hishām says the proposed rebuke is strained here, as at verses concerning human fashioning and standing. Other scholars therefore add a second, non-rebuking sense and permit beginning with kallā. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن هشام)]

## 75:19/q16 Who is addressed by the command and promise?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage addresses the Prophet receiving revelation, a resurrected reader of a record, or someone asking about the Day.
- 75:19/q16/p1 The Prophet Muhammad is addressed. — Al-Ālūsī calls the prophetic address the authentic majority transmission and favors al-Zamakhsharī's prophetic reading over the Day alternative. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القفال)+]
- 75:19/q16/p2 A person shown their record at resurrection is addressed. — Al-Qaffāl says fear makes the person hurry and stumble while reading the record; al-Balkhī and his followers prefer this scene, with the book implied by 75:13. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; ALUSI-FULL(القفال)-]
- 75:19/q16/p3 In the Day reading, the address can be to the Prophet, a generic addressee, or any suitable listener. — Al-Ālūsī reports these alternatives for the warning not to hurry to learn the Day's timing; he finds the Day reading seriously difficult. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:19/q17 How is the record-reading sentence syntactically joined to its context?  [* · grammar]
- 75:19/q17/p1 It is a circumstantial sentence introduced by an implied saying when the book is presented. — Al-Balkhī and his followers construe it this way in their preferred record reading, taking the book as implied by 75:13. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+]
