# 75:18: 19 questions

## 75:18/q01 What does 'when We recite it' mean?  [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether qaraʾa here denotes audible revelation, gathering, clarification, or an inward event; al-Ṭabarī tests gathering against the binding force of individual verses before compilation.
- 75:18/q01/p1 When the revelation is recited to Muhammad, through Gabriel. — Al-Farrāʾ specifies Gabriel's recitation. Al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn ʿAbbās glossing it as revelation to Muhammad. Al-Zajjāj and al-Bursawī stress that the recitation must finish before Muhammad starts; al-Ālūsī calls God's attribution figurative. Al-Wāḥidī's analysis also allows 'our reciting it.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the angel messenger recites on God's behalf. [8 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 75:18/q01/p2 When God gathers and composes its parts. — Abū ʿUbayda cites ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's use of qaraʾa for containing a fetus and the idiom about a woman never conceiving; Ibn Qutayba takes the verb as collection. Al-Ṭabarī grants that gathering is Arabic, but argues that making obligation depend on a compiled text would leave 96:1 and 74:1 unbinding before compilation. [6 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; TAB-FULL-]
- 75:18/q01/p3 When God makes it clear or explains it. — Ibn ʿAbbās explains qaraʾnāhu as clarification and couples it with acting upon what is clarified; al-Suyūṭī gives the same gloss. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 75:18/q01/p4 When God brings it into being in the servant's annihilation. — Al-Qāshānī gives an inward reading in which recitation occurs during annihilation and is followed by subsistence. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 75:18/q01/p5 When God reads the person's assembled deeds to them at Judgment. — On al-Qaffāl's record interpretation, God gathers the person's deeds, reads them to that person, and then explains their consequences and punishment; al-Ālūsī reports this as a severe threat of detailed reckoning. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القفال)]
- 75:18/q01/p6 When God discloses the Day's conditions through the Qurʾān. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative: God gathers creation, reveals the Day's conditions in the Qurʾān, and manifests the Day at the blast. Al-Ālūsī finds the account seriously difficult. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:18/q02 Who is the reciter denoted by 'We'?  [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · referent]
turns on: The distinction between God as source and Gabriel as immediate reciter, and how divine attribution works.
- 75:18/q02/p1 Gabriel recites to Muhammad by God's command, so God attributes the recitation to Himself. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil identify Gabriel as the immediate reciter. Al-Zamakhsharī says the Prophet must follow him rather than prompt him; al-Rāzī compares attribution to God with 4:80. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise distinguish the angel's delivery from God's source or command. KURANYOLU cites 2:97, 16:102 and 26:193 for Gabriel's mediation. Al-Biqāʿī says the pronoun can refer to the Qurʾān or, alternatively, to Gabriel's reading; Abū Ḥayyān reports the angel as its referent. Al-Burhān compares other divinely commanded agents, including winds and angels moving clouds and rain reviving earth. Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies that the angel messenger recites on God's behalf. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط,التفسير الكبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 75:18/q02/p2 God is the source of the recitation and enables its human understanding. — Asad grounds the divine attribution in God's revelation of the Qurʾān and His enabling people to understand it; al-Sāmarrāʾī says God directs while Gabriel transmits. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:18/q03 Why does God say 'We recite'?  [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · rhetoric]
- 75:18/q03/p1 The majestic plural honors and heightens God's command to wait for His agent's recitation. — Al-Bursawī says the plural magnifies the command to wait; al-Ālūsī says its emphasis reinforces waiting rather than competing. Al-Rāzī sees Gabriel's honor in attributing his act to God; al-Burhān and al-Sāmarrāʾī explain plural attribution through agents under God's command, comparing the Messenger's obedience with God's at 4:80 and other mediated acts. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(التفسير الكبير)]

## 75:18/q04 What do the pronouns referring to 'it' designate?  [قَرَأْنَٰهُ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Who is addressed in 75:16–19 and whether those lines are an inserted instruction about revelation or continue the resurrection scene.
- 75:18/q04/p1 The Qurʾān being revealed to the Prophet. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the object with the Qurʾān in 'do not move with it'; Elmalılı says the implied antecedent is sufficient as at 97:1, since the addressee is the Prophet and Qurʾān is subsequently named. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a narrower view that the pronoun means the portion already recited to him. Al-Ālūsī describes the majority transmission addressing the Prophet as authentic, in contrast to al-Qaffāl's record interpretation. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ELMALILI+]
- 75:18/q04/p2 The person's record of deeds at resurrection. — Al-Qaffāl and another account understand the passage as a frightened person's hurried reading of his record; God gathers and reads the deeds and later explains their requital. Al-Suyūṭī objects that a sound revelation report instead addresses Muhammad; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says an insertion needs no contextual signal and compares 20:114. Al-Qaffāl ties the record to the person's fear and rushed reading when it is presented; al-Balkhī and his followers prefer the record implied by 75:13. Al-Ālūsī notes that this differs from the authentic majority transmission addressing the Prophet. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(القفال); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; ITQAN-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:18/q04/p3 What is within a person, namely conscience or inward knowledge. — Elmalılı notes that the antecedent is unstated and that a universal human addressee permits this reference; he ultimately prefers the Qurʾān because the address is specifically to Muhammad. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]
- 75:18/q04/p4 The description of the coming Judgment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the interpretation that one should not hurry to ask when Judgment will come: God gathers its people, reveals its description, and clarifies it by the trumpet blast; he rejects this reading. Al-Ālūsī reports an alternative in which the pronoun denotes the Day of Resurrection: God gathers creation, discloses its conditions in the Qurʾān, and manifests it at the blast. He finds the account seriously difficult. [3 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:18/q05 What does 'Qurʾān' or 'its qurʾān' mean here?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: The proposed root-sense of qaraʾa, and whether the command takes effect at each recitation or only after collection.
- 75:18/q05/p1 It is the verbal noun 'recitation' or 'reading,' also used for the recited text. — Al-Farrāʾ compares qirāʾa/qurʾān with other paired verbal nouns: rujḥān/rujūḥ, maʿrifa/ʿirfān, ṭawāf/ṭawafān. Ibn Qutayba and al-Qurṭubī agree; al-Ṭabarī prefers Ibn ʿAbbās's derivation and cites ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm. He answers that an act-name can name its object, as kitāb names what is written. Elmalılı allows both noun and recited text; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares ghufrān and ʿudwān, saying the noun later became the revelation's proper name. [10 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TAB-FULL+]
- 75:18/q05/p2 It denotes collection or joining of parts, and by extension the assembled book. — Abū ʿUbayda derives the name from joining its sūras, adducing ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's line about containing a fetus and the idiom mā qaraʾat salyan. Qatāda explains qurʾān as the verbal noun of gathering; Ibn Qutayba glosses following its collection. Al-Ṭabarī concedes this is Arabic but rejects making the obligation wait for compilation, citing 96:1 and 74:1. [9 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); TAB-FULL-]
- 75:18/q05/p3 It denotes God's clarification through recitation. — Ibn ʿAbbās derives the word from qaraʾa in the sense of making clear and explains that one must follow the clarification once it is given; al-Ṭabarī prefers this reading over a collection sense. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)+]

## 75:18/q06 How is the Qurʾān's name related to Furqān and Kitāb?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: The distinct senses and usage patterns of the revelation's names.
- 75:18/q06/p1 Qurʾān names the gathered book; Furqān names its separation of truth from falsehood and believer from unbeliever. — Abū ʿUbayda repeats this distinction; he also compares Furqān's form with qanaʿān, a judgment satisfying both disputants. [4 notes: MAJAZ]
- 75:18/q06/p2 Qurʾān first names recitation and then revelation; Kitāb derives from writing and names what is already inscribed in the Preserved Tablet. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Qurʾān is a verbal noun before it becomes a proper name. He says a sūra introduced as Book tends to repeat kitāb more, one introduced as Qurʾān repeats qurʾān more, and one introduced by both uses them at roughly equal rates. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:18/q07 What must the addressee do when the recitation occurs?  [فَٱتَّبِعْ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether following is immediate attentive reception, later repetition, lasting obedience and conveyance, collection, or a reading of a different addressee; accounts often combine listening, subsequent recitation and action.
- 75:18/q07/p1 Listen silently and attentively to the recitation, allowing it to finish. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly glosses following as listening, sometimes lowering the head; al-Rāzī prefers listening over a legal command and al-Wāḥidī says this is why the Prophet was told to cease reading. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Sāmarrāʾī specify following with mind and thought. Al-Biqāʿī adds ear and mind; al-Suyūṭī says comprehension outranks hurried memorization. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts the attention gloss though he reads the command as subsequent recitation. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, says to follow the recitation with mind and thought, listening attentively. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(الأئمة); BURSEVI(أهل التفسير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 75:18/q07/p2 Recite it oneself after Gabriel has finished, following his reading. — Ibn ʿAbbās's reports, al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī specify reciting after Gabriel leaves; al-Bursawī says begin immediately without delay. Al-Bayḍāwī adds repetition until the wording is firmly established. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the completed divine recitation as the point after which the Prophet reads. Ibn Kathīr describes reception, recitation and explanation as three stages. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن عباس عن سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 75:18/q07/p3 Act on what the Qurʾān says, obeying its commands and avoiding its prohibitions. — Qatāda repeatedly specifies the lawful and forbidden; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Ibn ʿAbbās also give acting upon its contents. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this practical force because every revealed verse binds before compilation, giving 96:1 and 74:1 as examples; al-Naḥḥās praises Ibn Abī Ṭalḥa's report from Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the command-and-prohibition reading remote. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports this explanation jointly from Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [41 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); JISHUMI(قتادة، والضحاك); TABRISI(قتادة والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك); NAHHAS(ابن عباس)+; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 75:18/q07/p4 Follow the collected or composed Qurʾān as it is assembled. — Abū ʿUbayda understands the command as taking, following and acting on the joined portions; Ibn Qutayba glosses it as following its collection. Al-Ṭabarī argues that this cannot postpone obligation until compilation. [2 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL-]
- 75:18/q07/p5 Review or repeat the recitation until it becomes firmly fixed in memory. — Al-Bayḍāwī says repeated imitation establishes it in the mind; al-Biqāʿī adds fluency and practice until the passage becomes good character. Al-Ālūsī reports this as a further view, whereas al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it remote. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 75:18/q07/p6 Follow the message, act on it, and convey or call people to it. — Al-Jishumī reports conveying it to the Prophet's community as part of acting on it. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet should read, act and call others to the portion received, while God sets its pace and arrangement; Asad stresses following its expressed message, word by word. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:18/q07/p7 Conform to a prior recitation, the general sense of 'follow'. — Al-Jishumī explains ittibāʿ, iqtidāʾ and iʾtimām as near-synonyms: a second thing conforms to the first rather than differing from it. Al-Nasafī says the Prophet follows Gabriel's reading as read. [2 notes: JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 75:18/q07/p8 Return to subsistence after inward annihilation. — In al-Qāshānī's inward reading, God brings the recitation into being during annihilation; heart and self then appear in the detailed station. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 75:18/q07/p9 A person shown a record should acknowledge or reflect on the reading and confess the deeds. — Al-Qaffāl's record reading makes God gather and read the person's deeds, require confession, and explain punishment or requital; al-Rāzī calls it a strong warning but notes its lack of reports. Al-Suyūṭī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject the record context. Al-Qaffāl adds that acknowledgment or consideration of the assembled record precedes God's explanation of consequences and punishment, making it a severe threat of detailed reckoning. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(القفال); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ITQAN-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:18/q07/p10 A person should follow inward knowledge placed within the conscience. — Elmalılı presents this if the address is to humanity generally, though he prefers an address to the Prophet about Qurʾānic revelation. [1 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]
- 75:18/q07/p11 Do not hasten to ask when the Day of Resurrection will occur. — Al-Ālūsī reports an alternative that treats the prohibition as a ban on opening one's mouth to demand the Day's date: God will gather creation, describe its conditions, and manifest it at the blast; demanding its timing is contrary to wisdom. He finds this alternative seriously difficult. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:18/q08 How is the hamza in 'qaraʾnāhu' read?  [قَرَأْنَٰهُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the noncanonical form changes the verb to second person and removes hamza and alif.
- 75:18/q08/p1 The usual form retains the hamza in qaraʾnāhu. — Al-Samīn contrasts the noncanonical form with the expected qaraʾtahu, describing loss of hamza and alif in that form. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو العالية)]
- 75:18/q08/p2 Abū al-ʿĀliya reads a noncanonical qarat(t)ahu without hamza or alif, as second-person past 'when you intend to recite it'. — Abū Ḥayyān reports open qāf, rāʾ and tāʾ in the three occurrences and explains the altered morphology. Al-Samīn derives it from qaraʾtahu by changing hamza to alif and deleting that alif for ease, comparing wa-law tar mā al-ṣibyān where the expected form is tarā and mā is extra. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Abū al-ʿĀliya's unhamzated reading قرته and فاتبع قرته, without an alif in the latter form. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية); SAMIN-DURR(أبو العالية); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية)]

## 75:18/q09 How is the hamza in 'qurʾānahu' pronounced or lightened?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Which hamza easing operations Arabic grammar permits and which Qurʾānic readers actually transmit; the comparison extends beyond this word.
- 75:18/q09/p1 Ibn Kathīr transfers the hamza's vowel in this Qurʾān form; a lightened quranahu loses the medial glottal catch. — Ibn al-Jazarī includes fa-ttabiʿ qurʾānahu with qurʾān al-fajr and qurʾānan farraqnāhu. QIRAAT-ER notes that lightening preserves the recitational noun and possessive suffix. [2 notes: NASHR(ابن كثير); QIRAAT-ER(hmz)]
- 75:18/q09/p2 Some grammarians allow hamza substitution, assimilation or intermediate realization after a consonant, even across words. — Abū ʿAmr reports Arab usage; Ibn Sawwār and Ibn Shīṭā permit substitution and assimilation across words. Kufan grammarians allow intermediate hamza after any vowelless letter. Abū Ḥayyān objects that the unrestricted Kufan rule conflicts with Arabic usage. Abū al-ʿAlāʾ extends intermediate realization after long-vowel wāw and yā as after alif; Ibn al-Jazarī calls this very weak because transfer and assimilation remain possible after wāw and yā. Al-Dānī attributes examples in mawʾilan and al-mawʾūda to Ibn Abī Hāshim, finding the written form especially helpful in mawʾilan. [5 notes: NASHR(أبو العلاء الهمداني,أبو طاهر بن سوار وأبو الفتح بن شيطا,أبو عمرو الداني,بعض النحاة,نحاة الكوفة); NASHR(أبو حيان)-]
- 75:18/q09/p3 Readers restrict transfer after a vowelless consonant and retain full hamza after plural mīm, especially at a pause. — Ibn al-Jazarī endorses the readers' restriction: shifting hamza would change plural mīm's original ḍamma; some connect the mīm before hamza to restore it. Al-Sakhāwī says readers in his tradition agree on full hamza at a pause. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو الحسن السخاوي,القراء); NASHR+]
- 75:18/q09/p4 Some grammarians allow deletion of hamza after long-vowel wāw or yā, but Qurʾān readers reject it. — Examples given are tazdarī aʿyunukum and adʿū ilā. Ibn al-Jazarī reports that no Qurʾān reader accepts this deletion. [1 notes: NASHR(بعض النحاة); NASHR(القراء)-]
- 75:18/q09/p5 Abū al-ʿĀliya reads فاتبع قرته without hamza or alif. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Abū al-ʿĀliya's reading of فاتبع قرته and notes loss of both the hamza and the alif in this third form. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية)]

## 75:18/q10 What occasion or conduct led to this instruction, and what happened afterward?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported haste is explained solely by fear of forgetting or also by the urgency of the Prophet's mission; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes the fit of the fear account.
- 75:18/q10/p1 The Prophet hurried or moved his lips under the strain of revelation, fearing loss; afterward he listened to Gabriel and recited after Gabriel left. — Ibn ʿAbbās's versions mention bowed head, moving lips or tongue, fear of forgetting, God placing the Qurʾān in his breast, and subsequent recitation. Al-Qurṭubī cites Muslim and Bukhārī and notes Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd demonstrating the lip motion; Ibn Kathīr says the report comes by multiple routes and gives Bukhārī's lowered-head wording. Al-Biqāʿī gives no grading. Al-Suyūṭī calls the report sound and uses it against al-Qaffāl's record reading. Farāhī says fear of losing even a letter was tied to fear of responsibility. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن عباس عن سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,البخاري ومسلم); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; TABATABAI(ابن عباس,بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); TABATABAI-]
- 75:18/q10/p2 The Prophet hurried to memorize each new revelation; a sūra's end became known when Bismillāh descended. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits this separate Ibn ʿAbbās report, adding the sign that marked the end of a sūra. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 75:18/q10/p3 The Prophet's haste also arose from pressure to guide his people and answer opponents while revelation came gradually. — Iṣlāḥī says opponents pressed him and objected that a divine Qurʾān was not revealed at once; guidance was needed for his mission, strategy, spiritual, moral and intellectual needs and replies to continuing challenges. Revelation strengthened his heart and soul. During divinely wise pauses he looked heavenward; when Gabriel came he eagerly preserved every word. His love of knowledge and duty were noble, but patience was required. Farāhī adds fear of accountability and the wish for more guidance alongside fear of loss. [13 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]

## 75:18/q11 How do these words fit the surrounding sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 75:16–19 is an inserted revelation instruction or a continuation of the resurrection account; reported occasion and the parallel at 20:114 are used to decide.
- 75:18/q11/p1 They interrupt the resurrection passage to teach the Prophet etiquette in receiving revelation. — Al-Khaṭṭābī records the objection that 75:16–19 separates 75:14–15 from 75:20–21. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the instruction resembles 20:114 and an insertion needs no flanking sign; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares insertions at 19:64 and the prayer instruction at 2:238 amid marriage rulings. Al-Suyūṭī says attentive understanding has priority over rushed memorization and rejects the claim by some Shiʿites that material had dropped from the sūra; the sound revelation report weighs against a record reading. Al-Ālūsī points instead to al-Zamakhsharī's revelation account as fitting the Qurʾān's eloquence and subtle indications. [9 notes: KHATTABI; ITQAN(الأئمة,بعض الرافضة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 75:18/q11/p2 The revelation instruction is integrated with the sūra's account of conscience and resurrection. — Elmalılı says it joins the beginning and end through conscience. Al-Sāmarrāʾī sees a parallel between gathering bones and sun with moon at Judgment and gathering and reciting the Qurʾān in earthly life. Al-Bayḍāwī says a rebuke of haste in the foundational matter reinforces the sūra's wider rebuke of haste. Iṣlāḥī relates the passage to adversaries' pressure and gradual revelation; 20:114–115 gives a similar call for patience. Abū Ḥayyān, as reported by al-Ālūsī, sees a contrast between the denier absorbed in sinful desires and one who preserves, studies, and presents God's signs in hope of acceptance; opposites illuminate each other. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 75:18/q11/p3 They continue the resurrection scene as instructions to a person reading a record of deeds. — Al-Qaffāl cites the surrounding account of deeds at 75:13–15 and 17:14; the frightened person stammers while God gathers the record, requires confession and explains requital. Al-Rāzī says it is a strong warning but unsupported by reports. Al-Suyūṭī rejects it because of the sound revelation report; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the 20:114 parallel supports the revelation reading. Al-Qaffāl links the person's fearful haste to the presentation of the record after 75:13–14. Al-Balkhī and his followers prefer this as a circumstantial sentence introduced by an implied saying. Al-Ālūsī contrasts it with the authentic majority transmission. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(القفال,بعض الرافضة); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; ITQAN-; TABATABAI-]
- 75:18/q11/p4 They refer to the gathering and disclosure of Judgment itself. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this reading of the question about Judgment, the gathering of its people, its description and the trumpet blast, then dismisses it. In al-Ālūsī's reported alternative, the address interrupts the threat to intensify the Day's terror; asking for its date conflicts with wisdom. Al-Ālūsī rejects this as the best account. [3 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:18/q12 What does the verse teach about receiving and using revelation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the direct instruction to Muhammad also guides every later reader and listener.
- 75:18/q12/p1 Wait patiently for revelation, attend to it, trust God's preservation, then recite and practice it. — Al-Zajjāj forbids premature recitation; Ibn Kathīr describes collection in the heart, enabled recitation and divine explanation as three stages. Al-Bayḍāwī says haste is blameworthy even in religion's foundation. Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, says the Prophet hurried to memorize and ask about meaning, so preservation is assured and questions wait until the angel finishes. Al-Biqāʿī extends following to fluent repetition and character-forming practice. Iṣlāḥī adds that God determines timing, collection, arrangement and elucidation; the Prophet can accept each portion without anxiety or heeding demands for revelation at once. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī disputes that fear of forgetting explains the wording. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI-]
- 75:18/q12/p2 Believers too should read the whole message patiently, attend to its words and understand before concluding. — Asad applies it to all who read, hear or study the Qurʾān, comparing 20:114 and rejecting hasty conclusions from isolated verses, mechanical fluency and pleasure in beautiful sound without grasping the message. Study Quran likewise urges reflection on all teachings before hasty conclusions. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 75:18/q13 Does following this recital bear on the Qurʾān's arrangement?  [* · sciences]
- 75:18/q13/p1 The Qurʾān was collected and ordered during the Prophet's lifetime, and he conveyed its final sequence. — Farāhī infers this from the command to follow the newly recited text and says the sequence matched the Guarded Tablet. He cites Qurʾān and Hadith evidence that the Prophet recited whole sūras in order, Companions memorized them that way, and he assigned each new verse its place in a sūra. Authentic agreed reports say Gabriel recited the completed Qurʾān to him in its true sequence. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]

## 75:18/q14 What knowledge is needed to understand such Qurʾānic wording?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:18/q14/p1 Arabic usage and especially the rhetorical sciences of maʿānī and bayān are needed to grasp God's intended meanings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists lexicon, morphology, syntax, maʿānī and bayān, plus established speech, poetry and native intuition; he says maʿānī and bayān expose detailed meaning and inimitability and were once called dalāʾil al-iʿjāz. Al-Zamakhsharī says law, kalām, reports, preaching, grammar or lexicon alone do not suffice and warns against harming the Qurʾān's fine structure and challenge. Al-Sakkākī requires both rhetorical sciences and calls God al-Ḥakīm for wise speech's elevated aims; his 'warns' and warning of woe target untrained interpretation, whose rare successes do not justify it. Al-Jurjānī says unusually rich Qurʾānic style requires the sciences absent natural eloquence. Ibn Rushd cites 26:195 for Arabic's necessity, calls denial ignorant, and says corruptly motivated denial warrants repentance and discretionary discipline. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the aspiration to all divine meaning from meanings signaled by rhetoric and context; meanings are effectively inexhaustible and interpreters differ in acuity and knowledge. [14 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو الوليد ابن رشد,الزمخشري,السكاكي,السيد الجرجاني)]

## 75:18/q15 How do gathering and recitation relate inwardly and outwardly?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · inward]
- 75:18/q15/p1 Gathering is inward, whereas recitation is outward. — Al-Bursawī reports this distinction from al-Wāsiṭī. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الواسطي)]

## 75:18/q16 How well does the Ibn ʿAbbās revelation report fit the verses?  [* · reports]
- 75:18/q16/p1 The report says revelation weighed on the Prophet, who moved his lips; he was then told to listen and recite afterward. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes similar reports from Ibn Jubayr and others but finds the fit between this explanation and the verses obscure. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 75:18/q17 What is the syntax of the record-reading instruction?  [* · grammar]
- 75:18/q17/p1 It is a circumstantial sentence introduced by an implied saying at the presentation of the record. — Al-Balkhī and his followers, as reported by al-Ālūsī, prefer al-Qaffāl's reading and parse the sentence as circumstantial, with an implied saying when the person's book is presented. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+]

## 75:18/q18 Was the Prophet's earlier haste in reciting permitted?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:18/q18/p1 It may have been expressly permitted before the prohibition was revealed. — The Imam suggests prior express permission for the Prophet's haste; al-Ālūsī reports the suggestion but finds it problematic. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]

## 75:18/q19 Who is addressed by the instruction to follow the recitation?  [فَٱتَّبِعْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage addresses reception of revelation, a person's record, or the timing of Resurrection.
- 75:18/q19/p1 The Prophet is addressed in the revelation account. — Al-Ālūsī calls the majority transmission addressing the Prophet authentic, while contrasting it with al-Qaffāl's record reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القفال)]
- 75:18/q19/p2 A human being shown their record of deeds is addressed. — Al-Qaffāl reads the person receiving the record as the addressee; al-Balkhī and his followers prefer this reading, as al-Ālūsī reports. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+]
- 75:18/q19/p3 In the Day interpretation, the address can be to the Prophet, a generic person, or any suitable hearer. — Al-Ālūsī reports these alternative addressees when the pronoun denotes the Day of Resurrection and the sentence intensifies the threat. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
