# 87:6/q05 How is the hamza of “We shall make you recite” treated in pausal readings? [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · readings]
turns on: The extent of al-Akhfash’s substitution rule and the weight of written form versus transmitted pause practice.
- 87:6/q05/p1 The hamza may be replaced with yāʾ where the written form supports it, as in sanaqriʾuka.
  reasons: Al-Dānī and the majority attribute broad substitution after kasra or ḍamma to al-Akhfash; Abū al-ʿIzz permits it for Ḥamza at pause. Al-Dānī favors matching the rasm. Ibn al-Jazarī says the rasm uses yāʾ here rather than customary wāw.
  holders (5 notes): NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس بن أحمد، الحافظ أبو عمرو الداني)
  notes: NASHR:v1p444/r2 NASHR:v1p444/r4 NASHR:v1p445/r2 NASHR:v1p445/r5 NASHR:v1p454/r3
- 87:6/q05/p2 Substitution is restricted to final radical hamza; medial cases use Sibawayh’s between-between easing.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī prefers what al-Akhfash’s own book permits and notes authorities who rejected his supposed broad method; al-Dānī combines substitution where rasm supports it with Sibawayh’s easing elsewhere.
  holders (3 notes): NASHR(الحافظ أبو عمرو الداني); NASHR+
  notes: NASHR:v1p444/r3 NASHR:v1p445/r1 NASHR:v1p445/r2
- 87:6/q05/p3 Substitution or easing varies with word boundary, region, vowel, and rasm.
  reasons: Abū al-ʿAlāʾ permits substitution for a ḍamma hamza after kasra across or within words; Abū al-ʿIzz reports regional variation. Some non-Ḥijāzī Arabs use wāw, yāʾ or alif for final hamza by case. Ibn al-Jazarī distinguishes when rawm or ishām remains possible according to whose vowel governs replacement.
  holders (4 notes): NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس بن أحمد)
  notes: NASHR:v1p444/r5 NASHR:v1p445/r3 NASHR:v1p445/r4 NASHR:v1p445/r5
- 87:6/q05/p4 The al-Kisāʾī method attributed through Khalaf has no practical basis.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī agrees with al-Dānī and rejects al-Shāṭibī’s allusion for lack of transmission or analogy.
  holders (1 notes): NASHR+
  notes: NASHR:v1p444/r1
sources: NASHR = Ibn al-Jazarī, d. 833

# 87:6/q06 What does “forget” mean here in Arabic usage? [تَنسَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical sense and vocalization of nisyān.
- 87:6/q06/p1 It is the opposite of remembering: known information fails to come to mind, briefly or for long.
  reasons: Damghānī cites 18:63 and 18:73 and glosses this verse as lasting retention. Ṭabrisī calls it loss of meaning from the mind, like absent-mindedness, rather than simply loss of acquired knowledge. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows brief or long failure to recall.
  holders (4 notes): DAMGHANI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNASHUR
  notes: DAMGHANI:v1p453/r11 TABRISI:87:7-9/r9 IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p579#2/r4 IBNASHUR:87:6/r9
- 87:6/q06/p2 Nisyān in this sense has kasra on nūn and sukūn on sīn, unlike the similarly named anatomical term.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī calls the other, fully vowelled form the twin of the sciatic nerve.
  holders (1 notes): IBNJAWZI-NUZHA
  notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA:v1p579#2/r1
sources: DAMGHANI = al-Dāmghānī, d. 478; IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA = Ibn al-Jawzī, d. 597; TABRISI = al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548

# 87:6/q07 What is the nature of forgetting? [تَنسَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether forgetting is a created act or a privation of knowledge.
- 87:6/q07/p1 Forgetting is an act created by God.
  reasons: Jishumī reports Abū ʿAlī; Ṭabrisī attributes the point to Abū ʿAlī al-Jubbāʾī.
  holders (2 notes): JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي)
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7373/r1 TABRISI:87:7-9/r10
- 87:6/q07/p2 Forgetting is the loss of ordinarily necessary knowledge, not an entity or act.
  reasons: Jishumī reports Abū Hāshim in disagreement with Abū ʿAlī.
  holders (1 notes): JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7373/r2
sources: JISHUMI = al-Ḥākim al-Jishumī, d. 494; TABRISI = al-Ṭabrisī, d. 548

# 87:6/q08 Is “you will not forget” a statement or a prohibition? [فَلَا تَنسَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lā negates a predicted event or prohibits an avoidable cause of forgetting; the retained final alif also matters.
- 87:6/q08/p1 It is an asserted negation that functions as God’s promise of preserved memory.
  reasons: Naḥḥās prefers exegetical support and says most linguists reject commanding involuntary forgetting. Damghānī cites 20:108 for assertive lā; Burhān describes its general negative use. Rāzī likens this to promising clothing so the addressee will not be naked; he says it brings stronger reassurance because forgetting is under God’s control. Ibn ʿĀshūr says report form serves a guarantee.

# 87:6/q09 Why is the final long vowel written or retained in “tansā”? [تَنسَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form is explained by rhyme, orthography and pause, or a jussive retaining a weak letter.
- 87:6/q09/p1 It maintains the rhyme or regular verse endings.
  reasons: This is especially invoked for a prohibitive jussive. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī compare the form al-sabīlā at 33:67; the shorter Bayḍāwī note cites 17:67. Abū Ḥayyān reports it but later rejects it as weak.
