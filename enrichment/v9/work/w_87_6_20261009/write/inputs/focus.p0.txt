# 87:6/q01 What does “We shall make you recite” mean? [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether iqraʾa here is teaching recitation generally or listening for correction.
- 87:6/q01/p1 God will teach Muhammad the Qur’an and make him a reciter.
  reasons: Baghawī and Thaʿlabī specify Gabriel’s recitation; Mālik, transmitted by Ibn Wahb in Qurṭubī, glosses it as teaching and retention. Bursevī cites Tāj al-maṣādir for keeping the Qur’an in the ear. Nasafī’s shorter note awkwardly says 'until he forgets,' while its full version says 'until he does not forget.'
  holders (14 notes): BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)
  notes: BAGHAWI:87:6/r1 BAGHAWI-FULL:v8p401/r1 THALABI:v10p183#2/r17 WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p438/r1 WAHIDI-QT:87:6/r1 WAHIDI-WAJIZ:87:1-6/r6 QURTUBI:87:6/r1 QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r1 IBNJAWZI-ZAD:v9p89#3/r1 NASAFI:87:6/r1 NASAFI-FULL:v4p332#6/r1 BURSEVI:v10p314#2/r7 ELMALILI:87:6/r1 ASAD-NOTES:87:6/r1
- 87:6/q01/p2 Iqraʾa can mean receiving a reading by listening so errors can be corrected.
  reasons: Jishumī and Ṭabrisī also derive qirāʾa from gathering letters. Al-Majmaʿ, cited by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, gives the correction sense.
  holders (3 notes): JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI(المجمع); TABATABAI-
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7372/r11 TABRISI:87:7-9/r8 TABATABAI:87:1-19#2/r6 | against: TABATABAI:87:1-19#2/r7
- 87:6/q01/p3 Reading is joining letters and words in measured recitation.
  reasons: Al-Mufradāt, cited by Ṭabāṭabāʾī, excludes mere gathering or uttering a single letter.
  holders (1 notes): TABATABAI(المفردات)
  notes: TABATABAI:87:1-19#2/r5

# 87:6/q02 How will God make the Prophet recite? [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the divine making occurs through Gabriel’s instruction, inspiration, special memory, or an inward reading.
- 87:6/q02/p1 Gabriel conveys and recites the revelation, and God enables Muhammad to retain and deliver it.
  reasons: Zajjāj says the unlettered Prophet teaches his companions without needing repetition. Wāḥidī reports Abū Isḥāq and Qatāda likewise. Bayḍāwī allows Gabriel’s recital or inspired reading. Bursevī includes revelation now and later. Ālūsī says divine attribution is mediated by Gabriel. Elmalılı names Gabriel the Trustworthy and Holy Spirit and cites 42:52; he specifies reciting from memory, not reading a text.
  holders (17 notes): ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق، قتادة); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: ZAJJAJ:v5p315#2/r1 WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p438/r2 WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p438/r3 KASHSHAF:87:6/r1 KASHSHAF-FULL:v4p740#2/r1 TABRISI:87:7/r20 BAYDAWI:87:6/r1 BAYDAWI-FULL:87:4-7/r3 QURTUBI:87:6/r2 QURTUBI-FULL:87:6-7/r2 BURSEVI:v10p314#2/r8 ALUSI:87:6/r4 ALUSI-FULL:v30p105/r2 ELMALILI:87:6/r2 ELMALILI:87:6/r7 BIQAI:87:6/r3 BIQAI-FULL:v8p396/r9
- 87:6/q02/p2 God inspires the Prophet to become a reader.
  reasons: Rāzī relays Wāḥidī’s explanation. Bayḍāwī presents it alongside Gabriel’s recitation; Elmalılı says revelation makes him possess the Qur’an and recitation. Ālūsī reports Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq’s possibility that he could read writing without instruction though he did not write.

- 87:6/q02/p3 Gabriel repeats the revelation until the Prophet memorizes it.
  reasons: Rāzī distinguishes repeated instruction from memorization after one hearing; Kashshāf connects repetition with his earlier haste and possible lapse.

# 87:6/q03 What does the future prefix in “We shall make you recite” convey? [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether sīn chiefly strengthens the pledge or points to its ongoing fulfillment.
- 87:6/q03/p1 It intensifies or confirms the divine promise.
  reasons: Bursevī and Ālūsī report the emphatic sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a future marker confirms the coming act.
  holders (5 notes): BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: BURSEVI:v10p314#2/r6 ALUSI:87:6/r3 ALUSI-FULL:v30p105/r2 IBNASHUR:87:6/r5 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p280/r2
- 87:6/q03/p2 It promises continuing and renewed recitation, including revelation yet to come.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reasons that the act had begun and the Qur’an would keep growing; Biqāʿī speaks of repeated instruction. Corpus Coranicum calls it a promise of further revelation.

# 87:6/q04 Why is God the first-person speaker who “makes recite”? [سَنُقْرِئُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question concerns the change of voice or the mediated attribution of recitation.
- 87:6/q04/p1 The shift to the majestic first-person plural suits direct divine good news and heightens its authority.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum calls the shift from third person singular iltifāt; Abdel Haleem says it dramatizes divine authority without implying redaction. Ibn ʿĀshūr says direct address fits the giver of glad tidings.
  holders (5 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM(Abdel Haleem)
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:6/r6 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p280/r3 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1-8:kommentar/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1-8:kommentar/r5 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1-8:kommentar/r7
- 87:6/q04/p2 God is credited with recitation because He brings about and commands the words recited through Gabriel.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this rational attribution; Ālūsī calls it metaphorical attribution through Gabriel.
  holders (4 notes): IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL
