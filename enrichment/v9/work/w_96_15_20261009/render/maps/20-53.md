# 20:53: 29 questions

## 20:53/q01 How is الذي جعل لكم attached grammatically?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the relative continues Moses' description of his Lord or opens a divine resumption, especially in light of the later first person.
- 20:53/q01/p1 It is the predicate of an omitted subject, such as 'He is.' — Al-Zamakhshari lists this; Razi insists on it because the later first person is divine; Ibn Ashur takes the omitted subject to resume the Lord of 20:52. Alusi's Imam calls the divine resumption certain. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:53/q01/p2 It is an adjective of 'my Lord.' — Al-Zamakhshari and others allow this; Razi and Abu Hayyan object that it makes Moses continue speaking across the later first-person verbs; al-Samin identifies the same difficulty. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:53/q01/p3 It is an accusative of praise or an omitted 'I mean.' — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi and others give this parse; al-Samin says it can introduce God's speech; Razi objects if it makes Moses speak onward. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); RAZI-FULL-]

## 20:53/q03 What do mahd and mihād mean and how are they formed?  [مَهْدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the forms distinguish an action from a prepared object, form a singular and plural, or both function as verbal nouns.
- 20:53/q03/p1 Mihād names a spread, bed or carpet; mahd is primarily the act of preparing or spreading. — Al-Tabari compares farsh/firāsh; Abu Ali and al-Farisi call this clearest; al-Nahhas says mahd requires an implied 'possessing a cradle.' Al-Raghib says the prepared child's bed extends to a level place. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض من اختار قراءة ذلك كذلك); NAHHAS(الكوفيون); FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الإمام الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; NAHHAS+]
- 20:53/q03/p2 Mahd is itself a noun for a cradle or bed, and mihād may be its plural. — Al-Farisi allows the faʿl-to-fiʿāl plural; Ibn Atiyya and al-Mahdawi allow singular or plural mihād; Ibn Ashur says the plural can point to many regions; al-Alusi says each place is someone's cradle. [11 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(غيره); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:53/q03/p3 Both forms can function as verbal nouns or near synonyms. — Al-Mufaddal derives both from mahada, preparing a bed; al-Samin records this analysis; Ibn al-Jawzi says both denote a bed or spread. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(المفضل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفضل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:53/q04 In what sense was the earth made a cradle?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ مَهْدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the cradle image denotes present physical habitability or life's preparatory role.
- 20:53/q04/p1 It was spread and prepared as a settled, useful place for human life. — Muqatil, Yahya, Razi and others describe rest, sitting, standing, sleeping, dwelling, farming and travel; 2:22 and 71:19–20 are parallels. Biqai says most land is habitable amid mountains and rough regions; Ibn Ashur notes rare avoidable bumps. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عاصم، حمزة، الكسائي، خلف); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 20:53/q04/p2 Its comparison with a cradle points to human earthly life preparing for higher life. — Tabatabai compares the child raised in a cradle for a later, nobler life. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:53/q05 What does سَلَكَ mean here?  [وَسَلَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the local sense is opening a route or an image based on inserting something into another thing.
- 20:53/q05/p1 God opened, laid out and made routes passable. — Ibn Atiyya and al-Thaʿlabi gloss it as opening a way; Ibn Abbas says the roads were made easy. Al-Nahhas calls the wording figurative because the paths are made available. [7 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 20:53/q05/p2 Its root means to insert or cause to pass into; paths are described as running through earth. — Wahidi cites insertion examples, including 74:42, and says visible routes are treated as inserted; Ibn Ashur compares Quranic instances at 72:17, 74:42, 26:200 and 39:21 and al-Aʿsha's nail image. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:53/q06 What are the سُبُل and what do they enable?  [سُبُلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the ways are physical routes alone, broader resources, or also an image of spiritual travel.
- 20:53/q06/p1 They are roads through the earth for travel and access to needs. — Qatada glosses roads; sources locate them amid mountains, valleys, plains and sand. Biqai notes some land remains impassable; Ibn Ashur includes natural routes and trails worn by people. Ibn Kathir cites 21:31; Qurtubi cites 71:19–20 and 43:10. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:53/q06/p2 The ways include material and intellectual means of livelihood. — Asad takes the earth's ways and means as resources for earning a living on and from it. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:53/q06/p3 The paths also point to humanity's journey toward nearness to God. — Tabatabai compares worldly paths to the path into God's realm of honor. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:53/q09 What is meant by ٱلسَّمَآءِ?  [ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'sky' indicates the direction above, clouds or a heavenly origin.
- 20:53/q09/p1 It can mean the sky's direction or regions. — Al-Nahhas says its regions; al-Alusi offers 'from its direction.' [2 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:53/q09/p2 It can mean the heavenly sphere or the clouds, or heaven itself. — Bursevi says anything above may be called sky, including the sphere and clouds; al-Alusi reports a literal heavenly origin in some reports. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:53/q10 What is the water sent down?  [مَآءًۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the mention stops at falling rain or extends to water sourced from it.
- 20:53/q10/p1 It is rain. — Wahidi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Baydawi and others identify it as rainfall; Bursevi takes the indefinite wording to mean some water, and Biqai points to observable color and taste. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:53/q10/p2 The rainwater also encompasses waters found in springs, rivers and seas. — Tabatabai links rainfall with terrestrial waters in his description of God's provision. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:53/q11 Where does Moses' speech end, and who says فَأَخْرَجْنَا?  [ٱلَّذِى فَأَخْرَجْنَا · referent]
turns on: The point where the quoted dialogue ends and whether the first-person plural is God, cultivators, or a quoted divine voice.
- 20:53/q11/p1 Moses' speech ends at 'my Lord neither errs nor forgets'; God speaks from 'the One who made.' — Razi argues that the coordinated fāʾ prevents beginning divine speech only at 'We brought forth,' while eating, grazing and return to earth suit God; Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur cite the first person. Islahi sees an insertion addressing Quraysh. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:53/q11/p2 Moses' speech ends after 'sent down water'; God says 'We brought forth.' — The Nazm scholar cites the next command to eat and graze; Ibn Atiyya says God addresses Muhammad and creation. Al-Jurjani calls it iltifāt; al-Samin calls it remote and Razi objects to the coordinating fāʾ. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN(الجرجاني); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)-]
- 20:53/q11/p3 Moses continues speaking, and 'we' means people who cultivate plants with the rain. — The Nazm scholar cites 56:63 for attributing cultivation to people; Razi allows irrigation but argues people cannot bring forth varied plant natures. Al-Alusi ranks it second to divine speech. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 20:53/q11/p4 Moses continues, but quotes God's first-person words or yields rhetorically to the divine voice. — Al-Zamakhshari says 'for you' rather than 'for us' supports verbatim quotation; Ibn Atiyya allows quotation at 'We brought forth.' Ibn al-Munir prefers a rhetorical move from Moses' own words to divine narration. Al-Alusi reports denial of iltifāt or unchanged quotation. [7 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)+]

## 20:53/q12 What is the force of the shift to فَأَخْرَجْنَا?  [فَأَخْرَجْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the shift mainly marks divine sovereignty, renews attention, reflects Moses' state, or is identified as a formal turn.
- 20:53/q12/p1 The first-person plural emphasizes God's exclusive power, majesty, wisdom and command over diverse creation. — Al-Zamakhshari points to 6:99, 35:27 and 27:60; Baydawi calls it a sign of power and wisdom; Alusi likens it to a great ruler speaking of a great act. Ibn Ashur says nature obeys its creator. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب ( الكشاف )); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 20:53/q12/p2 The shift refreshes attention and makes the scene vivid. — Al-Samarrai says changes of person and number renew attention; al-Burhan also reports that striking acts are presented vividly. [3 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري); SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 20:53/q12/p3 Moses' absorption in divine majesty effaces him and makes God the true speaker. — Biqai describes Moses' self-effacement at the moment of the shift. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:53/q12/p4 It is a stylistic iltifāt from third-person God to first-person God. — Al-Nasafi, Elmalili and Ibn Ashur identify the formal turn; Tabatabai cites three parallel passages at 35:27, 27:60 and 6:99. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:53/q13 What do the fāʾ and 'by it' express?  [فَأَخْرَجْنَا بِهِۦٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fāʾ marks temporal succession or a further causal/willing relation alongside the bāʾ.
- 20:53/q13/p1 The fāʾ marks succession and the bāʾ links plant growth causally to water. — Abu Hayyan identifies the water as the pronoun's antecedent; Alusi prefers succession in the fāʾ because the bāʾ already conveys causation; Tabatabai says the preposition affirms cosmic cause and effect. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:53/q13/p2 The fāʾ makes divine willing follow rain, while the bāʾ links growth to water. — Al-Khafaji gives this analysis as reported by Alusi; Alusi finds simple succession more eloquent. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:53/q13/p3 Sending down and bringing forth may stand for God's willing those acts. — Alusi reports this to avoid ascribing physical work to God; he also says the perfect verb confirms the act. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:53/q14 Does water itself have a causal role in plant growth?  [بِهِۦٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether the preposition gives water divinely granted causal efficacy or only customary accompaniment.
- 20:53/q14/p1 God grants water properties by which growth follows, while God remains the true effective cause. — Razi says this does not violate Islamic principles; Alusi reports Maturidis and earlier authorities that properties work only with God's permission; Tabatabai takes 'by it' as affirming causal links. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:53/q14/p2 Water has no inherent causal effect; God creates growth when water is present. — Razi reports earlier theologians' denial of water's effect; Alusi describes the Ashʿari reading of 'by it' as 'at it,' comparing water's quenching and fire's burning, while calling the charge against causal powers extreme. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون المتقدمون); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:53/q15 What does أَزْوَٰجًۭ mean here?  [أَزْوَٰجًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'pairs' denotes associated species or actual sexual pairs.
- 20:53/q15/p1 It means kinds or varieties of vegetation. — Ibn Abbas and Mujahid give varieties; paired means associated or mutually resembling kinds, such as palms and grapes. Farra and al-Aʿsha's brocade verse support a sense of color; Asad cites 13:3. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الأعشى,الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(القوم); ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:53/q15/p2 It may mean actual male and female plant pairs. — Tabatabai proposes this alternative as a reality drawn to attention by the Qur'an; Bursevi includes sexual and other paired relations within the word's broad sense. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI]

## 20:53/q16 What range does نَبَاتٍ have?  [نَبَاتٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the note concerns the semantic extent of vegetation or the collective morphology of the noun.
- 20:53/q16/p1 It denotes vegetation broadly, including trees and stemless plants; ordinary usage may narrow it to fodder. — Al-Raghib defines every growing, feeding body as vegetation; Bursevi says the word can cover all growing things, including animals and humans in its widest sense. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإمام الراغب)]
- 20:53/q16/p2 It is a verbal or collective noun used for plants in singular and plural. — Razi and Nasafi say its form serves singular and plural; Ibn al-Anbari calls it collective without an ordinary singular, and Abu al-Baqa permits agreement with plural shattā. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 20:53/q17 What does شَتَّىٰ mean?  [شَتَّىٰ · meaning]
- 20:53/q17/p1 It means diverse, separated or scattered. — Wahidi and Qurtubi derive it from shatta and list related forms; Ruʾba's camel verse illustrates scattering. Samīn and Ibn Ashur call it plural of shatīt; Bint al-Shati distinguishes shattā's difference from ashtāt's dispersion, citing 59:14, 99:6 and 24:61. Abu Hayyan specifies that shattā is a feminine plural of shatīt, analogous to marḍā from marīḍ. ## 20:53/q18 What does شَتَّىٰ modify, and how is it parsed?  [أَزْوَٰجًۭ نَبَاتٍۢ شَتَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether shattā describes the kinds, collective vegetation, or functions adverbially as a circumstantial accusative.
- 20:53/q18/p1 It is an adjective describing azwāj, the diverse kinds. — Akhfash allows the reading; Abu Hayyan and Samīn prefer it, the latter for direct noun-adjective relation. Tabrisi makes min nabāt and shattā successive adjectives; Ibn al-Anbari allows 'varied varieties of plants.' [13 notes: AKHFASH; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الأخفش); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:53/q18/p2 It describes nabāt, the vegetation collectively. — Akhfash accepts this; al-Zamakhshari and Abu al-Baqa use nabāt as a verbal noun or collective whose form permits the plural description. Bursevi prefers this for the verse ending's rhyme. [13 notes: AKHFASH; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI+]
- 20:53/q18/p3 It is a circumstantial accusative linked to azwāj or to the preceding adjective's subject pronoun. — Samīn says the indefinite azwāj is specified by min nabāt, licensing the first ḥāl; he gives a third parse attaching it to the raised subject pronoun. Tabrisi also allows an accusative verbal noun. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI]

## 20:53/q20 What proof or teaching do earth, paths, rain and plants provide?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the signs are read chiefly as proof of monotheistic lordship or as instances of universal guidance.
- 20:53/q20/p1 They display God's exclusive lordship, unity, wise provision and power over creation. — Ibn Atiyya says these concrete acts could not be claimed by Pharaoh; Razi calls them specific proofs after a general one. Biqai argues varied ordered terrain cannot arise from a uniform natural cause, and links complete provision to God's lack of error or forgetfulness. Islahi addresses Quraysh's worship and judgment. - 20:53/q20/p2 The provision and earth's pathways guide creatures to sustain life and humans toward God. — Tabatabai links bodily food and grazing with universal guidance and lordship, while human earthly life prepares for the heavenly destination. [4 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:53/q21 How does this verse connect to its narrative and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the sentences continue Moses' proof or interrupt the story as a direct divine address.
- 20:53/q21/p1 It resumes or extends the Moses–Pharaoh exchange with concrete proofs of the Lord. — Razi says Pharaoh diverted Moses with old generations, and Moses resumed proof; Ibn Kathir says the earth and water description completes Moses' answer after an intervening clause. Al-Tabari recalls grace in rain after the answer and cites 2:22. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:53/q21/p2 It is a Quranic insertion that renews attention and addresses the present audience. — Ibn Ashur describes three sentences interrupting the story; Islahi says four verses directly address Quraysh; al-Jurjani explains that an unjoined 'he said' can answer a hearer's implicit next question, comparing 51:24–28 and 26:23–29. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:53/q21/p3 Its signs continue the preceding discussion of God's universal guidance and recur in other creation and rain passages. — Tabatabai links the earlier exchange to guidance; Study Quran compares 43:10–11, 6:99 and 13:4. Ibn Ashur says earth leads to vegetation and indirectly recalls heaven, citing 80:25–26; Burhan contrasts 'salaka' here with 'jaʿala' at 43:10. [4 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 20:53/q22 What inward sense is given to the earth, paths, water and plants?  [* · inward]
- 20:53/q22/p1 They signify the bodily faculties, their organs, spiritual perception and their varied states. — Qashani treats earth as bodily realm, paths as eye, ear, nose and other organs, heaven as spirit, rain as perception and aid, and plants as perceptions, actions, properties, states and dispositions. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:53/q23 What attribution is given for the poem challenging Pharaoh with creation signs?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported poet's identity.
- 20:53/q23/p1 Ibn Ishaq attributes it to Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl. — Biqai reports Ibn Kathir's citation; the poem challenges Pharaoh to claim he made earth without supports, raised sky without pillars, made light and sun, and grew crops. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن إسحاق)]
- 20:53/q23/p2 Ibn Hisham attributes it to Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt. — Biqai notes the competing attribution of the cited poem. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن هشام)]

## 20:53/q24 What background is reported about Abu Amr, a reader of mihādan?  [مَهْدًۭا · reports]
turns on: The varying biographical transmissions.
- 20:53/q24/p1 His personal name and lineage are reported in several forms. — Ibn Mujahid transmits Ibn Ammar ibn al-Aryan, Zabban, al-Aryan, al-Aryan ibn al-Ala, and the claim he had only a kunya. Lineage reaches Mazin with clientage among Banu al-Anbar; his brother Abu Sufyan also lacked another name. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو زيد,أبو عمرو,أبو عمرو بن العلاء,الفضل بن الحسن,اليزيدي,عبد الوارث)]
- 20:53/q24/p2 He was portrayed as an early, leading transmitted reader and Arabist. — Abu Amr says he led while al-Hasan al-Basri lived, with a report placing the latter's death at 110 AH. Saʿid ibn Jubayr urged him to sit with elders; Ibn Mujahid describes modesty and Hijazi reading practice. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,سعيد بن جبير)]

## 20:53/q25 What methodological caution about explaining the Qur'an accompanies these notes?  [* · reports]
- 20:53/q25/p1 Explanation by unsupported personal opinion is blameworthy even if it happens to reach the right answer. — Ibn Kathir cites Jundub's prophetic warning, with Tirmidhi calling the report gharīb and criticism of Suhayl; a version from Tirmidhi, Abu Dawud and Nasa'i says an accidental right answer is still error. He compares 24:13 and cites Abu Bakr's caution. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق,الترمذي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 20:53/q26 What codex and writing background accompanies the reading notes?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported history and differing scholarly rules for codex presentation.
- 20:53/q26/p1 Accounts differ on the spread of writing into Mecca and Arabia. — Hisham al-Kalbi traces it from al-Anbar through Bishr, Harb and Sufyan, with Umar and Muawiya learning from them; another account begins with Tayy people from Baqqa, while al-Shaʿbi traces Muhajirun through Hira to al-Anbar. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشعبي,هشام بن محمد بن السائب الكلبي)]
- 20:53/q26/p2 Early script and codex spelling developed through scribal practice. — Ibn Kathir calls al-maktufa the early script, credits Ibn Muqla and Ibn al-Bawwab with refinement, and says early codex word placement differences were orthographic rather than semantic; Abu Ubayd and Ibn Abi Dawud catalogued orthography. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:53/q26/p3 Authorities differed on permissible codex spelling and notation. — Malik required the authoritative codex's orthography while others allowed spelling variation; scholars differed over vowel marks and dots. Ibn Kathir preferred early practice over later surah labels, verse marks and divisions. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 20:53/q27 Does Qur'anic end sound make this passage sajʿ?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether resemblance of verse endings to rhyme is enough to classify the composition as sajʿ.
- 20:53/q27/p1 No; similar end sounds do not make the Qur'an ordinary rhymed prose. — Baqillani says uneven clauses would be ineloquent sajʿ and imitation by balanced sajʿ would then be possible; he rejects the implication associated with al-Nazzam, Abbad and Hisham that inimitability rests on diverted opposition. Bint al-Shati repeats the objection. [8 notes: BAQILLANI(النظام وعباد بن سليمان وهشام الفوطي); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الباقلاني)]
- 20:53/q27/p2 The view allowing Qur'anic sajʿ is associated with non-inimitability by composition and diverted opposition. — Baqillani and Bint al-Shati report this implication of al-Nazzam, Abbad ibn Sulayman and Hisham al-Fuwati/al-Quti, and argue against it; the name's spelling varies in the notes. [0 notes: BAQILLANI(النظام وعباد بن سليمان وهشام الفوطي)-; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]

## 20:53/q28 What unrelated lexical observation accompanies the shattā discussion?  [* · meaning]
- 20:53/q28/p1 Al-ins and al-insan share a broad contrast with wildness but differ in usage. — Bint al-Shati says al-ins appears against jinn, whose hiddenness marks unfamiliar beings outside familiar human life. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 20:53/q29 How is the cradle comparison construed?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ مَهْدًۭا · grammar]
- 20:53/q29/p1 The earth is likened directly to a cradle, or a possessive expression is omitted. — Al-Samin calls the comparison emphatic and also allows an implied 'possessor of a cradle'; al-Alusi allows a direct predicate or a circumstantial expression with an implied same-root verb. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
