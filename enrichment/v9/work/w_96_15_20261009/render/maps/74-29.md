# 74:29: 6 questions

## 74:29/q01 What does لَوَّاحَةٌۭ describe Saqar as doing or showing?  [لَوَّاحَةٌۭ لِّلْبَشَرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the root describes scorching alteration, visible appearance, or thirst; whether the effect is physical or allegorical; and whether 'it leaves nothing' in 74:28 permits skin merely to blacken.
- 74:29/q01/p1 Saqar scorches, burns, alters, and darkens human skin or appearance. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn Zayd and many later commentators describe burning or changed complexions. Abū Razīn and others say its blast leaves skin darker than night; al-Māwardī's ʿAṭiyya says it burns until bone shows. Ibn Zayd cites exposure to sky; Ibn Qutayba, al-Layth, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī and others compare sun, heat, cold, illness, thirst or grief changing appearance. Abū ʿUbayda, al-Aʿshā and other poetic lines attest the verb; al-Kashshāf cites a scorching poem, and al-Itqān identifies Azd Shanūʾa usage. Al-Jishumī and Elmalılı stress the intensive form; al-Biqāʿī adds blueness, flashing, convulsion and thirst. Al-Wāḥidī says 'burning' names the effect while the fuller sense is piercing alteration to blackness. Al-Islāḥī says the flames reach criminals from afar and cites 70:16. Al-Qāshānī relates the outward blackening to sins and dispositions. Al-Ṭabarī suspects an Ibn ʿAbbās report reading 'exposed' is a copyist's error for 'changed'. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives lawwāḥa from lawḥ, alteration of a person's substance through pain; he cites an unidentified poet's line in which scorching winds change a traveler. He also says the Fire turns human skin black. [77 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(أبو رزين,ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو رزين,عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو رزين,أبو عبيدة,الكلبي,الليث,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وزيد بن أسلم,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد بن أسلم,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، قتادة، أبو رزين، جمهور الناس); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الفراء,الليث); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو رزين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(جمهور الناس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو رزين,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو رزين,ابن عباس,مجاهد); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وأبو رزين والجمهور); ELMALILI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Most commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg, Ṭ); IBNASHUR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(القائلون بهذا الوجه)-; ASAD-NOTES-]
- 74:29/q01/p2 Saqar shines or becomes visible to people, even from five hundred years away. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, Ibn Kaysān and others give the five-hundred-year distance; Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes visibility to its vast size, terror and roaring. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī compare a lightning-like gleam. Al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī cite 26:91; al-Kashshāf cites 102:7; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī cite 79:36; Abū Ḥayyān cites 102:6 and 79:36. Kuranyolu also reports a tableau or mirror revealing Hell to one who deserves it. Study Quran says Saqar is made apparent to humanity. Asad prefers the root's sense of appearing or shining, though he applies it to recognition of truth. [23 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن، ابن كيسان,عطاء عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الحسن، والأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن وابن كيسان); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن، ابن كيسان); RAZI-FULL(الحسن والأصك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وابن كيسان); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وابن كيسان والأصم); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî); STUDYQURAN(Bg, Q); ASAD-NOTES+]
- 74:29/q01/p3 Saqar makes its inmates intensely thirsty. — Al-Akhfash derives lawwāḥa from intense thirst; al-Qurṭubī cites poetry for this usage. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn record the lexical sense, the latter citing 'saqatnī ʿalā lawḥ min al-māʾ'. Al-Biqāʿī includes thirst among the fire's effects; al-Layth counts thirst among causes of altered appearance. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 74:29/q01/p4 Saqar exposes or makes visible the sufferers' bodies or skin. — Zayd ibn Aslam says their bodies show upon it; Ibn Kathīr describes them as gleaming or visibly showing. Al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid saying bodily skin is exposed to the fire. A transmitted Ibn ʿAbbās wording also says 'exposed', but al-Ṭabarī suspects corruption and proposes 'changed'. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,زيد بن أسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); TAB-FULL-]
- 74:29/q01/p5 Saqar's appearance makes the viewer's complexion change. — Al-Jishumī reports this as a view: the person's color changes on seeing Saqar. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 74:29/q01/p6 The change in complexion is to redness. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this alternative to blackening. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 74:29/q01/p7 The word can mean turning away or being exposed. — Al-Itqān records these glosses without further explanation; al-Samīn also records lawḥ with ḍamma as the air between heaven and earth. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 74:29/q01/p8 Saqar is insatiably thirsty, scorching faces while watching for and attacking people. — Elmalılı draws these possible images from the word's range of thirst, solar heat, scorching, appearing, flashing and seeing; he suggests an unquenchable thirst that burns humans. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 74:29/q01/p9 The verse says hell makes truth visible to the sinner, bringing late recognition of past wrongs and responsibility for suffering. — Asad adopts the sense 'make visible' and reads descriptions of the hereafter allegorically in light of the following verses; his rendering says hell makes truth visible to mortal man. He compares the sinner's state of neither life nor death with 87:12–13. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES+]

## 74:29/q02 How is لَوَّاحَةٌۭ read and construed?  [لَوَّاحَةٌۭ · readings]
turns on: The case ending and what earlier word or unspoken subject governs the description; the accusative also permits circumstance or emphatic specification.
- 74:29/q02/p1 It is read accusative, as a description of Saqar, a circumstantial expression, or emphatic specification. — Al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others name ʿAṭiyya al-ʿAwfī; Ibn al-Jawzī adds Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn al-Sumayfiʿ and Ibn Abī ʿAbla; al-Qurṭubī names Naṣr ibn ʿĀṣim and ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī name Zayd ibn ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan and Ibn Abī ʿAbla. Al-Jishumī relates the adjective to Saqar under sa-uṣlīhi; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī relate the circumstance to Saqar or the pronoun under lā tubqī/lā tadhar. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī call it specification intensifying the threat; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn explain emphatic circumstance because a consuming fire necessarily alters skin. Al-Samīn says this reading supports treating lā tubqī as circumstantial. [16 notes: FARRA; JISHUMI(عطية العوفي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عطية العوفي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود، ابن السميفع، ابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,العوفي); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وابن أبي عبلة وزيد بن علي وعطية العوفي,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:29/q02/p2 The common nominative reads it as Saqar's adjective or as a predicate in a new clause with an omitted subject. — Al-Farrāʾ supplies an implicit repeated Saqar and compares 85:15–16 and a reading of 11:72. Al-Ṭabarī permits an indefinite description as praise. Al-Naḥḥās, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī supply 'it is'; al-Samīn says the nominative supports an independent lā tubqī. Al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī relate the adjective to Saqar in 'what is Saqar?'; Abū Ḥayyān treats the majority reading as a fresh clause about scorching fire. [10 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:29/q02/p3 The nominative lawwāḥa is a third predicate of Saqar. — Ibn ʿĀshūr construes it as Saqar's third predicate. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 74:29/q03 What is the function of the lām in لِّلْبَشَرِ?  [لِّلْبَشَرِ · grammar]
- 74:29/q03/p1 The lām is an added particle that strengthens the expression. — Al-Samīn compares the Yūsuf phrase in 12:43. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 74:29/q04 Who or what is الْبَشَرِ?  [لِّلْبَشَرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether bashar is the skin noun bashara in collective or plural use, or the human beings who are scorched or see Saqar.
- 74:29/q04/p1 It means human skin or its visible outer surface. — Mujāhid, Qatāda and many commentators take it as skin or complexion. Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī call bashar a plural of bashara; al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī also report the plural abshār. Al-Jishumī says humans are called bashar because their skin is uncovered by fur; Elmalılı similarly connects the name with visible skin. Al-Kashshāf specifies upper skin layers, and Ibn al-Jawzī calls it the visible outer layer. Ibn ʿĀshūr says bashar may mean human skin, whose color the Fire turns black. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد، قتادة، أبو رزين، جمهور الناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد، الفراء، الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:29/q04/p2 It means people or humanity, especially the human inhabitants of Hell. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the alternative to al-Akhfash and Ibn Qutayba; al-Qurṭubī attributes it to al-Akhfash and most exegetes. Al-Qurṭubī says Ibn ʿAbbās's distant-visibility gloss requires people rather than skins. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Samīn, al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı allow this sense; al-Ālūsī says it is possible even with skin darkening. Al-Jishumī explains the general name for humans from their exposed skin. Ibn ʿĀshūr says bashar may collectively denote people without a singular of the same form. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش، ابن قتيبة); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش,مجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:29/q05 How does this description relate to Saqar's leaving nothing in 74:28 and to nearby verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 'leaves nothing' excludes a lasting darkened skin, or allows stages, different subjects, or a further feature of punishment.
- 74:29/q05/p1 Blackening can precede destruction, affect those near the fire, or name one of its disfiguring effects. — Al-Ālūsī answers the apparent tension by making blackening precede burning and destruction, or distinguishing inmates from those nearby. He also allows the qualities without deliberate escalation: initial blackening adds hideous disfigurement to pain. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say the accusative description is emphatic because all-consuming fire necessarily alters skin. Al-Samīn says the nominative permits an independent 74:28, the accusative a circumstantial link. Ibn ʿĀshūr says sentence placement accommodates these readings and leaves the later pronoun unambiguously referring to Saqar. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(العوفي); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:29/q05/p2 If Saqar leaves nothing, blackening skin is inadequate; visibility from afar better fits the sequence. — Al-Rāzī reports the objection of the visibility proponents to the scorching account, citing 74:28. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بهذا الوجه)]

## 74:29/q06 What wider lesson is drawn from this depiction of Hell?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw different moral or inward implications rather than directly disputing one another.
- 74:29/q06/p1 The depiction warns that mockers' certainty is misplaced; seekers benefit from divine wisdom while the arrogant ruin their fate. — Al-Islāḥī says disbelievers mock Hell's description because they presume to know everything; its description rests on divine wisdom. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:29/q06/p2 The sufferer's outward blackening corresponds to sins and evil dispositions. — Al-Qāshānī compares physical fire changing colors and forms with the fire's effect on outward bodies according to inward deeds. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 74:29/q06/p3 Hell brings the sinner belated insight into truth, past wrongdoing, and responsibility for suffering. — Asad treats the hereafter imagery allegorically and relates the condition of neither life nor death to 87:12–13. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
