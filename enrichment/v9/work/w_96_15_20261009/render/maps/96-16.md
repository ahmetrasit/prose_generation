# 96:16: 17 questions

## 96:16/q01 What does nāṣiya denote?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · meaning]
- 96:16/q01/p1 The hair at the front of the head, or by extension the forehead, head, face, or person. — Māwardī says the front hair may stand for the whole person, as in a 'blessed forelock'; Ṭabrisī derives the name from attachment or joining and cites an anonymous rajaz poet's tanāsiha; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also identifies front hair; Ibn al-Shaykh allows its growing place and says it stands here for face and head, noting Abū Jahl's careful grooming; Elmalılı extends it from head to person; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ adds anything foremost or the leading people of a community; Bursevī also defines it as front hair. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الراجز); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]

## 96:16/q02 Whose forelock is meant, and whom can the threat include?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the historical target exhausts the warning or its indefinite wording extends it.
- 96:16/q02/p1 It is Abū Jahl's forelock and, by metonymy, Abū Jahl himself. — Muqātil links the threatened forelock to Abū Jahl, whose denial is warned of in the preceding passage; Māwardī, Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr identify him, and Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the definite forelock to the man who forbids prayer in 96:9–10. Ibn Qutayba explains that seizure of the part means humiliation of its owner, citing 55:41. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:16/q02/p2 The wording also reaches the class of offenders who obstruct worship, beyond Abū Jahl. — Qurṭubī says the initial threat concerns Abū Jahl but warns everyone who refuses or prevents obedience; Elmalılı says indefinite apposition generalizes it; Kur'an Yolu applies the eventual capture to self-satisfied tyrants and says it may occur here, hereafter, or both, citing Abū Jahl and others. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 96:16/q03 How does the second nāṣiya relate grammatically to the first?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Which prior expression is supplied as the appositive's antecedent.
- 96:16/q03/p1 It is a genitive appositive or substitute for the preceding forelock. — Sībawayh cites the verse repeatedly for apposition, including his broader examples of noun and appositive and of badal among raised constructions; Ṭabarī says the genitive repeats the previous noun. Al-Farrāʾ calls repetition emphatic and compares 'a straight path, the path of God'; Naḥḥās reports this term. Abū ʿUbayda, al-Kalbī's later exegetical tradition, and many commentators call it badal. Burhān says such substitution names an antecedent again with specification, making its quality the reason for seizure, and cites 26:47; Samarrāʾī says badal can blame, as here, or praise, as in 'this secure city.' Biqāʿī says the form approaches definiteness; al-Islāḥī says the adjective makes it count as definite. [37 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 96:16/q03/p2 It is a substitute for fajrahā. — Abū ʿUbayda's Majāz names fajrahā as the term replaced. [1 notes: MAJAZ]

## 96:16/q04 What licenses an indefinite appositive after a definite noun here?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether informative qualification and repeated wording are grammatical requirements or only features of this example.
- 96:16/q04/p1 Its qualifying adjectives make the indefinite informative enough; the Kufan formulation requires matching wording and a modifier. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn Yaʿīsh and others make the added description the source of useful independent meaning. Al-Samīn calls this the Kufan rule; al-Ālūsī says Kufans require both matching wording and a descriptive modifier, whereas Basrans require informativeness. Al-Islāḥī says the adjective lends definiteness. [13 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(ابن يعيش); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 96:16/q04/p2 Basran grammar does not require either a descriptive adjective or repetition of the antecedent's wording. — Abū Ḥayyān objects to al-Zamakhsharī's requirement on both points; al-Ālūsī states the Basran condition as informativeness alone, while al-Samīn contrasts the Kufan formulation. [5 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN+]

## 96:16/q05 Why is the repeated nāṣiya indefinite?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indefiniteness chiefly reveals this forelock's qualities or presents a general class.
- 96:16/q05/p1 It introduces newly disclosed qualities of a known forelock. — Al-Rāzī says its physical identity is familiar but its false speech and sinful deeds are newly exposed; al-Farrāʾ calls the repetition emphasis, and Burhān says naming it twice specifies why it is seized. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; FARRA; BURHAN]
- 96:16/q05/p2 It identifies a class of forelocks and thereby generalizes the threat. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a generic kind, a lying and erring forelock; Elmalılı says the indefinite apposition broadens the warning. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 96:16/q06 How are the three words read by case, and how are those readings construed?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted case ending and whether the phrase apposes the earlier noun or begins an implied clause or invective.
- 96:16/q06/p1 All three are genitive, with nāṣiya apposed to the preceding genitive noun. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the majority reading; the second noun repeats the earlier forelock. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TAB; TAB-FULL]
- 96:16/q06/p2 All three are accusative, as an insult or the object of an implied verb. — Al-Farrāʾ supplies the implied verb; Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Abū Ḥaywa. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī also name Ibn Abī ʿAbla and Zayd ibn ʿAlī. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī take the accusative as invective or blame. [14 notes: FARRA; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(أبو حيوة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حيوة والكسائي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قرئ); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(أبو حيوة وابن أبي عبلة وزيد بن علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة وابن أبي عبلة وزيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(أبو حيوة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة وابن عبلة وزيد بن علي)]
- 96:16/q06/p3 All three are nominative, as an independent phrase with implied 'it is,' or as an insult. — Reports ascribe this to al-Kisāʾī; al-Samīn specifies omitted hiya, while al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī allow an independent insult. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حيوة والكسائي); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قرئ); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(أبو حيوة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة وابن عبلة وزيد بن علي)]

## 96:16/q07 What variant wording replaces khāṭiʾa?  [خَاطِئَةٍۢ · readings]
- 96:16/q07/p1 Ibn Masʿūd's codex has fājira in place of khāṭiʾa. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the variant within the wording la-asfaʿanna bi-l-nāṣiya, nāṣiya kādhiba fājira; Corpus Coranicum separately reports Ibn Masʿūd's transmission. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن مسعود); CORPUSCORANICUM(Ibn Masʿūd)]

## 96:16/q08 How is the hamza in khāṭiʾah treated in recitation?  [خَاطِئَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Whether a transmitted route applies the regular hamza substitution or preserves it.
- 96:16/q08/p1 Abū Jaʿfar's ten-reader form replaces the hamza after kasrah with yāʾ, without changing the meaning. — Al-Nashr describes this as Abū Jaʿfar's common treatment of open hamza after kasrah. Qiraat-Er calls it a recognized dialectal treatment and semantically identical. Al-Nashr compares broader substitutions: Abū Jaʿfar and Warsh generally use wāw after ḍammah before open hamza; most Ibn Wardān routes do so in yuʾayyid; al-Azraq substitutes in muʾadhdhin; Ḥafṣ replaces final root hamza in huzuwan at ten places and in kufuwan. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو جعفر,ابن وردان,حفص,ورش); QIRAAT-ER]
- 96:16/q08/p2 Some Ibn Wardān and Zayd routes retain the hamza in khāṭiʾah. — Al-Nashr lists parallel preservation in al-khāṭiʾah, miʾah and fiʾah. It also notes exceptional retention in yuʾayyid by some Ibn Wardān routes, apparently to avoid three successive weak letters after doubled yāʾ, and al-Aṣbahānī's preservation for Warsh in muʾadhdhin, apparently linked to fa-adhdhin. The author accepts and has read both hamza and substitution in the separate word mawṭiyan. [4 notes: NASHR(أبو جعفر,ابن وردان,ورش)]

## 96:16/q09 What makes the forelock 'lying'?  [كَٰذِبَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Which of Abū Jahl's false claims the adjective singles out.
- 96:16/q09/p1 Its owner lies in speech, especially in denying Muḥammad's mission and disparaging him. — Māwardī and others distinguish false speech from wrongful acts. Al-Rāzī says Abū Jahl denied God's sending Muḥammad and called him a sorcerer, liar, or no prophet; Bursevī repeats denial and the charge of sorcery. Al-Kalbī adds lying about God and polytheism; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ links the term to Qur'anic disbelief and misguidance. [11 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; BINTSHATI]
- 96:16/q09/p2 It refers particularly to Abū Jahl's boast that he had the valley's largest gathering. — Al-Rāzī reports this as another explanation; Ibn ʿAbbās's report through al-Wāḥidī also recounts the boast before the threat passage was revealed. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 96:16/q10 What does khāṭiʾa mean here?  [خَاطِئَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khāṭiʾa entails culpable intention or can name inadvertent wrongdoing here.
- 96:16/q10/p1 It means a culpable sinner who deliberately rebels against God, unlike an unaccountable mistaken person. — Al-Rāzī contrasts khāṭiʾ with mukhṭiʾ and cites 69:37 for the wrongdoer; Qurṭubī likewise contrasts punishable deliberate wrongdoing with an unintended mistake. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the active participle from khaṭiʾa, 'to commit a sin.' Al-Kalbī speaks of polytheism; Bursevī cites harming Muḥammad; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ hears disbelief and misguidance in Qur'anic usage. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BURSEVI; BINTSHATI]
- 96:16/q10/p2 It can include unintentional wrongdoing alongside deliberate lies, reflecting Abū Jahl's corrupt disposition. — Biqāʿī assigns deliberateness to his lying but says his other errors may be without intent, because most of his conduct is unsound from his bad nature. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 96:16/q11 Why describe the forelock itself as lying and sinful?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ كَٰذِبَةٍ خَاطِئَةٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the transferred qualities exhaust the wording's sense or a literal description remains possible.
- 96:16/q11/p1 The traits belong to its owner and are figuratively assigned to his forelock for a more vivid, forceful threat. — Al-Zajjāj and others compare 'his day fasts and night prays'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares a thieving hand; al-Rāzī and Qurṭubī cite faces gazing in 75:23; al-Ālūsī cites 16:62 and the idiom of a face displaying beauty. Burhān distinguishes error as a whole-person quality from falsehood as the tongue's. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī say the transfer intensifies the charge, as though every part sins; Ibn ʿĀshūr pictures the sins visible on the forehead, making it fit for seizure. Abū Ḥayyān says the existing topic in verse 15 makes this figure apt; al-Samīn also allows the article to stand for annexation or conceal 'his.' Al-Biqāʿī adds that the enemy cannot harm the guided person except by God's permission and such harm can elevate the victim. Māwardī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr specify lying in speech and error in action. [48 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 96:16/q11/p2 The wording may also allow the forelock itself to be described literally. — Elmalılı acknowledges a literal forelock while explaining that the phrase clarifies the intended person. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 96:16/q12 What does the threatened seizure of this forelock entail?  [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Which consequence or event the image of seizing the forelock evokes.
- 96:16/q12/p1 It signifies forceful capture, humiliation, and dragging the offender to punishment, especially Hell. — Muqātil says angels drag Abū Jahl face-first into the Fire for sin; Ibn Qutayba says seizure raises and humiliates its owner and cites 55:41; Thaʿlabī specifies the front of the head. Bursevī says Arabs regarded forelock seizure as degrading subjugation and quotes ʿAyn al-Maʿānī on coercion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says grasping and pulling convey dragging to punishment and the prefixed bāʾ stresses contact. Quṭb says the raised, forward, conspicuous forehead of an arrogant tyrant deserves to be cast down. Biqāʿī says humiliating capture already implies death, making explicit face-first dragging to Hell unnecessary; al-Islāḥī says the forehead is a site of honor, especially the prostration mark, so its refusal merits Hell; Kur'an Yolu reports explanations of transport to Hell or disgrace. [13 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI; BIQAI; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 96:16/q12/p2 It can signify Abū Jahl's face-first seizure at Badr. — Bursevī relates the possibility to his resuming the obstruction of prayer and Muslims then being enabled to seize him; Kur'an Yolu allows worldly punishment and recalls Abū Jahl's defeat. [2 notes: BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 96:16/q12/p3 It can suggest branding or stamping his forehead. — Kur'an Yolu lists stamping among transmitted explanations; Bursevī connects Ibn Masʿūd's later strike at Badr with the branding threat. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); BURSEVI]

## 96:16/q13 How does the warning connect to the preceding verses?  [* · links]
- 96:16/q13/p1 It answers the prayer blocker with an oath-backed conditional threat of divine seizure if he does not desist. — Muqātil links Abū Jahl's denial and turning away to the threatened seizure, and the earlier promise of return to God to Resurrection. Ṭabarī says the preceding 'No' denies Abū Jahl's claim as he prevents Muḥammad's prayer. Bursevī expands desisting to repentance and submission before death. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the lām begins the oath, 'we will seize' answers it, the conditional answer is omitted, and the second kallā answers the first after suspense. Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain that the light emphatic nūn of the oath is written with alif for its pause pronunciation. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:16/q14 What was reported to occasion the threat passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which response by Abū Jahl the report highlights after he forbade prayer.
- 96:16/q14/p1 Abū Jahl forbade the Prophet's prayer and was rebuked, whereupon he boasted of the valley's largest council. — Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Wāḥidī says the rebuke and boast occasioned the threat; Ṭabrisī relates the confrontation, boast, rebuke and revelation of the passage about calling his gathering. Al-Suyūṭī's Ibn ʿAbbās report says the prohibition of prayer prompted the sequence through the lying, sinful forelock. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 96:16/q14/p2 After being rebuked for blocking prayer, Abū Jahl threatened to marshal his clan, cavalry and armed men. — Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Baghawī speaks of swift horses and strong men; the fuller report adds a large clan, cavalry and armed men. [2 notes: BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 96:16/q15 What happened when Abū Jahl tried to confront the praying Prophet?  [* · reports]
- 96:16/q15/p1 He retreated on seeing a trench of fire, awe and wings; the Prophet said angels would have torn him apart. — Abū Hurayra reports the threat, sudden retreat, vision and prophetic explanation; al-Ṭabarī places it in the revealed sequence from 96:10 and 96:15–19. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 96:16/q16 What happened between Ibn Masʿūd and Abū Jahl, from the Quraysh recitation to Badr?  [* · reports]
- 96:16/q16/p1 Ibn Masʿūd's public recitation brought Abū Jahl's blow; at Badr he found Abū Jahl wounded, killed him, and brought proof to the Prophet. — Bursevī says Ibn Masʿūd volunteered when others would not; the Prophet first feared for his small, weak companion but allowed him to recite Sūrat al-Raḥmān at the Kaaba. Abū Jahl slapped him, tearing and bloodying his ear; Ibn Masʿūd returned in tears and the Prophet grieved. Gabriel laughed and foretold the reversal. At Badr the Prophet sent Ibn Masʿūd to finish wounded survivors for a fighter's share. He speared Abū Jahl's neck from afar for fear of his remaining strength, was too weak to mount his chest and used a ploy; Abū Jahl called him shepherdling and he replied that Islam prevails. Abū Jahl said none was more hateful to him than Muḥammad, even in death. The Prophet called him more defiant than Pharaoh, who had at least professed belief. Ibn Masʿūd cut off the head with a blunt sword, could not carry it, cut the ear, tied on a cord and dragged it; Gabriel called it ear for ear. Bursevī associates the strike with the later branding threat. [8 notes: BURSEVI(أبو جهل,ابن مسعود,النبي عليه السلام)]

## 96:16/q17 What recitation reward is reported for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 96:16/q17/p1 A reciter is promised a reward as though reciting the whole set of shorter sūras. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this from the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
