# 79:37: 16 questions

## 79:37/q01 How does the clause beginning 'fa-ammā' relate to the earlier 'when'?  [فَأَمَّا · grammar]
turns on: Which following clause supplies the response to the earlier conditional, or whether that response is left implicit.
- 79:37/q01/p1 The clause beginning 'fa-ammā' is the answer to the earlier conditional 'when'. — Nasafī takes 'as for' as answering the earlier 'when' and paraphrases the result as the matter described. Abū Ḥayyān prefers this analysis; Ālūsī calls it sound and straightforward if the preceding clause is conditional, citing 2:38. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ links the nominal clause as the answer and that 'when' governs the subject-predicate relation in meaning; the subsequent division elaborates what precedes, since a thing cannot be branched from itself. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 79:37/q01/p2 The answer to 'when' is understood but omitted, and the following division explains the result. — Kashshāf supplies 'then the matter is as follows.' Biqāʿī supplies the division of people into one group destined for Hell and another for bliss. Ālūsī calls omission an alternative, but finds attempts to recover the answer specifically from preceding or following wording unconvincing. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:37/q01/p3 The statement that Hell is the abode is the answer, under a compound condition. — Rāzī offers this as an alternative: when the calamity arrives, whoever arrives as a transgressor has Hell as home. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 79:37/q02 What does 'ammā' contribute here?  [فَأَمَّا · grammar]
- 79:37/q02/p1 It marks a division and has conditional force, with the sense 'whatever may occur'. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes both its dividing and conditional functions. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:37/q03 What is the grammatical role of 'man'?  [مَن · grammar]
- 79:37/q03/p1 'Man' is nominative as the subject, with its predicate in the following clause. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this parsing. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 79:37/q04 What does 'ṭaghā' mean here?  [طَغَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is taken as the general crossing of God's bounds, its extreme of unbelief, or a more specific spiritual, intellectual, behavioral, or illustrative form.
- 79:37/q04/p1 It means disobeying God or exceeding His proper bounds in rebellion and sin. — Mujāhid glosses it as disobedience, as reported by Mujāhid, Ṭabarī, and Durr; Durr's composite report also glosses marsāhā as the Hour's time and the great calamity as being sent to Mālik. Ṭabarī adds arrogance toward the Lord and pride that prevents worship. Junayd calls it crossing the bounds of what is due. Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī specify exceeding limits through sins. Biqāʿī adds aggression and lack of fear of standing before God; his citation of al-Qāmūs gives exceeding the measure or rising, including rising water, disbelief, sins, and oppression. Ibn Kathīr says defiance of God, coupled with preference for worldly life over religion and the Hereafter. Ālūsī says disobedience reaches unbelief. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes it as transgressing God's command, using the contrast with fear of standing before the Lord in 79:40. [12 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); SULAMI(الجنيد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(القاموس); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:37/q04/p2 It describes transgression carried to disbelief, often with preference for worldly life. — Wāḥidī calls the person who transgresses and prefers worldly life a disbeliever. Baghawī says he disbelieves and favors worldly life over the Hereafter. Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains crossing human limits through disbelief and worldly preference as due to denial of the afterlife. Ibn al-Jawzī specifies persistence in disbelief. Bayḍāwī says transgression has reached disbelief. Nasafī combines exceeding the limit with disbelief. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 79:37/q04/p3 It is denying God's rights and rejecting His blessings. — Tustarī states both elements; Sahl is reported by Sulamī with the added link to pursuing desires and passions and preferring worldly life. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 79:37/q04/p4 It reflects a corruption of intellect or theoretical knowledge of God. — Rāzī says knowing God discloses one's insignificance and God's power, which ought to prevent rebellion. Study Quran reports his account of rebellion as intellectual corruption and resistance to arrogance. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 79:37/q04/p5 It is turning from the afterlife toward worldly life. — Sulamī reports this definition from Abū ʿUthmān. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان)]
- 79:37/q04/p6 It is exceeding balanced human nature toward animal or predatory ranks. — Qāshānī connects this excess to choosing sensory pleasure over real life, ending with Hell as home. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 79:37/q04/p7 It describes the impulsive, unthinking, stubborn, unbelieving attitude of pre-Islamic people. — Kur'an Yolu contrasts ṭughyān with ḥilm at 52:33. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:37/q04/p8 Even taking three dishes of one food is called transgression. — Qurṭubī transmits this concrete example from Yaḥyā ibn Abī Kathīr. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن أبي كثير)]

## 79:37/q05 Who is described by 'the one who transgressed'?  [مَن طَغَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether named people restrict the referent, and whether al-Ḥārith is reported as al-Naḍr's father or son.
- 79:37/q05/p1 The description applies generally to those with the stated traits, including disbelievers who prefer worldly life. — Wāḥidī and Baghawī characterize the person as a disbeliever; Ibn ʿAṭiyya links disbelief and worldly preference to denial of the afterlife. Qurṭubī says even a report about named people applies to every such disbeliever. Rāzī allows a particular occasion but argues that the wording and operative trait are general, rejecting restriction to named individuals. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL+]
- 79:37/q05/p2 The verses concern al-Naḍr and his father al-Ḥārith. — Wāḥidī reports this revelation occasion from Muqātil and al-Kalbī; Rāzī reports it from some. Rāzī accepts that their conduct may have occasioned the passage, while arguing against confining its application to them. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL-]
- 79:37/q05/p3 A report identifies al-Naḍr and his son al-Ḥārith. — Qurṭubī reports this kinship form, while explicitly extending the passage to every disbeliever who prefers this world. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]

## 79:37/q06 How is the written final alif of 'ṭaghā' read in Warsh's cited routes?  [طَغَىٰ · readings]
turns on: The Warsh transmission route and the treatment of a non-final form written with yāʾ.
- 79:37/q06/p1 Al-Azraq's Warsh routes through Ibn Ghalbūn and others open the vowel in this non-final form. — Ibn al-Jazarī lists this word among the applicable non-final forms. [1 notes: NASHR(الأزرق عن ورش)]
- 79:37/q06/p2 The al-Taysīr and related routes pronounce it between opening and imāla. — Ibn al-Jazarī explains this treatment by the word's being written with yāʾ. [1 notes: NASHR(الأزرق عن ورش)]

## 79:37/q07 Is 'fa-ammā man ṭaghā' counted as an āya ending?  [فَأَمَّا مَن طَغَىٰ · sciences]
turns on: The regional verse-counting tradition.
- 79:37/q07/p1 The Basran, Syrian, and Kufan counters count it as an āya ending. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports these regional verse counts. [1 notes: NASHR(البصري والشامي والكوفي)]
- 79:37/q07/p2 The two Medinan counters and the Meccan counter do not count it as an āya ending. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports these regional verse counts. [1 notes: NASHR(البصري والشامي والكوفي)]

## 79:37/q08 Why do some people prefer the immediate worldly life?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The motive or orientation assigned to preference for worldly life.
- 79:37/q08/p1 Leaders recognize the Messenger's truth but resent following someone else and losing authority. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this motive for leaders' worldly preference. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:37/q08/p2 They live for the immediate moment and overlook the passing world's limits. — Kur'an Yolu says this is their usual orientation. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:37/q08/p3 They favor sensory pleasure over real life. — Qāshānī links that preference to falling from balanced human nature into animal or predatory ranks. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:37/q09 How does this passage divide people, and why introduce the wretched first?  [* · interpretation]
- 79:37/q09/p1 It divides people into those destined for Hell and those destined for salvation or bliss, introducing the wretched first. — Rāzī says the division follows the general description of Resurrection and applies to morally responsible people. Biqāʿī uses the two destinies to supply the omitted conditional answer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls them two exemplary classes, defined by transgression and worldly preference versus fear and restraint, and says Hell's people come first because the address targets polytheists. Kur'an Yolu sees the brief verses as a summary of attitudes preparing immense loss or salvation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 79:37/q10 What fate follows the transgression and worldly preference described here?  [* · interpretation]
- 79:37/q10/p1 Hell becomes the transgressor's abode or refuge. — Baghawī and Ṭabrisī state the consequence. Kashshāf says the possessor of 'abode' is understood rather than expressed and 'hiya' can be a separator or a subject. Ibn Kathīr adds food from zaqqūm and drink of boiling water. Qāshānī applies the fate to those choosing sensory pleasure. Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies this as the first of two exemplary classes. Study Quran reports Rāzī's reason that combined intellectual and practical corruption reaches the level meriting eternal punishment. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 79:37/q11 What follows for a sinner who lacks the two traits defining Hell's people?  [* · theology]
- 79:37/q11/p1 Such a sinner is not described as having Hell for a permanent abode. — Rāzī infers this from the paired conditions of transgression and preferring worldly life; Study Quran reports his explanation that their combined intellectual and practical corruption merits eternal punishment. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 79:37/q12 How do fear of God and restraint of desire contrast with rebellion and worldly preference?  [* · interpretation]
- 79:37/q12/p1 Fear reflects knowledge of God and leads to obedience, while restraining caprice counters preference for worldly life. — Study Quran reports Rāzī's contrast: rebellion implies ignorance but fear of standing before God implies knowledge, with 35:28 cited. Its own explanation opposes forbidding caprice to worldly preference, says fear leads to good deeds and caprice leads astray, and cites 45:23. Ibn ʿĀshūr uses the contrast with fearing one's Lord's station in 79:40 to identify transgression as disobeying God's command. Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses fear and restraint to define the saved class against transgression and worldly preference. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 79:37/q13 What does the inward reading say separates a person from ignorance?  [* · inward]
- 79:37/q13/p1 Only one who prefers God over self, spirit, this world, and the afterlife escapes ignorance. — Tustarī gives this comprehensive spiritual criterion. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 79:37/q14 What warnings are transmitted about preferring this world over the next?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give distinct warnings and consequences for the same preference.
- 79:37/q14/p1 Hudhayfa feared that this community would choose what it sees over what it knows. — Qurṭubī reports this through Juwaybir from al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(حذيفة)]
- 79:37/q14/p2 An earlier-book report says God burdens the worldly-preferring servant with worry and loss and leaves the servant to perish. — Qurṭubī attributes this warning to a report found in earlier books. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رواية في الكتب)]

## 79:37/q15 How does this passage serve the sūra's purpose?  [* · links]
- 79:37/q15/p1 It displays humanity's final fate within the sūra's proof of resurrection and gathering. — Biqāʿī says earlier sūras had treated the matter fully and this sūra's oaths show it reaching a decisive climax. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:37/q16 What earlier Qur'anic description is linked to the account of final fates?  [* · links]
- 79:37/q16/p1 The preceding mention of people who do not hear even Hell's faintest sound connects to al-Anbiyāʾ 21:102. — Biqāʿī explicitly draws this link. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
