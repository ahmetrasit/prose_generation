# 96:9: 18 questions

## 96:9/q01 What does 'Have you seen?' express?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question conveys wonder, inquiry, warning or the later moral contrast.
- 96:9/q01/p1 It draws attention to the astonishing impropriety of forbidding worship. — Al-Wāḥidī and Baghawī say repetition intensifies wonder. Rāzī highlights the irony of Abū al-Ḥakam, an idol worshipper who forbids his Creator's service, and recalls the Prophet's prayer for Abū Jahl or ʿUmar. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the extraordinary-event question at 88:1; Qutb hears a rapid live address. [32 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; JISHUMI(أبو جهل,أبو مسلم); ITQAN(أبو شامة)]
- 96:9/q01/p2 It contrasts the guided, pious worshipper with the denying, turning preventer and God's seeing both. — Al-Farrāʾ, Baghawī and al-Kashshāf develop this contrast. Al-Ālūsī offers an escalating sequence from general prevention to claimed guidance to actual denial; the majority's contrast is broader. ʿIṣām al-Dīn makes a more elaborate sequence from tyranny to lost guidance to earned punishment, which al-Ālūsī calls far-fetched. [29 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري,الفراء); BURSEVI; ALUSI(الإمام,الجمهور,عصام الدين); TAB; TAB-FULL]
- 96:9/q01/p3 It means 'tell me' or 'inform me', using sight as an image of certain knowledge. — Al-Biqāʿī explains inward clarity through seeing; al-Rāghib compares the interrogative with akhbirnī. The construction can be visual or intellectual according to views reported by al-Ālūsī. [10 notes: KASHSHAF; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(بعض الأئمة,بعضهم); BINTSHATI(الراغب)]
- 96:9/q01/p4 It challenges the wrongdoer about the punishment or fate he deserves. — Jishumī and Abū ʿAlī supply the omitted question of punishment or tomorrow's state; another view asks whether he is astray and bound for Fire. Qurṭubī supplies whether he is safe from punishment. Ibn Kathīr calls the first rebuke gentle. [9 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 96:9/q02 How are the repeated interrogatives and omissions parsed?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether raʾayta takes a second object, whether a conditional can fill it, and whether an interrogative apodosis needs fāʾ.
- 96:9/q02/p1 The first question takes the prohibitor as object, later objects are omitted, and the final interrogative supplies the threatening complement. — Abū Ḥayyān requires the second object to be an interrogative clause, citing 53:33–35, 19:77–78, 56:58–59. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī call the omissions contextual rather than tanāzuʿ because whole clauses do not compete as omitted individual words. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the answer from 96:14. [23 notes: ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(أبو حيان,قيل); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 96:9/q02/p2 The relative clause and a conditional are two objects of the first question; the middle question is emphatic and the later answer supplies the first missing answer. — Al-Zamakhsharī's parse is transmitted by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn and held by al-Naṣafī. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Raḍī allow an interrogative answer without fāʾ, citing 6:47; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Damāmīnī, al-Samīn and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ object that fāʾ is required. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ALUSI(أبو حيان,الزمخشري,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-; SAMIN-DURR-; BINTSHATI-]
- 96:9/q02/p3 Three linked challenges have their expansive omitted conclusions compressed into the last question. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says one concise threat completes all three; al-Ṭabarī calls the repetitions substitutions, making the forbidding and denying man answerable to God's sight. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية); TAB; TAB-FULL]
- 96:9/q02/p4 The first question may be visual and the later ones cognitive, with conditional answers supplied at the end. — Al-Ālūsī reports this mixed analysis and another in which all three are cognitive; unjoining the last clause marks its independent threatening force. [3 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]
- 96:9/q02/p5 The interrogative is a stylistic prompt that need not take any inferred second object. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites analogous Meccan questions at 107:1, 19:77, 25:43, 45:23, 53:33 and 56:58, 63, 68, 71, resisting speculative grammatical complements. [4 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 96:9/q02/p6 As a challenge rather than cognitive vision, it does not take two objects. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes it from knowledge-based seeing. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA]

## 96:9/q03 Who is addressed?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the question addresses the Prophet, any observer, the prohibitor or alternating parties.
- 96:9/q03/p1 The Prophet is addressed. — Rāzī relates the address to his prayer for Abū Jahl or ʿUmar. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Baḥr favor the Prophet because the pronouns stay consistent; Elmalılı extends this first address to all. [8 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(البحر); ELMALILI]
- 96:9/q03/p2 Any capable observer, or humanity generally, is addressed. — Bursevī reads seeing visually. Al-Ālūsī relates the generic reading to 96:8; Ibn ʿĀshūr leaves the observer unspecified. [9 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI(جمع); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 96:9/q03/p3 The forbidding unbeliever is addressed. — Abū Ḥayyān reports a challenge asking whether he would still stop a guided worshipper; al-Ālūsī allows this in a generic scene. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ALUSI(البحر)]
- 96:9/q03/p4 The questions alternate between the litigants. — Al-Ālūsī reports a move to the unbeliever then back to the Prophet, like a judge addressing two parties; Elmalılı finds this account meaningful. [2 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ELMALILI]

## 96:9/q04 What does 'forbids' mean and what does its tense convey?  [يَنْهَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the imperfect emphasizes repeated conduct or vividly represents the past incident.
- 96:9/q04/p1 It means preventing or stopping an act. — Jishumī glosses ending as refraining. Al-Ālūsī says the word may indicate an attempted obstruction that failed; Ibn ʿAbbās's authenticated report also describes an explicit spoken prohibition. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted prohibited act is understood from prayer. [14 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI(ابن عباس,بعضهم,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(Tirmizî)]
- 96:9/q04/p2 The imperfect evokes habitual or persistent forbidding. — Al-Biqāʿī stresses renewal; Rāzī says the indefinite description portrays a habit of barring anyone he saw. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 96:9/q04/p3 The imperfect vividly depicts an extraordinary past incident as present. — Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain the historic present this way. [3 notes: ALUSI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

Rāzī, al-Ālūsī, Qutb and Elmalılı apply a specific occasion broadly; Qur'an Yolu favors an all-ages reading involving religion's social call to goodness, truth and justice. ## 96:9/q06 What happened in the incident that occasioned this warning?  [ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ about threat, direct confrontation, stone and camel entrails.
- 96:9/q06/p1 Abū Jahl threatened to trample Muhammad's neck or soil his face, approached him praying, recoiled from a fiery trench, terror or wings, and would have been seized by angels. — Abū Hurayra's reports sometimes give an oath by al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā, the Maqām or Kaʿba, and a huge camel as a barrier. Al-Tirmidhī calls one account ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ gharīb. A narrator doubts whether the revelation notice belongs to Abū Hurayra's report. Qatāda calls him this community's Pharaoh. [59 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNHISHAM(أبو جهل); TAB(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI(الحسن,النبي); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو جهل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة); RAZI(أبو جهل); RAZI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); BAYDAWI(أبو جهل); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR(أبو هريرة,قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI(أبو هريرة رضي الله عنه); BURSEVI(أبو جهل); ALUSI(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ELMALILI(Ebû Hüreyre); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو هريرة); BINTSHATI(الطبري)]
- 96:9/q06/p2 Abū Jahl confronted the Prophet in prayer, verbally forbade him and threatened to summon his clan; God's guards were promised. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports a direct challenge, authenticated in al-Tirmidhī. Versions give a vow to kill him at the Station; the council is his clan and the guards angels. [29 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(Tirmizî)]
- 96:9/q06/p3 Abū Jahl brought a rock to crush Muhammad's head, then recoiled. — Al-Biqāʿī cites Muslim; variants describe wings, a fiery trench or a huge camel. Bursevī places a stone attempt at noon prayer. [3 notes: BIQAI(أبو هريرة رضي الله عنه); BURSEVI]
- 96:9/q06/p4 Abū Jahl threw camel entrails on Muhammad as he prostrated. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies this detail in al-Durr. [7 notes: DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 96:9/q07 Which prayer was opposed and who joined it?  [ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The participants differ, and the historical fit depends on dating prayer's legislation relative to Abū Ṭālib's death.
- 96:9/q07/p1 It was noon prayer. — Al-Māwardī says so, and Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī repeat it. Elmalılı notes the source does not say it was obligatory. [9 notes: MAWARDI; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ELMALILI(Tebrizî)]
- 96:9/q07/p2 It was a first congregation with Muhammad, Abū Bakr, ʿAlī and other early believers; Abū Ṭālib and Jaʿfar passed by. — Abū Ṭālib urged kinship support in verse. Al-Ālūsī objects that he died around three years before Hijra whereas the Night Journey is usually placed near one year before it; Ibn Ḥazm, Ibn Fāris, al-Suddī and al-Zuhrī give differing dates. Elmalılı answers that prayers before the five daily prayers may have existed. [28 notes: ABUHAYYAN(أبو طالب,قيل); ALUSI(أبو حيان,أبو طالب,ابن حزم,ابن فارس,الزهري,السدي,القاضي عياض,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ELMALILI(Ebû Tâlib,Tebrizî,Âlûsî); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 96:9/q07/p3 It may have been the first congregation, with Muhammad and ʿAlī; Abū Ṭālib brought Jaʿfar to join. — Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad gives this account; Abū Ṭālib's quoted verses pledge his sons' support of the Prophet. [4 notes: MAWARDI(أبو طالب,جعفر بن محمد)]

## 96:9/q09 Why use the indefinite 'a servant'?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indefiniteness magnifies, generalizes or has no extra force.
- 96:9/q09/p1 It magnifies Muhammad's perfect servanthood and the audacity of barring his worship. — Rāzī invokes ʿAlī's answer to a Jewish visitor contrasting little worldly goods with the Prophet's great character at 68:4. Bursevī says 'a servant' stresses worship of one's Master rather than saying 'you'. Al-Ālūsī prefers this reading. [11 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL(علي); BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض); ALUSI+]
- 96:9/q09/p2 It generalizes the worshipper. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says prohibition is astonishing whoever the servant is, citing 17:5; Rāzī notes worshipping angels as a wider frame. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 96:9/q09/p3 It is simple grammatical indefiniteness. — Al-Ālūsī reports this reading. [3 notes: ALUSI(بعض,قيل)]

## 96:9/q10 Does the phrase prohibit prayer itself or interrupt someone during prayer?  [ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the temporal phrase identifies the target of prohibition or merely its setting.
- 96:9/q10/p1 The prohibition concerns prayer, occurring while the servant prays. — Al-Ālūsī derives contemporaneity from the time clause. Al-Farrāʾ pairs prayer with calling to piety: it reforms the worshipper and others. Elmalılı includes obligatory and voluntary prayer. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI; ELMALILI]
- 96:9/q10/p2 The prohibition occurs during prayer but may target prayer or another matter. — Al-Ālūsī says it can impede both the servant's worship and prayer's call to others. Ibn ʿĀshūr recovers the omitted act from context. [4 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:9/q11 What practical warning does the verse give?  [* · law]
turns on: The notes distinguish obstruction of worship from objection to an accompanying fault.
- 96:9/q11/p1 Preventing prayer or other obedience is blameworthy, whether by words, acts or distraction. — Jishumī cites the duty to command right and forbid wrong. Al-Ālūsī includes other worship and distraction; Iṣlāḥī calls prayer a person's foremost duty. Qushayrī says the forbidding man would have done better to abstain. [21 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو الليث,ابن الشيخ); ALUSI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QASHANI]
- 96:9/q11/p2 Some authorities avoid direct prohibitions of doubtful prayer practices. — ʿAlī would not forbid prayer before Eid, citing only the Prophet's example; Abū Ḥanīfa prescribed the favored praise after bowing without explicitly banning another formula. [18 notes: RAZI(أبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,علي); BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة,ابن الشيخ,علي); ALUSI(أبو حنيفة,أبو يوسف,علي); ELMALILI(Hazret-i Ali,İmâm-ı A‘zam Ebû Hanîfe)]
- 96:9/q11/p3 One may object to a wrong time, usurped place or other accompanying circumstance without opposing prayer itself. — Rāzī also allows a master to limit a slave's voluntary night prayer or fast and a husband his wife's retreat for stated interests; Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı distinguish the accompanying attribute. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI; ELMALILI]

## 96:9/q12 How does this scene connect to the surrounding sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the scene to preceding self-sufficiency and to the opening command to recite.
- 96:9/q12/p1 The specific prohibitor exemplifies human transgression born of self-sufficiency. — Jishumī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ālūsī and Qutb read the prohibition as the concrete outcome of the preceding general description. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the opening kallā anticipates this outrageous act, comparing Imruʾ al-Qays's initial negative oath. [23 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(عصام الدين); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); BURSEVI]
- 96:9/q12/p2 The move from recitation to prayer is apt because recitation is a pillar of prayer. — Elmalılı says prayer grounds worship and infers that these verses followed its legislation. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 96:9/q13 When was this passage revealed relative to the sūra's opening?  [* · sciences]
turns on: How the Abū Jahl occasion relates to the first revelation and the chronology of prescribed prayer.
- 96:9/q13/p1 The direct Abū Jahl passage starts at 96:9; verses 6–8 introduce it. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿĀshūr place the direct account here; Rāzī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ allow later revelation and attachment after the opening. [7 notes: RAZI; IBNASHUR(الطبري); IBNASHUR-FULL(الطبري); BINTSHATI]
- 96:9/q13/p2 Verses 6 through the end were revealed on the Abū Jahl occasion. — Abū Hurayra versions in al-Ālūsī give this extent; Qutb says all but the opening concerns him. [6 notes: ALUSI(أبو هريرة); BINTSHATI; QUTB-ZILAL(الروايات الصحيحة)]
- 96:9/q13/p3 The prayer passage may have followed public preaching rather than the first revelation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the difficulty if obligatory prayer began only at the Night Journey; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ finds multiple solutions possible while retaining general wording. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); ELMALILI]

- 96:9/q14/p1 All counters except the Syrian count it as an āya ending. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports the verse-counting tradition. [1 notes: NASHR(أهل العدد إلا الشامي)]

## 96:9/q15 What recitation merit is reported for the sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 96:9/q15/p1 A report promises reward as though the reciter read all the shorter sūras. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits it without a grading in the note. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 96:9/q16 How is the hamza in 'Have you seen?' recited?  [أَرَءَيْتَ · readings]
turns on: The variants concern sound or duration with no reported change in meaning.
- 96:9/q16/p1 The second hamza is facilitated to an intermediate sound. — Qirāʾāt-ER describes two canonical tashīl formulations without semantic difference. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 96:9/q16/p2 Madd lengthens the alif before the hamza. — Qirāʾāt-ER says the interrogative perfect structure remains. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 96:9/q17 Whom do the later conditional verbs and pronouns describe?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the conditional subject continues the prohibitor or shifts to the worshipper in the guidance condition.
- 96:9/q17/p1 The guided worshipper is the subject of the guidance condition, while the denying and turning man is the prohibitor. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Farrāʾ assign the guidance pronoun to the worshipper. Ibn ʿĀshūr says context resolves the shifting antecedents, and the omitted object of 'denied' is the servant. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية والفراء); IBNASHUR]
- 96:9/q17/p2 The concealed subjects of the conditional and following verbs refer to the prohibitor. — Al-Ālūsī gives this parsing. [1 notes: ALUSI]

## 96:9/q18 Why are the repeated challenge clauses separated?  [* · rhetoric]
- 96:9/q18/p1 Separation gives each censure and threat independent force and intensifies the warning. — Al-Ālūsī makes each clause independently deserving of condemnation. [2 notes: ALUSI]
