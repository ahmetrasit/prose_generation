# 26:167: 14 questions

## 26:167/q01 How forcefully is the conditional threat expressed?  [لَئِن لَّمْ تَنتَهِ لَتَكُونَنَّ · rhetoric]
- 26:167/q01/p1 It is an oath backed by a serious, decisive threat of expulsion. — Al-Biqāʿī says they swear to expel Lot if his protest continues. Iṣlāḥī says earlier threats had been made, but this warning was meant to be carried out. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:167/q02 What do they demand that Lot stop doing?  [لَّمْ تَنتَهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the demanded cessation concerns Lot's rebuke of their conduct or his prophetic claim and invitation.
- 26:167/q02/p1 He must stop condemning, reproaching, and preaching against their conduct. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī describe his speech against them; Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Nasafī specify reproach. Ibn Kathīr says Lot forbade their approach to males and directed them to wives. Al-Bayḍāwī includes prohibition, and al-Ālūsī includes objection to their conduct. Al-Biqāʿī calls it protest. Al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz and Elmalılı leave the activity unspecified. [11 notes: MUQATIL(قوم لوط); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 26:167/q02/p2 He must stop his prophetic claims and call to faith. — Al-Bayḍāwī offers what Lot claims as an alternative to his condemnation and prohibition; al-Ālūsī mentions his claim to prophethood and invitation to faith alongside objections to their conduct. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:167/q03 What conduct had Lot challenged?  [تَنتَهِ · interpretation]
- 26:167/q03/p1 Their forbidden sexual conduct, especially approaching males instead of wives. — Ibn Kathīr says Lot forbade approaching males and directed them to wives. Al-Thaʿlabī calls the people transgressors for crossing from what is lawful into what is forbidden. [2 notes: THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:167/q04 How should mīm be pronounced in this verse and comparable environments?  [لَّمْ مِنَ ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ · readings]
- 26:167/q04/p1 Mīm remains nasal; a vocalized mīm avoids emphatic coloring, while a sākin mīm is clear before most letters and assimilates with ghunna before another mīm. — Ibn al-Jazarī says the ghunna of assimilated or sākin mīm comes from the nose; he warns against coloring vocalized mīm, especially before emphatic letters or alif. He specifies assimilation before mīm, and clear, still pronunciation before other letters, especially fāʾ and wāw. Here the sākin mīm of لَّمْ precedes tāʾ. [3 notes: NASHR]

## 26:167/q05 How is sākin mīm pronounced before bāʾ in the related recitation rule?  [لَّمْ · readings]
turns on: The disagreement is between hiding the mīm with ghunna and pronouncing it fully clearly before bāʾ; the cited traditions differ.
- 26:167/q05/p1 It is hidden with ghunna (ikhfāʾ). — Ibn al-Jazarī attributes this to al-Dānī, Ibn Mujāhid, and western performance traditions; he accepts both treatments but prefers this one. [1 notes: NASHR+]
- 26:167/q05/p2 It is pronounced clearly (iẓhār). — Ibn al-Jazarī attributes this to Makkī and eastern traditions, and reports Aḥmad ibn Yaʿqūb's claim of consensus for it; he accepts it, though he prefers ikhfāʾ. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 26:167/q06 What is conveyed by calling him by name, يَٰلُوطُ?  [يَٰلُوطُ · rhetoric]
- 26:167/q06/p1 The bare name expresses deliberate harshness and coarseness. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts the name with an honorific form of address. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:167/q07 Why say مِنَ ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ rather than directly say they will expel him?  [مِنَ ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ · rhetoric]
- 26:167/q07/p1 The construction is more emphatic than a direct expulsion verb. — Al-Ālūsī contrasts it with 'we will expel you'; Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts it with لنخرجنك and compares من المرجومين in Noah's story (26:116). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:167/q08 What do they threaten will happen to Lot?  [لَتَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'expelled' means removal alive, possibly in humiliating conditions, or removal of his corpse after killing him.
- 26:167/q08/p1 They will expel him from their town and community. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and others locate the expulsion in their town, land, or midst. Al-Rāzī allows that expulsion could occur in the worst conditions; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Burṣawī, and al-Biqāʿī describe violent or public humiliation. Al-Biqāʿī says they want personally to remove an outsider and silence objection. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 27:56, which explicitly orders Lot's family driven from the town. Iṣlāḥī says this time they intended to carry the threat out; al-Qur'an Yolu notes 'he too' would be expelled. [23 notes: MUQATIL(قوم لوط); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 26:167/q08/p2 They will kill him and carry his body out of town. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām takes the threatened expulsion to mean being brought out dead. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 26:167/q09 Who are the others implied by مِنَ ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ?  [مِنَ ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the prior exiles were specifically critics of the sexual act or people who displeased the community more generally.
- 26:167/q09/p1 Earlier people who condemned or forbade the community's sexual wrongdoing. — Abū Ḥayyān reports earlier people who forbade the act; al-Ālūsī reports an account of people exiled for harshly condemning the obscenity. Al-Biqāʿī says they customarily exiled critics. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:167/q09/p2 People expelled for dissent or for angering the community on any issue. — Abū Ḥayyān also allows any dissenter, whatever the issue. Al-Ālūsī says they apparently expelled people who angered them for various reasons. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:167/q10 Why did they choose expulsion as their threat?  [ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain the choice through Lot's outsider status and through an existing practice of exiling objectors; these explanations can coexist.
- 26:167/q10/p1 Lot was an outsider to the town, so exile was an available threat. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Lot had immigrated and married into the community; unlike Noah's people, his opponents threatened expulsion. Al-Biqāʿī likewise calls him an outsider whom they wanted to remove personally. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:167/q10/p2 Expelling objectors was already their practice. — Al-Biqāʿī cites their custom of exiling critics; Abū Ḥayyān reports earlier expulsion of forbidders or other dissenters; al-Ālūsī mentions people driven out for angering them and, in another account, for harsh condemnation of the act. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:167/q11 What form is ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ and is another reading reported?  [ٱلْمُخْرَجِينَ · readings]
- 26:167/q11/p1 It is a Form IV passive participle, with no variant among the major transmissions cited. — The qirāʾāt source identifies the standard Ḥafṣ form and says it is universal across major transmissions. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Ḥafṣ)]

## 26:167/q12 How does this threat connect with other Qur'anic accounts?  [* · links]
- 26:167/q12/p1 It parallels other accounts of threatened expulsion and opposition to prophets. — Al-Wāḥidī compares the people's threat to Shuʿayb's opponents in 7:88. Ibn Kathīr and The Study Quran point to 7:82, where Lot and his followers are threatened with expulsion for claiming purity. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the explicit order to expel Lot's family in 27:56. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Noah's opponents, who threatened a different punishment; he also compares the construction with 26:116. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:167/q13 How does Lot respond to the threat?  [* · links]
- 26:167/q13/p1 He answers by detesting their deed rather than negotiating the threatened exile. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he simply declares detestation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says ignoring the threat shows no fear of exile and a wish to escape the inevitable punishment. Al-Biqāʿī adds that God brings him out honorably through angels instead of the humiliating expulsion they intend. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:167/q14 What sīn-to-ṣād sound shift is described in the associated linguistic notes?  [* · grammar]
- 26:167/q14/p1 Some speakers change sīn to ṣād near a retracted or emphatic consonant, especially qāf, without extending the change to less similar consonants. — Sībawayh gives صقت and صبقت with qāf and explains that ṣād matches its raised articulation; صالغ and صلخ show the effect of ġayn and ḫāʾ. Banu al-ʿAnbar say صاطع before ṭāʾ. Sībawayh compares the influence across intervening sounds to imāla and to ط in مصطبر and د in مزدجر. Sīn and ṣād share voicelessness, whistling, and frication. He rejects parallels with voiced zāy and the proposed ذ-to-ظ change, and says tāʾ and thāʾ before dāl lack the same conditions. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH(بنو العنبر)]
