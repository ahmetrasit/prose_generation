# 26:116: 12 questions

## 26:116/q01 How forceful is the conditional threat?  [لَئِن لَّمْ تَنتَهِ لَتَكُونَنَّ · rhetoric]
- 26:116/q01/p1 It is a sworn, firm and emphatic warning that they will carry out the threatened punishment. — Elmalılı notes that the people swear and say Noah will certainly be stoned unless he stops. Al-Tabatabaʾi says the emphatic assertion matches the warning's final and forceful character. Islahi infers that earlier threats of stoning had been ignored and that this emphatic wording now promises to carry them out. [3 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:116/q02 What does تَنتَهِ mean?  [تَنتَهِ · meaning]
- 26:116/q02/p1 To stop or desist, reaching a limit and going no further. — Al-Tabrisi explains intihāʾ as reaching a limit without passing the point where one has been forbidden, and nihāya as reaching a limit. Bursevi glosses intihāʾ as stopping or desisting. [2 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI]

## 26:116/q03 What did they demand Noah stop doing?  [لَّمْ تَنتَهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conditional clause is described as Noah's refusal to stop his call or, in Ibn Kathir's note, as his stopping it.
- 26:116/q03/p1 Stop speaking to them and calling them to his message, including his criticism of their gods and religion. — Muqatil and al-Wahidi specify speaking and keeping quiet; Yahya ibn Sallam and al-Qurtubi add that the speech insulted or reviled their gods and criticized their religion. Al-Tabrisi says to cease what he said and called them to. Al-Baghawi and al-Nasafi refer to what he was saying; Bursevi and al-Tabatabaʾi specify preaching or warning. Study Quran reports the disbelievers' grievance as denigration of their gods and religion. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 26:116/q03/p2 The threat is stated as applying if Noah stopped summoning them to his religion. — Ibn Kathir's note gives this condition in these terms, reversing the condition stated in the other notes. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:116/q04 Why did they address him as يَٰنُوحُ?  [يَٰنُوحُ · rhetoric]
- 26:116/q04/p1 Calling him by name rather than by a respectful title shows rudeness and lack of manners. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts the bare name with a respectful title. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:116/q05 What fate did they threaten by calling Noah one of ٱلْمَرْجُومِينَ?  [ٱلْمَرْجُومِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether rajm here is literal pelting, a general expression for killing, a metaphor for insults, or a designation of ill omen; the reports also differ for al-Ḍaḥḥāk.
- 26:116/q05/p1 He would be pelted with stones, commonly to death. — Qatāda is repeatedly reported to take rajm literally as stones (Yahya ibn Sallam, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī). Al-Zajjāj also says rocks; al-Jishumī and al-Tabrisi distinguish throwing stones from shooting arrows. Al-Baghawī reports Muqātil and al-Kalbī's killing by stones; al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Burūsawī via al-Rāghib, al-Biqāʿī, Asad and Study Quran explicitly connect stones and death. Al-Burūsawī calls it killing in the ugliest manner; al-Biqāʿī adds that Noah's weak followers could not protect him. Ibn ʿĀshūr says rajm means throwing stones and commonly death by stoning. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the threat physically and calls verbal abuse remote; al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān allow both physical and verbal senses. Al-Māwardī cites Abū Dāwūd's poetic tarajmunī as usage evidence without specifying in the note which sense it proves. Al-Rāzī elsewhere explains Satan's designation rajīm through meteors cast at devils from heaven, later extended to evil rebels. Islahi says the earlier threat of stoning was ignored and now will be executed. [29 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو داود,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل والكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 26:116/q05/p2 He would be killed; these glosses do not specify the manner of killing. — Muqātil, al-Ḥasan, Zayd ibn Aslam, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Kalbī and Muḥammad ibn al-Ḥasan are cited for killing. Al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz gives killing as an alternative to insults. Al-Thumālī, cited by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī, says Quranic stoning means killing except at 19:46, where it means insult. Al-Biqāʿī's note says al-mujrimīn means those killed, with Noah's weak followers unable to protect him. [12 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(ابن عباس، مقاتل,الثمالي); MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل والكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل,الثمالي); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:116/q05/p3 He would be verbally abused, insulted or reviled; 'stoning' pictures blame cast at him. — Al-Ṭabarī glosses the threat as reviling; al-Suddī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and other reports give verbal abuse. Al-Jishumī and al-Tabrisi explain the metaphor as pelting someone with blame; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds slander, and al-Ālūsī calls it taunting. Al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz, al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān include it as an alternative sense. Study Quran compares Satan called al-rajīm, usually understood as accursed. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this figurative view but rejects it as remote. Al-Thumālī's generalization, cited by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī, excludes this verse from the verbal sense by making 19:46 the exception. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); STUDYQURAN(some commentators (Q, Ṭ)); TABATABAI(قيل)-; THALABI(الثمالي)-; QURTUBI-FULL(الثمالي)-]
- 26:116/q05/p4 He would be among people marked by ill omen or misfortune. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this explanation from al-Ḍaḥḥāk, distinct from other sources' reports of al-Ḍaḥḥāk's verbal-abuse gloss. [1 notes: THALABI(الضحاك)]

## 26:116/q06 What does مِنَ ٱلْمَرْجُومِينَ add to the threat?  [مِنَ ٱلْمَرْجُومِينَ · grammar]
- 26:116/q06/p1 It places Noah in the class of people deemed deserving of that punishment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the classifying construction with Noah's words وما أنا من المهتدين in 6:56. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:116/q07 What does ṭard mean in the lexical discussion of ٱلْمَرْجُومِينَ?  [ٱلْمَرْجُومِينَ · meaning]
- 26:116/q07/p1 Ṭard is expulsion or banishment, driving someone away or making them an outcast. — Al-Jishumī equates the verbs ṭaradahu and aṭradahu with driving a person away or making an outcast; the note does not explicitly equate ṭard with this verse's threatened rajm. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:116/q08 How do other Quranic uses of stoning bear on this threat?  [ٱلْمَرْجُومِينَ · links]
turns on: The analogies differ: Abraham's father's threat in 19:46 is verbal abuse, while Satan's epithet is explained through meteors or as 'accursed'.
- 26:116/q08/p1 The threat in 19:46 means verbal abuse, showing that stoning language can be figurative. — Al-Qurṭubī identifies Abraham's father's words in 19:46 as an insult; Study Quran cites his comparison for a metaphorical reading of stoning here. Al-Thumālī, as quoted by al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī, agrees on 19:46 but makes it the sole exception to killing elsewhere in the Quran. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الثمالي); STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī); THALABI(الثمالي)]
- 26:116/q08/p2 The title rajīm for Satan relates to devils driven from heaven by meteors and is extended to evil rebels. — Al-Rāzī gives the meteors as an alternative explanation for Satan's title and cites 26:116 and 36:18. Study Quran reports the usual understanding of al-rajīm as 'accursed' when illustrating a figurative sense. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators (Q, Ṭ))]

## 26:116/q09 At what stage of Noah's dealings with his people did the threat come?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:116/q09/p1 It came near the end of prolonged, failed efforts to call and persuade them, as their opposition hardened. — Ibn Kathīr says Noah called night and day, openly and privately, while unbelief and resistance increased. Al-Biqāʿī says that after he refused to expel the followers they dropped deceptive inducement and threatened death; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they resorted to physical threats when argument failed. Al-Ālūsī quotes Irshād al-ʿAql al-Salīm that the threat came near the end. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it the last stage of dealings, al-Rāzī connects it with Noah's despair of their salvation, and Islahi infers an earlier ignored stoning threat. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:116/q10 What happened to Noah amid his people's hostility?  [* · reports]
- 26:116/q10/p1 They attacked and strangled him until he fainted; after recovering he prayed for their forgiveness. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits ʿAbd ibn ʿUmayr al-Laythī's report of Noah saying, 'Lord, forgive my people, for they do not know.' [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد بن عمير الليثي)]

## 26:116/q11 How does the threat lead into Noah's prayer in 26:117?  [* · links]
- 26:116/q11/p1 Threatened with death by stoning, Noah despaired of their salvation and asked God to judge between them. — Al-Rāzī explicitly joins the threat to the appeal in 26:117. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 26:116/q12 How does this narrative fit the sūra's other messenger accounts?  [* · links]
- 26:116/q12/p1 Noah's call, his people's beliefs and their response parallel those of Moses and Abraham; the narrative groups give the same message. — Kuranyolu compares the essential principles and responses across the three accounts and mentions 11:25 and 11:49. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
