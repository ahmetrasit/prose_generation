# 36:18: 19 questions

## 36:18/q01 What does تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ mean?  [تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the speakers blame the envoys for their own misfortune or voice a prediction of punishment; Study Quran leaves the direction of the prediction open.
- 36:18/q01/p1 They regarded the messengers as a bad omen and attributed misfortune to them. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Asad, Iṣlāḥī, and Study Quran give the basic sense. Qatāda says any evil that struck or would strike them was blamed on the envoys (Yaḥyā, ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Ibn Qutayba, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Durr). Ibn Kathīr says their arrival brought no good fortune. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī derive the verb from taking auguries from birds, favorable as well as unfavorable, though the verb commonly implies bad luck; al-Būrsawī specifies rightward flight as auspicious and leftward as ominous, then extension to every superstition. Al-Biqāʿī says the conspicuous, deliberate wording differs from 27:47 and 7:131. [49 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين,قتادة,مقاتل); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,قتادة,مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(النقشبندي); ALUSI-FULL(آخر,إبن عطية,مقاتل); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; THALABI-]
- 36:18/q01/p2 They anticipated that people such as these messengers would bring punishment on the town. — Mujāhid, as transmitted by Ibn Kathīr, says that when such people enter a town, its inhabitants are punished. Study Quran also allows that the speakers foretell punishment to befall the messengers, a different direction for the anticipated harm. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN]

## 36:18/q02 What prompted the townspeople's ill-omen claim?  [تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes identify different triggers: actual drought or leprosy, social discord, dislike of the call, or fear of its warning; Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers discord.
- 36:18/q02/p1 Rain stopped and drought or related hardship was blamed on the messengers. — Muqātil gives withheld rain; al-Qurṭubī reports his duration as three years. Yaḥyā ibn Sallām in al-Māwardī locates the possible drought in Antioch. Al-Naqshbandī in al-Būrsawī adds that calamities began after the envoys arrived and the people told them to leave. Al-Biqāʿī mentions drought, hardship and other trials, explaining that the arrival of the righteous becomes blessing or punishment according to the community's response. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and others report this as one explanation. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النقشبندي); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 36:18/q02/p2 They disliked the messengers' religion, message or apparent strangeness and treated what offended them as ominous. — Al-Māwardī proposes aversion to the invited religion; al-Bayḍāwī says the message seemed strange, offensive and repellent; al-Nasafī ties it to dislike of their religion. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī explain the ignorant habit of calling desired things auspicious and disliked things ominous even when real benefit and harm run otherwise. Al-Rāzī says the claim treats the envoys as a renewed cause of trouble and invokes the proverb that a false oath leaves homes desolate. Al-Ālūsī places the accusation after the opponents' arguments failed. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:18/q02/p3 Preaching caused social division and turmoil in the town. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya judges this likeliest and compares Quraysh's omen about Muḥammad with the charge addressed to Moses. Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's preference and cites 7:131 and 4:78. Al-Ālūsī says the wording implies the preaching triggered the discord. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إبن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 36:18/q02/p4 Leprosy spread rapidly after they rejected the messengers. — Some interpreters in Ibn ʿAṭiyya report the outbreak; Abū Ḥayyān mentions increased leprosy, and al-Ālūsī transmits the report as an alternative to withheld rain. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(آخر)]
- 36:18/q02/p5 They recoiled from the messengers' warning of harm if they did not believe. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as an alternative explanation of their fearful aversion to the call. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:18/q03 What does the omen claim teach about superstition and good omens?  [تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ · theology]
- 36:18/q03/p1 Superstition attributes good or harm to what people favor or dislike; a good omen rests on good opinion of God. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī describe desire rather than actual good or harm driving these judgments. Al-Būrsawī says the Prophet liked good omens but disliked superstition because the former rests on good opinion of God and the latter relies on something besides Him. He reports the Prophet's exchange with Burayda ibn Aslam on the road to Medina: hearing Burayda's name, he told Abū Bakr their affair had cooled and gone well; hearing his lineage, he said they were safe from enemy plotting, using the wordplays برد أمرنا وصلح and سلمنا من كيدهم. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:18/q04 What should someone do when an omen enters the heart?  [تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ · theology]
- 36:18/q04/p1 Affirm servitude to God and His power, deny that anything else brings good or removes evil, then proceed. — Al-Būrsawī cites a prophetic hadith prescribing these words and continuing one's course. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 36:18/q05 What legal judgment is reported for acting on bird omens?  [تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ · law]
- 36:18/q05/p1 Some jurists deem predicting death from a bird's cry, or turning back from travel because of one, unbelief. — Al-Būrsawī reports examples of an owl or other bird's cry taken to predict a patient's death and of a magpie's call causing a traveler to return. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 36:18/q06 What must the messengers stop doing under لَئِن لَّمْ تَنتَهُوا?  [لَئِن لَّمْ تَنتَهُوا · interpretation]
- 36:18/q06/p1 Stop claiming a divine mission and preaching to the town, especially speaking against its idols or religion. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī say to stop speaking; al-Ṭabarī specifies claiming to be sent and forbidding idol worship; al-Wāḥidī glosses the condition as simply ceasing; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Durr, al-Būrsawī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī specify preaching or delivering the message. Al-Būrsawī adds stopping this claim and falling silent about the people, with a Persian gloss of desisting from preaching. Al-Ālūsī says to stop making this statement, while al-Māwardī reports Qatāda's warning not to leave their religion. Al-Biqāʿī reports their demand that the envoys stop their summons. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أهل القرية); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(النقشبندي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 36:18/q07 What force does the oath in لَئِن لَّمْ تَنتَهُوا add?  [لَئِن لَّمْ تَنتَهُوا · rhetoric]
- 36:18/q07/p1 The speakers swear they will persist in carrying out their threat if the call continues. — Al-Biqāʿī says they insist under oath that they will not relent. Al-Būrsawī expands it as 'By God, if you do not stop this claim.' [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أهل القرية); BURSEVI]

## 36:18/q08 What does لَنَرْجُمَنَّكُمْ threaten?  [لَنَرْجُمَنَّكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether rajm is the physical act of stone throwing, its result of killing, a figurative verbal assault, or expulsion; reports attributed to Qatāda, al-Suddī, Mujāhid and al-Farrāʾ differ.
- 36:18/q08/p1 To kill the messengers; rajm can denote killing without specifying the method. — Al-Ḥasan and others in Yaḥyā, al-Suddī in Yaḥyā and al-Māwardī, al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī's other interpreters, al-Dāmghānī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī give this gloss. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās say most Qurʾānic uses mean killing; al-Farrāʾ cites 11:91, and al-Dāmghānī also cites 44:20. Ibn Qutayba explains the figurative extension from killing by stones, mentioning a report that the first murder used stones. Al-Biqāʿī allows brutal killing; al-Nasafī lists killing alongside expulsion and insult. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن وغيره,السدي); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 36:18/q08/p2 To stone the messengers to death with rocks. — Muqātil, al-Ḥasan in Yaḥyā, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya's Qatāda, al-Rāzī's literal alternative, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Ālūsī allow or specify lethal stoning. Al-Ālūsī reports Qatāda as killing with stones. Al-Rāzī says the next phrase can depict continued pelting until death, and al-Būrsawī says the threat does not end after one or two stones. Al-Rāzī elsewhere links rajīm to devils being driven with meteors by angels, extending the term to every evil rebel, with 26:116 and 36:18 cited. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن وغيره); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI]
- 36:18/q08/p3 To pelt or harm the messengers with stones, without stating that the pelting kills them. — Qatāda is reported by al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr as giving literal stone throwing. Al-Būrsawī gives the same physical act; al-Biqāʿī allows pelting until the envoys stop, and al-Ālūsī says it may instead mean harming them by stoning. Al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān mention stoning without specifying death. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 36:18/q08/p4 To curse, insult or revile the messengers verbally. — Al-Māwardī reports al-Naqqāsh as saying cursing and harming. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr report Mujāhid; al-Ālūsī identifies a report via ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd and says Mujāhid generalized this sense to rajm throughout the Qurʾān. Al-Rāzī says physical beating would then escalate the verbal threat. Al-Qurṭubī reports an anonymous gloss; al-Nasafī and al-Biqāʿī allow insult among alternatives. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); TABRISI(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 36:18/q08/p5 To expel the messengers. — Al-Nasafī lists expulsion as one possible force of the threat, alongside killing and insulting. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 36:18/q09 What is the عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ threatened by the townspeople?  [عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the threatened suffering is generic, precedes death, is death itself, is beating or burning, or denotes the Fire.
- 36:18/q09/p1 A painful affliction or severe punishment, with no particular method specified. — Muqātil calls it a painful affliction; al-Ṭabarī's Qatāda and al-Baghawī give a painful penalty; Ibn Kathīr calls it severe. Al-Qurṭubī's anonymous alternatives include painful punishment itself. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 36:18/q09/p2 Painful torture before the messengers are killed, possibly flaying, mutilation or crucifixion. — Yaḥyā says the suffering precedes killing; al-Māwardī proposes torture before death. Al-Qurṭubī's anonymous alternatives name flaying, mutilation and crucifixion as examples. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 36:18/q09/p3 Death itself. — Al-Māwardī proposes this; al-Qurṭubī includes killing among the anonymous glosses. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 36:18/q09/p4 Painful beating or bodily assault. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the gloss to exegetes; al-Rāzī makes beating the physical escalation after verbal rajm. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL]
- 36:18/q09/p5 Burning. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Qatāda's explanation; al-Ālūsī reports al-Baḥr's same gloss. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]
- 36:18/q09/p6 The Fire, as the most severe punishment. — Al-Nasafī explicitly identifies it with the Fire. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 36:18/q10 How do stoning and painful punishment relate within the threat?  [لَنَرْجُمَنَّكُمْ وَلَيَمَسَّنَّكُم مِّنَّا عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ · interpretation]
turns on: The relationship depends on whether rajm means words or stones, and whether the second punishment precedes, follows or describes the stoning.
- 36:18/q10/p1 Verbal cursing is followed by physical beating and pain. — Al-Rāzī gives this when rajm is read figuratively as insult, so the second clause escalates to bodily violence. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 36:18/q10/p2 The second clause intensifies literal stoning: stones keep coming until death or themselves inflict painful torment. — Al-Rāzī reads the next phrase as continued pelting until the messengers die. Al-Būrsawī says the attackers would not stop at one or two stones, or that stoning itself is the painful suffering. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 36:18/q10/p3 The threat combines lethal stoning with painful torment before death or, if the victim survives, after it. — Yaḥyā and al-Māwardī place torment before killing; al-Ālūsī allows stoning to death or excruciating punishment for survivors. Al-Qurṭubī's account allows torture before killing, including flaying, mutilation or crucifixion. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:18/q10/p4 The two threats can describe bodily and spiritual harm, with their assignments reversible. — Al-Ālūsī reports both allocations without choosing between them. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:18/q11 What does أَلِيمٌ mean grammatically?  [أَلِيمٌ · grammar]
turns on: Whether فعيل here has the rarer causative force of مفعل or the common possessive force of فاعل.
- 36:18/q11/p1 Pain-causing, with the فعيل form functioning like مفعل. — Al-Rāzī reports this as the rarer morphological analysis. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 36:18/q11/p2 Possessing pain, with فعيل functioning like فاعل. — Al-Rāzī calls this pattern common and compares 'a pleased evening' in 69:21. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 36:18/q12 What does مِّنَّا add to the threatened punishment?  [مِّنَّا · rhetoric]
- 36:18/q12/p1 The townspeople stress that they themselves will administer the punishment. — Al-Biqāʿī says the explicit source of the punishment emphasizes their personal agency. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:18/q13 Why say وَلَيَمَسَّنَّكُم for the threatened suffering?  [وَلَيَمَسَّنَّكُم · rhetoric]
- 36:18/q13/p1 Touch marks the punishment's effect actually taking hold of its victims. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts touching with mere contact or action, which need not produce its intended effect. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:18/q14 How is the townspeople's omen claim answered in the next verse?  [تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether the answer emphasizes the people's deeds and disbelief, their allotted good and evil, or the certainty of decree.
- 36:18/q14/p1 Their bad omen lies in their own deeds or disbelief, and they wrongly take God's reminder as ominous. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq and Ibn Abī Ḥātim report Qatāda's explanation; al-Thaʿlabī gives the envoys' answer as their disbelief, while he reports Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk as glossing their portent as their allotted good and evil and Qatāda as their deeds. Al-Durr repeats Qatāda's account that they regarded the reminder of God as ill-omened. Iṣlāḥī compares Pharaoh's people: calamities were warnings of their own deeds, yet they blamed Moses and his followers. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس والضحاك,قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:18/q14/p2 What is decreed for people will come upon them. — Al-Durr reports Mujāhid's explanation alongside his gloss of the stoning threat as reviling. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 36:18/q15 What comparable Qurʾānic omen claims illuminate this one?  [تَطَيَّرْنَا بِكُمْ · links]
turns on: The notes compare different precedents rather than dispute the availability of those comparisons.
- 36:18/q15/p1 Pharaoh's people blamed Moses and his followers for hardship, though their calamities called them to heed. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 7:131; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān compare Moses' opponents, and al-Ālūsī repeats Ibn ʿAṭiyya's link. Iṣlāḥī cites 7:130–131: drought and reduced fruit were warnings, yet Pharaoh's people took good times as their own due and blamed Moses for calamity, saying his message angered their deities; they also threatened stoning and harm, allegedly to defend those deities. Al-Biqāʿī notes that this verse's explicit wording differs from 7:131. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(إبن عطية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:18/q15/p2 Meccan polytheists similarly blamed Muḥammad for their misfortune. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 4:78; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī connect the two claims. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's comparison to both Pharaoh's people and Meccan polytheists. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عطية)]
- 36:18/q15/p3 The wording can also be compared with the omen speech in al-Naml. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts this passage's explicit wording with 27:47 and 7:131. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:18/q16 How does this threat fit the passage's dialogue and narrative form?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: One account tracks the exchange's escalation; another describes its implied question-and-answer structure.
- 36:18/q16/p1 The people escalate from denying the mission and oath to alleging an omen and openly threatening the envoys. — Al-Rāzī traces the reply after 36:14–16 as preaching intensifies; al-Ālūsī puts the omen accusation after their arguments fail. Iṣlāḥī recounts rejection of two envoys and a third helper, blame for warning signs, threats and destruction. Kuranyolu says 36:14–19 displays persistent disbelief through insults and threats. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:18/q16/p2 The narrative advances by implied questions and answers, including the arrival and this threat. — Al-Jurjānī says the pattern runs throughout the passage and compares 15:57–58, with references to 36:13, 15–17 and 19–20. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 36:18/q17 What historical reports frame the town's confrontation with the messengers?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ about whether the king believed, whether and when the envoys reached him, and the circumstances of their confrontation.
- 36:18/q17/p1 Shimʿun brought the king and some subjects to belief, while others remained unbelievers. — Al-Ṭabrisī says Shimʿun saw his words affecting the king and called him to God. A report through al-ʿAyyāshī from Abū Jaʿfar and Abū ʿAbd Allāh says God sent two envoys and then a third; Jesus sent Shimʿun to rescue them; the resurrected man was the king's son, after which the king and his people believed. Wahb's version has Jesus send Shimʿun al-Ṣafā under cover; the envoys restored a blind youth's sight and raised a man dead seven days, prompting some to believe. [3 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله عليهما السلام,وهب بن منبه)]
- 36:18/q17/p2 The king disbelieved and joined the people in resolving to kill the messengers. — Ibn Isḥāq, reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, adds that Ḥabīb ran from the city's far gate to urge obedience to the envoys. [1 notes: TABATABAI(ابن إسحاق)]
- 36:18/q17/p3 Jesus sent two disciples to Antioch, who healed Ḥabīb's son before the king interrogated and beat them. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports al-Majmaʿ's account: news of the envoys' miracles spread after the healing. [1 notes: TABATABAI(المجمع)]
- 36:18/q17/p4 The messengers did not reach the king before imprisonment and flogging; Shimʿun later entered the court secretly. — Wahb ibn Munabbih, as reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, says Shimʿun befriended the court and arranged a public test with a blind youth and a seven-day dead man. [1 notes: TABATABAI(وهب بن منبه)]
- 36:18/q17/p5 The envoys had warned the people for ten years. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as another account alongside Muqātil's three-year drought. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 36:18/q18 What warning does the town's rejection and threat convey in the sūrah?  [* · links]
- 36:18/q18/p1 The town's fate warns deniers of God's messengers, especially Quraysh, and encourages Muḥammad's followers. — Iṣlāḥī recounts the town rejecting two messengers and their third helper, blaming bad luck, threatening them and being destroyed, as a warning to Quraysh. Kuranyolu says the persistent rejection of three prophets and scorn for, and likely murder of, the believer supporting them is set before Muḥammad's Meccan opponents; it also strengthens his followers. Al-Biqāʿī says rejecting righteous visitors can turn their arrival into punishment, whereas faith and good conduct make it a blessing. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 36:18/q19 How is the people's own portent word read in the following verse?  [* · readings]
- 36:18/q19/p1 Al-Ḥasan and al-Aʿraj read طيركم in place of طائركم. — Al-Thaʿlabī transmits both readers' variant for the messengers' reply to this omen claim. [1 notes: THALABI(الحسن والأعرج)]
