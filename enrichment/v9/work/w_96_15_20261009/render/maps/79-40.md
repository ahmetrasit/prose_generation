# 79:40: 20 questions

## 79:40/q01 What is the feared station of the Lord?  [مَقَامَ رَبِّهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether maqam points to the servant's future standing, a present fear that checks sin, God's own lordly station, or an emphatic expression for God himself; Tabatabai dismisses the future-standing interpretation.
- 79:40/q01/p1 Standing before God at the resurrection for judgment and account, recalled even when one is tempted to sin. — Al-Rabi locates it on the Day of Reckoning; al-Nahhas and al-Tabrisi specify account for sins and duties. Muqatil describes intended sin abandoned on remembering judgment; al-Baydawi and Bursevi connect the fear to knowledge of origin and return. Bint al-Shati allows an act, time, or place of standing, citing 14:14 and 55:46; Islahi cites 83:3-4. Al-Biqa'i includes the encounter, its place, and its time and says belief in resurrection is presupposed. Study Quran also allows the Lord's own station. [24 notes: NAHHAS; SULAMI(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 79:40/q01/p2 Fearing God in this life at the point of sin and then desisting. — Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, and Abu Hayyan phrase the fear as present fear of God; al-Kalbi and Ibn Abbas extend the example to someone able to sin privately. Al-Baqir promises Paradise to one who leaves a possible sin for fear of God and His prohibition. Al-Qurtubi cites 55:46. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي وابن عباس,مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(محمد الباقر)]
- 79:40/q01/p3 God's station of lordship, whose knowledge, power, dominance, mercy, and wrath inspire fear. — Tabatabai calls it the source of forgiveness for believers and punishment for deniers, citing 20:81-82 and 15:49-50; he takes al-Sadiq's saying that awareness God sees, hears, and knows deeds restrains wrongdoing as support. Qatada says believers fear a station belonging to God. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI(جعفر الصادق); TABATABAI+]
- 79:40/q01/p4 Simply fearing the Lord, with 'station' functioning as an emphatic addition. — Alusi reports the alternative and points to discussion in al-Rahman; Tabatabai compares the idiom 'honor his abode.' [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 79:40/q02 What can maqam mean and how does its construction work?  [مَقَامَ رَبِّهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: Whether maqam contributes standing, place, time, or extended rank, or is emphatic and adds no separate referent.
- 79:40/q02/p1 An act, place, or time of standing, with the standing or its site linked to the Lord. — Bursevi lists a verbal noun and the servants' place of judgment and recompense. Bint al-Shati allows action, time, and place. Alusi compares the possessive construction to رقود حلب. Tabatabai says the original place or time sense extends to state or rank, citing 5:107, 10:71, and 37:164. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 79:40/q02/p2 An emphatic insertion, leaving the sense 'fears his Lord.' — Bursevi lists this grammatical possibility; Alusi and Tabatabai report it as another reading. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 79:40/q03 Why is the station attributed to the Lord?  [مَقَامَ رَبِّهِۦ · rhetoric]
- 79:40/q03/p1 The attribution magnifies the station and evokes God's majesty and awe. — Ibn Atiyya says it magnifies the terror and effect of resurrection; Abu Hayyan and Bint al-Shati say the possessive evokes awe. Ibn Ashur treats the station as a figurative way of conveying divine majesty, naming the standing place for what is associated with it, with 14:14 and 55:46. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:40/q04 What does al-hawa mean here?  [ٱلْهَوَىٰ · meaning]
- 79:40/q04/p1 The self's inclination or appetite, usually harmful or blameworthy in this context. — Damghani glosses desire or lust and cites 20:16, 28:50, 7:176, 25:43, and 46:23. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say it usually bears a blameworthy sense. Bint al-Shati says inclination can draw to good or evil, likens its origin to a raptor's plunge, and gathers Quranic examples of following it into misguidance. Ibn Ashur specifies appetite or anger contrary to truth and full benefit, takes 'without guidance' in 28:50 as emphatic, and says the article denotes the class. Al-Raghib explains the name by the way it drags a follower to worldly weakness and an afterlife abyss. [12 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 79:40/q05 What does restraining the self from desire require?  [وَنَهَى ٱلنَّفْسَ عَنِ ٱلْهَوَىٰ · interpretation]
- 79:40/q05/p1 Keeping the self from forbidden acts and desires, through obedience, patience, and discipline. — Tabari joins obligations and avoiding sins to disliked desires; Wahidi adds desires once followed before Islam. Al-Kashshaf calls for patience and choosing good; Ibn Kathir describes returning oneself to obedience. Al-Burhan says inclination itself cannot be forbidden, so following it is meant. Alusi warns against worldly ornament; Bursevi includes natural appetites for their harmful consequences. Al-Biqa'i says every desire must be checked, citing the Fire's enclosure by appetites and law's opposition to the lower self. Tabatabai distinguishes restraining desire from never sinning: weakness or ignorance may cause a sin without arrogance, and God forgives, citing 53:31-32 and 4:31. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 79:40/q06 What is the self that must be restrained?  [ٱلنَّفْسَ · referent]
- 79:40/q06/p1 The person's lower self, described as commanding evil and drawing one toward harmful cravings. — Al-Kashshaf and Nasafi identify the commanding soul. Ibn Ashur says portraying one's self as a second person urging sin captures inner dialogue, illustrated by Urwa ibn Udhayna's poem about heart and conscience. He takes the article as generic. Bursevi describes humans as between divine and created, angelic and animal aspects; the higher restrains the lower, and he applies this to the Prophet's human and angelic or unitive stations. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عروة بن أذينة); BURSEVI]

## 79:40/q07 What does naha add to the call to restrain desire?  [وَنَهَى · meaning]
- 79:40/q07/p1 It evokes reason and sound judgment answering the self's impulses. — Bint al-Shati cites 20:54 and 20:128 for the association with intellect. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 79:40/q08 Who receives Paradise for this fear and restraint?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the general promise directly describes every person with the qualities or the two classes are exemplary poles with mixed cases left open.
- 79:40/q08/p1 Whoever has the stated fear and restrains desire, fulfilling duties and avoiding sin, has Paradise as refuge. — Tabari places the abode on Resurrection Day; al-Farra applies it to anyone bearing the qualities. Ibn Abbas says the promise, first linked to Musab, extends to all later people with that quality. Al-Baqir says a person able to sin who refrains for fear of God is rewarded with Paradise. Qashani says arrival is according to rank. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء,مقاتل); JISHUMI; QASHANI; TABATABAI(محمد الباقر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 79:40/q08/p2 The two outcomes describe exemplary unbelieving wrongdoers and faithful pious people; mixed or weak cases remain subject to divine forgiveness or intercession. — Tabatabai says the passage leaves the fate of mixed deeds to God and points to forgiveness for sins arising from weakness or ignorance, citing 53:31-32 and 4:31. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 79:40/q09 How does one overcome desire, and what results from doing so?  [وَنَهَى ٱلنَّفْسَ عَنِ ٱلْهَوَىٰ · interpretation]
- 79:40/q09/p1 Opposing desire disciplines and heals the self and leads to right judgment and good deeds. — Ibn al-Sammak calls opposition a cure; Junayd's stranger says the illness is cured by opposing desire and had heard the same reply seven times. Al-Biqa'i repeats Junayd's maxim. Sages advise opposing desire to find what is right; Imran and Abu Imran al-Mirtalli warn in poetry of ruin from obeying it. Al-Fudayl calls opposition the best deed, and al-Jurayri makes sustained struggle the response to this divine address. Sahl says full freedom requires perfected discipline and character, attained only by prophets and some truthful people. [16 notes: TUSTARI(ابن السماك); SULAMI(الجريري,الجنيد,الفضيل,سهل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفضيل,بعض الحكماء,سهل التستري); QURTUBI-FULL(سهل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض الحكماء، عمران الميرتلي، الفضيل); BIQAI-FULL(الجنيد); BURSEVI(سهل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمران الميرتلي,بعض الحكماء)]
- 79:40/q09/p2 Desire opposed to truth is spiritually dangerous: it turns people from truth and can bind or ruin them. — Abu Bakr al-Warraq calls truth-opposing desire the vilest thing in either world. Ibn Masud contrasts an age guided by truth with one in which desire guides truth. The Prophet and Ali warn that desire turns people from truth and long hope makes them forget the afterlife. Bursevi's master reduces the seven desires of 3:14 to worldly play, then desire itself, saying escape frees one from every bond. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); BURSEVI(بعض الكبار,رسول الله); TABATABAI(علي)]

## 79:40/q10 What inner state does fear of God produce?  [خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِۦ · inward]
- 79:40/q10/p1 True fear sustains grief and turns a person away from comfort until safety appears. — Dhu al-Nun describes ten stations, including sorrow, poverty, awe, tears and supplication, flight from ease, bewilderment, a dissolved heart, troubled life, and agreement with grief. Another Sullami report says fear distracts from every comfort until safety from fear appears. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم,ذو النون)]

## 79:40/q11 How is fear and restraint read as an inward ascent?  [* · inward]
- 79:40/q11/p1 One ascends to the heart, witnesses divine governance, restrains desire through awe, and reaches a paradise proportionate to rank. — Qashani gives the sequence as an inward account of the verse. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:40/q12 How do these qualities answer the preceding description of Hell's people?  [* · links]
- 79:40/q12/p1 Fear counters transgression and restraining desire counters preference for worldly life. — Razi says knowledge causes fear, citing 35:28, and fear causes restraint, so the cause comes first; he also says the paired qualities cover all virtues against all vices, citing 79:17 and 79:38. Al-Biqa'i says each named trait implies the opposing trait. Ibn Ashur and Study Quran affirm the parallel; Study Quran cites 45:23 for caprice leading astray. Bursevi says fear comes first as a motive for obedience, followed by hope and love for ordinary, elect, and most elect people respectively. Ibn Ashur exempts worldly benefits that do not cost the afterlife and cites 28:77. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 79:40/q13 What does the contrast between fear and rebellion imply about human action and recompense?  [* · theology]
- 79:40/q13/p1 Humans perform obedience and sin, and the paired outcomes assign Paradise to obedience and Hell to rebellion. — Jishumi argues against determinism from the attribution of rebellion and fear to people and from reward and punishment for their choices; restraining desire includes preferring obedience and avoiding sin. [3 notes: JISHUMI]

## 79:40/q14 What occasion or persons do reports associate with these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the named people, battle, and event: Uhud killing and protection, Badr capture and binding or killing, an Abu Jahl contrast, or Abu Bakr's food.
- 79:40/q14/p1 Musab ibn Umayr is the model of fear and restraint, linked with his brother Abu Aziz and his defense of the Prophet at Uhud. — Ata, al-Kalbi, and Muqatil name Musab for the restraint clause; Ibn Abbas extends it beyond him. Al-Kashshaf and Razi relate that he killed his brother at Uhud; Alusi calls the brother Abu Aziz and says he was a wealthy mother's son. Qurtubi's Ibn Abbas report contrasts a worldly brother with Musab. Reports say Musab shielded the Prophet until arrows or spears pierced him; Alusi adds that he died, and the Prophet entrusted him to God and recalled his cloaks and golden sandal strap. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,عطاء والكلبي ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الكشاف)]
- 79:40/q14/p2 Musab and his brother Amir figure in a Badr capture: Musab orders the captive bound for ransom despite their kinship. — Al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas says Amir was captured, identified himself as Musab's brother, and at first received kind treatment. Musab denied the brotherhood, told the Ansar to bind him, and cited their mother's wealth. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس)]
- 79:40/q14/p3 Musab killed his brother Amir at Badr. — Qurtubi reports this as a distinct version from the capture-and-binding account. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 79:40/q14/p4 The paired verses concern Abu Jahl and Musab. — Qurtubi reports Ibn Abbas naming Abu Jahl al-Makhzumi and Musab al-Abdari; Alusi reports another version from Ibn Abbas. Alusi also reports that the first verse was instead assigned to al-Nadr and his son al-Harith. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 79:40/q14/p5 The verse was revealed about Abu Bakr after he expelled food bought with his servant's fortune-telling proceeds. — Al-Suddi says Abu Bakr ate unknowingly, vomited on learning the source, and asked God to hold any remainder in his veins; then the verse was revealed. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(السدي)]

## 79:40/q15 How does this verse fit the surah's account of resurrection and final outcomes?  [* · links]
- 79:40/q15/p1 The surah's resurrection proof leads to judgment and a contrast between loss and salvation that urges preparation for lasting reward. — Al-Biqa'i says the surah establishes resurrection and gathering after preceding surahs, with oaths marking a decisive climax. Ibn Ashur says warning and glad tidings follow proof because judgment follows resurrection. Tabatabai says Hell's class comes first because the address targets polytheists. Kuran Yolu calls the verses a summary of negative and positive attitudes. Study Quran reports a view that the surah continues the warning of 78:40. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 79:40/q16 Where else is the fear of standing before the Lord explained?  [مَقَامَ رَبِّهِۦ · links]
- 79:40/q16/p1 Al-Rahman 55:46 supplies the earlier explanation or parallel. — Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi direct the reader there; Qurtubi, Ibn Ashur, Bint al-Shati, and Study Quran also cite it in discussing the station. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:40/q17 What rhetorical work does portraying the self and the two outcomes perform?  [* · rhetoric]
- 79:40/q17/p1 Portraying the self as another person dramatizes inner resistance; the paired outcomes collect qualities before dividing people by fate. — Ibn Ashur uses Urwa ibn Udhayna's heart-and-conscience poem for the inner dialogue and describes the remembering/Garden pair as a collect-then-divide structure; he says 'the self' is generic like 'the abode.' [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عروة بن أذينة)]

## 79:40/q18 Can figurative language make a comparison appear literal?  [* · rhetoric]
- 79:40/q18/p1 Yes: wording can suppress awareness of a simile or metaphor and assert its imagined result as literal fact. — Al-Jurjani distinguishes an imaginative type from an earlier example explained by rationale. He cites Abu Tammam's ignorant man treating rising in rank as physical ascent, Ibn al-Rumi's Nubakht family climbing to the stars to learn astronomy, and the beautiful woman called a sun who shades someone from the sun. Naming a person sun, moon, sea, or lion can erase the felt simile. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 79:40/q19 Does rhetorical emphasis occur, and how is an expression identified as emphatic?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether repetition has a recognized rhetorical role and, if so, whether the second occurrence is literal or figurative; the author also disputes how to test for independent meaning.
- 79:40/q19/p1 Emphasis is recognized in the Quran, Sunna, and Arabic; verbal emphasis repeats a word or synonym and is literal when no independent sense can be assigned. — Al-Burhan reports the majority's acceptance and defenders' appeal to Arabic eloquence and benefits. He says denying emphasis would leave its name without a referent; distinguishes verbal and semantic forms; and prefers the literal view since calling a second identical 'hurry' figurative would also make the first figurative. He says emphasis is against the default and rejects an unspecified criterion treating any possible meaning as sufficient. [7 notes: BURHAN(الأصحاب,جمهور الأمة); BURHAN+]
- 79:40/q19/p2 Emphasis does not occur; every expression must contribute new meaning. — Some deny it in the Quran, Sunna, and language; critics object that repetition is pointless and violates concision, supposedly signaling inability to express a meaning otherwise. [2 notes: BURHAN(الملحدون,قوم); BURHAN-]
- 79:40/q19/p3 Emphasis is figurative because its second occurrence contributes no new meaning. — Al-Tartusi records this classification by some; al-Burhan's repeated-'hurry' argument is directed against it. [1 notes: BURHAN(قوم); BURHAN-]

## 79:40/q20 Does the singular Paradise support a rhyme explanation?  [* · rhetoric]
- 79:40/q20/p1 The singular Paradise in 79:41 may have prompted al-Farra's rhyme explanation, but the dual 'both with branches' remains an objection. — Al-Burhan raises the singular as a possible motive but retains the dual counterexample, citing 79:40-41 and 20:117. [1 notes: BURHAN]
