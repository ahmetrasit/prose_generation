# 12:32: 29 questions

## 12:32/q01 What does 'that is the one' identify?  [فَذَٰلِكُنَّ ٱلَّذِى لُمْتُنَّنِى فِيهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative indicates Joseph or love.
- 12:32/q01/p1 Joseph, whose beauty explains her passion. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī and others identify Yusuf. Al-Rāzī, citing al-Kashshāf, contrasts the imagined Canaanite slave with the man actually seen. Ibn al-Anbārī says he had left; alternatively an omitted 'this is' allows his presence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says pointing to what they see is strongest proof. [34 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)+]
- 12:32/q01/p2 Her love for Joseph is the referent. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya offers love as the referent of both dhālika and fīhi; al-Qurṭubī and al-Samīn report it. Al-Ālūsī calls it remote; al-Samīn objects that a demonstrative must refer to something present. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 12:32/q02 Why the distant demonstrative and plural address?  [فَذَٰلِكُنَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether distance signals distinction or separation.
- 12:32/q02/p1 It elevates Joseph's rare beauty and identifies the previously unknown slave. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī see exaltation; al-Ālūsī also notes his absence when first blamed. Al-Rāzī favors the Canaanite-slave explanation. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 12:32/q02/p2 It distances Joseph or the women after their disturbance. — Abū Ḥayyān says the kāf addresses the women and distance may give them room to recover; al-Ālūsī finds keeping Joseph physically away remote. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 12:32/q03 What does the opening fāʾ do?  [فَذَٰلِكُنَّ · grammar]
- 12:32/q03/p1 It introduces the explanation of their amazement. — Al-Ālūsī calls it explanatory; Ibn ʿĀshūr paraphrases: if he is the angel you claim, this is the man you blamed me over. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:32/q04 What were the women blaming her for?  [لُمْتُنَّنِى فِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether blame concerns emotion or deliberate action.
- 12:32/q04/p1 Her love and infatuation with Joseph. — Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly says love. Al-Ṭabarī, Muqātil and others agree. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Zarkashī construe fī causally; the former supplies 'matter/love of him' and cites a hadith. Al-Rāzī says one glance shocked the women more than long familiarity shocked her. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 12:32/q04/p2 Her deliberate attempted seduction. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī say custom supplies the omitted act: involuntary love is not ordinarily blamed, whereas seduction is. Kuran Yolu says the women condemned attempted adultery. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 12:32/q05 What does lawm mean?  [لُمْتُنَّنِى · meaning]
- 12:32/q05/p1 Reproach: attributing blameworthy ugliness, opposite praise. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī give the lexical account; al-Jishumī adds contemptuous disparagement. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 12:32/q06 Why did she speak openly to the women?  [قَالَتْ فَذَٰلِكُنَّ وَلَقَدْ رَٰوَدتُّهُۥ · interpretation]
- 12:32/q06/p1 Their shock at Joseph's beauty vindicated her and invited sympathy or help. — Al-Wāḥidī says she exposed them to his beauty until they cut their hands. Al-Bayḍāwī says she wanted them to soften Joseph. Al-Rāzī adds his angelic inaccessibility sharpened longing. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their praise was initially involuntary; she invoked it afterward. Study Quran notes their one glance against her daily proximity. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 12:32/q06/p2 She no longer feared blame and delighted in confessing. — Al-Qurṭubī contrasts her earlier private approach; al-Ālūsī says her oath makes delight in admission public. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:32/q06/p3 Her pointing answers an omitted narrative question. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls direct sight strongest proof and compares other Qurʾanic examples. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:32/q07 What happened to the women, and what did they tell Joseph?  [فَذَٰلِكُنَّ مَآ ءَامُرُهُۥ · reports]
- 12:32/q07/p1 They cut their hands or fingers, overwhelmed by his beauty. — Zayd ibn Aslam specifies citron and lack of pain; al-Suddī reports the noble-angel exclamation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says akbarnahu implies awe and the intensive cutting form repeated cuts. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي,زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); TABATABAI]
- 12:32/q07/p2 They urged him to obey her. — Al-Wāḥidī says they framed him as oppressor; al-Baghawī identifies his mistress; Abū Ḥayyān links their advice to his plural reply in 12:33. [3 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 12:32/q08 What does her admission show about Joseph?  [وَلَقَدْ رَٰوَدتُّهُۥ عَن نَّفْسِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 12:32/q08/p1 She solicited sex, he resisted, and her testimony establishes his innocence. — Muqātil names intercourse. Al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī regard her words as proof against allegations around 12:24. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds the child witness, torn shirt and door race as later signs. Al-Ālūsī rebukes contrary scandalous tales. Al-Baghawī says Joseph chose prison rather than sin. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 12:32/q08/p2 Her 'command' expresses social power and continuing pursuit. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says she asserted status in his hearing; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says she disclosed settled intent before the women. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:32/q09 Why swear to the confession?  [وَلَقَدْ رَٰوَدتُّهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether oath emphasizes her admission or his resistance.
- 12:32/q09/p1 To show delight in openly admitting known pursuit. — Al-Ālūsī notes the women already knew. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:32/q09/p2 To stress his improbable resistance under pressure. — Al-Ālūsī reports youth, proximity and pressure as this rationale, but calls it unpersuasive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 12:32/q10 What does fa-staʿṣama mean?  [فَٱسْتَعْصَمَ · meaning]
turns on: The morphological force of istifʿāl and whether protection is sought or achieved.
- 12:32/q10/p1 He resisted and refrained from sin. — Ibn Qutayba, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda and others gloss refusal. Al-Aṣmaʿī derives ʿiṣma from prevention; al-Ālūsī limits it to lexical restraint rather than later technical doctrine. Qatāda's variant wording fa-staʿṣā has the same force. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، المفسرون، أهل اللغة,الأصمعي,قتادة); JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(الصوفيون); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:32/q10/p2 It intensifies vigilant, already achieved restraint. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites istamsaka, istawsaʿa, istajmaʿa and istafḥala; al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿĀshūr follow the force. Abū Ḥayyān objects that these analogies largely match iftiʿāl and istafḥala matches tafaʿʿala. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's claim that the refusal disproves sinful desire. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 12:32/q10/p3 He sought and held fast to protection. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he sought and clung to it; Abū Ḥayyān objects that seeking does not ensure success. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 12:32/q10/p4 It equals iʿtaṣama: protection actually occurred. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers grammarians' equivalence; al-Samīn reports his argument that iʿtaṣama entails success; al-Ālūsī reports a mystic version. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الصوفيون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 12:32/q10/p5 He fled after verbal refusal failed. — Al-Ālūsī reports escape as his protective act. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 12:32/q11 Did he loosen his trousers before resisting?  [فَٱسْتَعْصَمَ · reports]
turns on: Whether al-Suddī's added report is credible.

Al-Rāzī calls it an unsupported corrupt addition, and al-Ālūsī rebukes storytellers. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); DURR-FULL; RAZI-FULL(السدي); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 12:32/q11/p2 The verse establishes resistance without that addition. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī appeal to the wife's explicit confession and reject the garment tale. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 12:32/q12 What was her command?  [مَآ ءَامُرُهُۥ · referent]
- 12:32/q12/p1 To comply with her sexual demand. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies her invitation; Study Quran links 12:23; Muqātil specifies intercourse. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say calling it an order asserts her authority. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:32/q13 How is mā āmuruhu construed?  [مَآ ءَامُرُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is relative or a verbal noun.
- 12:32/q13/p1 Mā is relative, with omitted preposition or first object. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ālūsī compare amartuka al-khayr; Abū Ḥayyān instead omits Joseph as first object. Al-Samīn allows hāʾ to refer to mā or Yusuf. Ibn al-Munīr says omission has conventionalized the accusative pronoun; identical attached pronouns cannot combine. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:32/q13/p2 Mā is a verbal noun meaning fulfillment of her order. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī give this alternative; hāʾ refers to Joseph, and the ordered act is implicit or figuratively attributed. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:32/q14 What is the threat and its force?  [لَيُسْجَنَنَّ وَلَيَكُونًۭا مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰغِرِينَ · interpretation]
- 12:32/q14/p1 If Joseph refuses, she will imprison and disgrace him. — Al-Rāzī calls this potent pressure on a high-minded man. Al-Nasafī pictures thieves, fugitives, lost food and sleep, and coarse bedding. Al-Biqāʿī emphasizes shame and separation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says she asserts power over her husband by threatening directly; Islahi sees thwarted desire becoming revenge. [21 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:32/q14/p2 The women achieved apparent imprisonment but not corruption of his faith. — Al-Aṣamm contrasts the external punishment with the failure to turn Joseph religiously. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم)]

## 12:32/q15 How is the related noun sijn read and understood?  [لَيُسْجَنَنَّ · readings]
turns on: The vowel on sīn distinguishes the act of confinement from its place.
- 12:32/q15/p1 Yaʿqūb reads sijn with fatḥa as the verbal noun for imprisonment. — Al-Jishumī and Ibn ʿĀshūr report Yaʿqūb alone with fatḥa; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the regular verbal noun. [2 notes: JISHUMI(يعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:32/q15/p2 The other readers use kasra for the prison as a place. — Al-Jishumī contrasts the majority's place noun with Yaʿqūb's verbal noun and defines sijn as confinement; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies the prison building. [3 notes: JISHUMI(يعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:32/q16 How does this threat differ from her earlier one?  [لَيُسْجَنَنَّ وَلَيَكُونًۭا مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰغِرِينَ · links]
turns on: Which penalty and social setting carry the comparison.
- 12:32/q16/p1 It is harsher: she combines jail and abasement and threatens directly. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts her earlier appeal to her husband to choose prison or painful punishment; al-Ālūsī also reports comparison with 12:25. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI+]
- 12:32/q16/p2 Her mood changed from angry denial to hopeful public vindication. — Abū Ḥayyān says she initially sought painful punishment while denying pursuit, then threatened prison; al-Ālūsī notes alternative rankings of the penalties. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 12:32/q16/p3 She could now renew the advance before witnesses without fear of blame. — Al-Qurṭubī contrasts her earlier private approach. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 12:32/q17 Why the passive form for imprisonment?  [لَيُسْجَنَنَّ · rhetoric]
- 12:32/q17/p1 Royal speech omits the agent and may imply immediate enforcement. — Al-Ālūsī gives both explanations. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:32/q18 How do lām and the emphatic nūns function?  [وَلَئِن لَّمْ يَفْعَلْ لَيُسْجَنَنَّ وَلَيَكُونًۭا · grammar]
- 12:32/q18/p1 The oath lām requires an emphatic nūn on its nonassertive future response. — Sībawayh cites 12:32 for heavy and light forms; Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes introductory and response lāms. Al-Zarkashī explains omitted oath verb and preposition. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Sāmarrāʾī stress future warning; the latter distinguishes oath lām from ordinary emphasis. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURHAN; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 12:32/q18/p2 Other nonassertive settings allow nūn with differing degrees of license. — Sībawayh cites command, prohibition, prayer, urging interrogatives, proverbs and conditional poetry. Bare conditionals and indicative verbs need poetic necessity; mā strengthens license. He accepts Yūnus on hallā and alā, but prefers omission after rubbamā and ḥaythumā. Ibn al-Khurʿ, al-Aʿshā, Zuhayr, two Nābighas and Jadhīma supply verse examples. [16 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس); SIBAWAYH+]

## 12:32/q19 How is wa-layakūna read and written?  [وَلَيَكُونًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the codex alif rules out the doubled nūn.
- 12:32/q19/p1 It has light emphatic nūn, written and paused as alif. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Kashshāf prefer the light reading on codex grounds; Ibn al-Jawzī reports the objection to heavy nūn. Al-Akhfash, al-Ṭabarī and others compare 96:15 and al-Aʿshā's faʿbudā; Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī pause with alif. Sāmarrāʾī stresses that the sign is emphatic nūn, not true tanwīn. [24 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الأعشى); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(فرقة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 12:32/q19/p2 A group reads doubled heavy nūn. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report it. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn explain the alif as majority or pause spelling, while al-Zajjāj and others object that it contradicts the codex. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(فرقة); ZAJJAJ-; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)-; BAYDAWI-FULL-]

## 12:32/q20 Why a heavy nūn for prison and light one for humiliation?  [لَيُسْجَنَنَّ وَلَيَكُونًۭا · rhetoric]
- 12:32/q20/p1 Prison is more certain or weighty, so its threat is strengthened. — Al-Zarkashī magnifies prison; al-Biqāʿī notes separation from the beloved; al-Ālūsī contrasts certainty of jail and uncertainty of humiliation or the latter's dependence on the former. Sāmarrāʾī likewise ranks certainty. [4 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 12:32/q20/p2 Both nūns emphasize the threat, with the heavy one stronger. — Al-Khalīl says both forms emphasize; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī identify heavy then light. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they frighten Yusuf. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 12:32/q21 What are al-ṣāghirīn?  [مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰغِرِينَ · meaning]
- 12:32/q21/p1 People humiliated and low in rank. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and the exegetes gloss disgrace. Al-Wāḥidī cites 9:29; al-Ālūsī distinguishes rank from physical smallness. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'among' is stronger than simply calling him humiliated, citing 2:67 and 9:119; Asad notes the literal phrase. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]

## 12:32/q22 What inward sense does the threat have?  [لَيُسْجَنَنَّ وَلَيَكُونًۭا مِّنَ ٱلصَّٰغِرِينَ · inward]
- 12:32/q22/p1 The soul threatens the heart with lost sensory pleasure and bodily rank. — Al-Qāshānī explains seclusion as discipline of the heart after struggle with the soul: it sheds attachments, knowledge and unveilings toward annihilation, distinct from worship and ascetic training. He compares the Prophet's retreat to Ḥirāʾ. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 12:32/q23 Who caused the plot and imprisonment?  [لَيُسْجَنَنَّ · theology]
- 12:32/q23/p1 The women acted; God did not create their plot and jailing. — Al-Jishumī explicitly attributes the acts to them. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 12:32/q24 What associated grammatical principles do the cited sources discuss?  [* · grammar]
- 12:32/q24/p1 Possessive yāʾ and pronominal kāf have dialectal and pause variants. — Sībawayh retains alif before possessive yāʾ to avoid two vowelless sounds, though some use yāʾ; afʿā varies at pause. Feminine kāf has kasra; Tamīm and Asad use shīn in pause, others append sīn or shīn, and some add gender-marking vowels before hāʾ. Al-Khalīl notes rare ḍarabtīhi; Sībawayh prefers shorter forms and explains hāʾ's weakness. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 12:32/q24/p2 Reduced nouns pluralize by analogy with restored or sound patterns. — Sībawayh discusses yad, dam, ab, akh, hana, sanat, umma, arḍ, ḥurra, samāʾ and ahl, including alternate plurals and changed feminine stems; al-Khalīl and Yūnus explain arḍ and ḥurra, while al-Mukhabbal and al-Qattāl supply poetic evidence. [24 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,يونس)]
- 12:32/q24/p3 Verb form and time determine the matching negative particle. — Sībawayh pairs faʿala with lam, qad faʿala with lammā, laqad with mā, present ongoing with mā, future or oath lām with lā, and sawfa with lan. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 12:32/q24/p4 Specification addresses a selected person without summoning them. — Sībawayh explains omitted yāʾ and examples with anā and ayyuhā al-rajul. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 12:32/q24/p5 A verb requires a subject but need not require an object or willing agent. — Al-Rāzī uses qāma, māta and ḍaraba, contrasts verb-first with topic-first order, summarizes pronoun evidence that subject is integral, and notes antecedent order in ḍaraba ghulāmuhu Zaydan. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 12:32/q24/p6 Negation scope and particles depend on order, tense and continuity. — Al-Zarkashī distinguishes two denial scopes and rejects ʿAbd al-Qāhir's existential inference. Al-Khūʾī and al-Suyūṭī derive lā, mā, lam, lan and lammā by letter form and temporal force. [8 notes: BURHAN(الخوي,عبد القاهر); ITQAN(الخويي)]
- 12:32/q24/p7 Reason, law, custom or context can indicate ellipsis. — Al-Zarkashī cites asking the town's people, bans on consuming carrion, God's 'coming' and the monotheism argument; al-Suyūṭī requires contextual or verbal evidence. [5 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 12:32/q24/p8 Wa coordinates without order; fa gives immediate sequence, thumma delayed sequence. — Sāmarrāʾī gives these contrasts. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 12:32/q26 What ancillary literary and recitation judgments occur?  [* · reports]
- 12:32/q26/p1 Hayta cannot mean 'I prepared myself for you' in the cited dialogue. — Abū ʿAlī argues that sense would require hayta lī. [1 notes: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 12:32/q26/p2 Poetic merit depends on subtle sense, diction, context and judgment. — Al-Bāqillānī reports competing assessments of al-Buḥturī, defers to the poet, critiques heavy and awkward phrases, and praises a lightning line. ## 12:32/q27 How do neighboring episodes illuminate this verse?  [* · links]
- 12:32/q27/p1 Joseph's innocence is corroborated by signs; the Qurʾanic account differs from Genesis. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists child testimony, torn shirt, door race and the wife's admission. Asad says 12:26–34 are absent from Genesis, and the Qurʾanic narrative illustrates hidden divine direction, citing 2:216. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:32/q27/p2 The women's preceding exclamations show awe and exoneration. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares their tasbīḥ with 24:16 and explains 'noble angel' as their image of goodness, beauty, knowledge and joy. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 12:32/q27/p3 The later badā lahum takes an implied verbal noun as subject. — Al-Ṭabrisī rejects a whole prison clause as subject and cites a she-camel line. [2 notes: TABRISI(الشاعر)]
- 12:32/q27/p4 The wife may have persuaded her husband to doubt Joseph and imprison him. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests she sought cover and coercive punishment. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 12:32/q28 What wider principles about occasions, oaths and emphatic nūn are noted?  [* · sciences]
- 12:32/q28/p1 Occasions can disclose meaning or rhetoric, while a legislative trigger need not restrict a rule. — Al-Zarkashī stresses early reliance on occasions. Ibn ʿĀshūr classifies five types after chain review, including necessary clarifications (58:1; 2:104) and nonrestricting legislative triggers (2:196; 4:32). [7 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:32/q28/p2 Oaths can omit their verb, answer, sworn object or markers. — Al-Zarkashī discusses possible answers to 38:1 and parallels in 51:6, 63:1 and 85:4; Abū ʿAlī distinguishes expressions with and without overt response. [3 notes: BURHAN(أبو علي الفارسي)]
- 12:32/q28/p3 Heavy and light nūns both emphasize; the light form is rare in the Qurʾan. — Al-Zarkashī counts two light instances including 12:32; al-Suyūṭī instead labels 12:32 heavy while discussing the forelock verse. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 12:32/q28/p4 The light nūn may become alif at pause. — Al-Akhfash, al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭabrisī cite 96:15, tanwīn analogy and al-Aʿshā. [6 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; TABRISI(الأعشى)]

## 12:32/q29 What ancillary accounts of nature and theology are collected?  [* · theology]
turns on: The premises about origination, eternity, emanation and empirical knowledge.
- 12:32/q29/p1 Kalam developed natural inquiry within revelation and proofs of creation. — Y. Khūlī traces rational kalam before Greek translation, Muʿtazilite and al-Jāḥiẓ's natural study, al-Juwaynī's definition, six topics including laṭāʾif, substance and accident, origination and divine attributes. Al-Jābirī explains the name laṭāʾif. Patronage supported science, but closed theological aims later limited inquiry. [34 notes: YKHULI-TAJDID(الجاحظ,الجويني,الشيخ مصطفى عبد الرازق,دي بور,محمد عابد الجابري)]
- 12:32/q29/p2 Philosophers and naturalists proposed competing origins and paths through nature. — Khūlī contrasts al-Kindī's argued origination, al-Fārābī and Ibn Sīnā's emanation, Ibn Rushd's eternal created world, Jābir's vitalism, al-Rāzī's five eternals and denial of prophecy, and Ibn al-Haytham's continuous creation and rejection of the origination proof. Al-Bīrūnī and Ibn al-Haytham move from geometry and natural signs to divine wisdom; al-Jābirī explains Hermetic influence. [24 notes: YKHULI-TAJDID(ابن الهيثم,ابن رشد,البيروني,الفارابي وابن سينا,الكندي,محمد عابد الجابري)]
- 12:32/q29/p3 Empirical natural science provided precursors yet stayed oriented toward divine ontology. — Khūlī reports Russell's critique of particular facts, credits naturalists' empirical laws, describes Greek appropriation and independent synthesis, and weighs internal and external causes of decline. [8 notes: YKHULI-TAJDID(برتراند رسل)]
