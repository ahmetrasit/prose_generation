# 8:38: 29 questions

## 8:38/q01 Who are the unbelievers addressed?  [لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the reported occasion limits the address.
- 8:38/q01/p1 Abu Sufyan and his companions. — Al-Kalbi, Ibn Abbas via Abu Salih, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi identify this group; al-Wahidi al-Wajiz adopts it. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 8:38/q01/p2 Disbelievers generally, including those beyond the immediate occasion. — Jishumi reports a general address beside the Abu Sufyan report; Ibn Ashur extends the rule to every disbeliever. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 8:38/q02 What is the force of 'say to' and the third-person wording?  [قُل لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوٓا۟ إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lam marks transmission or benefit, and whether the condition quotes the address directly.
- 8:38/q02/p1 The lam conveys a message; the Prophet tells them its meaning in these or other words. — Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin allow paraphrase. Sahib al-Nazm says third person suits reporting speech to absent people, whereas direct quotation would use second person as in 3:12. Ibn Ashur says the Prophet reports the sense as an informed witness. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 8:38/q02/p2 The lam is causal: the Prophet says this for their benefit, rather than directly addressing them in this wording. — Al-Zamakhshari argues direct speech would say 'if you desist, you will be forgiven'; Ibn Masud's wording illustrates it. Al-Samin cites 46:11. Al-Baydawi says 'say for their sake'. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(جمع); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 8:38/q02/p3 Third-person reference expresses turning away from unbelievers while believers receive direct address. — Al-Burhan contrasts this wording with the command to believers in 8:39. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 8:38/q03 What alternative readings of the promise are reported?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ يُغْفَرْ لَهُم · readings]
turns on: Whether speaker and addressees appear explicitly in the verb forms.
- 8:38/q03/p1 Ibn Masud's wording addresses them directly: 'if you desist, you will be forgiven.' — Al-Kisai reports Ibn Masud's codex; Ibn Atiyya says that wording would require verbatim delivery, and al-Alusi says its lam must convey speech. Razi also cites the direct address. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 8:38/q03/p2 The forgiveness verb is read actively, with God as its subject. — Al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report this reading. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 8:38/q03/p3 A passive reading is reported with the pronoun referred to God. — Al-Alusi reports this formulation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 8:38/q04 What does 'desist' mean lexically?  [يَنتَهُوا۟ · meaning]
- 8:38/q04/p1 To stop an act because it has been prohibited. — Jishumi, al-Tabrisi and Tabatabai derive the sense from someone forbidding an act and another ceasing it. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI]

## 8:38/q05 What must the unbelievers stop doing?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the condition requires conversion, peace, or both.
- 8:38/q05/p1 Abandon disbelief or idolatry and enter faith, with repentance. — Ibn Abbas specifies denial of Muhammad and association of partners; Ibn Atiyya reasons forgiveness of the past requires leaving disbelief. Jishumi stresses present idolatry. Others include accepting Islam and its law. Jishumi says fighting is obligatory only during persistent unbelief and forbidden after desistance. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(unspecified); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 8:38/q05/p2 Stop fighting or hostility, make peace, and resolve grievances. — Al-Kalbi specifies fighting Muhammad and companions; al-Mawardi and al-Tabrisi connect peace with removal of earlier liability. Study Quran notes that the wording itself does not demand conversion and cites 2:192–193. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN+]
- 8:38/q05/p3 Stop both unbelief and hostility or warfare against the Messenger. — Al-Asamm combines idolatry and fighting; Razi adds embracing Islamic law. Ibn Ashur highlights spending to obstruct God's path; Nasafi and Baydawi describe ending opposition by entering Islam. [10 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الأصم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]

## 8:38/q06 How is the promise of forgiveness expressed, and why?  [يُغْفَرْ لَهُم · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording highlights the resulting pardon or implies a fuller causal statement.
- 8:38/q06/p1 The passive centers the benefit: past sins are erased in substance and trace, without punishment or reproach. — Al-Biqai explains the passive wording and the completeness of the erasure. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 8:38/q06/p2 An omitted clause can explain that God forgives because He is forgiving and merciful. — Jishumi reports this supplied wording after repentance. [1 notes: JISHUMI(unspecified)]

## 8:38/q07 What does 'what has passed' mean lexically?  [مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · meaning]
- 8:38/q07/p1 What has gone before or preceded. — Al-Wahidi relates salafa to advance payment, the forward neck and the first extracted wine; Jishumi notes salafa and aslafa; Tabatabai gives the basic sense. Tabrisi says salaf are those before. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI]

## 8:38/q08 Which past acts are covered by the promise?  [يُغْفَرْ لَهُم مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which conduct the first condition ends and what sort of pardon follows.
- 8:38/q08/p1 Prior unbelief and sins, including hostile acts, are erased on faith and repentance. — Ibn Abbas lists fornication, idolatry, murder, usury and every disapproved act; Jishumi includes fighting and persecution with no worldly retaliation or afterlife punishment. Ibn Ashur emphasizes removal of afterlife punishment; Nasafi singles out past hostility. Kuranyolu likens conversion to a clean page. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 8:38/q08/p2 Past fighting and associated punishment or liability are forgiven when hostilities cease. — Al-Mawardi, al-Tabrisi and Ibn al-Jawzi state the peace reading; Study Quran allows ending hostilities and settling grievances without an explicit conversion requirement. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; STUDYQURAN+]

## 8:38/q09 What legal claims from before conversion survive?  [يُغْفَرْ لَهُم مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · law]
turns on: How the general promise interacts with divine duties, human claims, protected status and specific penalties.
- 8:38/q09/p1 A combatant convert's prior wrongs and missed worship are cleared broadly. — Malik is reported to waive accountability, including property and blood; scholars cite the hadith that Islam erases what preceded it, so no bodily or financial worship is made up. Ibn al-Arabi prefers broad pardon from the verse's general wording, the hadith and the incentive to convert. Al-Shafi'i's Iraq view waives preconversion adultery penalty; the Kufan authority agrees. Malik also says divorces pronounced before conversion do not count if the parties marry afterward. [19 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مالك,مالك بن أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي,الكوفي,مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض,عمرو بن العاص,مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن المنذر عن الشافعي,مالك); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Bekir İbnü’l-Arabî; İbn Âşûr); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي)+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]
- 8:38/q09/p2 God's claims lapse, but human claims or compensation remain. — Malik preserves Muslim slander and theft claims while waiving former divorce, oaths and some sexual penalties. A dhimmi convert must pay human claims, says Abu Hayyan; Hanafi jurists distinguish divine from human rights. Kuranyolu requires restitution of damaged, taken or consumed property. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو حنيفة,مالك); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(fıkıhçılar)]
- 8:38/q09/p3 A convert remains liable for admitted preconversion adultery. — Abu Thawr makes the penalty apply even when admission comes after Islam; the Kufan authority explicitly disagrees. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ثور); QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفي)-]
- 8:38/q09/p4 Pardon is stated generally for rights owed to God. — Al-Qurtubi records this formulation; the verse is combined with the report that Islam erases what came before. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(العلماء); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(fıkıhçılar)]

## 8:38/q10 Does an apostate who returns to Islam owe earlier liabilities?  [يُغْفَرْ لَهُم مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · law]
turns on: Whether conversion's erasure extends to apostasy and which claims arose before or during it.
- 8:38/q10/p1 He is treated like an original convert, owing no missed worship, offenses or damaged property from apostasy. — Abu Hanifa is cited for waived missed worship; Malikis extend conversion's erasure to apostates. Al-Alusi calls an attribution that lifelong sin disappears after apostasy and return highly strange. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,المالكية); IBNASHUR-FULL(علماء الحنفية)]
- 8:38/q10/p2 God's claims lapse, but human claims remain. — Abu Hanifa and Malik are reported in this form; Ibn al-Arabi prefers it because God needs no due but people do, comparing children's freedom from divine duty with liability to others. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]
- 8:38/q10/p3 All divine and human liabilities remain. — One Shafi'i opinion reasons that if human claims persist, divine claims should too; Ibn Ashur records a conflicting Shafi'i attribution. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); IBNASHUR-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 8:38/q10/p4 Liability depends on timing, territory, act and custody. — Al-Halwani requires making up prayers and fasts deliberately missed while Muslim. Al-Khaniyya preserves claims from before flight to enemy territory, waives some arising afterward, and distinguishes property, retaliation, defamation, theft, homicide, banditry and drinking limits. Al-Alusi says a blanket divine versus human formula is inadequate. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شمس الأئمة الحلواني,قاضيخان)]

## 8:38/q11 Is a zindiq's professed repentance accepted?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ يُغْفَرْ لَهُم · law]
turns on: Whether the general conditional promise applies to concealed heresy and whether outward profession suffices.
- 8:38/q11/p1 Yes; the promise covers every kind of unbelief and courts judge the outward profession. — Razi argues the zindiq's inner abandonment cannot be known, but legal decisions follow appearances. Abu Hanifa and al-Shafi'i are reported to accept this repentance. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL+]
- 8:38/q11/p2 No; Malik is reported not to accept the zindiq's repentance. — Abu Hayyan records Malik's contrary view beside Abu Hanifa and al-Shafi'i. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]

## 8:38/q12 Must conversion also include specific regret for earlier acts?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ يُغْفَرْ لَهُم · theology]
turns on: Whether additional regret for every prior deed is required.
- 8:38/q12/p1 Some require repentance and regret along with faith. — Al-Alusi reports this qualification but voices reservation about it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 8:38/q12/p2 The verse's forgiveness follows desisting from unbelief and turning to faith. — Jishumi describes repentance of present idolatry; al-Alusi prefers reading the passage about original unbelief, citing Amr ibn al-As's report in Muslim. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(عمرو بن العاص)]

## 8:38/q13 What does forgiveness after desistance teach about repentance?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ يُغْفَرْ لَهُم · theology]
turns on: Whether the conditional promise is applied narrowly to earlier acts or extended as an argument about repentance and hope.
- 8:38/q13/p1 Repentance is accepted even for grave unbelief and murder, and forgiveness is still needed after repentance. — Jishumi uses the verse against denying repentance to murderers and against claiming a penitent needs no pardon. Razi argues that an obligated person must have an available path to repentance and cites 42:25. Ibn Abbas's advice to a potential and an actual killer differs to deter murder and encourage repentance. [5 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 8:38/q13/p2 Making pardon accessible encourages conversion and prevents despair. — Ibn al-Arabi calls this divine kindness; Qurtubi's report of the killer of ninety-nine becoming a killer of one hundred illustrates despair's harm. Kuranyolu emphasizes the incentive to convert. A poem says admitting and stopping wrongdoing merits pardon. [6 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد أحمد بن محمد الزيدي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد أحمد بن محمد الزبيري,ابن العربي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 8:38/q13/p3 Pardon upon conversion gives hope that monotheism can also erase later sins. — Yahya ibn Muadh draws this hopeful analogy; al-Shibli says God who admits professing unbelievers will not expel those who remain within faith. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(يحيى بن معاذ); BAGHAWI-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ الرازي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يحيى بن معاذ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ الرازي); BURHAN(الشبلي); ITQAN(الشبلي)]

## 8:38/q14 How does 'return' work in Arabic?  [يَعُودُوا۟ · meaning]
- 8:38/q14/p1 Unqualified ʿada means returning to a state one had left; with a predicate it may mean 'became'. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi contrast the absolute and predicate constructions; Abu al-Salt or Umayyah ibn Abi al-Salt's verse about milk becoming urine witnesses the latter. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 8:38/q15 What would the unbelievers return to?  [وَإِن يَعُودُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ʿada must resume something already left, and whether the group first entered Islam.
- 8:38/q15/p1 Renewed fighting or opposition against the Prophet and believers. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur reason that they had never left unbelief, whereas fighting can cease and resume. Ibn Ashur mentions new preparations for war and the Arabs' 'Islam or war?' contrast. [23 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي); IBNABIHATIM(أبوه); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد والسدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 8:38/q15/p2 Disbelief or idolatry, with renewed persistence in it. — Ibn Abbas says denial of Muhammad; al-Mawardi and Ibn al-Jawzi pair this return with annihilating punishment. Bagawi and Razi state the return to disbelief. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 8:38/q15/p3 Apostasy back to disbelief after first entering Islam. — Abu Hanifa reads it as return after conversion; al-Alusi reports the general disbeliever reading with later apostasy. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد)]

## 8:38/q16 What does 'has passed' mean?  [مَضَتْ · meaning]
- 8:38/q16/p1 It has passed or gone by (khalat). — Abu Malik supplies the synonym. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك)]

## 8:38/q17 What does 'sunnah' mean lexically?  [سُنَّتُ · meaning]
- 8:38/q17/p1 A way, path or established practice. — Jishumi cites a poetic line for an established path and adds long-maintained action, including prophetic practice. Tabrisi equates sunna and tariqa; Tabatabai gives 'path or practice'. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; TABATABAI]

## 8:38/q18 What precedent is threatened if they return?  [سُنَّتُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'earlier peoples' points chiefly to Badr, ancient destroyed nations, or God's recurring aid to messengers.
- 8:38/q18/p1 God's recurring victory for prophets and believers, and destruction or humiliation of those who oppose them. — Al-Kalbi, Qatada and others cite Badr as a model; Qatada cites 58:21 and 37:171, while Razi and Elmalili add the righteous inheriting the earth, 21:105. Abu Muslim includes punishment in the next world. Tabrisi mentions capture and enslavement. Islahi says a nation receives no further respite once the messenger conclusively conveys truth. [30 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,الكلبي,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(commentators); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 8:38/q18/p2 Specifically the fate of Quraysh at Badr: killing and captivity of some, defeat of others. — Mujahid and al-Suddi include Quraysh, their likes and earlier peoples. Ibn Ishaq makes those killed at Badr the precedent. Al-Mawardi and Ibn al-Jawzi mention captives; Abu Hayyan says the witnessed, recent event makes the warning stronger. Al-Alusi reports this but favors a recurrent precedent. [19 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(سفيان,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد والسدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); JISHUMI(الحسن ومجاهد والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 8:38/q18/p3 The annihilating punishment of earlier nations that denied or plotted against their messengers. — Al-Mawardi associates it with return to unbelief; Razi contrasts this with the Badr and divine-victory readings. Qurtubi calls it both warning and threat. Asad speaks of disasters for persistent denial of moral truths. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,الحسن ومجاهد والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]

## 8:38/q19 Why is God's precedent attributed to earlier peoples?  [سُنَّتُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether attribution follows the sufferers, agents, cause or concrete action.
- 8:38/q19/p1 It is named for those who undergo it, although God causes it and messengers enact it. — Tabrisi says the same outcome can be attributed to the sufferers, to messengers, or to God and cites 17:77 and 48:23. Al-Alusi parallels 17:77. Study Quran notes that 'wont' can describe both what people do and what they undergo. [3 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 8:38/q19/p2 The spelling reflects sunnah here as a concrete retributive act. — Al-Burhan relates this to the surrounding warning and the fight command in 8:39. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 8:38/q20 What consequence does the second conditional imply?  [وَإِن يَعُودُوا۟ فَقَدْ مَضَتْ سُنَّتُ ٱلْأَوَّلِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the past-tense clause itself completes the answer or signals an elided consequence.
- 8:38/q20/p1 The stated past precedent implies an omitted future threat: God will punish them, destroy them or grant believers victory. — Abu Hayyan rejects treating 'the precedent has passed' as the entire answer. Al-Alusi cites al-Bahr for an omitted vengeance or victory clause; al-Itqan says the same fate will befall them. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 8:38/q20/p2 The conditional answer states that the established precedent has already occurred. — The past outcome is invoked as warning in Jishumi and Biqai; Abu Hayyan argues a further consequence is implicit. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 8:38/q21 How does the verse fit the surrounding warning and invitation?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the contrast concerns conversion versus apostasy or cessation versus renewed hostilities.
- 8:38/q21/p1 After warning, God opens a route to repentance and faith before the renewed threat. — Jishumi notes the preceding promise and threat; Ibn Ashur says the verse answers an implicit question whether escape remains. Biqai says past-tense labels for disbelievers could otherwise cause despair. Islahi calls the warning immediate after the invitation. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 8:38/q21/p2 The two conditions set cessation against renewed war preparation. — Ibn Ashur contrasts 'Islam or war?' and says the opponents had not left unbelief; the passage also follows their spending to obstruct God's path. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 8:38/q22 What report links the verse to the conquest of Mecca?  [* · reports]
- 8:38/q22/p1 After the conquest the Prophet asked Meccans what they expected; they appealed to his generosity, and he answered with Joseph's words before this verse was revealed. — Al-Mawardi transmits this from Ibn Abbas and points to 12:92. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 8:38/q23 How does this forgiveness promise relate to the wording of 71:4?  [يُغْفَرْ لَهُم مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · links]
turns on: How to read min in 71:4 and the reach of forgiveness across time and human rights.
- 8:38/q23/p1 The two verses address different peoples, so their different formulations need not conflict. — Al-Burhan contrasts Noah's people in 71:4 with this community. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 8:38/q23/p2 The 'min' of 71:4 may be extra, so forgiveness is full. — An objection says partial pardon would deter belief and conflict with Islam erasing prior sins; al-Akhfash gives this grammatical answer. [1 notes: BURHAN(الأخفش)]
- 8:38/q23/p3 The 'min' can retain partitive or starting-point force without promising pardon for everything. — Al-Burhan says human claims can remain; another answer confines pardon to past rather than future sins. Sibawayh supplies an omitted object, and al-Saffar analyzes 'forgive' as taking two objects. Another reading says the ellipsis magnifies what is forgiven and cites 69:1. [5 notes: BURHAN(الصفار)]

## 8:38/q24 What grammatical point does conditional 'in' illustrate?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ · grammar]
- 8:38/q24/p1 Unemphatic kasra in is conditional, with the consequent following it. — Al-Itqan identifies the particle here; al-Burhan calls this its common use and gives 3:29 as comparison. Al-Burhan also says a consequence is normally warranted by fulfillment of the condition. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 8:38/q24/p2 When in precedes lam or la, jussive government depends on the intervening particle. — Al-Itqan says lam governs its verb, whereas in governs after la, comparing 5:24, 9:40 and 7:143. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 8:38/q25 Can the phrase be treated as poetry by meter?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ يُغْفَرْ لَهُم مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · rhetoric]
- 8:38/q25/p1 It scans as rajaz or coincides with metrical poetry, without making Qur'anic speech poetry. — Ibn Ashur notes its rajaz embellishment. Al-Burhan discusses complete lines, multiple lines and hemistichs, listing this verse among examples alongside 50:40, 3:92, 18:22, 11:43, 111:1, 61:13 and 28:76. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 8:38/q26 How may writers borrow this verse's wording?  [إِن يَنتَهُوا۟ يُغْفَرْ لَهُم مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether explicit quotation qualifies as iqtibas and whether such use is fitting.
- 8:38/q26/p1 A poem may cite the divine promise to urge repentance; explicit attribution means it is not technically iqtibas. — Ibn al-Subki attributes the couplet to Abu Mansur al-Baghdadi; al-Itqan says explicit attribution excludes it from its definition. Abu Sa'id's verse similarly says confessing and ceasing merits pardon. [4 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد أحمد بن محمد الزيدي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد أحمد بن محمد الزبيري); ITQAN(تاج الدين ابن السبكي); ITQAN+]
- 8:38/q26/p2 Borrowing sacred wording should be avoided out of scrupulous piety. — Baha al-Din ibn al-Subki urges this; al-Nakha'i reportedly disliked using Qur'anic recitation for a worldly situation, amid wider disagreement reported by al-Nawawi. [2 notes: ITQAN(النخعي,بهاء الدين ابن السبكي)]

## 8:38/q27 What does the report that Islam erases what preceded it add?  [يُغْفَرْ لَهُم مَّا قَدْ سَلَفَ · reports]
turns on: How broadly the report's 'what' should be understood.
- 8:38/q27/p1 Islam, emigration and pilgrimage each erase what came before. — In Muslim's report, the dying Amr ibn al-As recalls asking the Prophet for forgiveness at his pledge and receiving this answer. Al-Alusi cautions that 'what' cannot mean absolutely everything in all three clauses. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عمرو بن العاص); ALUSI-FULL(عمرو بن العاص); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 8:38/q27/p2 Submission and repentance each cut off what came before. — Study Quran cites this hadith alongside the verse. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]

## 8:38/q28 What unrelated name-truncation examples occur in the collected grammatical material?  [* · grammar]
- 8:38/q28/p1 Proper names can be shortened in address, with the remaining form adjusted or preserved according to morphology and usage. — Sibawayh illustrates ta marbuta deletion with عرقوة, قمحدوة, رعوم, قطوان, طفاوة, معاوية and حيوة; al-Ajjaj's verse supports يا معا. The adjusted remainder is less common, and ordinary feminine nouns cannot be treated as masculine except dominant names. He gives حارث, مالك, عامر, يزيد, ليلى and لميس, and cites Imru al-Qays, a line attributed to Tarafa, al-Khalil's five-to-four/four-to-three rule, and the exceptional يا صاح from صاحب. [16 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 8:38/q29 What other cross-verse observations accompany the cited material?  [* · links]
- 8:38/q29/p1 Repeated prophetic accounts warn deniers of the fate of earlier peoples. — Al-Itqan favors this explanation for repetition: repeated denials of Muhammad prompt repeated warnings, while Joseph's story has another purpose; it cites 6:6. [1 notes: ITQAN+]
- 8:38/q29/p2 Apparent conflicts between Qur'anic passages require explanation. — Al-Itqan says Ibn Abbas also addressed such cases, as with apparently conflicting hadith. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 8:38/q29/p3 A separate comparison links guidance-word frequency to an added ya. — Samarrai counts guidance occurrences as seventeen in al-Araf, eight in al-Isra and six in al-Kahf, while describing mercy in Maryam. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
