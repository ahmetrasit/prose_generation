# 96:15/q13 Why seize the forelock in particular? [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources give humiliation, Abū Jahl's grooming, and the forehead's relation to pride and prostration as rationales.
- 96:15/q13/p1 Taking the forelock displays extreme humiliation and mastery.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Khansāʾ's verse support its humiliating force among Arabs; al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī call it an especially honored part. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī liken the display of control to handling animals; Asad notes the old Arabian idiom and compares 11:56. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says it signals the seizer's power.

# 96:15/q14 Why is the forelock definite without a possessive? [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · grammar]
turns on: The referent is agreed; the grammatical dispute is whether the article replaces a genitive possessor or accompanies an implicit attachment.
- 96:15/q14/p1 The article identifies the known offender's forelock, making a possessive suffix unnecessary.
  reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī say the owner is understood; Ibn ʿĀshūr points back to the forbider in 96:9–10. Al-Rāzī says its physical identity is known while its lying and sinful traits are newly revealed.

# 96:15/q16 How is the following 'a lying, sinful forelock' attached to this forelock? [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · grammar]
- 96:15/q16/p1 The indefinite, qualified second forelock is an appositive to the first, adding blame.
  reasons: Sībawayh uses 96:15–16 for apposition; al-Farrāʾ calls repetition emphasis and compares 'a straight path, the path of God.' Al-Zarkashī and Ibn Yaʿīsh explain that an indefinite substitute for a definite needs qualification. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls its lying and sin a rhetorical attribution to its owner's speech and acts; al-Sāmarrāʾī says badal can serve blame, contrasting praise in 'this secure town.'
