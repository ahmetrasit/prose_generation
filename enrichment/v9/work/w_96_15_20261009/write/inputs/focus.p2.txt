# 96:15/q08 How is lanasfaʿan read? [لَنَسْفَعًۢا · readings]
turns on: The readings vary in nūn weight and, for Ibn Masʿūd, in the verb's person and agency.
- 96:15/q08/p1 Most read the first-person plural with a light emphatic nūn.
  reasons: Sībawayh uses this verse as an example; al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Zarkashī, al-Suyūṭī and Ibn ʿĀshūr discuss this rare Qurʾānic light nūn. Al-Zarkashī says only two instances, comparing 12:32; al-Suyūṭī distinguishes the heavy nūn there.

# 96:15/q09 Why is the final emphatic nūn written as alif? [لَنَسْفَعًۢا · grammar]
- 96:15/q09/p1 The light nūn becomes alif at pause, and muṣḥaf spelling reflects that pronunciation.
  reasons: Al-Naḥḥās, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī cite poetic pause or rhyme forms; al-Samīn compares tanwīn and says nūn is dropped after ḍamma or kasra. Al-Ṭabrisī says Basrans favor alif for pause, while Kufans favor nūn for the underlying consonant. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the muṣḥaf writes alif in place of nūn. Al-Suyūṭī lists related written light-nūn forms (yakūnā, wa-idhā), contrasting kāyin, amid broader alif, wāw, yāʾ and tāʾ spelling examples.

# 96:15/q10 What grammatical comparison with lan appears beside this oath? [لَئِن لَنَسْفَعًۢا · grammar]
- 96:15/q10/p1 The negative particle lan strengthens future denial and can answer a claim introduced by sa.
  reasons: Al-Zarkashī contrasts lā abraḥ with lan abraḥ; he reports Sībawayh's account of lan as an answer to someone saying sa-yafʿal. These are comparative remarks about lan, distinct from the oath lām of la-in and emphatic la-nasfaʿan.

  reasons: Muqātil identifies him and links his forelock to the lying, sinful forelock of 96:16. Al-Māwardī names him directly; the conditional refers back to the man who forbids in 96:9–10.

# 96:15/q12 What does nāṣiya denote? [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · meaning]
turns on: The literal referent is front hair or its place; the figurative extent ranges from forehead and face to the whole person.
- 96:15/q12/p1 The hair or forelock at the front of the head.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī cites rajaz for the term; Ibn al-Shaykh says the word may also name where the hair grows. Later writers extend it to the forehead or front of the head.
  holders (18 notes): FARRA; MAWARDI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; RAZ
