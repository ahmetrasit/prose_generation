# 96:15/q19 What broader warning does the verse teach? [* · interpretation]
- 96:15/q19/p1 It warns every opponent of worship and shows that tyrannical power is subject to God.
  reasons: Al-Qurṭubī extends the lesson beyond Abū Jahl; al-Biqāʿī says reliance on followers overlooks heavenly and earthly forces, and that the conditional leaves a real chance to desist. Quṭb hears a decisive threat against tyranny; Islahi connects punishment to blocking prostration; Kur'an Yolu says tyrants may be left free temporarily but face ultimate punishment.

# 96:15/q20 How does this threat develop the sūra's argument? [* · links]
- 96:15/q20/p1 Questioning turns into an explicit divine threat and a preview of Judgment.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum calls 96:15–18 the fourth stanza and the first unequivocal divine first-person plural. Al-Ṭabarī links the three elliptical 'Have you seen?' clauses to the forbider, who also denies and knows God sees. Muqātil connects the earlier warning of return to God with this named threat. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the second kallā resolving earlier suspense.

- 96:15/q20/p2 The sound and accumulated force of the words convey anger and violent seizure.
