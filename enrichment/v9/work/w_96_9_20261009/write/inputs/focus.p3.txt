# 96:9/q10 Does the phrase prohibit prayer itself or interrupt someone during prayer? [ٱلَّذِى يَنْهَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the temporal phrase identifies the target of prohibition or merely its setting.
- 96:9/q10/p1 The prohibition concerns prayer, occurring while the servant prays.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī derives contemporaneity from the time clause. Al-Farrāʾ pairs prayer with calling to piety: it reforms the worshipper and others. Elmalılı includes obligatory and voluntary prayer.
  holders (8 notes): ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); ALUSI; ELMALILI
  notes: ABUHAYYAN:96:10/r11 ABUHAYYAN:96:9/r11 ALUSI:96:9/r16 ALUSI:96:9/r34 ALUSI:96:9/r35 ALUSI:96:12/r22 ALUSI:96:13/r23 ELMALILI:96:9-19#2/r4
- 96:9/q10/p2 The prohibition occurs during prayer but may target prayer or another matter.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī says it can impede both the servant's worship and prayer's call to others. Ibn ʿĀshūr recovers the omitted act from context.
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI(بعضهم، قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: ALUSI:96:9/r36 ALUSI:96:12/r37 ALUSI:96:13/r40 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p446/r6
sources: ABUHAYYAN = Abū Ḥayyān, d. 745; ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; ELMALILI = Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393
