# 96:9/q01 What does 'Have you seen?' express? [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question conveys wonder, inquiry, warning or the later moral contrast.
- 96:9/q01/p1 It draws attention to the astonishing impropriety of forbidding worship.
  reasons: Al-Wāḥidī and Baghawī say repetition intensifies wonder. Rāzī highlights the irony of Abū al-Ḥakam, an idol worshipper who forbids his Creator's service, and recalls the Prophet's prayer for Abū Jahl or ʿUmar. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the extraordinary-event question at 88:1; Qutb hears a rapid live address.

# 96:9/q02 How are the repeated interrogatives and omissions parsed? [أَرَءَيْتَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether raʾayta takes a second object, whether a conditional can fill it, and whether an interrogative apodosis needs fāʾ.
- 96:9/q02/p1 The first question takes the prohibitor as object, later objects are omitted, and the final interrogative supplies the threatening complement.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān requires the second object to be an interrogative clause, citing 53:33–35, 19:77–78, 56:58–59. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī call the omissions contextual rather than tanāzuʿ because whole clauses do not compete as omitted individual words. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the answer from 96:14.

# 96:9/q03 Who is addressed? [أَرَءَيْتَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the question addresses the Prophet, any observer, the prohibitor or alternating parties.
- 96:9/q03/p1 The Prophet is addressed.
  reasons: Rāzī relates the address to his prayer for Abū Jahl or ʿUmar. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Baḥr favor the Prophet because the pronouns stay consistent; Elmalılı extends this first address to all.
  holders (8 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI(البحر); ELMALILI

- 96:9/q03/p2 Any capable observer, or humanity generally, is addressed.
