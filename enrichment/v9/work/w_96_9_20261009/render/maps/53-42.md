# 53:42: 13 questions

## 53:42/q01 How is the opening clause read and connected to the earlier scriptures?  [وَأَنَّ · readings]
turns on: Whether the hamza is opened, coordinating the clause with the earlier anna, or broken with kasra to begin a new sentence.
- 53:42/q01/p1 With fatḥa on anna, this clause continues the statements in the scrolls of Moses and Abraham. — Al-Kashshaf says the fatḥa makes all these clauses part of the scriptures; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority reading and coordinates it with the understood anna of verse 38. Al-Razi calls it the common reading. Al-Samin says the coordinated hamzas share it. Al-Nasafi, al-Zajjaj, Ibn al-Jawzi reporting al-Zajjaj, Ibn Ashur, and Study Quran place the statements in those scrolls; Ibn Ashur says it has the syntactic position of verse 40 and notes Abraham's going to his Lord in 37:99 as a possible parallel. Study Quran says verses 42–50 except 46 begin wa anna; its introduction places these perennial truths after the call to reflect on creation. Islahi says Abraham's and Moses' real teachings and sacrifices challenge those assuming descent from them ensures Paradise. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:42/q01/p2 With kasra on inna, this and following clauses begin independent statements outside the earlier scriptures' contents. — Abu al-Sammal Qanab is named by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samin as reading kasra here and onward. Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Alusi, and Study Quran explain the break; Study Quran gives the independent sense as 'truly,' 'surely,' or 'verily.' [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو السمال قعنب); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو السمال); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السمال); STUDYQURAN]

## 53:42/q02 Who is addressed by 'your Lord'?  [رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular second-person address has an unrestricted listener or the Prophet as its referent.
- 53:42/q02/p1 Any listener or rational person is addressed, so each person's end is before God. — Al-Razi says this warns wrongdoers and encourages the righteous. For this general addressee, he takes the definite al-muntaha as the promised return already announced in revelation and by the Prophet. Ibn Ashur says the shift from third person to second person leaves the addressee unidentified and effectively says each returns to his own Lord. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:42/q02/p2 Muhammad is addressed specifically. — Al-Razi says this confirms his religion of the one self-sufficient Lord on whom contingent beings depend, the Lord of lords and cause of causes. It also consoles him about opponents, recalling God's knowledge of their words and their eventual return in 36:76 and 36:83. On this address, al-muntaha is generic: every terminus is to the Lord who is its origin. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:42/q03 What is the grammatical form and case of al-muntaha?  [ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: The notes discuss different grammatical aspects: its hidden accusative case ending and its nominal or verbal-noun form.
- 53:42/q03/p1 It is the accusative noun of anna, with an unpronounced case ending because it is a defective noun. — Al-Nahhas identifies its function and explains why the accusative vowel cannot appear. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 53:42/q03/p2 It is a verbal noun meaning ending, in a faʿlā pattern that can function as a noun. — Al-Nasafi and al-Tabatabai call it a verbal or infinitive noun; al-Tabatabai draws breadth from its unrestricted use. Al-Burhan gives al-shura, al-rujʿa, and al-ʿulya as parallels for faʿlā as a noun. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURHAN]

## 53:42/q04 What does al-muntaha mean lexically?  [ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · meaning]
- 53:42/q04/p1 The end, endpoint, termination, or final return; it can be the destination where action stops. — Al-Wahidi explicitly glosses it as endpoint or termination; al-Kashshaf as the end; al-Nasafi as a verbal noun for the end where creatures arrive and return, citing 3:28. Al-Jishumi adds the destination at which action ceases. Al-Bursevi glosses it as end or return. Al-Tabatabai calls it an infinitive noun and takes its unrestricted wording to include every kind of ending. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 53:42/q05 What does the definite article in al-muntaha specify?  [ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Al-Razi ties the article's reference to whether 'your' addresses people generally or the Prophet.
- 53:42/q05/p1 For a general addressee it identifies the already promised return. — Al-Razi says revelation and the Prophet have previously announced this return. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:42/q05/p2 For the Prophet as addressee it is generic, encompassing every terminus. — Al-Razi says every terminus goes to the Lord who is its origin; in the corresponding account perception starts from outward things and through reflection reaches God and stops. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:42/q06 What ultimately ends with the Lord?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes dispute whether the unrestricted 'ending' denotes bodily return, final outcomes, hopes, the limit of thought, causal explanation, or all kinds of ending; al-Tabatabai argues against restricting its scope.
- 53:42/q06/p1 Creatures return to God after death or at resurrection for judgment and recompense. — Al-Nahhas, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, and others add recompense for good and bad deeds; al-Wahidi calls this the exegetes' interpretation. Ibn ʿAtiyya calls it the gathering and destination after death, relative to worldly life. Al-Razi calls resurrection the well-known interpretation and says it answers when the prior recompense occurs: gratitude is rewarded and ingratitude punished. Al-Kashshaf cites 35:18; al-Nasafi cites 3:28. Ibn Kathir specifies Judgment Day, Study Quran says all then stand before God, and al-Alusi says no other being has an independent or shared part in their return. Al-Bursevi cites 42:53 and says beginning and end are in God. Al-Biqai includes bodily resurrection and the return of deeds; Elmalili includes full recompense and calls God First and Last. Islahi says no one can evade accountability by appealing to another and God's decisions are final. Kuranyolu says judgment gives full recompense without the slightest injustice, while grace and forgiveness remain God's choice and believers should hope without complacency. Al-Tabatabai rejects making this the only meaning as too narrow. [25 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 53:42/q06/p2 The ultimate destination is specifically God's reckoning, reward in Paradise, or punishment in Hell, where earthly action ends. — Ibn ʿAtiyya contrasts Paradise or Hell as the absolute endpoint with the postmortem gathering relative to worldly life, and posits an omitted genitive: the end of God's mercy or punishment. Al-Tabrisi names divine reward and punishment where deeds cease; Ibn Kathir reports Muʿadh ibn Jabal saying the return ends in Paradise or Fire. Al-Alusi reports reckonings, Paradise, and Fire as commentators' glosses. Study Quran cites 13:35 for the Garden of the reverent versus the Fire of disbelievers. Al-Tabatabai rejects restriction to reward and punishment or reckoning as unsupported narrowing. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(معاذ بن جبل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 53:42/q06/p3 All beneficence begins with God and all hopes, or security, finally end with Him. — Al-Thaʿlabi and al-Baghawi report beneficence and hopes; al-Qurtubi has favor and security. Al-Jishumi lists grace and hopes among reported senses. [4 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); JISHUMI]
- 53:42/q06/p4 Human inquiry and thought reach their limit at God, whose essence and attributes cannot be encompassed. — Al-Wahidi permits reasoning about created things but says divine knowledge, essence, and modality elude comprehension. Al-Jishumi reports the limit of thought; al-Alusi applies it to divine essence and attributes. Reports from Ubayy, Anas, Abu Hurayra, Ibn Abbas, and Abu Dharr support stopping or turning to creation, detailed in the reflection question. Al-Razi interprets Ubayy's and Anas's reports as reaching the uncaused necessary existent; he says reflection from outward objects stops at God. Al-Tabatabai rejects making the limit of thought the verse's only sense, while treating the Imam al-Sadiq's instruction as an expanded application of 'ending.' [41 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس,النبي); JISHUMI(أنس); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي بن كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب عن النبي); RAZI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبي بن كعب عن النبي,أنس عن النبي,الشاعر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,أبي بن كعب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو ذر,أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,الحكم عن بعض أئمة الكوفة,سفيان الثوري,يونس بن ميسرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر,أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,سفيان الثوري); TABATABAI(أبو ذر,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 53:42/q06/p5 All beings, their effects, governance, deeds, knowledge, hopes, and every other ending depend on God, their origin as well as final end. — Al-Tabatabai reads the unrestricted infinitive noun absolutely: God creates beings and their relations, directly or through causes, and all governance returns to Him; he cites 39:62–63 and 7:54. Al-Biqai includes bodily return and deeds, knowledge, hopes, affairs, place, and time. Asad includes the universe's beginning and end in time and space, God as source and goal. Bursevi cites 42:53 for affairs returning to God because there is no other deity. Al-Husayn's explanation of tawhid, transmitted by al-Sulami and Bursevi, says contingent effects arise from God, return to Him, and vanish while their cause remains; al-Sulami cites 'He is the First' in 57:3. Al-Razi reads the generic article as every terminus belonging to its origin. [10 notes: SULAMI(الحسين); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسين); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); TABATABAI+]
- 53:42/q06/p6 The chain of causes and rational inquiry ends at God as the uncaused Necessary Being and source of existence. — Al-Razi argues contingent things require causes and an explanatory chain cannot finish with another contingent thing. He takes Ubayy's and Anas's stop-at-God reports in this sense. He warns that extending the same reading to every return or ascent verse, such as 35:10, lacks apparent support in some cases though it is clear here. Study Quran reports this understanding from R. Al-Jishumi mentions God as final judge among several reported views, distinct from the causal proof. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,أنس); STUDYQURAN(R); RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:42/q06/p7 All knowledge ultimately ends with the Lord. — Muqatil states the epistemic endpoint without further argument. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 53:42/q07 What guidance about contemplating God is drawn from the verse and its reports?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Whether the transmitted prohibitions express the verse's whole sense or an application directed especially to those unready for difficult inquiry.
- 53:42/q07/p1 Stop speculation about God Himself and reflect on His creation instead. — Ubayy ibn Kaʿb's Prophetic report is transmitted by al-Thaʿlabi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqai, al-Durr, and al-Alusi; some explicitly give no grading. Anas or Sufyan al-Thawri transmit 'when the Lord is mentioned, stop.' Abu Hurayra, Ibn Abbas, and Abu Dharr transmit the creation-versus-Creator instruction because thought cannot encompass Him, with Abu Dharr warning of ruin. Al-Thaʿlabi's Abu Hurayra report describes layers of earth, heavens, and a sea in the seventh heaven. Ibn Kathir says the wording of Abu Hurayra's raised report is not preserved; he cites a sound Satanic-question hadith requiring refuge and stopping, and a Sunan report including an angel's three-hundred-year earlobe-to-shoulder distance. Al-Qurtubi also links the Satanic-question hadith and cites a poem directing thought from God's essence to His works. Al-Durr transmits Yunus ibn Maysara's account of the companions discussing God's greatness and the Prophet redirecting them, and a Kufa imam's report of reflection on the sun and a radiant western land inhabited by beings unaware whether Adam or Iblis was created. Al-Biqai adds Ibn Abbas's inability-to-grasp-God's-measure report. Imam al-Sadiq applies the verse to stopping speech when it reaches God. Imam al-Baqir warns Ziyad that forbidden disputation brings doubt, void deeds, and confusion, or wandering in a variant. Al-Wahidi specifies that divine knowledge, essence, and modality cannot be comprehended; al-Alusi says most transmitted proofs indicate the essence cannot be known in its full reality. Al-Jishumi gives no grading for the creation report. [41 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس,النبي); JISHUMI(أنس); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي بن كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي,أبي بن كعب عن النبي,أنس عن النبي); RAZI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبي بن كعب عن النبي,أنس عن النبي,الشاعر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,أبي بن كعب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو ذر,أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,الحكم عن بعض أئمة الكوفة,سفيان الثوري,يونس بن ميسرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر,أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,سفيان الثوري); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,أبو ذر,أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 53:42/q07/p2 The warning especially guides people unprepared for difficult rational inquiry; the stop-at-God report is an expanded application of 'ending.' — Al-Tabatabai says difficult speculation risks lasting ruin for such people, and interprets Imam al-Sadiq's instruction as a broader application rather than limiting the verse's entire meaning. He rejects making the thought-limit reading exclusive. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 53:42/q08 How does the verse support God's existence?  [* · theology]
- 53:42/q08/p1 Reason's chain of contingent causes must end with God's necessary, uncaused existence. — Al-Razi says each contingent thing requires a cause, and another contingent thing cannot be the final explanatory terminus. He reads the reports against speculation about the Lord as the point where one reaches the uncaused necessary existent. He rejects indiscriminately transferring this explanation to every return or ascent verse, including 35:10, though he finds it clear here. Study Quran reports God as Necessary Being and source of all beings. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,أنس); STUDYQURAN(R); RAZI-FULL+]

## 53:42/q09 What does the verse teach about God's unity and lordship?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes offer complementary proofs: al-Razi argues from necessary existence, while others describe the scope of divine governance.
- 53:42/q09/p1 The final necessary existent is one; two necessary beings could not each be the final terminus. — Al-Razi says the terminus is necessary existence precisely as necessary, making a plurality of necessary beings impossible. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:42/q09/p2 God's lordship embraces all creation, relations, affairs, and governance from origin through resurrection. — Al-Tabatabai says all systems of governance end with God, who creates beings and their relations, directly or through causes; he cites 39:62–63 and 7:54. Al-Bursevi cites 42:53, says there is no deity but God, and that all affairs return to Him. Al-Biqai says beginnings, destinations, deeds, knowledge, hopes, time, and place depend on Him. Al-Husayn, reported by al-Sulami and Bursevi, defines unity by God's encompassing or causal priority: contingent effects start and end with Him and vanish as He remains; al-Sulami cites 57:3. [6 notes: SULAMI(الحسين); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسين); TABATABAI]

## 53:42/q10 What cautions do commentators place on interpretations and titles for God?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether proposed theological interpretations and divine titles are acceptable to these commentators.
- 53:42/q10/p1 Reading the verse as union is heretical and must be rejected. — Al-Biqai rejects union and any other heretical reading, invoking a curse on its interpreter, defender, and silent supporter. [0 notes: BIQAI-FULL-]
- 53:42/q10/p2 The title 'cause of causes' should not be used to invoke or describe God. — Bursevi reports that some great authorities use it as a proof of unity for those lacking unveiling, but calls the title unscriptural and forbids it for invocation or description. Al-Razi uses 'cause of causes' in explaining the Prophet-directed address, without Bursevi's qualification. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)+]

## 53:42/q11 How is ending with God understood inwardly?  [* · inward]
turns on: These reports develop different experiential images of reaching God: transformed will, converging paths, vanished effects, and boundless reality.
- 53:42/q11/p1 Knowing God's lordship removes temptation and leaves the servant willing only what God chooses. — Ibn ʿAtaʾ, reported by al-Sulami, says every trial turns away when a servant reaches that knowledge. Bursevi reports his formulation that whoever begins from God ends with Him and loses any will apart from God's choice. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]
- 53:42/q11/p2 All paths to God converge in one end, despite the number of paths matching creatures' breaths. — Bursevi says their unity lies in the end and God brings the seeker after death to the aspiration sought. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 53:42/q11/p3 Contingent things begin and end in God, then disappear while their abiding cause remains. — Al-Husayn defines tawhid through God's encompassing presence in al-Sulami's account, citing 'He is the First' (57:3); Bursevi reports his cause-and-effect formulation. [2 notes: SULAMI(الحسين); BURSEVI(الحسين)]
- 53:42/q11/p4 God's boundlessness dwarfs all other existences as a sea dwarfs streams and seas. — Bursevi quotes Rumi's poetic comparison. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:42/q12 How does this verse advance the surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the clause to several parts of its context: recompense, witnessing deeds, and the earlier scriptures.
- 53:42/q12/p1 It locates the full recompense promised just before at the resurrection before God. — Al-Razi says it answers when the preceding recompense happens: the grateful receive reward and the ungrateful punishment. Kuranyolu says the judgment then gives each deed its full recompense, without the slightest injustice; grace and forgiveness remain subject to God's will. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:42/q12/p2 It closes the chain of proof by naming God as the ultimate witness over deeds. — Al-Biqai contrasts appointed angels who may see deeds with the supreme King himself, whose witnessing inspires greater awe. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:42/q12/p3 It continues the enduring teachings attributed to Moses' and Abraham's scriptures. — Al-Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi reporting him tie the whole passage to 53:36–37. Ibn Ashur says this clause may share verse 40's place and cites Abraham's 'going to my Lord' in 37:99 as a possible parallel. Study Quran's introduction says the sura's third section moves from reflecting on creation to these enduring scriptural truths. Islahi says this challenges claims that descent from those prophets guarantees Paradise and calls attention to their teachings and sacrifices. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:42/q13 What rhetorical device appears in the following clauses?  [* · rhetoric]
- 53:42/q13/p1 The subsequent pairs, such as laughing and weeping, form antithesis. — Al-Samin calls the paired terms opposites, contraries, or items incompatible in some respect. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
