# 107:7: 20 questions

## 107:7/q01 What do the verb and its omitted recipient suggest?  [وَيَمْنَعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 107:7/q01/p1 The missing recipient generalizes refusal to everyone, while the imperfect verb suggests repeated behavior. — Al-Samīn says people or petitioners are understood; al-Biqāʿī says no rank is exempt, and the imperfect marks a repeated habit. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

turns on: Whether māʿūn is a general benefit, a particular due such as zakāt, loaned goods, a regional lexical sense, or a named necessity; the reported authorities, dialect usage, poetry, and unqualified wording supply the grounds.
- 107:7/q02/p1 Obligatory alms, especially zakāt. — ʿAlī, Ibn ʿUmar, al-Ḥasan and many others transmit this gloss. Al-Rāʿī's poem pairs withholding māʿūn with neglect of tahlīl. ## 107:7/q03 How is the noun māʿūn derived?  [ٱلْمَاعُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether its form is traced to maʿn, maʿūna, a passive of aʿāna, or Hebrew māʿôn; the disputed sound changes and Arabic poetic use are decisive evidence in the notes.
- 107:7/q03/p1 From maʿn, a little thing, on the fāʿūl pattern. — Quṭrub cites an Arab expression for possessing little; zakāt is a small part of much wealth, as are minor gifts and implements. ## 107:7/q04 Why use the words manaʿūn and māʿūn together?  [وَيَمْنَعُونَ ٱلْمَاعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 107:7/q04/p1 Their near-anagram and shared sounds sharpen the clash between expected aid and refusal. — Corpus Coranicum calls the consonant permutation reversed paronomasia and suggests the rare noun was selected for assonance. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes that māʿūn occurs nowhere else in the Qurʾān. [4 notes: BURSEVI(برهان القرآن); BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 107:7/q05 When is withholding such things forbidden or blameworthy?  [وَيَمْنَعُونَ ٱلْمَاعُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether an item is owed, needed for survival, routinely lent, or liable to damage.
- 107:7/q05/p1 Withholding needed necessities is forbidden; refusal without necessity is blameworthy conduct. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī and Bursevī distinguish need from ordinary requests; the water, fire and salt report supports the duty. [18 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI(العلماء); RAZI-FULL(العلماء); QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(عائشة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الرازي,الزمخشري)]
- 107:7/q05/p2 Required alms and other established rights must be given. — Al-Biqāʿī makes the woe concern required rights, including zakāt and surplus water or pasture. [4 notes: THALABI(النبي,قيل); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:7/q05/p3 Lending ordinary articles was obligatory early in Islam, then became permissible. — Al-Jishumī reports that the emigrants' poverty first made lending obligatory, and changed circumstances relaxed that duty. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 107:7/q05/p4 Refusing to lend to a neighbor who damages the goods incurs no blame. — Abū ʿAbd Allāh gives this qualification in Abū Baṣīr's report. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 107:7/q07 What events or instructions are reported concerning māʿūn?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which transmitted incident or Prophetic instruction is meant, and how the reports are assessed.
- 107:7/q07/p1 A delegation was instructed by the Prophet to share water, stone cookware, and iron tools. — Reports through Qurra ibn Damʿūs and others give the instruction; Ibn Kathīr calls the Qurra report extremely strange and its Prophetic attribution objectionable because of an unknown narrator. [12 notes: IBNKATHIR(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن الحرث بن شريح,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن علي بن أبي طالب,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن قرة بن دعموص النميري); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 107:7/q07/p2 Hypocrites refused Muslims who sought to borrow household goods, and the verse was revealed. — Ibn Masʿūd's report specifies buckets, pots and axes. [3 notes: THALABI(النبي); DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 107:7/q08 Why does withholding bring the passage's warning?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the woe requires the combined faults, reaches each obligatory default, or censures the character revealed by even a trivial refusal.
- 107:7/q08/p1 Refusal of even trifling help exposes extreme miserliness and denial of judgment. — Al-Rāzī links the conduct to 4:37 and 68:12; Ibn Kathīr infers one who refuses minor loans will also withhold zakāt; al-Biqāʿī traces the vice to denial of recompense. [17 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR]
- 107:7/q08/p2 The warning concerns the combined traits of neglected prayer, display, and stinginess. — ʿIkrima says denial of a single household item alone does not merit woe; al-Qurṭubī prefers the portrait of hypocrites and cites 4:142 and 9:54. Al-Zamakhsharī calls prayer the pillar of faith, ostentation akin to associating partners, and zakāt its counterpart. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; TABRISI(علي، ابن عمر، الحسن، قتادة، الضحاك، أبو عبد الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); RAZI; QURTUBI(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة مولى ابن عباس); DURR(عكرمة); BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI+; BURHAN-]
- 107:7/q08/p3 Each fault earns a corresponding share of rebuke, especially denial of obligatory aid. — Al-Qurṭubī applies blame to a Muslim with one trait; al-Biqāʿī stresses that the threatened refusal must concern what is due. Al-Rāzī explicitly asks why three faults short of unbelief attract so severe a threat; al-Burhān instead calls withholding every listed item sinful. [6 notes: RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL+; BIQAI+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة)-; QURTUBI(عكرمة)-; QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة مولى ابن عباس)-; DURR(عكرمة)-]
- 107:7/q08/p4 Failure to help shows prayer lacked sincerity and moral effect. — Quṭb and Iṣlāḥī say genuine prayer should produce generosity and service; 29:45 and 2:177 connect devotion with conduct. [17 notes: IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 107:7/q09 How does this refusal connect with prayer and preceding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the consequential or causal connection makes one group, introduces a parenthetical second group, or stresses parallel duties and faults.
- 107:7/q09/p1 Prayer and giving are paired duties toward God and people. — Al-Rāzī says hypocrites show people what belongs to God while hiding from people what belongs to them; al-ʿĀshūr and Elmalılı appeal to the recurrent pairing of prayer and alms, including 9:18. [34 notes: TAB(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 107:7/q09/p2 It continues and intensifies the earlier portrait of moral and religious failure. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī understand fa-wayl as consequential escalation from mistreatment of orphans and poor to neglect of prayer and aid; Corpus Coranicum sees social omissions in verses 2–3 and 7. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:7/q09/p3 The final refusal reflects the opening denial of recompense. — Al-Biqāʿī sees the ending returning to the beginning, with unbelief breeding disdain for the weak and their rights. [14 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 107:7/q09/p4 It shows the praying group is the same group of deniers. — A causal analysis of fa-wayl identifies them as hypocrites; Ibn ʿĀshūr endorses it. Al-Kashf objects to forcing one defined referent and reads a parenthetical extension instead. Al-Zamakhsharī's analysis uses generic singular for plural, whereas Abū Ḥayyān objects to awkward coordination. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,بعضهم)-]
- 107:7/q09/p5 Withholding aid generalizes the preceding failure to support orphans and the poor. — Corpus Coranicum reads the final social omission as expanding the earlier examples. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 107:7/q09/p6 The praying people are a parenthetical extension to another group with similar faults. — Al-Kashf and al-Ālūsī find a strict identity of subjects unsuitable; Abū Ḥayyān objects to the unusual coordination and awkward demonstrative. Another analysis coordinates two groups, but al-Kashf finds the shared pronoun context unsuitable. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,بعضهم)]

## 107:7/q10 How do other passages or sūras answer this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighboring sūra or other Qurʾānic passage is used as a parallel.
- 107:7/q10/p1 Al-Kawthar answers its failures through generous giving, prayer, devotion and sacrifice. — Al-Biqāʿī, al-Itqān and al-Burhān pair 108:1–2 with refusal of aid, neglect of prayer and ostentation; sacrifice meat is charity. [9 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI]
- 107:7/q10/p2 Al-Anfāl parallels prayer, spending, social rights, display and warning. — Al-Biqāʿī compares 8:4, 8:32, 8:35, 8:36, 8:41 and 8:47, and relates each to a fault in this sūra. [9 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:7/q10/p3 Other verses condemn withholding help. — Corpus Coranicum cites 68:12 and 70:21; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares hypocrites' clasped hands in 9:67. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 107:7/q11 Where and when was the sūra or its closing portion revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether evidence from the setting of hypocrisy locates the entire sūra or only its closing verses in Medina.
- 107:7/q11/p1 The entire sūra is Meccan, early in the mission. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima and Qatāda are cited; Asad places it probably after sūra 102 and rejects a Medina ending for lack of evidence. Most commentators are also reported to call it Meccan. [5 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:7/q11/p2 The entire sūra is Medinan. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Wāqidī are cited; Quṭb leans this way because political hypocrisy and ostentation suit Medina, and Study Quran allows it. The Study Quran suggests the political advantage of hypocrisy in Medina. [5 notes: JISHUMI(الضحاك، الواقدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:7/q11/p3 Its opening is Meccan and its later verses Medinan. — Muqātil places the move from fa-wayl to the end in Medina; other accounts describe first and second halves. Quṭb allows later thematic attachment. Asad rejects this split for lack of historical or textual evidence. [5 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); BURHAN(مقاتل بن سليمان); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(some commentators)-]

## 107:7/q12 What is reported about reciting this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 107:7/q12/p1 A reciter who pays zakāt receives forgiveness. — A report through Ubayy or from the Prophet is transmitted without grading by al-Jishumī, al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī; al-Rāzī uses it for the zakāt gloss. [4 notes: JISHUMI(النبي); KASHSHAF(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 107:7/q13 What titles are reported for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 107:7/q13/p1 It is called al-Māʿūn, al-Dīn, Araʾayta, and al-Takdhīb. — Al-Biqāʿī lists the names and relates them to denial of recompense; al-Itqān gives the first three, and Study Quran says the title comes from the last verse. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 107:7/q14 What structural division is proposed for the sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 107:7/q14/p1 Joining verses 6–7 yields two balanced triads. — Corpus Coranicum observes a possible rhyme stop at yurāʾūn and two symmetrical triads when the verses are combined. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 107:7/q15 What numerical historical allusions are proposed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the numerical count includes the basmala and whether any further count is warranted.
- 107:7/q15/p1 Twenty-five words point to year twelve AH and the defeat of apostates and zakāt refusers. — Al-Biqāʿī links the count from the mission's beginning to Abū Bakr's campaigns against Banū Ḥanīfa, al-Nujayr and al-Aswad al-ʿAnsī. He sees the earlier prayer and almsgiving of zakāt refusers as show. [5 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:7/q15/p2 The word count plus the basmala points to year sixteen AH and the departure of non-Muslim groups from Arabia. — Al-Biqāʿī says many converted and others moved toward Byzantine lands. The basmala adds four words in this reckoning. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 107:7/q15/p3 Further pronoun or letter counts are unnecessary. — Al-Biqāʿī says public triumph made the religion manifest without a hidden numerical signal. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:7/q16 How is the adjacent verb for ostentation read?  [* · readings]
- 107:7/q16/p1 Yarāʾūn is also transmitted with shortening and either doubled or undoubled hamza. — Al-Ālūsī records Ibn Isḥāq and al-Ashhab's readings of the preceding verb. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق والأشهب)]

## 107:7/q17 What inward moral reading is given?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the refusal is read as total self-withholding or selfish failure of spiritual qualities.
- 107:7/q17/p1 Withholding wealth and life reflects a failure to give oneself for God's pleasure. — Al-Sulamī contrasts it with Abū Bakr, who gave everything and said he left God and His Messenger for himself. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 107:7/q17/p2 Selfishness rooted in lack of love, justice, fear and hope prevents useful aid. — Al-Qāshānī says they withhold money and goods for narrow interests and will not prosper. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 107:7/q18 What unusual gloss is transmitted from Ibn ʿAbbās?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted wording and its uncertain referent.
- 107:7/q18/p1 The owners or intended recipients have not yet come for the items. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr transmit this brief wording from Ibn ʿAbbās. [3 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 107:7/q18/p2 These people had not yet appeared. — Al-Durr transmits this variant from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 107:7/q18/p3 What these people brought afterward. — A further al-Durr report uses this unclear wording; its intended referent is not explained. [1 notes: DURR(ابن عباس)]

- 107:7/q19/p1 The water, fire and salt report has a weak chain. — Al-Qurṭubī says Ibn Mājah records the report cited by al-Thaʿlabī, but judges its chain weak. [2 notes: QURTUBI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(الثعلبي وابن ماجه)]

## 107:7/q20 Why is the unbeliever unnamed when Adam's lapse is named?  [* · interpretation]
- 107:7/q20/p1 Adam's lapse was mentioned after his death and with repentance, to teach later people and honor his rank. — Al-Rāzī cites 20:121, says Adam's one lapse and repentance honor him, and cautions that one should not expect Paradise while persisting in greater sin. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
