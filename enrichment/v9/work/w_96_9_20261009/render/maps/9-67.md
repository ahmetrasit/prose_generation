# 9:67: 30 questions

## 9:67/q01 Who are the hypocrites described here?  [ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقَٰتُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the designation focuses on concealed disbelief or the broader failure to act on professed Islam.
- 9:67/q01/p1 Those who profess faith while concealing disbelief and persist deliberately in hypocrisy. — Al-Ṭabarī describes their outward profession and inner denial. Asad distinguishes the deliberate group of 9:66 from fearful wavering. Ibn ʿĀshūr says pardon for some cannot cover those who persist; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes links between hypocrite groups. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 9:67/q01/p2 Those who profess Islam without acting on it; hypocrisy of denial is unbelief, but one guilty of hypocrisy in sins may be hoped for. — Ḥudhayfa gives the behavioral definition and al-Ḥasan distinguishes the two kinds. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(الحسن,حذيفة)]

## 9:67/q02 How many hypocrite men and women were reported in Medina?  [ٱلْمُنَٰفِقُونَ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقَٰتُ · reports]
- 9:67/q02/p1 Three hundred men and one hundred seventy women. — Al-Māwardī attributes the count to Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Nasafī and al-Bursawī repeat it. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 9:67/q03 Why are hypocrite women named explicitly?  [ٱلْمُنَٰفِقَٰتُ · rhetoric]
- 9:67/q03/p1 To show that women share the men's traits, status, and fate. — Al-Ālūsī emphasizes entrenched hypocrisy; Ibn ʿĀshūr prevents a separate pardon for women; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī points to women's active role; Kur'an Yolu compares the paired naming in 9:71. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 9:67/q04 What does 'some of them are of others' say about the hypocrites?  [بَعْضُهُم مِّنۢ بَعْضٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase describes common identity, transmission of hypocrisy, or a limited mutual bond.
- 9:67/q04/p1 They share religion, condition, nature, status, and alliance as one hypocrite group. — Muqātil calls them allies, Ibn ʿAbbās says one religion, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares ears' shared legal treatment with the head. Al-Biqāʿī adds common desire, custom, and imitation. Al-Qushayrī likens their support to flocking birds. Abū ʿAlī and al-Zarkashī exclude ancestry since the two groups can be one another's descendants. Al-Wāḥidī cites 4:25; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses every member's inclusion. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,ابن عباس,غيره من أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(commentators); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 9:67/q04/p2 They acquire their hypocrisy from one another. — Al-Māwardī offers transmission as an alternative; al-Biqāʿī includes imitation among the sources of their likeness. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 9:67/q04/p3 They resemble one another in hypocrisy but lack believers' open, loving mutual loyalty. — Al-Wāḥidī says their hearts differ while believers love and correct each other. Al-Suyūṭī compares 9:71, 8:73 and 59:14. Abū Bakr al-Warrāq says they hide faults, while believers mirror them; al-Qushayrī expands the corrective role. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل المعاني); ITQAN; SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); QUSHAYRI]

## 9:67/q05 What is the force of min in 'some of them are of others'?  [مِّنۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the local sense is associative or explained through the particle's broader uses.
- 9:67/q05/p1 It conveys association or figurative partitivity, rather than descent. — Abū ʿAlī and al-Zarkashī note believers can descend from hypocrites and the reverse. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts believers' awliyāʾ in 9:71; al-Ālūsī cites the Aaron and Moses comparison for connection. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:67/q05/p2 Min can more generally mark an origin or a partitive relation. — Al-Jishumī illustrates travel from Basra to Kufa and a door made of iron. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 9:67/q06 How is 'some of them are of others' parsed?  [بَعْضُهُم مِّنۢ بَعْضٍۢ · grammar]
- 9:67/q06/p1 It is a nominal construction with baʿḍuhum as subject and min baʿḍ as predicate; a second nominal start or substitution is also possible. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī give the alternatives. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 9:67/q07 How do the following conduct clauses function syntactically?  [يَأْمُرُونَ بِٱلْمُنكَرِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَقْبِضُونَ أَيْدِيَهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clauses are external explanation or a further predicate.
- 9:67/q07/p1 They explain the shared condition and have no syntactic position. — Al-Samīn applies this to the enjoining clause and its coordination; Ibn ʿĀshūr also calls the conduct an explanation. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:67/q07/p2 The enjoining clause may be an independent reinforcement or a second predicate. — Al-Ālūsī offers both grammatical analyses. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:67/q08 What wrong do they command?  [يَأْمُرُونَ بِٱلْمُنكَرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether munkar here is general wrong, denial, idol worship, or disobedience.
- 9:67/q08/p1 Disbelief in Muhammad and revelation, together with unbelief, shirk, sins, and other ugly deeds. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls denial the worst wrong. Al-Rāzī treats denial as the chief instance of every ugly deed. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān note their public action was limited because Islam was ascendant; al-Biqāʿī stresses social discord. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الضحاك,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL]
- 9:67/q08/p2 Munkar means worship of idols and Satan. — Abū al-ʿĀliya says this of every Qur'anic use; al-Māwardī reports shirk; al-Ālūsī allows these examples within a broader term. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية)]
- 9:67/q08/p3 It is disobedience to God or all that sound reason rejects. — Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān gives disobedience; al-Māwardī gives rational rejection with shirk as an example. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL]

## 9:67/q09 What right do they forbid?  [وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْمَعْرُوفِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether maʿrūf is broad faith or a specific practice of it.
- 9:67/q09/p1 Faith in God and Muhammad, monotheistic testimony, acceptance of revelation, and obedience. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls testimony the greatest good; al-Rāzī makes faith the chief instance of all good; al-Māwardī also reports everything good reason recognizes. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)]
- 9:67/q09/p2 Following the Messenger and Islam, including giving alms to him. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk specify these acts. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 9:67/q09/p3 Jihad in God's path. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās lists it with commanding hypocrisy and denial. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس)]
- 9:67/q09/p4 Sincerity with truthful intention toward God. — ʿAṭāʾ transmits this specification. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عنه)]

## 9:67/q10 How does their moral conduct compare with believers' conduct?  [يَأْمُرُونَ بِٱلْمُنكَرِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ ٱلْمَعْرُوفِ · links]
turns on: The notes contrast either the paired commands alone or the wider conduct pattern.
- 9:67/q10/p1 It reverses believers' duty to promote right and prevent wrong. — Asad cites 3:104, 3:110, 3:114, 9:71, 9:112 and 22:41; Study Quran adds 7:157 and 31:17. Al-Bursawī says the opposition is made explicit. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI]
- 9:67/q10/p2 Their stinginess, reluctant prayer, evasion of battle, and disregard of God contrast with believers' giving, sincere prayer, sacrifice, and obedience. — Kur'an Yolu details four contrasts; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts promoting good and spending as remembrance. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 9:67/q11 What is the literal range of qabḍ?  [وَيَقْبِضُونَ أَيْدِيَهُمْ · meaning]
- 9:67/q11/p1 It is closing or grasping, opposite to opening or spreading. — Al-Jishumī mentions sword-grip and death forms and distinguishes qabṣ with fingertips from qabḍ with the palm; Elmalılı renders tightly closed hands. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ELMALILI]

## 9:67/q12 Why does clenching the hands express stinginess?  [وَيَقْبِضُونَ أَيْدِيَهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 9:67/q12/p1 The giver extends and opens a hand, while a miser closes it. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Rāzī, and al-Ālūsī give the physical image; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī compare 5:64. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 9:67/q13 What do they withhold by clenching their hands?  [وَيَقْبِضُونَ أَيْدِيَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The referent of the closed hands; al-Rāzī invokes obligatory censure and al-Ālūsī ordinary usage.
- 9:67/q13/p1 Wealth due for charity, zakat, the Prophet, or God's cause. — Mujāhid says due spending; al-Farrāʾ names the Prophet; al-Ṭabarī names the poor owed zakat. Al-Rāzī prefers obligatory giving because only forsaking an obligation warrants censure. Al-Biqāʿī says they spend reluctantly. [30 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); SULAMI(قيل); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، الحسن، ومجاهد,الحسن ومجاهد,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والحسن ومجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(commentators); RAZI-FULL+]
- 9:67/q13/p2 Every good deed, including charity and good works. — Qatāda repeatedly gives the broad gloss; al-Suddī includes charity. Al-Rāzī reports it but prefers obligatory spending. [12 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(القرظي,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL-]
- 9:67/q13/p3 Jihad, including taking up arms against enemies. — Al-Māwardī transmits it from later commentators; Ibn Zayd specifies arms. Al-Qurṭubī couples jihad with what is due. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Jubbāʾī but rejects this use as contrary to the expression's ordinary usage. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتأخرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); STUDYQURAN(commentators); ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي)-]
- 9:67/q13/p4 Raising hands to ask God for needs. — Al-Sulamī illustrates with a Prophetic prayer at the standing and a poem; Sufyān and al-Kāshifī also give supplication. [6 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سفيان); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 9:67/q13/p5 Helping Muslims. — Al-Sulamī reports this distinct explanation. [1 notes: SULAMI(قيل)]
- 9:67/q13/p6 Both fighting and giving. — Study Quran explicitly combines the two; al-Qurṭubī pairs jihad with fulfilling dues. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(commentators); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 9:67/q14 What does nisyān mean here?  [نَسُوا۟ فَنَسِيَهُمْ · meaning]
- 9:67/q14/p1 It can mean abandoning or leaving, not only a lapse of memory. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Ṭabarī give this usage; al-Jishumī also records its contrast with remembering and forms for forgotten property; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites 2:237 and 28:77. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 9:67/q15 What does it mean that they forgot God?  [نَسُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of neglect the human forgetting names.
- 9:67/q15/p1 They abandoned God's commands and obedience, including the Prophet, law, jihad, or spending. — Muqātil includes refused giving; Ibn ʿAbbās includes jihad; Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions Prophet and law. Al-Rāzī says the commands were treated as if forgotten. [28 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,علي عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 9:67/q15/p2 They neglected remembering, worshipping, and praising God. — Al-Biqāʿī says they treated Him as irrelevant; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts enjoining right, preventing wrong, and spending as remembrance. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 9:67/q15/p3 They forgot God's blessings, then forgot gratitude for them. — Sahl's inward explanation occurs in al-Tustarī and al-Sulamī. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 9:67/q15/p4 They forgot good but remembered evil. — Qatāda is transmitted by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, and al-Suyūṭī. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 9:67/q16 What does God's forgetting them mean?  [فَنَسِيَهُمْ · theology]
turns on: The specified deprivation and whether punishment remains in view.
- 9:67/q16/p1 He leaves them without guidance, mercy, grace, reward, honor, or other good. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī distinguish worldly guidance from afterlife saving mercy; Ibn ʿAbbās specifies reward and honor; al-Wāḥidī mentions doubt; al-Qushayrī cites 2:17's darkness. ʿAlī says no afterlife share. Al-Bursawī excludes overpowering or active punishment. [32 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,علي عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 9:67/q16/p2 He treats them as forgotten with respect to good, while retaining them for punishment. — Qatāda contrasts good and evil; Mujāhid names punishment; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr specifies Resurrection Day and denies literal forgetting. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran cite 45:34; al-Riḍā cites 19:64, 39:19 and 45:34 and says they may forget themselves. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]

## 9:67/q17 Why is the reciprocal word 'forget' used of God?  [نَسُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ فَنَسِيَهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 9:67/q17/p1 It figuratively matches human abandonment with divine requital; literal divine forgetfulness is impossible. — Ibn Qutayba illustrates reciprocal requital with a prayer against a satirist; Abū ʿAlī cites 2:17 and 18:99 for leaving; al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī compare 42:40. Al-Rāzī says involuntary human forgetfulness is not blameworthy. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the figure intensifies abandonment; al-Ālūsī calls it verbal correspondence. Al-Riḍā and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr reject literal divine forgetfulness. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(النبي); FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام)]

## 9:67/q18 What does fāsiqūn mean here?  [ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether fisq is broad rebellion, unbelief here, lying, or violation of the Messenger's command.
- 9:67/q18/p1 Those who depart from obedience and the true path, fully rebellious and stripped of good. — Ibn Qutayba gives the root sense of going out; al-Farrāʾ compares a date leaving its skin; Ibn al-Aʿrābī gives fuwaysiqa for mouse and says fāsiq was unattested in pre-Islamic poetry or speech. Al-Samarrāʾī repeats the date image and distinguishes fisq from ẓulm: it can be lesser sin or reach unbelief, as 18:50, 24:55, 17:16, and 2:197 illustrate. [25 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن الأعرابي,ابن قتيبة,الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 9:67/q18/p2 Disbelievers in the Prophet and his message, deserving lasting Hell. — Dāmghānī links 9:66; Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies eternal punishment; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī emphasize unbelief. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 9:67/q18/p3 Liars. — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd ibn Aslam gives this gloss. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)]
- 9:67/q18/p4 Those who violate the Messenger's command. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports a fifth exegetical sense. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 9:67/q19 What does the emphatic identification as the fāsiqūn convey?  [إِنَّ ٱلْمُنَٰفِقِينَ هُمُ ٱلْفَٰسِقُونَ · rhetoric]
- 9:67/q19/p1 They are portrayed as the fullest instance of rebellion, a harsh name that deters Muslims. — Al-Ālūsī says the separating pronoun and definite predicate create restriction despite other sinners. Al-Zamakhsharī cites the Prophet's dislike of calling oneself lazy because 4:142 calls hypocrites lazy; al-Nasafī repeats the warning. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:67/q20 How does the verse relate to the hypocrites' claim in 9:56?  [بَعْضُهُم مِّنۢ بَعْضٍۢ · links]
- 9:67/q20/p1 It refutes their oath that they belong to believers and confirms that they do not. — Al-Zajjāj and later commentators tie the phrase to 9:56; their inverse conduct demonstrates their real affiliation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares the legal expression about ears and the head. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:67/q21 How does 9:67 fit its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether it explains the pardon-punishment distinction, starts a new paired account, or sums up prior faults.
- 9:67/q21/p1 It explains why persistent hypocrites may be punished despite the preceding possibility of pardon for some. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this: common nature and deeds yield common recompense, not punishment for neighbors' acts. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise links outcomes to continued hypocrisy and allows the clause to explain the punished group. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 9:67/q21/p2 It starts a fresh description and judgment of hypocrites paired with the believers' following account. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a new report. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports 9:68's hypocrite-disbeliever pairing as support. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an independent parenthesis or a parenthesis with 9:68 before 9:69. Kur'an Yolu contrasts 9:67–68 with 9:71–72. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI-]
- 9:67/q21/p3 It gathers the earlier faults and shared nature of hypocrites, including those who stayed from the campaign. — Al-Ālūsī offers summary of prior faults; al-Biqāʿī says unpardoned men would have acted alike on campaign; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares earlier disbelievers' worldly pursuit following the address in 9:66. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:67/q22 What reports about hypocrites accompany the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are different reports, not rival versions of one event.
- 9:67/q22/p1 Hypocrites in Ḥudhayfa's time were worse because they openly showed what earlier ones concealed. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report his comparison. [2 notes: THALABI(حذيفة); TABATABAI(حذيفة)]
- 9:67/q22/p2 The Prophet reportedly listed lying, betrayal, broken promises and covenants, abusive quarrels, neglected prayer, and little remembrance. — Al-Māwardī transmits Abū al-Dardāʾ's question through Makhūl. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مكحول عن أبي الدرداء)]

## 9:67/q23 How is the wāw in nasū Allāha read at a pause?  [نَسُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · readings]
- 9:67/q23/p1 The common reciters pause with wāw despite its reported omission in the rasm. — The Nashr records this contrast. [1 notes: NASHR(جميع القراء)]

## 9:67/q24 What can interrogative man express and how do its forms behave?  [مِّنۢ · grammar]
turns on: How far analogy and isolated attestation support unusual forms.
- 9:67/q24/p1 It can mark number and gender in pause, though its inflection is more limited than ayy. — Sībawayh lists manayn, manh, mantayn and manāt, singular oblique manw and manī, and first-mentioned gender governing mixed pairs. Al-Khalīl says nūn is silent in connection and adduces man yā fatā and man qāla dhāk. Ayy pluralizes, dualizes, takes tanwīn and iḍāfa more fully. Yūnus also reports unchanged pause forms among some speakers. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 9:67/q24/p2 Yūnus analogizes a feminine form to ayyah and reports a Bedouin's ḍaraba man minnā; Sībawayh considers this rare and distant. — Yūnus admits disagreement; Sībawayh says he heard the form only once in poetry and it was not widely used. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس); SIBAWAYH-]

## 9:67/q25 What is said about the metaphor of degrees?  [* · rhetoric]
- 9:67/q25/p1 It transfers the image of a physical stairway ascended or descended rung by rung to degrees. — Ibn Qutayba gives this concrete origin. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 9:67/q26 How is Qur'anic inimitability argued from the Arabs' language?  [* · theology]
- 9:67/q26/p1 Their inability to match the Qur'an despite a challenge and high poetic skill shows it exceeded ordinary speech. — Al-Bāqillānī allows convention, teaching, discovery and refinement in ordinary language but says none accounts for inimitability. The Arabs made elaborate poetry without practical need, so lack of skill did not explain failure. [3 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 9:67/q27 What is reported about the Qur'an as God's banquet?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the first saying belongs to ʿAlī or the Prophet; the second is separate.
- 9:67/q27/p1 A saying calls it God's banquet, rope, light, healing, protection, and salvation, with ten rewards per letter; its Prophetic attribution is uncertain. — Ibn Kathīr prefers ʿAlī's own saying despite sound meaning and a supporting Ibn Masʿūd report he calls gharīb. He notes Ḥamza's rank as reciter despite hadith weakness, questions of al-Ḥārith's belief rather than deliberate fabrication, and Abū Ḥātim and al-Azdī's criticisms of al-Hijrī. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو حاتم الرازي,عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب)+]
- 9:67/q27/p2 Love of the Qur'an indicates love of God and His Messenger. — Ibn Masʿūd says a person's state is known by the Qur'an. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 9:67/q28 How can an occasion of revelation guide interpretation?  [* · sciences]
turns on: How far the occasion restricts general wording.
- 9:67/q28/p1 It reveals context and wisdom, and the occasioning case remains certainly included if general wording is restricted. — Al-Zarkashī names ʿAlī ibn al-Madīnī and al-Wāḥidī as writers and al-Qushayrī on Companions' contextual clues. Excluding the original case would delay explanation or evade the question. [5 notes: BURHAN(أبو الفتح القشيري)]
- 9:67/q28/p2 For those following a specific cause, the occasion can confine a ruling to that case. — Al-Zarkashī reports this position. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 9:67/q29 What other rhetorical and grammatical examples occur in the ancillary notes?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The examples address separate usages.
- 9:67/q29/p1 Reproachful address forms humiliate by their form. — Al-Zarkashī cites 66:7 and 109:1. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 9:67/q29/p2 Shared divine and human attributes remain grammatically dependent in received usage. — Al-Zarkashī notes this convention. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 9:67/q29/p3 Istihzāʾ is a heard insult while sukhriya may be inward; prophets use mockery language rather than a literal insulting act. — Al-Zarkashī cites 11:38. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 9:67/q30 What further verse links or legal implications accompany the passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Distinct ancillary applications.
- 9:67/q30/p1 The withholding in 9:67 parallels al-māʿūn understood as charity or zakat. — Ibn ʿĀshūr applies the verse elsewhere. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:67/q30/p2 Being made forgotten can mark an act as prohibited rather than merely disliked. — Al-Suyūṭī says asking why an act occurred signals prohibition still more clearly. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 9:67/q30/p3 Ẓulm exceeds bounds and may reach unbelief, often harming others, whereas fisq extends even to wronging oneself. — Al-Samarrāʾī cites 2:254 and 31:13 and compares the degrees of fisq with 18:50, 24:55, 17:16 and 2:197. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
