# 7:126: 19 questions

## 7:126/q01 How is تنقم read?  [تَنقِمُ · readings]
turns on: The two attested dialectal imperfect forms and which readers use them.
- 7:126/q01/p1 The qāf has kasra in the majority reading, from the more eloquent verbal form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says most read kasra and Abū Ḥātim prefers it; Abū Ḥayyān calls the majority form more eloquent. Ibn ʿĀshūr says all reciters use kasra; al-Akhfash says he reads the first of two dialectal forms. Al-Samīn refers to the two forms at 5:59. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، وأبو حيوة، وأبو اليسر هاشم، وابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; AKHFASH+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 7:126/q01/p2 The qāf has fatḥa in a valid dialectal reading. — Al-Thaʿlabī names al-Ḥasan and Ibn Muḥayṣin and accepts both verb forms. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds Abū Ḥaywa and Ibn Abī ʿAbla and cites Ibn al-Ruqayyāt's verse; Abū Ḥayyān adds Abū al-Yusr Hāshim. Ibn ʿĀshūr acknowledges two patterns but says reciters use kasra. [4 notes: THALABI(الحسن وابن المحيصن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، وأبو حيوة، وأبو اليسر هاشم، وابن أبي عبلة); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:126/q02 What does تنقم منا mean?  [تَنقِمُ مِنَّآ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes Pharaoh's aversion or the harm he inflicts; Abū Ḥayyān appeals to من.
- 7:126/q02/p1 Pharaoh dislikes, resents, reproaches, or finds fault with them. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites a poem with نقموا for taking offense, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk glosses criticism. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī describe intense dislike and cite 5:59. Al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give variants of dislike, blame, objection, and fault; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes resentment expressed in speech or action. [13 notes: THALABI(الشاعر,الضحاك وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:126/q02/p2 Pharaoh punishes or inflicts harm upon them for their faith. — Al-Ṭabrisī says naqima can mean punish as well as object, distinguishing punishment from a blameworthy injury. Abū Ḥayyān prefers harm because من suggests what Pharaoh brings upon them, comparing ينتقم الله منه; he says نقم and انتقم then share a sense. ʿAṭāʾ's paraphrase denies any offense deserving punishment; al-Ālūsī reports his punishment gloss. [5 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 7:126/q03 How does the exception إلا أن آمنا work with تنقم منا?  [تَنقِمُ مِنَّآ إِلَّآ أَنْ ءَامَنَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether تنقم takes faith as its object or denotes an attack whose cause is faith.
- 7:126/q03/p1 Belief is the object of fault finding in an emptied exception. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it a free exception under fault-finding glosses and compares 5:59 and 85:8; he cites a sword-flaw poem for the pattern. Al-Samīn makes the infinitival clause the object. Al-Ālūsī calls the verbal noun the object in an absolute exception and cites a poem about guests distracted by longing. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the exception connected because faith is precisely what Pharaoh resents. Al-Ālūsī says من refers to the believing company; al-Samīn notes تضمين in governing من. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:126/q03/p2 Belief states the sole cause of the punishment or attack. — Abū Ḥayyān makes the exception causal under his preferred harm sense. Al-Samīn calls this the alternative restrictive, emptied exception. Al-Ālūsī reports ʿAṭāʾ's punishment gloss and says both analyses cut off Pharaoh's hope that the threat will succeed. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 7:126/q04 What was Pharaoh's grievance and what were the signs?  [إِلَّآ أَنْ ءَامَنَّا بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّنَا · interpretation]
- 7:126/q04/p1 His only grievance was their faith in God's signs brought by Moses. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn ʿAbbās specify the staff and hand, divine miracles beyond Pharaoh or anyone else; Ṭabāṭabāʾ suggests the staff's serpent, its swallowing the ropes and staffs, and its restoration. ʿAṭāʾ says they had caused Pharaoh no injury meriting punishment. Al-Biqāʿī calls God their benefactor. Al-Rāzī says manifest signs should make Pharaoh follow truth; Abū Ḥayyān says their reply repudiates his divinity. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(السحرة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 7:126/q05 How is لما جاءتنا construed?  [لَمَّا جَآءَتْنَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether لما is a temporal adverb governed by آمنا or a particle with an elided result.
- 7:126/q05/p1 لما is temporal, governed by آمنا: they believed when the signs arrived. — Abū Ḥayyān gives this analysis. Al-Samīn attributes it to al-Fārisī and as one view of Sībawayh. Al-Biqāʿī stresses that they did not delay once proof arrived. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الفارسي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:126/q05/p2 لما is a particle requiring a result, with an omitted answer: when the signs came, they believed. — Abū Ḥayyān supplies the elided answer; al-Samīn adds that belief followed without hesitation. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 7:126/q06 What force does their charge against Pharaoh have?  [وَمَا تَنقِمُ مِنَّآ إِلَّآ أَنْ ءَامَنَّا بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّنَا · rhetoric]
- 7:126/q06/p1 Pharaoh treats faith, their virtue and honor, as a fault; his threat cannot make them abandon evident truth. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī call faith the best deed and root of virtue; al-Nasafī compares a poet's sole 'blemish' of warriors, their sword-wear. Al-Biqāʿī says evident truth calls for gratitude and prompt recognition for honor, not vengeance. Elmalılı says Pharaoh's anger exposes injustice and ignorance; al-Rāzī calls the answer exemplary because Pharaoh should follow truth. Abū Ḥayyān says it disowns Pharaoh's lordship. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(السحرة); ELMALILI]

## 7:126/q07 What does أفرغ علينا صبرا picture?  [أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the image emphasizes an abundant infusion of patience or purifying help through endurance; al-Ālūsī distinguishes their figures.
- 7:126/q07/p1 Patience is poured over or into them like water, surrounding or filling them abundantly. — Ibn Qutayba glosses 'pour'; al-Zajjāj says cover. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Jishumī liken it to emptying a vessel; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾ develop water, flooding, and vessel imagery. Al-Rāzī says pouring requests all patience, stronger than sending some down. Al-Ṭabrisī defines ṣabr as restraining visible despair. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees hidden vessel metaphor, imagined pouring, complete filling, and an address shift to God; he cites 2:250. Al-Ālūsī calls pouring a derivative metaphor with patience as indicator, while Bursawī describes a concealed simile elaborated by metaphor. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(السحرة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:126/q07/p2 The pouring can picture what cleanses their sins as they endure the threat. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī give the purification sense. Al-Ālūsī parses patience as an original concealed metaphor and 'pour' as imaginative elaboration; he rejects the proposal that the two figurative readings work alike through flooding or purification. Elmalılı says the flood keeps them pure of unbelief, idolatry, deceit, and sin. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:126/q08 Why do they ask God for patience?  [رَبَّنَآ أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًۭا · interpretation]
- 7:126/q08/p1 They seek strength to endure Pharaoh's mutilation, crucifixion, and threat without fear, despair, or a return to disbelief. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, ʿAṭāʾ, Mujāhid, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Study Quran make steadfastness through torture explicit. Al-Ṭabarī specifies restraint against renouncing God. Al-Ṭabrisī adds bravery without fear; Ibn Kathīr relates their preference for enduring Pharaoh over God's harsher punishment to 20:72–75. Abū Ḥayyān says the prayer is conditional on the threat occurring and does not establish its fulfillment. Al-Nasafī and al-Biqāʿī stress abundant, encompassing patience; al-Ṭabrisī calls patience for truth an honor. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(السحرة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Ṭs)]

## 7:126/q09 What does the indefinite صبرا add?  [صَبْرًۭا · rhetoric]
- 7:126/q09/p1 It requests complete and perfect patience. — Al-Rāzī compares the indefinite ḥayātan in 2:96 for a complete life. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 7:126/q10 What does توفنا مسلمين ask for?  [وَتَوَفَّنَا مُسْلِمِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether they ask for steadfastness whenever death comes or for immediate death before Pharaoh can harm them.
- 7:126/q10/p1 They ask to remain steadfast and sincere in submission to God's religion until death. — Muqātil calls it sincere Islam; al-Ṭabarī mentions Abraham's religion. Mujāhid explains sincere devotion in worship, Ibn Kaysān and Ibn al-Jawzī adherence to Moses' religion, and al-Rāzī the true religion Moses brought. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī stress resistance to apostasy under threats; Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'Muslims' denotes the prophets' and ṣiddīqūn's state since Abraham, with 2:132. Study Quran takes it universally as full submission to the One God. Elmalılı sees certainty and indifference to death. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كيسان,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(السحرة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والكلبي والسدي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:126/q10/p2 They may ask to be taken immediately to God as submitters, escaping Pharaoh's punishment. — Study Quran reports al-Rāzī's possible reading and compares 20:72–73 and 26:50–51. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 7:126/q11 What theological lessons are drawn from the prayers for patience and a Muslim death?  [أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًۭا وَتَوَفَّنَا مُسْلِمِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether asking God for human steadfastness implies His creation of the act or His prompting help alongside human action.
- 7:126/q11/p1 God creates their patience, faith, and Islam, since they ask Him for acts realized in them. — Al-Rāzī says decisive motives and therefore the whole act come from God; he rejects al-Qāḍī's لطف explanation as contrary to the apparent wording and answers the Muʿtazilite appeal to لطف through the Muslim-death prayer. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-; JISHUMI-]
- 7:126/q11/p2 God grants prompting لطف while their belief and steadfast response are their own acts. — Al-Qāḍī says they ask for divine لطف to prompt steadfastness. Al-Jishumī contrasts their voluntary faith with Pharaoh's voluntary threat to refute determinism. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL-]
- 7:126/q11/p3 The prayer presupposes that patience is restraining oneself through hardship, and divine 'patience' needs separate qualification. — Al-Jishumī says God should not be called patient; Abū ʿAlī rejects al-Ṣabūr without a sound report and, if sound, explains it as forbearance. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 7:126/q12 What does the wording of their prayer imply about faith, Islam, and reward?  [ءَامَنَّا مُسْلِمِينَ · theology]
- 7:126/q12/p1 Faith and Islam are one and the same in this exchange. — Al-Qāḍī argues that they first identify their belief in the signs and then ask to die Muslims. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 7:126/q12/p2 Reward is attained through Islam and sincere faith culminates in a Muslim death and Paradise. — Al-Jishumī draws the reward lesson from their request; he says they inferred oneness and prophethood before believing. Abū ʿAlī says their answer proves sincere faith and God answered by letting them die Muslims and enter Paradise. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)]

## 7:126/q13 Was Pharaoh's threat against the magicians carried out?  [أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًۭا وَتَوَفَّنَا مُسْلِمِينَ · reports]
turns on: How to weigh martyrdom reports, the prayer's wording, the lack of an explicit execution report in the Qur'an, and the promise at 28:35.
- 7:126/q13/p1 Pharaoh mutilated or crucified them; they became martyrs the day they had been magicians. — Muqātil says dawn unbelievers became evening martyrs. Al-Ṭabarī transmits al-Suddī on killing and crucifixion, and ʿUbayd, Qatāda, and Mujāhid on same-day martyrdom; Ibn Abī Ḥātim names Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī. Al-Kalbī reports hand and foot amputation and crucifixion; Bursawī's Ibn ʿAbbās report locates it on the Nile bank. Al-Rāzī reports an argument from their plea for patience. Elmalılı favors execution from context: their self-sacrifice established sincere testimony and the miracle's truth; al-Ālūsī says promised victory can mean argumentative or ultimate victory despite deaths. Study Quran notes that later verses suggest martyrdom and reports purification from past sins through painful death. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي,عبيد بن عمير,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس، السدي); THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وعبيد بن عمير وقتادة وابن جريج); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes,ابن عباس والكلبي والسدي); ELMALILI(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ, Ṭs,Z); ELMALILI+; RAZI-FULL(آخرون)-; BIQAI-FULL-; BURSEVI(قيل)-]
- 7:126/q13/p2 Pharaoh never reached them; God protected them or his threat did not occur. — Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī cite God's promise of victory for Moses, Aaron, and their followers at 28:35. Al-Rāzī reports an inference from God's answer to their prayer to die Muslims and calls it close; al-Biqāʿī says their address to God as Lord alludes to the answer. Elmalılı notes the argument that the Qur'an never explicitly states execution. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows that God may have spared them disgrace and apparently reads Pharaoh as silenced by their fearlessness. [10 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); ELMALILI(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(آخرون)+; ELMALILI-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 7:126/q13/p3 The prayer itself does not settle whether Pharaoh inflicted the threatened punishment. — Abū Ḥayyān says patience was requested if punishment came; he also rejects inferring non-execution from their request that God take them as Muslims. Al-Rāzī reports both the argument from requested patience and the counterargument that they merely sought steadfastness against a threat. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Qur'an omits fulfillment to emphasize divine support, guidance, and faith; reconstruction does not add to the lesson of 79:26. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:126/q14 How did their new faith shape their response to the threat?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:126/q14/p1 Trust in return to God, His reward, and His greater authority made Pharaoh's threat bearable. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī link the reply to their return to God at 7:125. Al-Ṭabrisī says return means His recompense, with monotheism and sincerity, and confidence in harsher divine punishment; Ibn Kathīr cites 20:72–75. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān emphasize surrender, reliance, and Judgment Day reward. Bursawī says they welcome meeting Him whether death is inevitable or His mercy and reward follow execution. Abū Ḥayyān separately reports readings of hoped-for deliverance and inevitable death; he rejects al-Zamakhsharī's inclusion of Pharaoh in the return pronoun because later pronouns specify the believers. Their 'our Lord' disavows Pharaoh. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(السحرة); ELMALILI]
- 7:126/q14/p2 Their answer demonstrates sincere conviction and a turn to God for help against oppression. — Abū ʿAlī says their answer proves sincere faith and God answered it. Al-Jishumī requires patience under oppression and wholehearted recourse to God for protection or recompense. Al-Baghawī notes their turn from Pharaoh to God; Elmalılı sees certainty and disregard of death. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 7:126/q15 How do inward readings understand the magicians' response?  [* · inward]
turns on: The sources develop different spiritual images of the conversion, threat, and prayer.
- 7:126/q15/p1 God overturned their outward contest and disclosed inward love and gnosis, freeing them from fear of the self. — Al-Qushayrī says their apparent victory was a ruse defeated by divine power; God disclosed love under apparent enmity and brought the decreed outcome. Their hearts escaped imagined separation, witnessed God, and lost fear of threats; hardship became easy because they were going to Him. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 7:126/q15/p2 Those who suffer for God sincerely seek and rely entirely on His aid. — Al-Qushayrī draws this rule from their plea. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 7:126/q15/p3 Pharaoh is the ego, the city the body, and the threatened mutilation the ego's attempt to detach virtues from good works and fasten them to the world. — Bursawī says the ego accuses the soul's faculties of trusting the spirit without permission; bodily pleasures leave when the faculties side with the spirit. False suggestion cuts their limbs from good works; the soul's qualities seek patience in severing worldly attachments and death in servitude. [4 notes: BURSEVI]
- 7:126/q15/p4 Freed souls rejoice in divine love and receive revelation after the bodily veil falls. — Bursawī cites the Persian Mathnawī for this image. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:126/q16 What narrative context and aftermath are reported around this exchange?  [* · reports]
- 7:126/q16/p1 Pharaoh accused the magicians of conspiring with Moses to seize control and expel the city leaders. — Ibn Kathīr recounts Pharaoh's charge before the threat. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 7:126/q16/p2 After the magicians believed, six hundred thousand Israelites followed Moses, who then remained about a year in Egypt showing signs. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the first detail from Ibn ʿAbbās and the latter through al-Naqqāsh from Muqātil. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:126/q16/p3 Pharaoh's propaganda and terror still swayed his people for a time. — Elmalılı says deceit can temporarily affect people even in the presence of truth. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 7:126/q17 How does the scene connect with other Qur'anic portrayals and the sūra?  [* · links]
- 7:126/q17/p1 The magicians' faith and Pharaoh's vengeance echo other accounts of persecuted believers and prayers under threat. — Study Quran compares a believer at Pharaoh's court and condemnations at 40:28, 5:59, and 85:9; it notes the same patience prayer before Goliath's army at 2:250. Al-Karmānī says this sūra condenses Pharaoh's story while al-Shuʿarāʾ expands it through the drowning. Study Quran places 7:104–126 among the fullest Moses and Pharaoh accounts. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Ṭs); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكرماني)]
- 7:126/q17/p2 The defeated magicians' hoped-for reward and elevated standing foreshadow spiritual victory or martyrdom. — Study Quran finds worldly reward language ambiguously foreshadowing God's reward; 'brought nigh' describes prophets, angels, and the righteous in Paradise at 3:45, 4:172, 56:10–11, 56:88, 83:21, and 83:28. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 7:126/q18 How is the magicians' answer represented as a literary scene?  [* · rhetoric]
- 7:126/q18/p1 It exemplifies speech rendered within a vivid scene, with degrees of verbal expression and descriptive detail. — Al-Bāqillānī says inner thought can be conveyed by gesture, indication, or eloquent wording, and description varies in accuracy, beauty, brevity, and completeness. He contrasts concise fear in 18:18 with extended Resurrection scenes in 18:47 and 22:1–2, then relates the magicians' answer to their declaration of return to God and hope for forgiveness at 26:50. [4 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 7:126/q19 What grammatical detail is noted in the prayer's address?  [رَبَّنَآ · grammar]
- 7:126/q19/p1 Rabbana is a vocative with annexation. — Al-Jishumī also notes, in the surrounding exchange, that إنا contracts إننا by deleting a nūn. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
