# 83:1: 12 questions

## 83:1/q01 What does wayl signify here?  [وَيْلٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether wayl is the ordinary cry of ruin or punishment, a specific Hell valley, its pus, or the Fire; Ibn Qutayba rejects a literal valley gloss, while al-Rāghib treats it as an application rather than the dictionary sense.
- 83:1/q01/p1 Ruin, grief, reproof, calamity, or painful punishment is assigned to the defrauders. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Rāzī call it an expression uttered at calamity; al-Naḥḥās calls it reproof; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes lasting misery. Al-Māwardī lists destruction, the harshest punishment, a war cry of ruin, and a possible origin in 'way to so-and-so.' Ibn Kaysān calls it a distressed cry, and al-Aṣmaʿī includes disgrace and regret. Biqāʿī extends the ruin through this life and the next; Elmalılı says Turkish 'vay hâline' weakens the force. Islāhī says it also curses and censures. Al-Rāghib and Elmalılı distinguish Hell as its figurative or established religious application from its dictionary sense. [24 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(بعض أهل اللغة); QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الأصمعي); ELMALILI(Asma‘î,Âlûsî); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râgıb el-İsfahânî); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(آخر)]
- 83:1/q01/p2 Wayl is a valley in Hell. — Muqātil depicts it as seventy years long with nested fiery branches, chambers, trees, worms, snakes, and scorpions. Abū Saʿīd's report says a disbeliever falls forty years; other reports place it between two mountains. Al-Ālūsī calls the ʿUthmān report's chain questionable and suspends judgment on whether the valley is a literal lexical name. Al-Farrāʾ distinguishes the valley report from ordinary wayl; Ibn Qutayba rejects the valley gloss as unsound. Al-Rāghib says it denotes the deserved Hell abode rather than the dictionary meaning. [19 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد); ELMALILI(Abdullah,Asma‘î,Âlûsî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râgıb el-İsfahânî,hadisler); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(آخر)-]
- 83:1/q01/p3 Wayl is the pus of Hell's inhabitants or a valley of that pus. — Al-Ṭabarī describes a valley of Hell's pus; al-Māwardī attributes the pus gloss to Ibn Masʿūd; Elmalılı cites ʿAbd Allāh through Ibn Abī Ḥātim for a pus-filled valley. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ELMALILI(Abdullah)]
- 83:1/q01/p4 Wayl is the Fire itself. — Al-Māwardī reports this from ʿUmar, the client of ʿAfra. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عمر مولى عفرة)]

## 83:1/q02 How is wayl constructed grammatically?  [وَيْلٌۭ لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form is treated as an established nominative subject or an accusative verbal noun or imprecation in a hypothetical construction; annexation and verb derivation affect the preference.
- 83:1/q02/p1 Wayl is a nominative subject and li-l-muṭaffifīn its predicate, presenting the threat as established. — Al-Zajjāj prefers nominative because doom is fixed; Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts accusative wishing or awaiting. Al-Naḥḥās prefers nominative because derived verbs are exceptionally rare, as Muḥammad ibn al-Walīd illustrates with wāl, wāḥ, and wās. Al-Samīn says an indefinite curse or supplication can begin a sentence; al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī agree. Makkī prefers nominative for unannexed wayl. Al-Naḥḥās notes that with an omitted predicate one can use waylahu or wayḥahu. [12 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن الوليد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 83:1/q02/p2 Outside the Qur'anic wording, accusative waylan is possible as a verbal noun or imprecation. — Al-Zajjāj says it would express God assigning doom but the written Qur'an cannot be altered. Al-Naḥḥās says grammarians favor accusative for a form derived from a verb, comparing buʾsan lahu; Makkī favors it when wayl is annexed or definite. Al-Samīn also allows the accusative. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(النحويون); SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)+]

## 83:1/q03 Who are the mutaffifīn and what does their shortchanging cover?  [لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the warning is confined to commercial measure or extends by analogy to any reciprocal due, worship, self-scrutiny, or the inward scale.
- 83:1/q03/p1 They give others less than their due in measure or weight, often while demanding full measure for themselves. — Abū ʿUbayda, al-Mubarrad, Ibn Qutayba and many commentators give the commercial sense; al-Ṭabarī stresses withheld rights. Al-Rāzī says the small fraud is covert whereas a large one is obvious; al-Būrsawī calls it secret buyer fraud. Elmalılı and Asad appeal to the next two verses for the take-full/give-less contrast. Al-Nasafī makes the threat their predicate. Qatādah urges giving as one wishes to receive. [32 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة والمبرد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 83:1/q03/p2 The principle reaches every owed right, word, deed, or social relation. — Abū Ḥafṣ and al-Qushayrī describe taking one's rights without granting others'; al-Qushayrī adds hiding others' faults, overlooking one's own, and treating a Muslim as oneself. Some scholars and Mālik say every matter can be fulfilled or diminished; Ibn ʿAṭiyya accepts context-dependent excess as another form of taṭfīf. Wahb includes withheld reciprocal recompense; Asad includes moral relations and possessions; Study Quran includes anything God ordains. [10 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء,مالك); RAZI-FULL(القشيري); QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 83:1/q03/p3 It includes deficient prayer or worship, including public devotion withheld in private. — Salmān or Sulaymān compares prayer with a measure repaid in kind; Sufi interpreters and Abū ʿUthmān apply the verse to worship shown to people but neglected in private. Ibn Masʿūd is named in a later version. [6 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان); MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتعمقة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سلمان); DURR-FULL(سلمان); STUDYQURAN(Ibn Masʿūd)]
- 83:1/q03/p4 It includes scrutinizing others' faults while ignoring one's own. — Tustarī cites 2:44 about urging good while forgetting oneself; Abū Ṣāliḥ Ḥamdūn, Abū Ḥafṣ, al-Qushayrī and Study Quran repeat the reciprocal fault pattern. Tustarī adds that the ignorant expose others' slips and that disclosing a learned secret is accursed; the revelation to David says private sin and public repentance should not expose a servant. [7 notes: TUSTARI(الله); SULAMI(أبو حفص,أبو صالح حمدون); RAZI-FULL(القشيري); STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 83:1/q03/p5 On the inward scale of justice, the warning concerns a person's character and deeds. — Qāshānī says the true scale measures character and deeds, beyond physical trade. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 83:1/q04 Why are they called mutaffifīn?  [لِّلْمُطَفِّفِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is explained by the smallness of the missing amount, the brim of a nearly full measure, stealth, or another proposed root.
- 83:1/q04/p1 The name points to the tiny amount removed or withheld. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq and others derive it from ṭafīf, a slight thing. Al-Rāzī says a large fraud would be noticed; al-Ālūsī says the intensive form marks repeated acts, not large individual amounts. Saʿdī al-Muftī likewise reads habitual action, though allowing a causative form. Elmalılı prefers this derivation. Biqāʿī cites the saying that Adam's children are only a slight measure apart. [25 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Râzî); STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI+]
- 83:1/q04/p2 It comes from ṭaff or ṭafāf, the edge or nearly full rim of a measure. — Al-Zajjāj and others derive it from the edge; al-Thaʿlabī cites ṭifāf for an almost-full vessel and ka-ṭaff al-ṣāʿ for a vessel left short. Lexicographers say a measurer stops at that level; Biqāʿī suggests removing what rises above the top and cites Ibn ʿUmar's horse nearly reaching the Banū Zurayq mosque to illustrate nearness to a limit. Elmalılı notes usages for a filled measure, a leveled remainder, or its top. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر); ELMALILI(Râzî); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); STUDYQURAN]
- 83:1/q04/p3 It describes a secret theft of a small amount. — Al-Jishumī reports this as a distinct account and calls the shortfall subtle; al-Būrsawī also stresses concealment. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI]
- 83:1/q04/p4 Other proposed roots refer to taking what is close at hand or rationing. — Biqāʿī lists these alongside reducing a petty amount and says the verse suits a usually tiny theft. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 83:1/q05 What event occasioned this warning?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which report supplies the immediate setting and whether the reported Anṣār setting fits the surah's provenance and its moral argument.
- 83:1/q05/p1 Medinan traders' dishonest measuring prompted the warning when the Prophet arrived, and they then improved. — Muqātil says the Prophet warned traders and God confirmed him by revelation. Al-Farrāʾ and a second Ibn ʿAbbās report specify buyers taking extra and sellers giving less; Ibn ʿAbbās traditions describe Medina's people as the worst measurers and later exceptionally accurate. Al-Suyūṭī calls one chain sound. ʿAbd Allāh tells those praising Medinan measures that the warning of standing before God explains their fairness. Islāhī reports the Anṣār version but objects that Meccan traders were at least as plausible and the surah was Meccan. [27 notes: MUQATIL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الفراء); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(عكرمة، ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(exegetes); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 83:1/q05/p2 The warning addressed Abū Juhayna, who used separate measures for buying and selling. — Al-Suddī names him; Abū Hurayra calls him his uncle, and al-Qurṭubī also gives the name ʿAmr. Versions vary whether the larger measure is described as used for taking or buying and the smaller for giving or selling. Study Quran mentions separate scales. [10 notes: THALABI(السدي); WAHIDI-ASBAB(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); STUDYQURAN(Abū Hurayrah)]
- 83:1/q05/p3 The Prophet recited the warning to Medinan market traders engaged in deceptive, risky sales. — Al-Quraẓī or al-Qurṭubī names mulāmasa, munābadha, and mukhāṭara, likening them to chance transactions; al-Rāzī links the recitation to a report of five public wrongs. [4 notes: THALABI(القرطبي); WAHIDI-ASBAB(القرظي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL]
- 83:1/q05/p4 The specific Anṣār setting is doubtful; the verse can address Meccan commercial injustice. — Islāhī argues the surah is Meccan, Meccans traded while Anṣār farmed, and dishonest trade was at least as plausible in Mecca. He centers the verse's argument on innate justice and final accountability. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:1/q06 Where and when was this surah or its opening revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Conflicting Meccan, Medinan, and mixed reports, plus whether the early Medinan trade occasion applies to the entire surah or only its opening.
- 83:1/q06/p1 It was revealed in Medina, especially at the Prophet's arrival; some call it the first revelation there. — Al-Ḥasan and al-Ḍaḥḥāk place it in Medina; al-Farrāʾ and Ibn ʿAbbās place its warning at arrival. One Ibn ʿAbbās report calls it the first surah revealed there; Abū Ḥayyān records the same claim. The Medinan measurement reports support an early Medinan opening. [10 notes: FARRA; JISHUMI(الحسن والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود، الضحاك، مقاتل؛ الحسن، عكرمة، مقاتل؛ ابن عباس، قتادة,السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN]
- 83:1/q06/p2 It was revealed in Mecca, perhaps the last revelation there. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn al-Zubayr, Ibn Masʿūd, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Muqātil are cited for Meccan origin, though other reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil differ. Mujāhid calls this the last Meccan revelation, against other proposed last surahs, al-ʿAnkabūt and al-Muʾminūn. Al-Burhān says reliable transmitters establish the ordering and counts 85 Meccan surahs. Al-Suyūṭī, as cited by Asad, is among authorities calling it last Meccan; Islāhī treats the surah as Meccan. [11 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود، الضحاك، مقاتل؛ الحسن، عكرمة، مقاتل؛ ابن عباس، قتادة); BURHAN(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن الزبير,ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 83:1/q06/p3 Its parts span Mecca and Medina, or it was revealed between the cities. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda reportedly say it is Medinan except the final eight Meccan verses. One account places the first four after arrival and verses 5–28 in Medina, with the final eight among the last in Mecca. Asad weighs the main body as late Meccan and the opening as early Medinan, a threshold like surah 29; another account places revelation between the cities before arrival. [8 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود، الضحاك، مقاتل؛ الحسن، عكرمة، مقاتل؛ ابن عباس، قتادة); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Bg, IK, Ṭ); ASAD-NOTES(Asad)+]

## 83:1/q07 What consequences and moral warnings does the verse support?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How the warning is applied: direct afterlife threat, communal calamity, the gravity of slight fraud, duties of fair exchange, and illustrative reports.
- 83:1/q07/p1 Fraud in measure brings divine punishment, including Hell and standing before the Lord. — ʿIkrima repeatedly swears even his own son, if he measures or weighs, faces the Fire; Ibn ʿUmar warns cheaters stand in sweat, with versions reaching half the ears or the mouth. Al-Fuḍayl says the cheating balance blackens the face. Abū Ṣāliḥ Ḥamdūn tells a holder of scales to recall the scale that will weigh him. ʿAbd Allāh points to 83:6 for accountability. Ibn ʿUmar includes knowingly renting a false measure or employing a cheater; al-Rāzī says even intending fraud is a grave sin. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله,عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عمر); SULAMI(أبو صالح حمدون); THALABI(ابن عمر,عكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عمر,عكرمة); RAZI-FULL(الفضيل); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر,عكرمة,نافع); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر); STUDYQURAN(Ibn Masʿūd)]
- 83:1/q07/p2 Cheating measures brings failed crops, famine, or drought among five paired communal wrongs and calamities. — Prophetic reports pair broken covenants with enemy domination, unjust judgment with poverty, sexual immorality with death or plague, and withholding zakat with withheld rain. Al-Qurṭubī says al-Bazzār transmits the gist and Mālik gives an Ibn ʿUmar route; Study Quran states no grading. One shorter report pairs only covenants and crops. [10 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL(حديث); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 83:1/q07/p3 The warning covers even minute or intended wrongdoing and implies that greater theft is graver. — Al-Rāzī reports that proponents of the threat address Muslims: unbelief would already explain the threat and 83:4–5 reminds believers of resurrection. He says intention itself is major sin and favors a threat for any amount without conditioning it on the theft threshold, repentance, or outweighing good deeds. A Bedouin challenges ʿAbd al-Malik to consider seizure of much more Muslim property if slight fraud brings this threat. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحاب الوعيد,أعرابي)]
- 83:1/q07/p4 Fair measurement matters because transactions and reciprocal justice depend on it. — Al-Rāzī cites 55:7–9 and 57:25 on the balance and justice. Qatādah tells people to give fairly as they wish to receive; Islāhī sees the trader's protest against being cheated as evidence of innate recognition of justice. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ invokes Shuʿayb's ban on corruption in 11:85. Abū Ḥafṣ and Asad extend the reciprocal duty to all social rights. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(قتادة); SULAMI(أبو حفص); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 83:1/q07/p5 Justice should precede voluntary generosity when measuring. — ʿAlī stopped a saffron seller giving extra and ordered exact due weight first, then any freely chosen surplus; al-Kashshāf says this trains fairness and distinguishes obligation from extra giving. Mālik advises pouring freely until the buyer's measure is full and releasing one's hand without holding back. [5 notes: THALABI(علي); KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن القاسم عن مالك,عبد خير); BURSEVI(علي)]
- 83:1/q07/p6 A measurer should beware the trade's temptation and both measures and scales. — An Arab or Ubayy warns against seeking favors from those dependent on the rims of measures and tongues of scales; al-Aṣmaʿī also ascribes the saying to a Bedouin and perhaps ʿAlī. Ibn ʿAbbās warns Persians that earlier peoples perished through measure and scale, naming them because they use both whereas Mecca weighs and Medina measures. [5 notes: THALABI(أعرابي); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبي,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي); BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]
- 83:1/q07/p7 Dishonest gain can return as a terrifying afterlife image. — Mālik ibn Dīnār says a dying neighbor who used two measures saw them become two fiery mountains; striking them together made them appear larger. [2 notes: THALABI(مالك بن دينار); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن دينار)]
- 83:1/q07/p8 The warning can be heard in later public worship and market conduct. — Abū Hurayra reports Medina's deputy reciting the surah at dawn and then praying against a trader who bought full but sold short; another report says he heard the verse when the Prophet left Sabāʿ ibn ʿArṭafa in charge during Khaybar. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 83:1/q07/p9 The revelation to David warns against exposing another's private sin. — Tustarī reports God saying people sin privately and repent publicly, although God does not want their wrongdoing exposed. [1 notes: TUSTARI(الله)]

## 83:1/q08 How does this opening connect with the preceding surah and the theme of judgment?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is drawn from the preceding surah's judgment and records, from innate moral knowledge, or from God's attributes and nurture.
- 83:1/q08/p1 It develops al-Infiṭār's ending by detailing judgment, the righteous and the wicked. — Al-Jishumī, Biqāʿī and Islāhī say the prior surah ends with resurrection and divergent fates and this surah elaborates them. Al-Rāzī cites 82:19, where all command belongs to God; Abū Ḥayyān says the new opening singles out a contemptible small-gain sin. Biqāʿī says recording every deed in 82:10 and exact weighing in 21:47 make petty fraud a grave offense. The Study Quran says the surah moves from shortchanging to contrasting fates and ends with sinners mocking the Prophet and believers. [8 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 83:1/q08/p2 It argues from human moral awareness of justice to resurrection and God's fair judgment. — Islāhī contrasts al-Infiṭār's argument from bodily creation with this surah's appeal to moral nature: humans love justice, resent being cheated, and expect different treatment for good and evil. Injustice arises when other motives overpower that nature; 75:14–15 is cited for the Qur'anic argument. Verses 1–6 say cheating betrays failure to expect standing before the Lord, and deniers resist accountability. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:1/q08/p3 God's nurturing, wisdom, and mercy frame the surah's demand for fair reckoning. — Biqāʿī says the divine nurturer must question and distinguish those He raises; the name al-Taṭfīf signals the theme. He reads the basmala as perfect wisdom and power, universal mercy through creation and guidance, and special mercy for His party. Qushayrī stresses God's beginningless being, majesty, beauty, expected kindness, and rightful decree. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]

## 83:1/q09 What virtue is reported for reciting this surah?  [* · reports]
- 83:1/q09/p1 Its reciter will receive sealed nectar on the Day of Resurrection. — Al-Jishumī transmits this from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb reporting the Prophet. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي بن كعب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 83:1/q10 How many verses does the surah have?  [* · sciences]
- 83:1/q10/p1 It has thirty-six verses. — Al-Durr al-Manthūr gives this with its classification of the surah as Meccan. [1 notes: DURR-FULL]

## 83:1/q11 What variant reading is reported in the accompanying passage?  [* · readings]
- 83:1/q11/p1 Kūfan readers except ʿĀṣim and Yaḥyā read ran with kasra on rāʾ; the rest read it with fatḥa. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this reading of the later word ran. [1 notes: TABRISI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم إلا يحيى)]

## 83:1/q12 What does the near-full-measure comparison teach about people?  [* · interpretation]
- 83:1/q12/p1 Adam's descendants are close to one another; superiority belongs only to piety. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports a hadith likening Adam's descendants to a slightly underfilled measure and saying distinction is by piety. Al-Jishumī says no one surpasses another except through piety. [2 notes: TABRISI; JISHUMI]
