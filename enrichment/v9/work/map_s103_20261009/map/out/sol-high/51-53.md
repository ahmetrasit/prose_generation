# 51:53: 15 questions

## 51:53/q01 What does أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ mean?  [أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ · meaning]
- 51:53/q01/p1 They advised or enjoined one another, passed something along, or appeared to agree on it. — MAJAZ allows mutual agreement or taking a view from one another and says a dominant shared trait can make advice appear to have occurred. WAHIDI-BASIT explains it as mutual instruction to use an accusation; Abu ʿUbayda, reported by IBNJAWZI-ZAD, includes collusion and copying. TABATABAI defines mutual enjoining as a people's advising one another. [4 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); TABATABAI]

## 51:53/q02 What does بِهِۦ refer to?  [بِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun points to the repeated charge, rejection of the message, or belief in it.
- 51:53/q02/p1 The repeated accusation that the messengers, especially Muhammad, were sorcerers or madmen. — TAB-FULL identifies the charges against Muhammad as poet, sorcerer, and madman and the older peoples' charges against their messengers as sorcerer or madman. NASAFI-FULL, TABATABAI, and the rhetorical readings identify the pronoun with the accusation; STUDYQURAN specifies calling the Prophet a sorcerer or possessed. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 51:53/q02/p2 Denial of the messenger, truth, or revelation. — Qatāda says predecessors instructed successors to lie, deny the truth, or deny the messenger, as reported by ABDURRAZZAQ, TAB-FULL, and WAHIDI-BASIT. Muqātil, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT, says to speak this way about their messenger. STUDYQURAN reports that the urging entails denial of God and revelation. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Most commentators,R)]
- 51:53/q02/p3 Belief, which they do not exhort one another to embrace. — STUDYQURAN reports this alternative reading from Āl together with a negative understanding of the question. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Āl)]

## 51:53/q03 What force does the question أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِهِۦ have?  [أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِهِۦ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the interrogation chiefly voices rebuking wonder or directly negates the proposed exhortation.
- 51:53/q03/p1 It reproaches or expresses astonishment at the same charge appearing among separated generations, as though they had instructed one another. — ZAJJAJ and al-Zajjāj as reported by WAHIDI-BASIT call the opening hamza both interrogative and rebuking. BAGHAWI-FULL calls its alif reproachful; QURTUBI-FULL adds astonishment. IBNATIYYA-FULL, RAZI-FULL, ALUSI-FULL, and KURANYOLU-TEFSIR stress wonder at distant generations converging. STUDYQURAN reports wonder that people would urge denial of God and revelation; ISLAHI likens it to passing down a will. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirlerin çoğu); STUDYQURAN(Most commentators)]
- 51:53/q03/p2 It denies that such mutual exhortation took place. — ALUSI-FULL reports this as an alternative force of the question. STUDYQURAN reports Āl's negative reading, in which the pronoun instead means belief. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Āl)]

## 51:53/q04 Why did generations repeat the same denunciation?  [أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بَلْ هُمْ قَوْمٌۭ طَاغُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether similar accusations require transmission or arise independently from the same transgressive character; distance between generations is repeatedly cited against direct counsel.
- 51:53/q04/p1 Their shared transgression produced the same denial independently, without actual counsel across generations. — TAB-FULL, NAHHAS, WAHIDI-BASIT, IBNATIYYA-FULL, TABRISI, RAZI-FULL, QURTUBI-FULL, BAYDAWI-FULL, NASAFI-FULL, ABUHAYYAN-FULL, BIQAI-FULL, BURSEVI, ALUSI-FULL, ELMALILI, ISLAHI, and KURANYOLU-TEFSIR attribute recurrence to rebellion, not coordination; several note that the generations were too far apart or never met. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT and BAGHAWI-FULL, says prosperity bred rebellion and denial; RAZI-FULL adds self-sufficiency and forgetfulness of God and compares wealthy subjects who later resist a king's summons. IBNKATHIR-FULL says later hearts resembled earlier hearts. BIQAI-FULL reports Ibn al-Zubayr's emphasis on arrogance and darkened hearts, alongside 32:13 and God's ability to guide every soul. BURSEVI calls the disposition innate; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR adds prejudice and stubbornness. [28 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; NAHHAS+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 51:53/q04/p2 Earlier generations actually instructed later ones to deny the messenger or repeat the accusation. — Qatāda's reports in ABDURRAZZAQ, TAB-FULL, and WAHIDI-BASIT describe predecessors instructing successors to lie or deny. Muqātil, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT, says they instructed later people what to say about their messenger. ZAJJAJ says the first advised the last. WAHIDI-WAJIZ takes the rebuke as implying that some instructed others; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR reports Tabarī's formulation as intergenerational transmission. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); TAB-FULL-; NAHHAS-; WAHIDI-BASIT-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; TABRISI-; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; BAYDAWI-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; BIQAI-FULL-; BURSEVI-; ELMALILI-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 51:53/q04/p3 The charge persisted as inherited thinking or a cultural pattern. — ASAD-NOTES frames it as inherited thinking while also explaining rejection through arrogance. KURANYOLU-TEFSIR explicitly raises cultural inheritance but rejects it as the explanation. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 51:53/q05 Who are the rebellious people?  [هُمْ قَوْمٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the immediate pronoun is taken specifically for Quraysh or the recurrent class of opponents.
- 51:53/q05/p1 The Meccans or Quraysh who rejected Muhammad. — TAB-FULL names Quraysh as recipients of the rhetorical challenge. IBNJAWZI-ZAD explicitly identifies the addressed people as Meccans. ISLAHI compares Quraysh with earlier rejecters. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 51:53/q05/p2 Rejecters of messengers across earlier and later generations. — IBNATIYYA-FULL, TABRISI, RAZI-FULL, QURTUBI-FULL, BAYDAWI-FULL, and others compare people of separated eras. TAB-FULL names Noah's people, ʿĀd, Thamud, and Pharaoh's people alongside Quraysh. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 51:53/q06 What does طَاغُونَ describe?  [طَاغُونَ · meaning]
- 51:53/q06/p1 People who exceed bounds through arrogance, corruption, disobedience, and denial. — NAHHAS says they overstep God's commands and prohibitions; WAHIDI-BASIT says they exceed bounds in disobedience. IBNATIYYA-FULL describes earthly arrogance, corruption, and defiance; BIQAI-FULL adds excess in unbelief and wrongdoing and sees transgression as ingrained in the pronoun's subjects, whom God ruins through complete power and knowledge. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT and BAGHAWI-FULL, links rebellion to divine gifts and abundance. ASAD-NOTES calls it overweening arrogance. [9 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 51:53/q07 What does بَلْ do in the explanation?  [بَلْ · rhetoric]
- 51:53/q07/p1 It turns from the suggested mutual advice to the real common cause, transgression. — ALUSI-FULL calls it a redirection from shared counsel to participation in transgression. TAB-FULL, NASAFI-FULL, TABATABAI, and KURANYOLU-TEFSIR likewise contrast the imagined instruction with shared rebelliousness. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 51:53/q08 How does this verse continue the preceding account of accusations?  [* · links]
- 51:53/q08/p1 It explains why the same charge was made against Muhammad and earlier messengers. — TAB-FULL lists Quraysh's charges of poet, sorcerer, or madman and similar claims by Noah's people, ʿĀd, Thamud, and Pharaoh's people. RAZI-FULL explicitly connects the recurrent sorcerer or madman accusation with 51:52; IBNKATHIR-FULL says later hearts resembled earlier ones. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 51:53/q09 What lesson do earlier peoples' repeated rejections offer?  [* · interpretation]
- 51:53/q09/p1 Their accounts offer lessons to those who reflect and encourage the Prophet to persevere, turn from wrongdoers, and remind receptive people. — JISHUMI says earlier communities' narratives are recounted for reflection. ISLAHI-TADABBUR says each nation treated its messenger similarly and tells the Prophet to continue patiently, turn away from wrongdoers, remind those who respond, and trust God's support for him and his companions. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 51:53/q10 How is the shared defiance read inwardly?  [بَلْ هُمْ قَوْمٌۭ طَاغُونَ · inward]
- 51:53/q10/p1 Rebellious souls in every age reject both outward prophets and inward divine inspirations through a shared satanic disposition. — The Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya, reported by BURSEVI, names refusal, pride, and defiance, says they dismiss prophets and inspirations as magic or madness, and traces the disposition to abundance, comfort, arrogance, and wealth. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 51:53/q11 How do forms of predication convey a report?  [* · grammar]
- 51:53/q11/p1 A main predicate completes the clause, a circumstantial phrase reports secondarily, and nominal and verbal predicates present attributes differently. — JURJANI-DALAIL says nominal predicates or completing verbs provide indispensable reports; circumstantial phrases attach a secondary report to the main one; nominal predicates assert an attribute without renewal, whereas verbs present renewal or recurrence. These are general grammatical observations, with no specific application to this verse stated in the notes. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 51:53/q12 What accounts for Qur'anic eloquence and inimitability?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether eloquence resides in meaningful arrangement or the ease of individual sounds.
- 51:53/q12/p1 It rests in the arrangement of God's speech and its eloquence and balāgha. — JURJANI-DALAIL says nazm makes God's speech miraculous and reports rational thinkers' agreement that eloquence and balāgha take it beyond human capacity. He says judgments about burdensome sounds belong to sensation, not reasoning, and gives counterexamples to theories based on ease of individual letters. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; JURJANI-DALAIL+]
- 51:53/q12/p2 It depends on light and easy sounds or ease of articulation. — JURJANI-DALAIL rejects this: vulgar speech and bad verse can have light sounds, and changing every ḍamma and kasra to fatḥa could ease articulation even when meaning changes. He also says sensory perception, rather than reasoning, judges which sounds burden the tongue. [0 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL-]

## 51:53/q13 What is sound and what produces it?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether sound is the heard quality itself, a body, or the physical event that causes it; Rāzī repeatedly distinguishes audible sound from visible processes.
- 51:53/q13/p1 Sound is heard directly; air undulation, generated by impact or forceful separation, is its cause. — RAZI-FULL distinguishes sound's heard essence from its cause, rejecting visible processes as definitions because sound itself is invisible. He says undulation unfolds through successive impacts and pauses rather than one movement between fixed endpoints; impact is forceful contact and tearing is forceful separation. He refers readers to his rational works for the phoneme. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 51:53/q13/p2 Sound is a quality arising from compressed, undulating air between an impacting and an impacted body. — Avicenna, as reported by RAZI-FULL, gives this account; Rāzī replies that it describes sound's cause if accepted, rather than its essence. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو علي بن سينا); RAZI-FULL-]
- 51:53/q13/p3 Sound itself is a body. — RAZI-FULL reports this attribution to al-Naẓẓām but doubts he held it: bodies share corporeality but not sound, are visible and tangible while sound is not, and persist while sound does not. Rāzī suggests al-Naẓẓām may instead have meant that air undulation causes sound. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(النظام); RAZI-FULL-]
- 51:53/q13/p4 Sound is the collision of solid bodies. — Some, reported by RAZI-FULL, define it this way. Rāzī objects that collision is visible contact whereas sound is not visible. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 51:53/q13/p5 Sound is impact, tearing apart, or motion's undulation. — RAZI-FULL reports and rejects these definitions because the proposed states are visible but sound is not. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-]

## 51:53/q14 What is a letter and how do its kinds behave?  [* · meaning]
- 51:53/q14/p1 A letter is a perceptible distinguishing quality of sound; vowels are long and soft, while consonants vary in their capacity to continue. — Avicenna, reported by RAZI-FULL, defines the letter as distinguishing equally light or heavy sounds. RAZI-FULL divides letters into vowels and consonants. Bāʾ, tāʾ, dāl, and ṭāʾ exist at the instant between held and released breath and are called sound's edges; ḥāʾ and khāʾ may be successive instants too subtle to perceive; sīn and shīn probably continue through time with the sound. No specific application to this verse is supplied. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو علي بن سينا)]

## 51:53/q15 How can an omitted clause affect the mood after إذن?  [* · grammar]
- 51:53/q15/p1 An omitted condition can leave the following verb nominative, with tanwīn standing for the missing clause. — Ibn al-Juwaynī, reported by BURHAN, reads إذن أكرمك as 'if you come to me, I will honor you.' The note gives no application to the verse. [1 notes: BURHAN(القاضي ابن الجويني)]
