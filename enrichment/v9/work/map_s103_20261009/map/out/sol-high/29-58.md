# 29:58: 24 questions

## 29:58/q01 Who are the believers promised this reward?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description names all faithful workers or specifically the believers addressed by the migration passage.
- 29:58/q01/p1 Believers who do righteous deeds generally. — Yaḥyā al-Sallām, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī state the promise for believers who act well. Al-Ṭabarī specifies belief in God and His Messenger, obedience to commands, and avoidance of prohibitions; al-Qurṭubī connects them to patience and reliance in the next verse. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 29:58/q01/p2 The faithful migrants, especially vulnerable believers leaving Mecca. — Muqātil places the promise amid migration to Medina despite lack of livelihood. Al-Ṭabarī says Muḥammad urges believers to leave polytheist Mecca for Muslim Medina and persevere in worship (29:57). Al-Ṭabrisī explicitly identifies the promised people as migrants; Study Quran reads the lodging as compensation for leaving things for God (29:56; 16:41). [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; STUDYQURAN]

## 29:58/q02 What righteous work is in view?  [وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which acts the commentators single out from the general phrase righteous deeds.
- 29:58/q02/p1 Obedience to God, including worship and avoidance of forbidden acts. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies sincere obedience, worship, and avoiding prohibitions; he describes perseverance in obedience and jihad against God's enemies. Muqātil relates the good deeds to patient worship after migration. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL]
- 29:58/q02/p2 Migration for religion, sustained by patience and trust in God's provision. — Bursevī expressly counts migration among righteous deeds. Muqātil says migrants endure lack of money or livelihood in Medina; al-Ṭabarī says they endure polytheist harm, trust God for provision and victory, and know their apportioned provision cannot miss them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees the promise urging endurance and trust. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 29:58/q03 How is the opening clause related syntactically to the promise?  [وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَنُبَوِّئَنَّهُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether the initial relative phrase is treated by the ordinary opening analysis or by ishtighāl; al-Ālūsī also disputes Thaʿlab's restriction on an oath serving as predicate.
- 29:58/q03/p1 The opening permits an initial-subject analysis, with the emphatic oath and answer as its predicate. — Al-Samīn gives the common opening analysis. Al-Ālūsī holds that the oath together with its answer can predicate alladhīna, explicitly opposing Thaʿlab's ban on such a predicate; he notes that the verb denotes permanent settling. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:58/q03/p2 The opening can also be analysed as ishtighāl. — Al-Samīn reports this second grammatical analysis alongside the common opening analysis. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 29:58/q04 How is the lodging verb read and what does each form convey?  [لَنُبَوِّئَنَّهُم · readings]
turns on: The transmitted consonants, person prefix, and hamza or gemination; the roots b-w-ʾ and th-w-y differ in emphasis and in how they govern a location.
- 29:58/q04/p1 The common bāʾ and hamza reading, lanubawwiʾannahum, means God will establish them in a dwelling. — Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, and Bursevī gloss the verb as settling someone in a place; Bursevī cites al-Tāj. Al-Ṭabarī accepts it alongside the thāʾ form. Al-Khalawayh cites 2:8; Abū Zayd and Abū ʿAlī cite 10:93, 22:26, 14:37, and 39:74, discussing the lām or an understood object in Abraham's prayer. Al-Wāḥidī cites 39:74; Abū Ḥayyān cites 3:121 and 22:26 for two objects or a lām complement. Al-Baghawī specifies fatḥa, doubled wāw, and hamza; Ibn al-Jazarī notes Abū Jaʿfar's separately treated hamza substitution. Al-Ṭabarī says both famous readings are acceptable. [28 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد,حمزة والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل الكوفة غير عاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(رويس عن يعقوب والجحدري والسلمي); NASAFI-FULL(كوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان); NASHR(الباقون); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 29:58/q04/p2 The thāʾ reading, lanuthwiyannahum, means God will make them reside or lodge for a long stay. — Attributed by Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, and others to Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī; Ibn al-Jazarī and others add Khalaf; other reports add ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, al-Aʿmash, al-Rabīʿ ibn Khuthaym, Ibn Waththāb, Ṭalḥa, and Zayd ibn ʿAlī. The Kufans except ʿĀṣim are specified by al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī. Ibn Qutayba cites thawaytu bi-l-makān; al-Wāḥidī explains thaway as guest, quotes al-Zajjāj on athwaytuhu and Ḥassān's ten years among Quraysh; al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī cite 28:45 and poetry. Qirāʾāt-ER says it emphasizes long residence. Al-Akhfash and Abū al-Ḥasan object that athwaytuhu al-dār is unidiomatic; Abū ʿAlī answers by supplying an omitted preposition. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both famous readings. [27 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,حسان,حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل الكوفة غير عاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود والأعمش ويحيى بن وثاب وحمزة والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(كوفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وعبد الله والربيع بن خثيم وابن وثاب وطلحة وزيد بن علي وحمزة والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان); NASHR(حمزة، والكسائي، وخلف); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(علي); QIRAAT-ER; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش)-; TABRISI(أبو الحسن)-]
- 29:58/q04/p3 A variant uses yāʾ for the verb's person prefix, liyubawwiʾannahum, rather than the common nūn. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes the yāʾ reading to Ruways from Yaʿqūb, al-Jaḥdarī, and al-Sulamī; it retains the sense of settling them in rooms. Al-Ṭaʿlabī reports a tāʾ versus yāʾ difference in the verb and names Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, and Khalaf on one side. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رويس عن يعقوب والجحدري والسلمي); THALABI]
- 29:58/q04/p4 Another form removes the hamza while retaining the b-w-ʾ root and the usual meaning. — Qirāʾāt-ER explicitly says this form is semantically identical to the standard reading; Ibn al-Jazarī mentions Abū Jaʿfar's hamza substitution. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(الباقون)]
- 29:58/q04/p5 Another thāʾ form uses a doubled consonant to make the verb causative. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the doubled-thāʾ variant without hamza; al-Samīn explains that gemination makes the verb transitive just as causative hamza does. Abū Ḥayyān notes both mechanisms for making th-w-y transitive. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وعبد الله والربيع بن خثيم وابن وثاب وطلحة وزيد بن علي وحمزة والكسائي)]

## 29:58/q05 How does the lodging verb govern غرفا?  [لَنُبَوِّئَنَّهُم مِّنَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ غُرَفًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether غرفا is the b-w-ʾ verb's second object, or how the th-w-y form licenses an accusative place despite objections to athwaytuhu al-dār.
- 29:58/q05/p1 غرفا is the second object of the b-w-ʾ verb, with the believers as its first object. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn state this; al-Samīn cites 3:121 and the lām construction in 22:26. Al-Ālūsī gives this analysis of the chambers. Al-Fārisī and al-Ṭabrisī discuss b-w-ʾ constructions in 10:93 and 22:26, including an extra lām or an understood object. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); TABRISI(أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 29:58/q05/p2 On the th-w-y reading, غرفا is accusative after an omitted preposition meaning 'in chambers'. — Abū ʿAlī defends this against al-Akhfash and Abū al-Ḥasan's objection that athwaytuhu al-dār is not idiomatic. Al-Fārisī says Arabic omits a preposition even before a specified place (39:74); al-Wāḥidī cites the fox on the road, going to Syria, and reports with or without ilā before the lavatory. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī also give deletion. [11 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)+]
- 29:58/q05/p3 On the th-w-y reading, غرفا can be a direct object by treating the verb like 'lodge' or 'bring down'. — Al-Zajjāj says athwaytuhu is a transitive causative taking a second object. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī compare the construction to anzala; al-Samīn cites 7:16. Abū Ḥayyān gives direct-object analysis as an option. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش)-; TABRISI(أبو الحسن)-]
- 29:58/q05/p4 غرفا may be a locative accusative, treating a specified place as an unmarked location. — Al-Fārisī says a preposition can be omitted before a specific location; al-Wāḥidī records Abū ʿAlī's road, Syria, and lavatory examples. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī give the locative construction as an alternative. [7 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(علي)]

## 29:58/q06 Is there a different vowel reading of غرفا?  [غُرَفًۭا · readings]
turns on: The vowel on the rāʾ of the plural غرفا.
- 29:58/q06/p1 The usual form has the ordinary vowel on the rāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts Ibn ʿĀmir's ḍamma-vowel form with the other reading; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī likewise identify his variant. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر)]
- 29:58/q06/p2 Ibn ʿĀmir reads the rāʾ with ḍamma, ghurufan. — Reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر)]

## 29:58/q07 What are the promised غرف?  [غُرَفًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the notes describe the chambers by elevation, precious materials, or the transmitted transparency and visible ranks; these descriptions are not presented as mutually exclusive.
- 29:58/q07/p1 Lofty upper chambers or palaces in Paradise. — Al-Māwardī says chambers stand above ordinary houses and offer cleaner air and a more pleasant, dry dwelling; al-Rāzī takes their height to signify believers' exalted rank (39:20). Al-Biqāʿī and Bursevī describe views over rivers, orchards, fields, flowers, and greenery; Bursevī contrasts Heaven above with Hell below. Study Quran compares 39:20's abodes above abodes. Ibn Kathīr says the inhabitants direct the rivers as they wish. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 29:58/q07/p2 Palaces or chambers made of pearl, emerald or beryl, and ruby. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī transmit this from Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Ālūsī transmits pearl, beryl, and ruby from him. Bursevī gives pearl, emerald, and ruby. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 29:58/q07/p3 Transparent rooms whose inside and outside can be seen through each other. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits the Prophet's description; al-Māwardī cites Abū Mālik al-Ashʿarī, al-Qurṭubī cites al-Tirmidhī from ʿAlī, and Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran repeat the report. Al-Qurṭubī also cites Muslim from Sahl ibn Saʿd: inhabitants of chambers appear to those below like a bright star on the horizon because of differing ranks, and are believers affirming messengers, not prophets alone. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو مالك الأشعري); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,سهل بن سعد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو مالك الأشعري); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]

## 29:58/q08 For whom are the transparent chambers prepared?  [غُرَفًۭا · reports]
turns on: Which prophetic description of the inhabitants is being cited.
- 29:58/q08/p1 For people who feed others, speak or greet kindly, keep prayer and fasting, and pray at night while others sleep. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim's prophetic report mentions greeting people and maintaining prayer and fasting; al-Māwardī's Abū Mālik report says speaking kindly; al-Qurṭubī's report from ʿAlī says regular fasting. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran repeat feeding, good speech, fasting or prayer, and night prayer. The wording of these reports varies in these details. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو مالك الأشعري); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو مالك الأشعري); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 29:58/q08/p2 The elevated rooms are occupied by believers who affirm the messengers, beyond the prophets alone. — Al-Qurṭubī cites Muslim's report from Sahl ibn Saʿd: the occupants are seen from below like a bright star at the horizon because of their differing ranks. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سهل بن سعد)]

## 29:58/q09 Where do the rivers flow beneath the rooms?  [تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · interpretation]
turns on: What the pronoun and the description 'beneath' locate: the chambers or garden trees.
- 29:58/q09/p1 Beneath the chambers themselves. — Ibn ʿAbbās locates them beneath the chambers and relates their water to 47:15. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, and Bursevī describe rivers beneath or overlooked by the upper rooms; al-Rāzī contrasts pleasurable water beneath believers with fire beneath unbelievers. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 29:58/q09/p2 Beneath the trees of the gardens. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's explanation of the rivers' course. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]

## 29:58/q10 What rivers are meant?  [ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · interpretation]
- 29:58/q10/p1 Rivers of water, wine, honey, and milk, usable as their residents wish. — Ibn Kathīr lists the four liquids and says believers can direct the rivers as they wish. Al-Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās report links the water of Paradise here to 47:15. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 29:58/q11 How does the river clause attach grammatically?  [غُرَفًۭا تَجْرِى مِن تَحْتِهَا ٱلْأَنْهَٰرُ · grammar]
- 29:58/q11/p1 تجري من تحتها الأنهار qualifies the chambers as their adjective or descriptive clause. — Al-Samīn calls تجري an adjective of غرفا; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī likewise say the river clause describes the chambers. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:58/q12 What does abiding there forever entail?  [خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا · interpretation]
- 29:58/q12/p1 They stay permanently without death or departure, and without the afflictions of age or illness. — Yaḥyā al-Sallām denies death or exit; al-Wāḥidī adds aging and illness; al-Ṭabrisī relates their continuance to God's enduring existence. Al-Biqāʿī says no discomfort prompts leaving. Ibn Kathīr and Bursevī likewise stress perpetual residence. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 29:58/q13 What does فيها refer to, and how is خالدين parsed?  [خَٰلِدِينَ فِيهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the feminine pronoun resumes the chambers or Paradise.
- 29:58/q13/p1 فيها refers to the chambers; خالدين is an accusative circumstance of the verb's pronoun. — Al-Ālūsī allows the chambers as antecedent. Al-Ṭabrisī explicitly attaches the accusative circumstantial خالدين to the pronoun in the lodging verb. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 29:58/q13/p2 فيها refers to Paradise; خالدين still describes the people lodged there. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly gives Paradise as the alternative antecedent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:58/q14 Is a connective fāʾ read before نعم?  [نِعْمَ أَجْرُ ٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the praise begins directly or with a connective indicating succession.
- 29:58/q14/p1 The usual reading has نعم without a preceding fāʾ. — Abū Ḥayyān says the majority omit the fāʾ; al-Bayḍāwī mentions the alternative. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وثاب); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 29:58/q14/p2 Ibn Waththāb reads فنعم, adding fāʾ to indicate sequence. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī attribute the addition to Ibn Waththāb; al-Ālūsī explains that it marks succession. Al-Bayḍāwī also reports a reading with fāʾ. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وثاب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن وثاب); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 29:58/q15 What is the excellent reward?  [نِعْمَ أَجْرُ ٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the praise is specified as Paradise and its rooms or expressed as the recompense for obedient work.
- 29:58/q15/p1 Paradise, including its promised chambers. — Yaḥyā al-Sallām and Muqātil explicitly identify Paradise; Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Muqātil as calling it the reward for obedience. Al-Wāḥidī reports Muqātil's understood complement as either the chambers or Paradise. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Biqāʿī apply the praise to the promised rooms. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 29:58/q15/p2 The recompense earned by those who obey and do good. — Ibn Isḥāq calls it the recompense of the obedient; Bursevī says it belongs to people who perform righteous deeds. Al-Ālūsī says the praise commends their good works. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:58/q16 What is left understood in the praise نعم أجر العاملين?  [نِعْمَ أَجْرُ ٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the omitted element is the specifically praised thing or a distinguishing noun.
- 29:58/q16/p1 The specific object praised is omitted, understood as the chambers or Paradise. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Muqātil supplying the chambers or Paradise; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say the praised referent is elided. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:58/q16/p2 The distinguishing noun is omitted: 'excellent is a wage, the wage of workers'. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as an alternative ellipsis analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:58/q17 Who are the workers whose reward is praised?  [ٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether العاملين is explained by righteous activity generally or identified with the emigrants and Helpers.
- 29:58/q17/p1 Those who obey God and perform righteous work for Him. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Muqātil's obedience gloss and Ibn Isḥāq's recompense of the obedient. Al-Wāḥidī reports Muqātil's 'work for God'; Bursevī calls them doers of righteous acts. Al-Ṭabrisī says they work for Him; al-Biqāʿī emphasizes patient endurance of difficult obligations such as migration until patience becomes settled character. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 29:58/q17/p2 The emigrants and the Helpers. — Al-Wāḥidī explicitly identifies the rewarded workers with these two groups. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 29:58/q18 How does this promise compare with the lodging in 16:41?  [لَنُبَوِّئَنَّهُم مِّنَ ٱلْجَنَّةِ غُرَفًۭا · links]
- 29:58/q18/p1 The lodging of 16:41 is a worldly reward, whereas these Paradise chambers are the afterlife reward. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits al-Rabīʿ ibn Khuthaym's distinction. Study Quran also reads the afterlife lodging as assurance to emigrants who may lack a comparable abode in this world, citing 29:56 and 16:41. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الربيع بن خيثم); STUDYQURAN]

## 29:58/q19 How does the promise follow the preceding return to God and surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding passage the commentator uses to frame the promise: return and judgment or the preceding description of unbelievers' punishment.
- 29:58/q19/p1 Death and return for judgment lead to the disclosure of the believers' lasting reward. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī place the promise after the reminder that every soul dies and returns to God (29:57); Study Quran says each then learns its place in the Hereafter. Al-Ṭabrisī notes that the preceding return verb is read in third person by Yaḥyā from Abū Bakr and Hishām, but in second person by others, a shift like the address after al-ḥamdu lillāh and iyyāka naʿbudu. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI(يحيى عن أبي بكر وهشام); STUDYQURAN]
- 29:58/q19/p2 The believers' upper rooms and pleasurable rivers contrast with the unbelievers' encompassing fire. — Al-Rāzī compares 29:54–55: the unbelievers are under layers of fire while believers occupy an elevated rank with nothing above them (39:20); fire below hurts when it touches the feet, while water beneath gives pleasure regardless of distance. Study Quran also points to upper abodes in 39:20. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 29:58/q20 Why does the passage stress this reward to believers?  [* · rhetoric]
- 29:58/q20/p1 It encourages faith, righteous action, migration, patience, and reliance on God amid hardship. — Muqātil describes migrants without means in Medina; al-Ṭabarī describes harm, worship, jihad, trust in provision and victory. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī call the promise encouragement; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it urges endurance and trust. Study Quran sees assurance for those leaving things for God. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:58/q21 What occasion is reported for the first verse of this passage?  [* · reports]
- 29:58/q21/p1 It was revealed about vulnerable believers in Mecca who were ordered to migrate. — Al-Ṭabrisī transmits this from Muqātil and al-Kalbī; it concerns the first verse of the surrounding passage. [1 notes: TABRISI(مقاتل والكلبي)]

## 29:58/q22 Why is no warning about deficient faith included here?  [* · rhetoric]
- 29:58/q22/p1 The immediate aim is to encourage belief in any measure by promising elevated Paradise rooms to believers who do good. — Al-Biqāʿī observes that the passage could have described punishment for defective faith but omits it in service of encouragement. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:58/q23 What contrasts with unbelievers' punishment in the wording of this reward?  [* · rhetoric]
- 29:58/q23/p1 The excellent wage consoles believers without suggesting an end to reward, and their reward increases while unbelievers' punishment remains without increase. — Al-Rāzī contrasts the command 'taste' punishment, like the end of a hired worker's relation, with 'excellent is the wage'; he cites 10:26, 'the best and more,' for increase in the believers' reward and says the unbelievers' endless punishment is established elsewhere. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 29:58/q24 What pause is noted at العاملين?  [ٱلْعَٰمِلِينَ · grammar]
- 29:58/q24/p1 A pause is noted there, depending on a following syntactic analysis not preserved in the excerpt. — Al-Nasafī mentions the pause, but the supplied excerpt ends before its analysis is complete. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
