# 51:11: 10 questions

## 51:11/q01 Who are the people described by 'those who' here?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the preceding conjecturers are identified generally or with al-Walid's seventeen emissaries.
- 51:11/q01/p1 The conjecturers and deniers condemned in the preceding verse. — Qashani calls them cursed lying conjecturers; Bursevi says a conjecturer lies even if a guess proves true because the assertion lacks knowledge, sound probability, or report. Tabrisi calls them deniers. Tabatabai says the conjecturers ignore the truth told to them; Islahi says wild conjecture makes them doubt a certain warning and abandon divine light for desire; Study Quran reports that they wander in stupefying ignorance. [7 notes: STUDYQURAN(JJ); BURSEVI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; QASHANI+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+]
- 51:11/q01/p2 Seventeen men sent by al-Walid ibn al-Mughira to discredit Muhammad at festival roads. — Muqatil says al-Walid sent them in groups of four to call Muhammad a sorcerer, madman, poet, soothsayer, and liar; distracted, they mocked the timing of Judgment. [1 notes: MUQATIL+]

## 51:11/q02 How is the relative clause parsed?  [ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ فِى غَمْرَةٍۢ سَاهُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'in an immersion' is itself predicative or qualifies the heedlessness; Nahhas also discusses how the relative pronoun attaches to the preceding noun.
- 51:11/q02/p1 'Those who' is nominative, describing the conjecturers or beginning a clause, with 'heedless' predicated within its relative clause. — Nahhas gives the nominative adjectival and clause opening analyses; outside the Quran, he says an accusative sāhīn could instead be a circumstantial expression. [1 notes: NAHHAS+]
- 51:11/q02/p2 'Heedless' is a second predicate of 'they' alongside 'in an immersion'. — Razi says the two predicates describe both immersion and heedlessness. Islahi says the additional predicate stresses continuing negligence and refusal to wake. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 51:11/q02/p3 'Heedless' is the predicate, while 'in an immersion' specifies where their heedlessness occurs. — Razi says the construction permits a definite subject to take a clause as predicate. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 51:11/q03 What is the immersion (ghamrah) that envelops them?  [غَمْرَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Which enveloping state is stressed: epistemic error, heedlessness itself, sinful conduct, or desire and conjecture.
- 51:11/q03/p1 An overwhelming covering of ignorance, falsehood, misguidance, blindness, doubt, or confusion. — Nahhas explains the covering of falsehood and ignorance by a person or water covering one who enters. Wahidi says a river overwhelms a person and notes the expression for death throes. Ibn Atiyya likens it to deep water; Alusi and Tabatabai, citing al-Raghib, likewise explain the water metaphor. Tabari and reported Ibn Abbas emphasize persistent error; Qatada calls it delusion and doubt, or blind wandering in doubt; Tabrisi reports Ibn Abbas's persistence and Qatada's blind wandering and another ordering of ignorance from inattention through heedlessness to confusion, the most extreme ignorance of truth and fate. Tha'labi stresses doubt; Ibn Jawzi and BaghaWi stress blindness and ignorance of the afterlife. Biqa'i adds error, intoxication, instability like walking in the open sea, and disordered speech, conduct, and states. Elmalili adds torrent, swamp, stupor, drunkenness and ignorance. Ibn Kathir reports disbelief and doubt. Qurtubi stresses what covers and overwhelms. Tabarsi, Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Tabatabai, and Study Quran apply the covering to ignorance or error; Bursevi says it conceals the afterlife. [32 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وغير واحد); TABATABAI(الراغب); STUDYQURAN(JJ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; ITQAN+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NAHHAS+; NASAFI-FULL+; QASHANI+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 51:11/q03/p2 A state of heedlessness and distraction itself. — Ibn Abbas is reported to call it distraction in heedlessness; Wahidi's concise explanation says they are immersed in heedlessness. BaghaWi includes heedlessness in his gloss of ghamrah, and Tha'labi joins doubt to distraction. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 51:11/q03/p3 Immersion in sinful deeds and neglect of obligations. — Mawardi offers this as a possibility. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 51:11/q03/p4 The darkness of base desire, worldly greed, and self-made conjecture. — Islahi says these desires and conjectures engulf people who have abandoned divine light. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 51:11/q04 How is the heedless heart pictured?  [فِى غَمْرَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The two reports preserve different images attributed to Mujahid.
- 51:11/q04/p1 It is like a quiver. — Mujahid compares the heart of the heedless person to a quiver. [1 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد)]
- 51:11/q04/p2 It is enclosed in a covering. — Tabari reports this gloss from Mujahid. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 51:11/q05 What does 'heedless' mean, and of what are they heedless?  [سَاهُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The scope and object of sahw: general inattention, divine duties, the hereafter, conveyed truth, prayer specifically, or perfection.
- 51:11/q05/p1 Inattentive, distracted, or forgetful, with the heart losing track of what matters. — Wahidi reports al-Layth's Arabic definition of sahw as inattention and the heart losing track. Ibn Abbas is reported as saying heedlessly distracted; Ibn Jawzi and Biqa'i describe the heart turning away, forgetfulness and bewilderment, the latter adding conflicting dispositions under distress. Al-Raghib, as reported by Bursevi, distinguishes excused inadvertence not produced by one's acts from blameworthy error generated by one's acts, which is condemned here. Tabatabai reports sahw as general heedlessness. Razi describes forgetting oneself and failure to turn back; Islahi calls it continuing negligence. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); BURSEVI(الراغب); TABATABAI(الراغب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وغير واحد); BIQAI-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; ITQAN+; RAZI-FULL+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 51:11/q05/p2 Heedless of God's revealed commands, prohibitions, and obligations. — Ibn Zayd names what came, was revealed and was commanded, comparing submerged hearts in 23:63; Nahhas also reports him specifying commands and prohibitions. Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, and Alusi name what they were commanded; Alusi extends it to every negligence. Tabrisi mentions what they owe God. Study Quran includes God's commands alongside their coming fate. Mawardi's possible reading names neglected obligations. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 51:11/q05/p3 Heedless of the afterlife, requital, and their approaching fate. — Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas as calling them blind and ignorant of the afterlife. BaghaWi, Ibn Jawzi and Qurtubi make the hereafter the object of their inattention. Qashani names requital, Elmalili their coming disaster and God, and Study Quran their coming fate. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); BAGHAWI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; QASHANI+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 51:11/q05/p4 Heedless of the truth brought to them, the warning, and occasions for reflection. — Tabari names the truth brought by Muhammad; Ibn Atiyya says they miss both their ignorance and avenues of reflection. Tabatabai names the truth of what they were told. Islahi says conjecture leads them to doubt a certain warning. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 51:11/q05/p5 Specifically neglectful of their prayer. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn Abbas saying those lost in heedlessness persist in neglecting prayer. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 51:11/q05/p6 Heedless of spiritual perfection. — Qashani couples perfection with requital as what the conjecturers ignore. [1 notes: QASHANI+]

## 51:11/q06 Why add 'heedless' after saying they are immersed?  [فِى غَمْرَةٍۢ سَاهُونَ · rhetoric]
- 51:11/q06/p1 It emphasizes that their immersion continues without awareness or a return from it. — Razi says they fell into ignorance and falsehood, forgot themselves, and never turned back. Islahi says the further predicate emphasizes continuing negligence and refusal to awaken. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; RAZI-FULL+]

## 51:11/q07 What does this description imply about knowledge?  [* · theology]
- 51:11/q07/p1 Knowledge is acquired. — Jishumi infers this from describing people as in a flood of heedlessness. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 51:11/q08 How does this verse relate to submerged hearts and humility in al-Mu'minun?  [* · links]
- 51:11/q08/p1 Both passages picture submersion, but 23:63 names the hearts, the seat of humility, while this verse describes the people. — Ibn Zayd compares the heedlessness to hearts submerged in 23:63. Samarrai notes the different wording and says an engulfed heart cannot be humble. He connects 23:60's fearful givers to awe, 23:76's refusal to submit or supplicate to lack of humility, and 23:46's arrogance to the opposite of lowering oneself before God. He says al-Mu'minun opens with humility fitting its atmosphere and closes with prayer (23:1-2, 23:9), the qualities reported to disappear first and last. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]

## 51:11/q09 What reports about humility and prayer accompany the comparison?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports make different predictions about the loss of humility and prayer.
- 51:11/q09/p1 A time will come when a mosque has no humble person in it. — Samarrai reports this saying from Ubada ibn al-Samit. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(عبادة بن الصامت)]
- 51:11/q09/p2 Humility will be lost first and prayer last as the bonds of Islam unravel. — Samarrai reports this saying from Hudhayfa and uses it to explain the opening and ending of al-Mu'minun. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(حذيفة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]

## 51:11/q10 What rhetorical force does the verse's description carry?  [* · rhetoric]
- 51:11/q10/p1 It scolds and rebukes those immersed in heedlessness. — Islahi says the opening description itself forms a sentence of scolding and rebuke. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
