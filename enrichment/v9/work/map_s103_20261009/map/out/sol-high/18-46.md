# 18:46: 28 questions

## 18:46/q01 What do wealth and sons signify in worldly life?  [ٱلْمَالُ ٱلْبَنُونَ زِينَةُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the focus is their attractiveness, their practical support, or their testing and danger.
- 18:46/q01/p1 They are attractive but transient worldly adornments, unable by themselves to provide lasting benefit or indicate divine favor. — Muqatil contrasts their fleeting beauty with afterlife reward. Al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqa'i, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabataba'i, Islahi, Kuran Yolu, and Study Quran stress that they perish and cannot furnish the grave. Study Quran cites 18:34, 17:6, 18:7–8; Ibn Kathir cites 3:14 and 64:15. Al-Tabari illustrates rich men boasting over Salman, Khabbab, and Suhayb. Ibn Ashur cites Tarafa's poem as evidence of Arab pride in property and noble sons. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(طرفة); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:46/q01/p2 Wealth supplies beauty, benefit and livelihood; sons supply strength, defense, family continuity and public status. — Al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi distinguish wealth's beauty and benefit from sons' power and protection. Islahi explains Arabian tribal backing and displays of numbers. Study Quran calls them status and security. Al-Biqa'i adds that either may benefit if used to please God, or harm and disappoint. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:46/q01/p3 Wealth and children are a test and may even become enemies or a means of distance from God. — Al-Qurtubi cites 64:15 and 64:14. Ibn Kathir cites 64:15. Bursevi invokes verses on apparent increase and a divine report, with 23:55–56, saying abundance may distance a believer whereas restriction brings nearness. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 18:46/q02 Why is wealth named before sons?  [ٱلْمَالُ ٱلْبَنُونَ · rhetoric]
- 18:46/q02/p1 Wealth is the more fundamental, immediately needed ornament and support. — Al-Alusi says it benefits parents and children at all times, while sons aid only parents; wealth sustains an individual and can adorn without sons, whereas sons without wealth may bring distress. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:46/q03 What does zīna mean here and in related Qur'anic uses?  [زِينَةُ · meaning]
- 18:46/q03/p1 It is what beautifies something and makes it desirable; here it includes worldly display or luxury. — Ibn al-Jawzi defines adornment by its attraction to the self and distinguishes its Qur'anic senses: general beauty (2:212, 3:14, 67:5), jewelry (20:87), luxury or wealth (10:88 and this verse), and a public retinue such as Qarun's finery (28:79). [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 18:46/q04 How does the singular zīna predicate wealth and sons?  [ٱلْمَالُ ٱلْبَنُونَ زِينَةُ · grammar]
- 18:46/q04/p1 Zīna is a verbal noun used for the two concrete subjects, by emphasis or with an omitted description. — Ibn Atiyya notes that a description must be supplied, though the excerpt ends before giving it. Al-Samin says they are themselves adornment emphatically, or things that adorn. Abu Hayyan permits an omitted possessor or taking the pair as abundance. Al-Alusi says the verbal noun is transferred to the adorned thing. Al-Qurtubi permits singular and dual predicates. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 18:46/q05 Is zīna read in another number?  [زِينَةُ · readings]
- 18:46/q05/p1 A rare reading has dual zīnatā al-ḥayāh. — Al-Samin says its alif drops in pronunciation before the next consonant cluster, making the form resemble an accusative singular; al-Qurtubi says the dual is grammatically possible. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 18:46/q06 Why are wealth and sons called adornment of worldly life?  [زِينَةُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · rhetoric]
- 18:46/q06/p1 The genitive confines their adornment to this quickly decaying life. — Al-Alusi allows either ornaments belonging specifically to worldly life or ornaments found in it; if that life rapidly decays, its ornaments decay even sooner. Ibn Atiyya says the preceding parable devalues the life they adorn. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 18:46/q07 Whose boasting does the verse address?  [ٱلْمَالُ وَٱلْبَنُونَ · referent]
- 18:46/q07/p1 It rebukes rich unbelieving leaders who boasted over poor believers. — Al-Tabari names Uyayna and al-Aqra against Salman, Khabbab, and Suhayb; al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, and al-Qurtubi name Uyayna and his peers. Al-Baghawi calls the leader Utba. Abu Hayyan, al-Razi and Ibn Ashur identify wealthy polytheists; Kuran Yolu calls them wealthy Meccans rejecting monotheism. The poor believers' worship is the lasting contrast. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 18:46/q08 What occasion is reported for this passage?  [* · reports]
- 18:46/q08/p1 The passage beginning with the command to recite revelation is linked to Uyayna and al-Aqra's treatment of poor worshippers. — Al-Tabari says the section from 18:27 through this verse was revealed about them; al-Suddi relays Khabbab's account beginning with the order not to drive away those calling on God morning and evening (6:52). [2 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي)]

## 18:46/q09 What are the enduring righteous deeds?  [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · referent]

- 18:46/q09/p1 Every act or word of obedience whose reward God preserves, including the named forms of worship and remembrance. — Al-Tabari prefers the unrestricted wording because Scripture and Prophetic reports do not exclude other works; the report says four phrases are among the deeds. Al-Zajjaj, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi and the reported choice of Ibn Jarir agree. Ibn Abbas lists prayer, fasting, pilgrimage, charity, emancipation, jihad and kinship; al-Tha'labi adds testimony, forgiveness and blessings on the Prophet. Al-Razi includes deeds leading to knowledge, love and service. Al-Baydawi names Ramadan fasting and good speech; Kuran Yolu includes faith, moral conduct, enjoining good and kindness. Tabataba'i treats specific reports as examples. Al-Alusi reports al-Khafaji's inclusion argument but questions whether Prophetic 'these are' permits it. Al-Ghazali, as quoted by al-Razi, reads them as ascending knowledge: freedom from defect, source of good, oneness, and greatness beyond reason. ## 18:46/q10 Why are the deeds called enduring and righteous?  [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · interpretation]
- 18:46/q10/p1 Their reward or fruit remains with God after worldly benefits pass away. — Al-Tabari says each righteous act remains for its doer. Al-Kashshaf, al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Ibn Ashur, Asad (also citing 19:76), Study Quran (19:76), and Tabataba'i say God preserves its reward forever. Al-Alusi says the reward, rather than the transient act itself, endures. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وقتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:46/q10/p2 They are righteous because God commands them and promises reward, while warning against omission. — Jishumi and al-Tabrisi give this rationale. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 18:46/q11 What does ṣāliḥāt denote in this phrase?  [ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective is understood through righteous agents or beneficial things.
- 18:46/q11/p1 Righteous actors, named for the actions they perform. — Al-Mawardi says the righteous person performs the right deed. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:46/q11/p2 Beneficial things, named for their soundness. — Al-Mawardi gives this alternative. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 18:46/q12 How does bāqiyāt work grammatically?  [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ · grammar]
- 18:46/q12/p1 It qualifies omitted words or deeds, with the enduring reward understood by ellipsis. — Al-Alusi also allows attachment to a deleted noun. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:46/q13 Why does bāqiyāt precede ṣāliḥāt?  [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · rhetoric]
- 18:46/q13/p1 The reversed expected order stresses permanence against vanishing wealth and sons. — Ibn Ashur says ṣāliḥāt normally comes first as a noun for good works, citing 18:107 and Jarir's verse; the preceding scattered-plant parable (18:45) supplies the omitted thought that worldly ornaments vanish, while 'enduring' reinforces it. He also mentions 13:26. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:46/q14 What does 'better in reward' mean?  [خَيْرٌ عِندَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًۭا · interpretation]
- 18:46/q14/p1 Righteous deeds yield superior, lasting recompense with God in the Hereafter. — Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tha'labi, al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Biqa'i, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabataba'i, Asad and Islahi contrast certain enduring divine reward with wealth, sons and polytheists' recompense. Al-Razi argues spiritual and intellectual goods exceed sensory ones, and the Hereafter unites both. Al-Alusi says 'with your Lord' may mean in the Hereafter or by God's judgment; al-Durr distinguishes the believer's hope and the polytheist's reward. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:46/q15 What does thawāb mean here?  [ثَوَابًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether thawāb is glossed as recompense or benefit.
- 18:46/q15/p1 Recompense or reward, from the notion of returning. — Jishumi gives the root explanation; al-Alusi allows reward and recompense. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:46/q15/p2 Benefit. — Al-Alusi reports this alternative gloss. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:46/q16 What does 'better in hope' mean?  [وَخَيْرٌ أَمَلًۭا · interpretation]
- 18:46/q16/p1 Righteous deeds are a more reliable and worthy object of hope than wealth and children. — Ibn Qutayba glosses the best thing one can hope for; al-Wahidi says better than what one hopes from wealth and children. Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Nasafi contrast failible worldly expectations with God's true promise. Al-Baydawi, al-Biqa'i, Bursevi and al-Alusi say deeds fulfill hopes in the afterlife; Tabataba'i contrasts divine mercy and honor with deceiving worldly hopes. Yahya, Jishumi, al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, al-Durr and Islahi encourage hope in divine reward. Al-Mawardi says hope is truer and better already in this world. [20 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:46/q17 What does amal mean here?  [أَمَلًۭا · meaning]
- 18:46/q17/p1 Hope or expectation. — Jishumi treats these as near synonyms; Ibn Qutayba explains it as what one hopes for. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 18:46/q18 What is the grammar of thawāban and amalan?  [ثَوَابًۭا أَمَلًۭا · grammar]
- 18:46/q18/p1 They are accusative specifications distinguishing the two comparisons. — Jishumi gives this parsing. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:46/q19 How can the verse call the deeds 'better' than worldly adornment?  [خَيْرٌ وَخَيْرٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the comparison is rhetorical, answers mistaken evaluation, or compares recompenses.
- 18:46/q19/p1 The comparative need not grant genuine goodness to the worldly alternative. — Al-Wahidi cites Arabic comparison with an inferior that has no good, including 25:24; al-Qurtubi likewise points to Paradise as a 'better' resting place. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:46/q19/p2 The comparison answers what ignorant people merely suppose is good. — Al-Qurtubi reports the supposition view. Jishumi allows a hypothetical answer to those claiming another can reward, even though God would be best. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 18:46/q19/p3 It compares actual recompenses for different deeds without needing a real divine rival. — Jishumi offers this alternative. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:46/q20 Why repeat 'better' for reward and hope?  [خَيْرٌ وَخَيْرٌ · rhetoric]
- 18:46/q20/p1 It emphasizes superiority or marks two distinct grounds for it. — Al-Alusi gives both explanations. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:46/q21 What do reports say the remembrance formulas accomplish?  [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different benefits and contexts rather than competing definitions.
- 18:46/q21/p1 They plant trees or other growth in Paradise. — Abu Ayyub's Ascension report has Abraham describe good spacious soil and hawqala as planting; al-Tirmidhi's Ibn Mas'ud report adds sweet water and barren ground, graded hasan gharīb. Ibn Majah reports that each of four formulas plants a tree for its reciter. Muhammad ibn Ka'b's disputed hawqala is confirmed by Salim through Abu Ayyub. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو أيوب الأنصاري); THALABI(أبو أيوب الأنصاري عن النبي); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي,الماوردي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي,محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 18:46/q21/p2 They shield from the Fire and aid their reciter on Judgment Day. — Al-Tha'labi and al-Durr report that the formulas surround their reciter; al-Tabrisi and Bursevi report a shield. Al-Qurtubi cites a report graded sound by Abd al-Haqq. Al-Hasan's dream has a voice naming four formulas as protection against fear. [6 notes: THALABI(خالد بن عمران); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(الحسن البصري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI]
- 18:46/q21/p3 They erase sins like leaves falling from a tree. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Qurtubi, al-Durr and al-Alusi cite versions. Al-Tirmidhi calls one gharīb and notes al-A'mash saw but did not hear Anas; Ibn Majah reports a comparable version via Abu al-Darda. They are also called Paradise's treasures. [7 notes: THALABI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 18:46/q21/p4 They are urged especially when harder worship or fighting is beyond one's ability. — Al-Tabrisi reports the Prophet's counsel; Ibn al-Jawzi transmits it via Abu Hurayra. Al-Baghawi's Abu Sa'id report also urges increasing the formulas and gives no grading. [3 notes: TABRISI(النبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 18:46/q21/p5 They outweigh worldly goods and other sacrifices. — Al-Baghawi reports the Prophet calling them the best words and dearer than all the sun rises over. Al-Durr reports Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas comparing them to the Nile and Euphrates, the Prophet preferring them to equal dinars, and Abd Allah ibn Amr preferring them to bridled battle horses. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن المسيب,عبد الله بن عمرو)]
- 18:46/q21/p6 A night reciter who says each of four formulas a hundred times after ablution is forgiven except for bloodshed. — Ibn Kathir cites the report through Sa'd ibn Janadah; another Sa'd report says the Prophet taught him three short suras and the four formulas after his conversion. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي,سعد بن جنادة); DURR-FULL(سعد بن جنادة)]
- 18:46/q21/p7 They bring counted merits and divine remembrance. — Al-Durr reports good deeds and erased bad deeds for particular formulas; a heavenly circuit around the Throne with God's remembrance of their speaker; angels carrying phrases upward and praying for the speaker; and their use as a substitute for someone unable to memorize Qur'an. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 18:46/q21/p8 Four formulas are among five weighty deeds along with patiently losing a righteous child. — Ibn Kathir also reports five beliefs leading to Paradise: God, Last Day, Paradise, Hell, resurrection and reckoning; the enumerated beliefs vary in count from the stated five. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي)]
- 18:46/q21/p9 A report pairs the four formulas with refusal to support or believe unjust rulers. — Ibn Kathir gives the account through an unnamed Ansari. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي)]
- 18:46/q21/p10 A personal report values supplication for guidance and worship above hoarded gold and silver. — Ibn Kathir gives Shaddad ibn Aws's regret at asking for a knife to play with, then the Prophetic prayer for steadfastness, gratitude, sound heart, truthful tongue, good, refuge and forgiveness; Ahmad and al-Nasa'i transmit it. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(شداد بن أوس)]

## 18:46/q22 What larger lesson does the verse draw about worldly attachments?  [* · interpretation]
- 18:46/q22/p1 Prefer lasting righteous action and hope in the Hereafter to pride or dependence on perishing wealth and family. — Jishumi urges Paradise and detachment because life passes quickly; al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi quote Ali's two harvests, though God may give both. Al-Qurtubi warns that wealth, women and power change hands. Ibn Kathir urges worship rather than endless accumulation or worry. Al-Razi ranks lasting spiritual and intellectual happiness above sensory goods. Al-Biqa'i says worldly possessions fail when most needed. Kuran Yolu disapproves of arrogance and pressure against the poor; it also cites 7:32 that lawful enjoyment is permitted. Bursevi quotes ascetic remembrance of death. Ibn Ashur and Study Quran warn against mistaking status for favor. [14 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); BURSEVI(الضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 18:46/q23 What inward meanings are drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the enduring good is described as disciplined attachment, transformed adornment, or granted inward nearness.
- 18:46/q23/p1 The soul clings to worldly shares, while the spirit turns to reliance, certainty and worship. — Al-Sulami contrasts family, wealth and children with reliance, tranquility, trust and certainty. Al-Qushayri says heedless people adorn themselves with money and sons, the connected with works and certainty; status, praise and familiar attachments can also be worldly shares, even if results arrive later. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI]
- 18:46/q23/p2 Escape from worldly ornament requires inward knowledge and love, and outward service and aspiration. — Al-Sulami says such people witness their King in nearness; Bursevi repeats the inward and outward program. [3 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 18:46/q23/p3 Enduring deeds are inward divine light or nearness. — Al-Qushayri reports lights of monotheism beyond veiling, divine qualities adorning hidden secrets and scenting heaven, or intimacy already granted from the unseen; al-Alusi reports lasting love, knowledge, sincere unity and turning away from others. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 18:46/q24 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is to the local parable and resurrection sequence or to Qur'anic pairings elsewhere.
- 18:46/q24/p1 It concludes the image of worldly life vanishing like vegetation and prepares the resurrection scene. — Ibn Ashur says the order 'enduring righteous' answers the wind-scattered plant of 18:45 and the mountain scene of the next verse recalls resurrection. Tabataba'i says the conclusion returns to the sura's opening claim about earthly adornment. Islahi says the next parable warns people blinded by worldly love. Kuran Yolu describes flourishing vegetation drying and scattering; Study Quran locates the long parable among the sura's three main narratives. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:46/q24/p2 Other Qur'anic pairings present wealth and children as a linked trial or adornment. — Al-Farisi connects walad with wealth and children, citing 18:46, 64:15 and 64:14. Ibn Kathir cites 3:14 and 64:15; al-Qurtubi cites 64:15 and 64:14. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 18:46/q25 What ancillary points about the preceding worldly-life parable are recorded?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes give distinct lexical, theological, and grammatical comments on the adjacent parable.
- 18:46/q25/p1 Its vegetation becomes dry crumbling matter left after its brief beauty. — Abu Ubayda's Majaz glosses hashiman as dry crumbling plants and cites Labid's dry branches. Ibn Atiyya's fragment glosses a term as what a flood leaves, then turns to presentation in rows. Jishumi says the world resembles a briefly beautiful plant that yellows, crumbles and blows away, or water that never settles, remains unchanged, or always helps. [3 notes: MAJAZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 18:46/q25/p2 Earning livelihood serves as a divine trial and an occasion for reflection. — Jishumi records the question why people were not given enough to worship without earning; he answers that earning is worship, humbles people, displays mutual need, and teaches that God alone is self-sufficient. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 18:46/q25/p3 A grammatical fragment parses hashiman as the predicate of 'became'. — Jishumi says al-nabat is its omitted subject. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 18:46/q26 What ancillary literary examples appear in the collected notes?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The collected literary notes discuss different examples.
- 18:46/q26/p1 Rain imagery treats hills as close to clouds and sky as a source of bounty. — Jurjani says hills, unlike valleys, lie nearer rain-bearing clouds; a veiled sky plausibly promises rain. He cites Ibn al-Mu'tazz's blessing-and-gratitude image. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(إبن المعتز)]
- 18:46/q26/p2 Praise poetry attributes an object's apparent quality to a patron or invents a cause for it. — Jurjani uses the sun and musk, Ibn Babak's borrowed garden breeze and fickle garden, and a Persian couplet about Gemini's belt; he distinguishes invented cause from hyperbolic simile. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(إبن بابك)]
- 18:46/q26/p3 Other poetic pairs demonstrate thematic and imagistic craft. — Jurjani juxtaposes al-Mutanabbi and a modern poet on submission in love, then pairs Labid, Nafi ibn Laqit, a Kharijite captive, Abu Tammam and al-Nabigha. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 18:46/q27 What ancillary recitation examples appear in the collected notes?  [* · readings]
turns on: These recitation examples concern different expressions.
- 18:46/q27/p1 Al-Azraq has six connected-reading combinations for al-ān under hamza substitution. — Ibn al-Jazari says three further combinations arise at pause and facilitation gives three options for the second alif; he discusses full, medium and short madd transmissions through al-Shatibi, al-Hudhali, al-Unwan, Abu al-Fath Faris, Ibn Ghalbun and Ibn Bulayma, plus al-Asbahani, Qalun and Abu Ja'far. [4 notes: NASHR]
- 18:46/q27/p2 Alif-Lam-Mim permits lengthening or shortening the yāʾ of mīm when linked. — Ibn al-Jazari gives the apparent versus incidental madd rationale; al-Nahhas and Ibn Khayrun transmit the no-madd option from Warsh, and al-Dani, Makki and Abu al-Abbas record both. [2 notes: NASHR(إسماعيل بن عبد الله النخاس ومحمد بن عمر بن خيرون)]

## 18:46/q28 What ancillary etymological saying is reported?  [ٱلْمَالُ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · meaning]
- 18:46/q28/p1 Money is so called because it turns people; the world is so called because it is near. — Al-Durr attributes the saying to Sufyan al-Thawri. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(سفيان الثوري)]
