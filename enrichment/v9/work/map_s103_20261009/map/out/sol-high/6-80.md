# 6:80: 27 questions

## 6:80/q01 What does the people's disputing with Abraham mean?  [وَحَآجَّهُۥ قَوْمُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether reciprocal morphology requires an actual exchange, allows one-sided contention, or identifies who initiated it.
- 6:80/q01/p1 They contended with him, each side advancing an argument or asking for proof. — Ibn Abbas and al-Rabi gloss it as quarrelling or disputing. Jishumi defines muḥājja as reciprocal presentation and demand for evidence. Ibn Atiyya derives it from ḥujja. Ibn Ashur prefers a real exchange and cites other Quranic debates with Abraham, including 21:52–56 and 26:72–73. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الربيع); THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع بن أنس); ALUSI-FULL(الربيع); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 6:80/q01/p2 The reciprocal verb can describe an argument begun by the people while Abraham listens and then replies, or intensify a one-sided contention. — Ibn Ashur allows the opponent's reception and response to justify reciprocal morphology even if only one side argues; the people's words are omitted to focus on Abraham's firmness. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:80/q01/p3 The reciprocal form makes its grammatical subject the initiator. — Ibn Arafah applies this to 2:258, distinguishing Abraham's opening speech from Nimrod's opening of the debate; Ibn Ashur says grammarians do not establish this rule. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عرفة)]

## 6:80/q02 What claims did Abraham's people raise in the dispute?  [وَحَآجَّهُۥ قَوْمُهُۥ فِى ٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: The reports name inherited custom, rival deities, celestial powers, and threats; several sources give more than one.
- 6:80/q02/p1 They defended inherited worship and objected to the oneness of God. — Razi cites 43:23 and 38:5 for ancestral imitation and rejection of one God; Biqai says they insisted on inherited idols. Study Quran cites 21:53, 43:23 and 38:5. Abu Hayyan and Alusi describe traditional arguments as false. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الربيع); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:80/q02/p2 They claimed their gods were better, asked Abraham to describe God, or urged him to worship another god. — Tabari reports the superiority claim; Ibn Ishaq reports the request to describe God. Ibn Jurayj reports invocation of other gods; al-Durr reports a call to worship one. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 6:80/q02/p3 They argued for celestial bodies, idols, or created things as partners and causal powers. — Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq name stars, sun, moon, and idols; Qashani includes the alleged causal power of celestial and other created beings. The argument concerns divine oneness or attributes, as Ibn Ashur notes. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); QASHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:80/q02/p4 They threatened him with punishment or injury by their gods as part of their argument. — Ibn Ashur says they mistook intimidation for proof. Tabatabai says fear of divine anger was their stronger motive for worship; his account has them press Abraham to accept their gods and reject God's lordship. Elmalili says their verbal and practical pressure aimed to win. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]

## 6:80/q03 What events or narrative setting surround this exchange?  [وَحَآجَّهُۥ قَوْمُهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The notes situate the exchange in different episodes and stages of Abraham's public opposition.
- 6:80/q03/p1 Nimrod's claim to give life and death led Abraham to challenge him to bring the sun from the west. — Muqatil says Nimrod killed one man and spared another to claim life and death, then was confounded by Abraham's solar challenge. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 6:80/q03/p2 Abraham mocked and damaged idols made by Azar before his people disputed with him. — Baghawi says Azar gave grown Abraham idols to sell; Abraham mocked their uselessness, struck their heads in a river, and told them to drink. Alusi reports the threat may have followed abandonment, mockery, or damage. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes)]
- 6:80/q03/p3 He publicly opposed his people after the truth became clear; this report follows his declaration in 6:79. — Ibn Ashur says the opening address and waw mark a self-contained report following 6:79, with sequence understood. Islahi holds that Abraham's grand words in 6:79–80 were prophetic and occurred after appointment. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:80/q04 Why are the people's arguments left unstated?  [وَحَآجَّهُۥ قَوْمُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 6:80/q04/p1 Their claims are too feeble to recount; the narrative centers Abraham's answer and firmness. — Biqai points to the clear proofs already given. Kuranyolu says their counterarguments against signs of unity are baseless. Ibn Ashur says their argument is omitted to highlight his steadfastness; he nonetheless identifies real debates elsewhere. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:80/q05 How is أَتُحَٰٓجُّوٓنِّى read?  [أَتُحَٰٓجُّوٓنِّى · readings]
turns on: Whether two nūns are assimilated or one is deleted, and whether the light form is accepted Arabic.
- 6:80/q05/p1 With a doubled nūn, assimilating the indicative and pronoun-related nūns. — Ibn Mujahid lists Ibn Kathir, Abu Amr, Asim, Hamza and Kisa'i; other lists add the rest apart from the light-reading transmitters. Ibn Ashur says waw is lengthened to separate consonants. Qiraat-ER calls it emphatic. Sibawayh's general rules explain deletion of indicative nūn in other plural emphatic forms. [18 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، أبو عمرو، عاصم، حمزة، الكسائي، نافع، ابن عامر); JISHUMI(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة وابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عاصم); BURSEVI; QIRAAT-ER; SIBAWAYH]
- 6:80/q05/p2 With a single light nūn, one of the two original nūns omitted for ease. — Nafi and Ibn Amir are commonly named, with variant details for Hisham and Ibn Dhakwan; Jishumi, Ibn Ashur and others include Abu Jafar. Sibawayh cites Medinans and 15:54; ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib's فليني and 39:64 support the sound change. Farisi reports attribution to Ghatafan. Qiraat-ER says the lighter sound softens the tone. Nahhas, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Ibn Ashur defend its Arabic and canonical standing against Abu Amr's or Makki's criticism. [31 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض القراء وأهل المدينة,عمرو بن معد يكرب); NAHHAS(أبو عبيدة,سيبويه,نافع); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(عمرو); JISHUMI(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة وابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن ذكوان,مدني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER; NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي)-; SAMIN-DURR(مكي ابن أبي طالب)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء)-]

## 6:80/q06 Which nūn is omitted in the light reading?  [أَتُحَٰٓجُّوٓنِّى · grammar]
turns on: The disputed deletion is the first grammatical nūn or the second one before yāʾ.
- 6:80/q06/p1 The second, pronoun-related or protective nūn is omitted; the first inflectional nūn remains. — Farisi, Abu Ali, al-Akhfash and followers keep the grammatical subject and inflection marker. Abu Ali says the survivor takes kasra beside yāʾ. Al-Akhfash calls the second redundant and heavy, citing laytanī/laytī and poetry. The comparison with dropping one nūn in تبشرون and تأمروني is widely given. [16 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش)]
- 6:80/q06/p2 The first, indicative or inflectional nūn is omitted; the second remains before the speaker's yāʾ. — Sibawayh argues the inflectional marker can independently fall; its substitute can too. The remaining nūn already has kasra, avoiding a vowel change. Samin discusses poems, a hadith with negative lā, Abu Amr's readings at 3:160, 6:109 and 2:67, and examples of omitted ḍamma; he records objections that a root may drop while its derivative need not, and that retaining inflection has force. Alusi says the pronoun nūn is not merely protective. [10 notes: SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه)]
- 6:80/q06/p3 The form begins with two nūns, which Arabic may separate, retain, assimilate, or reduce. — Samin compares 39:64, 15:54, 16:27 and 46:17; Sibawayh's larger account distinguishes singular, dual, masculine plural and feminine forms before emphatic nūn and vowels used to avoid consecutive sukūns. [15 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SIBAWAYH; NAHHAS(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 6:80/q07 What does Abraham's question about God mean?  [قَالَ أَتُحَٰٓجُّوٓنِّى فِى ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: The force of the interrogative and the sense of the reciprocal verb.
- 6:80/q07/p1 It rebukes their attempt to dispute divine oneness or attributes after God has guided him. — Zajjaj, Ibn Jawzi, Baydawi and others take fī Allāh as concerning God's unity; Ibn Ashur specifies attributes and oneness rather than God's person. Wahidi and Alusi say the question dismisses an unworthy challenge; Ibn Kathir calls their claims sophistry. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 6:80/q07/p2 The question answers the challenge and closes the debate if the verb is reciprocal; otherwise it is a reply within it. — Ibn Ashur compares 3:20 and says the rebuke expresses no hope that they will return to Abraham's belief. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:80/q08 What does فِى ٱللَّهِ attach to?  [فِى ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the quote is syntactically coordinated with the narrative verb or begins independently.
- 6:80/q08/p1 It attaches to Abraham's quoted أتحاجوني, the second verb. — Abu Hayyan says coordination attaches it to the second verb. Samin reports the Shaykh's rationale: the first verb already has an object pronoun and would need an omitted complement. Samin questions whether coordination is necessary because the speech may simply start a new clause. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 6:80/q08/p2 The quoted question is a new clause, so the attachment need not be explained through coordination of the two verbs. — Samin finds this more natural than making the earlier ḥājjahu govern 'about God'. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 6:80/q09 What guidance had God given Abraham?  [وَقَدْ هَدَىٰنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance names settled knowledge, the method and proof of unity, or a hypothetical path of discovery.
- 6:80/q09/p1 God guided him to knowledge, truth, and affirmation and worship of divine oneness. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Thaʿlabi, Wahidi, Zamakhshari, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Baydawi and others state this. Tabatabai says settled knowledge followed God's showing the kingdom of heaven and earth. The guidance renders further false argument futile, as Razi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur explain. [22 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أكثر المفسرين); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 6:80/q09/p2 God taught him how to establish proof of divine unity; the act of guidance itself proves God's lordship. — Alusi reports the proof-making sense. Tabatabai prefers the deeper argument: guidance to reject other gods is divine governance, and a rival deity would not lead him to reject its own divinity. He objects to stopping at 'no further debate' when the context argues. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); TABATABAI(أكثر المفسرين); TABATABAI+]
- 6:80/q09/p3 The account describes a hypothetical following of their path followed by recognition of its complete invalidity. — Alusi reports the suggestion while insisting neither this nor guidance to proof means Abraham had been astray or ignorant of God. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes)]

## 6:80/q10 Whose circumstance is expressed by وَقَدْ هَدَىٰنِ?  [وَقَدْ هَدَىٰنِ · grammar]
turns on: The clause's circumstantial referent is Abraham or God.
- 6:80/q10/p1 It is a circumstantial clause of Abraham, the speaker in 'do you dispute with me?'. — Samin calls this most natural. Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur say the clause makes debate futile because God has already guided him; Ibn Ashur says the people knew it or are treated as knowing. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:80/q10/p2 It is a circumstantial clause of God: they dispute while He guides Abraham. — Samin reports this as an alternative that likewise makes their case futile. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:80/q11 How is the final yāʾ of هَدَىٰنِ read?  [هَدَىٰنِ · readings]
turns on: Which reader or transmission is followed, and whether the word is connected or paused.
- 6:80/q11/p1 Some retain the speaker's yāʾ in connection, pause, or both, with transmission-dependent variation. — Ibn Mujahid says Abu Amr and some Nafi transmissions retain it in connection but omit it at pause; Jishumi includes Abu Jafar, Nafi, Abu Amr and Yaqub among retainers. Nasafi reports Abu Amr retaining at both. Nashr reports Yaqub and a Qunbul route retaining at both, while Abu Jafar and Abu Amr omit in connection. Qiraat-ER describes full yāʾ at pause or retention only in connection. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,نافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو جعفر وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وقنبل); QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:80/q11/p2 Other transmissions omit the yāʾ in connection and pause, or only at pause, because context or kasra supplies it. — Ibn Mujahid lists Qalun, Warsh, Musayyibi and others as omitting throughout, though he also names Nafi transmissions retaining in connection. Nahhas says kasra indicates the omitted yāʾ and nūn compensates, while retention is sound. Ibn Ashur specifies Qalun's omission in both and compares 2:186 for omission at pause. [7 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,نافع); NAHHAS; JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وأبو عمرو ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:80/q12 Is the dāl of هَدَىٰنِ inclined by imāla?  [هَدَىٰنِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the yāʾ root warrants imāla or the surface form governs pronunciation.
- 6:80/q12/p1 Al-Kisa'i inclines the dāl toward kasra. — Ibn Khalawayh, Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Jawzi ground imāla in the final yāʾ of the root, still relevant after the suffix; Qiraat-ER says the sound changes without a change of meaning. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الكسائي); QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:80/q12/p2 The other readers give fatḥa or tafkhīm according to the surface form. — Ibn Mujahid contrasts them with al-Kisa'i; Ibn Khalawayh gives the phonetic rationale. [2 notes: IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 6:80/q13 What harm did the people threaten Abraham with?  [وَلَآ أَخَافُ مَا تُشْرِكُونَ بِهِۦٓ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over the threatened affliction and whether the divine punishment was future or already alleged.
- 6:80/q13/p1 Their gods would afflict him with madness or insanity. — Ibn Jurayj explicitly reports the threat of madness; Thaʿlabi, Wahidi, Baghawi and others include it among the possible afflictions. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 6:80/q13/p2 Their gods would inflict leprosy, disease, other illness, or calamity for abandoning, mocking, or damaging them. — Tabari names leprosy; Thaʿlabi and Ibn Atiyya give leprosy or illness, Abu Hayyan leprosy or disease. Alusi lists abandonment, mockery and damage as motives; Ibn Ashur infers a threat from the otherwise unmotivated denial of fear, comparing Hud in 11:54. Islahi calls threatened wrath a basis of polytheistic fear. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكفار); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes,ابن جريج); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 6:80/q13/p3 They claimed a deity had already afflicted him or turned him from their worship. — Tabatabai compares their possible charge with Hud's people at 11:52–55; Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Ibn Kathir also compare Hud's confrontation and threat. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 6:80/q14 Why does Abraham not fear what they associate with God?  [وَلَآ أَخَافُ مَا تُشْرِكُونَ بِهِۦٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the focus is the idols' intrinsic powerlessness or divine control over every apparent effect.
- 6:80/q14/p1 Idols and celestial objects cannot independently hear, understand, benefit, harm, or defend themselves. — Muqatil and Tabari stress carved wood and images without perception; Tabrisi notes a broken idol cannot defend itself and a setting star is created. Razi says fear properly concerns a capable agent. Ibn Kathir says if idols could act, they should plot and hasten harm. Biqai contrasts divine power to guide and harm with the idols' inability to harm or benefit. Abu Hayyan notes mā can include things or persons. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 6:80/q14/p2 Any apparent effect through idols, talismans, or stars ultimately comes from God. — Razi rejects making talismanic effects independent: even stellar powers are created by God. Qashani says harm from created things comes from Him. Tabatabai says events in God's ordered realm are prevented or permitted by Him, so hypothetical fear would affirm His lordship; Islahi stresses trust amid trials. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:80/q15 What does مَا تُشْرِكُونَ بِهِۦ refer to, and how is it constructed?  [مَا تُشْرِكُونَ بِهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical category of mā and whether bihī returns to God, mā, or an implied cause.
- 6:80/q15/p1 Mā is relative or indefinite descriptive, denoting the things or persons they associate; the object of 'associate' is understood. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Alusi and Ibn Ashur give this analysis. The pronoun may refer to God, with bāʾ marking what receives the association, or to mā, with bāʾ causal or meaning that by which they associate. Burhan's other examples show relative mā in worship and other clauses. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 6:80/q15/p2 Mā can be a verbal noun: Abraham denies fearing the act of associating partners with God. — Samin makes bihī refer to God and supplies an omitted object; Alusi also lists the verbal-noun analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:80/q15/p3 An implied 'cause' may stand before the pronoun. — Abu al-Baqa proposes this addition; Samin says it is unnecessary. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 6:80/q16 How does وَلَآ أَخَافُ relate syntactically to the preceding words?  [وَلَآ أَخَافُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the no-fear clause is a new assertion or a circumstantial qualification.
- 6:80/q16/p1 It is an independent assertion of confidence, coordinated with the disputation statement or God's guidance. — Samin describes an independent sentence answering the idol threat. Ibn Ashur says coordination with guidance is clearer, since the opponents already knew Abraham did not fear idols. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 6:80/q16/p2 It is a circumstantial clause of Abraham in the question, or nested under 'He guided me'. — Samin says the nested analysis needs an implied subject before the present verb after wāw. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:80/q17 How does the exception إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ رَبِّى شَيْـًۭٔا limit Abraham's denial of fear?  [إِلَّآ أَن يَشَآءَ رَبِّى شَيْـًۭٔا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether illā resumes with fear of God, excepts a time or state of harm through idols, or qualifies the very feeling of fear.
- 6:80/q17/p1 It is disconnected: he fears only harm or punishment his Lord independently wills, not the idols. — Zajjaj and Wahidi mention punishment for sin; Razi additionally allows worldly trials or lost blessings. Ibn Atiyya, al-Hawfi, Baghawi, Tabari's reported reading, and Ibn Ashur treat the clause as 'but I fear God's will'; Ibn Ashur prefers this for its rebuke of the idolaters. Al-Hasan likewise allows sin or independent harm. Ibn Jawzi calls the clause a fresh start, and Damghani compares 7:188 and 10:49. Tabatabai reports but objects that a general fear shifts too far from the partners. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء,ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية والطبري)+; TABATABAI-]
- 6:80/q17/p2 It is connected: they are not feared at any time or in any state unless God wills harm through or by empowering them. — Zamakhshari supplies an omitted span of time; Abu al-Baqa supplies circumstances. Tabrisi prefers the empowering sense because any bestowed power would prove creation and God's sole right to worship. Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Biqai allow harm through them only by divine power. Razi lists reviving and empowering idols as possible. Tabatabai rejects attributing benefit or harm to partners and says their createdness would not refute worshippers who already grant it. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري وابن جني); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو البقاء,الزمخشري ومتابعوه); TABATABAI(قيل); SAMIN-DURR+; TABRISI+; TABATABAI-]
- 6:80/q17/p3 It can mean that God does not will Abraham to fear the idols, or wills a test of his fear. — Qurtubi gives the non-willing of fear; Alusi gives a disconnected form in which he fears God's willing that he fear. Study Quran describes divine testing. Asad translates the wording as 'unless my Sustainer wills a thing.' [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 6:80/q18 What grammatical uses of إِلَّا are relevant here?  [إِلَّآ · grammar]
turns on: The notes distinguish the verse's resumed clause from ordinary exception and other attested uses of illā.
- 6:80/q18/p1 Here illā can introduce a fresh, disconnected clause resembling an exception. — Ibn Jawzi's reported exegetes classify 6:80 with 7:188, 7:89, 92:20, 88:23 and 95:6 as istiʾnāf; Damghani compares 7:188 and 10:49. Nahhas explicitly calls the exception disconnected. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); DAMGHANI; NAHHAS]
- 6:80/q18/p2 Illā is fundamentally the most common exception particle. — Abu Zakariya lists comparable nouns siwā, ghayr, bayd, mayd; verbs laysa, lā yakūn, ʿadā; and particles ḥāshā, khalā. Reported exegetes illustrate ordinary Quranic exceptions at 25:69, 29:14 and 43:67. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو زكريا,أهل التفسير)]
- 6:80/q18/p3 In other Arabic uses it may mean 'and' or 'other than'. — A poem about brothers and two stars illustrates the waw sense; exegetes cite 21:22 and 44:56 for the ghayr sense. These are background classifications, not claimed as the sense in 6:80. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أنشدوا,أهل التفسير)]

## 6:80/q19 How is شَيْـًۭٔا parsed?  [شَيْـًۭٔا · grammar]
turns on: Whether shayʾan names what is willed or intensifies the act of willing.
- 6:80/q19/p1 It is the direct object of 'wills': God may will some thing or harm. — Ibn Atiyya states this; Abu Hayyan permits it; Samin records it as the alternative to his preferred cognate analysis. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:80/q19/p2 It is a cognate accusative, 'a willing of some sort,' emphasizing the act of will. — Samin prefers this because repetition intensifies meaning and the direct object of willing is normally specified when unusual; he cites poetry for a remarkable named wish. Abu Hayyan allows this. Burhan permits an emphatic cognate object or an affair/clarification in a verbal noun's place, disputing a rule that a general object of will must be omitted. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 6:80/q20 Why does Abraham say رَبِّى rather than use a pronoun?  [رَبِّى · rhetoric]
- 6:80/q20/p1 Calling God 'my Lord' conveys care and Abraham's submission; repeating the name strengthens the point. — Alusi says any willed harm could have a nurturing purpose, or the wording confesses God's rule and ownership. He says repeating the divine designation instead of a pronoun is reinforcing and pleasurable. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:80/q21 What does God's encompassing knowledge establish here?  [وَسِعَ رَبِّى كُلَّ شَىْءٍ عِلْمًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause grounds divine permission of events, praises knowledge or rebukes idols, predicts harm, or denies creaturely influence.
- 6:80/q21/p1 God knows every thing and event, so any harm or benefit occurs within His knowledge and wise will. — Tabari grounds it in God's creation and contrasts ignorant idols. Kaʿb refers to the Mother Book, creation and deeds. Razi says misfortune may serve wisdom and benefit. Nasafi, Bursevi, Ibn Ashur and Study Quran relate the knowledge to any harm God permits; Tabatabai says God knows each event and prevents or allows it. Ibn Kathir says no hidden matter escapes Him. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(كعب); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 6:80/q21/p2 The statement praises God's knowledge, contrasts idols' ignorance, or explains His wise acts. — Tabatabai reports these as other commentators' readings, then objects that power would suit the taunt and wisdom should be named to support the claim of only good acts. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 6:80/q21/p3 God may already know an idol or natural object will injure Abraham, so His knowledge explains the exception. — Tabatabai reports examples of a falling idol and the sun's heat, but objects that power or wisdom would better explain harm than knowledge alone. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 6:80/q21/p4 Knowledge and will leave no creature able to affect God's actions through intercession. — A reported view cites 2:255; Tabatabai rejects it because knowledge alone needs power and will, while cited intercession verses acknowledge causal mediation by divine permission. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI-]

## 6:80/q22 How is عِلْمًا parsed?  [عِلْمًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ʿilman specifies the subject's knowledge or functions as a figurative verbal object.
- 6:80/q22/p1 It is an accusative specification converted from the subject: 'my Lord's knowledge encompasses everything'. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Samin give this; Samin prefers it and compares 19:4. Nahhas says the noun specifies or explains the preceding statement. Samin adds that 'everything' is the object of wasiʿa on either analysis. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:80/q22/p2 It is an absolute or cognate-like object because encompassing entails knowing. — Abu al-Baqa offers the figurative connection; Samin calls the metaphor remote. Alusi permits a cognate-like verbal noun. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 6:80/q23 What does the final call to remember ask the people to recognize?  [أَفَلَا تَتَذَكَّرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is abandonment of idols or explicit comparison of the two kinds of reasoning.
- 6:80/q23/p1 They should reflect that lifeless idols are powerless and leave their worship for the Creator. — Tabari, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Kathir, Biqai, Bursevi and Alusi say the question rebukes heedlessness. Biqai and Alusi say reason already contains the distinction and needs prompting; Ibn Ashur adds God's attributes evident in creation. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:80/q23/p2 They should compare sound with corrupt reasoning and the capable God with incapable partners. — Zamakhshari and Baydawi formulate the contrast; Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari and Razi's point that denial of rivals brings no punishment. Kuranyolu urges a rational comparison between demonstrated monotheism and idolatry. Razi says affirming unity does not itself deserve punishment. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:80/q24 What is the syntax of أَفَلَا تَتَذَكَّرُونَ?  [أَفَلَا تَتَذَكَّرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the last question is an independent sentence or coordinated with the earlier denial.
- 6:80/q24/p1 It is an independent admonitory and reproachful sentence, with no syntactic position. — Samin calls it a fresh sentence of rebuke. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:80/q24/p2 It is coordinated with Abraham's denial of their argument. — Ibn Ashur says it reproaches their failure to recall the idols' unfitness and God's attributes. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:80/q25 What wider lessons about argument and reliance on God are drawn from this exchange?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes draw both an epistemic lesson about proof and a theological lesson about fear and divine causation.
- 6:80/q25/p1 True proof and divine guidance defeat inherited error; debate is praiseworthy for truth and blameworthy for falsehood. — Razi uses 6:83 and says Quranic and hadith censure concerns false disputation. Abraham's clear proof makes the people's argument ineffectual in Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and Kuranyolu. Tabatabai treats guidance itself as proof of lordship. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:80/q25/p2 All hope and fear belong ultimately to God, and His wise will governs even trials. — Biqai says Abraham hopes in and fears God alone. Razi says impending hardship should not be mistaken for idol vengeance. Qashani denies independent created causation. Islahi compares Shuʿayb in 7:89: sincere aims still need God's help through trials. Tabatabai says the fear argument answers the opponents' strongest appeal; prophetic preaching uses warning as well as glad tidings. [11 notes: BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:80/q26 How does this exchange connect with other verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the contextual emphasis is narrative sequence, prophetic continuity, or the structure of Abraham's proof.
- 6:80/q26/p1 It continues Abraham's declaration of 6:79 and recalls Hud's rejection of idol threats. — Ibn Ashur links the waw to 6:79 and compares 11:54; Ibn Kathir narrates Hud's challenge in 11:53–56; Razi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Tabatabai also invoke Hud's people's threat. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); TABATABAI]
- 6:80/q26/p2 Abraham's example supports Muhammad's identical call to monotheism and guidance to preach without forcing belief. — Islahi says Abraham and later prophets bring the same message; Abraham is singled out because Israelites and Ishmaelites claim him, though Judaism, Christianity and Ishmaelite polytheism are later distortions. Muhammad revives and completes Abraham's religion for his descendants. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:80/q26/p3 The no-fear assertion completes the guidance proof and supplies its own argument against partners. — Tabatabai says the exception is a concession, divine knowledge explains it, and the final appeal invokes innate reason; his opponents take guidance merely as ending further argument. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أكثر المفسرين)]

## 6:80/q27 What adjacent linguistic illustrations are recorded with the verse's discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These source excerpts contain adjacent examples rather than competing explanations of a word in 6:80.
- 6:80/q27/p1 Poetic and everyday animal imagery can imply human rank, poverty, or distress. — Jurjani says uncracked hooves imply a king rather than a low servant; Ibn Durayd's cracked-hoof insult supports that association. He explains tawlab for a poor child and other animal descriptions of wretched people. A gazelle-young allusion makes a birth question apt; the anecdote connects anger, hunger and the family's tears. Al-Asmaʿi insists on jadʿan with dāl as 'poorly fed' against al-Mufaḍḍal's reading. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(أبو بكر بن دريد,الأصمعي)]
- 6:80/q27/p2 Attributing قوم to a rooster's group treats it as rational; an established lion metaphor licenses nonhuman adjective agreement. — Jurjani points to preceding hum and the rational pronoun in the rooster case, and to al-ḍāriya rather than a human plural for men pictured as lions. [2 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 6:80/q27/p3 Other lexical examples gloss الغمرة as overwhelming distress and فرادى as one by one. — Abu Hayyan derives the former from covering water and cites poetry; he records Tamim's tanwin of farād and differing derivations. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 6:80/q27/p4 Other recitation examples distinguish vowel length, yāʾ opening, and consonant conditions. — Nashr says the three madd lengths depend on a reader's established choice; large assimilation for Abu Amr permits three lengths though most shorten, and the chapter distinguishes the condition and cause of madd.
