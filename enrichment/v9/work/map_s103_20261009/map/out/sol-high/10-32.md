# 10:32: 19 questions

## 10:32/q01 Whom does 'that is Allah, your true Lord' identify, and on what grounds?  [فَذَٰلِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ رَبُّكُمُ ٱلْحَقُّ · interpretation]
- 10:32/q01/p1 The one whose creative, life-giving, providing and governing acts the audience acknowledged is Allah, their true Lord, alone entitled to worship. — Al-Zajjaj and Ibn Abbas in Wahidi ground the identification in the preceding admission and deny the partners any such powers. Tabari and Ibn Kathir draw exclusive worship from the audience's own acknowledgment. Baydawi lists creation, enlivening, provision and governance; Biqai says true lordship requires all the listed attributes. Razi adds certain sovereignty and mercy; Nasafi says careful reasoning makes lordship certain. Elmalili includes creation, command, mastery and authority; Asad excludes even a slight share of divinity. Ibn Ashur says the initial fa draws the conclusion from 10:31, the demonstrative gathers the qualities, and naming Allah exposes the polytheists' error. Study Quran connects it to 10:31 and, with 6:95 and 6:102, says reflection removes doubt. Islahi calls the demonstrative the logical consequence of the acknowledged attributes. Jishumi takes the preceding questions to establish both unity and resurrection through rain, plants, clouds, celestial bodies and vegetation (the latter list from al-Asamm). [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Z,Ṭ, Z)]

## 10:32/q02 How is 'that is Allah, your true Lord, the Truth' parsed?  [فَذَٰلِكُمُ ٱللَّهُ رَبُّكُمُ ٱلْحَقُّ · grammar]
turns on: The notes offer different grammatical analyses of the divine name, 'your Lord', and al-haqq.
- 10:32/q02/p1 The word al-haqq admits the same three accusative analyses as in verse 10:30. — Al-Farra invokes the three earlier analyses; al-Nahhas explicitly points to 10:30. [2 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS]
- 10:32/q02/p2 Allah may qualify the demonstrative and rabbukum be its predicate, or Allah may be the predicate and rabbukum an apposition; al-haqq qualifies or further predicates lordship. — Alusi gives these alternative nominal analyses and notes that the demonstrative sums the acknowledged qualities. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 10:32/q03 What does calling God al-Haqq mean?  [ٱلْحَقُّ · theology]
turns on: One answer explains divine existence; the other records the scope of a Prophetic supplication.
- 10:32/q03/p1 God is the real and established existent; His existence is necessary, without beginning or end, while all other existents depend on a creator and may perish. — Al-Khattabi, reported by Ibn al-Jawzi, defines the Real as established and valid in existence. Qurtubi contrasts God's existence with contingent things and cites Labid's 'everything except God is perishing' beside 28:88. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الخطابي); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 10:32/q03/p2 God's truth includes the reality of His promise, word, meeting, Paradise, Hell, Hour, prophets and Muhammad. — Qurtubi transmits Aisha's report of the Prophet's night-prayer supplication using these affirmations. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة)]

## 10:32/q04 What is the truth and error contrasted in 'what after truth except error'?  [ٱلْحَقِّ ٱلضَّلَٰلُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish worship from its objects, and the verse's specific polemic from a general truth/error principle.
- 10:32/q04/p1 Truth is worship of God alone, and error is abandoning it for worship of Satan, idols or any rival; association makes worship unacceptable. — Muqatil in Wahidi calls the alternative worship of Satan; Ibn Abbas in Wahidi names their adopted gods. Tabari, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Tabatabai apply the contrast to exclusive divine worship. Alusi says associated worship is not accepted. Wahidi and Baydawi stress that rivals cannot provide, enliven or cause death. Ibn Atiyya praises the Quran's concise clarity. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 10:32/q04/p2 Truth is the true Lord Himself, and error is the supposed gods that possess none of His powers. — Ibn Abbas in Wahidi names the adopted gods; Alusi explicitly reports this construal as an alternative to the worship-focused construal. Qurtubi cites 31:30, where God is Truth and invoked rivals are false. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 10:32/q04/p3 Truth and error are opposites with no middle ground: leaving manifest truth entails error, shirk and misery. — Jishumi says no third rank exists; Kashshaf adds shirk and misery. Razi argues contradictory propositions cannot both be true or false. Qurtubi compares the truth/falsehood contrast and 31:30. Abu Hayyan calls the question a negative assertion and rebuke. Ibn Ashur explains 'after' as 'other than' when truth is absent; Tabatabai sees paired truth/guidance and falsehood/error; Elmalili applies this to every creed other than monotheism. Tabatabai elsewhere says unified truths cohere, whereas falsehood is scattered and divisive. [14 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 10:32/q04/p4 The contrast can broadly include monotheism and worship beyond the specific opposition of God and idols. — Alusi reports this expanded construal alongside the two narrower construals. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 10:32/q05 Does this truth/error contrast extend to disputed acts and legal rulings?  [ٱلْحَقِّ ٱلضَّلَٰلُ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's contrast is restricted by its context of belief and worship or also covers legal deeds.
- 10:32/q05/p1 Here the no-middle principle governs foundational belief and unbelief, especially God's oneness; derivative legal rulings can have ambiguity and differing laws. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi cite 5:48 and the Prophetic distinction between clear lawful, clear forbidden and doubtful matters. Qurtubi says deeds are not the primary subject; Abu Hayyan rejects using this passage as proof that chess is error because laws and jurists differ. Qurtubi favors this reading from the preceding proof of God's acts and exclusive right to worship. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 10:32/q05/p2 The truth/error principle can judge conduct: what God forbids is error and what He permits is guidance. — Qurtubi reports this broader reading, based on God alone permitting and forbidding, but rejects it in favor of the context about worship. Malik also applies the verse to an unspecified disputed matter and to games; those applications are detailed below. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مالك); DURR-FULL(مالك بن أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 10:32/q06 What does al-dalal mean in Arabic and religious usage?  [ٱلضَّلَٰلُ · meaning]
- 10:32/q06/p1 It means straying from a straight route or losing something; religious usage especially applies it to unsound belief, while context can also mean lack of knowledge. — Ibn Arafa gives deviation and loss. Qurtubi derives the sense from leaving a road's direction and says 93:7 may mean the Prophet was unaware of scripture and faith before guidance, as 42:52 indicates, rather than unbelieving. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عرفة)]

## 10:32/q07 How does ma-dha and the exception in 'what after truth except error' work?  [فَمَاذَا إِلَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether dha merges with ma as one interrogative or functions as a relative pronoun.
- 10:32/q07/p1 Ma and dha form one emphatic interrogative 'what'; the question works as a negation, permitting illa. — Abu Hayyan gives 'after truth' as predicate. Al-Samin says interrogative force predominates over the demonstrative. Ibn Ashur says the combination often adds emphasis, citing 2:26 and 10:50 for other uses. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 10:32/q07/p2 Dha is a relative pronoun meaning 'that which', with its clause predicated of interrogative ma; the rhetorical negation still takes illa. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report this alternate grammatical construction. Ibn Ashur allows relative dha elsewhere but favors the emphatic combination here. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 10:32/q08 Why are the question and its opening fa phrased this way?  [فَمَاذَا بَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ · rhetoric]
- 10:32/q08/p1 The fa draws a reproachful inference from proof of God's lordship, and the question emphatically denies any alternative to truth. — Ibn Ashur explains the inferential fa; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin say an interrogative supplies a negative assertion with illa. Ibn Ashur takes 'after' as 'other than what follows when truth is absent.' [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 10:32/q09 How is the truth/error contrast expressed concisely?  [ٱلْحَقِّ ٱلضَّلَٰلُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the paired implication should be described directly or as a formal two-sided omission.
- 10:32/q09/p1 The wording pairs truth with guidance and, by implication, falsehood with error. — Tabatabai prefers this direct account of the compressed opposites and rejects the more technical account. Ibn Atiyya, cited by Ibn Ashur, praises the Quran's brevity, eloquence and clarity; Ibn Atiyya himself says its expression surpasses commentary. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI+]
- 10:32/q09/p2 The phrase uses ihtibak: 'falsehood' is omitted after 'truth', and 'guidance' after 'error'. — Tabatabai reports unnamed exegetes' analysis, then prefers his own account. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI-]

## 10:32/q10 What does 'how are you turned away?' ask?  [فَأَنَّىٰ تُصْرَفُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The Arabic interrogative is explained as 'how' or as a metaphorical 'where are you going?'
- 10:32/q10/p1 It asks how they can turn from acknowledged truth, guidance and God's worship to error, idols or beings unable to provide, enliven or kill. — Ibn Abbas glosses the interrogative as 'how' and, in Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi, asks how reason could choose powerless beings. Tabari invokes admitted guidance; Kashshaf and Nasafi specify monotheism to association; Razi stresses manifest truth; Ibn Kathir emphasizes the acknowledged creator and governor. Ibn Ashur treats the path as a metaphor for reasoning. Tabatabai asks how long such turning continues; Asad gives the literal question. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); SULAMI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 10:32/q10/p2 It asks where they are going or straying, even without a literal person turning them. — Abu Muslim in Jishumi makes the nonliteral point. Ibn Atiyya compares the reproach to 'where are you going?' in 81:26; Elmalili also asks where they are being led. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 10:32/q11 What force does the closing question carry?  [فَأَنَّىٰ تُصْرَفُونَ · rhetoric]
- 10:32/q11/p1 It rebukes and expresses astonishment at their actual turning from truth, with a further inferential fa. — Ibn Atiyya calls it reproach; Alusi says the interrogative expresses astonishment and denial of their real action, stronger than merely denying occurrence. Ibn Ashur says fa adds explicit rebuke after the denial; Tabari and Abu Hayyan connect the rebuke to their prior acknowledgment. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 10:32/q12 Who causes the turning described by the passive verb?  [تُصْرَفُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether humans create their turning or God acts while they remain blameworthy for its cause.
- 10:32/q12/p1 The people turn themselves: God cannot reproach unbelievers for a turning He compelled. — Al-Jubbai in Razi and Abu Hayyan argues from the verse's blame, comparing a blind person who would not claim to blind himself. Alusi reports the Mu'tazili view that the polytheists create and perform this shameful act, so its agent is left unnamed. Jishumi argues that idol worship and false claims are human acts deserving punishment, against the Compulsionists. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجبائي); ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة); JISHUMI]
- 10:32/q12/p2 God is the agent of the act, while the rebuke concerns its blameworthy cause rather than expressing surprise at God's own act. — Alusi states this as his doctrinal solution to the passive question. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 10:32/q13 How does the next decree relate to this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: Which preceding element grounds the decree and how divine predetermination is understood.
- 10:32/q13/p1 The following decree may follow from the truth/error contrast or from the people's being turned away. — Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari's two connections; he also reports Ibn Atiyya comparing God's attributes with the people's chosen course. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 10:32/q13/p2 God's prior decree explains why rebellious people will not believe. — Muqatil states this while explaining the true Lord and truth/error sequence. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 10:32/q14 What inward meaning is drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward emphasis falls on divine witnessing or the diversion of intention.
- 10:32/q14/p1 The Real is the sole goal and means of worship and witnessing; one cannot testify to divine unity through an independent witness. — Al-Husayn says God is the goal of worship and companion in obedience, witnessed and reached only through Him. Al-Wasiti says all else is misguidance beyond the lost person's standing or description. [2 notes: SULAMI(الحسين,الواسطي)]
- 10:32/q14/p2 Turning away is diversion from the Real into other aims, which can corrupt acts of devotion. — An unnamed saying in Sulami describes diversion from God. Bursevi says transactions mixed with ulterior motives turn toward falsehood and invalidate devotion. [2 notes: SULAMI(قيل); BURSEVI]

## 10:32/q15 Does God acquire judgments of good and bad from creatures?  [ٱللَّهُ رَبُّكُمُ ٱلْحَقُّ · theology]
- 10:32/q15/p1 No. God originated creatures and cannot be affected by anything He made. — Al-Husayn is reported by Sulami. [1 notes: SULAMI(الحسين)]

## 10:32/q16 What fate of idols and worshippers is invoked in the surrounding argument?  [* · links]
- 10:32/q16/p1 God gathers worshippers and idols, gives the idols life, then separates them as they disavow one another; each person receives good or evil's consequence. — Jishumi says manufactured deities cannot help, harm, avert punishment or independently intercede, reinforcing the verse's exclusive lordship. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 10:32/q17 What is the verse taken to imply about playing chess and backgammon?  [فَمَاذَا بَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ إِلَّا ٱلضَّلَٰلُ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse and game reports prohibit play generally, or allow chess and confine the strongest prohibition to gambling.
- 10:32/q17/p1 These games are false or blameworthy and can be forbidden even without gambling. — Malik applies the verse to chess, backgammon and the game of fourteen, calling chess false and vain; Zuhri agrees. Qurtubi favors general prohibition as safer: Muslim transmits the pork-and-blood comparison for nardshir; Abu Musa's sound report calls nard disobedience; al-Halimi cites a chess disobedience report; Ali rebukes players for wasted life; Ibn Umar calls chess worse than nard; Abu Musa calls its players sinners; Abu Jafar calls it Zoroastrian. Qurtubi also cites a long report condemning players and spectators of backgammon, chess, walnuts and dice, with loss of good deeds, and notes their pairing with wine in al-Ma'ida. Al-Halimi says an authenticated report binds everyone. Ibn Umar stopped children's al-kajja; Ibn Abbas calls even that game gambling, while Ibn al-Arabi describes a rolled rag ball. Qurtubi rejects using Umar's war-model remark or companion silence as proof of idle play's permission. Al-Turtushi replies that chess misteaches warfare by moving the king away from capture. [19 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الفضل عطاء المقدسي,أبو جعفر,أبو عبد الله الحليمي,أبو موسى الأشعري,ابن عباس,ابن عمر,الحليمي,عبد الله بن عمر,علي,مالك); IBNABIHATIM(مالك); DURR-FULL(مالك بن أنس); ALUSI-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 10:32/q17/p2 Occasional private chess without gambling may be excused; nard reports might be restricted to gambling, and chess can be valued as an exercise of thought or warcraft. — Qurtubi reports Maliki and majority allowance for occasional secret home play, and acknowledges that the nard prohibition might be limited to gambling by early permissions. Ibn al-Arabi reports Shafi'i reasoning that chess exercises thought, while chance-based backgammon resembles divining arrows; some Shafi'is even recommended chess after study in schools or mosques and ascribed it to early Muslims. Abu al-Fadl Ata al-Maqdisi says chess teaches warfare. Qurtubi rejects the claimed early attributions, mental benefit and the reading of Umar's remark as idle-play permission. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الفضل عطاء المقدسي,ابن العربي)-]

## 10:32/q18 When does play in chess or backgammon invalidate testimony?  [فَمَاذَا بَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ إِلَّا ٱلضَّلَٰلُ · law]
turns on: How habitual play, public reputation, gambling and general uprightness affect legal probity.
- 10:32/q18/p1 Habitual or public play damages probity and testimony; occasional private non-gambling chess is excused. — Malik doubts habitual players' testimony in Ibn Abi Hatim, Durr and Alusi; Qurtubi reports that Malikis and most jurists reject habitual public players because their reputation and probity fail, while excusing occasional secret home chess. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مالك بن أنس); ALUSI-FULL(مالك)]
- 10:32/q18/p2 An otherwise upright player's testimony stands unless foolishness, suspicion, grave sin or gambling compromises probity. — Qurtubi reports the Shafi'i school: gambling or becoming known for it invalidates probity through illicit wealth. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 10:32/q18/p3 All these amusements are disliked, but a player with more good deeds than faults and no evident major sin retains acceptable testimony. — Qurtubi reports Abu Hanifa's school, including chess, backgammon and the game of fourteen. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]

## 10:32/q19 What game names and materials are recorded?  [فَمَاذَا بَعْدَ ٱلْحَقِّ إِلَّا ٱلضَّلَٰلُ · reports]
- 10:32/q19/p1 Chess and backgammon pieces are described as boxwood and elephant bone; backgammon also bears the names al-batil, al-ka'b, al-arn and nardshir. — Qurtubi supplies the materials and names. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
