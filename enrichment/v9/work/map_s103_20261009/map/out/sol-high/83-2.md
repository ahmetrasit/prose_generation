# 83:2: 15 questions

## 83:2/q01 How does the relative clause beginning الذين relate to the condemned defrauders?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether الذين qualifies المطففين in the genitive or is set in the accusative for condemnation; the clause may define the group or describe its conduct.
- 83:2/q01/p1 It is a genitive adjective identifying the defrauders by their conduct. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the genitive adjectival analysis; al-Ālūsī says the clause can define which defrauders are intended; al-Būrsawī says it explains how they defraud and merit condemnation. [3 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 83:2/q01/p2 It describes the defrauders' characteristic conduct. — Al-Ālūsī offers description as an alternative to defining the class; al-Būrsawī connects their taking in full and more with their condemnation. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 83:2/q01/p3 It is accusative by way of condemnation. — Al-Naḥḥās offers this parsing and says it better suits the verse than the genitive adjective. [1 notes: NAHHAS+]

## 83:2/q02 What does اكتالوا mean here?  [ٱكْتَالُوا۟ · meaning]
- 83:2/q02/p1 They take or buy goods by measure from other people. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Būrsawī compare iktiyāl, taking by measure, with ittizān, taking by weight. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the measured object is omitted because the verb supplies it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies measured goods received from people. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 83:2/q03 What does على الناس mean and what does على attach to?  [عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether على simply substitutes for من, conveys an owed claim or harm and superiority, or belongs syntactically to يستوفون; sources also differ over whether the two prepositional constructions are synonymous.
- 83:2/q03/p1 It attaches to اكتالوا and means taking measure from people; على can stand for من here. — Al-Farrāʾ and those reporting him ground the alternation in collecting what someone owes. Ibn Qutayba adduces Ṣakhr al-Ghayy's على أقطارها glossed من أقطارها. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī and others give the from-people gloss. Al-Samīn calls this analysis clearer; Abū Ḥayyān prefers attaching the phrase directly to اكتالوا. Al-Farrāʾ's two descriptions distinguish an amount owed from the act of receiving it; al-Samīn reports a reversed formulation. Al-Ālūsī notes objections that ordinary purchases need no prior debt, then suggests debts acquired in trade as the usual case. Al-Zarkashī cites 5:107 and 19:71 as further examples of على conveying a from-among or from sense, alongside measuring from people here. [25 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(صخر الغي); TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء والأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,المفسرون واللغويون); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)+; SAMIN-DURR+; ALUSI-FULL-; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(قيل)-]
- 83:2/q03/p2 على implies that taking from people burdens, harms, or dominates them. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn connect the choice of على over من with injury and oppression. Al-Bayḍāwī allows either a due owed or oppressive taking. Al-Biqāʿī sees superiority over customers and exploitation of anyone, especially the weak. Al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and al-Sāmarrāʾī describe domination or seizure; al-Ālūsī cites compacting and shaking the measure as means of harm. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise reads harm in على. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts neutral اكتال منه, which need not imply unfair taking, with اكتال عليه, which signals domination and makes على more forceful than من. [17 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; SAMARRAI-ASRAR+]
- 83:2/q03/p3 على attaches to يستوفون; its fronting emphasizes that they take fully from others in particular. — Al-Zamakhsharī proposes this attachment and fronting; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān transmit it. Al-Samīn calls it good. Al-Ālūsī reports a criticism that taking fully from oneself is an implausible contrast, and a defense that the contrast is harm to others rather than themselves; he remains unconvinced. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 83:2/q03/p4 على here means مع, 'with the people' or 'at the people.' — Ibn Qutayba explicitly glosses it مع الناس; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Ṭabarī's 'at' or 'with' gloss. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري)]
- 83:2/q03/p5 اكتال عليه expresses taking what is owed, whereas اكتال منه expresses receiving full measure; the constructions are distinct. — Al-Naḥḥās explicitly denies synonymy. Al-Farrāʾ as reported by several sources distinguishes taking what is due from receiving the full measure, although the reports differ in which expression receives which gloss. [6 notes: NAHHAS; FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء)]

## 83:2/q04 How can the verb take على if its ordinary complement is من?  [ٱكْتَالُوا۟ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar]
- 83:2/q04/p1 The verb carries an implied sense of imposing or bearing down on others, which licenses على. — Al-Zamakhsharī as quoted in al-Burhān supplies تحاملوا. Al-Burhān says a verb's preposition can follow an implied meaning; its examples construe الرفث as الإفضاء in 4:187 and drinking by a spring as being quenched by it in 76:6. Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr gives a broader account of تضمين as unstated meaning supplied by form or semantic necessity: معلوم entails an عالم, and ضارب a مضروب; he calls it concise expression and illustrates it with the basmala's implied instruction to begin acts in God's name for reverence or blessing. [6 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري,القاضي أبو بكر)]

## 83:2/q05 Why is measuring, but not weighing, named for what they take from others?  [ٱكْتَالُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether measurement stands for both trading modes, reflects opportunities for fraud, or describes the actual goods and practices of the first traders.
- 83:2/q05/p1 Mentioning measurement suffices to imply weighing in trade. — Al-Zajjāj says buying and selling use both measured and weighed goods, so either term can indicate the other; al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Ṭabrisī transmit or accept this. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the reasoning applies equally to omitting measure and is arbitrary. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); TABRISI; TABATABAI-]
- 83:2/q05/p2 Buying by measure offered more scope than buying by weight to take deliberate extra fullness. — Al-Biqāʿī says selling short is possible by either measure or weight, while acquiring extra fullness is easier by measure. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 83:2/q05/p3 The buyers usually purchased produce by measure and paid with weighed coin. — Ibn ʿĀshūr uses the different treatment of buying and selling to identify the fraud as a merchants' practice. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 83:2/q05/p4 Dealers commonly bought grain in bulk and resold smaller measured portions. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this proposed explanation and calls it weak. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]
- 83:2/q05/p5 The original cheaters bought only by measure but sold by both measure and weight. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this explanation and rejects it as unsupported. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 83:2/q06 What does يستوفون say they take?  [يَسْتَوْفُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether taking in full names exact receipt, whose blame arises from the contrasting shortfall, or also names taking excess through force and manipulation.
- 83:2/q06/p1 They insist on receiving the full amount due to themselves when buying. — Al-Zajjāj extends the demand to weight; al-Wāḥidī's commentators, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Baghawī's al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Jawzī's al-Zajjāj, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad contrast this with shorting others. Ibn ʿĀshūr parses يستوفون as the answer to إذا and says the added letters intensify taking the full amount. Al-Ālūsī reports the objection that full receipt is ordinarily proper, then answers that the unequal paired conduct makes it blameworthy. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 83:2/q06/p2 They take more than their right or use tricks to obtain an abundant measure. — Al-Māwardī says the cheater takes more than owed; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Zajjāj's extra-for-self gloss. Al-Biqāʿī says full or more than full, and al-Būrsawī lists packing, shaking and taking from rims or scale tongues; al-Ālūsī also mentions compaction and shaking. Al-Būrsawī explicitly denies that this is merely fair receipt of an exact right. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 83:2/q07 How is the next clause's pronoun read and connected with الناس?  [ٱلنَّاسِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the following pronoun is the object of a joined verb or begins a new predication; the written alif, idiomatic construction and antecedent are cited.
- 83:2/q07/p1 The joined reading makes the pronoun the object: they measure for those people and give them short measure. — Al-Jishumī says the common reading is transitive; he prefers it because the written form lacks an alif, Arabic permits 'I measured your due' for 'for you,' and readers unanimously use it. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Akhfash on dropping the preposition before the object, and Abū Zayd's poetic example. Al-Ṭabarī makes hum the object of the people shortchanged. Al-Burhān points to الناس as the pronoun's antecedent and the missing alif against the rival analysis. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this direct-object construction is Hijazi and Qurʾanic, while other Arabs use a preposition. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(عامة القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو زيد,الفراء والأخفش); TABATABAI; BURHAN+; JISHUMI+]
- 83:2/q07/p2 A pause after 'they measure' makes the following pronoun a subject: 'they are defrauders.' — Al-Jishumī attributes this reading to ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and Ḥamzah. He and al-Burhān oppose it on the written form's lack of alif; al-Burhān also invokes الناس as antecedent of the object pronoun. [1 notes: JISHUMI(عامة القراء); JISHUMI-; BURHAN-]

## 83:2/q08 What is the actual pattern of trade described by this verse and its next clause?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the notes state the general unequal transaction or report the specific device of two sets of measures and scales.
- 83:2/q08/p1 The traders require full measure or weight when receiving goods but give less when supplying others. — Al-Wāḥidī's commentators, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī's al-Zajjāj, al-Qurṭubī's al-Zajjāj, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad state the two-sided pattern. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reference to measure and weight on the selling side shows merchants' conduct. Al-Ālūsī says the combination explains why a normally lawful full receipt is censured. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 83:2/q08/p2 Some traders used two measures and two scales, changing them between buying and selling. — Muqātil reports this practice of a pre-Islamic market: buyers received extra while sellers delivered short. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 83:2/q09 What is the small shortfall called taṭfīf that the clause exemplifies?  [* · meaning]
- 83:2/q09/p1 Taṭfīf is reducing someone's proper measure or weight by a slight, often inconspicuous amount. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Jishumī and Abū Ḥayyān derive it from something tiny or scant. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Jishumī cite a vessel nearly but not entirely full, including a saying about Adam's descendants close to a full measure. Al-Jishumī notes explanations from the tiny missing amount or secret theft. Iṣlāḥī glosses giving short measure or weight. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:2/q10 What moral wrong and wider lesson does the trading contrast expose?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How far the unequal transaction is generalized: reciprocal commercial justice, all social rights, or evidence of moral nature and final judgment.
- 83:2/q10/p1 It exposes selfish inconsistency: demanding one's own rights while denying others equal rights. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats both verses as one violation of balanced reciprocal claims that corrupts society. Iṣlāḥī says the trader's protest when cheated reveals an innate recognition of justice, overridden by selfishness when he cheats others; he compares thieves, murderers and adulterers who would reject like harm to their own households. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls short measure a combination of injustice, theft and meanness, traits Arabs separately disdained. Al-Jishumī's general maxim denies superiority except by piety. [9 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:2/q10/p2 The warning extends to every practical and moral social relation involving rights and obligations. — Asad explicitly extends the measure metaphor beyond commerce to each person's rights, obligations and possessions; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls reciprocal rights the basis of a balanced society. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); TABATABAI]
- 83:2/q10/p3 Innate recognition of justice points to resurrection and divine judgment, which wrongdoers wish to evade. — Iṣlāḥī reasons that the human moral nature distinguishes just and unjust and thus expects different treatment from God. He says motives can overpower that nature without erasing knowledge of right and wrong, connects the reasoning to 75:14–15, and says the traders' conduct shows no expectation of standing before their Lord. [8 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:2/q11 What inward reading is given to the cheaters' taking of full measure?  [* · inward]
- 83:2/q11/p1 They cheat the true scale of justice by exaggerating their own intellectual and practical virtues before others. — Al-Qāshānī extends measurement to character and deeds and attributes their inflated self-assessment to vanity and pride. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 83:2/q12 What reports illustrate the warning about fraudulent measure?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct transmitted warnings and examples, with different speakers and consequences.
- 83:2/q12/p1 A Prophetic warning links short measure to drought among five offenses and corresponding calamities. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's account: broken covenants bring enemies, rejecting God's law poverty, immorality plague, short measure drought, and withholding alms loss of rain; he says al-Bazzār transmits its gist and Mālik also reports it from Ibn ʿUmar. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 83:2/q12/p2 ʿIkrima warned that every measurer and weigher faces the Fire because none gives exactly what he demands. — Al-Ṭabarī reports ʿIkrima's oath and reason; al-Qurṭubī adds that he did not exempt his son when asked. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 83:2/q12/p3 A trader with two measures saw them become mountains of fire at death. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Mālik ibn Dīnār's account of a dying neighbor who had used two measures, which struck together in his vision. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن دينار)]
- 83:2/q12/p4 ʿAlī required an accurate weight before any voluntary excess. — Al-Qurṭubī reports ʿAbd Khayr saying ʿAlī overturned a scale giving excess saffron and ordered exact weight first. He also transmits al-Aṣmaʿī's Bedouin saying that manliness is not in measure rims or scale tongues, also attributed to ʿAlī. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,عبد خير)]
- 83:2/q12/p5 Ibn ʿUmar warned that short-measurers will stand in sweat up to half their ears. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits this through Nāfiʿ. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(نافع)]
- 83:2/q12/p6 A recitation of this sura at dawn prompted a prayer against a trader who bought full but sold short. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Abū Hurayra's account of Medina's deputy reciting the sura and the ensuing prayer. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 83:2/q12/p7 ʿAbd Allāh said Medina's people should fulfill measures because God warned against fraud and judgment. — Al-Ṭabarī reports his reply after being told Medina's people fulfill measures, invoking 83:1 and 83:6. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 83:2/q13 What setting or occasion of revelation is given for the warning?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports locate the occasion among the Anṣār, a wider early Medinan audience, or earlier market practice; Iṣlāḥī questions the Anṣār attribution.
- 83:2/q13/p1 Dishonest measuring among the Anṣār in Medina occasioned the verse. — Iṣlāḥī reports this from exegetes but challenges it: he treats the sura as Meccan and finds dishonest trade at least as plausible among Meccan traders as among the agricultural Anṣār. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(exegetes); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 83:2/q13/p2 The opening warning addressed widespread short measure in early Medina, including its polytheists and those of Mecca. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage warns Muslims against the practice and condemns the polytheists of both cities. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 83:2/q13/p3 The fraud was practiced in a pre-Islamic market using two sets of measures and scales. — Muqātil reports buyers taking extra and sellers giving short by switching measures and scales. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 83:2/q14 Where and when was this opening revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The Medinan arrival traditions and contextual inference versus Iṣlāḥī's Meccan dating and criticism of the Anṣār report.
- 83:2/q14/p1 At least the first four verses were revealed shortly after the Prophet arrived in Medina. — Asad cites authentic traditions in al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening context points to Medina. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); TABATABAI]
- 83:2/q14/p2 The sura is Meccan. — Iṣlāḥī uses its Meccan setting to challenge the report assigning the occasion to dishonest measuring among the Anṣār; he notes Meccan traders were at least as likely to practice it. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:2/q15 How does this warning connect to its surrounding verses and to al-Infiṭār?  [* · links]
turns on: The immediate sequence develops the judgment warning, while the inter-sura account compares two ways of arguing for that judgment.
- 83:2/q15/p1 The contrast in verses 1–6 leads from dishonest measure to resurrection and standing for judgment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening warns of the Great Day before the sura describes wicked and righteous fates. Iṣlāḥī says the cheaters act as though they will not stand before their Lord; al-Ṭabarī's report of ʿAbd Allāh invokes 83:1 and 83:6 together. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TAB-FULL(عبد الله)]
- 83:2/q15/p2 The sura supplements al-Infiṭār by developing its division of righteous and defiant people through an argument from moral nature. — Iṣlāḥī says al-Infiṭār reasons from God's attributes in the human body, while this sura reasons from the human recognition of justice and evil; the dishonest trader who objects to being cheated makes that recognition visible. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
