# 2:14: 36 questions

## 2:14/q01 What does meeting mean here?  [لَقُوا۟ · meaning]
- 2:14/q01/p1 Encountering or seeing someone face to face, nearby. — Al-Layth says it is facing another; al-Khalil defines proximity and encounter; Ibn al-Sikkit lists liqa, liqyan, liqya, and laqa. Baydawi relates ilqa to putting something where it meets another; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin list fourteen verbal nouns; al-Harali adds reciprocal turning and says intimacy with hypocrites is rare. [12 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحراني عن ابن السكيت,الليث); JISHUMI(الخليل); TABRISI(الخليل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]

## 2:14/q02 How is the form لقوا derived and pronounced?  [لَقُوا۟ · grammar]
- 2:14/q02/p1 It contracts from لقيوا by lightening or dropping the yāʾ and resolving adjacent silent letters. — Al-Akhfash discusses deletion of plural wāw before the following lām; al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi, al-Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, and al-Samin give differing sequences of vowel transfer, yāʾ deletion, and temporary wāw vowel. Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi contrast لاقوا, whose preceding fatḥa allows retention. [10 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:14/q03 How is لقوا read?  [لَقُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted form and a same-sense variant differ in verbal stem and authority for recitation.
- 2:14/q03/p1 The transmitted reading is لقوا. — Al-Jishumi says recitation follows widespread transmission from the Prophet and Companions; a report in al-Thaʿlabi gives the same-sense laqu form. [2 notes: THALABI(محمد بن السميقع); JISHUMI+]
- 2:14/q03/p2 A variant reads لاقوا, with the same meeting sense. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi trace it to لاقيوا and assign it to Muhammad b. al-Sumayfa; Ibn Atiyya also cites him, while al-Razi and al-Nasafi attribute the form to Abu Hanifa; al-Alusi mentions both. [7 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن السميفع اليماني); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن السميقع اليماني); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 2:14/q04 What is the function of إذا and the meeting clause?  [وَإِذَا لَقُوا۟ · grammar]
- 2:14/q04/p1 إذا is a temporal conditional adverb, and قالوا is its response. — Al-Tabrisi says its answer governs it and notes spatial and surprise usages; Abu Hayyan contrasts temporal, spatial, and particle analyses; al-Samin makes قالوا its governor and parses الذين as the object and آمنا as quoted speech. Al-Nahhas discusses the relative clause and response. [5 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:14/q04/p2 إذا portrays their behavior as a repeated, enduring habit. — Al-Itqan compares the continuing force of the imperfect and cites 4:142. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 2:14/q05 Who are the believers they meet?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: The notes vary between the Companions generally and Abu Bakr's circle.
- 2:14/q05/p1 The Prophet's Companions, including the Emigrants and Helpers. — Muqatil calls them Companions affirming Muhammad; Bursevi specifies the Muhajirun and Ansar. [2 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI]
- 2:14/q05/p2 Abu Bakr and his companions. — Al-Thaʿlabi and al-Wahidi report this identification. [2 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون)]

## 2:14/q06 What did the hypocrites mean by آمنا?  [قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verb asserts legal status, inner sincerity, or an omitted object.
- 2:14/q06/p1 They falsely professed Islamic belief outwardly, including belief in Muhammad's revelation, without inward conviction. — Al-Tabari's Jewish reports specify profession before companions or the Prophet; al-Jishumi and Bursevi describe a protective show; Ibn Ashur says they spoke Islam's formula and favors legal-religious believer status without an omitted object; Abu Hayyan notes ambiguity between false inward faith and mere outward words. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,الضحاك عن ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:14/q06/p2 They claim sincere inward faith, beyond an already known verbal profession. — Al-Razi argues that this claim reverses the inward denial they admit to their leaders; al-Alusi rejects the special distinction as unsupported. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 2:14/q06/p3 The omitted object is what was previously specified: God and the Last Day, or whatever the believers believe. — Al-Alusi says a Moses-only construal is remote; Ibn Ashur prefers taking the verb without any omitted object. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 2:14/q07 Why did they profess faith to believers?  [قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا · interpretation]
turns on: The sources give protective, opportunistic, and derisive motives that can coexist.
- 2:14/q07/p1 To protect themselves and gain worldly benefits while hiding allegiance and obtaining information. — Al-Tabari, al-Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, and Abu Hayyan name protection of life, property, and family; al-Razi adds secrets, charity, and spoils; Bursevi adds marriage and intelligence; al-Alusi says they fended believers off. Ibn Atiyya notes kinship clouded evidence and the Prophet feared a public accusation if he killed them. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:14/q07/p2 To mock believers and Muhammad's Companions or ingratiate themselves with their leaders. — Al-Jishumi reports these two proposed motives from Abu Ali; the mockery may include flattery of those they consider foolish. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); ELMALILI]

## 2:14/q08 What does خلوا mean here?  [خَلَوْا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether khalaw denotes solitude, motion away, or mockery itself.
- 2:14/q08/p1 They were privately alone with their leaders. — Al-Thaʿlabi equates constructions with bi, ila and maʿa; al-Tabrisi gives the same sense; al-Raghib, cited by al-Alusi, roots khalw in an empty place, space, or time. [9 notes: THALABI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]
- 2:14/q08/p2 They went away, withdrew, or returned to their leaders. — Abu Malik says 'went away'; Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi cite al-Farazdaq's verse to explain the sense of turning or departure; al-Biqaʿi describes a private return. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(بعض الكوفيين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:14/q08/p3 They privately mocked or recounted their mockery to the leaders. — Al-Razi reports al-Kashshaf's account; al-Alusi rejects an explicit 'mocked' sense for khala as unattested. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:14/q09 Why is خلوا followed by إلى?  [خَلَوْا۟ إِلَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether إلى has its own directional force or stands in for مع or باء.
- 2:14/q09/p1 إلى keeps its endpoint sense: they turned or departed from believers toward their leaders. — Al-Tabari favors Kufan directionality because a preposition should retain its proper sense without proof; al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur explain movement, goal of need, or customary refuge. Ibn Ashur says this makes the Muslim encounter brief; al-Alusi notes an omitted first object. [16 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TAB-FULL(نحويي أهل الكوفة)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 2:14/q09/p2 إلى means مع, 'with,' through preposition substitution. — Al-Akhfash, al-Nadr, and others cite 3:52, 4:2, 2:187, 61:14, a proverb, and poetry; al-Tabari reports the analogy and other preposition shifts, with Yunus and a poet giving Arabic examples. Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, and al-Alusi call it weak or unsupported. [14 notes: AKHFASH(الشاعر,يونس); TAB-FULL(الشاعر); THALABI(النضر بن شميل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(النضر); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURSEVI; TAB-FULL(نحويي أهل الكوفة)-; WAHIDI-BASIT-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم)-; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النضر بن شميل)-; ALUSI-FULL(النضر بن شميل)-]
- 2:14/q09/p3 إلى substitutes for باء in 'being alone with.' — Ibn Atiyya says the view is weak and against al-Khalil and Sibawayh; al-Samin attributes permissive substitution to Kufans and Basran resistance; al-Alusi finds no proof. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم)-; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم)-; ALUSI-FULL(النضر بن شميل)-]

## 2:14/q10 Why choose إلى rather than باء with خلوا?  [خَلَوْا۟ إِلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 2:14/q10/p1 إلى clarifies a private approach or need; باء can also suggest mockery. — A Basran grammarian, al-Akhfash, Makkī, al-Jishumi, and Abu Hayyan describe the ambiguity; al-Mawardi calls the wording more eloquent and literal. Al-Alusi disputes that khalā bi explicitly means mocking. [8 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(نحويي البصرة); MAWARDI-FULL(بعض البصريين); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:14/q11 How is the hamza at خلوا إلى read?  [خَلَوْا۟ إِلَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Hamza realization and vowel transfer.
- 2:14/q11/p1 The hamza is fully pronounced and the preceding wāw remains still. — Al-Tabrisi assigns this to other readers; Abu Hayyan calls it the majority practice. [2 notes: TABRISI(الباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:14/q11/p2 The hamza is omitted and its vowel transferred to the wāw. — Al-Nahhas calls this a Medinan softening; al-Tabrisi calls it Hijazi, and Abu Hayyan attributes it to Warsh. [3 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI(أهل الحجاز); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:14/q12 How is خلوا formed?  [خَلَوْا۟ · grammar]
- 2:14/q12/p1 An earlier wāw becomes alif and is deleted before plural wāw. — Al-Samin derives خلووا and says the preceding fatḥa signals the deletion. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:14/q13 What does shayṭān mean generally?  [شَيَٰطِينِهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: The breadth of the word's application and the nature of jinn satan.
- 2:14/q13/p1 A rebellious, malicious being, human, jinn, or even animal. — Al-Thaʿlabi cites 37:65, two prophetic reports, and Abu al-Najm's playful verse; al-Wahidi cites 6:112; al-Jishumi reports Abu Ubayda; al-Qurtubi says distance from good encompasses the groups; Elmalili recounts Umar calling a prancing horse a satan; Ibn Ashur distinguishes literal fiery jinn from figurative human corrupters. Asad and Raghib add evil impulses of the soul. [18 notes: THALABI(أبو النجم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Raghib,Zamakhshari); STUDYQURAN]
- 2:14/q13/p2 A jinn satan also has an immaterial spiritual aspect and material evil forces. — Elmalili contrasts philosophers' solely immaterial account with Ahl al-Sunna's broader view. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 2:14/q14 From what root and pattern is shayṭān derived?  [شَيَٰطِينِهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: Root status of nūn, evidence from derived verbs and poetry, and possible borrowing.
- 2:14/q14/p1 From shatana, 'be far,' on fayʿāl with original nūn. — Al-Mawardi says far from good or far along evil's way; al-Layth, Abu Ali and Ibn Atiyya cite Sibawayh's tashayṭana and Umayya's shāṭin, while Ibn al-Jawzi adds al-Nabigha's shaṭūn. Basrans and Ibn Ashur give distance from mercy or Paradise. Ibn Ashur objects that tashayṭana may be formed from the noun itself and not settle the root. [21 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي,الليث); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أمية بن أبي الصلت,النابغة); BAYDAWI-FULL(سيبويه); NASAFI-FULL(سيبويه); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(البصريون); ELMALILI(Sîbeveyh); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية,البصريون,سيبويه); ASAD-NOTES; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)+]
- 2:14/q14/p2 From shāṭa, 'burn' or 'perish,' on faʿlān with added nūn. — Al-Mawardi prefers burning as naming Satan for an eventual state; al-Aʿsha's yushīṭ and the cited warrior poem attest perishing. The Kufans include failure or burning anger; Elmalili notes implications for declension; Ibn Ashur says derivation alone does not determine declension and cites tanwīn at 15:17. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(قوم); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL(سيبويه); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكوفيون); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكوفيون,سيبويه); MAWARDI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)-]
- 2:14/q14/p3 It may be a fixed loanword from an earlier language. — Ibn Ashur notes similar ancient terms and reported Persian sīṭān; Elmalili reports Persian div and a claimed development into Western dieu with opposite meaning. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:14/q15 Who are 'their devils' in this verse?  [شَيَٰطِينِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the described private allies are human leaders, other associates, soothsayers, jinn, or inner impulses.
- 2:14/q15/p1 Human chiefs or leaders in disbelief or hypocrisy. — Al-Suddi, Qatada, Ibn Masʿud and Ibn ʿAbbas identify leaders; al-Jishumi prefers this as the majority, natural in context, and excluding soothsayers. Al-Razi says senior leaders can corrupt the land unlike juniors; Baydawi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur distinguish senior and junior hypocrites. Elmalili says exegetes agree these are human satans and describes secret pacts; Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi say distance from faith can encompass named groups. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI(ابن عباس)+]
- 2:14/q15/p2 Jewish leaders who urged denial, particularly Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf and others. — Ibn Abbas traditions identify Jews urging opposition to the Messenger; al-Thaʿlabi and al-Jishumi give five names across Medina, Aslam, Juhayna, Asad and Syria, with name variants; Ibn Abi Hatim names Kaʿb and cites Abu al-ʿAliya, al-Suddi and al-Rabiʿ. Muqatil calls them Kaʿb and followers. Some treat them as soothsayers. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI]
- 2:14/q15/p3 Polytheists or companions among polytheists and hypocrites. — Qatada and al-Rabiʿ name polytheists; Mujahid includes fellow hypocrites, while other reports include disbelieving associates or brothers. [12 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:14/q15/p4 Soothsayers, sometimes named and linked to their jinn companions. — Al-Dahhak and others identify them as soothsayers; Bursevi lists five with differing names, and explains their supposed knowledge of secrets, cures, and divination by attendant satans. Al-Alusi says this explains the name, but al-Jishumi says context excludes them. [10 notes: THALABI; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمع من المفسرين); TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(جمع من المفسرين); BURSEVI(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); JISHUMI(ابن عباس)-]
- 2:14/q15/p5 Jinn satans themselves. — Reports from al-Kalbi and Ibn Abbas are cited; Ibn Atiyya finds it unlikely in context, al-Alusi implausible, and al-Jishumi prefers the human leaders. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI(ابن عباس)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الكلبي)-; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي)-]
- 2:14/q15/p6 Inner demons or a person's evil impulses. — Asad and Raghib allow Qur'anic shaytan for satanic impulses opposed to truth and morality; Study Quran lists inner demons as an interpretation here. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Raghib); STUDYQURAN]

## 2:14/q16 How is شياطين formed and inflected?  [شَيَٰطِينِهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: Broken plural inflection versus anomalous sound-plural treatment.
- 2:14/q16/p1 It is an inflected broken plural, with original nūn retained in annexation. — Al-Nahhas gives genitive case after إلى and a genitive suffix. Al-Samin offers faʿālīl or faʿālīn patterns; al-Alusi confirms broken plural. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:14/q16/p2 A rare anomalous plural treats it like a sound masculine plural: الشياطون. — Al-Samin compares the colloquial بساتون and cites 26:210; al-Alusi notes the anomalous reading. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:14/q17 What does إنا معكم assert?  [إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'with' denotes religion, aid, intelligence sharing, or active harm.
- 2:14/q17/p1 They share their leaders' religion and remain in their faction. — Ibn Hisham, Ibn Abbas, al-Thaʿlabi, Damghani, al-Tabrisi, al-Nasafi and others read continuing religious allegiance. Damghani cites analogous use in 11:58 and 67:28; al-Alusi specifies Judaism and calls it a spiritual alliance. [22 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:14/q17/p2 They offer support or assistance against Muhammad and believers. — Al-Thaʿlabi, al-Jishumi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Abu Hayyan offer support alongside or instead of shared religion. [4 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:14/q17/p3 They share intelligence about believers. — Abu Hayyan gives this as one of four interpretations. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:14/q17/p4 They join in harming Muslims and suppressing God's message. — Abu Hayyan lists this as another interpretation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:14/q18 How is إنا معكم grammatically constructed?  [إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical status of مع, especially when its ʿayn is still.
- 2:14/q18/p1 إنا contracts إننا by deleting one nūn; معكم is a locative or adverbial predicate of companionship. — Al-Nahhas says contraction avoids successive nūns and treats معكم as accusative through an implied state; al-Wahidi calls مع adverbial; al-Samin identifies the middle nūn as omitted, cites 3:93 and analyzes an omitted place predicate rather than time. Al-Tabrisi cites a poetic accusative parallel. [7 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:14/q18/p2 A silent ʿayn can make مع function as a preposition. — Al-Nahhas reports this analysis; al-Samin disputes it and says even the poetic silent form remains an adverb. Yunus and al-Akhfash treat detached معا as a maqsur noun; a poet's شعباكما معا allows predicate or circumstantial parsing. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(يونس والأخفش); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:14/q19 How is معكم read?  [مَعَكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Vocalization of the ʿayn.
- 2:14/q19/p1 The ʿayn is vowelled. — Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi call this the majority reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 2:14/q19/p2 The ʿayn is still, an anomalous tribal form. — Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi assign it to Ghanm and Rabiʿa; al-Samin limits the still form to poetry. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:14/q20 How is the plural mīm read near معكم إنما?  [مَعَكُمْ إِنَّمَا · readings]
turns on: Whether ṣila is made before the following hamza; other plural-mīm environments supply the comparative rule.
- 2:14/q20/p1 The plural mīm has ṣila before a following hamzat al-qaṭʿ in Warsh's route. — Ibn al-Jazari lists معكم إنما; al-Hudhali gives Ibn Jammaz a generally no-ṣila route except this environment. [2 notes: NASHR(الهذلي عن الهاشمي عن ابن جماز,ورش)]
- 2:14/q20/p2 The other readers keep the mīm still in this environment and all readers still it at pause. — Ibn al-Jazari also gives dialectal mīm and preceding hāʾ vowel rules elsewhere: Abu Amr kasras both; Nafiʿ, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Amir, Asim and Abu Jaʿfar damma mīm; Hamza, Kisai and Khalaf damma both; Yaʿqub harmonizes. Ruways has two pause options in يَغْنِهِمُ الله; readers agree on damma after a dammated preceding letter. [7 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو,حمزة والكسائي وخلف,رويس,نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وعاصم وأبو جعفر,ورش,يعقوب)]

## 2:14/q21 Why is their claim to believers verbal and their claim to leaders emphatic and nominal?  [قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا قَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: How audience knowledge, intended reassurance, and social benefit motivate the differing clause forms.
- 2:14/q21/p1 They feign a new act of belief before believers but affirm stable allegiance to leaders, whose doubts need reassurance. — Al-Razi, Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Harali, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur explain the contrast. Hypocrites lack sincere belief and cannot convincingly claim perfect faith before perceptive Companions; Ibn Ashur says overemphasis itself would arouse suspicion. Al-Alusi prefers this account; the leaders might suspect genuine conversion after their careful Muslim-facing show. Al-Harali stresses their fixed allegiance. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:14/q21/p2 They assert strongly to leaders because they sincerely wish to reassure them, while they care little about believers. — Al-Alusi reports this but finds it unpersuasive. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 2:14/q21/p3 They avoid claiming perfect faith before perceptive believers, who would reject it, but their leaders accept emphatic reassurance. — Al-Razi, Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and al-Zamakhshari develop this; al-Alusi reports and finds the stand-alone explanation unpersuasive. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 2:14/q21/p4 Emphasis claims eligibility for Muslims' legal treatment and protection from conflict. — Al-Alusi notes they had already sworn by God and the Last Day to deceive believers, citing 63:1 as context. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:14/q22 What does إنما do in the last statement?  [إِنَّمَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether restriction belongs to the particle itself or to contextual interpretation.
- 2:14/q22/p1 The added ما stops إن from governing, leaving نحن subject and مستهزئون predicate. — Al-Tabrisi gives this analysis with a nearby مصلحون example; Abu Hayyan agrees on suspended government and says restriction arises from context, not the particle alone. He rejects deriving restriction from affirmative إن plus negative ما and denies attested governing إنما, citing 13:7, 18:110, and 79:45. [4 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:14/q22/p2 إنما restricts the hypocrites' claim: they are only mocking, not sincere believers. — Ibn Ashur says this emphatically reverses their public performance and confirms loyalty. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:14/q23 How does the final clause connect to إنا معكم?  [إِنَّا مَعَكُمْ إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُسْتَهْزِءُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the last clause is a fresh answer, semantic reinforcement, or syntactic apposition.
- 2:14/q23/p1 It answers the leaders' implied objection: why appear friendly to believers? — Al-Razi, Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Elmalili describe this resumption. Al-Alusi says learned interpreters prefer it though innamā weakens the implied-question scenario; Ibn Ashur prefers it because it confirms allegiance while resolving puzzlement. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:14/q23/p2 It emphasizes or explains their enduring unbelief and allegiance. — Al-Razi and Baydawi propose emphasis, replacement, or a stronger substitute because belittling Islam magnifies unbelief; al-Nasafi says mockery treats Islam as invalid and confirms Judaism; Ibn Ashur says restriction reinforces allegiance. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:14/q23/p3 It is syntactically an accusative apposition or explanatory replacement for the first statement. — Al-Samin offers the apposition against an independent sentence; Ibn Ashur lists encompassing apposition and al-Alusi lists badal or necessary implication, but Ibn Ashur prefers the explanatory restart. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 2:14/q24 What does مستهزئون mean?  [مُسْتَهْزِءُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression is lexical ridicule, contextual denial, or an alternative etymological sense.
- 2:14/q24/p1 Mockers who ridicule, deride, belittle, or treat others contemptuously. — Ibn Hisham says 'mocking and playing'; al-Wahidi defines huzʾ as outward display unlike the inward state; al-Jishumi calls derision and jest synonymous and seriousness their opposite. Al-Tabrisi gives related verbal patterns; Ibn Atiyya cites Aws b. Hajar and Sibawayh-type forms. Al-Razi and Baydawi connect the root with lightness and rapid movement; al-Qurtubi cites a rajaz example; al-Ghazali lists mimicry, speech, gesture, and sign. Al-Alusi rejects al-Razi's definition as outward agreement with inward harm as linguistically overbroad. [15 notes: IBNHISHAM; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الغزالي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:14/q24/p2 Denying what one is summoned to believe. — Al-Qurtubi gives this as the sense in context, alongside the separately reported sense of scoffing. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:14/q24/p3 Taking vengeance. — Al-Qurtubi reports this further etymology and cites a poetic line. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قول)]

## 2:14/q25 What exactly are they mocking?  [إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ مُسْتَهْزِءُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate target is people or the faith they profess.
- 2:14/q25/p1 Muhammad and his Companions or believers, through a false display of faith. — Ibn Abbas, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi and Bursevi identify the targets. Al-Wahidi says the outer show differs from inward belief; al-Tabari's Ibn Jurayj report says hypocrites claim fellowship when believers prosper then mock privately. The declarers seek to assure leaders of continued loyalty. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:14/q25/p2 Islam itself, whose validity they reject while claiming loyalty to Judaism. — Al-Nasafi makes the object Islam and says ridicule confirms disbelief; al-Qurtubi says the term means denying the summons to belief. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:14/q26 How is the hamza of مستهزئون read or softened?  [مُسْتَهْزِءُونَ · readings]
turns on: The realization or deletion of hamza and the preceding vowel, especially at pause.
- 2:14/q26/p1 The hamza is pronounced or softened to an intermediate sound following Sibawayh. — Al-Nahhas and al-Samin describe a sound between hamza and wāw; Ibn Atiyya notes ḍamma over wāw and Abu Ali's report of softening; al-Tabrisi explains relevant vowel environments, and Abu Hayyan says most readers follow Sibawayh. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); TABRISI(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه)]
- 2:14/q26/p2 The hamza becomes a full yāʾ: مستهزيون. — Al-Akhfash argues from preceding kasra and analogy with السفهاء; Ibn Jinni says the ḍamma remembers the original hamza. Ibn Yazid rejects the analogy because السفهاء requires a wāw but this form does not. Al-Tabrisi says al-Akhfash rejects intermediate sound as unparalleled. [6 notes: NAHHAS(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأخفش); TABRISI(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد)-]
- 2:14/q26/p3 The hamza is omitted, yielding مستهزون with a raised zāy. — Al-Nahhas says the replaced-hamza form matches the codex; Abu Jaʿfar deletes dammated hamza after kasra before wāw. Hamza's pause form is reported by al-Samin and al-Nashr; Ibn al-Jazari defends it as strong against al-Sakhawi and al-Fasi, distinguishing weak deletion that retains kasra. Qiraat-ER calls omission streamlined and common; al-Tabrisi mentions unspecified readers. [9 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI(بعض القراء); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASHR(أبو جعفر,حمزة); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; NASHR+]
- 2:14/q26/p4 Deletion with retained kasra before wāw is weak. — Ibn al-Jazari contrasts this with Hamza's sound pause performance. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 2:14/q27 What other hamza substitution rules are adduced with this reading?  [مُسْتَهْزِءُونَ · readings]
- 2:14/q27/p1 Hamza may be transferred or replaced according to several route-specific patterns. — Al-Nashr details al-Asbahani/Warsh variants in فبأي, disputed بأيكم المفتون, Abu al-Ala's شانيك, al-Hudhali's لنبوينهم, al-Azraq's لئلا, Ibn Wardan's المنشئون, and disputed scope for نبيوني and related forms. Ibn Mihran omits al-Asbahani's replacement. The treatise also lists transfer, uniform ḍamma, and selective transfer before final hamza, al-Ala's extra alif, Ibn Malik's unattested recitational pattern, and Kufan analogies; al-Farra, Abu Zayd and al-Zajjaj cite yāʾ-based verb forms including يستهزون. [16 notes: NASHR(أبو العلاء,أبو العلاء الحافظ,ابن مالك,ابن مهران والأزرق عن ورش,ابن وردان,الأصبهاني عن ورش,الحمامي وصاحب المبهج,الزجاج,الفراء وأبو زيد,الكوفيون,الهذلي,بعض النحاة)]

## 2:14/q28 Who spoke the professions in the reported incident?  [قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا · referent]
turns on: Whether the speakers are Ibn Ubayy's circle, Jewish hypocrites, or hypocrites generally.
- 2:14/q28/p1 Ibn Ubayy spoke for his companions. — Elmalili says the earlier singular wording of the incident supports this representation. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 2:14/q28/p2 Jewish hypocrites, including People of the Book, were among the speakers. — Al-Tabari transmits reports of Jews who professed belief and then consulted privately, including Jews who first believed and became hypocrites; al-Hasan says hypocrites and some People of the Book; al-Durr reports Jewish hypocrites professing the companions' religion. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,الضحاك عن ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:14/q28/p3 Hypocrites generally are the speakers. — Al-Qurtubi identifies the verse's subjects simply as hypocrites; Abu Hayyan reports a general occasion view. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون)]

## 2:14/q29 What occasion of revelation or incident is reported for this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Competing incident reports and al-Alusi's assessment of their chains.
- 2:14/q29/p1 Ibn Ubayy praised Abu Bakr, Umar and Ali publicly, then urged his companions to imitate his deceit; Muslims informed the Prophet and the verse descended. — Ibn Abbas reports the scene through al-Thaʿlabi, al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Durr; Baydawi, Bursevi, Elmalili and Study Quran retell it. - 2:14/q29/p2 Jewish men professed the Companions' religion, then reassured their own leaders of continued loyalty and mockery. — Ibn Abbas reports this through al-Tabari and al-Durr; another report makes them hypocrites among the People of the Book. - 2:14/q29/p4 God described a people before they existed, revealing unseen knowledge. — Abu Hayyan reports this third account. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون)]

## 2:14/q30 What other people and incidents illustrate the hypocrites' circle?  [* · reports]
- 2:14/q30/p1 Ibn Ubayy headed a circle including named associates; Qazman and al-Jadd b. Wadiʿa, Malik, Suwayd and Daʿis are associated with his group and 59:11. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:14/q31 What legal weight should an outward profession of Islam have when hypocrisy is suspected?  [قَالُوٓا۟ ءَامَنَّا · law]
turns on: Whether concealed disbelief can be proved and punished despite an outward Islamic profession.
- 2:14/q31/p1 A judge treats the outward profession as Islam and does not execute on concealed disbelief or private knowledge. — The Maliki account says the Prophet's restraint shows a judge cannot rule from private knowledge, with 33:60–61 applying to open hypocrisy; Qatada gives the same threshold. Al-Shafiʿi, Ahl al-Raʾy and al-Tabari spare an accused zindiq who denies the charge and professes Islam. Shafiʿi's school says outward Islam erases prior wrongs, citing 63:1; Hanbali and hadith authorities cite a prophetic report requiring testimony and prayer and the hope that descendants would believe. Al-Tabari says God alone judges hidden states. Ibn Atiyya also says the Prophet avoided seeming to kill his companions. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر الطبري,أصحاب مالك,أهل الحديث,ابن حنبل,الشافعي,الشافعي وأبو حنيفة وابن حنبل وأهل الحديث,الشافعي وأصحابه,القاضي أبو محمد,قتادة)]
- 2:14/q31/p2 A present-day zindiq may be executed on testimony without an offer of repentance. — Malik equates him with the Prophet's hypocrite; Ismaʿil al-Qadi says two witnesses would have led to executions in named cases. Ibn Atiyya counters that their reports implied rather than explicitly proved disbelief; the Malikis answer that 63:1 accused hypocrites generally but not individuals. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(إسماعيل القاضي,القاضي أبو محمد,مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد عبد الحق)-]

## 2:14/q32 What does this conduct teach about hypocrisy and company?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:14/q32/p1 It condemns religious hypocrisy, ostentation, mockery of truth, and allegiance to unbelief. — Al-Jishumi states these offenses; Elmalili says contemptuous mockery of faith entails unbelief and that the verse compresses hypocrites' psychology. Ibn Hisham compares secret confidants apart from God at 9:16 with their hidden allegiance. - 2:14/q35/p1 God repays their mockery by exposing or turning their deception against them. — Al-Biqaʿi says they receive outward Muslim treatment while tasting humiliation in both worlds; al-Harali says the imperfect suggests continuing recompense with room for repentance. ## 2:14/q36 What adjacent recitation and parsing discussions accompany this verse?  [* · readings]
