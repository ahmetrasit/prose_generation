# 95:6: 19 questions

## 95:6/q01 Who are those excepted?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the general phrase is retained or narrowed by reported reciter and allegorical interpretations.
- 95:6/q01/p1 Believers who do righteous deeds generally. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and Ibn al-Jawzī take the singular 'human' collectively; al-Biqāʿī calls the righteous few; al-Islāḥī says they honor innate guidance and accept the messengers. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Ālūsī object to narrowing the apparent general wording to reciters. [10 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعض أهل التأويل); TAB-FULL(آخرون); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 95:6/q01/p2 The exception specifically names Qurʾān reciters. — ʿIkrima and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās say recitation averts the senility in which one knows nothing after knowledge; al-Ālūsī says al-Ḥākim authenticated the Ibn ʿAbbās report and al-Bayhaqī included it in Shuʿab. Al-Ālūsī disputes both the restriction and the claim that no reciter becomes senile. [19 notes: TAB(عكرمة); TAB-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عباس,عكرمة); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(الحبر,عكرمة); ALUSI-]
- 95:6/q01/p3 The exception concerns a believer who knows and acts on knowledge. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū al-Layth say such a person retains reason and avoids senility; this is a narrower proposed referent. [3 notes: QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو الليث)]
- 95:6/q01/p4 The exception allegorically names the four caliphs. — An explicitly weak Ibn ʿAbbās report makes 'human' Muḥammad and the lowest the idolaters, alongside symbolic identifications of the oath's places. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 95:6/q02 From what fate are believers excepted?  [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The meaning of the preceding descent and whether the exception removes believers from that descent or from a consequence of it.
- 95:6/q02/p1 From Hell, misery, punishment, or spiritual degradation. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ḥasan compare the believers excepted from loss in 103:2–3; Mujāhid glosses the exception as whoever believes. Al-Qushayrī also describes misery and disbelief. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues the connected exception makes bodily aging unlikely, since all humans age. Al-Qutb and al-Islāḥī explain the fall as corruption of fitra; Study Quran finds inner spiritual fall more likely. Abū al-ʿĀliya calls Hell the worst form. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB(آخرون,الحسن,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وأبو العالية ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA(الحسن ومجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد وأبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو العالية وابن زيد وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو العالية وابن زيد وقتادة); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 95:6/q02/p2 From decrepit old age or loss of reason itself. — Reports from ʿIkrima and Ibn ʿAbbās specify Qurʾān reciters; al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī state believers avoid senility even in long life. Al-Biqāʿī says reason's light increases while desire fades. Al-Ālūsī doubts that all reciters avoid decrepitude. [26 notes: TAB(عكرمة); TAB-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(عكرمة,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عباس,عكرمة); BURSEVI(أبو الليث,ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(الحبر,عكرمة); ALUSI-]
- 95:6/q02/p3 From losing reward when old age impairs reason and work, although believers can still grow old. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this: prior righteousness is still credited in senility; his other report supplies an implied result after the physical decline. Al-Naḥḥās supplies omitted words about lost ability to worship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the bodily-aging reading because the exception seems connected yet aging applies to everyone. [28 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TUSTARI; TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(مفسرون); TAB-FULL+; TAB+; TABATABAI-]
- 95:6/q02/p4 From an ugly or low state at resurrection, with added beauty and radiance instead. — Al-Ālūsī links the collective pronoun to humankind and sets the contrast specifically at resurrection. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 95:6/q02/p5 From loss of the original fitra after idolatry, by returning through faith to the best form. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says shirk had obscured innate monotheism, while faith restores it and deeds deepen it; al-Biqāʿī and al-Qutb describe reason, divine guidance, and good works preserving or perfecting original nature. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 91:7–8, 35:10, 22:37, 58:11 and 20:75 for ascent through faith and deeds. [17 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(الأقليشي); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(في ظلال القرآن); STUDYQURAN]

## 95:6/q03 How is the exception construed grammatically?  [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · grammar]
turns on: What the preceding 'human' and descent denote, and whether exception removes believers from the intended ruling; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds a syntactic argument from fāʾ.
- 95:6/q03/p1 Connected to humankind when the prior descent means Hell or a fallen state. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and Ibn al-Jawzī explain that singular 'human' denotes the species; al-Ṭabarī allows plural exception and plural construal of the superlative. Al-Naḥḥās instead relates it to the plural sense of 'We returned him.' Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī take the exception as connected; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ excludes detachment. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB(آخرون,بعض أهل التأويل); TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو العالية وابن زيد وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد وأبو العالية وابن زيد وقتادة); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 95:6/q03/p2 Disconnected when the prior descent means senility: 'but' righteous elderly receive reward. — Believers also age, so the clause shifts to their enduring recompense; al-Ṭabarī explicitly permits this. Al-Ālūsī says intended exclusion from the ruling, rather than literal membership, decides connection, and the clause answers an inference of equal outcomes from equal aging. Ibn ʿĀshūr denies this parsing because of the fāʾ. [20 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض المحققين); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); TABATABAI(unnamed view); IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 95:6/q03/p3 Connected even with senility, by excepting believers from loss of understanding or from an implied failure of deeds. — Some early exegetes treat the collective singular as its antecedent; another group supplies an omitted consequence: minds and deeds fail, but former reward remains. Al-Naḥḥās reports this ellipsis. [6 notes: TAB(آخرون,بعض أهل التأويل); TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS]
- 95:6/q03/p4 Disconnected when 'human' is restricted to an unbeliever. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a view that makes the preceding human an unbeliever and the return Hell or bodily reversal; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a disconnected exception from unbelievers cast into Hell. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(الحسن ومجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد وأبو العالية); TABATABAI(unnamed view)]

## 95:6/q04 What does faith do for the people in the exception?  [ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 95:6/q04/p1 Faith in God and His messengers preserves or restores the original human disposition and directs the person upward. — Al-Biqāʿī says divine gentleness lets reason and fitra overcome desire and believers remain firm as in 41:30; al-Qutb calls faith a cord and light linking nature to its Creator; Ibn ʿĀshūr says faith returns idolaters to innate monotheism and is the fruit of rational constitution; al-Islāḥī includes accepting messengers and accountability. [18 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(الأقليشي); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(في ظلال القرآن)]

## 95:6/q05 What is the role of righteous deeds alongside faith?  [وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the deeds phrase states a condition or praises believers by describing faith's usual outcome.
- 95:6/q05/p1 They manifest and perfect faith, preserve fitra, and bring further virtue. — Al-Biqāʿī calls deeds beautiful acts and words, shaped by knowledge and the prophetic path into justice and beneficence; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the law makes them deepen character and cites the hadith about perfecting noble character. Al-Qutb says faith and deeds complete nature. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Qurʾānic ascent parallels. [13 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 95:6/q05/p2 Their mention praises believers descriptively, without restricting which believers are excepted. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says faith motivates action and the expression describes believers as they were at revelation. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 95:6/q05/p3 Faith and righteous deeds are the stated condition for escaping the low fate and earning reward. — Al-Islāḥī says the oaths establish this moral rule; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares al-ʿAṣr's added mutual counsel, which affects additional reward rather than this basic exemption. Al-Elmali says the deeds accord with God's pleasure. [8 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(التبيان)]

## 95:6/q06 Why is fāʾ used in 'fa-lahum,' unlike the parallel reward clause in al-Inshiqāq?  [فَلَهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fāʾ is licensed by a conditional or causal reading, serves the sura's rhetoric, or constrains the exception's syntax.
- 95:6/q06/p1 It joins two permissible Arabic constructions or follows a conditional sense in the exception. — Al-Nasafī says the verse combines two accepted usages despite 84:25 omitting fāʾ; al-Bursawī says the sense of 'but' can carry conditional force, or fāʾ is causal when Paradise rather than punishment is the outcome. [3 notes: NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 95:6/q06/p2 It suits al-Tīn's context and its emphasis on assured, continuing reward. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-Inshiqāq details disbelievers and briefly mentions believers, while al-Tīn does not expressly identify the fallen; al-Inshiqāq opens with human toil, whereas al-Tīn honors humanity. Its fāʾ connects the reward naturally. Al-Bayḍāwī calls the clause a confirmation of the exception's result. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 95:6/q06/p3 The fāʾ rules out a disconnected exception. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes the particle a grammatical argument against treating believers as an unrelated new subject. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 95:6/q07 What reward is promised to believers in the verse?  [فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reward is viewed as continued credit for former works, recompense for patient endurance, or the lasting final recompense; these can coexist in the notes.
- 95:6/q07/p1 Former good deeds keep being credited when old age, illness, or travel prevents their performance. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibrāhīm, Qatāda and ʿIkrima describe continued credit from youth and health; Ibrāhīm and ʿIkrima specify the best former deeds. Ibn Qutayba says God knows they would continue if strong. Prophetic reports apply the rule to illness and travel; the recording angels continue good deeds. Al-Biqāʿī adds sincere resolve and divine grace. - 95:6/q07/p2 Righteous elderly people earn continuing reward for obedience, patience in frailty, and worship despite weakness. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Bursawī stress patient response to the trial itself; Abū Ḥayyān calls al-Zamakhsharī's elaboration excessive. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(غير واحد); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 95:6/q07/p3 They receive lasting honor, salvation, or Paradise, in this life and especially the next. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says physical decline does not diminish afterlife reward; al-Qāshānī pictures gardens of hearts and souls, supplied by the holy world; al-Bursawī locates it in the abode of honor; al-Elmali describes absolute beauty and a good final state; Study Quran specifies Heaven's bounties. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 95:6/q07/p4 The reward reflects deeds and sincere effort but originates and continues by God's grace. — Al-Biqāʿī distinguishes reward's relation to action from God's favor in both founding and extending it; an elderly person's unfulfilled intention preserves the habitual credit. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 95:6/q08 How are sins and accountability treated when a believer loses reason in old age?  [فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · interpretation]
- 95:6/q08/p1 Sins or acts after loss of reason are not recorded or charged, while earlier good deeds continue. — Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿIkrima ground exemption in loss of understanding; al-Ṭabrisī qualifies the Anas report with 'if authentic.' Anas's report also says children's good deeds benefit parents before puberty and sins are unrecorded. Other reports describe the left-hand angel ceasing to record sins. Al-Māwardī transmits Ibn Masʿūd's broad wording that what one does in old age does not harm him. [27 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); TAB(ابن عباس,عكرمة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عكرمة); THALABI(النبي); MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(النبي); IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرسول)]

## 95:6/q09 What occasion is reported for the assurance to elderly believers?  [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · reports]
- 95:6/q09/p1 Some people in the Prophet's time became senile; inquiry about their lost understanding elicited an assurance that God excuses them and preserves their earlier reward. — The reports from Ibn ʿAbbās emphasize diminished minds and the reward earned before decline; al-Durr's version also identifies other oath locations in the sūra. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 95:6/q10 What further reports illustrate reward continuing despite incapacity?  [فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · reports]
turns on: The reports address distinct circumstances and stages; the notes give no explicit contest among them.
- 95:6/q10/p1 The Prophet said habitual good deeds continue to be recorded during illness or travel. — Abū Mūsā's version explicitly recites this verse; other versions state the same rule for a formerly healthy, resident servant. Al-Bursawī applies it to aging, and Study Quran notes permitted shortened prayer or broken fast during travel and illness. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(النبي); QURTUBI(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو موسى عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(رسول الله); ALUSI(أبو موسى); ALUSI-FULL(أبو موسى); STUDYQURAN(Āl and Q)]
- 95:6/q10/p2 Reports say angels continue recording the best habitual good deeds during illness, weakness or affliction and may withhold sins. — The Prophet's report to the angels runs until death; a report through Shaddād adds that a believer who praises God during trial rises cleansed of sin. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(النبي); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(شداد بن أوس); ALUSI-FULL(شداد بن أوس); THALABI(النبي)]
- 95:6/q10/p3 A prophetic report credits an aged believer with the best deeds done in strength. — Abū Mūsā's report links this to the passage; Abū Ḥayyān also cites an unnamed hadith about a centenarian and another about extreme age. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes an analogous long ʿIkrima report is not raised to the Prophet. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرسول); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 95:6/q10/p4 A report lists successive divine favors at ages fifty through one hundred. — Anas reports eased reckoning at fifty, repentance at sixty, heavenly love at seventy, good recording and overlooked sins at eighty, forgiveness and intercession at ninety, and lifelong practice credited at one hundred. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي)]
- 95:6/q10/p5 After a believer dies, angels worship at the grave until resurrection and their worship is credited to him. — Al-Qurṭubī reports God's command to two angels; al-Bursawī quotes a version from Abū al-Layth in which angels ask to worship in heaven and are sent to the grave. [3 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو الليث)]
- 95:6/q10/p6 A long life accompanied by good deeds is blessed. — Ibn ʿUmar attributes the saying to the Prophet; al-Bursawī cites the same maxim. [3 notes: QURTUBI(ابن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI]

## 95:6/q11 What does 'ghayr mamnūn' mean?  [غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: The possible senses of the root mann: weakness or withholding, cutting, counting, or conferring a favor; some sources intend several senses together.
- 95:6/q11/p1 A reward that is not reduced, diminished, weakened, or withheld. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives 'not reduced'; al-Ṭabarī prefers it as matching reward in youth, deriving it from manīn ('weak') and citing poetry about a gift without deficiency or flaw. Al-Māwardī cites a poem. Corpus Coranicum prefers 'not withheld' over Paret's favor sense and cites Labīd; its note mentions parallels at 84:25 and 68:3. [26 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA(مجاهد وغيره); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(مفسرون); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R); CORPUSCORANICUM+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة)+]
- 95:6/q11/p2 A reward that is never cut off or interrupted. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a weakened or broken cord; al-Jishumī derives mann as cutting, as in names for death because it cuts life; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites severing rope. Al-Islāḥī prefers the unending sense and rejects another interpretation on Arabic grammatical grounds. Other notes place duration in this life and the next, or describe Paradise's permanence. ## 95:6/q12 Why use the wording 'ghayr mamnūn'?  [غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the notes explain the phrase's range of senses or its use as a repeated formula.
- 95:6/q12/p1 It deliberately carries both lasting reward and reward free of reproach. — Al-Rāzī says both properties befit divine recompense; al-Sāmarrāʾī says a simpler 'not cut off' expression would lose the second sense. His account of verse endings says this sūra similarly gives multiple senses to al-amīn, asfal sāfilīn, dīn, and aḥkam al-ḥākimīn. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 95:6/q12/p2 It is a recurring Qurʾānic reward formula. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists al-Qalam, Fuṣṣilat, al-Inshiqāq and al-Tīn and counts sixteen occurrences of the root mann; she also compares rayb al-manūn at 52:30. Corpus Coranicum notes parallels at 84:25 and 68:3; Ibn al-Jawzī points back to 68:3 for the gloss. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; CORPUSCORANICUM(Paret)]

## 95:6/q13 Why is 'ajr' indefinite, and what does the reward clause add?  [أَجْرٌ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clause is treated as an added promise or an explicit confirmation of the exception's result.
- 95:6/q13/p1 Its indefinite form magnifies the reward, which adds a further favor beyond rescue from the low state. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes being excluded from degradation from receiving an additional immense reward. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 95:6/q13/p2 The reward clause confirms the consequence of the exception. — Al-Bayḍāwī says this when the phrase means unending reward; al-Ālūsī says it clarifies believers' state and counters the inference that equal bodily aging means equal outcomes. [4 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 95:6/q14 How does the exception compare with the one in Sūrat al-ʿAṣr?  [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · links]
turns on: The force of al-ʿAṣr's added requirement of mutual exhortation.
- 95:6/q14/p1 Both except believers from a collective human loss or fall. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and al-Ḥasan use 103:2–3 to justify excepting plural believers from the generic singular human here; Study Quran also compares the two sūras' account of human nature and fall. [7 notes: FARRA; TAB(الحسن,بعض أهل التأويل); TAB-FULL(آخرون,الحسن); STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 95:6/q14/p2 Al-Tīn requires faith and good works to avoid the lowest state; al-ʿAṣr adds mutual exhortation to avoid further loss of possible reward. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī and al-Tibyān distinguish personal duties from counseling others; enjoining right may be individual, communal, or recommended, and counsel covers required and recommended truth and patience. Omitting extra counsel loses additional reward without necessarily causing total ruin. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(التبيان)]

## 95:6/q15 How does this verse fit the sūra's depiction of humanity and its oaths?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize the oath's proof, the verse's implied symmetry, or the sura's overall religious message.
- 95:6/q15/p1 The oaths and best-form/lowest-state contrast establish that faith and deeds preserve humanity's sound nature and earn reward, while rejection brings descent. — Al-Islāḥī says honoring innate guidance and messengers determines the two outcomes; al-Sāmarrāʾī says prophetic revelation offers a way of life suited to human nature, and compares the oath's revelation sites to the human beginning and end. Al-Elmali calls this the sūra's moral rule and the law of accountability. [11 notes: ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 95:6/q15/p2 The passage uses implied counterparts: evil deeds and punishment before the exception, preserved original fitra within it. — Al-Biqāʿī calls the pattern iḥtibāk and links it to the initial best form and the fate of those led downward by desire. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 95:6/q15/p3 The exception expresses the sūra's moral and religious purpose: Islamic foundations and deeds embody primordial nature; rejection corrupts it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sūra praises followers' reward and warns rejecters; Asad calls its moral truth shared by true religious teachings. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 95:6/q16 How is the verse linked to neighboring sūras and their histories?  [* · links]
- 95:6/q16/p1 It follows al-Sharḥ's call to turn to God and prepares for al-ʿAlaq's command to read. — Al-Elmali says this sūra summarizes preceding sūras and establishes creation, religion, reward and judgment, making the next opening fitting; al-Ālūsī says the preceding sūra described Muḥammad as exemplary and this one turns to humanity's end and reward. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 95:6/q16/p2 The oath's places and the human outcomes recall Torah narratives of revelation, guidance, and judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī relates Sinai, Seir, and Paran to the Torah's account and its beginnings, laws and history. Farāhī says Sinai's deliverance of oppressed Israel illustrates reward and punishment; al-Islāḥī says Abraham's promise excludes idolatrous descendants, citing 2:124. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Imām Farāhī)]

## 95:6/q17 What inward meaning is given to believers' reward?  [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍۢ · inward]
- 95:6/q17/p1 Heart-light overcomes ego-darkness; knowledge and practical virtues lift believers to the holy world and its incorruptible gardens of hearts and selves. — Al-Qāshānī describes the reward's provision as coming from the holy world and its duration as free from corruption. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 95:6/q18 What is reported about the sūra's revelation and the formula's period?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Different dating approaches in the notes address the whole sūra and the formula's literary distribution.
- 95:6/q18/p1 The sūra is predominantly regarded as Meccan, with Medinan attributions, and was revealed after Sūra 85. — Al-Ālūsī says Qatāda and a report from Ibn ʿAbbās favor Medina, while Mecca as the secure city supports the majority; all agree on eight verses, and the title may omit the conjunction. Asad and Study Quran place it after 85, with Study Quran calling it early-middle Meccan. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 95:6/q18/p2 The believers-and-good-works formula is characteristic of late Meccan and Medinan texts. — Corpus Coranicum directs the reader to its literary discussion of Sūra 103. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
