# 18:28: 27 questions

## 18:28/q01 What occasion prompted the command to remain with the worshippers?  [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over Meccan chiefs, later influential converts, and the People of the Bench.
- 18:28/q01/p1 Quraysh leaders demanded that poor believers be dismissed before they would listen or sit with the Prophet. — Accounts list Bilal, Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, Salman and Ibn Masud, and mention shabby or smelly clothes and separate meeting times. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan prefer this occasion because the sura is Meccan; Ibn Kathir cites Sad's six companions and Muslim's transmission. Kashshaf compares Noah's opponents (26:111). [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,خباب); ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سلمان)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:28/q01/p2 Uyayna and al-Aqra, with other influential converts, asked for the poor wool-clad believers to be moved so they could sit near the Prophet. — Salman's report names the pair and poor Muslims including Salman and Abu Dharr. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say this implies a Medinan setting; Alusi thinks most reports support it. Some reports say the chiefs were not yet Muslim. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(خباب,سلمان الفارسي); WAHIDI-ASBAB(سلمان الفارسي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سلمان); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(سلمان الفارسي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سلمان); DURR-FULL(سلمان); ALUSI-FULL(سلمان); TABATABAI(سلمان); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 18:28/q01/p3 Uyayna alone requested separation, complaining of Salman's smell and promising elite acceptance. — Muqatil lists Bilal, Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, Amir ibn Fuhayra, Mihja, Ayman, Abu Hurayra and Ibn Masud, and calls Mihja Badr's first martyr. Ibn Jurayj says Uyayna was then unconverted. Tabatabai objects to Qummi's Medinan variant. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(عيينة بن حصن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن بريدة,ابن جريج,سلمان); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI-]
- 18:28/q01/p4 The verse concerned seven hundred poor People of the Bench who stayed in the mosque praying and waiting for prayer. — Qatada says they had neither trade nor farmland. [3 notes: THALABI(قتادة); JISHUMI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 18:28/q01/p5 Khabbab reports that the Prophet had left poor believers when he stood, before this instruction came. — Al-Suyuti transmits Khabbab's account of the sequence. [1 notes: SUYUTI-LUBAB(خباب)]

## 18:28/q02 Was the verse revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ · sciences]
turns on: Which occasion report fits the sura's context.
- 18:28/q02/p1 It was revealed in Mecca in response to Quraysh. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan favor this from the sura's Meccan setting; Tabatabai says Medinan reports conflict with the passage's context. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سلمان); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:28/q02/p2 It was a Medinan exception to the Meccan sura. — Qatada places revelation in Medina; Ibn Abbas and Qatada identify an exception. Alusi argues most reports and the following verses favor Medina. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سلمان)-; TABATABAI-]

## 18:28/q03 How did the Prophet respond to the command?  [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe distinct encounters.
- 18:28/q03/p1 He found poor worshippers remembering God, sat with them, and praised God for commanding their company in life and death. — Reports vary on whether he found them behind the mosque or in rough clothing, and whether he was first at home. Ibn Kathir notes Tabarani's transmission and Abd al-Rahman ibn Sahl's Companion status. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله); ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن سهل بن حنيف,قتادة); THALABI(رسول الله); WAHIDI-ASBAB(النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سلمان الفارسي); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن سهل بن حنيف); DURR-FULL(سلمان,عبد الرحمن بن سهل بن حنيف); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 18:28/q03/p2 He identified a Quran recitation or reminder gathering as the assembly he had been commanded to stay with. — Reports name a man reciting al-Kahf or al-Hijr, or Abdullah ibn Rawaha reminding companions. Ibn Kathir notes connected and disconnected routes. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة وأبو سعيد,عمر بن ذر,مجاهد)]
- 18:28/q03/p3 He urged companions to continue remembrance when they stopped at his arrival, wishing to share the descending mercy. — Thabit transmits this account. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ثابت)]
- 18:28/q03/p4 He turned from inattentive Umayya and found Salman remembering God. — Al-Rabi says Umayya was distracted while the Prophet spoke. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(الربيع); SUYUTI-LUBAB(الربيع)]

## 18:28/q04 What does 'restrain yourself with them' mean?  [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether read as a public command, inward account, or implication for others.
- 18:28/q04/p1 Remain steadfastly in the believers' company and keep oneself from leaving them. — Sabr's root sense is restraint: Ibn Atiyya, Razi and Abu Hayyan cite the prohibition on tying an animal as a target; Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Samin cite Abu Dhuayb's poetry. Ibn Ashur says ma'a conveys company; Alusi distinguishes literal restraint from extended steadfastness. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو ذؤيب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو ذؤيب); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:28/q04/p2 Inwardly, patience is stable presence through God without attention turned elsewhere. — Ibn Ata describes patient self, heart and spirit in divine presence; Qashani's stripped poor seek only God. [6 notes: SULAMI(إبراهيم بن شيبان,ابن عطاء); QASHANI; BURSEVI(ذو النون)]
- 18:28/q04/p3 The wording specifies the Prophet's self because his heart was already with God. — Qushayri and Alusi distinguish public conduct from the heart privately reserved for God. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل الأسرار)]
- 18:28/q04/p4 For others the phrase hints at the difficulty and merit of company with the poor. — Alusi says this implication applies to others, not to the Prophet; reports urge humility and say such company teaches gratitude and contentment. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 18:28/q05 Who are those calling on their Lord?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the description specifies prayer, broader worship, learning, or the historical companions.
- 18:28/q05/p1 They are people performing the obligatory prayers, including the five daily and congregational prayers. — Mujahid, Ibrahim, Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas and Kab identify prescribed prayer; Hasan rejects story-preachers in favor of congregational worship. Ibn Kathir says poor or rich, strong or weak may qualify. [17 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(معاوية بن قرة); ABDURRAZZAQ(إبراهيم ومجاهد); TAB-FULL(الحسن,عبد الله بن عمر,مجاهد); THALABI(كعب الأحبار); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد,الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عمر ومجاهد وإبراهيم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عمر ومجاهد وإبراهيم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(إبراهيم ومجاهد,ابن عباس,عبد الله بن عمر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر ومجاهد); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله عليهما السلام)]
- 18:28/q05/p2 They perform the dawn and afternoon prayers. — Qatada says prescribed prayers began one year before the emigration. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(عمرو بن شعيب); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 18:28/q05/p3 They continually worship and remember God through supplication, praise, glorification, prayer and good works. — Tabari and Ibn Kathir include several acts; Tabarsi says they begin and end each day in worship. Ibn Atiyya includes religious knowledge; Study Quran lists prayer and invocation. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(إبراهيم)]
- 18:28/q05/p4 They are Quran reciters or pupils whom the Prophet taught to recite. — Abu Jafar says the Prophet taught them; Ubayd Allah names reciters. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو جعفر); DURR-FULL(أبو جعفر,عبيد الله بن عدي بن الخيار); ALUSI-FULL(عبيد الله بن عبد الله بن عدي بن الخيار)]
- 18:28/q05/p5 They discuss distinctions in lawful and unlawful matters. — Abu Hashim and Said ibn Jubayr are cited. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هاشم,سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير)]
- 18:28/q05/p6 They are the poor companions whom elites wanted expelled. — Lists vary, including Salman, Abu Dharr, Suhayb, Ammar, Khabbab, Bilal and Ibn Masud. Alusi says their devotion warrants company. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(خباب); THALABI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس وغيره من المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 18:28/q06 What does 'morning and evening' signify?  [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the hours are literal, an idiom of continuity, or spiritual symbols.
- 18:28/q06/p1 It denotes continual devotion across all times. — Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan take both ends for the whole; Abu Hayyan likens it to front and back meaning the whole body. Ibn Ashur says prayer spans day and night. Tabatabai calls recurring times a metonym and rejects restriction to named prayers; Study Quran cites 33:42, 48:9 and 76:25. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 18:28/q06/p2 It marks actual morning and evening worship. — Mawardi describes seeking success at morning and pardon at evening; Alusi says work distracts people at these hours and favors the literal times. Ibn Ashur defines morning as dawn to sunrise; Qiraat-Er contrasts a single ghudwa act with ghadat as a period. [8 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)+]
- 18:28/q06/p3 Waking and sleeping resemble return to life and return to death, inviting remembrance. — Razi says the wise thank God at these transitions. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 18:28/q06/p4 Inwardly, morning and evening signify annihilation and abiding. — Qashani applies the symbols to stripped seekers. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 18:28/q07 How is the morning word read?  [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ · readings]
turns on: The reader transmission, grammar of ghudwa with the article, and value of codex spelling.
- 18:28/q07/p1 Most readers have bil-ghadati, the standard and grammatically clearer form. — Tabari follows reader consensus; Zajjaj says ghudwa is already definite. Nahhas says written waw proves no pronunciation, comparing salat and hayat. Farisi calls this form clearer. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNMUJAHID(باقي القراء); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER; TAB-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 18:28/q07/p2 Ibn Amir and some early readers have bil-ghudwati with waw. — Farra attributes it to Abu Abd al-Rahman alone; others add Nasr ibn Asim and Malik ibn Dinar. Defenders generalize or make indefinite the normally definite time word; Alusi compares explanations of Sibawayh, Khalil and Radi. Orthographic waw is disputed. [20 notes: FARRA(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي); TAB-FULL; IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر); NAHHAS(نصر بن عاصم ومالك بن دينار وأبو عبد الرحمن); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(نصر بن عاصم ومالك بن دينار وأبو عبد الرحمن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)+; TAB-FULL-; NAHHAS-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر النحاس)-]
- 18:28/q07/p3 Ibn Abi Abla reads plural morning and evening forms. — Jishumi explains the original waw turning to alif after fatḥa. [2 notes: JISHUMI(ابن أبي عبلة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن)]
- 18:28/q07/p4 Abu Abd al-Rahman is reported to read al-ghudu without final h. — Ibn Atiyya records the variant. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن)]

## 18:28/q08 What do worshippers seek by 'His face'?  [يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether face denotes approval, essence, attributes or vision.
- 18:28/q08/p1 They seek God's pleasure, favor and approved obedience rather than display, reputation or worldly gain. — Bursevi and Suhayli explain the face metaphor from where approval and anger show or the turn toward one favored; Alusi prefers this. Ibn Ashur says devotion makes them worthy company; Study Quran compares 2:272, 13:22, 30:38-39, 76:9 and 92:18-20. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السهيلي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+; TABATABAI-]
- 18:28/q08/p2 They seek God's essence or nearness by turning to Him. — Alusi reports essence with an omitted genitive and nearness as alternatives; Qashani's stripped seekers seek divine essence without worldly or afterlife needs. [3 notes: TABRISI; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI-]
- 18:28/q08/p3 God's face means His beautiful names and attributes through which He is addressed. — Tabatabai says the essence cannot be reached directly: seeking active attributes means receiving mercy and grace; seeking knowledge, power and majesty calls for humility, weakness and ignorance. He rejects pleasure, omitted essence and mere nearness. [4 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 18:28/q08/p4 At the highest mystical level it can mean seeking a vision of God. — Study Quran marks this as a highest-level interpretation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 18:28/q08/p5 They seek God's face through supplication. — Al-Mawardi reports supplication as one account of how they seek Him. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:28/q08/p6 They seek God's face through their day's work. — Al-Mawardi reports that work dedicated to God by day implies even greater devotion at night. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 18:28/q09 How does 'seeking His face' function grammatically?  [يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ · grammar]
- 18:28/q09/p1 It is a circumstantial expression describing their uninterrupted sincere invocation and intention. — Qushayri says devotion persists morning and evening. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 18:28/q10 What does 'do not let your eyes pass beyond them' mean?  [وَلَا تَعْدُ عَيْنَاكَ عَنْهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ada with an simply denotes passing or includes contemptuous recoil.
- 18:28/q10/p1 Keep attention on poor believers; do not turn the gaze past them toward wealthy people. — Ibn Abbas says not to pass believers for others. Tabari derives it from surpassing; Razi cites poetry; Tabatabai cites Raghib's root sense of overstepping. Abu Hayyan favors ordinary lexical turning away over semantic incorporation. [34 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(اليزيدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء,علي عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 18:28/q10/p2 The wording combines passing over the poor with recoiling from or looking down on them. — Zamakhshari explains ada with an through semantic inclusion of withdrawing or rising above, comparing 4:2; Nasafi says this intensifies the warning. Abu Hayyan objects that Basran grammar licenses inclusion only when necessary. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,الزمخشري); TABATABAI(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 18:28/q10/p3 Do not repel them or regard them with a contemptuous look. — Ata's Ibn Abbas report frames the gaze as rejection; Qurtubi describes recoil from one regarded as contemptible. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 18:28/q10/p4 Inwardly, do not turn attention from these seekers to anything other than God. — Wasiti and Dhu al-Nun reason that these devotees' eyes never turn from God; Qushayri says the Prophet's gaze leads them toward God until afterlife vision. [5 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(ذو النون)]

## 18:28/q11 How is 'do not let your eyes pass' read?  [وَلَا تَعْدُ عَيْنَاكَ · readings]
turns on: Verb form, case and number of the eyes, and whether causative morphology changes valency.
- 18:28/q11/p1 The common reading makes the two eyes the subject. — Farra and Akhfash explain the dual subject; Qurtubi rejects the objection that the verb must govern the eyes directly. [4 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:28/q11/p2 Hasan reads a causative form with 'your eyes' accusative. — Ibn Jinni glosses turning the eyes from worldly adornment. Zamakhshari and Lawamih say hamza makes the verb transitive; Abu Hayyan objects that the base is already transitive and should then take two objects. [15 notes: NAHHAS(الحسن); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن,الزمخشري وأبو الفضل); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري وصاحب اللوامح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 18:28/q11/p3 Hasan, Isa and al-Amash are reported with a doubled verb. — Zamakhshari and Abu al-Fadl invoke doubling to explain transitivity, citing al-Nabigha; Abu Hayyan disputes it. A still ayn is also reported from Hasan. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري,صاحب اللوامح); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن وعيسى والأعمش,الزمخشري وأبو الفضل); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري وصاحب اللوامح,عيسى والأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 18:28/q11/p4 A reported variant has the eye singular. — Kashshaf records it with a poetic witness for turning away. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 18:28/q12 What is the syntax of addressing the eyes?  [وَلَا تَعْدُ عَيْنَاكَ عَنْهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: How the eyes syntactically relate to the Prophet and the verb.
- 18:28/q12/p1 The two eyes are subject, while the prohibition addresses their owner through them. — Qurtubi compares 9:55; Ibn Ashur calls this elegant compression. Samin cites poetry treating the eyes as one unit and desire as metaphorically attributed to them. [7 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:28/q12/p2 The eyes can be parsed as a part-for-whole substitute for an addressee subject. — Samin prefers this parse; he says a following circumstance from the replaced pronoun is rare and cites poetry. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 18:28/q12/p3 The verb may have an omitted object, 'looking at them'. — Samin supplies al-nazar. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 18:28/q13 How is 'desiring worldly adornment' construed?  [تُرِيدُ زِينَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · grammar]
turns on: Where the circumstance attaches and how singular agreement is explained.
- 18:28/q13/p1 It is a circumstantial present clause describing the possible state of seeking worldly adornment. — The meaning experts, Zamakhshari and Qurtubi take it as hal; Qurtubi cites Imru al-Qays. Alusi favors the ordinary parse and says a genitive relation can license attachment. On anomalous readings the hidden verb subject supplies the agent. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 18:28/q13/p2 The circumstantial parse has agreement or attachment difficulties. — Abu Hayyan asks why a dual eye subject would not take turidan; if the Prophet is subject, attaching a circumstance to the genitive possessor is difficult. Samin allows singular agreement for paired eyes and cites poetry; Alusi rejects the objection. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:28/q14 What is the worldly adornment toward which the gaze is warned?  [زِينَةَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · referent]
turns on: Whether it is general elite company or a specific reported moment.
- 18:28/q14/p1 It is elite company, status and finery, as contrasted with poor believers' shabby dress. — Ibn Zayd calls it the worldly elite. Ibn Kathir recalls 20:131; others mention wealthy and noble people's clothing and appearance. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:28/q14/p2 One reported explanation places the gaze during the poor worshippers' seeking forgiveness and treats it as desire for status. — Thalabi reports this rebuke. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 18:28/q16 Who is the heedless person whose wishes must not be obeyed?  [مَنْ أَغْفَلْنَا قَلْبَهُۥ · referent]

## 18:28/q17 What is the heedlessness of the heart and what is 'Our remembrance'?  [أَغْفَلْنَا قَلْبَهُۥ عَن ذِكْرِنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the term is specified by revelation and faith, neglected duty, or inward attention.
- 18:28/q17/p1 It is inattentiveness to God, the Quran, faith or monotheism. — Muqatil says Quran; Ibn al-Jawzi names monotheism, Quran and Islam; Mufaddal specifies Quran. Ibn Ashur sees a constitution inattentive to divine unity; Ibn Abbas says the heart was sealed against monotheism. [10 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفضل); ALUSI-FULL(المفضل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 18:28/q17/p2 Heedlessness is wasted time, idleness or neglect of duties, while obedience gives the heart life. — Sahl and Qushayri define it through time; Tustari describes a thousand deaths ending in separation and lives ending in meeting the Real, and says sin kills while obedience revives. [6 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); MAWARDI-FULL(سهل بن عبد الله); QUSHAYRI]
- 18:28/q17/p3 It is forgetting commanded acts and blessings, following lower desires, or slackness. — Abu Uthman stresses duties and blessings; Ibn al-Jalla stresses slackness; Mawardi reports anything causing heedlessness. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان,ابن الجلاء,بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:28/q17/p4 It is long hope and preoccupation with gifts or creatures instead of their Giver. — Jawzajani and Mawardi cite long hope; Qushayri contrasts gifts and Giver. Razi calls divine remembrance light and creaturely preoccupation darkness, because God is necessary existence and contingent things derive from nonexistence. [9 notes: SULAMI(الجوزجاني,النوري); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL]
- 18:28/q17/p5 Heedlessness may be merciful bodily rest or punitive veiling from God. — Qasim distinguishes life's needed rest from forgetting God's watch; another inward report calls it the heart's punishment and veil. [2 notes: SULAMI(القاسم,بعضهم)]

## 18:28/q18 What does 'We made his heart heedless' mean?  [أَغْفَلْنَا قَلْبَهُۥ · theology]

- 18:28/q18/p1 God caused or created heedlessness in the heart. — Sibawayh calls initial hamza augmentative; Razi argues one cannot intend a particular or undirected heedlessness, so God creates it, and causative Afala is commoner. - 18:28/q18/p3 God left the heart to heedlessness, withdrew support, or did not mark it with faith. — Qaffal proposes worldly gifts, an unmarked heart and leaving it to Satan; Razi says each either still causally implicates God or fails to explain attribution. [12 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرماني,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الرماني,المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:28/q18/p4 God exposed the heart to punitive heedlessness after the person's deviation. — Tabarsi compares 61:5; Tabatabai compares coverings and deafness at 18:57 and says punishment follows stubborn opposition without cancelling prior choice. [4 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 18:28/q18/p5 God ascribed or reckoned heedlessness to the person, or found a heart unmarked. — Alusi cites Kumayt's poem for attribution. ## 18:28/q20 What does 'he followed his desire' mean here?  [وَٱتَّبَعَ هَوَىٰهُ · interpretation]

## 18:28/q22 Why must the demand to remove the poor be refused?  [وَلَا تُطِعْ · interpretation]
turns on: The weight of believers' dignity and the likely outcome of conceding to elites.
- 18:28/q22/p1 The poor believers' devotion and dignity require their company; social rank does not measure worth. — Bursevi says a person rejecting faith because of poor company is hypocritical, and expulsion violates prophethood; religious leaders attend to inner states. He cites a hadith that God looks at hearts and deeds, a Hafiz couplet, and Abraham's selfless flock gift. Ibn Ashur criticizes elite fixation on appearances. [11 notes: SULAMI(ذو النون,عمرو المكي); BURSEVI(التأويلات,الحافظ,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:28/q22/p2 The chiefs' uncertain or outward conversion cannot justify humiliating believers. — Alusi states the objection then answers it: exclusion could alienate hearts, induce apostasy among recent converts and deter future entrants, outweighing a few unbelievers' continued disbelief. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:28/q22/p3 The command blocks concessions to polytheists and tells the Prophet to attend to believers. — Ibn Ashur calls it a broad rule; Islahi links it to the cave story and sura's opening; Biqai says the cave sleepers foreshadow rescue and elevation over tyrants. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 18:28/q22/p4 Inwardly, do not defer to a veiled person governed by the lower self. — Alusi warns against humbling oneself for wealth, citing a hadith on losing two-thirds of religion; he and a poet describe rich patrons' painful reproach. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أهل الإشارة,بعض الشعراء,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 18:28/q27 What ancillary argument is made about the Quran's inimitability?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether proof rests on opponents' failure or an expert but undefined sense of eloquence.
- 18:28/q27/p1 The open challenge to produce any sura and opponents' failure show inability to match the Quran. — Khattabi says specifying no sura exposes an inadequate explanation; Baqillani argues opponents had motive, time and argumentative skill (43:58, 19:97, 16:4), yet offered shifting charges (8:31, 28:36, 15:6, 21:3, 37:36, 25:4-8, 15:91), war and demands for signs. [5 notes: KHATTABI; BAQILLANI]
- 18:28/q27/p2 Its inimitability lies in eloquence whose ranks skilled ears recognize though its cause is hard to define. — Khattabi reports speculative scholars and followers, then rejects the undefined impression as leaving ignorance untreated. Narrators' Jarir-Dhu al-Rumma story illustrates crafted eloquence. [3 notes: KHATTABI(الرواة,عامة أهل هذه المقالة,علماء أهل النظر); KHATTABI-]
