# 6:31: 34 questions

## 6:31/q01 Who are the people declared losers?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ · referent]
- 6:31/q01/p1 Deniers of resurrection and the afterlife, especially the previously mentioned worldly life claimants; Quraysh and their followers are included. — Al-Tabari specifies resurrection, judgment, Paradise and Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr points to 6:29 and 6:27 and says explicit naming develops their fate; Al-Ālūsī says the relative clause shows persistent denial caused their loss. Muqātil and Study Quran identify Quraysh/worldly life deniers. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:31/q02 What reports identify a particular denier or occasion for this verse?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَذَّبُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: The reports name different people or give different statements.
- 6:31/q02/p1 Abū Jahl acknowledged Muhammad's truth but refused to follow a man from Banū ʿAbd Manāf. — Abū Yazīd al-Madanī reports a handshake and this statement before the verse was revealed. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو يزيد المدني)]
- 6:31/q02/p2 Abū Jahl said he did not call Muhammad a liar but rejected the message. — Nājiya ibn Kaʿb reports the verse followed this admission. [1 notes: THALABI(ناجية بن كعب)]
- 6:31/q02/p3 Al-Ḥārith ibn ʿĀmir privately believed Muhammad truthful but feared Arab retaliation and loss of safety if he followed him. — Maqātil identifies him. [1 notes: THALABI(مقاتل)]

## 6:31/q03 What loss have the deniers incurred, and why?  [خَسِرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse is explained as the failed faith transaction, squandered human capacities, or the word's basic lexical sense.
- 6:31/q03/p1 They forfeit afterlife reward and gain punishment by exchanging faith for unbelief. — Al-Ṭabarī likens faith and Paradise to the capital of a ruined transaction; Wāḥidī's authorities say the worst loss is one's soul, like losing a merchant's capital; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies afterlife rather than worldly deprivation. Al-Rāzī counts lost reward and incurred punishment as two losses. Biqāʿī says every attainable reward is lost; Bayḍāwī adds lost blessing and enduring punishment; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a failed bargain. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب المعاني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 6:31/q03/p2 They squander faculties meant for lasting knowledge and virtue on passing pleasure, and lack provisions for the afterlife. — Al-Rāzī names bodily and intellectual faculties as capital, transient pleasure as imagined profit, and alienation after death from both spiritual preparation and worldly attachments; Biqāʿī adds outer senses, reason and prophetic miracles. Study Quran relays Rāzī's investment analogy. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 6:31/q03/p3 Loss fundamentally means decrease, though some derive it from destruction. — Al-Jishumī reports both derivations. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 6:31/q04 What does 'meeting God' mean here?  [بِلِقَآءِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether meeting is understood as resurrection and recompense, an implied object such as reward, literal vision, or a representational encounter.
- 6:31/q04/p1 Resurrection, return to God, and His judgment and recompense. — Ibn ʿAbbās includes reward and punishment and cites 2:46; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes God's power and the consequences of deeds; Rāzī says benefit and harm then lie with God alone. Kashshāf likens standing before the Lord to a guilty servant detained and rebuked. Qaffāl cites the warning concerning a false oath for property. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī observes the passage's progression from resurrection and gathering to meeting. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القفال وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:31/q04/p2 Meeting God's promised reward and punishment, or the recompense of one's deeds, is figuratively called meeting God. — Wāḥidī cites ordinary speech about a dead person meeting his deeds; Ṭabrisī attributes the promise gloss to Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḥasan; al-Ālūsī notes an implicit complement and possessive magnification in Abū Ḥayyān. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); TABRISI(ابن عباس,قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القفال وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:31/q04/p3 The phrase retains actual meeting or vision of God. — Al-Qushayrī rejects extending contextual figurative uses to this verse and argues denial of God and His vision implies denial of existence; Nasafī notes denying resurrection also denies vision. Kashshāf reports full recognition of Him as another alternative. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); NASAFI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القفال وغيره)-]
- 6:31/q04/p4 It pictures the afterlife's unimpeded manifestation of God's approval and wrath, like servants meeting their lord after absence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts God's delayed worldly warning and choice with immediate afterlife consequences (18:49), and cites the hadith on loving to meet God and 40:15. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:31/q04/p5 Meeting primarily means encounter, with extended uses for sight, contemporaneity, calamity and battle. — Al-Jishumī gives the lexical range. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 6:31/q05 What does 'until' terminate or connect?  [حَتَّىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic reach of ḥattā and whether loss can end.
- 6:31/q05/p1 Their denial continues until the Hour, while their loss has no endpoint. — Wāḥidī, Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and others say the governing verb is 'denied'; Ṭabrisī adds that regret then comes too late. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects ending loss because its deprivation occurs at resurrection and not all deniers lose worldly goods. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:31/q05/p2 The ordinary loss lasts until Judgment, when a worse calamity eclipses it. — Al-Ālūsī analogizes Satan's curse 'until Judgment' at 38:78. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports and rejects commentators who end the loss there. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 6:31/q05/p3 Before 'when', ḥattā is initial and indicates cause rather than an endpoint. — Ibn al-Ḥājib says the sense is effectively 'when the Hour comes'. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الحاجب)]

## 6:31/q06 What event is 'the Hour' here?  [ٱلسَّاعَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the lament and burden occur at actual resurrection, its onset, or death.
- 6:31/q06/p1 The resurrection, gathering and universal judgment. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies raising the dead from graves; Abū Ḥayyān argues simultaneous bearing of burdens better fits actual resurrection and says those in barzakh experience its interval as a day. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the conventional name for resurrection and gathering. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KASHSHAF-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 6:31/q06/p2 The moment resurrection begins, marked by sudden onset. — Wāḥidī prefers this because it accords with baghtatan; Elmalılı reports the onset time is known only to God. [2 notes: ELMALILI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 6:31/q06/p3 Death or one's personal Hour, the beginning of afterlife states. — Zamakhsharī and Rāzī cite the saying that whoever dies has had his resurrection/Hour; Biqāʿī and Elmalılı call death the beginning of transition. Al-Ālūsī reports death as the referent. Abū Ḥayyān argues actual resurrection fits better. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:31/q06/p4 The Hour is so close after death that it strikes like an immediate surprise. — Kashshāf offers this alternative. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 6:31/q07 Why is it called 'the Hour'?  [ٱلسَّاعَةُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The proposed relation of the name to speed, sudden onset, brevity, or fame.
- 6:31/q07/p1 Reckoning and recompense occur swiftly. — Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī, Samīn and Elmalılı explain the name through rapid accounting; al-Ālūsī says sāʿa first denotes a short interval. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل المعاني); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 6:31/q07/p2 It arrives at an unexpected, unknown instant. — Wāḥidī prefers sudden onset because baghtatan follows; Elmalılı says only God knows its hour. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 6:31/q07/p3 Its duration is brief compared with eternity. — Nasafī compares the intervening interval with the eternal sequel; al-Ālūsī also reports this. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:31/q07/p4 The definite article identifies the known, uniquely famous Hour. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it is established in minds and implied by meeting God; Samīn compares 'the star' and al-Thurayyā. Al-Ṭabarī calls it the known Hour. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:31/q08 What does 'suddenly' convey?  [بَغْتَةًۭ · meaning]
- 6:31/q08/p1 An unforeseen onset without knowing its time or receiving warning. — Al-Zajjāj cites Arab usage and a poem of unforeseen death; Ibn ʿAbbās says God alone knows the Hour; Samīn stresses mere speed of an expected event is insufficient and cites poetry. Biqāʿī says its precise time is unknowable even generally. [22 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 6:31/q09 How is 'baghtatan' parsed?  [بَغْتَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative describes a state or supplies/emphasizes a verbal action.
- 6:31/q09/p1 An accusative circumstantial verbal noun describing the Hour or those it overtakes. — Naḥḥās and Ibn ʿAṭiyya cite Sībawayh's nonproductive parallel qataltuhu ṣabran; Naḥḥās adds a poetic laʾyan bi-laʾy. Abū Ḥayyān allows either subject or object as its possessor. [13 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:31/q09/p2 An absolute or cognate accusative, possibly supplied by an implied verb. — Kashshāf and Nasafī allow a verbal noun of surprise; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn distinguish an omitted verb from the root or an omitted 'they came'; al-Ālūsī accepts these alternatives. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:31/q10 What reports describe the Hour's arrival?  [جَآءَتْهُمُ ٱلسَّاعَةُ بَغْتَةًۭ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different preceding circumstances.
- 6:31/q10/p1 It can arrive while someone eats or two people trade, leaving their acts unfinished. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits the Prophetic report through Abū Hurayra. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي ﷺ، أبو هريرة)]
- 6:31/q10/p2 A caller will announce the Hour three times before it arrives. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة)]

## 6:31/q11 How does 'jāʾa' here relate to Qur'anic use of 'atā'?  [جَآءَتْهُمُ · links]
- 6:31/q11/p1 Its force depends on the full verse and context; the supplied comparison is not completed. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the Hour here and 6:40–41; his other discussion describes Ghāfir 40:82–85, where peoples mocked messengers and believed only after seeing punishment, too late to help. He cautions against isolating either verb. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 6:31/q12 What does 'ḥasra' mean?  [حَسْرَتَنَا · meaning]
- 6:31/q12/p1 Intense regret or grief for something lost. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as regret. Baghawī compares a traveler whose mount fails; al-Rāghib derives it from exposure or exhausted strength. Ṭabrisī says remorse exhausts the regretful. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]

## 6:31/q13 What does calling out to 'our regret' do?  [يَٰحَسْرَتَنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the figurative address is described as summoning regret or alerting the hearers.
- 6:31/q13/p1 It figuratively summons regret as if its appointed moment has arrived, magnifying anguish. — Sībawayh explains 'O wonder/regret, come'; Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Samīn and others say the abstraction cannot literally answer. Samīn adds rebuke for causing this need; Biqāʿī says the image warns others. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'our' fixes their own fate, unlike a bare cry often made for others; the following ʿalā marks the cause. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:31/q13/p2 It alerts listeners to the terrible loss; the apparent addressee is the audience, not regret. — Zajjāj cites 'do not let me see you here' as an analogous displaced addressee; Sībawayh uses 'O wonder'; Wāḥidī cites 36:30, 39:56, 11:72 and 12:84. Ibn al-Jawzī cites 'O horses of God, ride'. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج وابن الأنباري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 6:31/q14 What are they lamenting?  [عَلَىٰ مَا فَرَّطْنَا فِيهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their regret is specified as missed deeds, lost Paradise homes, mocked messengers, the failed bargain, or wasted faculties.
- 6:31/q14/p1 Neglected faith, obedience and preparation for Paradise and the Hereafter. — Al-Suddī specifies deeds that would earn Paradise; Muqātil names remembrance of God; Rāzī says lost reward, while their burdens indicate earned punishment. Biqāʿī includes disciplining the soul and following messengers; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Study Quran specify afterlife work during worldly life. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد، وأبو عبيدة، وأكثر أهل اللغة، والحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الحسن,السدي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:31/q14/p2 They see the Paradise places they have lost and lament. — A Prophetic report is transmitted by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Qurṭubī, Ṭabrisī, Durr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī; Durr calls its chain sound. Samīn rejects the proposed pronoun reference to those places as remote; al-Ālūsī says the report cannot settle that reference. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي); IBNABIHATIM(أبو سعيد); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); TABRISI(السدي); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 6:31/q14/p3 They regret having mocked the messengers. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Mujāhid. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد)]
- 6:31/q14/p4 They regret the ruined transaction of trading faith and Paradise for disbelief and punishment. — Al-Ṭabarī infers the bargain from khusira; Qurṭubī cites 2:16. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); BIQAI-FULL]
- 6:31/q14/p5 They lament wasted capacities and separation from former pleasures. — Al-Rāzī says God-given faculties were meant to disclose enduring truth and virtue; after death they are unprepared for the spiritual realm and cut off from transient pleasures. Study Quran relays him. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 6:31/q15 What does 'farraṭnā' mean and derive from?  [فَرَّطْنَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether tafrīṭ is explained by ordinary neglect or by its figurative derivation from precedence.
- 6:31/q15/p1 We neglected, wasted, or fell short of what we could have done. — Zajjāj includes incapacity or neglect; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān stress that action remained possible; Ṭabrisī contrasts tafrīṭ as falling behind with ifrāṭ as excess. Abū ʿUbayda and others give waste/neglect. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد، وأبو عبيدة، وأكثر أهل اللغة، والحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيد); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 6:31/q15/p2 It evokes going ahead or letting another precede one, with the doubled form removing precedence. — Zajjāj and Wāḥidī describe putting shortcoming first; Ibn Baḥr explains yielding one's lead. Samīn likens the privative formation to skinning a camel and compares 17:79; al-Ālūsī repeats this morphological account. Qurṭubī says they let others precede them in obedience. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن بحر); SAMIN-DURR(ابن بحر); ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:31/q16 What is the grammatical role of 'mā' in their lament?  [مَا فَرَّطْنَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā names the act of neglect or the things neglected.
- 6:31/q16/p1 It is a verbal noun: they lament their neglect. — Samīn says 'for what we neglected' attaches to regret. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:31/q16/p2 It is a relative pronoun for deeds or acts of obedience they neglected. — Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī say on this analysis the antecedent is explicit in their speech; Abū Ḥayyān and Rāzī also report it. [5 notes: TABRISI(محمد بن جرير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 6:31/q17 What does the pronoun in 'fīhā' refer to?  [فِيهَا · referent]
turns on: Which implied antecedent—world, Hour, Paradise, bargain, deeds, or Paradise places—fits the context.
- 6:31/q17/p1 Worldly life, where they neglected work for the afterlife. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil say the unmentioned world is obvious from context; Rāzī says reason identifies it as the place of neglect. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والطبري); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:31/q17/p2 The Hour and the faith and preparation it required. — Al-Ḥasan and others construe 'in it' as neglecting its affairs or preparation; Kashshāf compares 'I neglected God's side' at 39:56. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والطبري); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 6:31/q17/p3 Paradise, which they failed to seek and work for. — Al-Suddī gives this reading and Ṭabrisī pairs it with the report of Hell's people seeing their Paradise homes. Al-Ālūsī considers it remote. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); TABRISI(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 6:31/q17/p4 The transaction implied by their loss. — Al-Ṭabarī argues loss implies sale, even without an explicit noun; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the implied bargain. Al-Ālūsī considers the reading remote. Samīn attributes it to al-Ḥasan. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جرير); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن جرير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والطبري); TABRISI(محمد بن جرير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL(محمد بن جرير الطبري); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(محمد بن جرير)]
- 6:31/q17/p5 The good deeds or acts of obedience expressed by a relative 'mā'. — Wāḥidī, Rāzī and al-Ālūsī note this makes the pronoun refer to an item in the utterance itself; Ibn al-Jawzī reports obedience as the antecedent. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(محمد بن جرير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes)]
- 6:31/q17/p6 Their former places in Paradise. — Samīn reports and rejects this proposal as remote. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:31/q18 How does 'while they carry' attach to the lament?  [وَهُمْ يَحْمِلُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the state is simultaneous with the cry or anticipated.
- 6:31/q18/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing them as they speak. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Samīn, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī identify the wāw as circumstantial; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn say the explicit subject pronoun strengthens attribution. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:31/q18/p2 The burden-bearing may be prospective relative to the lament. — Al-Ālūsī permits this alternate timing. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:31/q19 What are 'their awzār'?  [أَوْزَارَهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether awzār names sins, their punishment, divine wrath, ignored reminders, or inward attachments.
- 6:31/q19/p1 Their sins, errors and evil deeds, described as heavy burdens. — Ibn Qutayba cites 94:2–3; Ibn ʿAbbās and many others gloss sin; Qatāda says the load is their deeds and also their work or earning. Ṭabarī rejects physical-load gloss as unattested Arab usage, preferring the sinning sense. Asad adopts Ibn ʿAbbās through Rāzī. [37 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS; THALABI(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,ابن عباس؛ الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس,الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Ibn ‘Abbas); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:31/q19/p2 The punishment or suffering earned by their sins. — Naḥḥās records this as a second gloss; Zajjāj and others compare severe punishment to a crushing load, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes sin's consequence among possibilities. [10 notes: NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:31/q19/p3 God's wrath earned by sin. — Study Quran cites 2:61, 2:90, 3:112 and 3:162. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 6:31/q19/p4 The reminders and warnings they ignored. — Study Quran cites 20:100–101, where the reminder becomes an afterlife burden. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 6:31/q19/p5 For the veiled soul, attachments, bodily love, sins and sensory traits rooted within it. — Qāshānī says these bind, veil and torment the person. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:31/q20 What is the root sense and derivation of 'wizr'?  [أَوْزَارَهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the lexical starting point is carried weight or a refuge/support.
- 6:31/q20/p1 A heavy load; sins are named for the weight one carries. — Ibn Qutayba cites 94:2–3; Abū ʿUbayd describes carrying goods in a cloak and the loads of war. Wāḥidī cites 6:164; Samīn gives awzār as plural of wizr, war equipment and a poetic line, and wazīr as bearer of a ruler's burdens. Akhfash gives wazara/yaziru/wizran and mawzūr; Yūnus notes two vocalizations. Funeral usage prefers mawzūrāt over common maʾzūrāt, while Abū ʿAlī calls the latter sound-following. [22 notes: AKHFASH(يونس); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أبو عبيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 6:31/q20/p2 A mountain refuge or support on which one relies; heaviness and support extend to sin and a minister. — Zajjāj and Abū ʿUbayd cite Moses' request for Aaron as minister (20:29–30); Samīn and al-Rāghib cite 75:11. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls derivation from a refuge or helper unclear. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو عبيد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]

## 6:31/q21 How are their burdens borne 'on their backs'?  [يَحْمِلُونَ أَوْزَارَهُمْ عَلَىٰ ظُهُورِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the image presents embodied deeds, metaphorical hardship, constant attachment, or an inward state.
- 6:31/q21/p1 Literally: deeds take visible forms, and the sinner's ugly, foul deed rides or is carried on his back. — Muqātil says the deed demands to be carried; reports from al-Suddī, ʿAmr, Abū Marzūq and ʿUmayr include a grave-companion, a public exposure or a journey to Hell. Some describe the believer's fragrant good deed as a mount (19:85). Bursevī argues afterlife deeds can become visible though accidents here; al-Ālūsī sees no obstacle and cites Sunni belief in embodied deeds and weighing. Abū Ḥayyān's preferred report groups ʿUmayr, ʿAmr, al-Suddī and Ṭabarī. [34 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي,عمرو بن قيس الملائي); IBNABIHATIM(أبو مرزوق,السدي); THALABI(السدي وعمرو بن قيس الملائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون؛ قتادة؛ السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي وغيره); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عمير بن هانئ); RAZI-FULL(قتادة والسدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو هريرة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو مرزوق,السدي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مرزوق,السدي,عمرو بن قيس الملائي); BURSEVI(السدي وغيره); ALUSI-FULL(السدي,عمرو بن قيس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمير بن هانئ)+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:31/q21/p2 Figuratively: sins and their painful punishment weigh on them like loads. — Zajjāj says Arabic uses 'heavy' for severe aversion; Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats back-bearing as an intensified figure and questions the refuge derivation. Zamakhsharī compares hands as the normal instrument of deeds in 42:30; Bursevī calls sins bodily loads by metaphor. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:31/q21/p3 The image says sins never leave or release them. — Arabists compare a person always before one's eyes to one continually in mind; Nasafī and Qurṭubī say sin clings to the denier. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل العربية); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 6:31/q21/p4 Inwardly, rooted traits and attachments veil and torment the soul. — Qāshānī calls this the burden of bodily love and sensory traits. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 6:31/q22 Why mention the backs specifically?  [عَلَىٰ ظُهُورِهِمْ · rhetoric]
- 6:31/q22/p1 The back is the customary and strongest place for heavy burdens, intensifying their weight. — Ṭabarī notes loads might otherwise be borne on head or shoulder; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn say the back carries more, with Samīn comparing the hand's touch in 6:7. Kashshāf contrasts hand imagery for earned deeds in 42:30. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:31/q23 What does 'alā' add?  [أَلَا · rhetoric]
- 6:31/q23/p1 It announces and intensifies the gravity of the condemnation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares Prophetic calls to convey and witness; Nasafī and Biqāʿī emphasize severity. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:31/q24 What is condemned by 'evil is what they bear'?  [سَآءَ مَا يَزِرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the closing blame points to the evil burden itself or its recompense.
- 6:31/q24/p1 Their vile burden of sins or evil deeds. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq and Qatāda gloss the act as what they do; Ibn ʿAbbās calls it a wretched load. Zamakhsharī compares 7:177; Rāzī points to 4:22; Biqāʿī says the rebuke gathers its many defects and unbearable weight. [20 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:31/q24/p2 The punishment they suffer as recompense for evil deeds. — Ṭabrisī records this second sense. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 6:31/q25 How can 'sāʾa mā yazirūn' be parsed?  [سَآءَ مَا يَزِرُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether sāʾa functions as an ordinary verb, exclamation, or biʾsa-like blame.
- 6:31/q25/p1 A plain transitive past verb with mā as subject and an omitted object. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a poem; Samīn says mā may be relative, verbal or indefinite and notes the Shaykh will not fix the poem to a single parse. Al-Ālūsī lists the same mā options. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:31/q25/p2 An exclamatory expression: how awful what they carry. — Samīn says this reading is fixed and intransitive, with ḍamma on the second radical; al-Ālūsī accepts it. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:31/q25/p3 An intensified verb of blame equivalent to biʾsa. — Zajjāj compares 7:177, Samīn refers to mā in 2:90; the Shaykh distinguishes its subject rules and clause structure, but Samīn says the distinction needs interpretation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya permits this beside a declarative reading. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:31/q26 What do the burdens teach about the deniers' two losses?  [يَٰحَسْرَتَنَا أَوْزَارَهُمْ · interpretation]
- 6:31/q26/p1 They failed to gain the reward they could have prepared for, while earning punishment through sins. — Al-Rāzī pairs regret for omitted provision with burdens of committed wrong; Bursevī contrasts absent good deeds and physically borne sins; Elmalılı pairs loss of reward with enormous punishment. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 6:31/q27 What inward reading is given to denial, the Hour, and burdens?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward burden is embedded traits broadly or the self itself.
- 6:31/q27/p1 The veiled deny meeting the Real; minor resurrection exposes neglected work and attachments embedded in the soul. — Qāshānī says bodily love, sin and sensory habits become the burdens that obstruct aims and torment the self. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 6:31/q27/p2 The self and its very existence are the chief burden; repentance and effacement free the seeker by God's aid. — Bursevī cites Ḥāfiẓ against self-regard, a saying that the self cannot escape itself, and Tirmidhī on remembrance softening the heart while ego hardens it. Divine unity and following its people sustain spiritual life. [5 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ أبو عبد الله محمد بن علي الترمذي الحكيم,بعضهم,حافظ)]

## 6:31/q28 What does the pilgrimage dream illustrate about release from burdens?  [* · inward]
- 6:31/q28/p1 Sound-hearted companionship with the holy benefits the seeker, as following and hearing the Prophet could have relieved deniers' burdens. — Bursevī tells ʿAlī ibn al-Muwaffaq's dream: after giving up his litter to walk among pilgrims, maidens washed his feet and counted him among them because he loved their company. God guides whom He wills. [2 notes: BURSEVI(علي بن الموفق)]

## 6:31/q29 How does this verse connect with its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the pictured encounter is tied to universal resurrection or each death.
- 6:31/q29/p1 It completes the portrait of people claiming worldly life alone, showing the afterlife loss their denial entails. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the opening a fresh calamity report; Ibn ʿĀshūr says explicit naming makes their fate an independent statement; Ṭabāṭabāʾī links resurrection, gathering, the Hereafter and meeting God. Study Quran cites 6:29 and locates 4–31 among responses to Makkan excuses and earlier destroyed peoples; Iṣlāḥī contrasts Quran rejecters with afterlife deniers. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:31/q29/p2 The scene may portray each unbeliever's encounter with the afterlife after death. — Kur'an Yolu prefers this explanation: souls already dead see their error now and future dead will do so; the alternative treats prophetic past tense for future resurrection as a mark of certainty. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 6:31/q30 What general pattern of human loss does the verse illustrate?  [* · theology]
- 6:31/q30/p1 Wrong paths defeat the goals for which the created order provides proper causes; apparent unjust gain ends in loss. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares trying to sustain life without eating and digestion. Belief in God and the afterlife and practical law are routes to happiness; shirk, seizure of property, violation of honor or life, neglect of worship and deceit damage connected causes in this life and next. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 6:31/q31 What encounter with God is reported for a denier?  [بِلِقَآءِ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
- 6:31/q31/p1 God reminds the denier of His gifts, and the servant admits having forgotten God. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits the report through Abū Hurayra. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي ﷺ، أبو هريرة)]

## 6:31/q32 What is the clause structure around 'when they said'?  [إِذَا قَالُوا۟ · grammar]
- 6:31/q32/p1 'They said' answers the conditional 'when'. — Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr make the cry the apodosis; al-Ālūsī says the conditional is answered by 'O our remorse'. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:31/q33 How does the preceding challenge 'Is this not the truth?' relate to denial here?  [* · links]
- 6:31/q33/p1 The challenge rebukes denial of resurrection and recompense, and the ensuing punishment answers their disbelief. — Kashshāf explains the previous question and punishment; Kur'an Yolu says after death the denier recognizes the error. Ibn ʿĀshūr says deferred warning in this life yields immediate consequences in the next. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:31/q34 What do other terms in the adjacent verse mean?  [* · links]
- 6:31/q34/p1 ʿAql, nahy and ḥijā denote restraint or holding fast, showing how reason restrains wrongdoing. — Ṭabrisī says nahy may be verbal noun or plural and refers to water held in a low place; ḥijā derives from lingering and is supported by a verse and al-Azharī's taḥajjaytu. Al-Aṣmaʿī's Maʿqala holds water, as medicine restrains the belly. [4 notes: TABRISI(الأصمعي)]
