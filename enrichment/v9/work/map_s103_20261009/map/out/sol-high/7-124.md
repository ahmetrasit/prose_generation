# 7:124: 20 questions

## 7:124/q01 What does the cutting verb convey about the extent of the threat?  [لَأُقَطِّعَنَّ أَيْدِيَكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether the forms mark multiplied acts, the number of people threatened, or the scope of the definite plural.
- 7:124/q01/p1 Its intensive form conveys repeated or multiplied cutting across the magicians' limbs. — Al-Wāḥidī relates the intensive form to the plural hands. Al-Jishumī glosses taqṭīʿ as multiplying cuts. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI]
- 7:124/q01/p2 The verb forms imply that large numbers of sorcerers, or sorcerers with many followers, were threatened. — Asad reads numerical scale from the Arabic forms. He supports the possible large following with a Biblical report of many Egyptians joining Israel's exodus. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 7:124/q01/p3 The definite plural extends the threat to every hand and foot of every sorcerer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr draws this scope from the definite plural, alongside the omitted object of the preceding threat. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:124/q02 How are the cutting and crucifixion verbs read?  [لَأُقَطِّعَنَّ لَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the transmitted form is intensified or the lighter triliteral, and how the latter is vocalized.
- 7:124/q02/p1 Both verbs have lighter, triliteral readings in place of the intensified forms. — Al-Kashshāf mentions lightening the lām in both verbs. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports alternate vocalizations from Ḥumayd al-Makkī, Ibn Muḥayṣin and Mujāhid, including a report of kasra on the second verb's lām. Al-Samīn names the same readers and notes two imperfect-vowel patterns for ṣalaba. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(حميد المكي وابن محصن ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد بن جبر)]
- 7:124/q02/p2 The crucifixion verb is read with a doubled lām by the Qurʾānic readers. — Al-Ṭabrisī states this while deriving ṣalb from fastening to wood; the other sources also record lighter readings, so his statement is retained as his formulation. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 7:124/q03 What does 'min khilāf' mean and how does it attach to the cutting?  [مِّنْ خِلَٰفٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether khilāf identifies opposite sides of the body or the magicians' opposition, and whether min is circumstantial or causal.
- 7:124/q03/p1 One hand and the opposite foot are cut, conventionally the right hand and left foot, or the reverse; min describes the crosswise manner. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabrisī explicitly allow either right hand with left foot or left hand with right foot. Al-Kalbī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Bursawī give the right-hand/left-foot example; ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Yaḥyā also gives the right-hand/left-leg example. Al-Samīn calls min circumstantial, and al-Ālūsī agrees. Ibn ʿĀshūr says one hand and the opposite foot leaves a person able to walk or lean on a stick and points to 5:33. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also describes one hand and opposite foot. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد العزيز بن يحيى); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 7:124/q03/p2 Min is causal: the punishment is because of the magicians' opposition or perverseness. — Al-Samīn reports the causal analysis but says direct attachment to the verb is remote. Al-Ālūsī likewise calls it remote. Asad renders min khilāf 'because of perverseness' and refers to his note on 5:33. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:124/q04 What does crucifixion mean here?  [لَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether fastening to the support itself kills, serves as a nonfatal display, or names the general act of binding.
- 7:124/q04/p1 Ṣalb is fastening a person to wood or a raised support. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī define it as binding to wood. Al-Ālūsī gives that as the original sense. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes fastening to raised wood for public warning. Al-Biqāʿī allows a cross-shaped display. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:124/q04/p2 Crucifixion can mean hanging a person until death. — Al-Ālūsī says the word came to mean hanging by the neck until death. His Ibn ʿAbbās report describes binding beneath the arms and hanging until death. Ibn ʿĀshūr discusses killing on a cross with reference to 4:157. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:124/q04/p3 Crucifixion can be a painful display without killing on the support. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes suspension after amputation as a public warning. Al-Biqāʿī allows torture until bodily fluid runs. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:124/q05 Why does Pharaoh threaten crucifixion?  [لَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · interpretation]
- 7:124/q05/p1 Its public display would disgrace the magicians and deter others. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī name disgrace and deterrence. Bursawī places the display on palm trunks by the Nile for that purpose. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī call the suspension a public warning. Al-Biqāʿī says the drawn-out threat frightens the magicians and wavering spectators. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:124/q06 Where and on what were the victims to be crucified?  [لَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · reports]
- 7:124/q06/p1 On the Nile bank in Egypt, with reports specifying date-palm trunks there. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Kalbī locate the threatened crucifixion on the Nile bank. Al-Ṭabrisī reports date-palm trunks along the Nile; Bursawī and the Study Quran specify palm trees there. Ibn Kathīr and the Study Quran cite the parallel wording at 20:71 for palm trunks. [6 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]

## 7:124/q07 What sequence does 'then' imply between cutting and crucifixion?  [ثُمَّ لَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether crucifixion kills or displays a surviving amputee, and what temporal force the parallel conjunctions carry.
- 7:124/q07/p1 Cutting precedes crucifixion, with an interval before the second punishment. — Al-Biqāʿī says the delayed 'then' prolongs terror. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a nonfatal suspension after amputation allows time for healing. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-Aʿrāf grants respite whereas the waw of 26:49 in al-Shuʿarāʾ gives none and suits Pharaoh's hotter anger; his other treatment expressly contrasts the two wordings. Ibn al-Jawzī (Nuzhat al-Aʿyun) says thumma retains its ordinary sense of sequence and interval in many passages. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 7:124/q07/p2 The 'then' is compatible with the waw used in parallel passages because waw can also permit an interval. — Al-Samīn supplies the grammatical reconciliation of the two conjunctions. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 7:124/q07/p3 If crucifixion means killing, the punishments target different groups rather than following each other for each victim. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that cutting someone crosswise before killing them on a cross would make the crosswise detail pointless; he points to 4:157 for the killing sense. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:124/q08 What force does 'all' have, and whom does it cover?  [أَجْمَعِينَ · grammar]
- 7:124/q08/p1 It emphatically includes all the magicians addressed by Pharaoh. — Al-Samīn says ajmaʿīn is emphatic even without a preceding kull. Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Bayḍāwī and others explicitly describe the threat as extending to all. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes the universal threat from reports that only some were actually cut and crucified. [9 notes: SAMIN-DURR; MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 7:124/q09 Did Pharaoh carry out the threatened punishment?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the execution reports or the prayer to die as Muslims establish the outcome; the verse itself only records Pharaoh's words.
- 7:124/q09/p1 He did execute or mutilate the converts, though one report says only some suffered it. — Al-Suddī reports killing and cutting. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports cutting and crucifying some despite the threat to all; Ibn ʿAbbās and others say the converts were sorcerers in the morning and martyrs by evening. Al-Rāzī calls the Ibn ʿAbbās account of cross-amputation more apparent, as an example to deter others. He also reports an argument from the officials' later warning about Moses and his people in 7:127, while noting the reply that the magicians could fall under 'his people.' [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)+]
- 7:124/q09/p2 He did not carry out the threat; the magicians' death came from God. — Al-Rāzī reports others' inference from their prayer to die as Muslims and calls that inference close. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]
- 7:124/q09/p3 The verse states a threat but does not establish whether it was carried out. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes the text from reports of execution. Al-Rāzī likewise says the verse itself leaves the outcome open; his discussion of 7:127 records both an argument for execution and the reply that the magicians may be included among Moses' people. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 7:124/q10 Was Pharaoh the first to impose crosswise amputation or crucifixion?  [* · reports]
- 7:124/q10/p1 Pharaoh was the first to use crosswise amputation, crucifixion, or both. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for both acts by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr is cited by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī for crosswise amputation, with Ibn Abī Ḥātim also naming crucifixion. Al-Kashshāf reports the combined claim without naming a transmitter. Al-Ṭabrisī says a report gives both and specifies palm trunks. Al-Nasafī and the Study Quran report the first use of this punishment and crucifixion. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī transmit the first institution of crucifixion specifically. Al-Ālūsī's Ibn ʿAbbās report describes binding under the arms and hanging until death. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭs, Z)]

## 7:124/q11 How is Pharaoh's punishment related to the penalty for highway robbery?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether the link is a report about the later institution of the penalty or a comparison with its Qurʾānic form.
- 7:124/q11/p1 God prescribed crucifixion for highway robbers after Pharaoh's first use of it, because their crime is grave warfare against God and His messenger. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports this view and says the penalty is understood sequentially from divine mercy. Bursawī repeats the prior institution and later prescription. Al-Ālūsī invokes 5:33 and the gravity of the crime. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:124/q11/p2 The threatened punishment resembles the Qurʾānic penalty for warfare against God and His messenger and spreading corruption. — The Study Quran points to 5:33; Ibn ʿĀshūr also cites that verse when discussing opposite-side amputation. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:124/q12 How does the wording make Pharaoh's threat forceful?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Which feature of the wording supplies the emphasis: oath-like form or movement from an unstated threat to specified punishments.
- 7:124/q12/p1 It uses an oath-like sentence for emphasis. — Al-Samīn identifies the oath-like construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 7:124/q12/p2 A general warning is made terrifying by leaving its object unsaid, then specifying cutting and crucifixion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the object of what they will come to know is omitted to terrify. The Study Quran says the warning refers to the planned terrible punishment. Al-Kashshāf says the general threat is specified by the two acts. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Th); KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 7:124/q13 Why did Pharaoh threaten the magicians after their conversion?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate explanation is Pharaoh's defeat or his political story about an alleged prior conspiracy.
- 7:124/q13/p1 Moses' victory and Pharaoh's loss of divine support provoked the threat. — Al-Ṭabarī says Pharaoh threatened crucifixion after seeing God abandon him and Moses overcome him. Elmalılı situates the plot claim and punishments after the public defeat and conversion. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 7:124/q13/p2 He used an alleged conspiracy with Moses to discredit their conversion and protect his political power. — Al-Kashshāf says Pharaoh alleged a pre-exodus plot to expel Copts and settle Israelites, as propaganda against the conversion. Al-Ṭabrisī says he sought to fool the crowd into seeing politics instead of truth; he also reports an alternative that the magicians really arranged such a plan in Egypt before arriving. Ibn Kathīr reports his accusation that Moses and the magicians planned to seize the city and expel its leaders. Elmalılı reports Pharaoh's plot claim. Qur'an Yolu transmits al-Rāzī's view that public defeat made Pharaoh fear popular allegiance to Moses and the magicians, with loss of unpaid labor as his unspoken concern. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî)]

## 7:124/q14 What does the report about Moses and the chief magician say?  [* · reports]
- 7:124/q14/p1 Moses asked whether the chief magician would believe if defeated; the magician promised belief if his supposedly unbeatable magic lost, and Pharaoh heard. — Al-Kashshāf transmits this account. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 7:124/q15 How should Pharaoh's preceding challenge about believing without permission be understood?  [* · readings]
turns on: The reading as declaration or question, and whether the spoken question functions as a threat.
- 7:124/q15/p1 Its declarative reading rebukes the magicians, and the permission claim asserts control over their beliefs. — Al-Kashshāf gives the rebuking declarative reading. Qur'an Yolu says their belief was public, so Pharaoh's words function as a threat; the subsequent punishments confirm this. It says his demand for permission shows attempted control of actions, beliefs and consciences. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:124/q15/p2 Its interrogative reading expresses denial and incredulity. — Al-Kashshāf records this reading alongside the declarative one. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 7:124/q16 What does the magicians' endurance of the threat show?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether endurance is explained as public proof of sincere faith or as the strength given by direct witnessing.
- 7:124/q16/p1 Their unshaken resolve publicly demonstrates sincere faith and the truth of Moses' miracle. — Elmalılı says a sham collusion would have collapsed under Pharaoh's threat, whereas sincere believers became firmer. Their recognized skill at magic made suspicion understandable, so public testing dispelled it. He says divine wisdom required decisive public proof that their conversion was sincere and Moses' sign was not magic. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 7:124/q16/p2 Direct witnessing enables them to endure afflictions otherwise unbearable. — Al-Sulamī transmits Samnūn's inward explanation for why the magicians ignored Pharaoh's threat. [1 notes: SULAMI(سمنون)]

## 7:124/q17 What does the magicians' reply about returning to their Lord mean in answer to the threat?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether return to God stresses inevitable death, martyrdom and reward, or the final judgment of both parties.
- 7:124/q17/p1 Death is inevitable regardless of Pharaoh, so the threat cannot ultimately harm them. — Elmalılı gives this as one possible meaning. Ibn ʿĀshūr also says their return to God answers Pharaoh's inability to harm them. [2 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:124/q17/p2 Execution would make them martyrs who meet God and receive His mercy and reward. — Elmalılı says they could welcome death for truth. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds hope for forgiveness and punishment of Pharaoh, or welcome martyrdom and meeting God. [2 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:124/q17/p3 Pharaoh and magicians will all die and stand before God for judgment between oppressor and oppressed. — Elmalılı records this third possible meaning. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 7:124/q18 Why is the magicians' 'no harm' response stated in al-Shuʿarāʾ rather than here?  [* · links]
- 7:124/q18/p1 The more severe, detailed al-Shuʿarāʾ scene states their fearlessness once, thereby covering the milder, shorter al-Aʿrāf account. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-Shuʿarāʾ gives no respite before crucifixion, making its threat hotter; 'no harm' fits that fuller scene. Fearlessness there entails fearlessness under al-Aʿrāf's lesser threat, whereas placing the statement only here could suggest otherwise. Repeating it would impair concision. He contrasts al-Aʿrāf's account of Israel's history, signs, sins and ingratitude with al-Shuʿarāʾ's movement to Pharaoh's end and Israel's rescue, and says each wording suits its scene's artistic design. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 7:124/q19 How is tanqimū in the magicians' reply read and understood?  [* · readings]
- 7:124/q19/p1 The common reading has kasra on the qāf, while al-Ḥasan reads fatḥa; both mean taking vengeance or punishing. — Al-Jishumī reports both vocalizations and their shared sense from al-Ḥasan. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن)]

## 7:124/q20 Where does this confrontation sit in the sūra's account?  [* · links]
- 7:124/q20/p1 Verses 104–126 give one of the Quran's most detailed accounts of Moses confronting Pharaoh and the sorcerers. — The Study Quran locates this scene within that extended account. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
