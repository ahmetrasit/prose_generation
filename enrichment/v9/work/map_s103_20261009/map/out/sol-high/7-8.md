# 7:8: 24 questions

## 7:8/q01 What does weighing mean in this verse?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the text and transmitted reports require a physical instrument, and what supplies a just measure for deeds.
- 7:8/q01/p1 A real weighing with a created balance determines the fate of deeds and people. — Al-Tabari appeals to Prophetic reports, the ordinary sense, and lack of any rational or transmitted obstacle. Zajjaj and Ibn al-Anbari give sound reports, apparent wording, and broad scholarly agreement priority. Ibn Atiyya cites the visible balance in a Prophetic report; al-Amidi objects that justice itself cannot be heavy or light. Al-Qurtubi cites reports against a purely figurative reading. Al-Tabrisi considers this possible despite preferring justice. [38 notes: TAB-FULL(عمرو بن دينار); ZAJJAJ(قال بعضهم); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الأمة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جمهور الأمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(الآمدي,الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,مجاهد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن الأنباري)+; ZAJJAJ+; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(المعتزلة)-; TABATABAI(هشام بن الحكم)-]
- 7:8/q01/p2 Weighing is just judgment, exact reckoning, or recompense; balance language conveys fairness figuratively. — Mujahid, al-Dahhak, and al-A'mash transmit the justice/judgment gloss. Zajjaj and al-Wahidi note ordinary figurative usage, including a poet's 'scale' for a disputant; Ibn Atiyya cites Abu Talib. Al-Razi argues that transient acts cannot be physically weighed and an omniscient God's weighing would serve no purpose; al-Tabrisi cites Arabic usage. Ibn Ashur says the mode remains uncertain and language of the unseen approximates it. The Study Quran connects the image to fair commercial measures and cosmic balance. [48 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(الضحاك,قال بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة,مجاهد، الضحاك); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك والأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك والأعمش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد والأعمش والضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL(الراعي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); TABATABAI(هشام بن الحكم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI+; TAB-FULL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)-; ALUSI-FULL(الآمدي,الزجاج)-]
- 7:8/q01/p3 The measure is the truth proper to each deed; good has weight in proportion to its truth, while false deeds lack weight. — Al-Tabatabai argues that a prayer, alms, or speech must meet its own truth standard, rather than comparing two piles. Quranic parallels 23:102–103 and 101:6–10 pair good with heaviness, evil with lightness; 21:47 requires a just criterion even for mixed deeds. The book discloses truth in each act (39:69; 45:29). Prophets and successors embody the measure of right belief and action. [13 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام); TABATABAI+]
- 7:8/q01/p4 Weighing judges deeds relative to their due reward or punishment, with complete fairness. — Al-Baydawi describes comparison with recompense; Ibn Ashur specifies exact dues and allows divine pardon or raised rank. Kuran Yolu says quantity and quality both count, with increased reward or forgiven sin consistent with 6:160; Elmalili pictures final closing of credit and debt. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); ELMALILI]

## 7:8/q02 What does the noun ٱلْوَزْنُ mean in Arabic?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the noun denotes physical measurement or an established extension to assessment and equivalence.
- 7:8/q02/p1 It is the verbal noun of weighing: measuring or comparing things to determine amount or relative measure. — Al-Tabari gives وزن, وزنا, وزنة. Jishumi and al-Tabrisi describe comparing two objects; Jishumi adds extension to prosody and measured speech. Al-Raghib, reported by al-Alusi, defines determining measure, usually with a balance. Sībawayh distinguishes verbal-noun وزن in the accusative from concrete adjectival وزن in وزن سبعة. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; SIBAWAYH]
- 7:8/q02/p2 It can denote figurative equivalence, worth, or measured expression. — Al-Tabrisi explains poetic measured speech and mental equivalence. Al-Razi and al-Wahidi cite a poet's measure for every opponent; Ibn Ashur cites al-Ra'i's weighing for deliberation and expressions light on the tongue but heavy in the scale. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ(قال بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الراعي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN]

## 7:8/q03 How are ٱلْوَزْنُ يَوْمَئِذٍ ٱلْحَقُّ parsed?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ يَوْمَئِذٍ ٱلْحَقُّ · grammar]
turns on: Which term supplies the predicate, how the day phrase attaches, and whether الحق is adjective, predicate, appositive, or verbal noun.
- 7:8/q03/p1 ٱلْوَزْنُ is subject, يَوْمَئِذٍ its predicate, and ٱلْحَقُّ qualifies the weighing. — Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Alusi give this analysis. Alusi prefers its sense and the freedom of adverbs; Abu al-Baqa objects to separation from the noun, but Samin replies that the relative clause is already complete, while noting a predicate problem on one reconstruction. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,مكي); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 7:8/q03/p2 ٱلْحَقُّ is the predicate; يَوْمَئِذٍ is a temporal modifier of the weighing. — Al-Wahidi explicitly gives subject and predicate. Al-Asbahani objects that a definite verbal noun rarely governs an adverb but cites 4:148 as a precedent. Samin calls a broad-object analysis of the day weak. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان وعلي بن الحسن النحوي الأصفهاني); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المعربين)]
- 7:8/q03/p3 ٱلْحَقُّ is an accusative verbal noun while يَوْمَئِذٍ supplies the predicate. — Al-Farra, reported by Jishumi, and Qurtubi mention this alternative. Samin treats hypothetical accusative reading discussion as unnecessary given the fixed wording. [3 notes: JISHUMI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 7:8/q03/p4 ٱلْحَقُّ is an appositive to the pronoun implied in the adverb, or a predicate of an omitted subject. — Makki and al-Isfahani give the appositive analysis; Alusi calls it sound but favors the first parse. Abu al-Baqa also suggests an omitted subject or that الوزن predicates an omitted subject; Alusi finds these strained. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء,الأصفهاني)]

## 7:8/q04 What does يَوْمَئِذٍ refer to and how is its ending explained?  [يَوْمَئِذٍ · grammar]
- 7:8/q04/p1 It is the Day of Resurrection when nations and messengers are questioned and deeds recounted; tanwīn replaces the omitted clause after إذ. — Nasafi, Samin, and Ibn Ashur supply the elided questioning clause, referring to 7:6–7. Samin attributes the compensatory tanwin to the majority and notes al-Akhfash disagrees. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:8/q05 What does ٱلْحَقُّ say of the weighing?  [ٱلْحَقُّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether truth qualifies the fairness of the process or names the standard by which each deed has weight.
- 7:8/q05/p1 It is just, exact, sound, and proportionate, without injustice. — Mujahid glosses it as justice. Biqai says the result matches truth without excess. Ibn Ashur says justice if figurative, even proportion if a balance is depicted; verbal-noun form intensifies reality. Ibn Ashur and Kuran Yolu allow forgiveness and enhanced reward within justice. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 7:8/q05/p2 It is the truth present in a deed that gives that deed weight. — Al-Tabatabai argues that each act has its fitting measure of truth, and that acts without that truth are weightless; the book reveals its measure. [4 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 7:8/q06 Is there a reported alternate wording or reading of ٱلْحَقُّ?  [ٱلْحَقُّ · readings]
- 7:8/q06/p1 A reading substitutes ٱلْقِسْطُ, 'justice'. — Al-Alusi records the substitution while also citing al-Raghib's definition of weighing. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:8/q07 What is placed in the balance?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether nonbodily acts can have physical weight, and how reports about deeds, records, and persons relate.
- 7:8/q07/p1 Deeds themselves are weighed, sometimes embodied as beautiful good acts and ugly bad acts. — Al-Suddi and others simply say deeds. Ibn Abbas reports forms; Ibn Kathir cites traditions depicting Qur'an, suras, and righteous acts visibly. Some theologians propose accidents becoming bodies; Qurtubi rejects that mechanism, whereas others deem it sound. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المتكلمين,مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,جابر); ALUSI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(some commentators); STUDYQURAN]
- 7:8/q07/p2 Written records of deeds are weighed, with God giving them heaviness or lightness. — Al-Tabari describes records in opposed pans. Ibn Umar argues that actions are not bodies. The ninety-nine sin-scrolls and small testimony card supply repeated evidence; Qurtubi prefers this account, as do reports attributed to most exegetes. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say God creates weight in records; al-Qurtubi adds the believer's delivered record. [30 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); THALABI(عبد الله بن عمر); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(عبد الله بن عمر); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر,صفوان بن محرز,عبد الله بن عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(some commentators); STUDYQURAN(R); QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 7:8/q07/p3 Persons themselves are weighed; physical bulk does not secure spiritual weight. — Ubayd ibn Umayr repeatedly reports a huge eater and drinker lighter than a mosquito or fly wing; al-Baydawi and Ibn Ashur cite the Prophetic report. Ibn Kathir adds Ibn Masud's thin legs outweighing Uhud. Al-Alusi notes the report does not explicitly establish that persons enter the pans and asks about the other pan. [15 notes: MUJAHID(عبيد بن عمير الليثي); TAB-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); IBNABIHATIM(عبيد بن عمير); MAWARDI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(عبيد بن عمير); RAZI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI]
- 7:8/q07/p4 Visible signs of good and bad deeds appear in the two pans. — Al-Mawardi and al-Jubba'i propose that the signs display relative deeds to people. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(الجبائي)]
- 7:8/q07/p5 Deeds, records or forms, and their human agents can all be weighed on separate occasions. — Ibn Kathir explicitly reconciles the three reports in this way. Biqai leaves the mode open, including direct disclosure to hearts. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 7:8/q07/p6 Only the final outcomes of deeds are weighed. — Wahb ibn Munabbih says God grants a good or evil ending according to His will. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 7:8/q08 What sort of instrument or figure is the balance?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether reports describe a literal afterlife object, approximate its reality, or use balance as a name for another criterion.
- 7:8/q08/p1 A familiar physical balance has a beam or tongue and two pans. — Amr ibn Dinar, Ibn Abbas, Hasan, and majority reports describe it. Baghawi reports an east-to-west span; Ibn Salam places it facing the Throne with pans toward Paradise and Hell; Aisha and Salman describe vast pans. Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Ashur question reports detailing its size or form. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(عمرو بن دينار); ZAJJAJ(قال بعضهم); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الأمة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول,ابن عباس,عبد الله بن سلام); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جمهور الأمة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الآمدي,داود,عائشة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(سلمان,عائشة); TABATABAI-]
- 7:8/q08/p2 The balance is God's just criterion or disclosure of deeds, so its afterlife reality need not resemble a worldly object. — Al-Tabatabai interprets bodily-balance narrations as accessible depictions and says the Imam denies only worldly physical weight. Ibn Ashur leaves form unknown; Kuran Yolu says its manner and instrument are known to God. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ(قال بعضهم); TABATABAI(هشام بن الحكم); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]
- 7:8/q08/p3 The book recording deeds is itself the 'balance'. — Zajjaj and Qurtubi separately report this identification. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(قال بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 7:8/q09 Why weigh or record deeds when God already knows them?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether public disclosure provides a purpose despite divine omniscience, and whether deeds as attributes can bear weight.
- 7:8/q09/p1 It publicly establishes proof, justice, reward, or blame and removes excuses. — Al-Tabari likens weighing to recording in 45:29 and testimony of limbs. Tha'labi says it tests worldly faith and distinguishes saved and doomed. Ibn al-Anbari, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, and Study Quran stress public evidence; literal-balance proponents say the result increases joy or shame. Al-Tabatabai says it makes the real qualities of acts known. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أصحاب القول الأول); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جمهور الأمة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 7:8/q09/p2 Weighing is unnecessary as an information-gathering act of God; acts as transient attributes cannot be weighed physically. — Al-Razi argues believers already trust justice while skeptics might distrust a display. Imam al-Sadiq says God does not need to discover the amount; al-Tabatabai distinguishes moral qualities from bodily movements using marriage and adultery. Some Mu'tazilites hold a useless divine act improper. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد والأعمش والضحاك); TABATABAI(هشام بن الحكم); RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TAB-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)-]

## 7:8/q10 How does the weighing relate to the preceding questioning and later judgment?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ يَوْمَئِذٍ · links]
- 7:8/q10/p1 It follows the questioning and recounting of deeds, making their exact consequences manifest. — Al-Razi places it after questioning and accounting. Biqai connects it to God's 'We' in the prior verse. Ibn Ashur ties it to 7:7 and makes the conditional clauses its outcome; the Study Quran compares weighing with questioning in 7:6. Ibn al-Jawzi says God reports what messengers and nations did while never absent from them. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 7:8/q10/p2 It accords with other passages on just balances and revealed records. — Tha'labi and Wahidi cite 21:47; Ibn Kathir cites 21:47, 4:40, 101:6–11, and 23:101–103. The Study Quran connects judgment balances to honest commercial weights and a balanced creation. Ibn Abbas says the placed record speaks of people's deeds. [9 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]

## 7:8/q11 What does the plural مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ count?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the plural names instruments, their parts or instances, or the objects being weighed.
- 7:8/q11/p1 It denotes multiple actual balances, one per person, act, kind of worship, or kind of deed. — Hasan reports one balance per person; Razi prefers several on the plain plural and 21:47, perhaps for heart, bodily, and spoken acts. Tabrisi cites prayer as a balance; Elmalili totals each person's many scales. Ibn Atiyya rejects a separate balance per person and holds one instrument. [11 notes: THALABI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 7:8/q11/p2 It is one balance expressed plurally for persons, parts, pans, or repeated weighings. — Tha'labi cites al-A'sha's plural 'breasts' and 'forearms' for singular parts, and lists the assembly's pans, beam, and tongue. Ibn Jawzi and Ibn al-Anbari relate the plural to generic man and following plural pronouns. Abu Hayyan reports one balance for numerous objects; Qurtubi compares generic plural travel by ships or mules. Alusi proposes omitted 'pans'. [13 notes: THALABI(الأعشى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جمهور الأمة); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 7:8/q11/p3 It is plural for things weighed, especially a person's many deeds, rather than instruments. — Tha'labi, Zajjaj as reported by Razi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and others give this reading; Ibn Atiyya says plural highlights numerous acts. Tabatabai asks how such an account fixes their relative weight. [12 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL-]

## 7:8/q12 What makes someone's مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ heavy?  [ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether heaviness is a numerical preponderance of acts or their truth, quality, and special worth.
- 7:8/q12/p1 Many good deeds or good deeds prevailing over bad make them heavy. — Mujahid glosses heavy scales as good deeds. Muqatil says even an atom can tip them. Mawardi records both being judged obedient and good acts exceeding bad. Ibn Abbas says one extra good deed suffices; a grain can tip it, though he allows equality. Razi elsewhere denies an equal-total class. Elmalili compares credits and debts. Bursawi states that the weighing distinguishes good from bad and heavy from light; Jabir's report says the prevailing side determines Paradise or Hell, though Tabatabai accepts its content without making it an interpretation of this verse. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد، الضحاك); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتزلة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,جابر,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(جابر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 7:8/q12/p2 The truth, sincerity, and inner reality of a deed give it weight; evil and ostentation lack it. — Tabatabai reads each act against its proper truth, citing 23:102–103 and 101:6–10. Ibn Ashur contrasts fighting for God's word and for booty. Ali says inward substance outweighing outward appearance makes a heavy balance. Qashani says lasting virtues and sincere intentions weigh, while passing desires do not. [13 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); QASHANI; DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد والأعمش والضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI+]
- 7:8/q12/p3 Certain small but valuable acts outweigh large apparent burdens. — The ninety-nine-scroll hadith gives a card with the two testimonies; al-Qadi says testimony needs its duties. Al-Qurtubi reports a different card for prayers upon the Prophet, and Alusi records debate whether the testimony is a later good deed or final monotheistic words. Prophetic reports call good character the heaviest thing; another report describes taught knowledge tipping the scale. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); THALABI(عبد الله بن عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي,عبد الله بن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري,عبد الله بن عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحارث الأعور); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,القرطبي عن الحكيم الترمذي,عبد الله بن عمرو,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,غيره); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 7:8/q13 Can anyone's good and bad deeds be equal, and what follows?  [ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the two conditional outcomes exhaust all people, and whether equality constitutes a separate outcome.
- 7:8/q13/p1 There is no class whose good and bad totals are equal. — Razi divides humanity into heavy and light alone. Tabatabai says equal deeds cannot make a balance both heavy and light, and rejects one use of a Heights report as inconsistent with its surrounding verses. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:8/q13/p2 Equal deeds identify the people of al-Aʿrāf, the Heights. — Ibn Abbas says a single grain may tip a balance but equal totals place a person among the Heights; a report through Jabir also gives this result. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); TABATABAI-]
- 7:8/q13/p3 A person without any discernible preponderance may have no weighing. — Tabatabai suggests this for nullified unbelievers and vulnerable people not fully addressed by proof or duty, citing 7:8. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:8/q14 Whose deeds enter the weighing?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether disbelievers receive a physical weighing or their nullified acts have no weight.
- 7:8/q14/p1 Believers and unbelievers are assessed; believers' good deeds lead to Paradise and unbelievers' lack of good weight to ruin. — Ibn Abbas describes a believer and disbeliever in the two-pan account. Wahidi Wajiz says heavy scales belong to believers. Razi says light balances are generally unbelievers because they deny God's signs. [6 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); TABRISI(عبيد بن عمير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI]
- 7:8/q14/p2 Scales and records are set up for Muslims; polytheists' nullified deeds have no assigned weight. — Ali ibn al-Husayn's report has polytheists driven in groups to Hell. Tabatabai cites 18:105 and says unbelief erases deeds; Ibn Ashur says this passage chiefly addresses believers with good and polytheists without good. [5 notes: TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين عليه السلام); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:8/q15 How can a sinful believer be successful yet still enter Hell?  [ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether success means immediate deliverance, ultimate deliverance, or a case outside this passage.
- 7:8/q15/p1 Monotheism is weighed after other deeds and ultimately makes the scale heavy on release from Hell. — Ibn Atiyya reports this solution twice, following his statement of the difficulty. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طائفة)]
- 7:8/q15/p2 Eventual escape from Hell itself counts as success despite temporary punishment. — Ibn Atiyya gives this answer twice. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 7:8/q15/p3 Mixed sinful believers are outside the immediate purpose of this passage. — Ibn Ashur says the contrast here is between believers who did good and polytheists without good; their case is discussed elsewhere. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:8/q16 What does ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ mean?  [ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · meaning]
- 7:8/q16/p1 Those who gain what they seek: salvation, reward, victory, and escape from fear. — Ibn Abbas gives attainment and escape; Ibn Atiyya cites poetry for aflaḥa as attainment and another verse for survival. Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai, Bursawi, and Alusi gloss salvation or reward. [16 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:8/q17 How do مَن, فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ, هُمُ, and ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ work together?  [مَن فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · grammar]
- 7:8/q17/p1 Generic singular مَن permits plural demonstrative and pronouns; هُمُ restricts success to that group. — Bursawi and Ibn Ashur note agreement with the generic plural sense. Ibn Ashur says the demonstrative attributes success to heavy measures and its distance signals evaluative distance; المفلحون can be generic or recall 2:5. Alusi discusses the predicate analyses and its definite, prototypical sense. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:8/q18 What events or agents are reported at the balance?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports]
turns on: The differing transmitted descriptions of the balance's location, supervision, announcement, and human experience.
- 7:8/q18/p1 Gabriel oversees the balance and settles claims between people by transfer of good or bad deeds. — Hudhayfa's report in Tabari, Tha'labi, Ibn Atiyya, and al-Durr says good deeds pass from oppressor to victim; if exhausted, the victim's sins pass to the oppressor, leaving a mountain-like burden. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(حذيفة); THALABI(حذيفة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حذيفة بن اليمان); DURR-FULL(حذيفة)]
- 7:8/q18/p2 A caller or angel announces each person's lasting happiness or misery when the balance tips. — Abd Allah ibn al-Ayzar and Anas supply variant transmitters for the announcement. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أنس,عبد الله بن العيزار)]
- 7:8/q18/p3 At the balance people are too preoccupied to recognize or remember others. — Hasan's report through Aisha cites 80:37; Aisha and Qatada place the same distraction also at scattering records and the bridge, with 69:19 cited for books. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(عائشة,قتادة)]
- 7:8/q18/p4 The balance is placed between two trees near Jerusalem. — Ka'b supplies this location. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(كعب)]
- 7:8/q18/p5 A huge balance overwhelms David, and half a date of charity can fill one pan by God's pleasure. — Alusi relays this tradition as an illustration of divine valuation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(داود)]

## 7:8/q19 What does the testimony card contain, and why does it outweigh sin scrolls?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Which act the small card records, and whether opposite pans must contain faith against unbelief.
- 7:8/q19/p1 A small card bearing faith in God's oneness and Muhammad's messengership outweighs ninety-nine vast sin records. — Ibn Amr's report is cited across sources. Al-Tirmidhi calls it hasan gharib and says God's name outweighs all; al-Hakim authenticates it. Al-Qadi says faith must be accompanied by its obligations. Ibn Ashur also mentions advice to seek the Prophet at the scale. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); THALABI(عبد الله بن عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي,عبد الله بن عمر); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,غيره); IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 7:8/q19/p2 The card represents the believer's later utterance of testimony as a good act, rather than initial monotheistic faith. — Al-Hakim al-Tirmidhi, reported by Qurtubi and Alusi, reasons that opposed pans cannot contain both faith and disbelief in one person. Others reject the requirement for opposites and allow final words of monotheism. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي عن الحكيم الترمذي); ALUSI-FULL(غيره)-]
- 7:8/q19/p3 Another card bears the reward for a believer's prayers upon the Prophet and saves him when other deeds are light. — Al-Qushayri reports the Prophet putting in the card; Alusi transmits a version with Adam watching descendants and Muhammad interceding. Alusi conditions its implication for the Prophet's rank on the report being sound. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو)]
- 7:8/q19/p4 Bitāqa means a small paper slip in Egyptian usage. — Al-Qushayri gives its first vowel as kasra; Muhammad ibn Yahya in Ibn Majah confirms the Egyptian usage. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري,محمد بن يحيى)]

## 7:8/q20 What is the inward or moral scale?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · inward]
turns on: Which human faculties or ethical criteria a spiritual application treats as the pans, tongue, or weighted acts.
- 7:8/q20/p1 One weighs self, thoughts, and breaths by justice and truth, becoming humble or content with divine witnessing. — Sulami reports a spiritual instruction to weigh the self by justice and thoughts and breaths by truth. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 7:8/q20/p2 Soul and spirit weigh obedience by scripture; heart and intellect weigh reward by promise and warning; gnosis weighs divine approval. — Sulami distinguishes the scales and calls flight and seeking the two pans of gnosis and secret. [2 notes: SULAMI]
- 7:8/q20/p3 The inner scale has justice for its tongue, sense and intellect for pans; lasting virtue and sincere intention give it weight. — Qashani applies it at the lesser resurrection, yielding the heart's paradise of divine attributes; temporary sensations, desire, and vice lack weight. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 7:8/q20/p4 Reason, knowledge, and sound will function as balances for truth, lawful conduct, and felicity. — Tha'labi relays a moral interpretation distinguishing true and false, licit and illicit, and happiness and misery. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 7:8/q20/p5 Different balances suit bodily, spiritual, secret, and hidden ethical qualities. — Bursawi relays the Najmi interpretation and the Prophetic saying that good character is heaviest because humans emulate God's qualities. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,النبي)]

## 7:8/q21 How is physical heaviness itself explained?  [ثَقُلَتْ · theology]
turns on: Whether heaviness is an additional attribute or reducible to constituent parts.
- 7:8/q21/p1 Heaviness is a distinct downward tendency of a heavy object. — Abu Hashim, reported by Jishumi, treats it as a positive attribute. Tabrisi defines weight as downward force and lightness as upward force. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم); TABRISI]
- 7:8/q21/p2 Heaviness reduces to an object's composing parts; lightness is only absence of heaviness. — Abu Ali, reported by Jishumi, disagrees with Abu Hashim about heaviness, though both deny positive lightness. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)]

## 7:8/q22 What grammatical examples elsewhere clarify وزن as a noun of measure?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether measure expressions act as verbal nouns, concrete adjectives, or independent specifications.
- 7:8/q22/p1 Accusative measure specifies a quantity independently, while وزن سبعة can be a verbal noun in the accusative or a concrete adjective. — Sibawayh uses هذا درهم وزنا and عشرون درهما for specifications not identical with their head nouns. He distinguishes named descriptions from nonidentical measures; al-Khalil analyzes وزن سبعة by verbal versus concrete meaning. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 7:8/q22/p2 Related Arab expressions permit variant syntactic analyses and unusual exemplars. — Abu al-Khattab's هذا عربي حسبه is illustrative rather than normal speech; Yunus reports عشرون أضعافها beside common عشرون أضعافا. Sibawayh treats definite ضرب الأمير as a new predicate, not an indefinite adjective, and derives سواء in هذا درهم سواء from an otherwise unattested verbal noun. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو الخطاب,يونس)]

## 7:8/q23 What other arguments about sound and meter are associated with the word وزن in the supplied notes?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which aspect of verbal form, if any, is treated as the basis of inimitability.
- 7:8/q23/p1 Consonant harmony alone cannot account for Quranic inimitability; arrangement of meanings is decisive. — Jurjani allows harmony as an eloquence feature or ease of pronunciation, but says sound without meaningful order could otherwise qualify, and difficulty preserving sound with sense concedes priority to sense. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 7:8/q23/p2 Recognition of the Quran's miraculous character differs by audience. — Al-Ashari distinguishes immediate perception of appearance from inferred recognition of miracle; Burhan says non-Arabs infer it, while eloquent Arabs directly perceive inability to match it. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو الحسن الأشعري)]
- 7:8/q23/p3 Quranic miracle accounts based on meter, eloquence, unseen reports, or lack of contradiction have different objections. — Alusi rejects unusual meter by citing metrical verses and Musaylima, eloquence by uncertain comparisons, unseen reports by lack of a threshold, and no-contradiction by 36:69 beside metrical verses. He reports the no-contradiction view and says inimitability itself is certain. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الجاحظ,بعض المعتزلة,قيل); BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 7:8/q24 What separate reading and lexical comparisons mention the name al-Wazn?  [ٱلْوَزْنُ · readings]
turns on: These are distinct comparative uses in the supplied excerpts, not competing readings of 7:8.
- 7:8/q24/p1 A hamza can be prolonged or pronounced between full realization and substitution in an unrelated reading. — Ibn al-Jazari reports several authorities for hamza plus madd in أأعجمي, favors bayna bayn as al-Dani's wording and analogy, and notes Dhu al-Rumma's meter supports long or intermediate readings, not substitution. [5 notes: NASHR(ابن مجاهد والنقاش وابن شنبوذ وغيرهم,الحسن بن حبيب,ذو الرمة); NASHR+]
- 7:8/q24/p2 Al-Wazn is a star name in an Arabic example of uncertain identification. — Bint al-Shati cites al-Wazn and Hadar sometimes confused with Suhayl to explain محلف; she distinguishes Qur'anic حلف, used for false oaths, from أقسم and argues dictionaries' synonymy misses usage. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
