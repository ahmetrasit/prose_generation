# 101:6: 21 questions

## 101:6/q01 How does man function grammatically?  [مَن · grammar]
- 101:6/q01/p1 It is the subject, with the following clause as predicate. — Al-Nahhas states the subject-predicate analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 101:6/q02 Does the singular man describe one person or a group?  [مَن · grammar]
- 101:6/q02/p1 The singular relative denotes a plural group. — Islahi makes the collective reference explicit. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 101:6/q03 What makes these balances heavy?  [ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether weight describes predominating good deeds, adherence to truth, intrinsic worth and approval, divine determination, or inward perfections.
- 101:6/q03/p1 The person's good deeds outweigh evil deeds or otherwise prevail. — Muqatil, al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Zamakhshari, al-Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Elmalili, Islahi, and Study Quran explain the weight as prevailing good. Al-Tabrisi stresses abundance; al-Biqa'i says the many kinds of good deeds outweigh falsehood. Ibn Atiyya adds faith and says a believer's balance is never disastrously light. Ibn Abbas describes beautiful obedient deeds outweighing ugly unbelieving deeds. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل والكلبي); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 101:6/q03/p2 Adherence to truth gives them weight; falsehood is light. — Abu Bakr's counsel to Umar says a balance filled only with truth or good deeds rightly grows heavy, whereas one filled with falsehood or sins is light. Muqatil agrees; al-Nasafi and al-Bursevi adopt the point. Islahi says only truth, meaning good deeds, receives weight and invokes 7:8-9. [11 notes: MAWARDI(أبو بكر); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,مقاتل); KASHSHAF(أبو بكر); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو بكر); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 101:6/q03/p3 Heaviness is the weight and worth of deeds before God, signifying His approval. — Al-Farra's comparison of weights supports valued deeds; Qutb speaks of values counted by God. Ibn Ashur calls it metonymy for approval: Arabic weight denotes merit, honor and sound judgment, while 18:105 uses lack of weight for lack of merit. Kur'an Yolu links abundant good to approval. [11 notes: FARRA; RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 101:6/q03/p4 God makes the scales heavy or light as He wills under pre-eternal decree. — Al-Wasiti cites a report that the scales are in God's hand and He raises or lowers people. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 101:6/q03/p5 In the inward reading, real sciences, virtues and perfections carry the weight. — Al-Qashani identifies the weight with real sciences, psychic virtues and the perfections of heart and spirit; enduring righteous deeds ascend, whereas perishable pleasures have no weight. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 101:6/q04 How does the verse's wording convey its favorable sense?  [ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 101:6/q04/p1 Heaviness itself implies worthwhile righteous deeds, so the deeds need not be named. — Ibn Ashur says Arabic weight idiom already implies merit and approval; the Quranic opposite in 18:105 says some are given no weight. The omitted good deeds are understood. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 101:6/q05 What does mawazin mean here?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether mawazin names an actual scale, the valued things weighed, or a figure for judgment and proof.
- 101:6/q05/p1 It can be the plural of mawzun: weighed deeds of value and standing before God. — Al-Farra compares ordinary Arabic talk about matching a coin's weight; al-Nahhas, al-Razi, Elmalili and al-Alusi transmit this explanation. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Mawardi, al-Nasafi and al-Bursevi give both morphological possibilities. Kur'an Yolu prefers the deeds rendering; Elmalili says it needs no figurative account of the plural. [17 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); ELMALILI(Ferrâ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 101:6/q05/p2 It is the plural of mizan, referring to balances or scales. — Al-Mawardi and al-Zamakhshari set this beside the alternative plural of mawzun. Al-Alusi explains original mawzan and the change of quiescent waw to ya after kasra; Elmalili also gives the derivation. Study Quran distinguishes balance as revealed just order in 6:152, 7:85, 11:84-85, 42:17, 55:7-9, 57:25 from eschatological scales in 21:47. [12 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 101:6/q05/p3 The balances are a figure for reckoning or equitable judgment. — Mujahid denies a real scale; al-Mawardi illustrates reckoning through the saying that one's tongue is one's measure and a poem about disputants' speech. Ibn Atiyya says a balance is people's fairest measure. The Mu'tazila, Mujahid, al-Dahhak and al-A'mash reason that deeds are accidents that cannot be physically weighed; Elmalili reports many later scholars favoring just judgment. Al-Tabari and al-Farra cite Arabic weight for equivalence, and a poet's balance for answering speech. [15 notes: FARRA(شاعر); TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA(مجاهد); ALUSI(المعتزلة ومجاهد والضحاك والأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة,مجاهد والضحاك والاعمش); ELMALILI(Mücâhid, A‘meş ve Dahhâk); ELMALILI(Müteahhirînden birçokları)+; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-; ELMALILI-]
- 101:6/q05/p4 The balances are proofs and arguments. — Abd al-Aziz ibn Yahya cites the poet who calls each disputant's answering argument his balance. [3 notes: MAWARDI(عبد العزيز بن يحيى); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد العزيز بن يحيى); QURTUBI(عبد العزيز بن يحيى)]

## 101:6/q06 Why is the word mawazin plural?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the plural counts deeds, magnifies a single instrument, names its parts, or counts separate balances or aspects.
- 101:6/q06/p1 The plural reflects many kinds of deeds or things to be weighed. — Ibn Atiyya says each person has many distinct things weighed; al-Biqa'i stresses the many kinds of good deeds. Al-Alusi and Elmalili allow multiplicity by considerations; al-Qurtubi cites poetic plural usage for separate events. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA; QURTUBI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 101:6/q06/p2 The plural magnifies a single balance. — Al-Alusi compares Ad's rejection of the messengers in 26:123; al-Bursevi and Elmalili also give magnification or honor. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 101:6/q06/p3 The plural names the balance's parts. — Al-Alusi compares saying a person's temples have turned gray; Elmalili records the explanation too. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 101:6/q06/p4 The plural can indicate multiple balances for distinct aspects. — Al-Alusi cites poetry about flashes of lightning and rays of suns for multiplicity by aspect; Elmalili also allows conceptual multiplicity. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 101:6/q06/p5 The plural can distribute the weighing among people. — Al-Bursevi allows a scale per person; Study Quran says the plural suggests each person's experience of being weighed. [2 notes: BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Āl)]
- 101:6/q06/p6 The plural can refer to one actual balance in ordinary poetic usage. — Al-Qurtubi cites a poem using plural balances even on the view of one balance held by Gabriel. [1 notes: QURTUBI(قيل)]

## 101:6/q07 How many balances are used?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural counts actual instruments or has another explanation.
- 101:6/q07/p1 One balance weighs all communities and all deeds. — Al-Alusi calls this the better-known and sounder view; Elmalili calls it stronger; Study Quran says most hold it. Al-Qurtubi reports a single balance held by Gabriel. [5 notes: QURTUBI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Āl); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; ELMALILI+]
- 101:6/q07/p2 There may be a balance for each person or distinct balances for different aspects. — Al-Bursevi allows a scale assigned per person; al-Alusi allows multiple balances by considerations. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 101:6/q08 What is weighed in the balances?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: How deeds that have passed can be measured: as transformed acts, records, created counterparts, or visible signs.
- 101:6/q08/p1 Deeds themselves are weighed, taking beautiful luminous or ugly dark bodily forms. — Ibn Abbas describes beautiful obedient deeds and ugly unbelieving deeds. Al-Biqa'i says deeds are embodied; al-Alusi reports good forms in the right pan and bad forms in the left and rejects the claim that transformation is impossible. Elmalili says deeds can have bodily effects and afterlife forms; al-Razi reports the option among theologians. [17 notes: RAZI(ابن عباس,المتكلمون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,المتكلمون); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(آخرون,الرازي); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas)]
- 101:6/q08/p2 Written records of deeds are weighed. — Al-Qushayri reports this; al-Qurtubi says the two-pan balance weighs records. Al-Razi's theologians offer records because past acts no longer exist. Al-Bursevi says this displays justice and removes excuses; al-Biqa'i, al-Alusi and Elmalili include it among accounts. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI(المتكلمون); RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون); QURTUBI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Mütekellimîn)]
- 101:6/q08/p3 Light and darkness stand for good and evil in the weighing. — Al-Razi's theologians propose light and darkness as signs, and al-Bursevi and Elmalili include them as manifestations of deeds or their effects. [5 notes: RAZI(المتكلمون); RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون); BURSEVI; ELMALILI(Mütekellimîn)]
- 101:6/q08/p4 God creates separate substances or bodies corresponding to deeds, then weighs them. — Al-Qushayri describes a created substance for each part of an act. Al-Alusi says this avoids transforming acts and reports a claimed transmitted support. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 101:6/q09 What physical form does the balance have?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports]
- 101:6/q09/p1 It has a tongue and two pans, though its full nature is unknown. — Ibn Abbas gives tongue and pans; al-Hasan affirms two pans without describing their nature. Al-Qurtubi says records are placed in such a scale. Kanz al-Asrar and al-Alusi describe its breadth as the heavens and earth; Elmalili says God alone knows its nature. [16 notes: MAWARDI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); RAZI(ابن عباس,الحسن); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); QURTUBI; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس والحسن البصري,كنز الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، الحسن البصري); ELMALILI(Hasan,İbn Abbas); IBNATIYYA(جمهور العلماء والفقهاء والمحدثين)]

## 101:6/q10 Where is the balance and who attends it?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports]
- 101:6/q10/p1 It is between Paradise and Hell, attached to the Throne, with Gabriel and Michael attending it. — Nawadir al-Usul says Gabriel holds its column and Michael oversees it. Elmalili repeats the location; al-Qurtubi reports Gabriel holding the single scale. [4 notes: ALUSI(نوادر الأصول); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Nevâdiru’l-Usûl); QURTUBI(قيل)]

## 101:6/q11 How do weight and the result appear during weighing?  [ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports]
turns on: Whether the movement follows worldly gravity, reverses it, or the result is shown by luminous and dark signs.
- 101:6/q11/p1 The heavy side sinks first, then rises to Illiyyin; the light side rises first, then falls to Sijjin. — Al-Alusi adopts al-Qurtubi's description by analogy with worldly gravity. [2 notes: ALUSI(القرطبي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 101:6/q11/p2 Good deeds rise when they outweigh, while the unbeliever's side is heavy because the other side is empty. — A later scholar reverses ordinary gravity and cites 35:10; al-Alusi finds that verse inconclusive. [2 notes: ALUSI(بعض المتأخرين); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المتأخرين)]
- 101:6/q11/p3 All deeds are put on the scales together in bright right and dark left pans. — One account directs the right pan toward Paradise and the left toward Hell; God gives each person knowledge of the result. [2 notes: ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 101:6/q11/p4 A column of light or darkness rises from the prevailing pan to signal the result. — A reported account says the column covers the opposite pan; al-Alusi says these mechanics will be witnessed. [2 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 101:6/q12 Who undergoes weighing?  [مَن مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether the weighing includes all people or leaves classes who receive reward or punishment without it.
- 101:6/q12/p1 The weighing extends to hypocrites and believing and unbelieving jinn as well as humans. — Al-Alusi says hypocrites apparently fall with unbelievers but their deeds are weighed under the plain sense of many verses and hadiths; the intended weighing is beneficial weighing. Jinn follow the human rule. [4 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 101:6/q12/p2 Some people are exempt; God weighs only those He wills. — Al-Qurtubi cites a sound hadith that some of Muhammad's community enter without reckoning, and 55:41 for criminals recognized by marks and seized by forelocks and feet. The passage's two groups are the weighed people. [4 notes: ALUSI(القرطبي); ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي)]
- 101:6/q12/p3 People of patience receive reward without being weighed. — Qadi Mundhir ibn Sa'id al-Baluti says their reward is poured out. [2 notes: ALUSI(منذر بن سعيد البلوطي); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي منذر بن سعيد البلوطي)]

## 101:6/q13 When does the weighing occur?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports]
- 101:6/q13/p1 It follows the flight and receipt of records, questioning, and reckoning. — Al-Alusi attributes this order to al-Wahidi and others; Elmalili repeats it. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Vâhidî ve daha başkaları)]

## 101:6/q14 How certainly can the weighing and its physical details be known?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether revelation requires belief in an actual measure, supports a particular physical account, or warrants leaving mechanics unspecified.
- 101:6/q14/p1 The weighing is real and must be believed, while its exact nature remains unknown. — Al-Alusi says denial alone does not make a person an unbeliever. Elmalili says accidents such as heat and cold can be measured appropriately, deeds have bodily effects, and 42:17 and 7:8 support a true divine standard; detailed mechanism is not required. Ibn Atiyya says most scholars, jurists and hadith scholars affirm a literal balance because it gives a familiar, trusted form of judgment. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA(جمهور العلماء والفقهاء والمحدثين); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Âlûsî)]
- 101:6/q14/p2 No sound hadith establishes the balance's physical nature; no deed is lost, however God effects that. — Ibn al-Arabi says God could ensure complete accounting through an instrument, angels, or another means. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(ابن العربي); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 101:6/q14/p3 One should avoid speculation and verbal disputes over the mechanics. — Qutb says such disputes distract from the Qur'an's felt meaning; Kur'an Yolu says the unseen afterlife exceeds what can be asserted beyond revelation. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 101:6/q15 Why is the weighing displayed?  [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether its stated purpose is just accounting or public disclosure of the outcome.
- 101:6/q15/p1 It manifests justice and ensures that no deed is lost. — Elmalili calls the just scales of 21:47 a true law that resolves the gathering's upheaval; Islahi invokes 21:47. Al-Bursevi says records show justice and remove excuses. Ibn al-Arabi says no deed is lost regardless of instrument. [6 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR(ابن العربي); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 101:6/q15/p2 It publicly reveals each person's standing, bringing joy to the righteous and humiliation to evildoers. — Al-Razi says this is the purpose of exposing the outcome before all creatures. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 101:6/q16 What happens to the person with heavy balances?  [* · interpretation]
- 101:6/q16/p1 The person receives a pleasing life in Paradise or lasting bliss. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Qushayri, Ibn Kathir and Qatada explicitly name Paradise. Al-Biqa'i describes lasting consistent pleasure, and al-Bursevi explains that the life causes or possesses satisfaction. Islahi says the bliss exceeds even imaginable wishes; Kur'an Yolu speaks of richly provisioned Gardens. [10 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 101:6/q17 What does the weighing teach about deeds and salvation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How the weight of deeds relates to reward, intention, moral quality and divine mercy.
- 101:6/q17/p1 Deeds determine the person's honor, humiliation, bliss or doom according to their weight. — Ibn Kathir links honor and humiliation to what people do. Islahi says only deeds matter on that Day; the balance grants bliss when good outweighs evil and Hell otherwise. Tabataba'i says faith and obedience have weight, disbelief and sins none. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 101:6/q17/p2 Good and evil deeds are unequal in recompense. — Study Quran cites 41:34 and 11:114 for good removing evil, and 6:160 for tenfold good against one-for-one evil. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 101:6/q17/p3 Good character is especially weighty. — Study Quran cites a hadith that nothing placed in the Balance outweighs good character. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 101:6/q17/p4 Intentions govern the judgment of deeds. — Study Quran cites the Prophet's hadith that each receives what they intended. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 101:6/q17/p5 Entry to Paradise ultimately depends on God's mercy. — Study Quran cites the Prophet saying no one, including himself, enters Paradise through deeds alone except when God's mercy shelters him. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]

## 101:6/q18 How does this verse fit the sura's sequence?  [* · links]
turns on: How the contrast of heavy and light balances relates to the sura's earlier gathering and overall warning.
- 101:6/q18/p1 It divides the gathered people into two outcomes after their common Resurrection scene. — Al-Razi says God divides people by heavy or light balances; al-Biqa'i says the Day is described by its events before outcomes. Al-Alusi contrasts the groups' particular states; Ibn Ashur says this details the people scattered like moths in 101:4. Study Quran notes that the final six verses contrast their recompense. [7 notes: RAZI; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 101:6/q18/p2 The sura warns of Resurrection while also giving glad tidings. — Tabataba'i calls warning its dominant tone and the sura Meccan. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 101:6/q18/p3 The sura's teaching questions form a riddle that creates rhetorical tension. — Corpus Coranicum compares 101:1-11 to oath sequences in Q 100 and teaching questions in 90:12-16, 97:2-4, and 104:5-9. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 101:6/q18/p4 The lesser-resurrection reading sets the scene at bodily death and dissolution. — Al-Qashani describes separation from bodies, confused movement toward the light-world, and bodily members disintegrating like fluffed wool. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 101:6/q19 When was this sura revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 101:6/q19/p1 It is an early Meccan sura, probably after Surat al-Tin. — Asad Notes gives the tentative relative order; Tabataba'i also calls it Meccan. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI]

## 101:6/q20 What other Quranic passages illuminate these scales?  [* · links]
- 101:6/q20/p1 Earlier balance passages address weighing, and al-Haqqa describes the ensuing pleasing life. — Al-Qurtubi refers to al-A'raf 7:8, al-Kahf 18:105, al-Anbiya' 21:47 and his Tadhkira. Abu Hayyan refers to al-A'raf 7:8-9 and al-Haqqa 69:21; Tabataba'i says he has discussed the scale earlier. Study Quran adds 7:8 and 23:102 for heavy good deeds. [7 notes: QURTUBI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 101:6/q21 What does the light-balance contrast imply for the heavy-balance group?  [* · interpretation]
- 101:6/q21/p1 The light side has too little good and loses by rejecting God's revelations. — Al-Qummi glosses light balances as little good; Islahi links the loss to denial of revelation. [2 notes: TABATABAI(القمي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
