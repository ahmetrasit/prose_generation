# 6:153: 26 questions

## 6:153/q01 How is the opening particle of 'and this is My path' read?  [وَأَنَّ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowels and nūn forms alter whether the clause can be attached to what precedes or opens independently.
- 6:153/q01/p1 With open hamza and doubled nūn. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nashr, and Ibn ʿĀshūr assign it to most readers, including Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, and Abū ʿAmr; Ibn ʿĀshūr also names Abū Jaʿfar. Al-Ālūsī also rehearses this tripartite reading as part of the preceding command's syntax. [14 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، نافع، عاصم، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو); NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه); JISHUMI(ابن عامر ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عامر); NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وعاصم وأبو جعفر); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:153/q01/p2 With open hamza and light or quiescent nūn. — Ibn ʿĀmir is named throughout; several sources add Yaʿqūb, while al-Qurṭubī adds Ibn Abī Isḥāq. Sources differ whether the light particle is reduced emphatic inna or maṣdariyya. [22 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، نافع، عاصم، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عامر); JISHUMI(ابن عامر ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر ويعقوب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(شامي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عامر); NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر ويعقوب); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:153/q01/p3 With kasra and doubled nūn, beginning a fresh assertion. — Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are consistently named; al-Aʿmash, ʿAlī and Khalaf also appear in some reports. Al-Biqāʿī instead attributes a light kasra reading to Ibn ʿĀmir and Yaʿqūb. [18 notes: NAHHAS(الأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو); JISHUMI(ابن عامر ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة,على); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخوان); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:153/q01/p4 With kasra and light nūn. — Jishumī attributes this form to Ibn ʿĀmir and Yaʿqūb; al-Biqāʿī likewise reports kasra for them, against the usual fatḥa attribution. [2 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عامر ويعقوب); BIQAI-FULL]
- 6:153/q01/p5 Without the particle: 'and this is My path'. — Al-Aʿmash and the codex of Ibn Masʿūd/ʿAbd Allāh are reported; al-Samīn says it supports the independent-sentence analysis. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش)]

## 6:153/q02 What syntactic relation does 'and this is My path' have to its context?  [وَأَنَّ هَٰذَا فَٱتَّبِعُوهُ · grammar]
turns on: The open or closed particle, an implied causal lām, and the point of coordination with 6:151 decide the parse.
- 6:153/q02/p1 It gives the reason for the command: follow it because it is straight. — Al-Khalīl, Sībawayh, Abū ʿAlī, al-Zamakhsharī and others supply a causal lām. Comparisons include 106:1, 23:52 and 72:18. Al-Fārisī and Abū Ḥayyān discuss whether the following fāʾ would then be extra; al-Samīn says Sībawayh disallows that predicate fāʾ. Al-Ālūsī answers a double-conjunction objection by a fronted object or an omitted verb, citing 17:110 and 72:18. [22 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه); FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي عن سيبويه); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(شامي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفارسي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي,الزمخشري,الفارسي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي)]
- 6:153/q02/p2 It is an object of 'I recite': I recite that this is My path. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Kisāʾī, Abū al-Baqāʾ and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect it to the recitation beginning at 6:151; al-Ālūsī calls coordination with 'come' very unlikely. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء والكسائي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:153/q02/p3 It coordinates with the earlier divine prohibition, charge, or its prepositional object. — Al-Farrāʾ makes it genitive after the charge and coordinates with bihi; al-Ḥawfī attaches it to an lā tushrikū; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī attach it to the prior prohibition. Abū al-Baqāʾ objects that the preposition must be repeated and that God would be commanding the path's straightness; al-Samīn replies that the preposition can be understood after a long clause and that commanding its straightness means forbidding departure. [14 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء,الكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي,الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر ويعقوب); TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 6:153/q02/p4 With kasra, it is a fresh sentence, and the following fāʾ links the command. — Al-Naḥḥās, Abū ʿAlī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, and others read it as an independent declaration; the kasra still gives a reason to follow. Some connect the assertion to the Prophet's recitation. [17 notes: NAHHAS(الأعمش وحمزة والكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(حمزة,على); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخوان); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:153/q02/p5 The light form has an implicit pronoun of the matter. — Abū ʿAlī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn explain the omitted pronoun by al-Aʿshā's poetry and other examples, including 10:10. Al-Naḥḥās allows nominative or accusative; Qirāʾāt-Er instead calls the light particle maṣdariyya. [11 notes: NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر,الأعشى); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:153/q02/p6 The previous explanatory construction is itself disputed. — Al-Ālūsī says a nominalizing 'that you do not associate' appears to name nonassociation among forbidden things, while an explanatory particle strains to include later positive commands. He rejects a relative-clause solution ascribed to Sībawayh. Al-Zamakhsharī implies prohibitions opposite the positive commands; Abū Ḥayyān supplies an omitted 'what He commanded' or coordinates with 'I recite.' Imruʾ al-Qays's line permits an imperative beside a prohibition. [9 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الزمخشري,امرؤ القيس)]

## 6:153/q03 How is the consonant of ṣirāṭī pronounced?  [صِرَٰطِى · readings]
turns on: The transmitted dialectal or phonological form of the initial consonant.
- 6:153/q03/p1 With ṣād. — Ibn Mujāhid and later readers make it the reading of those other than the named sīn and intermediate-sound readers. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، ابن عامر، حمزة، الباقون); FARISI-HUJJA; RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); TABRISI]
- 6:153/q03/p2 With sīn, sirāṭī. — Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀmir are named; Qirāʾāt-Er specifies Qunbul from Ibn Kathīr and calls it a phonological variant of the same root. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، ابن عامر، حمزة، الباقون); FARISI-HUJJA; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); TABRISI; QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:153/q03/p3 With an intermediate ṣād–zāy sound. — Ḥamza's form has variant transmissions in Ibn Mujāhid; Qirāʾāt-Er names Khalaf from Ḥamza and describes ishmām of zāy. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير، ابن عامر، حمزة، الباقون); FARISI-HUJJA; RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); TABRISI; QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:153/q04 How is the possessive yāʾ of ṣirāṭī read?  [صِرَٰطِى · readings]
turns on: Transmission of the possessive yāʾ vowel, with the same sense in both readings.
- 6:153/q04/p1 The yāʾ is quiescent. — Most readers leave it still. Ibn Mujāhid distinguishes Nāfiʿ's treatment of this yāʾ from most other added yāʾs and records the related cases of maḥyāy and wajhī; the surah has forty added yāʾs, eight disputed. [8 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو بكر,ابن كثير، نافع، عاصم، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي,حمزة والكسائي وعاصم برواية أبي بكر,نافع); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); TABRISI; QIRAAT-ER]
- 6:153/q04/p2 The yāʾ is opened or vocalized. — Ibn ʿĀmir is repeatedly named. Ibn Mujāhid also gives his moving yāʾ in maḥyāy and wajhī; al-Nashr and Qirāʾāt-Er specify fatḥa, with no change in meaning. [10 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر,ابن كثير، نافع، عاصم، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); NASHR(ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:153/q05 What does 'this, My straight path' designate?  [هَٰذَا صِرَٰطِى · referent]
turns on: The demonstrative's scope ranges from immediately preceding laws to Islam and the entire sūra; inward or imamic applications identify a particular way of traversing it.
- 6:153/q05/p1 Islam, God's upright religion, including Muhammad's revealed law and Sunnah. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Dāmghānī, al-Qurṭubī and others identify it with the religion; al-Dāmghānī cites 6:126, Ibn ʿĀshūr 1:6 and 6:161, and Study Quran 5:15–16. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers the whole prophetic law to al-Ṭabarī's narrower commandments. [29 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 6:153/q05/p2 The commands and prohibitions in the two preceding verses, or all ten duties. — Muqātil is cited for this; al-Rāzī says these summarize the entire prophetic teaching. Al-Ālūsī distinguishes the source and calls the straight path comprehensive. Elmalılı includes preceding tawḥīd; Kur'an Yolu calls the final duty encompassing. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:153/q05/p3 The Qurʾān, revelation, or proofs. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī record Qurʾān as an alternative to law or Islam. Jishumī adds proofs and the absence of contradiction; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī stress following revelation and proof. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:153/q05/p4 The whole sūra, especially its tawḥīd, prophecy and law. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report this scope; al-Bursawī uses the sūra's content to explain the causal clause. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes,ابن عباس)]
- 6:153/q05/p5 Abraham's upright monotheistic religion restored by Muhammad. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls it the ḥanīf religion and best religion. Iṣlāḥī links it to Abraham's basic teaching, Moses' law, Quraysh's neglected moral duties and Muhammad's restoration after Israelite and Ishmaelite innovations. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:153/q05/p6 The divinely guided Imams, particularly ʿAlī, as an application. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī labels Abū Jaʿfar's identification an application; reports through Shiʿi and Sunni routes name ʿAlī. He connects kinship-love in 42:23 to seeking a way to the Lord in 25:57. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,روايات من طرق الشيعة وأهل السنة)]
- 6:153/q05/p7 The heart's route to God, without the self's desire or share. — Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad speaks of turning from everything other than God; Sahl excludes the self's desire. Al-Qāshānī describes balanced virtues, unity, annihilation and subsistence through divine attributes. [4 notes: SULAMI(جعفر بن محمد,سهل); QASHANI]
- 6:153/q05/p8 Every path ultimately begins from God and turns to Him. — Al-Bursawī cites a Persian tafsīr, Qūnawī's account of God's encompassing existence or knowledge, and a Persian verse that every road crosses the Beloved's street. [3 notes: BURSEVI(صدر الدين قونوي,محققان)]

## 6:153/q06 Whose is the possessive in 'My path'?  [صِرَٰطِى · referent]
turns on: Whether the speaking 'I' is God or the Prophet, and where the shift of person falls.
- 6:153/q06/p1 God's; the first person marks divine authorship and certainty. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 42:52–53 and says the later 'His path' shifts person; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this as the majority view. Al-Ālūsī says God institutes it and the Prophet follows and invites to it. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 6:153/q06/p2 The Prophet's; the path he teaches is God's path. — Abū al-Baqāʾ takes the speaker as Muhammad. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this since the Prophet delivers the commands and says the shift of person occurs at 'His path,' not 'My path'; he cites 1:5–6. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this but says mere prophetic possession would not persuade disbelievers. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI+]
- 6:153/q06/p3 A variant wording says 'the path of your Lord'. — The codices of ʿAbd Allāh and Ubayy are cited by al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī; al-Ālūsī notes plural and singular address variants. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأعمش); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر)]

## 6:153/q07 What does calling the way a straight path convey?  [صِرَٰطِى مُسْتَقِيمًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: The road image explains directness, destination, and contrast with branching deviation.
- 6:153/q07/p1 A clear, broad, direct road to Paradise and well-being, free of crookedness. — Al-Māwardī and al-Bursawī explain the path metaphor by its destination; Jishumī contrasts Satan's route to Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the straight road is visible, easy and quick; Ibn Qutayba cites an Arab road saying. Bursevī compares law to a bridge finer than hair and sharper than a sword. ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās pictures a traveler leaving the main road for branching tracks. [14 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن مسعود); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس)]
- 6:153/q07/p2 A coherent, balanced religion rooted in human nature. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 30:30 and says desire-driven routes lack internal coherence; al-Qāshānī sees the balanced middle of virtue after the self's annihilation. [4 notes: QASHANI; TABATABAI]
- 6:153/q07/p3 A road of the Prophet's guidance that leads to Paradise while side roads lead to Hell. — Ibn Masʿūd locates its start by Muhammad and its end at Paradise; the side roads have callers and end in Hell. The image is also carried in prophetic line-drawing reports. [6 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 6:153/q08 How is mustaqīman parsed, and why is it accusative?  [مُسْتَقِيمًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative describes the present path as a state or specifies its straightness.
- 6:153/q08/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative describing the demonstrative/path. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the ḥāl analysis; al-Samīn allows the attention particle or demonstrative as governor. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 11:72; Iṣlāḥī makes the demonstrative expression govern it. Al-Wāḥidī refers back to 6:126. [6 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:153/q08/p2 It may be a specification or emphasis that the path is also straight. — Jishumī records tamyīz as an alternative to ḥāl, saying the wording draws attention to straightness as well as pathhood. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 6:153/q09 What does following the path require?  [فَٱتَّبِعُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources develop following as practical obedience and adherence to transmitted communal guidance.
- 6:153/q09/p1 Act on the full revealed way, its commands, prohibitions, permissions and restraints. — Al-Māwardī defines following as acting; Jishumī adds affirmation and lawful/unlawful observance; Abū Ḥayyān includes all obligations. Al-Qurṭubī cites the Prophet's instruction to take commands and stop at prohibitions; al-Rāzī says follow in general and detail. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:153/q09/p2 Hold to the Prophet's Sunnah, early precedent and community amid disagreement. — Al-Qurṭubī adduces al-ʿIrbāḍ's farewell counsel, ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz's letter on moderation and predestination, Sahl's warning about future censure, and Abū al-ʿĀliya's appeal to the original practice. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية,العرباض بن سارية,سهل بن عبد الله التستري,عبد الله بن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود,عمر بن عبد العزيز)]

## 6:153/q10 What are the forbidden 'paths'?  [ٱلسُّبُلَ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'paths' denotes rival communities, doctrinal innovations, concrete forbidden customs, or all deviation from Islam.
- 6:153/q10/p1 Other religions and ways of disbelief, including Judaism, Christianity, Zoroastrianism and idolatry. — Ibn ʿAbbās and many commentators give these examples; al-Māwardī includes superseded books and former religions. Al-Bayḍāwī says desires multiply with temperaments and customs, while evidence yields one way. [16 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قول غير مسمى); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:153/q10/p2 Innovations, passions, doubtful doctrines, anomalous legal opinions and speculative disputation. — Mujāhid names innovations and doubts; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī add deviant legal doctrines and excessive debate or theology. Sahl, al-Qāshānī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe desires and customary doctrines. [20 notes: TUSTARI; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 6:153/q10/p3 Misguidances and any routes other than Islam. — Ibn ʿAbbās's broad gloss is transmitted repeatedly; Ibn Zayd says these routes separate people from Islam. Ibn Qutayba illustrates right and left side tracks. [10 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:153/q10/p4 Self-imposed bans on livestock and crops. — Muqātil identifies this concrete form of misguidance; Abū Ḥayyān's note names Muqātil as transmitter while wording attributes the identification to Mujāhid. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 6:153/q10/p5 Ways driven by the soul and its ulterior aims. — Al-Qāshānī calls them custom- and desire-made doctrines; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites criminals, corrupters, ignorance, conjecture and desire in 6:55, 7:142, 10:89 and 53:23. [3 notes: QASHANI; TABATABAI]

## 6:153/q11 Why is God's path singular but the forbidden paths plural?  [صِرَٰطِى ٱلسُّبُلَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The contrast may express theological unity or the concrete image of a main road and side tracks.
- 6:153/q11/p1 Truth and guidance are one; false routes multiply and branch, causing division. — Ibn Kathīr compares singular light and plural darknesses in 2:257; al-Burhān and al-Itqān compare singular guardian of believers with plural guardians of unbelievers. Elmalılı allows time- and place-specific true rulings while insisting each case has one truth; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links truth's unity to coherent propositions. Al-Bayḍāwī contrasts proof with variable desires. [12 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 6:153/q11/p2 The plural evokes crooked little tracks branching from the main road. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them baniyyāt al-ṭarīq leading to homes or pasture; he says sabīl can itself equal ṣirāṭ, citing 12:108. Ibn Qutayba gives the Arab counsel to avoid side tracks. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:153/q12 What line-drawing report illustrates the verse, and how is it transmitted?  [صِرَٰطِى ٱلسُّبُلَ · reports]
turns on: The narration's companion, line count, attribution and chain grading vary.
- 6:153/q12/p1 The Prophet drew one central line and side lines, each with a devil calling to it, then recited the verse. — Ibn Masʿūd and Jābir versions recur. Ibn Kathīr reports al-Ḥākim's authentication of both Ibn Masʿūd routes and suggests ʿĀṣim heard each; he prefers Ibn Masʿūd despite possible attribution as a Companion statement. Al-Qurṭubī calls al-Dārimī's route sound; Jābir's al-Shaʿbī route is called undependable by al-Ḥākim. Some reports call the sides Satan's paths; one Jābir form has two on each side, which Ibn ʿĀshūr says may count both sides together. [24 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(جابر); IBNABIHATIM(النبي ﷺ، جابر بن عبد الله,النبي ﷺ، عبد الله بن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود); NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QASHANI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر,عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ELMALILI(İbn Mes‘ud); IBNASHUR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود); TABATABAI(ابن مسعود,جابر بن عبد الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 6:153/q12/p2 The drawing has six slanting lines on each side, yielding twelve and then seventy-two routes. — Al-Nasafī alone specifies six each side, then subdivides each into six to obtain seventy-two divergent routes. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:153/q12/p3 Ibn Masʿūd instead describes a path starting near Muhammad and ending in Paradise, with side paths ending in Hell. — The report is conveyed by ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr; callers entice people toward the side roads. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 6:153/q12/p4 A second prophetic parable makes Islam the path, limits its walls, prohibitions its doors, Scripture its first caller and conscience its inner caller. — Al-Tirmidhī grades the report from al-Nawwās b. Samʿān ḥasan gharīb. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النواس بن سمعان)]

## 6:153/q13 How is fa-tafarraqa formed and read?  [فَتَفَرَّقَ · grammar]
turns on: The underlying two tāʾs can be reduced by deletion or assimilation.
- 6:153/q13/p1 It is subjunctive after an implied an as the consequence of the prohibition. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Samīn parse the verb as the response to 'do not follow'; this makes division the predicted result. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:153/q13/p2 It comes from fatatafarraqa, with one tāʾ deleted in the common form. — Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explain deletion of one of two adjacent tāʾs; al-Samīn notes the majority's light form. [4 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:153/q13/p3 The tāʾs can assimilate into a doubled form. — Al-Zamakhsharī reports assimilation, al-Samīn names al-Bazzī and allows deletion or assimilation, and Qirāʾāt-Er says the articulation does not alter meaning. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:153/q14 What does the bāʾ in 'fa-tafarraqa bikum' do?  [بِكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition directly marks caused scattering or accompaniment that implies causation.
- 6:153/q14/p1 It is causative: the paths divide and scatter you. — Al-Samīn allows an object-like semantic role; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say the bāʾ makes the paths the agent, stronger than saying travelers divide themselves. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:153/q14/p2 It marks accompaniment: you diverge together with the paths. — Al-Samīn reports a circumstantial reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares dhahabtu bi-Zayd with adhhabtuhu, so figurative accompaniment yields a causative force. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:153/q15 What is the result of following divergent paths?  [فَتَفَرَّقَ عَن سَبِيلِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: The phrase can be glossed as departure from religion, disruption of unity, and its ultimate destination.
- 6:153/q15/p1 They cause deviation from God's religion and shatter religious unity. — Mujāhid glosses deviation and opposition; al-Māwardī adds loss of unity in defending religion; al-Ṭabrisī calls it God's chosen religion. Al-Qurṭubī cites 6:159; Ibn Kathīr cites 42:13 and earlier peoples' disputes; Elmalılı says factionalism weakens those who abandon tawḥīd. [20 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شهاب); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 6:153/q15/p2 Side roads lead ultimately to Hell, while the one path leads to Paradise. — Ibn Masʿūd's road image and Qatāda's report give the contrasted destinations; al-Bursawī connects deviation here to slipping from the afterlife bridge and cites a report on many who slip. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن مسعود); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(عليه السلام)]
- 6:153/q15/p3 The word literally means becoming scattered. — Asad gives this literal rendering. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 6:153/q16 To whom does the pronoun in 'His path' refer?  [سَبِيلِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent is God, but the literary account depends on who says 'My path'.
- 6:153/q16/p1 To God; the phrase denotes His religion or Islam. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the context determines the referent, and if 'My path' is divine, the third person is iltifāt. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī locates the person shift here even when 'My path' belongs to Muhammad. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:153/q16/p2 It may follow the Prophet's quotation of God's command or a new divine address. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses these alternatives to account for the switch after a prophet-owned 'My path'. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 6:153/q17 What does 'that is what He charged you with' refer to?  [ذَٰلِكُمْ وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative gathers the immediate final injunction or the wider revelation and preceding ordinances.
- 6:153/q17/p1 The comprehensive charge to follow the one path and avoid divergent paths. — Al-Ālūsī says the closing repeats both commands; al-Bursawī counts this as the tenth duty; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the threefold repeated testament, each closing a group of duties. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:153/q17/p2 The Qurʾān, Islam, previous commands, avoidance of sin, or obedience that averts punishment. — Jishumī transmits this range without selecting one. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 6:153/q18 What is the standing of these commandments across scriptures and peoples?  [وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ · sciences]
turns on: Reports differ over the exact Torah formulation, while the universal and unabrogated status is repeatedly asserted.
- 6:153/q18/p1 They are decisive, universal, and never abrogated in any revealed book. — Muqātil extends them to all Adam's descendants; Ibn ʿAbbās calls them Mother of the Book. Several reports promise Paradise for practice and Hell for abandonment; Elmalılı distinguishes enduring fundamentals from rulings that vary by time and place. Al-Bursawī counts ten duties; Iṭqān notes three clear verses beginning at 6:151, with another three at 17:23. [13 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس,وقيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; ITQAN(ابن عباس)]
- 6:153/q18/p2 They were the Torah's first instruction or first ten verses. — Kaʿb swears to the Torah priority; some reports say first thing, others first ten verses. Al-Kashshāf and al-Ṭabrisī name the opening 'Say: come...' as the Torah start. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(كعب الأحبار); TABRISI(كعب الأحبار); NASAFI-FULL(كعب); DURR-FULL(كعب)]
- 6:153/q18/p3 The next verse's gift of Moses' Book links these commands to all prophetic communities. — Al-Zamakhsharī coordinates 'Then We gave Moses the Book' with 'He charged you,' making this an ancient command passed through each prophet. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 6:153/q19 What reports associate these three verses with the Prophet's testament or pledge?  [وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Different reports attach the verses to a sealed testament, a pledge, or a missionary encounter.
- 6:153/q19/p1 They are the Prophet's sealed testament, to be recited from 6:151 through 6:153. — Ibn Masʿūd's report is graded ḥasan gharīb by al-Tirmidhī and ḥasan by al-Ālūsī. Al-Rabīʿ b. Khuthaym recites or writes these verses when asked for a sealed sheet or advice. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(الربيع بن خثيم,عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); TIRMIDHI(عبد الله)]
- 6:153/q19/p2 The Prophet sought a pledge on the three verses, promising reward for fulfillment and leaving unpunished breaches to God's will. — ʿUbāda b. al-Ṣāmit reports that worldly punishment expiates a breach, while God may punish or pardon a deferred one. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت)]
- 6:153/q19/p3 At Minā the Prophet recited these commands to an Arab tribe after inviting them to monotheism, his mission, and his protection. — The Durr report says he then recited 16:90; Mafrūq praised the morals and Hāniʾ approved the words. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 6:153/q20 What does the closing hope for taqwā mean?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources dispute how the three endings relate to each duty group and whether the final aim primarily stresses following or avoiding division.
- 6:153/q20/p1 Obey God and fear His punishment by keeping His commands and avoiding prohibitions. — Mujāhid glosses mindfulness as obedience; ʿAṭāʾ as fear. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk mentions the five prayers guarding against Hell. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say the complete way protects from the Fire and brings lasting salvation. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); TABATABAI(أبو حيان); TABATABAI-]
- 6:153/q20/p2 Taqwā comes from adhering to the one path and refusing divisive alternatives. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses avoidance of division as the verse's main purpose; he objects to Abū Ḥayyān's priority on following the path. He links piety to human nature in 91:7–8, while Iṣlāḥī says divine reminder awakens nature. [6 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]
- 6:153/q20/p3 The endings progress from reason through remembrance to piety. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says obvious prior prohibitions call for reason and later desire-related ones for recollection; al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Iṣlāḥī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe reflection leading to heed and precaution. Al-Ṭabrisī notes the neighboring tadhakkarūn readings with light or doubled consonant, from tatadhakkarūn by deletion or assimilation. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; TABRISI]
- 6:153/q20/p4 The first duties are obvious, while the second group is harder to delimit. — Al-Rāzī contrasts five obvious duties with four requiring thought; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the claim of obscurity, allowing only care over orphan property and measures. [1 notes: TABATABAI(فخر الرازي); TABATABAI-]
- 6:153/q20/p5 The first ending addresses idolaters' ignorance and the second their remembered virtues. — Al-Quṭb al-Rāzī is quoted through Rūḥ al-Maʿānī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects it as historically inaccurate and confusing Qurʾānic recollection with ordinary memory. [1 notes: TABATABAI(القطب الرازي); TABATABAI-]

## 6:153/q21 How does the verse connect with its neighboring verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources connect the command to the three-verse sequence, Abrahamic and Qurʾānic guidance, and later anti-division warnings.
- 6:153/q21/p1 It gathers the preceding commands and prepares the renewed warning against religious division. — Al-Qurṭubī says prohibitions and orders culminate in the one-way command; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates 42:13 and treats the path declaration as prelude to the warning. Ibn ʿĀshūr finds three linked sections: social order, economic relations, and Islam's inclusive principle. Al-Biqāʿī compares 6:126 and the three testament endings. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]
- 6:153/q21/p2 It restates Abraham's religion against Quraysh's invented practices and prepares the surah's closing proclamation. — Iṣlāḥī traces the passage from Quraysh's invented food taboos through Abraham's duties to 6:155–165; he says Moses' law guided Israelites on the same path and a demanded sign cannot replace prophetic guidance. Kur'an Yolu contrasts baseless polytheist rules with nine duties and the encompassing tenth. [8 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:153/q21/p3 It connects to warnings against sitting with disputants and against sectarian division. — Al-Qurṭubī cites 6:68 and 4:140 for avoiding disputants and 6:159 for religious fragmentation; Ibn Kathīr links 42:13, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise invokes prophetic unity. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شهاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 6:153/q21/p4 The Qurʾān's revealed authority and universal prophetic mission underpin the call. — Ibn Kathīr says messengers remove excuses (4:165), Muhammad is the final universal messenger to humans and jinn (7:158; 6:19), and all reached by the Qurʾān are warned. He cites 11:17 and 68:44–45 on rejection, the 'red and black' report explained by Mujāhid, 41:42 on revelation's integrity, 4:82, 38:29 and 47:24 on reflection, and 3:187 and 3:77 against concealment. [9 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد)]
- 6:153/q21/p5 The sūra's final verses invite choice while making each person accountable. — Iṣlāḥī says people may follow the straight path or wander, and each bears responsibility for deeds. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:153/q22 Where were these three verses revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 6:153/q22/p1 They are an exception revealed in Medina within an otherwise Meccan sūra. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Shahr b. Ḥawshab report the exception; Shahr says the sūra came all at once with ranks of angels. Study Quran notes that some early authorities considered some verses possibly Medinan. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,شهر بن حوشب); STUDYQURAN(Some early authorities)]

## 6:153/q23 What response to religious innovations is drawn from the warning?  [وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا۟ ٱلسُّبُلَ · law]
turns on: The reports range from counsel to preserve Sunnah through social and legal sanctions to severe eschatological warnings.
- 6:153/q23/p1 Follow transmitted Sunnah and avoid innovations, excessive dispute and sectarian desires. — Al-Qurṭubī cites Ibn Masʿūd's rebuke of pebble-counting devotional circles: good intent does not validate an act. ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz advises simple inherited religion; Abū al-ʿĀliya urges the original practice, which al-Ḥasan confirms. Sahl says public disputation spread innovation; Ibn Masʿūd warns against hair-splitting. Al-Tustarī cites a severe no-repentance saying. [12 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو العالية,العرباض بن سارية,سهل,سهل بن عبد الله التستري,عبد الله بن مسعود,عمر بن عبد العزيز)]
- 6:153/q23/p2 Persistent company with innovators shares their judgment; avoid sitting with them even to dispute. — Al-Qurṭubī cites 6:68 and 4:140 and attributes the rule to Aḥmad, al-Awzāʿī and Ibn al-Mubārak; he compares ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz's treatment of companions of drinkers. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 6:153/q23/p3 Innovation can block repentance or Paradise and is spiritually worse than an ordinary sin. — Tustarī's attributed report says repentance is narrowed or barred for innovations. Sahl cites a report barring innovators from Paradise; Sufyān calls innovation dearer to Iblīs since its followers do not repent; al-Awzāʿī relates Iblīs's strategy. Al-Fuḍayl warns that love of an innovator harms deeds and heart. [6 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(الأوزاعي,الفضيل بن عياض,سفيان الثوري,سهل)]
- 6:153/q23/p4 Some sectarian doctrines and conduct are treated especially severely. — Sahl rejects prayer behind or marriage with the Muʿtazila and lists their disputed doctrines; al-Shaʿbī gives a fiery etymology of people of desires. Al-Qurṭubī recalls 72/73 sect reports, a proposed extra anti-scholar sect, and Rāfiʿ's warning about people equating Iblīs with God in creative power. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشعبي,رافع بن خديج,سهل بن عبد الله,مجاهد)]

## 6:153/q24 What is the rhetorical force of the repeated testament and appeal to piety?  [ذَٰلِكُمْ وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The rhetorical emphasis may fall on comprehensive obedience or on the closing anti-division warning.
- 6:153/q24/p1 The three repeated charges strengthen acceptance, with reason, recollection and piety marking a progression. — Al-Biqāʿī stresses the three verse endings; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them linked sections, and al-Nasafī and Iṣlāḥī explain the mental sequence. Al-Ālūsī says the comprehensive path warrants emphatic repetition. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 6:153/q24/p2 The path declaration leads to the prohibition on division, which explains the final piety clause. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts this discourse focus with Abū Ḥayyān's emphasis on following the comprehensive path. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 6:153/q25 What other word-choice and simile observations are cited alongside these notes?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited material ranges from composite image syntax to other terms for travel and number choice.
- 6:153/q25/p1 A composite simile makes a combined scene a single image, while independent similes remain separate. — Al-Jurjānī contrasts Bashshār's dust and flashing swords with separate comparisons; Ruʾba's markings and al-Buḥturī's horse and cloud also depend on combined appearance. He argues Bashshār's conjunction keeps the image attached, compares wa with maʿa, and cites the undivided she-camel and calf conditional. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 6:153/q25/p2 Sulūk can mean traveling along a path, broader than merely entering. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts Moses' usluk in Qasas, with its repeated journeys from the chest to Midian and back, with adkhil in Naml, where dakhal forms occur five times and the story opens at the fire; he cites 27:7, 27:12, 28:7, 28:11, 28:22, 28:29, 28:32 and 71:19–20. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 6:153/q25/p3 Other singular and plural choices follow distinct semantic or phonological patterns. — Al-Itqān says Hell is singular as one substance but Paradise may be many gardens; al-albāb occurs only in plural because its singular is heavy; east and west vary among singular direction, dual seasonal points and plural numerous points. [3 notes: ITQAN]

## 6:153/q26 How common is companionship on the straight religious path?  [صِرَٰطِى · interpretation]
- 6:153/q26/p1 True religion has again become strange, and genuine companions are rare. — Al-Bursawī laments that scarcely anyone now offers true religious companionship or deserves it. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
