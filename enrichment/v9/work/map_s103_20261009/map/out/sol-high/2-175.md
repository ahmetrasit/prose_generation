# 2:175: 16 questions

## 2:175/q01 Who are those indicated by 'those'?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description is tied to the preceding scripture concealors or applied to hypocrites.
- 2:175/q01/p1 The people who concealed scripture, specifically Jewish scholars or Jews who knew Muhammad's description. — Muqatil and al-Tabari identify prior belief or knowledge of Muhammad; al-Tabari specifies scholars concealing his prophethood for bribes. Al-Mawardi calls them the previously mentioned Jewish scholars; Abu Hayyan and Ata in al-Durr link them to the preceding concealors and 2:174. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:175/q01/p2 The hypocrites whose conduct shows readiness to enter the Fire. — Ibn Atiyya transmits Qatada, al-Hasan, Ibn Jubayr, and al-Rabi describing the subjects as hypocrites. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والحسن وابن جبير والربيع)]

## 2:175/q02 What does their purchase of error and punishment mean?  [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ بِٱلْهُدَىٰ وَٱلْعَذَابَ بِٱلْمَغْفِرَةِ · interpretation]
- 2:175/q02/p1 They chose error over available guidance and thereby chose punishment over the forgiveness that following guidance would have brought. — Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, al-Razi, al-Nasafi, and Ibn Kathir specify denial or concealment of Muhammad's scriptural description despite knowledge; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi add worldly profit. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi explain the second exchange as the consequence of the first. Abu Hayyan says buying implies desire and extreme loss; Bint al-Shati notes that bāʾ marks what is surrendered, citing 9:9. Damghani compares purchase in 9:111, where believers sell themselves to God. Al-Biqai says concealment misled others and added their burden; al-Tabatabai says the deed takes embodied form as eating fire and the persistent deed is concealment. Al-Razi contrasts knowledge with ignorance in this world and forgiveness with punishment in the next. Bursawi says the price is metaphorical, and Elmalili calls the acquired error and torment lasting. [26 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:175/q03 Why is the exchange of punishment for forgiveness stated after the exchange of error for guidance?  [وَٱلْعَذَابَ بِٱلْمَغْفِرَةِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second exchange restates the first exchange's result or adds a separate charge of knowing disobedience.
- 2:175/q03/p1 It reinforces the preceding exchange by naming its consequence. — Abu Muslim calls it reinforcement. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi explain that punishment follows chosen error and forgiveness would follow discarded guidance. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:175/q03/p2 It adds a distinct charge: they knowingly persisted in disobedience and chose punishment over forgiveness. — Al-Qadi's explanation is preferred by al-Tabrisi for adding a distinct meaning; al-Razi stresses their prior knowledge of reward and punishment. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(القاضي)+]

## 2:175/q04 How does the purchase sentence and its following fāʾ connect to the preceding threat?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ فَمَآ · links]
turns on: The sentence's syntactic relation to the earlier condemnation.
- 2:175/q04/p1 The fāʾ makes their astonishing relation to the Fire follow from their exchange of guidance and forgiveness. — Al-Wahidi says it answers the preceding description of their trade. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:175/q04/p2 The purchase sentence explains the severe threat as an independent answer or as a second predicate about the ugliness of concealment. — Al-Alusi gives both syntactic connections: the first predicate describes the threat's severity and the second describes concealment's ugliness. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:175/q05 What does 'how patient they are with the Fire' mean?  [فَمَآ أَصْبَرَهُمْ عَلَى ٱلنَّارِ · interpretation]
turns on: The force of mā (exclamation, interrogative rebuke, or negation), whether patience concerns present deeds or future punishment, and whether ṣabr denotes audacity, persistence, knowledge, confinement, or actual endurance.
- 2:175/q05/p1 It marvels at their audacity in doing what leads to the Fire, without claiming they can bear its pain. — Qatada, al-Hasan, al-Rabi, and others connect boldness to deeds that bring Hell near; several explicitly deny real patience. Al-Farra, al-Kisai, al-Asmai, and others cite a judge's or Bedouin's rebuke mā aṣbaraka ʿalā Allāh for daring against God; al-Thalabi and al-Qurtubi identify Yemeni usage. Al-Tabari prefers this sense from Arabic usage and explains Fire as punishment. Sahl applies it to issuing religious judgments without knowledge of Sunna or law. Al-Qurtubi says conduct leading to Hell is called courage. Al-Biqai likens it to defying a ruler's prison threat. Al-Tabatabai says al-Sadiq's three transmitted explanations converge. [34 notes: FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(الكسائي); TUSTARI(سهل); TAB-FULL(الحسن,الربيع بن أنس,قتادة,مجاهد أو سعيد بن جبير أو بعض أصحابه,مجاهد، الحسن، قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); THALABI(الحسن وقتادة والربيع,قاضي اليمن); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والحسن وابن جبير والربيع); TABRISI(الحسن وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الأصمعي,الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة وابن جبير والربيع,الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي عن قاضي اليمن); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,سفيان بن عيينة,قتادة); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام); TAB-FULL+]
- 2:175/q05/p2 It describes their persistence in deeds leading to the Fire, despite knowing their consequence. — Al-Muarrij says opposition to the Prophet is known to lead there; al-Kisai and Qutrub supply an understood 'deeds of the Fire's people.' Al-Sadiq speaks of doing what they know leads to it. Bursawi takes Fire as its cause and describes the persistence as the object of human wonder. The Qurtubi and Ibn Atiyya reports distinguish this from physical endurance. [15 notes: THALABI(الكسائي وقطرب,المورج); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي); TABRISI(الكسائي وقطرب,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي وقطرب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المؤرج,قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام)]
- 2:175/q05/p3 It is a rebuking question: what made them endure the Fire or pursue its causes after trading truth for falsehood? — Al-Majaz and Abu Ubayda take mā as 'what'; Ata, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd, and others call it a question. Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash argues from case endings. Al-Mubarrad cites a prohibition on tormenting a living creature and a poem for the sense 'make endure,' but Ibn Atiyya disputes his vocalization; Abu Hayyan argues the causative hamza cannot simply mean ṣabara. Al-Zajjaj uses the analogy of enduring hunger. Some phrase it as asking what drove them to the deeds; al-Qurtubi calls it belittling dismissal, and Kur'an Yolu a forceful warning. [30 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; AKHFASH(some); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش,ابن زيد,السدي,عطاء); ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); THALABI(عطاء والسدي وابن زيد وأبو بكر بن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء والسدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(المبرد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,عطاء); RAZI-FULL(عطاء وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي وعطاء وأبو عبيدة معمر بن المثنى,قول); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي,معمر بن المثنى والمبرد); DURR-FULL(السدي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); IBNATIYYA-FULL(معمر بن المثنى)-]
- 2:175/q05/p4 It marvels at how long the condemned endure or remain in the Fire itself. — Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi compare Qur'anic exclamations in 80:17 and 19:38. Ibn Kathir mentions torment and shackles. Ibn Ashur treats certain future suffering as already witnessed, comparing Zuhayr, Malik ibn al-Rayb, and 102:5–6. Abu Hayyan reports a figurative sense of remaining in the Fire. [10 notes: THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور ومنهم الحسن ومجاهد,الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:175/q05/p5 It asks what keeps them confined in the Fire, or how long God keeps them there. — Al-Mawardi takes ṣabara as confining; al-Tabrisi reports Ibn Abbas asking what keeps them confined. Al-Zajjaj compares asking how long someone bears imprisonment; al-Mubarrad cites a poem and a report about confining a living animal, though Ibn Atiyya challenges his vowel analysis. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المبرد); TABRISI(ابن عباس,الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد)-]
- 2:175/q05/p6 It marvels at how practiced or knowledgeable they are in the deeds of Hell's people or in falsehood. — Mujahid's reported expressions vary between ability, expertise, and knowledge; Ibn Qutayba calls the reading good and supplies an omitted 'deeds.' Al-Thalabi calls it sarcasm. Al-Tabari includes expertise within his preferred exclamation. [8 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(مجاهد)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:175/q05/p7 Their heedless pursuit of Hell's causes is described sarcastically as if they accept its consequence. — Al-Razi says knowingly choosing Hell's cause makes them seem willing to endure its result in this life. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Baydawi, and al-Alusi stress lack of concern. Elmalili contrasts unwillingness to give up worldly pleasure with persistence toward eternal burning; Abu Hayyan likens it to defying a ruler. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:175/q05/p8 It says how little they are distressed or grieve over the Fire. — Both al-Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan report this figurative gloss. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:175/q05/p9 It describes their actual future patience when silenced in Hell and deprived of escape. — Al-Asamm places the patience after the command to remain there, compared by al-Razi with 23:108. Al-Razi and Abu Hayyan reject the temporal shift from present wording and observe that the damned may cry out. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأصم); RAZI-FULL(الأصم)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:175/q05/p10 It negates endurance: God has not made them patient over the Fire. — Abu al-Baqa and another report take mā as negative; al-Samin dismisses it. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 2:175/q05/p11 It simply makes their state an object of astonishment. — Al-Akhfash compares 80:17; al-Tabrisi also cites it and an Arab saying about God's punishment. Abu Hayyan reports that most place the patience in worldly life, whether literal or metaphorical. [3 notes: AKHFASH(some); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]

## 2:175/q06 How is mā in 'فما أصبرهم' parsed?  [فَمَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is an exclamatory noun, a question, a relative or described noun requiring an unspoken predicate, or negation.
- 2:175/q06/p1 It is an exclamatory complete indefinite noun in the nominative, subject of a following clause. — Sibawayh and the majority supply 'something made Zayd beautiful' as a model; al-Wahidi calls it a subject and the following past-tense verb its predicate. He says mā's ambiguity creates surprise where shayʾ would diminish it or state a settled fact. Al-Baydawi cites poetry; al-Razi compares the other Arabic exclamation pattern in 19:38. Al-Tabari and Abu Hayyan cite 80:17, and Abu Hayyan adds 54:31. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل التأويل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور من المفسرين,سيبويه والجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه والجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL(الكوفيون)-]
- 2:175/q06/p2 It is interrogative with exclamatory force or rhetorical denial. — Al-Farra and Ibn Durustawayh make the next clause its predicate; al-Samin compares 2:28. Al-Razi reports al-Farra's model as denying that anything surpasses Zayd in beauty, with case endings distinguishing this from asking which part of Zayd is best. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عياش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء وابن درستويه); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 2:175/q06/p3 It is a relative pronoun with an omitted predicate. — Abu Ubayda and al-Majaz use 'what made them endure'; al-Akhfash analyzes the following clause as its relative. Al-Razi objects that the parallel relative sentence 'the one who made Zayd good' is malformed while the exclamation is complete. [6 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL(الأخفش)-]
- 2:175/q06/p4 It is an indefinite noun described by the following clause, with its predicate omitted. — This is al-Akhfash's second reported analysis, repeated by al-Samin and al-Alusi. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 2:175/q06/p5 It is negative: God has not made them patient with the Fire. — Reported by Abu Hayyan and attributed by al-Samin to Abu al-Baqa, who dismisses it. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 2:175/q07 Is the exclamatory aṣbara a verb or a noun, and how does its complement work?  [أَصْبَرَهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fixed exclamative pattern has verbal inflection and a direct object or nominal behavior and an object-like complement.
- 2:175/q07/p1 It is a past-tense verb, with the following accusative as its direct object. — Al-Wahidi says its form moved from triliteral to quadriliteral, its fixed final fatḥa marks a verb, and the object is genuinely acted upon; the poetic diminutive mā aḥaysana merely imitates a noun. Basrans cite agreement, fatḥa, and attached accusative pronoun. Al-Samin prefers this parsing; Abu Hayyan states its effect on the object. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(البصريون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(البصريون); SAMIN-DURR+; RAZI-FULL(الكوفيون)-]
- 2:175/q07/p2 It is a noun or comparative form, with the following accusative an object-like complement. — Kufans cite noun-like diminutives; al-Razi says an underlying verb would change a middle wāw to alif, allow an intervening adverb, and not be restricted to bare triliteral roots. He rejects the Basran subject paraphrase: it loses wonder, is uninformative, treats mā as an unsuitable indefinite subject, and would make the maker God require 'who,' not 'what.' Attached accusatives also occur with laʿalla and layta; aṣbara can be a comparative noun. Abu Hayyan records the resulting change in the complement's status. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكوفيون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكوفيون); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:175/q08 What can the root ṣ-b-r and the form aṣbara mean here?  [أَصْبَرَهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Which extension of restraint and which force of the hamza the context requires.
- 2:175/q08/p1 Ṣabr is restraint of the self, hence patience in hardship and, in another Qur'anic use, fasting. — Ibn al-Jawzi explains ṣābir as restraining panic, Ramadan as the month of patience, and Qur'anic uses in 3:17, 14:21, 38:44, and 2:45. Ibn al-Anbari alternatively connects its name to bitterness like the plant ṣabr. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن الأنباري,بعض المفسرين)]
- 2:175/q08/p2 Ṣabara can mean to confine or restrain someone, as with a tied animal used as a target. — Ibn al-Jawzi notes the prohibited shooting of a bound animal called maṣbūra; al-Mubarrad cites that prohibition and a poem for confinement, but Ibn Atiyya challenges the poem's vowels, reading aṣbarahā as a past form of ṣabara. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المبرد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد)-]
- 2:175/q08/p3 Aṣbara can mean to command patience or make someone patient, through the causative hamza. — Ibn Atiyya allows both senses for an interrogative reading; Abu Hayyan insists it means 'make patient' and rejects al-Mubarrad's unsupported equation with simple ṣabara. Al-Tabrisi cites al-Hutay'a's poem for subjecting someone to something disliked. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:175/q09 How can an exclamation or wonder be attributed to God?  [فَمَآ أَصْبَرَهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether the exclamation is assigned to human observers or its divine sense is restricted to magnification.
- 2:175/q09/p1 It directs human hearers to marvel or tells them the state merits wonder; God does not experience ignorance or astonishment. — Al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Anbari as reported by Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, and Bursawi give this account. Al-Tabrisi adds rebuke and reproach; Shurayh rejected a reading of 'I wonder' in 37:12 on the same ground. [6 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(شريح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 2:175/q09/p2 Divine wonder means magnification alone, whereas human wonder may include ignorance of its cause. — Al-Razi separates the two elements of ordinary wonder; al-Nakha'i compares divine laughter, mockery, and cunning, which likewise do not bear human meanings. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(النخعي)]

## 2:175/q10 What does 'the Fire' stand for in this phrase?  [ٱلنَّارِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether Fire names punishment and its worldly causes figuratively or evokes the future Fire directly.
- 2:175/q10/p1 It stands for its punishment or for deeds and causes leading to it. — Al-Tabari says Fire replaces 'its punishment' and compares ellipsis in praise of Hatim and Antara; al-Samin says 'deeds of Hell' is understood. Bursawi calls Fire the cause by metonymy; al-Tabrisi and al-Kisai/Qutrub understand the deeds of Hell's people. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(الكسائي وقطرب); TABRISI(الكسائي وقطرب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 2:175/q10/p2 It is the actual future Fire, presented as if already visible. — Ibn Ashur says certain future torment is imagined as present, comparing Zuhayr and Malik ibn al-Rayb's poetry and 102:5–6. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:175/q11 What ethical or theological lessons are drawn from their choice?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes draw distinct moral, theological, and embodied-deed consequences from the same choice.
- 2:175/q11/p1 Their unbelief is their own act, and culpable deeds merit punishment; knowing sin is graver. — Al-Jishumi cites the verse against the Jabriyya, says curse and punishment follow the actor's actions, and says knowledge increases guilt and punishment. [3 notes: JISHUMI]
- 2:175/q11/p2 Scholars are warned against hiding truth for gain, status, favors, office, luxury, or leadership. — Bursawi says greed, envy, and desire are an inner fire that devours good deeds; al-Alusi says the worldly motive explains the severe warning. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:175/q11/p3 Concealment and its worldly price become embodied as fire and punishment. — Al-Tabatabai says deeds take embodied form: choosing a small price becomes eating fire, then exchanging guidance and forgiveness for error and punishment. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 2:175/q12 How does the verse connect with the following account of the Book and division?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection explains the punishment, identifies the disputants, or extends the warning to disputes over the Qur'an.
- 2:175/q12/p1 The next verse explains the condemnation: God revealed the true Book, and those who differed over it are in deep division. — Ibn al-Jawzi cites 2:176; Bursawi says rejection or concealment of true scripture warrants torment. Abu Hayyan traces the sequence from disbelievers' deafness and blindness through lawful food and necessity to this condemnation, which intensifies from their choice to their opposition to the Book. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:175/q12/p2 The people divided over scripture are Jews and Christians, in deep enmity. — Al-Suddi identifies the parties and characterizes their schism. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(السدي)]
- 2:175/q12/p3 The distant schism warns against disputing the Qur'an and God's signs. — Abu al-Aliya cites 40:4, where only unbelievers dispute God's signs. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو العالية)]

## 2:175/q13 What rhetorical figures are associated with this verse or its imagery?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes name different rhetorical devices or illustrative imagery.
- 2:175/q13/p1 It exemplifies mumāthala or tamthīl, an allusive metaphor whose chosen words exemplify the intended meaning. — Al-Baqillani says Qudama calls it tamthil and sets it against irdaf. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 2:175/q13/p2 Hell's speech, sight, and rage are vivid personifying images like poetry about a corner kissing a footprint and a minbar walking. — Al-Baqillani cites 50:30, 25:12, and 67:8 for the Hell images. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 2:175/q13/p3 Muṭābaqa means mentioning a thing with its opposite, such as night and day or black and white. — Al-Baqillani attributes the common definition to al-Khalil, al-Asmai, and Ibn al-Mutazz. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(الخليل بن أحمد والأصمعي وعبد الله بن المعتز)]

## 2:175/q14 What do the nearby descriptions of God's treatment and painful punishment mean?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes discuss different adjacent expressions rather than one disputed reading.
- 2:175/q14/p1 God's not speaking to them marks His anger. — Al-Zajjaj compares a person refusing to speak to someone through anger. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعضهم)]
- 2:175/q14/p2 God sends no angels to greet them, or does not let them hear His speech as the righteous do. — Al-Zajjaj reports these two alternatives in the preceding verse's description. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعضهم)]
- 2:175/q14/p3 God's not purifying them means He does not praise them, so they are punished. — Al-Zajjaj glosses wa-lā yuzakkīhim. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 2:175/q14/p4 Alīm means painful or aching, describing intense pain. — Al-Zajjaj distinguishes muʾlim as intensified pain. Bint al-Shati cites poetry about pain and sleeplessness and notes painful punishment in sixty-eight Qur'anic verses, including 2:10 and 9:61. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 2:175/q14/p5 Kindling a beacon means displaying the light of truth to its seeker. — Bint al-Shati reports Ibn al-Athir's gloss of Ali's phrase. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأثير)]

## 2:175/q15 What orthographic principles are illustrated by nearby Qur'anic spellings?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The notes give distinct general and symbolic explanations for varying alif spellings.
- 2:175/q15/p1 Mushaf copies differ in spelling some names; omitted letters can motivate retaining another alif. — Al-Burhan lists Harut, Marut, Haman, and Qarun; Dawud retains alif after omission of waw, and most copies likewise retain alif in Isra'il after deleting ya. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 2:175/q15/p2 Sound masculine and feminine plurals generally omit alif, except when followed by hamza or a doubled letter. — Al-Burhan states the orthographic rule. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 2:175/q15/p3 Retaining alif may reflect a lower, visible worldly aspect, while omitting it reflects a higher spiritual aspect. — Abu al-Abbas supplies the principle, comparing 38:75 and 114:4. Al-Burhan applies it to al-firash and al-ta'am, the People of the Book's and believers' food in 5:5, the higher food of 5:75, and intensified shutting of spiritual doors in 12:23 versus concrete doors in 12:25; some contrasts are subtler than others. [6 notes: BURHAN(ابو العباس)]

## 2:175/q16 What interpretations of the seven aḥruf are catalogued?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports enumerate different kinds of content, grammar, or spiritual practice as the seven aḥruf.
- 2:175/q16/p1 They are grammatical varieties such as gender, conditionals, inflection, oaths, number, diminutive or augmentative forms, and particles. — Al-Itqan attributes this enumeration to grammarians. [1 notes: ITQAN(النحاة)]
- 2:175/q16/p2 They are seven forms of spiritual conduct, pairing asceticism, certainty, service, generosity, struggle, fear, hope, gratitude, patience, love, and longing. — Al-Itqan reports this as a Sufi interpretation. [1 notes: ITQAN(الصوفية)]
- 2:175/q16/p3 They are seven sciences concerning creation, divine unity, attributes, forgiveness and punishment, resurrection and reckoning, and prophecy. — Al-Itqan reports this as a sixteenth interpretation. [1 notes: ITQAN(قيل)]
- 2:175/q16/p4 Ibn Hibban's catalogue counted thirty-five views of the seven aḥruf. — Ibn Hajar reports al-Qurtubi's notice but could not locate Ibn Hibban's account; al-Suyuti says Ibn al-Naqib transmitted the count through al-Sharaf al-Mursi. Al-Qurtubi listed five. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن حجر)]
- 2:175/q16/p5 Exhortation, commands, lawful and forbidden matters, clear and ambiguous verses, and parables. — Ibn Hibban's first listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p6 Lawful and forbidden matters, commands, prohibitions, rebuke, future reports, and parables. — Ibn Hibban's second listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p7 Promise, threat, lawful and forbidden matters, admonitions, parables, and argument. — Ibn Hibban's third listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p8 Commands, prohibitions, glad tidings, warnings, reports, and parables. — Ibn Hibban's fourth listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p9 Clear and ambiguous, abrogating and abrogated, particular and general material, and stories. — Ibn Hibban's fifth listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p10 Commands, prohibitions, encouragement, deterrence, disputation, stories, and parables. — Ibn Hibban's sixth listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p11 Commands, prohibitions, legal limits, knowledge, secret and manifest, outward and inward senses. — Ibn Hibban's seventh listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p12 Abrogating and abrogated, promise, threat, rebuke, discipline, and warning. — Ibn Hibban's eighth listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p13 Lawful and forbidden, openings, reports, virtues, and punishments. — Ibn Hibban's ninth listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p14 Commands and warnings, parables, reports, rebuke, exhortation, and stories. — Ibn Hibban's tenth listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
- 2:175/q16/p15 Lawful and forbidden, parables, explicit texts, stories, and permissions. — Ibn Hibban's eleventh listed view. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبان)]
