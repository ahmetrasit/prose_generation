# 70:20: 11 questions

## 70:20/q01 What harm touches the person?  [ٱلشَّرُّ · referent]
turns on: Whether harm is read as misfortune generally, poverty specifically, or divine punishment for deeds.
- 70:20/q01/p1 Misfortune or hardship, including poverty, illness, pain, or fear. — Al-Wāḥidī specifies hardship and poverty; al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī give poverty or illness, paired with wealth or health in the next verse. Al-Burūsawī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's poverty or illness; al-Ālūsī says the definite article covers the class of such troubles. Elmalılı includes pain, hardship, poverty, and illness; Kur'an Yolu includes fear. The Study Quran reports both general misfortune and the more specific illness and poverty reading. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 70:20/q01/p2 Poverty in particular. — Sahl in al-Jishumī and Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Baghawī contrast poverty here with prosperity and withholding in the next clause; al-Tustarī and Yaḥyā ibn Sallām make the same contrast, the latter saying the poor person asks persistently. [4 notes: TUSTARI; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); JISHUMI(سهل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 70:20/q01/p3 Even slight punishment from God for a person's deeds. — Iṣlāḥī says people generally become frustrated and complain when God punishes them even a little for what they have done. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 70:20/q02 What does jazūʿan mean, and how does it show itself?  [جَزُوعًۭا · meaning]
- 70:20/q02/p1 Intensely impatient and distressed: a person panics, laments, complains, or despairs instead of bearing harm patiently. — Al-Jishumī, Jawharī as reported by Asad, and al-Sāmarrāʾī call jazaʿ the opposite of patience; al-Sāmarrāʾī cites the pairing of panic and patience in 14:21. Al-Ālūsī stresses repeated and intense panic and cites al-Rāghib: panic exceeds sadness by cutting a person off from his activity. Al-Rāghib traces the root through cutting a rope, a valley's end, partly colored beads or meat, and a half-ripened date. Al-Farrāʾ links the sense to halūʿ. Al-Biqāʿī says even slight harm produces extreme distress. Ibn Kathīr and the Ibn Kathīr report in al-Sāmarrāʾī add terror and despair of future good; al-Burūsawī's Ibn ʿAbbās report attributes excess despair to ignorance of God's decree. Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī add failure to endure or seek reward; Elmalılı adds writhing, crying out, and seeking others' help; Kur'an Yolu adds loss of hope. The Study Quran calls it lack of forbearance, opposite the patience commanded in 70:5. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Baghawī, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk in al-Ṭabarī describe anxiety under harm; ʿAṭiyya says one does not endure, while Abū ʿUbayda and Thaʿlab say one fails to be patient. Abū al-ʿĀliya describes the response to harm as despair, while Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Durr describes it as fretfulness. [30 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(عطية); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة وثعلب,عطية عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Jawhari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(ابن كثير,فتح القدير); STUDYQURAN; BURHAN(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 70:20/q03 How are jazūʿan and the following manūʿan construed grammatically?  [إِذَا جَزُوعًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the two descriptions are circumstantial states, adjectives, omitted-verb predicates, or accusatives by proximity; Makkī's objection to the adjectival order is disputed by al-Samīn.
- 70:20/q03/p1 They are circumstantial states overlapping with halūʿan; each time phrase modifies its state. — Al-Samīn prefers this analysis and explains that Abū al-Baqāʾ's phrase 'another circumstantial state' can mislead: his stated governor shows the states overlap. Al-Bayḍāwī says each idhā governs its following description and that the qualities are innate, whether actual or expected. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 70:20/q03/p2 They are adjectives modifying halūʿan. — Al-Naḥḥās offers this analysis. Al-Samīn reports that Makkī also permits it but finds it distant because the time phrase would be understood before the adjective. Al-Samīn rejects that objection: the issue is an adverbial phrase preceding its governor; the prohibited order concerns an adjective's governed element preceding the adjective. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 70:20/q03/p3 They are predicates of an omitted kāna or ṣāra: on being touched, the person becomes distressed or withholding. — Makkī supplies the omitted verb; al-Naḥḥās also allows the sense of becoming so when touched by harm or good. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
- 70:20/q03/p4 They are accusative by proximity to halūʿan, rather than sharing its grammatical role. — Al-Jishumī reports this as Abū Muslim's analysis. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 70:20/q04 Who is the person described as panicking at harm?  [مَسَّهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the later exception for worshippers implies a general human subject, or the description already singles out disbelievers.
- 70:20/q04/p1 Human beings as a species, with worshippers excepted in the following verses. — Al-Ḥasan in Mujāhid says humanity was created impatient in hardship and withholding in prosperity. Al-Nasafī explicitly takes 'human' as the species so worshippers can be excepted. Al-Rāzī reports the general reading on the same ground. The Study Quran says those with the eight qualities in 70:22–35 are exempt. Al-Bayḍāwī treats the dispositions as innate; Ibn Kathīr likewise calls humans innately impatient. [6 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); NASAFI-FULL+]
- 70:20/q04/p2 The disbeliever or associator. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies the human described as the disbeliever; he reports Ibn ʿAbbās applying the description to associators and al-Ḍaḥḥāk applying it to the disbeliever. Al-Rāzī also reports this reading from some interpreters. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 70:20/q05 How does this reaction explain the earlier description halūʿ?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether halūʿ chiefly names anxious impatience or acquisitive eagerness that produces both reactions.
- 70:20/q05/p1 Halūʿ is anxious impatience: harm brings distress or despair, while prosperity brings withholding. — Al-Farrāʾ calls halūʿ irritable impatience; Abū ʿUbayda calls it the worst impatience. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Jishumī, Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Baghawī, al-Ḥasan in Mujāhid, and Sahl in al-Jishumī explain it through the two clauses. ʿAṭiyya and Abū ʿUbayda with Thaʿlab recast the pair as failing in patience and gratitude. Yaḥyā ibn Sallām specifies withholding God's due in wealth and persistent asking in poverty. Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā as cited by al-Rāzī and Thaʿlab as reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Nasafī say God's following explanation is clearest. Al-Burhān and al-Itqān classify the clauses as explanatory; the people of rhetoric say such clauses remove prior ambiguity. Ibn Jinnī, reported by both, says one should not stop before them because they complete the preceding description like a relative clause or adjective. Al-Nasafī adds that a beneficiary hoards good and denies it to others; al-Sāmarrāʾī quotes Ibn Kathīr saying he refuses God's due. The cited wealthy person in al-Sāmarrāʾī withholds beneficence when healthy, then panics and makes bequests when ill. Abū al-ʿĀliya explicitly takes the following two clauses as the explanation of halūʿ; Thaʿlab likewise says God has explained it. Asked about halūʿ, Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Durr answers by repeating God's descriptions of fretfulness under harm and withholding under good. [24 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); FARRA; MAJAZ; TUSTARI; MAWARDI-FULL(عطية,يحيى بن سلام); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة وثعلب,سهل,عطية عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(محمد بن عبد الله بن طاهر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثعلب); BURHAN(أبو العالية,ابن جني); ITQAN(أبو العالية وغيره,أهل البيان,ابن جني); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(ابن كثير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 70:20/q05/p2 Halūʿ is intense acquisitiveness or eagerness for perceived benefit, which yields panic at loss and withholding when benefit arrives. — Al-Ṭabarī combines extreme anxiety with greed; al-Rāzī defines intense acquisitiveness and little patience. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers intense eagerness and explains that people pursue what they judge useful to themselves, so they recoil from harm and monopolize benefits. Fatḥ al-Qadīr, citing al-Ṣiḥāḥ in al-Sāmarrāʾī, glosses halūʿ as extreme greed together with the worst excessive panic. Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes anxious agitation amid fears and desires and begins comparison with a Prophetic saying. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير); TABATABAI+]

## 70:20/q06 What is the moral status of the disposition behind this panic?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the innate impulse is itself a blameworthy darkness or a useful capacity whose moral outcome depends on judgment and chosen conduct.
- 70:20/q06/p1 The inner disposition is innate, but people are responsible for the chosen speech and acts expressing it and can resist it. — The Qāḍī in al-Rāzī argues God would not blame His own act and believers can struggle against the trait. Al-Rāzī distinguishes God's creation of involuntary inner states from speech and actions a person can refrain from. Al-Nasafī says revealed law commands people to oppose their temperament. Abū Ḥayyān says anxiety and withholding are so entrenched that humanity is described as created with them, comparing 21:37. Al-Bayḍāwī calls the qualities innate, whether present or expected; Ibn Kathīr calls humans innately impatient. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 70:20/q06/p2 Innate self-love and eagerness can serve happiness; they become blameworthy through excess, deficient judgment, or unrestrained appetite. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says children initially follow instinctive judgments of good and resist sharing; reason later corrects those judgments. Unchecked appetite can lead to denying truth and others' rights, misery, withdrawal, and hoarding; developed reason can redirect eagerness to fear of sin and haste toward forgiveness. [4 notes: TABATABAI]
- 70:20/q06/p3 Attachment to the ego's dark nature produces the cowardice and greed shown by these reactions. — Al-Qāshānī calls the ego a source of evil and impurity; he reports a Prophetic saying naming consuming greed and ruinous cowardice a man's worst traits. [2 notes: QASHANI(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام)]

## 70:20/q07 What response to harm does this verse call for?  [جَزُوعًۭا · interpretation]
- 70:20/q07/p1 Bear hardship patiently, accept God's decree, and seek reward or the afterlife rather than fixating on immediate conditions. — Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī define the failure as neither enduring nor seeking reward. Al-Rāzī says seeking relief itself is not the fault; limiting concern to bodily conditions is. One should accept poverty or illness as God's decree and use health or wealth for the afterlife. The Study Quran reports al-Rāzī's counsel to recognize circumstances as from God, be content with them, and spend health and wealth toward the Hereafter. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says worldly hardship and duty require prescribed patience. Al-Nasafī says revealed law calls people to oppose their temperament. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R,al-Rāzī)]
- 70:20/q07/p2 Persistent prayer cultivates the patience that counters panic. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says continuous prayer treats panic; ordinary prayer may fail to keep attention because thoughts and distractions take much of it away. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:20/q07/p3 Give to the poor: parting with wealth without grieving as though afflicted cures panic at loss, while giving the needy their share cures withholding. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says giving to the poor cures both sides of halūʿ: the donor does not grieve when wealth leaves and does not deny the needy their share; he connects this with 70:24–25. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 70:20/q08 Why is harm mentioned before good?  [ٱلشَّرُّ · rhetoric]
- 70:20/q08/p1 It matches the sūra's opening demand for punishment and its recent scene of a criminal wishing to ransom himself from a skin-tearing blaze. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī points to 70:1, 70:11, and 70:15–16; he also says panic contrasts with the command to the Prophet for beautiful patience in 70:5. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 70:20/q09 How does the passage fit between the hoarder and the worshippers?  [* · links]
- 70:20/q09/p1 It interrupts the sequence to summarize the human disposition that drives greed and retention, while the worshippers are excepted from its blame. — Ibn ʿĀshūr places verses 19–21 between the hoarder of 70:17 and the exception at 70:22; he says fear of losing benefits explains the trait but does not excuse hoarding. The Study Quran says those with the eight qualities listed in 70:22–35 escape this blameworthy state. Al-Nasafī says the exception supports taking 'human' as the species. Al-Sāmarrāʾī connects the worshippers' giving to the poor in 70:24–25 with curing both panic when wealth leaves and withholding the needy person's share. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); NASAFI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 70:20/q10 What other Qur'anic passages are compared with this description?  [* · links]
- 70:20/q10/p1 It echoes 41:49–51: people ask for good, despair when harmed, and withdraw after mercy. — The Study Quran cites the sequence in 41:49–51. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 70:20/q10/p2 Its account of believers is strongly parallel to al-Muʾminūn 23:1–11, with differences in wording and order. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī observes both the parallel and the differences across the two passages. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:20/q10/p3 The earlier picture of a sinner beginning his ransom offer with his sons shows how panic at punishment drives him toward even his dearest kin. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī connects the person's panic at punishment with the sinner's offer of his nearest kin, beginning with sons; he says this shows the punishment exceeds imagination and that sons are dearest to him. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 70:20/q11 What does the report about a sick person's groaning say?  [جَزُوعًۭا · reports]
- 70:20/q11/p1 Groaning is recorded: a patient sick person gains good deeds, but one who despairs is recorded as halūʿ and receives no reward. — Al-Durr reports this as attributed to ʿAlī. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(علي)]
