# 83:3: 12 questions

## 83:3/q01 How do the pronouns in كَالُوهُمْ and وَّزَنُوهُمْ function?  [كَالُوهُمْ وَّزَنُوهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the suffix names recipients, their due goods, or the measuring agents; the disputed evidence is Arabic transitivity, discourse contrast, and the missing alif in the codex.
- 83:3/q01/p1 They are attached accusative objects referring to the people for whom goods are measured or weighed; the benefactive lām is omitted. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Akhfash, al-Kisāʾī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others cite Ḥijāz usage such as 'I measured you your food'; Ibn ʿĀshūr also mentions neighboring Qays and lightening of speech. Abū ʿUbaydah cites hunting poetry; al-Farrāʾ cites a Bedouin woman; Majāz cites Khuffāf; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī cite poetry; al-Naḥḥās cites Abū Zayd's جنيتك أكموءا وعساقلا and lines with أمرتك الخير, نبيت عبد الله and أستغفر الله ذنبا; Ibn Qutaybah's cited Ḥumayd, Ḥātim, Labīd, Ṭarafah, al-Farrāʾ and al-Muthaqib show other permissible implicit referents or complements. Al-Naḥḥās reports Abū ʿAmr, al-Kisāʾī and al-Akhfash with Sībawayh's كَلْتُكَ and صَدْتُكَ analogy. Al-Samīn notes one object is direct and another may lose its preposition, with disagreement over the basic verb. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, Abū ʿUbayd, al-Jishumī, al-Samīn, al-Bursawī and al-Itqān cite the absence of a separating alif in the written form. Al-Zamakhsharī objects that spelling is not uniformly conventional and alif has no spoken value; al-Rāzī answers that early spelling practice should be preserved. Al-Samīn additionally prefers this parse for maintaining the contrast of taking from and giving to others. Al-Qurṭubī says a nominative parse leaves the first verb without a predicate; al-Ālūsī reports that nominative emphasis would not distinguish merchants from servants and would call for a connective fa. Al-Biqāʿī says the object can represent the customers' due rights and foregrounds their presence. Abū Ḥayyān says the commodity itself remains unexpressed. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says direct-object and lām constructions are both possible. [67 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ(خفاف); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الفراء,المثقب العبدي,حاتم,حميد بن ثور,طرفة,لبيد); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو زيد,أبو عمرو بن العلاء); THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الفراء,الكسائي); JISHUMI(عامة القراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(امرأة أعرابية); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش والفراء,الزجاج والكسائي,الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء); SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; JISHUMI+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+; SAMIN-DURR+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+; THALABI(أبو عبيد)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج)+; ZAJJAJ+; KASHSHAF-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-; BIQAI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 83:3/q01/p2 The attached accusative pronoun stands for the measured or weighed amount due to the recipients, with an omitted noun understood. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī explain replacement of the omitted measured noun by its genitive pronoun. Al-Qurṭubī includes this beside preposition deletion. Al-Bayḍāwī names the omitted measured amount. Al-Ālūsī reports an omitted goods analysis. Al-Biqāʿī calls the object the customers' due rights; al-Samīn describes the goods as unexpressed. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 83:3/q01/p3 هم is nominative, a separate subject or emphasis of the verbs' plural subject; the goods and recipients are understood. — ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar, sometimes with a reading attributed to Ḥamzah, pauses after each plural wāw; accounts vary between a new subject and emphasis of the verbal subject. Al-Naḥḥās also reports an unnamed emphatic analysis like قاموا هم. Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh cited by al-Samīn defend it: emphasis leaves the action in focus and the recipients remain understood since cheats do not short themselves. Al-Zamakhsharī objects that it shifts the contrast from treatment of recipients to identity of performers; al-Qurṭubī objects that the first verb then lacks a predicate. Al-Zajjāj and others object from the missing alif; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Samīn report that spelling argument as weak, since the alif is silent and manuscript usage varies. ## 83:3/q02 Where may a reciter pause in كَالُوهُمْ or وَّزَنُوهُمْ?  [كَالُوهُمْ وَّزَنُوهُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether syntactic separation licenses a recited stop within a written word, and whether a sound transmission supports that stop.
- 83:3/q02/p1 Each written verb and pronoun is one unit, so a pause comes after the pronoun. — Abū ʿUbayd and the joined-reading advocates cite manuscript spelling without an alif and ordinary transitive Arabic. Al-Jishumī says readers unanimously use the joined reading. Ibn al-Jazarī in al-Nashr regards the established performance rule as keeping written-together words together; al-Aʿmash through Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim and Sūrah through al-Kisāʾī treat each as one word, while al-Dānī associates Nāfiʿ with adherence to the script. Al-Jaʿbarī says a connected pair takes a stop after the second word. Al-Zajjāj forbids pausing after kālū on the object analysis. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(عامة القراء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والكسائي); NASHR(الأعمش عن أبي بكر عن عاصم، وسورة عن الكسائي,الجعبري); BIQAI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة)+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; JISHUMI+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+; TAB-FULL+; THALABI(أبو عبيد)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج)+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 83:3/q02/p2 A pause may be made after each plural wāw, treating هم as a separate word. — ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar is repeatedly reported to stop after kālū or wazanū, with Ḥamzah sometimes associated with him. Al-Jaʿbarī permits pausing at an independent word and choosing according to spelling or linguistic origin; al-Nashr says al-Kisāʾī allowed division by meaning and Khalaf preferred following script, but the alleged Ḥamzah transmission comes only through ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ṣāliḥ al-ʿIjlī and al-Dānī says performance specialists oppose it. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(عيسى بن عمر); THALABI(حمزة,عيسى بن عمر); JISHUMI(عامة القراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); KASHSHAF-FULL(عيسى بن عمر، حمزة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); TABRISI(عيسى بن عمر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض الناس); RAZI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر وحمزة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى، حمزة); SAMIN-DURR(عيسى بن عمر وحمزة); NASHR(الجعبري); BIQAI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); ALUSI-FULL; NASHR(حمزة عن عبد الله بن صالح العجلي)-]

## 83:3/q03 Why is the entitlement lām omitted from the verbs?  [كَالُوهُمْ وَّزَنُوهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the omitted lām carries a deliberate implication of denied entitlement or reflects common transitive usage.
- 83:3/q03/p1 Its omission portrays the cheaters' refusal to give customers what is due to them. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the lām carries entitlement in both verbs; removing it in kālūhum and wazanūhum suits their withholding of the recipients' rights. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 83:3/q03/p2 Its omission is an idiomatic lightening of a normal Arabic construction. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verbs ordinarily take a measured object and the lām may be dropped frequently, as with common verbs such as thanking someone. Al-Sāmarrāʾī acknowledges both a direct object and an entitlement lām as possible usages. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH]

## 83:3/q04 Why is measuring alone mentioned for taking, but both measuring and weighing for giving?  [كَالُوهُمْ وَّزَنُوهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording mirrors actual trade methods, uses an implied parallel, argues from lesser to greater harm, or states a general moral contrast.
- 83:3/q04/p1 Cheaters take by measure because filling a vessel enables theft, but short others through both measures and scales. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān reporting al-Zamakhsharī, al-Samīn reporting him, and al-Ālūsī connect the asymmetry to practical fraud: overfilling when taking and shorting by either means when giving. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that claiming the original cheats actually bought only by measure lacks evidence. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 83:3/q04/p2 Merchants bought large or customary measured quantities, then sold smaller lots by measure or weight. — Al-Ālūsī reports the bulk-buying and retail-lot explanation but objects that it is not universal: Baghdad in his time weighed everything. Ibn ʿĀshūr says buyers generally took measured produce and paid weighed coin, while merchants sold through both methods. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the bulk-grain account as weak. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 83:3/q04/p3 Mentioning full measure implies full weight too, while both forms of shortchanging are named when giving. — Al-Zajjāj says trade uses both measure and weight, and al-Ṭībī describes one half of the parallel as implying the other. Al-Ālūsī is unpersuaded. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the inference arbitrary because the same reasoning could equally omit measuring. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); TABATABAI-]
- 83:3/q04/p4 The measure example implies an even worse possible fraud in weighed goods because a small weight difference can cost more. — Sayyid Ṣibghat Allāh al-Ḥaydawī argues weighed goods are often more valuable, so mentioning the lesser harm implies the greater. Al-Ālūsī replies that reversing the verse's contrast could support an inverse implication equally well, with ellipsis like antimetabole. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صبغة الله الحيدوي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 83:3/q04/p5 The verse condemns unfair taking and giving in general without specifying commodities or every method on each side. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this to other exegetes. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the rival attempts to account for the exact trading methods are weak; the pair of verses concerns unequal reciprocal rights. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 83:3/q05 What does يُخْسِرُونَ mean here?  [يُخْسِرُونَ · meaning]
- 83:3/q05/p1 They cause the other party loss by reducing the amount, weight, or right due to them. — Muqātil, Ibn Qutaybah, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī and others gloss it as shorting the recipient. Al-Zamakhsharī compares a scale that loses or causes loss; al-Zajjāj reports both akhsara and khasara al-mīzān for diminution, while al-Muʾarrij calls the expression Quraysh usage. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn distinguish intransitive khasira from causative akhsara; al-Samīn calls يُخْسِرُونَ the answer to إذا and supplies 'people of their goods' as omitted object. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies monetary loss. Al-Bursawī says the shortfall violates the equality intended by measures and scales. Al-Zajjāj accepts a form yukh'sirūn in Arabic but knows of no one reciting that form here. [28 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:3/q06 What kind of shortfall is meant by cheating in measure?  [يُخْسِرُونَ · interpretation]
- 83:3/q06/p1 It may be a tiny, easily hidden shortfall in another person's due measure or weight. — Al-Ṭabarī traces muṭaffif to something scant; al-Jishumī says ṭafīf means tiny and that even subtle theft or a slight shortfall counts. Abū Ḥayyān calls it an inconspicuous small reduction. Al-Jishumī reports one derivation from the small missing amount and another characterization as covert small theft. His related usage includes an underfilled vessel and descendants of Adam being near a full measure. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 83:3/q07 What conduct and moral lesson does this verse expose?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse's point is expressed as unfair exchange itself, the human recognition of justice and judgment, or a rule spanning duties outside trade.
- 83:3/q07/p1 The cheaters demand their full due when receiving but give others less, violating reciprocal justice. — Muqātil reports an early market with distinct buying and selling measures and scales. Al-Ṭabarī describes withholding what people are owed; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, Elmalılı, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Asad and Iṣlāḥī stress the one-sided demand. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls equal reciprocal claims foundational to society. Iṣlāḥī says even the dishonest trader protests when cheated and knows both sides should be treated honestly; selfishness overpowers moral awareness. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls short measure injustice, theft and meanness, traits Arabs separately disdained. Al-Biqāʿī notes shameless fraud in the customers' presence. Al-Bursawī says the conduct explains condemnation and the deserved warning. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:3/q07/p2 The unequal treatment reveals an innate sense of justice and points to final judgment. — Iṣlāḥī says God-given human nature approves justice and dislikes wrong even in thieves, murderers and adulterers who would reject such wrongs against their own households. Desire or emotion overcomes observance, not recognition; the Creator who gave this distinction must distinguish just and unjust people in judgment. He links the reasoning to 75:14–15 and says verses 1–6 rebuke those who act as if they will not face their Lord. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise reads the opening warning through resurrection and judgment. [11 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 83:3/q07/p3 The warning reaches every practical and moral relationship in which people owe rights or duties. — Asad extends the full-versus-short contrast to others' rights, obligations and possessions. Study Quran extends it to intentionally falling short in anything God ordained, with shame and suffering as a possible consequence. Ibn Masʿūd likens prayer to a measure that God repays fully when it is complete. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(ابن مسعود)]

## 83:3/q08 Where and amid what practice was the warning revealed?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether reports placing the first verses among Medinan merchants or the Anṣār establish the occasion and location, or whether the Meccan and transit accounts better fit.
- 83:3/q08/p1 It was revealed in Medina amid widespread dishonest measuring, after which people corrected their practice. — Ibn ʿAbbās says Medinan measuring was especially corrupt and the revelation reformed people; Study Quran likewise reports that Madinans weighed honestly afterward. Asad says traditions cited by al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr place at least the opening four verses just after the Prophet arrived. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes an early Medinan Muslim warning and condemnation of polytheists in both Medina and Mecca. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says context suggests a Medinan opening. Iṣlāḥī challenges the report's setting, saying Meccan traders were at least as plausible and the Anṣār were agricultural. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 83:3/q08/p2 A Medinan man named Abū Juhayna with a full and a short measure occasioned the warning. — Al-Suddī says Abū Juhayna took with the full measure and gave with the short one; the same account calls this the first surah revealed in Medina. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي)]
- 83:3/q08/p3 Dishonest measuring among the Anṣār occasioned the warning. — Iṣlāḥī reports this exegetical account but argues that Meccan merchants were no less likely cheats and that the passage's main point is justice, judgment and accountability. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(exegetes); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 83:3/q08/p4 The warning concerned a man who kept separate buying and selling scales. — Study Quran attributes this report to Abū Hurayrah. Muqātil more broadly reports a pre-Islamic market practice using separate measures and scales to take extra and give short. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Abū Hurayrah); MUQATIL]
- 83:3/q08/p5 The revelation came between Mecca and Medina to reform people before the Prophet arrived. — Abū Ḥayyān records this as another account alongside the Medinan report. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 83:3/q08/p6 The surah is Meccan, and the trade context is at least as plausible among Meccan merchants. — Iṣlāḥī argues against limiting the occasion to the Anṣār, who farmed, when Meccans traded; even if Anṣār cheated, he says that does not make the practice especially theirs. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:3/q09 What reported examples and warnings accompany this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The sources preserve distinct reports and exempla, not competing versions of one event.
- 83:3/q09/p1 Short measure is linked to drought or crop failure among five communal consequences of wrongdoing. — Ibn ʿAbbās transmits a Prophetic warning pairing broken covenants with enemies, abandoning God's judgment with poverty, immorality with plague, short measure with drought, and withheld alms with lack of rain. Al-Qurṭubī says al-Bazzār gives the gist and Mālik also relates it from Ibn ʿUmar. Study Quran's Prophetic version has fornication followed by death and fraud by crop failure and famine; it states no grading. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 83:3/q09/p2 ʿIkrima swore that every measurer or weigher faces the Fire. — Al-Ṭabarī adds that none gives the same amount they themselves demand. Al-Qurṭubī says ʿIkrima maintained this even when asked about his son. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 83:3/q09/p3 Ibn ʿUmar warned merchants that short-measurers stand at resurrection in sweat halfway up their ears. — Nāfiʿ reports Ibn ʿUmar telling merchants to fear God and give full measure and weight. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(نافع); QURTUBI-FULL(نافع)]
- 83:3/q09/p4 A dying man with two measures saw them become mountains of fire when struck together. — Mālik ibn Dīnār tells the story of his neighbor. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن دينار)]
- 83:3/q09/p5 Cheating the scale blackens the perpetrator's face on Resurrection Day. — Al-Fuḍayl is the reported speaker. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الفضيل)]
- 83:3/q09/p6 A Bedouin rebuked a ruler taking Muslims' property without measuring it. — The Bedouin challenged ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Marwān by comparing his conduct with the cheaters' threatened fate. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(عبد الملك بن مروان)]
- 83:3/q09/p7 ʿAlī required accurate weight first and any extra gift afterward. — ʿAbd Khayr says ʿAlī overturned a scale yielding excess saffron. Al-Aṣmaʿī's Bedouin saying places manliness outside measure rims and scale tongues; that saying is also attributed to ʿAlī. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,عبد خير)]
- 83:3/q09/p8 A deputy's dawn recitation prompted prayer against a trader who bought full and sold short. — Abū Hurayrah reports the Medinan deputy's recitation and his own prayer against the trader. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 83:3/q09/p9 Prayer resembles a measure: the one who fills it is repaid fully. — Ibn Masʿūd uses measuring as an analogy for completing prayer and God's full recompense. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن مسعود)]

## 83:3/q10 How does this warning connect to the surrounding surahs and judgment theme?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw different connections: surah sequence and judgment, innate moral knowledge, or another verse on the balance.
- 83:3/q10/p1 It follows the preceding surah's account of the blessed, the wretched and the Great Day by naming a petty sin for judgment. — Abū Ḥayyān says the preceding surah discussed those fates and judgment, then this surah singles out cheating for negligible gain. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this surah moves from warning cheaters of resurrection to the fates of wicked and righteous. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 83:3/q10/p2 It supplements al-Infiṭār by arguing for judgment from the moral distinction built into human nature. — Iṣlāḥī says both surahs divide the righteous and defiant; al-Infiṭār reasons from God's attributes seen in the body, while this surah uses the human approval of justice and rejection of wrong. He refers to his treatment of 75:14–15. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:3/q10/p3 The prohibition against reducing the balance in 55:9 reinforces the charge against short-measurers. — The commentators reported by al-Wāḥidī compare the sellers' reduction of goods with that prohibition. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون)]

## 83:3/q11 What does the conditional form contribute?  [وَإِذَا يُخْسِرُونَ · grammar]
- 83:3/q11/p1 The present-tense response states a general or habitual consequence: whenever they give by measure or weight, they short others. — Al-Burhān says most present-tense conditional responses are general, giving cheating in measure, mocking and arrogance as examples. Al-Samīn identifies يُخْسِرُونَ as the answer to إذا. Al-Bursawī calls this their characteristic conduct. [3 notes: BURHAN; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 83:3/q12 What inward reading is drawn from this warning?  [* · inward]
- 83:3/q12/p1 The true scale of justice weighs character and deeds; arrogance makes people discount others' virtues. — Al-Qāshānī extends cheating beyond literal measures to moral evaluation, linking arrogant love of superiority and depreciation of others to 3:188. Al-Jishumī states that only piety gives one person superiority over another. [3 notes: QASHANI; JISHUMI]
