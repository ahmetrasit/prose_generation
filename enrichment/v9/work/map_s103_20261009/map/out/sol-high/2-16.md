# 2:16: 32 questions

## 2:16/q01 Who are 'those' in this verse?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether context limits the group.
- 2:16/q01/p1 The hypocrites described above. — Abū Ḥayyān traces their false reform and mockery; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr favor the immediate context of 2:8 onward. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين)+]
- 2:16/q01/p2 The People of the Book, especially Jews expecting Muhammad. — Qatāda, al-Suddī and Muqātil are cited; Abū Ḥayyān notes concern for status and income. Al-Ālūsī calls this distant as an occasion. [7 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(مقاتل والكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي ومقاتل)]
- 2:16/q01/p3 All unbelievers. — Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās are cited; al-Ālūsī allows general application but rejects this as intended referent. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود)]
- 2:16/q01/p4 A people described before they existed. — Abū Ḥayyān reports divine disclosure of future unseen knowledge. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون)]

## 2:16/q02 What does the demonstrative and relative description convey?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative itself conveys contempt.
- 2:16/q02/p1 They mark extreme evil and concentrate the group's traits. — Bursevī sees exceptional evil; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the relative clause restricts the description rhetorically, despite other sinners. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:16/q02/p2 The demonstrative makes the described class vivid, without inherent contempt. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the far demonstrative for the Qurʾān's grandeur at 2:2 and Qays's near demonstrative for distant death. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:16/q03 How is the opening nominal sentence parsed?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether الذين is predicate or modifier.
- 2:16/q03/p1 أولئك is subject and الذين with its relative clause is predicate. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn agree. The alternative lacks a linking pronoun and strains definiteness and the past relative clause. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم)-]
- 2:16/q03/p2 الذين modifies أولئك; a later clause is predicate. — Al-Nasafī reports this; al-Samīn objects to the adjective or apposition analysis. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 2:16/q03/p3 الذين is a second subject or has conditional force, making فما ربحت predicate. — Al-Samīn reports and rejects it; Abū Ḥayyān rejects analogous parsing at 2:262 for lack of a linking pronoun. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:16/q04 What does 'purchased' mean in Arabic usage here?  [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Exchange versus choice.
- 2:16/q04/p1 It denotes exchange or substitution, extended to abstract things. — Ibn Qutayba and others distinguish buying, selling and exchange; al-Wāḥidī stresses desire for what is bought. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Bashāma and ʿAntara; al-Biqāʿī reports root persistence. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; THALABI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:16/q04/p2 It means choosing or preferring. — Qatāda cites 41:17; al-Ṭabarī adduces al-Aʿshā, Dhū al-Rumma and another poet but says following trade imagery favors exchange. Al-Zajjāj's poem describes adopting Christianity. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ذو الرمة,شاعر,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, R, Q); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TAB-FULL-; STUDYQURAN-]

## 2:16/q05 What exactly did they exchange, and had they possessed guidance?  [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ بِٱلْهُدَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether prior guidance was actual, available, innate, or only professed.
- 2:16/q05/p1 They took unbelief and abandoned faith. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and al-Suddī gloss guidance as the price. Al-Ṭabarī cites 2:8 against a prior true profession. Al-Jishumī prefers this; Qatāda describes shifts from unity, safety and Sunnah to division, fear and innovation. [35 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدى,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(أكثر المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن,علي بن عبيد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI(ابن عباس)+]
- 2:16/q05/p2 They chose error although guidance was available, without possessing it. — Al-Wāḥidī describes abandoning its obligation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī liken availability to possession; Abū Ḥayyān prefers a capacity for guidance as price, not attained guidance. Al-Ālūsī cites signs, miracles and counsel. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, R, Q); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; TAB-FULL-; STUDYQURAN-]
- 2:16/q05/p3 They believed and then disbelieved. — Mujāhid is transmitted repeatedly; Ibn Kathīr notes different kinds of hypocrites and 63:3. Al-Ṭabarī cites 2:8 and al-Jishumī rejects this for this passage. [10 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TAB-FULL-; JISHUMI(مجاهد)-]
- 2:16/q05/p4 Jews believed in Muhammad's advent before his mission, then rejected him. — Muqātil says they expected an Isaacite prophet and envied the Ishmaelite Muhammad. [7 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI(مقاتل والكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:16/q05/p5 They exchanged innate disposition or sound reason. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī cite a prophetic report; al-Ālūsī replies to the objection that fitra is common by pointing to parental Judaizing and later hypocrisy. [11 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:16/q05/p6 They gave up outward profession and worship while inwardly disbelieving. — Al-Jishumī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr report or develop this. Al-Ālūsī rejects outward ritual as the relevant purchase price. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:16/q05/p7 They exchanged the obedience for which God created them. — Ibn Kaysān proposes it; Abū Ḥayyān objects that one specifically created to obey could not disobey, citing 51:56 and 11:119. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كيسان)-]
- 2:16/q05/p8 They chose punishment and destruction instead of Paradise and reward. — Al-Jishumī reports this from Abū Muslim. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أكثر المفسرين)]

## 2:16/q06 What do misguidance and guidance mean?  [ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Which paired states the terms denote.
- 2:16/q06/p1 Unbelief versus faith. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda and al-Suddī are widely cited; al-Qurṭubī cites 41:17. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); JISHUMI(أكثر المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:16/q06/p2 Doubt versus certainty. — Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān report it. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:16/q06/p3 Ignorance versus knowledge. — Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān report it. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:16/q06/p4 Division versus community, or insecurity versus safety. — Abū Ḥayyān lists the pair; Qatāda adds movement from Sunnah to innovation. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 2:16/q06/p5 Worldly life versus afterlife, or Hell versus Paradise. — Abū Ḥayyān lists these pairs; al-Qurṭubī contrasts the trades of Paradise's and Fire's people. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري)]
- 2:16/q06/p6 Deviation, confusion or destruction, extended to religious error. — Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī define deviation; al-Qurṭubī calls confusion primary, citing 26:20, and destruction at 32:10. - 2:16/q07/p1 The canonical reading has ḍamma. — Ibn Mujāhid, Aḥmad ibn Mūsā and al-Ṭabrisī report agreement; al-Jishumī rejects variants for lacking transmission. - 2:16/q09/p1 The weak final yāʾ changes or drops before plural wāw and the next sukūn. — Al-Wāḥidī derives ishtariyū; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains vowel change and deletion; al-Samīn gives two routes. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; FARISI-HUJJA]

## 2:16/q10 How is بالهدى read?  [بِٱلْهُدَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Opening, intermediate sound and imāla among readers.
- 2:16/q10/p1 Nafiʿ generally uses an intermediate sound for yāʾ-final forms. — Ibn Mujāhid and Abū ʿAlī cite Qālūn and Warsh; al-Musayyibī instead reports Nafiʿ opening all. Ibn Khalawayh explains the range of sounds. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 2:16/q10/p2 Ibn Kathīr opens the forms. — Ibn Mujāhid and Abū ʿAlī report it; al-Musayyibī reports the same for Nafiʿ. [2 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير)]
- 2:16/q10/p3 Abū ʿAmr uses intermediate imāla at verse endings and opening elsewhere. — Certain feminine forms remain intermediate. Abū ʿAlī discusses rāʾ before yāʾ and its disappearance when yāʾ drops; two transmitters differ. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو)]
- 2:16/q10/p4 Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī incline hudā; others open it. — Abū Ḥayyān connects imāla to Tamīm and opening to Quraysh; Ibn Khalawayh says Ḥamza distinguishes final yāʾ and wāw. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو السماك)]
- 2:16/q10/p5 ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr opens most forms but inclines named exceptions. — Abū ʿAlī names رأى, رمى, رآه and نأى; he gives junction examples at 17:83, 41:51, 17:72 and 6:77. [1 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم)]

## 2:16/q11 What does the bāʾ in بالهدى mark?  [بِٱلْهُدَىٰ · grammar]
- 2:16/q11/p1 It marks the price or thing surrendered. — Al-Samīn says الضلالة is object and بالهدى attaches to اشتروا; he explains 4:74 by leaving hypocrisy for faith and jihad. Bursevī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ apply the same rule. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 2:16/q12 What legal inference is drawn from the exchange wording?  [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ بِٱلْهُدَىٰ · law]
turns on: Whether it supports agreement by conduct or limits an option.
- 2:16/q12/p1 Mutual conduct can conclude a sale without spoken offer and acceptance. — Al-Nasafī describes taking an item and leaving its agreed price; Bursevī agrees. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:16/q12/p2 A purchase option is barred for uneven units of one kind. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Mālik applying the metaphor to this ruling. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك)]

## 2:16/q13 How does the fāʾ link purchase to lack of profit?  [فَمَا · grammar]
turns on: Immediate coordination versus an implied condition.
- 2:16/q13/p1 It coordinates failure with purchase and marks immediate consequence. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī attach it to the relative clause; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the act exposes both failure and lack of guidance. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 2:16/q13/p2 It implies a condition: if they exchange, their trade fails. — Al-Jishumī suggests this; al-Samīn reports a related conditional parse, opposed by Abū Ḥayyān. [2 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:16/q14 What does the negative mā do in فما ربحت?  [فَمَا رَبِحَت · grammar]
- 2:16/q14/p1 With a past verb it negates the act without changing inflection, much like لم. — Al-Zarkashī contrasts nominal and imperfect uses, notes Ibn al-Ḥājib's contextual tenses and Sībawayh's emphatic mā, then cites 5:19 and a mā-plus-illā counting rule. [9 notes: BURHAN(ابن الحاجب,بعضهم,سيبويه)]

## 2:16/q15 What do profit and trade mean here?  [رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · meaning]
- 2:16/q15/p1 Profit is increase beyond capital, and trade seeks gain through dealing. — Al-Wāḥidī cites a poem for tajara; al-Jishumī gives تجار as plural of تاجر. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr give commercial definitions. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:16/q16 Why is profit attributed to trade rather than traders?  [فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Ordinary ellipsis versus attributional metaphor and its force.
- 2:16/q16/p1 Arabic attributes the trader's result to the trade; the traders gained nothing. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Akhfash, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and later writers cite sleeping nights, resolved matters, 34:33, 47:21, 2:177, 12:82 and Jarīr. Al-Farrāʾ requires clear context. Al-Jurjānī argues this syntactic figure has force beyond literal paraphrase, with poems about drought and concrete idioms. Al-Akhfash also reports an alternative 2:171 comparison: gods and sheep alike do not hear or understand their caller. [57 notes: FARRA(بعض بني أسد); AKHFASH(some,الشاعر); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الشاعر,جرير بن الخطفي); ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JURJANI-DALAIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(التفتازاني)]
- 2:16/q16/p2 Trade's causal or associative relation licenses figurative attribution. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls trade a cause; al-Zarkashī distinguishes lexical resemblance from rational association, citing verses on caused faith and ruin. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Taftāzānī treat even the negation as figurative. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(التفتازاني)+]

## 2:16/q17 Why does the verse continue the buying image with profit and trade?  [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 2:16/q17/p1 The commercial words complete and reinforce the purchase metaphor. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the end echoes the opening. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Zarkashī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite poems extending images through nests, silk, birds and lizards. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes a literal companion from a second metaphor. [14 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 2:16/q18 What does their failure to profit imply about the exchange?  [فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether no surplus alone proves loss of capital.
- 2:16/q18/p1 They lost guidance as capital and all true gain. — Al-Ṭabarī names confusion, blindness and fear now and punishment later; al-Ṭabrisī contrasts believers' trade. Al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr treat guidance, fitra or reason as capital. Sufyān al-Thawrī cites 61:10; worldly gains are no genuine profit. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(سفيان الثوري); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 2:16/q18/p2 The no-profit phrase alone denies only surplus; the next clause proves capital loss. — Abū Ḥayyān replies to al-Zamakhsharī that a trader may break even. The second clause completes the thought, illustrated by Imruʾ al-Qays; al-Ālūsī says religious trade cannot end neutrally. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 2:16/q18/p3 They gained nothing because they falsely expected reward. — Abū Ḥayyān cites claims of divine favor and short punishment, then a hadith that Ibn Judʿān's generosity did not benefit him without prayer for forgiveness on Judgment. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:16/q19 Is تجارتهم read in the singular or plural?  [تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · readings]
turns on: Collective singular versus individual trades.
- 2:16/q19/p1 The majority read singular 'their trade'. — Al-Jishumī notes weak transmission for variants; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī explain the collective sense. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة)]
- 2:16/q19/p2 Ibrāhīm ibn Abī ʿAbla reads plural 'their trades'. — Al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report that each person has a separate trade. [4 notes: THALABI(إبراهيم ابن أبي عبلة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(إبراهيم بن أبي عبلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة)]

## 2:16/q20 How are تجارة and ضلالة pluralized?  [تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ · grammar]
- 2:16/q20/p1 Ibn Kaysān allows تجاير or تجائر and ضلايل or ضلائل. — Al-Naḥḥās gives unhamzated forms and al-Qurṭubī hamzated ones; these are lexical plurals, not verse readings. [2 notes: NAHHAS(ابن كيسان); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كيسان)]

## 2:16/q21 What does 'they were not guided' mean?  [وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُهْتَدِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Faith generally, escape from error, purchase judgment, or divine knowledge.
- 2:16/q21/p1 They remained astray and could not escape misguidance. — Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī explain escape from error; al-Baghawī offers general astray. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the past form implies entrenched, longstanding failure. [7 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:16/q21/p2 They made no sound choice in this purchase or trade. — Al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī cite the purchase; al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿĀshūr portray ignorance of a profitable route. Ibn ʿĀshūr separates ordinary commercial discernment from religious guidance. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:16/q21/p3 They missed the believers' profitable trade. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī report it; al-Ṭabrisī contrasts the companions and believers. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:16/q21/p4 Their lack of guidance was already in God's knowledge. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya permits it; al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report it. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:16/q21/p5 They lacked knowledge of God. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports the explanation. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 2:16/q21/p6 They forfeited the capacity to receive guidance. — Abū Ḥayyān uses an absent road sign to illustrate missing means and distinguishes capacity surrendered from guidance attained; Bursevī describes invalidated innate readiness. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 2:16/q22 Why deny both profit and guidance?  [فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُهْتَدِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Intensification, proof of capital loss, or distinct sense.
- 2:16/q22/p1 The pair intensifies blame: a trader may fail to profit yet act sensibly. — Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān make the distinction. Al-Ālūsī calls it completion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it exposes foolish aims. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:16/q22/p2 The second denial establishes capital loss beyond missed surplus. — Abū Ḥayyān says no-profit may be break-even; al-Ālūsī says the final clause settles true loss. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it also denies ordinary navigation to their aim. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:16/q22/p3 The second denial attaches back to the earlier purchase. — Al-Sharīf prefers this; al-Ālūsī objects that the order and closeness of meanings weaken it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشريف); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:16/q23 How is مهتدين parsed and formed?  [مُهْتَدِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Predicate of كان versus circumstantial analysis.
- 2:16/q23/p1 It is the accusative predicate of كان, from plural مهتديين. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Ṭabrisī give the syntax. Al-Ṭabrisī compares complete كان at 2:282. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say form VIII responds to a transitive base; اشتال is no counterexample because it equals شال. [12 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; NAHHAS(الفراء)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)-]
- 2:16/q23/p2 It was originally a necessary circumstantial phrase. — Al-Farrāʾ says it cannot be omitted; Abū Ḥayyān reports and rejects his reasoning. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:16/q24 What occasion or setting is reported for this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: General hypocrites, foretelling, or Ibn Ubayy.
- 2:16/q24/p1 It concerns hypocrites generally. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the exegetes; Ibn ʿĀshūr reads 2:8 onward collectively. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:16/q24/p2 It foretells a people before they existed. — Abū Ḥayyān reports unseen disclosure. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون)]
- 2:16/q24/p3 The meeting verse concerns ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy and companions. — Abū Ḥayyān reports his praise of Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿAlī to believers, followed by his claim to have turned them away; the earlier passage remains general. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون)]

## 2:16/q25 How does this verse connect with the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 2:16/q25/p1 It sums up the hypocrites' portrait, explains wandering, and contrasts their end with the guided. — Al-Ālūsī relates it to 2:15; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the detached sentence restates that ending and contrasts 2:5 and 2:7. Elmalılı says following parables make the loss tangible. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:16/q26 What broader lesson does the trade image teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:16/q26/p1 Life is a trade whose true gain requires guidance and upright conduct. — Sufyān al-Thawrī asks everyone to examine their trade, citing 61:10. Elmalılı says religion, sound understanding and honesty sustain commerce; deception fails. Kur'an Yolu says hypocrites spend life, reason and will on error. [4 notes: THALABI(سفيان الثوري); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:16/q27 What inward loss does the purchase depict?  [* · inward]
- 2:16/q27/p1 They squander original light and receptivity, ending veiled from God. — Al-Qāshānī says knowledge, works, gnosis, wisdom and virtue could perfect innate light. Bursevī cites 83:14–15 and Najmī interpretations of desire and worldly attachment; al-Daqqāq's disciple must first remove love of the world before extra worship. [10 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 2:16/q28 Is metaphor possible in the Qur'an, as this attribution illustrates?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether divine speech can use metaphor.
- 2:16/q28/p1 Yes: rational metaphor attributes an action to a related cause. — Al-Zarkashī reports the majority, distinguishes resemblance from association, and cites Pharaoh, Satan, recited verses and other causes. He notes Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām's work, al-Ḥātimī's etymology and al-Aṣmaʿī on likenesses. [12 notes: BURHAN(الأصمعي,الجمهور,الحاتمي)]
- 2:16/q28/p2 No: God has no need for nonliteral expression. — Ibn al-Qaṣṣ and Ibn Khuwayz Mindād argue this. Al-Zarkashī replies that it would remove emphasis, ellipsis and much Qurʾānic beauty. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن القاص وابن خويز منداد); BURHAN-]

## 2:16/q29 How is the nearby pronoun hāʾ pronounced?  [* · readings]
- 2:16/q29/p1 Ibn Kathīr lengthens its vowel with a matching glide. — Ibn Khalawayh reports yāʾ after kasra and wāw after ḍamma; other readers indicate the vowel without an extra letter to avoid adjacent consonants around weak hāʾ. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(ابن كثير)]

## 2:16/q30 How is the nearby مستهزئون pronounced?  [* · readings]
turns on: Full hamza versus lightening or replacement.
- 2:16/q30/p1 The hamza is articulated fully, with a lighter intermediate form also possible. — Al-Zajjāj prefers full articulation; the lightened hamza lies between wāw and hamza. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ+]
- 2:16/q30/p2 Its hamza may become yāʾ, while dropping it outright is weak. — Al-Zajjāj gives مستهزيون and calls مستهزون weak except under a dialectal hamza-to-yāʾ change. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 2:16/q31 What do nearby يمدهم and طغيانهم mean?  [* · meaning]
- 2:16/q31/p1 They mean giving respite and their excess or unbelief. — Al-Zajjāj supplies the two glosses for the neighboring passage. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 2:16/q32 What does nearby يعمهون mean?  [* · meaning]
- 2:16/q32/p1 They wander in confusion, blind to guidance. — Al-Zajjāj cites عمه and عامه for a confused person and an anonymous rajaz poet for confused people blind to guidance. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ(الراجز)]
