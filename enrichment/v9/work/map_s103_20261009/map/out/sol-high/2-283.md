# 2:283: 53 questions

## 2:283/q01 What does safar mean and evoke?  [سَفَرٍۢ · meaning]
- 2:283/q01/p1 Travel, whose root conveys uncovering or disclosure. — Wahidi cites travel exposing character, the book making clear, dawn, uncovering a face, reconciliation, sweeping, and Ibn al-Aʿrabi's al-Akhtal line; al-Azhari says the traveler emerges into open country and cites daylight and Sirius; Razi repeats these cognates. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,ابن الأعرابي,الأزهري); RAZI-FULL(أهل اللغة); BURSEVI]

## 2:283/q02 How does 'on a journey' work in the sentence?  [عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍۢ · grammar]
- 2:283/q02/p1 It means being in the state of travel; ʿalā evokes firm establishment in it. — Abu Hayyan glosses the phrase 'while traveling'; Alusi calls it a dependent metaphor and allows coordination or a circumstantial reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:283/q03 Why mention travel here?  [عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conditional describes a typical obstacle or limits lawful pledging.
- 2:283/q03/p1 Travel is the common setting where writing or witnesses are unavailable; comparable obstacles also call for security. — Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Elmalılı and Ibn Ashur cite scarcity of scribes, tools, witnesses, night, busyness, or doubtful debtor standing. Burhan notes a conditional need not be legally restrictive. Razi compares the fear clause in 4:101. [25 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور من العلماء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TAB-FULL(الربيع,الضحاك); THALABI; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:283/q03/p2 The travel condition is restrictive for pledges. — Mujahid, Dahhak and Dawud follow the apparent conditional wording; Dahhak also requires absence of a scribe. Tabari rejects its restriction on pledges by the armor report, while others reject it as customary context. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد وغيرهم من السلف); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); TAB-FULL-; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك وداود)-; BAYDAWI-FULL-]

## 2:283/q04 What is read and meant by 'a scribe'?  [كَاتِبًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the absent resource is a writer, writing materials, or written documents, and how the consonantal text is read.
- 2:283/q04/p1 Kitāban, writing or writing materials, including pen, ink and paper. — Ibn Abbas, Ubayy, Mujahid and Abu al-Aliya say a writer may be present without materials; Tabari records variant specific lacks of ink, paper, or writer. Nahhas calls it anomalous; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi find its sense apt despite codex spelling. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,جماعة من المتقدمين,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس ومجاهد وعكرمة والضحاك وأبو العالية); THALABI(ابن عباس وأبو العالية ومجاهد); JISHUMI(ابن عباس ومجاهد وأبو العالية); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور,النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي، مجاهد، أبو العالية، ابن عباس، الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR(أبي ومجاهد وأبو العالية,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,الضحاك,عكرمة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); NAHHAS-]
- 2:283/q04/p2 Kātiban, a single writer. — Tabari prefers the codex and regional reading; Nahhas relates it to 2:282's 'let a writer write'; Thalabi cites the majority and codex. Other sources note the common reading. [9 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور,النحاس); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL+; THALABI+]
- 2:283/q04/p3 Kuttāban or kitāban as plural writers. — Dahhak reads a plural of 'writer'; Nahhas says kitāb can be a collective of scribes; Ibn Abbas reasons each case has a scribe. [8 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); JISHUMI(الضحاك); NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي، مجاهد، أبو العالية، ابن عباس، الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR(أبي ومجاهد وأبو العالية,ابن عباس والضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:283/q04/p4 Kutuban, written books or records. — Abu al-Aliya's plural of kitāb fits multiple cases. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي، مجاهد، أبو العالية، ابن عباس، الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR(أبو العالية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية)]

## 2:283/q05 How is 'you do not find a scribe' attached syntactically?  [وَلَمْ تَجِدُوا۟ كَاتِبًۭا · grammar]
- 2:283/q05/p1 It may coordinate with the condition, attach to kāna's predicate, or be a circumstantial clause. — Samin distinguishes the latter two as nominative-context constructions; Abu Hayyan also lists attachments. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:283/q06 What is a rahn or pledged security?  [فَرِهَٰنٌۭ · meaning]
- 2:283/q06/p1 An item retained or delivered as security for a debt; the root also conveys permanence, detention or firmness. — Wahidi and Razi explain transfer from verbal noun to object name; Qurtubi says it is recoverable by sale proceeds. Ibn Ashur adduces 74:38 and Zuhayr; Farisi cites Abu Zayd and 66:12; poetry in Aʿsha and others shows pledged property, persons and enduring food. [44 notes: MAJAZ(أبو عمرو,قعنب بن أم صاحب); ZAJJAJ(الشاعر,غير واحد); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي,الزجاج وابن الأعرابي); TABRISI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(الأعشى,بعيث); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن السكيت); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); IBNASHUR-FULL(الأعشى,عبد الله بن همام السلولي,كعب بن الأشرف); JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:283/q07 Do rahana and arhana differ in use?  [فَرِهَٰنٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the two verbs are interchangeable in collateral usage and whether arhana carries a separate bidding sense.
- 2:283/q07/p1 Both can mean to pledge collateral. — Zajjaj, Ibn al-Aʿrabi and al-Akhfash allow both; poetry attributed to Ibn Hammam is adduced, but al-Asmaʿi disputes its wording. Wahidi, Qurtubi, Samin and Tabrisi record the disagreement. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ(الشاعر,غير واحد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي,الزجاج وابن الأعرابي); TABRISI(الأصمعي); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج وابن الأعرابي والأخفش,عبد الله بن همام السلولي); SAMIN-DURR(الأصمعي,الفراء)]
- 2:283/q07/p2 Rahana is standard for pledging, while arhana may mean outbidding, giving another's pledge, or keeping something going. — Abu Ali and Abu Zayd distinguish loans and sales from bidding high; Farra gives another-person sense; Ibn Atiyya and Samin cite the high-price camel, and rahantu lisani excludes arhana. [11 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي وأبو زيد); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); JISHUMI]

## 2:283/q08 How is 'pledges' read and formed?  [فَرِهَٰنٌۭ · readings]

## 2:283/q09 How does 'pledges held' function grammatically?  [فَرِهَٰنٌۭ مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: The omitted clause element and whether rahn functions as object noun or verbal noun.
- 2:283/q09/p1 A nominative subject with omitted predicate, or predicate of an omitted subject such as 'the security'; an implied command to take one is also possible. — Jishumi allows nominative and imperative or accusative ellipses; Razi, Baydawi, Qurtubi's Mahdawi and Samin lay out the alternatives; Alusi treats it as security, a thing to take, or a prescription. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:283/q09/p2 The pledge noun is a lexicalized verbal noun used as an object, not a cognate accusative. — Farisi, Wahidi, Razi and Samin distinguish object usage from a verbal noun used adverbially; Ibn Atiyya says its noun status permits the plural with kasra. [6 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:283/q10 When may a pledge be taken?  [عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍۢ فَرِهَٰنٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether the travel and scribe conditions restrict the law or describe a typical need for security.
- 2:283/q10/p1 Pledges are valid during travel or residence, with or without an available scribe. — The Prophet pledged armor to a Jewish seller in Medina; reports differ over twenty or thirty measures of barley and the seller's name. Majority jurists treat travel as an example; Razi compares 4:101. Ibn Atiyya says the verse's general meaning itself extends to home. [36 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد,جمهور من العلماء); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور من العلماء,عائشة,مجاهد والضحاك وداود); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس); DURR-FULL(عائشة); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:283/q10/p2 Only during travel, and for some authorities only if a writer is unavailable. — Mujahid, Dahhak and Dawud take the travel and lack-of-scribe conditions literally; Dahhak directs writing instead whenever possible. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد وغيرهم من السلف); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); TAB-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك وداود)-; BAYDAWI-FULL-]
- 2:283/q10/p3 If writing is available, pledging is permissible but ordinarily not recommended without another reason. — Elmalılı distinguishes legal permission from prudent resort to collateral. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 2:283/q11 What does qabḍ mean in 'held in possession'?  [مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · meaning]
- 2:283/q11/p1 Grasping and holding something in the palm. — Jishumi contrasts emphatic ḍād with qabṣ using un-emphatic ṣād, which means holding with the fingertips. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:283/q12 What does possession do to a pledge's legal effect?  [مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · law]
turns on: At what stage the contract binds, and whose physical custody satisfies maqbudah.
- 2:283/q12/p1 Taking possession is required for validity or binding completion; a mere verbal pledge has no operative security. — Shafi'i and Abu Hanifa treat possession as a condition of validity; many cite 'held.' Sa'id ibn Jubayr and some reports specify receipt by creditor. Ibn Atiyya says creditor or agent certainly counts, and Baghawi says the pledgor cannot reclaim collateral while debt remains. [25 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الشافعي وأبو حنيفة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي,المالكية); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشافعي، أبو حنيفة، المالكية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الشافعي والجمهور,رواية عن أحمد وطائفة); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور,مالك); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hanefî ve Şâfiîler); BAYDAWI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(مالك)-]
- 2:283/q12/p2 Malik treats offer and acceptance as a binding contract before delivery; possession completes its benefit and can be compelled. — Malikis invoke 5:1 and 17:34 and covenant fulfillment; reports distinguish validity, bindingness and usefulness. KuranYolu says valid but binding upon delivery, while other notes say already binding, a nuance in reporting. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المالكية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشافعي، أبو حنيفة، المالكية); ALUSI-FULL(مالك); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hanefî ve Şâfiîler)]

## 2:283/q13 Whose custody counts as possession of a pledge?  [مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether a proxy or neutral custodian counts as a valid holder.
- 2:283/q13/p1 Only creditor possession counts, not holding by a neutral third party. — Al-Hakam, Qatada, Ata, Ibn Abi Layla and a report from Ahmad require the creditor's own receipt. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحكم بن عتيبة وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور العلماء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رواية عن أحمد وطائفة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك وجميع أصحابه وجمهور العلماء)-]
- 2:283/q13/p2 Possession by the creditor's agent suffices. — Ibn Atiyya reports consensus, and Alusi infers it from the passive maqbudah; Abu Hayyan agrees. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:283/q13/p3 A neutral custodian's possession counts. — Malik, his school and the majority treat the custodian as creditor's representative; Qurtubi says loss in the custodian's care creates no liability for creditor or trustee. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك وجميع أصحابه وجمهور العلماء)+; QURTUBI-FULL(جمهور العلماء)+]

## 2:283/q14 What if a pledge returns to the debtor?  [مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether renewed debtor custody undoes the security, and whether its legal form matters.
- 2:283/q14/p1 Return to the debtor voids the pledge. — Ibn Atiyya relates this to its defining held state; Qurtubi follows loss of possession. Abu Hayyan reports Malik says absolute return voids it. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، أبو حنيفة، الشافعي); ALUSI-FULL(مالك)]
- 2:283/q14/p2 A loan or deposit back to the debtor preserves it. — Abu Hanifa makes this exception. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، أبو حنيفة، الشافعي)]
- 2:283/q14/p3 Prior possession continues to count after return. — Qurtubi reports Shafi'i this way, but Abu Hayyan reports him as voiding it whenever returned; the reports conflict. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، أبو حنيفة، الشافعي)]

## 2:283/q15 Can debt claims or uncertain property be pledged?  [رِهَٰنٌۭ مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether the collateral can be physically possessed and realized, especially for debt claims and common shares.
- 2:283/q15/p1 Only valuable, saleable and deliverable property; debts and uncertain property cannot be pledged. — The majority require realizable security and physical possession. A cited Razi argument excludes an undivided share because it cannot be physically received. KuranYolu bars human beings despite pre-Islamic child hostages. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، المالكية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر)-]
- 2:283/q15/p2 A debt or uncertain property can serve as collateral. — Malik permits debt claims and examples such as runaway slave, stray camel, fetus, fish and unripe fruit; Qurtubi argues anything saleable has value. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور، المالكية)]

## 2:283/q16 Can an undivided share be pledged?  [فَرِهَٰنٌۭ مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether an undivided share can be received as security.
- 2:283/q16/p1 An undivided share cannot be pledged. — Abu Hanifa and the argument cited by Razi and Elmalılı rely on inability to take definite possession. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، الشافعي، أبو حنيفة، الحسن بن صالح); ELMALILI]
- 2:283/q16/p2 An undivided property share may be pledged. — Malik and Shafi'i allow divisible or indivisible shares; Ibn al-Mundhir compares sale and notes a Hanafi joint-creditor exception. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، الشافعي، أبو حنيفة، الحسن بن صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر)+]
- 2:283/q16/p3 An undivided share may be pledged only if it cannot be divided. — Al-Hasan ibn Salih's intermediate rule. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، الشافعي، أبو حنيفة، الحسن بن صالح)]

## 2:283/q17 Which growth of collateral remains pledged?  [رِهَٰنٌۭ مَّقْبُوضَةٌۭ · law]
- 2:283/q17/p1 The pledged property's inseparable growth follows the pledge, but separable fruits do not unless stipulated. — Qurtubi distinguishes fattening and offspring from crops, milk, wool and fruit, citing independent alms treatment of the latter. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:283/q18 Does a secured creditor have priority over other creditors?  [فَرِهَٰنٌۭ · law]
- 2:283/q18/p1 The secured creditor has priority over other creditors. — Malik and many jurists give priority when the debtor has not already been declared insolvent; Qurtubi rejects a report of equal sharing. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)+]

## 2:283/q19 May the creditor use pledged property?  [رِهَٰنٌۭ · law]
turns on: Who owns benefits, who pays upkeep, and whether maintenance or the contract permits creditor use.
- 2:283/q19/p1 Benefits belong to the owner, who bears upkeep; creditor use requires permission or is forbidden. — A report says the owner gets yield and bears loss. Malik, Shafi'i, Sha'bi and Ibn Sirin hold it; Khattabi prefers the explicit wording. Tahawi and Sha'bi argue the permissive report preceded the usury bar or was abrogated; Ibn Abd al-Barr reports agreement and warns of unknown benefit and usury. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عبد البر,الطحاوي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL(الخطابي)+]
- 2:283/q19/p2 A creditor who feeds a pledged animal may ride it or drink its milk in proportion to upkeep. — A more explicit Abu Hurayra report is adopted by Ahmad and Ishaq; Abu Thawr, Awza'i and Layth permit analogous use when creditor maintained neglected collateral. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو ثور,أبو هريرة,أحمد وإسحاق,الخطابي)]
- 2:283/q19/p3 A creditor may stipulate use in a sale or lease pledge, but not in a loan pledge. — Ibn Khuwayzimandad says use on a loan is interest. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد)]

## 2:283/q20 May a pledge become the creditor's property after nonpayment?  [رِهَٰنٌۭ · law]
- 2:283/q20/p1 No; pre-Islamic foreclosure at a deadline is barred. — Tabrisi, Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur cite the Prophetic 'the pledge is not foreclosed.' Qurtubi explains the indicative wording and disputed mursal chains; Zuhayr's verse recalls the earlier practice. [8 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 2:283/q21 What happened when the Prophet pledged his armor?  [رِهَٰنٌۭ · reports]
- 2:283/q21/p1 He pledged his armor to a Jewish seller for food on credit while in Medina. — Reports variously give twenty or thirty measures of barley, name Abu al-Shahm or leave the seller unnamed, and say the armor was still pledged at death. Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi recount his response that he was trustworthy. [13 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس); DURR-FULL(عائشة); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TAB-FULL]

## 2:283/q22 What does mutual trust permit in a debt transaction?  [فَإِنْ أَمِنَ بَعْضُكُم بَعْضًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether the trust clause is a general independent option or a concession confined to inability to document.
- 2:283/q22/p1 The parties may omit writing, witnesses and collateral when they trust one another. — Sha'bi, Hasan and many exegetes take the clause as a general concession. Ibn Ashur extends it to each party and home or travel; Tabarisi sees evidence the safeguards are advisory. [35 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الشعبي); IBNABIHATIM(الشعبي,حماد بن أبي سليمان,سعيد بن جبير); ZAJJAJ(قوم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون,أبو عبيد,الحسن); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,الشعبي); DURR-FULL(الشعبي,سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Ṭ); IBNASHUR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)+]
- 2:283/q22/p2 When documentation and witnesses are available, they must still be used; trust is a limited travel concession. — Tabari accepts a restriction on trust while rejecting restriction on pledges; Dahhak ties trust to travel and absence of a writer. Ibn Ashur finds Tabari's explanation unclear and suggests a traveler unable to pledge may rely on trust. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL(الطبري); TAB-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 2:283/q23 Who trusts whom in the trust clause?  [أَمِنَ بَعْضُكُم بَعْضًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the clause describes only reliance on the debtor or obligations on either party.
- 2:283/q23/p1 Ordinarily the creditor trusts the debtor with property or the debt. — Wahidi cites the language of security from betrayal and 8:11, 12:64; Ibn Ashur specifies lender and borrower. Biqai says the undefined parties could operate in either direction. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(اللحياني); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:283/q23/p2 Trust can run both ways between creditor and debtor. — Ibn Ashur says a debtor can trust a creditor holding collateral worth more than the debt; the creditor must return the excess faithfully. Biqai calls trust mutual. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr)]

## 2:283/q24 How is 'if some trust others' alternatively read?  [فَإِنْ أَمِنَ بَعْضُكُم بَعْضًۭا · readings]
- 2:283/q24/p1 Ubayy reads a passive, 'if some are trusted.' — Zamakhshari explains public confidence in the debtor; Samin treats baʿḍan as governed by a deleted preposition and an omitted property or debt complement. Sajaawandi reports an alternate reflexive form. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي، السجاوندي); SAMIN-DURR(أبي,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبي); THALABI]

## 2:283/q25 Who is 'the one entrusted' and what must be given?  [ٱلَّذِى ٱؤْتُمِنَ أَمَٰنَتَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the entrusted person is debtor, pledgee, or the party recording the debt.
- 2:283/q25/p1 The trusted debtor must repay the creditor's debt or property, without denial or delay. — Jishumi and Razi prefer this over pledgee. Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi cite consensus, judgments and reports. Tabari warns against absconding; Ibn Ashur includes returning the same thing or its equivalent. The name encourages faithful payment. [41 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(قوم); IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN; THALABI; JISHUMI+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:283/q25/p2 The pledge holder must return collateral after the debt is paid. — Jishumi and Razi report this alternative because collateral itself is held in trust; Ibn Ashur includes a creditor holding an overvalued pledge as an entrusted party. [4 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI-]
- 2:283/q25/p3 The entrusted party must write the true sum without increase or decrease. — Zajjaj equates faithful rendering with justice in recording the obligation. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 2:283/q26 What is meant by 'his trust' and whose is it?  [أَمَٰنَتَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether amāna names the owed object or the fiduciary relation, and whether its pronoun belongs to creditor or debtor.
- 2:283/q26/p1 It is the debt or property owed, called a trust because security was waived. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai and Alusi explain the nomenclature; the possessive may refer to debtor or creditor. Ibn Ashur says amāna can mean the object or quality of trust and compares a verbal noun joined to its object. [14 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:283/q26/p2 It is the act or obligation of trust, with an omitted 'debt' or a charge to preserve honor by repayment. — Ibn Atiyya gives the honor nuance; Samin gives a verbal noun with omitted possessive and rejects using it as the verb's absolute object; Abu Hayyan lists both referents. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:283/q27 How is 'entrusted' formed and pronounced?  [ٱؤْتُمِنَ · grammar]
- 2:283/q27/p1 It is an eighth-form passive from amāna with an original root hamza. — Akhfash and Nahhas explain the two hamzas and joining alif; Wāḥidī and Jishumi give the active and passive forms. At a fresh start the joining alif prevents two sounded hamzas. [6 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI]

## 2:283/q28 Which recitations of 'entrusted' are accepted?  [ٱؤْتُمِنَ · readings]
turns on: Whether reported vowel gestures, letter substitution, assimilation and pause length fit transmission and Arabic phonology.
- 2:283/q28/p1 The usual connected reading retains a quiescent hamza after the joining hamza drops. — Ibn Mujahid, Farisi and Ibn Atiyya reject reported ḍamma indications as incorrect; Farisi argues the first radical of iftaʿala is still and vowel transfer goes backward. Ibn Ashur describes the majority pronunciation. [15 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، عاصم عن يحيى بن آدم عن أبي بكر، حفص); FARISI-HUJJA(أحمد بن موسى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أحمد بن موسى,سليم عن حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(سليم عن حمزة,عاصم); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أحمد بن موسى)+; IBNMUJAHID+]
- 2:283/q28/p2 A ḍamma-tinged or rounded hamza is transmitted from Asim, Hamza or Abu Bakr. — The transmission is described as ishām or a raised initial alif. Abu Ali defends Ahmad ibn Musa's account against Ibn Atiyya; other authorities reject the notation or performance. [9 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، عاصم عن يحيى بن آدم عن أبي بكر، حفص); FARISI-HUJJA(أحمد بن موسى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أحمد بن موسى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(سليم عن حمزة,عاصم); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي)+; IBNMUJAHID-; FARISI-HUJJA(أحمد بن موسى)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أحمد بن موسى,سليم عن حمزة)-]
- 2:283/q28/p3 An assimilated itamin or attamin form is reported, but its acceptability is disputed. — Zamakhshari and Baydawi reject assimilation because the replacement letter retains hamza's status; Alusi cites Arabic usage, Aisha's speech and a Kufan grammatical defense. - 2:283/q28/p5 At a pause or initial pronunciation, substitute a matching vowel letter with shortening. — Samin explains the wāw-like onset; Ibn al-Jazari rejects restored madd or verified hamza, against Abu Shama's analogy; NASHR sets general rules for still hamza and competing madd causes. ## 2:283/q29 Why add 'fear God, his Lord'?  [وَلْيَتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ رَبَّهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 2:283/q29/p1 The doubled divine reference solemnly presses the debtor to repay and avoid betrayal. — Ibn Ashur says repeating God's name rather than a pronoun adds awe; Abu Hayyan notes 'his Lord' emphasizes piety at both acknowledgment and repayment; Alusi says human rights need guarding. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:283/q30 Who is forbidden to conceal testimony?  [وَلَا تَكْتُمُوا۟ ٱلشَّهَٰدَةَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the prohibition directly addresses transaction witnesses alone or also the debtor's acknowledgment.
- 2:283/q30/p1 Witnesses who know the transaction must disclose their testimony. — Tabari and early authorities address summoned witnesses; Jishumi includes those who attended the contract or pledge without formal appointment. Rabi insists even adverse evidence about self or kin is covered. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع); IBNABIHATIM(الربيع,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:283/q30/p2 Debtors too must acknowledge their own obligation rather than defeat testimony. — Baydawi allows debtors testifying against themselves, Elmalılı and Asad include a debtor denying an unsecured loan; Alusi rejects making debtors the primary addressee. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 2:283/q31 When must testimony be offered?  [وَلَا تَكْتُمُوا۟ ٱلشَّهَٰدَةَ · law]
turns on: Whether only a formal request triggers the duty, or known danger to a right calls for earlier disclosure.
- 2:283/q31/p1 When summoned or when a right risks loss, a witness must testify accurately; the duty may arise before being asked. — Tabari, Ibn Abbas and Ibn Atiyya speak of summons and risk; Qurtubi treats testimony as collective duty becoming individual for needed witnesses. Al-Qaffal and Ibn Ashur cite 'best witness before asked'; Ibn Ashur adds danger of death, absence or forgetting; KuranYolu reconciles reports criticizing unsolicited testimony as about false testimony or God's rights. [42 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الربيع,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو موسى عن النبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال,النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:283/q31/p2 One should avoid disclosing testimony in a venue where it will assist falsehood. — Ibn Atiyya qualifies Ibn Abbas's advice under corrupt conditions when ineffective disclosure may conceal the truth. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 2:283/q32 What does concealing testimony mean?  [وَلَا تَكْتُمُوا۟ ٱلشَّهَٰدَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether concealment is silence when evidence is needed or a false denial of knowledge.
- 2:283/q32/p1 Withholding known evidence, especially failing to speak when needed. — Jishumi contrasts kitmān with disclosure; Razi says refusal can defeat property rights. Ibn Ashur relates the recurring prohibition to 2:282. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:283/q32/p2 Denying that one knows of the event. — Razi cites the denial about Abraham's family in 2:140 as a parallel. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:283/q33 How is the prohibition against concealment read?  [وَلَا تَكْتُمُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: The opening consonant of the prohibitive verb and whether address remains direct.
- 2:283/q33/p1 Third-person plural, 'let them not conceal.' — Abu Abd al-Rahman and al-Sulami transmit yāʾ; Samin relates the form to the preceding scribe and witness, while noting a singular expression can mean a group. [6 notes: NAHHAS(أبو عبد الرحمن); THALABI(السلمي); JISHUMI(السلمي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السلمي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبد الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:283/q33/p2 Second-person plural, directly addressing the hearers. — Jishumi calls this the apparent reading. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:283/q34 What is the force of 'his heart is sinful'?  [فَإِنَّهُۥٓ ءَاثِمٌۭ قَلْبُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase foregrounds a corrupt state of the heart or the sin incurred by this act.
- 2:283/q34/p1 The concealer's heart becomes guilty, wicked or corrupt; concealment is a grave sin. — Tabari, Ibn Abbas, Suddi and others call the heart wicked. Ibn Abbas ranks concealment with shirk and false testimony; Wahidi mentions a sealed, hypocritical heart. A Prophetic report equates it to false testimony. Elmalılı says it approaches unbelief. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع,السدي); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدى,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو موسى عن النبي,ابن عباس,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL(عمر)]
- 2:283/q34/p2 It states the heart has incurred the sin of suppressing testimony. — Mawardi's alternative and Ibn Atiyya stress the guilt attaching to the act. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 2:283/q35 Why attribute sin to the heart?  [ءَاثِمٌۭ قَلْبُهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether attribution is rhetorical causal language or grounds for a stronger claim that the heart is the acting subject.
- 2:283/q35/p1 Concealment begins in inward intention and knowledge, and the heart governs outward acts; naming it intensifies blame. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan cite the Prophetic sound-body morsel; Tabrisi cites 58:22; Qurtubi calls it part-for-whole; Ibn Ashur names perception, emotion and intention. Biqai and Bursevi link a corrupted heart to the body. Alusi rejects extra black-mark and body-effect theories. [21 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكيا); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:283/q35/p2 The heart itself is the acting and accountable subject. — Many theologians infer this from the verse and 26:193. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(كثير من المتكلمين)]
- 2:283/q35/p3 The act can be attributed to an organ because it supplies the act's chief cause. — Opponents of the theological inference compare the eye seeing and ear hearing; Razi says bodily acts follow heart motives. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(من خالف في هذا القول)]

## 2:283/q36 How is 'āthim qalbuhu' parsed?  [ءَاثِمٌۭ قَلْبُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: The governing role of the active participle and whether definiteness permits a specification analysis.
- 2:283/q36/p1 Āthim is the predicate and qalbuhu its nominative agent or subject. — Nahhas lists this; Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Samin report it, and Abu Hayyan and Samin prefer it. It says 'his heart sins.' [7 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:283/q36/p2 Āthim is an advanced predicate and qalbuhu a delayed subject. — Zamakhshari and Abu al-Baqa offer this; Samin notes a Kufan objection to a pronoun pointing to the later noun. [5 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:283/q36/p3 Qalbuhu is partitive apposition to the pronoun or to the hidden subject. — Ibn Atiyya proposes apposition; Basrans object to an unsupported active-participle government in one version, while Farra and Akhfash allow it. [7 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية، ابن أبي عبلة، مكي، الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:283/q36/p4 An intensive explanation makes qalb an accusative specification. — Abu Hatim proposes an analogy, but Nahhas rejects it because qalbuhu is definite. [1 notes: NAHHAS(أبو حاتم)+; NAHHAS-]

## 2:283/q37 What variant forms of 'his heart is sinful' are read?  [ءَاثِمٌۭ قَلْبُهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the heart is nominative, accusative, or object of a finite verb.
- 2:283/q37/p1 Qalbahu in the accusative. — Ibn Abi Abla's variant is parsed as apposition, object-like accusative or specification. - 2:283/q37/p2 Athama or āthama qalbahu, 'he made his heart sinful.' — Ibrahim ibn Abi Aylah and Ibn Abi Abla transmit a verbal reading, with the heart as object or as whole-person metonymy. [5 notes: THALABI(إبراهيم بن أبي عيلة); RAZI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية، ابن أبي عبلة، مكي، الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة)]

## 2:283/q38 To whom does the pronoun in 'indeed he' refer?  [فَإِنَّهُۥٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun resumes 'whoever' or is impersonal with an explanatory clause.
- 2:283/q38/p1 It refers to the person who conceals testimony. — Samin considers this apparent, and Alusi says most natural. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:283/q38/p2 It is a pronoun of the matter explained by what follows. — Samin and Alusi report the option but note a grammatical objection: the following participial phrase is not a full explanatory sentence under dominant Basran and Kufan rules. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:283/q39 What does God's knowledge in the close imply?  [وَٱللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ عَلِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the closing universal statement is heard only as warning or also as promise.
- 2:283/q39/p1 God knows hidden and public deeds, including testimony, and will judge and repay them; the close threatens concealment. — Tabari, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai, Ibn Ashur and others draw the warning. Ibn Atiyya says the general wording can also promise reward. Razi connects 2:284; Ibn Ashur argues complete knowledge assures requital. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:283/q39/p2 The conclusion also promises reward for truthful action. — Ibn Atiyya says its generality permits promise as well as threat; Alusi states good and evil repayment. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:283/q40 How is 'what you do' read?  [بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ · readings]
turns on: The person prefix of the closing verb.
- 2:283/q40/p1 Third-person plural, 'what they do.' — Al-Sulami keeps the third-person form used in his other variants. [2 notes: THALABI(السلمي); SAMIN-DURR(السلمي)]
- 2:283/q40/p2 Second-person plural, direct address. — The verse's apparent wording addresses the hearers. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:283/q41 How does this verse continue the debt documentation rules?  [* · links]
- 2:283/q41/p1 It offers pledges when writing or witnessing cannot be done, then allows mutual trust and reminds witnesses to disclose evidence. — Jishumi and Ibn Ashur connect 2:282's scribe and witnesses to this substitute. Ibn Ashur notes the scribe may also witness and calls the final ban a renewed charge. Tabarisi sees practical divine guidance in both verses; Biqai sees a closure of dealings among people. [13 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الحرالي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 2:283/q42 What wordplay and ellipsis mark the debt passage?  [* · rhetoric]
- 2:283/q42/p1 The passage displays coordinated wordplay, ellipsis and antithesis. — Abu Hayyan and Samin identify debt/indebtedness, writer/writing, entrustment/trust, concealment/concealer, and the earlier err/remember and small/large pairs; Abu Hayyan inventories implied subjects and objects. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:283/q43 Do the trust words abrogate the earlier commands to document debts?  [فَإِنْ أَمِنَ بَعْضُكُم بَعْضًۭا · sciences]
turns on: Whether earlier directives were obligatory, recommended, or later specified by the trust exception.
- 2:283/q43/p1 Yes; permission to rely on trust supersedes previous requirements. — Abu Sa'id, Amir, Sha'bi, Hasan, Hakam, Ibn Jurayj and others are reported. Ibn Kathir calls Abu Sa'id's chain good; Abu Ubayd says Hijaz and Iraq scholars agree sale witnesses optional. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو سعيد الخدري,عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون,أبو عبيد,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); ELMALILI(Hasan, Şa‘bî, Hakem b. Uteybe); IBNASHUR-FULL(الشعبي); STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Ṭ)]
- 2:283/q43/p2 No formal abrogation: safeguards were recommendations, and trust is a permitted choice. — Razi invokes Ibn Abbas that the debt passage contains no abrogation; Elmalılı notes no proof of later revelation and majority agreement. Ibn Ashur suggests early 'abrogation' can mean specifying a circumstance. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI+]
- 2:283/q43/p3 The clause specifies travel cases where documenting is unavailable, preserving obligatory writing and witnessing elsewhere. — Tabari and other defenders of obligation confine trust to the pledge case; Ibn Ashur finds the account unclear. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL(الطبري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Ṭ); TAB-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 2:283/q44 Are writing, witnesses and collateral required or recommended?  [* · law]
turns on: How imperative commands relate to the later trust permission.
- 2:283/q44/p1 They are prudent safeguards, while parties may rely on trust. — Tabarisi, Razi, Ibn Ashur and Elmalılı use permission to trust as evidence; majority juristic opinion is recommendation. Qurtubi says they prevent disputes and hostility; Elmalılı says piety makes them effective. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الشعبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد,الحسن); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Ṭ)]
- 2:283/q44/p2 Writing and testimony are required when feasible, though a pledge is an alternative if they are impossible. — Tabari insists on documentation even alongside pledge when accessible; Ata, Ibn Jurayj and Nakha'i reportedly recommend pledge but oblige feasible writing and witnesses. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الطبري)]
- 2:283/q44/p3 Some treat all three initial safeguards as duties later lifted by mutual trust. — Wahidi reports this historical interpretation and the related abrogation claim. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون,أبو عبيد,الحسن); ELMALILI(Hasan, Şa‘bî, Hakem b. Uteybe); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:283/q45 How should lawful wealth be treated?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether wealth's moral risk calls for divestment or prudent stewardship, and how debt affects that judgment.
- 2:283/q45/p1 Lawful wealth should be preserved and used responsibly for family, debts and public good. — Qurtubi cites 4:5-6, the Prophet's advice to Sa'd, praise of sound wealth, Abu Bakr, Anas, Ka'b and defense of property; Jawzi weighs intention and charity. Elmalılı links lawful security to earning, spending and avoiding usury. [16 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الفرج الجوزي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن المسيب); ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:283/q45/p2 An aspirant should keep only necessities because wealth distracts from God. — Abu Hamid argues even charitable wealth distracts; Muhasibi's report depicts Abd al-Rahman delayed by lawful riches. Qurtubi disputes the confrontation story and weak crawling report, citing a faulty Ibn Lahi'a route, chronology, and criticized transmitter Ammara. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حامد,المحاسبي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الفرج الجوزي)-]

## 2:283/q46 What harms and duties accompany debt?  [* · law]
- 2:283/q46/p1 Debt can burden and endanger a person and requires sincere intention to repay. — Qurtubi cites Prophetic prayers, the pledged soul until payment, and reports that God aids repayment intended sincerely and condemns squandering; Maymuna, Anas and others add fear and hardship. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أنس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ميمونة)]

## 2:283/q47 How should the verse be read inwardly?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the spiritual journey follows disciplined walking or overwhelming divine attraction.
- 2:283/q47/p1 The spiritual traveler leaves bodily confinement while a law-guided walker keeps the heart as God's pledge. — Bursevi contrasts stationary people with walkers and flyers; walker follows law and reason, escapes outward surveillance, and keeps the heart between the All-Merciful's fingers. Reports of mystics' scribes illustrate the inward state. [5 notes: BURSEVI]
- 2:283/q47/p2 The flying lover is carried by divine attraction and has no heart or reason to pledge. — Bursevi says such a lover remains tethered to law yet is overcome by attraction; only the destitute lover bears the trust. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 2:283/q48 What does Ibn Jinni say about reliable reading transmission?  [* · reports]

- 2:283/q48/p1 Responsible transmission checks reports and evaluates anomalous readings with verified evidence. — Ibn Jinni's introduction stresses fidelity, independent testing and Abu Ali's evidence; it favors Ibn Mujahid for anomalous readings, contrasts Abu Hatim and Qutrub, and records a report on Qutrub's reading transmission in Egypt in 249 AH. ## 2:283/q49 What rhetorical qualities does Jurjani illustrate?  [* · rhetoric]

[12 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(أبو العتاهية,الجاحظ,المرزباني)]
