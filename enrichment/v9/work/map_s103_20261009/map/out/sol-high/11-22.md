# 11:22: 9 questions

## 11:22/q01 What does لا جرم assert here?  [لَا جَرَمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether la jarama is treated as a fixed assertion of certainty, a rebuttal followed by a verb of earning or becoming true, a negation of cutting or prevention, or a negation of doubt; the cited poetry's case and sense are disputed.
- 11:22/q01/p1 It affirms as certain or true that they are the greatest losers; the idiom can also convey inevitability, absence of doubt, or oath-like emphasis. — Muqatil, Ibn Abbas as reported by Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi, and other exegetes gloss it 'indeed' or 'truly'; al-Baghawi also gives 'rather indeed' and al-Farra's 'without doubt.' Al-Tabari calls it an idiom of certainty associated literally with earning sin and inevitability. Al-Farra, reported by al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi and al-Tabataba'i, derives the familiar emphatic use from 'no escape' or 'inevitably' through frequent use and notes its oath-like force; al-Sirafi's report in al-Alusi similarly traces an older sense of keeping someone from sin. Al-Khalil, Sibawayh, al-Farra, Muhammad ibn Yazid and others transmit the 'truly' account in al-Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin. Al-Tabrisi reports the decisive-assertion sense; al-Baydawi says no one is more manifestly a loser. Ibn Ashur calls it a certainty idiom, treating jarama as a fossilized noun for inevitability or no alternative. Al-Biqa'i says denying the contrary establishes the assertion, comparing the emphatic force of la to innama; he reports al-Farahi's derivation from cutting and its oath-like use. Al-Nasafi and al-Alusi report the compound or conventional certainty sense. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه والفراء ومحمد بن يزيد,الكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ أكثر المفسرين,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج والكسائي,سيبويه والخليل); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل,الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي,الخليل وسيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(الخليل); BIQAI-FULL(الرضي والسفاقسي,الفراهي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الزجاج,سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الفراء)]
- 11:22/q01/p2 The negation rebuts a supposed benefit, while jarama says their deed or unbelief earned the loss or punishment that follows. — Abu Ishaq in al-Nahhas and al-Zajjaj in al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi read la as rejecting expected benefit, especially from idols; Ibn al-Anbari specifies the unbelievers' hope that punishment will be diverted. Al-Azhari calls this among the best explanations, and Ibn al-Anbari supplies disbelief as the implicit doer of jarama. Al-Wahidi also reports an extra-la account yielding earned regret. Al-Razi connects the earning sense with 5:2 and includes loss in this world and the next. Al-Samin cites 75:1 and 4:65 for a rebuttal and poetry where jaramat means earned; jarima can mean a tribe's provider or earner. Al-Mawardi cites the thrust that caused Fazara's anger, and al-Wahidi records al-Farra and Abu al-Abbas reading Ghatafan as the poem's accusative object: a wound caused the tribe's anger. Ibn Atiyya says the earning sense has poetic support, though Jarir's line can bear another sense; al-Alusi also cites a poet using jaramat for earning or committing. Al-Nasafi says their claim earns loss. Al-Tabari associates the idiom literally with earning sin. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء؛ أبو العباس,قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه والخليل); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج,الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج؛ الأزهري؛ ابن الأنباري)+]
- 11:22/q01/p3 Nothing can cut off, avert, restrain, or interrupt the certainty of their loss. — Al-Kisa'i in al-Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi speaks of no denial, turning away, opposition or prevention. Al-Razi reports a negation plus jarama 'cutting': nothing cuts off the conclusion. Al-Qurtubi illustrates the root with harvesting dates, shearing wool and removing meat from bone. Al-Samin offers jarama as a noun meaning cutting or preventing. Al-Alusi extends the account to the unbroken duration of their loss. Al-Tabataba'i suggests a cutting root and concludes that nothing cuts off the assertion. [12 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج والكسائي); RAZI-FULL(النحويون); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الفراهي); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي); TABATABAI]
- 11:22/q01/p4 There is no uncertainty or wavering about their punishment. — Al-Biqa'i prefers a recurrent root idea of disturbance: reflection unsettles an opinion and opens what was closed; related forms denote doubt, confusion, sin, mixture or separation. La then negates such wavering, thereby affirming certainty; he treats the negation as emphatic and says it explains Sibawayh's 'it is true.' [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 11:22/q01/p5 Jarama means 'became true' or 'was deserved': their unbelief makes the stated consequence rightfully certain. — Al-Wahidi reports al-Akhfash and Sibawayh making la a reply to the disbelievers and citing the spear-thrust poem for a consequence made certain; al-Razi also reports them. Ibn al-Anbari in Ibn al-Jawzi allows either earned punishment or made-certain punishment, with a poem for the latter. Al-Alusi reports Sibawayh's jarama as the verb haqqa, 'became true.' Al-Wahidi reports al-Farra and Abu al-Abbas objecting to the poem's use for this reading: Ghatafan is accusative, and the wound caused the tribe's anger. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش؛ سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه والأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء؛ أبو العباس)-]

## 11:22/q02 How are لا جرم and the following أن clause constructed?  [لَا جَرَمَ أَنَّهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase is compounded, a rebuttal followed by a past verb, or a negated noun; these analyses assign different cases and syntactic roles to the following an clause.
- 11:22/q02/p1 La jarama is a fixed compound of negation and jarama meaning 'certainly'; the following an clause is nominative, as its subject. — Al-Khalil, Sibawayh, al-Farra and Muhammad ibn Yazid are reported by al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi; al-Khalil says la responds to prior speech rather than opening a statement. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi transmit al-Khalil and Sibawayh's compound analysis. Al-Samin compares its fixed form with khamsata ashar and cites 16:62. Al-Nasafi reports the compound analysis. Ibn Atiyya contrasts Sibawayh's nominative an with al-Zajjaj's accusative one. Al-Biqa'i reports a related compound-past-verb account from Sibawayh and al-Khalil. [9 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه والفراء ومحمد بن يزيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج والكسائي,سيبويه والخليل); QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل وسيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(الخليل); BIQAI-FULL(الرضي والسفاقسي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج)-]
- 11:22/q02/p2 La rebuts a preceding claim; jarama is a past verb meaning 'earned,' with an implicit doer and the following an clause as its accusative object. — Abu Ishaq and al-Zajjaj in al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi reject the supposed benefit and give the deed or unbelief as jarama's omitted subject. Ibn al-Anbari specifies an implicit subject referring to disbelief; al-Samin gives the deeds as subject and cites 75:1 and 4:65 for reply-like la. Abu Hayyan notes that al-Zajjaj rejects compounding and cites poetry for the earning verb. Al-Razi and al-Tabrisi explain the same rebuttal and earned loss. Al-Nasafi gives the claim as what earns the loss. Al-Alusi supplies an omitted clause such as 'their deed benefits them' and an unnamed poet for the verbal sense. [16 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج والكسائي,سيبويه والخليل); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو إسحاق الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج,الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج؛ الأزهري؛ ابن الأنباري)+]
- 11:22/q02/p3 La negates jarama as a noun of cutting or prevention; the an clause is its predicate or depends on an omitted preposition. — Al-Razi reports the negation-plus-cutting analysis. Al-Qurtubi says the agent of cutting may be omitted through frequent use. Al-Samin allows an as predicate or following an omitted preposition. Al-Alusi reports a noun of cutting off, with no interruption to their loss. Al-Kisa'i, as reported by al-Alusi, treats jarama as la's indeclinable noun, with omitted predicate and omitted preposition before an, yielding no opposition or prevention; al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi record his no-prevention interpretation. [7 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(النحويون); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي)]
- 11:22/q02/p4 La is an extra emphatic particle, leaving the assertion or earned outcome to follow. — Al-Mawardi reports extra la for the emphatic 'truly'; al-Wahidi reports an extra-la analysis in which their deed earns regret. Al-Biqa'i reports al-Radi and al-Saqafsi allowing extra la because the phrase bears oath force. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(قوم); BIQAI-FULL(الرضي والسفاقسي)]
- 11:22/q02/p5 La negates a genus and jarama is its accusative noun; the pair is the subject and the following an clause its predicate. — Al-Samin gives this analysis, with the sense 'certainly.' Abu Hayyan reports al-Hawfi taking negated jarama as a nominative topic and the an clause as predicate; al-Biqa'i reports other grammarians treating jarama as the noun of la and 'they' as predicate. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 11:22/q02/p6 The negated-noun analysis is retained, but the an clause is genitive or accusative after an omitted preposition. — Al-Samin gives this variant of the preceding construction, glossing it as certainty about their afterlife loss. Al-Kisa'i's analysis as reported by al-Alusi also omits a preposition before an; Ibn Ashur's fossilized-noun account likewise makes an depend on an omitted preposition. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:22/q02/p7 La is negative and jarama is the verb 'became true'; the following clause functions as its subject. — Al-Alusi reports this from Sibawayh and explains that the unbelievers' expected benefit fails because loss is certain. Al-Wahidi and al-Razi report Sibawayh and al-Akhfash with la as reply and jarama as 'deserved/was certain'; al-Wahidi records al-Farra and Abu al-Abbas objecting to their poetic proof because Ghatafan is accusative. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش؛ سيبويه); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه والأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء؛ أبو العباس)-]
- 11:22/q02/p8 La jarama is a fossilized noun of inevitability, with an governed by an omitted preposition. — Ibn Ashur gives this construction and notes that the expression can introduce an oath-like assertion. Al-Biqa'i distinguishes fath after la jarama, reflecting the original 'no alternative that' construction, from kasra arising with later oath-like usage. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:22/q03 What forms of لا جرم are reported?  [لَا جَرَمَ · readings]
- 11:22/q03/p1 The expression has dialectal and transmitted variants in particles, vowels, and omission of final mīm. — Al-Kisa'i as reported by al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi lists la jarama, la an dha jarama and la an/dha-related forms, including a Fazara form without mim. Al-Farra adds Banu Amir's la dha jarama and a damma on jim. Ibn Atiyya and Ibn al-Jawzi list la dha jarama, la an dha jarama, la an dha or la an/dha jarama, and la jar, explaining shortened la jar by frequent use. Al-Samin records different vowels, dha- and in-initial forms, and Abu Amr's la jar and a changed-rhyme form; Abu Amr likens dropped mim to shortened sawfa. Abu Hayyan notes the variants transmitted by al-Nahhas and al-Farra. Al-Alusi says inserted particles are generally extra, tentatively treats a form lacking la as needing no further assertion, and notes that lexicographers doubt the eloquent-Arabic status of some forms. Al-Biqa'i distinguishes fath from kasra through original no-alternative and later oath-like senses. [12 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء,الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:22/q04 Who are 'they' in this judgment?  [أَنَّهُمْ · referent]
- 11:22/q04/p1 The disbelievers just described, including those who denied the afterlife and relied on fabricated objects of worship. — Muqatil identifies them as these unbelievers. Al-Biqa'i calls them afterlife deniers. Ibn Ashur connects the class to 11:18 and to those who lost themselves by failing to use guidance, citing 6:12; he says their claims included idol intercession and relief from hardship, with 46:28 illustrating the mockery of idols 'straying' from worshipers. Islahi says they squandered their abilities on false worship; Study Quran says fabricated idols, ideas and beliefs forsake them, citing 6:24, 6:54, 10:30, 16:87 and 28:75. [7 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 11:22/q05 What comparison does 'in the afterlife, the greatest losers' imply?  [فِى ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ ٱلْأَخْسَرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the superlative compares them with other afterlife inhabitants or compares their enduring afterlife loss with their worldly loss; al-Tabataba'i favors the first from the verse's apparent restriction.
- 11:22/q05/p1 Among the inhabitants of the afterlife, they are the most ruined, with no prospect of salvation while they persist in defiance. — Al-Tabataba'i treats the superlative as comparing them with other people there and invokes 6:12, 36:9-10 and 45:23. He prefers this reading because the verse appears to restrict the greatest losers to them in the afterlife. Al-Baghawi says all are losers but these are the greatest; al-Baydawi says no one is more plainly or greatly a loser there. Al-Alusi reads degree or amount, and Ibn Ashur says they combine forms of misery dispersed among deluded nations. [6 notes: TABATABAI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 11:22/q05/p2 Their afterlife loss exceeds their loss in this brief worldly life, since the former lasts forever; they lose both lives. — Al-Tabataba'i reports this as another possible comparison and cites 46:35, while preferring the comparison with afterlife inhabitants. Al-Razi says the worldly object chosen over faith perishes; Abu Hayyan distinguishes their endless loss from sinners whose punishment ends. Al-Tabataba'i also says deeds intensify through their afterlife consequences, citing 17:72; Ibn Ashur says they lose this world and the next. [5 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:22/q06 What does هم do in the sentence?  [هُمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether hum is parsed as separator, emphatic pronoun, or the subject of a new sentence; all accounts give it an emphatic effect.
- 11:22/q06/p1 It is a separating pronoun that emphasizes and restricts the superlative judgment to them. — Al-Samin lists the separator analysis. Al-Biqa'i says the repeated pronoun and 'they' intensify the claim. Al-Alusi says the definite predicate conveys exclusivity and the separator strengthens it. Ibn Ashur says the restriction is rhetorical: they reach maximal loss as if none shared it. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:22/q06/p2 It is the subject of a fresh clause whose predicate is الأخسرون, reinforcing the judgment. — Al-Samin gives this parse. Al-Alusi says taking hum as subject, as with separator, further emphasizes the superlative claim. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:22/q06/p3 It is an emphatic pronoun repeating the people already named. — Al-Samin lists the independent emphatic-pronoun analysis. Al-Biqa'i attributes superlative emphasis to repeated pronominal reference. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:22/q07 Why are they called the greatest losers?  [ٱلْأَخْسَرُونَ · interpretation]
- 11:22/q07/p1 Their rejection brings the fullest, enduring loss and punishment in the afterlife. — Al-Tabari says they exchanged their Paradise homes for the homes of Paradise's people in Hell. Ibn Kathir expands the exchange: Paradise's heights and delights for Hell's depths and punishments, and God's nearness and vision for anger. Al-Razi says the worldly thing chosen instead of noble faith cannot last. Al-Nasafi says they themselves turned away and led others astray. Abu Hayyan contrasts their endless loss with sinners' finite punishment and eventual relief. Ibn Ashur calls loss a trader metaphor: they expected gain and suffered harm; he says they combine punishments scattered across deluded peoples and suffer misery precisely where they expected joy, losing both worlds, citing 18:103-104. Al-Tabataba'i says disbelief destroys themselves with no salvation while defiance persists, and reports that their afterlife loss exceeds worldly loss because it lasts forever; he adds that deeds intensify through their afterlife consequences, citing 17:72. Al-Alusi says greatest can indicate degree or amount, while an alternative cutting analysis makes their loss uninterrupted. Al-Baghawi observes that others also lose, but these lose most. Muqatil, al-Baydawi and al-Biqa'i affirm the superlative. Islahi traces their ruin to capacities squandered on false worship and the failure of imagined divine supports. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 11:22/q08 How does this judgment sum up the preceding account?  [* · links]
- 11:22/q08/p1 It concludes the account of their rejected guidance, false worship, failed claims, and inability to avert punishment by naming their final loss. — Ibn Ashur says the final sentence sums up their exposure, punishment, refusal to hear warnings and neglect of evidence for God's unity, linking it to 11:18; he also treats the related preceding sentence as a new sentence reinforcing 11:18 and a class who lost themselves, citing 6:12. He identifies the claims as idol intercession and protection from hardship and sees the idols' disappearance as mockery, citing 46:28. Al-Suddi, reported in al-Durr, says those who lost themselves cheated or deprived themselves. Al-Biqa'i explains the immediately preceding inability to thwart as inability to prevent God's will, doubling as increase by an equal or greater amount, and capacity as bodily power. Islahi says invented gods and hoped-for support are useless and leave worshipers without insight. Study Quran says fabricated idols, ideas and beliefs ultimately abandon them, citing 6:24, 6:54, 10:30, 16:87 and 28:75. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 11:22/q09 How does this verse lead into the account of believers?  [* · links]
- 11:22/q09/p1 The completed account of disbelievers' conduct and fate gives way to believers' good outcome, completing the contrast. — Al-Alusi says the discourse turns from the disbelievers to believers and completes a contrast with the preceding praise of believers. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
