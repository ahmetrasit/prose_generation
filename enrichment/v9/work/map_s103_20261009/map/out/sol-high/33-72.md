# 33:72: 16 questions

## 33:72/q01 What does the plural in إِنَّا convey?  [إِنَّا · rhetoric]
- 33:72/q01/p1 Majesty, or to mystics the plurality of divine names and attributes. — Bursevī attributes the first reading to scholars and the second to knowers. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 33:72/q02 How did the offer expressed by عَرَضْنَا occur?  [عَرَضْنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Actual speech, figurative comparison, lexical presentation, and the status of the offer.
- 33:72/q02/p1 It was a real offer to the created bodies, given perception and speech. — Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer direct address; Ibn al-Anbārī says Adam heard their reply. Bursevī cites speaking ants and stones. Qurṭubī says inanimate things would need life. [20 notes: NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض أهل العلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(müfessirler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı tefsirciler); STUDYQURAN(many commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 33:72/q02/p2 It is a hypothetical parable about the trust's gravity. — Qaffāl and Qurṭubī compare 59:21; Zamakhsharī cites Arabic imaginary speech of fat and animals; Ṭabrisī cites address to ruins; Ibn ʿAṭiyya tests a camel against its load. Ālūsī says counterfactual language is made vivid. [24 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭū); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABRISI+]
- 33:72/q02/p3 It means comparing or weighing the trust against the cosmic bodies. — Ibn Baḥr says it outweighed them; Abū Muslim compares commands, prohibitions, books, messengers, and covenant. Ṭabāṭabāʾī assesses fitness for divine guardianship. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن بحر); TABATABAI]
- 33:72/q02/p4 It means displaying, offering, or inspecting. — Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī compare presenting a maiden for sale; Bursevī lists offering merchandise or inspecting soldiers. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 33:72/q02/p5 The offer was a choice, whereas humanity's charge was binding. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Rāzī and Qurṭubī say refusal could not have been permissible had the charge been imposed. Qushayrī distinguishes the human's imposed duty. Jishumī argues instead that beneficial obligation cannot depend on choice. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(القشيري); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI-]
- 33:72/q02/p6 The offer described obligation or tested readiness. — Jishumī says God obligates for welfare without coercion; Bayḍāwī says it tested readiness; Māwardī says acceptance followed informed understanding. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; JISHUMI+]

## 33:72/q03 What is ٱلْأَمَانَةَ?  [ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · meaning]
turns on: General charge, particular trust, moral faculty, covenant, and inward perfection.
- 33:72/q03/p1 The general charge of obedience, religious duties, commands, prohibitions and accountability. — Ṭabarī prefers the unrestricted sense, including worldly trusts. Ibn Kathīr reconciles duty, obedience and limits. Rāzī stresses action against inclination while angels obey naturally (21:20). Elmalılı calls it voluntary vicegerency over God's and people's rights. - 33:72/q03/p2 People's deposits, property, promises and covenants. — Ibn Masʿūd gives deposits special weight; Ibn ʿAbbās forbids cheating believers or protected non Muslims. Cain's betrayal supplies an early example; Samarrāʾī includes vows, oaths and contracts. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(بعض المفسرين); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الضحاك,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود,الجمهور); TABRISI(السدي والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة عن بعض المفسرين,السدي عن أشياخه); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود,السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الجمهور)]
- 33:72/q03/p3 Prayer, fasting and major ritual washing, sometimes singled out as washing or prayer. — A prophetic report names three duties one may falsely claim to perform (86:9); Abū al-Dardāʾ names washing. ʿAṭāʾ also lists zakāt and pilgrimage; Wahb cites the Psalms. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABDURRAZZAQ(زيد بن أسلم,وهب الديناري); TAB-FULL(أبو الدرداء); THALABI(أبو الدرداء,زيد بن أسلم وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT(زيد بن أسلم,عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الدرداء); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الدرداء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,بعضهم ومالك عن زيد بن أسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 33:72/q03/p4 Chastity and entrusted bodily faculties. — Ubayy names a woman's chastity; Ibn ʿAmr lists sexual organs, hearing, sight, tongue, belly, hand and foot. Sulamī extends the trust to self, heart, spirit and limbs; looking into homes breaches the eye's trust. [18 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أبي بن كعب); TAB-FULL(أبي بن كعب); IBNABIHATIM(أبي بن كعب); SULAMI(أبو عثمان); MAWARDI-FULL(أبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,عبد الله بن عمرو); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(أبيّ بن كعب); STUDYQURAN(IJ, Q)]
- 33:72/q03/p5 Reason, relative free will and the capacity for moral choice. — Asad prefers reason and volition. Bayḍāwī says reason governs anger and appetite; duties moderate them. Kuran Yolu groups senses under free will and accountability. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES+]
- 33:72/q03/p6 Tawḥīd, faith, or the primordial testimony to God. — Sulamī's Ibn ʿAṭāʾ names singular devotion; Qushayrī adds observance of its limits. Study Quran cites 7:172. Rāzī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject bare monotheism because cosmos already proclaims it. [8 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN(Aj,Q, Sy); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 33:72/q03/p7 Signs of God's lordship placed in creation. — Some theologians say created bodies display the signs, whereas humans conceal and deny them; Ibn Isḥāq names signs of divine unity. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتكلمين); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المتكلمين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن إسحاق)]
- 33:72/q03/p8 Divine guardianship, the perfection of sincere servanthood. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this because bare knowledge or monotheism cannot account for hypocrisy, shirk and faith. He interprets the report on ʿAlī's wilāya as an exemplary application. [17 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله); TABATABAI+]
- 33:72/q03/p9 Divine identity and self manifestation, or love carried inwardly. — Qāshānī contrasts cosmic disclosure of attributes with human selfhood's concealment. Bursevī describes duties, love amid trial, and unmediated effusion; Ḥāfiẓ and Jāmī contrast angels' worship with Adam's love. [16 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(الحافظ,المولى الجامي); SULAMI(فارس,فارس عن الحسين); QUSHAYRI]
- 33:72/q03/p10 The liability of reward and punishment itself. — Naḥḥās reports this as an alternative. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 33:72/q03/p11 A binding entrusted covenant or right to be kept intact and returned. — Jishumī calls it a contract; Ṭabāṭabāʾī a charge to preserve. Ibn al-Jawzī and Elmalılı derive it from security and confidence; Biqāʿī contrasts fulfillment and betrayal. [14 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أنشدوا,ابن فارس,علي بن عبيد الله); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 33:72/q04 What happened during the offer according to the reports?  [عَرَضْنَا ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · reports]
turns on: These reports differ in sequence, time, form of the trust and whether the subject is duty or Adam's family.
- 33:72/q04/p1 Adam accepted reward and punishment for good and evil, then sinned within hours or the same day. — Reports vary between noon and afternoon, afternoon and sunset, and noon and night; some credit Satan's temptation. Al-Ḥasan includes seven heavens and earths and Throne bearers. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس والضحاك,مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن البصري); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد)]
- 33:72/q04/p2 God promised Adam safeguards for sight, speech and sexual modesty. — Versions say he accepted between ears and shoulders; he received eyelids, a restrainable tongue and covered private parts. Ibn ʿAṭiyya doubts further details of the report. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(زياد بن أسلم,غير مسمى); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,زيد بن أسلم وأبو حازم); DURR-FULL(راوٍ غير مسمى)]
- 33:72/q04/p3 Creation pleaded for three days and nights, lacking capacity and not seeking reward. — Ibn al-Adshūʿ gives this prolonged groaning. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن اضشوع); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن أشوع); DURR-FULL(ابن أشوع)]
- 33:72/q04/p4 The trust appeared as a rock that Adam carried to his shoulder. — Ibn Masʿūd says the burden passed to descendants until Resurrection. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 33:72/q04/p5 Adam sought a guardian or successor; Cain accepted, then murdered Abel. — One version is at Adam's death; another is before pilgrimage to Mecca. Al-Suddī adds offerings and a marriage dispute (5:28, 5:30). Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm rejects the deathbed account as unsupported and inconsistent with legal demands on mountains. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(بعض المفسرين); TAB-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); TABRISI(السدي والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة عن بعض المفسرين,السدي عن أشياخه); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبد الله محمد بن علي الترمذي الحكيم)-]
- 33:72/q04/p6 The heavens were offered it one after another, followed by earth and mountains. — An unnamed report supplies this order; al-Ḥasan adds Throne bearers. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري)]
- 33:72/q04/p7 Adam was promised honor and Paradise for obedience, punishment for disobedience. — Ibn Ḥayyān gives a dialogue; Ibn ʿUthmān says Adam saw the alternatives. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن حيان,ابن عثمان)]

## 33:72/q05 Who received the offer addressed to the heavens, earth, and mountains?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱلْجِبَالِ · referent]
turns on: Direct address, ellipsis for inhabitants, or an imagined comparison.
- 33:72/q05/p1 The created bodies themselves. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid affirm direct address; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer it. God could grant perception and speech; the mountains emphasize the burden. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض أهل العلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(many commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 33:72/q05/p2 Their accountable inhabitants, including angels, humans and jinn. — Al-Ḥasan supplies inhabitants; Jishumī and al-Jubbāʾī say physical bodies cannot bear moral responsibility. Study Quran includes people, jinn and angels; Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows the angels. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs, Ṭū); JISHUMI]
- 33:72/q05/p3 They are imagined comparators rather than actual addressees. — Qaffāl, Zamakhsharī, Ālūsī and Kuran Yolu read cosmic scale as representing the trust's weight. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 33:72/q06 Why did the created bodies refuse and fear it?  [فَأَبَيْنَ أَن يَحْمِلْنَهَا وَأَشْفَقْنَ مِنْهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Fearful choice, natural incapacity, or a figure for avoiding betrayal.
- 33:72/q06/p1 They revered God's religion and feared failure and punishment, without disobeying. — Ibn ʿAbbās stresses reverence; Rāzī contrasts Iblis's arrogant refusal of an obligation (15:31). Qushayrī calls their fear compassion. Al-Ḥasan says they did not want reward or punishment. [45 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج,الحسن,مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,عبد الله بن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,محمد بن كعب); BURSEVI(القشيري); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(علي); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, IJ, IK, Q, Ṭ)]
- 33:72/q06/p2 They lacked natural capacity or readiness. — Ibn Baḥr and Abū Muslim make refusal inability; Bayḍāwī contrasts human rational capacity. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their size (40:57) does not mean fitness for guardianship. [21 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI(أبو مسلم,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن بحر); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭū,R, Z)]
- 33:72/q06/p3 Already subjected to God's command, they declined added accountability and reward. — Ibn Jurayj describes heaven as a protected roof; Mujāhid says creation already bears inhabitants. Ibn al-Adshūʿ records a three day plea. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن اضشوع,ابن جريج); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج,مقاتل بن حيان); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن أشوع,مجاهد وابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن أشوع,ابن جريج); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, IJ, IK, Q, Ṭ)]
- 33:72/q06/p4 They refused treachery and the burden of sin while fulfilling their duties. — Zajjāj compares bearing sins (35:18); Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī cite 29:13. Jishumī rejects meritorious refusal of a command as disbelief and prefers this; al-Jubbāʾī assigns refusal of liability to angels. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي,الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي عن الزجاج); ELMALILI; JISHUMI+]
- 33:72/q06/p5 They initially withheld divine perfections but ultimately displayed them. — Qāshānī's inward account contrasts them with human selfhood's concealment. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 33:72/q07 How does refusal fit the creation's obedience elsewhere?  [فَأَبَيْنَ · links]
turns on: Optional offer versus distinct modes of obedience.
- 33:72/q07/p1 This offer was optional while 41:11 records a direct command. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes command from choice. Zajjāj and Baghawī cite 41:11, 2:74, 22:18; Bursevī invokes 17:44 and examples of mountains, wind, earth, a moaning trunk and a greeting stone. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي عن الزجاج); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, IJ, IK, Q, Ṭ)]
- 33:72/q07/p2 Creation fulfilled its own form of submission while accountable humans could betray theirs. — Zajjāj, Nasafī and Biqāʿī distinguish creative from moral command. Jishumī says angels do not disobey, unlike humans and jinn. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي,الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي عن الزجاج)]

## 33:72/q08 Why is فَأَبَيْنَ feminine?  [فَأَبَيْنَ · grammar]
- 33:72/q08/p1 Nonrational broken plurals may take feminine agreement. — Al-Samīn says feminine heavens need not override masculine mountains. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 33:72/q09 What does حَمَلَهَا mean?  [وَحَمَلَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Acceptance, unfulfilled liability, capacity, or divine enabling.
- 33:72/q09/p1 The human accepted responsibility for the trust and its due. — Māwardī and Qurṭubī stress undertaking. Ālūsī allows innate capacity or a primordial pledge; Bursevī cites 17:70, 55:60 and fruitful weak trees. [46 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(غير مسمى); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن حيان,ابن عباس,ابن عثمان,عطاء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير); TABRISI(أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد,جنيد); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:72/q09/p2 It means betraying or continuing to bear an undischarged liability. — Zajjāj compares sin's load (35:18); Zamakhsharī compares debt riding its custodian. Ṭabrisī rejects a cited poem as proof; Abū Ḥayyān says it could mean accepting more trusts. ## 33:72/q11 What do ظَلُومًۭا and جَهُولًۭا mean?  [ظَلُومًۭا جَهُولًۭا · interpretation]

## 33:72/q12 What inward account explains the trust and its bearer?  [وَحَمَلَهَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · inward]
turns on: Divine help, love's stages, or disclosure of divine attributes.
- 33:72/q12/p1 The heart bears through divine aid what the body cannot. — Junayd, Fāris and al-Ḥusayn differ over Adam's awareness of help and joy in the gift. Qushayrī cites wisdom's mountains; Bursevī quotes Persian verse. [19 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,الجنيد,فارس,فارس عن الحسين); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(جنيد)]
- 33:72/q12/p2 The trust rises through legal duties, divine love, then unmediated effusion. — Bursevī assigns levels to common people, elect and most elect; annihilation and subsistence remove veils. Ḥāfiẓ and Jāmī contrast angels with Adam's love. [11 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد,الحافظ,المولى الجامي)]
- 33:72/q12/p3 Cosmic perfections reveal divine attributes, while human selfhood can conceal them. — Qāshānī says heaven and earth deliver their perfections whereas humans claim ownership and block knowledge. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 33:72/q13 How does this verse connect to nearby or comparable passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Surah context, cosmic submission, dignity, and other trust passages.
- 33:72/q13/p1 It magnifies duties after commands and promises, before judgment of hypocrites, polytheists and believers. — Qurṭubī links earlier laws to compliance; Bayḍāwī sees reinforcement of the prior promise. Samarrāʾī compares 33:24 and 33:73: lām, divine will, repentance, women and forgiveness vary with context. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 33:72/q13/p2 Other passages show cosmic submission and human elevation or fall. — Zajjāj cites 41:11, 2:74, 22:18; Qurṭubī cites 59:21. Qushayrī and Bursevī invoke 17:70; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 95:4–6. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭū)]
- 33:72/q13/p3 Trust passages connect duty with covenant keeping. — Damghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī cite 8:27; Samarrāʾī cites 23:8 and says patience in keeping trust curbs impulsiveness. [5 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 33:72/q13/p4 A general trust command can follow a related case despite being revealed later. — Al-Itqān cites 4:51–58: Ibn al-ʿArabī calls concealment of Muḥammad's description betrayal. ## 33:72/q14 What specific teachings about trust do reports give?  [ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · reports]
turns on: Particular duties, warnings about breach, and trust's transmission.
- 33:72/q14/p1 Keep deposits, debts, chastity, speech and confidences. — Ibn Masʿūd adds prayers, zakāt, fasting, pilgrimage, truthful speech and fair weights. The muezzin guards prayer times; gatherings and intimate speech are confidential. Ibn Qutayba distinguishes betrayal from theft and its amputation penalty. [21 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(النمر بن تولب); TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الضحاك,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(أبيّ بن كعب,الجمهور)]
- 33:72/q14/p2 Martyrdom does not erase breach of trust; its bearer must retrieve it from Hell. — Ibn Masʿūd describes it slipping and the bearer falling; al-Barāʾ confirms. Ibn Kathīr calls a second route good though not in canonical collections. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:72/q14/p3 Five deeds with faith lead to Paradise; the last trust is major ritual washing. — Abū al-Dardāʾ lists prayer, willing zakāt, Ramadan fasting and pilgrimage if able. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو الدرداء); THALABI(أبو الدرداء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 33:72/q14/p4 Trust came through prophets, then trust and covenants were withdrawn while scripture remained. — Al-Ḥakam's report warns against temptation; Ibn Kathīr calls it rare but corroborated. Ḥudhayfa's report of trust leaving hearts in stages is in Bukhārī and Muslim. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(الحكم بن عمرو); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحكم بن عمير عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,حذيفة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:72/q14/p5 Guard trust with truthfulness, good character and lawful food. — Ibn Kathīr records Ibn ʿAmr and Ibn ʿUmar routes with a chain and attribution difference. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:72/q14/p6 Swearing by trust is forbidden or strongly disapproved. — ʿUmar forbade it; Burayda reports a prophetic warning transmitted by Abū Dāwūd. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بريدة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,زياد بن حدير)]
- 33:72/q14/p7 Preservation is faithful; withholding or misuse is betrayal. — Biqāʿī contrasts delivery with retention. Al-Ḥasan rebukes currying rulers and unlawful wealth. Jishumī says human acts are their own, making commands and threats coherent. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SULAMI(الحسن البصري); JISHUMI; ELMALILI]

## 33:72/q15 What ancillary language evidence appears in the notes?  [ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · meaning]
turns on: Lexical trust evidence and ancillary language examples.
- 33:72/q15/p1 Amāna comes from security, confidence and trustworthiness. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites Ibn Fāris on amīna, amīn, amān and amūn plus a merchant verse; Elmalılı prefers the verbal noun sense. Samarrāʾī explains plural amānāt versus singular ʿahd. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أنشدوا,ابن فارس,علي بن عبيد الله); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]
- 33:72/q15/p2 Khiyāna is failure to discharge an entrusted object. — Ibn Qutayba says all betrayers steal but not conversely; theft entails amputation, breach of entrusted goods does not. Al-Namir's house keeper line illustrates. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(النمر بن تولب)]
- 33:72/q15/p3 Lamentative forms allow optional alif after yā or wā with vowel and possessive changes. — Sībawayh discusses fatḥa before alif, adjacent sukūns, retained yā, pause hāʾ; al-Khalīl and Yūnus allow variants. - 33:72/q15/p4 Prose and poetry illustrate balanced expression, wordplay and metaphor. — Al-Bāqillānī cites ʿAlī's aphorisms, Khālid's ring pun and Imru al-Qays's swift horse as a fetter for game. [3 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 33:72/q15/p5 Poetry attests faithful custody of a trust. — Biqāʿī cites al-Nābigha and Ibn al-Furāt's report of ʿUmar's judgment. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النابغة زياد بن معاوية الذبياني)]

## 33:72/q16 What conditions and consequences did the offer state?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported terms stress preservation and grave sin or reward and punishment.
- 33:72/q16/p1 It commanded preservation of the trust, with grave sin for wasting it. — Māwardī reports this as one account of the offer's terms. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتكلمين)]
- 33:72/q16/p2 Good performance earned reward, while failure incurred punishment. — Al-Ḥasan's account, transmitted by Qurṭubī through Maʿmar, says the heavens, earth and mountains refused after hearing these terms. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]
