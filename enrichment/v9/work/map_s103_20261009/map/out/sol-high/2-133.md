# 2:133: 47 questions

## 2:133/q01 How does the opening am work?  [أَمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether am is disconnected, connected through ellipsis, assertive, or a simple question.
- 2:133/q01/p1 Disconnected am means 'rather' plus a question that reproaches the audience and denies their presence. — Majaz cites al-Akhtal after completed speech. Zajjaj, Wahidi, Jishumi, Tabarsi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, Bursevi and others explain the shift. Razi contrasts disconnected 32:1–3 with connected 79:27. [26 notes: MAJAZ(الأخطل); AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الشاعر); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğu); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم)+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:133/q01/p2 Connected am supplies an omitted first alternative about the claim that the prophets were Jews or about absence at the deathbed. — Wahidi cites 2:108; Zamakhshari prefers this for Jewish addressees. Razi, Biqaʿi and Alusi offer alternatives. Abu Hayyan and Samīn reject deletion of the first coordinate as unattested, distinguishing deletion of the second and rare omission after other conjunctions. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); KASHSHAF-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 2:133/q01/p3 It means 'rather' alone, asserting that the audience or its ancestors witnessed the testament. — Abu Hayyan reports this reading; Asad says the construction has no interrogative force. Majaz also offers 'rather, you saw.' [6 notes: MAJAZ(الأخطل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES]
- 2:133/q01/p4 It functions as interrogative hamza or hal. — Abu ʿUbayda cites al-Akhtal's Wāsiṭ line. A Yemeni dialect is alleged; Abu Hayyan and Samīn say grammarians do not attest it and call Tabari's mid-speech account unusual. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري,بعض النحويين); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الطبري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 2:133/q02 Whom does the opening challenge address?  [كُنتُمْ · referent]
turns on: Which audience the second-person pronoun denotes.
- 2:133/q02/p1 The People of the Book, especially Jews, with Christians included in some explanations. — Rabiʿ, Abu al-ʿAliya and Hasan identify them; many authors relate their claim that Jacob bequeathed Judaism. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,الحسن); THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğu); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 2:133/q02/p2 Believers are addressed and learn the unseen event through revelation. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi and Alusi report this; Alusi calls that knowledge a prophetic sign. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:133/q02/p3 The people of Mecca are addressed. — Abu al-ʿAliya's report names Meccans. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو العالية)]

## 2:133/q03 What does the witness question prove?  [كُنتُمْ شُهَدَآءَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether lack of presence or inherited testimony is the rebuke's premise.
- 2:133/q03/p1 They did not witness Jacob's testament and cannot claim he prescribed their later religions. — Jishumi and Tabarsi say the question form forces admission. Ibn ʿAtiyya and Abu Hayyan note missing eyewitness, transmitted, or rational proof; Biqaʿi says their scripture refutes them. Al-Hasan likewise says Jews, Christians and others were absent, though the sons affirmed the covenant. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğu); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 2:133/q03/p2 Their ancestors witnessed or transmitted Jacob's Islamic testament, so descendants knowingly misattribute a religion to him. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Zamakhshari, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Kuranyolu connect this to the alternative reading of am. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî)]

## 2:133/q04 What occasion of revelation is reported?  [كُنتُمْ شُهَدَآءَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported Jewish claim is an established occasion.
- 2:133/q04/p1 Jews told Muhammad that Jacob instructed his sons to follow Judaism at death. — Muqatil, Thaʿlabi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi and others report this occasion. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:133/q04/p2 The Jewish-claim occasion was not located and is treated with reservation. — Al-Suyuti could not find it, as Alusi notes; one believers-address reading discounts it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي)]

## 2:133/q05 What does shuhada mean?  [شُهَدَآءَ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical scope of shuhada.
- 2:133/q05/p1 People present as witnesses, opposite of absent. — Wahidi, Jishumi and Tabarsi explain the root and related courtyard or gathering usages; Alusi allows 'witnesses' or 'present.' [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:133/q05/p2 It may also mean martyrs. — Samīn mentions the lexical alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:133/q06 How is shuhada parsed?  [شُهَدَآءَ · grammar]
- 2:133/q06/p1 It is the predicate of kana and is diptote. — Nahhas explains the final plural feminine pattern; Qurtubi also identifies the predicate. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:133/q07 What does death's arrival mean?  [حَضَرَ يَعْقُوبَ ٱلْمَوْتُ · meaning]
- 2:133/q07/p1 The causes or signs of death approached Jacob while speech remained possible. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and Qurtubi argue that speech at the instant of death is impossible. Abu Hayyan cites 14:17 and a poet's 'I am death'; Samīn calls the expression metonymic. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(شاعر); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 2:133/q08 What form and usage does hadara have?  [حَضَرَ · meaning]
- 2:133/q08/p1 It denotes being present or witnessing, as opposed to absence. — Wahidi gives hadrat for a courtyard; Abu Hayyan notes an anomalous imperfect pattern. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:133/q09 How is hadara read?  [حَضَرَ · readings]
turns on: Vowel of the dad.
- 2:133/q09/p1 The familiar form is hadara, with fathah on dad. — The reported kasra is a dialect alternative. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:133/q09/p2 A dialect reads hadira, with kasra on dad. — Abu Hayyan and Samīn call the damma imperfect unusual, perhaps blending verbal patterns. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:133/q10 How are Jacob and death inflected?  [يَعْقُوبَ ٱلْمَوْتُ · grammar]
turns on: Which noun is object and which is subject.
- 2:133/q10/p1 Jacob is a fronted accusative object; death is the nominative subject. — Sibawayh says Jacob receives emphasis as the item requiring explanation; Ibn ʿAtiyya, Abu Hayyan and Samīn agree. [4 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:133/q10/p2 Some reverse the cases. — Samīn reports Jacob nominative and death accusative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:133/q11 Is Yaqub an Arabic derived name?  [يَعْقُوبَ · grammar]
- 2:133/q11/p1 The prophet's name is foreign and indeclinable. — Abu Hayyan distinguishes a declinable Arabic partridge word and rejects derivations from following a twin or numerous descendants, which would require declension. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:133/q12 What does the image of death coming add?  [حَضَرَ يَعْقُوبَ ٱلْمَوْتُ · rhetoric]
- 2:133/q12/p1 It portrays death as an absent thing that must arrive. — Abu Hayyan calls it a striking euphemism and cites a prayer calling death the best absent thing awaited. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:133/q13 How does the second idh relate to the first?  [إِذْ حَضَرَ يَعْقُوبَ ٱلْمَوْتُ إِذْ قَالَ لِبَنِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: Apposition or direct adverbial governance.
- 2:133/q13/p1 The second idh apposes and explains the first. — Akhfash, Tabari, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Jishumi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn and Alusi give this; Alusi calls it inclusion, and grammarians differ over repeating the governor. [12 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:133/q13/p2 The second idh is directly governed by hadara. — Jishumi, Tabarsi, Qaffal, Nasafi and Samīn allow this; Qaffal says both clauses mark one time. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:133/q13/p3 The second idh is an adverb governed by shuhada. — Jishumi and Nasafi report it; Tabarsi places the first idh under shuhada, while Qaffal distinguishes the witnesses' and Jacob's times. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال); NASAFI-FULL]

## 2:133/q14 What happened before Jacob questioned his sons?  [إِذْ قَالَ لِبَنِيهِ · reports]
turns on: Which reported circumstances preceded the exchange.
- 2:133/q14/p1 In Egypt he saw idol and fire or celestial worship and gathered his sons out of fear. — Al-Kalbi is cited by Thaʿlabi and Baghawi; Qaffal, Abu Hayyan and Alusi give variants. [5 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:133/q14/p2 Offered the prophets' choice of life or death, Jacob chose death but requested time to advise sons and descendants. — ʿAtaʾ's report is cited by Thaʿlabi, Wahidi and Baghawi; Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan give variants, including twelve sons. [6 notes: THALABI(عطاء); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 2:133/q14/p3 Jacob's sons themselves worshipped idols and fire at death. — Razi transmits Ibn ʿAbbas's version through the Qadi, who rejects it because their prompt answer and the Quran imply settled righteousness. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]

## 2:133/q15 Why did Jacob ask about worship?  [مَا تَعْبُدُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Formal worship or one's wider orientation to God.
- 2:133/q15/p1 He tested and renewed his sons' pledge to continue monotheism. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalılı and Kuranyolu stress the continuing charge; Razi and Abu Hayyan see the prophets' concern for children's religion. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:133/q15/p2 He urged all acts to seek God alone and feared worldly distraction more than idols. — Abu Hayyan attributes this to Qaffal; Bursevi cites Raghib, 14:35 and the Mathnawi's account of heedlessness. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب,المثنوي)]

## 2:133/q16 Why is ma used rather than man?  [مَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Breadth of objects or inquiry into qualities.
- 2:133/q16/p1 Ma covers unknown, rational and nonrational objects, including idols, and tests the sons without suggesting an answer. — Kashshaf contrasts man; Ibn ʿAtiyya names idols, fire, sun and stones. Tabarsi, Qurtubi and Biqaʿi stress exclusion of inanimate gods; Taftazani says man applies after rational identity becomes known. [15 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قائل); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التفتازاني)]
- 2:133/q16/p2 Ma asks the nature or qualities of what is worshipped. — Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Samīn compare asking whether Zayd is jurist or physician. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قائل); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]

## 2:133/q17 What is ma's grammatical role?  [مَا تَعْبُدُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Accusative object or nominative subject.
- 2:133/q17/p1 It is the fronted accusative direct object of taʿbudun. — Nahhas, Abu Hayyan and Samīn explain the interrogative's initial position. [3 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:133/q17/p2 It is nominative with an omitted resumptive pronoun. — Alusi says object parsing loses the progression from asking what a thing is to identifying who it is. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:133/q18 What does 'after me' mean and add?  [مِنۢ بَعْدِى · interpretation]
- 2:133/q18/p1 It means after Jacob's death, when his sons must keep the charge without him. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samīn give the temporal sense. Biqaʿi notes successors may stray and sons' lives do not cover all later time. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:133/q19 How does the answer match the question?  [قَالُوا۟ نَعْبُدُ · rhetoric]
- 2:133/q19/p1 The sons repeat 'we worship' to answer Jacob directly; dialogue omits a conjunction. — Abu Hayyan notes explicit matching, and Ibn ʿAshur says the reply completes the question. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:133/q20 Why say 'your God' and 'God of your fathers'?  [إِلَٰهَكَ وَإِلَٰهَ ءَابَآئِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Shared tradition, honor, or creator argument.
- 2:133/q20/p1 It names one shared deity and reassures Jacob of continuity with his and his fathers' path. — Razi stresses reassurance; Abu Hayyan the common deity; Ibn ʿAshur says 'your God' invokes taught attributes amid surrounding idolatry. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:133/q20/p2 It honors Jacob's rank and the sons' lower station. — Qushayri connects the phrase with reverence and submission. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 2:133/q20/p3 It may point to rational proof through the creator of their ancestors. — Razi connects this possibility with 2:21. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:133/q21 Why repeat ilah before 'your fathers'?  [وَإِلَٰهَ ءَابَآئِكَ · grammar]
- 2:133/q21/p1 It avoids coordinating directly onto a genitive pronoun and can heighten eloquence. — Abu Hayyan and Samīn cite the grammatical convention; Ibn ʿAshur says repetition is eloquent but not obligatory. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:133/q22 What does ilah mean here?  [إِلَٰهَكَ · meaning]
- 2:133/q22/p1 Ilah is the generic noun 'god'. — Study Quran distinguishes it from the proper name Allah. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 2:133/q23 Who counts as a father here?  [ءَابَآئِكَ · meaning]
turns on: Literal breadth, figurative extension, or predominance.
- 2:133/q23/p1 The term includes parent and ancestor, and can include an uncle by kinship usage or likeness. — Majaz and many commentators cite the Prophet calling al-ʿAbbas father, father's peer or remnant of forefathers, including the ʿUrwa episode. Ibn ʿAbbas invokes grandfather; Tabatabaʾi cites 12:38 and 6:74. Thaʿlabi cites Joseph's aunt as mother in 12:100. [50 notes: MAJAZ(عكرمة); TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,محمد بن كعب); NAHHAS; THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النحاس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,محمد بن كعب); BURSEVI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:133/q23/p2 The uncle or grandfather sense is figurative rather than literal identity with a parent. — Jishumi says one may deny 'father' of an uncle; Razi says Ibn ʿAbbas's assertion concerns legal standing, not lexicon. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL]
- 2:133/q23/p3 Ishmael's inclusion can arise from predominance of actual fathers in a mixed list. — Ibn Kathir and Alusi give this; Samīn objects that singular uncle usage needs another explanation. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم)-]

## 2:133/q24 What forms of ab are reported?  [ءَابَآئِكَ · meaning]
- 2:133/q24/p1 Arabic has dialectal and plural forms of ab and verbs for nourishing or adopting a father. — Wahidi cites Ahmad ibn Yahya, Sibawayh and poetry for abuka, abak, abuk, duals and plurals; al-Layth gives yaʾbu and taʾabba; Kashshaf cites poetic al-abina. [5 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:133/q25 Which readings of 'your fathers' are reported?  [ءَابَآئِكَ · readings]
turns on: Plural, singular, plural analysis of abika, or omission.
- 2:133/q25/p1 The common reading has plural abaʾika. — Zajjaj and Tabari cite consensus; Jishumi opposes Yaḥya's singular as against it, and Razi says anomalous Ubayy does not displace transmission. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(يحيى بن يعمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL]
- 2:133/q25/p2 A variant has singular abika, with Abraham as father and Ishmael and Isaac coordinated separately. — Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbas, Hasan, Yahya ibn Yaʿmar, Jahdari and Abu Raja. Its motive is avoiding calling the uncle father; Nahhas criticizes the rationale and awkward separation of Isaac. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس، الحسن، يحيى بن يعمر، عاصم الجحدري، أبو رجاء); THALABI(يحيى بن يعمر الجحدري); WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وابن يعمر والجحدري وأبو رجاء); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ويحيى بن يعمر والجحدري وأبو رجاء العطاردي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وابن يعمر والجحدري وأبو رجاء); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن ويحيى وأبو رجاء); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); TAB-FULL-; NAHHAS-; JISHUMI(يحيى بن يعمر)-]
- 2:133/q25/p3 Written abika may be a sound plural of ab with nun omitted in annexation. — Ibn Jinni prefers it over forced singular-for-plural; Sibawayh's abin and abun and cited poems support the analysis. [12 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس، الحسن، يحيى بن يعمر، عاصم الجحدري، أبو رجاء); WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وابن يعمر والجحدري وأبو رجاء); QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB+]
- 2:133/q25/p4 Ubayy omits the ancestor word and reads 'the God of Abraham'. — Thaʿlabi, Kashshaf, Razi, Abu Hayyan and Samīn report it; Razi calls it anomalous. [4 notes: THALABI(أبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن ويحيى وأبو رجاء); RAZI-FULL-]

## 2:133/q26 How do the ancestor names relate to 'fathers'?  [ءَابَآئِكَ إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ وَإِسْمَٰعِيلَ وَإِسْحَٰقَ · grammar]
turns on: The underlying reading of the ancestor word.
- 2:133/q26/p1 They are genitive appositions or explanations; an omitted 'I mean' may also govern an accusative. — Akhfash, Zajjaj, Nahhas, Tabarsi, Qaffal, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samīn analyze the names. Nasafi likewise identifies the names as appositional clarification, though his list says Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. [10 notes: AKHFASH; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:133/q26/p2 With singular abika, Abraham alone apposes father, while the others are separately coordinated. — Zajjaj gives the analysis, intended to avoid calling Ishmael father. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(يحيى بن يعمر الجحدري); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وابن يعمر والجحدري وأبو رجاء); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن ويحيى بن يعمر والجحدري وأبو رجاء العطاردي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وابن يعمر والجحدري وأبو رجاء); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 2:133/q27 Why are these ancestors named and ordered?  [إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ وَإِسْمَٰعِيلَ وَإِسْحَٰقَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Seniority or rhetorical honor.
- 2:133/q27/p1 Ishmael precedes Isaac because he was older, Abraham's firstborn. — Ibn Zayd, Abu Zayd, Tabarsi, Qaffal, Qurtubi, Alusi and Asad say so; Ibn ʿAshur gives a fourteen-year gap. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو زيد); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:133/q27/p2 The list honors Jacob as questioner, Abraham as origin, Ishmael as Muhammad's ancestor, and outstanding forefathers. — Abu Hayyan gives the sequence and says they were the best of their eras; Ibn ʿAshur compares genealogical poetry. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:133/q28 How do the foreign ancestor names decline and pluralize?  [إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ وَإِسْمَٰعِيلَ وَإِسْحَٰقَ · grammar]
turns on: Foreign-name declension and rival plural forms.
- 2:133/q28/p1 The foreign proper names are diptote; an actually Arabic-derived Ishaq or Yaqub would decline. — Akhfash, Nahhas, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samīn explain; Kisaʾi permits Arabic derivations while Abu Hayyan distinguishes an Arabic verbal noun. [7 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:133/q28/p2 Broken plurals vary; regular sound masculine plurals preserve the stems. — Sibawayh and Khalil give barahim and samaʿil; Kufans offer forms with replacement ha. Ibn Yazid/Abu al-ʿAbbas contests lost hamza and permits abarih and asamiʿ; Ahmad ibn Yahya cites diminutive analogy. Nahhas favors sound plurals. [6 notes: NAHHAS(أحمد بن يحيى,الكوفيون,سيبويه والخليل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NAHHAS+; NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العباس)-]

## 2:133/q29 How is Ibrahim pronounced in canonical readings?  [إِبْرَٰهِۦمَ · readings]
turns on: The vowel before the final mim.
- 2:133/q29/p1 Ibrahima has long i before the final mim. — Qiraat-ER calls this the standard canonical form. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 2:133/q29/p2 Ibrahama has long a before the final mim. — Qiraat-ER calls it a canonical phonological adaptation. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 2:133/q30 What does the sons' worship pledge teach?  [نَعْبُدُ إِلَٰهَكَ وَإِلَٰهَ ءَابَآئِكَ · interpretation]
- 2:133/q30/p1 They affirm the one God without partners and continue the patriarchs' submission. — Tabari stresses exclusive worship and lordship; Dahhak says their reply affirms unity; Qushayri calls it inherited monotheism. Razi says fuller divine attributes are omitted for brevity; Ibn Kathir cites 3:83 and 21:25. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,عطاء); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 2:133/q31 What does naming Ishmael show about kinship?  [إِسْمَٰعِيلَ · interpretation]
- 2:133/q31/p1 Jacob's sons belong to Abraham's wider family, including Ishmael's line. — Elmalılı uses this to interpret the promised prophet from Israel's brothers as an Ishmaelite. [4 notes: ELMALILI]

## 2:133/q32 Which son was the intended sacrifice?  [إِسْمَٰعِيلَ وَإِسْحَٰقَ · reports]
turns on: The identity of the sacrificial son.
- 2:133/q32/p1 Isaac is the sacrifice in a well-known view. — Ibn ʿAtiyya mentions this beside 'son of the two sacrificed.' [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 2:133/q32/p2 Ishmael was the intended sacrifice. — Ibn ʿAshur rejects the Jewish Isaac claim: the Torah's son is unique, while Isaac was never Abraham's only son. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 2:133/q33 What does ilahan mean syntactically?  [إِلَٰهًۭا وَٰحِدًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Apposition, circumstance, or specification.
- 2:133/q33/p1 It apposes 'your God' or 'God of your fathers' to state oneness expressly. — Nahhas allows definite/indefinite apposition; Kashshaf compares 96:15–16. Baydawi notes possible plural misunderstanding; Samīn describes Basran allowance of unmodified indefinite apposition. [18 notes: NAHHAS; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]
- 2:133/q33/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative describing one deity in worship. — Akhfash, Tabari and Ibn ʿAtiyya favor this. Ibn ʿAshur says wahidan carries the description while the repeated noun clarifies. [12 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني); IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 2:133/q33/p3 It is an accusative of specification, 'we mean one God'. — Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Samīn offer it; Abu Hayyan and Samīn's Sheikh object that specification cannot be indefinite or ambiguous. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 2:133/q34 Why explicitly add 'one God'?  [إِلَٰهًۭا وَٰحِدًۭا · rhetoric]
- 2:133/q34/p1 It prevents inference of multiple ancestral gods and reinforces strict monotheism. — Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, Bursevi, Alusi and Tabatabaʾi stress the ambiguity. Ibn ʿAshur cites Quranic and poetic repetition where a new predicate adds meaning; Ibn Jinni contrasts pointless bare repetition. [15 notes: THALABI; IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني); TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:133/q35 What does muslimun mean?  [مُسْلِمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Submission as disposition or entry into Islam.
- 2:133/q35/p1 They submit humbly and obediently to God alone, affirming His oneness in belief and practice. — Tabari defines obedience and worship; Ibn ʿAbbas says affirmation of unity. Tabarsi adds servitude and humility; Alusi includes obeying commands and Biqaʿi no choice against God's command. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:133/q35/p2 They entered and remain in Islam, God's religion and that of every prophet. — Tabarsi cites 3:19; Ibn Kathir cites 3:83, 21:25 and the prophets' shared religion; Tabatabaʾi says Abraham's descendants inherited it. [5 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 2:133/q36 How is 'we submit to Him' attached?  [وَنَحْنُ لَهُۥ مُسْلِمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Circumstance, coordination, or an independent clause.
- 2:133/q36/p1 It is circumstantial, attached to worship's subject, object or both. — Tabari prefers it; Kashshaf, Tabarsi, Baydawi and Razi give different attachments. Bursevi ties it to worshippers. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:133/q36/p2 It coordinates with 'we worship' as a second enduring answer. — Abu Hayyan prefers direct coordination over ellipsis: the verb denotes renewed worship and the nominal clause stable surrender. Ibn ʿAshur agrees. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 2:133/q36/p3 It is a separate nominal assertion or emphatic parenthesis. — Ibn ʿAtiyya prefers a complete statement, 'we were and will be so.' Kashshaf proposes parenthesis; Abu Hayyan and Samīn's Sheikh reject that technical label because no linked part of the same speaker follows. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 2:133/q37 How does Jacob's testament connect to Abraham and the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How ancestral testimony bears on present claims.
- 2:133/q37/p1 Jacob renews Abraham's bequest of monotheism and refutes later exclusive Jewish and Christian claims. — Razi says it strengthens Abraham's proof; Biqaʿi relates 2:112 and 2:134 to religious kinship. Kuranyolu and Tabatabaʾi call Islam inherited; Study Quran calls it primordial monotheism. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin çoğu); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 2:133/q37/p2 Ancestral deeds and lineage do not save a person; one must obey God oneself. — Biqaʿi cites 2:134 and places religious kinship over blood. Abu Hayyan cites a warning to Banu Hashim and Fatima. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 2:133/q37/p3 Jacob's story is developed in Surah 12 and prophet lists. — Study Quran cites 12:38, 2:136 and 4:163. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 2:133/q38 What do adjacent reports say about the tribes and revelation?  [* · links]
turns on: Two related topics recorded alongside this verse.
- 2:133/q38/p1 Believers must affirm every prophet and revelation, including Jesus. — Tabari cites Qatada and Ibn ʿAbbas's report of Jews rejecting Jesus; versions name Rafiʿ or Nafiʿ ibn Abi Rafiʿ, and 5:59 is invoked. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة)]
- 2:133/q38/p2 Jacob's twelve sons founded the tribes. — Qatada and Rabiʿ report twelve peoples; al-Suddi lists eight named sons; Ibn Ishaq gives wives and genealogies, citing 7:160 and leaving full knowledge to God. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,السدي,قتادة,محمد بن إسحاق)]

## 2:133/q39 Can ancestor testimony alone ground knowledge of God?  [* · theology]
turns on: How the sons acquired their faith.
- 2:133/q39/p1 The sons' reference to forefathers shows imitation suffices. — Abu Hayyan reports the traditionalists' inference; Razi says the verse itself proves neither imitation nor reasoning. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المقلدة); RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:133/q39/p2 God is known only by instruction from a prophet or imam. — Abu Hayyan reports the Taʿlimiyya's inference; Razi and Abu Hayyan deny that the verse establishes it. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التعليمية); RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:133/q39/p3 The sons' faith rested on reasoning, though the verse only records confession. — Razi argues that exclusive imitation or instruction is disproved; Abu Hayyan says the confession alone gives no acquisition method. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 2:133/q40 Does a grandfather exclude siblings in inheritance?  [ءَابَآئِكَ · law]
turns on: Whether extended kinship language entails the father's legal rule.
- 2:133/q40/p1 Yes; he stands as father and excludes siblings. — Abu Hanifa cites this verse and 12:38; Ibn ʿAbbas swears at the Black Stone and compares 4:11. Razi lists Abu Bakr and Aisha; Ibn Kathir adds Bukhari's Abu Bakr report and Abu Yusuf and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan's later choice. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الصديق وجماعة من الصحابة وأبو حنيفة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر,أبو يوسف ومحمد بن الحسن,عائشة والحسن البصري وطاوس وعطاء وأبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)-]
- 2:133/q40/p2 No; he shares with siblings, receiving the better of a share or one-third. — Razi lists Shafiʿi, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Ibn Masʿud, Zayd, Malik, Abu Yusuf and Muhammad. Zayd and Shafiʿi give the better share or third; Ibn Kathir includes Ahmad among non-exclusion advocates. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي,زيد بن ثابت); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن ثابت ومالك وأبو يوسف والشافعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك والشافعي وأحمد)]
- 2:133/q40/p3 No; he counts as a sibling but receives at least one-sixth. — Razi reports it; Abu Hayyan attributes it to Ali and Ibn Abi Layla. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وابن أبي ليلى)]
- 2:133/q40/p4 Calling him father in this verse does not settle his literal or legal status. — Shafiʿi argues generational rank, ordinary speech, Companion disagreement, grandmother inheritance and 4:11's grandson case against literal identity. Razi says Ibn ʿAbbas may mean legal status only. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)]

## 2:133/q41 What levels of Islam and faith are described?  [وَنَحْنُ لَهُۥ مُسْلِمُونَ · theology]
turns on: Degrees of outward profession and inward surrender.
- 2:133/q41/p1 Outward Islam professes God's oneness and the Messenger, while faith adds inward guidance. — Al-Sadiq compares Islam to the sanctuary and faith to the Kaaba inside it; outward profession governs life, marriage and inheritance. Tabatabaʾi calls this a first level. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام)]
- 2:133/q41/p2 Deeper Islam is surrender with certainty and without inward objection. — Ali defines surrender as certainty; al-Sadiq says ritual acts with inward objection can still be association. Tabatabaʾi assigns this to a third level. [2 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام,علي عليه السلام)]
- 2:133/q41/p3 A fourth level is continual remembrance, choosing God's will and an illuminated heart. — Miʿraj and Haritha reports describe divine love, hidden knowledge, visions and eased judgment. Tabatabaʾi says each level has a matching subtler unbelief or association; he relates 50:35 and unimaginable reward. [10 notes: TABATABAI(الإمام عليه السلام,الله,النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم,حارثة بن مالك)]

## 2:133/q42 What does the verse imply about human acts?  [* · theology]
turns on: The relation of divine creation to human capacity.
- 2:133/q42/p1 Humans are compelled without choice. — Abu Hayyan reports the Jabriyya's moved-branch analogy; Ibn ʿAtiyya says the verse refutes denial of acquisition. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجبرية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 2:133/q42/p2 Humans can bring their own acts into existence without compulsion. — Abu Hayyan reports the Qadariyya position. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القدرية)]
- 2:133/q42/p3 God creates capacity before the act, leaving a choice to do or omit it. — Abu Hayyan attributes this to the Muʿtazila. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتزلة)]
- 2:133/q42/p4 God creates capacity alongside the act, which the servant performs and acquires without compulsion. — Abu Hayyan presents the Sunni view. Abu al-Hasan defines acquisition as relation to capacity; Baqillani locates the act's moral quality in created capacity; Isfarayini joins divine and created capacities. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو إسحاق الأسفرائيني,أبو الحسن,أهل السنة والجماعة,ابن عطية,الباقلاني)]

## 2:133/q43 How is Jacob's question applied inwardly?  [مَا تَعْبُدُونَ · inward]
- 2:133/q43/p1 It applies to the Qaʾim from Muhammad's family, whose members ask children the same question at death. — Al-Baqir states the application; al-Safi explains it. Tabatabaʾi allows inner applications beyond the outward verse. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الباقر عليه السلام,الصافي)]

## 2:133/q44 What do earlier scriptures add to the argument?  [* · links]
- 2:133/q44/p1 Elmalılı reads preserved scriptural promises as foretelling Muhammad, the culminating Ishmaelite prophet whom earlier communities had to follow. — He identifies Israel's 'brothers' with Ishmaelites; Habakkuk's Paran with Mecca; Isaiah's visitors and new praise with pilgrimage and talbiya; Torah's twelve princes with Muhammad's community. Greek Torah, Psalms, Gospel and Paraclete reports are cited, including Faraklit as intercessor or truth separator. Ibn ʿAbbas's Torah report promises two rewards; another names Mecca, Tayba, Syria and Hammādūn. He says Jews and Christians conceal or distort signs and links 2:40–41 and 2:121. [21 notes: ELMALILI(Nasârâ,Seman,İbn Abbas)]

## 2:133/q45 What does the following verse's 'that community passed away' mean?  [* · links]
- 2:133/q45/p1 It refers to Abraham, Jacob and monotheistic descendants, a community now dead and separate. — Bursevi derives umma from an intended goal and followed group; 'passed away' evokes becoming empty. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 2:133/q46 What spiritual rank has Jacob's family?  [* · inward]
- 2:133/q46/p1 They inherited a single monotheistic path as purified people near God, each with an assigned rank. — Qushayri states the inherited path and different shares of felicity. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 2:133/q47 What indirect command is discussed beside the witness challenge?  [* · rhetoric]
- 2:133/q47/p1 'Do not let me see you here' forbids the presence causing the sight and conveys anger. — Abu Hayyan explains the indirect expression. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
