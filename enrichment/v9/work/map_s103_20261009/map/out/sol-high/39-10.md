# 39:10: 29 questions

## 39:10/q01 Why is the Prophet told to say “My servants”?  [قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِ · rhetoric]
- 39:10/q01/p1 God's own address honors and reassures believers while strengthening the command. — Al-Biqāʿī says the possessive promises nearness against opponents. Bursawī says the Prophet repeats God's very words. Ālūsī calls it direct divine exhortation; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the command marks importance and elevates believers. Asad supplies “God speaks” to clarify the possessive. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 39:10/q01/p2 The command follows praise of the devout in the preceding verse to strengthen their resolve. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads an implied “say to the believers” after 39:9 and notes the vocative yā is elided. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q01/p3 The command to say it fits al-Zumar's indirect, third-person relay of divine speech, unlike the sustained first-person speech in al-ʿAnkabūt. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the continued first-person divine speech in al-ʿAnkabūt with al-Zumar's use of qul to relay the address indirectly; he says that pattern continues through the two procession verses. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]

## 39:10/q02 How is يَٰعِبَادِ read and written?  [يَٰعِبَادِ · readings]
turns on: Whether this verse has a pronounced possessive yāʾ, or reports of it belong to 39:53.
- 39:10/q02/p1 The possessive yāʾ is omitted in this verse. — Ibn Mujāhid reports Qunbul from Ibn Kathīr with kasra on dāl and no yāʾ, as with other readers; al-Nasafī says most omit it. Bursawī says the kasra suffices. Ibn ʿĀshūr says all ten omit it at pause and liaison, following the consonantal text and distinguishing 39:53. Ibn al-Jazarī says readers unanimously delete the yāʾ at pause, apart from Abū al-ʿAlāʾ's unique transmission from Ruways. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(قنبل عن النبال عن أصحابه عن ابن كثير); NASAFI-FULL(الأكثر); BURSEVI; NASHR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 39:10/q02/p2 A possessive yāʾ is read in at least some circumstances. — Ibn Mujāhid says Abū ʿAmr separates at an āya ending but joins عباد الذين with yāʾ. Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes open yāʾ to most, silent yāʾ to Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim and al-Aʿmash, and deletion in liaison to others. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects this attribution as confusion with 39:53. Abū al-ʿAlāʾ al-Hamadhānī alone reports from Ruways a route retaining the yāʾ in yā ʿibād al-ladhīna āmanū at pause, by analogy with yā ʿibādi fa-ttaqūn; Ibn al-Jazarī acknowledges this exception to the otherwise unanimous deletion at pause. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); NASHR(أبو العلاء الهمداني عن رويس); IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 39:10/q03 Why is the shorter form يَٰعِبَادِ used?  [يَٰعِبَادِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the deletion is explained by the size of the addressed group or the sufficiency of the kasra.
- 39:10/q03/p1 The shorter form suits the smaller group addressed here. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī relates written length to group size, contrasting longer ʿibādī at 39:53 and 39:17; he says additions and deletions are contextual across passages. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the universal death and return statement of 29:56 with the few patient people in 39:10: the larger audience fits an explicit first-person yāʾ, while Zumar's narrower group fits its absence. He also observes five first-person divine pronouns in the ʿAnkabūt passage against the omitted possessive suffix in Zumar. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]
- 39:10/q03/p2 The omitted yāʾ is sufficiently signaled by the preceding kasra. — Bursawī links the spelling to grammatical economy while stressing the honor of God's possessive address. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 39:10/q04 Who are the believing servants addressed?  [يَٰعِبَادِ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is read in its historical setting, broadly of monotheists, or inwardly.
- 39:10/q04/p1 Jaʿfar ibn Abī Ṭālib and companions preparing to migrate to Abyssinia. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Baghawī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī report the same occasion, with variants in whether the group resolved to depart or had migrated. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:10/q04/p2 Believers who affirm God's unity and are pressed by persecution. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls them monotheists. Ibn ʿĀshūr says their established faith survives pressure that could force outward disbelief; Iṣlāḥī describes oppressed poor Muslims in Mecca. [3 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:10/q04/p3 The spiritually elect servants, freed from all attachment but God. — Bursawī's Kashf al-Asrār calls them those preferring God's pleasure to ego desire; Bursawī describes seekers moved by longing and love, illustrates self-abasement through a Prophetic saying and Abū Yazīd's striking wish concerning Hell. Ālūsī echoes longing and exclusive seeking. [5 notes: BURSEVI(أبو يزيد,كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:10/q05 What does “fear your Lord” require?  [ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taqwā is explained as outward obedience, the deepening of existing faith, or inward detachment.
- 39:10/q05/p1 Obey God, avoid sins, and remain firm in reverent responsibility. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Baghawī give obedience and avoidance; al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī clarify avoidance of punishment or prohibitions. Bursawī adds persistence; Kuranyolu calls taqwā careful responsibility and cites 7:26 and 6:160. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:10/q05/p2 Deepen existing faith and sincerity despite hardship. — Al-Biqāʿī says basic belief can coexist with incomplete piety, and God needs nothing from obedience. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the already pious may weaken under pressure, so the command prepares migration. Kuranyolu names faith, piety and excellence as conditions of a good Muslim life. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:10/q05/p3 Use God as protection from everything besides Him and remove the veil of attachment. — Bursawī describes escape from separation and joy in divine beauty; al-Baqlī makes taqwā stripping the self of attachment until it stands unveiled. [2 notes: BURSEVI(البقلي)]

## 39:10/q06 Does the command to believers imply grave sin removes faith?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether grave sin cancels faith and its reward or faith remains despite sin.
- 39:10/q06/p1 No: faith remains even when a believer has not fulfilled taqwā. — Al-Rāzī argues that commanding believers to add God-consciousness presupposes faith with sin; exhortation is stronger if they remain believers when they fall short. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 39:10/q06/p2 Avoidance of major sins preserves faith's reward; grave sin cancels it. — Al-Qāḍī says the command protects faith and its recompense from nullification. Al-Rāzī rejects the implication. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-]

## 39:10/q07 What is the iʿrāb of ٱتَّقُوا۟?  [ٱتَّقُوا۟ · grammar]
- 39:10/q07/p1 Its tāʾ replaces an original wāw. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the morphological observation, repeated by al-Qurṭubī. [2 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 39:10/q08 What does doing good, أَحْسَنُوا۟, entail?  [أَحْسَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether excellence is described by outward acts, the Prophetic definition of worship, or an inward realization.
- 39:10/q08/p1 It includes faithful obedience, righteous conduct, and kindness to others. — Al-Baghawī calls the doers believers acting well; al-Qushayrī says excellence is doing all one can; al-Wāḥidī specifies monotheists who obey; al-Jishumī includes generosity and inviting others to religion; al-Ṭabrisī says virtuous acts and kindness. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates them to the God-fearing in 39:9 and says the relative phrase embraces all with the quality. Bursawī stresses sincerity and the testimony of faith; a bad deed cannot earn good recompense. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 39:10/q08/p2 Iḥsān is worshipping God as though seeing Him, aware that He sees the worshipper. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the Prophetic definition; Bursawī and Ālūsī invoke the Gabriel report, with Bursawī adding that witnessing brings full sincerity and guards against ostentation and shirk. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates this to the devout of 39:9. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q08/p3 Inward excellence is seeking God alone and witnessing His beauty. — Al-Qāshānī speaks of effacing attributes and taking on divine attributes; Najm al-Dīn says sincere seekers ask no good from others and find God; al-Khujandī's poem makes seeking the key to the treasure; al-Baqlī describes unveiling in heart and spirit. Ālūsī gives exclusive seeking. [5 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(البقلي,الخجندي,نجم الدين); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:10/q09 What good is promised to those who do good?  [لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا۟ فِى هَٰذِهِ ٱلدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether “in this world” locates good deeds or the reward, and whether the promise is confined to one realm.
- 39:10/q09/p1 Paradise is the good reward for goodness done in this world. — Al-Ṭabarī, Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Jishumī, al-Rāzī and many later sources locate reward in the Hereafter. Al-Rāzī argues the indefinite good is majestic and lasting, that unbelievers share health, and that the restrictive wording excludes shared worldly benefits (43:33); he cites the saying that worldly life is a believer's prison and unbeliever's garden. Al-Qushayrī makes the same worldly-benefits objection in al-Qurṭubī. Al-Ālūsī's opening exposition treats the indefinite as magnification; Bursevi adds vision of God. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(JJ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي)+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)+; RAZI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL-]
- 39:10/q09/p2 Worldly health, safety, sufficiency, and well-being are the good. — Al-Suddī is cited by al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī and others. Al-Rāzī reports a saying naming security, health and sufficiency as inexhaustible blessings. Abū Ḥayyān adds success and divine protection; al-Ṭabrisī adds thanks and reputation. Al-Ālūsī describes this as an immediate small share, but objects that unbelievers too receive health, citing 41:50; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the proposed attachment to ḥasana. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL(السدي); NAHHAS; TABRISI(السدي); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,السدي,الطبرسي,الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL(السدي); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 39:10/q09/p3 Goodness includes reward in this world and the Hereafter. — Al-Māwardī offers worldly reward besides final recompense; al-Qurṭubī replies that grateful believers receive both; Ibn Kathīr states both. Al-Biqāʿī prefers breadth across both realms. Ālūsī later prefers both, with inward and outward action; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists peace of mind, sound soul and freedom from dependence here, lasting delight there. Kuranyolu prefers the unrestricted promise; Study Quran likewise allows health and peace here and good beyond. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); ALUSI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)+]
- 39:10/q09/p4 The worldly good is praise, good reputation, kind words, or God's guardianship. — Al-Naḥḥās includes God's friendship, praise and good names; al-Māwardī proposes praise with next-life recompense. Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī cite praise and gratitude; al-Qurṭubī reports a good reputation; al-Ālūsī says God's guardianship is distinctive to believers. [8 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي)]
- 39:10/q09/p5 The worldly good is victory, spoils, divine support, or relief from enemies. — Al-Naqqāsh says victory and spoils; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports al-Suddī's victory and support gloss, though he prefers the Hereafter. ʿAṭāʾ, reported by Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī, ties relief from enemies to emigrating to Medina; al-Qurṭubī includes victory and booty. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 39:10/q09/p6 Inwardly the good is finding God, seeing His enduring Face, and divine unveiling. — Al-Qāshānī calls divine vision the good of those witnessing; Najm al-Dīn and Ālūsī describe finding God. Al-Baqlī distinguishes worldly inspirations from immersion in eternity and abiding in the attributes hereafter. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(البقلي,نجم الدين); ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:10/q09/p7 The worldly good is obedience granted in this life, with Paradise in the next. — Al-Ḥasan's distinction is transmitted by al-Māwardī; al-Qurṭubī likewise distinguishes first good as obedience from second as Paradise. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 39:10/q09/p8 It is whatever good God gives in worldly life. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām states the broad gloss; al-Jishumī also reports Muqātil's generic “goodness.” Ibn ʿĀshūr says the promise of worldly goodness explains the address to the pious and prepares the assurance of broad safe lands; it may also ground the command to fear God. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام)]

## 39:10/q10 How does فِى هَٰذِهِ ٱلدُّنْيَا attach syntactically?  [أَحْسَنُوا۟ فِى هَٰذِهِ ٱلدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase modifies the act, the reward, or deliberately allows both.
- 39:10/q10/p1 It modifies أَحْسَنُوا۟, placing the doing of good in this world. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās and others report this; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī favor it with Paradise as reward. Al-Samīn says an omitted qualifier magnifies ḥasana. Bursawī parses ḥasana as subject and li-lladhīna as predicate. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this also urges action before opportunity passes; Ālūsī's primary exposition agrees. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 39:10/q10/p2 It locates or describes حَسَنَةٌۭ, making worldly good the direct promise. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās report this parse; al-Zamakhsharī says a preceding phrase can specify location despite not being a following adjective. Al-Samīn makes it a fronted adjective with an omitted circumstantial element. Al-Biqāʿī calls a new subject-predicate worldly clause too narrow; Ālūsī records al-Suddī and al-Zamakhsharī, yet criticizes a circumstantial modifier of an indefinite subject and al-Akhfash's weak analysis. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the attachment. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL(السدي); NASAFI-FULL(السدي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,السدي); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q10/p3 Its central placement permits both worldly and afterlife senses. — Al-Biqāʿī prefers a broad reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the placement is deliberate and allows multiple meanings; the indefinite reward can magnify either otherworldly essence or worldly quality. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]

## 39:10/q11 What does the word حَسَنَةٌۭ contribute to the promise?  [حَسَنَةٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 39:10/q11/p1 Its indefiniteness magnifies the promised good. — Al-Zamakhsharī's view, via al-Samīn, supplies an omitted “great”; Ālūsī says the indefinite magnifies Paradise. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it can magnify afterlife reward intrinsically or worldly good qualitatively, with 2:201 as a comparison. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q11/p2 It stands for a good condition, with a noun elided. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the transfer of adjective to noun in 2:201 and the inverse expression in 42:40. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q11/p3 The fronted لِلَّذِينَ draws attention to the recipients of divine kindness. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the word order to an invitation to become God-fearing doers of good. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:10/q12 What does أَرْضٌ mean in Arabic usage?  [وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the question concerns the ordinary root sense or its particular Qur'anic geographical uses.
- 39:10/q12/p1 Earth is named for breadth; related expressions extend the root to expansiveness. — Ibn al-Jawzī calls earth the familiar broad expanse. Ibn al-Sikkīt says a sore “arḍ” when it spreads; Ibn Fāris says any broad thing is arḍ and arīḍ describes a man suited to good. The same lexicon records termite-eaten wood, a wool or hair carpet, approaching someone, a foreigner, lush land, and Ibn ʿAbbās's distinction between earth's and one's own trembling. [9 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن السكيت,ابن عباس,ابن فارس)]
- 39:10/q12/p2 Across Qur'anic passages, arḍ can denote Paradise, Mecca, Medina, or Syria. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites Paradise at 21:105 and 39:74, Mecca at 4:97 and 13:41, Medina at 4:97, 4:100, 17:76 and 29:56, and Syria at 7:137 and 21:71; he includes 39:10 among Medina examples. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 39:10/q13 What does “God's earth is vast” refer to here?  [وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ · referent]
turns on: Whether the land is a worldly destination, the Garden, provision from the earth, or inward spiritual space.
- 39:10/q13/p1 The broad worldly earth offers other places where believers can worship safely. — Mujāhid explains leaving idol worship; al-Ṭabarī says leave polytheist territory; Ibn ʿAbbās ties it to leaving Mecca and 4:97; al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī cite prophets and the righteous as precedents. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says nearby lands fit the migration story; al-Rāzī and Ālūsī prefer this because subsequent patience fits migration. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the obvious fact an indirect exhortation like a proverb, with 4:97 explicit. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes the historically Meccan pressure as particular but wording general. [39 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قيل); THALABI(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عطاء,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(zayıf bir görüş); STUDYQURAN(IK, JJ, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 39:10/q13/p2 It is Paradise or the broad Garden sought through righteous deeds. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the Garden; al-Thaʿlabī reports Muqātil; Ibn ʿĪsā, Abū Muslim and al-Ṭabrisī offer it to encourage goodness. Abū Muslim cites 39:74 and 3:133 and the movement from piety to Paradise. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it unsupported, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī remote, and Kuranyolu weak. [13 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); JISHUMI(مقاتل وأبو مسلم); TABRISI(مقاتل، أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مسلم,قوم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(zayıf bir görüş); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)-; TABATABAI-]
- 39:10/q13/p3 It designates Medina, the destination of migration. — Muqātil identifies Medina; ʿAṭāʾ likewise calls it the migration land or Medina, with relief from enemies as the good; Ibn al-Jawzī cites this verse among Medina uses. [5 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(عطاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عطاء); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]
- 39:10/q13/p4 Its breadth signals abundant provision or fruitful lands available to believers. — Al-Māwardī prefers this because the clause presents a favor; al-Jishumī reports fruitful lands; al-Qurṭubī finds provision plausible and recalls Sufyān al-Thawrī's advice to move to cheaper bread. [4 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل وأبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); MAWARDI-FULL+]
- 39:10/q13/p5 Inwardly it is the expanded self or heart, or limitless divine presence. — Al-Qāshānī describes the contented self freed by certainty; al-Baqlī says the Real expands the heart. The Najmī interpretations and Rūmī say no unveiling is a final arrival; Ālūsī calls it the limitless domain of majesty and beauty. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(البقلي,التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:10/q14 What practical migration does the spacious earth call for?  [وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ · law]
turns on: Whether migration is the concrete response to persecution, a move for provision, or a broader departure from conditions that impede goodness.
- 39:10/q14/p1 Leave a place where religion cannot be practiced or sin is forced or openly practiced. — Al-Zajjāj cites 4:97 for leaving idol pressure; al-Naḥḥās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr extend flight to sinful settings. Al-Qushayrī and later exegetes say persecution cannot excuse abandoning worship. Bursawī cites a report promising Paradise for migration to protect faith and distinguishes that motive from worldly moves to a more sinful place. Iṣlāḥī says move if Makkah makes worship impossible. [23 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قيل); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عطاء); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:10/q14/p2 Migration may include moving to land with cheaper living or better provision. — Al-Qurṭubī draws this from al-Māwardī's provision reading and quotes Sufyān al-Thawrī on cheaper bread. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]
- 39:10/q14/p3 The enduring sense includes spiritual migration from evil and changes in work or place. — Asad sees the capacity always to do good and cites 4:97; Kuranyolu ranges from changing jobs to changing countries when one cannot live one's values or improve conditions. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:10/q15 What migration setting lies behind the verse?  [وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ · reports]
turns on: Whether the historical allusion concerns the first migration to Abyssinia or departure toward Medina.
- 39:10/q15/p1 It alludes to Jaʿfar and companions' migration to Abyssinia. — Al-Baghawī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya report the group preparing or enduring trials and emigrating; al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr attribute the identification to Ibn ʿAbbās. Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī repeat it. Ibn ʿĀshūr dates the Abyssinian move to year five before the later Medinan migration, with 83 men and 19 women excluding children, under a just king; he also recounts Abū Bakr's interrupted attempted departure and says the Prophet remained to establish Islam in Mecca until Medina was ready. [10 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:10/q15/p2 It urges leaving Mecca for Medina or another safe land. — Muqātil names Medina; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Rāzī make Mecca the point of departure. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reassurance eases leaving home and 4:97 makes migration explicit; Iṣlāḥī describes Quraysh pressure and a land that would receive expelled Muslims. [5 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:10/q16 How is the spacious-earth clause linked to the promise around it?  [وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ · links]
turns on: Whether the aside addresses practical worship and migration or climate and bodily welfare.
- 39:10/q16/p1 It removes the excuse that homeland prevents excellence and prepares the special reward for patient migrants. — Al-Ālūsī calls it an aside and argues the next clause distinguishes migrants, whose sacrifices follow prophets and righteous people; he prefers this to a climate-and-health connection, citing 4:97 and 29:56 and the nearer attachment rule. Al-Biqāʿī says all land lies under God's power. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees an indirect call that consoles those leaving home. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:10/q16/p2 It meets concerns about climate and bodily health while contrasting immediate worldly good with later full reward. — Al-Kashf calls this explanatory connection finer than a restrictive circumstantial parse; Ālūsī acknowledges its merit but prefers the obedience-and-migration connection. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 39:10/q17 Who are ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ here?  [ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether patience is specified by migration, affliction, obedience, fasting, or inward discipline.
- 39:10/q17/p1 They endure persecution, migration, exile from home and kin, and hardship for religion. — Muqātil connects patience to emigration. Al-Baghawī mentions steadfast faith and Jaʿfar's companions; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes hardship, emigration and aid to faith. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī emphasize the cost of leaving home. Kuranyolu points to sacrifice for values and 4:97–100. [16 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:10/q17/p2 They endure all afflictions and trials with composure. — Al-Ṭabarī takes life's ordeals generally; al-Zajjāj links hardship to obedience; Qushayrī describes restraining the self and accepting decree without protest. Al-Qurṭubī cites Mālik on calamities and griefs; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines composure when pain arrives. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ālūsī say the wording is unrestricted despite migration's context. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن أنس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:10/q17/p3 They persevere in obedience, duties, and avoidance of sin. — Al-Naḥḥās says ṣābir praises restraint from sin and distinguishes patience “with” sin from patience “on” misfortune. Junayd adds restraining limbs while seeking reward; Ṭāhir al-Maqdisī distinguishes patience from, for, upon and in God, calling obedience easiest. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes commands and prohibitions, with reward varying by degree. [10 notes: NAHHAS; SULAMI(الجنيد,طاهر المقدسي); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,مالك بن أنس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(طاهر المقدسي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q17/p4 They are those tested with illness and worldly calamity. — Reports from Anas and others identify the afflicted whose reward surpasses ordinary deeds; al-Qurṭubī's Mālik includes grief and trial. [12 notes: THALABI(الحسن بن علي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أنس,الحسين بن علي,قتادة,مالك بن أنس); BAYDAWI-FULL(حديث); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI]
- 39:10/q17/p5 Inwardly they bear affliction, restrain the self, and steadily advance through unveiled states. — Al-Muḥāsibī's arrows of affliction occur in al-Sulamī and Bursawī; Junayd speaks of carrying hardship to its end. Al-Khawwāṣ criticizes reliance on worldly means and Dhū al-Nūn's poem depicts love's tears. Yūsuf ibn al-Ḥusayn asks for delight and contentment rather than aversion; al-Qāshānī and al-Baqlī speak of effacement and concealed unveiling. [10 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد,الحارث المحاسبي,الخواص,ذو النون); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(البقلي,الحارث المحاسبي,يوسف بن الحسين)]
- 39:10/q17/p6 They are those who fast. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this from the divine saying that fasting belongs to God, with its reward uniquely His; other scholars say other deeds are weighed while fasting is rewarded in handfuls. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]

## 39:10/q18 What does بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ mean for the reward?  [أَجْرَهُم بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥisāb means numerical measure, matching deeds, restricted amount, or a person's judgment audit.
- 39:10/q18/p1 It is immeasurably abundant, beyond counting, weighing, or any scale. — Al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda deny measure or scale; al-Zajjāj says reward is poured out, while spiritual joy cannot literally be weighed and measurement imagery conveys abundance. Al-Māwardī cites a rajaz verse about generous unmeasured watering and ʿAlī's handfuls. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a metaphor for uncountable plenty; Ibn ʿAbbās says no calculator can encompass it. Bursawī adds that reward answers suffering, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says ajr in Qur'anic usage is afterlife reward. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports commentators' interpretation that reward is poured out abundantly, so the phrase emphasizes its great measure. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قيل); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأوزاعي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)+]
- 39:10/q18/p2 The patient undergo no accounting, reproach, record, or audit. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Māwardī's al-Suddī mention no follow-up claim; Ibn ʿAṭiyya associates the patient with those who enter Paradise unreckoned. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī permit this parse; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers no record, scale or audit, and Study Quran reports no weighing. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports commentators' interpretation that the patient receive reward without being called to account, their sins forgiven and Paradise granted without reckoning. [11 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Z); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المفسرون); TABATABAI+]
- 39:10/q18/p3 It exceeds what deeds alone would calculate or deserve. — Al-Naḥḥās says an exact deed-for-deed match would be counted; Ibn Jurayj says God increases the calculated amount. Al-Jubbāʾī proposes deserved recompense plus grace, while al-Qāḍī objects that due reward alone is still reward. Ibn ʿĀshūr says innamā corrects the assumption of merely matched hardship. [9 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); JISHUMI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي,القاضي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q18/p4 The reward is endless, because countable things are finite. — Al-Rāzī reasons from the finitude of what can be enumerated; al-Qāshānī calls spiritual gardens infinite unlike finite material effects, and Ālūsī calls manifestations endless, citing 55:29. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:10/q18/p5 Its magnitude exceeds human conception. — Al-Rāzī cites a Prophetic report that Paradise is greater than its people imagined; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī describe abundance beyond enumeration or imagination. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:10/q18/p6 The abundant reward is located in Paradise. — Al-Suddī gives the location; Muqātil calls it Paradise and provisions. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]
- 39:10/q18/p7 The reward has no constriction or restriction. — Abū Muslim contrasts unrestricted generosity with a measured and therefore constrained allotment; al-Māwardī gives wide, freely bestowed reward. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 39:10/q18/p8 It is manifestations of God without limit. — Ālūsī cites 55:29 to explain never-ending divine manifestations; al-Qāshānī describes gardens of attributes beyond material measure. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:10/q19 How does the closing promise work grammatically and rhetorically?  [إِنَّمَا يُوَفَّى ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ أَجْرَهُم بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the circumstantial phrase describes the reward or its recipients.
- 39:10/q19/p1 بغير حساب is circumstantial for the reward, expressing uncountable abundance. — Al-Samīn permits the reward attachment; al-Nasafī says it describes the reward circumstantially. Ālūsī takes abundance as the apparent sense and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this analysis while preferring no accounting of persons. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports commentators attaching 'without reckoning' to the reward, indicating reward poured out abundantly. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 39:10/q19/p2 بغير حساب is circumstantial for the patient, who are not called to account. — Al-Samīn permits the people attachment; Ālūsī calls it less immediate. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers it with no audit, record or scale. Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports commentators attaching 'without reckoning' to the patient themselves: they are not called to account. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المفسرون); TABATABAI+]
- 39:10/q19/p3 The passive verb foregrounds patience as the reason for full recompense. — Ālūsī says the addressees are implicitly assured of the same reward as earlier patient people; Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain tawfiya as giving in full. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q19/p4 إِنَّمَا restricts the clause to reward without account, exceeding ordinary recompense. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it corrects an expectation that hardship receives only an equal measured return; he says calling it “their reward” comforts them, while all is divine grace, and compares 84:25. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:10/q20 Does calling recompense أَجْرَهُم mean it is owed by God?  [أَجْرَهُم · theology]
turns on: Whether ajr denotes an entitlement earned by human acts or God's promised gift.
- 39:10/q20/p1 No; it is reward secured by God's promise and grace. — Al-Rāzī says the term ajr grants no entitlement. Ibn ʿĀshūr says “their reward” comforts believers by treating it as earned, though everything remains grace. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q20/p2 The patient receive deserved reward plus a gratuitous excess. — Al-Jubbāʾī gives this account of unreckoned abundance. Al-Qāḍī rejects it, reasoning that due recompense alone would still be reward. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]
- 39:10/q20/p3 Good-doers and evildoers deserve fitting recompense for their own acts. — Al-Jishumī says the verse depends on people's acting and God's just rewarding of good and evil. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 39:10/q21 Which reports illustrate the special reward for afflicted patient people?  [ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ أَجْرَهُم بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether reports distinguish the afflicted by absence of scale, absence of record, particular acts of patience, or general trial.
- 39:10/q21/p1 Afflicted people receive poured-out reward without a scale or record, making the spared envy them. — Anas reports contrast measured worship with limitless affliction reward; al-Ṭabrisī says no scale or register when balances appear. Al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Durr and Bursawī repeat variants. Some say healthy people wish their skins had been cut with scissors; Ibn Masʿūd reports the same wish. Al-Durr adds a report that God may intensify trials of a beloved servant while hearing supplication, then grant, store or avert harm. [11 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس,قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL(حديث); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(أنس بن مالك عن النبي)]
- 39:10/q21/p2 The afflicted receive unmeasured reward beneath a tree of tribulation in Paradise. — Al-Thaʿlabī recounts Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī's report of obligations, contentment and the tree; al-Qurṭubī attributes a version to al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī from his grandfather and says it concludes by reciting this verse; al-Durr records a tree and no register or scale. [4 notes: THALABI(الحسن بن علي); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسين بن علي); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 39:10/q21/p3 Further Prophetic counsel commends endurance and ties greater trials to greater rank. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports advice to endure harm and a warning through Abū Dharr that impatience leads toward Hell, citing 13:24. Bursawī reports prophets are tested most, that trial can raise one to an otherwise unreachable rank, and that loved servants receive trials whose acceptance brings divine pleasure. [5 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(رسول الله)]
- 39:10/q21/p4 The Prophet sought ever more reward for his community until this verse satisfied him. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the sequence 2:261 then 2:245 then 39:10; Bursawī's Sufyān report starts from tenfold at 6:160, then seven-hundredfold at 2:261, before this verse. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI(سفيان)]
- 39:10/q21/p5 The afflicted have a scale but no record. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits this distinctive report through ʿAbd Allāh ibn Sinān from Abū ʿAbd Allāh, followed by the Prophet's recitation of the verse; he notes al-Durr's Anas transmission of a related account. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أنس بن مالك عن النبي,عبد الله بن سنان عن أبي عبد الله)]
- 39:10/q21/p6 Seventy thousand enter Paradise unreckoned through trust in God. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a Prophetic group who shun omens, cauterization and requesting incantations, and rely on God. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites the Prophetic report that seventy thousand of his community enter Paradise without reckoning, without giving a grading. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(رسول الله - صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 39:10/q21/p7 A woman chose patience with illness over cure and was promised no reckoning. — Al-Biqāʿī cites al-Bazzār and Ibn Ḥibbān's Ṣaḥīḥ transmission from Abū Hurayra. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:10/q21/p8 Fasting is rewarded by God Himself beyond the scale used for other works. — Al-Qurṭubī cites the divine saying and scholars' comparison with other deeds, also related from ʿAlī. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]

## 39:10/q22 How does patience support the migration assurance?  [ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ · links]
- 39:10/q22/p1 The close reassures migrants that separation and hardship earn a special abundant reward. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the conclusion to promised good and safe lands. Ālūsī says patient migrants merit special recompense beyond those able to worship at home; al-Biqāʿī observes patience continues with every breath and differs in hidden strength. Kuranyolu ties sacrifice to 4:97–100. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:10/q22/p2 The statement has broader reach than its migration setting. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya extends it to obedience and support of faith; Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress all affliction and patience, while al-ʿĀlūsī says the verse praises patience's high merit. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:10/q23 How does 39:10 connect with nearby verses and parallel commands?  [* · links]
- 39:10/q23/p1 The migration command echoes 4:97 and 29:56. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad and Iṣlāḥī cite or describe these migration passages; Iṣlāḥī emphasizes worship wherever one is. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:10/q23/p2 It continues the portrait of devout believers, consoles persecuted monotheists, and contrasts steadfastness with wavering. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to devout worshippers in 39:9 and to those addressed with good and migration; Iṣlāḥī says 39:9–21 consoles the oppressed against polytheists' false intercession. Study Quran contrasts the patient with those wavering in 39:8. Kuranyolu says 39:10 condenses faith, worship, piety and character. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:10/q23/p3 The promised good parallels 16:30 and the prayer for good in 2:201. — Ibn al-Jawzī points back to the exposition of 16:30; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its promise that the Hereafter is better supplies an unstated counterpart here, and compares the indefinite good to 2:201. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q23/p4 The sequence moves from ordinary promised good to the special unmeasured recompense of patience. — Ālūsī says the closing clause continues the explanatory aside; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a conclusion to the migration assurance. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:10/q23/p5 Compared with 29:56, the verse addresses a narrower group and uses less emphatic, indirect divine speech within al-Zumar's context. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-ʿAnkabūt's broad address, universal death and return, five first-person divine pronouns, possessives and emphatic inna with al-Zumar's few patient servants, omitted possessive suffix and indirect relay through qul; he says extra emphasis on the earth's vastness is unnecessary in 39:10. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]

## 39:10/q24 What doctrinal implications are drawn from the verse and its context?  [* · theology]
- 39:10/q24/p1 The verse gathers the qualities of faith, taqwā, excellence, and patience. — Al-Baqlī names four traits; direct knowledge of God and attributes without inference from created events is his inward account of faith. Kuranyolu says the verse encapsulates Muslim duties. [4 notes: BURSEVI(البقلي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:10/q24/p2 It testifies to divine power, knowledge, just judgment, and human responsibility for action. — Al-Jishumī infers perception without instruments, no divine willing of evil or punishment without guilt, and encouragement of knowledge, migration and obedience. He connects 39:7 and 39:9 to denials that God approves disbelief, transfers another's guilt, or creates acts within humans against their will; he says good and evil agents receive fitting recompense. [4 notes: JISHUMI]

## 39:10/q25 What inward recompense and progress are read across the verse?  [* · inward]
- 39:10/q25/p1 Faith, piety, excellence, and patience unfold as direct knowledge, unveiled nearness, vision, and abiding in God. — Al-Baqlī describes direct knowledge, shedding attachments, unveiled divine beauty, steadfast concealed states, and rewards of inspiration here and immersion in eternity and abiding in attributes hereafter. Al-Qāshānī likewise joins effacement, contentment and infinite gardens of attributes. [9 notes: BURSEVI(البقلي); QASHANI]
- 39:10/q25/p2 The seeker never exhausts the divine domain or its manifestations. — The Najmī interpretations quoted by Bursawī and Rūmī counsel continuing beyond each unveiling; Ālūsī describes unending beauty and manifestations, relating them to 55:29. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:10/q26 Was verse 39:10 assigned a different place of revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 39:10/q26/p1 Some commentators place verses 10 and 23 in the Madinan period. — Study Quran reports this view without naming its proponents. Al-Sakhāwī, as recorded by al-Suyūṭī, says some add the address to believing servants to al-Zumar's Madinan exceptions. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Other commentators); ITQAN(السخاوي)]

## 39:10/q27 What accompanying source excerpts discuss literary comparison rather than directly explaining 39:10?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited passages themselves concern different poetic and rhetorical material.
- 39:10/q27/p1 Al-Bāqillānī criticizes a poem's weak joins, familiar wordplay, diction, forced rhyme, and uneven imagery. — He finds an opening disconnected and derivative, the distance line pleasing but commonplace, the stellar reins objectionable, two panegyric lines alien, tribal names stylistically clumsy despite a sound threat, and genealogical rhyme forced; he contrasts a stronger Banu Tamīm line. [6 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 39:10/q27/p2 Al-Jurjānī surveys rational and conventional similes, including figurative spaciousness. — In Asrār he compares night and pilgrim stars, moon emerging from clouds as relief, clear light interrupted by darkness as joy and grief, fire and coal as justice and injustice, a generous person's wide character as a road, and the breadth of hope against a barren plain; he introduces another darkness comparison. The width examples illustrate why a moral metaphor can feel sensory. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 39:10/q27/p3 Al-Jurjānī argues rhetorical composition is the ordered relation of meanings. — In Dalāʾil he reasons that verbal sequence alone cannot explain unequal judgments of quality; thought must work on the craft's object, and word order follows meanings already ordered in the mind. ## 39:10/q28 What other adjacent lexical or exegetical extracts appear in the notes?  [* · links]
- 39:10/q28/p1 A destination may be described as a place judged by God alone. — Al-Jishumī reports Abū ʿAlī's comparison with a matter referred to a prince; this excerpt is from a different part of his commentary. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)]
- 39:10/q28/p2 Mabwaʾ denotes a settled, secure and suitable dwelling or station. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites al-Harawī's definition and applies the root to the Prophet's placing believers in defensible battle stations, mentioning 59:9, 39:74 and 3:121. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الهروي)]
- 39:10/q28/p3 A separate report says only sincere religion is accepted. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits the Prophet's answer that ostentatious charity earns no reward and connects it to 39:3. [1 notes: TABATABAI(يزيد الرقاشي عن النبي)]
- 39:10/q28/p4 Gratitude should recur more often than patience. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī reasons that blessings persist moment by moment, while patience arises in particular acts or hardships. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 39:10/q29 Why does 39:10 state that God's earth is vast with less emphasis than 29:56?  [وَأَرْضُ ٱللَّهِ وَٰسِعَةٌ · rhetoric]
- 39:10/q29/p1 The emphasis supplied by possessives and inna in 29:56 is unnecessary in 39:10. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī observes that 29:56 reinforces the servants' and earth's relation to God with possessives and inna, while 39:10 gives the spacious-earth statement without that extra emphasis. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]
