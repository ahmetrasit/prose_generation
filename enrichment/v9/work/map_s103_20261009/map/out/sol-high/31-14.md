# 31:14: 34 questions

## 31:14/q01 Whose speech is the command about parents, and how does it fit Luqmān’s counsel?  [وَوَصَّيْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ · links]
turns on: Whether the first-person divine command interrupts the quoted counsel or is itself part of that counsel or an address to Luqmān.
- 31:14/q01/p1 It is a divine interjection in Luqmān’s speech, reinforcing his warning against shirk before his counsel resumes. — Al-Naḥḥās calls it an interruption; Bursevī links it to 31:13. Al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects attribution to Luqmān or a command addressed to him. Elmalılı and Samarrāʾī call it God's own inserted speech; Samarrāʾī says God takes over because parents' standing is great. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says gratitude to parents is itself obedience to God. [9 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 31:14/q01/p2 It remains part of Luqmān’s advice to his son, reported by God. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report this possibility; al-Ālūsī attributes it to al-Baḥr and dismisses it. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 31:14/q01/p3 God speaks these words to Luqmān as an additional instruction to him. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report the view; al-Ālūsī reports and dismisses it. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 31:14/q02 To whom does the singular command concerning parents apply?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular refers to all people or the person named in the reported occasion.
- 31:14/q02/p1 It addresses human beings generally despite the singular form. — Ibn Kāmil states that its singular wording has universal force; al-Ālūsī says God enjoined people to care for their parents. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن كامل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:14/q02/p2 It specifically concerns Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ and his parents. — Muqātil names his father Mālik and mother Ḥamna bint Sufyān; al-Naqqāsh gives Mālik and Ḥamna daughter of Abū Sufyān ibn Umayya. These names vary in wording across the reports. The occasion reports are given separately below. [2 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش)]

## 31:14/q03 What is enjoined concerning the parents?  [وَوَصَّيْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ · interpretation]
- 31:14/q03/p1 God enjoins kindness, care, and dutifulness toward both parents. — Al-Suddī glosses the command as kindness; al-Ṭabarī, al-Ālūsī, and others express it as care and filial honor. Samarrāʾī adds that a helpless newborn would perish without parental care. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:14/q04 Why say walidayn rather than abawayn here?  [بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · rhetoric]
- 31:14/q04/p1 Walidayn evokes birth, especially the mother’s role, and suits the ensuing pregnancy and weaning account and her greater claim. — Samarrāʾī says both duals mean mother and father by masculine predominance, like al-qamarān for sun and moon. He contrasts Qur'anic duties and prayers using walidayn (17:23, 4:36, 6:151, 14:41, 71:28, 29:8, 46:15) with abawayn in inheritance, and notes the father’s burden is not described here. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:14/q05 What happened in the reported occasion of revelation?  [وَوَصَّيْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ · reports]
- 31:14/q05/p1 The passage was revealed concerning Saʿd when his mother threatened to starve unless he left Islam; he refused, and she eventually ate. — Muṣʿab says she refused food and drink, fainted, and her sons gave her water; Saʿd says relatives pried open her mouth for liquid. A variant says she went three days without food and Saʿd would remain Muslim even if she had a hundred lives. Reports connect 29:8 and 31:15; Abū Hubayra specifically assigns the following no-obedience command to him. Al-Qurṭubī prefers the Saʿd attribution, echoed as his preference by Abū Ḥayyān. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هبيرة,سعد بن أبي وقاص,سعد بن مالك,مصعب بن سعد); NAHHAS(سعد); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القرطبي); ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 31:14/q06 How far does obedience to parents extend?  [بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ · law]
turns on: How a parent's request weighs against religious duties and recommended worship.
- 31:14/q06/p1 Care and obedience do not extend to shirk, major sin, or neglect of an individual duty; obey in permissible matters and consider yielding in recommended matters. — Muqātil says Saʿd should refuse shirk yet treat his parents kindly and follow the Prophet. Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes forbidden, obligatory, permissible, and recommended acts. The counsel leads into 31:15. [2 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 31:14/q06/p2 A child should not obey a mother who prevents attendance at night prayer out of concern. — Al-Ḥasan supplies this as a disputed example within the broader obedience question. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 31:14/q07 Why is the mother given special care, and how much more than the father?  [حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُۥ وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ · interpretation]
- 31:14/q07/p1 The mother has a greater claim because she alone bears pregnancy, birth, nursing, sleepless care, and upbringing; reports give her three shares of care before the father’s one. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī infer three maternal measures and one paternal measure from the wording and the Prophet's repeated answer 'your mother' three times, then 'your father.' Al-Ālūsī calls the Bahz ibn Ḥakīm report sound. Ibn Kathīr cites 17:24 and recalls her nights awake; al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, and Kuran Yolu stress her unique sacrifices. Al-Ālūsī cites a poem about pregnancy, feeding and self-sacrifice and an Arab carrying his mother to pilgrimage; the latter report shows repayment remains small. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله); ELMALILI(Resûl-i Ekrem); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:14/q08 What does wahn upon wahn describe?  [وَهْنًا عَلَىٰ وَهْنٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the weakness belongs to the mother, the child, both, parental seed, or body and resolve; and whether the layers are continuous growth or distinct burdens.
- 31:14/q08/p1 The mother undergoes repeated or increasing weakness and hardship as pregnancy advances and through birth. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan and others vary the gloss between weakness, hardship, strain, burden and effort. Ibn ʿAbbās also mentions development upon development. Al-Ṭabarī cites Zuhayr's use of wahn for frailty; al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite Qanab ibn Umm Ṣāḥib's verse. Al-Zajjāj and later readers specify pregnancy, labor, delivery or postpartum recovery. Al-Jishumī cites 3:139. Al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī emphasize day-by-day increase. [41 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن وعطاء,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس والكلبي والسدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,الزجاج,الضحاك,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو مسلم,ابن عباس وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك وعاصم الجحدري); QURTUBI-FULL(قعنب بن أم صاحب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK,IK, Ṭs)]
- 31:14/q08/p2 The mother’s inherent or bodily weakness is compounded by pregnancy. — Abū Muslim and al-Qurṭubī report a naturally weak constitution becoming weaker in pregnancy; al-Biqāʿī likewise says inherent weakness increases. Bursevī includes weakness of constitution and character. [4 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 31:14/q08/p3 The child’s weakness is layered on the mother’s weakness. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Māwardī report Mujāhid's referent. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī include it among alternatives; al-Ālūsī says this avoids an objection concerning the child's decreasing weakness. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:14/q08/p4 It is the weakness of the father’s seed followed by the mother’s seed. — Ibn Baḥr is reported by al-Māwardī; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Ḥasan are reported by al-Ṭabrisī. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); TABRISI(الضحاك والحسن)]
- 31:14/q08/p5 It describes the child’s successive weak stages of development. — Abū Kāmil lists seed, clot, fetus, bones, infant, nursing child and weaned child; al-Samīn says this can make the word the object of 'carried'; Abū Ḥayyān mentions this alternative. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو كامل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 31:14/q08/p6 It combines bodily weakness with weakness of resolve. — Al-Māwardī proposes this as a further possible sense. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 31:14/q09 How is wahnan ʿalā wahn constructed grammatically?  [حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُۥ وَهْنًا عَلَىٰ وَهْنٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Which word governs the accusative and whether the circumstance belongs to mother or child.
- 31:14/q09/p1 Wahnan is a circumstantial accusative describing the mother as she carries the child. — Al-Zamakhsharī says she carried him while weakened. Al-Ṭabrisī takes it as a verbal noun standing for an omitted tahnu; ʿalā wahn can qualify it or attach to its verb. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow intensified noun or implied possessor constructions. Al-Ṭabrisī also construes the preceding carrying clause as circumstantial with implied qad, comparing 2:28. Al-Bayḍāwī and Elmalılı agree on maternal circumstance. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 31:14/q09/p2 Wahnan is a cognate or absolute object of an implied 'she weakened' because of pregnancy. — Al-Wāḥidī calls it an accusative verbal noun and says the pregnancy clause implies that she weakened; al-Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī allow an omitted verb or emphatic cognate object. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 31:14/q09/p3 An omitted preposition makes it 'in weakness,' and ʿalā wahn describes that weakness. — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers this analysis in al-Samīn; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Naḥḥās's second-object analysis after an omitted preposition. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 31:14/q09/p4 Wahnan is the object of 'carried,' describing the child through embryonic stages, or a circumstance of the child. — Al-Samīn contrasts it with the mother's circumstance; al-Ālūsī relates the child circumstance to Mujāhid's interpretation. Abū Ḥayyān calls it a developmental alternative. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 31:14/q10 How is wahn vocalized?  [وَهْنًا عَلَىٰ وَهْنٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Dialectal or morphological vocalization of the hāʾ; the notes say the sense is unchanged.
- 31:14/q10/p1 Both occurrences have a quiescent hāʾ, the majority form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān identify this as the majority reading; the alternate has the same meaning. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى الثقفي وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 31:14/q10/p2 Both occurrences have fatḥa on the hāʾ. — Reported from ʿĪsā al-Thaqafī and a transmission from Abū ʿAmr, also al-Ḍaḥḥāk and ʿĀṣim al-Jaḥdarī. Ibn Jinnī allows a verbal noun like waḍar and waḥar. Abū ʿAlī suggests guttural influence; Abū Ḥayyān mentions dialectal analogy and regular verbal noun; al-Ālūsī mentions the Kufan guttural pattern and notes al-Qāmūs does not restrict either noun form. Al-Zamakhsharī cites two conjugations; al-Qurṭubī notes multiple Arabic forms. Meaning is unchanged. [13 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو عمرو، عيسى الثقفي); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى الثقفي وأبو عمرو); TABRISI(أبو علي,عيسى الثقفي وبعضهم عن أبي عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك وعاصم الجحدري); QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى الثقفي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الثقفي وأبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(عيسى الثقفي)]

## 31:14/q11 Why insert the mother's bearing and weaning between the command and gratitude?  [حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُۥ وَهْنًا عَلَىٰ وَهْنٍۢ وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ · rhetoric]
- 31:14/q11/p1 The inserted account makes the mother's burden and special right vivid, motivating gratitude and kindness to both parents, especially her. — The author of al-Naẓm says the opening awaits its complement and the intervening pregnancy and nursing establish her claim. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Burhān and al-Ālūsī call it parenthetical emphasis; al-Bayḍāwī says the gratitude clause explains or motivates the charge. Al-Biqāʿī notes the child's dependence and the hardship of two years of sleep and waking, known fully only to God. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it prompts thanks to both parents. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الوسيط); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 31:14/q12 What does fiṣāl mean here?  [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether fiṣāl names the specific end of breastfeeding or the broader period of early maternal dependence.
- 31:14/q12/p1 It means weaning, the child’s separation from the breast and end of suckling. — Al-Wāḥidī defines the separation as no longer nursing; al-Jishumī derives the name from cutting off from the maternal breast; Bursevī compares a weaned camel calf, faṣīl. Ibn Jinnī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Ālūsī say faṣl is broader but fiṣāl particularly suits nursing. Al-Qurṭubī says both are dialect forms named by their endpoint. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الحسن، أبو رجاء الجحدري، قتادة، يعقوب); TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 31:14/q12/p2 It can denote the whole span from conception through gestation, birth and earliest dependence on the mother. — Asad reports this broader philological usage. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(philologists)]

## 31:14/q13 How is the weaning word read?  [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Form and vowels of the noun: fiṣāl, faṣl, or a fatḥa on fiṣāl's fāʾ.
- 31:14/q13/p1 The common reading is fiṣāluhu, with an alif. — Al-Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān identify it as the reading of the other or most readers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says its form points to the counted nursing duration and endpoint. Ibn Jinnī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Ālūsī find it more apt to this nursing context. [6 notes: JISHUMI(يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وأبو رجاء والجحدري ويعقوب); TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:14/q13/p2 It is read faṣluhu without an alif, meaning the child's separation or weaning. — Reports name al-Ḥasan, Abū Rajāʾ, al-Jaḥdarī, Qatāda, Yaʿqūb, Ubayy, Ṭalḥa b. Muṣarrif and others; the named transmitters vary. Ibn Jinnī and Abū ʿAlī note faṣl is broader than specific weaning. Ibn al-Jawzī specifies fatḥa on fāʾ and sukūn on ṣād; al-Ṭabrisī describes the fatḥa on ṣād in a reported pronunciation. Al-Bayḍāwī uses it in discussing the maximum nursing term. [13 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الحسن، أبو رجاء الجحدري، قتادة، يعقوب); JISHUMI(يعقوب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وأبو رجاء والجحدري ويعقوب); TABRISI(أبو علي,الحسن وأبو رجاء والجحدري وقتادة ويعقوب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب والحسن وأبو رجاء وطلحة بن مصرف وعاصم الجحدري وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري وقتادة وأبو رجاء); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 31:14/q13/p3 Fiṣālihi is read with a fatḥa on the fāʾ. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī, Abū ʿImrān and al-Aʿmash. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(إبراهيم النخعي وأبو عمران والأعمش)]

## 31:14/q14 Does 'in two years' require nursing for the whole period?  [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fī names the full endpoint alone or allows a weaning event within the two-year interval.
- 31:14/q14/p1 The expression describes weaning at the completion of two full years, the complete nursing term. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn Kathīr compare 2:233; al-Ṭabrisī explicitly says two full years. Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Bursevī say the endpoint is two years. Al-Damghānī compares 2:233's two complete ḥawls and 2:240's ḥawl as a year. Al-Biqāʿī notes hardship through those years; Study Quran says full breastfeeding is best when possible, comparing 2:233 and 46:15. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الوسيط); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:14/q14/p2 Two years is the normal or maximum period; parents may wean earlier when the child’s welfare warrants it. — Al-Biqāʿī draws this from the preposition fī; Kuran Yolu compares 2:233. Asad says the literal phrase describes weaning within two years. This position need not deny that two years completes the term. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 31:14/q15 How is 'his weaning is in two years' parsed?  [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether to supply an implied word of completion or state the surface subject-predicate relation.
- 31:14/q15/p1 An implied 'completion' means his weaning is at the completion of two years. — Al-Akhfash supplies the omitted completion; al-Wāḥidī explains the completed two-year sense. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش)]
- 31:14/q15/p2 The noun is subject and the prepositional phrase is predicate. — Al-Wāḥidī gives this construction and says it foregrounds two years of maternal nursing hardship. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 31:14/q16 Why use ʿāmayn for the two years?  [عَامَيْنِ · rhetoric]
- 31:14/q16/p1 ʿĀm can evoke ease or joy, unlike sana for hardship, reflecting parental joy even in demanding infant care. — Bursevī contrasts عام with سنة, the latter often used for hardship or drought; al-Biqāʿī says the parents rejoice in caring for the child despite the hard two years. Al-Damghānī relates the term to ḥawl as a year in 2:233 and 2:240. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 31:14/q17 What does the two-year term govern for nursing duties and maintenance?  [فِى عَامَيْنِ · law]
turns on: The notes distinguish the agreed maintenance period from stages of obligation, recommendation and permission.
- 31:14/q17/p1 Two years is the agreed nursing or maintenance period, and the father’s nursing wage ends then. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān state agreement on the period for maintenance or rulings while separating milk kinship. Bursevī says by agreement the father's nursing wage ceases after two years. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 31:14/q17/p2 Nursing is obligatory until the child is self-sufficient, recommended through two years, and permissible through two and a half. — Bursevī reports this framework with al-Shāfiʿī and says the remaining dispute is about the effect on milk kinship. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 31:14/q18 When does nursing establish milk kinship or its prohibition?  [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse fixes two years as a hard legal limit, permits adjacent days or six months, or whether actual weaning or age rather than the verse's term controls.
- 31:14/q18/p1 Only nursing within two years establishes milk kinship; even a moment afterward does not. — Al-Shāfiʿī uses the two-year limit and a Prophetic report; Yaḥyā cites a report that there is no nursing after weaning and that Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās did not count nursing after two years. Al-Ālūsī lists al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Abū Yūsuf, Muḥammad, al-Ṭaḥāwī and a report from Mālik. Elmalılı says the fatwa follows the two Imams and prefers it over Abū Ḥanīfa's precaution. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(الشافعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 31:14/q18/p2 Nursing for a few contiguous days or up to an adjacent month after two years still establishes milk kinship. — Al-Māwardī attributes a few extra days to Mālik; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes connected days; al-Qurṭubī mentions one adjacent month if nursing continues. The reports vary in precise extension. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مالك); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 31:14/q18/p3 The prohibition extends six months beyond two years, for thirty months total. — Abū Ḥanīfa relates the thirty months of pregnancy and weaning in 46:15 to each named period; al-Ālūsī gives his analogy of two debts due in a year or two obligations from one debtor. Elmalılı calls it precaution and says the fatwa instead follows the two Imams. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ELMALILI(İmâm-ı A‘zam); ELMALILI-]
- 31:14/q18/p4 The prohibition has no fixed age limit and applies to adults as to children. — Al-Māwardī reports this from some Medinans. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض أهل المدينة)]
- 31:14/q18/p5 Only nursing in the first year establishes milk kinship. — Al-Qurṭubī lists this among differing juristic opinions. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 31:14/q18/p6 Early weaning can end the effect of later milk, even before the two years expire. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī record disagreement on whether milk after early weaning still creates the prohibition. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 31:14/q19 What minimum pregnancy duration follows from the two-year nursing term?  [فِى عَامَيْنِ · law]
- 31:14/q19/p1 Six months is the minimum: thirty months for pregnancy and weaning in 46:15 minus the full twenty-four months of nursing. — Ibn ʿAbbās and other imams make the inference in Ibn Kathīr, comparing 46:15 with 2:233; Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Samarrāʾī state the same deduction, the latter attributing it to some jurists. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:14/q20 What maximum gestation does ʿĀʾisha's report give in this discussion?  [فِى عَامَيْنِ · reports]
- 31:14/q20/p1 A child is never in the womb longer than two years, even by a spindle coil’s time. — Al-Ālūsī reports ʿĀʾisha's statement as treated as transmitted from the Prophet; it shortens the gestation allocation while preserving an explicit nursing term, although al-Ālūsī says the argument needs discussion. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عائشة)]

## 31:14/q21 How does an ishkur relate grammatically to waṣṣaynā?  [أَنِ ٱشْكُرْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an explains the preceding command, supplies its governed verbal noun, or expresses its purpose or apposition.
- 31:14/q21/p1 An is explanatory: the command means 'We said, give thanks.' — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Wāḥidī's author of al-Naẓm, al-Nasafī, Elmalılı and Ṭabāṭabāʾī explain the charge this way. Al-Naḥḥās prefers this against the infinitival analysis; Abū Ḥayyān records that preference. Al-Ālūsī says the imperative does not bar this parse. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(النحاس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النحاس)+]
- 31:14/q21/p2 An introduces an accusative verbal-noun complement governed by waṣṣaynā: God enjoined giving thanks. — Al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Jawzī give the accusative parse; al-Samīn and al-Qurṭubī report it. Al-Ālūsī says an imperative does not invalidate the analysis. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:14/q21/p3 It expresses the command's purpose, or is an apposition to parents: care for them by gratitude. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows explanation, motivation or apposition; Bursevī allows purpose. Al-Ālūsī reconstructs li-taʿlīl or an apposition, calls the last remote, and answers the objection based on the imperative. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:14/q22 What form does commanded gratitude to God and parents take?  [ٱشْكُرْ لِى وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether to state the general obligation and its grounds or a concrete devotional expression; al-Ālūsī treats the latter as an example.
- 31:14/q22/p1 Thank God for creation, faith and His blessings through praise and obedience; thank parents for birth, raising and care through kindness, obedience, service, ties and prayer. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī name Islam or faith and parents' benefits; Ibn ʿAbbās glosses gratitude as obedience. Al-Ṭabarī adds care until the child's strength matures. Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and Study Quran contrast praise and obedience with kindness and kinship. Bursevī adds reverence toward God and compassion toward parents; Elmalılı adds service and prayer, referring to al-Isrāʾ. Al-Ālūsī records prayer and fasting toward God and kinship toward parents; al-Ālūsī says the five-prayer example is illustrative rather than exhaustive. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:14/q22/p2 Performing the five daily prayers thanks God, and supplicating for one's parents after them thanks the parents. — Sufyān b. ʿUyayna's explanation is repeated by al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī; al-Ālūsī says it illustrates some forms, not the entire duty. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عيينة); BURSEVI(سفيان بن عيينة); ALUSI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة)]

## 31:14/q23 Why is thanks to parents paired with thanks to God, and why is God named first?  [ٱشْكُرْ لِى وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · rhetoric]
- 31:14/q23/p1 God is the ultimate creator and giver; parents are appointed, proximate causes of birth and care, so gratitude to them follows gratitude to Him. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ distinguishes ultimate Cause and means, noting that absorption in one can eclipse the other. Al-Māwardī says Qatāda distinguishes God's right from theirs. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Asad and Kuran Yolu emphasize source versus parental means; Bursevī cites Sharḥ al-Ḥikam and the hadith 'who does not thank people does not thank God,' giving two readings: a condition of valid thanks, or God's reward limited to those who thank His servants. Al-Ālūsī says their gratitude depends on His and that this explains divine priority. Asad compares 17:23–24. [11 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî; Şevkânî); TABATABAI]

## 31:14/q24 What does the lām in ushkur lī do?  [ٱشْكُرْ لِى · grammar]
- 31:14/q24/p1 It strengthens the verb 'give thanks' and is conventionally attached to shukr. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the usage at 2:152; he adds that the present tense of yashkur in the related passage signals renewed gratitude. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:14/q25 What does 'to Me is the return' mean?  [إِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause is explained through resurrection and accounting or through God's sole sovereignty at the final state.
- 31:14/q25/p1 All return to God at resurrection for judgment and recompense for gratitude, ingratitude and deeds, with Paradise or Fire as the outcome. — Yaḥyā names Resurrection; al-Ṭabarī says judgment on thanks and filial kindness. Al-Māwardī specifies Paradise and Fire; Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil through al-Wāḥidī say each is repaid for deeds. Muʿādh's prophetic mission report in Ibn Kathīr identifies everlasting Paradise or Hell. Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı stress reward or questioning. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(معاذ بن جبل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 31:14/q25/p2 Returning to God is reaching the state where no judge or owner exists but Him. — Bursevī gives this further explanation of the return. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 31:14/q26 What role does the final return clause play in the parental counsel?  [إِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ · rhetoric]
- 31:14/q26/p1 It warns and gives the reason to obey the command, since God will reckon gratitude and disobedience. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call it an inserted warning; al-Ṭabrisī calls it a threat. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say return explains why obedience is required. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the shift of person accords with 31:12. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 31:14/q27 What reports link parental pleasure or neglect with the final reward?  [بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ إِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ · reports]
turns on: The cited reports give distinct formulations of the reward and consequence of filial conduct.
- 31:14/q27/p1 God's pleasure follows a parent's pleasure, and His anger follows a parent's anger; pleasing or angering parents bears on Paradise or Hell. — Yaḥyā reports the correspondence and gate imagery even when a child was wronged; al-Māwardī transmits the Prophetic saying through ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿUmar. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر)]
- 31:14/q27/p2 A servant who fulfills other obligations but neglects filial piety will not enter Paradise. — Al-Biqāʿī quotes al-Firdaws via Abū al-Dardāʾ, without grading, and connects filial honor to monotheism, prayer and remembrance. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]

## 31:14/q28 How can one honor parents after their death?  [وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · reports]
turns on: The reports prescribe different posthumous acts, one general and one fixed devotional sequence.
- 31:14/q28/p1 Keep ties with a deceased father's friends; prayer and charity for deceased parents can restore dutifulness after earlier neglect. — Bursevī transmits these practices as reports. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 31:14/q28/p2 A specified Thursday-night two-rakʿah prayer with repeated recitations and fifteen istighfārs dedicated to parents fulfills their right even after disobedience. — Bursevī cites reports from al-Iḥyāʾ and Qūt al-Qulūb for this distinct prescription. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 31:14/q29 How grave is disobedience to parents?  [بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ · reports]
- 31:14/q29/p1 It is among the worst major sins, paired with associating partners with God. — Al-Ḥasan reports a Prophetic scale in which good deeds culminate in giving one's life for God and sins culminate in parental disobedience. Study Quran cites the Prophet naming shirk and disrespect of parents among the greatest major sins. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]

## 31:14/q30 How does this injunction connect to other Qur'anic commands?  [* · links]
- 31:14/q30/p1 Kindness to parents is repeatedly paired with worship of God alone and follows the warning against shirk here. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it a further call to obey God after Luqmān's warning. Ibn Kathīr compares 17:23; Study Quran compares 4:36, 29:8, 46:15 and 17:23 and says the parental command first appears in the covenant of 2:83. Al-Biqāʿī says gratitude to God entails gratitude to people. Al-Samīn notes the construction was discussed at 29:8. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:14/q31 How is the nūn of an before the imperative pronounced?  [أَنِ ٱشْكُرْ · readings]
- 31:14/q31/p1 A reported form changes the nūn from kasra to ḍamma, anu, by phonetic assimilation before the imperative. — Qiraat-ER records and explains this pronunciation. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 31:14/q32 How do a teacher's benefits compare with a parent's in gratitude?  [وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · interpretation]
- 31:14/q32/p1 A teacher can deserve greater gratitude because a parent gives mortal life while a teacher guides toward lasting or higher life. — Bursevī cites Alexander's comparison of father bringing him from heaven to earth and tutor lifting him toward heaven; Burzjumhr similarly contrasts mortal and lasting life. [2 notes: BURSEVI(برزجمهر)]

## 31:14/q33 What does the human being's place in creation illustrate here?  [* · interpretation]
- 31:14/q33/p1 The human being is creation's fruit and kernel, combining both worlds and receiving verbal revelation. — Al-Biqāʿī reports al-Rāzī's tree image: the human is its fruit, kernel rather than rind, and completion. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الرازي)]

## 31:14/q34 What larger point does gratitude to God make in the adjoining Luqmān passage?  [* · theology]
- 31:14/q34/p1 Gratitude benefits and perfects the grateful human, while God is self-sufficient and already praised by creation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts human benefactors' possible reciprocal gain with God's lack of need; turning away is human ingratitude. He cites 13:15 for creation's praise, says Luqmān's wisdom begins immediate gratitude on receiving a blessing, quotes 'praise is the head of gratitude,' and notes ḥamīd is used because God has no name from the gratitude root except al-Shakūr. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
