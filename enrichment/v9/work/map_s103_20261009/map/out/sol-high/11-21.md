# 11:21: 6 questions

## 11:21/q01 How is 'those who lost themselves' constructed?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ · grammar]
- 11:21/q01/p1 It is a nominal sentence: 'those' is the subject and 'the ones who lost themselves' is the predicate. — Al-Qurṭubī identifies the subject and predicate explicitly. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 11:21/q02 In what sense did they lose themselves?  [خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'self' denotes forfeited salvation, the punished person, the choice exchanged, lost comfort, worldly gain, wasted disposition, or an entrusted self; al-Ālūsī explicitly prefers literal loss in continuing torment over an omitted modifier for comfort.
- 11:21/q02/p1 They cheated or deprived themselves of salvation and divine mercy. — Muqātil specifies salvation; al-Ṭabarī specifies their share of God's mercy and says their path leads to Hell; al-Suddī calls it cheating or depriving themselves; al-Baghawī also calls it cheating themselves. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي)]
- 11:21/q02/p2 Their deeds make them deserve destruction and punishment, ending in Hell; losing the self is the gravest loss. — Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, says it ends in Hell and that the self has no replacement. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī likewise give deserved punishment and the absence of any replacement as the reason this loss is greatest. Ibn Kathīr places the deniers in blazing Hell, citing 17:97. Al-Ālūsī prefers the literal wording: their continued existence in torment itself keeps them in loss. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 11:21/q02/p3 They exchanged worship of God for worship of idols. — Al-Rāzī calls this the greatest kind of loss. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī give the same exchange. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:21/q02/p4 They lose the happiness and comfort of their souls, although their persons remain to suffer punishment. — Abū Ḥayyān reads 'souls' as implying their comfort or happiness; al-Ālūsī reports this omitted modifier from al-Baḥr. Al-Ālūsī prefers taking the verse's wording literally instead. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 11:21/q02/p5 They traded guidance and the afterlife for error and this world, then lost the worldly gains obtained by the trade. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the enjoyment and authority gained through this exchange disappear. Al-Rāzī says the noble religion was traded for a base worldly gain that perishes, completing loss in both lives. Al-Bayḍāwī also gives loss of what they gained, leaving regret, as an alternative explanation. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:21/q02/p6 Their continued existence in torment is itself their literal loss. — Al-Ālūsī expressly prefers this account of the wording over supplying an omitted 'happiness and comfort' of their souls. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 11:21/q02/p7 They wasted the original disposition that readily follows good and resists evil. — Al-Biqāʿī identifies this original disposition as what their loss squandered. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 11:21/q02/p8 They squandered the selves entrusted to them by God through disbelief and sin. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains losing oneself as a metaphor grounded in God's entrusting each person with himself. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:21/q03 What does 'went astray from them' mean?  [وَضَلَّ عَنْهُم · meaning]
turns on: Whether ḍalla stresses disappearance and failure to help or the separation of worshippers from supposed partners onto different paths.
- 11:21/q03/p1 Their inventions disappeared or were lost, providing none of the help or benefit they had expected. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says ḍalla means lost or perished and not found where hoped. Al-Ṭabarī says supposed deities were only stone, wood, or copper, and no longer accompany worshippers. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ḥasan, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, and others describe failed hopes of benefit or intercession. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their imaginary inventions vanish when worldly life ends and truth appears. Ibn Kathīr adds that idols harm rather than help and disown their worshippers, citing 46:6, 19:81–82, 29:25, and 2:166. The Study Quran says the fabrications forsake them, mentioning 6:24, 6:54, 10:30, 16:87, and 28:75. Al-Ṭabarī also mentions 6:24. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:21/q03/p2 Their alleged partners part company with them and take a different path. — Al-Ṭabarī says the worshippers head to Hell, while a supposed deity that is God's ally may instead head to Paradise; their paths do not coincide. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 11:21/q04 When do the inventions go astray from them?  [وَضَلَّ عَنْهُم · interpretation]
- 11:21/q04/p1 At the Resurrection or in the afterlife, when worldly life has ended. — Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, specifies the Day of Resurrection. Al-Wāḥidī says their fabrications bring no benefit in the Afterlife. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says worldly life ends and the truth then becomes manifest. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABATABAI]

## 11:21/q05 What are the things they had fabricated?  [مَّا كَانُوا۟ يَفْتَرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the fabrication names supposed deities, lies and beliefs, imagined intercession, association of partners, or wider deceptive powers and half-truths; Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's lies and Qatāda's association of partners separately.
- 11:21/q05/p1 Their supposed gods, idols, or divine partners. — Muqātil and al-Ḥasan speak of fabricated gods or hoped-for idols. Al-Ṭabarī says they were actually stone, wood, or copper, not deities. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Asad, and The Study Quran also identify fabricated deities, idols, or partners; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls them imaginary. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:21/q05/p2 Their lies, fabricated claims, ideas, or religious beliefs. — Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, calls them lies told in worldly life. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī mention claims alongside gods. Asad adds deceptive half-truths and mentions 6:112; The Study Quran includes ideas and religious beliefs. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:21/q05/p3 The intercession they imagined from angels, idols, or supposed intercessors. — Al-Baghawī specifies invented intercession for angels and idols; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī also mention hoped-for or imagined intercession. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:21/q05/p4 Their association of partners with God. — Qatāda, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, gives this as an alternative to Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation in terms of worldly lies. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]
- 11:21/q05/p5 False powers and deceptive half-truths such as luck, wealth, personal power, nationalism, and materialism. — Asad says these obscure spiritual values and waste people's lives; he mentions 6:112. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 11:21/q06 How does the surrounding description bear on the loss announced here?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes supply distinct explanations of the surrounding passage rather than competing readings of one expression.
- 11:21/q06/p1 The description of the unjust forger shows that ignorance of God can lead to lying about Him. — Al-Ṭabrisī says this answers those who supposed that ignorance of God would do no harm, since none is more unjust than one who lies about Him. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 11:21/q06/p2 A poetic gloss describes culpable action as what the hands earned or committed. — Al-Māwardī reports a poetic example for the surrounding verb jarama, glossing it as what the hands earned or committed. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 11:21/q06/p3 The surrounding terms describe inability to prevent God's will, multiplied punishment, and bodily capacity. — Al-Biqāʿī defines inability to thwart as inability to prevent what God wills, doubling as increase by an equal or larger amount, and capacity as a power by which limbs act. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
