# 70:21: 16 questions

## 70:21/q01 What does al-khayr denote here?  [ٱلْخَيْرُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the wording is taken as material wealth, health, worldly standing, or good fortune broadly.
- 70:21/q01/p1 Wealth, provision, or material abundance. — Al-Wāḥidī identifies receiving wealth; Tustarī, Sahl, Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Baghawī, and others contrast poverty with wealth. Abū Ḥayyān explicitly glosses al-khayr as wealth. Al-Biqāʿī calls it provision. Al-Ālūsī includes abundance. Al-Samarrāʾī cites the wealthy person's contrasting behavior in health and illness. [17 notes: TUSTARI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(سهل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 70:21/q01/p2 Health. — Al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī pair illness with health as an alternative to poverty and wealth. Al-Ālūsī likewise allows health; al-Būrsawī says health can increase stinginess and commends charity while healthy. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu include health among blessings, and Study Quran reports illness versus health. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 70:21/q01/p3 Good or beneficial fortune generally. — Al-Rāzī gives poverty/wealth and illness/health as possibilities; Study Quran reports general misfortune versus good fortune as well as particular examples. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says people pursue what they perceive as beneficial. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 70:21/q01/p4 Status or security among worldly blessings. — Elmalılı names status alongside wealth and health; Kur'an Yolu names security alongside wealth and health. [2 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 70:21/q02 What does manūʿan say the person does?  [مَنُوعًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether withholding is expressed as refusing benefits and due rights or, in the ethical gloss, failing to thank God.
- 70:21/q02/p1 He intensely withholds the good he receives, hoarding it and refusing others their share, including God's due rights. — Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, and Study Quran specify God's due in wealth; Study Quran also names other people's rights and charity. Al-Nasafī says he hoards and denies others; Elmalılı adds that he forgets earlier distress and blocks good. Al-Būrsawī attributes the refusal to ignorance of divine apportionment and generosity's reward, adding that health can intensify stinginess and charity then is better. Al-Biqāʿī attributes it to love of immediate gain. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Sahl, and others describe withholding upon prosperity; al-Ālūsī calls it intense. Kur'an Yolu says blessings can be treated as personal achievement, with no spending for God or help to others. Al-Samarrāʾī cites Ibn Kathīr: blessing leads to denying others and God's due. [40 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); FARRA; MAJAZ; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(سهل,عطية عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثعلب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(ابن كثير,فتح القدير); STUDYQURAN(IK, JJ); BURHAN(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 70:21/q02/p2 In blessing, he fails to give thanks. — Abū ʿUbayda and Thaʿlab pair lack of thanks in prosperity with lack of patience in affliction. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة وثعلب)]

## 70:21/q03 How are manūʿan and its paired adjective construed grammatically?  [مَنُوعًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the two accusatives are circumstantial states, adjectives of halūʿ, predicates of an omitted verb, or accusatives by proximity.
- 70:21/q03/p1 The two words are circumstantial states of the pronoun in halūʿan, or all three words are circumstantial states. — Al-Samīn prefers overlapping states of the pronoun in halūʿan. Al-Ālūsī reports the analysis treating all three words as circumstantial; they may describe traits actual after maturity or present potentially from creation. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 70:21/q03/p2 The two words qualify halūʿan as adjectives or revealing descriptions. — Al-Naḥḥās allows adjectives of halūʿ; al-Ālūsī reports revealing descriptions while halūʿ itself is circumstantial. Makkī allows this but calls it distant because the time phrase would precede its adjective. Al-Samīn rejects that objection: an adverbial phrase can precede its governor; the forbidden order is moving an adjective's governed element before the adjective. [4 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]
- 70:21/q03/p3 An omitted kāna or ṣāra makes each adjective a predicate: he becomes distressed or withholding when touched. — Makkī supplies the omitted verb; al-Naḥḥās also allows the sense that he becomes so when harm or good touches him. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)]
- 70:21/q03/p4 The accusatives arise by proximity to halūʿan, rather than sharing its grammatical function. — Abū Muslim gives the proximity analysis as reported by al-Jishumī. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم)]

## 70:21/q04 What does each idhā govern in the paired clauses?  [وَإِذَا مَسَّهُ مَنُوعًا · grammar]
- 70:21/q04/p1 Each idhā time phrase governs the corresponding following description. — Al-Bayḍāwī says each idhā governs its subsequent quality; al-Ālūsī repeats that account. Al-Samīn relates the two time phrases to the respective circumstantial states. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 70:21/q05 What disposition does this clause help define as halūʿ?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether halūʿ is emphasized as fretful greed, as eagerness for perceived benefit, or as failures of patience and gratitude.
- 70:21/q05/p1 Extreme impatience and alarm in hardship, joined to greed and withholding in ease. — Al-Ḥasan says this is how humanity was created. Al-Farrāʾ derives the explanation from the pattern of jazaʿ; Abū ʿUbayda calls halāʿ the verbal noun for the worst impatience. Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Sahl, and others use the two reactions to explain it. Al-Rāzī includes intense acquisitiveness and little patience, citing al-Farrāʾ and al-Mubarrad's 'fretful' gloss and Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā's appeal to the next verses. Thaʿlab tells Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ṭāhir that God's explanation is clearest; Abū Ḥayyān reports the same exchange. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls halʿ anxious agitation amid fear and desire. Al-Samarrāʾī quotes Fatḥ al-Qadīr citing al-Ṣiḥāḥ for extreme greed and excessive panic, and Ibn Kathīr for terror and despair of future good under harm. Al-Iṣlāḥī says people even complain at slight divine punishment for their deeds. Abū al-ʿĀliya specifies despair when harm touches the person. Al-Samarrāʾī says giving to the poor counters both grief when wealth leaves and denial of the needy person's share. [23 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); JISHUMI(سهل,عطية عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(محمد بن عبد الله بن طاهر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثعلب); QASHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(ابن كثير,فتح القدير); BURHAN(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 70:21/q05/p2 Intense eagerness for what one perceives as good, whose misdirection produces panic and selfish withholding. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers 'intense eagerness'; self-love seeks what seems useful and resists harm, while limited judgment makes a person withhold benefits. Al-Rāzī's gloss also includes strong acquisitiveness. [5 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 70:21/q05/p3 Failure to be patient in affliction and grateful in blessing. — Abū ʿUbayda and Thaʿlab give this ethical explanation as reported by al-Jishumī. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة وثعلب)]

## 70:21/q06 Why do the two clauses follow the description halūʿ?  [* · rhetoric]
- 70:21/q06/p1 They explain and remove ambiguity from halūʿ, completing its sense. — Al-Burhān says explanatory clauses are common in the Qur'an. Al-Itqān cites the rhetoricians' definition of explanation as removing ambiguity and reports Abū al-ʿĀliya and others applying it here. Ibn Jinnī, cited in both works, says one should not pause before the explanatory clause because it completes the preceding expression like an adjective or relative clause. Thaʿlab's reply and Ibn ʿAbbās's glosses likewise appeal to the following clauses as God's own explanation. [17 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; JISHUMI(عطية عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(محمد بن عبد الله بن طاهر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثعلب); BURHAN(أبو العالية,ابن جني); ITQAN(أبو العالية وغيره,أهل البيان,ابن جني); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 70:21/q07 Whom does the human trait described here encompass?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether 'the human' is the species subject to an exception or specifically the unbeliever.
- 70:21/q07/p1 Human beings generally, with worshippers excepted by the following verses. — Al-Nasafī says 'the human' denotes the species, permitting the exception for those who pray. Al-Rāzī reports this general reading. Al-Ḥasan and several interpreters present the behavior as human temperament. Study Quran says those with the eight qualities listed in 70:22–35 escape the blameworthy state. Al-Ālūsī gives connected and disconnected readings of the following exception. [7 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 70:21/q07/p2 The disbeliever or associator. — Al-Ṭabarī takes the intensely anxious person to be the disbeliever; Ibn ʿAbbās specifies associators; al-Ḍaḥḥāk also says the disbeliever. Al-Rāzī reports this as one view. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 70:21/q08 Is the trait innate, and what makes its expression blameworthy?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether creation of the tendency is literal or figurative, and whether the initial tendency or its accountable expression is morally at issue.
- 70:21/q08/p1 A disposition can be created in humans, while blame attaches to chosen conduct after accountability. — Al-Qāḍī says God cannot blame His own act and believers can struggle against the trait. Al-Rāzī distinguishes involuntary inward anxiety created by God from speech and conduct that people can restrain. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say the qualities can be actual after maturity or potential from creation; al-Ālūsī affirms God can create the disposition and points to infants panicking at hunger and pain. He says accountable expression, rather than creation itself, is blamed. Ibn Kaysān says people were made to love pleasure and flee pain, then commanded to give what they love and endure what they dislike. Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses the wording rebukes hoarding rather than excusing it. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); RAZI-FULL(القاضي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:21/q08/p2 The wording is figurative: panic and withholding are so entrenched that the person seems born with them. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this reading to al-Zamakhsharī. Abū Ḥayyān likewise says the traits are so entrenched that humanity is described as created bearing them, comparing 21:37. Al-Ālūsī objects that infants visibly panic at hunger and pain, and separates creation from culpability. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 70:21/q08/p3 Innate self-love and eagerness are useful; excess, deficient judgment, or surrender to appetite turn them into vice. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says eagerness enables pursuit of happiness. Children first follow instinctive ideas of good and resist sharing; reason later corrects judgments. Continued appetite can reject truth and others' rights. Ignoring reason turns a God-given means of happiness into misery and hoarding. Al-Qāshānī instead describes attachment to the ego's dark nature as producing cowardice and greed. [6 notes: TABATABAI; QASHANI]

## 70:21/q09 Can the human tendency to withhold be overcome?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether discipline removes a trait, restrains its effects, changes only incidental traits, or redirects the person's aims.
- 70:21/q09/p1 Discipline can remove natural traits. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as one scholarly view. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 70:21/q09/p2 Traits remain, but people can suppress their visible effects and follow revealed commands. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative. Al-Nasafī says people are ordered to oppose their temperament and follow revealed law; al-Qāḍī says believers can struggle against the disposition. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 70:21/q09/p3 Traits rooted in basic temperament remain, while traits from incidental conditions can change. — Al-Ālūsī reports this further distinction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 70:21/q09/p4 Reason and worship can redirect eagerness toward real good and generosity. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says mature reason turns eagerness toward fear of sin and haste to forgiveness, and prescribed patience governs worldly hardship and duty. Al-Samarrāʾī says persistent prayer gives God one's time and self and frees one from immediate desires, even when worship costs opportunity or offers no worldly gain; he cites 62:11. Ibn Kaysān says God commanded people to give what they love and patiently bear what they dislike. Al-Samarrāʾī says giving to the poor cures both reactions: the donor does not grieve as though afflicted when wealth leaves and does not withhold the needy person's share. [6 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان)]

## 70:21/q10 What conduct does the verse censure and call the person to correct?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which failure is foregrounded: withheld dues, short-sighted attachment to worldly conditions, or lack of gratitude and patience.
- 70:21/q10/p1 Withholding required rights and charity from blessings, instead of using them for God and others. — Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, and Study Quran name God's or others' rights in wealth. Al-Rāzī and Study Quran say health and wealth should be used for gain in the Hereafter. Kur'an Yolu says people may claim blessings as their own achievement and fail to spend or help. Al-Būrsawī says ignorance of divine apportionment and reward fuels withholding, and generosity during health is better. Al-Samarrāʾī presents giving to the poor as the corrective act that prevents withholding the needy person's share. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, JJ,R); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:21/q10/p2 Fixation on immediate bodily gain and fear of loss, instead of patient acceptance of God's decree. — Al-Rāzī says seeking relief is not itself wrong: the fault is caring only about immediate poverty, illness, wealth, or health, rather than accepting decree and seeking the afterlife. Ibn ʿĀshūr says fear of losing benefits explains the trait but does not excuse hoarding. Al-Biqāʿī says love of immediate gain narrows sight and opposes faith; Elmalılı adds quick forgetfulness of past distress. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:21/q10/p3 Ingratitude in blessing and impatience at affliction. — Abū ʿUbayda and Thaʿlab state the paired ethical failures; Ibn Kaysān sets giving beloved things and bearing disliked things against humanity's natural attraction and aversion. Al-Iṣlāḥī says people complain even at slight punishment for their deeds. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة وثعلب); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 70:21/q11 Why is harm mentioned before good in the paired description?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order is explained chiefly by the sūra's punishment setting or by preparation for the withholding clause.
- 70:21/q11/p1 Harm first fits the sūra's preceding punishment scenes and the command to be patient. — Al-Samarrāʾī cites the opening demand for punishment (70:1), the criminal's wish to ransom himself (70:11), and the blaze tearing skin (70:15–16). He also contrasts human panic with the Prophet's command to beautiful patience (70:5). [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:21/q11/p2 The harm clause prepares and completes the main point about withholding upon good. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the main point is withholding in prosperity, and distress in harm supplies its paired preparation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 70:21/q12 How does the trait here connect to earlier hoarding and the fire?  [* · links]
- 70:21/q12/p1 It supplies the human motive behind accumulating and retaining goods, and thus the conduct called by the fire. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says verses 19–21 interrupt the line from the hoarder to the worshippers' exception, summarizing the greed behind retention; the wording rebukes rather than excuses it. Elmalılı says the fire calls one who turns from right and good, gathers possessions, and stores them. Al-Samarrāʾī places the description after the criminal's attempted ransom and the blaze. [4 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 70:21/q13 How are the worshippers in the following verses related to this trait?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the following exception is connected to humanity's disposition or a disconnected contrast with those who turned away.
- 70:21/q13/p1 They are a connected exception: believers do not remain trapped in this human tendency. — Al-Nasafī says the generic 'human' allows worshippers to be excepted. Al-Ālūsī reports a connected exception by meaning: believers did not persist in panic; he says the following words about worshippers convey the exception. Study Quran says the eight qualities of 70:22–35 exempt their possessors. Al-Samarrāʾī explains that persistent prayer trains a person to give God time and self and resist immediate desires, citing 62:11. Al-Samarrāʾī connects giving to the poor in 70:24–25 with curing both sides of halūʿ: grief at losing wealth and denial of the needy person's share. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:21/q13/p2 The exception is disconnected, contrasting worshippers in gardens with those who turned away. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative account of the following exception. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 70:21/q14 What other Qur'anic passages are compared with this description?  [* · links]
turns on: Which comparison passage and feature of the comparison is in view.
- 70:21/q14/p1 Qur'an 41:49–51 echoes repeated requests for good, despair under harm, and withdrawal after mercy. — Study Quran makes this comparison. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 70:21/q14/p2 Qur'an 21:37 parallels the description of a deeply rooted human trait. — Abū Ḥayyān compares the wording that portrays anxiety and withholding as if created in humankind with 21:37. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 70:21/q14/p3 The descriptions of believers in al-Muʾminūn and al-Maʿārij strongly parallel each other but differ in wording and order. — Al-Samarrāʾī observes this across 23:1–11 and the present sūra's later descriptions. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 70:21/q15 What Prophetic saying is cited about the paired traits?  [* · reports]
- 70:21/q15/p1 The worst traits in a person are anxious or consuming greed and paralyzing or ruinous cowardice. — Al-Qāshānī reports the saying; Ibn Kathīr transmits it through Abū Hurayra and notes Abū Dāwūd gives the same report by that route. Ibn ʿAṭiyya begins a comparison of anxious agitation with a Prophetic saying. [3 notes: QASHANI(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 70:21/q16 What poetic citations appear alongside the comparison to manūʿan?  [مَنُوعًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the citation is a direct lexical parallel or other poetry in the same source passage.
- 70:21/q16/p1 Ibn al-Rūmī uses mamnūʿan manūʿan for a beloved who is both forbidden and withholding. — Al-Jurjānī supplies this direct poetic parallel to the verse's manūʿan. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(ابن الرومي)]
- 70:21/q16/p2 Al-Jurjānī's surrounding citations illustrate other poetic formulations and scenes. — These are adjacent citations, without a stated interpretation of this verse. Al-Buḥturī withholds credit for Abū al-ʿAlā until others grant it (r1), and depicts a beloved whose offered union still feels withheld (r10); Abū Tammām calls a good request a fault and blames bad fortune (r12), and al-Buḥturī turns merits into sins (r13). Khālid al-Kātib and Bashshār describe endless lovers' nights (r2–3). Abū Tammām, al-Buḥturī, and Muslim depict fear and submission spreading across eastern and western lands (r4–6). Muḥammad ibn Bashīr requests help from a busy person lest a free one be perpetually asked (r7). Al-Jurjānī cites Abū ʿAlī al-Baṣīr's image of Saʿīd worn like a rope and the warning that hopes cling to him even when busy (r8–9). Abū Tammām and al-Buḥturī picture a donkey driven by fear toward a blood-clawed lion (r14–15). Maʿn ibn Aws and al-ʿAbbās ibn al-Aḥnaf depict a heart or soul scarcely turning back once it turns away (r16–17). [16 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(أبو تمام,البحتري,العباس بن الأحنف,بشار,خالد الكاتب,محمد بن بشير,مسلم,معن بن أوس)]
