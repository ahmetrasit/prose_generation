# 28:29: 42 questions

## 28:29/q01 What does qaḍā al-ajal say Moses completed?  [قَضَىٰ ٱلْأَجَلَ · meaning]
- 28:29/q01/p1 He fulfilled the agreed period of service and its work. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies the agreement with the father-in-law who gave his daughter in marriage; al-Biqāʿī says Moses fulfilled all promised work. Al-Ālūsī treats the opening fāʾ as resuming the sequence of contract, work, completion, and departure. Al-Dāmghānī gives completion of a term as one sense of qaḍā, alongside completion of revelation. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; DAMGHANI]

## 28:29/q02 How long did Moses fulfill or remain after his contract?  [ٱلْأَجَلَ · reports]
turns on: The reports give ten or twenty years; some authors judge the extra decade weak, while others stress that the verse itself does not specify eight versus ten.
- 28:29/q02/p1 He fulfilled the longer, better term of ten years. — Ibn ʿAbbās argues prophets keep promises, eight years were binding, and God fulfills the fuller promise; Saʿīd's Jewish questioner accepted this answer. Reports attribute the answer to the Prophet directly, via Gabriel or a higher angel, or to God after successive consultations. Al-Ṭabarī's reports variously call it the last, fullest, most virtuous, or most honored term. Al-Ālūsī cites Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bukhārī transmissions; Elmalılı reasons a noble person does not leave a promise incomplete. [35 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد,محمد بن كعب القرظي); ZAJJAJ(يروى); IBNABIHATIM(النبي ﷺ، أبو ذر,النبي ﷺ، ابن عباس,النبي ﷺ، جبريل); THALABI(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمران الجوني,القرظي,سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(أبو ذر,ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(أبو ذر رضي الله عنه); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:29/q02/p2 He served ten years, then remained a further ten, leaving after twenty. — Mujāhid supplies the additional decade in numerous transmissions. Al-Bayḍāwī reports it with his summary; al-Biqāʿī infers some interval because 'and he traveled' is coordinated rather than a direct result. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the twenty-year report weak, as do Abū Ḥayyān and his source cited by al-Qurṭubī; Ibn Kathīr has not found other support beyond Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Abī Ḥātim. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI(bazıları); STUDYQURAN(Q, R); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 28:29/q02/p3 He stayed additional unspecified years after taking his wife. — Wahb ibn Munabbih reports years beyond the marriage without giving a number. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه)]
- 28:29/q02/p4 The verse and Torah do not settle whether the service was eight or ten years. — Islāḥī states both texts leave the term unspecified; Kuran Yolu likewise distinguishes the Qur'an's silence from exegetical ten-year reports. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]

## 28:29/q03 What happened to the flock given to Moses?  [قَضَىٰ ٱلْأَجَلَ · reports]
- 28:29/q03/p1 A promised share of differently colored newborn lambs multiplied after Moses used a device at the water. — Anas describes a reflection at the water and nearly all lambs born piebald. The report through ʿUtba says Moses served for chastity and food, placed his staff at the trough, and received multiplied varied-color newborns. Ibn Lahiʿa also defines five livestock types named in this account: abundant-milk fashūsh, long-udder tanūb, low-yield ʿazūz, two-nub thaʿūl, and small-udder kamsha. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(أنس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن لهيعة,النبي ﷺ، عتبة بن الندر)]

## 28:29/q04 Which daughter did Moses marry?  [بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ · reports]
turns on: Conflicting reports identify the bride as elder or younger; one source conditions following the Prophetic younger-daughter report on its soundness.
- 28:29/q04/p1 He married the younger daughter. — Reports attribute this to the Prophet; al-Thaʿlabī says the report must be followed if sound. Al-Wāḥidī transmits Gabriel's instruction to say it; al-Ṭabrisī identifies her as the one who urged her father to hire Moses. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī note conflict with another account. [5 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمران الجوني); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(أبو ذر)]
- 28:29/q04/p2 He married the elder daughter. — Wahb supplies this report. Abū Ḥayyān notes exegetical disagreement also over her name. [2 notes: THALABI(وهب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 28:29/q05 Where did Moses travel after the term?  [وَسَارَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the destination was Egypt to revisit kin or Jerusalem/Syria; Elmalılı cites 28:33 against Egypt, while al-Ālūsī finds Egypt likelier.
- 28:29/q05/p1 He headed toward Egypt, his homeland and relatives. — Al-Ṭabarī calls Egypt his home; al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān likewise identify it. Mujāhid says he sought permission to visit his mother and brother. Ibn Kathīr adds longing for family and secret travel because of Pharaoh. Al-Ālūsī says Moses thought the old affair forgotten and judges Jerusalem less likely; Elmalılı objects that Egypt conflicts with Moses' earlier fear of being killed at 28:33. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(bazıları); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:29/q05/p2 He headed toward Syria or Jerusalem. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives Syria in the narrative ellipsis and Abū Baṣīr reports Jerusalem. Elmalılı prefers Jerusalem as safer and more fitting; al-Ālūsī reports it but regards it as less likely. Islāḥī describes the mount destination without fixing Egypt. [4 notes: TABRISI(أبو بصير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI(bazıları)+; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 28:29/q06 What does sāra imply?  [وَسَارَ · meaning]
- 28:29/q06/p1 It means to travel through the land, especially sustained movement toward a purpose or destination. — Al-Dāmghānī groups uses at 40:21 and 6:11; Būrsawī defines motion through land. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes purposive extended travel from mere walking, citing 6:11 and 25:63. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:29/q07 Who accompanied Moses as his family?  [بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ · referent]
turns on: The plural household is variously expanded beyond the wife, and accounts disagree when a child was born.
- 28:29/q07/p1 His wife accompanied him. — Al-Māwardī states the minimal identification; other reports add companions. Kashīfī, as cited by Būrsawī, treats the bāʾ as causative, 'he took his family.' [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 28:29/q07/p2 His wife and child or children accompanied him. — Muqātil adds a flock. Kashf al-Asrār names his wife Ṣafūriyya and a child born before departure. Al-Ālūsī reports two sons, Gershom and Eliezer, requiring extra years if marriage was consummated only after the contract. Islāḥī says the Torah gives wife and two children. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:29/q07/p3 His wife and a servant accompanied him. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as one identification of the household. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:29/q07/p4 A child was born during the journey on the night of the fire. — Wahb supplies this alternative timing of the child's birth. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(وهب)]

## 28:29/q08 What hardships marked the journey and sighting?  [وَسَارَ ءَانَسَ · reports]
- 28:29/q08/p1 They traveled on a dark, cold winter night, lost the route, and had trouble making fire. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī say they lost the road; Mujāhid says Moses avoided Syrian rulers, his wife was pregnant or in labor, and his flint failed. Al-Naqqāsh adds Friday, a divine light in a tree, lost fire, and severe cold. Other accounts add rain, snow, scattered sheep, alternating caravan travel, and a remote flame; some place it on the mount's western right side. Al-Biqāʿī infers winter from their need to warm themselves. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); TABRISI(أبو بصير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirler); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 28:29/q09 What happened to the family after Moses left them?  [بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ sharply on the duration and aftermath of the waiting.
- 28:29/q09/p1 They waited while Moses went to the fire. — Būrsawī says Moses left them and went to the fire before the call came from the right bank. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 28:29/q09/p2 His wife, child, and flock were left in the wilderness for thirty years. — Muqātil says a shepherd eventually found her and returned her to her father. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 28:29/q10 What does al-Ṭūr refer to?  [ٱلطُّورِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the term is treated as the specific Sinai mount or a general mountain word in Arabic.
- 28:29/q10/p1 It is Mount Sinai, a particular known mountain. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya locates it in Syria and says ṭūr may mean any mountain, though some restrict it to a bare mountain. Asad consistently translates it Mount Sinai because he takes the Qur'anic term to refer invariably to it. Būrsawī identifies the definite form as a particular mountain. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; IBNATIYYA-FULL; YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 28:29/q10/p2 It means a mountain generally, with a possible restriction to one without vegetation. — Yaḥyā glosses ṭūr simply as mountain; Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the broader usage and the bare-mountain qualification. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 28:29/q11 Where was the fire in relation to Moses and the mount?  [مِن جَانِبِ ٱلطُّورِ · referent]
- 28:29/q11/p1 It appeared from the mount's side, described as the right or western side from Moses' viewpoint. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Būrsawī take jānib as the side facing him. Ibn Kathīr places it to Moses' right when facing the qiblah, on the western side near the valley's green tree. Mujāhid's report likewise calls it the western right side. Būrsawī locates the subsequent call at the right bank relative to Moses, a watercourse between mountains. [6 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 28:29/q12 What does ānasa mean here?  [ءَانَسَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether īnās is simple visual perception or carries familiarity and comfort.
- 28:29/q12/p1 He perceived or saw the fire, especially by sight. — Al-Ṭabarī cites al-Aʿshāʿj's line about spotting a bird near the mountain; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adduces 4:6 and Ḥassān's line. Al-Qurṭubī cites al-Azharī's idiom of looking for anyone present and al-Nābigha's watchful bull. Al-Ālūsī says perception is the broader original sense and reports al-Zamakhsharī's clear, unambiguous sight; al-Māwardī includes knowing. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:29/q12/p2 He perceived something familiar, intimate, or reassuring. — Al-Jishumī stresses reassurance. Būrsawī says the light cheered him; Elmalılı links a humanly intimate perception to recognizing maturity at 4:6. Al-Ālūsī reports this as a distinct nuance. Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir reads it inwardly as familiarity with the pure site and divine conversation. [5 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر)]

## 28:29/q13 What was the fire Moses saw?  [نَارًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the apparent fire was light, fire, or fire with a reality beyond ordinary perception.
- 28:29/q13/p1 It was divine light appearing to Moses as fire. — Qatāda says it only seemed fire; Ibn ʿAbbās calls it God's light. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and Jaʿfar describe prophetic and sacred lights. Būrsawī says light appeared dressed as fire to meet Moses' wintry need, comparing Gabriel's familiar appearance as Diḥya. Al-Ālūsī reports mystics calling it real light misnamed fire; al-Qushayrī says God showed fire, then light, to lead Moses to hearing Him. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); SULAMI(ابن عطاء,جعفر); QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:29/q13/p2 It appeared as actual fire on the mountain, though its reality exceeded ordinary reason. — Al-Suddī and other narrative reports describe seeing fire on the mount. Al-Ālūsī transmits a mystical view that its appearance was actual fire while its reality surpassed reason. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:29/q14 What tree is associated with the fire and call?  [نَارًۭا · reports]
turns on: Reports name different tree species; Ibn Kathīr grades the samurah report's chain fair.
- 28:29/q14/p1 It was an acacia or samurah tree. — Ibn Masʿūd calls it a fresh green samurah; Ibn Kathīr rates that chain fair. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also transmits an acacia identification from Ibn Masʿūd. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب وقتادة وابن مسعود)]
- 28:29/q14/p2 It was an oleander. — Wahb is named for this identification by Ibn ʿAṭiyya. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب وقتادة وابن مسعود)]
- 28:29/q14/p3 It was hawthorn or azarole. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes hawthorn to Qatāda and notes another report of azarole. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب وقتادة وابن مسعود)]
- 28:29/q14/p4 It was an ʿullayq or ʿawsaj bush. — Ibn Kathīr transmits ʿullayq from Wahb and ʿawsaj from some People of the Book. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 28:29/q15 What does the indefinite nāran suggest?  [نَارًۭا · rhetoric]
- 28:29/q15/p1 It signals a strange, wondrous or unlikely fire. — Elmalılı reads wonder in the indefinite tanwīn; al-Biqāʿī says Moses' emphatic claim of seeing fire responds to the improbability of a flame in a remote cold place. [2 notes: ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:29/q16 Who says 'wait' and what does amkuthū mean?  [قَالَ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · interpretation]
- 28:29/q16/p1 Moses told his family to remain in place while he went toward the fire. — Būrsawī explicitly identifies Moses as speaker and glosses amkuthū as staying and waiting. [3 notes: BURSEVI; TAB-FULL]

## 28:29/q17 Why is the command to the family plural and masculine?  [لِأَهْلِهِ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · grammar]
- 28:29/q17/p1 The plural indicates several companions; masculine agreement follows precedence or includes sons. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the plural establishes more than one companion. Al-Biqāʿī invokes grammatical masculine precedence, possible sons, and the need to shelter women. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:29/q18 What news did Moses hope to obtain?  [بِخَبَرٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether khabar concerns the lost route or whether the fire itself offers welcome or danger.
- 28:29/q18/p1 Directions or information about the road and destination. — Al-Suddī, Ibn ʿAbbās and many commentators say the party was lost and Moses expected people by the fire. Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī specify whether the route was straight or diverted; Būrsawī specifies Egypt. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses the parallel 20:10 as stronger evidence. Asad treats 'about which route to take' as ellipsis; al-Zajjāj says he wanted to know why the fire was lit. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:29/q18/p2 He sought to learn whether the fire signaled comfort or danger. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī give this as an alternative to route information. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 28:29/q19 Why call the hoped-for information khabar?  [بِخَبَرٍ · meaning]
- 28:29/q19/p1 Khabar is ordinary news; nabaʾ carries greater importance and benefit. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī's linguists distinguish the two; he derives nabaʾ from emergence and observes singular khabar twice in Moses' story, against nabaʾ for weighty accounts of earlier peoples and messengers at 27:22, 38:67, 78:2, 64:5, 14:9, 10:71, and 11:120. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(أهل اللغة)]

## 28:29/q20 What does jadhwah denote?  [جَذْوَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical range runs from a thick log without flame through burning wood to flame or ember itself; poetry and reports support different restrictions.
- 28:29/q20/p1 A substantial piece of wood or log, with or without fire. — Abū ʿUbayda compares jadhwah with jadhmah, a thick log; al-Zajjāj and al-Mubarrad agree. Ibn Muqbil's jadhā and Kuthayyir's verse attest large firewood; al-Samīn notes the poem about nonflaring logs but also a poem describing a blaze. Ibn ʿAṭiyya suggests roots used as desert fuel, citing al-Sulamī's smoking-roots line. Al-Rāghib and al-Mufradāt describe wood left after burning; the plural appears as jadhā or judhiyy. Al-Kashshāf allows fire at its end or none, while this context calls for a usable burning piece. [28 notes: MAJAZ(ابن مقبل); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,ابن مقبل,المبرد والزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL(كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مقبل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,ابن مقبل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ابن مقبل); BAYDAWI-FULL(عاصم وحمزة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن مقبل,السلمي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); JISHUMI]
- 28:29/q20/p2 A piece, stump, root or branch of wood containing fire, sometimes without a flame. — Qatāda specifies a tree base with fire at the tip, Mujāhid its base, al-Kalbī a partly burned stick, and Ibn Zayd fire within wood. Reports vary between burning end, partly burned stick, glowing coal and ember. Al-Samīn says this context requires a burning piece; al-Ālūsī says min al-nār clarifies it. Qatāda links it to winter warmth. [38 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,مجاهد); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(قتادة ومقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,ابن قتيبة,قتادة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري,مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,ابن زيد,قتادة); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قيل)]
- 28:29/q20/p3 A flame, meteor, or glowing firebrand itself. — Al-Kalbī speaks of a flame; Ibn ʿAbbās uses meteor, ember, or flaming brand in different reports. Al-Kashshāf cites an anonymous poem of a blazing brand. Al-Samarrāʾī calls it an ember or piece of fire; Elmalılı renders it Turkish 'eksi'. [16 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(شاعر غير مسمى); TABRISI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:29/q21 How is the jīm of jadhwah read?  [جَذْوَةٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Three Arabic dialect vowelings of the first radical; kasra is most widespread and explicitly preferred by two lexicographic commentators.
- 28:29/q21/p1 With kasra, jidhwah. — Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and al-Kisāʾī are named among its readers; al-Ṭabarī prefers the best-known Ḥijāzī, Baṣran and some Kūfan form without rejecting fatḥa. Ibn Khālawayh calls kasra most eloquent. The forms are dialectal, and Qiraat-ER notes a slight stump nuance. [14 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI(عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والأعمش وعاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; TAB-FULL+]
- 28:29/q21/p2 With fatḥa, jadhwah. — ʿĀṣim is the main named reader; al-Qurṭubī also names al-Sulamī and Zirr ibn Ḥubaysh, and Abū Ḥayyān excepts al-Jaʿfī from ʿĀṣim. Al-Ṭabarī accepts the less common form. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI(عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والأعمش وعاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(عاصم وحمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(عاصم)]
- 28:29/q21/p3 With ḍamma, judhwah. — Ḥamza is the main named reader, with al-Aʿmash, Ṭalḥa and Abū Ḥaywa also reported; Ibn al-Jawzī adds Khalaf and al-Walīd from Ibn ʿĀmir, while al-Qurṭubī names Yaḥyā. Qiraat-ER notes dictionaries attest all three vowels. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الأعمش); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI(عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والأعمش وعاصم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(عاصم وحمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:29/q22 How does min al-nār attach and function?  [مِّنَ ٱلنَّارِ · grammar]
- 28:29/q22/p1 It qualifies jadhwah and marks the source of the burning piece. — Al-Samīn attaches the phrase to jadhwah rather than ātīkum, saying this fire is not simply the first-mentioned fire and Arabic may repeat a noun with a pronoun or definite article. Al-Ālūsī, citing Qatāda's burning tree base, takes min as source from the fire Moses had noticed. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]

## 28:29/q23 What does taṣṭalūn mean?  [تَصْطَلُونَ · meaning]
- 28:29/q23/p1 To warm yourselves or shelter from cold by fire. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya traces it to ṣalā, the heat of fire, with tāʾ changed to ṭāʾ. Būrsawī notes variant forms of the noun ṣalāʾ. Reports tie the purpose to the winter cold; al-Biqāʿī says they might draw near the fire. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:29/q24 What would Moses do if he found no news?  [بِخَبَرٍ أَوْ جَذْوَةٍۢ · interpretation]
- 28:29/q24/p1 He would bring back a burning branch or ember for warmth. — Ibn ʿAbbās sets the firebrand as the fallback if no information emerged. Al-Ṭabarī compares 20:10; other commentators stress the two immediate needs, guidance and warmth. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 28:29/q25 Why does Moses say laʿallī and laʿallakum?  [لَعَلِّىٓ لَعَلَّكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 28:29/q25/p1 They express hope and humility amid uncertainty. — Al-Biqāʿī relates tentative speech to the sūra's theme of humility; al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts hopeful laʿallī in al-Qaṣaṣ with future sīn and resolve in al-Naml, saying circumstances may let speakers move between commitment and hope without contradiction. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:29/q26 How does this wording compare with the fire scene in al-Naml?  [لَعَلِّىٓ ءَاتِيكُم جَذْوَةٍۢ · links]
- 28:29/q26/p1 Al-Qaṣaṣ uses hope, one 'come,' and a possible ember to fit a fearful scene; al-Naml uses certainty, repeated coming, and a bright flame for strength. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī links al-Qaṣaṣ's fear back before Moses' birth and al-Naml's future sīn and repetition to empowerment; atā occurs twelve times in al-Naml and six in al-Qaṣaṣ. A shihāb is a bright flame, qabas a flame taken from a larger fire and also a root for acquiring knowledge; it brings more light and warmth than a jadhwah, which may lack flame. His lecture notes say al-Qaṣaṣ only promises a possible ember, not expressly taking it from the fire, and that each wording accurately suits its context. [14 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:29/q27 What other textual links are made to this scene?  [* · links]
- 28:29/q27/p1 The fire episode recurs or is explained at 20:9–10, 27:7–8 and elsewhere. — Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān and Asad point to earlier discussions; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses 20:10 for guidance. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Qur'an adds the mountain fire to Exodus's journey account. Study Quran also names 26:10; its introduction continues from the fire to God's speech, miracles, and Aaron's help at 28:30–35. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:29/q27/p2 Moses' promise of news echoes the sūra's closing promise that God will show signs. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī sees a deliberate verbal echo between saʾātīkum and sayurīkum at 28:93. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 28:29/q28 What reading variants occur in the nearby pronouns?  [لِأَهْلِهِ إِنِّىٓ لَعَلِّىٓ · readings]
turns on: Distinct phonetic vowel variants of the suffix and possessive yāʾ.
- 28:29/q28/p1 The suffix of li-ahlihi has ḍamma instead of kasra in one reading. — Ibn Mujāhid names Ḥamza and Ibn Saʿdān from Isḥāq al-Musayyibī for both 20:10 and 28:29; the rest have kasra. Qiraat-ER connects the shift to the following imperative's hamzat al-waṣl and says it does not change meaning. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، ابن سعدان عن إسحق المسيبي); QIRAAT-ER]
- 28:29/q28/p2 The first-person possessive yāʾ is opened with fatḥa in innīya or laʿalliya. — Qiraat-ER calls both prosodic variants without semantic change. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:29/q29 What legal point is drawn from traveling with his family?  [وَسَارَ بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ · law]
- 28:29/q29/p1 A husband may take his wife where he wishes under guardianship, subject to a binding agreement. — Al-Qurṭubī says marriage covenants especially deserve fulfillment. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:29/q30 What inward meaning is given to Moses' journey and fire?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward readings emphasize distinct symbols: intimacy, faculties and detachment, or providential redirection of a mundane need.
- 28:29/q30/p1 The journey marks approach to divine intimacy and prophetic light, shared with his family. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ reads completion as the end of a period of love and travel as nearing divine beneficence. Jaʿfar says sacred lights and intimacy enveloped Moses, God spoke, granted his request, and secured him from fear; Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir stresses the pure place and familiarity. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر,ابن عطاء,جعفر); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]
- 28:29/q30/p2 The journey figures spiritual perfection and inward movement of every faculty. — Al-Qāshānī reads the shorter term as perfection, family as faculties, mount as secret, fire as Holy Spirit, and blessed spot as perfected heart. Najm al-Dīn reads detachment from family, property and both worlds, then successive lights until lordship appears; he says frozen human qualities warm by love or divine attraction, supported by Kamāl al-Khujandī's verse on a heart burned by love. [5 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(الكمال الخجندي,نجم الدين)]
- 28:29/q30/p3 God ordered an apparently ordinary need to lead Moses to speech and prophethood. — Al-Qushayrī says God arranges causes for decreed events: Moses feared a trial, but fire and then light led to hearing 'Lord of the worlds.' Maymūn compares Sheba's queen finding Islam on a worldly journey and Moses finding divine speech while seeking fire. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; DURR-FULL(ميمون بن مهران)]

## 28:29/q31 What virtues and guidance does the Midian-to-fire episode illustrate?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:29/q31/p1 Moses' and Shuʿayb's virtues prepare the revelation scene and invite moral imitation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists generosity, aid to the distressed, compassion, modesty, gratitude, chastity, fidelity, hospitality, protection and gentleness. He says Meccan polytheists could compare Moses' premission virtue with Muhammad's, while Muslims should emulate prophetic character. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:29/q32 What is reported about Moses' staff before the journey?  [* · reports]
turns on: The accounts differ over its origin and chain of custody.
- 28:29/q32/p1 The staff came from Paradise through Adam and Gabriel to Moses. — ʿIkrima says Gabriel took it when Adam died and gave it to Moses at night; another report says Gabriel brought the myrtle branch from Paradise as Moses headed for Midian. [3 notes: TABRISI(عبد الله بن سنان,عكرمة)]
- 28:29/q32/p2 Prophets inherited it until Shuʿayb gave it to Moses. — Al-Ṭabrisī records this transmission route. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 28:29/q32/p3 An angel deposited it with Shuʿayb, whose daughter could bring no other staff. — Al-Suddī recounts her repeated unsuccessful attempts and her father's gift of this staff to Moses. [1 notes: TABRISI(السدي)]
- 28:29/q32/p4 His father-in-law gave it to protect the flock. — Al-Ṭabrisī also records this functional origin story. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 28:29/q33 What happened around Shuʿayb's farewell?  [وَسَارَ بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ · reports]
- 28:29/q33/p1 He grieved at Moses' departure, prayed, and recovered strength and sight. — Būrsawī reports Moses' longing to see his mother, aunt, Aaron and sister; Shuʿayb invoked Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph, Moses said amen, and God restored him. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 28:29/q34 What adjacent grammatical points are recorded with this verse's source material?  [* · grammar]
- 28:29/q34/p1 After ḥattā, the verb's case depends on whether the journey causes the event; coordination depends on repeating ḥattā. — Sībawayh contrasts entry or baggage arrival caused by travel with sunrise, dawn or hearing a call not caused by it; exhaustion can result from travel. With a repeated ḥattā, a second clause can be raised, as Imruʾ al-Qays illustrates; one shared ḥattā cannot raise 'sun rises' or make a second subjunctive follow a raised first verb. Coordinated 'I traveled and he traveled until we enter' means a joint journey. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 28:29/q34/p2 At the following call, min marks origin and min al-shajarah may appose the valley bank. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the call came from the valley's bank beside the tree; the tree grew on the bank, so min al-shajarah is an inclusion apposition. He cites 43:33 as a parallel. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 28:29/q35 What is said about the later command involving Moses' wing and fear?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the later expression denotes a physical hand movement, self-control metaphor, or a purported sleeve sense of rahb.
- 28:29/q35/p1 He was told to tuck his hand under his arm after recoiling from the serpent. — Ibn Khālawayh explains fear of the serpent and calming by pressing hand to ribs. Al-Zamakhsharī calls the hands wings by bird analogy; the tucked right hand both calms fear and displays another miracle without a shameful pose. He reconciles 20:22 by taking the right hand as one wing and the left as the other, with distinct aims of white-hand display and concealing fear. [6 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 28:29/q35/p2 He was commanded to steady himself, as a frightened bird spreads its wings. — Al-Zamakhsharī explains the metaphor through a bird returning its wings to normal; he cites ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz telling an embarrassed scribe to draw in his wing and calm down. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز)]
- 28:29/q35/p3 Rahb could mean a sleeve or pocket rather than fear. — Ibn Khālawayh says a nonquiescent hāʾ is compatible with Arabic vowel reduction. Al-Zamakhsharī rejects the Himyaritic sleeve claim for weak attestation, poor contextual fit, and the unsupported sleeveless-garment story; he records four vowel patterns of the fear sense. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL-]

## 28:29/q36 What readings and word derivations are noted for the later signs?  [* · readings]
- 28:29/q36/p1 Al-buqʿah, rahb and dhānika have reported variant vocalizations. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives ḍamma or fatḥa for buqʿah, four vowel patterns for rahb meaning fear, and light dual dhānika from dhāka versus doubled dual from dhālika. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 28:29/q36/p2 Burhān and sulṭān name illuminating proof. — Al-Zamakhsharī derives burhān's brightness from a fair woman's description, treats n as extra by comparison with abraha, and links sulṭān to salīṭ, oil, because proof gives light. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 28:29/q37 How does the Torah account of Moses before the fire relate to the Qur'anic account?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether specific Torah details can be reconciled with Qur'anic narration or indicate textual alteration.
- 28:29/q37/p1 The Torah recounts Israel's oppression, Moses' rescue and flight, and his Midian marriage; details differ from the Qur'an. — Al-Biqāʿī quotes Jacob's seventy descendants in Egypt, Pharaoh's forced labor and infanticide, midwives Puah and Shiphrah, Moses' basket, Pharaoh's daughter and naming, his killing of an Egyptian, escape, Reuel's seven daughters, marriage to Zipporah and son Eliezer. He says a claim that both disputants were Hebrews should be reconciled with the Qur'an or deemed alteration; al-Zamakhsharī's identification of the Qur'anic two among seven daughters does not prove the seven. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise summarizes Qur'anic birth, flight and marriage, and says the Qur'an selects episodes for guidance rather than all detail. [15 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:29/q37/p2 The Torah's history and its account of Aaron's calf conflict with Qur'anic presentation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Torah was altered, relating Nebuchadnezzar's 588 BCE temple destruction and burning, Cyrus's 538 BCE return permission, and Ezra's rewriting. He quotes Aaron's alleged collection of earrings and casting of the calf, says Qur'anic passages indirectly criticize it, and lists other differences: two Hebrew disputants, Aaron casting the staff, omission of seventy men and revival, and stone tablets. [7 notes: TABATABAI]

## 28:29/q38 How is Moses' wider prophetic story and standing described?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:29/q38/p1 He is a highly honored prophet whose mission and story span many Qur'anic passages. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts 166 explicit names in 34 sūras and many signs; he recounts the winter fire and commission, the later journey with the righteous servant at 18:60–82, and Moses' rank as sincere, chosen, guided, righteous, near in private speech and recipient of Torah as criterion, guidance, light and admonition. God links Moses and Aaron in covenant, favor and peace at 33:7, 37:114–122 and 42:13; Moses asks Aaron's aid at 20:29–35 and Aaron defends his stewardship at 7:142–150. [13 notes: TABATABAI]

## 28:29/q39 What is reported about emphatic lengthening of lā in adjacent reading material?  [* · readings]
- 28:29/q39/p1 Emphatic lā is lengthened moderately in several negative formulas. — Ibn al-Jazarī attributes it to emphasis and hamza, says supported lengthening is preferable, and reads a moderate degree because the non-hamza cause is weaker. He cites Hamza routes through Ibn Sawwār, Sibt al-Khayyāṭ, Ibn Fāris, al-Khuzāʿī, Khalaf, Sulaym and Khallād, plus Ḥafṣ through Hubayra for lā rayba. Al-Nawawī favors lengthening lā ilāha illā Allāh to aid reflection; two weak devotional reports promise divine vision or erasure of sins. [8 notes: NASHR(حمزة,محيي الدين النووي); NASHR+]

## 28:29/q40 How is the report that only four companions collected the Qur'an understood?  [* · sciences]
turns on: What 'collected' means and whether Anas's naming four was exhaustive.
- 28:29/q40/p1 It does not exclude the Qur'an's collective preservation by others. — Al-Suyūṭī rejects the heretical exclusion because distributed memorization yields tawātur. Al-Qurṭubī says Anas named four from familiarity or immediate recollection. Al-Bāqillānī says the wording is nonexclusive or only four met a narrower criterion. [3 notes: ITQAN(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني,القرطبي)]
- 28:29/q40/p2 The four uniquely collected particular forms, channels or timing of the Qur'an. — Al-Bāqillānī proposes all readings and modes, both abrogated and surviving recitation, direct reception from the Prophet, publicly known teaching, writing together with memorization, or completed memorization before the Prophet died. [6 notes: ITQAN(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)]
- 28:29/q40/p3 Collection can mean hearing, obeying and acting on the Qur'an. — Al-Bāqillānī gives this as an eighth answer; Abū al-Dardāʾ calls the true collector one who hears and obeys. [2 notes: ITQAN(أبو الدرداء,القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)]

## 28:29/q41 What virtues or protection are reported for reciting and carrying the Qur'an?  [* · reports]
- 28:29/q41/p1 The Qur'an protects, intercedes and rewards those who recite and practice it. — Al-Suyūṭī cites Ibn al-Anbārī and reports through ʿIṣma and Sahl likening its immunity from Fire to a hide containing Qur'an; Anas says faithful readers are protected, aided at Judgment and among Paradise's notables and God's elect. Other reports call God's Book the best speech, promise ranks to readers in God's cause, crowns to parents of learners, and value three prayer verses above three pregnant camels. An unnamed harsh report says a collector who enters Fire is worse than a pig. [13 notes: ITQAN(أبو هريرة,أنس,ابن الأنباري,جابر بن عبد الله,سهل بن سعد,عصمة بن مالك,غيره,معاذ بن أنس)]

## 28:29/q42 Where was the sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 28:29/q42/p1 All of al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]
