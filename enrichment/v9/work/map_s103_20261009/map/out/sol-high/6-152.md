# 6:152: 44 questions

## 6:152/q01 Who is the orphan and why is the orphan's property singled out?  [مَالَ ٱلْيَتِيمِ · interpretation]
- 6:152/q01/p1 The orphan is a fatherless child whose vulnerable property especially needs protection. — Mawardi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, Qurtubi, Bursevi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur, and Kuran Yolu cite absent parental protection, weak oversight, temptation to seize, and the child's inability to defend property; Mawardi says others' property is equally protected; Qurtubi says God opposes its abuser; Bursevi adds a Persian couplet about an orphan's weeping. Jishumi defines a human orphan as fatherless; Bursevi adds an animal without its mother. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; JISHUMI]

## 6:152/q02 What does prohibiting even approach to the orphan's property cover?  [وَلَا تَقْرَبُوا۟ مَالَ ٱلْيَتِيمِ · interpretation]
- 6:152/q02/p1 Every handling, consumption, use, or disposition is barred unless it benefits the orphan. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say the broad prohibition blocks harmful means; Tabatabai enumerates consumption, use, and disposal; Ibn Ashur says even slight taking is warned against because the child cannot defend himself, citing 2:188; Bursevi addresses guardians and trustees. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; JISHUMI]

## 6:152/q03 What is the best way to deal with the orphan's property?  [إِلَّا بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the exception requires active growth, mere preservation, or permits particular uses; several authors combine preservation and growth.
- 6:152/q03/p1 Preserve and increase the property for the orphan, using beneficial management and investment. — Muqatil specifies profit; Zajjaj, Thalabi, Mawardi, Wahidi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalili, Biqai, and others join preservation to growth. Qurtubi prefers this comprehensive reading; Jishumi and Ibn Ashur stress the child's benefit, and Asad says any use must aim at improvement. Qashani excludes waste and use for personal needs. [30 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك، السدي,حسن بن صالح); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق؛ السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مجاهد والضحاك والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 6:152/q03/p2 Trade with the property to earn profit for the orphan. — Mujahid, Atiyya, Said ibn Jubayr, Dahhak, and Suddi report trade; Ibn Atiyya specifies a guardian who has other means. Dahhak bars taking its profits personally; Qurtubi reports Mujahid forbidding purchase from or borrowing the estate. [17 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عطية); THALABI(الضحاك,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك,مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد والضحاك والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,عطية)]
- 6:152/q03/p3 Preserve the property until it can be delivered to its mature owner. — Kalbi and Ibn al-Saib emphasize guarding principal and handing it over at the endpoint; Tabrisī reports this as another view. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); JISHUMI(مجاهد والضحاك والسدي); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب)]
- 6:152/q03/p4 Using the orphan's animal or servant can count as permitted management. — Zajjaj reports the view but says the wording gives no indication of it; Abu Hayyan says the proposal avoids household mixing. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المروزي); JISHUMI(مجاهد والضحاك والسدي); ZAJJAJ-]
- 6:152/q03/p5 Borrowing from the orphan's estate is permitted as its best use. — Jishumi reports borrowing among the explanations; Abu Hayyan rejects an only-loan interpretation as inconsistent with the child's best interest; Qurtubi reports Mujahid forbidding borrowing. [1 notes: JISHUMI(مجاهد والضحاك والسدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)-]

## 6:152/q04 May a guardian use the orphan's property for personal support?  [إِلَّا بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ · law]
turns on: Whether the guardian's need and useful management make personal consumption part of the best treatment, and whether 4:6 allows food only.
- 6:152/q04/p1 A poor guardian may take a customary reasonable amount, while a wealthy guardian abstains. — Ibn Zayd and Ibn Abbas cite need and 4:6; Ibn Atiyya says without support the property might be neglected, and Abu Hayyan puts the allowance in trading profit. Several restrict the allowance to food, excluding clothing. Study Quran reports an alternative small amount to repay when possible. [15 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي وابن زيد,مجاهد والضحاك والسدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن زيد,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 6:152/q04/p2 The guardian must take nothing for himself, including profit or personal items. — Ikrima forbids even a cap or turban; Hasan ibn Salih and Dahhak require seeking gain entirely for the orphan, with Dahhak forbidding personal profit. Qashani rules out use for the guardian's needs. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(حسن بن صالح,عكرمة); THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 6:152/q04/p3 Necessary mixing to manage the estate is allowed; mixing merely to consume it is not. — Al-Sha'bi distinguishes the manager's purpose. The report from Ibn Abbas describes food separation causing waste and 2:220 then permitting beneficial mixing. [3 notes: THALABI(الشعبي); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 6:152/q05 May the guardian buy from the orphan's estate?  [مَالَ ٱلْيَتِيمِ · law]
- 6:152/q05/p1 The guardian may not buy from the estate. — Abu Ishaq states the rule, and Qurtubi reports Mujahid forbidding purchase along with borrowing. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 6:152/q06 What happened after the warning about orphan property?  [مَالَ ٱلْيَتِيمِ · reports]
- 6:152/q06/p1 Guardians separated orphan food until leftovers spoiled, then 2:220 allowed beneficial mixing. — Ibn Abi Hatim transmits Ibn Abbas: God distinguished reformers from corrupters; Ibn Kathir cites Abu Dawud and describes separated food and drink after the warning at 4:10. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 6:152/q07 How does the exception except the best manner work grammatically?  [إِلَّا بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the omitted feminine referent describes the manner of handling or the guardian.
- 6:152/q07/p1 It excepts the best state or act of handling, with an understood feminine noun. — Samin allows a circumstantial reading or an omitted verbal-noun adjective and says ahsan demands the best, not merely good. Ibn Ashur supplies state or act and cites 23:96, the idiom al-lutayya wa-allati, and Salmi's line; Alusi considers the alternative of describing the guardian's quality remote. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(سلمي بن ربيعة الضبي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:152/q07/p2 The guardian himself must embody the best quality. — Alusi mentions this analysis only to call it remote. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 6:152/q08 What does until the orphan reaches maturity limit?  [حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ · grammar]
- 6:152/q08/p1 It limits the implied duty to preserve the estate, which is then delivered; it does not license harmful handling afterward. — Zajjaj, Wahidi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi, Alusi, and Tabatabai read the endpoint semantically. Samin says a literal endpoint to the prohibition would yield an absurd permission; Thalabi and Qurtubi expressly continue the best-treatment rule after maturity. Asad says subsequent borrowing or use needs the new owner's consent. [16 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:152/q09 What does ashudd mean in sense?  [أَشُدَّهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term itself names physical vigor or the developed judgment needed in this legal context.
- 6:152/q09/p1 Developed strength, vigor, or youth at maturity. — Thalabi compares the height of day; Antara's shadd al-nahar line is cited by Thalabi, Jishumi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Alusi, with another poetic line in Qurtubi. Ibn Qutayba says full youthful strength before decline; Baydawi calls it adulthood; Jawhari gives an eighteen-to-thirty span. Ibn Ashur's context concerns ability to manage property. [18 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI(المفضل,علي بن عيسى); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عنترة); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء,عنترة); ALUSI-FULL(عنترة); BURSEVI(الجوهري); TABRISI]
- 6:152/q09/p2 Mature reason, knowledge, firmness, and competence in managing assets. — Layth says adult knowledge and judgment; Abu al-Aliya combines understanding with bodily powers; Ibn Atiyya specifies firm financial decisions and distinguishes this from the seasoned reason of age forty in 46:15. [8 notes: THALABI(أبو العالية); WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الليث); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير ومقاتل والسدي والثوري وعكرمة وعائشة وأبو العالية); STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 6:152/q10 What is the morphological form and derivation of ashudd?  [أَشُدَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an actual singular was heard and whether the af'al pattern can be singular; authorities also dispute its derivation from strength or rising daylight.
- 6:152/q10/p1 It is a plural of shiddah, strength or toughness. — Abu al-Haytham treats the feminine ending as extra before the af'ul plural; Sibawayh gives shiddah, but Jawhari objects that fa'lah normally does not form af'ul. Abu Muslim compares ni'mah/an'um. Farrā and others derive it from strength. [11 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الهيثم); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,علي بن عيسى); RAZI-FULL(أبو الهيثم); QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الهيثم,الفراء); BURSEVI(الجوهري); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 6:152/q10/p2 It is a plural of shadd, with different reported vowelings. — Farrā says shadd is analogically expected but unattested; Abu Ubayd cites poetry for the plural. Basrans give shudd with damma, another analysis shadd with fatha; Nasafi compares shadd/fals. Qurtubi warns that analogical singulars are not heard and cites the analogous proposed ibul for ababil, plus Abu Zayd's shada and ashadda. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد عن الفراء,ابن الأنباري؛ جماعة من البصريين); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأنباري,الفراء)]
- 6:152/q10/p3 It is a plural without an attested singular. — Farrā reports no heard singular despite analogy; Qurtubi compares the lexical dispute over forms; Abu Hayyan lists this among five analyses. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد عن الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 6:152/q10/p4 It is itself a singular form. — Ibn al-Anbari reports this and compares al-anuk; Abu Hayyan rejects it because af'al is not established as a singular noun pattern; Samin transmits that objection from the shaykh. Baydawi and Tabrisī note the possibility. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري؛ جماعة من البصريين); JISHUMI(علي بن عيسى); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن الأنباري); BURSEVI(الجوهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري)+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]

## 6:152/q11 At what point does the orphan reach ashudd here?  [حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ · law]

Ibn Atiyya prefers Malik's puberty plus absence of foolishness; Tabrisī prefers no fixed age. Zajjaj rejects eighteen if puberty and judgment arrive earlier; Ibn al-Jawzi prefers puberty and doubts that older age reports belong to this verse. Alusi refers readers to his earlier treatment of the disputed handover age. ## 6:152/q12 When must the guardian hand over the orphan's property?  [حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ · law]
turns on: Whether 4:6 supplies a further prudence condition to this verse's maturity endpoint.
- 6:152/q12/p1 At adulthood together with sound judgment; the property is then delivered. — Zajjaj, Wahidi, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisī, Qurtubi, and Tabatabai appeal to 4:6; Qurtubi warns that an imprudent adult may squander it. Ibn al-Jawzi says the judgment and Islam qualifications come from another verse, limiting this verse's unqualified wording. Islahi adds delivery with witnesses. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المدينة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك)+; TABRISI+]
- 6:152/q12/p2 At puberty, with delivery implied directly by the endpoint. — Nasafi states delivery at puberty; the separate debate over required financial prudence turns on 4:6. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD-]

## 6:152/q13 Was the orphan maturity limit abrogated by 4:6?  [حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ · sciences]
turns on: Whether the later testing and marriage-age instruction replaces the earlier threshold or explains it.
- 6:152/q13/p1 Yes, the later marriage-age and testing verse replaced this limit. — Suddi's thirty-year report is coupled with this suggestion by Ibn al-Jawzi and Tabrisī; Alusi reports the same view. [3 notes: TABRISI(الشعبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes)]
- 6:152/q13/p2 The verses are read together: 4:6 clarifies maturity by adding sound judgment. — Zajjaj, Qurtubi, Tabrisī, Tabatabai, and others apply both conditions; Ibn al-Jawzi frames it as qualifying an unrestricted statement rather than deriving all details from this verse alone. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI+]

## 6:152/q14 What do kayl and mizan name here?  [ٱلْكَيْلَ ٱلْمِيزَانَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether an omitted measured or weighed noun is necessary, or kayl can directly denote the vessel or amount.
- 6:152/q14/p1 The measured amount and weighed amount, with measure also able to name its vessel and mizan the scale. — Wahidi says wafā means completion, kayl can imply a vessel, and mizan names weighing on the scale; Samin derives mizan from weighing on the mif'al pattern, underlying mawzan changing like miqat. Ibn Ashur names dates and raisins sold by measure and gold and silver by weight; Alusi allows the commodity sense. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:152/q14/p2 They are verbal nouns standing for measured goods or acts of measuring, with supplied nouns. — Abu al-Baqa proposes omitted nouns; Wahidi proposes an understood weight with mizan. Samin rejects unnecessary substitution because kayl itself can name the vessel; Alusi still allows an omitted noun. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الواحدي); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 6:152/q15 What does fair fulfillment of measure and weight require?  [وَأَوْفُوا۟ ٱلْكَيْلَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ بِٱلْقِسْطِ · law]
turns on: Whether fairness means exact equality without extra or a small excess to guarantee no deficit.
- 6:152/q15/p1 Give and take the full due accurately, without shortchanging or demanding excess. — Ibn Abbas and many commentators gloss qist as justice. Wahidi defines wafā as completing without shortfall; Razi, Thalabi, Baghawi, Bursevi, and Alusi apply equity to seller and buyer alike. Ibn Kathir cites 83:1-6 and destruction for fraudulent scales. Islahi links honest measures to a just economy and 17:35; Elmalili generalizes protection of others' rights. [39 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب المعاني,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة)]
- 6:152/q15/p2 Slightly over-measure to ensure that the due is certainly fulfilled. — Abu Hayyan reports this recommendation and a report instructing an added weight; he also glosses qist as exact balance. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 6:152/q16 How is with fairness attached grammatically?  [بِٱلْقِسْطِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase qualifies the doer or the object of fulfillment.
- 6:152/q16/p1 It describes the measurer's just action. — Samin and Abu al-Baqa allow the agent reading; Bursevi takes it as the state of fulfillment, and Ibn Ashur says the preposition directs just conduct. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:152/q16/p2 It describes the measures or measured objects as complete and fair. — Samin and Abu al-Baqa report the object reading. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 6:152/q17 Why is the measure duty phrased as a positive command?  [وَأَوْفُوا۟ ٱلْكَيْلَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ · rhetoric]
- 6:152/q17/p1 It urges active justice and implies the prohibition of cheating. — Ibn Ashur contrasts Shuayb's prohibition at 11:84 and says the command confronts Arabs who boasted of generosity while shorting buyers. Islahi compares 83:1-3; Biqai says full equity rules out merely coming close; Bursevi says the apparent repetition spells out both seller and buyer duties. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 6:152/q18 What does the capacity clause qualify and excuse?  [لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the intervening sentence is limited by the immediately preceding exact-measure command or extends to all nearby duties.
- 6:152/q18/p1 It qualifies measuring and weighing: reasonable care is required, unavoidable minute error is excused. — Mawardi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisī, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Ashur, Asad, and many others say microscopic exactness is impossible. Ibn Kathir cites Said ibn al-Musayyib's sincere-measure report as rare and disconnected; Bursevi says deliberate fraud is not excused. Ibn Ashur says the concession keeps fear of error from stopping trade. [32 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب,سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:152/q18/p2 It states generally that all the listed obligations lie within human ability. — Ibn Abbas compares 22:78; Sufyan identifies capacity with performing duties. Farisi, Jishumi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai, Islahi, and Study Quran permit the broader scope; Ibn Ashur cites 2:286 and Study Quran gives 2:286, 7:42, 23:62, 65:7. Islahi rejects both scrupulous excess and self-set limits of obedience. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سفيان); FARISI-HUJJA; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 6:152/q19 What does wus' or capacity mean here?  [وُسْعَهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether capacity is ordinary ability, one's own action, or performance of duties.
- 6:152/q19/p1 One's ability or what one can bear. — Said ibn Jubayr glosses it as ability; Ibn Abbas says obligations are manageable, citing 22:78; Ibn Kathir and later commentators apply this to honest effort. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:152/q19/p2 What a person has done for himself. — Ibn Abi Hatim transmits this gloss from Sha'bi. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الشعبي)]
- 6:152/q19/p3 Performing the assigned obligations. — Ibn Abi Hatim transmits this gloss from Sufyan. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سفيان)]

## 6:152/q20 What does the capacity clause imply about obliging an incapable unbeliever?  [لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the easing of an attainable measurement task proves a broader rule against the disputed obligation to believe.
- 6:152/q20/p1 God cannot oblige faith from someone incapable while creating and willing that person's disbelief. — Al-Qadi argues from the easing of exact measurement: if the attainable task is eased, such an impossible obligation would be incoherent. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 6:152/q20/p2 The objection fails when divine knowledge and the motive are considered. — Razi gives this reply to al-Qadi and his teachers. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 6:152/q21 How is the capacity sentence placed in the syntax?  [لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا · grammar]
- 6:152/q21/p1 It is a parenthetical sentence between commands. — Samin calls it parenthetical; Ibn Ashur notes the shift into God's first-person voice as a special favor that confirms the messenger. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:152/q22 What reported consequences follow dishonest measures and weights?  [ٱلْكَيْلَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ · reports]
turns on: Different reports present collective destruction, famine, or individual deathbed consequences.
- 6:152/q22/p1 Earlier peoples perished through measuring and weighing; a raised prophetic warning is weak while an Ibn Abbas version is sound. — Thalabi and Qurtubi transmit Ibn Abbas's warning to non-Arabs. Ibn Kathir says Tirmidhi knows the prophetic version only through weak Husayn ibn Qays but authenticates its stop at Ibn Abbas; Durr calls the prophetic report weak. A second Ibn Abbas report says the community inherited the two perilous practices. [5 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:152/q22/p2 Short measure brings loss of provision or famine, among punishments for communal wrongdoing. — Qurtubi transmits a report reaching Ibn Abbas that also associates embezzlement with fear, adultery with death, unjust rule with bloodshed, and broken covenants with enemy domination; Durr reports the Prophet's famine warning. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:152/q22/p3 Cheating measures can torment the perpetrator at death. — Bursevi tells of a dying trader whose scale lay on his tongue, and Malik ibn Dinar's neighbor who saw two fiery mountains after using separate buying and selling measures; breaking them did not ease his agony. [2 notes: BURSEVI(مالك بن دينار)]

## 6:152/q23 What speech does the command to be just cover?  [وَإِذَا قُلْتُمْ فَٱعْدِلُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the unqualified word speech is universal or primarily refers to testimony and adjudication because of the kin clause.
- 6:152/q23/p1 All consequential speech must be truthful and fair, including testimony, judgment, advice, preaching, reports, and mediation. — Jishumi lists wills, legal opinions, rulings, and schools; Qadi adds concise comprehensible preaching, restrained enjoining of right, accurate narratives and message transmission. Abu Hayyan rejects restriction without evidence. Ibn Ashur details evaluations, reconciliation, promises, oaths, praise, insults, and commercial disclosure; Study Quran and Kuran Yolu generalize integrity in speech. Tabrisī says habitual just speech also leads to just action. [42 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); ASAD-NOTES(Razi); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)+]
- 6:152/q23/p2 Its primary application is testimony or judgment, especially in disputes involving kin. — Razi reports commentators' restriction to testimony or commands and prohibitions; Asad prefers an evidentiary setting because kin are mentioned. Qurtubi and Baydawi emphasize judgments, and Ibn Ashur and Islahi take this as a central application without denying wider speech. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 6:152/q24 What is the force of when you speak?  [وَإِذَا قُلْتُمْ · rhetoric]
- 6:152/q24/p1 It means when intending to speak and allows silence where just speech is impossible. — Burhan explains Arabic can name intended action by the act, comparing ablution before prayer in 5:6. Ibn Ashur says the condition allows and sometimes requires silence; he cites Umar rebuking a man for revealing his sister's former transgression to a suitor. [4 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 6:152/q25 Why command just speech rather than only forbid falsehood?  [فَٱعْدِلُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 6:152/q25/p1 The positive command calls for disclosing truth while allowing permissible indirect speech where no right is at stake. — Ibn Ashur says a flat falsehood ban could condemn equivocal speech under fear or accommodation; he cites a hadith that indirect expressions leave room to avoid lying. Burhan argues justice in speech implies justice in action more strongly, comparing the ban on saying uff to parents at 17:23. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 6:152/q26 Who is the relative in even if a relative?  [وَلَوْ كَانَ ذَا قُرْبَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Which omitted person is supplied as the subject of kāna.
- 6:152/q26/p1 The person spoken about, testified for or against, or judged may be the speaker's relative. — Wahidi reports Zajjaj's explanation that the omitted party may be beneficiary or accused, comparing 5:106. Ibn Ashur identifies the circumstantial waw and concessive law, comparing 4:135; affection must neither invent a right nor conceal liability. Samin also allows the person spoken to to be kin. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ الزجاج); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:152/q26/p2 The person addressed in speech may be a relative. — Samin explicitly includes the addressee as well as the person spoken about; the grammatical subject is omitted. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:152/q27 How is even if he is a relative parsed?  [وَلَوْ كَانَ ذَا قُرْبَىٰ · grammar]
- 6:152/q27/p1 Dha qurba is the predicate of kana, whose subject is omitted; the waw is circumstantial and law concessive. — Jishumi gives the predicate analysis; Ibn Ashur explains the concessive circumstantial construction and its emphasis on the exceptional kin case; Thalabi and Wahidi explain the omitted party. [4 notes: JISHUMI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:152/q28 What inward discipline is read in just speech?  [وَإِذَا قُلْتُمْ فَٱعْدِلُوا۟ · inward]
turns on: Whether the spiritual emphasis falls on God-oriented speech itself or prior correction of the speaker.
- 6:152/q28/p1 True speech is of, through, and for God, free of self-interest, and should be sparse and grounded in knowledge. — Tustari distinguishes God-directed speech from self-directed speech, warns that evil suspicion, irrelevant talk, and misuse of limbs destroy certainty, truthfulness, and scruple. Abu Sulayman teaches remembrance before speech; Muhammad ibn Hamid counsels words with no liability. Bursevi says only realized people can speak through God without claims and cites a Persian hoopoe verse. [7 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو سليمان,محمد بن حامد العدل); BURSEVI]
- 6:152/q28/p2 One should correct one's own faults before criticizing others. — Al-Rabi ibn Khaytham says dissatisfaction with himself keeps him from fault-finding; Jawzajani requires self-discipline before enjoining right and lists hunger, night vigil, remembrance, righteous company, travel, solitude, restrained speech, and patience. [3 notes: TUSTARI(الربيع بن خثيم); SULAMI(الجوزجاني)]

## 6:152/q29 What is God's covenant that must be fulfilled?  [وَبِعَهْدِ ٱللَّهِ أَوْفُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the genitive identifies a covenant imposed by God, made to God, or protected by God; the broad reading includes these together.
- 6:152/q29/p1 It embraces God's commands and prohibitions together with lawful vows, oaths, and human agreements He orders people to keep. — Jishumi says all senses are intended; Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur, Islahi, and Kuran Yolu include obligations and valid pledges. Nasafi also lists promise and threat. Ibn Ashur includes polytheists' alliances and peace pacts, Arab poetry praising fidelity, Meccan Hilf al-Fudul and Mutayyibin pacts, and violation of Mecca's safety promise to Abraham. Razi stresses secret oaths can be broken unseen; Biqai includes what one owes or is owed. [28 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الجوزي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 6:152/q29/p2 It chiefly means the religious duties and ordinances God imposed. — Maturidi, Ibn Jarir, and Study Quran identify commands and prohibitions; Ibn Kathir adds God's Book and the Messenger's Sunnah. Tabatabai prefers this in context, citing 17:34 and 5:106, but allows a pledge to God. Baydawi names justice and religious law. [6 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماتريدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABATABAI+]
- 6:152/q29/p3 It is a vow, oath, or pledge made to God or sworn by God's name. — Said ibn Jubayr cites confirmed vows and 16:91; Nahhas mentions covenants with God or oaths to people; Mawardi excludes sinful oaths. Razi notes private oath fidelity is subtle. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:152/q29/p4 It is the primordial covenant. — Nasafi lists it; Tabrizi places it on the Day of the Covenant. Qashani and Bursevi draw inward implications from it. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي); QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 6:152/q30 How can the genitive God's covenant be understood?  [بِعَهْدِ ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether God makes the covenant, receives the pledge, or authorizes a covenant between people.
- 6:152/q30/p1 God is the covenanting subject, imposing obligations on people. — Abu Hayyan and Samin explain this possessive relation; Samin cites 33:23 and 48:10. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:152/q30/p2 God is the object: people pledge to Him. — Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Ibn Ashur allow this analysis; Samin cites 33:23 and 48:10. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:152/q30/p3 The attribution explains that God commands keeping human covenants. — Samin calls this an explanatory possessive; Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur also permit human-to-human covenants under God's command. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:152/q31 Why is God's covenant placed first in its clause?  [وَبِعَهْدِ ٱللَّهِ أَوْفُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 6:152/q31/p1 Fronting emphasizes special priority for fidelity to God's covenant. — Alusi says the fronted phrase emphasizes it; Ibn Ashur appeals to an audience proud of pacts and contrasts 2:217: they should keep their chosen covenant with God. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:152/q32 What inward covenant does the phrase suggest?  [بِعَهْدِ ٱللَّهِ · inward]
turns on: Different inward applications emphasize exclusive devotion or self-discipline before social correction.
- 6:152/q32/p1 It means unity, obedience, exclusive worship and love of God under a primordial pledge renewed later. — Qashani joins unity and obligation; Bursevi says see none beside the Lord and cites Persian verses on love for the Friend from primordial morning to time's end. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 6:152/q32/p2 The chief covenant is enjoining right and forbidding wrong after disciplining oneself. — Jawzajani says personal correction precedes correcting others and specifies ascetic exercises when the self resists good. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجوزجاني)]

## 6:152/q33 What does that which He enjoined point back to?  [ذَٰلِكُمْ وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative simply collects the immediately listed duties or identifies their scriptural addressees.
- 6:152/q33/p1 The duties just enumerated: orphan care, fair measures within ability, just speech, and covenant keeping. — Farisi and Wahidi list these duties; Tabrisī, Bursevi, Alusi, and Biqai say the closing gathers and stresses them. Tabatabai says the covenant is reinforced; Wahidi and Farisi cite 48:10's reward for covenant keeping. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:152/q33/p2 It may refer to Torah commands given to Jews or Qur'anic commands given to Muslims. — Mawardi reports both possible audiences of the demonstrative. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 6:152/q34 What is the purpose of the closing call to remember?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ · interpretation]
- 6:152/q34/p1 People should recall the instructions, reflect, take heed, and act rather than neglect them. — Jishumi lists remembrance, admonition, avoiding neglect, reflection, and recalling punishment. Wahidi, Tabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Kathir, Elmalili, Bursevi, and Alusi stress action; Ibn Kathir says it stops former conduct. Ibn Atiyya treats la'allakum as hope from the human perspective. [12 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 6:152/q35 Why does this group end with remembrance rather than understanding?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the ending reflects complexity, familiar but forgotten virtues, or the different form of prohibitions and commands.
- 6:152/q35/p1 The first five duties are obvious, while these four need reflection to find a just balance. — Razi gives this explanation, followed by Abu Hayyan, Samin, and Alusi; Tabatabai says remembering social harm to weak persons, commerce, and truth demands reflection. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); TABATABAI]
- 6:152/q35/p2 The audience already practiced and praised these duties but might forget them. — Qutb al-Razi contrasts heedless violation of the first set; he explains parents' place in the first set by their unique favor after God. Ibn Ashur says Arab virtues had been obscured by desire and idolatry. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(القطب الرازي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:152/q35/p3 The first set's prohibitions suit reason's restraint, while these positive duties must be kept in memory. — Alusi offers this as his own alternative explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:152/q36 How is tadhakkarun read?  [تَذَكَّرُونَ · readings]

- 6:152/q36/p1 It is read with a doubled dhal through assimilation of the underlying tatadhakkarun. — Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr double related forms throughout; Nafi, Abu Bakr from Asim, and Ibn Amir generally do so but lighten 19:67. Ibn Ashur adds Abu Jafar and Yaqub; Farisi and Ibn Mujahid detail further differences at 25:62, 17:41, 25:50, and 74:56, where all lighten and Nafi has ta versus others' ya. - 6:152/q36/p2 It is read with a light dhal by deleting one ta from tatadhakkarun. — Hamza, Kisai, Hafs, and in later listing Khalaf use the light form; traditions disagree over which ta is omitted. ## 6:152/q37 What are the form and sense of the remembrance verb?  [تَذَكَّرُونَ · grammar]
- 6:152/q37/p1 It is a responsive or reflexive form meaning repeated recollection or taking heed. — Wahidi calls tadhakkur repeated remembering; Farisi reports Sibawayh that simple dhakara takes one object, doubled dhakkara two, and Form V is its responsive form, citing Qur'an and poetry. Tabrisī likewise notes dhikr and dhakar as equivalent, with a poetic illustration. Farisi also surveys iddikar, dhikra, the plural al-dhikr, assimilated dal pronunciation, and transitive Form VIII, citing 73:8, 12:45, 54:15, 11:54; he compares grateful recollection in 8:26 and 7:86. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(سيبويه); NASAFI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:152/q38 How are these commands connected to the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connected sequence is explained chiefly as public duties or as a scheme of inward virtues.
- 6:152/q38/p1 They form the second group of linked divine duties, dealing especially with interpersonal, economic, and civic order. — Razi counts five clearer duties in the previous verse and four subtler here. Biqai says three groups each close with a testament and progress from reason to remembrance to taqwa; Ibn Ashur describes social, interpersonal, and straight-path sections, each closed by a repeated charge. Islahi ties them to Abraham's religion against Quraysh's invented food taboos and compares 17:22-38 and 16:90; Kuran Yolu counts nine main commands and a final encompassing straight-path duty. Kashshaf and Islahi say positive and negative forms imply their opposites. Ibn Ashur begins the second group with vulnerable property and mutual trust; Islahi says justice marks Abrahamic and Islamic conduct. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:152/q38/p2 The sequence is an inward scheme of vices, virtues, and the comprehensive virtue of justice. — Qashani says three vice classes entail injustice, while virtues entail justice; four listed vices are followed by four corresponding virtues. He calls the balanced middle difficult and encompassing all vice and virtue. Bursevi reads lifetime and divine rights as inward measure and scale, supported by a Persian verse about each person's wings and strength. [5 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 6:152/q39 Are these commandments universal and abrogated?  [* · sciences]
- 6:152/q39/p1 They are clear, universal commands preserved across scriptures and never abrogated. — Ibn Abbas calls them Mother of the Book, with Paradise for practitioners and Hell for abandoners; Baghawi, Thalabi, Tabrisī, Alusi, Qashani, and Bursevi transmit or affirm this. Qashani says scriptural communities agree. Itqan places three clear verses at qul ta'alaw, and another report adds three at 17:23. ## 6:152/q40 Where were these verses revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 6:152/q40/p1 These three verses were revealed in Medina although al-An'am otherwise came in Mecca. — Durr transmits Ibn Abbas and Shahr ibn Hawshab; Shahr says the sura descended all at once accompanied by ranks of angels, with 6:151-153 excepted. ## 6:152/q41 What reports present these verses as a testament or pledge?  [* · reports]

## 6:152/q43 What stories of Quss ibn Sa'ida accompany truthful speech?  [وَإِذَا قُلْتُمْ فَٱعْدِلُوا۟ · reports]
