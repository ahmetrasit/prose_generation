# 6:151: 42 questions

## 6:151/q01 How does the invitation answer the surrounding dispute?  [قُلْ تَعَالَوْا۟ أَتْلُ مَا حَرَّمَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَيْكُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether the address is tied to the polytheists' invented bans or explicitly extends to everyone.
- 6:151/q01/p1 It replaces the polytheists' invented food and livestock prohibitions with God's certain revealed duties. — Muqatil excludes their camel taboos; Thalabi, Baghawi, Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir contrast revelation with conjecture; Biqai and Islahi identify God's exclusive authority and the Quraysh's claim to Abraham's religion. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:151/q01/p2 The verse announces a universal Islamic law to all hearers. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan explicitly include all humanity; Qurtubi says scholars must also explain the lawful and unlawful, citing 3:187. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 6:151/q01/p3 The invitation calls people upward from ignorance and imitation to knowledge and virtue. — Biqai transmits Zamakhshari's ethical reading; Qashani locates the vice in whim ruling what is lawful. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QASHANI]

## 6:151/q02 Whom does the command to come address?  [قُلْ تَعَالَوْا۟ · referent]
turns on: The immediate audience in context versus the scope of the message.
- 6:151/q02/p1 The Messenger addresses the polytheists in the immediate context. — Abu Hayyan cites the preceding fabricated bans; Ibn Kathir specifies idol worship, banning God's provision, and child-killing. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 6:151/q02/p2 It summons all people to hear the law. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say Muhammad's message is universal; Abu Hayyan adds the same ruling applies even on the narrower immediate address. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 6:151/q03 What does تَعَالَوْا۟ mean and suggest?  [تَعَالَوْا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the original spatial force remains rhetorically active.
- 6:151/q03/p1 It originally meant come upward from a lower place, then became a general invitation to approach. — Jishumi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi and others derive it from elevation; Qurtubi notes singular and plural forms and 33:28; Tabatabai reports the derivation. [12 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الشجري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 6:151/q03/p2 Its upward imagery also invites listeners out of ignorance. — Biqai cites Zamakhshari; Alusi says this implication remains even after the word became common. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:151/q04 What does أَتْلُ mean?  [أَتْلُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the terms distinguish first from repeated speech or act from content.
- 6:151/q04/p1 It means recite or read in ordered sequence. — Jishumi allows reading or retelling; Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Biqai stress the following of letters or utterances; Tabatabai equates tilawa and qira'a. [7 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:151/q04/p2 Tilāwa and qirāʾa are the spoken act; matluw and maqruʾ are what is uttered or the original speech. — Mawardi prefers this distinction; Jishumi and Tabarsi compare a recitation with a retold narration. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; MAWARDI-FULL+]
- 6:151/q04/p3 Tilāwa and qirāʾa are first utterances, while matluw and maqruʾ are repetitions. — Ali ibn Isa's proposed distinction is reported by Mawardi. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن عيسى)]

## 6:151/q05 How does أَتْلُ relate grammatically to the invitation?  [تَعَالَوْا۟ أَتْلُ · grammar]
- 6:151/q05/p1 The jussive verb answers the imperative invitation: if they come, he will recite. — Alusi states the conditional relation; Jishumi explains the deletion of the initial alif under jussive formation; Bursevi calls it the answer to come. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:151/q06 What is the grammatical function of مَا حَرَّمَ رَبُّكُمْ?  [أَتْلُ مَا حَرَّمَ رَبُّكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether recitation governs mā directly, a following question, or a verbal noun.
- 6:151/q06/p1 Mā is chiefly a relative pronoun and object of atlu: recite what the Lord forbade. — Ibn Atiyya prefers this and calls Zajjaj's other construction strained; Samin prefers it, with an omitted resumptive pronoun; Baydawi also permits a relative reading. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج وابن الأنباري); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:151/q06/p2 Mā is governed by ḥarrama, with recitation conveying a question about what the Lord forbade. — Zajjaj links this to 6:145; Ibn Atiyya objects that suspending atlu is strained, and Abu Hayyan and Samin say only verbs of thought permit suspension; Alusi defends it because recitation is a kind of saying. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(البصير الأصفهاني); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 6:151/q06/p3 Mā is a verbal noun, 'the prohibition,' standing for the prohibited thing. — Samin explains that a prohibition itself cannot literally be recited; Baydawi allows the verbal noun sense. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:151/q06/p4 Mā introduces or is interpreted as an explanatory quotation of the prohibited matter. — Tabarsi records the explanatory possibility alongside the relative and object analyses. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 6:151/q07 What does حَرَّمَ mean?  [حَرَّمَ · meaning]
turns on: The narrow lexical ban versus an inclusive sense of firm prescription.
- 6:151/q07/p1 It fundamentally means to prevent or forbid, opposed to releasing or permitting. — Jishumi relates it to ḥajr and ḥaẓr; Tabatabai notes that forbidding can also carry the sense of commanding avoidance. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI(المجمع)]
- 6:151/q07/p2 It may denote a fixed and delineated divine prescription in this list. — Razi uses this wider sense to explain why positive duties occur under the introductory wording. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 6:151/q08 Where does عَلَيْكُمْ attach?  [حَرَّمَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Which of the two verbs governs the prepositional phrase.
- 6:151/q08/p1 It attaches to the nearer ḥarrama, stressing what God has prohibited for the hearers. — Qurtubi identifies a Basran analysis; Abu Hayyan says most adopt it; Alusi prefers it for the appeal to the audience's Lordship and need to abstain; Samin notes competing governance. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:151/q08/p2 It attaches to atlu, a Kufan competing-governance analysis. — Qurtubi reports the Kufan view; Abu Hayyan attributes preference to Ibn al-Shajari, though his account of proximity differs from Qurtubi's. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:151/q09 How is أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ constructed, and how do positive commands enter the list?  [أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَٰنًۭا · grammar]
turns on: The scope of ḥarrama, whether lā negates or prohibits, and whether a suppressed verb or clause supplies the positive duties.
- 6:151/q09/p1 An is explanatory and lā prohibitive, introducing a jussive prohibition; positive commands also imply bans on their opposites. — Farra favors jussive by the following prohibitions; Zamakhshari invokes implied bans on parental abuse, short measure, unjust speech and broken covenant; Samin defends him; Razi and E lmalili make the entailment explicit. Razi raises the apparent problem that duties appear under what God forbade. Razi objects that on an explanatory parse the coordinated parental kindness can appear forbidden. [29 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; JISHUMI(أبو علي وأبو مسلم,الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ITQAN(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء)+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 6:151/q09/p2 A pause after 'your Lord forbade' leaves 'upon you not to associate' as an independent exhortation or obligation. — Ibn al-Anbari and Tabarsi compare 5:105; Itqan defends direct address; Samin and Abu Hayyan call the break weak or distant; Tabatabai rejects imperative-noun ʿalaykum on contextual grounds. [14 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر؛ الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(البصير الأصفهاني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الشجري); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو بكر بن الأنباري,ابن الأنباري); ITQAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; TABATABAI-]
- 6:151/q09/p3 An is nominalizing with ordinary negative lā, in nominative or accusative apposition: the recited item is that they not associate. — Nahhas lists accusative apposition and nominative predicate; Ibn Atiyya admits both; Samin prefers the omitted subject 'the recited thing is that you not associate' over 'the forbidden thing'; Alusi sees semantic difficulty. [10 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 6:151/q09/p4 Anlā is governed by an understood 'I recite the prohibition of shirk' or 'I enjoin you'. — Zajjaj, Wahidi and Baghawi allow both ellipses; the closing waṣṣākum supports the enjoin reading; Abu Hayyan objects that added ellipsis departs from the apparent wording. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الزجاج); JISHUMI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:151/q09/p5 An is nominalizing; lā is extra, so the forbidden item is association itself. — Baghawi cites 7:12; Thalabi, Samin and Itqan report this; Zamakhshari and Abu Hayyan object that the ensuing positive commands and ordinary prohibitive lā resist it. [9 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ITQAN(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ)-]
- 6:151/q09/p6 An explains both prohibitions and commands through an omitted 'what He commanded'. — Abu Hayyan proposes the understood phrase; Ibn Kathir cites the closing testament and poetry; Imru al-Qays supplies an example of joining command and prohibition. Jishumi notes that the passage may detail recited commands rather than only forbidden acts. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(امرؤ القيس); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); TABATABAI; JISHUMI]
- 6:151/q09/p7 Anlā is governed by an omitted purpose lām: the recital prevents their association. — Zajjaj and Wahidi tie inventing bans to treating others as equal to God; Samin reports objections that this cannot account for following commands; Alusi rejects the strained construction. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 6:151/q09/p8 The verb tushrikū may be subjunctive after an or jussive under prohibitive lā. — Tabarsi and Wahidi note that later clauses can shift mood; Ibn Atiyya prefers the jussive because the prohibited act must remain in the recitation; Tabari adduces rajaz; Samin totals ten syntactic analyses: one genitive, three nominative, six accusative. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر؛ الفراء); JISHUMI(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; THALABI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 6:151/q09/p9 An is explanatory, but the positive commands coordinate with the opening invitation and recitation. — Abu Hayyan calls this clear, avoiding inclusion of duties under the prohibited acts; Samin reports the same response. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 6:151/q09/p10 Taḥrīm can mean a fixed, clearly delineated divine prescription, embracing both bans and duties. — Razi gives this as a separate answer to the objection that the list contains filial kindness. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 6:151/q10 What principle guides choosing among the grammatical analyses?  [أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ · grammar]
- 6:151/q10/p1 Prefer the likely, strong and eloquent construction over strained or anomalous parses. — Itqan advises listing plausible alternatives only where no reading clearly prevails; its separate example defends direct-address exhortation here as eloquent. [2 notes: ITQAN]

## 6:151/q11 What does شَيْـًۭٔا add?  [شَيْـًۭٔا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the indefinite names an object or an act of association.
- 6:151/q11/p1 It can be a cognate or verbal noun, excluding any act or form of association. — Baydawi and Samin allow object versus cognate noun; Alusi includes any form of association. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:151/q11/p2 It is an unrestricted object, excluding every possible thing worshipped alongside God. — Ibn Atiyya makes it comprehensive; Alusi includes any thing whatsoever. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:151/q12 What forms of association does the prohibition cover?  [أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether shirk denotes worship and belief alone or includes obedience and inward attachment.
- 6:151/q12/p1 It forbids worshipping or assigning any partner to God, including idols, celestial bodies, jinn, and attributed children. — Razi relates the four groups to Abraham's argument in 6:74, 6:76; Abu Hayyan and Study Quran enumerate the same partners; Qashani reads serving whim and Satan as idolatry. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI; QASHANI]
- 6:151/q12/p2 It also forbids obeying another person in disobedience to God. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives this as a second sense; Elmalili and Qashani connect illicit religious decrees with unauthorized authority. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الجوزي); ELMALILI; QASHANI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)]
- 6:151/q12/p3 Inward association includes recognizing anything besides God in the heart. — Bursevi describes the inward form and quotes a Persian verse urging exclusive unity. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 6:151/q13 Why is the ban on association placed first?  [أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا · rhetoric]
- 6:151/q13/p1 It is the gravest prohibition and foundation of sound belief and reform. — Biqai says removing vice precedes virtue; Ibn Ashur says belief opens success; Tabatabai calls shirk the greatest injustice, citing 4:116; Elmalili counts nine prohibitions with tawhid as foundation; Kuranyolu calls unity Islam's first condition. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:151/q13/p2 It directly counters the preceding polytheist errors and invented prohibitions. — Razi's four groups comprise idol, star, jinn and child-attribution worshippers; Qashani says whims had ruled lawful and forbidden. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî)]

## 6:151/q14 What consequences or assurances are attached to avoiding shirk?  [أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا · theology]
- 6:151/q14/p1 The duty holds even under severe torture. — Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Kathir report Ubadah's seven counsels and warnings even if burned, cut or crucified. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي ﷺ، عبادة بن الصامت); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:151/q14/p2 Dying without shirk leaves a person eligible for Paradise and forgiveness of lesser sins. — Ibn Kathir cites Abu Dharr's Gabriel report, a sacred report promising forgiveness with hope, 4:48 and 4:116, and Ibn Masud; the reports explicitly mention adultery, theft and wine. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الله تعالى,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 6:151/q15 What does kindness to parents require?  [وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَٰنًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The minimum legal prohibition and the full positive extent of iḥsān.
- 6:151/q15/p1 It requires positive care, honor, obedience within God's bounds, gentle speech and fulfillment of their rights. — Mawardi and Ibn Abi Hatim list rights and truthful speech; Qurtubi adds freeing them from bondage and avoiding domination; Alusi reports Ibn Abbas's ban on harsh replies, looks and raised voice; Ibn Kathir cites 17:23, 31:15, 2:83, and deeds loved by God; Elmalili forbids obeying parents in shirk. [24 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعض الأولياء); QASHANI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:151/q15/p2 Merely refraining from mistreatment is insufficient; the command also prohibits harming or neglecting parents. — Baydawi and Bursevi emphasize the positive wording; Razi, Nasafi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur, Elmalili and Islahi derive a ban on neglect or scolding. Ibn Kathir notes a variant 'wa-waṣṣā rabbuka' reinforcing the imperative. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(reader)]
- 6:151/q15/p3 Filial service is spiritually transformative. — Bursevi reports a holy man's service to his mother leading to meeting al-Khidr and cites the Persian depiction of Paradise beneath mothers' feet. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الأولياء)]

## 6:151/q16 Why does parental kindness immediately follow God's oneness?  [أَلَّا تُشْرِكُوا۟ بِهِۦ شَيْـًۭٔا وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَٰنًۭا · rhetoric]
- 6:151/q16/p1 Parents are the proximate cause of existence and care, after God as true cause; gratitude and social order require honoring them. — Jishumi, Tabarsi, Razi, Qashani, Biqai, Bursevi, Alusi and Study Quran cite nurture and parental blessing; Tabatabai says family mercy supports procreation, kinship and society; Ibn Kathir and Study Quran cite repeated pairings with worship. Abu Hayyan points readers to the related explanation in 2:83. [18 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(القطب الرازي); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 6:151/q16/p2 Disobedience to parents is the greatest sin after shirk. — Qashani links ignorance of parents' rights to ignorance of God's; Bursevi makes the ranking explicit; Tabatabai calls it perhaps the greatest thereafter. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 6:151/q17 How does إِحْسَٰنًۭا function grammatically?  [وَبِٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ إِحْسَٰنًۭا · grammar]
- 6:151/q17/p1 It is an accusative verbal noun standing for an omitted command to show kindness. — Nahhas calls it a verbal noun; Wahidi coordinates an understood 'do kindness' with not associating; Ibn Atiyya calls it an absolute object; Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur supply the command. Asad reports commentators' agreement that the command is implied. [7 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); ASAD-NOTES(commentators)]

## 6:151/q18 Why is الوالدين used for parents?  [ٱلْوَٰلِدَيْنِ · rhetoric]
- 6:151/q18/p1 Commands about goodness, prayer and care consistently use الوالدين rather than الأبوين. — Samarrai adduces 17:23 and 4:36 alongside this verse. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 6:151/q19 What child-killing practice and scope does this prohibition address?  [وَلَا تَقْتُلُوٓا۟ أَوْلَٰدَكُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate Arab practice exhausts the ruling or illustrates its wider scope.
- 6:151/q19/p1 It addresses pre-Islamic infanticide, especially burying daughters from poverty or shame, while wording includes sons and daughters. — Ibn Atiyya emphasizes walad includes both; Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir report boys also killed; Thalabi and others mention daughters buried alive; Islahi notes custom and honor; Tabatabai adds fathers' mistaken ownership claim. [30 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس؛ الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس وقتادة والسدي وابن جريج والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(إسحاق بن خلف); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 6:151/q19/p2 The wording reaches killing an unborn child for economic reasons. — Elmalili expressly extends it to abortion; Asad says it may refer to abortions; Kuranyolu says the ban may include unborn children. [3 notes: ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:151/q19/p3 The prohibition prevents parents from destroying God's creation, descendants and children entrusted to them. — Bursevi cites 11:6; Elmalili says children have a right to life; Islahi calls parents custodians. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:151/q20 What does إِمْلَٰقٍ mean?  [إِمْلَٰقٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root names a state of want, fear of it, hunger, spending, or ruin.
- 6:151/q20/p1 It means poverty, lack or depletion of property and provisions. — Majaz compares amlaqa with property vanishing; Mawardi gives insolvency and a bankrupt person's flattery; Jishumi and Qurtubi discuss the related root; Shammar allows intransitive impoverishment or transitive destruction; Aws ibn Hajar's verse witnesses wealth consumed by calamities; Ibn Atiyya speculates about black stones. - 6:151/q33/p2 Its violation destroys public security and the structure of society. — Tabatabai gives this consequence; Ibn Ashur says this verse's first set preserves life and ends mutual killing. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:151/q34 What inward meaning is given to killing and life here?  [وَلَا تَقْتُلُوا۟ ٱلنَّفْسَ · inward]
- 6:151/q34/p1 The deeper death is dying in pursuit of God, while one killed in His path remains alive with Him. — Bursevi joins this reflection to an anecdote in which a dead, handsome youth told Abu Saʿid al-Kharraz that lovers live after death, moving from one home to another. [2 notes: BURSEVI(أبو سعيد الخراز)]

## 6:151/q35 What does ذَٰلِكُمْ وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ refer to and mean?  [ذَٰلِكُمْ وَصَّىٰكُم بِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: The referent and nuance of the testament formula, and a proposed lexical distinction.
- 6:151/q35/p1 It points collectively to the preceding five commands and calls them God's firm, compassionate charge. — Nahhas allows 'that is the command' or 'God clarified and commanded'; Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn Abbas glossing the testament as God's religion; Ibn Atiyya cites poetry; Razi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi explain the tender presentation; Ibn Ashur says singular demonstrative can refer to a set. [21 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعشى); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 6:151/q35/p2 The wording affirms a decisive command and renewed covenant. — Qurtubi parses the attached address pronouns as vocative, subject pronoun as God; Samin allows demonstrative as subject with verbal predicate or accusative under an omitted verb; Alusi calls the nominal form emphatic. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:151/q35/p3 Waṣṣā and awṣā both mean enjoin, though one later distinction assigns the former to religion and latter to worldly matters. — Jishumi equates them; Samarrai contrasts religious counsel with worldly inheritance and cites 19:31 as an exception. [2 notes: JISHUMI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 6:151/q36 Why end with لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ تَعْقِلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The immediate sense of reasoning and why this ending differs from the next two.
- 6:151/q36/p1 Reason lets people understand duties, discern benefit and harm, restrain themselves and comply. — Mawardi gives understanding or rational avoidance of punishment; Jishumi, Tabarsi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Razi and Alusi stress grasping benefit and self-restraint; Samin calls reason the basis of responsibility; Tabatabai says these five are apparent once passions no longer obscure judgment. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:151/q36/p2 The first ending suits obvious prohibitions heedlessly violated; later endings concern remembrance and piety. — Qutb al-Razi connects heedlessness with reason and familiar duties with memory; Imam Razi instead distinguishes obvious first duties and subtler later duties; Alusi stresses restraint in mostly negative first set; Biqai and Islahi see a progression through reason, remembrance, then taqwa. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,القطب الرازي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 6:151/q36/p3 Laʿalla expresses hope from the human hearer's perspective. — Ibn Atiyya says God wants reason to distinguish benefit from harm, while hope belongs to the audience's viewpoint. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 6:151/q37 How do these commandments form the verse group and connect to the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: How the five duties relate to the next two sections and to the preceding controversy.
- 6:151/q37/p1 The list mixes positive and negative duties, and each form implies the opposite. — Kashshaf, Nasafi, Biqai, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur and Islahi explain why commands appear under an opening about prohibitions; Tabatabai says recitation reproduces revelation's mixed form. Tabarsi says forbidding shirk and commanding its avoidance are equivalent. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI]
- 6:151/q37/p2 The three sections set social, economic and religious order, each sealed by God's testament. — Ibn Ashur distinguishes belief and family in the first, orphan property and justice in the second, and the straight path in the third; Biqai says the repeated closure strengthens acceptance; Islahi links Abraham's and Moses' guidance. Kashshaf explains the fatha reading of the straight-path clause in 6:153 with an omitted causal lām and says the kasra reading conveys the same cause, supported by 72:18. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 6:151/q37/p3 They restate Abraham's religion and expose Quraysh's neglect beneath invented food taboos. — Islahi describes 6:145–154 as a reply to idolaters who claimed God ordained their way; 16:90 supplies a parallel criterion of justice and goodness; Kuranyolu identifies nine principal duties plus the straight path. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:151/q37/p4 The first verse begins with wrongs apparent to sound reason: shirk, filial neglect, infanticide, indecency and murder. — Tabatabai groups the five, and Ibn Ashur says they establish right belief, stable family and protected life. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:151/q38 What status do these commandments have across revealed scriptures?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the label emphasizes universal fixed law or a particular identification with Moses' tablets.
- 6:151/q38/p1 They are decisive, universal and unabrogated commandments, the Mother of the Book. — Ibn Abbas reports them as the muhkamāt of 3:7, common to all scriptures, with Paradise for observance and Hell for neglect; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi mention the Ten Commandments identification. Elmalili counts nine listed prohibitions with tawhid as their first foundation. Ibn Kathir reports Ibn Abbas calling them foundations of scripture. [18 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(كعب); ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); STUDYQURAN(Some early authorities); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 6:151/q38/p2 They correspond to the ten commandments given to Moses. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan transmit this identification; Itqan and Elmalili give lists including monotheism, false oath, filial duty, murder, adultery, theft, testimony, coveting and Sabbath rules. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN(أبو عبيد); ELMALILI(Bikâî)]

## 6:151/q39 What do reports say about the opening of the Torah?  [قُلْ تَعَالَوْا۟ أَتْلُ مَا حَرَّمَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَيْكُمْ · reports]
turns on: The wording and count of the reported Torah correspondence.
- 6:151/q39/p1 Kaʿb says this opening command or the ten commandments were first in the Torah. — Thalabi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabarsi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Durr, Itqan, Elmalili and Alusi transmit variants: first thing, first verse with basmala, or first ten revealed verses. [11 notes: THALABI(كعب الأحبار); IBNATIYYA-FULL(كعب الأحبار); TABRISI(كعب الأحبار); QURTUBI-FULL(كعب الأحبار); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(كعب الأحبار); DURR-FULL(كعب); ITQAN(أبو عبيد,كعب); ELMALILI(Ka‘bu’l-Ahbâr); ALUSI-FULL(كعب)]
- 6:151/q39/p2 Some describe them more generally as the ten commandments revealed to Moses. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan present this alongside Ibn Abbas's universal-commandment report. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(Bikâî)]

## 6:151/q40 What is said of the Prophet's sealed testament and the pledge on these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: Distinct reported uses of the verses as testament, pledge, and prophetic address.
- 6:151/q40/p1 Ibn Masud calls 6:151–153 the Prophet's sealed written testament. — Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Alusi and Tirmidhi transmit it; Tirmidhi grades it ḥasan gharīb, while Alusi says ḥasan. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); TIRMIDHI(عبد الله)]
- 6:151/q40/p2 The Prophet took a pledge on the commands: fulfillment is rewarded; a breach may be punished here or left to God's will after death. — Ibn Abi Hatim gives seven traits and the punishment-or-pardon rule; Ibn Kathir says Hakim graded the extended report authentic while the two Sahihs have shorter pledges; Durr and Alusi repeat the three-verse pledge, with worldly punishment expiating. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(النبي ﷺ، عبادة بن الصامت); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت)]
- 6:151/q40/p3 Al-Rabi ibn Khuthaym treated the verses as a sealed letter or written counsel from the Prophet. — Thalabi, Qurtubi and Durr report reciting them to a companion and writing them for a questioner. [4 notes: THALABI(الربيع بن خيثم); QURTUBI-FULL(ربيع بن خيثم); DURR-FULL(الربيع بن خثيم)]
- 6:151/q40/p4 At Mina, the Prophet recited the commandments to a tribe while inviting them to monotheism, messenger-belief and protection. — Durr names Mafruq and Hani's approval, a following recital of 16:90, a promise concerning Persian lands, and a quotation of 33:45 before the Prophet left with Abu Bakr. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 6:151/q40/p5 At the Farewell Pilgrimage the Prophet named shirk, unlawful killing, adultery and theft as prohibitions. — Durr transmits this four-item report. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 6:151/q41 What is reported about the revelation and virtue of al-Anʿām?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the sūra came all at once and which verses, if any, were Medinan; reports also differ on escorting angels.
- 6:151/q41/p1 These three verses, 6:151–153, were a Medinan exception. — Ibn Abbas and Shahr ibn Hawshab report this against the whole-sūra Meccan account; Study Quran notes early claims of some Medinan verses. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,شهر بن حوشب); STUDYQURAN(Some early authorities)]
- 6:151/q41/p2 The sūra descended as a whole in Mecca, accompanied by angels. — Reports vary in count: one thousand, seventy thousand, or five hundred, and one describes ranks from heaven to earth; the Prophet saw the horizon filled. Ali's report says usual revelation was five verses at a time and claims its recitation heals the ill. [7 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو جحيفة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,شهر بن حوشب,عطاء,علي بن أبي طالب,مجاهد,محمد بن المنكدر)]
- 6:151/q41/p3 Other reports except 6:91 or 6:111 from the Meccan revelation. — Kalbi locates 6:91 with a Jewish man who denied revelation; Abu Juhayfa excepts 6:111. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو جحيفة,الكلبي)]
- 6:151/q41/p4 A weakly transmitted report promises Paradise to one who loves and recites al-Anʿām. — Durr attributes it to Anas and labels its transmission weak. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أنس)]

## 6:151/q42 What do the commands imply about human acts and restraint?  [* · theology]
turns on: Human moral agency and whether deliberate restraint counts as an act.
- 6:151/q42/p1 People are unforced, responsible agents whose choices merit reward and punishment. — Jishumi says God leaves people free for testing and evidence-based obedience, against the Compulsionists' view of created human acts; he condemns habit, imitation, doubt, neglect of inquiry and worldly motives. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 6:151/q42/p2 Refraining from a forbidden act is itself an obligatory act with deserved reward or punishment. — Tabarsi identifies this as his school's principle and takes the verse as evidence. [1 notes: TABRISI]
