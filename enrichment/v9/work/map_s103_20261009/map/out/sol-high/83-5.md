# 83:5: 12 questions

## 83:5/q01 What does the lām in 'for a tremendous day' express?  [لِيَوْمٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām specifies when resurrection occurs or its purpose, and whether 'account of' is supplied.
- 83:5/q01/p1 It marks the appointed time of resurrection. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the temporal lām also answers the presumed objection that earlier generations have not yet been raised: the event has a set time; he mentions 17:78. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 83:5/q01/p2 It makes the Day the cause or purpose of resurrection. — Al-Ālūsī says the Day, great because of what it contains, is the cause of resurrection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 83:5/q01/p3 An omitted 'account of' is understood before 'day.' — Al-Ālūsī reports this as an alternative supplied wording. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 83:5/q02 How is the following 'day when people stand' read and related to 'a tremendous day'?  [لِيَوْمٍ عَظِيمٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The case of the following yawm and whether it is governed by an implied or preceding verb, refers back to 'a tremendous day,' or begins a new clause.
- 83:5/q02/p1 Its yawm is accusative, governed by an implied 'they are raised.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies a verb such as 'they are raised'; al-Zajjāj, reported by al-Rāzī, likewise makes it governed by 'they will be resurrected,' hence resurrection on the Day; Abū Ḥayyān lists the implied verb. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 83:5/q02/p2 Its yawm is an accusative expression of time, like 'today' meaning 'now.' — Al-Akhfash compares 'so-and-so is good today' and says one could say 'he is still today' even at night, using 'today' for the present time. [2 notes: AKHFASH]
- 83:5/q02/p3 It can be genitive as an appositive or substitute for 'a tremendous day.' — Al-Rāzī reports a third, genitive reading as apposition; al-Akhfash says genitive is possible but knows no one who reads it so; Abū Ḥayyān allows construal with 'a tremendous day' and reports al-Farrāʾ's substitute analysis. [3 notes: AKHFASH; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 83:5/q02/p4 It is accusative because it is annexed to an imperfect verb, although a genitive construction is possible. — Al-Farrāʾ's account is reported by al-Rāzī. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 83:5/q02/p5 Its accusative can be governed by the preceding 'they will be resurrected.' — Abū Ḥayyān lists mabʿūthūn as a possible governor. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 83:5/q02/p6 It is read nominative, with the sense 'that is a day.' — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this reading to Zayd ibn ʿAlī. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 83:5/q03 What is the tremendous day, and what makes it tremendous?  [يَوْمٍ عَظِيمٍۢ · referent]
- 83:5/q03/p1 It is the Day of Resurrection and judgment, dreadful for its gathering, reckoning, and consequences. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Asad, Iṣlāḥī, and Kur'an Yolu identify resurrection or final judgment. Al-Dāmghānī glosses ʿaẓīm as dreadful or awesome. Al-Ṭabarī stresses its terrible significance; al-Ālūsī says it exceeds measure and is great because of what it contains. Al-Qurṭubī and Asad mention standing before the Lord of all worlds; Kur'an Yolu includes punishment and entry into Paradise or Hell. Al-Biqāʿī mentions gathering, accounting, reward and punishment, and says only God knows its full reality; he likens resurrection to an awakening from sleep. Bursevī says even a speck or mustard seed is reckoned. Qāshānī says inner virtues and vices become visible, with shame and consequence. Ibn ʿAmr, reported by Mujāhid, describes distress rising until sweat bridles an unbeliever, while believers sit on golden chairs beneath cloud shade. [17 notes: MUJAHID(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 83:5/q04 What does the question imply about the defrauders' expectation of that Day?  [* · interpretation]
- 83:5/q04/p1 Their cheating betrays failure to expect resurrection and its accounting; even expectation would restrain them. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī cast the words as a warning and question about being raised after death. Al-Zajjāj and al-Nasafī say belief or expectation would stop short measure, including the smallest amount; al-Nasafī calls the audacity astonishing. Qāshānī links denial to exposure of hidden qualities. Al-Biqāʿī says habitual vice follows from feeling safe from punishment and denying final accounting, although decisive proofs exist. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī hear wonder and rebuke; Iṣlāḥī says people seek fair treatment while cheating others. Asad says wrongdoers will be raised and called to account. Al-Zamakhsharī, reported by al-Ālūsī, portrays these defrauders as worse than other unbelievers: others are described as at least weakly supposing, while these are not. Al-Ālūsī explains that the denial rhetorically treats them as lacking even weak belief. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:5/q05 Why does the warning speak of merely thinking of the great Day?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether ẓann retains its ordinary sense of supposition or denotes certainty; the former makes even the weaker expectation sufficient to deter fraud.
- 83:5/q05/p1 Even a weak supposition of resurrection should deter wrongdoing; certainty would deter it still more. — Abū al-Fatḥ, reported by the Burhān author, permits both senses of ẓann but calls its ordinary sense stronger; the author prefers it for the same ascending argument. Al-Biqāʿī says even mere suspicion should restrain them despite decisive proofs. Bursevī says mention of mere supposition intensifies the warning, though believers need certainty. Al-Ālūsī finds the ordinary meaning more apt and eloquent, and says even weak belief in such a Day would restrain these crimes. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; BURHAN(أبو الفتح)+]
- 83:5/q05/p2 The 'thinking' can mean certainty of resurrection. — Abū al-Fatḥ allows this sense, though he ranks ordinary supposition stronger; al-Ālūsī reports that some take the belief to mean certainty, while he favors the ordinary sense. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو الفتح); ALUSI-FULL]

## 83:5/q06 What rhetorical force does the question and its wording have?  [* · rhetoric]
- 83:5/q06/p1 It rebukes the cheaters and expresses astonishment at their conduct. — Al-Nasafī says the interrogative joins a negation for rebuke, rather than serving as a particle of attention. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the far demonstrative distances the cheaters from God's mercy. Iṣlāḥī says all should fear the Day attested by human nature; their attitude is astonishing. Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān say the rebuke, the Day's greatness, and people's humbled standing before the Lord of all worlds emphasize the gravity of cheating; Abū Ḥayyān also points to the wording of divine lordship. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 83:5/q07 What does the great-Day warning establish about fraud and other injustice?  [* · interpretation]
- 83:5/q07/p1 Fraud, even when it appears slight, is gravely wrong, and the threat extends to injustice in dealings and acts. — Al-Qurṭubī points to the rebuke, the Day's description, and people's submission as signs of the gravity of fraud and injustice in all dealings and acts. Abū Ḥayyān similarly cites the Day's greatness, humbled standing, and divine lordship. Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by al-Biqāʿī, says every deed is recorded in 82:10 and weighed exactly in 21:47, so seemingly small fraud is a major crime warranting the warning. The Arab's challenge to ʿAbd al-Malik, given by al-Qurṭubī and al-Nasafī, applies it to taking Muslims' property as a ruler. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(عبد الملك بن مروان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 83:5/q08 What did the Arab say to ʿAbd al-Malik about the warning?  [* · reports]
- 83:5/q08/p1 He asked how the threat to short measurers applied to a ruler who took Muslims' wealth without measuring or weighing it. — Al-Qurṭubī says the Arab rebuked ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Marwān for taking Muslim property without measure. Al-Nasafī reports his challenge after he had heard the mighty threat against cheaters. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(عبد الملك بن مروان)]

## 83:5/q09 What inward meaning is given to short measure here?  [* · inward]
- 83:5/q09/p1 The limited self shortchanges the spirit in true essential monotheism because it cannot contain it. — Bursevī says true essential tawḥīd has no counterpart and should not be weighed; formal monotheism, by contrast, is the self's due from the spirit. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 83:5/q10 What supports the necessity of a Day of judgment?  [* · theology]
turns on: Different grounds supplied for the necessity of judgment: human moral nature or the divine title 'Lord of the worlds.'
- 83:5/q10/p1 God's justice and the human moral sense require distinct outcomes for the righteous and wrongdoers. — Iṣlāḥī says God created people with love of justice and awareness of good and evil, so He must reward those honoring that nature and punish those disregarding it. Deniers resist the resulting judgment. He says the Qur'an repeatedly uses this reasoning and refers to his treatment of 75:14–15; he also says the great Day is attested by human nature. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:5/q10/p2 The description 'Lord of the worlds' itself supports the Day's majesty, necessity, and judgments. — Iṣlāḥī points to arguments he says he explained elsewhere, without repeating them in these notes. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 83:5/q11 How does this warning connect with al-Infiṭār and other Qur'anic passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Which related passage and line of reasoning the source uses to connect the warning.
- 83:5/q11/p1 This sūrah supplements al-Infiṭār's division of the righteous and defiant, arguing from moral nature where al-Infiṭār argues from the human body. — Iṣlāḥī says the two sūrahs share a theme; al-Infiṭār points to God's attributes shown in the human body, while this sūrah develops the distinction at its close through people's God-given moral nature. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 83:5/q11/p2 The warning follows from the recording and exact weighing of every deed. — Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by al-Biqāʿī, connects it to 82:10 on the record of deeds and 21:47 on their exact weighing; seemingly small fraud deserves a great warning. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 83:5/q12 Where does one report place this verse's revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 83:5/q12/p1 It places this verse among the verses revealed in Makkah, excepting the first four verses of the sūrah. — The Study Quran reports this as another account of the sūrah's place of revelation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Others)]
