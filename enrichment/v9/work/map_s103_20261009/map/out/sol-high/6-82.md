# 6:82: 19 questions

## 6:82/q01 What does faith mean here?  [ءَامَنُوا۟ إِيمَٰنَهُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether faith names assent alone or also the acts and freedom from wrongdoing that perfect it.
- 6:82/q01/p1 It is belief in God alone and in what must be believed. — Ibn Ashur says the omitted object is God, supplied by the context. Al-Alusi says it includes what is obligatory to believe. Tabatabai says initial assent to lordship is qualified by rejection of partners; Ibn Ashur notes Abraham's people acknowledged God while adding gods, like Arab polytheists. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 6:82/q01/p2 In religious usage faith includes doing obligations and avoiding major sins. — Jishumi distinguishes the linguistic sense, affirmation, from this religious usage. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 6:82/q01/p3 Faith can mean assent, even where a major sin or association is also present. — Razi's school argues that adding freedom from wrongdoing would be redundant if it already formed part of faith. Al-Alusi cites 12:106 for faith in God coexisting with association; he also says faith may be obscured or alternate with unbelief. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:82/q01/p4 The secure combine faith with good works of every kind. — Study Quran attributes this to Razi. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 6:82/q02 Which believers receive this promise?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wording's general form governs or the occasion and an unrestricted reading of wrongdoing limit its subjects.
- 6:82/q02/p1 Believers generally, across communities, who meet the verse's condition. — Ibn al-Jawzi records a general view. Abu Hayyan calls general scope apparent. Tabatabai argues that faith and wrongdoing affect human nature in every era, rejecting restriction to Abraham's community. Wahidi derives ultimate safety for whoever dies without shirk; the traveler reports illustrate application beyond Abraham. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); WAHIDI-BASIT; ITQAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة)+; TABATABAI+]
- 6:82/q02/p2 Abraham alone. — Ali is reported to exclude this Muslim community. Some accounts specify Abraham alone rather than his companions. Tabari says the unrestricted wrong reading was limited by some to Abraham. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(علي); IBNABIHATIM(علي); MAWARDI-FULL(علي كرم الله وجهه); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب وعكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن أبي طالب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); TABATABAI-]
- 6:82/q02/p3 Abraham and his companions. — A report from Ali names Abraham and his companions and expressly excludes this Muslim community; another version narrows it to Abraham alone. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن أبي طالب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي); DURR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI-]
- 6:82/q02/p4 Those who migrated to Medina. — Attributed to Ikrima; Tabari lists the Emigrants as another restriction used to explain an all-wrongdoing reading. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب وعكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 6:82/q03 What does 'mix' mean here?  [يَلْبِسُوٓا۟ إِيمَٰنَهُم · meaning]
- 6:82/q03/p1 It means combining or covering faith with something alien to it. — Jishumi glosses lubs as mixing. Ibn Ashur describes a metaphor of mixing like substances, citing 2:42, especially claims about lordship. Tabatabai calls it covering innate faith and obstructing its beneficial effect rather than erasing it. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 6:82/q04 How is yulbisū read?  [يَلْبِسُوٓا۟ · readings]
turns on: The verb's vocalization and form, and whether Mujahid's wording counts as a reading or an explanatory gloss.
- 6:82/q04/p1 The majority read yalbisū with an open yāʾ, 'they mix'. — Al-Samin gives the majority vocalization and its simple-verb sense. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:82/q04/p2 Ikrima reads yulbisū with a rounded yāʾ, 'they make mix'. — Al-Samin derives it from form IV; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan also report the vocalization. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:82/q04/p3 Mujahid's bi-shirk replaces bi-ẓulm as an explanatory wording. — Ibn Atiyya reports the wording as a reading; Abu Hayyan says it is a gloss, not a canonical reading, since it differs from the written text. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وعكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 6:82/q05 What does ẓulm mean in this verse?  [بِظُلْمٍ · meaning]

- 6:82/q05/p1 It is shirk, associating partners with God, including idol worship. — The recurrent proof is the Prophet's explanation to worried Companions using Luqman 31:13; Tirmidhi grades that report hasan sahih. Abu Bakr, Ibn Abbas, Ubayy, Salman, Hudhayfa, Alqama, Ibrahim, Qatada, Mujahid and others are reported with this gloss. Tabari prefers the sound prophetic report. Ibn Ashur says shirk violates God's exclusive right to worship; Biqai says the indefinite term includes every degree of shirk. Al-Alusi says 'great injustice' points to its fullest instance, though the word can denote other wrongs. Idolaters claimed intercession and nearness to God (39:3). - 6:82/q05/p2 It covers every forbidden act or neglected command, or injury to oneself or others. — Tabari says the fully general reading would assure only the sinless and its proponents restrict the verse's audience; others leave lesser sinners to God's will. Mawardi records general and restricted versions. Baydawi mentions 'sin'. A commentator cited by Tabatabai includes religious and worldly harm, but Tabatabai rejects treating accidental bodily damage as the verse's concern and says that scope would leave no real instance. Ibn al-Jawzi's root definitions include putting something outside its place or acting concerning what one does not own; Tabarsi cites poetry on cutting a camel wrongly and diverting rain. Tabatabai takes these varied reports as degrees of a general term. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 6:82/q05/p5 It is hidden association through any remnant of self or turning to other than God. — Qashani makes essential monotheism unmingled with self-manifestation the condition of true security; Ibn Tahir says the faithful do not turn elsewhere during trials. [2 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(ابن طاهر)]
- 6:82/q05/p6 In broader Qur'anic usage the root can mean denial, diminution, or theft. — Ibn al-Jawzi cites denial of signs at 7:9, 7:136, 17:59; diminution at 4:49, 18:33, 21:47; theft at 5:38-39 and 12:75. These are lexical comparisons, not claims that 6:82 means theft or diminution. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 6:82/q06 How is the verse's clause structure parsed?  [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَلَمْ يَلْبِسُوٓا۟ أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمُ ٱلْأَمْنُ وَهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the opening relative phrase is an answer or independent subject, how the nested nouns predicate one another, and whether the two wāw clauses are relative, circumstantial or independent.
- 6:82/q06/p1 The relative phrase is a subject; 'those' starts a nested subject clause, with 'for them is security' as its predicate. — Nahhas makes alladhina and ulaika successive subjects, al-amn the subject of lahum, and the full nested clause the first predicate; al-Alusi prefers this broad parse. Al-Samin gives the explicit three-subject nesting; Ibn Atiyya makes al-amn nominative and lahum prepositional predicate. Abu Hayyan notes an omitted subject or predicate can be supplied. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:82/q06/p2 The relative phrase is predicate of an omitted 'they'; 'those' begins a new sentence. — Al-Samin ties this to a spoken answer by Abraham, his people, or God instructing the Prophet, and permits an omitted 'say/they said'. Al-Alusi calls the omitted-subject analysis remote. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 6:82/q06/p3 'Those' is apposition to the relative phrase and 'for them' its predicate, with security a governed subject or delayed subject. — Al-Samin records both variants of the placement/function of lahum and al-amn; al-Alusi calls the appositional alternative remote. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 6:82/q06/p4 'Those' is a second subject, 'for them' its predicate, and 'security' an active subject. — Al-Samin lists this as a fourth independent-report parse. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:82/q06/p5 'Those' is a separator; 'for them is security' predicates the relative phrase. — Attributed to Nahhas and al-Hawfi by al-Samin, who calls it odd because demonstratives do not serve as separators; Abu Hayyan calls their analysis remote. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر النحاس والحوفي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:82/q06/p6 'They did not mix' continues the relative clause. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin allow coordination under alladhina. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:82/q06/p7 'They did not mix' is a circumstantial clause. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin cite 19:20 as a parallel negative circumstance. Al-Samin rejects Ibn Asfur's rarity claim and Ibn Khuruf's mandatory-waw claim. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:82/q06/p8 'And they are guided' is a second nominal sentence, either independent or circumstantial. — Nahhas gives it a subject-predicate structure. Al-Samin allows independence or circumstance; Ibn Ashur prefers the full sentence as coordinated with the earlier nominal sentence, citing 64:1 and 57:2. [4 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:82/q07 What security is promised?  [لَهُمُ ٱلْأَمْنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether security excludes any punishment or only eternal punishment, and whether it includes worldly and inward safety.
- 6:82/q07/p1 Safety from God's punishment, especially on Judgment Day. — Tabari contrasts believers' safety with idolaters' worldly fear and painful afterlife punishment. Ibn Abbas specifies punishment; Jishumi Judgment Day; Tabarsi adds reward; Bursawi says only shirk-free believers have this protection. Ibn Kathir includes the Day of Judgment. The believer who dies without shirk ultimately escapes the Fire, says Wahidi. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 6:82/q07/p2 Safety specifically from eternal punishment; lesser sinners remain between fear and hope. — Al-Alusi reports this qualification and says even a sinless-condition reading does not prove all sinners certainly punished. Tabatabai says the immediate debate concerns shirk and eternal punishment; a cited commentator says lesser sins leave hope and fear. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين)]
- 6:82/q07/p3 Security includes worldly safety or consequences and afterlife safety. — Ibn Ashur says al-amn is the same generic security of 6:81 and covers worldly destruction and afterlife punishment since monotheism was then required. The commentator quoted by Tabatabai includes religious punishment and worldly consequences, with mercy or punishment possible in both. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI-]
- 6:82/q07/p4 It is true inward safety from turning to any refuge besides God. — Ibn Tahir says the believer relies on God in trials; Qashani makes freedom from hidden self-association its condition. Tabatabai elsewhere cites assurances of God's friends having no fear or grief. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن طاهر); QASHANI; TABATABAI]
- 6:82/q07/p5 Absolute safety from punishment is unavailable to ordinary accountable people. — A commentator cited by Tabatabai reserves it for the awe of spiritually complete people; Tabatabai objects that this would leave the verse without a real instance or argumentative force. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI-]

## 6:82/q08 What does it mean that they are guided?  [وَهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance names truth and religion now, entrance to Paradise, or both worldly and final guidance.
- 6:82/q08/p1 They follow truth and God's religion. — Ibn Abbas says directed to God's religion; Tabarsi says judged to follow truth; Bursawi and Al-Alusi contrast them with all others in error. Ibn Ishaq adds proof through knowledge and uprightness; Islahi says reliance on God in all affairs rather than deities. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL(الصديق); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:82/q08/p2 They are guided to Paradise or the path ensuring safety from eternal punishment. — Jishumi and Tabarsi report Paradise as a specific gloss; Al-Alusi reports the secure path. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:82/q08/p3 They are guided in this worldly life. — Qurtubi specifies the present world; Ibn Kathir extends guidance to this life and the next. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 6:82/q08/p4 They are guided both in this life and the next. — Ibn Kathir states both horizons. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 6:82/q09 What emphasis does the wording give to security and guidance?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ لَهُمُ ٱلْأَمْنُ وَهُم مُّهْتَدُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pronoun in 'they are guided' functions as a separator or the subject of a second sentence.
- 6:82/q09/p1 The demonstrative and nominal clauses strongly establish the promised predicates for the described believers. — Ibn Ashur says 'those' points to their description as the basis of entitlement, comparing 2:5; the lam marks exclusive settled possession, stronger than merely 'they are safe'. Tabatabai notes several linked nominal clauses. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:82/q09/p2 If 'they' is a separator, guidance is exclusive to the monotheists described. — Ibn Ashur cites 2:55 and 9:104 for the construction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:82/q09/p3 If 'they are guided' is its own nominal sentence, it adds guidance without grammatical exclusivity. — Ibn Ashur says this makes coordination smoother, with analogies at 64:1 and 57:2. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:82/q10 Who speaks the answer to Abraham's question?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether this clause is quoted speech continuing the exchange or God's independent verdict, and whether the idolaters could credibly concede Abraham's case.
- 6:82/q10/p1 God declares the ruling that resolves Abraham's dispute. — Tabari prefers this: idolaters would not concede the monotheism they opposed. Other sources call it a divine verdict or independent resumption; Al-Alusi says a resumption lacks syntactic attachment despite semantic relation and answers Ibn Hisham's objection. Ibn Ashur says it confirms Abraham. Zajjaj allows it. Abu Hayyan also reports God commanding Abraham to say it or the Prophet to answer. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد وابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج); JISHUMI(ابن زيد وابن إسحاق وأبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(محمد بن إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن هشام,محمد بن إسحاق وابن زيد والجبائي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 6:82/q10/p2 Abraham answers his own rhetorical question. — Ibn Abbas compares this to a teacher who poses and answers a question; Abu Hayyan calls it the apparent speaker, and Ibn Ashur says its undeniable answer silences opponents. Zajjaj allows the attribution. Razi calls it Abraham's answer defining perfected faith and action. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الزجاج); TABRISI(علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(علي); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,جمهور المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 6:82/q10/p3 Abraham's people answer and inadvertently concede his proof. — Ibn Jurayj says their answer became evidence against them. Muqatil says they conceded. Ibn Ashur objects that their continued obstinacy and attempted burning make concession unlikely, and Tabari likewise objects that idolaters would not admit monotheism. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); JISHUMI(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TAB-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 6:82/q11 What happened when early Muslims heard this verse?  [* · reports]
- 6:82/q11/p1 They feared ordinary self-wronging disqualified them; the Prophet explained that the wrong was shirk and cited Luqman 31:13. — Ibn Masud transmits the recurrent account in Bukhari, Muslim, Ahmad and others; Tirmidhi calls his route hasan sahih. ## 6:82/q12 Which newly converted people were counted among the secure?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported person's identity and the circumstances of conversion and death.
- 6:82/q12/p1 A traveler or Bedouin accepted Islam, died in an accident after little work, and was declared secure and guided. — Jarir's versions mention a camel stumbling in a rodent hole, hunger, fruit from Paradise or houris, and niche burial; Ibn Abbas's version describes a destitute man who left family and possessions to learn from the Prophet. The details vary while the convert's prompt accidental death and great reward agree. [11 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس، النبي ﷺ); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جرير بن عبد الله); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(جرير بن عبد الله,رسول الله)]
- 6:82/q12/p2 A former enemy who had killed Muslims converted, fought for them, and died. — The man asked whether Islam would benefit him despite his past. The narrator thought the verse was revealed about him. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM; DURR-FULL(بكر بن سوادة)]
- 6:82/q12/p3 A new Muslim fought and was martyred after a question about the verse. — Ibrahim al-Taymi tells this as an application by the Prophet; Tabatabai notes a similar report. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); TABATABAI]
- 6:82/q12/p4 The Prophet himself was told that he belonged among those described. — Ibn Kathir cites Ibn Mardawayh's report following revelation. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 6:82/q13 What conduct is associated with the secure and guided?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the report describes practical traits of the secure or the monotheistic basis of their worship.
- 6:82/q13/p1 They thank God for gifts, endure deprivation, seek forgiveness after wrong, and pardon others' wrongs. — The four traits recur in reports from Ibn Umar and others; variant wording speaks of patience in trials and gratitude in ease. - 6:82/q13/p2 They worship God alone and associate nothing with Him. — Kuranyolu quotes the Prophet on God's right over His servants; Muqatil and Ibn Kathir describe exclusive worship. ## 6:82/q14 What follows for believers who commit major sins?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether faith can coexist with grave sin, whether 'wrongdoing' means shirk or sin generally, and whether lack of assurance entails certain punishment.
- 6:82/q14/p1 A believer may sin gravely; absence of shirk assures eventual escape from the Fire, while lesser punishment remains possible. — Wahidi explicitly infers ultimate security for one dying without shirk. Razi's school treats faith as compatible with major sin. Tabarsi answers al-Balkhi that the verse's implication does not itself prove security for an unrepentant major sinner and other evidence makes him insecure. Al-Alusi says denying certainty of security does not prove certainty of punishment; Tabatabai holds shirk is unforgivable unlike other sins. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); TABRISI(البلخي); ALUSI-FULL(some exegetes); TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين)]
- 6:82/q14/p2 A grave sinner lacks the verse's security and faces the threat of punishment. — The Mu'tazilites infer two conditions, faith plus no wrongdoing. Al-Balkhi argues shirk alone would give the unrepentant major sinner security and conflicts with opposition to irja; Zamakhshari appeals to the verb 'mix' and 9:36. Al-Alusi reports their rejection of the solitary hadith as unable to override categorical proof. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي والبلخي,المعتزلة); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); TABRISI(البلخي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 6:82/q14/p3 Security varies by the spiritual level of the person and the wrong that obstructs faith at that level. — Tabatabai begins with shirk at monotheism's threshold, then major sins under law, then disliked acts, omitted recommendations and inward obstacles to nearness. Unintentional mistakes do not block faith. The immediate Abrahamic argument still addresses shirk. [9 notes: TABATABAI]
- 6:82/q14/p4 The verse assures only a particular sinless group; other monotheist sinners remain subject to God's will. — Tabari reports this solution to the general wrongdoing interpretation, identifying Abraham or the Emigrants as proposed groups and leaving pardon or punishment of other sinners open. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 6:82/q15 How does this answer complete Abraham's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is read as the immediate adjudication of 6:81 alone or as part of the sura's wider prophetic argument.
- 6:82/q15/p1 It decides which side of the dispute deserves security: exclusive worship of God defeats idols and their claimed protection. — Tabari links it to 6:81. Mawardi offers three accounts: powerless idols versus the controller of benefit and harm; one God versus many; and small gods paired with the great God. The proof arose through God's prompting or direct teaching. Ibn Zayd contrasts fearing other beings without fearing God. Muqatil says God gave Abraham the proof and raised him. Razi frames the answer as perfected knowledge and practice. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:82/q15/p2 It links Abraham's pure worship with Muhammad's call to restore that religion for Abraham's descendants. — Islahi says Israelite and Ishmaelite claims to Abraham explain his prominence; Judaism, Christianity and Ishmaelite polytheism are later fabrications. Muhammad follows earlier prophets' guidance and preaches without coercing belief. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:82/q16 What authority does the transmitted explanation carry?  [* · reports]
turns on: The evidentiary rank of Prophetic and Companion explanations against arguments drawn from general wording and theology.
- 6:82/q16/p1 The Prophetic explanation specifying shirk is decisive for this verse. — Tabari prefers Ibn Masud's sound report; Abu Hayyan rejects Zamakhshari's grave-sin reading because the Prophet expressly explained the word. Burhan presents Bukhari's report as a Prophetic gloss, alongside Prophetic glosses of easy reckoning as presentation (84:8), strength as archery rather than cavalry (8:60), and worship as supplication (40:60). [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; TAB-FULL+]
- 6:82/q16/p2 A solitary report cannot override a categorical argument for the sin reading. — Al-Alusi reports the Mu'tazili response to the hadith; their argument is that shirk and faith are opposites and 'mixing' must therefore refer to sin. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:82/q16/p3 Companion exegesis has transmitted authority, though the basis and strength of reports require assessment. — Al-Hakim likens Companion interpretation to a raised report. Abu al-Khattab questions its binding force; Burhan favors evidentiary force because tafsir is transmitted, while explaining Ahmad's warning that many reports lack sound connected chains, not that none are sound. Ibn Masud's knowledge of revelations and learning ten verses with practice illustrate the tradition. The discussion of 9:14 raises an analogy about human action and divine power. [5 notes: BURHAN(الحاكم,الميموني عن أحمد بن حنبل,مسروق عن عبد الله بن مسعود); BURHAN(أبو الخطاب)+]
- 6:82/q16/p4 An interpretation may specify the intended application of general wording. — Itqan says Ibn Abbas recognized the broad wording while naming its intended referent, just as Companions heard general ẓulm and the Prophet specified shirk. Tabatabai treats varied applications of wrongdoing as degrees of a general word. [2 notes: ITQAN; TABATABAI]

## 6:82/q17 Does Abraham's community need only monotheism because an existing civil code supplied law?  [* · theology]
turns on: The proposed chronology, the code's polytheism, and whether later Torah material can establish Mosaic borrowing.
- 6:82/q17/p1 Yes; a commentator proposes Hammurabi's code supplied civil and ethical rules. — The proposal explains a monotheism-only condition for Abraham's people while Muslims have a complete revealed law. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI-]
- 6:82/q17/p2 No; the Hammurabi proposal fails historically and textually. — Tabatabai says its chronology does not fit Abraham, Hammurabi invokes many gods, and the Torah's legal material cannot show Mosaic dependence on Hammurabi: the Torah was lost in Babylonian captivity and later reconstructed amid Babylonian customs; nonrevealed rules can still be sound. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 6:82/q18 What relative revelation order is inferred from the Prophetic explanation?  [* · sciences]
- 6:82/q18/p1 Al-An'am followed Luqman. — Tabatabai identifies the 'righteous servant' quoted in the report as Luqman and infers the order from the explanation's use of Luqman 31:13. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 6:82/q19 How is the nearby statement about raising degrees read?  [* · readings]
turns on: The person marker and case/construction of 'degrees', with a separate parallel at 12:76.
- 6:82/q19/p1 It is read with nūn and a genitive annexation. — Jishumi lists this as one reading of the adjacent 'We raise' wording. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 6:82/q19/p2 It is read with nūn and an accusative indefinite 'degrees'. — Jishumi lists this as another reading. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
