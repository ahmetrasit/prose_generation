# 39:15: 18 questions

## 39:15/q01 What occasion and audience prompted the challenge to worship as they wished?  [فَٱعْبُدُوا۟ مَا شِئْتُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ · reports]
- 39:15/q01/p1 It addresses the polytheists who urged Muhammad to abandon his religion and follow his forefathers' faith. — Jishumi reports an invitation to follow the ancestors; Nasafi says they called him a loser for leaving them; Bursevi records the taunt; Ibn Ashur cites Muqatil's report that Quraysh asked why he adopted this religion and urged the faith of their fathers and leaders, and identifies the two parties from context. [5 notes: JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 39:15/q02 How does the opening challenge follow the Prophet's declaration of exclusive worship?  [فَٱعْبُدُوا۟ مَا شِئْتُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ · links]
- 39:15/q02/p1 His exclusive devotion and disavowal of partners lead to a direct challenge to the polytheists, followed by an indirect warning about their loss. — Tabari says the Prophet declares sincere undivided obedience to God alone and renounces rivals. Ibn Ashur says repeated 'say' and the declaration reinforce 39:11, establishing exclusivity three times; the fāʾ marks the consequence, their choice follows whim rather than evidence, and after apparent leave-taking they are warned as though absent. Islahi says he declares acquittal, invites them to worship God alone, and then leaves their chosen worship to them. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:15/q03 What force does the command to worship whatever they wish have?  [فَٱعْبُدُوا۟ مَا شِئْتُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 39:15/q03/p1 It is a threatening rebuke that leaves persistent idolaters to their choice and its punishment, while disavowing their worship. — Tabari says they may choose created objects but will learn the result before their Lord. Zajjaj compares the command to enjoying disbelief briefly (18:29); Thalabi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Bursevi, and Abu Hayyan compare 41:40 or 39:8. Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan stress abandonment; Razi says the case for monotheism is complete, so this is no genuine command. Ibn Jawzi says it is a threat, not an operative command. Qushayri calls it the utmost threat; Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, and Study Quran pair threat with repudiation, the latter citing 6:19, 6:78, 10:41, and 109:6. Biqai says nothing below God can provide His favor. Bursevi, al-Irshad, and Alusi hear anger at persistence after prohibition, inviting punishment. Ibn Ashur hears an indifferent leave-taking grounded in whim, paralleling 18:29. Tabatabai says the choice will not benefit them. Islahi says the Prophet's public disavowal withstands coercion and oppression. Qurtubi calls it warning and rebuke. [24 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]

## 39:15/q04 What is the relation of this command to the later command to fight?  [فَٱعْبُدُوا۟ مَا شِئْتُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ · sciences]
turns on: Whether a threatening, nonoperative imperative is susceptible to abrogation; Zajjaj supplies only chronology.
- 39:15/q04/p1 The fighting verse abrogated this command. — Thalabi and Qurtubi report this as another view. [2 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD-]
- 39:15/q04/p2 It cannot be abrogated because it is a threat rather than an operative command. — Ibn Jawzi expressly rejects abrogation on this ground. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 39:15/q04/p3 Its threatening wording indicates that it preceded the command to fight. — Zajjaj infers this chronology from the threat and compares 18:29; he does not state that fighting abrogated it. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 39:15/q05 Is the juxtaposition of worshipping God and worshipping as one wishes a wordplay?  [فَٱعْبُدُوا۟ مَا شِئْتُم مِّن دُونِهِۦ · rhetoric]
- 39:15/q05/p1 Some count the paired worship forms as a kind of jinas, or near-matching wordplay. — Baqillani reports this classification alongside the paired forms of the challenge in 21:37. He explains that jinas may add or omit a letter or pair nearly similar forms, illustrating it with poetry by al-Buhturi, Ibn Muqbil, Zuhayr, Abu Tammam, Abu Nuwas, Ibn al-Mu'tazz, and al-Mutanabbi. [2 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 39:15/q06 Who are 'the losers' identified by the relative clause?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ ٱلَّذِينَ خَسِرُوٓا۟ · referent]
- 39:15/q06/p1 They are the unbelievers, especially the polytheists addressed by the preceding challenge. — Tabari reports Ibn Abbas identifying unbelievers destined for Hell and excluded from Paradise, with 22:11. Zajjaj calls them unbelievers. Alusi says the preceding addressees are directly or foremost intended. Ibn Ashur says the context points to the debated polytheists and that the relative clause substitutes indirect warning for direct address, with 39:7 and 39:15; he and Bursevi say the clause defines the complete losers. Nasafi says they combine all kinds of loss. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 39:15/q07 What does the verb for loss mean here?  [خَسِرُوٓا۟ · meaning]
- 39:15/q07/p1 It is deprivation or being cheated of one's capital, applied here to oneself and one's family. — Ibn Jawzi's exegetes and Razi give the sense of being cheated or defrauded. Damghani says the losers are cheated of themselves and families. Al-Raghib, reported by Bursevi, defines loss as diminished capital in wealth, standing, health, safety, intellect, faith, or reward; the last is manifest loss. Tabatabai calls it wasted capital. Ibn Ashur compares the self-caused ruin to a merchant's lost capital and cites 7:9. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL; DAMGHANI; BURSEVI(الراغب); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:15/q08 How do the losers lose themselves?  [خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously locate self-loss in punishment, in the unbelieving choices that waste one's capacities, or in the combined loss of worldly and future good.
- 39:15/q08/p1 They perish or suffer lasting punishment in Hell, shut out from Paradise and reunion. — Muqatil relates punishment to failure to affirm God's oneness. Tabari says they perish under God's punishment; Ibn Abbas adds lost worldly life and barred Paradise (22:11), Ibn Zayd says loss starts in this world, and Mujahid says they die while still alive in the Fire. Zajjaj, Wahidi, and Razi specify everlasting Hell; Mawardi and Bagawi give entry into Hell; Ibn Kathir and Abu Hayyan stress separation. Durr reports Mujahid's conscious regret; Bursevi and Alusi specify everlasting exposure; Kuranyolu says the punishment is deserved. Asad describes destruction of true human identity and uniqueness; Study Quran says the chance for good and repentance has been squandered (22:11). [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة,مجاهد وابن زيد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي,مجاهد وابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:15/q08/p2 By choosing unbelief and misguidance they squander their God-given capacities, deeds, and possibility of happiness. — Bursevi and Kashifi describe ruin or straying within oneself. Baydawi and Nasafi say they misguide or destroy themselves. Razi compares reason and good deeds to capital: first principles yield acquired knowledge, life and ability should yield good works, but disbelievers expend intellect and strength on delusion and evil, lose everything at death, and incur punishment. Alusi says they squander what they needed to secure. Ibn Ashur says they cause punishment while imagining they secure bliss, like a trader (7:9). Tabatabai says unbelief destroys their capacity for happiness; Islahi warns against sacrificing the afterlife for brief pleasure. [14 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:15/q08/p3 They forfeit both this world and the afterlife, with the final deprivation worse than any worldly loss. — Ibn Abbas in Tabari and Durr says they lose worldly life and Paradise. Study Quran ties the two losses to missed good deeds and repentance (22:11). Qushayri says desire loses the world and disobedience the afterlife; the Najmi interpretations reported by Bursevi add loss of the Lord through allegiance to another. Islahi calls worldly pleasures brief against eternal ruin. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:15/q09 Who are the families lost, and how are they lost?  [وَأَهْلِيهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'family' denotes actual kin or followers who share or escape the punishment, or a Paradise household that obedience would have secured; al-Alusi questions the value of reunion with saved kin and the houri identification, while Tabatabai cites 23:101 for the Paradise household.
- 39:15/q09/p1 They lose a home and family prepared for each person in Paradise, which the obedient obtain and the disobedient forfeit. — Abd al-Razzaq reports Qatada and Mujahid. Ibn Abbas, reported by Nahhas, Thalabi, Bagawi, Razi, Qurtubi, and Alusi, says everyone has an allotted spouse or home, family, and servants; the disobedient go to Hell and their places pass to obedient Muslims as inheritance, linked by Thalabi and Qurtubi to 23:10. Qurtubi says the promised home and family are additional to worldly possessions. Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Durr, and Tabatabai give the counterfactual: belief or obedience would have brought this family. Durr includes Qatada's version. Tabatabai prefers it because worldly ties are severed on Resurrection Day (23:101). Alusi reports Ibn Abbas saying the prepared Paradise companions are themselves deprived too. [19 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة,مجاهد); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وميمون بن مهران); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة، مجاهد، ميمون بن مهران); TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI+]
- 39:15/q09/p2 Their existing families share their fate in Hell or, if saved, are permanently separated from them in Paradise. — Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Biqai, and Alusi state the two outcomes. Muqatil includes spouses and servants lost through unbelief. Tabari's Mujahid says they cannot rejoin their families, and Ibn Atiyya allows loss of worldly kin through punishment. Mawardi and Ibn Jawzi specify unbelievers in Hell with believing kin in Paradise; Wahidi says they fail to join believing families there. Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Asad, and Kuranyolu stress separation or inability to meet or help relatives. Alusi objects that separation from Paradise-bound kin would not be meaningful loss if reunion brought the loser no benefit. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:15/q09/p3 They lose followers or relatives whom they themselves led into unbelief, so both sides suffer ruin. — Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, and Tabatabai explain the family as misled people. Bursevi says family may include spouses, children, servants, relatives, companions, or all. Ibn Ashur specifies spouses and children, says neither side can benefit or help the other, and cites 80:37 and 66:6. Alusi calls followers the main interpretation. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 39:15/q09/p4 They have no family in Hell, though they had family in worldly life. — Tabari attributes this to Ibn Zayd; Mawardi and Ibn Jawzi to Mujahid and Ibn Zayd. Tabari says those in Hell have no family. Study Quran describes having no one to comfort or commiserate with them. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وابن زيد); STUDYQURAN]
- 39:15/q09/p5 The lost Paradise family specifically consists of the houris prepared for them had they obeyed. — Mawardi and Ibn Jawzi attribute this to al-Hasan and Qatada; Abu Hayyan and Alusi report al-Hasan. Alusi regards the houri identification as somewhat remote. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 39:15/q09/p6 The family lost includes spouses and servants, described as passing to others in Paradise. — Muqatil and Bagawi identify spouses and servants; Damghani says they are lost to others in Paradise. Ibn Abbas in Razi also names servants among the assigned Paradise household. [4 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 39:15/q10 What responsibility does the loss of family imply?  [وَأَهْلِيهِمْ · interpretation]
- 39:15/q10/p1 One must seek the family's salvation; misleading dependents or setting a bad example makes one answerable for their ruin. — Islahi says adding family makes this responsibility explicit and cites the Prophet's teaching that every shepherd is accountable for those under their care. Ibn Ashur says they led spouses and children into disbelief, with 66:6, and neither side can help the other (80:37). [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Prophet (sws)); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:15/q11 How are 'those who lost themselves' and 'their families' parsed?  [ٱلَّذِينَ خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَأَهْلِيهِمْ · grammar]
- 39:15/q11/p1 'Those who' is nominative as the predicate of inna; 'their families' is accusative, coordinated with 'themselves' and marked by yāʾ. — This is Nahhas's explicit iʿrāb. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 39:15/q12 Why repeat the language of loss in 'the losers who lost'?  [ٱلْخَٰسِرِينَ خَسِرُوٓا۟ · rhetoric]
- 39:15/q12/p1 The relative clause defines and intensifies the complete loss, identifying the people warned without naming them directly. — Razi says repetition underscores gravity. Bursevi says the clause identifies the losers. Ibn Ashur says emphatic opening and definite 'losers' produce hyperbolic restriction, while the relative clause turns the direct address into indirect admonition of the polytheists. Alusi says it identifies the prior addressees directly or as leading instances. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:15/q13 What does specifying the Day of Resurrection add to their loss?  [يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the time phrase denotes the decisive punishment itself or the disclosure and full realization of prior loss.
- 39:15/q13/p1 It is the decisive time when entry into Hell makes the loss permanent and beyond repair. — Biqai says what is lost that day cannot be repaired. Bursevi and Alusi say entry into the Fire exposes them and their families or followers to everlasting torment. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:15/q13/p2 It is when a loss already incurred becomes manifest and fully realized. — Alusi reports this further interpretation rather than identifying the day only with the initial entry into Hell. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:15/q14 Why is this called 'the manifest loss'?  [أَلَا ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ ٱلْخُسْرَانُ ٱلْمُبِينُ · rhetoric]
- 39:15/q14/p1 The independent, emphatic conclusion singles out this immense, unmistakable loss as the loss beside which others are negligible. — Kashshaf and Baydawi list the alerting particle, independent or resumed sentence, separating pronoun, definite noun, and 'manifest.' Razi says 'alā' alerts to a terror beyond the mind, 'huwa' conveys exclusivity, and manifest wording magnifies fear. Nasafi and Abu Hayyan stress horror and unmistakability. Biqai says the demonstrative and emphatic khusrān uniquely identify the loss. Alusi adds the remote demonstrative, emphatic pronoun, intensive verbal-noun form stronger than khusr, and no greater loss. Ibn Ashur adds rhetorical restriction, demonstrative, and verbal noun. Tabatabai says the self's eternal loss cannot be replaced, unlike wealth or standing. Tabrisi says it is obvious and cannot be hidden. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI]

## 39:15/q15 What makes this loss greater than ordinary worldly loss?  [ٱلْخُسْرَانُ ٱلْمُبِينُ · interpretation]
- 39:15/q15/p1 It trades irreplaceable, lasting life and Paradise for Fire, whereas worldly goods and rank can be replaced or end soon. — Razi calls life, intellect, and ability God's capital for acquiring knowledge, good deeds, and afterlife; misuse wastes the investment and can turn exertion into punishment. Nasafi and Kashf al-Asrar, reported by Bursevi, picture an exchange of Paradise and ranks for Fire and pits. Ibn Ashur says losing oneself and dearest kin is greatest loss. Tabatabai and Islahi contrast eternal self-loss with temporary wealth, status, or worldly interests; Islahi says brief pleasure cannot justify it. Study Quran says good deeds and repentance were possible but squandered, citing 22:11. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:15/q16 How is the warning contrasted with the reward and family of believers?  [* · links]
- 39:15/q16/p1 Disbelievers face severe punishment and lost kin or Paradise homes, while believers receive abundant reward and are joined by righteous family in Paradise. — Zajjaj says the passage contrasts reward and punishment. Tabrisi sees an echo of Paradise's inheritors in 23:10; Thalabi and Qurtubi also cite 23:10 for believers inheriting allotted homes. Study Quran contrasts Paradise with righteous fathers, spouses, and descendants, citing 13:23, 40:8, and 43:70. Islahi says the Prophet's disavowal warns polytheists while giving committed monotheists glad tidings. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; THALABI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:15/q17 How do inward readings understand the loss of self and family?  [خَسِرُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَأَهْلِيهِمْ · inward]
turns on: Whether the lost family is spiritual faculties and kindred beings, or the verse depicts successive losses of world, afterlife, and nearness to God.
- 39:15/q17/p1 Attachment to anything besides God veils one from the Real and loses the soul and its spiritual kindred. — Qashani calls the lost kin kindred sacred substances in the spiritual world. Alusi's inward reading identifies family as hearts, secrets, and spirits, with the self's capacity for arrival and union ruined through turning away from God. [2 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:15/q17/p2 Desire and disobedience lose the world and afterlife, while estrangement from the Master brings an enduring inner Hell. — Qushayri says desire, disobedience, and shamelessness sever worldly life, the afterlife, and relationship with the Master; Hell has no escape or respite and a person can already dwell behind the veil of false beliefs. He urges fear of God now to avert that Day's fear. Bursevi reports the Najmi interpretation that allegiance elsewhere loses the Lord. Alusi reports an eminent scholar likening knowledge and good works to two trades: squandered reason, health, and ability leave ignorance and separation. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]

## 39:15/q18 What does the surrounding warning teach about the Prophet's own duty?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:15/q18/p1 He must convey God's rulings faithfully and obey them himself, facing punishment if he does otherwise. — Kuranyolu applies the surrounding warning to the Prophet's exact conveyance and personal obligation. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
