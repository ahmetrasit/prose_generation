# 6:81: 30 questions

## 6:81/q01 What does Abraham deny fearing, and why?  [وَكَيْفَ أَخَافُ مَآ أَشْرَكْتُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the feared object is the idols' alleged power or punishment for the people's shirk.
- 6:81/q01/p1 He denies fearing their powerless idols, while their association with the God who controls harm and benefit is what warrants fear. — Al-Tabari, Ibn Ishaq, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, and others contrast inert idols with God's power; al-Baghawi says they neither see nor hear. Ibn ʿAtiyya calls them stone and wood. Al-Biqaʿi says fear of harm from idols is the danger denied (7:89). Al-Bursevi describes associating created things with the incomparable God as the greatest danger. Ibn ʿAshur says they acknowledge God; Islahi says their error corrupts monotheism rather than denying God. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 6:81/q01/p2 He need not fear divine punishment for his people's association, because he is free of their shirk. — Al-Tabrisi reports this as another interpretation of Abraham's question. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]

## 6:81/q02 What force does 'How should I fear?' have?  [وَكَيْفَ أَخَافُ · rhetoric]
- 6:81/q02/p1 It is a rebuking denial and challenge: fear of idols has no basis, and their failure to fear God is more astonishing. — Al-Wahidi says it challenges them to prove his fear justified. Al-Alusi says a question about the manner of fearing intensifies denial of fear itself and answers their own assumption. Ibn ʿAshur says it reverses their challenge and denies both their proposed fear and their misplaced fearlessness; Abu Hayyan calls it astonishment. Al-Qurtubi calls it rebuke. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:81/q03 What is 'mā ashraktum'?  [مَآ أَشْرَكْتُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether mā names the objects, describes an indefinite object, or names the act of association.
- 6:81/q03/p1 It denotes the idols or other powerless objects they associate with God; mā may be a relative pronoun with an understood complement. — Al-Wahidi and Ibn ʿAbbas identify idols. Abu Hayyan says mā fits non-rational objects. Ibn ʿAshur supplies the omitted 'with which you associate' from the preceding clause and makes mā the object of ashraktum. Al-Samin and al-Alusi also allow a relative mā with omission. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(author؛ ابن عباس); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:81/q03/p2 Mā may be an indefinite noun qualified by an omitted description or complement. — Al-Samin and al-Alusi give this syntactic alternative alongside the relative analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:81/q03/p3 Mā may be a verbal noun, referring to their act of associating others with God. — Al-Samin gives the paraphrase 'your association'; al-Alusi also lists the verbal noun analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:81/q04 How is 'you do not fear' joined to Abraham's question?  [وَلَا تَخَافُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether wāw coordinates two clauses in the question or introduces a circumstance.
- 6:81/q04/p1 It is coordinated with 'I fear,' so both failures of fear remain within the question and rebuke. — Abu Hayyan says the conjunction places both under one rebuke. Al-Samin lists this analysis. Ibn ʿAshur says it condemns both his alleged fear and their actual lack of fear. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:81/q04/p2 It is a circumstantial clause: how could he fear while they do not fear the consequences of their shirk? — Al-Samin says an implied subject can precede the present verb negated by lā, while wāw can itself link the circumstance without a resumptive pronoun. Al-Alusi says the circumstance sharpens the contrast between safety and real danger. Ibn ʿAshur prefers this force, comparing 2:44. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 6:81/q05 Why does the verse contrast fear of their objects with fear of their association?  [أَخَافُ مَآ أَشْرَكْتُمْ تَخَافُونَ أَنَّكُمْ أَشْرَكْتُم بِٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 6:81/q05/p1 It avoids treating God and idols as comparable objects of fear while exposing their inverted judgment. — Abu Hayyan says the changed objects avoid equating God with idols. Al-Samin says Abraham fears their idols whereas they should fear their own act of associating with God. Al-Alusi contrasts their fearless conduct in actual danger with Abraham's safety. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:81/q06 Why is God named explicitly in the second fear clause?  [بِٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the divine name serves emphasis and reverence or a grammatical necessity.
- 6:81/q06/p1 His name magnifies the offense of associating with Him and reflects their failure to honor Him; it was already mentioned in the preceding verse. — Al-Alusi reports that the first clause omits the name because 6:80 supplied it and to avoid even suggesting Abraham's association with God. He rejects the proposal that the second mention merely provides a pronoun antecedent or marks a different scope of denial; the antecedent was already available and the clauses do not share the proposed negation. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(some precise scholars)+]
- 6:81/q06/p2 The explicit name is needed as the antecedent of the following pronoun, or for a different scope of denial. — Al-Alusi reports and rejects these explanations: an antecedent was already available, and the two clauses do not combine under one negation. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 6:81/q07 What do they fail to fear in 'that you associated with God'?  [تَخَافُونَ أَنَّكُمْ أَشْرَكْتُم بِٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 6:81/q07/p1 They fail to fear the danger and consequences of their own association of partners with God. — Ibn ʿAshur says min is omitted after 'fear' before an, as often happens; they knew and feared God yet failed to fear their act of association. Ibn Zayd contrasts fearing creatures without fearing God against fearing God alone; Ibn Kathir and al-Tabari stress that God can harm or benefit. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,محمد بن إسحاق); NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:81/q08 What does the 'mā' in 'what He did not send down' refer to?  [مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ سُلْطَٰنًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is relative or indefinite; a verbal noun is excluded by sense.
- 6:81/q08/p1 It is a relative noun, the object of 'associate': a partner for which God sent no warrant. — Al-Samin allows a relative noun and rejects the verbal noun here because that would distort the sense. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:81/q08/p2 It is an indefinite noun describing a purported partner without divine warrant. — Al-Samin gives this as the other permissible construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:81/q09 How do the prepositions with 'sent down' attach?  [بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'upon you' modifies the verb or the noun 'authority'.
- 6:81/q09/p1 Both 'with it' and 'upon you' modify 'sent down'; 'with it' may express accompaniment or cause. — Al-Samin attaches both phrases to 'send down.' Al-Tabatabai gives accompaniment and causation as senses of the preposition in 'with it.' [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 6:81/q09/p2 'Upon you' can instead be circumstantial from 'authority.' — Al-Samin lists this as an alternative attachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:81/q10 What does sulṭān mean here?  [سُلْطَٰنًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word denotes evidentiary authority in this verse or its broader sense of power.
- 6:81/q10/p1 It is proof, warrant, or authoritative argument for the alleged partners, which they have not received. — Abu ʿUbayda also offers excuse; al-Zajjaj says clear proof. Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn ʿAbbas and seven other authorities saying every Qur'anic sulṭān is proof. Ibn al-Jawzi cites 6:81 and 11:96, 17:33, 30:35, 27:21, 55:33. Ibn ʿAshur says proof prevails over an opponent's mind. Al-Wahidi and al-Jishumi also allow power enabling one to prevail; al-Samin's entry supplies no separate definition. [26 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس، أبو مالك، محمد بن كعب، عكرمة، سعيد بن جبير، الضحاك، السدي، نضر بن عربي); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(author؛ ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 6:81/q10/p2 In other Qur'anic contexts sulṭān can mean kingship or coercive power. — Ibn al-Jawzi derives it from salāṭa, forceful extension or dominance, on the pattern faʿlān; he cites Satan's denial of power in 14:22 and 34:21. Al-Wahidi and al-Jishumi note the broader sense of enabling power even while discussing this verse. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(المفسرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(author؛ ابن عباس); JISHUMI]

## 6:81/q11 What does the lack of revealed authority establish, and could partners ever receive it?  [مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ سُلْطَٰنًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether the denial is solely the lack of disclosed warrant, rules out partnership in principle, or addresses images as a possible prayer direction.
- 6:81/q11/p1 Their claimed partners have no divine warrant; the absence of proof defeats their worship and fear. — Al-Tabari, al-Baydawi and Ibn ʿAshur specify no scripture or command authorizes worship. Abu Hayyan says rational refutation is joined by denial of revealed evidence. Ibn Kathir compares denials of invented religion in 42:21 and 53:23; Study Quran cites 7:33, 7:71, 12:40, 22:71, 53:23. Al-Tabatabai says any genuine partnership would require revelation or outward signs, and neither exists; if a warrant existed fear would be justified. Al-Biqaʿi says none shields them from God's anger. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(author؛ ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 6:81/q11/p2 No authorization of genuine divine partners is rationally possible. — Al-Razi presents this implication and compares 23:117. Al-Alusi chooses it, dismissing the Imam's analogy of treating idols as a prayer direction as outside the question of partnership. Al-Tabatabai says the negative wording does not imply God might authorize partners. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 6:81/q11/p3 The wording denies a revealed command to worship images, while directing prayer toward them is not rationally impossible. — Al-Razi reports this alternative; al-Alusi says the Imam's prayer-direction analogy does not address actual divine partnership. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 6:81/q12 What principle about religious claims follows from the missing warrant?  [مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ سُلْطَٰنًۭا · interpretation]
- 6:81/q12/p1 A religious claim needs evidence from God; an unsupported claim is wrong. — Al-Tabrisi states the general rule. Al-Bursevi and al-Alusi call the nonexistent 'authority' sarcastic, emphasizing evidence revealed by God. Al-Tabatabai says the absence of evidence itself settles the issue without needing to label the qualification sarcastic or inherent in false partners. Ibn Kathir links this to unauthorized religious law in 42:21 and 53:23. [5 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 6:81/q13 How is 'He sends down' read?  [يُنَزِّلْ · readings]
- 6:81/q13/p1 Both Form II yunazzil and Form IV yunzil are canonical readings. — Qiraat-Er says Form II suggests gradual sending, whereas Form IV suggests a single act. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:81/q14 How is sulṭān vocalized?  [سُلْطَٰنًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the lām retains sukūn or assimilates to the preceding ḍamma.
- 6:81/q14/p1 Another reading gives the lām ḍamma. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin say it may reflect an independent dialect or vowel following, which would not establish an independent pattern. Al-Alusi calls it dialectal harmonization with the preceding ḍamma. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:81/q14/p2 Most read sulṭānan with a still lām. — Al-Samin identifies the majority reading. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:81/q15 Who are the two parties?  [ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the groups are named historically, by their enduring beliefs, or by their misplaced sense of safety.
- 6:81/q15/p1 They are monotheists and polytheists, broadly the party of God and the party of the idols. — Most commentators identify the parties by their creeds. Al-Biqaʿi explicitly extends them beyond Abraham and his people. Ibn ʿAshur allows future members of Abraham's community, fitting 6:82; al-Wahidi, al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi and others state the general contrast. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:81/q15/p2 They are Abraham, perhaps with present or future followers, and his own people. — Al-Tabrisi explicitly names Abraham and his people. Al-Baghawi says Abraham and adherents of his religion. Ibn ʿAshur says 'party' can describe Abraham alone, present followers, or future members; he cites 29:26 and 6:82. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:81/q15/p3 They are those confident where safety belongs and those confident where fear belongs. — Al-Bursevi reports Abu al-Suʿud's rhetorical description of the same opposing groups. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو السعود)]

## 6:81/q16 Which party deserves security, and on what grounds?  [فَأَىُّ ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ أَحَقُّ بِٱلْأَمْنِ · interpretation]
- 6:81/q16/p1 Those who worship God alone deserve security; idolaters who worship powerless beings without proof do not. — Ibn Jurayj says Abraham's opponents themselves answer that believers in one Lord deserve security. Ibn Zayd says God answers in 6:82: believers without mixed wrongdoing. Al-Thaʿlabi cites security and guidance for unmixed belief. Ibn Ishaq, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Kathir ground the distinction in God's sole power over harm and benefit; al-Qurtubi and others define the risk as divine punishment. Al-Tabrisi says true knowledge follows proof rather than partisan stubbornness. Islahi says their unsupported association makes them culpable while Abraham did nothing wrong. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن زيد,محمد بن إسحاق); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,محمد بن إسحاق); NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:81/q17 What kind of security is al-amn?  [ٱلْأَمْنِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether al-amn is specified as exemption from punishment, a broad absence of fear, or inner calm.
- 6:81/q17/p1 It is safety from God's punishment. — Ibn Ishaq explicitly includes this world and the next; Ibn Kathir specifies punishment at resurrection. Al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi give safety from punishment without restricting the time. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 6:81/q17/p2 It is security in general, the opposite of fear. — Ibn ʿAshur calls al-amn generic security. Al-Alusi's argument contrasts an actually safe position with a dangerous one. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:81/q17/p3 It is inward calmness of the soul. — Al-Jishumi defines security this way, connecting īmān and security from punishment through deeds. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 6:81/q18 Why ask 'which of the two parties' rather than 'which of us'?  [فَأَىُّ ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ · rhetoric]
- 6:81/q18/p1 The indirect phrasing avoids Abraham's self-praise while inviting his opponents to make the judgment. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin stress modesty; al-Samin contrasts a poem that directly asks which of two men is champion. Al-Biqaʿi says generalizing the parties compels an answer. Al-Alusi says it avoids direct blame, extends security to every monotheist, and uses 'more entitled' gently to draw them from obstinacy. Al-Tabatabai says it avoids provoking pride and marks the foundational division. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 6:81/q19 How is 'which party is more entitled to security' constructed and used?  [فَأَىُّ ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ أَحَقُّ بِٱلْأَمْنِ · grammar]
- 6:81/q19/p1 Ayyu al-fariqayn is the subject and ahaqqu bi-l-amn the predicate; the question presses them to state the evident answer. — Al-Nahhas gives the subject-predicate parsing. Abu Hayyan says a known answer is posed as an open question. Al-Alusi notes the comparative 'more entitled' grants apparent room for an answer to coax them. Al-Itqan contrasts the placement of interrogative ayyu after fa with hamza's initial placement before conjunctions. [4 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN]

## 6:81/q20 What does 'if you know' ask of them?  [إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether taʿlamūn has an implicit object or simply means 'are knowledgeable'; the conditional answer is unspoken.
- 6:81/q20/p1 An omitted answer invites them to tell which party is safer if they know or discern. — Abu Hayyan supplies 'identify which party is safer.' Al-Samin supplies 'then tell me'; Ibn ʿAshur treats it as a new sentence urging a reply. Al-Alusi says it challenges them to answer. Al-Tabrisi asks for reasoning without partisan stubbornness. Kuranyolu says only knowledge enables the correct answer and escape from false beliefs. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 6:81/q20/p2 The object of 'know' is omitted: if you know which group merits security. — Al-Samin gives the omitted-object analysis; al-Alusi allows 'what they know' as the implied object. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:81/q20/p3 The verb is intransitive: if you are knowledgeable. — Al-Samin gives the objectless analysis, and Abu Hayyan describes knowledgeable and discerning addressees. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 6:81/q21 What happened in Abraham's dispute with his people?  [* · reports]
- 6:81/q21/p1 His people urged him to worship another god and threatened him with their idols; he answered with the fear and security challenge. — Ibn Jurayj's report in al-Durr supplies the invitation and threat. Al-Tabari and Ibn Abi Hatim report his answer that idols cannot harm or benefit; Mujahid calls the security question his argument. Al-Rabi says God gave him victory in this disputation, linking it to 6:83. [8 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(الربيع,مجاهد,محمد بن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد,محمد بن إسحاق)]

## 6:81/q22 How does the argument connect with verses 6:82–83?  [* · links]
- 6:81/q22/p1 Verse 6:82 supplies the answer about unmixed belief and security; verse 6:83 identifies Abraham's God-given argument and victory. — Ibn Zayd in al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Durr cites 6:82. Al-Thaʿlabi gives its believers' security and guidance. Ibn ʿAshur uses it to include Abraham's community. Al-Rabi links the dispute's proof and victory to 6:83; Mujahid calls the question Abraham's argument. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الربيع,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,مجاهد); THALABI; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:81/q23 How does this question continue Abraham's preceding reply?  [* · links]
- 6:81/q23/p1 It continues his rejection of their threat, moving from his lack of fear to their greater reason for fear. — Al-Tabari calls it his reply; Ibn ʿAtiyya says his speech and decisive proof continue through taʿlamūn. Al-Alusi relates the new question to his preceding denial, and Ibn ʿAshur says their admission of God intensifies the reversal. Al-Biqaʿi compares Abraham's invocation of Lord for protection with Shuʿayb's divine name for a different issue in 7:89; he also links their conditional speech with 18:24. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:81/q24 What does the argument imply about God's power and other claimed powers?  [* · theology]
- 6:81/q24/p1 God alone creates, owns, and controls harm and benefit; supposed partners have no independent power. — Ibn Ishaq and others oppose idols' impotence to God's power. Al-Tabatabai says divine ownership would require any real partnership to be disclosed by revelation or external signs. He also reports an interpretation of God's encompassing knowledge and will that excludes creaturely influence over His acts, even by intercession, citing 2:255. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 6:81/q25 What wider purpose does the Abraham account serve in the sūra?  [* · links]
- 6:81/q25/p1 It presents Abraham and later prophets as heralds of Muhammad's message, calling him to follow their guidance and preach without forcing belief. — Islahi says Abraham is singled out because Israelite and Ishmaelite descendants both claimed him and ascribed inherited practices to him. He calls Judaism, Christianity, and Ishmaelite polytheism later fabrications unrelated to Abraham, and Muhammad's mission a revival and completion of Abraham's religion. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:81/q26 What figurative transfers help describe security and related Qur'anic wording?  [ٱلْأَمْنِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which relation licenses the figurative substitution: place and state, instrument and effect, or contextual opposition.
- 6:81/q26/p1 A state can be named through its place, and a place can be described through its people or condition. — Al-Zarkashi says security or goodness predicated of a town refers to its people or air (14:35, 7:96); 'in God's mercy' means Paradise (3:107), and plotting of night and day occurs during them (34:33). He also lists heart for intellect, mouths for tongues, tongue for language, and village for inhabitants (7:179, 48:11, 14:4, 12:82). [2 notes: BURHAN]
- 6:81/q26/p2 Other transfers include an instrument for its effect, an abstract state for its place, and even a word for its opposite. — Al-Zarkashi gives 'tongue of truth' as good remembrance, eyes as God's sight and a messenger's tongue as his people's language (19:50, 54:14, 14:4); adornment and mosque as place and prayer (7:31); and calling evil's requital evil from the offender's view, or obedience ihsan in reward context (42:40, 55:60). He reports al-Hasan's 'in your eye' for 'in your sleep,' which al-Zamakhshari deems remote and interprets as 'in your vision' (8:43). [4 notes: BURHAN(الحسن)]

## 6:81/q27 How does the interrogative here compare with hamza?  [فَأَىُّ · grammar]
- 6:81/q27/p1 Ayyu follows the conjunction fa here, while hamza characteristically precedes conjunctions and has a wider range of interrogative uses. — Al-Suyuti says hamza is the basic interrogative: it asks identification or confirmation, enters affirmative and negative clauses, and can recall, alert, astonish or warn (25:45, 2:243, 77:16). He reports Abu Hayyan's source saying hamza can imply a prior leaning toward assertion unlike neutral hal; hamza can precede a conditional (3:144) and take other rhetorical meanings. He contrasts all this with fa-ayyu in 6:81. [7 notes: ITQAN(أبو حيان)]

## 6:81/q28 What reading of the preceding dispute verb is reported?  [* · readings]
- 6:81/q28/p1 Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿAmir and Abu Jaʿfar lighten the nūn of a-taḥājjūnī by deleting one of two nūns; others assimilate them into a doubled nūn. — Al-Jishumi reports both readings and explains their nūn treatment. He defines mutual argument as each disputant's presentation and demand for proof, and guidance as direction toward truth. [2 notes: JISHUMI(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر)]

## 6:81/q29 How do the clauses signal the priority of the warrant issue?  [عَلَيْكُمْ سُلْطَٰنًۭا · rhetoric]
- 6:81/q29/p1 Mentioning and fronting 'upon you' emphasizes the seriousness of the foundational question of divine authority. — Al-Biqaʿi says no revealed warrant can shield idolaters from God's anger, and relates the emphasis to 7:89 and 18:24. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:81/q30 How is kayfa construed grammatically?  [وَكَيْفَ · grammar]
- 6:81/q30/p1 Kayfa functions circumstantially in the question. — Al-Alusi specifies the circumstantial function. Al-Samin says its analysis is analogous to his discussion at the opening of al-Baqara (2:1). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
