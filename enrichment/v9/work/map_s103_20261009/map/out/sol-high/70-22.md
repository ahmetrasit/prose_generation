# 70:22: 14 questions

## 70:22/q01 How does the exception from humanity work grammatically?  [إِلَّا ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · grammar]
- 70:22/q01/p1 The plural worshippers are an accusative exception from the singular generic, collective 'human.' — Nahhas specifies the accusative case. Farra, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Samin, and Bursevi explain that al-insan denotes humanity collectively, allowing a plural exception. Farra, Baghawi, and Samin compare 103:2–3; Qurtubi uses this construction alongside the generic al-nafs of 6:151, whose prohibition covers protected Muslim and treaty-holder lives unless a valid legal cause exists. Bursevi adds that the fronted 'they' strengthens the subsequent assertion of persistence in prayer. [7 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 70:22/q02 From what are the worshippers excepted?  [إِلَّا ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the exception is stated only against the immediate human traits or also against their final outcome.
- 70:22/q02/p1 They are exempt from the human tendency to panic in adversity and withhold good in prosperity. — Muqatil names anxiety and miserliness. Sahl, as reported by Sulami, contrasts despair in poverty and withholding in wealth with enabled worshippers. Samarrai explicitly applies the exception to both sides of haluʿ in 70:20–21. Abu Hayyan contrasts patience in hardship and the noble traits that follow. Biqai says prayer restrains this nature so neither distress nor withholding becomes extreme; the worshippers seek God's pleasure. Bursevi calls this a transformation of blameworthy traits. Baydawi and Alusi describe obedience, care for others, belief in judgment, fear of punishment, restrained desire, and preference for the hereafter as the opposing qualities. Tabatabai says prayer heads the good works because of its strong effect against greed, citing 29:45 for its restraint of shameful conduct and wrongdoing. Kuranyolu connects the cure to responsibility rooted in afterlife belief, worship, and constructive conduct. Study Quran's R and Z prefer the implication that the condemned traits describe humanity broadly because praying people are exempted. Al-Tabatabai cites this exception from anxiety in hardship and withholding in prosperity as evidence of prayer's importance against Satan's effects in his discussion of 5:91. [13 notes: MUQATIL; SULAMI(سهل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN(R, Z)+]
- 70:22/q02/p2 Believing worshippers are also spared the torment and evil end and honored in gardens. — Elmalili ties the exception to the following virtues and the final promise of honor in gardens. Samarrai connects their deliverance from Hell's punishment to their turning toward obedience and truth, in contrast to those whom Hell calls for turning away. [2 notes: ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]

## 70:22/q03 Who are the worshippers named here?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description identifies believers broadly, obligatory worshippers, the Companions, or people at a particular inward station.
- 70:22/q03/p1 They are believers whom God guides and protects and who maintain prayer. — Ibn al-Jawzi explicitly calls them believers who pray; Ibn Kathir adds divine protection and guidance. Elmalili describes believing people who pray. Qurtubi also reports an interpretation identifying believers generally, who overcome panic through trust and certainty. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 70:22/q03/p2 They are people who perform the obligatory prayer. — Qurtubi transmits this identification from al-Nakha‘i; the reports from Ibn Masud and others further describe guarding prescribed times. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود,النخعي); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,ابن مسعود)]
- 70:22/q03/p3 They are gnostics whose knowledge of things' worth turns their joy and rest toward God. — Tustari says they rejoice only in God, rest only in Him, and grieve at separation. Sulami transmits Ibn Ata's description of gnostics who find relief and rest only in God. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 70:22/q03/p4 They are the Companions. — Qurtubi reports this as another identification alongside believers generally. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 70:22/q03/p5 They are those who strive fully, shed ego qualities, and attain direct witnessing. — Qashani reads their continuous prayer as the soul's lasting contemplation, eclipsing all besides what it witnesses. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 70:22/q04 What causes the panic and withholding contrasted with worshippers?  [إِلَّا ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes apply human distress to divine allotment, sin and repentance, or punishment, and identify withholding as hypocrisy.
- 70:22/q04/p1 Despair reflects ignorance of God's apportionment; trust in His decree prevents panic. — Sulami transmits al-Wasiti's explanation of despair and Ibn Ata's explanation that praying people do not panic because they trust their Lord and His decree. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي)]
- 70:22/q04/p2 Withholding is a trait of hypocrites. — Sulami attributes this account to al-Wasiti. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 70:22/q04/p3 Despair after sin stems from ignorance of repentance. — Sulami transmits Ibn Ata's application to a person who commits indecency or sin. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 70:22/q04/p4 People complain when God gives even a little punishment for their deeds. — Islahi describes this as the general human response. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 70:22/q05 What does constancy in prayer require?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dawām concerns prescribed timing, frequent additional prayers, attention in the rite, full care around the rite, or inward continuity.
- 70:22/q05/p1 Maintaining the obligatory prayers at their appointed times without neglect. — Mawardi reports Ibn Masud on guarding obligatory times. Qurtubi reports him on prescribed times and adds his statement that abandoning prayer is unbelief. Durr reports Ibrahim al-Taymi on obligatory prayers and Ibn Masud and Masruq on appointed times. Razi defines constancy as never omitting prayer and distinguishes 70:34 as guarding its excellence and proper performance. Study Quran reports Tabari's alternatives of prescribed rites without neglect or all five daily prayers. Samarrai says dawām means regular observance, not praying continuously at every moment. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,ابن مسعود); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 70:22/q05/p2 Performing many voluntary prayers. — Mawardi reports this explanation from Ibn Jurayj. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 70:22/q05/p3 Remaining attentive without turning or looking around during prayer. — Mawardi transmits Uqba ibn Amir; Durr transmits Imran ibn Husayn. Samarrai includes attention through completion and freedom from distraction in regular observance. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عقبة بن عامر); DURR-FULL(عمران بن حصين); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:22/q05/p4 Caring for prayer before, during, and after its performance. — Razi includes readiness for its time, ablution, covering, direction, purity, congregation, and focus; attention to recitation and remembrance; and afterward avoidance of idle activity and sins. He distinguishes this full guarding from merely never omitting prayer. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 70:22/q05/p5 Maintaining inward prayer or contemplation continuously. — Qashani interprets it as enduring contemplation of the witnessed by the soul, eclipsing all else. Study Quran reports Aj's interpretation as prayer of the heart at all times. [2 notes: QASHANI; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 70:22/q06 What inward qualities and practices characterize these worshippers?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · inward]
turns on: The inward readings stress different aspects: trust and longing, witnessing, stages of purification, or giving knowledge.
- 70:22/q06/p1 Their knowledge and trust bring rest in God and longing for beneficial knowledge and deeds. — Tustari describes gnostics' joy in God and grief at separation. Sulami reports Ibn Ata on their knowledge of things' worth, rest only in God, trust in His decree rather than panic, and hearts that long for useful knowledge and righteous deeds when they hear of them. [4 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]
- 70:22/q06/p2 Their soul's continual prayer is direct contemplation after ego qualities are shed. — Qashani associates complete striving and direct witnessing with a soul that sees nothing besides the witnessed. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 70:22/q06/p3 Prayer purifies successive inner faculties and culminates in annihilation in God. — Bursevi reports al-Taʾwilat al-Najmiyya: the soul's prayer purifies forbidden acts; the heart's, worldly desire; the secret's, high stations; the spirit's uses divine unveiling; and the hidden prayer is annihilation in God. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 70:22/q06/p4 Detached worshippers give outward and inward wealth, including true knowledge, to those ready or prevented from seeking it. — Qashani says distribution reaches prepared seekers and people distracted from seeking, extending giving beyond material wealth. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 70:22/q07 Why is prayer placed first in the sequence of virtues?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · rhetoric]
- 70:22/q07/p1 Prayer has central rank and a strong power to restrain the vices described. — Tabatabai explicitly takes its first position to signal rank and power against greed and links its restraint of wrongdoing to 29:45. Bursevi calls prayer the first obligation and first matter of reckoning; illness may excuse fasting but not prayer, and it was the Prophet's final counsel. Study Quran observes that the list both begins and ends with prayer, showing its central place. Biqai says prayer refines the haluʿ disposition. [5 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 70:22/q08 What virtues does the ensuing description present, and how complete is the list?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the enumerated qualities are treated as a defined set of eight or illustrative examples that may vary.
- 70:22/q08/p1 It describes a broad set of moral and ritual duties beginning with prayer. — Razi says eight qualities follow. Baydawi and Alusi name obedience, concern for others, belief in judgment, fear of punishment, restraint of desire, and preference for the lasting future. Kuranyolu names regular prayer, the poor's due, afterlife concern, chastity, fidelity to trusts, and truthful witness. Study Quran calls it a thorough portrait of Islam's moral and ritual duties. Biqai says the eight qualities correspond to Paradise's eight gates and complete faith's two halves, patience and gratitude; he also describes good company, admonition, avoiding corrupt places and companions, and cultivated noble habits as means of refining nature, with adherence to religious limits despite contrary impulses an honor. Tabrisi reports Ibn Kathir's singular reading of amānah in the ensuing list of virtues, against the other readers' plural. Tabrisi explains that the singular is a collective verbal noun encompassing each person's trust, while the plural reflects the variety of trusts; he says the same applies to testimony. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(ابن كثير); TABRISI+]
- 70:22/q08/p2 The named virtues are examples, not an exhaustive fixed set. — Kuranyolu says their number changes with circumstances while their aim remains resisting impatience, ingratitude, and selfishness and enriching character. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 70:22/q09 What prophetic report describes the best people of this community?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · reports]
- 70:22/q09/p1 They laugh openly at God's broad mercy, weep privately in fear, remember and petition Him, long for Him, burden others lightly, and discipline themselves. — Tustari reports this description from the Prophet. [1 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 70:22/q10 What did Daniel reportedly say about this community's prayer?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · reports]
- 70:22/q10/p1 Their prayer could have saved earlier peoples from disaster, and prayer is a fine trait of believers. — Durr reports Qatada's account of Daniel's description of Muhammad's community. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 70:22/q11 What prophetic practice illustrates constancy in worship?  [ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · reports]
- 70:22/q11/p1 The Prophet practiced steadily and called the most constant deed the most excellent, even if small. — Bursevi reports this from Aisha. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عائشة)]

## 70:22/q12 How does this portrait connect with other Quranic portraits of believers?  [* · links]
- 70:22/q12/p1 It closely parallels 23:1–11, with differences in wording and order, and also resembles 33:35. — Samarrai observes the strong parallel between al-Muʾminun and al-Maʿarij and their variations of wording and sequence. Study Quran notes that 23:3 additionally mentions turning from idle talk and cites 33:35 as another parallel. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 70:22/q13 What is the possible revelation period of this passage?  [* · sciences]
- 70:22/q13/p1 If the connected passage is Medinan, the fourteen verses from this exception through the honored gardens and at least the prior three verses are Medinan too. — Tabatabai bases the inference on coherence across the unit, including the description of human anxiety and withholding before this verse. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 70:22/q14 How does the exception answer Hell's call to those who turn away?  [إِلَّا ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ · links]
- 70:22/q14/p1 The worshippers turn toward obedience and maintain prayer, reversing the turning away of those Hell calls and escaping its punishment. — Samarrai connects Hell's call in 70:17 to the exception in 70:22 and constancy in 70:23. He says the paired words أدبر وتولى reinforce the condemned people's turning away, which the worshippers' sustained obedience answers. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]
