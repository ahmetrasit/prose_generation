# 3:200: 26 questions

## 3:200/q01 What does 'be patient' require?  [ٱصْبِرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether general obedience or a specific setting defines the command.
- 3:200/q01/p1 Steadfastness in religion and obedience, including duties, abstaining from sin and trials. — Tabari favors unrestricted scope because God specified no exception. Hasan includes ease and hardship until death; Said names obligations, Qatada God's rights, Razi doctrinal argument, worship, avoiding sins, illness, poverty, drought and fear. Alusi cites patience in affliction, obedience and abstinence. [59 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,الحسن,الضحاك,قتادة,محمد بن كعب القرظي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,محمد بن كعب القرظي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن وقتادة وابن جريج والضحاك,محمد بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن,الضحاك ومقاتل بن سليمان,الكلبي,قتادة,مقاتل بن حيان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر,الحسن وقتادة وابن جريج والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الأصم,الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك وابن جريج); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TAB-FULL+]
- 3:200/q01/p2 Patience in jihad beside the Prophet. — Zayd reads all three military; Ibn al-Anbari prefers this for the first. Al-Farra' says endure with the Prophet. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNABIHATIM(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); JISHUMI(زيد بن أسلم); TABRISI(زيد بن أسلم); RAZI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 3:200/q01/p3 Restrain oneself at affliction's first shock and meet trials without despair. — The Prophet locates true patience at the first shock; Harith bears affliction, Jurayri prepares for it, Junayd restrains agitation, Dhu al-Nun gives five signs. Ibn Ata links certainty; other sayings name worldly and eternal fruit and a path through contentment and modesty. [13 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الجريري,الجنيد,الحارث,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جعفر الخلدي,ذو النون,قيل); NASAFI-FULL(الجنيد); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 3:200/q01/p4 Endure the five prayers and maintain mosque worship. — Hasan glosses patience as prayers; Abu Hurayra applies the whole verse to timely prayer, resisting desires and remaining in mosques. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو غسان,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو غسان,أبو هريرة,النبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 3:200/q02 How is the first imperative parsed?  [ٱصْبِرُوا۟ · grammar]
- 3:200/q02/p1 It is a plural imperative with its nūn dropped. — Nahhas gives this grammatical analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 3:200/q03 What does 'vie in patience' require?  [وَصَابِرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether reciprocal patience faces an enemy, a social trial, delayed relief, or serves emphasis.
- 3:200/q03/p1 Outlast enemies in war and other opponents. — Tabari invokes the reciprocal Arabic form. Farra', Zamakhshari and Nasafi require at least as much endurance as enemies; Ibn Ashur says the first to yield loses. Antara, Zufar and an unnamed poet illustrate battle. Biqai includes hypocrites; Razi adds enjoining right and answering doubts. [61 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,الحسن,الضحاك,زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الحسن,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن وقتادة وابن جريج والضحاك,زيد بن أسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الكلبي,بعض أرباب اللسان); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر,الحسن وقتادة وابن جريج والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الأصم,الحسن,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم,عنترة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك وابن جريج,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(زفر بن الحارث,شاعر العرب الذي لم يعرف اسمه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN(R, Z); THALABI(أصحاب اللسان); TAB-FULL+]
- 3:200/q03/p2 Patiently await God's promise of relief and victory. — Muhammad ibn Ka'b and Ata emphasize expectation; a saying calls waiting for relief worship. Abu Umar prefers this, Qurtubi says the enemy reading is majority. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب القرظي); MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن كعب); JISHUMI(محمد بن كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); TABRISI(محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء والقرظي)+]
- 3:200/q03/p3 Bear the conduct of relatives and neighbors and act generously. — Razi includes nonretaliation (7:199; 25:72), preferring others (59:9), and forgiveness (2:237), distinguishing shared from private hardship. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 3:200/q03/p4 Persevere in maintaining daily prayers. — Hasan applies the verb to the prayers. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 3:200/q03/p5 Persevere against the lower self and its desires. — Baydawi and Bursevi call the ego a chief adversary; Study Quran reports the saying that return from battle is return from lesser to greater struggle against passions. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 3:200/q03/p6 The second verb repeats the first for emphasis. — Abu Hayyan transmits this as another interpretation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 3:200/q04 Why are the three endurance commands ordered separately?  [ٱصْبِرُوا۟ وَصَابِرُوا۟ وَرَابِطُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the escalation is military, collective, or inward.
- 3:200/q04/p1 They progress from general patience to harder mutual struggle and persistent vigilance. — Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi single out a harder special form; Alusi compares Gabriel after angels and the middle prayer after prayers. Ibn Ashur emphasizes the endurance contest; Alusi rejects an elaborate alternative as strained. [16 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 3:200/q04/p2 They expand from individual patience to shared endurance and coordinated community strength. — Razi distinguishes personal and social duties; Tabatabai says mutual reliance multiplies strength and ribat binds religious activity in all circumstances. Alusi reports but criticizes Razi's elaborate faculty scheme. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 3:200/q04/p3 They describe inward ascent from restraint to binding the heart to God. — Junayd intensifies patience then binds the secret to God outwardly and inwardly; Bursevi ranks patience below musabara below ribat. [4 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد,بعضهم); BURSEVI]

Ibn Kathir questions one attribution and calls the Abu Ayyub verse link unusual. Razi links jihad and prayer by heart-binding; Qurtubi includes disciplined intention and body; Elmalili includes congregational attachment. [14 notes: MAJAZ(الأخطل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة وابن الأنباري); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الشيباني); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 3:200/q05/p4 Hold the inner secret in witnessing and guard the self. — Junayd binds the secret to God; Ja'far binds spirits to direct witnessing; Qashani assigns watchfulness to the spirit. - 3:200/q05/p6 Remain attached to the exemplary guides or Imams. — Reports from Ja'far vary: exemplary guides, watchfulness with the Imam, or attachment to the Imams; preceding patience commands also vary. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام)]
- 3:200/q05/p7 Hold to God's promise of victory without tiring. — Abu Hayyan attributes this to Ubayy and Muhammad ibn Ka'b. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي ومحمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 3:200/q06 Who technically counts as a frontier guard?  [وَرَابِطُوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether the question concerns the guard's status or safe residence.
- 3:200/q06/p1 One who travels to a frontier and stays a period; a settled resident is not technically a murābiṭ. — Ibn al-Mawwaz and Ibn Atiyya distinguish local families who still protect the post. Elmalili includes a modern armed sentry in barracks or outposts. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن المواز); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,محمد بن المواز); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن المواز); ELMALILI]
- 3:200/q06/p2 A fighter may take family to a secure post, but should leave them from an unsafe frontier. — Ibn Khuzaymandad fears their capture at an insecure post. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد)]

## 3:200/q07 What reward do reports attach to frontier service?  [وَرَابِطُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in duration, numerical reward and conditions; some large figures have chain criticism.
- 3:200/q07/p1 A day and night surpass a month of fasting and prayer. — Salman adds continuing provision and deeds after death and safety from grave testing. Some versions except necessary interruption; Ibn Kathir defends the Salman route via Shurahbil. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(سلمان الخير); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(سلمان); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن كثير,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سلمان الفارسي); DURR-FULL(النبي)]
- 3:200/q07/p2 A day of guard is better than the world and all it contains. — Sahl's report is transmitted by Bukhari; versions mention a whip's space in Paradise and morning or evening travel. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(سهل بن سعد الساعدي); QURTUBI-FULL(سهل بن سعد الساعدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(سهل بن سعد); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(IK, Q)]
- 3:200/q07/p3 A guard dying on duty receives continuing deeds and provision and protection from grave trials. — Fadala, Uqba, Abu Hurayra and Salman transmit variants; Ibn Kathir cites Tirmidhi's hasan sahih and corroboration for Ibn Lahi'a. Qurtubi makes death on duty the condition, contrasting charity, useful knowledge and a child's prayer; guarding faith enables all worship. [20 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,فضالة بن عبيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن كثير,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سلمان الفارسي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(IK, Q)]
- 3:200/q07/p4 A night equals or exceeds a thousand nights of worship. — Uthman's versions differ between equality and superiority. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عثمان بن عفان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عثمان)]
- 3:200/q07/p5 A day creates seven vast trenches against Hell. — Jabir describes each as seven heavens and earths wide; Tabarani calls its chain acceptable. [2 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(جابر)]
- 3:200/q07/p6 A day outside Ramadan equals a century; in Ramadan a millennium. — Ubayy's report promises continued reward even after safe return; Qurtubi draws that inference. Ibn Kathir calls the route gharib and munkar and its transmitter accused. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبي بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 3:200/q07/p7 One night surpasses a thousand years of worship at home. — Anas counts 360 days per year and a thousand years per day; Ibn Kathir calls it gharib and its transmitter weak and accused. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 3:200/q07/p8 Guarding wins special praise and reward. — At Hunayn Anas ibn Abi Mirthad guarded a pass against Hawazin; a sentry-prayer report has a broken link. A contented dusty horseman, Ibn al-Mubarak's Tarsus poem, and even a campaign horse tether illustrate merit. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن المبارك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:200/q07/p9 Prayer at a frontier is worth two million prayers. — Abu al-Shaykh transmits Anas's report. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أنس); ELMALILI]
- 3:200/q07/p10 Frontier guarding exceeds combat in merit. — Ibn Umar reasons that guarding Muslim lives outweighs shedding enemy blood. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر); ELMALILI(عبدالله بن عمر)]
- 3:200/q07/p11 A day is better than a thousand days elsewhere. — Tirmidhi calls the Uthman route hasan gharib; Ahmad adds Uthman's invitation to serve. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عثمان)]
- 3:200/q07/p12 Three coastal guard days equal a year of ribat. — Umm al-Darda' transmits this. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أم الدرداء)]
- 3:200/q07/p13 Eyes that guard in God's cause escape Hell. — Abu Rayhana's cold-night report and Ibn Abbas pair guarding with weeping from God-fear; Mu'adh's unpaid sentry report excepts only passage under 19:71. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 3:200/q08 What circumstances are reported for this verse or its citation?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reports concern revelation, later citation or adjacent history.
- 3:200/q08/p1 It addressed mosque worship and waiting between prayers. — Abu Ghassan reports revelation about remaining in mosques. Abu Salama and Abu Hurayra invoke an alleged lack of military frontiers; Alusi and Ibn Ashur dispute that premise, Ibn Kathir questions one attribution. [12 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو غسان); WAHIDI-ASBAB(أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن); THALABI(سلمة بن عبد الرحمن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن,أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو غسان,أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن)]
- 3:200/q08/p2 Umar cited it to reassure Abu Ubayda against Roman forces. — His letter says relief follows hardship and one hardship cannot defeat two eases. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 3:200/q08/p3 Najran's treaty and later dispersal are transmitted nearby. — Muqatil reports garment tribute, protections for Christians, provisions for loans, tax and justice, named witnesses, Abu Bakr's copy; Salem recounts forty thousand fighters, conflict, Umar's dispersal to Syria and Iraq, and Ali's refusal to criticize him. [4 notes: MUQATIL(ابن عباس,سالم بن أبي الجعد)]

## 3:200/q09 What does fearing God require here?  [وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether piety is described in outward duties or inward stations.
- 3:200/q09/p1 Obey God and avoid every prohibition in all affairs. — Tabari defines piety by commands and limits; Nahhas says it governs every affair, not merely war. Qurtubi requires piety in battle; Alusi says ostentation could corrupt preceding deeds. Ibn Kathir quotes advice to Mu'adh; Abduh links piety to knowledge of what pleases God through scripture, Sunna and early exemplars. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,محمد بن كعب القرظي); NAHHAS; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Abduh)]
- 3:200/q09/p2 Purify the inward self from attachment to other than God. — Ja'far names gracious intimacy; Baydawi and Bursevi contrast disavowal and avoidance of vice as law, path and reality; Qashani lists display, objection, pride and selfhood at successive stations. [8 notes: SULAMI(جعفر الصادق); THALABI(أصحاب اللسان,بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:200/q10 How does 'perhaps' work?  [لَعَلَّكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Hope or purpose.
- 3:200/q10/p1 It urges believers to hope for success while continuing good deeds. — Zajjaj says 23:1 assures success but the verse addresses human hope and avoidance of acts nullifying work. Ibn Atiyya calls it the human viewpoint; Nasafi says uncertainty prevents complacency. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 3:200/q10/p2 It can state the purpose of the commands. — Qurtubi allows hope or purpose; Jishumi names reward or escape from invalidated deeds. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 3:200/q11 What is the promised flourishing?  [تُفْلِحُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Root meaning or concrete promised ends.
- 3:200/q11/p1 Attained reward, worldly victory and happiness in the next life. — Muhammad ibn Ka'b points to meeting God tomorrow; Ibn Kathir includes both lives, Biqai victory and martyrs' bliss. Jishumi argues against Murji'a denial of acts' role; Tabatabai sees complete happiness in both worlds. [13 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب القرظي); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:200/q11/p2 Enduring success and everlasting life. — Ibn Qutayba derives falah from remaining; Dahhak agrees. Nasafi specifies staying with what one loves after hardship. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 3:200/q12 How does the closing verse connect to the sūra and its neighbors?  [* · links]
turns on: Immediate summary, inter-sūra link or wider sūra context.
- 3:200/q12/p1 It gathers beliefs and duties, renewing resolve after Uhud. — Razi lists monotheism, justice, prophecy, resurrection, pilgrimage and jihad; Asamm connects many duties. Ibn Ashur locates vigilance after Uhud and before later surprise; Alusi recalls suffering and enemy stratagems. Tabatabai calls this the sura's result. [11 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(الأصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:200/q12/p2 It links al-Baqara's end and al-Nisa's opening. — Biqai connects 2:286's plea for victory, 2:186's nearness, earlier scripture, and a close echoing the opening. Alusi notes taqwa at both sura boundaries and Uhud links (3:172; 4:84,88); Elmalili sees further instruction on family and society. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 3:200/q12/p3 The preceding sūra treats theology, Mary and Jesus, People of the Book, and Uhud. — Study Quran notes the name from 3:33; interpretation's levels at 3:7; Mary, Jesus, mixed portrayals of scriptural communities, and Uhud's cowardice, hypocrisy, attachment and afterlife questions. [6 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 3:200/q13 What is reported about the sūra's revelation and Najran debate?  [* · reports]
- 3:200/q13/p1 The first thirty to eighty verses are associated with a Najran Christian visit and debate. — Christians argued from Jesus's miracles, fatherless birth and plural divine speech; the Prophet answered with his mortality, limited knowledge, bodily needs and inability to sustain creation. A delegate had Byzantine honors; the opening was reportedly revealed afterward. [9 notes: STUDYQURAN(Christians,Prophet,R)]

## 3:200/q14 What virtues and recitations of Āl ʿImrān are reported?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in reward and Friday timing; Alusi rejects the bridge report.
- 3:200/q14/p1 Friday recitation wins divine and angelic blessings until sunset or sunrise. — Kashshaf and Alusi give sunset; Baydawi gives sunrise. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 3:200/q14/p2 The two radiant sūras plead for reciters at Judgment. — Nasafi and Study Quran describe clouds, sunrays or birds interceding. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN]
- 3:200/q14/p3 Each recited verse grants safety on the bridge over Hell. — Kashshaf and Baydawi transmit it; Alusi calls it fabricated and censures transmitters. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 3:200/q14/p4 Reciting the ending at night earns vigil reward; the Prophet recited its final ten at night. — Alusi transmits Uthman and Abu Hurayra; Kur'an Yolu reports tahajjud practice. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة,عثمان); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:200/q15 What is said of al-Nisāʾ after Āl ʿImrān?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Date and verse-count traditions.
- 3:200/q15/p1 It is Medinan; one Meccan verse does not classify the whole sūra. — Alusi cites Aisha's post-Hijra setting, rejects Nahhas's inference from the Kaaba key verse, and quotes Suyuti's distinction. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي,عائشة)]
- 3:200/q15/p2 It was revealed around the Hijra. — Alusi reports an alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:200/q15/p3 Its verse counts are Syrian 177, Kufan 176, and other 175. — Alusi locates the difference in two clause divisions. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

- 3:200/q16/p1 Razi completed this sūra's commentary on Thursday, 1 Rabīʿ al-Ākhir 595 AH. — Razi dates his completion. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 3:200/q16/p2 Alusi finished al-Fatiha and the two radiant sūras and hoped to finish the tafsīr. — Alusi records this hope. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:200/q17 Why does the verse address believers as a collective?  [* · interpretation]
- 3:200/q17/p1 Human life is intrinsically social, and revelation consciously organizes shared religious life. — Tabatabai traces marriage and household into clan and rule through strength, wealth and political skill; this early hierarchy encouraged idolatry. Conflict led prophets and books to restore unity (10:19; 2:213; 42:13). Islam uniquely orders communal activity explicitly. Shared origins and kinship (25:54; 49:13; 3:195) give communities a real collective lifespan, knowledge and deeds; group panic and custom can overwhelm individuals. The Qur'an commands brotherhood, reconciliation, cooperation and avoiding division (3:104-105; 6:153,158; 8:46; 5:2). Government and every believer sustain worship, giving and enjoining right. [31 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:200/q18 Does popularity or historical success decide which social order is true?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether popularity, current power or objective reality determines truth.
- 3:200/q18/p1 No; truth is external reality, and a system may be true before prevailing. — Tabatabai reports objections from early Islamic decline, Western longevity, and majority-governed modern order. He replies that new systems begin amid hostility, prophetic groups began small, Islam spread rapidly and transformed history, and present weakness is no refutation (3:137). Frequent causal outcomes differ from majority opinion: even skeptics assent to evident reality, while majorities can oppose truth (23:70-71). Law may account for predominant practical benefit (5:6; 2:183). He disputes attributing modern progress to Christianity. [27 notes: TABATABAI]
- 3:200/q18/p2 The objection takes modern majority rule, durability and social strength as evidence of superiority. — Tabatabai reports claims that Western order refines conduct and Islamic law cannot now operate; proponents liken majority rule to prevalent natural outcomes. [6 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 3:200/q19 How does Islamic social happiness compare with material civilization?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether happiness comprises bodily and spiritual truth or chiefly material satisfaction.
- 3:200/q19/p1 It joins bodily welfare to knowledge of God, virtue and worship. — Tabatabai says Islamic law uses sound reason, social enforcement and inward education, whereas material civil law follows appetite and regulates mainly visible conduct. He traces material idolatry into collective systems, critiques European domination and class division, and urges judging nations by treatment of outsiders while exempting just individuals from group blame. A society has a common purpose like a body's organs; competition requires law, but excluding morals and theology turns religion into political custom. He attributes communism and world wars to inequality and greed and rejects wealth alone as happiness. [42 notes: TABATABAI]
- 3:200/q19/p2 Material civil law principally pursues collective enjoyment and majority preference. — Tabatabai describes its outward restrictions and freedom outside them, but disputes its claim to moral completeness. [13 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 3:200/q20 What sustains virtue and endurance without a visible worldly reward?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether divine accountability supports or displaces worldly responsibility.
- 3:200/q20/p1 God's knowledge and final judgment motivate good even in hidden or costly cases. — Tabatabai says patriotism, praise and imagined posthumous fame are unstable without afterlife. Antara's poem offers glory or relief, but fails for unrecognized sacrifice. The Qur'an treats victory and death as two good ends (9:51-52) and records every hardship (9:120-121); the Companions' confession of crimes illustrates seeking purification. He rejects the charge of monasticism: fitra (30:30) demands honoring proper worldly causes alongside the hereafter, with worship central. [19 notes: TABATABAI(عنترة)]
- 3:200/q20/p2 The objection says afterlife pursuit would damage society and turn believers to monasticism. — Tabatabai reports and rejects this and the claim that social justice is religion's sole end. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 3:200/q21 How can divine law allow freedom and change?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether stable foundations impede or enable adaptive social life.
- 3:200/q21/p1 Human will operates within divine norms, while policy and technical means adapt. — Tabatabai says free will is natural, but society needs limits; Islam frees people from service to others and permits good provisions (7:32; 2:29; 45:13). Cognitive belief itself cannot be legislated, but related public acts can. Industry and understanding may advance, while monotheism, prophecy and judgment remain stable. Basic human needs persist, methods change, and leaders consult on changing war, finance and administration (3:159) without abrogating Qur'an and Sunna. He rejects relativism and compulsive change. [27 notes: TABATABAI]
- 3:200/q21/p2 The objection calls for creed freedom and says fixed religious law blocks progress. — Some researchers cite 2:256; other objectors invoke dialectical change, variable morals and the distance between ancient law and modern industry. Tabatabai distinguishes private inquiry from public disruption. [4 notes: TABATABAI(المعترض,بعض الباحثين والمفسرين); TABATABAI-]

## 3:200/q22 How should the Muslim community govern and maintain its bonds?  [* · law]
- 3:200/q22/p1 It shares responsibility under prophetic guidance, consultation, faith-based belonging and collective duty. — Tabatabai cites Prophetic authority (64:12; 4:105; 33:6; 3:31) and general commands to believers, while the Prophet uniquely teaches and purifies (62:2). Rank depends on piety (49:13), not class; all enjoin right. He reports majority election and Shi'a designation of twelve Imams, then gives governance during occultation to Muslims preserving divine law and consulting on contingent affairs (33:21). Faith, not homeland, bounds the community and even affects marriage and inheritance. Practice persists under coercion and weakness (16:106; 3:28; 64:16); collective forms vary among defense, fasting, hajj, Friday and daily prayer, the latter strongly encouraged in congregation. [25 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:200/q23 How should believers investigate truth while preventing division?  [* · interpretation]
- 3:200/q23/p1 Use education, shared Qur'anic study and qualified guidance, allowing thought without coercion. — Tabatabai says fitra inclines to truth, and God excuses those lacking proof (8:42; 4:98). Moral disposition and geography affect understanding, so preachers address listeners by capacity; a report says prophets do likewise. Groups learn and return to warn (9:122). Believers hold the Qur'an and Messenger's rope (3:102-103), reflect (4:82) and refer disputes to Prophet or leaders (4:59,83). Doubts deserve communal inquiry; public unreviewed opinion risks division, while force, takfir and exclusion are rejected. He laments loss of inquiry and affirms balanced spiritual, bodily and social fitra. [21 notes: TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 3:200/q24 Will Islam's complete social goal eventually prevail?  [* · theology]
- 3:200/q24/p1 Yes, its collective realization is humanity's eventual natural goal. — Tabatabai cites people who love God and strive (5:54), believers' succession and security (24:55), and righteous inheritance (21:105). He says earlier failed practice proves no impossibility: orders emerge through resolute people without prior decisive experiment, and deviations misapply fitra. [8 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:200/q25 What other inward readings interpret the sequence?  [* · inward]
- 3:200/q25/p1 The commands form stations of certainty, worship, witnessing and self-purification. — Tustari names reliance, surrender, acceptance and gratitude with piety, then certainty as faith's heart, patience its support, sincerity its completion. Sufi reports describe outward worship, struggle against sins, divine witnessing, and the fall of anxious thoughts; Sari links ribat to the reproachful soul. Qashani distinguishes self, heart and spirit. [18 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(الجنيد,بعضهم,جعفر الصادق); THALABI(أصحاب اللسان,السري السقطي,بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 3:200/q26 What related prayer for Paradise and protection from Hell is reported?  [* · reports]
- 3:200/q26/p1 Paradise welcomes those who ask for it three times, and Hell seeks protection for those who ask refuge three times. — Bursevi transmits Anas's report and prays for Paradise and good deeds. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أنس بن مالك)]
