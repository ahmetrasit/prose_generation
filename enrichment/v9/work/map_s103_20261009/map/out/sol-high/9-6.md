# 9:6: 29 questions

## 9:6/q01 Who is the polytheist eligible to seek protection?  [أَحَدٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the indefinite 'one' specifies universal coverage and how the preceding fighting order bounds it.
- 9:6/q01/p1 Any individual polytheist covered by the fighting order, including a person from a hostile or treaty breaking group after the grace period. — Ibn Ishaq identifies those ordered fought; Ibn Abbas calls him treatyless; Ibn Ashur says 'one' makes coverage universal even after a broken pact, citing 5:2 and the ban on answering betrayal with betrayal. Other authors specify the expired sacred months. Al-Burhan argues from the grammar of an indefinite noun under a conditional particle that the example of any polytheist seeking protection has general scope. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNHISHAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,محمد بن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURHAN+]
- 9:6/q01/p2 The phrasing includes any polytheist who seeks to hear revelation or has a sound reason to visit. — Mujahid says whoever comes to hear the Prophet and revelation; al-Biqa'i calls it a limited middle course after general restrictions; Ibn Ashur stresses the individual noun and hypothetical conditional. Bint al-Shati identifies people who have not yet heard God's words as the intended listeners and says accomplished eloquence is no prerequisite. [9 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]

## 9:6/q02 How is 'if one of the polytheists seeks protection' parsed?  [وَإِنْ أَحَدٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ ٱسْتَجَارَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the nominative is governed by a recovered verb or is an initial subject; why in permits the construction.
- 9:6/q02/p1 A hidden verb raises ahad, and istajāraka explains it; conditional in is normally followed by a verb. — Al-Akhfash calls this the more analogical analysis; al-Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, al-Baydawi and others reject a bare subject after in. Al-Farra says in uniquely tolerates separation from its jussive verb; 4:176 and poetry illustrate it. Sibawayh calls in the mother of conditionals. Al-Burhan places this conditional construction among cases where an omitted verb is explained by another expression, comparing 'iyyāya fa-rhabūn' and 'wa-l-samāʾa rafaʿahā'. [21 notes: AKHFASH; FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); NAHHAS(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); AKHFASH+; BURHAN+; TABRISI(أبو علي)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 9:6/q02/p2 Ahad can be a nominal subject after the conditional, with a following verbal predicate; the implied verb is only an analytical device. — Al-Akhfash adduces noun-initial constructions after an, including poems 'ʿāwid Harāʾa', 'lā tajzaʿī', and 'atajzaʿ'; Abu Ali answers al-Zajjaj by invoking Sibawayh and an underlying verb in the response. Ibn Ashur appeals to generic indefiniteness and conditional generality. [6 notes: AKHFASH(some,الشاعر); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNASHUR-FULL; AKHFASH-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; TABRISI-; RAZI-FULL-; BAYDAWI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج)-]
- 9:6/q02/p3 The special grammar of in is not explained by saying it occurs only in conditions. — Ibn Yazid notes that in can mean mā, be extra, or be lightened from inna; its ambiguity remains distinctive. Al-Qurtubi transmits the same objection to Sibawayh. [2 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن يزيد); NAHHAS(سيبويه)-; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 9:6/q03 Can a noun stand before the jussive conditional response?  [وَإِنْ أَحَدٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ ٱسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ · grammar]
turns on: How to parse the poetic accusative and whether the response has an implied fa.
- 9:6/q03/p1 An accusative object may intervene, but a nominative subject may not. — Al-Kisa'i cites poetry with accusative al-khayr after a jussive conditional, reasoning that the response verb recalls its own subject. [2 notes: FARRA(الكسائي)]
- 9:6/q03/p2 Without fa or an equivalent jussive, a nominal answer is invalid; the cited accusative is adjectival. — Al-Farra rejects al-Kisa'i's exception, parses al-khayr as an adjective of al-ayyām and reads taʿqub as feminine. [1 notes: FARRA; FARRA(الكسائي)-]

## 9:6/q04 What force do the indefinite ahad and fronted conditional have?  [وَإِنْ أَحَدٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ · rhetoric]
- 9:6/q04/p1 They foreground any individual seeker and strengthen the protection rule. — Sibawayh, as reported by Razi, sees fronting the important object as protecting blood despite the fight command. Ibn Jinni finds breadth in conditional in and allows ahad to mean one individual, citing 4:128 and 4:176. Al-Burhan adds expected occurrence and special identification; Ibn Ashur says the rare hypothetical precludes an excuse that access was denied. Al-Burhan gives the general Arabic rule that an indefinite noun under a conditional particle covers every instance, illustrating it with seeing any person and with this verse's seeker. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); BURHAN(ابن جني); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN+]
- 9:6/q04/p2 The doubled explanatory construction gives emphasis. — Al-Burhan says an object or subject mentioned first and resumed by a pronoun or explanatory verb has greater force than ordinary order, citing 17:100, 76:31, 7:30, 82:1, and 84:1. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 9:6/q05 What does istajāraka mean?  [ٱسْتَجَارَكَ · meaning]
- 9:6/q05/p1 To ask for shelter, security or a protector. — Al-Jishumi relates it to taking someone into one's neighborhood; Asad invokes the Arabian duty to protect neighbors. Ibn Ashur calls neighboring a metaphor from safe residence and says ajāra means securing rather than literally making a neighbor. Al-Tha'labi adduces Arabic usage and poetry. [7 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 9:6/q06 For what purposes does the verse require or allow safe conduct?  [ٱسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ · law]
turns on: Whether hearing God's speech defines the seeker's reason for asking or the host's purpose in granting refuge.
- 9:6/q06/p1 Protection to hear and examine Islam is obligatory; other purposes can also be protected according to their circumstances and Muslim interests. — Qurtubi calls religious inquiry the verse's apparent case and other grants matters of public interest. Elmalili distinguishes obligatory religious protection from context dependent worldly requests. Ibn Kathir lists missions, trade, peace, truce and tribute; Wahidi lists trade, envoys, and property retrieval. Ibn Ashur allows reasonable motives and says discussion of Islam will occur during a temporary stay. Malik would return an ambiguous enemy encountered on a road to safety, and Ibn al-Qasim applies the same protection to a coastal trader expecting peaceful sale. Ibn Ashur cites the Prophet's prohibition on betraying a betrayer. [12 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن القاسم,مالك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل العلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İslâm âlimleri)]
- 9:6/q06/p2 The request itself is general, while hearing God's word is the grantor's purpose. — Elmalili prefers no restriction of the seeker to religious needs; Alusi says Ali's reported 'need' may be general, although he identifies its immediate case as religious. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI+; ELMALILI-; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)-; TABATABAI-]
- 9:6/q06/p3 The verse specifically protects a request for religious inquiry or a comparable religious need. — Tabataba'i denies that the verse establishes general asylum for unrelated reasons; Shaykh al-Islam ties the request to the stated end of hearing. Some interpreters read Ali's 'need' religiously. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام); ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]

## 9:6/q07 Who may grant effective safe conduct?  [فَأَجِرْهُ · law]
turns on: Which persons can bind the community by a pledge and how to treat an ambiguous pledge.
- 9:6/q07/p1 The ruler, a free Muslim, a slave, a free woman, and a fighting capable minor may grant it. — Qurtubi says the ruler acts for the public benefit and all accept a free man's pledge; he prefers the majority's permission for a slave, citing the hadith that even the lowest Muslim grants protection, and says lack of a war stipend is immaterial. Abu Hayyan lists the Awza'i, Thawri, Shafi'i, Ahmad, Ishaq, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan, Abu Thawr, Dawud and the known Maliki view; a fighting boy belongs to the defenders. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأوزاعي، والثوري، والشافعي، وأحمد، وإسحاق، ومحمد بن الحسن، وأبو ثور، وداود,الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الملك بن الماجشون)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الملك بن الماجشون)-]
- 9:6/q07/p2 A slave's or woman's pledge needs the imam's review or approval; some deny the slave's pledge. — Abu Hanifa and another Maliki view deny a slave's effective pledge; Ibn Habib refers it to the imam. Ibn al-Majishun alone conditions a free woman's pledge on imam approval, which Abu Hayyan calls anomalous. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الملك بن الماجشون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأوزاعي، والثوري، والشافعي، وأحمد، وإسحاق، ومحمد بن الحسن، وأبو ثور، وداود,الجمهور,عبد الملك بن الماجشون); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 9:6/q07/p3 Even an ambiguous pledge by a child or insane person may protect the entrant pending safe return. — Wahidi's jurists treat a minor's or insane person's promise as ambiguous safe conduct; Elmalili reports protection for lawful religious and trade entrants even under such a guarantee. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل العلم); ELMALILI]

## 9:6/q08 What does the command 'grant him protection' require?  [فَأَجِرْهُ · law]
- 9:6/q08/p1 Secure the seeker from killing and attack while he hears the message, then preserve his safety until return. — Al-Zajjaj specifies protection from killing; al-Nahhas removes him from the kill order. Al-Dahhak says maintain him during the stay. Al-Biqa'i requires protection against anyone who might harm him; Kuranyolu stresses absence of fear and pressure. [33 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); NAHHAS; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 9:6/q08/p2 A treatyless arrival can either be received or returned safely. — Aṭāʾ's report leaves the two practical dispositions open. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عطاء)]

## 9:6/q09 How long may the protection last?  [فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّىٰ يَسْمَعَ · law]
turns on: Whether a fixed four month interval governs or the ruler and circumstances determine the stay.
- 9:6/q09/p1 It continues through genuine inquiry and until the visitor reaches safety; its exact duration is set by circumstances or the ruler. — Razi says custom governs, continuing while truth seeking is sincere but not for a deceptive delay. Alusi prefers the ruler's judgment. Ibn Kathir reports four months permissible, a full year disallowed, and disagreement between. Ibn Ashur says the return duty can take long. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 9:6/q09/p2 Four months is the stipulated respite. — Alusi reports al-Nisaburi calling four months the sound Shafi'i view; Ibn Kathir reports its permissibility. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 9:6/q10 What does it mean for the seeker to 'hear' God's speech?  [يَسْمَعَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether physical recitation is enough and how much teaching suffices for understanding.
- 9:6/q10/p1 He must hear and understand the Quran, its proofs, commands and prohibitions; a non Arabic speaker needs comprehension. — Ibn Atiyya says hearing stands for comprehension because the ear leads to it. Tabataba'i makes intelligibility rather than Arabic sound decisive for non Arabs; Alusi and Elmalili say an eloquent Arab may understand from hearing. Al-Tabrisi requires time to consider; Abu Hayyan calls the Quran the greatest miracle. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:6/q10/p2 The amount heard may be the whole Quran or the essential message of this surah. — Study Quran reports both thresholds without choosing; Maʾwardi gives the corresponding scopes of 'speech'. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(R); MAWARDI-FULL]

## 9:6/q11 What is 'God's speech' here?  [كَلَٰمَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: How broadly the relevant Quranic speech is identified.
- 9:6/q11/p1 The Quran as a whole, using passages relevant to belief, duties and the listener's doubts. — Al-Suddi, Qatada, Muqatil and others identify the Quran or Book. Ibn Ashur says God originated it, sent it through an angel, and it is unlike a human composed hadith qudsi. Tabataba'i argues there is no evidence to confine it to monotheism, Bara'a, or seasonal verses. Al-Damghani glosses kalām Allāh as the revealed Quran and gives the same sense for God's words in 48:15. [23 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي، قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي؛ مقاتل,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); DAMGHANI+; TABATABAI+]
- 9:6/q11/p2 Sūrat al-Tawba or its opening rulings are meant. — Maʾwardi says it teaches treaties and breaches and distinguishes dealings with polytheists and hypocrites; Alusi records the view; Study Quran reports the limited surah threshold. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI-]
- 9:6/q11/p3 The verses establishing God's oneness are meant. — Alusi records this scope among the interpretations. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 9:6/q12 What does hearing God's speech imply about its nature?  [كَلَٰمَ ٱللَّهِ · theology]
turns on: Whether the audible recitation is the eternal attribute, created sounds, or a sign of a separate attribute.
- 9:6/q12/p1 The recited and heard Quran is genuinely God's speech, an eternal attribute rather than merely created text. — Tabrisi appeals to ordinary attribution of an author's speech across recitation; Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin call the annexation an attribute to its described noun. Qurtubi cites Abu al-Hasan, al-Qadi Abu Bakr, al-Qalanisi, Ibn Mujahid and al-Isfarayini for hearing divine speech when recited, while affirming speech is not letters or sounds. Al-Ash'ari invokes the verse for hearing eternal speech. [7 notes: TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الأشعري)]
- 9:6/q12/p2 Only originated letters and sounds are heard; therefore God's audible speech is created. — Razi and Abu Hayyan report the Mu'tazilite inference from successively produced audible sounds; Razi answers that present recitation is a human act even on their account and distinct from God's original production. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 9:6/q12/p3 The very letters and sounds heard are eternal. — Razi attributes this to the Hashwiyya and reports others using 9:6 as evidence from what an idolater hears; he rejects identifying eternal speech with audible sound. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحشوية والحمقى,القائلون بقدم الحروف); RAZI-FULL-]
- 9:6/q12/p4 Hearing the recited letters also amounts to hearing a separate eternal speech. — Ibn Furak proposes this; Razi says other Ash'ari scholars reject it because no distinct entity beyond the sounds is heard. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر بن فورك); RAZI-FULL-]
- 9:6/q12/p5 One hears a sign conveying God's eternal attribute, not the attribute itself through the senses. — Abu Mansur rejects sensory hearing of the attribute; Bursevi uses the analogy of hearing news that indicates a person's knowledge. Alusi says hearing can be ascribed to a sign and that kalam can denote inward or voiced speech literally or figuratively, so the verse does not refute inner speech. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ أبو منصور); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(الأشعري)-]
- 9:6/q12/p6 God's speech is a distinct entity persisting with every reader. — Razi reports al-Jubba'i's proposal and says the Mu'tazilites rejected it as unusual. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي)]

## 9:6/q13 Does the requirement to hear the Quran establish a proof for faith?  [حَتَّىٰ يَسْمَعَ كَلَٰمَ ٱللَّهِ · theology]
- 9:6/q13/p1 Hearing and considering the Quran supplies a binding miraculous proof; inquiry, rather than imitation, grounds faith. — Razi and Abu Hayyan infer the high rank of inquiry and insufficiency of imitation. Tabataba'i asks for certain knowledge, not strong probability. Al-Burhan and al-Itqan infer inimitability from the command to hear; al-Baqillani says eloquence, doubts, pride and attention explain varied listener responses. Tabari's discussion says unmatched eloquence is a messenger's proof, with speakers differing in capacity, citing 43:18 and works by al-Baqillani, al-Khattabi and al-Rummani. Al-Baqillani says the instruction to let the seeker hear shows recitation itself can establish proof against him. Bint al-Shati says its intended listeners need not be accomplished rhetoricians: early converts recognized inimitability through linguistic instinct, many later converts believed on first hearing after being kept from the Quran, and the eloquent miracle also confronted polytheists defending inherited religious, economic, and social arrangements. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو بكر الباقلاني); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BAQILLANI+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 9:6/q13/p2 The duty to explain proof extends to anyone seeking evidence or answers to religious doubts. — Razi extends protection by the stated reason of ignorance; Elmalili requires Muslims to explain divine unity and prophethood to a belligerent before killing, and to answer questions about affairs; Kuranyolu reports the duty for any non Muslim inquirer. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İslâm âlimleri)]
- 9:6/q13/p3 The verse rebuts the claim that religious knowledge is innate and necessary. — Tabrisi reasons that the people must hear and consider proofs precisely because they do not yet know them. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 9:6/q14 How does hattā work in 'until he hears'?  [حَتَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether hatta marks limit or purpose, and whether it may govern an unspoken pronoun.
- 9:6/q14/p1 It attaches to 'grant him protection' and can mark either an endpoint or a purpose. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin allow both readings; the majority reject syntactic competition with istajāraka because it would force hatta to govern an omitted pronoun, tolerable only in poetic necessity. Alusi transmits the same objection. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الفاضل ابن العادل)]
- 9:6/q14/p2 It expresses purpose or cause: protect him so that he hears. — Alusi prefers this on context and the later requirement to send him safely away; Sari al-Din al-Misri says a merely terminal reading cannot account for protection after hearing. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(سري الدين المصري); ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 9:6/q14/p3 With a grammatically permitted hidden pronoun, both verbs can compete to govern hattā. — Al-Samin reports grammarians who allow the omitted governed pronoun, comparing farḥtu wa marartu bi-Zayd. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 9:6/q15 What must happen after the seeker has heard the message?  [ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُۥ · law]
- 9:6/q15/p1 A non convert must remain unharmed and be returned or allowed to reach a safe place before hostilities resume. — Mujahid names the place he came from; Muqatil, Tha'labi and Baghawi permit fighting or killing if he later fights and is captured. Nasafi disallows harming, detaining, or letting him settle in Muslim territory. Biqa'i forbids betrayal before arrival. Tabataba'i stresses free choice; Asad cites 2:256. Ibn Ashur says this means release and non harm, without a duty to arrange escort or transport. [46 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNHISHAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد,الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي,الضحاك,سعيد بن أبي عروبة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 9:6/q15/p2 Acceptance of Islam places him within Muslim protection, so the return duty concerns refusal. — Wahidi says the convert becomes a brother in religion; al-Hasan says he enters Islam's protection; al-Dahhak and Sa'id ibn Abi Aruba say conversion is the desired outcome. [6 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك,سعيد بن أبي عروبة)]
- 9:6/q15/p3 Once the proof has been established and he persists in rejection, fighting may be resumed after safe return. — Tabataba'i says killing is warranted only when hope of guidance ends; Kuranyolu says the later choice to leave the sacred zone or enter conflict is knowing and the kill restriction is geographically limited. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 9:6/q16 Why is thumma used before the command to bring him to safety?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 9:6/q16/p1 It underscores the higher priority of sustained protection through the whole return, even if that takes time. — Ibn Ashur reads the sequencing particle as stressing the duty after hearing. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 9:6/q17 How is 'bring him to his place of safety' constructed grammatically?  [أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُۥ · grammar]
- 9:6/q17/p1 Abligh has two objects here, one understood through an omitted preposition. — Al-Nahhas notes the plural of maʾman is maʾāmin. Al-Jishumi connects balāgh and īṣāl as conveying to an endpoint and notes that reaching a term can mean approaching its end, citing 2:234. [2 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI]

## 9:6/q18 What does maʾmanahu denote?  [مَأْمَنَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether maʾman is a place noun or a verbal noun.
- 9:6/q18/p1 His safe place, normally his own people or homeland. — Ibn Ashur says the possessive distinguishes the person's home safety from temporary asylum; Razi, as reported by Asad, glosses it as homeland. Alusi prefers the place noun; Abu Hayyan permits 'place of safety'. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 9:6/q18/p2 The attainment of safety itself, as a verbal noun. — Al-Samin reports this reading; Abu Hayyan allows 'safety itself', while Alusi prefers the place reading and says a verbal noun would require an omitted modifier. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 9:6/q19 What does dhālika point back to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative covers the whole process or a particular stage.
- 9:6/q19/p1 The kindness and whole sequence of granting asylum, allowing hearing, and securing return. — Ibn Atiyya calls these the acts of gentleness; Abu Hayyan reports him. Ibn Ashur says the closing clause explains the whole command despite past harm. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:6/q19/p2 The safe return of a listener who does not convert. — Ibn al-Jawzi records this alternative alongside the reason for initial shelter and teaching. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 9:6/q19/p3 The protection and instruction at the start. — Ibn al-Jawzi records this alternative: ignorance explains teaching and asylum. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 9:6/q20 What do 'they do not know' and its stated reason mean?  [بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌۭ لَّا يَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The object of their ignorance and whether the clause explains teaching, return, or both.
- 9:6/q20/p1 They are ignorant of Islam, God's oneness, the message and its proofs; safety and teaching remove their excuse. — Ibn Abbas glosses this as not reasoning; Maʾwardi also mentions not distinguishing guidance from error. Biqa'i says some had no contact with scripture or prophethood. Ibn Ashur says the clause both condemns ignorance and explains accommodation. Bursevi notes jurists excuse ritual make up for converts in enemy territory who did not learn obligations, unlike converts within Muslim territory. [25 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNHISHAM; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:6/q20/p2 They may not know that their lives again become exposed when the protection expires. — Maʾwardi gives this alternative object of their ignorance. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 9:6/q21 Does the protection ruling remain in force?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The chronology and scope of 9:5, 9:6, and 47:4, and whether their commands are compatible.
- 9:6/q21/p1 It remains operative indefinitely and qualifies fighting commands. — Al-Hasan repeatedly says until Resurrection; Mujahid and Ibn Zayd agree. Qurtubi endorses it with Ali's after deadline answer and rejects the four month restriction; Abu Hayyan and Alusi prefer it. Tabari rejects abrogation because no prior universal duty to kill has been proved, and cites the Prophet's Badr practice of killing, releasing and ransoming. Tabataba'i says proof must precede severity. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); THALABI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك والسدي)-; ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن أبي عروبة)-]
- 9:6/q21/p2 It was abrogated by a later command to fight polytheists. — Al-Dahhak, al-Suddi, Mujahid in one transmission, and Sa'id ibn Abi Aruba are named. Alusi's report cites 9:36; other transmitters cite 9:5. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك والسدي); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن أبي عروبة); ALUSI-FULL(سعيد بن أبي عروبة); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 9:6/q21/p3 The rule was confined to the four month respite. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report this limiting view; Qurtubi explicitly rejects it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 9:6/q21/p4 Neither the asylum rule nor the prisoner release and ransom option in 47:4 is abrogated. — Study Quran calls this dominant; Tabari says options were already practiced from Badr, and without proof of a prior universal command no cancellation follows. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 9:6/q21/p5 The order to kill in 9:5 was superseded by release or ransom in 47:4. — Al-Dahhak is reported with this direction of abrogation. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 9:6/q21/p6 The order to take and bind captives in 47:4 was superseded by 9:5. — Qatada reports the reverse direction. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة)]

## 9:6/q22 How does this verse relate to the preceding fighting order and treaty context?  [* · links]
- 9:6/q22/p1 It qualifies the general order to fight by requiring safe access to hear the proof. — Ibn Abbas says the 9:5 command is modified by repentance, prayer, alms or requested hearing. Abu Hayyan and Tabataba'i call it a qualification; Alusi resolves an apparent conflict. Tabataba'i says guiding from shirk is the mission and Kuranyolu stresses mercy and freedom from coerced, merely outward conversion. [9 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:6/q22/p2 It elaborates the repentance exception or qualifies the kill order. — Ibn Ashur gives both syntactic connections to 9:5 and mentions an embassy as well as instruction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:6/q22/p3 The surah's treaty cancellation, exceptions and exposure of hypocrites provide the wider setting. — Ibn Hisham connects Bara'a to the general pact, special tribal agreements, Tabuk absentees and hypocrites. Biqa'i ties the kept treaty exception to 9:1 and Hudaybiyyah. Tabataba'i notes reprieve, fidelity to kept pacts and notice before cancellation for feared betrayal, citing 8:58. [3 notes: IBNHISHAM; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:6/q23 What does the reported exchange with Ali establish?  [* · reports]
- 9:6/q23/p1 After the deadline, Ali said a polytheist coming to hear God's speech or meet a need must not be killed, and cited this verse. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibn Abbas transmit variants of the question and answer; the Qummi account asks about meeting the Prophet after four months. Ibn Ashur could not locate the report and observes that it would postdate revelation. Alusi and Elmalili debate whether 'need' extends beyond religion. [9 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(علي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام)]

## 9:6/q24 What other reported cases illustrate protected entry or contact with enemies?  [* · reports]
- 9:6/q24/p1 An enemy combatant asked to hear God's speech, accepted testimony and repudiated idols. — Sa'id's report says the Muslims asked for testimony and rejection of idols, which he affirmed. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد)]
- 9:6/q24/p2 The Prophet protected Quraysh envoys at Hudaybiyyah, and their reports of Muslim reverence aided conversion. — Ibn Kathir names Urwa, Makraz, and Suhayl and says their impressions spread on return. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 9:6/q24/p3 A Musaylimah envoy was safe while on mission but was executed after later public endorsement outside it. — Ibn Kathir reports the Prophet's refusal to kill an envoy and Ibn Mas'ud's later execution. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 9:6/q24/p4 Battle reports show fighting and restraint in other encounters with polytheists. — Ibn Ishaq reports twenty two polytheists killed at Uhud. At Hunayn, pregnant Umm Sulaym urged killing fugitives, carried a dagger for attackers, and the Prophet said God might suffice her. [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق,عبد الله بن أبي بكر)]
- 9:6/q24/p5 Abu Sufyan, Abu Jahl, and al-Akhnas listened secretly to the Prophet's nighttime recitation, then reproached one another. — Bint al-Shati reports Ibn Ishaq's account that the three listened separately until dawn and afterwards reproached one another. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن إسحاق)]

## 9:6/q25 What inward lesson is drawn from granting refuge to the ignorant?  [* · inward]
- 9:6/q25/p1 Divine care for an ignorant seeker implies greater care for believers seeking nearness and for God's knowledgeable friends. — Qushayri appeals to protection from separation and 21:103, contrasting sincere and spiritually bankrupt, unveiled and veiled, lover and hater; he quotes a poem on faithful and deficient beloveds. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 9:6/q25/p2 God gives the ignorant ego time to return; the servant should repent early, especially in youth. — Bursevi recounts an Israelite youth who worshipped twenty years then disobeyed twenty and heard acceptance promised on return. Sa'di's verses criticize late repentance; another Persian verse praises young solitude; an Arabic poem portrays old sins darkening the heart. [6 notes: BURSEVI(السعدي)]

## 9:6/q26 What Arabic ellipsis supports analysis of an unspoken verb here?  [وَإِنْ أَحَدٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ ٱسْتَجَارَكَ · grammar]
- 9:6/q26/p1 Context and convention permit a recoverable verb, especially with a bare accusative or familiar command. — Sibawayh gives 'Zaydan' for 'act on Zayd', warning repetitions 'lion, lion' and 'wall, wall', 'clear the road', proverbs 'amr mubkiyātika' and 'al-ẓibāʾ ʿalā al-baqar', the prayer for hyena and wolf, elliptical replies about children and a water place, and al-Miskin's 'brother' line. Jarir's poetry supports the transitive road command. He rules out eliding a preposition with its governed noun or an absent third person through a hearer. Razi adds 'Zayd' as the answer to 'Who did it?'. Al-Burhan says a following expression can explain an omitted verb, citing 'iyyāya fa-rhabūn', 'wa-l-samāʾa rafaʿahā', and conditional clauses; his broader omitted-governor category includes accusatives licensed by an expressed, understood, or estimated verb. [13 notes: SIBAWAYH(المسكين,جرير); RAZI-FULL; BURHAN+]

## 9:6/q27 Does negative in require an illā in its predicate?  [وَإِنْ · grammar]
- 9:6/q27/p1 No; negative instances lacking illā refute that grammatical restriction. — Al-Burhan cites 93:7 and 72:25-26 against grammarians requiring illā or an equivalent; this is collateral evidence about in's Arabic uses. [1 notes: BURHAN(بعضهم)]

## 9:6/q28 What idiom illustrates asking someone to wait?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 9:6/q28/p1 A teacher's reply offering the Tigris and Euphrates to a student asking to swallow saliva exemplifies expansive permission to wait. — Ibn Ashur reports the anecdote through al-Zamakhshari. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري)]

## 9:6/q29 Do commentators use this verse to debate divine speech?  [كَلَٰمَ ٱللَّهِ · theology]
- 9:6/q29/p1 Yes; the hearing clause prompts discussion of the nature of divine speech. — Kuranyolu notes the exegetical debate without adopting a particular view. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
