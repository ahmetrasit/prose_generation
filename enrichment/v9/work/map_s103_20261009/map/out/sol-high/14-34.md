# 14:34: 23 questions

## 14:34/q01 What does God giving from all people asked mean?  [وَءَاتَىٰكُم مِّن كُلِّ مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether asking is verbal or implicit, whether كل is literal or abundant, and whether من limits the gifts.
- 14:34/q01/p1 God grants a portion or some kinds of what people ask, according to their benefit and His wisdom. — Al-Akhfash and al-Tabari supply an omitted 'thing' and cite 27:23; Tabari prefers this common reading. Jishumi and Tabrisi say harmful requests are withheld; al-Alusi says each kind, rather than every individual member, is covered. Bursawi cites 17:18 and 6:44 for abundance. Asad conditions fulfillment on ultimate benefit. [25 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الأخفش); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); JISHUMI(الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الكاملين); ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 14:34/q01/p2 God granted every thing that people literally requested or desired. — Mujahid, Hasan and Ikrima give the broad wording; al-Farra's explanation below extends it to hypothetical requests. Abu al-Hasan and al-Akhfash's extra min permits totality. [12 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الحسن,ابن الأنباري؛ الكسائي); JISHUMI(القاضي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة,مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة)-]
- 14:34/q01/p3 God gives unasked gifts as well as asked gifts, or gifts never asked for. — Muqatil cites mercy; al-Dahhak names sun, moon, night and day, and says some gifts were not imagined. Ibn al-Anbari cites 16:81 for wording that implies an unmentioned counterpart. Qushayri names prior obedience and forgiveness; Sahl and Yahya name Muhammad and unity. Tabataba'i rejects the verbal-asking premise. [40 notes: MUQATIL(أبو صالح); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(الضحاك,ركانة بن هاشم,قتادة); THALABI(الحسن والضحاك وسلام,الضحاك); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); JISHUMI(الضحاك,الضحاك وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض السلف); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); STUDYQURAN(Q); SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ); TABATABAI-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 14:34/q01/p4 The address is collective: humanity receives every useful kind, distributed among individuals. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan deny that any one person possesses all kinds. Al-Akhfash and Jishumi say different people requested and received different things; Tabataba'i calls the species' requests genuine, though individuals may be denied. Islahi sees the fit of human form and provision as evidence of divine design. [11 notes: AKHFASH(some); TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:34/q01/p5 The gifts answer needs expressed by people's condition or preparedness, whether spoken or not. — Al-Kisa'i says asking can denote wishing; al-Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi and Alusi treat need as implicit asking. Qashani cites 55:29; al-Alusi cites 7:172. Tabataba'i distinguishes speech or signs from beings' intrinsic dependence on God and says all genuine requests go to Him. Tabrisi compares 2:29 for God's provision of what people need. [23 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري؛ الكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI]
- 14:34/q01/p6 God gives what people would ask for if they asked. — Al-Farra and Ibn Atiyya express the hypothetical request; Ibn Atiyya says it approximates the collective provision view. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 14:34/q01/p7 God gives from what people ask, sometimes wholly and sometimes partly. — Tabataba'i says min indicates source, not a fixed fraction; wisdom governs the measure, and treating every request as only partly fulfilled would misread the wording. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 14:34/q02 How does من كل ما سألتموه work grammatically?  [مِّن كُلِّ مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether من is partitive or extra, and what object is omitted.
- 14:34/q02/p1 من is partitive, and an object such as 'thing' or 'portion' is understood. — Al-Akhfash and Tabari compare 27:23; Abu Ali compares 2:61 and 27:16. Ibn Atiyya requires a supplied second object. Samin attributes this analysis to Sibawayh; al-Alusi explains that كل still spans the kinds. [13 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الأخفش); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه)+]
- 14:34/q02/p2 من is extra in an affirmative clause; every requested thing is the second object. — Abu al-Hasan analyzes the prepositional phrase as accusative; Samin reports this from al-Akhfash. Qurtubi records the resulting complete-grant sense. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش)]
- 14:34/q02/p3 كل is an expansive expression for many kinds, not exhaustive totality. — Al-Akhfash compares everyday 'knows everything'; Tabari and others cite 6:44, the opening of every door. Bursawi also cites 17:18. [7 notes: AKHFASH(some); TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الكاملين)]

## 14:34/q03 How is من كل read?  [مِّن كُلِّ · readings]
turns on: The standard genitive annexation contrasts with tanwīn; on tanwīn, mā may be relative or negative.
- 14:34/q03/p1 The common reading attaches كل to ما without tanwīn. — Al-Tabari prefers the authoritative reciters' agreement and rejects the alternative; Ibn Jinni says the object is an understood share of each kind. Jishumi and Thalabi identify it as the common reading. [10 notes: MUQATIL(أبو صالح); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس، الحسن، الضحاك، محمد بن علي، جعفر بن محمد، عمرو بن فائد، يعقوب); THALABI; JISHUMI(الحسن والضحاك وسلام وزيد عن يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 14:34/q03/p2 A reported reading puts tanwīn on كل; its sense varies with the parsing of ما. — Abu Salih contrasts it with the reading without min; Ibn Jinni lists Ibn Abbas, Hasan, Dahhak, Muhammad and Jafar ibn Ali, Amr ibn Fa'id and Yaqub. Other lists add Salam, Qatada and Nafi. It can mean requested gifts, need by condition, or unasked gifts. [23 notes: AKHFASH(some); TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس، الحسن، الضحاك، محمد بن علي، جعفر بن محمد، عمرو بن فائد، يعقوب); THALABI(الحسن والضحاك وسلام); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الحسن والضحاك وسلام وزيد عن يعقوب,الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك بن مزاحم والحسن وقتادة وسلام); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك والحسن ومحمد بن علي وجعفر بن محمد وعمرو بن قائد وقتادة وسلام ويعقوب ونافع,الزمخشري وابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR+; TAB-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 14:34/q04 What can ما and the pronoun in سألتموه denote?  [مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · grammar]
turns on: Relative, descriptive, infinitival, and negative readings assign different functions and pronoun references.
- 14:34/q04/p1 ما is relative or descriptive, referring to what was requested or needed. — Ibn Atiyya, Samin and al-Alusi allow a relative; Samin prefers it on the tanwīn reading as second object. Abu Hayyan objects to some proposed restored resumptive pronouns, while al-Alusi reports a defense. [14 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); BAYDAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 14:34/q04/p2 ما is infinitival, with the suffix pronoun referring to God. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Samin say the verbal noun stands for the request or requested thing; Samin distinguishes the pronoun's referent from the relative or descriptive analysis. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 14:34/q04/p3 On the tanwīn reading, ما is negative and the phrase is circumstantial: gifts came without asking. — Al-Dahhak's account is widely transmitted; al-Zamakhshari gives it, while Abu Hayyan says it clashes with the common annexed reading and Samin prefers relative mā for harmony. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); THALABI(الحسن والضحاك وسلام); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الحسن والضحاك وسلام وزيد عن يعقوب,الضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري وابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 14:34/q05 Why can a person's request go unanswered?  [سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether the limitation lies in benefit, the petitioner's knowledge, or the distinction between species and individual requests.
- 14:34/q05/p1 God grants only what is beneficial, at its right time, and may avert harmful requests. — Jishumi invokes partitive من, distinguishes the informed petitioner, and Tabrisi names harm to religion or another person. Asad appeals to ultimate divine wisdom; the Study Quran reports a Prophetic account of grant now, stored reward, or equivalent evil averted, excluding sin and severed kinship. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 14:34/q05/p2 The species' genuine intrinsic requests are met, while individual verbal prayers may be insincere or misdirected. — Tabataba'i cites 2:186 and distinguishes all beings' dependence from explicit verbal requests. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 14:34/q06 What is the reading of وآتاكم?  [وَءَاتَىٰكُم · readings]
- 14:34/q06/p1 A reading has أتاكم in place of آتاكم. — Ibn Kathir records the variant without further argument. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 14:34/q07 What does نعمة الله mean, and why is it singular?  [نِعْمَتَ ٱللَّهِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the singular is a verbal noun substitute or a collective noun for bestowed things.
- 14:34/q07/p1 It is a noun standing for the verbal noun إنعام, God's act of bestowal. — Al-Wahidi says a verbal noun need not be pluralized; Razi, Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit this analysis. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الواحدي); ALUSI-FULL(الواحدي)]
- 14:34/q07/p2 It means the bestowed blessings collectively; a singular construct or generic noun covers them all. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi prefer this over the verbal-noun view. Baydawi and Bursawi point to construct definiteness; Tabataba'i calls it the whole genus; Samin says the bestowed thing. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:34/q07/p3 Blessing means anything agreeable, including conditions for benefit and harms warded off. — Alusi says this lexical sense is wider than the religious technical sense of a result praised in the end; it admits continuing existence and absent impediments. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:34/q08 Why can God's blessings not be enumerated?  [وَإِن تَعُدُّوا۟ نِعْمَتَ ٱللَّهِ لَا تُحْصُوهَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the point is sheer number, hidden detail within each gift, inability to preserve them, or inability to repay them.
- 14:34/q08/p1 Their number and the detail within even one favor exceed human counting and knowledge. — Tabrisi, Qurtubi and Ibn Atiyya name senses, bodily form, health, provision, creation and guidance; Qushayri adds protection from trials. Razi traces bread through seasons, elements, weather, iron, tools, anatomy and medicine, and nerves through every branch and organ; Alusi repeats these examples and argues that conditions, continuing existence and averted afflictions are unbounded. Baydawi distinguishes kinds from instances. Ibn Ashur notes overlooked breath, digestion and circulation. Tustari and Sulami name Muhammad as a surpassing blessing. Abu al-Darda warns against reducing favors to food and drink. [59 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,سهل,قيل); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الدرداء,طلق بن حبيب); TABRISI(طليق بن حبيب); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الدرداء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء,أبو السعود,أبو علي الشبلي,ابن مسعود,الإمام,سفيان بن عيينة,طلق بن حبيب); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 14:34/q08/p2 People cannot fulfill gratitude for favors, since thanking is itself a further favor. — Talq calls for repentance morning and evening; Ibn Kathir transmits David's admission, Shafi'i's recursive gratitude, and a poem that every limb's praise falls short. Qushayri and Sulami stress unending obligation. Al-Sajjad calls acknowledging inability itself gratitude; Sulayman al-Taymi says God asks thanks according to capacity. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(طلق بن حبيب); SULAMI(ابن عطاء,قيل); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلق بن حبيب); TABRISI(طليق بن حبيب); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(قوم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الشافعي,القائل,داود); DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(سليمان التيمي,طلق بن حبيب); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين); STUDYQURAN(Bg)]
- 14:34/q08/p3 They cannot all be preserved or remembered. — Al-Kalbi gives the memory sense; Ibn al-Jawzi cites 18:49 and 58:6 for exhaustive recording. Ibn Ashur observes that even known blessings are forgotten. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:34/q08/p4 They cannot be endured, performed, or managed in full. — Al-Farra compares 73:20 on keeping night-vigil times; others construe that verse as inability to bear it. Ibn al-Jawzi reports Muqatil's night-prayer capacity sense. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية؛ الفراء؛ غيره); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(قوم,مقاتل)]

## 14:34/q09 What does إحصاء mean here and what is its origin?  [تُحْصُوهَآ · meaning]
turns on: Counting, encompassing, recording, and capacity are reported senses.
- 14:34/q09/p1 It means counting accurately, originally with pebbles, and by extension encompassing a total. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives counting as fundamental, cites 72:28 for knowledge, and folds it into counting. Alusi cites al-A'sha's pebbles line and reports a rival account of pebbles marking bundles; al-Raghib favors the older pebble-counting account. Ibn Ashur says pebbles once fixed large totals without error. [11 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,قوم); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأعشى); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)+]
- 14:34/q09/p2 In other Qur'anic uses it can mean exhaustive preservation, recording, or capacity. — Ibn al-Jawzi cites 18:49 and 58:6 for preserving, 36:12 and 78:29 for written record, and 73:20 for capacity; al-Wahidi also compares 73:20. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العالية؛ الفراء؛ غيره,الكلبي)]

## 14:34/q10 Why use a conditional when failure to count is certain?  [وَإِن تَعُدُّوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 14:34/q10/p1 The conditional addresses the human impression that counting might be possible. — Alusi says the details of even one blessing defeat the attempt. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:34/q11 Who is الإنسان?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite generic names all humanity, all unbelievers, idolaters specifically, or Abu Jahl.
- 14:34/q11/p1 It denotes humanity as a species, with wronging and ingratitude appearing in different people or circumstances. — Ibn Atiyya allows a denier and sinful believer to exemplify different forms; Nasafi applies it wherever traits occur. Razi and Kashshaf treat complaint and hoarding as species behavior. Umar applies the verse to himself. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:34/q11/p2 It is grammatically generic but intends disbelievers or idolaters. — Zajjaj compares the exception for believers at 103:2–3; Tabrisi similarly cites al-Asr. Ibn Ashur compares 19:66 and says idolaters worship powerless things. Islahi says the general wording avoids direct address to the unworthy idolaters. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:34/q11/p3 It refers to Abu Jahl. — Ibn Abbas is cited by Ibn al-Jawzi and Qurtubi. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس)]

## 14:34/q12 What do ظلوم and كفار say about a person?  [لَظَلُومٌۭ كَفَّارٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wrong is directed at self, God, blessings, or gratitude's rightful recipient, and whether hardship and ease distinguish the traits.
- 14:34/q12/p1 A person wrongs himself by disobeying or withholding thanks, and denies or misuses God's favors. — Muqatil links sins and failure to worship; Tabari adds redirecting worship. Tabrisi says both adjectives are intensive; Ibn Ashur connects their intensity to abundant favors. Islahi calls polytheism a violation of God's and the soul's rights. Alusi describes self-imposed deprivation. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 14:34/q12/p2 The injustice is complaint or despair in hardship; ingratitude is hoarding and withholding in prosperity. — Thalabi, Jishumi, Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit the contrast. [10 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 14:34/q12/p3 Injustice misdirects gratitude to someone other than the benefactor; ingratitude denies the benefactor. — Thalabi and Baghawi distinguish misplaced thanks from denial; Jishumi names attribution to another originator; Islahi glosses kaffar as thanking someone else. [6 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 14:34/q12/p4 Inwardly, a person ruins innate preparedness by turning to perishing nature and is veiled from grace. — Qashani and Bursawi describe the loss of potential; Sulami and Alusi add the error of imagining human gratitude can repay God. [5 notes: SULAMI(قيل); QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:34/q13 Why does the verse close with human injustice and ingratitude?  [إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَظَلُومٌۭ كَفَّارٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clause explains failed gratitude, the preceding rebuke, or the infinity of favors.
- 14:34/q13/p1 It explains why humans fail to honor favors and reinforces the earlier rebuke of exchanging blessings for disbelief. — Abu Hayyan connects 14:28–30 and setting up rivals; Samin concurs. Alusi prefers it as a fresh explanatory statement and rejects taking it as the reason favors are infinite. Ibn Ashur calls the close a rebuke of idolaters. [11 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 14:34/q13/p2 It underscores the abundance of blessings and the human inability to thank or notice them. — Razi says people tire, forget and overlook other favors while reflecting on some; Tabataba'i links the explanation to heedlessness. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 14:34/q14 How does this ending relate to the ending of 16:18?  [إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَظَلُومٌۭ كَفَّارٌۭ · links]
turns on: Whether the two endings describe recipient and giver in their contexts, or one replaces the other.
- 14:34/q14/p1 Here the recipient's injustice is foregrounded, while 16:18 names God's forgiveness and mercy as giver. — Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn al-Munir, Samin, Biqai and Itqan contrast human failure with continuing divine generosity; Ibrahim follows disbelief and rivals, while al-Nahl details favors, divine attributes and the postponement of punishment. Alusi also reports that expanded favors make mercy apt and invite repentance. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(ناصر الدين ابن المنير); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن المنير,غيره); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 14:34/q14/p2 The Ibrahim ending was abrogated by the al-Nahl ending. — Ibn Zayd's claim is transmitted by al-Sakhawi; Abu Hayyan dismisses it, and Alusi says it deserves no attention. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)-; ALUSI-FULL(عبدالرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)-]

## 14:34/q15 What place does this verse have in its surrounding passage and sūra?  [* · links]
- 14:34/q15/p1 It completes the proofs of God's sole benefaction and calls people away from rivals to worship, thanks, and obedience. — Razi counts ten signs in creation, rain, provisions, ships, rivers, sun, moon and answered requests after the saved and damned. Tabrisi says the Creator alone deserves worship; Biqai links 14:2 and Judgment Day; Islahi sees 14:28–34 warning Quraysh leaders and urging Muslims to prayer and charity. Study Quran names gratitude versus ingratitude as a sūra theme. [14 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 14:34/q16 What practice follows from recognizing these gifts?  [* · law]
- 14:34/q16/p1 People should worship and obey God, repent, pray, give alms, and thank the true benefactor. — Jishumi explicitly draws prayer and alms; Tabari, Qurtubi and Tabrisi link gifts to exclusive worship; Talq urges repentance morning and evening. Bursawi quotes a Persian couplet calling gratitude the key to increased blessing. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(طلق بن حبيب); JISHUMI; TABRISI(طليق بن حبيب); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(طلق بن حبيب)]

## 14:34/q17 What inward meaning is drawn from divine gifts and human asking?  [* · inward]
- 14:34/q17/p1 God's prior gifts and a person's readiness lead toward knowledge, obedience and closeness; turning away corrupts that readiness. — Yahya ibn Mu'adh prizes unasked unity and says absorption in God opens the gnostic station. Qushayri names forgiveness before asking and gnosis before existence; Qashani, Bursawi and Alusi explain requests through preparedness. Bursawi reads heavens as hearts, earth as souls, rain as wisdom and fruits as obedient acts. [17 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,يحيى بن معاذ); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 14:34/q18 What reports illustrate gratitude or the scale of blessings?  [* · reports]
- 14:34/q18/p1 Reports and sayings portray gratitude as unable to repay even one favor, while recognition of dependence itself counts as thanks. — Ibn Kathir reports a Bukhari praise from Abu Hurayra and a rare weak-chain three-ledger report from Anas. David is told breath is the least gift; Wahb's ascetic's fifty years equal one night's relief. Shafi'i says thanks needs new thanks; al-Sajjad recognizes inability as thanks; Umar applies the verse to himself. The Prophet's reported sermon urges God-consciousness, worship, remembrance and fidelity. [12 notes: IBNHISHAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,أنس,الشافعي,القائل,داود); DURR-FULL(داود,عمر بن الخطاب,وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(داود); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين)]
- 14:34/q18/p2 A person who counts only food and drink as God's favors misunderstands their scope. — Abu al-Darda is transmitted by Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi; he warns of scant knowledge and punishment. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الدرداء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الدرداء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]

## 14:34/q19 What textual and performance details are attached to this verse?  [* · readings]
- 14:34/q19/p1 Niʿma is usually written with closed tāʾ; its spelling in Ibrahim is among eleven extended forms. — Burhan ties the extended form here to an actual worldly favor and the human-centered ending. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 14:34/q19/p2 In idghām, plain assimilation is the received basis; rawm and ishmām are instructional variants with articulation limits. — Al-Nashr says rawm voices part of a vowel and so differs from full idghām; al-Dani and most authorities mention both signals. Lip closure restricts mīm cases, and authorities differ on fāʾ before fāʾ. [7 notes: NASHR(أهل الأداء)]

## 14:34/q20 What other linguistic examples accompany the discussion?  [* · grammar]
- 14:34/q20/p1 Sibawayh describes the shifted feminine فعال pattern and its names, commands, nouns, and dialectal declension. — He says commands keep one form across persons and numbers; poetry supplies مناع, تراك, حذار, نظار, نزال, نعاء. Hyena names and death's حلاق illustrate lexical shifts. He contrasts productive triliteral vocative and command forms with attested quadriliterals; Tamim declines feminine proper names differently from Hijaz, and al-Khalil comments on imāla. [24 notes: SIBAWAYH(الجعدي,رؤبة); SIBAWAYH(بنو تميم)+]
- 14:34/q20/p2 A poetic saying values meeting a need before it is explicitly asked. — Al-Asma'iyyat cites Suhayr ibn Umayr's line; it echoes the verse's request wording. [1 notes: ASMAIYYAT(صحير بن عمير)]
- 14:34/q20/p3 The form فعول can be emphatic, as in ظلوم, شكور and ودود. — Burhan says شكور is more emphatic than شاكر. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 14:34/q20/p4 The preceding creation and provision clause admits several grammatical parses. — Kashshaf takes God as subject and 'who created' as predicate; min al-thamarat clarifies provision or is the object of 'brought forth,' with rizqan circumstantial or a verbal noun. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 14:34/q21 What principles of expression and interpretation are discussed in material attached to the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 14:34/q21/p1 Subtle language requires thought beyond general vocabulary knowledge or memorization. — Jurjani analyzes a couplet's ordered comparison of distant high moon and emphatic nearness, the words أفرط, شاسع and جد قريب, and quotes the camel saying and Ibn al-Rumi's satire of an unappreciated poet. [7 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(ابن الرومي)+]
- 14:34/q21/p2 An exegete begins with clear surface meaning, then may draw context-supported implications and relevant elaborations. — Ibn Ashur permits legal and ethical deductions, and astronomy or sound science when they clarify Qur'anic aims; he cites Moses and al-Khidr, creation, economic distribution, mutual interference against multiple gods, moving mountains and a darkened sun. [10 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 14:34/q21/p3 A Quranic encounter with divine generosity may produce love, remembrance and desire for nearness. — Itqan says perceiving a majestic and merciful Lord makes hearts prefer His approval. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 14:34/q22 What unrelated report is present in the source material?  [* · reports]
- 14:34/q22/p1 Umm Salama reports rules for a pilgrim leaving ihram after stoning and before tawaf. — The Abu Dawud excerpt gives no grading or link to this verse. [1 notes: ABUDAWUD(أم سلمة)]

## 14:34/q23 Why are gratitude and disbelief expressed in different tenses?  [* · rhetoric]
- 14:34/q23/p1 Recurring gratitude is expressed in the present; disbelief in the past signals it should not recur. — Samarrai states this contrast. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
