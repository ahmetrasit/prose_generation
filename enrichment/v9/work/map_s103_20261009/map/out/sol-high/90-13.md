# 90:13: 16 questions

## 90:13/q01 What does فك mean?  [فَكُّ · meaning]
- 90:13/q01/p1 It is releasing something from a binding restraint or another person's hold. — Wahidi's authorities explain separation by breaking a chain, untying a knot, releasing a pledge, or opening a seal. Razi cites al-Akhtal's line about fetters; Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite poetry about a captive. Ibn Ashur stresses removal from a possessor and the effort of pulling something from a resistant holder. Bursevi compares separating one thing from another. [21 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; RAZI(الأخطل); RAZI-FULL(الأخطل); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:13/q01/p2 The verbal noun is fakk or fakāk, with fatḥa rather than kasra in fakāk. — Al-Farra gives the vocalization; Wahidi, Razi, and Alusi transmit it. Alusi also explicitly names fakk as a verbal noun. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); ALUSI(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء)]

## 90:13/q02 Why is the person called a رقبة?  [رَقَبَةٍ · rhetoric]
- 90:13/q02/p1 The neck stands for the whole person, especially a slave or captive whose neck is bound. — Mawardi likens enslavement to a captive's neck restraint and cites Hassan ibn Thabit. Qurtubi says bondage binds the neck; Ibn Ashur calls it part for whole and says it is the first shackled part noticed. Elmalili likewise says part for whole; Bursevi compares 'head' or 'back' for a mount. Samarrai says it evokes distress. [13 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL(حسان بن ثابت); QURTUBI(حسان); QURTUBI-FULL(حسان); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير)]

## 90:13/q03 What does فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ ask a person to do?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'neck' denotes an enslaved or captive person, a broader external restraint, or the person's own moral and inward bondage.
- 90:13/q03/p1 Emancipate an enslaved person. — Ibn Qutayba, Tabari, Baghawi and others give the ordinary sense. Tabarisi says removal of the restraining status establishes liberty. Qutb says direct release and aid yield the same freedom. Ibn Ashur calls this a basic legal preference for freedom. Elmalili treats a single slave as the simplest case. [38 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB; TAB-FULL(الحسن); NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 90:13/q03/p2 Rescue a captive from captivity. — Mawardi distinguishes captivity from manumission, citing Hassan's verse for releasing captives. Ibn Atiyya says ransom is one person's undertaking a captive's release. Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan include captive release. [12 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL(حسان بن ثابت); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 90:13/q03/p3 Help pay the price of another person's freedom, including a contracted slave. — Wahidi states contribution to the price. Razi and Nasafi include a contracted slave's payment; al-Bara's report distinguishes outright manumission from helping. Biqai says 'release' covers even a small contribution. Alusi reasons that if full manumission is included, helping is included all the more. [16 notes: WAHIDI-QT; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI(البراء); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(القاضي أبو محمد); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(البراء); ALUSI; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI(Berâ’); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 90:13/q03/p4 Release a person from other restraints, such as debt, retaliation, oppression, illness or social subjugation. — Wahidi compares a pledge and seal; Kashshaf includes another obligation, and the reported saying specifies retaliation or debt. Biqai lists captivity, injustice, debt and illness. Asad adds economic, social and political exploitation; Samarrai contrasts broad fakk with the narrower taḥrīr of expiation and applies it to oppressed peoples. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF(النبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:13/q03/p5 Free one's own soul from sins through repentance and obedience. — Ikrima gives repentance. Mawardi and Qurtubi prefer avoiding sins and doing good deeds, saying the emancipation hadith does not exclude it. Al-Jubba'i speaks of accepting the burden of obedience. Elmalili connects each person's captivity to deeds and cites 52:21. [11 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI(الجبائي,عكرمة); QURTUBI(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Ikrimah and Razi); STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 90:13/q03/p6 Free oneself through worship that leads to Paradise and release from the Fire. — Razi calls this the greater freedom. Alusi reports it but finds the reading unlikely because later deeds would become particulars following a general statement. Study Quran reports it separately from repentance. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الإمام); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 90:13/q03/p7 Free the inward self from ego, greed, desire, and self-regard. — Al-Wasiti names the self, one's deeds, seeing one's own merit and seeking closeness; al-Qasim names greed's humiliation. Qushayri describes release from desire and people through a guide, and surrender of personal planning. Qashani speaks of detaching the heart from natural inclinations, or cultivating virtues when detachment fails. [5 notes: SULAMI(القاسم,الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]

## 90:13/q04 How is فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ read?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vocalization and whether fakk and the feeding word are nouns or finite verbs, with further case variants in the following phrase.
- 90:13/q04/p1 As nominal fakku raqabatin with nominal iṭʿām: 'freeing a neck or feeding'. — Reported for most Medinan, Kufan and Syrian readers, including Nafi, Asim, Ibn Amir and Hamza; Ibn Ashur also names Abu Jafar, Yaqub and Khalaf. Some notes give nominative fakk and iṭʿām with genitive raqaba. Al-Farra reports Hasan and Ali with the nouns. Abu Ubayd and Akhfash prefer it because it directly defines the obstacle; Akhfash, Abu Hatim and Abu Ubayd also favor it. [32 notes: FARRA(الحسن البصري وعلي بن أبي طالب,العوام); TAB(عامة قرّاء المدينة والكوفة والشأم); TAB-FULL(عامة قراء المدينة والكوفة والشام); NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم وابن عامر ونافع وحمزة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(الآخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(نافع وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو إلا عبد الوارث والكسائي والداجوني عن ابن ذكوان); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI(غيرهم); ABUHAYYAN(باقي السبعة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة وأبو جعفر ويعقوب وخلف); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة وأبو جعفر ويعقوب وخلف); QIRAAT-ER; NASHR; AKHFASH+; FARRA+; NAHHAS(أبو عبيد,الأخفش)+]
- 90:13/q04/p2 As past verbs fakka raqabatan aw aṭʿama: 'he freed a neck or fed'. — Reported for Ibn Kathir, Abu Amr and al-Kisai, and in some accounts Hasan, Abu Raja and Ali. Abu Amr and Tabari argue it aligns verbs with the subsequent thumma kāna; al-Farra also prefers this coordination in some reports. Tabari accepts both sound readings. The verb has an implicit subject from the earlier human being and raqaba is its accusative object. Abu Amr's transmissions show small attachment differences. - 90:13/q04/p3 As mixed noun and verb forms, with reported differences over the case of raqaba and coordination of feeding. — Ibn Atiyya reports some Successors' fakki raqabatin and an accusative form followed by nominal iṭʿām. Samīn explains the omitted feeding agent by a Basran grammatical rule. ## 90:13/q05 How does the phrase explain the steep obstacle grammatically?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the obstacle is directly defined by the deed, requires an implied 'crossing', or is explained by finite verbs.
- 90:13/q05/p1 The nominal phrase directly defines the obstacle by figuratively identifying a hard deed with an ascent. — Tabari likens this to the Qur'anic explanation of Hāwiya as fire at 101:10–11; Farisi notes explanatory syntax. Alusi and Ibn Ashur say metaphor removes the need for an inserted noun. Tabatabai rejects supplying 'crossing'. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan supply an implicit explanatory question; Ibn Ashur supplies 'it is'. [17 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA; ABUHAYYAN; KASHSHAF; NASAFI(غيرهم); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة وأبو جعفر ويعقوب وخلف); AKHFASH(بعضهم); ALUSI+; IBNASHUR+; TABATABAI+]
- 90:13/q05/p2 The nominal construction needs an implied 'crossing' so that an action explains an action. — Farisi says without implied iqtihām the pass would be equated literally with emancipation, citing explanatory constructions at 104:5–6 and 101:10–11. Samīn similarly supplies iqtihām. Alusi allows this if the reports describing a real ascent are sound. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو رجاء); IBNASHUR-; TABATABAI-]
- 90:13/q05/p3 The finite verbs replace or explain 'he crossed the obstacle', with the intervening question parenthetical. — Ibn Atiyya says the verbal reading needs no implied question. Farisi argues an explanatory clause need not copy a nominal predicate and cites 69:4 and 3:59. Nasafi and Ibn Ashur treat the verbs as apposition or explanatory replacement; Ibn Ashur also allows repeated negative force. Tabari connects the verbs to thumma kāna. [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو والكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(مكي وأبو عمرو وعلي); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو، ابن كثير، الكسائي); ALUSI(ابن كثير والنحويان); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو والكسائي)]
- 90:13/q05/p4 The noun reading can be related to later verbs by an elided an. — Farra and Tabari cite a poetic line of Tarafa where overt an in a second clause permits it to be understood earlier. Nahhas objects that this would demand the accusative and an cannot vanish without a substitute. [5 notes: FARRA(شاعر); TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء)]

## 90:13/q06 How does the negation before this verse apply to freeing and feeding?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding clause is straightforward negation or a rebuking omitted question.
- 90:13/q06/p1 One lā covers the coordinated deeds, although Arabic often repeats it. — Farra compares 75:31. Ibn Ashur says 'crossed' entails freeing and feeding, or the later faith clause supplies the second negation; any context preventing ambiguity suffices, as al-Hariri illustrates. Alusi says the negation repeats in sense through the definition. Baydawi says lā suits the multiple intended repeated deeds better than lam. Burhan attributes to al-Zamakhshari the explanation that the single lā recurs in meaning: the man neither freed a slave nor fed a needy person. [5 notes: FARRA; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI; BAYDAWI; BURHAN(الزمخشري)]
- 90:13/q06/p2 The expression can be a rebuking question with an omitted interrogative particle. — Ibn Ashur prefers asking why the boaster spent on gambling and wine but did not spend on liberation and famine relief. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 90:13/q06/p3 The expression is an imprecation urging the person to do good. — Burhan reports this as another view of the clause. [1 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري)]

## 90:13/q07 What is the steep obstacle that this deed crosses?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the ascent is the morally difficult deed itself or a reported place beyond this life.
- 90:13/q07/p1 It is the difficult moral ascent of freeing, feeding, faith and other righteous deeds. — Majaz and Qatada identify its practical acts. Baydawi calls the mountain pass a metaphor requiring self-discipline. Alusi and Ibn Ashur identify the hard deed with ascent. Bint al-Shati rejects allegory because the Qur'an itself answers with these deeds; Wahidi objects to the obviousness of a merely physical Hell obstacle. Samarrai identifies release and feeding as the answer. [16 notes: MAJAZ; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); IBNKATHIR(قتادة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; BINTSHATI+]
- 90:13/q07/p2 It is a steep, slippery mountain in Hell. — Ibn Umar and Hasan are reported with this location. Abu Raja reports 7,000 years up and 7,000 down; Alusi requires sound Prophetic evidence before treating this as exegesis. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو رجاء,ابن عمر); ALUSI-FULL-; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)-]
- 90:13/q07/p3 It is Hell itself. — Ibn Abbas is reported with this identification; Hasan also identifies the obstacle as Hell in Durr. Alusi withholds acceptance without a sound Prophetic report. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL-; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)-]
- 90:13/q07/p4 It is a barrier between Paradise and Hell. — One Ibn Abbas report gives this location; Alusi says a sound Prophetic report is needed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 90:13/q07/p5 It is the bridge over Hell. — Mujahid, Dahhak and Kalbi give this; Alusi notes it may be the same as the barrier report and requires sound Prophetic authority. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك والكلبي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 90:13/q07/p6 It is gratitude for God's favors, conceived as a difficult ascent. — Alusi reports the suggestion and finds it unconvincing. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 90:13/q08 What rewards and guidance do reports connect to freeing a neck?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · reports]

## 90:13/q09 Which is more virtuous, emancipation or charity?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · law]
turns on: Whether precedence in the verse establishes comparative merit, against the companions' contrary ranking.
- 90:13/q09/p1 Emancipation is more virtuous. — Abu Hanifa and Sha'bi hold this; Sha'bi specifically prefers it to giving surplus to kin and cites the limb-for-limb promise. Kashshaf, Razi and Qurtubi favor it from the verse's placing emancipation before feeding; Alusi and Study Quran report this argument. [16 notes: KASHSHAF(أبو حنيفة,الشعبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو حنيفة,الشعبي); QURTUBI(الشعبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الشعبي); ALUSI(أبو حنيفة,الشعبي); STUDYQURAN(some); KASHSHAF+; QURTUBI+; RAZI-FULL(أبي حنيفة)+; RAZI+]
- 90:13/q09/p2 Charity is more virtuous. — Abu Hanifa's two companions prefer it; Kashshaf, Razi and Qurtubi transmit their position, without a separate argument in these notes. [5 notes: KASHSHAF(صاحبا أبي حنيفة); RAZI-FULL(صاحبيه); QURTUBI(صاحبيه); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI(أبو حنيفة)]

## 90:13/q10 Which slave is best to free when price and faith differ?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · law]
turns on: Whether the 'most costly' report includes an unbelieving slave or presumes a Muslim slave.
- 90:13/q10/p1 An expensive unbelieving slave is better to free than a cheap believing slave. — Asbagh invokes the Prophetic answer that the best slave to free is the most expensive. [2 notes: QURTUBI(أصبغ); QURTUBI-FULL(أصبغ)]
- 90:13/q10/p2 A believing slave freed for worship and pure monotheism takes priority over mere price. — Ibn al-Arabi calls Asbagh mistaken: he takes the expensive-slave report to concern Muslims, citing reports about freeing a Muslim man and a believing slave. Qurtubi relays his preference. [3 notes: QURTUBI(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); QURTUBI(ابن العربي)+; QURTUBI(أصبغ)-; QURTUBI-FULL(أصبغ)-]

## 90:13/q11 Why use 'release' here, and why place it first?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording stresses breadth of aid, the rank and difficulty of release, or the alternative ways of serving people.
- 90:13/q11/p1 The broad word 'release' includes even a small contribution toward another's freedom. — Biqai contrasts complete emancipation with helping pay; Samarrai contrasts fakk with the narrower taḥrīr used for expiation and includes debt and coercion. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(البراء); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:13/q11/p2 Its first place marks emancipation as an especially important, difficult act of human service. — Samarrai says it emphasizes Islam's concern for liberation. Bint al-Shati calls it the first repair of human dignity, before wider social reform; Islahi says the good is universally recognized and early Qur'anic preaching later fostered gradual elimination of slavery. Bursevi says people spend on desires and buildings but begrudge a gift to the despised needy. Biqai connects the first pass to discipline of appetite, chastity and generosity. [6 notes: BIQAI; BURSEVI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 90:13/q11/p3 The conjunction aw offers alternative acts of mercy rather than requiring them all together. — Samarrai says one may feed either needy class separately or both, and release or feeding gives a variety of possible routes. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 90:13/q12 What does this deed teach about faith, wealth and social duty in the sūra?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How emancipation and feeding relate to faith and mercy, and what their order implies.
- 90:13/q12/p1 Wealth should be spent on freeing and feeding the vulnerable as gratitude to God, with faith and mercy. — Kashshaf and Nasafi contrast this with boasting and waste; Baydawi calls the effort self-discipline. Corpus Coranicum links God's care and Meccan social and ritual life, and Samarrai says the boastful spender neglected the enslaved and hungry. Kur'an Yolu treats the list as examples of social ethics, not an exhaustive catalogue. Biqai calls for grateful compassion without display or reward-seeking. Elmalili extends liberation from one person to whole peoples. [22 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 90:13/q12/p2 The passage establishes human freedom as a foundational aim of Islamic law. — Ibn Ashur says the Lawgiver strongly seeks freedom; Bint al-Shati says the first move toward dignity is ending slavery, and Islahi connects the early call to later gradual elimination. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 90:13/q12/p3 Faith is a higher rank and a necessary ground for the deeds' acceptance. — Burhan takes thumma as rank, not time; Study Quran cites 9:54 for belief as a condition of acceptance. Ibn Khalawayh says the noun reading's actions are conditioned on faith. [3 notes: BURHAN; STUDYQURAN; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 90:13/q12/p4 Social justice precedes and grounds genuine faith in the sequence. — Bint al-Shati reads thumma temporally: emancipation and feeding first restore dignity and justice, enabling faith, patience and mercy. She rejects separating faith from resistance to tyranny, slavery and starvation. [3 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]
- 90:13/q12/p5 The list echoes Isaiah and recasts heroic poetry through a call to protect the vulnerable. — Corpus Coranicum identifies Isaiah 58:5–7 and 58:6–7: both lists begin with slave release and move to the poor and kin, though the framing differs. It says the Qur'an grounds duty in God's grace rather than merely criticizing ritual, Arabizes the list, recalls 107:2–3, and challenges the poetic values mentioned at verse 6. [6 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 90:13/q13 What historical setting or examples illustrate freeing slaves here?  [* · reports]
- 90:13/q13/p1 The Meccan setting made slave release an urgent response to abused converts; Abu Bakr freed Bilal and six others. — Qutb says Muslims lacked a state and slavery was widespread. Ibn Ishaq says Umayya tortured Bilal; Abu Bakr bought and freed him, then six more before the migration. Abu Qahafa objected that weak freed people offered no protection; Abu Bakr said he acted for God. [4 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(ابن إسحاق,ابن إسحاق عن بعض أهله)]
- 90:13/q13/p2 The sūra addresses Meccan injustice, wealth boasting and vulnerable people around the sanctuary. — Bint al-Shati says powerful owners enslaved people and neglected hungry kin and the poor in the Prophet's sanctuary. Corpus Coranicum connects worship with ordinary social support. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 90:13/q13/p3 The preceding address may be read as the Prophet's direct address to the people. — Ibn Khalawayh records the preceding explanation and Kufan justification of an added alif by the closeness of simple and intensive verb forms. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 90:13/q14 May one rank the two canonical readings?  [فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ · readings]
turns on: Whether grammatical and explanatory arguments justify a preference among transmitted communal readings.
- 90:13/q14/p1 Both established readings are sound; preferring one so strongly as to disparage the other is impermissible. — Tabari accepts both interpretations. Nahhas rejects attacks on communal readings and counters the past-verb coordination argument; Zarkashi says both are mutawatir and transmits Tha'lab's distinction between recitation and ordinary speech. Al-Kawashi allows explaining evidential bases or supporting an interpretation, while Nahhas says calling an authenticated communal reading better is sinful and Companion leaders objected. [10 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; BURHAN(أبو جعفر النحاس,الكواشي,ثعلب); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 90:13/q14/p2 The verbal reading is grammatically smoother because it aligns with the later verb kāna. — Tabari and Farra give verb-to-verb coordination; Abu Amr argues the person's prior faith is implied by thumma kāna. Nahhas objects to making this a reason to downgrade the nominal reading. [7 notes: TAB(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); FARRA+; NAHHAS(الفراء)+; RAZI-FULL(الفراء)+; RAZI(الفراء)+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+; NAHHAS-]
- 90:13/q14/p3 The nominal reading is preferable because nouns coordinate and directly answer what the obstacle is. — Farra's own preference for noun-with-noun coordination differs from later reports attributing a verbal preference to him. Abu Ubayd says it directly explains the obstacle; Akhfash, Abu Hatim and Abu Ubayd favor it. Nahhas denies ranking either communal reading. [4 notes: AKHFASH+; FARRA+; NAHHAS(أبو عبيد,الأخفش)+; NAHHAS-]

## 90:13/q15 How does the teaching question about the obstacle work rhetorically?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form itself guarantees an explanation, and how the question prepares the stated answer.
- 90:13/q15/p1 The question creates curiosity and heightened tension before the answer in deeds. — Biqai says it stirs curiosity. Corpus Coranicum compares other teaching questions at 97:2–4, 101:1–11 and 104:5–9 and the oath sequence of Q 100. [2 notes: BIQAI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 90:13/q15/p2 Claims that every 'what will make you know' question has an answer, unlike 'what makes you know,' do not hold. — Sufyan ibn Uyayna states the rule; Nahhas rejects it because the Qur'an explains both the Calamity and the Reality after asking what they are, citing 101:3 and 69:3. [1 notes: NAHHAS(سفيان بن عيينة); NAHHAS-]

## 90:13/q16 What adjacent reading and vocabulary details do the commentaries record?  [* · links]
- 90:13/q16/p1 Masghaba, maqraba and matraba evoke hunger, kinship and dust-poverty. — Samīn reports al-Raghib's 'hunger with fatigue,' sometimes thirst with fatigue, and al-Zamakhshari's matching derivations from hunger, closeness and poverty. Samīn adds that tariba is poverty with skin touching dust, whereas atraba means becoming rich. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الراغب,الزمخشري)]
- 90:13/q16/p2 Abu Jaʿfar reads the earlier labadan with gemination while others read it lightly. — Tabarisi records this neighboring reading without tying it to a different interpretation of freeing. [1 notes: TABRISI]
