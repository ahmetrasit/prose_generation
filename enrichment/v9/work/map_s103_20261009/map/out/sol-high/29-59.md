# 29:59: 9 questions

## 29:59/q01 How is الذين صبروا connected syntactically?  [ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The case and syntactic attachment of الذين, and whether an omitted subject or possessive construction is supplied.
- 29:59/q01/p1 It is genitive, modifying the preceding 'workers' (العاملين). — Al-Tabrisi gives it as a genitive adjective for al-ʿāmilīn and mentions an omitted praised subject. Al-Nasafī explicitly prefers this connection to beginning a new clause. Al-Samīn allows the genitive case; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī also give this analysis. [5 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 29:59/q01/p2 It is nominative as the predicate of an omitted subject. — Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī report the omitted-subject analysis; al-Samīn allows nominative among the possible cases. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:59/q01/p3 It is accusative as an expression of praise. — Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī report the accusative of praise; al-Samīn allows accusative among the possible cases. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:59/q01/p4 An implied possessive construction makes the patient ones' reward the omitted subject. — Al-Tabrisi reports this alternative implied iḍāfa construction. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 29:59/q02 Who are those described as patient and reliant?  [ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description is stated broadly of believing workers or applied particularly to persecuted migrants.
- 29:59/q02/p1 They are the believing workers promised reward, especially believers enduring persecution and migration. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Thaʿlabī identify the praised people as patient believers who rely on God. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God first promises the faithful who do good a reward, calls them workers, then defines these workers by patience and reliance as marks of true belief. Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī describe their religious endurance; the latter sources include migration among its trials. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 29:59/q02/p2 In the migration setting, they are believers leaving their homes and property for their religion. — Muqātil identifies faithful, righteous migrants and their want in Medina. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, describes loss of property, children, and homes; al-Wāḥidī applies reliance to their migration. Al-Bayḍāwī names pagan abuse and migration; Abū Ḥayyān identifies Meccan Muslims ordered to migrate; Ibn Kathīr includes departure from enemies, family, and kin. Al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also place the patience in persecution and migration. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 29:59/q03 What does their patience involve?  [صَبَرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether patience is described by its religious acts and hardships, by its kinds or acknowledgment, or by one's inward reception of affliction.
- 29:59/q03/p1 Steadfastness through religious hardship, including persecution, migration, privation, obedience, and restraint from sin. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, specifies perseverance in God's command. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by al-Wāḥidī, lists religion, hunger, distress, and loss of property, children, and homes. Muqātil adds lack of money or livelihood in Medina. Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī say hardship and harm do not make them abandon religion; al-Ṭabrisī adds demanding worship. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī include obedience and avoiding sin or desires; al-Nasafī also lists leaving home, polytheists, trials, and calamities. Al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī name pagan abuse and migration; Ibn Kathīr adds leaving enemies and family. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Meccan persecution is especially intended, while patience extends to calamity, obedience, and resisting sin. The Study Quran contrasts believers' patient endurance now with disbelievers' impatience. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 29:59/q03/p2 Patience is of two kinds, which this note does not specify. — ʿUmar states that patience has two kinds, as reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عمر)]
- 29:59/q03/p3 Patience is the servant's acknowledgment. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr gives this formulation, as reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim; the note supplies no further object or explanation of the acknowledgment. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 29:59/q03/p4 Patience means keeping good company with affliction as with ease. — An unnamed authority reported by al-Sulamī distinguishes this from merely forced endurance. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 29:59/q03/p5 Patience means receiving affliction with a spacious heart. — Abū Bakr al-Warrāq gives this definition, as reported by al-Sulamī. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق)]

## 29:59/q04 What does reliance on their Lord mean and cover?  [وَعَلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether reliance is explained by exclusive hope and practical dependence on God or by al-Kharrāz's paradoxical inward description.
- 29:59/q04/p1 They trust and hope in God alone for their affairs, including migration, provision, and religious and worldly needs. — Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, says they hope in no one besides Him. Al-Wāḥidī says they trust Him in migration after leaving houses and property. Muqātil notes migrants' lack of money and livelihood. Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī pair trust with hardship, the latter specifying important affairs and migration. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī stress reliance on God alone. Ibn Kathīr applies it in every religious and worldly circumstance; al-Biqāʿī includes provision and struggle with enemies, with renewed trust rather than dependence on family or homeland. Al-Bursawī says strong faith loosens attachment to home, property, and provision, makes exile and home alike, and leaves God's reward sufficient. Al-Ālūsī extends reliance to all they do and leave undone. The Study Quran puts future provision beside present endurance. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 29:59/q04/p2 Trust in God is agitation without stillness and stillness without agitation. — Abū Saʿīd al-Kharrāz gives this paradoxical definition in al-Sulamī; al-Jurayrī explicitly approves it as a good statement. [2 notes: SULAMI(أبو سعيد الخراز); SULAMI(الجريري)+]

## 29:59/q05 How do patience and reliance work together?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain the pairing through present and future needs, its breadth of virtues, or knowledge and certainty that support it.
- 29:59/q05/p1 Patience meets present hardship, while reliance concerns future needs. — Al-Rāzī says the past cannot be recovered or changed, so patience suits present difficulty and reliance future provision. The Study Quran likewise contrasts believers' present endurance and trust in what God will provide, against disbelievers who demand hastened punishment, citing 29:53–54 and 16:42. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 29:59/q05/p2 Together they encompass the good of religious life. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they gather all good, including obedience and restraint from desires. Abū Ḥayyān says patience and reliance comprise all good while describing leaving home, obeying commands, and avoiding prohibitions. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes both the defining traits of true believing workers. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 29:59/q05/p3 Knowledge and certainty ground patience and trust; sound patience supports trust and matures into gratitude and contentment. — Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī, reported by al-Sulamī, says patience grows from certainty, cannot exist soundly without it, supports trust, and on maturing leads to gratitude and contentment. Al-Rāzī says awareness that the world passes makes patience easier, while knowledge that God endures supports reliance on His provision. [2 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن علي); RAZI-FULL]

## 29:59/q06 When should believers endure harm where they are, and when should they migrate?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the distinction is the believer's ability to migrate or ability to practice religion in the present land.
- 29:59/q06/p1 Those able to leave a harmful place should migrate and rely on God; those unable should endure and continue worship. — Al-Rāzī reads the surrounding exhortation, including 29:56, as addressing these two groups according to their ability to depart. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 29:59/q06/p2 Endure harm while religious practice remains possible; migrate and endure migration's hardship when it becomes impossible. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes ability to practice religion in one's land the criterion for staying or leaving. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 29:59/q07 How does this verse connect to the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding theme supplies the connection: migrants' provision, the promised reward, or the contrast with impatient disbelievers.
- 29:59/q07/p1 It reassures migrants who feared losing home and livelihood that God will provide. — Muqātil says migrants lacking money or livelihood in Medina were admonished to take heed. Al-Wāḥidī reports Muqātil's account that Meccan migrants feared no home or livelihood in Medina. Abū Ḥayyān says the next animal example reassures Muslims ordered from Mecca that God provides for creatures unable to stock provisions; they feared poverty as strangers without property or anyone to feed them. Al-Biqāʿī applies renewed trust to provision after migration rather than dependence on family or homeland. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 29:59/q07/p2 It identifies those promised reward in the preceding verses as patient, reliant believers. — Muqātil frames the passage with everyone's death and return for judgment and the eternal rooms with rivers promised to faithful, righteous migrants. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the promise to believers doing good first calls them workers and then defines those workers by patience and reliance. [2 notes: MUQATIL; TABATABAI]
- 29:59/q07/p3 It contrasts patient believers with disbelievers who demand that punishment be hastened. — The Study Quran contrasts believers' endurance and hope for God's future provision with the disbelievers' demand, mentioning 29:53–54 and 16:42. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 29:59/q08 What reports are cited about reward for the faithful in this passage?  [* · reports]
- 29:59/q08/p1 The Prophet describes transparent chambers prepared for people who feed others, speak well, fast, and pray while others sleep. — Ibn Kathīr reports this prophetic statement through Abū Mālik al-Ashʿarī in connection with the promised chambers. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو مالك الأشعري)]
- 29:59/q08/p2 A person who flees even a short distance with his religion merits Paradise and companionship with Abraham and Muhammad. — Al-Bursawī cites the hadith and explains Paradise as recompense for giving up home for religion, and their companionship as recompense for following their migration and reviving their practice. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 29:59/q09 What can migration mean for the spiritual traveler?  [* · inward]
- 29:59/q09/p1 Leaving social prestige and public acceptance for obscurity. — Al-Bursawī gives this inward sense. He reports Abū Saʿīd al-Kharrāz leaving when his fame grew so much that people competed to buy a melon fallen from his hand; he welcomed a place where people called him an unbeliever and stoned him twice daily. He also reports Ibrāhīm ibn Adham saying he had felt worldly joy only three times: in Antioch he went barefoot and bareheaded, welcomed being called a runaway slave, and told his self it could return peacefully if it had fled. [3 notes: BURSEVI(أبو سعيد الخراز,إبراهيم بن أدهم)]
