# 76:3: 20 questions

## 76:3/q01 Why is divine guidance stated emphatically as 'We guided him'?  [إِنَّا هَدَيْنَٰهُ · rhetoric]
- 76:3/q01/p1 The emphatic divine attribution marks guidance as crucial, perhaps even surpassing creation in importance. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says it echoes 'We created' and reasons from creation for worship at 51:56; only the Creator knows the sound way and unaided human reasoning fragments into factions. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:3/q02 What kind of guidance does 'We guided him' denote?  [هَدَيْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the guidance is a common disclosure of the way or a disclosure of each person's particular route.
- 76:3/q02/p1 God makes the way known and its evidence clear; this guidance need not bring the person to the destination. — Al-Farrāʾ defines hidāya as making the way known. Al-Wāḥidī distinguishes showing from creating faith, after which God may enable or abandon; al-Rāzī cites 8:42 and al-Bursawī 90:10. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes showing the route from arrival. Al-Ālūsī says it applies to either response, citing 2:26. Ibn Kathīr prefers the well-known majority account over emergence from the womb. [24 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة وعطية وابن زيد ومجاهد والجمهور)+]
- 76:3/q02/p2 Guidance directs each person along the path of that person's kind, believer toward faith and unbeliever toward disbelief. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya offers this as an alternative while still explaining hadaynāhu as showing, not creating faith. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 76:3/q03 By what means does God show the way?  [هَدَيْنَٰهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the note identifies a combined rational and revealed disclosure or singles out innate capacity.
- 76:3/q03/p1 God uses both human faculties or rational signs and prophetic revelation to make guidance accessible. — Al-Wāḥidī names evidence and messengers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya senses, innate endowment and signs; al-Rāzī senses before intellect, then prophets and books; al-Zamakhsharī rational and transmitted proofs; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī innate prompting and prophetic speech (91:7–8; 30:30); Asad reason, moral instinct and revelation; Study Quran intellect, messengers and signs in revelation, creation and the self (41:53). Al-Biqāʿī adds insight separating divine from human speech; Elmalılı adds origin, destination and duties. Al-Ṭabrisī says accountable people use reason and revelation to attain reward. [26 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ,R)]
- 76:3/q03/p2 God endowed people with instinctive awareness of benefit and harm or completed their intellect. — Al-Māwardī states the two alternatives; al-Qurṭubī mentions instinct and developed reason; Kur'an Yolu describes capacity to understand one's profit and loss. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]

## 76:3/q04 Does this guidance reach every human being in the same way?  [هَدَيْنَٰهُ · theology]
turns on: The distinction between universal innate guidance and uneven access to a clear prophetic call.
- 76:3/q04/p1 Innate guidance belongs universally to human creation, while the spoken religious call reaches societies with unequal individual access. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says distractions and stubbornness weaken innate guidance (45:23); a clear call establishes proof, while those it does not reach clearly are left to divine grace and judgment (4:98). He distinguishes warning every community from warning a particular people whose ancestors lacked warning (35:24; 36:6). [4 notes: TABATABAI]
- 76:3/q04/p2 The path is shown to all people, including those who later follow it and those who turn away. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī read the contrasting states as circumstances of the same guided human referent or as division of humanity after guidance; Asad says each person then proves grateful or ungrateful. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 76:3/q05 Why does 'guided him' take 'the path' directly?  [هَدَيْنَٰهُ ٱلسَّبِيلَ · grammar]
- 76:3/q05/p1 Arabic permits the verb with the path directly, or with ilā or li, while conveying that the way is made known. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Wāḥidī explicitly give all three constructions; al-Rāzī repeats them. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes direct use for one on a path, ilā for directing toward it, and li for explaining its features, saying direct use here embraces arrival and explanation; he cites 1:6, 10:35, 42:52, 17:9, 48:2, 14:12, 19:43 and 4:68. [4 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:3/q06 What is the path, al-sabīl, that God shows?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ · referent]
turns on: Whether sabīl denotes disclosed moral alternatives, the single true religion, assigned outcomes, innate benefit and harm, or physical birth; Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject the birth reading.
- 76:3/q06/p1 The paths of good and evil, truth and falsehood, guidance and error are made clear. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and others name both moral directions; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and Kur'an Yolu cite the two high roads of 90:10. Ibn Kathīr also cites Thamūd at 41:17. Al-Rāzī says the singular is generic and invokes 103:2. Al-Ṭabrisī says evidence allows recognition; al-Bursawī pairs good/evil with salvation/ruin. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(عطية,عكرمة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عطية العوفي); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 76:3/q06/p2 The path is the true religion, monotheism or Islam, leading to God and felicity. — Al-Wāḥidī says proofs and messengers disclose monotheism; al-Ṭabrisī calls it knowledge of religion through reason and revelation; al-Rāzī says only the guidance path deserves the absolute name, whereas an error path is so called relationally (33:67). Elmalılı connects Islam with 75:30, 75:12, 53:42 and the Opening; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it leads to nearness to God. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 76:3/q06/p3 The way comprises felicity and wretchedness, or their respective routes. — Al-Zajjāj states this directly; Mujāhid's explanation is reported by al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Durr. Al-Wāḥidī glosses these as truth/falsehood or good/evil and cites 90:10; the later Turkish commentary reports the same alternative. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أهل التفسير); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 76:3/q06/p4 The path is the route by which the infant emerges from the womb. — Al-Māwardī reports Abū Ṣāliḥ, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Suddī; al-Qurṭubī repeats them, Abū Ḥayyān names al-Suddī, and Ibn Kathīr also attributes the unusual view to Mujāhid. Ibn Kathīr rejects it for the majority explanation, al-Ālūsī calls it untenable, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it unsound. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صالح والضحاك والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك وأبو صالح والسدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد وأبو صالح والضحاك والسدي)-; ALUSI-FULL(السدي)-; TABATABAI-]
- 76:3/q06/p5 It is the route of benefit and harm discernible by instinct, or the completion of intellect. — Al-Māwardī gives the alternatives; al-Qurṭubī explains benefits and harms as recognized through instinct and developed reason; Kur'an Yolu speaks of capacity to understand profit and loss. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler)]
- 76:3/q06/p6 It is each person's own path, the believer's toward faith and the unbeliever's toward disbelief. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this as an alternative and says 'guided' means showed, not created faith. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 76:3/q07 Why portray guidance as showing a path?  [ٱلسَّبِيلَ · rhetoric]
- 76:3/q07/p1 The path depicts God as guide, people as travelers, religious deeds as their road and success as its destination. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this an extended representation; he adds that a traveler may disregard directions and enter danger. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the easy, traversable sabīl calls for gratitude, whereas the two difficult najd paths demand striving and patience (90:10, 90:17). [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:3/q08 How is immā read in the two alternatives?  [إِمَّا وَإِمَّا · readings]
turns on: The recorded vowel of the initial hamza and the dialectal or detailed construction attached to the open reading.
- 76:3/q08/p1 The common reading has kasra and shadda on immā. — Al-Samīn calls it the common reading and says it functions like 'or'; al-Suyūṭī catalogs its functions with this vocalization, citing 9:106, 18:86, 20:65 and 47:4; Abū Ḥayyān contrasts it with the minority reading. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو السمال وأبو العاج)]
- 76:3/q08/p2 Both instances are read with an open initial vowel, ammā. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Abū al-ʿAj, identifying him as Kathīr ibn ʿAbd Allāh al-Sulamī, a Syrian governor in Basra. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī add Abū al-Sammāl; Abū Zayd reports it as a dialect, and poetry attests the paired form. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn allow an omitted response in the detailed ammā construction. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو السمال وأبو العاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو السمال وأبو العجاج); ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 76:3/q09 What grammatical function does the paired immā have?  [إِمَّا وَإِمَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether immā is a non-governing divider, a live conditional in plus mā, or a different construction in the open-vowel reading; the conditional parse draws explicit objections about nouns and accusative case.
- 76:3/q09/p1 It divides or specifies two alternatives without governing the following accusatives; waw joins the alternatives. — Al-Akhfash compares another paired alternative; the Basrans take the following words as circumstantial accusatives. Al-Suyūṭī explicitly calls 76:3 a division. Most grammarians, says Ibn ʿĀshūr, treat immā as a simple specifying particle; Sībawayh derives it historically from conditional in and negative mā with both functions lost. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects calling the second immā the coordinator, since the two particles are alike and waw coordinates; Taʾabbaṭa Sharran's couplet is his evidence. Ibn Jinnī reads the couplet's genitive isār as annexation with dual nūn deleted; al-Marzūqī alternatively explains a nominative reading by length. [12 notes: AKHFASH; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو الداني); BURHAN(البصريون,صاحب الأزهية); ITQAN; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن جني,الجمهور,المرزوقي,سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 76:3/q09/p2 It is conditional in plus an added mā: 'if grateful or ungrateful.' — Al-Farrāʾ and the Kufans allow the conditional reading. Al-Qurṭubī says al-Farrāʾ prefers it. Al-Qaffāl illustrates an omitted conditional answer by 'I advised you; if you wish, accept.' Al-Basrans and Makkī object that in cannot govern a noun without an implied verb; Abū Jaʿfar adds that the verse has nouns rather than verbs. Al-Samīn replies that an implied verb may govern the accusative, countering the claim that it must be nominative. [9 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(القفال); SAMIN-DURR(مكي); BURHAN(الكوفيون); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)+; NAHHAS-]
- 76:3/q09/p3 It marks alternatives in what God makes of a person, wretched or fortunate, as in a divine choice to punish or relent. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Wāḥidī compare 9:106. Al-Burhān notes indeterminacy and a reading about God's declaration that He does what He wills; another construes gratitude as a sign of felicity and disbelief as a sign of misery. Al-Naḥḥās reports exegetes' womb account of blessedness or misery and allows prospective states. [5 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(أهل التفسير); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BURHAN]
- 76:3/q09/p4 With open ammā, it may be a dialectal coordinating form or the detailed 'as for' form with omitted responses. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite the dialect and paired poetry; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn supply a tacit answer. Al-Zamakhsharī gives the contrasted responses as God's enabling gratitude and the person's bad choice in unbelief. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو السمال وأبو العاج); SAMIN-DURR(أبو السمال وأبو العجاج,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 76:3/q09/p5 One immā can be omitted conceptually and the following terms begun independently in the nominative. — Al-Akhfash draws a parallel with 'either punishment or the Hour.' Al-Burhān's Azhiyya author says immā is ordinarily repeated, but a substitute such as illā may replace the repetition. [2 notes: AKHFASH; BURHAN(صاحب الأزهية)]

## 76:3/q10 What do shākiran and kafūran grammatically describe?  [شَاكِرًا كَفُورًا · grammar]
turns on: The antecedent of the two accusatives and whether an omitted verb or clause is supplied.
- 76:3/q10/p1 They are circumstantial accusatives of the guided human, describing states or the two groups after guidance. — Al-Naḥḥās, Abū ʿAmr al-Dānī, Ibn Kathīr, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī attach them to the object pronoun. Abū al-Baqāʾ permits prospective states; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they follow delivery and consideration of guidance. Al-Bayḍāwī says all are shown the path before some follow and others turn away; al-Ālūsī allows either two states of one person or a division of people. [12 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو الداني); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 76:3/q10/p2 They figuratively describe the path or its two ways through the people who walk them. — Al-Wāḥidī attributes an adverbial path construction to al-Akhfash; al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report the metaphor. Al-Samīn and al-Zamakhsharī compare 90:10. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 76:3/q10/p3 An omitted clause yields 'whether he was grateful or ungrateful' or 'He made him grateful or ungrateful.' — Al-Wāḥidī gives an understood if-be or God-made-him clause; al-Rāzī supplies omitted kāna; al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī report a result construction in which God makes the path clear regardless of outcome. Al-Thaʿlabī records a consequence parse preferred by al-Farrāʾ. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; THALABI+]
- 76:3/q10/p4 They state the distinction that guidance enables people to make between gratitude and disbelief or obedience and sin. — Al-Rāzī relates this reading to testing deeds in 11:7, 29:3 and 47:31; he also reports a warning paraphrase: choose belief or disbelief and face its fitting result (18:29). [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 76:3/q11 What do 'grateful' and 'ungrateful' mean here?  [شَاكِرًا كَفُورًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pair is glossed by faith, treatment of blessings, ultimate fate, the source of each response, or its recompense.
- 76:3/q11/p1 They are the believer or monotheist and the unbeliever or polytheist. — Muqātil defines the grateful as one crediting God with creation and the ungrateful as one denying unity or ascribing blessings elsewhere. Ibn ʿAbbās adds obedience versus polytheism; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and Study Quran identify the two with faith and disbelief, the last citing 18:29. Al-Bursawī says gratitude affirms benefaction and unity, disbelief begins with denial. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī add fortunate versus wretched. [12 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 76:3/q11/p2 They describe acknowledging or denying God's blessings and acting accordingly. — Qatāda says gratitude or ingratitude toward blessing; Qāshānī says the grateful use faculties in obedience toward the Benefactor while the ungrateful are veiled by gifts and misuse them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says gratitude acknowledges the giver while using a gift and true gratitude is sincerity (3:144); Elmalılı adds belief, endurance and moral growth; al-Sādiq describes taking versus leaving the guided way. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites Satan's ingratitude at 17:27 and relates the form to 43:15 and 34:17; his discussion of li-rabbihi says li intensifies the clause, while disbelief takes bi and denial of a blessing a direct object (4:150; 2:91; 85:16). [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(حمران بن أعين); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:3/q11/p3 They are the ultimately happy or blessed and the wretched. — Al-Naḥḥās connects the states with the Prophetic account of womb destiny and allows them as future states; al-Qushayrī offers bliss and misery; Ibn Kathīr says the guided person's circumstantial state means eventual happiness or wretchedness; Study Quran cites Ibn Kathīr for this gloss. [4 notes: NAHHAS(أهل التفسير); QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 76:3/q11/p4 They indicate gratitude through God's enabling grace and disbelief through abandonment or the person's bad choice. — Al-Qushayrī attributes enabled gratitude to God's friends and abandoned disbelief to His enemies; al-Qurṭubī says proofs of unity precede created faith or abandonment; al-Zamakhsharī gives enabling and bad choice, with al-Samīn supplying these as omitted responses; al-Ālūsī calls that conditional gloss strained. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 76:3/q11/p5 They foreshadow reward for the thankful and punishment for the ungrateful. — Al-Ālūsī gives this alternative; al-Rāzī also reads a warning that each choice has its fitting result (18:29). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 76:3/q12 What does the verse imply about human choice and divine action?  [هَدَيْنَٰهُ شَاكِرًا كَفُورًا · theology]
turns on: How disclosure of the way relates to divine knowledge and enabling, human willing, and responsibility.
- 76:3/q12/p1 God shows the way and people freely choose how to respond, making them accountable. — Al-Zamakhsharī says God enables both possibilities and calls through rational and transmitted proofs; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God's will concerns the person's willing, not coercion (80:20; 76:29–30). Kur'an Yolu cites 17:18–19 and 18:29 for capacities and choice; Study Quran parallels 90:8–10. Asad says each person proves grateful or ungrateful. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 76:3/q12/p2 The alternatives occur within God's prior knowledge and guidance to felicity or misery, serving as an argument against the Qadariyya. — Ibn ʿAbbās places the alternatives in God's knowledge; al-Wāḥidī invokes God's guidance to the paths of happiness and misery against the Qadariyya. Al-Farrāʾ's analogy with 9:106 has God creating a person wretched or happy. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الفراء); FARRA]
- 76:3/q12/p3 Faith follows God's created guidance or enabling, while unbelief follows abandonment or a bad human choice. — Al-Wāḥidī distinguishes showing the way from creating guidance; al-Qurṭubī speaks of proofs followed by created guidance or abandonment. Al-Zamakhsharī credits gratitude to divine enabling and disbelief to bad choice; al-Ālūsī rejects the conditional form of that gloss as strained, favoring two responses to guidance (2:26). [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 76:3/q13 Why use the ordinary shākir but intensive kafūr?  [شَاكِرًا كَفُورًا · rhetoric]
- 76:3/q13/p1 No human can fully thank every divine favor, while ingratitude can be abundant or extreme. — Al-Māwardī, quoted by al-Qurṭubī, says favors cannot be repaid. ʿAbd al-Jabbār says each thank-you is small beside many gifts but each ingratitude great. Al-Biqāʿī says even one denied favor can reveal deep ingratitude; Abū Ḥayyān calls gratitude rare and unbelief common. Al-Bayḍāwī adds the rhyme and indulgence as the source of culpability; al-Ālūsī also mentions rhyme and pervasive lesser ingratitude. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says shakūr would exclude ordinary grateful people while kāfir is paired with believer elsewhere (34:13; 64:2). [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(عبد الجبار بن أحمد); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:3/q14 Why is gratitude mentioned before ingratitude?  [شَاكِرًا كَفُورًا · rhetoric]
- 76:3/q14/p1 Gratitude accords with the prior blessings and the sura's priority of mercy and its longer account of the grateful. — Al-Biqāʿī calls it the human norm and says mercy precedes anger. Al-Sāmarrāʾī points to seventeen verses of reward versus one of punishment (76:4–5, 22), mercy at both ends of the sura (76:31), and common Quranic order (27:40; 31:12; 2:152). He contrasts al-Zumar, where deniers and their punishment come first (39:3, 7, 71, 73). [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:3/q15 Why say 'grateful' here rather than simply 'believing'?  [شَاكِرًا · rhetoric]
- 76:3/q15/p1 Creation, senses, reason, choice and guidance are favors calling for gratitude, which also fits the sura's praise of generous service. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 64:2, where creation alone is paired with believer and unbeliever, and cites feeding solely for God and rewarded effort at 76:9 and 76:22. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:3/q16 How is the guidance of verse 3 related to the creation and testing of verses 1–2?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link emphasizes the faculties and moral test or the preceding proof from bodily creation.
- 76:3/q16/p1 God first equips and tests the human, then explains that hearing, sight, reason and guidance make the test of gratitude possible. — Al-Thaʿlabī says the test is through commands and prohibitions and notes a proposed fronting and delay after hearing and sight. Al-Rāzī traces senses to elementary judgments and reasoning; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says hearing and sight receive prophetic calls and signs of origin and return. Ibn ʿĀshūr says religious duties distinguish humanity and the explanatory guidance clause elaborates the faculties and leads to warning and promise. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says hearing and sight come first as channels to understanding, contrasts nabtalī with al-Mulk 67:2, and inventories tester, human, faculties, paths, responses and outcomes. Qatāda traces embryonic stages (23:14) and says God explains origins to test knowledge of His rights and gratitude. Islahi also moves from the drop to awareness of good and evil. [13 notes: THALABI; RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 76:3/q16/p2 The guidance clause follows the intricate creation of the body as a further proof of its unseen Creator. — Al-Biqāʿī details specified bones, joints, tendons and ligaments; purposive eyes, ears, nose, teeth and tongue; and specialized digestion and circulation from a tiny drop in three darknesses. He says people admire human design on walls yet overlook the greater craftsmanship of their bodies and cites the preceding deniers' pride against resurrection (75:3, 31–33, 37–38). [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 76:3/q17 How do the alternatives lead into the following recompense?  [* · links]
- 76:3/q17/p1 The two responses lead to promised reward and threatened punishment, with warning placed next to the nearer mention of unbelief. — Al-Nasafī says the text follows the two groups with what God prepared. Elmalılı compares promise and threat at 41:40. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the unbelievers come first because they were mentioned nearest and the strong warning disallows leniency. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says 'unbelievers' in 76:4 covers ordinary unbelievers as well as intensive kafūr; because people had freedom in life, mistaken choice is met by chains on legs and fetters at neck and hands (5:64). His sura-wide comparison pairs the opening's grateful/ungrateful with the ending's mercy/punishment. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; TABATABAI]

## 76:3/q18 What reports illustrate the grateful and ungrateful outcomes?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct transmitted illustrations, not rival versions of a single event.
- 76:3/q18/p1 Every child begins on the fiṭrah, then becomes grateful or ungrateful as speech and upbringing develop. — Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr transmit Jābir's report with speech as the turning point; Ibn Kathīr links it to 30:30. Al-Biqāʿī quotes the variant in which parents make the child Jewish, Christian or Magian and names transmissions from Abū Hurayrah, Ibn ʿAbbās, Jābir and al-Aswad ibn Surayʿ. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 76:3/q18/p2 Each person starts the day freeing or ruining himself. — Ibn Kathīr cites Abū Mālik al-Ashʿarī's Prophetic report and says Muslim transmits it. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو مالك الأشعري عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 76:3/q18/p3 The purpose of a person leaving home determines whether an angel's or Satan's banner follows him. — Ibn Kathīr transmits this from Abū Hurayrah, distinguishing what pleases or angers God. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 76:3/q18/p4 The Prophet warned Kaʿb ibn ʿUjra against endorsing rulers' lies or helping oppression, with fasting, charity, prayer and illicit earnings also named. — Ibn Kathīr gives the report's two parts: refusal preserves association with the Prophet and his Pond, while complicity disowns one; fasting shields, charity erases sin, prayer is a قربان or proof, and flesh nourished by illicit earnings belongs in Hell. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 76:3/q19 How is this passage connected to the sura's wider argument and Sūrat al-Qiyāmah?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources trace either a paired argument with the preceding sura or the internal arc of this sura.
- 76:3/q19/p1 The sura pairs with al-Qiyāmah to establish Judgment through human origin and the need to recompense gratitude and ingratitude. — Islahi says its first three verses dovetail with al-Qiyāmah's last four (75:37–40); al-Qiyāmah also invokes the reproaching soul (75:2). Al-Biqāʿī places the passage after deniers' prideful rejection of resurrection (75:3, 31–33, 37–38). [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 76:3/q19/p2 The sura begins with creation and the two responses, elaborates punishment and righteous blessings, and ends by calling the Prophet to patience and worship. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī summarizes this arc; al-Sāmarrāʾī notes that the opening contrast of grateful and ungrateful becomes mercy and punishment at the end. [2 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 76:3/q20 Was the sura containing this verse revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether a muṣḥaf classification or the sura's subject matter decides its place of revelation.
- 76:3/q20/p1 Some muṣḥafs classify the sura as Medinan. — Islahi reports the classification without endorsing it. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some maṣāḥif); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 76:3/q20/p2 The whole sura is Meccan. — Islahi rejects a Medinan designation for any verse because he takes subject matter as the criterion and judges this sura's subject to confirm Meccan origin. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
