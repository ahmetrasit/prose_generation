# 70:19: 16 questions

## 70:19/q01 Who is the human described here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the generic noun and the exception for worshippers indicate all humanity, or the warning primarily identifies unbelievers; two reports name particular men.
- 70:19/q01/p1 Humanity as a species is meant, with worshippers excepted from the blameworthy response. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi, al-Itqan, and Study Quran infer generality from the exception for those who pray (70:22-23); al-Qurtubi adduces the comparable generic al-nafs and exception in 6:151 and 103:1-3. Ibn Ashur compares generic human sayings at 96:6-7 and 21:37. Al-Alusi and al-Jishumi report this as one possible scope; Study Quran reports both scopes and prefers generality. Al-Akhfash observes that al-insan is singular in form but denotes people collectively, as the plural exception for those who pray in 70:22 shows. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ITQAN; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; AKHFASH+; STUDYQURAN(R, Z)+]
- 70:19/q01/p2 Disbelievers are especially or specifically intended. — Al-Tabari calls the subject the disbeliever, and transmits Ibn Abbas's identification with associators and al-Dahhak's description of the disbeliever. Al-Mawardi transmits al-Dahhak and al-Kalbi; al-Qurtubi transmits al-Dahhak. Ibn Atiyya says the generic noun particularly targets unbelievers because the warning applies to them most forcefully. Al-Alusi, al-Jishumi, and Study Quran record this possible scope. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك والكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 70:19/q01/p3 It concerns Abu Jahl as a particular example. — Ibn Abbas's report in al-Durr says the verse was revealed about Abu Jahl. Al-Alusi transmits another Ibn Abbas report through al-Tusti but says a specific instance does not rule out the general meaning. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 70:19/q01/p4 It identifies Umayya ibn Khalaf al-Jumahi. — Ibn al-Jawzi transmits this identification from Muqatil. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]
- 70:19/q01/p5 Al-Akhnas ibn Shariq is the human identified here. — Al-Damghani identifies al-insan with al-Akhnas ibn Shariq; Ibn al-Jawzi's Nuzha reports the same identification from some exegetes. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); DAMGHANI+]

## 70:19/q02 What does halūʿ mean?  [هَلُوعًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term itself names the paired reactions, one reaction or desire, a deeper lack of restraint, or eager pursuit of perceived good; Ibn Ashur appeals to poetry to distinguish halʿ from distress.
- 70:19/q02/p1 It is a twofold disposition: intense distress or impatience at harm and greed or withholding when good comes. — Al-Hasan, al-Farra, Abu Ubayda, Tha'lab, Ibn Abbas, and many authors use 70:20-21 as God's explanation; reports vary between panic, despair, irritation, insufficient patience or gratitude, greed, and refusal to share. Al-Nahhas specifies poverty and withholding obligatory rights when wealthy; al-Kashshaf and al-Nasafi allow wealth or health versus poverty or illness; al-Qurtubi specifies God's due and people's share. Al-Farra derives the pattern from jazaʿ. Al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, and al-Alusi cite a swift, light she-camel to explain rapid reactions; al-Qurtubi cites a poetic line for that usage. Al-Durr cites Bashr ibn Abi Hazim's poetry. Al-Wahidi records Amr ibn Ma'dikarib's line and al-Mubarrad's appeal to seeking refuge from panic in battle. The prophetic reports describe anxious greed and disabling cowardice among the worst traits. Ibn Ashur reports Tha'lab's explanation but objects that the two reactions do not entail one another as one lexical definition. Abu al-Aliya in al-Burhan and Ibn Abbas in al-Durr Full explicitly take God's following description as the answer to what haluʿ means. Bint al-Shati agrees with al-Farra and Tha'lab that the Quran explains it through panic at harm and withholding at good; al-Farra adds that one is readily distressed. Al-Samarra'i likewise reads 70:20-21 as defining it and reports al-Kashshaf's gloss of quick despair and quick withholding, with wealth and poverty as one account of good and harm. [57 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد,عمرو بن معديكرب); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة وثعلب,أبو مسلم,سهل,عطية عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(أحمد بن يحيى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، أبو عبيدة، الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ثعلب,قتادة ومجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(محمد بن عبد الله بن طاهر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ثعلب); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيد,ثعلب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,عكرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL(أحمد بن يحيى (ثعلب)); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف,فتح القدير); STUDYQURAN(Q); BURHAN(أبو العالية); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء); BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 70:19/q02/p2 It means distress, fretfulness, agitation, grief, or impatience. — Al-Majaz appeals to the next verses for failure to endure. Al-Tabari transmits Ikrima, Ibn Zayd, and Qatada; al-Tha'labi also reports sorrow or grief from Qatada and Ibn Zayd. Al-Wahidi transmits al-Farra. Ibn Atiyya relates agitation to fears and desires; Ibn al-Jawzi includes Ibn Qutayba's intense distress; al-Durr cites Bashr ibn Abi Hazim for Arabic usage. Ibn Ashur says many linguists use distress but argues it is an effect rather than the trait itself, testing the distinction with Amr ibn Ma'dikarib's verse. Abu Hayyan glosses the related jazaʿ as fear and cites a poetic line. Al-Itqan reports the distressed and impatient gloss with Bishr ibn Abi Hazim's verse as evidence. Bint al-Shati reports Ibn Abbas's exchange with Nafi ibn al-Azraq, where he glosses the word as distressed and prone to lament and cites Bishr's line; she also reports lexicographers who narrow the sense to extreme distress, sometimes specifically panic and lack of patience at calamity. [25 notes: MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,عكرمة,قتادة); THALABI(عكرمة,قتادة وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI(قتادة وابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,عكرمة وقتادة ومقاتل والفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,قتادة); ITQAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,المعجميون); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 70:19/q02/p3 It means miserliness or stinginess. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Hasan, and al-Dahhak are among those reported with this gloss. Al-Qurtubi explains withholding God's due from received wealth. Ibn Ashur classifies this as an effect of the broader weakness of restraint. [9 notes: THALABI(الضحاك والحسن,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); JISHUMI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك والحسن,سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جبير,الحسن والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 70:19/q02/p4 It means greed, covetousness, or voracious desire, sometimes specifically for what is unlawful. — Al-Hasan, Husayn, Ikrima, Mujahid, and Ibn Abbas are variously transmitted with the gloss; al-Dahhak describes someone never satiated, or never satisfied with amassed wealth. The al-Suddi report from Ibn Abbas and Ibn al-Jawzi's report specify unlawful objects. Ibn Ashur treats greed and hunger as effects of a broader trait. [14 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(حصين); THALABI(الضحاك,حصين,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); JISHUMI(مجاهد والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,حصين بن عبد الرحمن); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 70:19/q02/p5 It is love of pleasure and flight from pain. — Ibn Kaysan and al-Asamm add the moral countercommand: give what one loves and patiently endure what one dislikes. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن كيسان); JISHUMI(الأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كيسان)]
- 70:19/q02/p6 It is restlessness as the soul follows desires and changes with possession or loss. — Sahl describes fluctuation with appetite. Ibn Ata describes contentment when possessing and resentment at loss, even of a small worldly gain; Bursevi repeats this. Abu al-Hasan al-Warraq adds cursing in affliction and supplication under trial. [6 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو الحسن الوراق,ابن عطاء,سهل); BURSEVI]
- 70:19/q02/p7 It means haste, swift agitation, or quickness. — Ata supplies the haste gloss, though al-Tha'labi's excerpt cuts off another gloss. Abu Muslim compares a fast, light she-camel. Ibn Ashur also lists haste among effects rather than the central trait. Bint al-Shati reports lexical uses of related forms for a quick-moving ostrich whose lightness makes it startle, as well as for a fast-moving she-camel. [4 notes: THALABI(عطاء); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,عطاء); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(المعجميون)]
- 70:19/q02/p8 It means narrowness of heart. — This gloss is attributed to Muqatil by al-Tha'labi, al-Jishumi, and al-Baghawi. [3 notes: THALABI(مقاتل); JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 70:19/q02/p9 It is weak self-restraint before actual or anticipated pleasure or pain. — Ibn Ashur prefers this broader trait. Amr ibn Ma'dikarib's line separately denies halʿ and jazaʿ, so he rejects treating them as synonyms; he says al-Marquzi's mild-versus-severe-grief explanation fails. Greed, irritation, miserliness, hunger, and cowardice are effects. The condition becomes apparent on perceiving benefit or harm. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عمرو بن معد يكرب); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 70:19/q02/p10 It is intense eagerness for perceived personal good. — Al-Tabatabai prefers eagerness; perceived harm frustrates it and causes panic, while perceived benefits prompt selfish retention. Children's pursuit of apparent good, resistance to sharing, later rational revision, and persistent appetite illustrate how it works. [3 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 70:19/q02/p11 It means weakness. — Al-Mawardi attributes this gloss to Abu al-Ghiyath. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو الغياث)]
- 70:19/q02/p12 It expresses the ego's dark, impure attachment, producing cowardice and greed. — Al-Qashani interprets the distress and withholding of the following verses as manifestations of attachment to the ego's nature. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 70:19/q03 How does the syntax describe the human as halūʿ?  [خُلِقَ هَلُوعًا · grammar]
- 70:19/q03/p1 Khuliqa is the predicate of inna, and halūʿan is a circumstantial accusative, possibly prospective. — Al-Nahhas parses the two words this way; Abu Muslim in al-Jishumi also calls halūʿan a circumstantial expression; al-Samin specifies a prospective circumstance. [3 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); SAMIN-DURR]

## 70:19/q04 How do the following descriptions of distress and withholding relate to halūʿ?  [هَلُوعًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the next two clauses are a lexical definition of one compound trait or name distinct effects of a deeper trait.
- 70:19/q04/p1 They are explanatory clauses or descriptions that unpack the preceding word. — Al-Burhan says explanatory clauses are common in the Quran. Al-Itqan cites rhetoricians that such clauses remove ambiguity, and Abu al-Aliya applies this here. Ibn Jinni, quoted in al-Burhan and al-Itqan, says one should not pause before the explanatory clause because it completes its antecedent like a relative clause or adjective. Al-Qurtubi says jazuʿan and manuʿan are adjectives brought before the condition, or predicates of an implicit kana; many exegetes take God's following words as the clearest explanation of halūʿ. Abu al-Aliya in al-Burhan, Ibn Abbas in al-Durr Full, al-Farra as reported by Bint al-Shati, Bint al-Shati herself, and al-Samarra'i explicitly say the next verses explain or define haluʿ through the paired reactions. [18 notes: BURHAN(أبو العالية,ابن جني); ITQAN(أبو العالية وغيره,أهل البيان,ابن جني); QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(أحمد بن يحيى); NASAFI-FULL(محمد بن عبد الله بن طاهر); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، أبو عبيدة، الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفراء); BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 70:19/q04/p2 The two distinct reactions are effects of a broader underlying trait, so they do not jointly define a single lexical sense. — Ibn Ashur rejects Tha'lab's loose equation: if two clauses defined one compound word, their different meanings would have to imply one another. He treats distress and other glosses as consequences of weak restraint. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 70:19/q05 Why is the statement introduced with emphatic inna?  [إِنَّ · rhetoric]
- 70:19/q05/p1 It emphasizes a startling human trait, warns against it, and explains the preceding statement. — Al-Alusi says the emphatic sentence gives the reason for what precedes; Ibn Ashur says inna draws attention to the surprising trait and warns against it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 70:19/q06 In what sense was the human created with this disposition?  [خُلِقَ هَلُوعًا · theology]
turns on: Whether creation names a real useful human impulse, a latent weakness, or figuratively describes deeply habituated blameworthy reactions; the infant and believers' exception are invoked for the figurative reading.
- 70:19/q06/p1 A real, innate but governable impulse is created for human benefit; blame falls on its misuse. — Al-Tabatabai says eagerness and self-love enable pursuit of happiness, while excess, deficiency, poor judgment, and appetite turn the gift toward misery, withdrawal, hoarding, rejection of truth, and violated rights. Reason can redirect eagerness toward fear of sin and haste to forgiveness; patience is prescribed in worldly hardship and duty. The believers' exception means the impulse is rightly governed, not absent. Ibn Ashur says the latent inclination surfaces on sensing benefit or harm, need not exclude moral training or contrary states, and hoarding can be checked by contemplating consequences. Al-Jishumi says desire is needed for moral responsibility but requires instruction in patience and restraint; al-Nasafi says revelation commands opposition to temperament. Al-Tabatabai rejects al-Zamakhshari's metaphorical escape. [15 notes: JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 70:19/q06/p2 The bad reactions are so habitual that 'created' is a metaphor for an acquired, seemingly innate condition. — Al-Zamakhshari says infants do not yet have despair and withholding, and believers discipline themselves out of them; the believers' exception also supports the figurative wording. Abu Hayyan compares 21:37 on human haste. Al-Tabatabai reports al-Zamakhshari's argument and objects that the real created impulse can persist in a good form. Ibn Ashur allows khuliqa to be metaphorical for persistent association, citing Urwa ibn Udhayna's poem about lovers created disposed to love. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 70:19/q06/p3 The wording denotes a latent human weakness or quick attachment to feelings, rather than fully formed infant behavior. — Al-Jishumi reports that humans are born needy, desirous, and weak in bearing such traits, which later generate impatience and withholding. Ibn Ashur says a nonmaterial trait attributed to creation may mean a disposition rapidly attaches to feelings and actions, comparing 76:2 and 21:37. The phrasing can acknowledge weakness or distinguish good original creation from later deterioration, as at 4:28 and 95:4-5; a generic statement of essence is comparable to 'man is better than woman' and Imru al-Qays's verse about young war. [5 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 70:19/q07 How does attributing the defect to creation assign moral responsibility?  [خُلِقَ · theology]
- 70:19/q07/p1 People are responsible for failing to discipline their impulses, while God gives the faculty and guidance for good. — Ibn Ashur says humans indulge desire and neglect revelation and wise counsel. Good is attributed to God's gift of the faculty seeking good and His guidance, with 4:78-79 as evidence. He rejects the Mu'tazilite position in this theological dispute without further detail in these notes. He also says the wording rebukes, rather than excuses, hoarding; it can soften blame by recognizing weakness (4:28) and contrast original good creation with deterioration (95:4-5). Al-Tabatabai likewise distinguishes the created beneficial disposition from people's misuse, and says the condemnation contains no contradiction. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 70:19/q08 What prophetic report describes the corresponding vices?  [هَلُوعًا · reports]
- 70:19/q08/p1 The Prophet called anxious or grasping greed and disabling cowardice among the worst traits given to a person. — Al-Kashshaf calls them the worst gifts to Adam's son; al-Qurtubi says gifts to a servant; Ibn Kathir transmits Abu Hurayra's report and notes Abu Dawud's parallel route. The descriptions vary between miserly despair, grasping greed, fear stripping courage, and paralyzing cowardice. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 70:19/q09 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 70:19/q09/p1 Verses 19-21 explain the human tendency behind turning away and hoarding, before exempting worshippers. — Ibn Ashur calls them an interruption between the hoarder in 70:17 and the exception in 70:22; the fear of losing benefit explains but does not excuse hoarding. Al-Tabatabai says misused innate panic leads to withdrawal, hoarding, and Hell, though created to guide humans to good. Al-Biqai says self-absorption causes forgetfulness of the Lord and sins. Study Quran says those with the eight qualities in 70:22-35 are exempt. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 70:19/q09/p2 Human panic at punishment explains the sinner's offer of his nearest kin, starting with his dearest possession, his sons. — Al-Samarra'i links the human anxiety described here to the preceding ransom scene: the sinner first offers his nearest kin, and naming sons first shows both how dear they are and how unimaginable the punishment is. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]

## 70:19/q10 What other passages parallel this description?  [* · links]
- 70:19/q10/p1 Other verses describe the same haste, despair, and withdrawal after mercy. — Study Quran cites 41:49-51 for asking for good, despair at harm, and withdrawal after mercy, and 21:37 and 17:11 for haste. Al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, and Ibn Ashur also compare 21:37; Ibn Ashur cites 96:6-7 for generic human arrogance. [5 notes: STUDYQURAN; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:19/q10/p2 The descriptions of believers in al-Mu'minun and al-Ma'arij are strongly parallel but differ in order and wording. — Al-Samarra'i points to 23:1-11 and the corresponding qualities here. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:19/q10/p3 Other verses contrast passive wording for human weakness with active divine attribution of praiseworthy creation and justice. — Al-Samarra'i compares the passive blameworthy descriptions here, at 4:28, and at 21:37 with active divine attribution in 95:4, 7:11, and 7:181. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]

## 70:19/q11 How can the disposition be restrained or overcome?  [* · interpretation]
- 70:19/q11/p1 Steady prayer and the qualities of the exempt believers train patience, generosity, desire, and attention. — Al-Kashshaf distinguishes regularly performing prayer from guarding its ablution, time, pillars, sunna, manners, and protection against invalidating sins; the Prophet calls consistent work best even if little, and Aisha reports his practice was continuous. Al-Samarra'i says persistent attentive prayer treats panic and withholding by giving time and self to God despite worldly costs (62:11), while guarding chastity trains restraint against tempting opportunities (23:5-7). Study Quran says the eight qualities of 70:22-35 exempt their possessors. Al-Tabatabai says developed reason redirects eagerness toward real good, fear of sin, forgiveness, and patience under duty. Al-Samarra'i says giving to the poor (70:24-25) cures both reactions: the giver does not grieve over wealth leaving and does not withhold the needy person's share. He also says faith in Judgment and fear of punishment bring reassurance, patience under harm in hope of God's reward, and generosity in expectation of multiplied repayment (70:26-28). [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]
- 70:19/q11/p2 Accept God's decree and direct health and wealth toward gain in the Hereafter. — Study Quran reports al-Razi's advice to recognize that circumstances come from God and be content, and to spend health and wealth seeking the Hereafter rather than this life. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(R,al-Rāzī)]

## 70:19/q12 What wisdom does al-Razi find in the created human body's capacity for speech?  [* · interpretation]
- 70:19/q12/p1 The body and breathing serve the soul's pursuit of knowledge and right action, while articulated speech enables easy, precise communication. — Al-Razi says people need others to meet their needs and speech is easier than writing or gesture. Human perfection is knowledge of truth and action on good; the body serves the initially unperfected soul. The heart supplies heat, draws in cooling air by expansion, and expels warmed air by contraction. Expelled air becomes sound; throat, tongue, teeth, and lips shape letters and limitless combinations of words. Sounds appear only when needed and vanish afterward; their variety avoids the ambiguity of gestures or clapping. The hierarchy runs from the soul through body, heart, breath, sound, and words, displaying the Creator's profound wisdom. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 70:19/q13 What reading of amānāt is reported alongside this discussion?  [* · readings]
turns on: Presence or absence of an alif after the nun in the plural.
- 70:19/q13/p1 Ibn Kathir reads the plural amānāt without an alif after the nun. — Al-Jishumi reports this reading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير)]
- 70:19/q13/p2 The remaining readers read the plural with an alif after the nun. — Al-Jishumi contrasts this with Ibn Kathir's reading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كثير)]

## 70:19/q14 How does the verse describe ordinary reactions to divine punishment?  [* · interpretation]
- 70:19/q14/p1 People often complain and become frustrated even at a small punishment for their deeds. — Islahi applies the human tendency to impatience under God's punishment. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 70:19/q15 How can singular al-insan denote people collectively?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · grammar]
- 70:19/q15/p1 The singular noun has a collective sense, as the plural exception for worshippers shows. — Al-Akhfash points to the plural exception in 70:22 as evidence that singular al-insan denotes people collectively. [1 notes: AKHFASH+]

## 70:19/q16 Why is creation expressed in the passive voice?  [خُلِقَ · rhetoric]
- 70:19/q16/p1 The passive avoids attributing a blameworthy quality directly to God, unlike active wording for praiseworthy divine creation and justice. — Al-Samarra'i contrasts this passive description with active divine attribution in 95:4, 7:11, and 7:181, and compares other passive descriptions of human weakness and haste in 4:28 and 21:37. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+]
