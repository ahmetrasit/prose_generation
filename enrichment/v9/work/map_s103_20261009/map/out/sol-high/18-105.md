# 18:105: 19 questions

## 18:105/q01 Who are the people identified by 'those'?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the following denial of God's meeting identifies idolaters exclusively or whether earlier descriptions can include other groups by partial resemblance.
- 18:105/q01/p1 The idol-worshipping polytheists, especially the Arabs who denied resurrection. — Ibn Atiyya says the following description of denying the meeting excludes Jews, Christians, and Kharijites as whole groups; Saʿd and ʿAli may have cited partial examples. Tabatabai says the indirect address of 18:103 targets polytheists and idolaters, who mistake shirk for obedience according to Kur'an Yolu. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 18:105/q01/p2 The People of the Book, especially Jewish and Christian worshippers and monks. — Mujahid names the People of the Book; ʿAli identifies monks by comparison with 88:3; Saʿd specifies Jewish and Christian monks and hermits. Ibn Atiyya objects that these communities do not deny resurrection as the sequel says. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(علي,مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 18:105/q01/p3 The Kharijites or people of Harura. — ʿAli reportedly told Ibn al-Kawwaʾ that he and his companions were among the greatest losers; another report says some were from Harura. Ibn Atiyya objects that Kharijites do not deny resurrection. Alusi answers that 'among' may indicate shared misguidance only, or ʿAli may have judged them unbelievers. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 18:105/q01/p4 The previously described people whose efforts went astray while they thought they acted well. — Bursevi connects the demonstrative directly to the preceding description; Alusi calls this a new divine statement specifying the greatest losers. Study Quran describes them as disbelievers whose efforts are futile. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 18:105/q02 What does the demonstrative and its placement contribute?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · rhetoric]
- 18:105/q02/p1 It emphatically identifies the previously described losers as fit for the ensuing judgment. — Ibn Ashur compares the demonstrative's force to 2:5; Alusi says this is a new divine statement completing the description, outside the preceding command. Bursevi links it to those misguided in effort. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 18:105/q03 What are the signs of their Lord that they reject?  [بِـَٔايَٰتِ رَبِّهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'signs' is restricted to the Quran or includes all divine proofs.
- 18:105/q03/p1 God's proofs of unity and power, including rational, revealed, and prophetic signs. — Wahidi Basit calls them evidence of oneness and power; Wahidi Wajiz includes the Quran. Tabrisi calls them proofs; Ibn al-Jawzi adds rejection of the Messenger and Quran; Baydawi adds proofs of prophethood; Ibn Kathir includes worldly signs and messengers; Bursevi and Alusi specify rational and transmitted evidence. Tabatabai includes signs in the world and oneself and prophetic miracles; Ibn Ashur names Quran and miracles. Alusi prefers this breadth to Quran alone. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 18:105/q03/p2 The Quran specifically. — Muqatil glosses the signs as the Quran. Baydawi reports Quran as one possible gloss; Alusi reports this narrower interpretation while preferring the broader one. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:105/q04 What is the meeting with God that they deny?  [وَلِقَآئِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether liqa points to resurrection, to seeing God, or to an implied encounter with His recompense; Razi and Alusi dispute the need to supply omitted wording.
- 18:105/q04/p1 Resurrection and return for gathering, reward, and punishment. — Muqatil, Yahya's context, Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Bursevi, Ibn Kathir, and Tabatabai connect the meeting to resurrection or afterlife recompense. Jishumi says gathering creation for reward and punishment warns morally responsible people. Alusi calls resurrection and its events the majority's metonymic interpretation, rather than a literal meeting. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:105/q04/p2 Seeing God. — Razi says liqa originally denotes arrival or encounter but commonly serves as a metaphor for seeing; he prefers this to supplying unexpressed words about meeting a reward and cites 54:12. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 18:105/q04/p3 Encountering God's recompense or punishment. — Tabrisi explains meeting as meeting His recompense; Baydawi offers punishment. Razi objects to inserting an unstated phrase for reward, and Alusi rejects an omitted 'punishment' as unsound. [2 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:105/q05 Why do their deeds come to nothing?  [فَحَبِطَتْ أَعْمَٰلُهُمْ · interpretation]
- 18:105/q05/p1 Disbelief nullifies their deeds and their expected reward. — Muqatil, Ibn al-Jawzi, Biqai, and Bursevi say faith is the foundation missing from their good works. Ibn Atiyya includes whatever good they did; Ibn Abbas, as Wahidi reports, says their striving was null. Tabrisi says the acts ran contrary to God's command; Tabatabai says they neither act for God nor seek afterlife happiness. Biqai says denial removes restraint on desire; Kur'an Yolu contrasts seeking God with worldly aims and says idolaters count shirk as obedience. Ibn Ashur says the nullification explains their lack of weight; Study Quran compares 7:147, 2:217, 3:21-22, 5:5, 5:53, 6:88, 9:17, 33:19, 39:65, 47:9-10, 47:32 and the scattered dust of 25:23. Ibn Ashur glosses habita as becoming void and refuted. [23 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 18:105/q06 How is the bāʾ of faḥabiṭat read?  [فَحَبِطَتْ · readings]
turns on: The vowel on the bāʾ.
- 18:105/q06/p1 With kasra, ḥabiṭat, the majority reading. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, and Alusi identify it as the usual or majority reading. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو السمال); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 18:105/q06/p2 With fatḥa, ḥabaṭat. — Ibn Atiyya and Alusi attribute it to Ibn Abbas and Abu al-Sammal; Qurtubi and Samin name Ibn Abbas. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو السمال); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 18:105/q07 How is the loss of deeds expressed grammatically?  [فَحَبِطَتْ أَعْمَٰلُهُمْ · grammar]
- 18:105/q07/p1 The fāʾ makes the nullification a consequence of their disbelief; the earlier aʿmālan specifies the kind of loss. — Alusi explicitly calls the fāʾ consequential. Ibn Atiyya analyzes aʿmālan in the preceding description as accusative tamyiz. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 18:105/q08 Do deeds cancel each other, and can sinners' good deeds still count?  [فَحَبِطَتْ أَعْمَٰلُهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether adverse deeds remove the good from the reckoning or merely outweigh good that remains counted.
- 18:105/q08/p1 Deeds can be abated or canceled by opposing deeds or sins. — Jishumi takes nullification as proof that deeds cancel one another. Razi reports the Mu'tazilite inference of abatement and expiation, referring detail to al-Baqara. His Qadi says when sins outweigh obedience, that obedience is treated as nonexistent and not weighed; Mawridi reports sins erasing all weight. [4 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي,المعتزلة); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:105/q08/p2 Good deeds still count but are outweighed by godlessness. — Asad quotes Qadi Iyad through Razi and cites 99:7. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Al-Qadi ‘Iyad)]

## 18:105/q09 What does assigning them no weight on Judgment Day mean?  [فَلَا نُقِيمُ لَهُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ وَزْنًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether weight names personal worth, an actual scale and its contents, or a public weighing of the people themselves; accounts also differ on whether no good deeds remain or sins counterbalance them.
- 18:105/q09/p1 They have no worth, rank, honor, or regard before God. — Baqhawi, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Arabi through Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Ashur, and others appeal to Arabic usage of weight for status. Wahidi prefers this even though reports picture men on scales; the immense glutton lacks value without monotheism. Mawridi gives sins and ignorance or folly as causes of contempt and lightness. Baydawi and Bursevi link disdain to erased good deeds. Alusi prefers it to a literal absent scale, arguing the latter repeats nullification and rejecting the charge that this is Mu'tazilite; he says contempt's dependence on void works should not be overlooked. Ibn Ashur calls the disregarded, unweighed thing a sustained metaphor; heavy scales indicate approval through abundant good works. Biqai adds that no one can object or intercede without leave. Islahi sees the humbling of mockers who thought themselves superior. [23 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأعرابي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(author؛ أبو العباس عن ابن الأعرابي)+]
- 18:105/q09/p2 No scales are set up for them, because their works have no surviving good to weigh. — Kashshaf and Razi say scales are for monotheists with good and bad deeds. Wahidi reports that only righteous deeds joined to monotheism are weighed, and that unbelievers' good sides are light. Baydawi, Bursevi, and Alusi connect absent scales to deeds made void or dust; Alusi prefers the contempt interpretation because this repeats the prior clause. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:105/q09/p3 They have no good deeds or reward on the scale, though their evil remains for punishment. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi say a person without good deeds is bound for Hell; Mawridi says unbelief voids good but evil deeds are weighed against them. Ibn al-Jawzi reports that only obedience weighs and an unbeliever has none. Ibn Kathir calls their scales empty of good; Tabatabai says the heaviness of good is what the judgment scale measures, citing 7:8-9. Study Quran says deeds without faith have no weight; Yahya compares 23:103. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:105/q09/p4 Their sins erase their weight until nothing balances them. — Mawridi gives this as a separate explanation; Razi's Qadi similarly says excess sins make obedience as though nonexistent, on the doctrine of abatement. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 18:105/q09/p5 Scales are not erected in their favor because the weighing goes against them. — Ibn al-Anbari gives this third interpretation through Ibn al-Jawzi. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]
- 18:105/q09/p6 The persons themselves may be weighed and publicly shown lighter than an atom or gnat's wing. — Ibn al-Anbari through Wahidi says the literal weighing humiliates them before all creatures. Wahidi prefers an idiom about deeds and status instead; reports of huge men illustrate the apparent reading. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); WAHIDI-BASIT(author؛ أبو العباس عن ابن الأعرابي)-]

## 18:105/q10 How is 'we establish' read, and what is the subject of the alternative verbs?  [نُقِيمُ وَزْنًۭا · readings]
turns on: The prefix consonant and vowel, whether the verb is transitive, and whether weight is an object, subject, or specification.
- 18:105/q10/p1 Nuqīmu, first-person plural, with accusative waznan. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, and Samin call this the majority or common reading; Qurtubi calls its nūn majestic. Ibn Ashur says it may continue the earlier divine voice or shift from 'their Lord.' [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، عبيد بن عمير، ابن محيصن، يعقوب); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:105/q10/p2 Yuqīmu, third-person singular: God, or His signs, establishes no weight. — Kashshaf reports the yāʾ variant; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi attribute it to Mujahid, Ibn al-Jawzi to Ibn Masʿud and al-Jahdari; Abu Hayyan also reports it. Samin takes the pronoun back to God's signs, and Alusi says their immediate mention motivates the reading. The referent of the singular subject varies. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، عبيد بن عمير، ابن محيصن، يعقوب); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 18:105/q10/p3 Yaqūmu, intransitive, with nominative wazn as its subject: no weight stands for them. — Ibn Atiyya attributes it to Ubayd ibn Umayr; Qurtubi likewise, while Samin names Mujahid and Alusi names Mujahid, Ibn Muhaysin, and Yaqub. Abu Hayyan reports these third-person or intransitive forms. Ibn Atiyya also mentions a separate Mujahid yāʾ reading of 'yaqūlu lahum' in the surrounding passage. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); QURTUBI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، عبيد بن عمير، ابن محيصن، يعقوب); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 18:105/q10/p4 Yaqūmu with accusative waznan, construed as transitive or with deeds as the subject. — Samin attributes this to Ubayd ibn Umayr and prefers making their deeds or effort the subject, with weight circumstantial or specification, over making qāma transitive. Alusi notes a prefix fatḥa reading treating qāma as transitive. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); SAMIN-DURR(عبيد بن عمير)+]

## 18:105/q11 What wording and grammatical ellipsis shape the denial of weight?  [رَبِّهِمْ نُقِيمُ وَزْنًۭا · rhetoric]
- 18:105/q11/p1 The wording can shift divine person, and 'weight' implies weight that benefits them. — Ibn Ashur says 'their Lord' is either an overt noun in place of a pronoun or expected wording, depending on the prior reading of 'shall We inform you'; the nūn is correspondingly ordinary or a shift. Itqan says an omitted adjective after 'weight' means beneficial weight. Ibn Ashur says Arabic weight conveys merit without explicitly naming deeds. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN]

## 18:105/q12 What reports illustrate a person or deeds having no weight?  [فَلَا نُقِيمُ لَهُمْ يَوْمَ ٱلْقِيَٰمَةِ وَزْنًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different subjects and measures: a person's body, enormous deeds, a grain, a particular Qurashi, or personified deeds.
- 18:105/q12/p1 A large, tall, or gluttonous man weighs less than a mosquito's wing or gnat's wing. — Abu Hurayra transmits the Prophetic example through Thalabi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Durr, and Kur'an Yolu; wording varies in size, appetite, and drink. Uthman, Ka'b, and Umayr voice similar comparisons. Qurtubi says Ubayd's version is supported by a sound report in Bukhari and Muslim; Ibn Kathir specifies Bukhari and a second Muslim route. Durr mentions 101:8. Abu Hayyan combines this and the Tihama example; Bursevi ties the man's lightness to unbelief and lack of worth. [17 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة,عثمان بن عفان); MAWARDI-FULL(كعب); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون عن أبي هريرة عن النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأعرابي); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبيد بن عمير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,كعب); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,عمير); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber)]
- 18:105/q12/p2 People bring deeds thought as great as the mountains of Tihama, but those deeds weigh nothing. — Abu Saʿid reports this through Thalabi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, and Qurtubi; Abu Hayyan repeats it. Their estimate of the deeds' size does not make them weighty. [5 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 18:105/q12/p3 A man is lighter than a grain or seed, and even a mosquito's wing. — Wahidi reports Kaʿb ibn Ujra and Ubayd ibn Umayr testing a man against a grain and then a wing; Durr's Kaʿb version varies the grain as wheat or barley. Ibn Kathir gives a separate report of an enormous eater and drinker not outweighing a seed, transmitted by Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Jarir. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(كعب بن عجرة؛ عبيد بن عمير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(كعب بن عجرة)]
- 18:105/q12/p4 A finely clothed Qurashi man struts but receives no weight at resurrection. — Ibn Kathir transmits Burayda's report and notes al-Bazzar's grading: its narrators Wasil and Awn are weak and unsupported. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بريدة)]
- 18:105/q12/p5 An unbeliever's deeds appear foul and hideous; he rides them to God and receives no weight. — Durr reports this from an unnamed transmitter. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(راوٍ غير مسمى)]

## 18:105/q13 What do the corpulence reports teach about indulgence?  [وَزْنًۭا · law]
- 18:105/q13/p1 Deliberately acquired obesity through luxury and overeating is forbidden excess. — Qurtubi distinguishes indulgence beyond sufficiency and weight gain from ordinary body size; gluttony and unchecked desire make one serve the self and incline to unlawful behavior. He cites a Prophetic report calling a corpulent scholar most hated by God and Imran ibn Husayn's warning about later witnesses, betrayal, broken vows, and corpulence. The large-man weight report supports his inference. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمران بن حصين)]

## 18:105/q14 What does Uthman's report say is the worst usury?  [وَزْنًۭا · reports]
- 18:105/q14/p1 Insulting or slandering a Muslim brother is the worst usury. — Thalabi's two Uthman reports vary between insulting a Muslim and slandering one's Muslim brother; one says usury has seventy gates and its least is like incest, then adds the glutton's gnat-wing weight. [2 notes: THALABI(عثمان بن عفان)]

## 18:105/q15 What determines a person's and deeds' true weight in an inward reading?  [وَزْنًۭا · inward]
- 18:105/q15/p1 Sincerity gives both person and deeds their weight; without it neither has value. — Bursevi reports the Najmiyya interpretation and connects worthless deeds to the scattered dust of 25:23. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 18:105/q16 How does this passage fit the surah's larger argument?  [* · links]
- 18:105/q16/p1 It concludes the surah's account of worldly delusion and prepares its final declaration. — Tabatabai says these six verses draw together seduction by the world, reliance on other protectors, spiritual blindness, and bad outcomes, leading to 18:110. Islahi says they humble those who mocked revelation and thought themselves superior, ending in insignificance and Hell. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:105/q17 How is the surah's rhetorical force recognized, including shifts of cadence?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These are complementary answers about rhetorical effect and how one develops the judgment to recognize it.
- 18:105/q17/p1 Cadence shifts renew attention and heighten beauty and feeling. — Bint al-Shati presents Abduh's account of changes in rhyme and cadence sharpening the listener's ear and the reader's response. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 18:105/q17/p2 Cultivated experience of eloquent Arabic is needed to judge the Quran's rhetoric. — Abduh says rhetorical taste is learned through studying and using fine speech, and favors Asrar al-Balagha and Dalail al-Ijaz for linking principles to felt responses. Bint al-Shati says rules arise from eloquent speech and cannot replace reading, memorizing, and understanding it; poetry and prose cultivate judgment, while the Quran's effect on Arabs and capable non-Arabs is evidence of eloquence addressing reason and feeling. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(محمد عبده)]

## 18:105/q18 How can the preceding phrase 'those whose efforts were lost' be parsed?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic relation of the preceding description to its context.
- 18:105/q18/p1 As nominative, answering the preceding question. — Kashshaf gives this as one grammatical possibility. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 18:105/q18/p2 As accusative, expressing censure. — Kashshaf gives this as another grammatical possibility. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 18:105/q18/p3 As genitive, in apposition. — Kashshaf gives this as a third grammatical possibility. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 18:105/q19 How does the following verse grammatically present their recompense?  [* · grammar]
- 18:105/q19/p1 The demonstrative points to abandonment of weighing, with recompense predicated of it and Hell appositional. — Ibn Atiyya also takes mā in bimā kafarū as verbal and huzʾ as mockery. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
