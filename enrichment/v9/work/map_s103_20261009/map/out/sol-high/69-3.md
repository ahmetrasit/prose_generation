# 69:3: 18 questions

## 69:3/q01 How are the first mā and adrāka construed?  [وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the analysis foregrounds the nominal interrogative clause or the underlying prepositional construction of darā.
- 69:3/q01/p1 The first interrogative mā is the subject and adrāka its predicate; the addressee is its first object. — Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, Bursawī, and Elmalılı give the subject–predicate analysis. Elmalılı identifies -ka as first object. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the expression a fixed idiom with causative adrā, explains the shifting object roles, and says the following interrogative suspends its governance; he notes grammarians' inadequate or omitted treatment. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:3/q01/p2 The causative verb derives from darā, normally construed with bi; its objects can therefore have different constructions. — Abū Ḥayyān says the preposition bi is sometimes omitted and the causative hamza changes the verb's argument structure; he applies this to the following clause. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 69:3/q02 What does 'what made you know' ask?  [وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the question seeks knowledge of the event's nature or what will occur in it.
- 69:3/q02/p1 It asks what could inform the hearer of the Reality and its nature. — Al-Ṭabarī addresses Muhammad; al-Zajjāj asks what thing could inform the hearer; Bursawī derives adrāka from knowledge and glosses it 'what informed you, Muhammad.' Al-Nasafī asks what could inform one of its nature. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; BURSEVI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 69:3/q02/p2 It asks what will happen at the Resurrection. — Al-Māwardī transmits this reading from Yaḥyā ibn Sallām. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام)]

## 69:3/q03 Who is addressed by the singular 'you'?  [أَدْرَىٰكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the grammatical singular is specifically Muhammad or a general audience addressed through him.
- 69:3/q03/p1 The Prophet Muhammad is directly addressed. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, and Bursawī speak of the Prophet as the addressee; Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Baghawī emphasize that he had not witnessed the terrors. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني)]
- 69:3/q03/p2 The singular address is generic, or reaches hearers through the Prophet. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the addressee is indefinite and the masculine singular is generic; Bursawī allows that hearers are addressed through the Prophet. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 69:3/q04 Does the formula 'what made you know' imply an explanation will follow?  [وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether this instance is read through the reported Quran-wide distinction between adrāka and yudrīka, or as an unanswered question about inconceivable reality.
- 69:3/q04/p1 Yes: Qur'anic 'what made you know' is followed by teaching, unlike 'what makes or could make you know.' — Al-Ṭabarī transmits Sufyān's contrast and cites 33:63; al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī transmit Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, and al-Qurṭubī also Sufyān ibn ʿUyaynah. Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes the report to Ibn ʿAbbās, Sufyān, and Yaḥyā, qualifies it 'if sound,' and cites 33:63 and 42:17; he says al-Rāghib's observation clarifies it, citing 101:10–11, 97:2–3, and 82:18–19. Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests Ibn ʿAbbās meant that God informed the Prophet, while the contrasting form withholds knowledge. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة,يحيى بن سلام); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 69:3/q04/p2 This rhetorical question receives no answer because the ultimate reality exceeds anticipation or imagination. — Asad explicitly links the absence of an answer to the event's inconceivability. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 69:3/q05 What effect does the question produce?  [وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes emphasize different features of the interrogative: awe, a jolt to attention, reproach, or an unfamiliar name.
- 69:3/q05/p1 It magnifies the Resurrection, evokes its terror, and conveys that its full reality exceeds imagination. — Muqātil points to its severity; al-Ṭabarī reports Qatādah; al-Qushayrī stresses gravity; al-Wāḥidī's language scholars say the Prophet knows it by description but is addressed as though he did not; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah invokes unknown horrors and details; al-Qurṭubī combines grandeur with incomplete knowledge. Al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Asad, Iṣlāḥī, and Study Quran stress the limits of conception. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains an association between asking about extraordinary things and their greatness, and offers a denial-of-knowledge reading with the same awe. Bursawī says every imagined state falls short; Study Quran compares 101:2–3; Iṣlāḥī asks what will befall stubborn deniers. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 69:3/q05/p2 Its abrupt, ambiguous wording jolts an indifferent listener into attention. — Iṣlāḥī says the truncated expression focuses attention on the opening subject through uncertainty, and compares the startling address in al-Qāriʿah, where he promises further explanation. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 69:3/q05/p3 The question also reproves the listener. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah joins reproach to the evocation of terrors beyond knowledge. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 69:3/q05/p4 It introduces a name unfamiliar to the Prophet and his people. — Al-Māwardī reports al-Aṣamm's observation that al-Ḥāqqah was unfamiliar in their speech. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأصم)]

## 69:3/q06 How much can anyone know of the Reality before witnessing it?  [وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is the distinction between reported knowledge and sight, or the general inability of creatures to encompass the event.
- 69:3/q06/p1 Its occurrence can be known by divine description or report, but its full nature and terrors cannot be comprehended before they are seen. — Al-Wāḥidī's language scholars distinguish knowledge by description from direct knowledge; al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī cite the Prophet's lack of witness. Al-Biqāʿī says the past-tense formula leaves imagination open. Bursawī says the Prophet knew it would occur but not its manner; Elmalılı says divine report and faith disclose it beforehand without encompassing it, whereas occurrence will disclose it fully. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 69:3/q06/p2 No creature can grasp or describe its true magnitude or nature. — Al-Nasafī stresses the Hour's greatness and severity; Bayḍāwī calls its true nature beyond anyone's grasp; Abū Ḥayyān says its ordeals exceed description; Ālūsī says every estimate falls short; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its extraordinary nature defeats imagination; Asad calls it beyond anticipation. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 69:3/q07 How is the second mā al-Ḥāqqah parsed?  [مَا ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · grammar]
turns on: Which term functions as the nominal predicate, and how the interrogative blocks governance by the preceding verb.
- 69:3/q07/p1 It is a nominal question with interrogative mā as subject and al-Ḥāqqah as predicate, referring back to the first mention. — Al-Naḥḥās describes subject and predicate and a predicate about the first al-Ḥāqqah; al-Wāḥidī identifies the second noun as predicate and referring to the first. Al-Thaʿlabī allows the first al-Ḥāqqah to be subject with its predicate following. Bursawī reports nested nominal clauses linked by the repeated noun. [4 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; THALABI; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 69:3/q07/p2 The interrogative mā is the predicate, making the Reality a wondrous, terrifying subject of inquiry. — Bursawī expressly prefers this analysis over the reported nested-clause analysis. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]
- 69:3/q07/p3 The second mā is nominative because an interrogative is not governed by the preceding adrāka. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī state the rule; al-Wāḥidī glosses the resulting sense as 'What could make you know what it is?' Ibn ʿĀshūr says the third mā suspends adrā from governing its objects. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:3/q08 What syntactic role does the second question have after adrāka?  [أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the interrogative clause fills a direct object slot or depends on an elided preposition.
- 69:3/q08/p1 The question 'what is al-Ḥāqqah?' supplies adrāka's second object. — Al-Nasafī calls it a clause in the second-object slot; Bursawī and Elmalılı say the same. Ibn ʿĀshūr discusses the verb's object structure and suspension by the interrogative. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:3/q08/p2 The clause is accusative after an omitted preposition, suspended as an indirect question rather than governed directly as the second object. — Abū Ḥayyān derives the construction from darā with bi and its causative form. Ālūsī explicitly rejects direct second-object governance in favor of the omitted-preposition analysis. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:3/q09 What does al-Ḥāqqah refer to here?  [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether al-Ḥāqqah is explained as the Day of Resurrection, its disclosure of truth, or an inward manifestation.
- 69:3/q09/p1 It is the Resurrection, the Hour, or the Day of Judgment. — Al-Jishumī reports al-Aṣamm's identification; al-Durr transmits Qatādah, who adds that it makes each worker realize his deeds; Bursawī calls it a name for the Resurrection; Study Quran says the surah is named for the Day of Judgment in its opening; al-Thaʿlabī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatādah identifying the coming calamity with the Resurrection. [6 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم,الكسائي); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; THALABI]
- 69:3/q09/p2 It is the ultimate laying-bare of truth, an overwhelming and inevitable reality. — Asad renders it the laying-bare of truth and stresses awe and the limits of human conception; Study Quran calls ḥāqqah an emphatic form of ḥaqq, truth or reality, which none can avoid. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN]
- 69:3/q09/p3 Inwardly, it is the absolute singular manifestation in unity that annihilates distinctions under the One, the Subduer. — Bursawī transmits al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyyah: this manifestation is true because established in itself; it invokes 40:16. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 69:3/q10 Why is the event named al-Ḥāqqah?  [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · meaning]
turns on: The named event is linked to certainty, fulfilled divine truth, disclosed deeds and recompense, the overpowering of unbelievers, or inward self-establishment.
- 69:3/q10/p1 Its arrival is established, due, inevitable, and truly beyond denial. — Bursawī derives the name from ḥaqq, 'to be due and established,' citing 20:15. Al-Jishumī reports Ibn al-Anbārī's 'inevitable event' and other glosses of a true event without denial; Study Quran explains its emphatic derivation from ḥaqq as an unavoidable reality. [4 notes: BURSEVI; JISHUMI(ابن الأنباري,الكسائي); STUDYQURAN]
- 69:3/q10/p2 God's promise and threat become true at the Resurrection, so it is the Day of truth. — Al-Jishumī reports Abū Muslim on the fulfillment of promise and threat and al-Kisāʾī's 'Day of truth.' [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,الكسائي)]
- 69:3/q10/p3 The realities of deeds are disclosed and their recompense becomes due there. — Al-Jishumī reports Ibn ʿAbbās on deeds becoming clear and Ibn al-Anbārī on each person's necessary recompense; Bursawī reports al-Rāghib's figurative attribution of the name to the day on which recompense becomes due; Qatādah says workers realize their deeds. [4 notes: JISHUMI(ابن الأنباري,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 69:3/q10/p4 The Day overpowers the unbelievers. — Al-Jishumī lists this among explanations of the name alongside the Day of truth and an undeniable true event. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الكسائي)]
- 69:3/q10/p5 In its inward sense, it is true because the singular manifestation is established in itself. — Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyyah, transmitted by Bursawī, applies the name to the One's manifestation and cites 40:16. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 69:3/q11 Why repeat al-Ḥāqqah in the question instead of using a pronoun?  [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · rhetoric]
- 69:3/q11/p1 The explicit repetition magnifies the event and evokes grandeur, wonder, or terror. — Al-Naḥḥās says it adds grandeur and emphasis; al-Māwardī, al-Qushayrī, and al-Wāḥidī emphasize exaltation. Al-Wāḥidī reports Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm's comparison with 101:1–2 and cites Imruʾ al-Qays's poetry for awe conveyed by an interrogative. Al-Thaʿlabī describes wonder, compares asking 'What is Zayd?', and mentions 101:1 and 56:27. Bursawī says explicit noun for pronoun underscores majesty and terror and that the question asks its state and character. Study Quran compares 101:2–3 and says repetition exceeds rational comprehension. [9 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(امرؤ القيس,صاحب النظم); THALABI; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 69:3/q12 How does this question join the preceding clause?  [* · grammar]
- 69:3/q12/p1 The question as a whole coordinates with the preceding short clause, 'What is al-Ḥāqqah?' — Ālūsī explicitly gives coordination; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sentence may coordinate with the prior question. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:3/q13 How does the opening connect to the preceding surah?  [* · links]
- 69:3/q13/p1 It develops the preceding warning by describing Resurrection, its rewards and punishments, and destroyed peoples as warnings. — Al-Jishumī links it to al-Qalam's Resurrection warning and command to endure abuse. Abū Ḥayyān links it to the previous surah's fortunate, wretched, and deniers, cites 68:44, and says past peoples warn the Meccans. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 69:3/q14 What calamity did Thamūd and ʿĀd deny in the following verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether their denial concerns the messengers' threatened historical calamity or the Resurrection.
- 69:3/q14/p1 The calamity promised through their messengers, which struck them. — Al-Thaʿlabī gives this account before reporting the alternative identification with the Resurrection. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 69:3/q14/p2 The Resurrection, named the Striker in the following passage. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatādah for the identification; Ibn ʿĀshūr proposes it if the clauses are connected and says 'the Striker' replaces a pronoun to disclose the event's terrifying force. [2 notes: THALABI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:3/q15 What is the Striker in the following verse, and why name it?  [* · links]
- 69:3/q15/p1 The Striker is the Hour, synonymous with al-Ḥāqqah; naming it exposes its terrifying force. — Al-Durr transmits Qatādah's identification of al-Qāriʿah with the Hour. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the explicit synonym replaces a pronoun to explain its terror when the clauses are connected. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:3/q16 How do deniers react to the reported event?  [* · interpretation]
- 69:3/q16/p1 Because reason cannot grasp it, they dismiss divine report as superstition, fable, poetry, invention, or deception. — Elmalılı describes these dismissals by those who reject the transmitted report of Resurrection. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 69:3/q17 When was this surah revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 69:3/q17/p1 It is Meccan, specifically dated by some to the late Meccan period shortly after al-Mulk. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it Meccan; Study Quran describes the late Meccan dating and placement shortly after al-Mulk. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 69:3/q18 What merit is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah?  [* · sciences]
- 69:3/q18/p1 The reciter will receive an easy reckoning from God. — Al-Jishumī transmits the report from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb from the Prophet. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي بن كعب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
