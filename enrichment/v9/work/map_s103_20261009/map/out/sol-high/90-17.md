# 90:17: 27 questions

## 90:17/q01 How does this clause connect grammatically with the earlier failed ascent?  [ثُمَّ كَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the earlier negative or an implied question governs this clause, and whether an ellipsis is supplied.
- 90:17/q01/p1 It is coordinated with 'he did not cross the ascent,' so the rebuke also denies his faith. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān attach it to 90:11; al-Ālūsī prefers the negative reading, saying the repeated negation singles out faith as the noblest part of the ascent, not something outside it. Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise infer that the rebuked man neither crossed nor believed; al-Zajjāj says the second clause shows his unbelief. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 90:17/q01/p2 The earlier lā may be read as a prayer, an urging question, or with an omitted interrogative. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya records denial, exhortation, and prayer as readings of 90:11. Al-Ālūsī attributes alternatives to Ibn Zayd, al-Jubbāʾī and Abū Muslim and objects on grammatical grounds; grammarians criticize a line by ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa used for omitted interrogative hamza. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد والجبائي وأبو مسلم,عمر بن أبي ربيعة); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 90:17/q01/p3 The phrase can be supplied as 'then his being among the believers.' — Al-Ṭabrisī compares 3:86, where he understands an omitted conjunction before 'they testified.' [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 90:17/q02 Why is one negative lā used across the coordinated ascent clauses?  [ثُمَّ كَانَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether customary repetition is required.
- 90:17/q02/p1 One lā can cover the connected clauses; repetition is usual but not obligatory. — Al-Farrāʾ says the single negation covers freeing, feeding, and believing though Arabic commonly repeats it, comparing 75:31. Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī says instances of repetition do not prove a rule; al-Ālūsī cites 25:67 and poetry via al-Ṭībī and allows omission. [3 notes: FARRA; ALUSI(أبو علي الفارسي); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي عن أبي علي الفارسي)]
- 90:17/q02/p2 A repeated negative is grammatically expected. — Al-Ālūsī reports al-Zajjāj's objection, which al-Fārisī answers; al-Farrāʾ describes repetition as normal usage. [3 notes: FARRA; ALUSI(أبو علي الفارسي); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي عن أبي علي الفارسي)]

## 90:17/q03 What does thumma convey between the good deeds and faith?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether thumma orders time, rank, expression, conjunction, or the believer's final state; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ makes social deeds a prior ground of faith.
- 90:17/q03/p1 It marks faith's higher rank, not later time. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burhān, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Quṭb and al-Sāmarrāʾī say faith precedes or validates deeds. Al-Ālūsī adds that faith alone can save before duties fall due. Quṭb says faith grounds action in a stable divine way rather than whim or praise. Al-Qāshānī and al-Bursawī present the rise from generosity/chastity to wisdom, patience/courage and mercy/justice. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports but rejects this detachment of faith from social deeds. [30 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان,الزمخشري); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو حيان,الزمخشري); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT+; BINTSHATI-; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 90:17/q03/p2 It orders the mention or wording, while faith already existed when the deeds were done. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī cite a poem that names a son's eminence before his father and grandfather though the elders attained it first. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Itqān and al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise distinguish report order from event order; the Ḥamāsa commentator says thumma can delay words but need not delay clauses. [13 notes: NAHHAS; HAMASA; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 90:17/q03/p3 Here it functions like 'and,' joining faith to freeing and feeding without temporal order. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī and al-Itqān report this reading. Al-Jishumī adds that faith encompasses obedience. Al-Burhān argues that a rank reading removes the perceived need to replace thumma with 'and.' [12 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN; NAHHAS; BURHAN-; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 90:17/q03/p4 It can mark later faith after deeds performed before conversion. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Biqāʿī allow a beneficent pre-Islamic person to believe later; the Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām report supports the reception of earlier good. Al-Biqāʿī describes a later second ascent that purifies reason. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers real sequence, making emancipation and feeding prerequisites for sincere social faith. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]
- 90:17/q03/p5 It can indicate the person's final state: he remained faithful until death. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn make dying in faith a condition for earlier deeds' benefit; al-Qurṭubī compares 20:82. [11 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 90:17/q03/p6 It marks a real sequence in which freeing and feeding establish the social ground for faith. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says a society accepting slavery and starvation cannot sustain genuine faith; she objects to readings that separate belief from resisting tyranny and neglect of the poor. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ argues that thumma's plain sense marks order with delay and that non-temporal readings strain the passage; she connects the sequence to 107:1–2, where rejecting Judgment appears in repelling the orphan and failing to urge feeding the poor. [6 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; BINTSHATI+; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان,الزمخشري)-]

## 90:17/q04 What is the role of faith in the acceptance of the earlier deeds?  [ءَامَنُوا۟ · theology]
turns on: Whether faith must accompany deeds, can validate good from before conversion, or includes confidence in the deeds' value.
- 90:17/q04/p1 Faith is required for freeing and feeding to count as a successful ascent or receive reward. — Muqātil says duties without faith do not secure acceptance of charity and expiation. Al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and others say unbelief nullifies reward; al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran cite 9:54. Ibn Kathīr adds inward belief, intention for God's reward, 17:19 and 16:97. Quṭb says faith connects action to a divine norm. Al-Ālūsī adds that faith alone can save before obligations arise. [37 notes: MUQATIL; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الزمخشري)]
- 90:17/q04/p2 If the person later accepts Islam, earlier good done for God can come forward with that faith. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr invoke the Prophet's reply to Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the deeds are praised and rewarded as though done in Islam; al-Biqāʿī says later faith embraces them. Al-Qurṭubī separately denies benefit when belief arrives merely after spending, appealing to 9:54. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(حكيم بن حزام عن النبي); RAZI(بعضهم,حكيم بن حزام); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(حكيم بن حزام,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(حكيم بن حزام عن النبي); BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR(حكيم بن حزام); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hakîm b. Hizâm); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 90:17/q04/p3 Belief here can mean conviction that the good deeds are beneficial and necessary before God. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the view that the doer believes the deeds will benefit him with God. Kur'an Yolu allows belief in their value and necessity alongside religious faith. [3 notes: QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 90:17/q04/p4 Emancipation and compassion for the hungry are necessary prior steps for genuine faith; an enslaver or one callous to an orphan or poor person lacks that faith. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reads thumma as a real sequence and compares 107:1–2, where denial of Judgment is shown by repelling an orphan and not urging food for the poor. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]

## 90:17/q05 What does being 'among those who believed' add to the description?  [كَانَ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase emphasizes duration, communal identity, or the content of faith.
- 90:17/q05/p1 It means steadfast, continuing faith rather than a passing act of belief. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Nasafī explain kāna as persistence; al-Qushayrī stresses one's state at life's end. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī say the believer performs the deeds and remains faithful until death. [8 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 90:17/q05/p2 It identifies a person with the established community of believers and intensifies praise. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says membership conveys firmer socially recognized faith than simply calling him a believer, comparing 2:67; mentioning the group prepares the praise of mutual counsel and the people of the right. [7 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 90:17/q05/p3 Its unstated object is God alone, Muhammad's messengerhood, and Islam. — Muqātil enumerates God, angels, scriptures, messengers, Paradise and Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verb's object is omitted as understood and the verb functions without an explicit object; Corpus Coranicum compares 69:33. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 90:17/q06 Who exemplifies the believers described here?  [مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the text is illustrated by exemplary Companions or read as a rebuke of the boastful man.
- 90:17/q06/p1 The foremost Companions, including the four caliphs, are examples. — Al-Wāḥidī names Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf; al-Rāzī says the leading Companions including four caliphs were patient in religion and merciful to people. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 90:17/q06/p2 Abū Bakr is the exemplary case, including his noble conduct before conversion. — Al-Biqāʿī ranks him immediately after prophets, citing Ibn al-Dughunnah's praise of kinship, hospitality, helping the burdened and distressed, Khadīja's similar reassurance to the Prophet, his swift conversion, his response at Ḥudaybiyya matching the Prophet's words, and his refusal of wine to preserve reason. [11 notes: BIQAI(أبو بكر,أبو بكر رضي الله عنه,ابن الدغنة,خديجة رضي الله عنها); BIQAI-FULL]
- 90:17/q06/p3 It addresses the boastful spender, contrasting his failure with the believers' path. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies the man who boasted of spending much; al-Nasafī says he failed to thank the Benefactor by freeing, feeding and believing. Ibn ʿĀshūr says kāna implies the rebuked man lacked faith; al-Iṣlāḥī says the failure shows unovercome moral and spiritual obstacles. [6 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NASAFI; IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 90:17/q07 What does tawāṣaw require of believers?  [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether emphasis falls on mutual support, public moral guidance, or the counselors' own practice.
- 90:17/q07/p1 They advise and strengthen one another in good, sharing responsibility for patience and mercy. — Al-Qurṭubī glosses it as mutual advice. Al-Rāzī says believers must guide others to truth and turn them from evil; al-Ālūsī says counsel perfects others after one's own good works. Al-Biqāʿī says patience needs cooperation because it is bitter. Quṭb says joint encouragement prevents defeat, discouragement and panic without erasing individual responsibility. Elmalılı says even those unable to free or feed can console others. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects speech against injustice with God-given lips, 90:9. [16 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 90:17/q07/p2 The wording also implies that the counselors themselves practice the mercy they commend. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says those who know its value and recommend it first act on it, connecting the point with 89:18 and the mercy among believers in 48:29. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 90:17/q08 What patience do believers urge one another to have?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which demand for self-command is emphasized: worship, faith, abstention, trial, or the hardships surrounding the ascent.
- 90:17/q08/p1 Patience in obeying God and carrying out His obligations. — Al-Ḥasan and Tustarī say obedience; Hishām ibn Ḥassān, Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil specify obligations and Quranic commands. Al-Biqāʿī includes enjoining right even when dangerous. Al-Iṣlāḥī relates it to persevering against inner desire on the steep climb. [40 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI(الحسن,هشام بن حسان); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن,هشام بن حسان); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(هشام بن حسان); DURR-FULL(هشام بن حسان); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 90:17/q08/p2 Steadfastness in faith itself. — Al-Zamakhsharī presents this as one gloss and elsewhere joins it with doing duties; al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and al-Sāmarrāʾī include persistence in belief. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]
- 90:17/q08/p3 Restraint from sin and worldly desires. — Al-Māwardī separately suggests resisting worldly desire; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes both desire and sin, while al-Zajjāj, al-Zamakhsharī and many later authors combine abstention with obedience. [23 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]
- 90:17/q08/p4 Endurance of trials, calamities, hostile people and divine decree. — Sufyān focuses on what befalls people; al-Ṭabarī says trials for God's sake; Ibn Kathīr singles out people's harm. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds God's decree, while Quṭb and Study Quran stress opposition and communal hardship. [22 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI(سفيان); MAWARDI-FULL(سفيان); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 90:17/q08/p5 Endurance in the hard work of the ascent and life in this world. — Al-Māwardī mentions patience with worldly life. Al-Iṣlāḥī says ṣabr is steadfast effort against the soul's desires; al-Sāmarrāʾī lists choosing the path, freeing slaves, famine, feeding orphans and the destitute, raising children and the Messenger's Meccan abuse. [7 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 90:17/q09 What does marḥama mean, and to whom does it extend?  [بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether marḥama denotes human compassion, particular recipients or kin, acts attracting divine mercy, or the merciful next life.
- 90:17/q09/p1 Compassion and merciful action toward people and other creatures, including fellow believers. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly says all people; Tustarī says creatures, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya says mutual care sustains human life. Al-Māwardī includes one another and everyone. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and others give the broad gloss. Al-Bursawī says it is wider than pity for only visibly deserving people. [40 notes: TUSTARI; TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(آخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 90:17/q09/p2 It especially calls for care of the poor, weak, oppressed, and other vulnerable people. — Al-Wāḥidī names orphans, poor and weak; al-Jishumī names needy believers, relatives and oppressed people. Al-Rāzī includes helping the oppressed and preventing imminent wrongdoing. Al-Ṭabrisī extends it from poor and needy to all. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to feeding and 89:18. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 90:17/q09/p3 It includes maintaining ties of kinship. — Muqātil explicitly defines it as preserving family ties and not cutting them; al-Jishumī lists relatives among mercy's recipients. [2 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI]
- 90:17/q09/p4 It means doing or urging whatever brings God's mercy. — Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this breadth; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī offer it alongside compassion to creatures. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]
- 90:17/q09/p5 It refers to the Hereafter, the abode of mercy, and turning away from worldly life toward it. — Al-Māwardī records this alternative, also preserved in his full account. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 90:17/q10 What does the form marḥama contribute?  [ٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · meaning]
- 90:17/q10/p1 It is a verbal noun for mercy; its form expresses a wide, encompassing compassion. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a verbal noun. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says marḥama is broader than raḥma and observes the mafʿala pattern shared with famine, kinship, destitution, and the right and left groups. [3 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 90:17/q11 Why repeat the verb and preposition for each mutual counsel?  [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · rhetoric]
- 90:17/q11/p1 The repetition emphasizes each virtue separately and strengthens the call. — Al-Naḥḥās notes the added emphasis. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says repeating both verb and preposition is stronger than only the preposition or a simple conjunction; al-Biqāʿī says the repeat highlights how few practice mercy. [5 notes: NAHHAS; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]

## 90:17/q12 Why is patience mentioned before mercy?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · rhetoric]
- 90:17/q12/p1 The ascent's preceding toil calls first for patience, then its freeing and feeding call for mercy. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī points to the two paths, hardship and difficult climb before the vulnerable recipients of feeding; al-Biqāʿī makes mercy the fruit of endurance and justice, a further ascent moderating excess and deficiency. Al-Iṣlāḥī says sympathy needs patience because the good work opposes desire. [5 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 90:17/q13 Why single out patience and mercy as the believers' paired virtues?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pair is read as religious foundations, cardinal virtues, a shared social ethic, or a contrast with the idolaters.
- 90:17/q13/p1 They gather duty toward God and care for creation, the foundations of obedience. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī describe patience as honoring God's command and mercy as compassion for creatures; cited investigators call sincerity with God and good character with people the foundations of Sufism. Ibn ʿĀshūr says all good deeds need restraint of desire and mercy sustains the community, comparing 48:29. [9 notes: RAZI(بعض المحققين); RAZI-FULL(بعض المحققين); BURSEVI; ALUSI(بعض المحققين); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 90:17/q13/p2 They complete a progression of four cardinal virtues: generosity or chastity, faith as wisdom, patience as courage, and mercy as justice. — Al-Qāshānī says courage depends on certainty and each named trait stands for its broader virtue; al-Bursawī quotes a teacher who calls feeding in hardship supreme restraint, faith wisdom, patience courage and mercy justice. Al-Biqāʿī calls faith wisdom's second ascent, courage the third and mercy a fourth balanced pass. [12 notes: QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 90:17/q13/p3 They define a community that resists injustice with steadfastness and merciful solidarity. — Quṭb stresses collective mutual support alongside personal judgment; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says genuine faith bears struggle for good and refusal to stay silent about injustice. Kur'an Yolu identifies slavery, poverty and mercilessness as the era's grave problems; these duties are examples of Islamic social ethics, not an exhaustive list. [8 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 90:17/q13/p4 Their praise exposes the idolaters' lack of patience and compassion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the indirect rebuke is explicit in passages on calling to God, patience, honoring orphans and feeding the poor: 41:33, 41:35, 89:17 and 89:18. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 90:17/q14 How does this verse complete the account of crossing the obstacle?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the ascent is a set of acts requiring faith, a moral progression toward it, or a socially grounded sequence.
- 90:17/q14/p1 The earlier freeing and feeding are joined by faith, patience, and mercy as the complete route. — Al-Farrāʾ counts the three parts under one negative. Al-Iṣlāḥī says bodily faculties and moral awareness in 8–17 should overcome greed and serve God. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the Qur'an itself defines the obstacle, rejecting a need for allegorical readings. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the explicit deeds already replace the usual phrase 'righteous deeds' after faith; patience and mercy follow the difficult works. [7 notes: FARRA; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:17/q14/p2 The preceding social acts can show innate purity before faith, which then purifies and elevates the person. — Al-Biqāʿī says noble pre-conversion conduct shows high aspiration and freedom from vanity; the convert accepts guidance despite becoming a follower after being followed. He links insight to 20:54 and 20:128 and says faith brings illumination and duties contrary to nature. [10 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 90:17/q14/p3 The verse makes an organized society of mercy and justice the setting for sincere faith. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reads the order literally: dignity through emancipation and feeding comes before faith and its communal counsels; a society tolerating enslavement and hunger cannot sustain sincere belief. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects this social reading with 107:1–2: denial of Judgment appears in turning away an orphan and failing to urge feeding the poor. [5 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+; BINTSHATI+]
- 90:17/q14/p4 Failure to undertake the deeds and join believers shows that the moral ascent remains uncrossed. — Al-Nasafī describes failure to thank the Benefactor; al-Iṣlāḥī says the person has not overcome the barrier to moral and spiritual growth. [3 notes: NASAFI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 90:17/q15 How does patience and mercy echo the rest of this sura?  [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · links]
turns on: Whether the close is read as integral thematic development or, in Corpus Coranicum, a secondary stanza.
- 90:17/q15/p1 The sura's toil, parenthood, persecution, ascent, slavery, hunger, and poverty all call for patience and mercy. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī relates the parent and child oath to the patience of raising and protecting children; the Prophet's Meccan abuse to patience; and his mercy at the conquest to the city's reference. The boastful spender, enslaved person, famine, orphan and destitute person call for compassion. He finds five threads: toil, breadth of meaning, collective action, patience and mercy. [11 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:17/q15/p2 The verse extends the sura's collective pattern. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī connects parents and offspring, paired faculties and paths, multiple ascent duties, mutual advice and the gathered unbelievers; he says 'labad' contributes to the same pattern, unlike a plain word for many, comparing 80:20 and 76:3. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 90:17/q15/p3 The close shifts the ethical demand into the opposed destinies of saved and damned. — Corpus Coranicum calls 17–20 probably secondary: it extends the ethical demand into group identities amid conflict around Muhammad, continues the rhyme of 11–16 and completes a chiastic 4+6+6+4 pattern. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 90:17/q16 How does this verse relate to Sūrat al-ʿAṣr and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighboring or parallel passage is being used to explain the verse.
- 90:17/q16/p1 It resembles 103:3 in joining faith with mutual exhortation. — Corpus Coranicum calls 103:3 likely secondary. Al-Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran connect perseverance here with 103:1–3. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-ʿAṣr begins with truth because its loss is general and truth directs patience; al-Balad focuses on one part of truth, mercy and feeding, so its patience comes first. [6 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف)]
- 90:17/q16/p2 The sura answers the neglect of orphans and poor condemned in al-Fajr. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Fajr 89:15–18's rich and poor and condemnation of neglect with al-Balad's expenditure, poverty and commanded feeding. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:17/q16/p3 It anticipates the people of the right in the following verse. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī links belief, counsel, emancipation and feeding to blessing, happiness and right-hand records; Ibn al-Jawzī points to 56:7–8 and 10:46, while al-Qurṭubī records explanations involving right-side records, felicity, Adam's right side and a right-side station before summing them as Paradise's people. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب ويحيى بن سلام وابن زيد وميمون بن مهران); IBNASHUR]
- 90:17/q16/p4 The description of believers leads to the promise for the people of the right. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says naming believers prepares both praise of their mutual counsel and the glad tidings in the next verse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 90:17/q16/p5 It parallels Sūrat al-Māʿūn: denial of Judgment shows in repelling an orphan and failing to urge feeding the poor. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 107:1–2 to support her reading that emancipation and compassion precede genuine faith. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 90:17/q17 What do the pre-Islamic good-deed reports say about this verse?  [كَانَ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · reports]
turns on: The cited reports distinguish earlier good carried into conversion from earlier good without faith at death.
- 90:17/q17/p1 The Prophet told Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām that he entered Islam with good already done. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu cite his question about earlier charity, manumission and kinship. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the report sound, glosses ḥinath as worship and says the old deeds receive reward as though done in Islam; al-Biqāʿī's full note phrases the Prophet's saying in the first person. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(حكيم بن حزام عن النبي); RAZI(حكيم بن حزام); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(حكيم بن حزام); QURTUBI-FULL(حكيم بن حزام عن النبي); BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR(حكيم بن حزام); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hakîm b. Hizâm)]
- 90:17/q17/p2 The Prophet said Ibn Judʿān's earlier generosity would not benefit him because he never sought forgiveness for Judgment Day. — ʿĀʾisha asks about his care for relatives and needy people, hospitality, feeding, emancipation and freeing captives. Al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr use the reply when discussing deeds without faith. [4 notes: QURTUBI(عائشة); QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة عن النبي); IBNASHUR(عائشة); IBNASHUR-FULL(عائشة)]

## 90:17/q18 Which Prophetic sayings support the call to mercy?  [بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · reports]

- 90:17/q18/p1 The Merciful shows mercy to those who show mercy on earth. — Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran cite the instruction to show mercy to those on earth so those above show mercy; Ibn Kathīr's full account attributes it to the Prophet. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN]

## 90:17/q19 What does the report about Islam and faith say?  [ءَامَنُوا۟ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ · reports]
- 90:17/q19/p1 The Prophet defined Islam by patience and generosity, and faith by good speech and feeding. — Tustarī also identifies the best speech as remembrance of God. [2 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 90:17/q20 How is the earlier reading of 'freeing a neck' supported by this clause?  [ثُمَّ كَانَ · readings]
- 90:17/q20/p1 Abū ʿAmr ibn al-ʿAlāʾ prefers fakka raqabah because the crossing must occur as a believer. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports that 'then he was among believers' coordinates with the act of crossing; conversion only afterward would leave an unbeliever's earlier crossing without benefit. ## 90:17/q21 How do inward readings understand the obstacle and its virtues?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which inner difficulty is called the obstacle.
- 90:17/q21/p1 The obstacle is the self: liberate it from servitude to creatures and occupy it in worship of God. — Al-Bursawī reports al-Qāsim's inward gloss. [1 notes: BURSEVI(القاسم)]
- 90:17/q21/p2 The afterlife obstacle demands restraint from forbidden and doubtful food, taking only what preserves life. — Al-Bursawī reports al-Muḥāsibī's teaching. [1 notes: BURSEVI(المحاسبي)]
- 90:17/q21/p3 The ascent of faith purifies the mind and frees it from vanity before patience and mercy complete character. — Al-Biqāʿī reads earlier noble traits as preparation, faith as intellectual purification and courage and mercy as successive moral passes. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 90:17/q22 What additional points are made about the poem cited for thumma?  [ثُمَّ · meaning]
- 90:17/q22/p1 Its 'then' can order words without necessarily placing whole clauses later in time. — The Ḥamāsa commentator applies this directly to 90:17 and to 90:12–16, saying faith is not postponed until after the listed deeds. The poem praises courage in deadly danger; 'son of a free woman' invokes pure ancestry, ghamāʾ denotes covering, ghawāshī refers to sword grips or scabbards versus blades, qisma stands for muqāsama, and sharr is an accusative verbal noun. [6 notes: HAMASA]

## 90:17/q23 What does the cited grammar discussion say about kāna and sequence elsewhere?  [كَانَ · grammar]
- 90:17/q23/p1 The cited uses can express continuing divine attributes or a relation ordered without simple elapsed time. — Al-Itqān records the difficulty that a past-tense 'was merciful' could imply mercy before recipients or its absence now. It reports a conjunction reading, order of report, sequence between two creations, khalaqa meaning 'decreed,' al-Kirmānī's continuing attributes whose effects occur by will, and Ibn ʿAbbās's kāna as permanence. [7 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس,الشمس الكرماني)]

## 90:17/q24 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Balad?  [* · sciences]

## 90:17/q25 What does the visiting orator report say about believers?  [مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · reports]

## 90:17/q26 What forms of mercy fit the believers' conduct?  [بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · interpretation]
- 90:17/q26/p1 Mercy should be cultivated in ways suited to time and place, with love and aversion for God's sake. — Al-Biqāʿī says believers cultivate great mercy and orient themselves toward what brings divine love; his fuller account pairs this with faith's purification from ostentation and self-admiration.
