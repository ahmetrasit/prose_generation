[ELMALILI:2:283/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A pledge is permissible while resident too, as shown by the Prophet pledging his coat of mail in a settled state.
[ELMALILI:2:283/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | When writing can secure a debt, requesting a pledge between believers is permissible but not recommended unless there is an excuse.
[ELMALILI:2:283/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Taking possession is required to complete a pledge; an unpossessed pledge gives no security, and an undivided fractional share cannot properly be pledged.
[ELMALILI:2:283/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Writing, witnessing, and pledging are the three means of securing debts and transactions; faith and piety should encourage these protections of people’s rights and lawful wealth.
[ELMALILI:2:283/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Securing lawful wealth enables lawful earning and spending in God’s path and helps avoid usury and similar sins so that piety can continue.
[ELMALILI:2:283/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The strongest assurance for human rights is obedience to God through faith and piety; without it, secondary safeguards have limited benefit.
