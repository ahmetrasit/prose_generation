21:18/q12 Who receives the closing address and threat? [وَلَكُمُ ٱلْوَيْلُ · referent] (turns on: Whether direct address continues the present opponents or shifts to another group in the surrounding narrative.)
  21:18/q12/p1 It addresses the unbelievers or polytheists being argued against, especially Quraysh or Arab unbelievers. — reasons: Alusi explicitly identifies Quraysh or all Arab unbelievers and says they face the fate of earlier communities; he says the direct address plainly returns to those just mentioned. Tabari, Bagawi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and others locate the threat among opponents describing God falsely.
  21:18/q12/p2 The address turns to those denying the messengers and calling the Quran magic or confused dreams. — reasons: Razi gives this audience; Abu Hayyan reports it but calls the proposed shift from third person to direct address remote. Ibn Ashur connects it to allegations against Muhammad and the Quran in 21:3 and 21:5.
  21:18/q12/p3 The address shifts to the former inhabitants of ruined towns. — reasons: Alusi reports and rejects this as far-fetched because it relies on their earlier plea.
21:18/q13 What descriptions bring woe? [مِمَّا تَصِفُونَ · referent] (turns on: The referent of 'what you describe' in the immediate argument: divine attributes, lies and shirk, accusations about revelation, or other beliefs.)
  21:18/q13/p1 Attributing to God a child, spouse, partner or other unfitting qualities. — reasons: Qatada specifies angels called daughters; Muqatil specifies Jesus as son; Tabari includes wife and child and says lies and shirk both fit, while nearest wording favors this reading. Zajjaj, Wahidi citing Abu Ishaq, Bagawi, Tabrisi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi and Alusi agree on false divine attributes.
  21:18/q13/p2 Their lies generally, including untrue descriptions of God. — reasons: Tabari cites Qatada on lies; Wahidi cites Mujahid with 6:139; Bagawi repeats Mujahid; Durr cites Hasan that every liar is liable. Study Quran lists lies as one reading; Tabari says lying and shirk can both fit.
  21:18/q13/p3 Their idolatry and attribution of partners to God. — reasons: Tabari cites Ibn Jurayj and his comparison with 21:22 and Mujahid on 6:139; Study Quran cites 7:190, 23:92 and 27:63.
  21:18/q13/p4 Their accusations about the Prophet or Quran, such as magic and confused dreams. — reasons: Razi and Abu Hayyan mention rejection of the Messenger; Ibn Ashur links allegations to 21:3 and 21:5. Study Quran reports a reading tied to 21:3–5; Bursevi includes these accusations as well as claims about God.
  21:18/q13/p5 Their claim that the world is sport and that polytheistic intercessors will save them. — reasons: Islahi says idolaters expected their deities to rescue them in the hereafter and therefore felt no fear.
  21:18/q13/p6 Defining God as immanent in creation. — reasons: Asad says describing also means defining Him; he cites 6:100 and argues such definition of God's Being is erroneous.
21:18/q14 How widely does the warning apply? [وَلَكُمُ ٱلْوَيْلُ مِمَّا تَصِفُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the specific address also states a standing rule for later false descriptions.)
  21:18/q14/p1 It extends to anyone making a false description until the Resurrection, including false claims about God's attributes or rulings. — reasons: Wahidi reports Hasan and applies it to attributes and rulings; Durr reports Hasan; Biqai extends it to descriptions of Quran, God and world made on whim without divine warrant because people lack full grasp of realities.
  21:18/q14/p2 The warning specifically condemns the contemporary unbelievers' false assertions. — reasons: Wahidi and Bagawi describe their unfitting attribution; Alusi identifies the immediate addressees; Tabataba'i identifies deniers of resurrection and prophethood.
21:18/q15 What lasting relation between truth and falsehood does the verse teach? [* · theology] (turns on: The notes distinguish an unrestricted metaphysical rule from the specific means of its enactment.)
  21:18/q15/p1 Truth has real stability and ultimately overcomes transient falsehood throughout belief, practice and creation. — reasons: Tabataba'i says falsehood only resembles truth, like mirage water; its deficiencies depend on a more complete reality and it exists by God's permission. He invokes 13:17, 24:25, 2:147 and 38:27, calls God absolute truth, and says the imperfect verb signals God's continuing practice. Kuranyolu says falsehood cannot remain in truth's presence; the Sadiq reports generalize the rule. Study Quran points to 17:81, 8:8, 34:49 and 42:24.
  21:18/q15/p2 God's truth overcomes falsehood through revealed proof, warning, destruction, discernment or force against wrongdoers. — reasons: Ibn Ashur enumerates these means and cites 8:12; Tabrisi says God who manifests truth by proofs cannot play falsehood; Ibn Kathir says He makes truth plain and erases falsehood.
21:18/q16 Why is falsehood given time before truth defeats it? [* · theology]
  21:18/q16/p1 Respite tests human choice and does not put good and evil on equal footing; God then makes truth prevail. — reasons: Islahi states the trial of those choosing truth or clinging to evil; Tabataba'i says God gives falsehood time but preserves true belief, perfection and prophetic victory despite few followers or strong opposition.
21:18/q17 What does the verse imply about human knowledge and freedom? [* · theology] (turns on: These are two distinct doctrinal implications drawn by Jishumi, rather than rival readings.)
  21:18/q17/p1 It refutes necessary knowledge, since universal compulsion to know would leave no disagreement. — reasons: Jishumi argues that if knowledge were necessary everyone would form one community.
  21:18/q17/p2 It refutes determinism, since sending prophets presupposes that people can move from unbelief to faith. — reasons: Jishumi draws this conclusion from prophetic missions.
21:18/q18 Must every era contain someone who speaks the truth? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether the verse itself establishes continuous witnesses or that continuity requires separate transmitted proof.)
  21:18/q18/p1 Yes: each era has some witnesses for truth, even if few. — reasons: Abu Ali reads broad wording as allowing this despite predominating unbelief. The Qadi says the argument works only if the existence of such a person in every era is established, which it has not been.
  21:18/q18/p2 The verse permits an era with no truth-speaker, though transmitted Islamic law rules that out. — reasons: Jishumi says consensus is transmitted proof that no era lacks affirmers of truth; the verse alone permits all people to be unbelievers.
21:18/q19 How does the verse connect to other passages and the sura's argument? [* · links] (turns on: The connections differ by neighboring argument versus verbal and thematic parallels elsewhere.)
  21:18/q19/p1 It follows the denial of purposeless creation and supports the surrounding argument for God's wisdom and oneness. — reasons: Islahi says a wise, just Creator will bring a day when truth wins; multiple independent gods would undo the universe, all prophets taught monotheism, and angels are servants rather than daughters or autonomous intercessors. Jishumi links 21:7 on human messengers and creation by idols, and 21:17 on child and partner claims. Abu Muslim links good overcoming evil to 21:18.
  21:18/q19/p2 Other verses echo the disappearance of falsehood and the punishment of false description. — reasons: Wahidi compares 17:81 and Mujahid's gloss of 6:139; Tabari compares 21:22 and 6:139; Ibn Ashur cites 13:17, 9:55 and 17:81; Study Quran compares 17:81, 8:8, 34:49, 42:24 and the sura's close at 21:112.
21:18/q20 What reports apply the truth's victory more broadly? [* · reports]
  21:18/q20/p1 Al-Sadiq is reported to say truth defeats falsehood whenever they meet, and reaches every person. — reasons: Yunus transmits the first report through al-Mahasin; Ayyub ibn al-Hurr transmits the second, saying truth breaks the heart of one who accepts or leaves it. Tabataba'i says both generalize the verse.
21:18/q21 What inward readings are drawn from truth overcoming falsehood? [* · inward] (turns on: The degree of inward defeat: moral acts, attributes, or all created selfhood.)
  21:18/q21/p1 Admonition or divine command defeats sin and forbidden acts. — reasons: Sulami twice transmits Wasiti's admonition of spiritual elders; Mawardi reports contemplatives' admonitions against sins. Bursevi cites Najm al-Din's first level, divine commands against forbidden acts, with 28:88 and 17:81.
  21:18/q21/p2 Manifestation of divine attributes or Essence effaces servant attributes and created essences. — reasons: Bursevi cites Najm al-Din's second and third levels. He reports Maghribi's explanation of ana al-haqq as an ecstatic utterance after the speaker's false self vanishes in manifestation; Maghribi's verse calls the Real the servant's defender, and Khujandi's verses link the secret to self-annihilation at the gallows and a costly, lofty insight.
