[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | The passage's purpose is to rebuke heedlessness and rivalry, not to promote grave visitation; this resolves the tension with the following prohibition.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r21] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The warning can mean that rivalry deludes people until their souls leave and their bodies enter the graves.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r22] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The rebuke warns people not to waste worldly life in rivalry; they will learn the gravity and outcome of their heedlessness later.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Visitors may recite Qur'an and dedicate its reward, while seeking Allah's will and preparing in complete faith for their own death.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The visit should prompt reflection on resurrection, judgment, the scales, and the destinations of deeds, strengthening resolve for faith and righteous action over rivalry and accumulation. | mentions 101:4, 101:5, 101:6, 101:7, 101:8, 101:9, 101:10, 101:11
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The dead have returned or been returned to Allah and enter their final states according to their standing before Him.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A prophetic report says a person will be resurrected with those he loves, so those who love the good join the good and those who love evil join the evil.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Love is valuable when it is for Allah and follows the command to follow the Prophet. | mentions 2:165, 3:31
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The dead have passed on: their deeds are theirs and yours are yours, so boasting or competing over them has no point. | mentions 2:141
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Worship and seeking aid belong to Allah; helping people for Allah is praised, while begging people is blameworthy, and asking the dead is especially unfitting.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Ilhā derives from lahw, whose root sense is heedlessness; it means idly occupying and diverting people from their work and duty.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Übey b. Ka‘b | reports | Ubay reports that the saying about a person with two valleys of wealth longing for a third, and being filled only by earth, was once recited as Quran until this surah came; God accepts the repentance of the penitent.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Abdullah b. Şihhir | reports | He reports that the Prophet explained a person's real wealth as what he gives away, consumes, or wears out; Tirmidhi grades the report hasan and sahih.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Cerîr | reports | Ibn Jarir infers from the Prophet's recitation followed by the saying that wealth rivalry is intended by takathur.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Some exegetes understand rivalry in numbers from the occasion reports and others rivalry in wealth from the prophetic report; combining both best fits the verse's unrestricted wording and 57:20. | mentions 57:20
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Takāthur is rivalry and pride in having more, a delusive worldly contest in numbers or abundance.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The verse leaves both the object of distraction and the kind of abundance unstated, giving the wording comprehensive rhetorical scope; context still supplies qualifications.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | In context, distraction means neglecting duties and useful remembrance, knowledge, reflection, gratitude, worship, and obedience; takathur means worldly abundance useless at the Scale and leaving only loss and punishment. | mentions 57:20
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Abbas | reports | Ibn Abbas explains the takathur in this context as competition in wealth and children. | mentions 57:20
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The definite article on takathur points to a known occasion of revelation, with reports describing tribal boasts over which group had more people.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Many exegetes report that Banu Abd Manaf and Banu Sahm rivaled over their numbers, then counted or invoked their dead; the surah was revealed in response.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ebû Büreyde | reports | Abu Burayda reports that the Ansari clans Banu Harith and Banu Hars listed their living notables, then went to the graves and compared their dead before the verse was revealed.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The reports both describe boasting by numerical comparison with the dead, but only the second has the rivals physically visit graves; it also suggests a Medinan setting, unlike the famous Meccan classification.
[QUTB-ZILAL:102:1/r1] QUTB-ZILAL, d. 1386 · author | holds | The Meccan surah sounds like a grave warning calling to heedless, intoxicated people who compete in wealth, children, and worldly honors while nearing death, deceived about what follows. | mentions 102:2
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r1] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · سعيد بن أبي هلال | reports | Al-Alusi reports from Ibn Abi Hatim, from Saʿid ibn Abi Hilal, that the Prophet's Companions called this chapter al-Maqbarah.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r10] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبو بريدة الجرمي | reports | Abu Buraydah al-Jarmi reports that the Ansari clans Banu Harithah and Banu al-Harith boasted of living members, then pointed to their dead in graves, prompting revelation.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r11] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | On the Meccan chronology, this is the sixteenth chapter revealed, after al-Kawthar and before al-Maʿun; it has eight verses.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r12] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The chapter rebukes neglect of Qur'anic guidance for wealth, accumulation, and ancestral boasting through death; it warns, urges reflection on salvation, and reminds them of resurrection and accountability for gratitude.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r13] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | ألهاكم means it occupied and diverted you from what you ought to attend to; amusement is an occupation that blocks an important aim.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r14] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | التكاثر is competing to accumulate something valued in quantity, such as wealth, children, or allies used for prestige. | mentions 34:35
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r15] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The word includes rivalry in wealth and in numbers of children or allies, as people boast that their wealth and children protect them from punishment. | mentions 34:35
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r16] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿsha's verse contrasts being outnumbered with the fact that prestige belongs to the larger group, illustrating takathur as competition in numbers.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r17] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن الشخير | reports | Ibn al-Shikhkhir reports the Prophet reciting the chapter and explaining that a person's wealth is only what he consumes, wears out, or gives away in charity.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r18] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The hadith offers one meaning of competing over possessions that the verse can bear, prompted by that moment's admonition.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r19] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The address targets polytheists, especially their affluent leaders, because of the severe threats and because such conduct was not a Muslim trait then. | mentions 102:3, 102:6
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r2] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Most Qur'an codices and commentaries call it Surat al-Takathur; Tirmidhi uses that title, as do some old Qayrawan codices, while other codices and Bukhari call it Surat Alhakum.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r20] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The intended addressees are wealthy chiefs who chose luxury over hearing Muhammad's call and obstructed ordinary people; the unspoken object of diversion is plainly Qur'an, reflection, and fair acceptance. | mentions 102:8
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r21] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The object of ألهاكم is omitted because its reference to the Qur'an, reflection, and accepting its truth is evident; the past tense presents their distraction as accomplished.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r22] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Although the rebuke addresses polytheists, believers too must avoid love of wealth distracting them from good and heed the risk of sudden death; another verse warns them about worldly rivalry. | mentions 57:20
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r3] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | reports | Most authorities regard the chapter as Meccan, and Ibn ʿAtiyya says he knows no disagreement; Ibn ʿAbbas, al-Kalbi, and Muqatil report a dispute between Quraysh clans as its occasion.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r4] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · ابن عباس والكلبي ومقاتل | reports | Ibn ʿAbbas, al-Kalbi, and Muqatil say the chapter arose from a boastful competition between Banu ʿAbd Manaf and Banu Sahm in Mecca.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r5] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · السيوطي | reports | Al-Suyuti's preferred view in al-Itqan is that the chapter is Medinan, citing a report about Ansari clans and a hadith from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r6] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · أبي بن كعب | reports | Ubayy reports the Prophet's saying that Adam's child would want a second valley of gold, be filled only by earth, and can repent; Ubayy says they thought this saying Qur'an until the chapter descended.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r7] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | rejects | Ibn ʿAshur says Ubayy's report does not establish a Medinan date: we could mean Muslims generally, who stopped treating the saying as Qur'an after clarification.
[IBNASHUR:102:1/r8] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | prefers | He prefers a Meccan date because the chapter's themes and severe threats suit a group of polytheists
