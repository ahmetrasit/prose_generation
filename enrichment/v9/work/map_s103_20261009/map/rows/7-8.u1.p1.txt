7:8/q10 How does the weighing relate to the preceding questioning and later judgment? [ٱلْوَزْنُ يَوْمَئِذٍ · links]
  7:8/q10/p1 It follows the questioning and recounting of deeds, making their exact consequences manifest. — reasons: Al-Razi places it after questioning and accounting. Biqai connects it to God's 'We' in the prior verse. Ibn Ashur ties it to 7:7 and makes the conditional clauses its outcome; the Study Quran compares weighing with questioning in 7:6. Ibn al-Jawzi says God reports what messengers and nations did while never absent from them.
  7:8/q10/p2 It accords with other passages on just balances and revealed records. — reasons: Tha'labi and Wahidi cite 21:47; Ibn Kathir cites 21:47, 4:40, 101:6–11, and 23:101–103. The Study Quran connects judgment balances to honest commercial weights and a balanced creation. Ibn Abbas says the placed record speaks of people's deeds.
7:8/q11 What does the plural مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ count? [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the plural names instruments, their parts or instances, or the objects being weighed.)
  7:8/q11/p1 It denotes multiple actual balances, one per person, act, kind of worship, or kind of deed. — reasons: Hasan reports one balance per person; Razi prefers several on the plain plural and 21:47, perhaps for heart, bodily, and spoken acts. Tabrisi cites prayer as a balance; Elmalili totals each person's many scales. Ibn Atiyya rejects a separate balance per person and holds one instrument.
  7:8/q11/p2 It is one balance expressed plurally for persons, parts, pans, or repeated weighings. — reasons: Tha'labi cites al-A'sha's plural 'breasts' and 'forearms' for singular parts, and lists the assembly's pans, beam, and tongue. Ibn Jawzi and Ibn al-Anbari relate the plural to generic man and following plural pronouns. Abu Hayyan reports one balance for numerous objects; Qurtubi compares generic plural travel by ships or mules. Alusi proposes omitted 'pans'.
  7:8/q11/p3 It is plural for things weighed, especially a person's many deeds, rather than instruments. — reasons: Tha'labi, Zajjaj as reported by Razi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, and others give this reading; Ibn Atiyya says plural highlights numerous acts. Tabatabai asks how such an account fixes their relative weight.
7:8/q12 What makes someone's مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ heavy? [ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether heaviness is a numerical preponderance of acts or their truth, quality, and special worth.)
  7:8/q12/p1 Many good deeds or good deeds prevailing over bad make them heavy. — reasons: Mujahid glosses heavy scales as good deeds. Muqatil says even an atom can tip them. Mawardi records both being judged obedient and good acts exceeding bad. Ibn Abbas says one extra good deed suffices; a grain can tip it, though he allows equality. Razi elsewhere denies an equal-total class. Elmalili compares credits and debts. Bursawi states that the weighing distinguishes good from bad and heavy from light; Jabir's report says the prevailing side determines Paradise or Hell, though Tabatabai accepts its content without making it an interpretation of this verse.
  7:8/q12/p2 The truth, sincerity, and inner reality of a deed give it weight; evil and ostentation lack it. — reasons: Tabatabai reads each act against its proper truth, citing 23:102–103 and 101:6–10. Ibn Ashur contrasts fighting for God's word and for booty. Ali says inward substance outweighing outward appearance makes a heavy balance. Qashani says lasting virtues and sincere intentions weigh, while passing desires do not.
  7:8/q12/p3 Certain small but valuable acts outweigh large apparent burdens. — reasons: The ninety-nine-scroll hadith gives a card with the two testimonies; al-Qadi says testimony needs its duties. Al-Qurtubi reports a different card for prayers upon the Prophet, and Alusi records debate whether the testimony is a later good deed or final monotheistic words. Prophetic reports call good character the heaviest thing; another report describes taught knowledge tipping the scale.
7:8/q13 Can anyone's good and bad deeds be equal, and what follows? [ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the two conditional outcomes exhaust all people, and whether equality constitutes a separate outcome.)
  7:8/q13/p1 There is no class whose good and bad totals are equal. — reasons: Razi divides humanity into heavy and light alone. Tabatabai says equal deeds cannot make a balance both heavy and light, and rejects one use of a Heights report as inconsistent with its surrounding verses.
  7:8/q13/p2 Equal deeds identify the people of al-Aʿrāf, the Heights. — reasons: Ibn Abbas says a single grain may tip a balance but equal totals place a person among the Heights; a report through Jabir also gives this result.
  7:8/q13/p3 A person without any discernible preponderance may have no weighing. — reasons: Tabatabai suggests this for nullified unbelievers and vulnerable people not fully addressed by proof or duty, citing 7:8.
7:8/q14 Whose deeds enter the weighing? [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · referent] (turns on: Whether disbelievers receive a physical weighing or their nullified acts have no weight.)
  7:8/q14/p1 Believers and unbelievers are assessed; believers' good deeds lead to Paradise and unbelievers' lack of good weight to ruin. — reasons: Ibn Abbas describes a believer and disbeliever in the two-pan account. Wahidi Wajiz says heavy scales belong to believers. Razi says light balances are generally unbelievers because they deny God's signs.
  7:8/q14/p2 Scales and records are set up for Muslims; polytheists' nullified deeds have no assigned weight. — reasons: Ali ibn al-Husayn's report has polytheists driven in groups to Hell. Tabatabai cites 18:105 and says unbelief erases deeds; Ibn Ashur says this passage chiefly addresses believers with good and polytheists without good.
7:8/q15 How can a sinful believer be successful yet still enter Hell? [ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · theology] (turns on: Whether success means immediate deliverance, ultimate deliverance, or a case outside this passage.)
  7:8/q15/p1 Monotheism is weighed after other deeds and ultimately makes the scale heavy on release from Hell. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya reports this solution twice, following his statement of the difficulty.
  7:8/q15/p2 Eventual escape from Hell itself counts as success despite temporary punishment. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya gives this answer twice.
  7:8/q15/p3 Mixed sinful believers are outside the immediate purpose of this passage. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the contrast here is between believers who did good and polytheists without good; their case is discussed elsewhere.
7:8/q16 What does ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ mean? [ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · meaning]
  7:8/q16/p1 Those who gain what they seek: salvation, reward, victory, and escape from fear. — reasons: Ibn Abbas gives attainment and escape; Ibn Atiyya cites poetry for aflaḥa as attainment and another verse for survival. Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqai, Bursawi, and Alusi gloss salvation or reward.
7:8/q17 How do مَن, فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ, هُمُ, and ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ work together? [مَن فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ · grammar]
  7:8/q17/p1 Generic singular مَن permits plural demonstrative and pronouns; هُمُ restricts success to that group. — reasons: Bursawi and Ibn Ashur note agreement with the generic plural sense. Ibn Ashur says the demonstrative attributes success to heavy measures and its distance signals evaluative distance; المفلحون can be generic or recall 2:5. Alusi discusses the predicate analyses and its definite, prototypical sense.
7:8/q18 What events or agents are reported at the balance? [ٱلْوَزْنُ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports] (turns on: The differing transmitted descriptions of the balance's location, supervision, announcement, and human experience.)
  7:8/q18/p1 Gabriel oversees the balance and settles claims between people by transfer of good or bad deeds. — reasons: Hudhayfa's report in Tabari, Tha'labi, Ibn Atiyya, and al-Durr says good deeds pass from oppressor to victim; if exhausted, the victim's sins pass to the oppressor, leaving a mountain-like burden.
  7:8/q18/p2 A caller or angel announces each person's lasting happiness or misery when the balance tips. — reasons: Abd Allah ibn al-Ayzar and Anas supply variant transmitters for the announcement.
  7:8/q18/p3 At the balance people are too preoccupied to recognize or remember others. — reasons: Hasan's report through Aisha cites 80:37; Aisha and Qatada place the same distraction also at scattering records and the bridge, with 69:19 cited for books.
  7:8/q18/p4 The balance is placed between two trees near Jerusalem. — reasons: Ka'b supplies this location.
  7:8/q18/p5 A huge balance overwhelms David, and half a date of charity can fill one pan by God's pleasure. — reasons: Alusi relays this tradition as an illustration of divine valuation.
