[QASHANI:6:152-153/r5] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Fulfilling God's covenant means unity, obedience, and all obligations arising from the primordial covenant renewed by a later pledge.
[QASHANI:6:152-153/r6] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The path of virtue and unity is difficult, especially the balanced middle in action; Qashani says these instructions encompass all vices and virtues.
[QASHANI:6:152-153/r7] QASHANI, d. 736 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas calls these decisive verses that no scripture has abrogated; Qashani reports agreement among the scriptural communities and religious schools.
[QASHANI:6:152-153/r8] QASHANI, d. 736 · كعب الأحبار | reports | Ka'b al-Ahbar swears that these were the first words in the Torah.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says the guardian should preserve and increase the orphan's wealth.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Dahhak says to retain profits by trading the property and take nothing from it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r12] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid permits trade; a wealthy guardian should take no expense or fee, while a needy guardian may use a reasonable amount from the trading profit.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r13] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · المروزي | reports | One view permits using the orphan's animals and employing enslaved women so guardians need not mix households.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r14] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | A view allowing only a loan from the orphan is rejected as inconsistent with what is best for the child.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The prohibition on approaching orphan property covers every kind of handling and blocks means to misuse.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | It singles out the orphan because guardians are especially tempted by the wealth and the child has little power to protect it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p687#2/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عباس وابن زيد | reports | They explain best treatment as improving the property and taking a customary amount only when the guardian needs it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The endpoint is semantic rather than syntactic: guard the orphan's wealth until maturity, then hand it over.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الطبري | reports | Al-Tabari says the obligation in measure is the amount humanly possible; perfect accuracy beyond capacity is not required.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the clause may mean duties must stay within human capacity rather than demanding perfect precision in every transaction.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الشعبي وزيد بن أسلم ويحيى بن يعمر وربيعة ومالك | reports | They define the orphan's reaching maturity as puberty; al-Shaʿbi, Rabiʿa, Malik, and Abu Hanifa are also reported to require puberty without established misconduct.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas gives adulthood as ages eighteen to thirty, and elsewhere thirty-three.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن جبير ومقاتل والسدي والثوري وعكرمة وعائشة وأبو العالية | reports | Reported age definitions range from eighteen, twenty-five, thirty, thirty-four, or forty; Abu al-ʿAliya instead describes maturity as sound judgment and full strength, and other reports say fifteen to thirty or sixty.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن الأنباري | reports | Ibn al-Anbari prefers treating ashudd as a singular with no plural form, but Abu Hayyan rejects this because afʿal is not used as a singular noun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Ashudd has five proposed morphological analyses: plural of shidda, plural of shadd, a plural without a matching singular, or a singular without a plural, among them.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The word derives from strength and firmness; another etymology connects it to rising, as in Antar's description of the day rising.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · عنترة | reports | Antar's line uses shadda al-nahār to witness the sense of the sun's height.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p688/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Qist means fairness and exact balance; another view recommends slight over-measure to ensure the duty is certainly fulfilled, supported by a report to add weight.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Fair speech means neither adding nor withholding when a relative is concerned; the speaker's own self, parents, and kin are included. | mentions 4:135
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The command covers matters known only by speech, including judgments, testimony, admonition, and mediation; limiting it to a particular category lacks evidence.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The genitive in God's covenant can mean a covenant God made with people or one people made with God; it may also refer to covenants between people, whose keeping God commands.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الماتريدي | reports | Al-Maturidi interprets God's covenant as His commands and prohibitions regarding lawful and unlawful things.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · التبريزي | reports | Al-Tabrizi takes the covenant as the primordial covenant on the Day of the Covenant.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن الجوزي | reports | Ibn al-Jawzi says the wording includes God's instructions and what people obligate themselves to, such as vows.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The first five duties are clear and end with understanding; the remaining four are subtle and require effortful reflection, so they end with remembrance.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · حفص والأخوان | reports | Hafs and the two Kufan readers lighten the dhāl by dropping one tā from tatadhakkarūn; they differ over which tā is deleted.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p689/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · باقي السبعة | reports | The other canonical readers pronounce the word with a doubled dhāl, assimilating the tāʾ of the form into it.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | الكيل first names the act of measuring and then the measuring vessel; الميزان is the instrument pattern مفعال from weighing, with origin موزان changed like ميقات.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | بالقسط may describe the doer as acting justly or the measures as being made complete and fair.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | He treats الكيل and الميزان as verbal nouns meaning measured goods and proposes omitted nouns for the constructions.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author sees no need for Abu al-Baqa's substitutions because the meaning works directly; الكيل itself can mean the measuring vessel.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الواحدي | reports | He understands الوفاء as completing the weighing, like completing the measure, and proposes an omitted noun with الميزان.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects the omission analysis: the verse can speak directly of filling the measure because الكيل also names the vessel.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | لا نكلف is parenthetical between the commands; ولو كان ذو قربى means whether the person spoken to or spoken about is a relative.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | عهد الله may be a possessive construction where God is the covenanting subject, or where He is its object, as in the cited verses. | mentions 33:23, 48:10
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third reading treats the possessive as explanatory: the covenant is attributed to God because He commands its preservation.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The ending with remembrance suits the four less obvious instructions that need thought to discern fairness; the other five are evident to reason and understanding.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The two reciters and Hafs from Asim lighten تتذكرون by deleting one ت, while the others double it by assimilation; scholars differ which ت is deleted.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:152-153/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | إلا بالتي هي أحسن is an exceptive construction meaning only by the best course; it may be circumstantial or an adjective of an omitted verbal noun, and the comparative urges the best rather than merely good.
