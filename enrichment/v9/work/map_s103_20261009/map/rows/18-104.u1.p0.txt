## THE CURRENT MAP
18:104/q01 How is the relative clause beginning الذين parsed? [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether it modifies the prior noun, starts an independent predication, or is set in the accusative for explanation or blame.)
  18:104/q01/p1 It is genitive, describing or substituting for the preceding 'greatest losers'. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī call it an adjective; al-Samīn adds apposition and explanatory substitution. Al-Ālūsī allows description or substitution; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifically takes it as apposition and says the extended description builds suspense.
  18:104/q01/p2 It is nominative, beginning an implied 'they are those' predication or answering who the losers are. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj permits a new sentence; al-Naḥḥās and al-Wāḥidī supply 'they'; al-Bayḍāwī allows a predicate; al-Ālūsī says it may answer an implied 'who are they?'.
  18:104/q01/p3 It is accusative by an implied 'I mean' or as censure. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās supplies 'I mean'; al-Samīn and al-Bayḍāwī allow blame. Al-Ālūsī says accusative rebuke fits poorly because the clause begins without naming the essential loss.
18:104/q02 Who are those whose efforts went astray? [ٱلَّذِينَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the transmitted identifications name a specific historical group or illustrate a broader pattern; 18:105 and the sūra's Meccan setting are invoked to assess the Ḥarūriyya identification.)
  18:104/q02/p1 They are Jews and Christians, or the People of the Book. — reasons: Yaḥyā names the People of the Book. Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿd are cited for Jews and Christians; Saʿd says Jews rejected Muḥammad and Christians denied food and drink in Paradise, in a report transmitted by Bukhārī. Al-Ālūsī says their worship could follow abrogated rules. Reports from ʿAlī through al-ʿAyyāshī add disbelief and religious innovation and say the river people are near them.
  18:104/q02/p2 They are the people of Ḥarūrāʾ, the Kharijites. — reasons: ʿAlī is reported to give this answer to Ibn al-Kawwāʾ; al-Ḍaḥḥāk also gives it. Saʿd instead calls the Ḥarūriyya covenant breakers and profligates. Al-Ālūsī records an objection from 18:105: Kharijites do not deny resurrection; he says مِن may mark a shared misguidance, or ʿAlī may have considered them unbelievers. He prefers to read ʿAlī's answer as a severe allusion, not precise identification.
  18:104/q02/p3 The description broadly includes anyone who follows an unapproved way while supposing the deed accepted. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr explicitly includes Jews, Christians, Ḥarūriyya, and others: the sūra is Meccan and precedes the relevant Jewish-Christian address and the Khawarij; he compares 88:2–4, 25:23, and 24:39. Al-Ālūsī extends the description broadly to unbelievers, supported by the ʿAyyāshī report. Ibn Kathīr says the deeds lack a lawful, pleasing religious basis.
  18:104/q02/p4 They are disbelievers whose deeds bring no benefit. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj cites 47:1 for God frustrating unbelievers' deeds. Al-Ālūsī's reconciliation extends the referent to unbelievers generally.
  18:104/q02/p5 They are idol worshippers who treat idols as patrons and expect their favor. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage answers reliance on useless idols; Kur'an Yolu says polytheists believe idols bring them near God and that shirk is obedience. Al-Jishumī says worshippers of others exhaust themselves in supposedly beneficial deeds.
  18:104/q02/p6 They are monks devoted to retreat or self-imposed ascetic exercise. — reasons: Al-Baghawī reports confinement to monasteries; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits a report about monks secluded in pillars; al-Ālūsī describes harsh ascetic exercises; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Būrsawī use monks as examples of wasted effort.
  18:104/q02/p7 They are the disbelievers of Mecca or sinful Quraysh. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's Meccan identification; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records ʿAlī's answer to Ibn al-Kawwāʾ as sinful Quraysh.
  18:104/q02/p8 They are Christians specifically. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes a separate report identifying Christians.
  18:104/q02/p9 They are Sabians. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this as a separate identification alongside monks.
  18:104/q02/p10 They include ostentatious people and seekers of reputation or thanks. — reasons: Abū Bakr al-Warrāq says reproaching beneficiaries, demanding thanks, and showy worship destroy good works. Al-Qushayrī mentions show, self-admiration, and reminders of favors; al-Būrsawī calls slight ostentation hidden shirk, citing 39:65; al-Ālūsī names lodge dwellers who attract attention and ensnare wealthy people.
  18:104/q02/p11 They include people who scrutinize and magnify their own deeds, lacking spiritual certainty. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī reports self-important scrutiny of deeds, and says some lack verification, knowledge, and certainty, remain in delusion, and have no weight in the next life.
18:104/q03 What does ضل سعيهم say happened to their effort? [ضَلَّ سَعْيُهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether loss describes the invalidity of their own deeds or the failure of an attempted attack on religion.)
  18:104/q03/p1 Their effort became fruitless, void, and unable to secure its goal. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Suddī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Nasafī, and Abū Ḥayyān express loss or nullity. Ibn ʿĀshūr likens it to a traveler missing the path; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likens it to a mistaken journey or lost capital. Al-Qushayrī and al-Ālūsī say it has no benefit or weight before God.
  18:104/q03/p2 Their attempted destruction of religion failed to achieve its aim. — reasons: Al-Jishumī explicitly offers this as one sense of their lost effort.
18:104/q04 Why is straying attributed to the effort itself? [ضَلَّ سَعْيُهُمْ · rhetoric]
  18:104/q04/p1 It is a figurative attribution: the people themselves went astray in their effort. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr states the application. Al-Jurjānī distinguishes lexical metaphor, which transfers a word's sense through resemblance or association, from rational metaphor, in which the speaker figuratively predicates an action of a subject. His example is spring making or embroidering; the lexicon fixes action and tense, whereas the speaker assigns its agent. He notes that words and scripts are conventional signs across languages.
18:104/q05 What does سعيهم mean here? [سَعْيُهُمْ · meaning]
  18:104/q05/p1 Striving or work, by extension from running or vigorous walking. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr gives vigorous walking as the literal sense and cites 17:19 for metaphorical work. Al-Sāmarrāʾī gives running or brisk walking; al-Wāḥidī calls it effort and work.
18:104/q06 Why does their striving go to waste? [ضَلَّ سَعْيُهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: The notes locate the failure in disbelief and misdirected worship, corruption of motive, or an unwarranted religious path.)
  18:104/q06/p1 Disbelief or worship directed to something besides God leaves the deeds without divine benefit. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj invokes 47:1; al-Qushayrī says they worked for other than God. Al-Jishumī describes worship of others; al-Biqāʿī says they turned from the only one who can benefit or harm them to powerless things. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu describe idol worship; al-Būrsawī says even intrinsically good charity and manumission gain no reward alongside disbelief or hidden association.
  18:104/q06/p2 Ostentation, pursuit of thanks, self-admiration, and reputational display undo deeds. — reasons: Abū Bakr al-Warrāq singles out reminders of favors and demands for thanks. Al-Qushayrī adds self-regard and magnifying deeds. Al-Bayḍāwī combines disbelief and vanity; al-Būrsawī invokes hidden shirk and 39:65; al-Ālūsī applies the warning to lodge dwellers courting public attention.
  18:104/q06/p3 Their deeds fail because their religious way lacks valid warrant or spiritual knowledge. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr says their work is invalid under a lawful, pleasing religious path despite their belief it is accepted. Al-Qushayrī says they act without verification, knowledge, or certainty. Ibn al-Jawzī says leaders knowingly preserve falsehood for status while followers imitate without evidence.
18:104/q07 Does في الحياة الدنيا limit when their effort is lost? [فِى ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ ٱلدُّنْيَا · grammar]
  18:104/q07/p1 It locates their striving in worldly life; the loss also reaches the hereafter. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī attaches the phrase to striving because restricting the loss to this world would be wrong. Al-Būrsawī likewise says the loss is not limited to worldly life.
18:104/q08 How is a feminine ending such as that in الحياة treated in pause and imāla? [ٱلْحَيَوٰةِ · readings]
  18:104/q08/p1 Feminine tāʾ becomes hāʾ at pause, with dialectal and reader-specific imāla in conditioned cases. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī says some Arabs incline it like alif; al-Kisāʾī calls this natural Arabic; al-Dānī calls it a surviving Kufan dialect and al-Akhfash reports it. The author says it is widespread and easier; Sībawayh explains hāʾ as alif-like. He gives al-Kisāʾī's agreed conditioned class of fifteen consonants, mnemonic فَجَثَت زينب لذود شمس, then catalogs examples and counts: fāʾ 21, jīm 8, thāʾ 4, tāʾ 4, zāy 6, yāʾ 64 (including ḥaya), nūn 37, bāʾ 28, lām 45, dhāl 2, wāw 17, dāl 28, shīn 4, mīm 32, and sīn 3.
18:104/q09 What does وهم يحسبون modify? [وَهُمْ يَحْسَبُونَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the mistaken belief is concurrent with the people's straying or with their striving.)
  18:104/q09/p1 It is circumstantial of the people who went astray. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says this makes their misguidance coincide with their mistaken belief and more sharply reveals their error.
  18:104/q09/p2 It is circumstantial of their striving. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative attachment.
18:104/q10 What does يحسبون mean? [يَحْسَبُونَ · meaning]
  18:104/q10/p1 They suppose or think that they act well. — reasons: Al-Jishumī treats ḥisbān and ẓann as near synonyms; in the same lexical list he notes liqāʾ as nearness without separation
