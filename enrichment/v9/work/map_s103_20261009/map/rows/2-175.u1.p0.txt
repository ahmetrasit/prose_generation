## THE CURRENT MAP
2:175/q01 Who are those indicated by 'those'? [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the description is tied to the preceding scripture concealors or applied to hypocrites.)
  2:175/q01/p1 The people who concealed scripture, specifically Jewish scholars or Jews who knew Muhammad's description. — reasons: Muqatil and al-Tabari identify prior belief or knowledge of Muhammad; al-Tabari specifies scholars concealing his prophethood for bribes. Al-Mawardi calls them the previously mentioned Jewish scholars; Abu Hayyan and Ata in al-Durr link them to the preceding concealors and 2:174.
  2:175/q01/p2 The hypocrites whose conduct shows readiness to enter the Fire. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya transmits Qatada, al-Hasan, Ibn Jubayr, and al-Rabi describing the subjects as hypocrites.
2:175/q02 What does their purchase of error and punishment mean? [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ بِٱلْهُدَىٰ وَٱلْعَذَابَ بِٱلْمَغْفِرَةِ · interpretation]
  2:175/q02/p1 They chose error over available guidance and thereby chose punishment over the forgiveness that following guidance would have brought. — reasons: Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, al-Razi, al-Nasafi, and Ibn Kathir specify denial or concealment of Muhammad's scriptural description despite knowledge; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi add worldly profit. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi explain the second exchange as the consequence of the first. Abu Hayyan says buying implies desire and extreme loss; Bint al-Shati notes that bāʾ marks what is surrendered, citing 9:9. Damghani compares purchase in 9:111, where believers sell themselves to God. Al-Biqai says concealment misled others and added their burden; al-Tabatabai says the deed takes embodied form as eating fire and the persistent deed is concealment. Al-Razi contrasts knowledge with ignorance in this world and forgiveness with punishment in the next. Bursawi says the price is metaphorical, and Elmalili calls the acquired error and torment lasting.
2:175/q03 Why is the exchange of punishment for forgiveness stated after the exchange of error for guidance? [وَٱلْعَذَابَ بِٱلْمَغْفِرَةِ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the second exchange restates the first exchange's result or adds a separate charge of knowing disobedience.)
  2:175/q03/p1 It reinforces the preceding exchange by naming its consequence. — reasons: Abu Muslim calls it reinforcement. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi explain that punishment follows chosen error and forgiveness would follow discarded guidance.
  2:175/q03/p2 It adds a distinct charge: they knowingly persisted in disobedience and chose punishment over forgiveness. — reasons: Al-Qadi's explanation is preferred by al-Tabrisi for adding a distinct meaning; al-Razi stresses their prior knowledge of reward and punishment.
2:175/q04 How does the purchase sentence and its following fāʾ connect to the preceding threat? [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ فَمَآ · links] (turns on: The sentence's syntactic relation to the earlier condemnation.)
  2:175/q04/p1 The fāʾ makes their astonishing relation to the Fire follow from their exchange of guidance and forgiveness. — reasons: Al-Wahidi says it answers the preceding description of their trade.
  2:175/q04/p2 The purchase sentence explains the severe threat as an independent answer or as a second predicate about the ugliness of concealment. — reasons: Al-Alusi gives both syntactic connections: the first predicate describes the threat's severity and the second describes concealment's ugliness.
2:175/q05 What does 'how patient they are with the Fire' mean? [فَمَآ أَصْبَرَهُمْ عَلَى ٱلنَّارِ · interpretation] (turns on: The force of mā (exclamation, interrogative rebuke, or negation), whether patience concerns present deeds or future punishment, and whether ṣabr denotes audacity, persistence, knowledge, confinement, or actual endurance.)
  2:175/q05/p1 It marvels at their audacity in doing what leads to the Fire, without claiming they can bear its pain. — reasons: Qatada, al-Hasan, al-Rabi, and others connect boldness to deeds that bring Hell near; several explicitly deny real patience. Al-Farra, al-Kisai, al-Asmai, and others cite a judge's or Bedouin's rebuke mā aṣbaraka ʿalā Allāh for daring against God; al-Thalabi and al-Qurtubi identify Yemeni usage. Al-Tabari prefers this sense from Arabic usage and explains Fire as punishment. Sahl applies it to issuing religious judgments without knowledge of Sunna or law. Al-Qurtubi says conduct leading to Hell is called courage. Al-Biqai likens it to defying a ruler's prison threat. Al-Tabatabai says al-Sadiq's three transmitted explanations converge.
  2:175/q05/p2 It describes their persistence in deeds leading to the Fire, despite knowing their consequence. — reasons: Al-Muarrij says opposition to the Prophet is known to lead there; al-Kisai and Qutrub supply an understood 'deeds of the Fire's people.' Al-Sadiq speaks of doing what they know leads to it. Bursawi takes Fire as its cause and describes the persistence as the object of human wonder. The Qurtubi and Ibn Atiyya reports distinguish this from physical endurance.
  2:175/q05/p3 It is a rebuking question: what made them endure the Fire or pursue its causes after trading truth for falsehood? — reasons: Al-Majaz and Abu Ubayda take mā as 'what'; Ata, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd, and others call it a question. Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash argues from case endings. Al-Mubarrad cites a prohibition on tormenting a living creature and a poem for the sense 'make endure,' but Ibn Atiyya disputes his vocalization; Abu Hayyan argues the causative hamza cannot simply mean ṣabara. Al-Zajjaj uses the analogy of enduring hunger. Some phrase it as asking what drove them to the deeds; al-Qurtubi calls it belittling dismissal, and Kur'an Yolu a forceful warning.
  2:175/q05/p4 It marvels at how long the condemned endure or remain in the Fire itself. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi compare Qur'anic exclamations in 80:17 and 19:38. Ibn Kathir mentions torment and shackles. Ibn Ashur treats certain future suffering as already witnessed, comparing Zuhayr, Malik ibn al-Rayb, and 102:5–6. Abu Hayyan reports a figurative sense of remaining in the Fire.
  2:175/q05/p5 It asks what keeps them confined in the Fire, or how long God keeps them there. — reasons: Al-Mawardi takes ṣabara as confining; al-Tabrisi reports Ibn Abbas asking what keeps them confined. Al-Zajjaj compares asking how long someone bears imprisonment; al-Mubarrad cites a poem and a report about confining a living animal, though Ibn Atiyya challenges his vowel analysis.
  2:175/q05/p6 It marvels at how practiced or knowledgeable they are in the deeds of Hell's people or in falsehood. — reasons: Mujahid's reported expressions vary between ability, expertise, and knowledge; Ibn Qutayba calls the reading good and supplies an omitted 'deeds.' Al-Thalabi calls it sarcasm. Al-Tabari includes expertise within his preferred exclamation.
  2:175/q05/p7 Their heedless pursuit of Hell's causes is described sarcastically as if they accept its consequence. — reasons: Al-Razi says knowingly choosing Hell's cause makes them seem willing to endure its result in this life. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Baydawi, and al-Alusi stress lack of concern. Elmalili contrasts unwillingness to give up worldly pleasure with persistence toward eternal burning; Abu Hayyan likens it to defying a ruler.
  2:175/q05/p8 It says how little they are distressed or grieve over the Fire. — reasons: Both al-Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan report this figurative gloss.
  2:175/q05/p9 It describes their actual future patience when silenced in Hell and deprived of escape. — reasons: Al-Asamm places the patience after the command to remain there, compared by al-Razi with 23:108. Al-Razi and Abu Hayyan reject the temporal shift from present wording and observe that the damned may cry out.
  2:175/q05/p10 It negates endurance: God has not made them patient over the Fire. — reasons: Abu al-Baqa and another report take mā as negative; al-Samin dismisses it.
  2:175/q05/p11 It simply makes their state an object of astonishment. — reasons: Al-Akhfash compares 80:17; al-Tabrisi also cites it and an Arab saying about God's punishment. Abu Hayyan reports that most place the patience in worldly life, whether literal or metaphorical.
2:175/q06 How is mā in 'فما أصبرهم' parsed? [فَمَآ · grammar] (turns on: Whether mā is an exclamatory noun, a question, a relative or described noun requiring an unspoken predicate, or negation.)
  2:175/q06/p1 It is an exclamatory complete indefinite noun in the nominative, subject of a following clause. — reasons: Sibawayh and the majority supply 'something made Zayd beautiful' as a model; al-Wahidi calls it a subject and the following past-tense verb its predicate. He says mā's ambiguity creates surprise where shayʾ would diminish it or state a settled fact. Al-Baydawi cites poetry; al-Razi compares the other Arabic exclamation pattern in 19:38. Al-Tabari and Abu Hayyan cite 80:17, and Abu Hayyan adds 54:31.
  2:175/q06/p2 It is interrogative with exclamatory force or rhetorical denial. — reasons: Al-Farra and Ibn Durustawayh make the next clause its predicate; al-Samin compares 2:28. Al-Razi reports al-Farra's model as denying that anything surpasses Zayd in beauty, with case endings distinguishing this from asking which part of Zayd is best.
  2:175/q06/p3 It is a relative pronoun with an omitted predicate. — reasons: Abu Ubayda and al-Majaz use 'what made them endure'; al-Akhfash analyzes the following clause as its relative. Al-Razi objects that the parallel relative sentence 'the one who made Zayd good' is malformed while the exclamation is complete.
