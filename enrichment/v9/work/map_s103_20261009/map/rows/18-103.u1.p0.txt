## THE CURRENT MAP
18:103/q01 What does the command قُلْ signal, and who carries it out? [قُلْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the command is understood as the Prophet's delivery alone or as also involving believers.)
  18:103/q01/p1 Muhammad is commanded to deliver an important warning to the heedless and unbelievers. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says opening with 'say' marks an utterance worthy of attention; Biqāʿī describes recipients whose eyes and ears fail to heed remembrance.
  18:103/q01/p2 The Prophet and the believers who follow him announce the warning. — reasons: Bursevī extends the command's delivery to believers alongside the Prophet.
18:103/q02 What force does هَلْ have in this question? [هَلْ · rhetoric] (turns on: The notes distinguish an inviting offer, rebuke, ironic offer, and several reported uses of hal.)
  18:103/q02/p1 It is an inviting prompt meaning 'shall we' or 'would you like us to'. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī's lexicon applies the 'alā' use to 18:103 and compares 20:10, 26:221, 34:7, and 61:10.
  18:103/q02/p2 It is a rebuking or emphatic question. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba likens hal to the interrogative hamza, citing 27:60; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this question a taunting rebuke of unbelievers.
  18:103/q02/p3 It offers to inform the hearers ironically, since the disclosure does not depend on their assent. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the speaker will inform them regardless; Ālūsī calls the supposed request for leave deeply sarcastic.
  18:103/q02/p4 In ordinary use, it can be a genuine question. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī's lexicon illustrates this with 7:53, 10:35, 30:28, and 50:30; it does not explicitly select this use for 18:103.
  18:103/q02/p5 It can mean qad, introducing a report. — reasons: The lexicon cites questions introducing Moses, the disputants, Abraham's guests, the human being, and the Overwhelming Event: 20:9, 38:21, 51:24, 76:1, 88:1.
  18:103/q02/p6 It can mean mā, with a negative or exclusive implied answer. — reasons: The lexicon compares 2:210, 6:158, 7:53, 16:35, 43:66, and 55:60.
  18:103/q02/p7 It is said to mean illā here. — reasons: Al-Burhān lists 18:103 as an example of this usage.
18:103/q03 Who is meant by the plural speaker in نُنَبِّئُكُم? [نُنَبِّئُكُم · rhetoric] (turns on: The nūn may be divine majesty, a shared speaker, or a signal about prior knowledge.)
  18:103/q03/p1 It is God's majestic plural, with a shift from the expected third-person 'He informs you'. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this explanation of the plural nūn.
  18:103/q03/p2 It refers to God together with the Messenger. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as one alternative.
  18:103/q03/p3 It refers to the Messenger together with the Muslims. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as the other alternative; Bursevī includes believers among those told to deliver the announcement.
  18:103/q03/p4 The plural either identifies the speaker at once or intimates that believers already know the news. — reasons: Ālūsī gives both rhetorical explanations.
18:103/q04 To whom is the address كُم directed? [نُنَبِّئُكُم · referent] (turns on: The notes specify the audience at different levels: unbelievers, contextual polytheists, and the heedless.)
  18:103/q04/p1 It addresses unbelievers, particularly the polytheists in the surrounding passage. — reasons: Bursevī names unbelievers; Ālūsī says the question addresses unbelievers; Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists are rebuked and alerted to their failed efforts; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their identity is confirmed by the later denial of God's signs and meeting.
  18:103/q04/p2 It warns people heedless of remembrance, whose eyes and ears miss the warning. — reasons: Biqāʿī takes the addressees from the preceding description.
18:103/q05 Why ask before naming the greatest losers? [هَلْ نُنَبِّئُكُم بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ · rhetoric]
  18:103/q05/p1 The question rebukes the hearers and builds curiosity before exposing them as the losers. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a taunting rebuke; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the shift from second to third person delays the reveal that the listeners themselves are meant, and calls the offer ironic; Ālūsī notes its sarcasm.
18:103/q06 Who are the greatest losers in deeds? [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَٰلًا · referent] (turns on: The disagreement concerns whether named communities are exclusive referents, illustrative cases, or displaced by the immediate polytheist context; reports from ʿAlī and Saʿd differ.)
  18:103/q06/p1 They are Jews and Christians, or the disbelieving People of the Book. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī cites 88:3–4; Saʿd says Jews denied Muhammad and Christians denied food and drink in Paradise, while calling the Kharijites covenant breakers; Biqāʿī adds that both deny bodily resurrection. Al-ʿAyyāshī's report describes scriptural communities that disbelieved and innovated. Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya treat specific reports as applications, not the original referent.
  18:103/q06/p2 They are monks and priests, especially Christian ascetics confined in cells or monasteries. — reasons: ʿAlī is repeatedly cited; al-Ḍaḥḥāk also appears. Abū Ḥayyān compares the 'toiling, weary' language of 88:3. Reports vary between cells, monasteries, and pillars; Jishumī gives this as an occasion of revelation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects its status as the original contextual referent.
  18:103/q06/p3 They are the polytheists who worship idols or other beings and think that worship benefits them. — reasons: Qurṭubī says the question rebukes those worshipping other than God; Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to reliance on useless idol patrons; Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeals to 18:106 and the surrounding passage. Biqāʿī lists angels, stars, prophets, idols, and other unbelief; Kur'an Yolu says idolaters deem shirk obedience. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the next verse fits Meccan idolaters.
  18:103/q06/p4 They include the Harurites or Kharijites. — reasons: Reports attribute this to ʿAlī, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and Abū ʿAlī; one al-ʿAyyāshī report says they are 'not far' from the People of the Book. Jishumī would accept ʿAlī's report if authentic, arguing that kufr can include rejection of some signs. Al-Aṣamm objects that the verse describes unbelievers while the Harurites are Muslims; Saʿd explicitly names another group. Ibn Kathīr calls them included but not exclusive; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the Jewish-to-Kharijite report unsound.
  18:103/q06/p5 The label broadly covers anyone whose unapproved religious path seems sound to its follower. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says named groups are examples rather than an exhaustive definition, including outsiders to Islam, show-offs, and heretics whose innovations reach unbelief. Ibn Kathīr says the Meccan date precedes Kharijites and the relevant Jewish-Christian address, and cites 88:2–4, 25:23, and 24:39. He says these people believe their deeds accepted and beloved.
  18:103/q06/p6 They are the sinful or transgressive people of Quraysh. — reasons: Two reports attribute this answer to ʿAlī when Ibn al-Kawwāʾ asked him; the second cites 18:103–104.
  18:103/q06/p7 They are adherents of religious whims or heresies. — reasons: Māwardī records the first description; Abū Ḥayyān includes innovators whose heresy leads to unbelief within the broad warning.
  18:103/q06/p8 They are show-offs or people who boast of good deeds. — reasons: Māwardī reports boasters; Abū Ḥayyān includes show-offs as an application.
  18:103/q06/p9 They are hypocrites whose deeds contradict belief. — reasons: Māwardī records this possible referent.
  18:103/q06/p10 They are people who seek this world and abandon the next. — reasons: Māwardī gives this possibility twice, once alongside hypocrites.
  18:103/q06/p11 They are the Sabians. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān records this among the named groups.
  18:103/q06/p12 They are Christians specifically. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes a report naming Christians without a wider group.
18:103/q07 What degree or kind of loss does ٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ express? [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the explanation stresses the comparative degree or the practical image of fruitless investment.)
  18:103/q07/p1 It marks the greatest or more severe loss among losers or among creation. — reasons: Sāmarrāʾī says akhsar exceeds khāsir and ranks some losers below others; Wāḥidī says these deeds make them the greatest losers among creation; Bursevī similarly says greatest loss among creatures.
  18:103/q07/p2 It portrays exhausting effort that ends in ruin, like a merchant who expects profit but loses the investment. — reasons: Thaʿlabī and Baghawī use the failed merchant analogy; Wāḥidī al-Wajīz says their deeds are disastrously lost.
18:103/q08 How is أَعْمَٰلًا parsed, and why is it plural? [بِٱلْأَخْسَرِينَ أَعْمَٰلًا · grammar] (turns on: The main analysis takes aʿmālan as tamyīz; alternatives differ over its underlying singular and whether it denotes workers.)
  18:103/q08/p1 It is an accusative specification of the loss; its plural reflects diverse deeds, desires, or agents. — reasons: Sībawayh notes that the definite plural adjective retains nūn, preventing annexation; al-Akhfash likewise says the article and plural ending require accusative specification. Al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Jawzī say it clarifies what is lost. Bayḍāwī, Samīn, Bursevī, and Ālūsī explain the plural by diverse actions or agents; Nasafī contrasts expected singular specification and refers to many desires. Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses varied losses; Biqāʿī names worship of angels, stars, prophets, idols, and other disbelief.
  18:103/q08/p2 It is a rare plural of ʿāmil, 'worker'. — reasons: Ālūsī reports this analysis and says some grammarians deny such plurals except for a few established forms.
  18:103/q08/p3 It is a plural of ʿamal used for people possessed of deeds. — reasons: Ālūsī attributes the proposal to al-Qāmūs and finds it strained.
18:103/q09 What do ʿamal and the related work terms imply here? [أَعْمَٰلًا · meaning]
  18:103/q09/p1 ʿAmal denotes work without itself requiring the skill and intent conveyed by ṣunʿ. — reasons: Sāmar
