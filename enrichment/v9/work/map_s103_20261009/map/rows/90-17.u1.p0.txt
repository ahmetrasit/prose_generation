## THE CURRENT MAP
90:17/q01 How does this clause connect grammatically with the earlier failed ascent? [ثُمَّ كَانَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the earlier negative or an implied question governs this clause, and whether an ellipsis is supplied.)
  90:17/q01/p1 It is coordinated with 'he did not cross the ascent,' so the rebuke also denies his faith. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān attach it to 90:11; al-Ālūsī prefers the negative reading, saying the repeated negation singles out faith as the noblest part of the ascent, not something outside it. Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise infer that the rebuked man neither crossed nor believed; al-Zajjāj says the second clause shows his unbelief.
  90:17/q01/p2 The earlier lā may be read as a prayer, an urging question, or with an omitted interrogative. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya records denial, exhortation, and prayer as readings of 90:11. Al-Ālūsī attributes alternatives to Ibn Zayd, al-Jubbāʾī and Abū Muslim and objects on grammatical grounds; grammarians criticize a line by ʿUmar ibn Abī Rabīʿa used for omitted interrogative hamza.
  90:17/q01/p3 The phrase can be supplied as 'then his being among the believers.' — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī compares 3:86, where he understands an omitted conjunction before 'they testified.'
90:17/q02 Why is one negative lā used across the coordinated ascent clauses? [ثُمَّ كَانَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether customary repetition is required.)
  90:17/q02/p1 One lā can cover the connected clauses; repetition is usual but not obligatory. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ says the single negation covers freeing, feeding, and believing though Arabic commonly repeats it, comparing 75:31. Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī says instances of repetition do not prove a rule; al-Ālūsī cites 25:67 and poetry via al-Ṭībī and allows omission.
  90:17/q02/p2 A repeated negative is grammatically expected. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports al-Zajjāj's objection, which al-Fārisī answers; al-Farrāʾ describes repetition as normal usage.
90:17/q03 What does thumma convey between the good deeds and faith? [ثُمَّ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether thumma orders time, rank, expression, conjunction, or the believer's final state; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ makes social deeds a prior ground of faith.)
  90:17/q03/p1 It marks faith's higher rank, not later time. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burhān, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Quṭb and al-Sāmarrāʾī say faith precedes or validates deeds. Al-Ālūsī adds that faith alone can save before duties fall due. Quṭb says faith grounds action in a stable divine way rather than whim or praise. Al-Qāshānī and al-Bursawī present the rise from generosity/chastity to wisdom, patience/courage and mercy/justice. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports but rejects this detachment of faith from social deeds.
  90:17/q03/p2 It orders the mention or wording, while faith already existed when the deeds were done. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī cite a poem that names a son's eminence before his father and grandfather though the elders attained it first. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Itqān and al-Sāmarrāʾī likewise distinguish report order from event order; the Ḥamāsa commentator says thumma can delay words but need not delay clauses.
  90:17/q03/p3 Here it functions like 'and,' joining faith to freeing and feeding without temporal order. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī and al-Itqān report this reading. Al-Jishumī adds that faith encompasses obedience. Al-Burhān argues that a rank reading removes the perceived need to replace thumma with 'and.'
  90:17/q03/p4 It can mark later faith after deeds performed before conversion. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Biqāʿī allow a beneficent pre-Islamic person to believe later; the Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām report supports the reception of earlier good. Al-Biqāʿī describes a later second ascent that purifies reason. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers real sequence, making emancipation and feeding prerequisites for sincere social faith.
  90:17/q03/p5 It can indicate the person's final state: he remained faithful until death. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn make dying in faith a condition for earlier deeds' benefit; al-Qurṭubī compares 20:82.
  90:17/q03/p6 It marks a real sequence in which freeing and feeding establish the social ground for faith. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says a society accepting slavery and starvation cannot sustain genuine faith; she objects to readings that separate belief from resisting tyranny and neglect of the poor.
90:17/q04 What is the role of faith in the acceptance of the earlier deeds? [ءَامَنُوا۟ · theology] (turns on: Whether faith must accompany deeds, can validate good from before conversion, or includes confidence in the deeds' value.)
  90:17/q04/p1 Faith is required for freeing and feeding to count as a successful ascent or receive reward. — reasons: Muqātil says duties without faith do not secure acceptance of charity and expiation. Al-Fārisī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and others say unbelief nullifies reward; al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran cite 9:54. Ibn Kathīr adds inward belief, intention for God's reward, 17:19 and 16:97. Quṭb says faith connects action to a divine norm. Al-Ālūsī adds that faith alone can save before obligations arise.
  90:17/q04/p2 If the person later accepts Islam, earlier good done for God can come forward with that faith. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr invoke the Prophet's reply to Ḥakīm ibn Ḥizām. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the deeds are praised and rewarded as though done in Islam; al-Biqāʿī says later faith embraces them. Al-Qurṭubī separately denies benefit when belief arrives merely after spending, appealing to 9:54.
  90:17/q04/p3 Belief here can mean conviction that the good deeds are beneficial and necessary before God. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports the view that the doer believes the deeds will benefit him with God. Kur'an Yolu allows belief in their value and necessity alongside religious faith.
90:17/q05 What does being 'among those who believed' add to the description? [كَانَ مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the phrase emphasizes duration, communal identity, or the content of faith.)
  90:17/q05/p1 It means steadfast, continuing faith rather than a passing act of belief. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Nasafī explain kāna as persistence; al-Qushayrī stresses one's state at life's end. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī say the believer performs the deeds and remains faithful until death.
  90:17/q05/p2 It identifies a person with the established community of believers and intensifies praise. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says membership conveys firmer socially recognized faith than simply calling him a believer, comparing 2:67; mentioning the group prepares the praise of mutual counsel and the people of the right.
  90:17/q05/p3 Its unstated object is God alone, Muhammad's messengerhood, and Islam. — reasons: Muqātil enumerates God, angels, scriptures, messengers, Paradise and Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verb's object is omitted as understood and the verb functions without an explicit object; Corpus Coranicum compares 69:33.
90:17/q06 Who exemplifies the believers described here? [مِنَ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent] (turns on: Whether the text is illustrated by exemplary Companions or read as a rebuke of the boastful man.)
  90:17/q06/p1 The foremost Companions, including the four caliphs, are examples. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī names Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf; al-Rāzī says the leading Companions including four caliphs were patient in religion and merciful to people.
  90:17/q06/p2 Abū Bakr is the exemplary case, including his noble conduct before conversion. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī ranks him immediately after prophets, citing Ibn al-Dughunnah's praise of kinship, hospitality, helping the burdened and distressed, Khadīja's similar reassurance to the Prophet, his swift conversion, his response at Ḥudaybiyya matching the Prophet's words, and his refusal of wine to preserve reason.
  90:17/q06/p3 It addresses the boastful spender, contrasting his failure with the believers' path. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī identifies the man who boasted of spending much; al-Nasafī says he failed to thank the Benefactor by freeing, feeding and believing. Ibn ʿĀshūr says kāna implies the rebuked man lacked faith; al-Iṣlāḥī says the failure shows unovercome moral and spiritual obstacles.
90:17/q07 What does tawāṣaw require of believers? [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether emphasis falls on mutual support, public moral guidance, or the counselors' own practice.)
  90:17/q07/p1 They advise and strengthen one another in good, sharing responsibility for patience and mercy. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī glosses it as mutual advice. Al-Rāzī says believers must guide others to truth and turn them from evil; al-Ālūsī says counsel perfects others after one's own good works. Al-Biqāʿī says patience needs cooperation because it is bitter. Quṭb says joint encouragement prevents defeat, discouragement and panic without erasing individual responsibility. Elmalılı says even those unable to free or feed can console others. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects speech against injustice with God-given lips, 90:9.
  90:17/q07/p2 The wording also implies that the counselors themselves practice the mercy they commend. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says those who know its value and recommend it first act on it, connecting the point with 89:18 and the mercy among believers in 48:29.
