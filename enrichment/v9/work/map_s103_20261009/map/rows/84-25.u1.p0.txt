## THE CURRENT MAP
84:25/q01 How does the exception introduced by إِلَّا relate believers to those previously addressed? [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · grammar] (turns on: Whether believers are members of the previously addressed group, including repentant former disbelievers, or a contrasting group; al-Samīn also makes the syntax of the reward clause decisive.)
  84:25/q01/p1 It is a disconnected exception: 'but' introduces believers as a contrasting group outside the disbelievers threatened with punishment. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī contrasts those affirming both testimonies and fulfilling obligations with the unbelievers. Al-Samīn allows this analysis when 'for them is a reward' is an independent or circumstantial clause, and requires it when the relative expression is a subject with that clause as predicate; this is an exception of statements, not individuals. Ibn Kathīr contrasts inward belief and outward deeds. Kashifī, reported by Bursawī, places believers outside the unbelievers addressed. Elmālılı calls this the general analysis. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the exception from the pronoun in the sarcastic command to give the damned glad tidings: illā functions like 'but', and the standard account of istidrāk as removing an assumed implication is only approximate. Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise takes it from the pronoun in the command to announce punishment. The Study Quran reads 'but' to distinguish the groups' fates and compares 41:8 and 95:6. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Bayḍāwī allow the analysis as an alternative.
  84:25/q01/p2 It can be connected: believers are excepted from the preceding group, especially disbelievers who repent and believe. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās makes 'those who' accusative as an exception from the preceding pronoun, while allowing a detached exception. Muqātil, reported by al-Wāḥidī, draws the believers from the unbelievers mentioned before. Al-Bayḍāwī speaks of those who repent and believe. Al-Samīn permits this when the reward clause is independent or circumstantial. Elmālılı permits connection for believers formerly among the disbelievers. Asad treats the warning of future suffering as excepting those who repent, believe, and do good; the Study Quran reads 'save those who' of disbelievers who repent.
84:25/q02 What syntactic role does لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ play? [لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the relative expression and reward clause form a subject-predicate sentence or the reward clause stands independently or circumstantially.)
  84:25/q02/p1 It may be a new independent clause or a circumstantial clause. — reasons: Al-Samīn says either construction allows the exception to be connected or disconnected.
  84:25/q02/p2 It may be the predicate of ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟, which is the subject. — reasons: Al-Samīn says this construction makes the exception disconnected and concerns the contrasting statement about believers rather than an exception of individuals.
84:25/q03 What do the believers' faith and righteous works include? [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · interpretation] (turns on: How concretely or inwardly the words faith and righteous works are read.)
  84:25/q03/p1 Faith is inward belief or confession, and righteous works are its outward fulfillment and proof. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī specifies affirmation of both testimonies and fulfillment of obligations. Ibn Kathīr distinguishes inward faith from outward deeds. Al-Biqāʿī says confession is verified by deeds. Bursawī includes sincere belief, commanded worship, and virtues.
  84:25/q03/p2 Faith and works also signify purifying the inner self and acquiring virtues. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī describes clearing the heart of psychic traits and acquiring virtues through deeds; Bursawī describes knowledge that cleanses the heart of the self's traits and works that acquire virtues.
  84:25/q03/p3 Inwardly, spiritual faculties believe, and good deeds are their turn from the world toward God. — reasons: The Najmī taʾwīlāt, reported by Bursawī, names the spirit, secret, heart, and spiritual powers and says they turn toward God.
84:25/q04 What reward is credited to someone whose age or weakness ends earlier good works? [لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · reports]
  84:25/q04/p1 The person continues to receive the reward of the good deeds performed while strong. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by al-Naḥḥās, says an elderly person weakened after earlier good deeds is credited with the same reward.
84:25/q05 What does أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ mean? [أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ · meaning] (turns on: Whether mamnūn is understood through cutting, counting or diminution, or mann as reminding someone of a favor; the latter also raises whether reward is owed to deeds or remains God's bounty.)
  84:25/q05/p1 The reward is uninterrupted, never cut off or ending. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba glosses the term as not cut off. Al-Zajjāj and Ibn ʿĀshūr derive this sense from cutting a rope; al-Dāmghānī compares 95:6 and 41:8. ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī, and Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Ṭabrisī, relate it to the ever-growing, enduring bliss of the Hereafter. Ibn ʿAbbās tells Nāfiʿ ibn al-Azraq of Arabic usage in al-Qurṭubī and cites al-Ḥārith ibn Ḥilliza's manīn for dust left after a swift rider in Ibn ʿĀshūr. Ibn Kathīr joins the glosses of Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and al-Ḍaḥḥāk into 'uninterrupted', comparing 11:108. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the uncut fruit of 56:32–33. Al-Biqāʿī includes its never ending; Bursawī says this is the more apparent sense because capacity and guidance to act are already God's favor. Asad and Iṣlāḥī link the enduring reward to repentance or faith and good works. The Study Quran refers to 41:8 and 95:6.
  84:25/q05/p2 The reward is not diminished. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī gives both this and the uncounted sense, reporting Ibn ʿAbbās for undiminished reward. ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ṭabrisī's Ibn ʿAbbās tie it to enduring afterlife bliss. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ibn ʿAbbās's related reckoning and diminishment by reminders. Al-Ṭabrisī reports al-Jubbāʾī, also related from al-Ḥasan, as pairing this sense with freedom from reproach. Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī and synthesizes it with 'uncounted'. Al-Biqāʿī includes never diminishing.
  84:25/q05/p3 The reward comes without reproach or reminders of God's favor that would spoil it. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj pairs freedom from reproach with an unmerited reward. Al-Naḥḥās says God does not remind them of His favor reproachfully. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ibn ʿAbbās's 'reckoned against them' or diminished by reminders. Al-Ṭabrisī's al-Jubbāʾī, also related from al-Ḥasan, says unclouded by reproach; al-Ṭabrisī illustrates mann through Labīd's severed rope. Bursawī reports al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī recalling generous people who gave wealth without reminding recipients. Ibn ʿĀshūr says generous reward honors the recipient, citing 46:14 and 2:264; al-Nābigha's verse describes blessings without the sting of reproach, and al-Zamakhsharī says blessings taste sweeter than reminders. Al-Biqāʿī includes unreproachful bounty, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it free of burdensome reproach.
  84:25/q05/p4 The reward is uncounted or not reckoned against its recipients. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī gives this sense and reports Mujāhid for it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ibn ʿAbbās saying counted or reckoned against them; Ibn Kathīr reports Mujāhid and al-Ḍaḥḥāk, combining their sense with Ibn ʿAbbās's undiminished reward as uninterrupted.
  84:25/q05/p5 The reward carries no claim of indebtedness or favor over recipients because it is earned. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī reports this reading, relating the root for favor to cutting, illustrated by Labīd's poem. Elmālілі states its rationale as reward earned without indebtedness. Ibn Kathīr rejects denying God's favor to the people of Paradise: entry is through grace and mercy, not deeds, and His favor continues eternally; they praise Him as naturally as breathing, with 10:10 cited.
  84:25/q05/p6 The reward is an unmerited gift, grounded in divine bounty and mercy rather than deeds alone. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj calls it unmerited. Al-Biqāʿī says God's great bounty includes pardon and concealment of minor sins, and that works do not truly cause reward; the missing causal fa signals enveloping mercy, unlike Sūrat al-Tīn 95:6. Ibn Kathīr says Paradise is entered by grace and mercy rather than deeds and that recipients remain under God's favor, praising Him as naturally as breathing, with 10:10 cited. The Najmī taʾwīlāt in Bursawī ascribes reward to God's grace and mercy, not self-effort or self-congratulation. Bursawī notes even ability and guidance to act are God's favor.
  84:25/q05/p7 Inwardly, the reward is enduring, immaterial life or attributes in the gardens of soul and heart. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī describes an unending immaterial reward in the gardens of self and heart. Bursawī transmits al-Qāshānī's description of enduring traces and attributes there, free from corruption and materiality; he also reports the Najmī reading of spiritual reward through grace and mercy.
84:25/q06 Why is the causal fa omitted before the reward clause? [لَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the omission points to the cause of reward or to the sūra's concise treatment of believers.)
  84:25/q06/p1 Its omission signals that reward does not truly result from deeds but rests on God's mercy. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī contrasts the construction with Sūrat al-Tīn 95:6, whose context uses a causal fa.
  84:25/q06/p2 Its omission suits the sūra's brief statement of reward after its extended treatment of disbelievers. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī surveys the unequal space given to punishment and believers through 84:7–25; the concise reward statement fits without fa.
