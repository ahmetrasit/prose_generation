## THE CURRENT MAP
102:1/q01 What does أَلْهَىٰكُمُ mean? [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the verb is rendered by its ordinary effect, distraction, or by the alluring manner of that diversion.)
  102:1/q01/p1 It occupied, distracted, or made you forget what matters. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī derive it from turning away or forgetting; Ibn Fāris calls anything occupying one lāhī. Imruʾ al-Qays's line illustrates 'occupied her' (Qurṭubī). Al-Ālūsī traces lahw from heedlessness to any diversion, conventionally pleasurable play; al-Rāghib defines it by neglect of what matters. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes Qurʾānic examples of person, wealth, children, trade, hopes and speech (63:9); play is paired separately with lahw (6:32, 29:64).
  102:1/q01/p2 It allures or deceives people. — reasons: Islāḥī uses the sense of enticement for the verb.
102:1/q02 What did rivalry distract people from? [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the unspoken object is read broadly or supplied specifically from the audience and context.)
  102:1/q02/p1 Obedience, worship, remembrance of God, and preparation for the Hereafter. — reasons: Muqātil says remembrance of the afterlife; al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī and others say obeying God and escaping His anger. Rāzī lists duties, recommended works, knowledge, reflection, and contemplation. Al-Biqāʿī adds death and saving acts; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies hearing Qurʾānic evidence and accepting the Prophet's call. 63:9 is used for the warning (Jishumī); al-Qāriʿah and the scales ground it for al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı.
  102:1/q02/p2 The omitted object allows every important concern neglected by diversion. — reasons: Rāzī says an absolute construction intensifies blame; al-Biqāʿī says omission magnifies the importance of what is neglected, and Elmalılı notes both distraction's object and accumulation's kind are unstated.
  102:1/q02/p3 In its immediate address, it diverted polytheist chiefs from Qurʾānic guidance and fair acceptance of Muhammad's call. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr infers the object from the wealthy chiefs' choice of luxury and their obstruction of others; past tense presents this distraction as already accomplished.
102:1/q03 Why is the object of أَلْهَىٰكُمُ unstated? [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether ellipsis deliberately leaves all neglected duties open or expects a definite object to be inferred.)
  102:1/q03/p1 Its omission broadens and intensifies the rebuke. — reasons: Rāzī says an absolute construction lets the mind supply every neglected object: remembrance, duties, recommended acts, knowledge and reflection. Al-Bayḍāwī calls it grandeur and emphasis; al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı say it magnifies the neglected religious concern.
  102:1/q03/p2 The object is self-evident from the addressees: Qurʾān, reflection, and accepting its truth. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr links the omission to polytheist leaders who preferred comfort to Muhammad's call; the past tense marks the diversion as accomplished.
102:1/q04 How is أَلْهَىٰكُمُ read and what force does the opening have? [أَلْهَىٰكُمُ · readings] (turns on: The transmitted opening hamza and whether an assertion or question better conveys the rebuke.)
  102:1/q04/p1 It is a declarative statement of an actual distraction, used as censure. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān attributes the statement to the majority. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls its news form rebuke and lament. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers it as stronger: it asserts the failure occurred. Al-Ālūsī records the interrogative alternatives.
  102:1/q04/p2 It is a reproachful interrogative with a lengthened opening hamza. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿImrān al-Jawnī, and Abū Ṣāliḥ are reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī add ʿĀʾisha, Muʿāwiya, Mālik b. Dīnār, Abū al-Jawzāʾ and others. Rāzī compares interrogative forms at 2:6 and 17:49. Abū Ḥayyān says it makes the ugliness explicit but sits uneasily with zurtum; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ objects that declaration has greater force.
  102:1/q04/p3 It is an interrogative with two opening hamzas, seeking confirmation and reproach. — reasons: Abū Bakr, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Shaʿbī, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Ibn Abī ʿAbla, and al-Kisāʾī are named by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī; the latter says the question seeks affirmation.
102:1/q05 What does ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ mean here? [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · meaning] (turns on: Whether form VI names mutual boasting, acquisitive increase beyond boasting, or a possible Greek-derived expression.)
  102:1/q05/p1 Mutual boasting and rivalry in having more people, possessions, or valued distinctions. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba names people and kin; al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī say each group claims numerical superiority. Al-Aʿshā's lines link numbers with honor (Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn ʿĀshūr). Rāzī equates takāthur with tafākhur at 57:20; Jishumī and Abū Muslim note form VI can express reciprocity, effort, or acquisition. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this majority gloss but challenges its equation with boasting.
  102:1/q05/p2 It is acquisitive striving for ever more, extending beyond competitive boasting. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the root is abundance or growth in quantity and 57:20 distinguishes takāthur from tafākhur; she prefers striving to monopolize worldly goods. Paret glosses it 'urge to have more.' Islāḥī notes greed outruns rising living standards.
  102:1/q05/p3 It may render Greek pleonexia, covetous desire for more. — reasons: Künstlinger proposes Arabic form VI renders the Greek comparative; Corpus Coranicum says a Greek New Testament setting, including Luke's warning against greed, is likelier than Peshitta wording if this connection holds. Andrae compares Ephrem's worldly person who always wants more.
102:1/q06 What does the form of ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ express? [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · grammar] (turns on: Which attested force of form VI is active in this context.)
  102:1/q06/p1 It expresses reciprocal boasting, each party claiming more. — reasons: Jishumī lists reciprocity among the possible senses of tafāʿala; Rāzī and Bursevī describe rival groups or individuals comparing wealth and followers.
  102:1/q06/p2 It can express determined effort to increase or acquisition of quantity, without reciprocal boasting. — reasons: Abū Muslim lists effort and an intransitive increase alongside reciprocity; Rāzī applies effort to the ambitious pursuit of wealth. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ grounds it in the root 'abundance' and distinguishes it from tafākhur at 57:20.
102:1/q07 What kind of abundance or pursuit is meant by ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ? [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · referent] (turns on: Whether the unrestricted noun points to all worldly increase or to a particular object established by a gloss, report, or occasion.)
  102:1/q07/p1 Worldly abundance broadly, including wealth, children, people, status, allies, and pleasures. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿĀshūr hold wealth and numbers together; Rāzī lists money, ranks, kin, supporters, armies, every pleasure. Elmalılı prefers combining wealth and numbers because the verse is unrestricted and 57:20 supports both. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects restriction to money, numbers, or the dead; Qurṭubī likewise extends application to all such causes. Al-Sulamī reports a view that accumulating wealth and status absorbs life even through remembrance of the dead.
  102:1/q07/p2 Rivalry particularly in wealth and children. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan and Ibn ʿAbbās give this pair; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, and al-Ṭabrisī echo it. The prophetic 'my wealth' saying is cited by al-Ṭabarī and Rāzī as evidence for wealth within the pair.
  102:1/q07/p3 Wealth specifically, with greed and hoarding as its forms. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī cites the Prophet's food/clothes/charity saying after recitation; Ibn ʿAbbās's reported gloss specifies unlawful gathering, withholding dues, and storage. Rāzī reports deferring alms and pilgrimage provision until death.
  102:1/q07/p4 Boasting over clan size, ancestry, and dead forebears. — reasons: Qatāda describes tribal headcounts ending in death. Al-Aʿshā's poetry makes numerical size a mark of honor. Al-Qāshānī adds boasting even over ancestral bones.
  102:1/q07/p5 Preoccupation with livelihood and trade. — reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is cited by al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī; ʿAmr b. Dīnār swears the sūra came concerning merchants.
102:1/q08 What does the definite article in ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ identify? [ٱلتَّكَاثُرُ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether definiteness narrows the referent to a familiar evil or occasion, or the unsupplied object leaves accumulation open.)
  102:1/q08/p1 It identifies familiar blameworthy worldly competition. — reasons: Rāzī distinguishes this pursuit of passing pleasure from commendable rivalry in knowledge and virtue; Bursevī likewise names money, status, supporters, and kin.
  102:1/q08/p2 It points to the known tribal occasion of boasting in numbers. — reasons: Elmalılı takes the article as referring to the reported occasion of comparing groups.
  102:1/q08/p3 The verse still leaves the type of increase unrestricted. — reasons: Elmalılı says the unexpressed kind of abundance gives wide rhetorical scope; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ resists importing a restriction to particular goods or only blameworthy kinds.
