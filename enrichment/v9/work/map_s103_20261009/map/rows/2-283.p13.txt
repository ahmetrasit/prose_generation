[TABATABAI:2:282-283/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | He defines the key terms: mutual debt, dictation, shortchanging, weariness, causing harm, disobedience, and the two plural forms for pledged collateral.
[TABATABAI:2:282-283/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The two verses contain nearly twenty legal rulings, including rules for debt and pledges; detailed legal debate belongs in jurisprudence, so he leaves it aside here.
[ASAD-NOTES:2:283#n276/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The ban on concealing testimony covers transaction witnesses and a debtor who accepted a trust-based loan without writing or witnesses and later denies the debt.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Collateral is a material asset taken from the debtor and convertible to payment; Islam forbids using a human being as collateral, despite pre-Islamic practices that sometimes pledged children.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | When travel makes a scribe hard to find, a suitable pledge can replace writing; the Prophet's practice shows pledges are allowed outside travel as well.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hanefî ve Şâfiîler | reports | Hanafis and Shafiis say a pledge contract is invalid if the collateral has not been taken out of the debtor's control; Malik says it is valid but binding only upon delivery, and the debtor can be compelled to deliver it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | A creditor may not benefit from property pledged as security for a debt.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | reports | Among jurists who consider writing, dictation, and witnesses obligatory, some take the mutual-trust clause to lift that obligation, while others limit pledges as a substitute to travel; those who see security as recommended view the clause as reinforcing that advice.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Âşûr | prefers | Ibn Ashur's wider reading, which the author endorses, extends fidelity to trust across all debt relations; without trust and responsibility, parties can wrong one another even when security exists.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Those who know relevant testimony may not conceal it; they should come forward even if not asked, while hadiths criticizing unsolicited testimony are understood as concerning God's rights or false testimony.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:2:283/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Concealing testimony is a sin of the mind and will rather than an outward bodily act, which explains calling the heart sinful.
[QIRAAT-ER:2:283:11#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | reports | Ruhun and rihān are both canonical plurals of rahn: the former is broken and the latter sound.

[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p149/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | The author says riyāʿa is broader than mere preservation: it includes care, correction, stewardship, and monitoring, all needed to discharge a trust.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p149/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | A trusted guardian must attend to dependents’ needs, a ruler must oversee subjects, and one entrusted with crops, livestock, or religious obligations must actively discharge the charge and seek what pleases God.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p149/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | holds | Unlike chastity, which remains with the person, trusts may be remote or hidden from view and exposed to thieves, so the trustee must inspect and tend them like a shepherd; riyāʿa is the apt term. | mentions 23:5-7, 70:29-31
