## THE CURRENT MAP
14:34/q01 What does God giving from all people asked mean? [وَءَاتَىٰكُم مِّن كُلِّ مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether asking is verbal or implicit, whether كل is literal or abundant, and whether من limits the gifts.)
  14:34/q01/p1 God grants a portion or some kinds of what people ask, according to their benefit and His wisdom. — reasons: Al-Akhfash and al-Tabari supply an omitted 'thing' and cite 27:23; Tabari prefers this common reading. Jishumi and Tabrisi say harmful requests are withheld; al-Alusi says each kind, rather than every individual member, is covered. Bursawi cites 17:18 and 6:44 for abundance. Asad conditions fulfillment on ultimate benefit.
  14:34/q01/p2 God granted every thing that people literally requested or desired. — reasons: Mujahid, Hasan and Ikrima give the broad wording; al-Farra's explanation below extends it to hypothetical requests. Abu al-Hasan and al-Akhfash's extra min permits totality.
  14:34/q01/p3 God gives unasked gifts as well as asked gifts, or gifts never asked for. — reasons: Muqatil cites mercy; al-Dahhak names sun, moon, night and day, and says some gifts were not imagined. Ibn al-Anbari cites 16:81 for wording that implies an unmentioned counterpart. Qushayri names prior obedience and forgiveness; Sahl and Yahya name Muhammad and unity. Tabataba'i rejects the verbal-asking premise.
  14:34/q01/p4 The address is collective: humanity receives every useful kind, distributed among individuals. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan deny that any one person possesses all kinds. Al-Akhfash and Jishumi say different people requested and received different things; Tabataba'i calls the species' requests genuine, though individuals may be denied. Islahi sees the fit of human form and provision as evidence of divine design.
  14:34/q01/p5 The gifts answer needs expressed by people's condition or preparedness, whether spoken or not. — reasons: Al-Kisa'i says asking can denote wishing; al-Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi and Alusi treat need as implicit asking. Qashani cites 55:29; al-Alusi cites 7:172. Tabataba'i distinguishes speech or signs from beings' intrinsic dependence on God and says all genuine requests go to Him. Tabrisi compares 2:29 for God's provision of what people need.
  14:34/q01/p6 God gives what people would ask for if they asked. — reasons: Al-Farra and Ibn Atiyya express the hypothetical request; Ibn Atiyya says it approximates the collective provision view.
  14:34/q01/p7 God gives from what people ask, sometimes wholly and sometimes partly. — reasons: Tabataba'i says min indicates source, not a fixed fraction; wisdom governs the measure, and treating every request as only partly fulfilled would misread the wording.
14:34/q02 How does من كل ما سألتموه work grammatically? [مِّن كُلِّ مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · grammar] (turns on: Whether من is partitive or extra, and what object is omitted.)
  14:34/q02/p1 من is partitive, and an object such as 'thing' or 'portion' is understood. — reasons: Al-Akhfash and Tabari compare 27:23; Abu Ali compares 2:61 and 27:16. Ibn Atiyya requires a supplied second object. Samin attributes this analysis to Sibawayh; al-Alusi explains that كل still spans the kinds.
  14:34/q02/p2 من is extra in an affirmative clause; every requested thing is the second object. — reasons: Abu al-Hasan analyzes the prepositional phrase as accusative; Samin reports this from al-Akhfash. Qurtubi records the resulting complete-grant sense.
  14:34/q02/p3 كل is an expansive expression for many kinds, not exhaustive totality. — reasons: Al-Akhfash compares everyday 'knows everything'; Tabari and others cite 6:44, the opening of every door. Bursawi also cites 17:18.
14:34/q03 How is من كل read? [مِّن كُلِّ · readings] (turns on: The standard genitive annexation contrasts with tanwīn; on tanwīn, mā may be relative or negative.)
  14:34/q03/p1 The common reading attaches كل to ما without tanwīn. — reasons: Al-Tabari prefers the authoritative reciters' agreement and rejects the alternative; Ibn Jinni says the object is an understood share of each kind. Jishumi and Thalabi identify it as the common reading.
  14:34/q03/p2 A reported reading puts tanwīn on كل; its sense varies with the parsing of ما. — reasons: Abu Salih contrasts it with the reading without min; Ibn Jinni lists Ibn Abbas, Hasan, Dahhak, Muhammad and Jafar ibn Ali, Amr ibn Fa'id and Yaqub. Other lists add Salam, Qatada and Nafi. It can mean requested gifts, need by condition, or unasked gifts.
14:34/q04 What can ما and the pronoun in سألتموه denote? [مَا سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · grammar] (turns on: Relative, descriptive, infinitival, and negative readings assign different functions and pronoun references.)
  14:34/q04/p1 ما is relative or descriptive, referring to what was requested or needed. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya, Samin and al-Alusi allow a relative; Samin prefers it on the tanwīn reading as second object. Abu Hayyan objects to some proposed restored resumptive pronouns, while al-Alusi reports a defense.
  14:34/q04/p2 ما is infinitival, with the suffix pronoun referring to God. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Samin say the verbal noun stands for the request or requested thing; Samin distinguishes the pronoun's referent from the relative or descriptive analysis.
  14:34/q04/p3 On the tanwīn reading, ما is negative and the phrase is circumstantial: gifts came without asking. — reasons: Al-Dahhak's account is widely transmitted; al-Zamakhshari gives it, while Abu Hayyan says it clashes with the common annexed reading and Samin prefers relative mā for harmony.
14:34/q05 Why can a person's request go unanswered? [سَأَلْتُمُوهُ · theology] (turns on: Whether the limitation lies in benefit, the petitioner's knowledge, or the distinction between species and individual requests.)
  14:34/q05/p1 God grants only what is beneficial, at its right time, and may avert harmful requests. — reasons: Jishumi invokes partitive من, distinguishes the informed petitioner, and Tabrisi names harm to religion or another person. Asad appeals to ultimate divine wisdom; the Study Quran reports a Prophetic account of grant now, stored reward, or equivalent evil averted, excluding sin and severed kinship.
  14:34/q05/p2 The species' genuine intrinsic requests are met, while individual verbal prayers may be insincere or misdirected. — reasons: Tabataba'i cites 2:186 and distinguishes all beings' dependence from explicit verbal requests.
14:34/q06 What is the reading of وآتاكم? [وَءَاتَىٰكُم · readings]
  14:34/q06/p1 A reading has أتاكم in place of آتاكم. — reasons: Ibn Kathir records the variant without further argument.
14:34/q07 What does نعمة الله mean, and why is it singular? [نِعْمَتَ ٱللَّهِ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the singular is a verbal noun substitute or a collective noun for bestowed things.)
  14:34/q07/p1 It is a noun standing for the verbal noun إنعام, God's act of bestowal. — reasons: Al-Wahidi says a verbal noun need not be pluralized; Razi, Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit this analysis.
  14:34/q07/p2 It means the bestowed blessings collectively; a singular construct or generic noun covers them all. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and Alusi prefer this over the verbal-noun view. Baydawi and Bursawi point to construct definiteness; Tabataba'i calls it the whole genus; Samin says the bestowed thing.
  14:34/q07/p3 Blessing means anything agreeable, including conditions for benefit and harms warded off. — reasons: Alusi says this lexical sense is wider than the religious technical sense of a result praised in the end; it admits continuing existence and absent impediments.
14:34/q08 Why can God's blessings not be enumerated? [وَإِن تَعُدُّوا۟ نِعْمَتَ ٱللَّهِ لَا تُحْصُوهَآ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the point is sheer number, hidden detail within each gift, inability to preserve them, or inability to repay them.)
  14:34/q08/p1 Their number and the detail within even one favor exceed human counting and knowledge. — reasons: Tabrisi, Qurtubi and Ibn Atiyya name senses, bodily form, health, provision, creation and guidance; Qushayri adds protection from trials. Razi traces bread through seasons, elements, weather, iron, tools, anatomy and medicine, and nerves through every branch and organ; Alusi repeats these examples and argues that conditions, continuing existence and averted afflictions are unbounded. Baydawi distinguishes kinds from instances. Ibn Ashur notes overlooked breath, digestion and circulation. Tustari and Sulami name Muhammad as a surpassing blessing. Abu al-Darda warns against reducing favors to food and drink.
  14:34/q08/p2 People cannot fulfill gratitude for favors, since thanking is itself a further favor. — reasons: Talq calls for repentance morning and evening; Ibn Kathir transmits David's admission, Shafi'i's recursive gratitude, and a poem that every limb's praise falls short. Qushayri and Sulami stress unending obligation. Al-Sajjad calls acknowledging inability itself gratitude; Sulayman al-Taymi says God asks thanks according to capacity.
  14:34/q08/p3 They cannot all be preserved or remembered. — reasons: Al-Kalbi gives the memory sense; Ibn al-Jawzi cites 18:49 and 58:6 for exhaustive recording. Ibn Ashur observes that even known blessings are forgotten.
  14:34/q08/p4 They cannot be endured, performed, or managed in full. — reasons: Al-Farra compares 73:20 on keeping night-vigil times; others construe that verse as inability to bear it. Ibn al-Jawzi reports Muqatil's night-prayer capacity sense.
14:34/q09 What does إحصاء mean here and what is its origin? [تُحْصُوهَآ · meaning] (turns on: Counting, encompassing, recording, and capacity are reported senses.)
  14:34/q09/p1 It means counting accurately, originally with pebbles, and by extension encompassing a total. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi gives counting as fundamental, cites 72:28 for knowledge, and folds it into counting. Alusi cites al-A'sha's pebbles line and reports a rival account
