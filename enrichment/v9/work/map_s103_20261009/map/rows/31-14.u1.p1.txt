31:14/q12 What does fiṣāl mean here? [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: Whether fiṣāl names the specific end of breastfeeding or the broader period of early maternal dependence.)
  31:14/q12/p1 It means weaning, the child’s separation from the breast and end of suckling. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī defines the separation as no longer nursing; al-Jishumī derives the name from cutting off from the maternal breast; Bursevī compares a weaned camel calf, faṣīl. Ibn Jinnī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Ālūsī say faṣl is broader but fiṣāl particularly suits nursing. Al-Qurṭubī says both are dialect forms named by their endpoint.
  31:14/q12/p2 It can denote the whole span from conception through gestation, birth and earliest dependence on the mother. — reasons: Asad reports this broader philological usage.
31:14/q13 How is the weaning word read? [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ · readings] (turns on: Form and vowels of the noun: fiṣāl, faṣl, or a fatḥa on fiṣāl's fāʾ.)
  31:14/q13/p1 The common reading is fiṣāluhu, with an alif. — reasons: Al-Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān identify it as the reading of the other or most readers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says its form points to the counted nursing duration and endpoint. Ibn Jinnī, Abū ʿAlī and al-Ālūsī find it more apt to this nursing context.
  31:14/q13/p2 It is read faṣluhu without an alif, meaning the child's separation or weaning. — reasons: Reports name al-Ḥasan, Abū Rajāʾ, al-Jaḥdarī, Qatāda, Yaʿqūb, Ubayy, Ṭalḥa b. Muṣarrif and others; the named transmitters vary. Ibn Jinnī and Abū ʿAlī note faṣl is broader than specific weaning. Ibn al-Jawzī specifies fatḥa on fāʾ and sukūn on ṣād; al-Ṭabrisī describes the fatḥa on ṣād in a reported pronunciation. Al-Bayḍāwī uses it in discussing the maximum nursing term.
  31:14/q13/p3 Fiṣālihi is read with a fatḥa on the fāʾ. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī, Abū ʿImrān and al-Aʿmash.
31:14/q14 Does 'in two years' require nursing for the whole period? [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether fī names the full endpoint alone or allows a weaning event within the two-year interval.)
  31:14/q14/p1 The expression describes weaning at the completion of two full years, the complete nursing term. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn Kathīr compare 2:233; al-Ṭabrisī explicitly says two full years. Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Bursevī say the endpoint is two years. Al-Damghānī compares 2:233's two complete ḥawls and 2:240's ḥawl as a year. Al-Biqāʿī notes hardship through those years; Study Quran says full breastfeeding is best when possible, comparing 2:233 and 46:15.
  31:14/q14/p2 Two years is the normal or maximum period; parents may wean earlier when the child’s welfare warrants it. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī draws this from the preposition fī; Kuran Yolu compares 2:233. Asad says the literal phrase describes weaning within two years. This position need not deny that two years completes the term.
31:14/q15 How is 'his weaning is in two years' parsed? [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether to supply an implied word of completion or state the surface subject-predicate relation.)
  31:14/q15/p1 An implied 'completion' means his weaning is at the completion of two years. — reasons: Al-Akhfash supplies the omitted completion; al-Wāḥidī explains the completed two-year sense.
  31:14/q15/p2 The noun is subject and the prepositional phrase is predicate. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī gives this construction and says it foregrounds two years of maternal nursing hardship.
31:14/q16 Why use ʿāmayn for the two years? [عَامَيْنِ · rhetoric]
  31:14/q16/p1 ʿĀm can evoke ease or joy, unlike sana for hardship, reflecting parental joy even in demanding infant care. — reasons: Bursevī contrasts عام with سنة, the latter often used for hardship or drought; al-Biqāʿī says the parents rejoice in caring for the child despite the hard two years. Al-Damghānī relates the term to ḥawl as a year in 2:233 and 2:240.
31:14/q17 What does the two-year term govern for nursing duties and maintenance? [فِى عَامَيْنِ · law] (turns on: The notes distinguish the agreed maintenance period from stages of obligation, recommendation and permission.)
  31:14/q17/p1 Two years is the agreed nursing or maintenance period, and the father’s nursing wage ends then. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān state agreement on the period for maintenance or rulings while separating milk kinship. Bursevī says by agreement the father's nursing wage ceases after two years.
  31:14/q17/p2 Nursing is obligatory until the child is self-sufficient, recommended through two years, and permissible through two and a half. — reasons: Bursevī reports this framework with al-Shāfiʿī and says the remaining dispute is about the effect on milk kinship.
31:14/q18 When does nursing establish milk kinship or its prohibition? [وَفِصَٰلُهُۥ فِى عَامَيْنِ · law] (turns on: Whether the verse fixes two years as a hard legal limit, permits adjacent days or six months, or whether actual weaning or age rather than the verse's term controls.)
  31:14/q18/p1 Only nursing within two years establishes milk kinship; even a moment afterward does not. — reasons: Al-Shāfiʿī uses the two-year limit and a Prophetic report; Yaḥyā cites a report that there is no nursing after weaning and that Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās did not count nursing after two years. Al-Ālūsī lists al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, Abū Yūsuf, Muḥammad, al-Ṭaḥāwī and a report from Mālik. Elmalılı says the fatwa follows the two Imams and prefers it over Abū Ḥanīfa's precaution.
  31:14/q18/p2 Nursing for a few contiguous days or up to an adjacent month after two years still establishes milk kinship. — reasons: Al-Māwardī attributes a few extra days to Mālik; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes connected days; al-Qurṭubī mentions one adjacent month if nursing continues. The reports vary in precise extension.
  31:14/q18/p3 The prohibition extends six months beyond two years, for thirty months total. — reasons: Abū Ḥanīfa relates the thirty months of pregnancy and weaning in 46:15 to each named period; al-Ālūsī gives his analogy of two debts due in a year or two obligations from one debtor. Elmalılı calls it precaution and says the fatwa instead follows the two Imams.
  31:14/q18/p4 The prohibition has no fixed age limit and applies to adults as to children. — reasons: Al-Māwardī reports this from some Medinans.
  31:14/q18/p5 Only nursing in the first year establishes milk kinship. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī lists this among differing juristic opinions.
  31:14/q18/p6 Early weaning can end the effect of later milk, even before the two years expire. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī record disagreement on whether milk after early weaning still creates the prohibition.
31:14/q19 What minimum pregnancy duration follows from the two-year nursing term? [فِى عَامَيْنِ · law]
  31:14/q19/p1 Six months is the minimum: thirty months for pregnancy and weaning in 46:15 minus the full twenty-four months of nursing. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and other imams make the inference in Ibn Kathīr, comparing 46:15 with 2:233; Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Samarrāʾī state the same deduction, the latter attributing it to some jurists.
31:14/q20 What maximum gestation does ʿĀʾisha's report give in this discussion? [فِى عَامَيْنِ · reports]
  31:14/q20/p1 A child is never in the womb longer than two years, even by a spindle coil’s time. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports ʿĀʾisha's statement as treated as transmitted from the Prophet; it shortens the gestation allocation while preserving an explicit nursing term, although al-Ālūsī says the argument needs discussion.
31:14/q21 How does an ishkur relate grammatically to waṣṣaynā? [أَنِ ٱشْكُرْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether an explains the preceding command, supplies its governed verbal noun, or expresses its purpose or apposition.)
  31:14/q21/p1 An is explanatory: the command means 'We said, give thanks.' — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Wāḥidī's author of al-Naẓm, al-Nasafī, Elmalılı and Ṭabāṭabāʾī explain the charge this way. Al-Naḥḥās prefers this against the infinitival analysis; Abū Ḥayyān records that preference. Al-Ālūsī says the imperative does not bar this parse.
  31:14/q21/p2 An introduces an accusative verbal-noun complement governed by waṣṣaynā: God enjoined giving thanks. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Jawzī give the accusative parse; al-Samīn and al-Qurṭubī report it. Al-Ālūsī says an imperative does not invalidate the analysis.
  31:14/q21/p3 It expresses the command's purpose, or is an apposition to parents: care for them by gratitude. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī allows explanation, motivation or apposition; Bursevī allows purpose. Al-Ālūsī reconstructs li-taʿlīl or an apposition, calls the last remote, and answers the objection based on the imperative.
31:14/q22 What form does commanded gratitude to God and parents take? [ٱشْكُرْ لِى وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether to state the general obligation and its grounds or a concrete devotional expression; al-Ālūsī treats the latter as an example.)
  31:14/q22/p1 Thank God for creation, faith and His blessings through praise and obedience; thank parents for birth, raising and care through kindness, obedience, service, ties and prayer. — reasons: Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī name Islam or faith and parents' benefits; Ibn ʿAbbās glosses gratitude as obedience. Al-Ṭabarī adds care until the child's strength matures. Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and Study Quran contrast praise and obedience with kindness and kinship.
