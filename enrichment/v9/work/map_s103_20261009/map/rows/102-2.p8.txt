[BINTSHATI:v1p199/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بعض المفسرين | reports | One interpretation takes the visit literally: people went to the graves to count their dead.
[BINTSHATI:v1p199/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أبو حيان | rejects | A second interpretation treats visiting as metaphorical mention of dead ancestors while boasting; Abū Ḥayyān rejects it because the wording visit is awkward for that sense.
[BINTSHATI:v1p199/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · بنو سهم وبنو عبد مناف | reports | A reported occasion says these clans competed over numbers, then counted their dead as well as the living after one claimed losses in pre-Islamic feuding.
[BINTSHATI:v1p199/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · الطبري | prefers | A third interpretation takes visiting the graves as dying; al-Ṭabarī adopts it, citing familiar Arabic usage and poetry by Jarīr.
[BINTSHATI:v1p200/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | Reading grave-visiting as death implies a temporary stay: visitors leave, so the wording itself hints at resurrection, reckoning, and recompense.
[BINTSHATI:v1p200/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أعرابي | reports | A Bedouin who heard the verse inferred resurrection, saying visitors depart rather than remain.
[BINTSHATI:v1p200/r3] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · عمر بن عبد العزيز | reports | ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz is also reported to have expressed a view like the Bedouin’s insight.
[BINTSHATI:v1p200/r4] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · أبو حيان | reports | Abū Ḥayyān passes quickly over the Bedouin’s insight and instead cites a view that the verse rebukes boasting through repeated cemetery visits, distinguishing later permitted visits for admonition.
[BINTSHATI:v1p200/r5] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | holds | Al-Maqābir appears only in this verse, while qubūr appears five times and the singular qabr is used in al-Tawbah. | mentions 9:84
[BINTSHATI:v1p201/r1] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | prefers | Although maqābir suits takāthur in sound, its stronger reason is semantic: it evokes the vast collective cemetery across ages and classes, broader than individual graves. | mentions 9:84
[BINTSHATI:v1p201/r2] BINTSHATI, d. 1419 · author | rejects | Reducing maqābir to a lexical synonym for graves misses its broad setting and the verse’s compressed journey from worldly rivalry to death.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p275/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | Although rhetoricians notice the cadence of al-maqābir, its broader sense is decisive: it evokes the gathering of the dead across ages and classes, unlike qubūr, and suits the end of worldly accumulation.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:102:2:anmerkung/r1] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | prefers | The grave phrase most simply means until death or throughout one's life.
[CORPUSCORANICUM:102:2:anmerkung/r2] CORPUSCORANICUM · author | rejects | Tradition says Meccans counted dead relatives at graves to include them in rivalry for more; Paret treats the perfect as evidence of a past event, but the author favors a general statement and gnomic aspect.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p426/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Islahi pairs al-Takāthur with al-Qāriʿah because both address what matters in the Hereafter. | mentions 101:6, 101:8, 101:11
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p426/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Qāriʿah teaches that good deeds alone weigh in the Hereafter: more good deeds bring salvation, while greater evil brings lasting doom despite worldly treasure. | mentions 101:6, 101:8, 101:11
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p426/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | This sūrah warns people who spend life competing for wealth and luxuries that they will face the dreadful consequences of forgetting accountability.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p428/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Zurtum derives from a noun meaning simply to see, not the Urdu sense of a sacred visitation; here it means seeing the graves, or being consigned to them.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p428/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · Ḥam āsī poet | reports | A Ḥamāsī poem describes seeing one’s land after long absence and finding one’s friends gone while the land remains unchanged, illustrating the sense of visiting or seeing.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p428/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Although Arabs kept and boasted of records of their dead, that custom is not what this verse means.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p428/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The grave image preserves the rhyme and conveys pity for people who forfeited the Hereafter’s reward through endless rivalry for wealth.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:102:1/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Maqabir is the plural of maqbara, meaning graves; commentators read “you visited the graves” metaphorically as dying while the contest continued, metaphorically as boasting over the dead, or literally as visiting graves to boast.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:102:1/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Müfessirler | reports | Pre-Islamic Arabs boasted of abundant wealth, children, kin, and servants, then visited ancestors' graves to prove their tribe's superiority through its many notable dead.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:102:1/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Even if reports describe a specific occasion, the general target is human boasting in abundant wealth, offspring, and supporters; true superiority appears in the afterlife.
