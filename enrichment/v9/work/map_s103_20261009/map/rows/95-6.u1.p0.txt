## THE CURRENT MAP
95:6/q01 Who are those excepted? [ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent] (turns on: Whether the general phrase is retained or narrowed by reported reciter and allegorical interpretations.)
  95:6/q01/p1 Believers who do righteous deeds generally. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and Ibn al-Jawzī take the singular 'human' collectively; al-Biqāʿī calls the righteous few; al-Islāḥī says they honor innate guidance and accept the messengers. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Ālūsī object to narrowing the apparent general wording to reciters.
  95:6/q01/p2 The exception specifically names Qurʾān reciters. — reasons: ʿIkrima and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās say recitation averts the senility in which one knows nothing after knowledge; al-Ālūsī says al-Ḥākim authenticated the Ibn ʿAbbās report and al-Bayhaqī included it in Shuʿab. Al-Ālūsī disputes both the restriction and the claim that no reciter becomes senile.
  95:6/q01/p3 The exception concerns a believer who knows and acts on knowledge. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī and Abū al-Layth say such a person retains reason and avoids senility; this is a narrower proposed referent.
  95:6/q01/p4 The exception allegorically names the four caliphs. — reasons: An explicitly weak Ibn ʿAbbās report makes 'human' Muḥammad and the lowest the idolaters, alongside symbolic identifications of the oath's places.
95:6/q02 From what fate are believers excepted? [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation] (turns on: The meaning of the preceding descent and whether the exception removes believers from that descent or from a consequence of it.)
  95:6/q02/p1 From Hell, misery, punishment, or spiritual degradation. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ḥasan compare the believers excepted from loss in 103:2–3; Mujāhid glosses the exception as whoever believes. Al-Qushayrī also describes misery and disbelief. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues the connected exception makes bodily aging unlikely, since all humans age. Al-Qutb and al-Islāḥī explain the fall as corruption of fitra; Study Quran finds inner spiritual fall more likely. Abū al-ʿĀliya calls Hell the worst form.
  95:6/q02/p2 From decrepit old age or loss of reason itself. — reasons: Reports from ʿIkrima and Ibn ʿAbbās specify Qurʾān reciters; al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī state believers avoid senility even in long life. Al-Biqāʿī says reason's light increases while desire fades. Al-Ālūsī doubts that all reciters avoid decrepitude.
  95:6/q02/p3 From losing reward when old age impairs reason and work, although believers can still grow old. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī prefers this: prior righteousness is still credited in senility; his other report supplies an implied result after the physical decline. Al-Naḥḥās supplies omitted words about lost ability to worship. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the bodily-aging reading because the exception seems connected yet aging applies to everyone.
  95:6/q02/p4 From an ugly or low state at resurrection, with added beauty and radiance instead. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī links the collective pronoun to humankind and sets the contrast specifically at resurrection.
  95:6/q02/p5 From loss of the original fitra after idolatry, by returning through faith to the best form. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says shirk had obscured innate monotheism, while faith restores it and deeds deepen it; al-Biqāʿī and al-Qutb describe reason, divine guidance, and good works preserving or perfecting original nature. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 91:7–8, 35:10, 22:37, 58:11 and 20:75 for ascent through faith and deeds.
95:6/q03 How is the exception construed grammatically? [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · grammar] (turns on: What the preceding 'human' and descent denote, and whether exception removes believers from the intended ruling; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds a syntactic argument from fāʾ.)
  95:6/q03/p1 Connected to humankind when the prior descent means Hell or a fallen state. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and Ibn al-Jawzī explain that singular 'human' denotes the species; al-Ṭabarī allows plural exception and plural construal of the superlative. Al-Naḥḥās instead relates it to the plural sense of 'We returned him.' Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī take the exception as connected; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ excludes detachment.
  95:6/q03/p2 Disconnected when the prior descent means senility: 'but' righteous elderly receive reward. — reasons: Believers also age, so the clause shifts to their enduring recompense; al-Ṭabarī explicitly permits this. Al-Ālūsī says intended exclusion from the ruling, rather than literal membership, decides connection, and the clause answers an inference of equal outcomes from equal aging. Ibn ʿĀshūr denies this parsing because of the fāʾ.
  95:6/q03/p3 Connected even with senility, by excepting believers from loss of understanding or from an implied failure of deeds. — reasons: Some early exegetes treat the collective singular as its antecedent; another group supplies an omitted consequence: minds and deeds fail, but former reward remains. Al-Naḥḥās reports this ellipsis.
  95:6/q03/p4 Disconnected when 'human' is restricted to an unbeliever. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a view that makes the preceding human an unbeliever and the return Hell or bodily reversal; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a disconnected exception from unbelievers cast into Hell.
95:6/q04 What does faith do for the people in the exception? [ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
  95:6/q04/p1 Faith in God and His messengers preserves or restores the original human disposition and directs the person upward. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says divine gentleness lets reason and fitra overcome desire and believers remain firm as in 41:30; al-Qutb calls faith a cord and light linking nature to its Creator; Ibn ʿĀshūr says faith returns idolaters to innate monotheism and is the fruit of rational constitution; al-Islāḥī includes accepting messengers and accountability.
95:6/q05 What is the role of righteous deeds alongside faith? [وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the deeds phrase states a condition or praises believers by describing faith's usual outcome.)
  95:6/q05/p1 They manifest and perfect faith, preserve fitra, and bring further virtue. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī calls deeds beautiful acts and words, shaped by knowledge and the prophetic path into justice and beneficence; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the law makes them deepen character and cites the hadith about perfecting noble character. Al-Qutb says faith and deeds complete nature. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites Qurʾānic ascent parallels.
  95:6/q05/p2 Their mention praises believers descriptively, without restricting which believers are excepted. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says faith motivates action and the expression describes believers as they were at revelation.
  95:6/q05/p3 Faith and righteous deeds are the stated condition for escaping the low fate and earning reward. — reasons: Al-Islāḥī says the oaths establish this moral rule; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares al-ʿAṣr's added mutual counsel, which affects additional reward rather than this basic exemption. Al-Elmali says the deeds accord with God's pleasure.
95:6/q06 Why is fāʾ used in 'fa-lahum,' unlike the parallel reward clause in al-Inshiqāq? [فَلَهُمْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether fāʾ is licensed by a conditional or causal reading, serves the sura's rhetoric, or constrains the exception's syntax.)
  95:6/q06/p1 It joins two permissible Arabic constructions or follows a conditional sense in the exception. — reasons: Al-Nasafī says the verse combines two accepted usages despite 84:25 omitting fāʾ; al-Bursawī says the sense of 'but' can carry conditional force, or fāʾ is causal when Paradise rather than punishment is the outcome.
  95:6/q06/p2 It suits al-Tīn's context and its emphasis on assured, continuing reward. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-Inshiqāq details disbelievers and briefly mentions believers, while al-Tīn does not expressly identify the fallen; al-Inshiqāq opens with human toil, whereas al-Tīn honors humanity. Its fāʾ connects the reward naturally. Al-Bayḍāwī calls the clause a confirmation of the exception's result.
  95:6/q06/p3 The fāʾ rules out a disconnected exception. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr makes the particle a grammatical argument against treating believers as an unrelated new subject.
95:6/q07 What reward is promised to believers in the verse? [فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the reward is viewed as continued credit for former works, recompense for patient endurance, or the lasting final recompense; these can coexist in the notes.)
  95:6/q07/p1 Former good deeds keep being credited when old age, illness, or travel prevents their performance. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibrāhīm, Qatāda and ʿIkrima describe continued credit from youth and health; Ibrāhīm and ʿIkrima specify the best former deeds. Ibn Qutayba says God knows they would continue if strong. Prophetic reports apply the rule to illness and travel; the recording angels continue good deeds. Al-Biqāʿī adds sincere resolve and divine grace.
  95:6/q07/p2 Righteous elderly people earn continuing reward for obedience, patience in frailty, and worship despite weakness. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Bursawī stress patient response to the trial itself; Abū Ḥayyān calls al-Zamakhsharī's elaboration excessive.
  95:6/q07/p3 They receive lasting honor, salvation, or Paradise, in this life and especially the next. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says physical decline does not diminish afterlife reward; al-Qāshānī pictures gardens of hearts and souls, supplied by the holy world; al-Bursawī locates it in the abode of honor; al-Elmali describes absolute beauty and a good final state; Study Quran specifies Heaven's bounties.
  95:6/q07/p4 The reward reflects deeds and sincere effort but originates and continues by God's grace. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī distinguishes reward's relation to action from God's favor in both founding and extending it; an elderly person's unfulfilled intention preserves the habitua
