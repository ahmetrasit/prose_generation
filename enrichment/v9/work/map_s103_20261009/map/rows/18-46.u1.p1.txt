18:46/q11 What does ṣāliḥāt denote in this phrase? [ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the adjective is understood through righteous agents or beneficial things.)
  18:46/q11/p1 Righteous actors, named for the actions they perform. — reasons: Al-Mawardi says the righteous person performs the right deed.
  18:46/q11/p2 Beneficial things, named for their soundness. — reasons: Al-Mawardi gives this alternative.
18:46/q12 How does bāqiyāt work grammatically? [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ · grammar]
  18:46/q12/p1 It qualifies omitted words or deeds, with the enduring reward understood by ellipsis. — reasons: Al-Alusi also allows attachment to a deleted noun.
18:46/q13 Why does bāqiyāt precede ṣāliḥāt? [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · rhetoric]
  18:46/q13/p1 The reversed expected order stresses permanence against vanishing wealth and sons. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says ṣāliḥāt normally comes first as a noun for good works, citing 18:107 and Jarir's verse; the preceding scattered-plant parable (18:45) supplies the omitted thought that worldly ornaments vanish, while 'enduring' reinforces it. He also mentions 13:26.
18:46/q14 What does 'better in reward' mean? [خَيْرٌ عِندَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًۭا · interpretation]
  18:46/q14/p1 Righteous deeds yield superior, lasting recompense with God in the Hereafter. — reasons: Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tha'labi, al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Biqa'i, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabataba'i, Asad and Islahi contrast certain enduring divine reward with wealth, sons and polytheists' recompense. Al-Razi argues spiritual and intellectual goods exceed sensory ones, and the Hereafter unites both. Al-Alusi says 'with your Lord' may mean in the Hereafter or by God's judgment; al-Durr distinguishes the believer's hope and the polytheist's reward.
18:46/q15 What does thawāb mean here? [ثَوَابًۭا · meaning] (turns on: Whether thawāb is glossed as recompense or benefit.)
  18:46/q15/p1 Recompense or reward, from the notion of returning. — reasons: Jishumi gives the root explanation; al-Alusi allows reward and recompense.
  18:46/q15/p2 Benefit. — reasons: Al-Alusi reports this alternative gloss.
18:46/q16 What does 'better in hope' mean? [وَخَيْرٌ أَمَلًۭا · interpretation]
  18:46/q16/p1 Righteous deeds are a more reliable and worthy object of hope than wealth and children. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba glosses the best thing one can hope for; al-Wahidi says better than what one hopes from wealth and children. Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Nasafi contrast failible worldly expectations with God's true promise. Al-Baydawi, al-Biqa'i, Bursevi and al-Alusi say deeds fulfill hopes in the afterlife; Tabataba'i contrasts divine mercy and honor with deceiving worldly hopes. Yahya, Jishumi, al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, al-Durr and Islahi encourage hope in divine reward. Al-Mawardi says hope is truer and better already in this world.
18:46/q17 What does amal mean here? [أَمَلًۭا · meaning]
  18:46/q17/p1 Hope or expectation. — reasons: Jishumi treats these as near synonyms; Ibn Qutayba explains it as what one hopes for.
18:46/q18 What is the grammar of thawāban and amalan? [ثَوَابًۭا أَمَلًۭا · grammar]
  18:46/q18/p1 They are accusative specifications distinguishing the two comparisons. — reasons: Jishumi gives this parsing.
18:46/q19 How can the verse call the deeds 'better' than worldly adornment? [خَيْرٌ وَخَيْرٌ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the comparison is rhetorical, answers mistaken evaluation, or compares recompenses.)
  18:46/q19/p1 The comparative need not grant genuine goodness to the worldly alternative. — reasons: Al-Wahidi cites Arabic comparison with an inferior that has no good, including 25:24; al-Qurtubi likewise points to Paradise as a 'better' resting place.
  18:46/q19/p2 The comparison answers what ignorant people merely suppose is good. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi reports the supposition view. Jishumi allows a hypothetical answer to those claiming another can reward, even though God would be best.
  18:46/q19/p3 It compares actual recompenses for different deeds without needing a real divine rival. — reasons: Jishumi offers this alternative.
18:46/q20 Why repeat 'better' for reward and hope? [خَيْرٌ وَخَيْرٌ · rhetoric]
  18:46/q20/p1 It emphasizes superiority or marks two distinct grounds for it. — reasons: Al-Alusi gives both explanations.
18:46/q21 What do reports say the remembrance formulas accomplish? [ٱلْبَٰقِيَٰتُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ · reports] (turns on: The reports describe different benefits and contexts rather than competing definitions.)
  18:46/q21/p1 They plant trees or other growth in Paradise. — reasons: Abu Ayyub's Ascension report has Abraham describe good spacious soil and hawqala as planting; al-Tirmidhi's Ibn Mas'ud report adds sweet water and barren ground, graded hasan gharīb. Ibn Majah reports that each of four formulas plants a tree for its reciter. Muhammad ibn Ka'b's disputed hawqala is confirmed by Salim through Abu Ayyub.
  18:46/q21/p2 They shield from the Fire and aid their reciter on Judgment Day. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi and al-Durr report that the formulas surround their reciter; al-Tabrisi and Bursevi report a shield. Al-Qurtubi cites a report graded sound by Abd al-Haqq. Al-Hasan's dream has a voice naming four formulas as protection against fear.
  18:46/q21/p3 They erase sins like leaves falling from a tree. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi, al-Qurtubi, al-Durr and al-Alusi cite versions. Al-Tirmidhi calls one gharīb and notes al-A'mash saw but did not hear Anas; Ibn Majah reports a comparable version via Abu al-Darda. They are also called Paradise's treasures.
  18:46/q21/p4 They are urged especially when harder worship or fighting is beyond one's ability. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi reports the Prophet's counsel; Ibn al-Jawzi transmits it via Abu Hurayra. Al-Baghawi's Abu Sa'id report also urges increasing the formulas and gives no grading.
  18:46/q21/p5 They outweigh worldly goods and other sacrifices. — reasons: Al-Baghawi reports the Prophet calling them the best words and dearer than all the sun rises over. Al-Durr reports Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas comparing them to the Nile and Euphrates, the Prophet preferring them to equal dinars, and Abd Allah ibn Amr preferring them to bridled battle horses.
  18:46/q21/p6 A night reciter who says each of four formulas a hundred times after ablution is forgiven except for bloodshed. — reasons: Ibn Kathir cites the report through Sa'd ibn Janadah; another Sa'd report says the Prophet taught him three short suras and the four formulas after his conversion.
  18:46/q21/p7 They bring counted merits and divine remembrance. — reasons: Al-Durr reports good deeds and erased bad deeds for particular formulas; a heavenly circuit around the Throne with God's remembrance of their speaker; angels carrying phrases upward and praying for the speaker; and their use as a substitute for someone unable to memorize Qur'an.
  18:46/q21/p8 Four formulas are among five weighty deeds along with patiently losing a righteous child. — reasons: Ibn Kathir also reports five beliefs leading to Paradise: God, Last Day, Paradise, Hell, resurrection and reckoning; the enumerated beliefs vary in count from the stated five.
  18:46/q21/p9 A report pairs the four formulas with refusal to support or believe unjust rulers. — reasons: Ibn Kathir gives the account through an unnamed Ansari.
  18:46/q21/p10 A personal report values supplication for guidance and worship above hoarded gold and silver. — reasons: Ibn Kathir gives Shaddad ibn Aws's regret at asking for a knife to play with, then the Prophetic prayer for steadfastness, gratitude, sound heart, truthful tongue, good, refuge and forgiveness; Ahmad and al-Nasa'i transmit it.
18:46/q22 What larger lesson does the verse draw about worldly attachments? [* · interpretation]
  18:46/q22/p1 Prefer lasting righteous action and hope in the Hereafter to pride or dependence on perishing wealth and family. — reasons: Jishumi urges Paradise and detachment because life passes quickly; al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi quote Ali's two harvests, though God may give both. Al-Qurtubi warns that wealth, women and power change hands. Ibn Kathir urges worship rather than endless accumulation or worry. Al-Razi ranks lasting spiritual and intellectual happiness above sensory goods. Al-Biqa'i says worldly possessions fail when most needed. Kuran Yolu disapproves of arrogance and pressure against the poor; it also cites 7:32 that lawful enjoyment is permitted. Bursevi quotes ascetic remembrance of death. Ibn Ashur and Study Quran warn against mistaking status for favor.
18:46/q23 What inward meanings are drawn from the verse? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether the enduring good is described as disciplined attachment, transformed adornment, or granted inward nearness.)
  18:46/q23/p1 The soul clings to worldly shares, while the spirit turns to reliance, certainty and worship. — reasons: Al-Sulami contrasts family, wealth and children with reliance, tranquility, trust and certainty. Al-Qushayri says heedless people adorn themselves with money and sons, the connected with works and certainty; status, praise and familiar attachments can also be worldly shares, even if results arrive later.
  18:46/q23/p2 Escape from worldly ornament requires inward knowledge and love, and outward service and aspiration. — reasons: Al-Sulami says such people witness their King in nearness; Bursevi repeats the inward and outward program.
  18:46/q23/p3 Enduring deeds are inward divine light or nearness. — reasons: Al-Qushayri reports lights of monotheism beyond veiling, divine qualities adorning hidden secrets and scenting heaven, or intimacy already granted from the unseen; al-Alusi reports lasting love, knowledge, sincere unity and turning away from others.
