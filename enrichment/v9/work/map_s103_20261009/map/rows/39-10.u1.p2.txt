39:10/q18 What does بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ mean for the reward? [أَجْرَهُم بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether ḥisāb means numerical measure, matching deeds, restricted amount, or a person's judgment audit.)
  39:10/q18/p1 It is immeasurably abundant, beyond counting, weighing, or any scale. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda deny measure or scale; al-Zajjāj says reward is poured out, while spiritual joy cannot literally be weighed and measurement imagery conveys abundance. Al-Māwardī cites a rajaz verse about generous unmeasured watering and ʿAlī's handfuls. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it a metaphor for uncountable plenty; Ibn ʿAbbās says no calculator can encompass it. Bursawī adds that reward answers suffering, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says ajr in Qur'anic usage is afterlife reward.
  39:10/q18/p2 The patient undergo no accounting, reproach, record, or audit. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Māwardī's al-Suddī mention no follow-up claim; Ibn ʿAṭiyya associates the patient with those who enter Paradise unreckoned. Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī permit this parse; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers no record, scale or audit, and Study Quran reports no weighing.
  39:10/q18/p3 It exceeds what deeds alone would calculate or deserve. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās says an exact deed-for-deed match would be counted; Ibn Jurayj says God increases the calculated amount. Al-Jubbāʾī proposes deserved recompense plus grace, while al-Qāḍī objects that due reward alone is still reward. Ibn ʿĀshūr says innamā corrects the assumption of merely matched hardship.
  39:10/q18/p4 The reward is endless, because countable things are finite. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reasons from the finitude of what can be enumerated; al-Qāshānī calls spiritual gardens infinite unlike finite material effects, and Ālūsī calls manifestations endless, citing 55:29.
  39:10/q18/p5 Its magnitude exceeds human conception. — reasons: Al-Rāzī cites a Prophetic report that Paradise is greater than its people imagined; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī describe abundance beyond enumeration or imagination.
  39:10/q18/p6 The abundant reward is located in Paradise. — reasons: Al-Suddī gives the location; Muqātil calls it Paradise and provisions.
  39:10/q18/p7 The reward has no constriction or restriction. — reasons: Abū Muslim contrasts unrestricted generosity with a measured and therefore constrained allotment; al-Māwardī gives wide, freely bestowed reward.
  39:10/q18/p8 It is manifestations of God without limit. — reasons: Ālūsī cites 55:29 to explain never-ending divine manifestations; al-Qāshānī describes gardens of attributes beyond material measure.
39:10/q19 How does the closing promise work grammatically and rhetorically? [إِنَّمَا يُوَفَّى ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ أَجْرَهُم بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the circumstantial phrase describes the reward or its recipients.)
  39:10/q19/p1 بغير حساب is circumstantial for the reward, expressing uncountable abundance. — reasons: Al-Samīn permits the reward attachment; al-Nasafī says it describes the reward circumstantially. Ālūsī takes abundance as the apparent sense and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this analysis while preferring no accounting of persons.
  39:10/q19/p2 بغير حساب is circumstantial for the patient, who are not called to account. — reasons: Al-Samīn permits the people attachment; Ālūsī calls it less immediate. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers it with no audit, record or scale.
  39:10/q19/p3 The passive verb foregrounds patience as the reason for full recompense. — reasons: Ālūsī says the addressees are implicitly assured of the same reward as earlier patient people; Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain tawfiya as giving in full.
  39:10/q19/p4 إِنَّمَا restricts the clause to reward without account, exceeding ordinary recompense. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says it corrects an expectation that hardship receives only an equal measured return; he says calling it “their reward” comforts them, while all is divine grace, and compares 84:25.
39:10/q20 Does calling recompense أَجْرَهُم mean it is owed by God? [أَجْرَهُم · theology] (turns on: Whether ajr denotes an entitlement earned by human acts or God's promised gift.)
  39:10/q20/p1 No; it is reward secured by God's promise and grace. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says the term ajr grants no entitlement. Ibn ʿĀshūr says “their reward” comforts believers by treating it as earned, though everything remains grace.
  39:10/q20/p2 The patient receive deserved reward plus a gratuitous excess. — reasons: Al-Jubbāʾī gives this account of unreckoned abundance. Al-Qāḍī rejects it, reasoning that due recompense alone would still be reward.
  39:10/q20/p3 Good-doers and evildoers deserve fitting recompense for their own acts. — reasons: Al-Jishumī says the verse depends on people's acting and God's just rewarding of good and evil.
39:10/q21 Which reports illustrate the special reward for afflicted patient people? [ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ أَجْرَهُم بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍۢ · reports] (turns on: Whether reports distinguish the afflicted by absence of scale, absence of record, particular acts of patience, or general trial.)
  39:10/q21/p1 Afflicted people receive poured-out reward without a scale or record, making the spared envy them. — reasons: Anas reports contrast measured worship with limitless affliction reward; al-Ṭabrisī says no scale or register when balances appear. Al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Durr and Bursawī repeat variants. Some say healthy people wish their skins had been cut with scissors; Ibn Masʿūd reports the same wish. Al-Durr adds a report that God may intensify trials of a beloved servant while hearing supplication, then grant, store or avert harm.
  39:10/q21/p2 The afflicted receive unmeasured reward beneath a tree of tribulation in Paradise. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī recounts Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī's report of obligations, contentment and the tree; al-Qurṭubī attributes a version to al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī from his grandfather and says it concludes by reciting this verse; al-Durr records a tree and no register or scale.
  39:10/q21/p3 Further Prophetic counsel commends endurance and ties greater trials to greater rank. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī reports advice to endure harm and a warning through Abū Dharr that impatience leads toward Hell, citing 13:24. Bursawī reports prophets are tested most, that trial can raise one to an otherwise unreachable rank, and that loved servants receive trials whose acceptance brings divine pleasure.
  39:10/q21/p4 The Prophet sought ever more reward for his community until this verse satisfied him. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the sequence 2:261 then 2:245 then 39:10; Bursawī's Sufyān report starts from tenfold at 6:160, then seven-hundredfold at 2:261, before this verse.
  39:10/q21/p5 The afflicted have a scale but no record. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits this distinctive report through ʿAbd Allāh ibn Sinān from Abū ʿAbd Allāh, followed by the Prophet's recitation of the verse; he notes al-Durr's Anas transmission of a related account.
  39:10/q21/p6 Seventy thousand enter Paradise unreckoned through trust in God. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a Prophetic group who shun omens, cauterization and requesting incantations, and rely on God.
  39:10/q21/p7 A woman chose patience with illness over cure and was promised no reckoning. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī cites al-Bazzār and Ibn Ḥibbān's Ṣaḥīḥ transmission from Abū Hurayra.
  39:10/q21/p8 Fasting is rewarded by God Himself beyond the scale used for other works. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī cites the divine saying and scholars' comparison with other deeds, also related from ʿAlī.
39:10/q22 How does patience support the migration assurance? [ٱلصَّٰبِرُونَ · links]
  39:10/q22/p1 The close reassures migrants that separation and hardship earn a special abundant reward. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the conclusion to promised good and safe lands. Ālūsī says patient migrants merit special recompense beyond those able to worship at home; al-Biqāʿī observes patience continues with every breath and differs in hidden strength. Kuranyolu ties sacrifice to 4:97–100.
  39:10/q22/p2 The statement has broader reach than its migration setting. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya extends it to obedience and support of faith; Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress all affliction and patience, while al-ʿĀlūsī says the verse praises patience's high merit.
