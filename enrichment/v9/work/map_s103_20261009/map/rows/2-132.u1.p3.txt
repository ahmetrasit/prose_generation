  2:132/q37/p1 Renewed kalām should preserve ethical roots while updating its methods to address contemporary science and culture. — reasons: Y. Khūlī says Islamic identity can resist cultural loss; classical kalām proved creed and routed natural science through ontology and origination proofs, while new kalām starts from accepted creed and aims at historical presence. He favors connection with the old alongside an epistemological break, invoking Bachelard and Ḥasan Ḥanafī, and explains revolution as effective progress. He argues methods endure better than changing scientific claims, so nature belongs in epistemology and philosophy of science rather than ideology; modern science uses creative hypotheses, tests and revisions rather than pure induction. He compares Newtonian determinism with relativity and quantum observation, and cites Heisenberg, Bohr and Margolis on science as a human dialogue/adventure.
