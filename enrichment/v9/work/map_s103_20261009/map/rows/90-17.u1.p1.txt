90:17/q09 What does marḥama mean, and to whom does it extend? [بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether marḥama denotes human compassion, particular recipients or kin, acts attracting divine mercy, or the merciful next life.)
  90:17/q09/p1 Compassion and merciful action toward people and other creatures, including fellow believers. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly says all people; Tustarī says creatures, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya says mutual care sustains human life. Al-Māwardī includes one another and everyone. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and others give the broad gloss. Al-Bursawī says it is wider than pity for only visibly deserving people.
  90:17/q09/p2 It especially calls for care of the poor, weak, oppressed, and other vulnerable people. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī names orphans, poor and weak; al-Jishumī names needy believers, relatives and oppressed people. Al-Rāzī includes helping the oppressed and preventing imminent wrongdoing. Al-Ṭabrisī extends it from poor and needy to all. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to feeding and 89:18.
  90:17/q09/p3 It includes maintaining ties of kinship. — reasons: Muqātil explicitly defines it as preserving family ties and not cutting them; al-Jishumī lists relatives among mercy's recipients.
  90:17/q09/p4 It means doing or urging whatever brings God's mercy. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this breadth; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī offer it alongside compassion to creatures.
  90:17/q09/p5 It refers to the Hereafter, the abode of mercy, and turning away from worldly life toward it. — reasons: Al-Māwardī records this alternative, also preserved in his full account.
90:17/q10 What does the form marḥama contribute? [ٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · meaning]
  90:17/q10/p1 It is a verbal noun for mercy; its form expresses a wide, encompassing compassion. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a verbal noun. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says marḥama is broader than raḥma and observes the mafʿala pattern shared with famine, kinship, destitution, and the right and left groups.
90:17/q11 Why repeat the verb and preposition for each mutual counsel? [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · rhetoric]
  90:17/q11/p1 The repetition emphasizes each virtue separately and strengthens the call. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās notes the added emphasis. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says repeating both verb and preposition is stronger than only the preposition or a simple conjunction; al-Biqāʿī says the repeat highlights how few practice mercy.
90:17/q12 Why is patience mentioned before mercy? [بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · rhetoric]
  90:17/q12/p1 The ascent's preceding toil calls first for patience, then its freeing and feeding call for mercy. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī points to the two paths, hardship and difficult climb before the vulnerable recipients of feeding; al-Biqāʿī makes mercy the fruit of endurance and justice, a further ascent moderating excess and deficiency. Al-Iṣlāḥī says sympathy needs patience because the good work opposes desire.
90:17/q13 Why single out patience and mercy as the believers' paired virtues? [بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the pair is read as religious foundations, cardinal virtues, a shared social ethic, or a contrast with the idolaters.)
  90:17/q13/p1 They gather duty toward God and care for creation, the foundations of obedience. — reasons: Al-Rāzī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī describe patience as honoring God's command and mercy as compassion for creatures; cited investigators call sincerity with God and good character with people the foundations of Sufism. Ibn ʿĀshūr says all good deeds need restraint of desire and mercy sustains the community, comparing 48:29.
  90:17/q13/p2 They complete a progression of four cardinal virtues: generosity or chastity, faith as wisdom, patience as courage, and mercy as justice. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says courage depends on certainty and each named trait stands for its broader virtue; al-Bursawī quotes a teacher who calls feeding in hardship supreme restraint, faith wisdom, patience courage and mercy justice. Al-Biqāʿī calls faith wisdom's second ascent, courage the third and mercy a fourth balanced pass.
  90:17/q13/p3 They define a community that resists injustice with steadfastness and merciful solidarity. — reasons: Quṭb stresses collective mutual support alongside personal judgment; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says genuine faith bears struggle for good and refusal to stay silent about injustice. Kur'an Yolu identifies slavery, poverty and mercilessness as the era's grave problems; these duties are examples of Islamic social ethics, not an exhaustive list.
  90:17/q13/p4 Their praise exposes the idolaters' lack of patience and compassion. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the indirect rebuke is explicit in passages on calling to God, patience, honoring orphans and feeding the poor: 41:33, 41:35, 89:17 and 89:18.
90:17/q14 How does this verse complete the account of crossing the obstacle? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the ascent is a set of acts requiring faith, a moral progression toward it, or a socially grounded sequence.)
  90:17/q14/p1 The earlier freeing and feeding are joined by faith, patience, and mercy as the complete route. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ counts the three parts under one negative. Al-Iṣlāḥī says bodily faculties and moral awareness in 8–17 should overcome greed and serve God. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the Qur'an itself defines the obstacle, rejecting a need for allegorical readings. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the explicit deeds already replace the usual phrase 'righteous deeds' after faith; patience and mercy follow the difficult works.
  90:17/q14/p2 The preceding social acts can show innate purity before faith, which then purifies and elevates the person. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says noble pre-conversion conduct shows high aspiration and freedom from vanity; the convert accepts guidance despite becoming a follower after being followed. He links insight to 20:54 and 20:128 and says faith brings illumination and duties contrary to nature.
  90:17/q14/p3 The verse makes an organized society of mercy and justice the setting for sincere faith. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reads the order literally: dignity through emancipation and feeding comes before faith and its communal counsels; a society tolerating enslavement and hunger cannot sustain sincere belief.
  90:17/q14/p4 Failure to undertake the deeds and join believers shows that the moral ascent remains uncrossed. — reasons: Al-Nasafī describes failure to thank the Benefactor; al-Iṣlāḥī says the person has not overcome the barrier to moral and spiritual growth.
90:17/q15 How does patience and mercy echo the rest of this sura? [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْمَرْحَمَةِ · links] (turns on: Whether the close is read as integral thematic development or, in Corpus Coranicum, a secondary stanza.)
  90:17/q15/p1 The sura's toil, parenthood, persecution, ascent, slavery, hunger, and poverty all call for patience and mercy. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī relates the parent and child oath to the patience of raising and protecting children; the Prophet's Meccan abuse to patience; and his mercy at the conquest to the city's reference. The boastful spender, enslaved person, famine, orphan and destitute person call for compassion. He finds five threads: toil, breadth of meaning, collective action, patience and mercy.
  90:17/q15/p2 The verse extends the sura's collective pattern. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī connects parents and offspring, paired faculties and paths, multiple ascent duties, mutual advice and the gathered unbelievers; he says 'labad' contributes to the same pattern, unlike a plain word for many, comparing 80:20 and 76:3.
  90:17/q15/p3 The close shifts the ethical demand into the opposed destinies of saved and damned. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum calls 17–20 probably secondary: it extends the ethical demand into group identities amid conflict around Muhammad, continues the rhyme of 11–16 and completes a chiastic 4+6+6+4 pattern.
90:17/q16 How does this verse relate to Sūrat al-ʿAṣr and other passages? [* · links] (turns on: Which neighboring or parallel passage is being used to explain the verse.)
  90:17/q16/p1 It resembles 103:3 in joining faith with mutual exhortation. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum calls 103:3 likely secondary. Al-Iṣlāḥī and Study Quran connect perseverance here with 103:1–3. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-ʿAṣr begins with truth because its loss is general and truth directs patience; al-Balad focuses on one part of truth, mercy and feeding, so its patience comes first.
