83:2/q10 What moral wrong and wider lesson does the trading contrast expose? [* · interpretation] (turns on: How far the unequal transaction is generalized: reciprocal commercial justice, all social rights, or evidence of moral nature and final judgment.)
  83:2/q10/p1 It exposes selfish inconsistency: demanding one's own rights while denying others equal rights. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats both verses as one violation of balanced reciprocal claims that corrupts society. Iṣlāḥī says the trader's protest when cheated reveals an innate recognition of justice, overridden by selfishness when he cheats others; he compares thieves, murderers and adulterers who would reject like harm to their own households. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls short measure a combination of injustice, theft and meanness, traits Arabs separately disdained. Al-Jishumī's general maxim denies superiority except by piety.
  83:2/q10/p2 The warning extends to every practical and moral social relation involving rights and obligations. — reasons: Asad explicitly extends the measure metaphor beyond commerce to each person's rights, obligations and possessions; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls reciprocal rights the basis of a balanced society.
  83:2/q10/p3 Innate recognition of justice points to resurrection and divine judgment, which wrongdoers wish to evade. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī reasons that the human moral nature distinguishes just and unjust and thus expects different treatment from God. He says motives can overpower that nature without erasing knowledge of right and wrong, connects the reasoning to 75:14–15, and says the traders' conduct shows no expectation of standing before their Lord.
83:2/q11 What inward reading is given to the cheaters' taking of full measure? [* · inward]
  83:2/q11/p1 They cheat the true scale of justice by exaggerating their own intellectual and practical virtues before others. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī extends measurement to character and deeds and attributes their inflated self-assessment to vanity and pride.
83:2/q12 What reports illustrate the warning about fraudulent measure? [* · reports] (turns on: These are distinct transmitted warnings and examples, with different speakers and consequences.)
  83:2/q12/p1 A Prophetic warning links short measure to drought among five offenses and corresponding calamities. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's account: broken covenants bring enemies, rejecting God's law poverty, immorality plague, short measure drought, and withholding alms loss of rain; he says al-Bazzār transmits its gist and Mālik also reports it from Ibn ʿUmar.
  83:2/q12/p2 ʿIkrima warned that every measurer and weigher faces the Fire because none gives exactly what he demands. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī reports ʿIkrima's oath and reason; al-Qurṭubī adds that he did not exempt his son when asked.
  83:2/q12/p3 A trader with two measures saw them become mountains of fire at death. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports Mālik ibn Dīnār's account of a dying neighbor who had used two measures, which struck together in his vision.
  83:2/q12/p4 ʿAlī required an accurate weight before any voluntary excess. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports ʿAbd Khayr saying ʿAlī overturned a scale giving excess saffron and ordered exact weight first. He also transmits al-Aṣmaʿī's Bedouin saying that manliness is not in measure rims or scale tongues, also attributed to ʿAlī.
  83:2/q12/p5 Ibn ʿUmar warned that short-measurers will stand in sweat up to half their ears. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī transmits this through Nāfiʿ.
  83:2/q12/p6 A recitation of this sura at dawn prompted a prayer against a trader who bought full but sold short. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports Abū Hurayra's account of Medina's deputy reciting the sura and the ensuing prayer.
  83:2/q12/p7 ʿAbd Allāh said Medina's people should fulfill measures because God warned against fraud and judgment. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī reports his reply after being told Medina's people fulfill measures, invoking 83:1 and 83:6.
83:2/q13 What setting or occasion of revelation is given for the warning? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether the reports locate the occasion among the Anṣār, a wider early Medinan audience, or earlier market practice; Iṣlāḥī questions the Anṣār attribution.)
  83:2/q13/p1 Dishonest measuring among the Anṣār in Medina occasioned the verse. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī reports this from exegetes but challenges it: he treats the sura as Meccan and finds dishonest trade at least as plausible among Meccan traders as among the agricultural Anṣār.
  83:2/q13/p2 The opening warning addressed widespread short measure in early Medina, including its polytheists and those of Mecca. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage warns Muslims against the practice and condemns the polytheists of both cities.
  83:2/q13/p3 The fraud was practiced in a pre-Islamic market using two sets of measures and scales. — reasons: Muqātil reports buyers taking extra and sellers giving short by switching measures and scales.
83:2/q14 Where and when was this opening revealed? [* · sciences] (turns on: The Medinan arrival traditions and contextual inference versus Iṣlāḥī's Meccan dating and criticism of the Anṣār report.)
  83:2/q14/p1 At least the first four verses were revealed shortly after the Prophet arrived in Medina. — reasons: Asad cites authentic traditions in al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening context points to Medina.
  83:2/q14/p2 The sura is Meccan. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī uses its Meccan setting to challenge the report assigning the occasion to dishonest measuring among the Anṣār; he notes Meccan traders were at least as likely to practice it.
83:2/q15 How does this warning connect to its surrounding verses and to al-Infiṭār? [* · links] (turns on: The immediate sequence develops the judgment warning, while the inter-sura account compares two ways of arguing for that judgment.)
  83:2/q15/p1 The contrast in verses 1–6 leads from dishonest measure to resurrection and standing for judgment. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening warns of the Great Day before the sura describes wicked and righteous fates. Iṣlāḥī says the cheaters act as though they will not stand before their Lord; al-Ṭabarī's report of ʿAbd Allāh invokes 83:1 and 83:6 together.
  83:2/q15/p2 The sura supplements al-Infiṭār by developing its division of righteous and defiant people through an argument from moral nature. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says al-Infiṭār reasons from God's attributes in the human body, while this sura reasons from the human recognition of justice and evil; the dishonest trader who objects to being cheated makes that recognition visible.
