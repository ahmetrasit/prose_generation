[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#13/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | The Prophet says whoever is killed defending his property is a martyr, showing that property deserves protection.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#2/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو عمرو وابن كثير | reports | Abū ʿAmr and Ibn Kathīr read rahun; other readings include ruhun and ruhan, with grammarians disputing whether these are plural forms or collective nouns.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#2/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · المهدوي | reports | Al-Mahdawī parses 'pledges' as a subject with an omitted predicate: possessed pledges suffice for security.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#2/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | A pledge is property retained as security so a creditor can recover from its price or proceeds if collection from the debtor fails; the Arabic root also means permanence and continuity.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#2/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو علي الفارسي | reports | Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī says the small plural of rahn is unattested; if it existed, its pattern would be afʿāl, as in kalb and aklab.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#3/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو علي وأبو زيد | reports | Abū ʿAlī and Abū Zayd distinguish forms of the pledge verb: arhantu is used for bidding high, while raḥantu is used for loans and sales.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#3/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الزجاج وابن الأعرابي والأخفش | reports | Al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Aʿrābī, and al-Akhfash also allow both raḥantu and arhantu for pledging.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#3/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عبد الله بن همام السلولي | reports | A verse by ʿAbd Allāh ibn Hammām al-Salūlī uses arhantu; most transmitters retain that form, while al-Aṣmaʿī reads a present-tense verb after the past and explains the wāw as circumstantial.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#3/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The pledged person is the one receiving the pledge; an object may be called marhūn or rahīn, a woman rahīna, and rahantu is standard for pledging in a sale or loan.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#3/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Qurṭubī adopts the view that a pledge ends if it voluntarily returns to the pledgor, following the requirement that it be possessed; Abū Ḥanīfa exempts return as a loan or deposit, while al-Shāfiʿī preserves the earlier possession.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#3/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī says a pledge made verbally but never physically possessed creates no legal effect, because the verse predicates the ruling on possession.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#3/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · المالكية | reports | The Mālikīs say the contract binds the pledgor and require delivery; possession completes the pledge's benefit, while two other schools make it a condition of bindingness and validity. | mentions 5:1, 17:34
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#4/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · المالكية | reports | The Mālikīs support the pledge's binding contract by commands to fulfill contracts and promises, and the Prophetic rule that believers fulfill their conditions. | mentions 5:1, 17:34
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#4/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · جمهور العلماء | prefers | Possession by a neutral custodian counts as receipt because the custodian represents the creditor; Ibn Abī Laylā, Qatāda, al-Ḥakam, and ʿAṭāʾ require the creditor to hold it.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#4/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | If collateral with a neutral custodian is lost, neither creditor nor custodian is liable: the creditor never possessed it and the custodian is a trustee.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#4/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن المنذر | reports | Ibn al-Mundhir argues that a Ḥanafī exception allowing two creditors jointly to take a plot as collateral concedes that a share in common property may be pledged.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#4/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن المنذر | prefers | Ibn al-Mundhir considers pledging jointly owned property valid just as selling it is valid; al-Qurṭubī's school permits it against Abū Ḥanīfa's school.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#4/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The Mālikīs permit pledging a debt owed by another: anything saleable may be collateral, and a debt has value, though opponents say it cannot be physically possessed or directly realized.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#4/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو هريرة | reports | Al-Bukhārī transmits from Abū Hurayra the Prophet's rule that an animal pledged as collateral may be ridden and its milk drunk in return for the expense borne by the user.

[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#5/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أحمد وإسحاق | reports | A more explicit Abū Hurayra report says the creditor who feeds a pledged animal may ride or drink its milk in proportion to the expense; Aḥmad and Isḥāq adopt that rule.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#5/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو ثور | reports | Abū Thawr permits a creditor who maintained neglected collateral to ride it or use an enslaved servant; al-Awzāʿī and al-Layth share this view.

[QURTUBI-FULL:2:283#5/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الخطابي | prefers | Al-Khaṭṭābī considers the view that collateral's benefits belong to its owner strongest, supported by the explicit wording about its yield and loss.
