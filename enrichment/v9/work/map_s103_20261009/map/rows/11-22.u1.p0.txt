## THE CURRENT MAP
11:22/q01 What does لا جرم assert here? [لَا جَرَمَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether la jarama is treated as a fixed assertion of certainty, a rebuttal followed by a verb of earning or becoming true, a negation of cutting or prevention, or a negation of doubt; the cited poetry's case and sense are disputed.)
  11:22/q01/p1 It affirms as certain or true that they are the greatest losers; the idiom can also convey inevitability, absence of doubt, or oath-like emphasis. — reasons: Muqatil, Ibn Abbas as reported by Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi, and other exegetes gloss it 'indeed' or 'truly'; al-Baghawi also gives 'rather indeed' and al-Farra's 'without doubt.' Al-Tabari calls it an idiom of certainty associated literally with earning sin and inevitability. Al-Farra, reported by al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi and al-Tabataba'i, derives the familiar emphatic use from 'no escape' or 'inevitably' through frequent use and notes its oath-like force; al-Sirafi's report in al-Alusi similarly traces an older sense of keeping someone from sin. Al-Khalil, Sibawayh, al-Farra, Muhammad ibn Yazid and others transmit the 'truly' account in al-Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and al-Samin. Al-Tabrisi reports the decisive-assertion sense; al-Baydawi says no one is more manifestly a loser. Ibn Ashur calls it a certainty idiom, treating jarama as a fossilized noun for inevitability or no alternative. Al-Biqa'i says denying the contrary establishes the assertion, comparing the emphatic force of la to innama; he reports al-Farahi's derivation from cutting and its oath-like use. Al-Nasafi and al-Alusi report the compound or conventional certainty sense.
  11:22/q01/p2 The negation rebuts a supposed benefit, while jarama says their deed or unbelief earned the loss or punishment that follows. — reasons: Abu Ishaq in al-Nahhas and al-Zajjaj in al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi read la as rejecting expected benefit, especially from idols; Ibn al-Anbari specifies the unbelievers' hope that punishment will be diverted. Al-Azhari calls this among the best explanations, and Ibn al-Anbari supplies disbelief as the implicit doer of jarama. Al-Wahidi also reports an extra-la account yielding earned regret. Al-Razi connects the earning sense with 5:2 and includes loss in this world and the next. Al-Samin cites 75:1 and 4:65 for a rebuttal and poetry where jaramat means earned; jarima can mean a tribe's provider or earner. Al-Mawardi cites the thrust that caused Fazara's anger, and al-Wahidi records al-Farra and Abu al-Abbas reading Ghatafan as the poem's accusative object: a wound caused the tribe's anger. Ibn Atiyya says the earning sense has poetic support, though Jarir's line can bear another sense; al-Alusi also cites a poet using jaramat for earning or committing. Al-Nasafi says their claim earns loss. Al-Tabari associates the idiom literally with earning sin.
  11:22/q01/p3 Nothing can cut off, avert, restrain, or interrupt the certainty of their loss. — reasons: Al-Kisa'i in al-Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi speaks of no denial, turning away, opposition or prevention. Al-Razi reports a negation plus jarama 'cutting': nothing cuts off the conclusion. Al-Qurtubi illustrates the root with harvesting dates, shearing wool and removing meat from bone. Al-Samin offers jarama as a noun meaning cutting or preventing. Al-Alusi extends the account to the unbroken duration of their loss. Al-Tabataba'i suggests a cutting root and concludes that nothing cuts off the assertion.
  11:22/q01/p4 There is no uncertainty or wavering about their punishment. — reasons: Al-Biqa'i prefers a recurrent root idea of disturbance: reflection unsettles an opinion and opens what was closed; related forms denote doubt, confusion, sin, mixture or separation. La then negates such wavering, thereby affirming certainty; he treats the negation as emphatic and says it explains Sibawayh's 'it is true.'
  11:22/q01/p5 Jarama means 'became true' or 'was deserved': their unbelief makes the stated consequence rightfully certain. — reasons: Al-Wahidi reports al-Akhfash and Sibawayh making la a reply to the disbelievers and citing the spear-thrust poem for a consequence made certain; al-Razi also reports them. Ibn al-Anbari in Ibn al-Jawzi allows either earned punishment or made-certain punishment, with a poem for the latter. Al-Alusi reports Sibawayh's jarama as the verb haqqa, 'became true.' Al-Wahidi reports al-Farra and Abu al-Abbas objecting to the poem's use for this reading: Ghatafan is accusative, and the wound caused the tribe's anger.
11:22/q02 How are لا جرم and the following أن clause constructed? [لَا جَرَمَ أَنَّهُمْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the phrase is compounded, a rebuttal followed by a past verb, or a negated noun; these analyses assign different cases and syntactic roles to the following an clause.)
  11:22/q02/p1 La jarama is a fixed compound of negation and jarama meaning 'certainly'; the following an clause is nominative, as its subject. — reasons: Al-Khalil, Sibawayh, al-Farra and Muhammad ibn Yazid are reported by al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi; al-Khalil says la responds to prior speech rather than opening a statement. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi transmit al-Khalil and Sibawayh's compound analysis. Al-Samin compares its fixed form with khamsata ashar and cites 16:62. Al-Nasafi reports the compound analysis. Ibn Atiyya contrasts Sibawayh's nominative an with al-Zajjaj's accusative one. Al-Biqa'i reports a related compound-past-verb account from Sibawayh and al-Khalil.
  11:22/q02/p2 La rebuts a preceding claim; jarama is a past verb meaning 'earned,' with an implicit doer and the following an clause as its accusative object. — reasons: Abu Ishaq and al-Zajjaj in al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi reject the supposed benefit and give the deed or unbelief as jarama's omitted subject. Ibn al-Anbari specifies an implicit subject referring to disbelief; al-Samin gives the deeds as subject and cites 75:1 and 4:65 for reply-like la. Abu Hayyan notes that al-Zajjaj rejects compounding and cites poetry for the earning verb. Al-Razi and al-Tabrisi explain the same rebuttal and earned loss. Al-Nasafi gives the claim as what earns the loss. Al-Alusi supplies an omitted clause such as 'their deed benefits them' and an unnamed poet for the verbal sense.
  11:22/q02/p3 La negates jarama as a noun of cutting or prevention; the an clause is its predicate or depends on an omitted preposition. — reasons: Al-Razi reports the negation-plus-cutting analysis. Al-Qurtubi says the agent of cutting may be omitted through frequent use. Al-Samin allows an as predicate or following an omitted preposition. Al-Alusi reports a noun of cutting off, with no interruption to their loss. Al-Kisa'i, as reported by al-Alusi, treats jarama as la's indeclinable noun, with omitted predicate and omitted preposition before an, yielding no opposition or prevention; al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi record his no-prevention interpretation.
  11:22/q02/p4 La is an extra emphatic particle, leaving the assertion or earned outcome to follow. — reasons: Al-Mawardi reports extra la for the emphatic 'truly'; al-Wahidi reports an extra-la analysis in which their deed earns regret. Al-Biqa'i reports al-Radi and al-Saqafsi allowing extra la because the phrase bears oath force.
  11:22/q02/p5 La negates a genus and jarama is its accusative noun; the pair is the subject and the following an clause its predicate. — reasons: Al-Samin gives this analysis, with the sense 'certainly.' Abu Hayyan reports al-Hawfi taking negated jarama as a nominative topic and the an clause as predicate; al-Biqa'i reports other grammarians treating jarama as the noun of la and 'they' as predicate.
  11:22/q02/p6 The negated-noun analysis is retained, but the an clause is genitive or accusative after an omitted preposition. — reasons: Al-Samin gives this variant of the preceding construction, glossing it as certainty about their afterlife loss. Al-Kisa'i's analysis as reported by al-Alusi also omits a preposition before an; Ibn Ashur's fossilized-noun account likewise makes an depend on an omitted preposition.
  11:22/q02/p7 La is negative and jarama is the verb 'became true'; the following clause functions as its subject. — reasons: Al-Alusi reports this from Sibawayh and explains that the unbelievers' expected benefit fails because loss is certain. Al-Wahidi and al-Razi report Sibawayh and al-Akhfash with la as reply and jarama as 'deserved/was certain'; al-Wahidi records al-Farra and Abu al-Abbas objecting to their poetic proof because Ghatafan is accusative.
  11:22/q02/p8 La jarama is a fossilized noun of inevitability, with an governed by an omitted preposition. — reasons: Ibn Ashur gives this construction and notes that the expression can introduce an oath-like assertion. Al-Biqa'i distinguishes fath after la jarama, reflecting the original 'no alternative that' construction, from kasra arising with later oath-like usage.
