2:16/q13 How does the fāʾ link purchase to lack of profit? [فَمَا · grammar] (turns on: Immediate coordination versus an implied condition.)
  2:16/q13/p1 It coordinates failure with purchase and marks immediate consequence. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī attach it to the relative clause; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the act exposes both failure and lack of guidance.
  2:16/q13/p2 It implies a condition: if they exchange, their trade fails. — reasons: Al-Jishumī suggests this; al-Samīn reports a related conditional parse, opposed by Abū Ḥayyān.
2:16/q14 What does the negative mā do in فما ربحت? [فَمَا رَبِحَت · grammar]
  2:16/q14/p1 With a past verb it negates the act without changing inflection, much like لم. — reasons: Al-Zarkashī contrasts nominal and imperfect uses, notes Ibn al-Ḥājib's contextual tenses and Sībawayh's emphatic mā, then cites 5:19 and a mā-plus-illā counting rule.
2:16/q15 What do profit and trade mean here? [رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · meaning]
  2:16/q15/p1 Profit is increase beyond capital, and trade seeks gain through dealing. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī cites a poem for tajara; al-Jishumī gives تجار as plural of تاجر. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr give commercial definitions.
2:16/q16 Why is profit attributed to trade rather than traders? [فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · rhetoric] (turns on: Ordinary ellipsis versus attributional metaphor and its force.)
  2:16/q16/p1 Arabic attributes the trader's result to the trade; the traders gained nothing. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ, al-Akhfash, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and later writers cite sleeping nights, resolved matters, 34:33, 47:21, 2:177, 12:82 and Jarīr. Al-Farrāʾ requires clear context. Al-Jurjānī argues this syntactic figure has force beyond literal paraphrase, with poems about drought and concrete idioms. Al-Akhfash also reports an alternative 2:171 comparison: gods and sheep alike do not hear or understand their caller.
  2:16/q16/p2 Trade's causal or associative relation licenses figurative attribution. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī calls trade a cause; al-Zarkashī distinguishes lexical resemblance from rational association, citing verses on caused faith and ruin. Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Taftāzānī treat even the negation as figurative.
2:16/q17 Why does the verse continue the buying image with profit and trade? [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · rhetoric]
  2:16/q17/p1 The commercial words complete and reinforce the purchase metaphor. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the end echoes the opening. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Zarkashī and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite poems extending images through nests, silk, birds and lizards. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes a literal companion from a second metaphor.
2:16/q18 What does their failure to profit imply about the exchange? [فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether no surplus alone proves loss of capital.)
  2:16/q18/p1 They lost guidance as capital and all true gain. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī names confusion, blindness and fear now and punishment later; al-Ṭabrisī contrasts believers' trade. Al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr treat guidance, fitra or reason as capital. Sufyān al-Thawrī cites 61:10; worldly gains are no genuine profit.
  2:16/q18/p2 The no-profit phrase alone denies only surplus; the next clause proves capital loss. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān replies to al-Zamakhsharī that a trader may break even. The second clause completes the thought, illustrated by Imruʾ al-Qays; al-Ālūsī says religious trade cannot end neutrally.
  2:16/q18/p3 They gained nothing because they falsely expected reward. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān cites claims of divine favor and short punishment, then a hadith that Ibn Judʿān's generosity did not benefit him without prayer for forgiveness on Judgment.
2:16/q19 Is تجارتهم read in the singular or plural? [تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ · readings] (turns on: Collective singular versus individual trades.)
  2:16/q19/p1 The majority read singular 'their trade'. — reasons: Al-Jishumī notes weak transmission for variants; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī explain the collective sense.
  2:16/q19/p2 Ibrāhīm ibn Abī ʿAbla reads plural 'their trades'. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report that each person has a separate trade.
2:16/q20 How are تجارة and ضلالة pluralized? [تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ · grammar]
  2:16/q20/p1 Ibn Kaysān allows تجاير or تجائر and ضلايل or ضلائل. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās gives unhamzated forms and al-Qurṭubī hamzated ones; these are lexical plurals, not verse readings.
2:16/q21 What does 'they were not guided' mean? [وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُهْتَدِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Faith generally, escape from error, purchase judgment, or divine knowledge.)
  2:16/q21/p1 They remained astray and could not escape misguidance. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī explain escape from error; al-Baghawī offers general astray. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the past form implies entrenched, longstanding failure.
  2:16/q21/p2 They made no sound choice in this purchase or trade. — reasons: Al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī cite the purchase; al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿĀshūr portray ignorance of a profitable route. Ibn ʿĀshūr separates ordinary commercial discernment from religious guidance.
  2:16/q21/p3 They missed the believers' profitable trade. — reasons: Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī report it; al-Ṭabrisī contrasts the companions and believers.
  2:16/q21/p4 Their lack of guidance was already in God's knowledge. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya permits it; al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān report it.
  2:16/q21/p5 They lacked knowledge of God. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports the explanation.
  2:16/q21/p6 They forfeited the capacity to receive guidance. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān uses an absent road sign to illustrate missing means and distinguishes capacity surrendered from guidance attained; Bursevī describes invalidated innate readiness.
2:16/q22 Why deny both profit and guidance? [فَمَا رَبِحَت تِّجَٰرَتُهُمْ وَمَا كَانُوا۟ مُهْتَدِينَ · rhetoric] (turns on: Intensification, proof of capital loss, or distinct sense.)
  2:16/q22/p1 The pair intensifies blame: a trader may fail to profit yet act sensibly. — reasons: Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān make the distinction. Al-Ālūsī calls it completion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it exposes foolish aims.
  2:16/q22/p2 The second denial establishes capital loss beyond missed surplus. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says no-profit may be break-even; al-Ālūsī says the final clause settles true loss. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it also denies ordinary navigation to their aim.
  2:16/q22/p3 The second denial attaches back to the earlier purchase. — reasons: Al-Sharīf prefers this; al-Ālūsī objects that the order and closeness of meanings weaken it.
2:16/q23 How is مهتدين parsed and formed? [مُهْتَدِينَ · grammar] (turns on: Predicate of كان versus circumstantial analysis.)
  2:16/q23/p1 It is the accusative predicate of كان, from plural مهتديين. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Ṭabrisī give the syntax. Al-Ṭabrisī compares complete كان at 2:282. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say form VIII responds to a transitive base; اشتال is no counterexample because it equals شال.
  2:16/q23/p2 It was originally a necessary circumstantial phrase. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ says it cannot be omitted; Abū Ḥayyān reports and rejects his reasoning.
2:16/q24 What occasion or setting is reported for this passage? [* · reports] (turns on: General hypocrites, foretelling, or Ibn Ubayy.)
  2:16/q24/p1 It concerns hypocrites generally. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports the exegetes; Ibn ʿĀshūr reads 2:8 onward collectively.
  2:16/q24/p2 It foretells a people before they existed. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports unseen disclosure.
  2:16/q24/p3 The meeting verse concerns ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy and companions. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports his praise of Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿAlī to believers, followed by his claim to have turned them away; the earlier passage remains general.
2:16/q25 How does this verse connect with the surrounding passage? [* · links]
  2:16/q25/p1 It sums up the hypocrites' portrait, explains wandering, and contrasts their end with the guided. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī relates it to 2:15; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the detached sentence restates that ending and contrasts 2:5 and 2:7. Elmalılı says following parables make the loss tangible.
2:16/q26 What broader lesson does the trade image teach? [* · interpretation]
  2:16/q26/p1 Life is a trade whose true gain requires guidance and upright conduct. — reasons: Sufyān al-Thawrī asks everyone to examine their trade, citing 61:10. Elmalılı says religion, sound understanding and honesty sustain commerce; deception fails. Kur'an Yolu says hypocrites spend life, reason and will on error.
2:16/q27 What inward loss does the purchase depict? [* · inward]
  2:16/q27/p1 They squander original light and receptivity, ending veiled from God. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī says knowledge, works, gnosis, wisdom and virtue could perfect innate light. Bursevī cites 83:14–15 and Najmī interpretations of desire and worldly attachment; al-Daqqāq's disciple must first remove love of the world before extra worship.
2:16/q28 Is metaphor possible in the Qur'an, as this attribution illustrates? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether divine speech can use metaphor.)
  2:16/q28/p1 Yes: rational metaphor attributes an action to a related cause. — reasons: Al-Zarkashī reports the majority, distinguishes resemblance from association, and cites Pharaoh, Satan, recited verses and other causes. He notes Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām's work, al-Ḥātimī's etymology and al-Aṣmaʿī on likenesses.
