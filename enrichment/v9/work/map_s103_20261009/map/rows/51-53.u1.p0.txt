## THE CURRENT MAP
51:53/q01 What does أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ mean? [أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ · meaning]
  51:53/q01/p1 They advised or enjoined one another, passed something along, or appeared to agree on it. — reasons: MAJAZ allows mutual agreement or taking a view from one another and says a dominant shared trait can make advice appear to have occurred. WAHIDI-BASIT explains it as mutual instruction to use an accusation; Abu ʿUbayda, reported by IBNJAWZI-ZAD, includes collusion and copying. TABATABAI defines mutual enjoining as a people's advising one another.
51:53/q02 What does بِهِۦ refer to? [بِهِۦ · referent] (turns on: Whether the pronoun points to the repeated charge, rejection of the message, or belief in it.)
  51:53/q02/p1 The repeated accusation that the messengers, especially Muhammad, were sorcerers or madmen. — reasons: TAB-FULL identifies the charges against Muhammad as poet, sorcerer, and madman and the older peoples' charges against their messengers as sorcerer or madman. NASAFI-FULL, TABATABAI, and the rhetorical readings identify the pronoun with the accusation; STUDYQURAN specifies calling the Prophet a sorcerer or possessed.
  51:53/q02/p2 Denial of the messenger, truth, or revelation. — reasons: Qatāda says predecessors instructed successors to lie, deny the truth, or deny the messenger, as reported by ABDURRAZZAQ, TAB-FULL, and WAHIDI-BASIT. Muqātil, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT, says to speak this way about their messenger. STUDYQURAN reports that the urging entails denial of God and revelation.
  51:53/q02/p3 Belief, which they do not exhort one another to embrace. — reasons: STUDYQURAN reports this alternative reading from Āl together with a negative understanding of the question.
51:53/q03 What force does the question أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِهِۦ have? [أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِهِۦ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the interrogation chiefly voices rebuking wonder or directly negates the proposed exhortation.)
  51:53/q03/p1 It reproaches or expresses astonishment at the same charge appearing among separated generations, as though they had instructed one another. — reasons: ZAJJAJ and al-Zajjāj as reported by WAHIDI-BASIT call the opening hamza both interrogative and rebuking. BAGHAWI-FULL calls its alif reproachful; QURTUBI-FULL adds astonishment. IBNATIYYA-FULL, RAZI-FULL, ALUSI-FULL, and KURANYOLU-TEFSIR stress wonder at distant generations converging. STUDYQURAN reports wonder that people would urge denial of God and revelation; ISLAHI likens it to passing down a will.
  51:53/q03/p2 It denies that such mutual exhortation took place. — reasons: ALUSI-FULL reports this as an alternative force of the question. STUDYQURAN reports Āl's negative reading, in which the pronoun instead means belief.
51:53/q04 Why did generations repeat the same denunciation? [أَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بَلْ هُمْ قَوْمٌۭ طَاغُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether similar accusations require transmission or arise independently from the same transgressive character; distance between generations is repeatedly cited against direct counsel.)
  51:53/q04/p1 Their shared transgression produced the same denial independently, without actual counsel across generations. — reasons: TAB-FULL, NAHHAS, WAHIDI-BASIT, IBNATIYYA-FULL, TABRISI, RAZI-FULL, QURTUBI-FULL, BAYDAWI-FULL, NASAFI-FULL, ABUHAYYAN-FULL, BIQAI-FULL, BURSEVI, ALUSI-FULL, ELMALILI, ISLAHI, and KURANYOLU-TEFSIR attribute recurrence to rebellion, not coordination; several note that the generations were too far apart or never met. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT and BAGHAWI-FULL, says prosperity bred rebellion and denial; RAZI-FULL adds self-sufficiency and forgetfulness of God and compares wealthy subjects who later resist a king's summons. IBNKATHIR-FULL says later hearts resembled earlier hearts. BIQAI-FULL reports Ibn al-Zubayr's emphasis on arrogance and darkened hearts, alongside 32:13 and God's ability to guide every soul. BURSEVI calls the disposition innate; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR adds prejudice and stubbornness.
  51:53/q04/p2 Earlier generations actually instructed later ones to deny the messenger or repeat the accusation. — reasons: Qatāda's reports in ABDURRAZZAQ, TAB-FULL, and WAHIDI-BASIT describe predecessors instructing successors to lie or deny. Muqātil, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT, says they instructed later people what to say about their messenger. ZAJJAJ says the first advised the last. WAHIDI-WAJIZ takes the rebuke as implying that some instructed others; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR reports Tabarī's formulation as intergenerational transmission.
  51:53/q04/p3 The charge persisted as inherited thinking or a cultural pattern. — reasons: ASAD-NOTES frames it as inherited thinking while also explaining rejection through arrogance. KURANYOLU-TEFSIR explicitly raises cultural inheritance but rejects it as the explanation.
51:53/q05 Who are the rebellious people? [هُمْ قَوْمٌۭ · referent] (turns on: Whether the immediate pronoun is taken specifically for Quraysh or the recurrent class of opponents.)
  51:53/q05/p1 The Meccans or Quraysh who rejected Muhammad. — reasons: TAB-FULL names Quraysh as recipients of the rhetorical challenge. IBNJAWZI-ZAD explicitly identifies the addressed people as Meccans. ISLAHI compares Quraysh with earlier rejecters.
  51:53/q05/p2 Rejecters of messengers across earlier and later generations. — reasons: IBNATIYYA-FULL, TABRISI, RAZI-FULL, QURTUBI-FULL, BAYDAWI-FULL, and others compare people of separated eras. TAB-FULL names Noah's people, ʿĀd, Thamud, and Pharaoh's people alongside Quraysh.
51:53/q06 What does طَاغُونَ describe? [طَاغُونَ · meaning]
  51:53/q06/p1 People who exceed bounds through arrogance, corruption, disobedience, and denial. — reasons: NAHHAS says they overstep God's commands and prohibitions; WAHIDI-BASIT says they exceed bounds in disobedience. IBNATIYYA-FULL describes earthly arrogance, corruption, and defiance; BIQAI-FULL adds excess in unbelief and wrongdoing and sees transgression as ingrained in the pronoun's subjects, whom God ruins through complete power and knowledge. Ibn ʿAbbās, reported by WAHIDI-BASIT and BAGHAWI-FULL, links rebellion to divine gifts and abundance. ASAD-NOTES calls it overweening arrogance.
51:53/q07 What does بَلْ do in the explanation? [بَلْ · rhetoric]
  51:53/q07/p1 It turns from the suggested mutual advice to the real common cause, transgression. — reasons: ALUSI-FULL calls it a redirection from shared counsel to participation in transgression. TAB-FULL, NASAFI-FULL, TABATABAI, and KURANYOLU-TEFSIR likewise contrast the imagined instruction with shared rebelliousness.
51:53/q08 How does this verse continue the preceding account of accusations? [* · links]
  51:53/q08/p1 It explains why the same charge was made against Muhammad and earlier messengers. — reasons: TAB-FULL lists Quraysh's charges of poet, sorcerer, or madman and similar claims by Noah's people, ʿĀd, Thamud, and Pharaoh's people. RAZI-FULL explicitly connects the recurrent sorcerer or madman accusation with 51:52; IBNKATHIR-FULL says later hearts resembled earlier ones.
51:53/q09 What lesson do earlier peoples' repeated rejections offer? [* · interpretation]
  51:53/q09/p1 Their accounts offer lessons to those who reflect and encourage the Prophet to persevere, turn from wrongdoers, and remind receptive people. — reasons: JISHUMI says earlier communities' narratives are recounted for reflection. ISLAHI-TADABBUR says each nation treated its messenger similarly and tells the Prophet to continue patiently, turn away from wrongdoers, remind those who respond, and trust God's support for him and his companions.
51:53/q10 How is the shared defiance read inwardly? [بَلْ هُمْ قَوْمٌۭ طَاغُونَ · inward]
  51:53/q10/p1 Rebellious souls in every age reject both outward prophets and inward divine inspirations through a shared satanic disposition. — reasons: The Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya, reported by BURSEVI, names refusal, pride, and defiance, says they dismiss prophets and inspirations as magic or madness, and traces the disposition to abundance, comfort, arrogance, and wealth.
51:53/q11 How do forms of predication convey a report? [* · grammar]
  51:53/q11/p1 A main predicate completes the clause, a circumstantial phrase reports secondarily, and nominal and verbal predicates present attributes differently. — reasons: JURJANI-DALAIL says nominal predicates or completing verbs provide indispensable reports; circumstantial phrases attach a secondary report to the main one; nominal predicates assert an attribute without renewal, whereas verbs present renewal or recurrence. These are general grammatical observations, with no specific application to this verse stated in the notes.
51:53/q12 What accounts for Qur'anic eloquence and inimitability? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether eloquence resides in meaningful arrangement or the ease of individual sounds.)
  51:53/q12/p1 It rests in the arrangement of God's speech and its eloquence and balāgha. — reasons: JURJANI-DALAIL says nazm makes God's speech miraculous and reports rational thinkers' agreement that eloquence and balāgha take it beyond human capacity. He says judgments about burdensome sounds belong to sensation, not reasoning, and gives counterexamples to theories based on ease of individual letters.
  51:53/q12/p2 It depends on light and easy sounds or ease of articulation. — reasons: JURJANI-DALAIL rejects this: vulgar speech and bad verse can have light sounds, and changing every ḍamma and kasra to fatḥa could ease articulation even when meaning changes. He also says sensory perception, rather than reasoning, judges which sounds burden the tongue.
