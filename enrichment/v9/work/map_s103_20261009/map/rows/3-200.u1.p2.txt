3:200/q21 How can divine law allow freedom and change? [* · law] (turns on: Whether stable foundations impede or enable adaptive social life.)
  3:200/q21/p1 Human will operates within divine norms, while policy and technical means adapt. — reasons: Tabatabai says free will is natural, but society needs limits; Islam frees people from service to others and permits good provisions (7:32; 2:29; 45:13). Cognitive belief itself cannot be legislated, but related public acts can. Industry and understanding may advance, while monotheism, prophecy and judgment remain stable. Basic human needs persist, methods change, and leaders consult on changing war, finance and administration (3:159) without abrogating Qur'an and Sunna. He rejects relativism and compulsive change.
  3:200/q21/p2 The objection calls for creed freedom and says fixed religious law blocks progress. — reasons: Some researchers cite 2:256; other objectors invoke dialectical change, variable morals and the distance between ancient law and modern industry. Tabatabai distinguishes private inquiry from public disruption.
3:200/q22 How should the Muslim community govern and maintain its bonds? [* · law]
  3:200/q22/p1 It shares responsibility under prophetic guidance, consultation, faith-based belonging and collective duty. — reasons: Tabatabai cites Prophetic authority (64:12; 4:105; 33:6; 3:31) and general commands to believers, while the Prophet uniquely teaches and purifies (62:2). Rank depends on piety (49:13), not class; all enjoin right. He reports majority election and Shi'a designation of twelve Imams, then gives governance during occultation to Muslims preserving divine law and consulting on contingent affairs (33:21). Faith, not homeland, bounds the community and even affects marriage and inheritance. Practice persists under coercion and weakness (16:106; 3:28; 64:16); collective forms vary among defense, fasting, hajj, Friday and daily prayer, the latter strongly encouraged in congregation.
3:200/q23 How should believers investigate truth while preventing division? [* · interpretation]
  3:200/q23/p1 Use education, shared Qur'anic study and qualified guidance, allowing thought without coercion. — reasons: Tabatabai says fitra inclines to truth, and God excuses those lacking proof (8:42; 4:98). Moral disposition and geography affect understanding, so preachers address listeners by capacity; a report says prophets do likewise. Groups learn and return to warn (9:122). Believers hold the Qur'an and Messenger's rope (3:102-103), reflect (4:82) and refer disputes to Prophet or leaders (4:59,83). Doubts deserve communal inquiry; public unreviewed opinion risks division, while force, takfir and exclusion are rejected. He laments loss of inquiry and affirms balanced spiritual, bodily and social fitra.
3:200/q24 Will Islam's complete social goal eventually prevail? [* · theology]
  3:200/q24/p1 Yes, its collective realization is humanity's eventual natural goal. — reasons: Tabatabai cites people who love God and strive (5:54), believers' succession and security (24:55), and righteous inheritance (21:105). He says earlier failed practice proves no impossibility: orders emerge through resolute people without prior decisive experiment, and deviations misapply fitra.
3:200/q25 What other inward readings interpret the sequence? [* · inward]
  3:200/q25/p1 The commands form stations of certainty, worship, witnessing and self-purification. — reasons: Tustari names reliance, surrender, acceptance and gratitude with piety, then certainty as faith's heart, patience its support, sincerity its completion. Sufi reports describe outward worship, struggle against sins, divine witnessing, and the fall of anxious thoughts; Sari links ribat to the reproachful soul. Qashani distinguishes self, heart and spirit.
3:200/q26 What related prayer for Paradise and protection from Hell is reported? [* · reports]
  3:200/q26/p1 Paradise welcomes those who ask for it three times, and Hell seeks protection for those who ask refuge three times. — reasons: Bursevi transmits Anas's report and prays for Paradise and good deeds.
