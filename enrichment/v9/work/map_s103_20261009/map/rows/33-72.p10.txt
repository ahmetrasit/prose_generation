[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-6-2:p161/r7] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Human beings alone were deemed worthy of God’s trust and covenant; their dignity depends on it, and fulfilling its obligation raises them to the highest status.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The verse uses simile and parable; the author's preferred reading is figurative: the trust is too weighty for seemingly stronger heavens, earth, and mountains, yet humans undertook it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · bazı tefsirciler | reports | A literal reading says God gave the heavens, earth, and mountains consciousness in eternity, offered them the trust, and they refused before the ignorant, poorly judging human accepted it.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Humans have the capacity to carry the trust but may not understand it or fulfill it; they must recognize their identity, abilities, and duty, since ignorance is folly and neglect is injustice.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Lexically, amana means the removal of fear and anxiety and secure confidence; it can name either this state or the thing entrusted for protection.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The closest religious senses are tawhid and its belief, justice, literacy, reason, capacity for moral responsibility, and trust in the Turkish sense; these gather under reason- and free-will-based obligation.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Other creatures follow their created programming, whereas humans can learn and change decisions and actions but face serious barriers to recognizing and doing good.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r7] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Without divine knowledge and guidance, humans know far less than they do not know and fail more often at moral good, explaining the verse's descriptions as extremely ignorant and unjust.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r8] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The entrusted capacity for obligation can make a person the noblest creature if honored, but misusing it under Satan's influence brings the person to the lowest level.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:33:72/r9] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | If amana is taken in its ordinary Turkish sense, the verse highlights material possessions and spiritual duties as important examples within broader obligations.

[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p147/r6] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · author | reports | In the supplication entrusting a person’s religion and amāna to God, amāna is explained as family, dependents left behind, and entrusted property.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p147/r7] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · الجمهور | reports | The majority of commentators take trusts and covenants broadly: duties from God and people, including religious obligations, deposits, oaths, vows, and contracts.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT:p147/r8] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT · روح المعاني | reports | Rūḥ al-Maʿānī reports that amānāt is plural because trusts are numerous and varied for each person, whereas ʿahd is singular because it is less numerous or because it is a verbal noun.
