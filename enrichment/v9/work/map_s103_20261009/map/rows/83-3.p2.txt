[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p425/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The prior surah discussed the blessed, the wretched, and the great Day of Judgment; this surah turns to a particularly contemptible sin, cheating in measure for negligible gain.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p426/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Measure and weigh normally take their recipient with لام, which can be omitted so the suffix pronoun becomes a direct object; the measured commodity itself is omitted.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p426/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · عيسى، حمزة | reports | They treat the object as omitted and هم as a nominative pronoun reinforcing the subject wāw.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p426/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | He rejects taking هم as a subject pronoun, arguing it would make the sentence about who personally does the weighing rather than the action of cheating.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p426/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abu Hayyan says there is no dissonance: reinforcing the pronoun changes nothing about the action being discussed; the recipient is understood in the second clause and the cheats short only other people.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p426/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Asked why the verse says measure or weigh when they short others, he answers that cheats take by measure to enable overfilling and theft, but use either measure or weight when giving to enable shorting in both ways.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p427/r15] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | يخسرون means to reduce or make short; خسر الرجل is intransitive while أخسره makes another person lose.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says they took goods by measure alone because measuring let them overfill and steal, but used both measures and scales when giving so they could short either amount.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | يخسرون answers إذا and is causative, as in خسر الرجل versus أخسرته; its omitted object is الناس متاعهم.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Because the written form omits the alif after the verb’s plural wāw, the pronoun هم can be accusative, referring to الناس: they measure or weigh the people’s goods.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī says the plural wāw itself conveys plurality; the written alif merely distinguishes it from a nonplural wāw, so context can make it unnecessary.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عيسى بن عمر وحمزة | reports | ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and Ḥamza use the nominative pronouns referring to the short-measurers, pausing after each wāw to show their intended parsing.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | On the object-pronoun analysis, the verbs take two objects: one directly and the other through a preposition that may be omitted; scholars differ over which verb is basic.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhsharī cites the line جنيتك as Arabic evidence for deleting the preposition, with the sense جنيت لك.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively, هم is a nominative pronoun emphasizing the plural wāw and referring to the short-measurers; on this reading, the goods and their recipients are omitted.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | rejects | Al-Zamakhsharī rejects the nominative reading because it would say they short-measure when acting as measurers themselves, disrupting the contrast between taking from people and giving to them.
[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh denies any incoherence: emphasis does not change the focus on the act, and the recipients of measuring are understood because they would not short themselves.

[SAMIN-DURR:83:3/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The missing alif after the plural wāw favors the accusative pronoun analysis by indicating its connection to the verb.
