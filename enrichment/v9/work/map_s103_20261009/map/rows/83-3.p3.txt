[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · صبغة الله الحيدوي | reports | Sayyid Sibghat Allah al-Haydawi argues that measurement cheating usually removes a negligible amount, whereas a small weighing trick can cost much more because weighed goods tend to be more valuable; mentioning cheating even in measures implies worse theft by weight.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Alusi says this does not answer why the verse does not instead say they exact in measures and cheat by weight, which would imply the inverse by a fortiori reasoning; ellipsis could make that an instance of antimetabole-like omission.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj says mentioning full measure stands for full weight as well, since buying and selling goods use both measures and weights; al-Tibi explains that one side of the parallel implies the other, which Alusi finds unpersuasive.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69#2/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Another explanation says these merchants buy large quantities at once, commonly measured, then sell smaller lots; sales vary in quantity and buyers choose weight or measure, so both are named when they give goods.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69#2/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | rejects | Alusi cautions that buying large quantities by measure is not universal: in Baghdad of his day, people weighed everything and did not use measures.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69#2/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Other exegetes say neither statement specifies the commodity measured or weighed, because the passage condemns misconduct in taking and giving generally.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The detached pronoun refers to the people from whom they receive goods: when they measure or weigh for them in a sale, they give short measure or weight.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Because the verb can mean measure for someone or measure that person's goods, some grammarians posit deletion of a preposition; poetry and a proverb illustrate such deletion, while another analysis supplies the omitted measured goods.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Isa ibn Umar and Hamza treat the pronoun as an emphatic subject following the verb's subject, pausing after the two waw letters to clarify that reading.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الزمخشري | rejects | Al-Zamakhshari rejects reading the pronoun as subject: it would say they exact full measure when buying but cheat only when personally doing the measuring, an incoherent contrast because the passage concerns conduct, not who performs it.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | A further objection says subject emphasis does not fit the context, which is not distinguishing them from their servants, and the expected consequence would have a connective fa; the detached-object reading also has support from the written form.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p69/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | They used measures to take goods because this allowed them to demand full amounts and cheat; when paying they used both measures and weights to shortchange in either way, so the verse mirrors their practice.
[ELMALILI:83:2-3/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | When measuring or weighing for those same people, they reduce the amount or otherwise shortchange them; habitual fraud explains their deserving Wail.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p191/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | In kālūhum and wazanūhum the sellers are subjects and “them” refers to customers; the construction omits the prepositional lām from kālū lahum and wazanū lahum.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p191/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Since kāla and wazana normally take a measured object, omitting the lām is a frequent lightening of “measure/weigh for them,” like common verbs such as thanking someone.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p191/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ says the people of the Ḥijāz and nearby Qays say “measure for us” as well as “measure for them”; others use the direct-object construction only.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p191/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The buyers' side mentions measuring alone because they usually bought produce by measure and paid in weighed coin; sellers use both measure and weight, revealing that the cheating was merchants' practice.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p192/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Yukhsirūn means they cause their customers to incur a monetary loss by shorting a transaction.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p192/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The passage warns Muslims against widespread short-measure practices in early Medina and condemns the polytheists of Medina and Mecca.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p192/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Short measure combines injustice, theft, and meanness, traits the Arabs disdained separately yet practiced together.
[TABATABAI:83:1-5/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The sura opens by warning those who cheat in measure and weight that they will be resurrected for judgment on the Great Day, then describes the fates of wicked and righteous; context suggests the opening was revealed in Medina.
[TABATABAI:83:1-5/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | They take their full due when buying but reduce what they give when selling, thereby making others lose.
[TABATABAI:83:1-5/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Both verses condemn one wrong: they insist on rights for themselves but deny others equal reciprocal rights, corrupting a society built on balanced claims.
[TABATABAI:83:1-5/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | One explanation says only buying by measure is mentioned because dealers commonly bought bulk grain and resold small amounts, usually by measure; the author finds this weak.
[TABATABAI:83:1-5/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Another says weighing need not be mentioned when buying because weight and measure jointly indicate buying and selling; the author says the reasoning also applies to measures and is arbitrary.
[TABATABAI:83:1-5/r8] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | A third explanation says the original cheats bought only by measure but sold by measure and weight; the author calls this an unsupported claim, and finds the other explanations weak too.
[ASAD-NOTES:83:1#n1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | The warning extends beyond commerce to every practical and moral social relation, including each person's rights, obligations, and possessions.
[ASAD-NOTES:83:1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | The condemned demand full measure when receiving from others but give less than their due when measuring or weighing what they owe.
[ASAD-NOTES:83:intro/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | reports | Authentic traditions cited by Tabari, Baghawi, and Ibn Kathir place at least the first four verses shortly after the Prophet's arrival in Medina.
