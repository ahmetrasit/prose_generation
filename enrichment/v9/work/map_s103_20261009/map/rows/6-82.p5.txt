[TABATABAI:6:82#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Not every kind of wrongdoing harms faith: mistakes from forgetfulness, ignorance, or lack of awareness do not obstruct faith's path to true felicity.
[TABATABAI:6:82#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse conditions faith's protection from the misery of sins on avoiding wrongdoing that corrupts faith's effect.
[TABATABAI:6:82#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Voluntary wrongdoing varies with understanding: at the threshold of monotheism, shirk is the wrong that blocks faith's safety.
[TABATABAI:6:82#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | After monotheism, major sins such as disobeying parents, consuming an orphan's property, murder, adultery, and drinking wine are the relevant wrongdoing; avoiding major sins brings forgiveness for lesser ones. | mentions 4:31
[TABATABAI:6:82#3/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | At higher spiritual levels, wrongdoing includes disfavored acts, neglecting recommended acts, indulging in permissible things, moral failings, and whatever obstructs love and nearness to God.
[TABATABAI:6:82#3/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Faith secures each person only when free of wrongdoing appropriate to that spiritual rank; in this debate the wrong is shirk and the safety is from eternal punishment, while the verse independently has broader force.
[TABATABAI:6:82#4/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Faith here first means belief in lordship; excluding wrongdoing specifies belief in Allah's true lordship and rejection of partners, distinguishing warranted faith from unsupported partner worship.
[TABATABAI:6:82#4/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verse's universal application concerns every sin that harms faith, safety from its misery, and release from its misguidance, though its immediate debate concerns shirk.
[TABATABAI:6:82#4/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعض المفسرين | reports | A commentator reads wrongdoing universally to include harm to oneself or others, in religious or worldly life, and takes security to cover both religious punishment and worldly consequences.
[TABATABAI:6:82#4/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعض المفسرين | reports | The commentator says God may forgive many worldly wrongs through mercy and may punish or forgive in the afterlife for sins short of shirk.
[TABATABAI:6:82#5/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعض المفسرين | reports | The commentator says absolute safety from divine punishment is unavailable to accountable people except for the reverential awe of the spiritually complete.
[TABATABAI:6:82#5/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعض المفسرين | reports | If the wrongdoing means major shirk, the commentator says believers are safe from eternal punishment while remaining between fear and hope for lesser sins.
[TABATABAI:6:82#5/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعض المفسرين | reports | He also claims the verse is universal and cites the Companions' understanding as evidence.
[TABATABAI:6:82#5/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Tabatabai rejects the commentator's reading: the Companions understood wrongdoing as sin, whereas his universal definition includes non-sinful acts.
[TABATABAI:6:82#5/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | The verse describes faith's effects when not covered by wrongdoing; it does not describe every effect of wrongdoing, such as accidental damage to bodily health.
[TABATABAI:6:82#5/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | If no accountable person can meet the commentator's absolute condition, his reading leaves the verse with no real instance and no argumentative benefit.
[TABATABAI:6:82/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Several linked nominal clauses strongly assert that safety and guidance belong to believers who have not covered their faith with wrongdoing.
[TABATABAI:6:82/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Covering is a metaphor: wrongdoing does not erase innate faith, but conceals it and blocks its proper beneficial effect.
[TABATABAI:6:82/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Although wrongdoing is general in form, the context narrows it to the kind that harms faith and suppresses its good effects.
[TABATABAI:6:82/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | People broadened wrongdoing from unjustly violating social rights to violating laws, committing sins, disobeying God, and even unintentionally damaging their own health.
