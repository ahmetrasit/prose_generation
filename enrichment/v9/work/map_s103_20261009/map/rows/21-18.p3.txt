18#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | God's continuing practice is to give falsehood time, then cast truth upon it so that it vanishes; truth's beliefs, perfection, and prophetic victory endure even against few followers or severe opposition.
[TABATABAI:21:18#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The repeated verb signals an ongoing law, truth's casting on falsehood signals superiority, and the sudden vanishing signals a surprising reversal; the rule is unrestricted across belief, practice, and creation.
[TABATABAI:21:18#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعضهم | reports | Some interpret the verse as saying that God sets earnestness, as a kind of truth, against amusement, but Tabataba'i rejects this because it assumes divine amusement which the preceding passage denied.
[TABATABAI:21:18#2/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The warning is directed at those who deny resurrection and prophethood; return to God means God continuously purifies truth from whatever obscures it until only the absolute truth remains.
[TABATABAI:21:18/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Qadhf is throwing at a distance, damgh is a head wound reaching the brain, and zahūq is perishing; truth has real stability, whereas falsehood only resembles truth, as water differs from a mirage.
[TABATABAI:21:18/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Qur'an treats factual beliefs and the afterlife as truth, worldly possessions and secondary causes as falsehood, and God alone as absolute truth. | mentions 2:147, 38:27
[TABATABAI:21:18/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Falsehood arises as a deficiency relative to something more complete, and is related to God only by permission; a salt flat that appears like water illustrates a merely imagined mirage.
[ASAD-NOTES:21:18#n20/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The truth being cast against falsehood is God’s transcendence, which rejects the idea that God is immanent in or coexists with creation.
[ASAD-NOTES:21:18#n21/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The finite, perishable universe cannot be a projection of its Creator, who is infinite and eternal.
[ASAD-NOTES:21:18#n22/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | The phrase about what people attribute to God also means defining Him; treating God as immanent in creation attempts to define His Being. | mentions 6:100
