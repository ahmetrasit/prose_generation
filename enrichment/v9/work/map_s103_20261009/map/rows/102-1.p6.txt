[ALUSI:102:1/r1] ALUSI, d. 1270 · سعد بن أبي هلال | reports | The Companions called this sūra al-Maqbara, the Graveyard Sūra.
[ALUSI:102:1/r10] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ناصر الدين بن الميلق | reports | Ibn al-Milq explains the thousand-verse merit as one-sixth of the Qur'an's aims: knowing God, the straight path, return to God, the obedient, deniers, and stages of the way; al-Ālūsī says the true reason is beyond this.
[ALUSI:102:1/r11] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-Ālūsī says the sūra's connection to the preceding sūra is evident.
[ALUSI:102:1/r12] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Lahw originally means heedlessness, then came to mean anything distracting; convention narrows it to a pleasurable distraction, close to play. Here it makes people heedless.
[ALUSI:102:1/r13] ALUSI, d. 1270 · الراغب | reports | Al-Rāghib defines lahw as what distracts one from what matters and concerns; al-Ālūsī applies the sense of heedlessness here.
[ALUSI:102:1/r14] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Takāthur is competing and boasting over numbers, each group claiming to be more numerous.
[ALUSI:102:1/r2] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو حيان والجلال السيوطي | reports | Abū Ḥayyān says all exegetes regard the sūra as Meccan; al-Suyūṭī calls that the more common view.
[ALUSI:102:1/r3] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو بريدة | reports | Abū Burayda reports that two Ansari clans, Banū Ḥāritha and Banū al-Ḥārith, boasted of living members, then their dead, prompting the revelation.
[ALUSI:102:1/r4] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | prefers | Al-Ālūsī selects the Medinan view, citing the Ansari occasion and other evidence for a Medinan revelation.
[ALUSI:102:1/r5] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبي بن كعب | reports | Ubayy ibn Kaʿb says they had treated the saying about Adam's two valleys of wealth, desire for a third, and a belly filled only by earth as Qur'an until this sūra came; God accepts the repentance of the repentant.
[ALUSI:102:1/r6] ALUSI, d. 1270 · علي | reports | ʿAlī reports that they continued to doubt punishment in the grave until this sūra was revealed; al-Ālūsī adds that grave punishment is mentioned only in Medinan material.
[ALUSI:102:1/r7] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | The sūra has eight verses by consensus.
[ALUSI:102:1/r8] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn ʿUmar transmits the Prophet's question whether anyone can recite a thousand verses daily and his suggestion to recite this sūra.
[ALUSI:102:1/r9] ALUSI, d. 1270 · عمر بن الخطاب | reports | ʿUmar reports that whoever recites a thousand verses meets God smiling; the Prophet recited this sūra and said it equals a thousand verses.
[ALUSI:102:2/r12] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو مسلم | rejects | Abū Muslim says God addresses the sūra to unbelievers on Judgment Day, reproaching them for graves they had already visited; al-Ālūsī finds this astonishing.
[ALUSI:102:2/r16] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عباس وعائشة ومعاوية وأبو عمران الجوني وأبو صالح ومالك بن دينار وأبو الجوزاء وجماعة | reports | These readers lengthen the initial hamza in alhākum as an interrogative.
[ALUSI:102:2/r17] ALUSI, d. 1270 · أبو بكر الصديق وابن عباس والشعبي وأبو العالية وابن أبي عبلة والكسائي | reports | These readers use two hamzas in a question; the interrogative is for affirmation.
[ALUSI:102:2/r6] ALUSI, d. 1270 · author | holds | Boasting distracts from religion, the most important concern; suppressing the object of distraction magnifies it and intensifies the blame, condemning anything that distracts.
[ALUSI:102:2/r7] ALUSI, d. 1270 · ابن عباس والحسن | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḥasan explain that wealth and children distract people until death, with grave-visiting standing for dying.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#2/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-lahw originally means heedlessness, then broadened to anything that occupies someone; customary usage narrows it to a pleasurable distraction near play, while here it makes people heedless.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#2/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · الراغب | reports | Al-Raghib defines diversion as whatever distracts someone from what matters to them.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#2/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | Al-takathur is competing and boasting over who has more, each group claiming numerical superiority.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#3/r17] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس وعائشة ومعاوية وأبو عمران الجوني وأبو صالح ومالك بن دينار وأبو الجوزاء وجماعة | reports | These readers lengthen the initial alif in alhakum, making the phrase interrogative.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#3/r18] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو بكر الصديق وابن عباس والشعبي وأبي العالية وابن أبي عبلة والكسائي | reports | A transmission from these readers has two hamzas at the start, also forming an interrogative for confirmation.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#3/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The rivalry distracted people from the religious duty that truly matters; omitting the object of distraction magnifies the reproach through ambiguity and emphasizes the blame.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223#3/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ابن عباس | reports | An alternative reading says wealth and children distracted people until death and burial; Ibn Abbas is cited for this interpretation, with al-Hasan also reported by Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Abi Shayba.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223/r1] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو بريدة | reports | Although many call the surah Meccan, al-Alusi chooses Medina based on Abu Burayda's report: two Ansari clans boast of living ancestors, then go to the graves to boast of the dead, prompting its revelation. A report from Ubayy says the warning about Adam's desire for a third valley continued until the surah descended; Ali says it ended doubts about grave punishment, which al-Alusi takes as Medinan evidence because that punishment is mentioned in a Medinan context.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223/r2] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The surah has eight verses unanimously; reports also call it equal to a thousand verses, and its connection with the preceding surah is evident.
[ALUSI-FULL:v30p223/r3] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · ناصر الدين بن الميلق | reports | Ibn al-Milq explains the thousand-verse merit as one sixth of the Qur'an's roughly 6,200 verses: al-Ghazali's six aims include knowledge of the return to God, which this surah contains. Al-Alusi says the matter lies beyond this calculation.
