[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Katâde | reports | Qatada says rival clans claimed superiority and seniority while their people kept dying until all became inhabitants of graves.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The verse's apparent sense best suits numerical boasting; other vain rivalries are implied, and the following kalla rebukes them.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | A second reading says desire for wealth, children, and numbers consumed the whole lifespan until death, leaving no effort for the afterlife; Ahtal and Jarir use grave-visiting for death.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ahtal | reports | Ahtal's verse uses visiting a grave as an expression for dying.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Cerîr | reports | Jarir likewise says Abu Malik visited the graves, meaning he died.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Alî | reports | Ali reports that they remained uncertain about grave punishment until this surah was revealed, yielding the sense until you taste the punishment of the grave.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | On the grave-punishment reading, worldly hoarders awaken when they enter the grave, leaving transient possessions but carrying their burden; in both death readings takathur ends at burial.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The past form zurتم can convey a certain future, include those already dead, depict dying groups, or be construed as a time phrase meaning until the visit.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ebû Müslim | reports | Abu Muslim says God will address unbelievers with this surah on Resurrection Day, but the next clause about certain knowledge fits an address in this world or the grave better.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · A‘râbî | reports | An Arab listener swore that the people would be raised for judgment because a visitor departs rather than remaining at the visited place.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ömer b. Abdülaziz | reports | Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz says every visitor must leave for either Paradise or Hell, and the image also suggests the dead do not remain long in graves.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The first interpretation, tied to the tribal reports, is that people were distracted into counting and boasting of the dead; grave visits should instead awaken reflection and prompt deeds that weigh heavily.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Keşşâf | prefers | On the first report, visiting graves is a metaphor or metonymy for boasting by counting the dead; Kashshaf prefers this reading.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#2/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | A second interpretation takes the visits literally, following the report in which the groups actually went to the graves.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The endpoint clause may attach to takathur or ilhā: people multiplied until visiting graves, were distracted into grave-boasting, or stayed distracted until death or grave-punishment.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | People misuse grave visits when they boast in ancestors without inheriting their virtues, hold displays over graves, waste money for show, and ignore hungry neighbors; such conduct falls under the rebuke.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Dürr-i Muhtâr | reports | The juristic manual says visiting graves is unobjectionable even for women, citing the prophetic instruction to visit.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmdâd, Müctebâ, Bahr ve Redd-i Muhtâr | reports | These manuals say grave-visiting is recommended because the hadith commands it.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Redd-i Muhtâr | reports | Radd al-Muhtar recommends weekly visits and lists Friday, Saturday, Sunday, Monday, and Thursday as preferred days.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Fethu’l-Kadîr | reports | Fath al-Qadir says the established Sunnah during a grave visit is prayer, following the Prophet's practice at Baqi and his greeting to the believers.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Şerh-i Lübâb | reports | The visitor should approach from the feet rather than the head when possible; reports also place the Prophet at the head of one deceased person and the feet of another while reading Baqara.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Şerh-i Lübâb | reports | Adab includes greeting with assalamu alaykum, standing for supplication, sitting according to the deceased's former status if needed, and reciting whatever Quran is easy, including the listed surahs and repeated Ikhlas.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Âbidîn | reports | Ibn Abidin details the dedication of recitation and other acts' rewards to the deceased; the jurists allow transferring rewards from prayer, fasting, charity, and other deeds.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Tatarhâniyye | reports | The best practice is to intend one's charity for all believing men and women, since its reward reaches them without reducing one's own.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Hafız İbn Teymiyye | reports | Ibn Taymiyya wished to forbid dedicating Quran recitation's reward to the Prophet, arguing that it requires his specific permission and that one should instead send blessings and ask for wasila.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | The surface reading favors the first reports, where the endpoint is included and grave-visiting marks the maximum of blameworthy boasting; the rebuke is limited to visits for rivalry.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Sübkî ve daha diğerleri | rejects | Subki and others reject Ibn Taymiyya's restriction, arguing that no specific permission is needed and citing Ibn Umar and others.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The verse leaves other motives outside its explicit condemnation; the Prophetic report permits visiting graves because they remind people of the afterlife.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Because this hadith is not mutawatir, it cannot qualify, restrict, or abrogate the verse, but it can clarify what the verse leaves silent or general and reports an earlier prohibition.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r5] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Râzî | reports | Razi reads the verse as divine astonishment: counting more dead has no benefit, while the proper purpose of visiting is softening the heart, loosening worldly attachment, and recalling the afterlife.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r6] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Râzî | reports | Razi says the visitors did the reverse, approaching graves with hardened hearts and absorption in worldly love; the prophetic report calls grave visits a reminder.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r7] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The verse rebukes visits made to boast over the dead, not visits made for admonition and remembrance; the hadith establishes the latter's legitimacy.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r8] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Âlûsî | reports | Alusi objects that the verse has no bearing on legitimate grave-visiting, then condemns ignorant visitors who boast over saints and commit other wrongs there.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#3/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | rejects | The author says Alusi's polemic risks making even reflective visits seem impermissible; Alusi likely meant the verse is silent on them, but should not have criticized the prior account of their legitimacy.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#4/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Ebû Hanîfe | reports | In advice to Abu Yusuf, Abu Hanifa counsels measured private correction of a ruler, debate with an heretical ruler through Quran and Sunnah, prayer for protection if rejected, remembrance of death, forgiveness for teachers, continued recitation, and frequent visits to graves, shaykhs, and blessed places.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r1] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Reports on grave visitation establish its legitimacy, wisdom, timing, method, and etiquette.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r10] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | One benefits from the deceased by reflecting on their lives and works and keeping their good example alive.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r11] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İbn Âbidîn | reports | Saints differ in their nearness to Allah, and visitors benefit in proportion to their own knowledge and insight into them.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r12] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | A grave visit can affect a person like reading a book, awakening memories and reflection.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r13] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The best visit awakens the afterlife through tenderness of heart and sends the visitor back to life resolved to spend the remaining years in good works.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r14] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | A hadith says grave visitation reminds people of the afterlife.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r15] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Visiting graves to boast and compete reverses their proper lesson and expresses extreme hardheartedness and heedlessness, prompting the rebuke of 102:1–2.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r16] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | On a literal reading, competition may end when graves are visited, or grave visitation itself may end worldly greed.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r17] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Exegetes generally take grave visitation here as a metaphor for death, since death certainly ends competition.
[ELMALILI:102:1-5#5/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Seeing a dead person, attending burial, or later visiting a grave can awaken reflection in receptive hearts, but this is not universal and is derivative of death.
