[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Calling God “your god” and “the god of your fathers” emphasizes that questioner and respondents share one deity.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Repeating “and the god” before a genitive pronoun follows the usual rule that a conjunction on such a pronoun repeats its preposition; omitting it with an explicit noun would better signal unity.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Jacob is named first because he asked, Abraham because he is the origin, and Ishmael before Isaac because he was older or honored as the Prophet’s ancestor.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Only these ancestors are named because they were the best people of their eras; the answer does not enumerate every being people worshipped.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | “One God” can be an apposition or a circumstantial qualifier; either makes explicit that one shared deity is a single individual, not several.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari parses “one God” as specification, but grammarians disallow specification with an indefinite or ambiguous noun.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | “We submit to Him” is best read as a second coordinated part of the answer: worship is recurring action, while the nominal “submitting” expresses their permanent surrender.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p642/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari calls the clause parenthetical and explanatory; Abu Hayyan rejects this because it is not inserted between tightly linked parts of one speaker’s statement.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p643/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | A parenthetical clause strengthens a relation between parts that belong together, such as a relative clause and antecedent or a condition and result; “we submit to Him” is not such an insertion.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p643/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | The sons’ statement is complete before “we submit,” and God’s statement follows it, so it cannot be a parenthesis inserted by the same speaker between linked parts.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p643/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | There is no need to supply Ibn Atiyya’s omitted “thus we were”; the clause can coordinate directly with “we worship,” which is preferable to ellipsis.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p644#2/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya says this verse refutes the determinists who deny that a servant acquires or performs acts.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p644#2/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أهل السنة والجماعة | holds | The Sunni position is that God creates capacity and empowerment alongside the act; a person performs good or evil without compulsion, and this grounds reward and punishment.
