[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Most codices and tafsirs, including al-Tirmidhi's heading, call it al-Takathur; some call it Alhakum, as al-Bukhari titles it, while companions reportedly called it al-Maqbara.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · سعيد بن أبي هلال | reports | Said ibn Abi Hilal reports that the Prophet's companions called the surah al-Maqbara.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn Abbas, al-Kalbi, and Muqatil report a Meccan occasion involving a rivalry between Banu Abd Manaf and Banu Sahm; al-Qurtubi attributes the account to them and Ibn Abbas.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Al-Itqan prefers a Madinan setting, citing a report about two Ansari clans and Ubayy ibn Kaʿb's statement that Muslims had recited a saying about Adam's wealth as Qur'an until this surah came.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | rejects | The Ubayy report does not prove a Madinan revelation: “we” could mean Muslims generally, who had mistaken a saying for Qur'an until the Prophet clarified it.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | prefers | The warning's severe tone and content indicate a Meccan audience of polytheists, since the behavior described does not fit Muslims at that time.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · مقاتل | reports | The reported rivalry had Banu Abd Manaf count living nobles, then their dead; Banu Sahm surpassed them by three graves because their pre-Islamic numbers had been greater.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p517/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Al-Wahidi and al-Baghawi transmit the rivalry account from Muqatil and al-Kalbi, with al-Qurtubi also citing them and Ibn Abbas.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p518/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · أبو بريدة الجرمي | reports | Abu Burayda al-Jurmi reports that Banu Haritha and Banu al-Harith of the Ansar boasted of living people, then pointed to notable graves in each group; the verse was revealed.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p518/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | On the Meccan chronology, it is the sixteenth surah revealed, after al-Kawthar and before al-Maʿun, and has eight verses.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p518/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The surah rebukes neglect of Qur'anic evidence and Islam's call in favor of wealth, accumulation, and ancestral boasting until death, and warns against that course.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alhakum means that something occupied people and diverted them from what they ought to attend to; takathur is competitive accumulation of desired things.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The competition can concern wealth or numbers of children and allies used for prestige, as in the boast about having more wealth and children.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The author connects the numbers boast with the statement that people claimed greater wealth and children and therefore no punishment.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿsha's line says he is not the one with the most counted heads, but honor belongs to the larger group, illustrating rivalry in numbers.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · عبد الله بن الشخير | reports | Muslim reports that Ibn al-Shikhkhir heard the Prophet recite the verse and explain that a person keeps saying “my wealth,” though only what he consumes, wears out, or gives away remains.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The hadith illustrates one sense of accumulation, selected to suit the occasion of that admonition, and is compatible with the verse.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r7] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The severe threats identify the addressees as polytheists, since this behavior was not a Muslim custom then; wealthy chiefs are especially targeted for preferring comfort to the Prophet's call and suppressing it.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p519/r8] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Although directly addressed to polytheists, the warning teaches Muslims to resist love of wealth when it distracts from good and to fear death arriving during heedlessness.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p520/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, the endpoint is the object of rivalry: they compete over everything, even graves, as in the report about Banu Abd Manaf and Banu Sahm counting tombs.
[TABATABAI:102:1-6/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The surah sharply rebukes people distracted by competing over wealth, children, and numbers, warning of loss and punishment and that they will be questioned about blessings they met with ingratitude.
[TABATABAI:102:1-6/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Raghib defines diversion as whatever occupies a person from what matters, and takathur as competing to have more wealth and prestige.
[TABATABAI:102:1-6/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The contextual meaning is that competition for worldly goods and adornment distracts from remembering God until death, leaving people heedless throughout life.
[TABATABAI:102:1-6/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · unnamed view | reports | Another interpretation has people boast about having more living men, then count and boast about their dead after all the living have died.
[TABATABAI:102:1-6/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The men-counting interpretation rests on reports that two Anṣar tribes first competed over the living and then the dead, or that Banu Abd Manaf and Banu Sahm did so in Mecca, prompting the surah.
[TABATABAI:102:1-8/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · قتادة | reports | A report says the surah concerned Jews boasting that their group outnumbered others; the rivalry kept them deluded until death.
[TABATABAI:102:8/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو بريدة | reports | One reported occasion says the chapter came down about a clan of the Ansar competing in boasts.
