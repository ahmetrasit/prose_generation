[ASAD-NOTES:83:1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | The condemned demand full measure when receiving from others but give less than their due when measuring or weighing what they owe.
[ASAD-NOTES:83:intro/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | reports | Many authorities, including Suyuti, regard the surah as the last Meccan revelation; some commentators assign the whole surah to Medina.
[ASAD-NOTES:83:intro/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | reports | Authentic traditions cited by Tabari, Baghawi, and Ibn Kathir place at least the first four verses shortly after the Prophet's arrival in Medina.
[ASAD-NOTES:83:intro/r3] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | prefers | Weighing the evidence, Asad assigns the main body to late Mecca and the opening to early Medina, making the surah a threshold between periods like surah 29. | mentions 29:1
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p223/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Sūrah al-Muṭaffifīn supplements al-Infiṭār, sharing its theme and elaborating the division of righteous and defiant people at its end.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p223/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Infiṭār reasons from God’s attributes displayed in the human body, while this sūrah reasons from the moral nature God gave people.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p223/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Human nature loves justice and good and dislikes injustice and evil; this moral distinction shows that the Creator differentiates the just from the unjust rather than treating them alike.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p223/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | People commit injustice when other motives overpower their nature, as shown by the dishonest trader who protests when others cheat him.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p223/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The Qur’an repeatedly uses this reasoning for the Day of Judgement; the author points to his discussion of 75:14–15. | mentions 75:14, 75:15
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p224/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 1–6 censure people who demand fair treatment while cheating others; their conduct shows they do not expect to be raised before their Lord for judgement.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p226/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The opening warning is not a neutral statement; it also conveys a curse and censure.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p226/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Taṭfīf means giving short measure or weight.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p227/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | This nature shows that people distinguish good from evil and expect different treatment; God must therefore judge righteous and wrongdoers, and the deniers resist because they wish to evade that outcome.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p227/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · exegetes | reports | Exegetes report that dishonest measuring among the Anṣār occasioned revelation of this verse.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p227/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | The author challenges that report’s setting: the sūrah is Meccan, and dishonest trade was at least as plausible among the Meccan traders as among the agricultural Anṣār.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p228/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | Even if the Anṣār had the practice, it was no more prevalent than among the Meccans, who were traders, while the Anṣār’s profession was agriculture; the verse’s main point is innate recognition of justice, which implies judgement and accountability.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:83:1/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · hadisler | reports | Hadith reports also describe Wayl as the name of a valley in Hell.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:83:1/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râgıb el-İsfahânî | reports | Raghib says the valley meaning is figurative: those addressed by wayl will suffer Hell's punishment.
[STUDYQURAN:83:1/r1] STUDYQURAN · Ibn ʿAbbās | reports | When the Prophet first reached Madinah, its people were notorious defrauders; after revelation of these verses, they weighed honestly.
[STUDYQURAN:83:1/r2] STUDYQURAN · Abū Hurayrah | reports | The verses refer directly to a man who used separate scales for buying and selling.
[STUDYQURAN:83:1/r3] STUDYQURAN · Prophet | reports | The Prophet linked covenant-breaking with enemies, judging apart from revelation with poverty, fornication with death, fraud with crop failure and famine, and withholding alms with withheld rain; no grading is stated.
[STUDYQURAN:83:1/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The warning extends beyond commerce: anyone who intentionally falls short in anything ordained by God may face shame and suffering.
[STUDYQURAN:83:1/r5] STUDYQURAN · Ibn Masʿūd | reports | Ibn Masʿūd compares prayer to a measure: completing it brings full reward, while reducing it recalls the warning against defrauders.
[STUDYQURAN:83:intro/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The sūrah may be late Makkan, early Madinan, or a combination of both.
[STUDYQURAN:83:intro/r2] STUDYQURAN · Bg, IK, Ṭ | reports | One account places the first four verses just after the Prophet arrived in Madinah, verses 5–28 in Madinah, and the final eight among the last verses revealed in Makkah.
[STUDYQURAN:83:intro/r4] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The name al-Muṭaffifīn means those who skim a little off the top, shortchanging others; the word derives from ṭafīf and ṭaff, the edge.
[STUDYQURAN:83:intro/r5] STUDYQURAN · Q, R | reports | More broadly, defrauders demand that their own rights be observed while neglecting others' rights, or see others' faults but not their own.
[STUDYQURAN:83:intro/r6] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The sūrah condemns shortchanging, contrasts the fates of profligates and pious people, and closes with sinners mocking the Prophet and believers.
