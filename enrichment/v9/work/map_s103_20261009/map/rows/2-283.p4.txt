Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects both limits, saying trust is common and sound at home too.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385#3/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · جمهور القراء | reports | Most readers have kātiban, “a writer”; Ubayy b. Kaʿb and Ibn ʿAbbās read kitāban, a verbal noun or a plural of writer, and Mujāhid and Abū al-ʿĀliya explain it as absence of writing tools or supplies. Ibn ʿAbbās also has a plural reading for writers, and Abū al-ʿĀliya reads kutuban, books; Ibn ʿAṭiyya accepts the readings except insofar as the consonantal text favors one.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p386/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · نافع وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي | reports | They and most scholars read rihān; Abū ʿAmr and Ibn Kathīr read ruhun, sometimes with a lightened hāʾ, and groups read both forms.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p386/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Rahn in Arabic can mean to remain or continue; a poem's “meat and bread are lasting” illustrates the sense. Jurists consequently void a pledge when it leaves the creditor's possession, since it departs from its defining state.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p386/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Arhana can mean to bid heavily for an item until obtaining it at a high price, illustrated by a poem about a noble she-camel for which dinars were paid.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p386/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو علي | reports | Some attest arhantu irhānan for collateral, while Abū ʿAlī distinguishes arhantu for outbidding from rahantu for loan or sale; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says rahantu rahnan is standard without disagreement, with the verbal noun transferred to the pledged object and treated as a noun.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p386/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The pledge noun's plural takes kasra like nouns and lexicalized verbal nouns; in “I pledged a garment,” the verb takes an object rather than an internal verbal noun. Abū ʿAlī nevertheless allows arhantu, though rahantu is more frequent.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p386/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | Poetic examples from al-Aʿshā and Hammām b. Murra attest forms from both rahana and arhāna in the pledge sense.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p387/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الزجاج وابن الأعرابي | reports | Both rahantu and arhantu are said for pledging a security; rahantu is used for pledging one’s tongue, not arhantu.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p387/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The readings farhān, farhun with both vowels, and farhun with a silent hāʾ are respectively a plural, another plural pattern, and a lightened form of rahn.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p387/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو علي | holds | He knows no broken plural of rahn for a small number; if one existed, its expected pattern would be afʿul, but usage relies on other plural patterns.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p387/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الطبري والفراء | reports | They report an interpretation of rahnun with damma as plural of rihān, itself a plural of rahn.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p387/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The word maqbuḍa implies the pledge passes into the mortgagee’s possession; possession by the mortgagee or the mortgagee’s agent is unanimously valid.
