## THE CURRENT MAP
33:72/q01 What does the plural in إِنَّا convey? [إِنَّا · rhetoric]
  33:72/q01/p1 Majesty, or to mystics the plurality of divine names and attributes. — reasons: Bursevī attributes the first reading to scholars and the second to knowers.
33:72/q02 How did the offer expressed by عَرَضْنَا occur? [عَرَضْنَا · interpretation] (turns on: Actual speech, figurative comparison, lexical presentation, and the status of the offer.)
  33:72/q02/p1 It was a real offer to the created bodies, given perception and speech. — reasons: Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer direct address; Ibn al-Anbārī says Adam heard their reply. Bursevī cites speaking ants and stones. Qurṭubī says inanimate things would need life.
  33:72/q02/p2 It is a hypothetical parable about the trust's gravity. — reasons: Qaffāl and Qurṭubī compare 59:21; Zamakhsharī cites Arabic imaginary speech of fat and animals; Ṭabrisī cites address to ruins; Ibn ʿAṭiyya tests a camel against its load. Ālūsī says counterfactual language is made vivid.
  33:72/q02/p3 It means comparing or weighing the trust against the cosmic bodies. — reasons: Ibn Baḥr says it outweighed them; Abū Muslim compares commands, prohibitions, books, messengers, and covenant. Ṭabāṭabāʾī assesses fitness for divine guardianship.
  33:72/q02/p4 It means displaying, offering, or inspecting. — reasons: Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī compare presenting a maiden for sale; Bursevī lists offering merchandise or inspecting soldiers.
  33:72/q02/p5 The offer was a choice, whereas humanity's charge was binding. — reasons: Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Rāzī and Qurṭubī say refusal could not have been permissible had the charge been imposed. Qushayrī distinguishes the human's imposed duty. Jishumī argues instead that beneficial obligation cannot depend on choice.
  33:72/q02/p6 The offer described obligation or tested readiness. — reasons: Jishumī says God obligates for welfare without coercion; Bayḍāwī says it tested readiness; Māwardī says acceptance followed informed understanding.
33:72/q03 What is ٱلْأَمَانَةَ? [ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · meaning] (turns on: General charge, particular trust, moral faculty, covenant, and inward perfection.)
  33:72/q03/p1 The general charge of obedience, religious duties, commands, prohibitions and accountability. — reasons: Ṭabarī prefers the unrestricted sense, including worldly trusts. Ibn Kathīr reconciles duty, obedience and limits. Rāzī stresses action against inclination while angels obey naturally (21:20). Elmalılı calls it voluntary vicegerency over God's and people's rights.
  33:72/q03/p2 People's deposits, property, promises and covenants. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd gives deposits special weight; Ibn ʿAbbās forbids cheating believers or protected non Muslims. Cain's betrayal supplies an early example; Samarrāʾī includes vows, oaths and contracts.
  33:72/q03/p3 Prayer, fasting and major ritual washing, sometimes singled out as washing or prayer. — reasons: A prophetic report names three duties one may falsely claim to perform (86:9); Abū al-Dardāʾ names washing. ʿAṭāʾ also lists zakāt and pilgrimage; Wahb cites the Psalms.
  33:72/q03/p4 Chastity and entrusted bodily faculties. — reasons: Ubayy names a woman's chastity; Ibn ʿAmr lists sexual organs, hearing, sight, tongue, belly, hand and foot. Sulamī extends the trust to self, heart, spirit and limbs; looking into homes breaches the eye's trust.
  33:72/q03/p5 Reason, relative free will and the capacity for moral choice. — reasons: Asad prefers reason and volition. Bayḍāwī says reason governs anger and appetite; duties moderate them. Kuran Yolu groups senses under free will and accountability.
  33:72/q03/p6 Tawḥīd, faith, or the primordial testimony to God. — reasons: Sulamī's Ibn ʿAṭāʾ names singular devotion; Qushayrī adds observance of its limits. Study Quran cites 7:172. Rāzī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject bare monotheism because cosmos already proclaims it.
  33:72/q03/p7 Signs of God's lordship placed in creation. — reasons: Some theologians say created bodies display the signs, whereas humans conceal and deny them; Ibn Isḥāq names signs of divine unity.
  33:72/q03/p8 Divine guardianship, the perfection of sincere servanthood. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this because bare knowledge or monotheism cannot account for hypocrisy, shirk and faith. He interprets the report on ʿAlī's wilāya as an exemplary application.
  33:72/q03/p9 Divine identity and self manifestation, or love carried inwardly. — reasons: Qāshānī contrasts cosmic disclosure of attributes with human selfhood's concealment. Bursevī describes duties, love amid trial, and unmediated effusion; Ḥāfiẓ and Jāmī contrast angels' worship with Adam's love.
  33:72/q03/p10 The liability of reward and punishment itself. — reasons: Naḥḥās reports this as an alternative.
  33:72/q03/p11 A binding entrusted covenant or right to be kept intact and returned. — reasons: Jishumī calls it a contract; Ṭabāṭabāʾī a charge to preserve. Ibn al-Jawzī and Elmalılı derive it from security and confidence; Biqāʿī contrasts fulfillment and betrayal.
33:72/q04 What happened during the offer according to the reports? [عَرَضْنَا ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · reports] (turns on: These reports differ in sequence, time, form of the trust and whether the subject is duty or Adam's family.)
  33:72/q04/p1 Adam accepted reward and punishment for good and evil, then sinned within hours or the same day. — reasons: Reports vary between noon and afternoon, afternoon and sunset, and noon and night; some credit Satan's temptation. Al-Ḥasan includes seven heavens and earths and Throne bearers.
  33:72/q04/p2 God promised Adam safeguards for sight, speech and sexual modesty. — reasons: Versions say he accepted between ears and shoulders; he received eyelids, a restrainable tongue and covered private parts. Ibn ʿAṭiyya doubts further details of the report.
  33:72/q04/p3 Creation pleaded for three days and nights, lacking capacity and not seeking reward. — reasons: Ibn al-Adshūʿ gives this prolonged groaning.
  33:72/q04/p4 The trust appeared as a rock that Adam carried to his shoulder. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd says the burden passed to descendants until Resurrection.
  33:72/q04/p5 Adam sought a guardian or successor; Cain accepted, then murdered Abel. — reasons: One version is at Adam's death; another is before pilgrimage to Mecca. Al-Suddī adds offerings and a marriage dispute (5:28, 5:30). Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm rejects the deathbed account as unsupported and inconsistent with legal demands on mountains.
  33:72/q04/p6 The heavens were offered it one after another, followed by earth and mountains. — reasons: An unnamed report supplies this order; al-Ḥasan adds Throne bearers.
  33:72/q04/p7 Adam was promised honor and Paradise for obedience, punishment for disobedience. — reasons: Ibn Ḥayyān gives a dialogue; Ibn ʿUthmān says Adam saw the alternatives.
33:72/q05 Who received the offer addressed to the heavens, earth, and mountains? [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ وَٱلْجِبَالِ · referent] (turns on: Direct address, ellipsis for inhabitants, or an imagined comparison.)
  33:72/q05/p1 The created bodies themselves. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid affirm direct address; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer it. God could grant perception and speech; the mountains emphasize the burden.
  33:72/q05/p2 Their accountable inhabitants, including angels, humans and jinn. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan supplies inhabitants; Jishumī and al-Jubbāʾī say physical bodies cannot bear moral responsibility. Study Quran includes people, jinn and angels; Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows the angels.
  33:72/q05/p3 They are imagined comparators rather than actual addressees. — reasons: Qaffāl, Zamakhsharī, Ālūsī and Kuran Yolu read cosmic scale as representing the trust's weight.
33:72/q06 Why did the created bodies refuse and fear it? [فَأَبَيْنَ أَن يَحْمِلْنَهَا وَأَشْفَقْنَ مِنْهَا · interpretation] (turns on: Fearful choice, natural incapacity, or a figure for avoiding betrayal.)
  33:72/q06/p1 They revered God's religion and feared failure and punishment, without disobeying. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās stresses reverence; Rāzī contrasts Iblis's arrogant refusal of an obligation (15:31). Qushayrī calls their fear compassion. Al-Ḥasan says they did not want reward or punishment.
  33:72/q06/p2 They lacked natural capacity or readiness. — reasons: Ibn Baḥr and Abū Muslim make refusal inability; Bayḍāwī contrasts human rational capacity. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their size (40:57) does not mean fitness for guardianship.
  33:72/q06/p3 Already subjected to God's command, they declined added accountability and reward. — reasons: Ibn Jurayj describes heaven as a protected roof; Mujāhid says creation already bears inhabitants. Ibn al-Adshūʿ records a three day plea.
  33:72/q06/p4 They refused treachery and the burden of sin while fulfilling their duties. — reasons: Zajjāj compares bearing sins (35:18); Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī cite 29:13. Jishumī rejects meritorious refusal of a command as disbelief and prefers this; al-Jubbāʾī assigns refusal of liability to angels.
  33:72/q06/p5 They initially withheld divine perfections but ultimately displayed them. — reasons: Qāshānī's inward account contrasts them with human selfhood's concealment.
33:72/q07 How does refusal fit the creation's obedience elsewhere? [فَأَبَيْنَ · links] (turns on: Optional offer versus distinct modes of obedience.)
  33:72/q07/p1 This offer was optional while 41:11 records a direct command. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes command from choice. Zajjāj and Baghawī cite 41:11, 2:74, 22:18; Bursevī invokes 17:44 and examples of mountains, wind, earth, a moaning trunk and a greeting stone.
  33:72/q07/p2 Creation fulfilled its own form of submission while accountable humans could betray theirs. — reasons: Zajjāj, Nasafī and Biqāʿī distinguish creative from moral command. Jishumī says angels do not disobey, unlike humans and jinn.
