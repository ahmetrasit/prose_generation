[RAZI:102:1/r18] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The definite article is not generic; it points to the familiar worldly multiplication of pleasures and attachments that distracts from God's worship, a known evil across reason and religions.

[RAZI:102:1/r2] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | In ordinary Arabic, a person who says something distracted him means that it made him forget and occupied him.
[RAZI:102:1/r20] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Razi sees the grave-visiting wording as matching a past rivalry; the rebuke asks what it would profit people even if they were more numerous.
[RAZI:102:1/r23] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A second view identifies the rivalry as pursuit of wealth, supported by a report in which the Prophet recites the verse and explains what a person's wealth actually consists of.
[RAZI:102:1/r3] RAZI, d. 606 · الزبير | reports | A report says al-Zubayr would leave his conversation when he heard thunder; the phrase لهى عن حديثه means he abandoned and turned away from it.
[RAZI:102:1/r30] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A third view says greed for wealth prevented people from paying financial obligations until death, when they finally bequeathed money for zakat and pilgrimage.
[RAZI:102:1/r31] RAZI, d. 606 · author | reports | A fourth view says rivalry turns hearts to stone, leaving only the small amount of religious feeling associated with visiting graves; Razi compares this with the rebuke that people give little thanks. | mentions 7:10
[RAZI:102:1/r32] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The verse leaves the object of ألهاكم unstated because an absolute construction intensifies blame and lets the mind supply every possible object.
[RAZI:102:1/r33] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The omitted object includes neglect of remembrance, duties, recommended acts, religious knowledge, worship, reflection, and contemplation.
[RAZI:102:1/r34] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Read against the preceding discussion of the Calamity, the verse means that worldly rivalry distracted people from contemplating it and preparing for it before death. | mentions 101:1
[RAZI:102:1/r35] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Read with the following grave-visitation clause, the verse means that rivalry made people forget the grave until they actually visited it.
[RAZI:102:1/r4] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Takāthur is boasting against others about abundance in wealth, status, and distinctions.
[RAZI:102:1/r5] RAZI, d. 606 · أبو مسلم | reports | Abū Muslim notes that the reciprocal pattern tafāʿul can express an exchange between two people, a deliberate effort to do something, or an action occurring intransitively.
[RAZI:102:1/r6] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | The word may describe reciprocal rivalry, each person claiming more wealth and followers, or strenuous efforts by the ambitious to keep multiplying wealth.
[RAZI:102:1/r7] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | Al-Rāzī treats takāthur and tafākhur as synonymous and links the verse to the wording وتفاخر بينكم. | mentions 57:20
[RAZI:102:1/r8] RAZI, d. 606 · author | holds | People boast about three ranks of felicity: the inward soul, the body, and what surrounds the body; inward felicity includes knowledge and virtuous character, bodily felicity includes health and beauty, and external goods include wealth, status, relatives, and friends.
