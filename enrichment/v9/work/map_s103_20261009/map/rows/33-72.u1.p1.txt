33:72/q09 What does حَمَلَهَا mean? [وَحَمَلَهَا · interpretation] (turns on: Acceptance, unfulfilled liability, capacity, or divine enabling.)
  33:72/q09/p1 The human accepted responsibility for the trust and its due. — reasons: Māwardī and Qurṭubī stress undertaking. Ālūsī allows innate capacity or a primordial pledge; Bursevī cites 17:70, 55:60 and fruitful weak trees.
  33:72/q09/p2 It means betraying or continuing to bear an undischarged liability. — reasons: Zajjāj compares sin's load (35:18); Zamakhsharī compares debt riding its custodian. Ṭabrisī rejects a cited poem as proof; Abū Ḥayyān says it could mean accepting more trusts.
  33:72/q09/p3 It signifies readiness or capacity, before complete performance. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes capacity for knowledge and justice decisive; Bayḍāwī says reason can govern anger and appetite.
  33:72/q09/p4 It can be borne through God's help and the heart. — reasons: Junayd says Adam trusted God; another saying makes joy obscure the burden. Sulamī faults self reliance; Qushayrī cites poetry about wisdom's mountains; Rāzī cites 1:5.
33:72/q10 Who is ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent] (turns on: Adam, humanity collectively, guilty people alone, or Cain.)
  33:72/q10/p1 Adam, who accepted the trust and passed it to descendants. — reasons: Reports tell his quick fall; Ibn ʿAbbās calls him gullible about God's command. Bursevī links Adam to vicegerency (2:30).
  33:72/q10/p2 The human species or most people collectively, not every individual. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī prefer this for the universal trust. Biqāʿī says a generic singular need not include all and compares 7:85. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ studies 65 uses linking insān to speech, learning, agency and trial, unlike fire created jinn.
  33:72/q10/p3 The unbeliever and hypocrite who betrayed it. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan and Zajjāj say obedient prophets and believers are not unjust or ignorant; 33:73 distinguishes punishment and repentance. Others include sinful believers by degree.
  33:72/q10/p4 Cain, Adam's appointed family guardian who murdered Abel. — reasons: Al-Suddī and related reports place Adam's trip at Mecca.
33:72/q11 What do ظَلُومًۭا and جَهُولًۭا mean? [ظَلُومًۭا جَهُولًۭا · interpretation] (turns on: Rash undertaking, betrayal, ignorance of God, innate potential, or general human tendency.)
  33:72/q11/p1 The bearer wrongs himself or the trust, ignorant of its due and consequences. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās calls Adam rash; Qatāda says he wronged the trust. Bursevī defines injustice as misplacing by excess, deficiency, time or place, distinguishing Adam from Iblis; Kuran Yolu stresses the need for guidance.
  33:72/q11/p2 He is ignorant of his Lord, describing the unbeliever or hypocrite. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan restricts blame; 33:73 distinguishes believers' repentance.
  33:72/q11/p3 Ignorance and injustice are initial capacities permitting ascent to knowledge and justice. — reasons: Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 95:4–6; he reports intensive adjectives as either characteristic dispositions or extreme degrees.
  33:72/q11/p4 The fault is imagining one can bear the trust independently of God. — reasons: Sulamī faults self reliance; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says Adam failed to fear what creation feared; Junayd emphasizes aid.
  33:72/q11/p5 The adjectives describe most people while righteous individuals are excepted. — reasons: Ālūsī says the clause foreshadows breach of original nature (30:30). Biqāʿī says desire overcomes reason; Elmalılı and Asad qualify the generic statement.
33:72/q12 What inward account explains the trust and its bearer? [وَحَمَلَهَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · inward] (turns on: Divine help, love's stages, or disclosure of divine attributes.)
  33:72/q12/p1 The heart bears through divine aid what the body cannot. — reasons: Junayd, Fāris and al-Ḥusayn differ over Adam's awareness of help and joy in the gift. Qushayrī cites wisdom's mountains; Bursevī quotes Persian verse.
  33:72/q12/p2 The trust rises through legal duties, divine love, then unmediated effusion. — reasons: Bursevī assigns levels to common people, elect and most elect; annihilation and subsistence remove veils. Ḥāfiẓ and Jāmī contrast angels with Adam's love.
  33:72/q12/p3 Cosmic perfections reveal divine attributes, while human selfhood can conceal them. — reasons: Qāshānī says heaven and earth deliver their perfections whereas humans claim ownership and block knowledge.
33:72/q13 How does this verse connect to nearby or comparable passages? [* · links] (turns on: Surah context, cosmic submission, dignity, and other trust passages.)
  33:72/q13/p1 It magnifies duties after commands and promises, before judgment of hypocrites, polytheists and believers. — reasons: Qurṭubī links earlier laws to compliance; Bayḍāwī sees reinforcement of the prior promise. Samarrāʾī compares 33:24 and 33:73: lām, divine will, repentance, women and forgiveness vary with context.
  33:72/q13/p2 Other passages show cosmic submission and human elevation or fall. — reasons: Zajjāj cites 41:11, 2:74, 22:18; Qurṭubī cites 59:21. Qushayrī and Bursevī invoke 17:70; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 95:4–6.
  33:72/q13/p3 Trust passages connect duty with covenant keeping. — reasons: Damghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī cite 8:27; Samarrāʾī cites 23:8 and says patience in keeping trust curbs impulsiveness.
  33:72/q13/p4 A general trust command can follow a related case despite being revealed later. — reasons: Al-Itqān cites 4:51–58: Ibn al-ʿArabī calls concealment of Muḥammad's description betrayal. The general verse was revealed about six years later but placed by prophetic instruction.
33:72/q14 What specific teachings about trust do reports give? [ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · reports] (turns on: Particular duties, warnings about breach, and trust's transmission.)
  33:72/q14/p1 Keep deposits, debts, chastity, speech and confidences. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd adds prayers, zakāt, fasting, pilgrimage, truthful speech and fair weights. The muezzin guards prayer times; gatherings and intimate speech are confidential. Ibn Qutayba distinguishes betrayal from theft and its amputation penalty.
  33:72/q14/p2 Martyrdom does not erase breach of trust; its bearer must retrieve it from Hell. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd describes it slipping and the bearer falling; al-Barāʾ confirms. Ibn Kathīr calls a second route good though not in canonical collections.
  33:72/q14/p3 Five deeds with faith lead to Paradise; the last trust is major ritual washing. — reasons: Abū al-Dardāʾ lists prayer, willing zakāt, Ramadan fasting and pilgrimage if able.
  33:72/q14/p4 Trust came through prophets, then trust and covenants were withdrawn while scripture remained. — reasons: Al-Ḥakam's report warns against temptation; Ibn Kathīr calls it rare but corroborated. Ḥudhayfa's report of trust leaving hearts in stages is in Bukhārī and Muslim.
  33:72/q14/p5 Guard trust with truthfulness, good character and lawful food. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr records Ibn ʿAmr and Ibn ʿUmar routes with a chain and attribution difference.
  33:72/q14/p6 Swearing by trust is forbidden or strongly disapproved. — reasons: ʿUmar forbade it; Burayda reports a prophetic warning transmitted by Abū Dāwūd.
  33:72/q14/p7 Preservation is faithful; withholding or misuse is betrayal. — reasons: Biqāʿī contrasts delivery with retention. Al-Ḥasan rebukes currying rulers and unlawful wealth. Jishumī says human acts are their own, making commands and threats coherent.
33:72/q15 What ancillary language evidence appears in the notes? [ٱلْأَمَانَةَ · meaning] (turns on: Lexical trust evidence and ancillary language examples.)
  33:72/q15/p1 Amāna comes from security, confidence and trustworthiness. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī cites Ibn Fāris on amīna, amīn, amān and amūn plus a merchant verse; Elmalılı prefers the verbal noun sense. Samarrāʾī explains plural amānāt versus singular ʿahd.
  33:72/q15/p2 Khiyāna is failure to discharge an entrusted object. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba says all betrayers steal but not conversely; theft entails amputation, breach of entrusted goods does not. Al-Namir's house keeper line illustrates.
  33:72/q15/p3 Lamentative forms allow optional alif after yā or wā with vowel and possessive changes. — reasons: Sībawayh discusses fatḥa before alif, adjacent sukūns, retained yā, pause hāʾ; al-Khalīl and Yūnus allow variants. Ibn Qays and Ruʾbah supply poetic forms.
  33:72/q15/p4 Prose and poetry illustrate balanced expression, wordplay and metaphor. — reasons: Al-Bāqillānī cites ʿAlī's aphorisms, Khālid's ring pun and Imru al-Qays's swift horse as a fetter for game.
  33:72/q15/p5 Poetry attests faithful custody of a trust. — reasons: Biqāʿī cites al-Nābigha and Ibn al-Furāt's report of ʿUmar's judgment.
33:72/q16 What conditions and consequences did the offer state? [* · reports] (turns on: Whether the reported terms stress preservation and grave sin or reward and punishment.)
  33:72/q16/p1 It commanded preservation of the trust, with grave sin for wasting it. — reasons: Māwardī reports this as one account of the offer's terms.
  33:72/q16/p2 Good performance earned reward, while failure incurred punishment. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan's account, transmitted by Qurṭubī through Maʿmar, says the heavens, earth and mountains refused after hearing these terms.
