[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p120/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari says traders seek both safe capital and profit; the hypocrites lost guidance, their capital, and gained no true profit even if they obtained worldly goods, since a trader whose capital is gone has not profited.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p120/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abu Hayyan finds that response inconclusive: denying profit alone does not prove that capital was lost or diminished, because profit is only the excess over capital.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p120/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Wa-mā kānū muhtadīn completes the meaning by stating what the denial of profit does not: that they lost their capital; this is rhetorical completion, illustrated by an Imruʾ al-Qays line.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p120/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The exchange is called trade though disbelief has no benefit because they believed or hoped for worldly and afterlife returns; groups claimed divine favor or that punishment would last only a few days, while disbelief nullifies deeds. | mentions 25:23, 5:18, 2:80, 37:59
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p120/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | A hadith says that when asked whether Ibn Judʿan benefited from kinship and feeding the poor, the Prophet answered no because he never prayed for forgiveness on the Day of Judgment. | mentions 26:82
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p120/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Possible senses of they were not guided include not being guided in God's knowledge, being unguided from error, lacking a profitable trade or a sound choice, or combining loss of profit with lack of guidance; some traders may fail to profit yet remain rightly guided.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p120/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | Abu Hayyan prefers that they had never actually received guidance before, preventing the phrase bought guidance from implying that they once possessed it; the segment breaks as he begins to elaborate.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p121/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The exchange of guidance is metaphorical: they exchanged the capacity to apprehend guidance, whereas the closing denial concerns guidance actually attained.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p121/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farrāʾ treats مهتدين as originally circumstantial because the subject is occupied by كان; its semantic usefulness lets it function as a definite predicate.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v1p121/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | rejects | Abū Ḥayyān rejects the circumstantial analysis attributed to al-Farrāʾ, preferring the straightforward predicate analysis.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:16/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Making الذين a second subject leaves no link between أولئك and its predicate, and its past-tense relative clause also resists the proposal.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:16/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | Neither apposition nor adjective analysis works: it would pair a specific noun with another specific, and الذين is more definite than أولئك.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:16/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The usual damma on plural waw in اشتروا has been explained by analogy with subject tāʾ, distinction from root waw, ease, the deleted yāʾ vowel, or its plural meaning.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:16/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The waw is also read with kasra, its basic sukūn-junction vowel, or fatḥa, as lighter; al-Kisa'i's hamza is weak because its damma is not obligatory.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:16/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | rejects | Abu al-Baqa calls deletion of the waw's vowel by اختلاس extremely weak, since the preceding fatḥa cannot indicate it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:16/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | اشتروا derives from استريوا: the moving yāʾ became alif and was deleted at two sukūns, or its vowel was removed and the yāʾ itself deleted.
