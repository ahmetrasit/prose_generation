## THE CURRENT MAP
28:29/q01 What does qaḍā al-ajal say Moses completed? [قَضَىٰ ٱلْأَجَلَ · meaning]
  28:29/q01/p1 He fulfilled the agreed period of service and its work. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī identifies the agreement with the father-in-law who gave his daughter in marriage; al-Biqāʿī says Moses fulfilled all promised work. Al-Ālūsī treats the opening fāʾ as resuming the sequence of contract, work, completion, and departure. Al-Dāmghānī gives completion of a term as one sense of qaḍā, alongside completion of revelation.
28:29/q02 How long did Moses fulfill or remain after his contract? [ٱلْأَجَلَ · reports] (turns on: The reports give ten or twenty years; some authors judge the extra decade weak, while others stress that the verse itself does not specify eight versus ten.)
  28:29/q02/p1 He fulfilled the longer, better term of ten years. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās argues prophets keep promises, eight years were binding, and God fulfills the fuller promise; Saʿīd's Jewish questioner accepted this answer. Reports attribute the answer to the Prophet directly, via Gabriel or a higher angel, or to God after successive consultations. Al-Ṭabarī's reports variously call it the last, fullest, most virtuous, or most honored term. Al-Ālūsī cites Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bukhārī transmissions; Elmalılı reasons a noble person does not leave a promise incomplete.
  28:29/q02/p2 He served ten years, then remained a further ten, leaving after twenty. — reasons: Mujāhid supplies the additional decade in numerous transmissions. Al-Bayḍāwī reports it with his summary; al-Biqāʿī infers some interval because 'and he traveled' is coordinated rather than a direct result. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the twenty-year report weak, as do Abū Ḥayyān and his source cited by al-Qurṭubī; Ibn Kathīr has not found other support beyond Ibn Jarīr and Ibn Abī Ḥātim.
  28:29/q02/p3 He stayed additional unspecified years after taking his wife. — reasons: Wahb ibn Munabbih reports years beyond the marriage without giving a number.
  28:29/q02/p4 The verse and Torah do not settle whether the service was eight or ten years. — reasons: Islāḥī states both texts leave the term unspecified; Kuran Yolu likewise distinguishes the Qur'an's silence from exegetical ten-year reports.
28:29/q03 What happened to the flock given to Moses? [قَضَىٰ ٱلْأَجَلَ · reports]
  28:29/q03/p1 A promised share of differently colored newborn lambs multiplied after Moses used a device at the water. — reasons: Anas describes a reflection at the water and nearly all lambs born piebald. The report through ʿUtba says Moses served for chastity and food, placed his staff at the trough, and received multiplied varied-color newborns. Ibn Lahiʿa also defines five livestock types named in this account: abundant-milk fashūsh, long-udder tanūb, low-yield ʿazūz, two-nub thaʿūl, and small-udder kamsha.
28:29/q04 Which daughter did Moses marry? [بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ · reports] (turns on: Conflicting reports identify the bride as elder or younger; one source conditions following the Prophetic younger-daughter report on its soundness.)
  28:29/q04/p1 He married the younger daughter. — reasons: Reports attribute this to the Prophet; al-Thaʿlabī says the report must be followed if sound. Al-Wāḥidī transmits Gabriel's instruction to say it; al-Ṭabrisī identifies her as the one who urged her father to hire Moses. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī note conflict with another account.
  28:29/q04/p2 He married the elder daughter. — reasons: Wahb supplies this report. Abū Ḥayyān notes exegetical disagreement also over her name.
28:29/q05 Where did Moses travel after the term? [وَسَارَ · reports] (turns on: Whether the destination was Egypt to revisit kin or Jerusalem/Syria; Elmalılı cites 28:33 against Egypt, while al-Ālūsī finds Egypt likelier.)
  28:29/q05/p1 He headed toward Egypt, his homeland and relatives. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī calls Egypt his home; al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān likewise identify it. Mujāhid says he sought permission to visit his mother and brother. Ibn Kathīr adds longing for family and secret travel because of Pharaoh. Al-Ālūsī says Moses thought the old affair forgotten and judges Jerusalem less likely; Elmalılı objects that Egypt conflicts with Moses' earlier fear of being killed at 28:33.
  28:29/q05/p2 He headed toward Syria or Jerusalem. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī gives Syria in the narrative ellipsis and Abū Baṣīr reports Jerusalem. Elmalılı prefers Jerusalem as safer and more fitting; al-Ālūsī reports it but regards it as less likely. Islāḥī describes the mount destination without fixing Egypt.
28:29/q06 What does sāra imply? [وَسَارَ · meaning]
  28:29/q06/p1 It means to travel through the land, especially sustained movement toward a purpose or destination. — reasons: Al-Dāmghānī groups uses at 40:21 and 6:11; Būrsawī defines motion through land. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes purposive extended travel from mere walking, citing 6:11 and 25:63.
28:29/q07 Who accompanied Moses as his family? [بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ · referent] (turns on: The plural household is variously expanded beyond the wife, and accounts disagree when a child was born.)
  28:29/q07/p1 His wife accompanied him. — reasons: Al-Māwardī states the minimal identification; other reports add companions. Kashīfī, as cited by Būrsawī, treats the bāʾ as causative, 'he took his family.'
  28:29/q07/p2 His wife and child or children accompanied him. — reasons: Muqātil adds a flock. Kashf al-Asrār names his wife Ṣafūriyya and a child born before departure. Al-Ālūsī reports two sons, Gershom and Eliezer, requiring extra years if marriage was consummated only after the contract. Islāḥī says the Torah gives wife and two children.
  28:29/q07/p3 His wife and a servant accompanied him. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this as one identification of the household.
  28:29/q07/p4 A child was born during the journey on the night of the fire. — reasons: Wahb supplies this alternative timing of the child's birth.
28:29/q08 What hardships marked the journey and sighting? [وَسَارَ ءَانَسَ · reports]
  28:29/q08/p1 They traveled on a dark, cold winter night, lost the route, and had trouble making fire. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī say they lost the road; Mujāhid says Moses avoided Syrian rulers, his wife was pregnant or in labor, and his flint failed. Al-Naqqāsh adds Friday, a divine light in a tree, lost fire, and severe cold. Other accounts add rain, snow, scattered sheep, alternating caravan travel, and a remote flame; some place it on the mount's western right side. Al-Biqāʿī infers winter from their need to warm themselves.
28:29/q09 What happened to the family after Moses left them? [بِأَهْلِهِۦٓ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · reports] (turns on: The reports differ sharply on the duration and aftermath of the waiting.)
  28:29/q09/p1 They waited while Moses went to the fire. — reasons: Būrsawī says Moses left them and went to the fire before the call came from the right bank.
  28:29/q09/p2 His wife, child, and flock were left in the wilderness for thirty years. — reasons: Muqātil says a shepherd eventually found her and returned her to her father.
28:29/q10 What does al-Ṭūr refer to? [ٱلطُّورِ · referent] (turns on: Whether the term is treated as the specific Sinai mount or a general mountain word in Arabic.)
  28:29/q10/p1 It is Mount Sinai, a particular known mountain. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya locates it in Syria and says ṭūr may mean any mountain, though some restrict it to a bare mountain. Asad consistently translates it Mount Sinai because he takes the Qur'anic term to refer invariably to it. Būrsawī identifies the definite form as a particular mountain.
  28:29/q10/p2 It means a mountain generally, with a possible restriction to one without vegetation. — reasons: Yaḥyā glosses ṭūr simply as mountain; Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the broader usage and the bare-mountain qualification.
28:29/q11 Where was the fire in relation to Moses and the mount? [مِن جَانِبِ ٱلطُّورِ · referent]
  28:29/q11/p1 It appeared from the mount's side, described as the right or western side from Moses' viewpoint. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī and Būrsawī take jānib as the side facing him. Ibn Kathīr places it to Moses' right when facing the qiblah, on the western side near the valley's green tree. Mujāhid's report likewise calls it the western right side. Būrsawī locates the subsequent call at the right bank relative to Moses, a watercourse between mountains.
28:29/q12 What does ānasa mean here? [ءَانَسَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether īnās is simple visual perception or carries familiarity and comfort.)
  28:29/q12/p1 He perceived or saw the fire, especially by sight. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī cites al-Aʿshāʿj's line about spotting a bird near the mountain; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adduces 4:6 and Ḥassān's line. Al-Qurṭubī cites al-Azharī's idiom of looking for anyone present and al-Nābigha's watchful bull. Al-Ālūsī says perception is the broader original sense and reports al-Zamakhsharī's clear, unambiguous sight; al-Māwardī includes knowing.
  28:29/q12/p2 He perceived something familiar, intimate, or reassuring. — reasons: Al-Jishumī stresses reassurance. Būrsawī says the light cheered him; Elmalılı links a humanly intimate perception to recognizing maturity at 4:6. Al-Ālūsī reports this as a distinct nuance. Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir reads it inwardly as familiarity with the pure site and divine conversation.
28:29/q13 What was the fire Moses saw? [نَارًۭا · referent] (turns on: Whether the apparent fire was light, fire, or fire with a reality beyond ordinary perception.)
  28:29/q13/p1 It was divine light appearing to Moses as fire. — reasons: Qatāda says it only seemed fire; Ibn ʿAbbās calls it God's light. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and Jaʿfar describe prophetic and sacred lights. Būrsawī says light appeared dressed as fire to meet Moses' wintry need, comparing Gabriel's familiar appearance as Diḥya. Al-Ālūsī reports mystics calling it real light misnamed fire; al-Qushayrī says God showed fire, then light, to lead Moses to hearing Him.
