2:283/q44 Are writing, witnesses and collateral required or recommended? [* · law] (turns on: How imperative commands relate to the later trust permission.)
  2:283/q44/p1 They are prudent safeguards, while parties may rely on trust. — reasons: Tabarisi, Razi, Ibn Ashur and Elmalılı use permission to trust as evidence; majority juristic opinion is recommendation. Qurtubi says they prevent disputes and hostility; Elmalılı says piety makes them effective.
  2:283/q44/p2 Writing and testimony are required when feasible, though a pledge is an alternative if they are impossible. — reasons: Tabari insists on documentation even alongside pledge when accessible; Ata, Ibn Jurayj and Nakha'i reportedly recommend pledge but oblige feasible writing and witnesses.
  2:283/q44/p3 Some treat all three initial safeguards as duties later lifted by mutual trust. — reasons: Wahidi reports this historical interpretation and the related abrogation claim.
2:283/q45 How should lawful wealth be treated? [* · law] (turns on: Whether wealth's moral risk calls for divestment or prudent stewardship, and how debt affects that judgment.)
  2:283/q45/p1 Lawful wealth should be preserved and used responsibly for family, debts and public good. — reasons: Qurtubi cites 4:5-6, the Prophet's advice to Sa'd, praise of sound wealth, Abu Bakr, Anas, Ka'b and defense of property; Jawzi weighs intention and charity. Elmalılı links lawful security to earning, spending and avoiding usury.
  2:283/q45/p2 An aspirant should keep only necessities because wealth distracts from God. — reasons: Abu Hamid argues even charitable wealth distracts; Muhasibi's report depicts Abd al-Rahman delayed by lawful riches. Qurtubi disputes the confrontation story and weak crawling report, citing a faulty Ibn Lahi'a route, chronology, and criticized transmitter Ammara.
2:283/q46 What harms and duties accompany debt? [* · law]
  2:283/q46/p1 Debt can burden and endanger a person and requires sincere intention to repay. — reasons: Qurtubi cites Prophetic prayers, the pledged soul until payment, and reports that God aids repayment intended sincerely and condemns squandering; Maymuna, Anas and others add fear and hardship.
2:283/q47 How should the verse be read inwardly? [* · inward] (turns on: Whether the spiritual journey follows disciplined walking or overwhelming divine attraction.)
  2:283/q47/p1 The spiritual traveler leaves bodily confinement while a law-guided walker keeps the heart as God's pledge. — reasons: Bursevi contrasts stationary people with walkers and flyers; walker follows law and reason, escapes outward surveillance, and keeps the heart between the All-Merciful's fingers. Reports of mystics' scribes illustrate the inward state.
  2:283/q47/p2 The flying lover is carried by divine attraction and has no heart or reason to pledge. — reasons: Bursevi says such a lover remains tethered to law yet is overcome by attraction; only the destitute lover bears the trust.
2:283/q48 What does Ibn Jinni say about reliable reading transmission? [* · reports] (turns on: The notes provide separate methodological illustrations rather than rival exegeses of a verse phrase.)
  2:283/q48/p1 Responsible transmission checks reports and evaluates anomalous readings with verified evidence. — reasons: Ibn Jinni's introduction stresses fidelity, independent testing and Abu Ali's evidence; it favors Ibn Mujahid for anomalous readings, contrasts Abu Hatim and Qutrub, and records a report on Qutrub's reading transmission in Egypt in 249 AH.
2:283/q49 What rhetorical qualities does Jurjani illustrate? [* · rhetoric]
  2:283/q49/p1 Clear expression guides thought, while subtle rhetorical reversals join unlike ideas into a coherent point. — reasons: Jurjani contrasts opaque and clear paths, intellectual discovery and competition, then analyzes a poem's two hands and blameworthy withholding recast as benefit; al-Marzubani credits al-Khalil, and Abu al-Atahiya supplies an example.
2:283/q50 What examples illustrate semantic feminine agreement? [* · grammar] (turns on: These are auxiliary grammatical observations, each grounded in its own example.)
  2:283/q50/p1 Feminine verbal agreement may follow an understood feminine referent. — reasons: Farra cites تكون ميتة, إن تك مثقال حبة and poetry involving hand, death or hooked sticks.
2:283/q51 What auxiliary orthographic and particle rules are cited? [* · grammar]
  2:283/q51/p1 A feminine tāʾ is written closed for name or quality and open when showing an act or effect. — reasons: Burhan describes the orthographic distinction.
  2:283/q51/p2 An open-hamza an governs a subjunctive verb and its clause can fill several syntactic roles. — reasons: Burhan also notes conditional examples where surface condition is implausible.
2:283/q52 How do other verses express care of entrusted things? [* · links]
  2:283/q52/p1 Riyāʿa denotes active, constant care of remote trusts, beyond simple keeping. — reasons: Samarrai cites Ruh al-Maʿani on shepherding and applies it to dependents, subjects, crops, livestock and religious obligations; the noun rāʿūn implies continuity, contrasting chastity kept with the person.
2:283/q53 What did a report say about knowledge? [* · reports]
  2:283/q53/p1 Knowledge sustains faith; one who learns and acts is taught more by God. — reasons: Durr transmits the report from Ibn Abbas ascribed to the Prophet.
