## THE CURRENT MAP
12:16/q01 What happened to Joseph before the brothers returned? [وَجَآءُوٓ · reports] (turns on: The reports place the return at different points relative to Joseph's removal, and Abū Ḥayyān questions the evidentiary basis of detailed well narratives.)
  12:16/q01/p1 The brothers cast Joseph into the well and returned without him. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī place their return after the casting. Ibn Abī Ḥātim's al-Suddī says they slaughtered a young sheep and bloodied his shirt. Al-Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās says they left Joseph naked in the well and bloodied the shirt with a lamb. In ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn's report through Abū Ḥamza, the eldest brother persuaded the others to use the well instead of killing him; Jacob later trusted God's truthful dream despite his grief.
  12:16/q01/p2 They returned after the caravan had departed with Joseph. — reasons: Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits this sequence from Muḥammad b. Isḥāq: they realized Joseph was gone and then came to Jacob crying at night.
  12:16/q01/p3 A detailed narrative has Jacob consent to Joseph's outing, embrace and bless him, and the brothers cast him down once out of sight. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports the brothers had Joseph ask leave; Jacob promised permission next morning, and they carried him while still in view.
  12:16/q01/p4 Detailed tales of the casting and Joseph's pleas are unestablished. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says neither the Qur'an nor a sound hadith establishes such elaborations; tafsīr books may be consulted without treating them as established.
12:16/q02 What story did the brothers give Jacob for Joseph's absence? [وَجَآءُوٓ · reports]
  12:16/q02/p1 They claimed a wolf ate Joseph. — reasons: Muqātil adds that they said they had gone hunting, left Joseph to guard their belongings, and anticipated Jacob's disbelief even if they spoke truth. Al-Qushayrī says they concealed their real reason for crying. ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn's report says the claim was a lie. Abū Ḥayyān says the omitted exchange is recoverable: Jacob asked where Joseph was and they gave this answer.
12:16/q03 How was Joseph's bloodied shirt produced, and how is its blood described? [* · reports] (turns on: The reported wording of the later blood description differs by the consonant read.)
  12:16/q03/p1 The shirt's blood was fabricated and not Joseph's. — reasons: ʿAbd al-Razzāq transmits Qatāda's statement. Ibn Abī Ḥātim's al-Suddī says they slaughtered a young sheep and smeared its blood on the shirt; al-Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās calls the animal a lamb and says Joseph was left naked in the well. The animal wording varies.
  12:16/q03/p2 One reported reading calls the blood lying; ʿĀʾisha's variant calls it fresh. — reasons: Al-Jishumī reports the usual reading with zāy and ʿĀʾisha's reading with dāl, giving the latter the sense of fresh blood.
12:16/q04 What time does ʿishāʾ denote? [عِشَآءًۭ · meaning] (turns on: The sources give different boundaries for ʿishāʾ and different estimates of the brothers' arrival within the late day.)
  12:16/q04/p1 It denotes evening or the late part of the day, extending into the first darkness. — reasons: Al-Jishumī lists the day's end, first darkness, a broad span from noon to morning, or sunset prayer until night; with fatḥa, the form means food. Al-Ṭabrisī gives late day, early night, or evening prayer. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī say late day or day's end. Bursevī gives late day through midnight. Al-Rāghib, quoted by al-Ālūsī, narrows it to sunset prayer until night prayer and calls the two evening prayers al-ʿishāʾān.
  12:16/q04/p2 They arrived after the afternoon prayer. — reasons: Bursevī quotes Abū al-Layth for this narrower timing.
  12:16/q04/p3 They arrived at the evening prayer. — reasons: Muqātil places the return at the evening prayer.
12:16/q05 How is ʿishāʾ read and explained in the reported variants? [عِشَآءًۭ · readings] (turns on: The vowel and shortened or diminutive form affect whether the word denotes time, blurred eyes, darkness, or a flame; grammarians contest the proposed plural morphology.)
  12:16/q05/p1 The ordinary reading is ʿishāʾ, an evening time word. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts the group's reading with al-Ḥasan's; al-Ālūsī prefers the ordinary reading as evening. Al-Samīn prefers its time adverb construal and rejects a plural noun analysis.
  12:16/q05/p2 Al-Ḥasan is reported to read a diminutive ʿashiyyan. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report this form; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Abū al-Fatḥ's comparison with a plural whose feminine tāʾ is dropped, illustrated by a line of ʿAdī. Al-Ālūsī reports objections to the proposed deletion and plural pattern.
  12:16/q05/p3 A short form or ḍamma reading is analyzed as a plural of people whose eyes were clouded by crying. — reasons: Ibn Jinnī transmits al-Ḥasan's عشا through ʿĪsā b. Maymūn, deriving it from ʿashāh with feminine ending removed, but Abū al-Fatḥ objects that a day's crying would not blind them. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn transmit the clouded-eye or plural explanation, with varying short forms; Ibn al-Jawzī names Abū Hurayra, al-Ḥasan, Ibn al-Ṣumayfiʿ and al-Aʿmash for initial ḍamma. Abū al-Baqāʾ gives alternate derivations; al-Ālūsī reports plural and sound-change analyses, then objections that deletion of tāʾ and the pattern lack license. Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains the wording as a blurred weeping gaze and links Shurayḥ's court comparison.
  12:16/q05/p4 A ḍamma reading derives from ʿashwah, darkness or a venture in confusion. — reasons: Al-Samīn reports al-Ḥasan's derivation from darkness. Al-Ālūsī reports a proposed plural of ʿashwah that stresses the brothers' deceit.
  12:16/q05/p5 One proposed construal takes the variant word as a flame, suggesting haste and glee. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this alongside the darkness or confusion explanation.
12:16/q06 How is ʿishāʾ parsed in the sentence? [عِشَآءًۭ · grammar] (turns on: The grammatical category depends on whether the word is read as the ordinary time expression or an alternative plural form.)
  12:16/q06/p1 It is an adverb of time modifying the brothers' arrival. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj states the time adverb analysis; al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān also give it. Al-Samīn explicitly prefers it over a plural noun reading.
  12:16/q06/p2 In an alternative reading it is a plural noun, with competing derivations. — reasons: Abū al-Baqāʾ, reported by al-Samīn, offers morphological derivations including loss of the feminine ending of ʿashāh; Abū Ḥayyān reports plural and diminutive possibilities for al-Ḥasan's forms. Al-Samīn rejects the plural as the proper parse of the received wording.
12:16/q07 Why did the brothers return in the evening? [عِشَآءًۭ · interpretation] (turns on: The notes propose concealment and persuasive effect, travel time, or prevention of a search as the motive for arriving late.)
  12:16/q07/p1 Darkness made their false excuse easier and concealed signs of their deception. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī gives both ease of presenting the excuse and deliberate delay to deceive. Al-Wāḥidī says darkness strengthened their apparent innocence; al-Baghawī attributes ease of excuse to rhetoricians. Al-Ṭabrisī says they could mislead Jacob more boldly. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Nasafī stress boldness or concealment; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Biqāʿī cite the proverb about seeking favors at night and avoiding apologies in daylight. Al-Biqāʿī adds Jacob could not read their faces. Al-Kawāshī, via Bursevī, says the excuse would seem more emphatic. Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connect evening with staging the deception and winning trust; a reported view in al-Ālūsī says darkness hid the joy of achieving their aim.
  12:16/q07/p2 They could not reach home earlier. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī presents this as one possibility alongside staged deception.
  12:16/q07/p3 A late return reduced Jacob's chance to search for Joseph. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says they probably returned late at night for this purpose.
12:16/q08 Was this the evening of the same day or a later day? [عِشَآءًۭ · reports] (turns on: Whether the report of Joseph's three days in the well and Judah's food is taken to date the brothers' return.)
  12:16/q08/p1 It was the evening of the same day. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports that some understand the verse this way.
  12:16/q08/p2 It was a later evening. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports an inference from the account that Joseph remained in the well three days while Judah brought him food.
12:16/q09 What did Jacob do when his sons arrived and told him about Joseph? [وَجَآءُوٓ يَبْكُونَ · reports] (turns on: The reports differ on Jacob's first question and whether he wept, demanded the shirt, or fainted afterward.)
  12:16/q09/p1 He heard their weeping, became alarmed, asked whether the flock was harmed, and then asked where Joseph was. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī's al-Suddī adds that Jacob demanded Joseph's shirt, saw its blood and wept. Al-Baghawī and al-Kashshāf report hearing cries or wailing before he came out. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he wept, cried aloud and requested the shirt after the sheep question. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and the Study Quran report the flock or pasture question; al-Bayḍāwī reports alarm and the question about Joseph. The accounts vary in those added actions.
  12:16/q09/p2 After the flock question Jacob collapsed, and Judah checked him for life. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports Judah lamenting that they lost their brother and killed their father with grief.
  12:16/q09/p3 Jacob asked whether some affliction or wage had struck them and then asked where Joseph was. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this wording of the initial question rather than a question about the flock.
  12:16/q09/p4 On hearing Joseph was dead Jacob fainted and remained unconscious until the next morning. — reasons: The Study Quran reports that the brothers poured water on him.
