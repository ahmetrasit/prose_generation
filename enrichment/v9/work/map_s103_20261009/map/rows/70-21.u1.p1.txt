  70:21/q09/p2 Traits remain, but people can suppress their visible effects and follow revealed commands. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative. Al-Nasafī says people are ordered to oppose their temperament and follow revealed law; al-Qāḍī says believers can struggle against the disposition.

  70:21/q09/p4 Reason and worship can redirect eagerness toward real good and generosity. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says mature reason turns eagerness toward fear of sin and haste to forgiveness, and prescribed patience governs worldly hardship and duty. Al-Samarrāʾī says persistent prayer gives God one's time and self and frees one from immediate desires, even when worship costs opportunity or offers no worldly gain; he cites 62:11. Ibn Kaysān says God commanded people to give what they love and patiently bear what they dislike.
70:21/q10 What conduct does the verse censure and call the person to correct? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Which failure is foregrounded: withheld dues, short-sighted attachment to worldly conditions, or lack of gratitude and patience.)
  70:21/q10/p1 Withholding required rights and charity from blessings, instead of using them for God and others. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, and Study Quran name God's or others' rights in wealth. Al-Rāzī and Study Quran say health and wealth should be used for gain in the Hereafter. Kur'an Yolu says people may claim blessings as their own achievement and fail to spend or help. Al-Būrsawī says ignorance of divine apportionment and reward fuels withholding, and generosity during health is better.
  70:21/q10/p2 Fixation on immediate bodily gain and fear of loss, instead of patient acceptance of God's decree. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says seeking relief is not itself wrong: the fault is caring only about immediate poverty, illness, wealth, or health, rather than accepting decree and seeking the afterlife. Ibn ʿĀshūr says fear of losing benefits explains the trait but does not excuse hoarding. Al-Biqāʿī says love of immediate gain narrows sight and opposes faith; Elmalılı adds quick forgetfulness of past distress.
  70:21/q10/p3 Ingratitude in blessing and impatience at affliction. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda and Thaʿlab state the paired ethical failures; Ibn Kaysān sets giving beloved things and bearing disliked things against humanity's natural attraction and aversion. Al-Iṣlāḥī says people complain even at slight punishment for their deeds.
70:21/q11 Why is harm mentioned before good in the paired description? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the order is explained chiefly by the sūra's punishment setting or by preparation for the withholding clause.)
  70:21/q11/p1 Harm first fits the sūra's preceding punishment scenes and the command to be patient. — reasons: Al-Samarrāʾī cites the opening demand for punishment (70:1), the criminal's wish to ransom himself (70:11), and the blaze tearing skin (70:15–16). He also contrasts human panic with the Prophet's command to beautiful patience (70:5).
  70:21/q11/p2 The harm clause prepares and completes the main point about withholding upon good. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the main point is withholding in prosperity, and distress in harm supplies its paired preparation.
70:21/q12 How does the trait here connect to earlier hoarding and the fire? [* · links]
  70:21/q12/p1 It supplies the human motive behind accumulating and retaining goods, and thus the conduct called by the fire. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says verses 19–21 interrupt the line from the hoarder to the worshippers' exception, summarizing the greed behind retention; the wording rebukes rather than excuses it. Elmalılı says the fire calls one who turns from right and good, gathers possessions, and stores them. Al-Samarrāʾī places the description after the criminal's attempted ransom and the blaze.
70:21/q13 How are the worshippers in the following verses related to this trait? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the following exception is connected to humanity's disposition or a disconnected contrast with those who turned away.)
  70:21/q13/p1 They are a connected exception: believers do not remain trapped in this human tendency. — reasons: Al-Nasafī says the generic 'human' allows worshippers to be excepted. Al-Ālūsī reports a connected exception by meaning: believers did not persist in panic; he says the following words about worshippers convey the exception. Study Quran says the eight qualities of 70:22–35 exempt their possessors. Al-Samarrāʾī explains that persistent prayer trains a person to give God time and self and resist immediate desires, citing 62:11.
  70:21/q13/p2 The exception is disconnected, contrasting worshippers in gardens with those who turned away. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative account of the following exception.
70:21/q14 What other Qur'anic passages are compared with this description? [* · links] (turns on: Which comparison passage and feature of the comparison is in view.)
  70:21/q14/p1 Qur'an 41:49–51 echoes repeated requests for good, despair under harm, and withdrawal after mercy. — reasons: Study Quran makes this comparison.
  70:21/q14/p2 Qur'an 21:37 parallels the description of a deeply rooted human trait. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān compares the wording that portrays anxiety and withholding as if created in humankind with 21:37.
  70:21/q14/p3 The descriptions of believers in al-Muʾminūn and al-Maʿārij strongly parallel each other but differ in wording and order. — reasons: Al-Samarrāʾī observes this across 23:1–11 and the present sūra's later descriptions.
70:21/q15 What Prophetic saying is cited about the paired traits? [* · reports]
  70:21/q15/p1 The worst traits in a person are anxious or consuming greed and paralyzing or ruinous cowardice. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī reports the saying; Ibn Kathīr transmits it through Abū Hurayra and notes Abū Dāwūd gives the same report by that route. Ibn ʿAṭiyya begins a comparison of anxious agitation with a Prophetic saying.
70:21/q16 What poetic citations appear alongside the comparison to manūʿan? [مَنُوعًا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the citation is a direct lexical parallel or other poetry in the same source passage.)
  70:21/q16/p1 Ibn al-Rūmī uses mamnūʿan manūʿan for a beloved who is both forbidden and withholding. — reasons: Al-Jurjānī supplies this direct poetic parallel to the verse's manūʿan.
  70:21/q16/p2 Al-Jurjānī's surrounding citations illustrate other poetic formulations and scenes. — reasons: These are adjacent citations, without a stated interpretation of this verse. Al-Buḥturī withholds credit for Abū al-ʿAlā until others grant it (r1), and depicts a beloved whose offered union still feels withheld (r10); Abū Tammām calls a good request a fault and blames bad fortune (r12), and al-Buḥturī turns merits into sins (r13). Khālid al-Kātib and Bashshār describe endless lovers' nights (r2–3). Abū Tammām, al-Buḥturī, and Muslim depict fear and submission spreading across eastern and western lands (r4–6). Muḥammad ibn Bashīr requests help from a busy person lest a free one be perpetually asked (r7). Al-Jurjānī cites Abū ʿAlī al-Baṣīr's image of Saʿīd worn like a rope and the warning that hopes cling to him even when busy (r8–9). Abū Tammām and al-Buḥturī picture a donkey driven by fear toward a blood-clawed lion (r14–15). Maʿn ibn Aws and al-ʿAbbās ibn al-Aḥnaf depict a heart or soul scarcely turning back once it turns away (r16–17).
