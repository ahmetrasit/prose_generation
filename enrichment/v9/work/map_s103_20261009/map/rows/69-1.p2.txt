[ELMALILI:69:1/r9] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | It is the Hour when all effects of people's deeds become fully realized and rewards and punishments come to pass.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p114/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, if the clauses connect, Thamūd and ʿĀd denied the Resurrection; “the Striker” is a synonym for the Reality, brought forward instead of a pronoun to explain its terrifying force.
[TABATABAI:69:1-5/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The sura names the Resurrection al-Haqqa and also al-Qari'a and al-Waqi'a; it first recalls destroyed deniers, then describes the Resurrection and people's fates, and finally affirms the Qur'an's truth. Its Meccan setting follows from its style.
[TABATABAI:69:1-5/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Al-Haqqa means the Great Resurrection, named for its irrevocable, certain reality; haqq means an established and real fact.
[TABATABAI:69:1-5/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The interrogative ما magnifies the event; repeating its name instead of a pronoun intensifies the emphasis, and the first question is predicated of al-Haqqa.
[ASAD-NOTES:69:1#n1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | The Day of Resurrection and Judgment will disclose the true quality of each person's past life and deeds, revealing the destiny those deeds entail. | mentions 37:19, 39:68, 50:21, 50:22
[ASAD-NOTES:69:1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | Asad renders al-haqqah as the laying-bare of truth, emphasizes its awe, and says its reality exceeds human conception.
[ASAD-NOTES:69:intro/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | Asad dates the surah shortly after surah 67, around three or four years before the emigration to Medina. | mentions 67:1
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p445/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Ḥāqqah resembles the preceding al-Wāqiʿah in affirming the Resurrection and depicting its terrors.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p445/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Ḥāqqah shares al-Qalam’s central theme, substantiating the Day of Judgement, though their arguments differ.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p445/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Verses 1–12 cite earlier nations as evidence that rejecting messengers brings certain punishment in this world and the Hereafter.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p447/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Ḥāqqah names an event certain to occur according to common sense and morality; it is inevitable.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p447/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The word al-Ḥāqqah substitutes for an entire sentence.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p447/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The truncated, ambiguous wording jolts an indifferent addressee by focusing attention on the opening subject and drawing attention through uncertainty.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p447/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | As a name for the Day of Judgement, al-Ḥāqqah conveys both its certainty and why it must occur.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p448/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The rational and moral arguments for the Day’s necessity are explained in earlier sūrahs, with indications also in this and later sūrahs.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p448/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Al-Ḥāqqah secondarily includes the inevitable punishment nations face for rejecting their messenger.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p448/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Punishment for rejecting a messenger prefigures and substantiates the Hereafter’s punishment because it follows rejection of that Day.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p448/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Messengers warn of both the Hereafter and the punishment that follows denial; after the truth is conclusively conveyed, deniers’ punishment arrives, and every divine promise and messenger’s warning is true.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:69:1/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The Resurrection is called al-Haqqa because it certainly occurs and reveals the real value and consequences of people's earthly deeds.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:69:1/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · müfessirlerin büyük çoğunluğu | reports | The great majority of commentators understand al-Haqqa to mean the Resurrection.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:69:1/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Ateş | reports | Some instead connect al-Haqqa with the crushing defeat that would befall Quraysh for opposing the Prophet, prompted by the following mention of punished peoples.
[STUDYQURAN:69:1/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Ḥāqqah is an emphatic form of ḥaqq, meaning truth or reality; here it evokes the overwhelming, inevitable reality none can avoid.
[STUDYQURAN:69:1/r2] STUDYQURAN · All commentators | reports | All commentators agree that the Undeniable Reality refers to Resurrection and Judgment, though they differ over the aspect meant.
[STUDYQURAN:69:1/r3] STUDYQURAN · JJ | reports | One view is that everything disbelievers rejected in earthly life will be realized and made evident.
[STUDYQURAN:69:1/r4] STUDYQURAN · JJ | reports | Another view is that everyone will see the true nature of their deeds, as in 67:11. | mentions 67:11
[STUDYQURAN:69:intro/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Al-Ḥāqqah is a late Makkan sūrah, generally dated shortly after al-Mulk, and named for the Day of Judgment in its opening verses.
[STUDYQURAN:69:intro/r2] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | It links to the preceding sūrah by answering the charge that the Prophet was possessed and by describing Resurrection and Judgment more fully. | mentions 68:2, 68:51
[STUDYQURAN:69:intro/r3] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | The sūrah warns through earlier deniers, describes Judgment and the differing reception of the deeds' book, then reaffirms the Quran's revealed nature.
