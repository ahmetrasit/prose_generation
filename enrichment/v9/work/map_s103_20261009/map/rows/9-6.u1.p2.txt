9:6/q22 How does this verse relate to the preceding fighting order and treaty context? [* · links]
  9:6/q22/p1 It qualifies the general order to fight by requiring safe access to hear the proof. — reasons: Ibn Abbas says the 9:5 command is modified by repentance, prayer, alms or requested hearing. Abu Hayyan and Tabataba'i call it a qualification; Alusi resolves an apparent conflict. Tabataba'i says guiding from shirk is the mission and Kuranyolu stresses mercy and freedom from coerced, merely outward conversion.
  9:6/q22/p2 It elaborates the repentance exception or qualifies the kill order. — reasons: Ibn Ashur gives both syntactic connections to 9:5 and mentions an embassy as well as instruction.
  9:6/q22/p3 The surah's treaty cancellation, exceptions and exposure of hypocrites provide the wider setting. — reasons: Ibn Hisham connects Bara'a to the general pact, special tribal agreements, Tabuk absentees and hypocrites. Biqa'i ties the kept treaty exception to 9:1 and Hudaybiyyah. Tabataba'i notes reprieve, fidelity to kept pacts and notice before cancellation for feared betrayal, citing 8:58.
9:6/q23 What does the reported exchange with Ali establish? [* · reports]
  9:6/q23/p1 After the deadline, Ali said a polytheist coming to hear God's speech or meet a need must not be killed, and cited this verse. — reasons: Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibn Abbas transmit variants of the question and answer; the Qummi account asks about meeting the Prophet after four months. Ibn Ashur could not locate the report and observes that it would postdate revelation. Alusi and Elmalili debate whether 'need' extends beyond religion.
9:6/q24 What other reported cases illustrate protected entry or contact with enemies? [* · reports]
  9:6/q24/p1 An enemy combatant asked to hear God's speech, accepted testimony and repudiated idols. — reasons: Sa'id's report says the Muslims asked for testimony and rejection of idols, which he affirmed.
  9:6/q24/p2 The Prophet protected Quraysh envoys at Hudaybiyyah, and their reports of Muslim reverence aided conversion. — reasons: Ibn Kathir names Urwa, Makraz, and Suhayl and says their impressions spread on return.
  9:6/q24/p3 A Musaylimah envoy was safe while on mission but was executed after later public endorsement outside it. — reasons: Ibn Kathir reports the Prophet's refusal to kill an envoy and Ibn Mas'ud's later execution.
  9:6/q24/p4 Battle reports show fighting and restraint in other encounters with polytheists. — reasons: Ibn Ishaq reports twenty two polytheists killed at Uhud. At Hunayn, pregnant Umm Sulaym urged killing fugitives, carried a dagger for attackers, and the Prophet said God might suffice her.
9:6/q25 What inward lesson is drawn from granting refuge to the ignorant? [* · inward]
  9:6/q25/p1 Divine care for an ignorant seeker implies greater care for believers seeking nearness and for God's knowledgeable friends. — reasons: Qushayri appeals to protection from separation and 21:103, contrasting sincere and spiritually bankrupt, unveiled and veiled, lover and hater; he quotes a poem on faithful and deficient beloveds.
  9:6/q25/p2 God gives the ignorant ego time to return; the servant should repent early, especially in youth. — reasons: Bursevi recounts an Israelite youth who worshipped twenty years then disobeyed twenty and heard acceptance promised on return. Sa'di's verses criticize late repentance; another Persian verse praises young solitude; an Arabic poem portrays old sins darkening the heart.
9:6/q26 What Arabic ellipsis supports analysis of an unspoken verb here? [وَإِنْ أَحَدٌۭ مِّنَ ٱلْمُشْرِكِينَ ٱسْتَجَارَكَ · grammar]
  9:6/q26/p1 Context and convention permit a recoverable verb, especially with a bare accusative or familiar command. — reasons: Sibawayh gives 'Zaydan' for 'act on Zayd', warning repetitions 'lion, lion' and 'wall, wall', 'clear the road', proverbs 'amr mubkiyātika' and 'al-ẓibāʾ ʿalā al-baqar', the prayer for hyena and wolf, elliptical replies about children and a water place, and al-Miskin's 'brother' line. Jarir's poetry supports the transitive road command. He rules out eliding a preposition with its governed noun or an absent third person through a hearer. Razi adds 'Zayd' as the answer to 'Who did it?'.
9:6/q27 Does negative in require an illā in its predicate? [وَإِنْ · grammar]
  9:6/q27/p1 No; negative instances lacking illā refute that grammatical restriction. — reasons: Al-Burhan cites 93:7 and 72:25-26 against grammarians requiring illā or an equivalent; this is collateral evidence about in's Arabic uses.
9:6/q28 What idiom illustrates asking someone to wait? [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
  9:6/q28/p1 A teacher's reply offering the Tigris and Euphrates to a student asking to swallow saliva exemplifies expansive permission to wait. — reasons: Ibn Ashur reports the anecdote through al-Zamakhshari.
9:6/q29 Do commentators use this verse to debate divine speech? [كَلَٰمَ ٱللَّهِ · theology]
  9:6/q29/p1 Yes; the hearing clause prompts discussion of the nature of divine speech. — reasons: Kuranyolu notes the exegetical debate without adopting a particular view.
