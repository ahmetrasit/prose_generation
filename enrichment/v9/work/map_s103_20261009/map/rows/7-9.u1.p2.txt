  7:9/q16/p2 Bi-āyātinā attaches to 'wronging' because the verb includes the sense of denying or rejecting. — reasons: Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and Bursevi explain the nonusual construction by implied denial; al-Samin compares 3:11 and 27:14. Islahi says the implication also includes wronging oneself.
  7:9/q16/p3 The construction compresses denial of revelation and self-wronging into one expression. — reasons: Islahi calls it tadmīn and says the suppression conveys a comprehensive meaning briefly.
7:9/q17 Why is 'Our signs' placed before the verb, and what does the tense convey? [بِـَٔايَٰتِنَا يَظْلِمُونَ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the order serves cadence, emphasis, or both; how the verbs depict duration.)
  7:9/q17/p1 The prepositional phrase is advanced for the verse ending. — reasons: Al-Samin and Alusi point to the rhyme or end cadence.
  7:9/q17/p2 Fronting the signs gives them emphasis. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says the placement highlights the signs.
  7:9/q17/p3 Past kānū with imperfect yaẓlimūn depicts repeated, persistent past wrongdoing. — reasons: Alusi and Ibn Ashur both read ongoing denial in the combined verb forms.
7:9/q18 Why does a singular 'whoever' take the plural 'those'? [وَمَنْ فَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · grammar]
  7:9/q18/p1 Man is singular in form but plural in sense, so the result uses a plural; a singular result would also be eloquent. — reasons: Tabari explicitly gives both grammatical possibilities.
7:9/q19 How does this outcome connect to the surrounding passage? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the link emphasizes sequence, guidance, or parallel language of justice and records.)
  7:9/q19/p1 The heavy and light clauses explain the result of the earlier weighing after questioning and accounting. — reasons: Razi places weighing after earlier questioning; Ibn Ashur identifies the conditionals as its outcomes. Ibn Kathir cites other heavy/light judgment passages including 21:47, 23:102–103 and 101:6–11.
  7:9/q19/p2 The opening verses urge following guidance and warn of the cost of rejecting it. — reasons: Study Quran describes verses 1–10 this way; the named wrong against signs supplies the warning here.
  7:9/q19/p3 The balance image is linked to just balances elsewhere and to a truthful record. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi cites 21:47 and 45:29; al-Tabataba'i invokes 21:47, 39:69 and 45:29. Study Quran joins judgment balance with fair commercial measures and cosmic balance passages.
7:9/q20 What does weighing mean in ordinary language? [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · meaning]
  7:9/q20/p1 It compares things to disclose relative measure and extends figuratively to patterned verse and measured speech. — reasons: Jishumi gives the lexical extension; al-Farra reports mizan used for equality or proximity and cites a poem of a person's words. Razi cites a poetic argument-measure.
7:9/q21 How do judgment and mercy affect the measured result? [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · theology]
  7:9/q21/p1 God rewards or punishes by deed quantity and quality while mercy, repentance and intercession may increase reward or forgive. — reasons: Kuranyolu cites 6:160: good can be rewarded more than its equivalent and evil receives equivalent punishment; these allowances do not violate justice. Ibn Kathir stresses God wrongs no one (4:40).
