[TABATABAI:7:1-9#3/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · جابر | reports | A report says good and evil deeds are weighed, and whichever side prevails determines entry into Paradise or Hell; the author accepts its content but not as an explanation of these verses.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#3/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · علي بن أبي طالب | reports | Ali says a person whose outward appearance outweighs their inner state has a light scale, while inner reality outweighing outward appearance makes it heavy; the author says it does not explain the verses.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#4/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · عائشة | reports | A report says God fashioned the balance’s pans like heaven and earth and the path like a sword, saying he weighs and grants passage to whomever he wills.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#4/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · سلمان | reports | Al-Hakim reports a similar account from Salman.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#4/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | He says these narrations depict an afterlife balance resembling the worldly instrument and are intended to make the idea accessible to unsophisticated hearers, as later reports clarify.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#4/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · هشام بن الحكم | reports | In a debate with a skeptic, Imam al-Sadiq says deeds are attributes rather than bodies, God does not need weighing to know their amount, and the balance means justice.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#5/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | He explains deeds as moral qualities attached to actions, not the physical movements themselves: the same movement is lawful marriage or adultery according to its social or religious status.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#5/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Imam’s argument has two parts: deeds as attributes have no physical weight, and God needs no weighing because nothing is hidden from him.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#5/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · بعضهم | reports | Some argue that embodied deeds can acquire weight, with the scale frightening sinners, encouraging the obedient, and displaying justice; they find problems in the Imam’s report.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#5/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | He replies that deeds may appear embodied and justice may take the form of a balance; the report denies only worldly physical weight and does not require two-pan comparison.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#5/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام | reports | A report says good deeds weigh down the balance while sins make it light; the author says this supports the truth in a deed as its measure.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#5/r6] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · أبو عبد الله عليه السلام | reports | Asked about the just balances, Imam al-Sadiq identifies them as the prophets and their successors.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9#5/r7] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | He explains the report by saying prophets and Imams embody the measure of right action and belief.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The Quran’s ‘just balances’ means God’s just placement, not that the balance itself is justice; the wording ‘no soul is wronged’ shows that the justice belongs to God.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Commentators who take weighing as justice explain heaviness metaphorically as predominance of good deeds, and lightness as predominance of sins and loss of the self.
[TABATABAI:7:1-9/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | That interpretation must still identify a just criterion for mixed, unequal good and bad deeds; otherwise the outcome would be arbitrary and would not complete the proof against people. | mentions 21:47
[TABATABAI:7:37-53#10/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | rejects | Although reports identify the people of al-Aʿrāf with balanced deeds, some make their words the phrase 'they did not enter, though they hope'; this does not fit the verses, and equal deeds cannot make the scale both heavy and light.
[TABATABAI:7:37-53#11/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | A person with no discernible preponderance might have no weighing at all, as with an unbeliever whose deeds are nullified or a vulnerable person who was not fully addressed by proof or obligation. | mentions 7:8
[TABATABAI:7:37-53#9/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم | reports | Jābir reports that deeds are weighed on Judgment Day: whichever side outweighs the other determines Garden or Fire, while equal deeds identify the people of al-Aʿrāf.
[TABATABAI:7:7-8/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The weighing concerns deeds, or people insofar as they acted; 21:47 places it within reckoning, while 99:7–8 explicitly assigns weight to good and evil deeds.
[TABATABAI:7:7-8/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Deeds erased through unbelief have no weight, so only deeds of those whose works are not nullified remain to be weighed. | mentions 18:105
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#2/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | He reads the weight as truth contained in deeds: good works have weight to the extent they embody truth, while evil is falsehood and has none.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#2/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The placed book identifies the truth in each deed and the weighing makes its amount manifest; he links this to judgment by truth and the book that speaks truth. | mentions 39:69, 45:29
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#2/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The plural ‘balances’ suits the distinct standards for different acts, since the truth of prayer differs from that of almsgiving, fasting, and pilgrimage.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#2/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Most commentators parse ‘the weighing is the truth’ as an initial subject with ‘the truth’ describing the weighing, meaning a just weighing; he considers another parsing possible but its reading of al-Kashshaf unusual.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#2/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | If ‘balances’ means weighed deeds, as some commentators suggest, their account still leaves unclear what determines relative heaviness when good and bad acts are mixed.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#3/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · علي بن الحسين عليه السلام | reports | Ali ibn al-Husayn says scales and records are set up for Muslims; polytheists are driven to Hell in groups, and he cites the verse about no weight for disbelievers.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#3/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | He notes that the narration alludes to the statement that God will not assign the disbelievers any weight on Judgment Day. | mentions 18:105
[TABATABAI:7:8-9#3/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | reports | Al-Qummi’s commentary interprets weighing as recompense according to deeds, good for good and evil for evil.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The verses portray good deeds as heavy and evil deeds as making the scales light, rather than weighing two piles and comparing their total mass.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Quranic parallels consistently place heaviness with good and lightness with evil, as in 23:102–103 and 101:6–10. | mentions 23:102, 23:103, 101:6, 101:10
[TABATABAI:7:8-9/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | prefers | He proposes that each deed is tested by its own measure of truth: complete prayer, rightful almsgiving, and truthful speech exemplify standards that good deeds meet and sins fail.
[TABATABAI:7:8-9/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The comparison with a physical balance illustrates a single measure placed against the item; the measure itself determines whether the item has matching weight or is rejected.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p130/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | Bint al-Shati says balagha became trapped in technical forms and logic, while the question of inimitability receded into Quranic-sciences books and tafsir. | mentions 2:23, 2:24
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p221/r1] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | Although dictionaries often equate حلف with أقسم and poetry uses it for swearing, Arabic idioms pair it with falsehood; true-oath forms are otherwise unattested except metaphorically.
[BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p221/r2] BINTSHATI-IJAZ, d. 1419 · author | holds | Several Arabic uses of محلف denote uncertain identity or classification, including حضار and al-Wazn, two stars sometimes mistaken for Suhayl.

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:8/r1] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | Judgment aims to reward or punish justly according to the quantity and quality of deeds, which requires a measure; here ‘weight’ names that measure.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:8/r2] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · İbn Âşûr | reports | Ibn Ashur reads ‘true’ as justice or equality: deeds are judged justly, with no injustice, or everyone receives an equivalent return.

[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:7:8/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · some commentators | reports | Some commentators say the things weighed are deeds made into tangible objects; others say they are the records of deeds.
