95:6/q08 How are sins and accountability treated when a believer loses reason in old age? [فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · interpretation]
  95:6/q08/p1 Sins or acts after loss of reason are not recorded or charged, while earlier good deeds continue. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿIkrima ground exemption in loss of understanding; al-Ṭabrisī qualifies the Anas report with 'if authentic.' Anas's report also says children's good deeds benefit parents before puberty and sins are unrecorded. Other reports describe the left-hand angel ceasing to record sins. Al-Māwardī transmits Ibn Masʿūd's broad wording that what one does in old age does not harm him.
95:6/q09 What occasion is reported for the assurance to elderly believers? [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · reports]
  95:6/q09/p1 Some people in the Prophet's time became senile; inquiry about their lost understanding elicited an assurance that God excuses them and preserves their earlier reward. — reasons: The reports from Ibn ʿAbbās emphasize diminished minds and the reward earned before decline; al-Durr's version also identifies other oath locations in the sūra.
95:6/q10 What further reports illustrate reward continuing despite incapacity? [فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ · reports] (turns on: The reports address distinct circumstances and stages; the notes give no explicit contest among them.)
  95:6/q10/p1 The Prophet said habitual good deeds continue to be recorded during illness or travel. — reasons: Abū Mūsā's version explicitly recites this verse; other versions state the same rule for a formerly healthy, resident servant. Al-Bursawī applies it to aging, and Study Quran notes permitted shortened prayer or broken fast during travel and illness.
  95:6/q10/p2 Reports say angels continue recording the best habitual good deeds during illness, weakness or affliction and may withhold sins. — reasons: The Prophet's report to the angels runs until death; a report through Shaddād adds that a believer who praises God during trial rises cleansed of sin.
  95:6/q10/p3 A prophetic report credits an aged believer with the best deeds done in strength. — reasons: Abū Mūsā's report links this to the passage; Abū Ḥayyān also cites an unnamed hadith about a centenarian and another about extreme age. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes an analogous long ʿIkrima report is not raised to the Prophet.
  95:6/q10/p4 A report lists successive divine favors at ages fifty through one hundred. — reasons: Anas reports eased reckoning at fifty, repentance at sixty, heavenly love at seventy, good recording and overlooked sins at eighty, forgiveness and intercession at ninety, and lifelong practice credited at one hundred.
  95:6/q10/p5 After a believer dies, angels worship at the grave until resurrection and their worship is credited to him. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī reports God's command to two angels; al-Bursawī quotes a version from Abū al-Layth in which angels ask to worship in heaven and are sent to the grave.
  95:6/q10/p6 A long life accompanied by good deeds is blessed. — reasons: Ibn ʿUmar attributes the saying to the Prophet; al-Bursawī cites the same maxim.
95:6/q11 What does 'ghayr mamnūn' mean? [غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍۢ · meaning] (turns on: The possible senses of the root mann: weakness or withholding, cutting, counting, or conferring a favor; some sources intend several senses together.)
  95:6/q11/p1 A reward that is not reduced, diminished, weakened, or withheld. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās gives 'not reduced'; al-Ṭabarī prefers it as matching reward in youth, deriving it from manīn ('weak') and citing poetry about a gift without deficiency or flaw. Al-Māwardī cites a poem. Corpus Coranicum prefers 'not withheld' over Paret's favor sense and cites Labīd; its note mentions parallels at 84:25 and 68:3.
  95:6/q11/p2 A reward that is never cut off or interrupted. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a weakened or broken cord; al-Jishumī derives mann as cutting, as in names for death because it cuts life; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites severing rope. Al-Islāḥī prefers the unending sense and rejects another interpretation on Arabic grammatical grounds. Other notes place duration in this life and the next, or describe Paradise's permanence.
  95:6/q11/p3 A reward not held over recipients as a favor, reproach, or humiliating reminder. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan says it is not spoiled by injury or reproach; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes the beneficiary feeling indebted. Paret calls it something owed by right; Corpus Coranicum instead prefers non-withholding. Al-Rāzī and al-Sāmarrāʾī explicitly accept this together with permanence.
  95:6/q11/p4 A reward not reckoned or counted. — reasons: Mujāhid and Ibrāhīm give the gloss; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports 'counted or measured' as a possible sense.
  95:6/q11/p5 Reward bestowed without the recipient currently performing a corresponding deed. — reasons: Al-Ḍaḥḥāk applies this to old-age incapacity; several sources report his gloss separately from the sense of duration.
  95:6/q11/p6 Reward free of distress. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī lists this among alternative explanations, without an argument in the note.
95:6/q12 Why use the wording 'ghayr mamnūn'? [غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍۢ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the notes explain the phrase's range of senses or its use as a repeated formula.)
  95:6/q12/p1 It deliberately carries both lasting reward and reward free of reproach. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says both properties befit divine recompense; al-Sāmarrāʾī says a simpler 'not cut off' expression would lose the second sense. His account of verse endings says this sūra similarly gives multiple senses to al-amīn, asfal sāfilīn, dīn, and aḥkam al-ḥākimīn.
  95:6/q12/p2 It is a recurring Qurʾānic reward formula. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists al-Qalam, Fuṣṣilat, al-Inshiqāq and al-Tīn and counts sixteen occurrences of the root mann; she also compares rayb al-manūn at 52:30. Corpus Coranicum notes parallels at 84:25 and 68:3; Ibn al-Jawzī points back to 68:3 for the gloss.
95:6/q13 Why is 'ajr' indefinite, and what does the reward clause add? [أَجْرٌ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the clause is treated as an added promise or an explicit confirmation of the exception's result.)
  95:6/q13/p1 Its indefinite form magnifies the reward, which adds a further favor beyond rescue from the low state. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes being excluded from degradation from receiving an additional immense reward.
  95:6/q13/p2 The reward clause confirms the consequence of the exception. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī says this when the phrase means unending reward; al-Ālūsī says it clarifies believers' state and counters the inference that equal bodily aging means equal outcomes.
95:6/q14 How does the exception compare with the one in Sūrat al-ʿAṣr? [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · links] (turns on: The force of al-ʿAṣr's added requirement of mutual exhortation.)
  95:6/q14/p1 Both except believers from a collective human loss or fall. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and al-Ḥasan use 103:2–3 to justify excepting plural believers from the generic singular human here; Study Quran also compares the two sūras' account of human nature and fall.
  95:6/q14/p2 Al-Tīn requires faith and good works to avoid the lowest state; al-ʿAṣr adds mutual exhortation to avoid further loss of possible reward. — reasons: Al-Sāmarrāʾī and al-Tibyān distinguish personal duties from counseling others; enjoining right may be individual, communal, or recommended, and counsel covers required and recommended truth and patience. Omitting extra counsel loses additional reward without necessarily causing total ruin.
95:6/q15 How does this verse fit the sūra's depiction of humanity and its oaths? [* · links] (turns on: The notes emphasize the oath's proof, the verse's implied symmetry, or the sura's overall religious message.)
  95:6/q15/p1 The oaths and best-form/lowest-state contrast establish that faith and deeds preserve humanity's sound nature and earn reward, while rejection brings descent. — reasons: Al-Islāḥī says honoring innate guidance and messengers determines the two outcomes; al-Sāmarrāʾī says prophetic revelation offers a way of life suited to human nature, and compares the oath's revelation sites to the human beginning and end. Al-Elmali calls this the sūra's moral rule and the law of accountability.
  95:6/q15/p2 The passage uses implied counterparts: evil deeds and punishment before the exception, preserved original fitra within it. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī calls the pattern iḥtibāk and links it to the initial best form and the fate of those led downward by desire.
  95:6/q15/p3 The exception expresses the sūra's moral and religious purpose: Islamic foundations and deeds embody primordial nature; rejection corrupts it. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sūra praises followers' reward and warns rejecters; Asad calls its moral truth shared by true religious teachings.
95:6/q16 How is the verse linked to neighboring sūras and their histories? [* · links]
  95:6/q16/p1 It follows al-Sharḥ's call to turn to God and prepares for al-ʿAlaq's command to read. — reasons: Al-Elmali says this sūra summarizes preceding sūras and establishes creation, religion, reward and judgment, making the next opening fitting; al-Ālūsī says the preceding sūra described Muḥammad as exemplary and this one turns to humanity's end and reward.
  95:6/q16/p2 The oath's places and the human outcomes recall Torah narratives of revelation, guidance, and judgment. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī relates Sinai, Seir, and Paran to the Torah's account and its beginnings, laws and history. Farāhī says Sinai's deliverance of oppressed Israel illustrates reward and punishment; al-Islāḥī says Abraham's promise excludes idolatrous descendants, citing 2:124.
