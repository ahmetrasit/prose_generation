Ibn Ashur says a nonmaterial trait attributed to creation may mean a disposition rapidly attaches to feelings and actions, comparing 76:2 and 21:37. The phrasing can acknowledge weakness or distinguish good original creation from later deterioration, as at 4:28 and 95:4-5; a generic statement of essence is comparable to 'man is better than woman' and Imru al-Qays's verse about young war.
70:19/q07 How does attributing the defect to creation assign moral responsibility? [خُلِقَ · theology]
  70:19/q07/p1 People are responsible for failing to discipline their impulses, while God gives the faculty and guidance for good. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says humans indulge desire and neglect revelation and wise counsel. Good is attributed to God's gift of the faculty seeking good and His guidance, with 4:78-79 as evidence. He rejects the Mu'tazilite position in this theological dispute without further detail in these notes. He also says the wording rebukes, rather than excuses, hoarding; it can soften blame by recognizing weakness (4:28) and contrast original good creation with deterioration (95:4-5). Al-Tabatabai likewise distinguishes the created beneficial disposition from people's misuse, and says the condemnation contains no contradiction.
70:19/q08 What prophetic report describes the corresponding vices? [هَلُوعًا · reports]
  70:19/q08/p1 The Prophet called anxious or grasping greed and disabling cowardice among the worst traits given to a person. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf calls them the worst gifts to Adam's son; al-Qurtubi says gifts to a servant; Ibn Kathir transmits Abu Hurayra's report and notes Abu Dawud's parallel route. The descriptions vary between miserly despair, grasping greed, fear stripping courage, and paralyzing cowardice.
70:19/q09 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage? [* · links]
  70:19/q09/p1 Verses 19-21 explain the human tendency behind turning away and hoarding, before exempting worshippers. — reasons: Ibn Ashur calls them an interruption between the hoarder in 70:17 and the exception in 70:22; the fear of losing benefit explains but does not excuse hoarding. Al-Tabatabai says misused innate panic leads to withdrawal, hoarding, and Hell, though created to guide humans to good. Al-Biqai says self-absorption causes forgetfulness of the Lord and sins. Study Quran says those with the eight qualities in 70:22-35 are exempt.
70:19/q10 What other passages parallel this description? [* · links]
  70:19/q10/p1 Other verses describe the same haste, despair, and withdrawal after mercy. — reasons: Study Quran cites 41:49-51 for asking for good, despair at harm, and withdrawal after mercy, and 21:37 and 17:11 for haste. Al-Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, and Ibn Ashur also compare 21:37; Ibn Ashur cites 96:6-7 for generic human arrogance.
  70:19/q10/p2 The descriptions of believers in al-Mu'minun and al-Ma'arij are strongly parallel but differ in order and wording. — reasons: Al-Samarra'i points to 23:1-11 and the corresponding qualities here.
70:19/q11 How can the disposition be restrained or overcome? [* · interpretation]
  70:19/q11/p1 Steady prayer and the qualities of the exempt believers train patience, generosity, desire, and attention. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf distinguishes regularly performing prayer from guarding its ablution, time, pillars, sunna, manners, and protection against invalidating sins; the Prophet calls consistent work best even if little, and Aisha reports his practice was continuous. Al-Samarra'i says persistent attentive prayer treats panic and withholding by giving time and self to God despite worldly costs (62:11), while guarding chastity trains restraint against tempting opportunities (23:5-7). Study Quran says the eight qualities of 70:22-35 exempt their possessors. Al-Tabatabai says developed reason redirects eagerness toward real good, fear of sin, forgiveness, and patience under duty.
  70:19/q11/p2 Accept God's decree and direct health and wealth toward gain in the Hereafter. — reasons: Study Quran reports al-Razi's advice to recognize that circumstances come from God and be content, and to spend health and wealth seeking the Hereafter rather than this life.
70:19/q12 What wisdom does al-Razi find in the created human body's capacity for speech? [* · interpretation]
  70:19/q12/p1 The body and breathing serve the soul's pursuit of knowledge and right action, while articulated speech enables easy, precise communication. — reasons: Al-Razi says people need others to meet their needs and speech is easier than writing or gesture. Human perfection is knowledge of truth and action on good; the body serves the initially unperfected soul. The heart supplies heat, draws in cooling air by expansion, and expels warmed air by contraction. Expelled air becomes sound; throat, tongue, teeth, and lips shape letters and limitless combinations of words. Sounds appear only when needed and vanish afterward; their variety avoids the ambiguity of gestures or clapping. The hierarchy runs from the soul through body, heart, breath, sound, and words, displaying the Creator's profound wisdom.
70:19/q13 What reading of amānāt is reported alongside this discussion? [* · readings] (turns on: Presence or absence of an alif after the nun in the plural.)
  70:19/q13/p1 Ibn Kathir reads the plural amānāt without an alif after the nun. — reasons: Al-Jishumi reports this reading.
  70:19/q13/p2 The remaining readers read the plural with an alif after the nun. — reasons: Al-Jishumi contrasts this with Ibn Kathir's reading.
70:19/q14 How does the verse describe ordinary reactions to divine punishment? [* · interpretation]
  70:19/q14/p1 People often complain and become frustrated even at a small punishment for their deeds. — reasons: Islahi applies the human tendency to impatience under God's punishment.
