6:152/q12 When must the guardian hand over the orphan's property? [حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ · law] (turns on: Whether 4:6 supplies a further prudence condition to this verse's maturity endpoint.)
  6:152/q12/p1 At adulthood together with sound judgment; the property is then delivered. — reasons: Zajjaj, Wahidi, Jishumi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisī, Qurtubi, and Tabatabai appeal to 4:6; Qurtubi warns that an imprudent adult may squander it. Ibn al-Jawzi says the judgment and Islam qualifications come from another verse, limiting this verse's unqualified wording. Islahi adds delivery with witnesses.
  6:152/q12/p2 At puberty, with delivery implied directly by the endpoint. — reasons: Nasafi states delivery at puberty; the separate debate over required financial prudence turns on 4:6.
6:152/q13 Was the orphan maturity limit abrogated by 4:6? [حَتَّىٰ يَبْلُغَ أَشُدَّهُۥ · sciences] (turns on: Whether the later testing and marriage-age instruction replaces the earlier threshold or explains it.)
  6:152/q13/p1 Yes, the later marriage-age and testing verse replaced this limit. — reasons: Suddi's thirty-year report is coupled with this suggestion by Ibn al-Jawzi and Tabrisī; Alusi reports the same view.
  6:152/q13/p2 The verses are read together: 4:6 clarifies maturity by adding sound judgment. — reasons: Zajjaj, Qurtubi, Tabrisī, Tabatabai, and others apply both conditions; Ibn al-Jawzi frames it as qualifying an unrestricted statement rather than deriving all details from this verse alone.
6:152/q14 What do kayl and mizan name here? [ٱلْكَيْلَ ٱلْمِيزَانَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether an omitted measured or weighed noun is necessary, or kayl can directly denote the vessel or amount.)
  6:152/q14/p1 The measured amount and weighed amount, with measure also able to name its vessel and mizan the scale. — reasons: Wahidi says wafā means completion, kayl can imply a vessel, and mizan names weighing on the scale; Samin derives mizan from weighing on the mif'al pattern, underlying mawzan changing like miqat. Ibn Ashur names dates and raisins sold by measure and gold and silver by weight; Alusi allows the commodity sense.
  6:152/q14/p2 They are verbal nouns standing for measured goods or acts of measuring, with supplied nouns. — reasons: Abu al-Baqa proposes omitted nouns; Wahidi proposes an understood weight with mizan. Samin rejects unnecessary substitution because kayl itself can name the vessel; Alusi still allows an omitted noun.
6:152/q15 What does fair fulfillment of measure and weight require? [وَأَوْفُوا۟ ٱلْكَيْلَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ بِٱلْقِسْطِ · law] (turns on: Whether fairness means exact equality without extra or a small excess to guarantee no deficit.)
  6:152/q15/p1 Give and take the full due accurately, without shortchanging or demanding excess. — reasons: Ibn Abbas and many commentators gloss qist as justice. Wahidi defines wafā as completing without shortfall; Razi, Thalabi, Baghawi, Bursevi, and Alusi apply equity to seller and buyer alike. Ibn Kathir cites 83:1-6 and destruction for fraudulent scales. Islahi links honest measures to a just economy and 17:35; Elmalili generalizes protection of others' rights.
  6:152/q15/p2 Slightly over-measure to ensure that the due is certainly fulfilled. — reasons: Abu Hayyan reports this recommendation and a report instructing an added weight; he also glosses qist as exact balance.
6:152/q16 How is with fairness attached grammatically? [بِٱلْقِسْطِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase qualifies the doer or the object of fulfillment.)
  6:152/q16/p1 It describes the measurer's just action. — reasons: Samin and Abu al-Baqa allow the agent reading; Bursevi takes it as the state of fulfillment, and Ibn Ashur says the preposition directs just conduct.
  6:152/q16/p2 It describes the measures or measured objects as complete and fair. — reasons: Samin and Abu al-Baqa report the object reading.
6:152/q17 Why is the measure duty phrased as a positive command? [وَأَوْفُوا۟ ٱلْكَيْلَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ · rhetoric]
  6:152/q17/p1 It urges active justice and implies the prohibition of cheating. — reasons: Ibn Ashur contrasts Shuayb's prohibition at 11:84 and says the command confronts Arabs who boasted of generosity while shorting buyers. Islahi compares 83:1-3; Biqai says full equity rules out merely coming close; Bursevi says the apparent repetition spells out both seller and buyer duties.
6:152/q18 What does the capacity clause qualify and excuse? [لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the intervening sentence is limited by the immediately preceding exact-measure command or extends to all nearby duties.)
  6:152/q18/p1 It qualifies measuring and weighing: reasonable care is required, unavoidable minute error is excused. — reasons: Mawardi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisī, Ibn Kathir, Ibn Ashur, Asad, and many others say microscopic exactness is impossible. Ibn Kathir cites Said ibn al-Musayyib's sincere-measure report as rare and disconnected; Bursevi says deliberate fraud is not excused. Ibn Ashur says the concession keeps fear of error from stopping trade.
  6:152/q18/p2 It states generally that all the listed obligations lie within human ability. — reasons: Ibn Abbas compares 22:78; Sufyan identifies capacity with performing duties. Farisi, Jishumi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai, Islahi, and Study Quran permit the broader scope; Ibn Ashur cites 2:286 and Study Quran gives 2:286, 7:42, 23:62, 65:7. Islahi rejects both scrupulous excess and self-set limits of obedience.
6:152/q19 What does wus' or capacity mean here? [وُسْعَهَا · meaning] (turns on: Whether capacity is ordinary ability, one's own action, or performance of duties.)
  6:152/q19/p1 One's ability or what one can bear. — reasons: Said ibn Jubayr glosses it as ability; Ibn Abbas says obligations are manageable, citing 22:78; Ibn Kathir and later commentators apply this to honest effort.
  6:152/q19/p2 What a person has done for himself. — reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim transmits this gloss from Sha'bi.
  6:152/q19/p3 Performing the assigned obligations. — reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim transmits this gloss from Sufyan.
6:152/q20 What does the capacity clause imply about obliging an incapable unbeliever? [لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا · theology] (turns on: Whether the easing of an attainable measurement task proves a broader rule against the disputed obligation to believe.)
  6:152/q20/p1 God cannot oblige faith from someone incapable while creating and willing that person's disbelief. — reasons: Al-Qadi argues from the easing of exact measurement: if the attainable task is eased, such an impossible obligation would be incoherent.
  6:152/q20/p2 The objection fails when divine knowledge and the motive are considered. — reasons: Razi gives this reply to al-Qadi and his teachers.
6:152/q21 How is the capacity sentence placed in the syntax? [لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا · grammar]
  6:152/q21/p1 It is a parenthetical sentence between commands. — reasons: Samin calls it parenthetical; Ibn Ashur notes the shift into God's first-person voice as a special favor that confirms the messenger.
6:152/q22 What reported consequences follow dishonest measures and weights? [ٱلْكَيْلَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ · reports] (turns on: Different reports present collective destruction, famine, or individual deathbed consequences.)
  6:152/q22/p1 Earlier peoples perished through measuring and weighing; a raised prophetic warning is weak while an Ibn Abbas version is sound. — reasons: Thalabi and Qurtubi transmit Ibn Abbas's warning to non-Arabs. Ibn Kathir says Tirmidhi knows the prophetic version only through weak Husayn ibn Qays but authenticates its stop at Ibn Abbas; Durr calls the prophetic report weak. A second Ibn Abbas report says the community inherited the two perilous practices.
