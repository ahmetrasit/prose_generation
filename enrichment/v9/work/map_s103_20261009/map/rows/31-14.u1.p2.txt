Isrāʾ. Al-Ālūsī records prayer and fasting toward God and kinship toward parents; al-Ālūsī says the five-prayer example is illustrative rather than exhaustive.
  31:14/q22/p2 Performing the five daily prayers thanks God, and supplicating for one's parents after them thanks the parents. — reasons: Sufyān b. ʿUyayna's explanation is repeated by al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī; al-Ālūsī says it illustrates some forms, not the entire duty.
31:14/q23 Why is thanks to parents paired with thanks to God, and why is God named first? [ٱشْكُرْ لِى وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · rhetoric]
  31:14/q23/p1 God is the ultimate creator and giver; parents are appointed, proximate causes of birth and care, so gratitude to them follows gratitude to Him. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭāʾ distinguishes ultimate Cause and means, noting that absorption in one can eclipse the other. Al-Māwardī says Qatāda distinguishes God's right from theirs. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Asad and Kuran Yolu emphasize source versus parental means; Bursevī cites Sharḥ al-Ḥikam and the hadith 'who does not thank people does not thank God,' giving two readings: a condition of valid thanks, or God's reward limited to those who thank His servants. Al-Ālūsī says their gratitude depends on His and that this explains divine priority. Asad compares 17:23–24.
31:14/q24 What does the lām in ushkur lī do? [ٱشْكُرْ لِى · grammar]
  31:14/q24/p1 It strengthens the verb 'give thanks' and is conventionally attached to shukr. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the usage at 2:152; he adds that the present tense of yashkur in the related passage signals renewed gratitude.
31:14/q25 What does 'to Me is the return' mean? [إِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the clause is explained through resurrection and accounting or through God's sole sovereignty at the final state.)
  31:14/q25/p1 All return to God at resurrection for judgment and recompense for gratitude, ingratitude and deeds, with Paradise or Fire as the outcome. — reasons: Yaḥyā names Resurrection; al-Ṭabarī says judgment on thanks and filial kindness. Al-Māwardī specifies Paradise and Fire; Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil through al-Wāḥidī say each is repaid for deeds. Muʿādh's prophetic mission report in Ibn Kathīr identifies everlasting Paradise or Hell. Bursevī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı stress reward or questioning.
  31:14/q25/p2 Returning to God is reaching the state where no judge or owner exists but Him. — reasons: Bursevī gives this further explanation of the return.
31:14/q26 What role does the final return clause play in the parental counsel? [إِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ · rhetoric]
  31:14/q26/p1 It warns and gives the reason to obey the command, since God will reckon gratitude and disobedience. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān call it an inserted warning; al-Ṭabrisī calls it a threat. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say return explains why obedience is required. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the shift of person accords with 31:12.
31:14/q27 What reports link parental pleasure or neglect with the final reward? [بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ إِلَىَّ ٱلْمَصِيرُ · reports] (turns on: The cited reports give distinct formulations of the reward and consequence of filial conduct.)
  31:14/q27/p1 God's pleasure follows a parent's pleasure, and His anger follows a parent's anger; pleasing or angering parents bears on Paradise or Hell. — reasons: Yaḥyā reports the correspondence and gate imagery even when a child was wronged; al-Māwardī transmits the Prophetic saying through ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿUmar.
  31:14/q27/p2 A servant who fulfills other obligations but neglects filial piety will not enter Paradise. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī quotes al-Firdaws via Abū al-Dardāʾ, without grading, and connects filial honor to monotheism, prayer and remembrance.
31:14/q28 How can one honor parents after their death? [وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · reports] (turns on: The reports prescribe different posthumous acts, one general and one fixed devotional sequence.)
  31:14/q28/p1 Keep ties with a deceased father's friends; prayer and charity for deceased parents can restore dutifulness after earlier neglect. — reasons: Bursevī transmits these practices as reports.
  31:14/q28/p2 A specified Thursday-night two-rakʿah prayer with repeated recitations and fifteen istighfārs dedicated to parents fulfills their right even after disobedience. — reasons: Bursevī cites reports from al-Iḥyāʾ and Qūt al-Qulūb for this distinct prescription.
31:14/q29 How grave is disobedience to parents? [بِوَٰلِدَيْهِ · reports]
  31:14/q29/p1 It is among the worst major sins, paired with associating partners with God. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan reports a Prophetic scale in which good deeds culminate in giving one's life for God and sins culminate in parental disobedience. Study Quran cites the Prophet naming shirk and disrespect of parents among the greatest major sins.
31:14/q30 How does this injunction connect to other Qur'anic commands? [* · links]
  31:14/q30/p1 Kindness to parents is repeatedly paired with worship of God alone and follows the warning against shirk here. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān calls it a further call to obey God after Luqmān's warning. Ibn Kathīr compares 17:23; Study Quran compares 4:36, 29:8, 46:15 and 17:23 and says the parental command first appears in the covenant of 2:83. Al-Biqāʿī says gratitude to God entails gratitude to people. Al-Samīn notes the construction was discussed at 29:8.
31:14/q31 How is the nūn of an before the imperative pronounced? [أَنِ ٱشْكُرْ · readings]
  31:14/q31/p1 A reported form changes the nūn from kasra to ḍamma, anu, by phonetic assimilation before the imperative. — reasons: Qiraat-ER records and explains this pronunciation.
31:14/q32 How do a teacher's benefits compare with a parent's in gratitude? [وَلِوَٰلِدَيْكَ · interpretation]
  31:14/q32/p1 A teacher can deserve greater gratitude because a parent gives mortal life while a teacher guides toward lasting or higher life. — reasons: Bursevī cites Alexander's comparison of father bringing him from heaven to earth and tutor lifting him toward heaven; Burzjumhr similarly contrasts mortal and lasting life.
31:14/q33 What does the human being's place in creation illustrate here? [* · interpretation]
  31:14/q33/p1 The human being is creation's fruit and kernel, combining both worlds and receiving verbal revelation. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī reports al-Rāzī's tree image: the human is its fruit, kernel rather than rind, and completion.
31:14/q34 What larger point does gratitude to God make in the adjoining Luqmān passage? [* · theology]
  31:14/q34/p1 Gratitude benefits and perfects the grateful human, while God is self-sufficient and already praised by creation. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts human benefactors' possible reciprocal gain with God's lack of need; turning away is human ingratitude. He cites 13:15 for creation's praise, says Luqmān's wisdom begins immediate gratitude on receiving a blessing, quotes 'praise is the head of gratitude,' and notes ḥamīd is used because God has no name from the gratitude root except al-Shakūr.
