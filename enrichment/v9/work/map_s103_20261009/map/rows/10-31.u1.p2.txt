  10:31/q19/p1 They move from human needs and familiar gifts to the origin of life and then universal management. — reasons: Biqai says the questions first invoke experienced gifts and then prior creation. 10:31/q20 What does the verse imply about predestination, human acts, and divine provision? [* · theology] (turns on: Whether exclusive divine creation and provision determine all acts or leave independent human acts and sources of sustenance.)
  10:31/q20/p1 All created events remain under God's settled will, decree, and power; causes cannot reverse His decree. — reasons: Qushayri says the decree has already passed, all creatures and events are subject to it, and created causes cannot change what is eternal. He rejects both assigning creation-partners within predestination and denying resurrection as unbelief. Sulami's report from al-Wasiti says ongoing divine management leaves no independent agency based on human works.
  10:31/q20/p2 People themselves perform idol worship and fabrication and are rightly punished for them. — reasons: Jishumi argues this against the Compulsionists' account of human acts; the reproach and punishment assume accountable human action.
  10:31/q20/p3 God's provision includes forbidden as well as lawful sustenance. — reasons: Alusi reports Sahib al-Intisaf's use of the verse against the Qadariyyah claim that God supplies only lawful sustenance and humans supply themselves with forbidden sustenance; he calls that hidden associationism.
10:31/q21 How do the surrounding verses prepare or extend this challenge? [* · links]
  10:31/q21/p1 The preceding exposure of idols, their helplessness, and their worshippers' accountability prepares this challenge. — reasons: Jishumi says idols and worshippers are gathered then separated and disavow one another; even animated idols cannot help, harm, avert punishment or intercede independently, and each person receives the consequence of good or evil. Abu Ja'far in Samarrai says earlier questions about provision and the senses have already awakened the audience, leaving the punishment reminder. Samarrai emphasizes setting and context.
  10:31/q21/p2 The provision and life questions connect with other verses explaining the same signs. — reasons: Ibn Jawzi refers life from death to 3:27; Ibn Ashur compares 3:27 but says this passage has no hidden allusion. Study Quran points from the life question to 30:19 and 6:95 and from governance to 10:3. Ibn Kathir invokes 80:27–31, 67:21, 67:23, 6:46 and 55:29 for produce, senses and divine dependence.
  10:31/q21/p3 The adjoining discussion of truth contrasts sure knowledge of God with conjecture and spiritual blindness. — reasons: Qushayri says Truth is a divine name, God possesses and actualizes truth, and human truth is good action, sound belief and permissible speech. Conjecture is a preference without certainty; it is defective about God, while certainty about one's own unseen future is also defective. He cites 12:108 for calling with insight and says hearing the Qur'an increases some people's blindness but guides the spiritually realized. The verse's answer itself, he says, may be mere words without inner conviction.
10:31/q22 What report illustrates the marvel of the created senses? [ٱلسَّمْعَ وَٱلْأَبْصَٰرَ · reports]
  10:31/q22/p1 Ali praised God for sight through fat or fluid, hearing through bone, and speech through flesh. — reasons: Razi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai and Kuranyolu relay the same saying; Kuranyolu renders the sight medium as fluid, the others as fat. The wording varies slightly while the three faculties are the same.
10:31/q23 Does the old polytheist argument have a modern counterpart? [* · theology]
  10:31/q23/p1 Modern idolaters repeat the same pattern despite describing their practices as monotheistic. — reasons: Islahi equates their claimed mediation with the Arabs' treatment of favored creatures as delegated powers and intercessors.
