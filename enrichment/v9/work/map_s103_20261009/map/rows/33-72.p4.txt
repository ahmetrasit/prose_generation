[TABRISI:33:70-72/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One explanation takes the presentation as addressed to the inhabitants of heavens, earth, and mountains, with a deleted possessive; they were shown the grave sin of violating God's trusts and laws, displaying human boldness and the angels' fear.
[TABRISI:33:70-72/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي الجبائي | reports | The inhabitants refused the liability, sin, and punishment for neglecting the trust, and feared it; humankind carried it by sinning, ignorant of the consequences of betrayal.
[TABRISI:33:70-72/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو علي الجبائي | holds | Because the physical heavens, earth, and mountains cannot bear moral responsibility, the verse must mean their accountable inhabitants; bearing the trust means violating it, since angels already perform it.
[TABRISI:33:70-72/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · الزجاج | reports | Zajjaj says a betrayer bears the trust while one who fulfills it has not; likewise, sinners bear the burden of sin.
[TABRISI:33:70-72/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو العالية | reports | One view defines the trust as everything God commands people to obey and forbids them to violate.
[TABRISI:33:70-72/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس ومجاهد | reports | Another view defines the trust as God's legal rulings and obligations; the author says it is close to the first view.
[TABRISI:33:70-72/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · السدي والضحاك | reports | A further view means people's trusts and keeping covenants; it begins with Adam entrusting his family to Cain before traveling to Mecca, after which Cain betrayed him by killing Abel.
[TABRISI:33:72/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن | reports | Al-Hasan understands carrying the trust as betraying it; the author says the apparent sense does not support this reading. | mentions 29:13
[TABRISI:33:72/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | rejects | The poet's usage makes accepting a trust the opposite of returning it, so it cannot show that carrying here means betrayal.
[TABRISI:33:72/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو مسلم | reports | The offer means comparison: God's trust comprises commands, prohibitions, books, messengers, and the covenant, outweighing the heavens, earth, and mountains in importance.
[TABRISI:33:72/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو مسلم | reports | The heavens, earth, and mountains could not bear the trust and feared it; the human accepted but neglected it through injustice and ignorance of reward and punishment.
[TABRISI:33:72/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Hypothetically, if the heavens, earth, and mountains were rational, they would fear failing the religious duties and promises or threats; a weaker human accepted without fear.
[TABRISI:33:72/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās's report that the trust was offered to the actual heavens and earth is understood on this hypothetical reading.
[TABRISI:33:72/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | Alternatively, offer and refusal figuratively exalt the trust rather than describe speech to inanimate things; Arabs similarly address ruins and houses as if they could answer.
