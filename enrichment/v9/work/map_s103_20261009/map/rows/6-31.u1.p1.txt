6:31/q14 What are they lamenting? [عَلَىٰ مَا فَرَّطْنَا فِيهَا · interpretation] (turns on: Whether their regret is specified as missed deeds, lost Paradise homes, mocked messengers, the failed bargain, or wasted faculties.)
  6:31/q14/p1 Neglected faith, obedience and preparation for Paradise and the Hereafter. — reasons: Al-Suddī specifies deeds that would earn Paradise; Muqātil names remembrance of God; Rāzī says lost reward, while their burdens indicate earned punishment. Biqāʿī includes disciplining the soul and following messengers; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Study Quran specify afterlife work during worldly life.
  6:31/q14/p2 They see the Paradise places they have lost and lament. — reasons: A Prophetic report is transmitted by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Qurṭubī, Ṭabrisī, Durr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī; Durr calls its chain sound. Samīn rejects the proposed pronoun reference to those places as remote; al-Ālūsī says the report cannot settle that reference.
  6:31/q14/p3 They regret having mocked the messengers. — reasons: Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Mujāhid.
  6:31/q14/p4 They regret the ruined transaction of trading faith and Paradise for disbelief and punishment. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī infers the bargain from khusira; Qurṭubī cites 2:16.
  6:31/q14/p5 They lament wasted capacities and separation from former pleasures. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says God-given faculties were meant to disclose enduring truth and virtue; after death they are unprepared for the spiritual realm and cut off from transient pleasures. Study Quran relays him.
6:31/q15 What does 'farraṭnā' mean and derive from? [فَرَّطْنَا · meaning] (turns on: Whether tafrīṭ is explained by ordinary neglect or by its figurative derivation from precedence.)
  6:31/q15/p1 We neglected, wasted, or fell short of what we could have done. — reasons: Zajjāj includes incapacity or neglect; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān stress that action remained possible; Ṭabrisī contrasts tafrīṭ as falling behind with ifrāṭ as excess. Abū ʿUbayda and others give waste/neglect.
  6:31/q15/p2 It evokes going ahead or letting another precede one, with the doubled form removing precedence. — reasons: Zajjāj and Wāḥidī describe putting shortcoming first; Ibn Baḥr explains yielding one's lead. Samīn likens the privative formation to skinning a camel and compares 17:79; al-Ālūsī repeats this morphological account. Qurṭubī says they let others precede them in obedience.
6:31/q16 What is the grammatical role of 'mā' in their lament? [مَا فَرَّطْنَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether mā names the act of neglect or the things neglected.)
  6:31/q16/p1 It is a verbal noun: they lament their neglect. — reasons: Samīn says 'for what we neglected' attaches to regret.
  6:31/q16/p2 It is a relative pronoun for deeds or acts of obedience they neglected. — reasons: Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī say on this analysis the antecedent is explicit in their speech; Abū Ḥayyān and Rāzī also report it.
6:31/q17 What does the pronoun in 'fīhā' refer to? [فِيهَا · referent] (turns on: Which implied antecedent—world, Hour, Paradise, bargain, deeds, or Paradise places—fits the context.)
  6:31/q17/p1 Worldly life, where they neglected work for the afterlife. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil say the unmentioned world is obvious from context; Rāzī says reason identifies it as the place of neglect.
  6:31/q17/p2 The Hour and the faith and preparation it required. — reasons: Al-Ḥasan and others construe 'in it' as neglecting its affairs or preparation; Kashshāf compares 'I neglected God's side' at 39:56.
  6:31/q17/p3 Paradise, which they failed to seek and work for. — reasons: Al-Suddī gives this reading and Ṭabrisī pairs it with the report of Hell's people seeing their Paradise homes. Al-Ālūsī considers it remote.
  6:31/q17/p4 The transaction implied by their loss. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī argues loss implies sale, even without an explicit noun; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes the implied bargain. Al-Ālūsī considers the reading remote. Samīn attributes it to al-Ḥasan.
  6:31/q17/p5 The good deeds or acts of obedience expressed by a relative 'mā'. — reasons: Wāḥidī, Rāzī and al-Ālūsī note this makes the pronoun refer to an item in the utterance itself; Ibn al-Jawzī reports obedience as the antecedent.
  6:31/q17/p6 Their former places in Paradise. — reasons: Samīn reports and rejects this proposal as remote.
6:31/q18 How does 'while they carry' attach to the lament? [وَهُمْ يَحْمِلُونَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the state is simultaneous with the cry or anticipated.)
  6:31/q18/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing them as they speak. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Samīn, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī identify the wāw as circumstantial; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn say the explicit subject pronoun strengthens attribution.
  6:31/q18/p2 The burden-bearing may be prospective relative to the lament. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī permits this alternate timing.
6:31/q19 What are 'their awzār'? [أَوْزَارَهُمْ · meaning] (turns on: Whether awzār names sins, their punishment, divine wrath, ignored reminders, or inward attachments.)
  6:31/q19/p1 Their sins, errors and evil deeds, described as heavy burdens. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba cites 94:2–3; Ibn ʿAbbās and many others gloss sin; Qatāda says the load is their deeds and also their work or earning. Ṭabarī rejects physical-load gloss as unattested Arab usage, preferring the sinning sense. Asad adopts Ibn ʿAbbās through Rāzī.
  6:31/q19/p2 The punishment or suffering earned by their sins. — reasons: Naḥḥās records this as a second gloss; Zajjāj and others compare severe punishment to a crushing load, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes sin's consequence among possibilities.
  6:31/q19/p3 God's wrath earned by sin. — reasons: Study Quran cites 2:61, 2:90, 3:112 and 3:162.
  6:31/q19/p4 The reminders and warnings they ignored. — reasons: Study Quran cites 20:100–101, where the reminder becomes an afterlife burden.
  6:31/q19/p5 For the veiled soul, attachments, bodily love, sins and sensory traits rooted within it. — reasons: Qāshānī says these bind, veil and torment the person.
6:31/q20 What is the root sense and derivation of 'wizr'? [أَوْزَارَهُمْ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the lexical starting point is carried weight or a refuge/support.)
  6:31/q20/p1 A heavy load; sins are named for the weight one carries. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba cites 94:2–3; Abū ʿUbayd describes carrying goods in a cloak and the loads of war. Wāḥidī cites 6:164; Samīn gives awzār as plural of wizr, war equipment and a poetic line, and wazīr as bearer of a ruler's burdens. Akhfash gives wazara/yaziru/wizran and mawzūr; Yūnus notes two vocalizations. Funeral usage prefers mawzūrāt over common maʾzūrāt, while Abū ʿAlī calls the latter sound-following.
  6:31/q20/p2 A mountain refuge or support on which one relies; heaviness and support extend to sin and a minister. — reasons: Zajjāj and Abū ʿUbayd cite Moses' request for Aaron as minister (20:29–30); Samīn and al-Rāghib cite 75:11. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls derivation from a refuge or helper unclear.
6:31/q21 How are their burdens borne 'on their backs'? [يَحْمِلُونَ أَوْزَارَهُمْ عَلَىٰ ظُهُورِهِمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the image presents embodied deeds, metaphorical hardship, constant attachment, or an inward state.)
  6:31/q21/p1 Literally: deeds take visible forms, and the sinner's ugly, foul deed rides or is carried on his back. — reasons: Muqātil says the deed demands to be carried; reports from al-Suddī, ʿAmr, Abū Marzūq and ʿUmayr include a grave-companion, a public exposure or a journey to Hell. Some describe the believer's fragrant good deed as a mount (19:85). Bursevī argues afterlife deeds can become visible though accidents here; al-Ālūsī sees no obstacle and cites Sunni belief in embodied deeds and weighing. Abū Ḥayyān's preferred report groups ʿUmayr, ʿAmr, al-Suddī and Ṭabarī.
  6:31/q21/p2 Figuratively: sins and their painful punishment weigh on them like loads. — reasons: Zajjāj says Arabic uses 'heavy' for severe aversion; Ibn ʿAṭiyya treats back-bearing as an intensified figure and questions the refuge derivation. Zamakhsharī compares hands as the normal instrument of deeds in 42:30; Bursevī calls sins bodily loads by metaphor.
  6:31/q21/p3 The image says sins never leave or release them. — reasons: Arabists compare a person always before one's eyes to one continually in mind; Nasafī and Qurṭubī say sin clings to the denier.
  6:31/q21/p4 Inwardly, rooted traits and attachments veil and torment the soul. — reasons: Qāshānī calls this the burden of bodily love and sensory traits.
6:31/q22 Why mention the backs specifically? [عَلَىٰ ظُهُورِهِمْ · rhetoric]
  6:31/q22/p1 The back is the customary and strongest place for heavy burdens, intensifying their weight. — reasons: Ṭabarī notes loads might otherwise be borne on head or shoulder; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn say the back carries more, with Samīn comparing the hand's touch in 6:7. Kashshāf contrasts hand imagery for earned deeds in 42:30.
6:31/q23 What does 'alā' add? [أَلَا · rhetoric]
  6:31/q23/p1 It announces and intensifies the gravity of the condemnation. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares Prophetic calls to convey and witness; Nasafī and Biqāʿī emphasize severity.
