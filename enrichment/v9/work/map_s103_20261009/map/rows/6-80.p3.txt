-FULL:v4p569/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya treats “unless my Lord wills something” as a disconnected exception: Abraham fears only a harm God wills, not harm caused by powerless idols; al-Hawfi agrees.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p569/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari treats the exception as connected to the general times covered by the negation: Abraham fears no idol at any time unless God wills a frightening harm through it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p569/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa allows connected or disconnected exception, and for the connected reading takes it from circumstances, meaning “except in the state in which my Lord wills.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p569/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | “Something” is accusative either as an object or as a cognate accusative meaning “a willing.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p569/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | God's knowledge of all events explains why a feared harm cannot be ruled out under either reading of the exception; “knowledge” is an accusative specification transformed from the subject.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p569/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The closing question rebukes their heedlessness in worshiping things that neither harm nor benefit, and urges them to consider Abraham’s proof against their divinity.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v4p569/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari glosses the question as a call to distinguish sound from corrupt reasoning and the capable from the incapable; al-Razi says denying rivals does not itself entail punishment.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh argues that if the inflectional ending itself is sometimes dropped, its substitute nūn can be dropped too; a critic answers that a root may be deletable while its derivative is not.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A further objection says deleting the protective nūn would leave the inflectional nūn to be deleted under a jussive or subjunctive, while deleting the inflectional nūn avoids a second deletion.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh notes that the protective nūn is already kasra, so retaining it avoids changing the indicative nūn from fatḥa to kasra; he also cites poetry with a deleted nūn before nā.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The counterargument says the inflectional nūn itself marks case and therefore also has a strong claim against deletion.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh analyzes the deleted nūn in the cited ‘they dislike you’ poem as the subject nūn, not the protective nūn.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash argues that the second nūn causes the heaviness, is redundant, and may be dropped because the first nūn already receives the kasra it was meant to protect.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash also points to the loss of the protective nūn in laytani, pronounced laytī, with a poetic example.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Deleting one nūn in this construction is fluent, accepted Arabic, not limited to poetic necessity; the attributed objections are false given its canonical transmission and Ghatafan usage.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مكي ابن أبي طالب | reports | Makki calls the deletion distant, ugly, and permissible only in poetry, arguing that the Qurʾan has no need for it.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | The author rejects claims that the light-nūn reading is a solecism because it is mutawātir and attested Arabic.
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Fī Allāh attaches to atuḥājjūnī rather than ḥājjahu; the author questions this coordination analysis because the quoted clause seems separate and means ‘about God and His oneness.’
[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#3/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | reports | The Shaykh says the second verb governs under coordination because the first is pronominalized and would require an omitted complement if it governed.

[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#4/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Mā may be a verbal noun, relative pronoun, or indefinite description; if verbal, bihā refers to God and the sense is ‘I fear no associating partners with God,’ with an omitted object.

[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#4/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa instead makes the connected exception circumstantial: he fears them in every state except the state of God’s willing.

[SAMIN-DURR:6:80#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The cited poetic line illustrates naming the object of a wish when it is remarkable.
