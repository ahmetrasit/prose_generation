[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p743/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الشافعي، أبو حنيفة، المالكية | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī and Abū Ḥanīfa make possession a condition of validity; Mālik makes the contract binding before delivery and compels transfer, possession being required for full benefit. All agree the pledge is incomplete without it.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p743/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مالك، أبو حنيفة، الشافعي | reports | Mālik says return to the pledgor voids the pledge; Abū Ḥanīfa preserves it if returned as loan or deposit; al-Shāfiʿī voids it whenever returned.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p743/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجمهور، المالكية | reports | The majority require valuable, saleable, deliverable property and reject a debt as collateral and uncertain items; Mālik permits a debt and uncertain property such as runaway slave, stray camel, fetus, fish in water, or unripe fruit.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p743/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · مالك، الشافعي، أبو حنيفة، الحسن بن صالح | reports | Mālik and al-Shāfiʿī permit an undivided share whether divisible or not; Abū Ḥanīfa rejects it altogether; al-Ḥasan b. Ṣāliḥ permits it only if indivisible.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p743/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | “On a journey” means “while travelling”; wa-lam tajidū has several syntactic attachments, and farhān is predicate of an omitted subject: “the security is a pledge.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p744/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | If a creditor trusts the debtor and advances property without writing, witnesses, or pledge, the debtor must repay it without evasion or delay; jurists agree repayment is obligatory and enforceable.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p744/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · أبي، السجاوندي | reports | Ubay reads fa-in ūmina in passive form, “if people trust him”; al-Sajāwandī reports fa-in iʾtamana as trust without a written instrument or pledge.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p744/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The pronoun in “his trust” may refer to creditor or debtor; amāna may mean the owed thing or act of trust, with “debt” understood elliptically.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p744/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Reported readings alter the hamza of alladhī uʾtumina by vowel indication, ishām, or substitution with yāʾ; Abū Ḥayyān questions some notations and explains its original two hamzas.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p744/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · عاصم، الزمخشري | reports | A rare ʿĀṣim reading assimilates the tāʾ substituted for hamza; al-Zamakhsharī rejects it, though other grammarians report it as a poor dialect.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p745/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The debtor must fear God's punishment in repaying entrusted property; “God, his Lord” stresses piety at acknowledgment and repayment, echoing 2:282. | mentions 2:282
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p745/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Concealing testimony is forbidden where a right may be lost; Ibn ʿAbbās says testify when summoned and report directly to the person concerned, not merely to an official.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p745/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · السلمي | reports | Al-Sulamī reads the prohibition in third-person plural.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p745/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Concealment means withholding testimony; sin is assigned to the heart because testimony is knowledge held there, using part for whole and emphasizing the heart as source of outward action; a prophetic hadith compares it to the body's governing morsel.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p745/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The majority parse āthim as an active participle with qalbuhu its raised subject; al-Zamakhsharī offers a fronted predicate, while Ibn ʿAṭiyya's analysis is rejected by Basran grammarians except on Abū al-Ḥasan's approach.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p746/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · ابن عطية، ابن أبي عبلة، مكي، الزمخشري | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya suggests qalbuhu is partitive apposition; Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads it accusative, parsed by Makkī as explanatory despite definiteness. Other parses compare it with object-like constructions or apposition to inna; a variant has athama qalbahu, “he made his heart sinful.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p746/r2] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | prefers | Abū Ḥayyān identifies the first grammatical analysis as preferred.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p746/r3] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | God's knowledge covers all acts, including concealment and proper delivery of testimony; it is a severe threat because knowledge entails recompense.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p746/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The passage uses contrasting paronomasia in debt/indebtedness, writer/writing, and entrustment/trust.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p747/r1] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | It uses matching paronomasia in “do not conceal testimony” and “whoever conceals it.”
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v2p747/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The closing catalogue identifies ellipsis and explains implied subjects and objects in writing, dictation, testimony, repayment, and not concealing testimony.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | prefers | Al-Zajjaj prefers ruhan because it matches the consonantal outline of the codex, has a sound meaning, and is a transmitted recitation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Arhana can also mean bidding heavily on an item until it is bought at a high price, as in the cited verse.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | One says rahantu lisānī, ‘I pledged my tongue,’ but not arhantu in this usage.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The written form has no alif after hāʾ although fuʿul plurals are rare; Abu Amr says rihān is otherwise known only for horses.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · يونس | reports | Yunus says both rahn and rihān are Arabic, with rahn more usual for a pledge and rihān more usual for horses; poetry also supports rahn.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ and his teacher analyze rahn as plural of rihān and rihān as plural of rahn, a double plural; Sibawayh’s school considers plural-of-plural formation irregular.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The majority’s rihān is a regular, common plural of rahn on the faʿāl pattern; hāʾ may be made quiescent for ease.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Rahn originally is the verbal noun of rahana, giving an item as collateral to someone.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ distinguishes rahana from arhana, the latter meaning to give an item for someone else to pawn.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الفراء | reports | Al-Farraʾ treats rahana and arhana as synonymous and adduces a Hamam al-Saluli verse; al-Asmaʿi rejects that transmission and reads the phrase as circumstantial.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#2/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الأصمعي | rejects | Al-Asmaʿi rejects the transmitted wording, preferring ‘wa-arhanuhū Mālikan’ with a circumstantial wāw and an implied subject.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Rahn can denote the pledged object by transferring a verbal noun to the passive-object meaning, like ‘this is God’s creation’ or ‘a dirham minted by the ruler.’ | mentions 31:11
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | In ‘I pledged a garment as a pledge,’ rahnan is only a verbal noun; in ‘I pledged a pledge,’ it can be the object meaning the pledged item, or an emphatic verbal noun with a second object omitted.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Rahn has no commonly used broken plural for paucity: its frequent plurals ruhan and rihān replace the expected arhān.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The root meaning of rahn is permanence and stability; an established thing is described as rāhin.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#3/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | A pledged object is called rahn because it remains and stays with the pledgee.
