[JISHUMI:v1p257/r7] JISHUMI, d. 494 · author | holds | The verse condemns religious hypocrisy and ostentation, mockery of people of truth, and the grave offense of siding with unbelievers.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#2/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The form liqaw derives from liqiyū, with the yāʾ dropped after the vowel was lightened; Ibn al-Sumayfa reads “lāqaw.”
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#2/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Hypocrites professed Islam before believers and showed disbelief in private; kinship clouded proof, while their public profession protected their lives and the Prophet refrained from killing them lest people say he killed his companions.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#2/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | reports | When ʿUmar urged killing ʿAbd Allāh b. Ubayy after his “honorable” and “humiliated” remark, the Prophet told him to leave the man alive to avoid that public accusation.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#3/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أصحاب مالك | reports | The Maliki position explains the Prophet's restraint despite knowing the hypocrites' general disbelief as a precedent that a judge does not rule from private knowledge; it cites the warning in 33:60–61 if they openly manifest hypocrisy. | mentions 33:60, 33:61
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#3/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · قتادة | reports | He interprets the threat against hypocrites as applying when they openly declare their hypocrisy. | mentions 33:60, 33:61
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#3/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · مالك | reports | Malik equates the hypocrite of the Prophet's era with the present-day zindīq and says a zindīq is executed on testimony without an opportunity to repent, since he conceals what would be repented of.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#3/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · إسماعيل القاضي | reports | He says only Zayd b. Arqam testified against ʿAbd Allāh b. Ubayy and only ʿUmayr b. Saʿd against al-Jullās; two witnesses to either man's disbelief or hypocrisy would have led to execution.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#3/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · القاضي أبو محمد عبد الحق | rejects | He argues the testimony was not explicit proof of disbelief; rather, disbelief was inferred from the force of the words, making this stronger than the mere fact that one witness stood alone.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#3/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī says an accused zindīq who denies the charge, openly professes Islam, and disavows other religions is not to be killed; the Ahl al-Raʾy and al-Ṭabarī agree.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p94#3/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الشافعي وأصحابه | reports | They explain that outward Islam erased what preceded it, as it did for the Prophet's hypocrites; imposing harsher treatment on zindīqs conflicts with Qurʾān and Sunna and elevates witness testimony above God's testimony about hypocrites. | mentions 63:1
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p95/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الشافعي وأبو حنيفة وابن حنبل وأهل الحديث | reports | They say the Prophet was barred from killing hypocrites who outwardly professed Islam and prayed; the same rule applies to a zindīq.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p95/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن حنبل | reports | He cites the report of ʿUbayd Allāh b. ʿAdī from an Anṣārī about a man accused of hypocrisy: the Prophet asked whether he testified to the two testimonies and prayed, then said these were people God forbade him to kill.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p95/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أهل الحديث | reports | They also transmit the Prophet's hope that descendants of the hypocrites would believe in God, affirm the messengers, and devote worship sincerely to the Lord of all worlds.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p95/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو جعفر الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī says legal judgments concern outward conduct, while God alone judges hidden states; the Prophet treated hypocrites as Muslims according to their profession, despite God's disclosure of their lying in 63:1. | mentions 63:1
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p95/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · القاضي أبو محمد | holds | The Malikis answer that 63:1 rebukes anyone suspected of hypocrisy generally without identifying individuals; each could deny being its subject, so it did not expose a particular person's lie in a way that canceled his profession. | mentions 63:1
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p95/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Khala is normally joined to bi, but here ilā follows it because khala means “went away,” parallel to liqaw; al-Farazdaq's verse likewise uses a verb meaning to turn or depart.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · مكي | reports | Makkī says khala bi-flān can mean to mock him, so the verse's ilā removes the possible ambiguity associated with bi.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r10] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Most reciters follow Sībawayh's form; haziʾa and istahzaʾa are synonymous, like ʿajiba and istaʿjaba, and Aws b. Ḥajar's verse illustrates the latter form.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · قوم | reports | Some take ilā to mean maʿa (“with”); Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this weak and points ahead to 3:52 and 61:14. | mentions 3:52, 61:14
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · قوم | reports | Some say ilā substitutes for bi because particles can replace one another; Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects this as weak and contrary to al-Khalīl and Sībawayh.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās interprets the hypocrites' satans as the leaders of disbelief.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن الكلبي | reports | Ibn al-Kalbī and others identify the satans as jinn; Ibn ʿAṭiyya regards that as unlikely in this context.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · جمع من المفسرين | reports | A group of commentators say the satans are soothsayers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the term's sense of distance from faith and good can encompass all these groups and the hypocrites themselves.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | “We are only mocking” means they regard the believers whom they flatter by professing faith as objects of ridicule and contempt.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh writes the hamza of mustahziʾūn with ḍamma over wāw; Abū ʿAlī reports his allowance of a softened hamza.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p96/r9] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash reads mustahziyun by turning the hamza into a full yāʾ; Ibn Jinnī explains its ḍamma as recalling the original hamza, since Arabs dislike a yāʾ with ḍamma after kasra.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · بعض القراء | reports | Some readers omit the hamza in مستهزؤون.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The accusative construction is illustrated by the poet's expression وريشي منكم وهواي معكم.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Hypocrites tell believers that they believe what was sent to Muhammad, then tell their leaders privately that they share their religion and only mock Muhammad's companions.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the hypocrites' shayāṭīn as their unbelieving leaders.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | Another reported view identifies the shayāṭīn as Jews who instructed the hypocrites to deny the message.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r14] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو جعفر الباقر | reports | Abū Jaʿfar al-Bāqir says the shayāṭīn are their soothsayers.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r15] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | إنما نحن مستهزئون means they claim to be with their leaders in religion and to mock and ridicule Muhammad's companions when professing faith.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · أهل الحجاز | reports | The Hijazi reading of خلوا إلى drops the hamza and transfers its vowel to the preceding wāw.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · الباقون | reports | The other readers leave the wāw unvowelled and pronounce the hamza fully.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh says a hamza may be softened to an intermediate sound in the cited vowel environments.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · الأخفش | reports | Al-Akhfash instead fully changes the hamza of مستهزيون to yāʾ because of the preceding kasra; he rejects intermediate treatment or wāw because they yield an unparalleled form.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl defines meeting as anything encountering or coming across something, originally coming together by proximity.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | خلوت إليه and خلوت معه mean being alone with someone; خلوت به can also mean mocking him.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | يستهزئون means they mock; the same verb-pattern shifts meaning in examples such as يستسخرون, يستقر, and استعلى قرنه.
[TABRISI:2:14#2/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | إنا is shortened from إننا by dropping the second nūn; معكم is adverbial, meaning settled with you.
[TABRISI:2:14/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الكسائي | reports | Al-Kisāʾī reads the listed passive forms with initial ḍamma, including qīla and jīʾa.
[TABRISI:2:14/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | In وإذا لقوا, إذا is built because it carries the meaning of في and adheres to it; it can also be spatial, as in خرجت فإذا الناس وقوف.
[TABRISI:2:14/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The expression إنما makes إن non-governing, leaving نحن مبتدأ and مصلحون its predicate; نحن is built like a particle and marked by ḍamma as a plural nominative pronoun.
[TABRISI:2:14/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The clause لا تفسدوا في الأرض is parsed as a raised clause understood as something said to them.
[TABRISI:2:14/r13] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | إلا introduces and opens a self-sufficient statement; it began as لا preceded by interrogative alif, which turns denial into confirmation.
[TABRISI:2:14/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · يعقوب | reports | Yaʿqūb is also reported to use the initial-ḍamma reading for all these forms.
