## THE CURRENT MAP
2:16/q01 Who are 'those' in this verse? [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ · referent] (turns on: Whether context limits the group.)
  2:16/q01/p1 The hypocrites described above. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān traces their false reform and mockery; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr favor the immediate context of 2:8 onward.
  2:16/q01/p2 The People of the Book, especially Jews expecting Muhammad. — reasons: Qatāda, al-Suddī and Muqātil are cited; Abū Ḥayyān notes concern for status and income. Al-Ālūsī calls this distant as an occasion.
  2:16/q01/p3 All unbelievers. — reasons: Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās are cited; al-Ālūsī allows general application but rejects this as intended referent.
  2:16/q01/p4 A people described before they existed. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān reports divine disclosure of future unseen knowledge.
2:16/q02 What does the demonstrative and relative description convey? [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the demonstrative itself conveys contempt.)
  2:16/q02/p1 They mark extreme evil and concentrate the group's traits. — reasons: Bursevī sees exceptional evil; al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the relative clause restricts the description rhetorically, despite other sinners.
  2:16/q02/p2 The demonstrative makes the described class vivid, without inherent contempt. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the far demonstrative for the Qurʾān's grandeur at 2:2 and Qays's near demonstrative for distant death.
2:16/q03 How is the opening nominal sentence parsed? [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · grammar] (turns on: Whether الذين is predicate or modifier.)
  2:16/q03/p1 أولئك is subject and الذين with its relative clause is predicate. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn agree. The alternative lacks a linking pronoun and strains definiteness and the past relative clause.
  2:16/q03/p2 الذين modifies أولئك; a later clause is predicate. — reasons: Al-Nasafī reports this; al-Samīn objects to the adjective or apposition analysis.
  2:16/q03/p3 الذين is a second subject or has conditional force, making فما ربحت predicate. — reasons: Al-Samīn reports and rejects it; Abū Ḥayyān rejects analogous parsing at 2:262 for lack of a linking pronoun.
2:16/q04 What does 'purchased' mean in Arabic usage here? [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · meaning] (turns on: Exchange versus choice.)
  2:16/q04/p1 It denotes exchange or substitution, extended to abstract things. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba and others distinguish buying, selling and exchange; al-Wāḥidī stresses desire for what is bought. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Bashāma and ʿAntara; al-Biqāʿī reports root persistence.
  2:16/q04/p2 It means choosing or preferring. — reasons: Qatāda cites 41:17; al-Ṭabarī adduces al-Aʿshā, Dhū al-Rumma and another poet but says following trade imagery favors exchange. Al-Zajjāj's poem describes adopting Christianity.
2:16/q05 What exactly did they exchange, and had they possessed guidance? [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ بِٱلْهُدَىٰ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether prior guidance was actual, available, innate, or only professed.)
  2:16/q05/p1 They took unbelief and abandoned faith. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd and al-Suddī gloss guidance as the price. Al-Ṭabarī cites 2:8 against a prior true profession. Al-Jishumī prefers this; Qatāda describes shifts from unity, safety and Sunnah to division, fear and innovation.
  2:16/q05/p2 They chose error although guidance was available, without possessing it. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī describes abandoning its obligation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Nasafī liken availability to possession; Abū Ḥayyān prefers a capacity for guidance as price, not attained guidance. Al-Ālūsī cites signs, miracles and counsel.
  2:16/q05/p3 They believed and then disbelieved. — reasons: Mujāhid is transmitted repeatedly; Ibn Kathīr notes different kinds of hypocrites and 63:3. Al-Ṭabarī cites 2:8 and al-Jishumī rejects this for this passage.
  2:16/q05/p4 Jews believed in Muhammad's advent before his mission, then rejected him. — reasons: Muqātil says they expected an Isaacite prophet and envied the Ishmaelite Muhammad. Al-Kalbī agrees; Abū Ḥayyān mentions status and income; al-Ālūsī cites Torah belief and 2:89.
  2:16/q05/p5 They exchanged innate disposition or sound reason. — reasons: Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī cite a prophetic report; al-Ālūsī replies to the objection that fitra is common by pointing to parental Judaizing and later hypocrisy.
  2:16/q05/p6 They gave up outward profession and worship while inwardly disbelieving. — reasons: Al-Jishumī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr report or develop this. Al-Ālūsī rejects outward ritual as the relevant purchase price.
  2:16/q05/p7 They exchanged the obedience for which God created them. — reasons: Ibn Kaysān proposes it; Abū Ḥayyān objects that one specifically created to obey could not disobey, citing 51:56 and 11:119.
  2:16/q05/p8 They chose punishment and destruction instead of Paradise and reward. — reasons: Al-Jishumī reports this from Abū Muslim.
2:16/q06 What do misguidance and guidance mean? [ٱلضَّلَٰلَةَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ · meaning] (turns on: Which paired states the terms denote.)
  2:16/q06/p1 Unbelief versus faith. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda and al-Suddī are widely cited; al-Qurṭubī cites 41:17.
  2:16/q06/p2 Doubt versus certainty. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān report it.
  2:16/q06/p3 Ignorance versus knowledge. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān report it.
  2:16/q06/p4 Division versus community, or insecurity versus safety. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān lists the pair; Qatāda adds movement from Sunnah to innovation.
  2:16/q06/p5 Worldly life versus afterlife, or Hell versus Paradise. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān lists these pairs; al-Qurṭubī contrasts the trades of Paradise's and Fire's people.
  2:16/q06/p6 Deviation, confusion or destruction, extended to religious error. — reasons: Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī define deviation; al-Qurṭubī calls confusion primary, citing 26:20, and destruction at 32:10. Al-Jishumī surveys other senses and warns against attributing immoral leading astray to God.
2:16/q07 How is the plural wāw of اشتروا read? [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · readings] (turns on: Canonical ḍamma versus rare vowel and hamza forms.)
  2:16/q07/p1 The canonical reading has ḍamma. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid, Aḥmad ibn Mūsā and al-Ṭabrisī report agreement; al-Jishumī rejects variants for lacking transmission. Al-Akhfash says the wāw preserves plurality.
  2:16/q07/p2 Some read kasra on the wāw. — reasons: Ibn Abī Isḥāq and Yaḥyā ibn Yaʿmar are named; it is the basic two-sukūn linker and analogizes لو. Al-Ālūsī reports kasra on the initial hamza.
  2:16/q07/p3 Abū al-Sammāl Qunb reads fatḥa on the wāw. — reasons: Abū Zayd transmits it; Ibn Jinnī calls fatḥa light but least common. Al-Jishumī fears confusion with dual marking.
  2:16/q07/p4 Some replace the wāw with hamza. — reasons: Al-Kisāʾī compares أقتت and أدؤر; al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, Abū ʿAlī and others object that the ḍamma is temporary.
  2:16/q07/p5 A clipped or lengthened pause pronunciation is reported. — reasons: Al-Samīn reports Abū al-Baqāʾ rejecting clipped vowel; Ibn Jinnī describes glides at a hesitant pause. Al-Kisāʾī's poem restores alif after incidental vowel.
2:16/q08 Why does the plural wāw take ḍamma in the common reading? [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · grammar] (turns on: Plural distinction versus transferred final-radical vowel.)
  2:16/q08/p1 It resolves two sukūns and marks the plural wāw distinctly. — reasons: Sībawayh, Abū ʿAlī and Ibn Jinnī distinguish root or conjunction wāw; al-Fārisī cites 3:186, 102:6 and 72:16. Al-Zajjāj compares نحن; Ibn Kaysān cites ease; al-Jishumī contrasts لو at 9:42.
  2:16/q08/p2 The ḍamma transfers from the lost final radical. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ proposes transfer; Abū ʿAlī rejects a forward transfer and observes movement only before a separate sukūn.
  2:16/q08/p3 Ḍamma follows from the wāw's meaning as subject. — reasons: Some grammarians say so; al-Wāḥidī objects that agentive yāʾ may take kasra and variant vowels are not inflection.
  2:16/q08/p4 Other vowels arise by analogy or ease. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī compares kasra with لو; al-Ṭabrisī reports Yaḥyā b. Waththāb's ḍamma on لو; Ibn Jinnī explains light fatḥa.
2:16/q09 How is the verb اشتروا formed? [ٱشْتَرَوُا۟ · grammar]
  2:16/q09/p1 The weak final yāʾ changes or drops before plural wāw and the next sukūn. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī derives ishtariyū; Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains vowel change and deletion; al-Samīn gives two routes.
2:16/q10 How is بالهدى read? [بِٱلْهُدَىٰ · readings] (turns on: Opening, intermediate sound and imāla among readers.)
  2:16/q10/p1 Nafiʿ generally uses an intermediate sound for yāʾ-final forms. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid and Abū ʿAlī cite Qālūn and Warsh; al-Musayyibī instead reports Nafiʿ opening all. Ibn Khalawayh explains the range of sounds.
  2:16/q10/p2 Ibn Kathīr opens the forms. — reasons: Ibn Mujāhid and Abū ʿAlī report it; al-Musayyibī reports the same for Nafiʿ.
  2:16/q10/p3 Abū ʿAmr uses intermediate imāla at verse endings and opening elsewhere. — reasons: Certain feminine forms remain intermediate. Abū ʿAlī discusses rāʾ before yāʾ and its disappearance when yāʾ drops; two transmitters differ.
  2:16/q10/p4 Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī incline hudā; others open it. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān connects imāla to Tamīm and opening to Quraysh; Ibn Khalawayh says Ḥamza distinguishes final yāʾ and wāw.
  2:16/q10/p5 ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr opens most forms but inclines named exceptions. — reasons: Abū ʿAlī names رأى, رمى, رآه and نأى; he gives junction examples at 17:83, 41:51, 17:72 and 6:77.
2:16/q11 What does the bāʾ in بالهدى mark? [بِٱلْهُدَىٰ · grammar]
