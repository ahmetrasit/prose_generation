ted by al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, Abu Yusuf, Muhammad, and al-Tahawi, and reported from Malik.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r4] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · أبو حنيفة | reports | Abu Hanifa holds that nursing which establishes the legal prohibition lasts thirty months, citing 46:15; he argues that two named periods each receive the full stated duration, by analogy with two debts due in one year or two obligations from one debtor. | mentions 46:15
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r5] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · عائشة | reports | Aisha, whose statement is treated as transmitted from the Prophet, says a child is never in the womb longer than two years, even by the time needed for a spindle's coil; thus gestation is shortened and the explicit nursing term remains, though the author says this needs discussion.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r6] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · النحاس | reports | Al-Nahhas takes an ishkur li walidayk as an explanatory clause interpreting God's command to care for parents.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r7] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | reports | Alternatively, it is a verbal noun with an implied li-taʿlil tied to the command, or a verbal noun in apposition to parents, meaning care for them by gratitude; mentioning gratitude to God comes first because gratitude to parents depends on it, but this last analysis is remote.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r8] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · author | holds | The objection that the imperative forbids explanation, rationale, and apposition is invalid; under all three parses, the account from pregnancy through weaning is a parenthetical emphasis on the mother's distinctive burden.
[ALUSI-FULL:v21p86/r9] ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 · رسول الله | reports | In a sound report from Bahz ibn Hakim through his father and grandfather, the Prophet names the mother three times when asked whom to honor before naming the father.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r18] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The parenthetical command to honor parents is a standalone divine statement inserted while recounting Luqman's counsel, reinforcing the ban on shirk.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r19] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | The verse, or at least its second verse, was reportedly revealed about Saʿd ibn Abi Waqqas and his mother.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r20] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | reports | Elmalılı reports that Saʿd refused to leave Islam when his mother threatened to starve herself; she eventually ate.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r21] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Wahn ʿalā wahn is a circumstantial description of the mother; wahn means weakness in movement, increasing weakness upon weakness.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r22] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The apparent meaning of weaning in two years sets the maximum nursing period at two years, as held by the two Imams and al-Shafiʿi.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r23] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · İmâm-ı A‘zam | reports | Abu Hanifa instead held a thirty-month nursing period as a precaution, using 46:15, though the fatwa follows the two Imams. | mentions 46:15
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r24] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | prefers | Although Abu Hanifa held thirty months, the fatwa follows the position of the two Imams.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r25] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | The phrase "that you give thanks" explains the instruction to humanity: God enjoined gratitude to Him and to one's parents.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r26] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · Resûl-i Ekrem | reports | When asked whom one should treat well, the Prophet answered: one's mother three times, then one's father.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r27] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Gratitude to parents means honoring their rights through obedience, kindness, service, and prayer; the topic is also explained in Surat al-Isra.
[ELMALILI:31:12-14/r28] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Since the return is to God, He will question people about whether they gave thanks.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v21p152/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Gratitude benefits the grateful person, not God: its fruits perfect the worshipper, while God needs no thanks; the conditional and exclusive wording stresses this relation.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v21p152/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Turning from gratitude is ingratitude for a blessing, but God is self-sufficient and praiseworthy; human benefactors may gain reciprocal benefits from gratitude, unlike God.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v21p152/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | God is praised by all creation, even the unbeliever's condition, as 13:15 says. | mentions 13:15
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v21p152/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | أن اشكر لله condenses Luqman's core wisdom and begins the requested gratitude immediately upon receiving a blessing; الحمد رأس الشكر is cited, and حميد is used because God has no name from the gratitude root except الشكور.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v21p152/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The present tense in يشكر signals renewed gratitude, and the لام in اشكر لي is a strengthening marker conventionally attached to the verb شكر, as in 2:152. | mentions 2:152
[TABATABAI:31:12-14/r9] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | The divine instruction about parents interrupts Luqman's quoted speech to establish that gratitude to parents is obligatory like gratitude to God and is itself part of obeying God.
[TABATABAI:31:14-15/r1] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Mentioning the mother's hardship in carrying and raising the child should prompt gratitude to both parents, especially the mother.
[TABATABAI:31:14-15/r2] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Wahn means weakness and is either circumstantial or an emphatic cognate object; fiṣāl is weaning, and its completion by two years means nursing lasts two full years.
[TABATABAI:31:14-15/r3] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | Combining two years of nursing with the thirty-month pregnancy-and-weaning period in 46:15 leaves six months as the minimum gestation. | mentions 46:15
[TABATABAI:31:14-15/r4] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · author | holds | “Be grateful to Me and your parents” explains the earlier command to honor them, and the return to God warns and reinforces the duty; the shift in person matches verse 31:12. | mentions 31:12
[ASAD-NOTES:31:14#n14/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Fisāl literally describes weaning that takes place within two years.
[ASAD-NOTES:31:14#n14/r2] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · philologists | reports | Some philologists use fisāl for the whole period of conception, gestation, birth, and earliest infancy, when a child is wholly dependent on its mother.
[ASAD-NOTES:31:14#n15/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · author | holds | Gratitude to parents accompanies gratitude to God because parents enable one’s birth while God is the ultimate cause and source of existence. | mentions 17:23, 17:24
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:31:14/r3] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The weaning phrase indicates that two years is the normal nursing period but need not be completed; parents may wean earlier. | mentions 2:233
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:31:14/r4] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Râzî; Şevkânî | reports | Gratitude to God and parents is jointly commanded because God creates and sustains, while parents enable birth and care for, raise, feed, and educate a child.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:31:14/r5] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · author | holds | The verse specially stresses the mother's sacrifices, indirectly indicating her greater need for attention and affection.
[KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:31:14/r6] KURANYOLU-TEFSIR · Hz. Peygamber | reports | Asked who most deserves kindness, the Prophet answered 'your mother' three times before answering 'your father' on the fourth.
[QIRAAT-ER:31:14:15#1/r1] QIRAAT-ER · author | reports | The final nūn changes from kasra to ḍamma in anu, described as phonetic assimilation before the imperative.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p101/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The words ‘We enjoined’ are God’s speech, an interjection in Luqman’s testament before his counsel resumes, rather than Luqman speaking.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p101/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | God takes over the injunction to honor parents because their standing before Him is so great.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p1014/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Thirty months combines two years of nursing with a minimum six-month pregnancy; Samarrāʾī says some jurists derive the minimum pregnancy term from these verses.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p103/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Both الوالدان and الأبوان designate mother and father through Arabic masculine-gender predominance, as in calling the sun and moon القمران.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p103/r2] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | He says الوالدين suits the surrounding reference to pregnancy and weaning: the verses describe the mother, and the term evokes الولادة, which is the mother's role.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p103/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Mentioning the parents recalls that a child is helpless and weak at birth and would perish without them.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p103/r4] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The wording signals that kindness and companionship should favor the mother over the father because childbirth is the mother's responsibility.
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p103/r5] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | The Qur'an's commands and recommendations about goodness, prayer, and care speak of الوالدين, not الأبوين; he illustrates this with 17:23, 4:36, and 6:151. | mentions 17:23, 4:36, 6:151
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p104/r1] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Samarrāʾī says Qur'anic commands to honor parents and prayers for them use walidayn, unlike abawayn in inheritance, hinting that the mother's claim should be greater. | mentions 14:41, 71:28, 29:8, 46:15
[SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p104/r3] SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT · author | holds | Because Luqmān 31:14 mentions the mother's burden but not the father, Samarrāʾī considers walidayn the most fitting term here.
