14:34/q10 Why use a conditional when failure to count is certain? [وَإِن تَعُدُّوا۟ · rhetoric]
  14:34/q10/p1 The conditional addresses the human impression that counting might be possible. — reasons: Alusi says the details of even one blessing defeat the attempt.
14:34/q11 Who is الإنسان? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the definite generic names all humanity, all unbelievers, idolaters specifically, or Abu Jahl.)
  14:34/q11/p1 It denotes humanity as a species, with wronging and ingratitude appearing in different people or circumstances. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya allows a denier and sinful believer to exemplify different forms; Nasafi applies it wherever traits occur. Razi and Kashshaf treat complaint and hoarding as species behavior. Umar applies the verse to himself.
  14:34/q11/p2 It is grammatically generic but intends disbelievers or idolaters. — reasons: Zajjaj compares the exception for believers at 103:2–3; Tabrisi similarly cites al-Asr. Ibn Ashur compares 19:66 and says idolaters worship powerless things. Islahi says the general wording avoids direct address to the unworthy idolaters.
  14:34/q11/p3 It refers to Abu Jahl. — reasons: Ibn Abbas is cited by Ibn al-Jawzi and Qurtubi.
14:34/q12 What do ظلوم and كفار say about a person? [لَظَلُومٌۭ كَفَّارٌۭ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the wrong is directed at self, God, blessings, or gratitude's rightful recipient, and whether hardship and ease distinguish the traits.)
  14:34/q12/p1 A person wrongs himself by disobeying or withholding thanks, and denies or misuses God's favors. — reasons: Muqatil links sins and failure to worship; Tabari adds redirecting worship. Tabrisi says both adjectives are intensive; Ibn Ashur connects their intensity to abundant favors. Islahi calls polytheism a violation of God's and the soul's rights. Alusi describes self-imposed deprivation.
  14:34/q12/p2 The injustice is complaint or despair in hardship; ingratitude is hoarding and withholding in prosperity. — reasons: Thalabi, Jishumi, Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit the contrast.
  14:34/q12/p3 Injustice misdirects gratitude to someone other than the benefactor; ingratitude denies the benefactor. — reasons: Thalabi and Baghawi distinguish misplaced thanks from denial; Jishumi names attribution to another originator; Islahi glosses kaffar as thanking someone else.
  14:34/q12/p4 Inwardly, a person ruins innate preparedness by turning to perishing nature and is veiled from grace. — reasons: Qashani and Bursawi describe the loss of potential; Sulami and Alusi add the error of imagining human gratitude can repay God.
14:34/q13 Why does the verse close with human injustice and ingratitude? [إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَظَلُومٌۭ كَفَّارٌۭ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the clause explains failed gratitude, the preceding rebuke, or the infinity of favors.)
  14:34/q13/p1 It explains why humans fail to honor favors and reinforces the earlier rebuke of exchanging blessings for disbelief. — reasons: Abu Hayyan connects 14:28–30 and setting up rivals; Samin concurs. Alusi prefers it as a fresh explanatory statement and rejects taking it as the reason favors are infinite. Ibn Ashur calls the close a rebuke of idolaters.
  14:34/q13/p2 It underscores the abundance of blessings and the human inability to thank or notice them. — reasons: Razi says people tire, forget and overlook other favors while reflecting on some; Tabataba'i links the explanation to heedlessness.
14:34/q14 How does this ending relate to the ending of 16:18? [إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَظَلُومٌۭ كَفَّارٌۭ · links] (turns on: Whether the two endings describe recipient and giver in their contexts, or one replaces the other.)
  14:34/q14/p1 Here the recipient's injustice is foregrounded, while 16:18 names God's forgiveness and mercy as giver. — reasons: Razi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn al-Munir, Samin, Biqai and Itqan contrast human failure with continuing divine generosity; Ibrahim follows disbelief and rivals, while al-Nahl details favors, divine attributes and the postponement of punishment. Alusi also reports that expanded favors make mercy apt and invite repentance.
  14:34/q14/p2 The Ibrahim ending was abrogated by the al-Nahl ending. — reasons: Ibn Zayd's claim is transmitted by al-Sakhawi; Abu Hayyan dismisses it, and Alusi says it deserves no attention.
14:34/q15 What place does this verse have in its surrounding passage and sūra? [* · links]
  14:34/q15/p1 It completes the proofs of God's sole benefaction and calls people away from rivals to worship, thanks, and obedience. — reasons: Razi counts ten signs in creation, rain, provisions, ships, rivers, sun, moon and answered requests after the saved and damned. Tabrisi says the Creator alone deserves worship; Biqai links 14:2 and Judgment Day; Islahi sees 14:28–34 warning Quraysh leaders and urging Muslims to prayer and charity. Study Quran names gratitude versus ingratitude as a sūra theme.
14:34/q16 What practice follows from recognizing these gifts? [* · law]
  14:34/q16/p1 People should worship and obey God, repent, pray, give alms, and thank the true benefactor. — reasons: Jishumi explicitly draws prayer and alms; Tabari, Qurtubi and Tabrisi link gifts to exclusive worship; Talq urges repentance morning and evening. Bursawi quotes a Persian couplet calling gratitude the key to increased blessing.
14:34/q17 What inward meaning is drawn from divine gifts and human asking? [* · inward]
  14:34/q17/p1 God's prior gifts and a person's readiness lead toward knowledge, obedience and closeness; turning away corrupts that readiness. — reasons: Yahya ibn Mu'adh prizes unasked unity and says absorption in God opens the gnostic station. Qushayri names forgiveness before asking and gnosis before existence; Qashani, Bursawi and Alusi explain requests through preparedness. Bursawi reads heavens as hearts, earth as souls, rain as wisdom and fruits as obedient acts.
14:34/q18 What reports illustrate gratitude or the scale of blessings? [* · reports]
  14:34/q18/p1 Reports and sayings portray gratitude as unable to repay even one favor, while recognition of dependence itself counts as thanks. — reasons: Ibn Kathir reports a Bukhari praise from Abu Hurayra and a rare weak-chain three-ledger report from Anas. David is told breath is the least gift; Wahb's ascetic's fifty years equal one night's relief. Shafi'i says thanks needs new thanks; al-Sajjad recognizes inability as thanks; Umar applies the verse to himself. The Prophet's reported sermon urges God-consciousness, worship, remembrance and fidelity.
  14:34/q18/p2 A person who counts only food and drink as God's favors misunderstands their scope. — reasons: Abu al-Darda is transmitted by Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi; he warns of scant knowledge and punishment.
14:34/q19 What textual and performance details are attached to this verse? [* · readings]
  14:34/q19/p1 Niʿma is usually written with closed tāʾ; its spelling in Ibrahim is among eleven extended forms. — reasons: Burhan ties the extended form here to an actual worldly favor and the human-centered ending.
  14:34/q19/p2 In idghām, plain assimilation is the received basis; rawm and ishmām are instructional variants with articulation limits. — reasons: Al-Nashr says rawm voices part of a vowel and so differs from full idghām; al-Dani and most authorities mention both signals. Lip closure restricts mīm cases, and authorities differ on fāʾ before fāʾ.
14:34/q20 What other linguistic examples accompany the discussion? [* · grammar]
  14:34/q20/p1 Sibawayh describes the shifted feminine فعال pattern and its names, commands, nouns, and dialectal declension. — reasons: He says commands keep one form across persons and numbers; poetry supplies مناع, تراك, حذار, نظار, نزال, نعاء. Hyena names and death's حلاق illustrate lexical shifts. He contrasts productive triliteral vocative and command forms with attested quadriliterals; Tamim declines feminine proper names differently from Hijaz, and al-Khalil comments on imāla.
  14:34/q20/p2 A poetic saying values meeting a need before it is explicitly asked. — reasons: Al-Asma'iyyat cites Suhayr ibn Umayr's line; it echoes the verse's request wording.
  14:34/q20/p3 The form فعول can be emphatic, as in ظلوم, شكور and ودود. — reasons: Burhan says شكور is more emphatic than شاكر.
  14:34/q20/p4 The preceding creation and provision clause admits several grammatical parses. — reasons: Kashshaf takes God as subject and 'who created' as predicate; min al-thamarat clarifies provision or is the object of 'brought forth,' with rizqan circumstantial or a verbal noun.
14:34/q21 What principles of expression and interpretation are discussed in material attached to the verse? [* · rhetoric]
  14:34/q21/p1 Subtle language requires thought beyond general vocabulary knowledge or memorization. — reasons: Jurjani analyzes a couplet's ordered comparison of distant high moon and emphatic nearness, the words أفرط, شاسع and جد قريب, and quotes the camel saying and Ibn al-Rumi's satire of an unappreciated poet.
