[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | prefers | Qurtubi’s preferred account is that deeds written in records are placed in the balance’s pans, as the report of the shahada card demonstrates.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · صفوان بن محرز | reports | A Sahih Muslim narration from Safwan preserves Ibn Umar’s report of the Prophet’s conversation with God about the believer’s record.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The verse means deeds are weighed on the balance; Ibn Umar says the records of people’s deeds are weighed, which Qurtubi calls correct based on transmitted reports.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | reports | Another view says the balance is the book in which people’s deeds are written.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid says the good and evil deeds themselves are weighed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مجاهد والضحاك والأعمش | reports | Mujahid, al-Dahhak, and al-A‘mash take weighing and balance as metaphors for justice and judgment, as ordinary speech uses weight to mean equivalence.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الزجاج | reports | Al-Zajjaj accepts the metaphor as linguistically sound, but Qurtubi prefers the sound reports affirming a real balance.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · القشيري | reports | Al-Qushayri argues that metaphorizing the balance would also invite metaphorizing the Path, Paradise and Fire, jinn, and angels; the early community accepted these descriptions literally.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · المعتزلة | reports | The Mu‘tazilites deny the balance because accidents cannot be weighed and do not subsist independently.
[QURTUBI-FULL:7:8-9/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · بعض المتكلمين | reports | Some theologians say God turns accidents into bodies for weighing; Qurtubi rejects this and holds that written records make the scales heavier or lighter.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:7:8/r1] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Al-wazn may mean judgment or the weighing of deeds by comparison with their recompense.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:7:8/r2] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | Most scholars hold that records of deeds are weighed on a balance with a tongue and two pans, publicly demonstrating justice and removing excuses, as people's deeds are also elicited and acknowledged by speech and limbs.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:7:8/r3] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | reports | A report describes a man given ninety-nine scrolls, each as far as the eye can reach, and a card bearing the two testimonies; the card outweighs the scrolls.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:7:8/r4] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم | reports | Another view weighs people themselves, supported by the prophetic report that a huge, heavy man will weigh no more than a gnat's wing with God.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:7:8/r5] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Al-haqq is either an adjective describing the weighing or the predicate of an omitted subject; it means just and sound.
[BAYDAWI-FULL:7:8/r6] BAYDAWI-FULL, d. 685 · author | holds | Heavy scales mean good deeds or what weighs them; the plural reflects differing weighed things or multiple weighings, and the successful are those saved and rewarded.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p4#3/r1] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | The weighing is the weighing and distinguishing of deeds by relative heaviness; يومئذ means the day God questions nations and messengers, with the clause omitted and replaced by nunation.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p4#3/r2] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | reports | One view says records of deeds will be weighed on a scale with a tongue and two pans, manifesting the attribute and ending excuses; another takes the phrase as just judgment, while God knows its manner.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p4#3/r3] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Mawāzīn can mean scales or weighed deeds; what has weight and worth is good deeds or the measure used for them.
[NASAFI-FULL:v2p4#3/r4] NASAFI-FULL, d. 710 · author | holds | Those whose balances are heavy are the successful, meaning victorious.
[QASHANI:7:8-9/r1] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | Weighing is assessment of deeds at the lesser resurrection and is just or real; lasting good deeds weigh heavily and yield the heart's paradise of divine attributes.
[QASHANI:7:8-9/r2] QASHANI, d. 736 · author | holds | The scale's tongue is justice, with sense and intellect as its pans; enduring rational acquisitions, virtues, good works, and sincere intentions have weight, unlike transient sensations, desires, and vices.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r10] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الحسن | reports | Hasan holds that every person has a separate balance on the Day of Resurrection.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r11] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Balances can also stand for good deeds by ellipsis: the balance-pan of a person’s weighed deeds becomes heavy or light.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r12] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Grammatically, weighing is the subject and the time expression supplies its predicate, with the sense that the weighing occurs when God questions people and recounts their deeds on Judgment Day.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r13] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | The word true may qualify the weighing, or the time phrase may modify weighing and true may be its predicate.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Exegetes disagree whether the weighing is a real balance or a figure for complete justice, sound judgment, and exact reckoning.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r5] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · المعتزلة | reports | The Muʿtazila, preceded in this view by Mujahid, al-Dahhak, and al-Aʿmash, deny a literal balance and take heaviness and lightness as abundance or scarcity of good deeds.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · جمهور الأمة | reports | The majority affirm a real balance with a shaft, two pans, and a tongue, shown to all creatures to establish proof, justice, and the end of excuses.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | The visible balance confirms the judgment alongside questioning, people’s admissions, testimony by limbs, prophets, angels, and witnesses.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r8] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Reports say the weighed objects are the records on which deeds were written; God creates heaviness or lightness in them, while reports about the balance’s dimensions and form have unsound chains.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v5p13/r9] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · جمهور الأمة | reports | The majority explain plural balances by the multiplicity of objects weighed, although there is one balance.

[SAMIN-DURR:7:8/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الجمهور | reports | The majority treat the tanwin in يومئذ as compensation for the omitted genitive clause after إذ; al-Akhfash disagrees.
