## THE CURRENT MAP
104:2/q01 Who is the one described by ٱلَّذِى? [ٱلَّذِى · referent] (turns on: Whether named occasion reports identify the subject or the description applies to a recurring type; Quṭb doubts reports naming individuals.)
  104:2/q01/p1 The same slanderer and mocker condemned in the preceding verse, represented by each individual in that class. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the singular relative describes each individual; the later plural points to the group. Quṭb calls it a recurring type from the early mission. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls him the slanderer.
  104:2/q01/p2 A specific wealthy mocker associated with an early incident, identified in some reports as al-Walīd or al-Akhnas. — reasons: Muqātil connects him to 74:11–13, his Meccan and Taif gardens and seven sons; al-Ālūsī cites reports of al-Akhnas's fortune as four or ten thousand dinars. Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions reports that known Muslim defamers occasioned the warning.
104:2/q02 How is ٱلَّذِى connected grammatically to the preceding verse? [ٱلَّذِى · grammar] (turns on: Case and whether definiteness allows an adjectival link to kull; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī challenge the adjective analysis.)
  104:2/q02/p1 It is a genitive appositive to kull or the preceding condemned person. — reasons: Al-Nahhās, al-Tabrisī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give this parsing. Al-Samīn rejects treating it as an adjective or explanatory apposition because definiteness differs. Bursawī also denies adjective agreement.
  104:2/q02/p2 It is accusative by an implied expression of censure or clarification. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās says 'I mean'; al-Kashshāf and others call it accusative censure.
  104:2/q02/p3 It is nominative with an implied subject or as a disjoined expression of blame. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās supplies 'he is'; al-Samīn calls it disjunction; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī call it nominative dispraise.
  104:2/q02/p4 It is a partitive appositive to kull. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī reports this as an alternative to full apposition.
  104:2/q02/p5 It is another adjective describing the same slanderer. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the unlinked descriptions in 68:10–13. Al-Tabrisī mentions a codex variant; al-Jarbardī appeals to kull's definiteness and 50:21.
104:2/q03 What does the root of جَمَعَ convey? [جَمَعَ · meaning]
  104:2/q03/p1 It brings dispersed or varied things together, evoking massing, mingling, or assembly. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys eighteen simple-verb occurrences and notes no Qur'anic derivative of its doubled form; thirteen concern God gathering people, bones or humanity for Judgment. She cites prayer, the Day of Gathering, goods, troops, weapons, and 4:23.
104:2/q04 How is جَمَعَ read and what does each form imply? [جَمَعَ · readings] (turns on: Whether doubling adds sustained or abundant collection; the light form can also carry these senses, and reader attributions differ.)
  104:2/q04/p1 Jamaʿa has a light mīm; it means gathering and can itself describe either abundant or successive collection. — reasons: Read by ʿĀṣim, Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr and others, though lists vary. Abū ʿAmr associates it with much gathered; Abū ʿAlī permits itemwise gathering, supported by al-Aʿshā's equipment verse and 18:99. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Samīn allow little or much; al-Biqāʿī says collection itself suggests abundance. Ibn ʿĀshūr says intensive counting implies further amassing.
  104:2/q04/p2 Jammaʿa has a doubled mīm; it intensifies gathering, often as repeated collection from different places or item by item. — reasons: Reader lists include Abū Jaʿfar, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī, with variants involving al-Ḥasan, Khalaf, Rūḥ or Ruways. Abū al-Ḥasan considers it apt for gathering from place to place; Abū ʿAmr distinguishes itemwise accumulation. Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī and others note its formal match with ʿaddadahu; Abū ʿUbayd prefers it for that reason. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both regional forms as close in meaning.
104:2/q05 What counts as مَالًۭا here? [مَالًۭا · meaning] (turns on: Whether the word is being defined by general usage or the man's reported holdings.)
  104:2/q05/p1 Acquired resources for needs, with the familiar form varying by livelihood: camels, gardens and palms, or trade money. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Zuhayr on camels as blood money, Abū Hurayra and Abū Ṭalḥa on Ansārī gardens and palms, the Prophet's question to al-ʿAbbās on Meccan cash, and earlier discussion at 3:92.
  104:2/q05/p2 The man’s possessions include gold, silver, lands, gardens, slaves, servants and livestock. — reasons: Muqātil's narrative lists the inventoried property, preparation to purchase servants and livestock, and gardens in Mecca and Taif bearing fruit in winter and summer.
104:2/q06 Why is مَالًۭا indefinite? [مَالًۭا · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether indefiniteness enlarges the imagined amount or diminishes its worth.)
  104:2/q06/p1 It magnifies the amount or the wealth's corrupt, vile character. — reasons: Al-Rāzī gives the latter account; al-Ālūsī gives magnification and cites competing reports that al-Akhnas had four or ten thousand dinars.
  104:2/q06/p2 It belittles an individual's wealth as trivial before God or beside all worldly wealth. — reasons: Al-Rāzī invokes 18:46 and the whole world's wealth; al-Ālūsī calls it contemptible in God's sight; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it serves only brief bodily needs.
104:2/q07 How is وَعَدَّدَهُۥ read? [وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · readings] (turns on: Regional reading consensus and the written two dāls versus reports of lightening and whether the light form is a noun or exceptional verb.)
  104:2/q07/p1 The established or majority reading doubles the dāl, ʿaddadahu. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says all regional readers agree and rejects a weak early alternative for opposing that consensus. Al-Biqāʿī says undoubled counting can mean only a few things; doubled form emphasizes repetition or abundance. Al-Bayḍāwī notes a separated doubling as support.
  104:2/q07/p2 A minority reading lightens the dāl; it can be parsed as a noun for number or an unusual separated verb meaning counted. — reasons: Attributed variously to al-Ḥasan, al-Kalbī, Naṣr ibn ʿĀṣim, Abū al-ʿĀliya, Ibn Yaʿmar and al-Sulamī. Al-Ṭabarī rejects its transmission; al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī call verbal lightening remote or strained. Poetry with ḍannanū supports poetic license; al-Mahdawī instead parses a coordinated noun, avoiding irregularity.
104:2/q08 What does وَعَدَّدَهُۥ mean? [وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · meaning] (turns on: Whether ʿaddadahu is intensive counting, provision from ʿuddah, increase in amount or kinds, or a coordinated noun for supporters; some explanations add heirs or boasting.)
  104:2/q08/p1 He counted and repeatedly inventoried his wealth, often guarding the amount from decrease. — reasons: Al-Akhfash derives it from ʿidda; al-Farrāʾ, al-Suddī and many others gloss tallying. Mujāhid specifies types; ʿIkrima counts enough for years. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says guarding the sum prevented charity. Al-Suddī pictures saying 'this is mine' by day and hiding it at night; Ibn Kathīr cites 70:18. Al-Ālūsī says love drove repetition; al-Iṣlāḥī adds business forecasts and investment calculations.
  104:2/q08/p2 He stored or prepared wealth as a reserve for calamities or future need. — reasons: Muqātil invokes provision and storage; al-Naẓm derives ʿaddadahu from ʿuddah. Al-Baghawī cites Arabic aʿdadtu and ʿadadtuhu for keeping back. Al-Jubbāʾī adds unlawful acquisition and unpaid dues. Al-ʿĀṣim's rendering via al-Jawharī is 'counts it a safeguard.'
  104:2/q08/p3 He multiplied the quantity or kinds of wealth. — reasons: Al-Naẓm connects ʿadad/ʿadīd with largeness; al-Rāzī analogizes a numerous clan. Al-Ālūsī specifies real estate, goods and coins; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 3:14. Al-Wāḥidī also presents great quantity.
  104:2/q08/p4 He gathered a numerous clan, followers, or supporters alongside wealth. — reasons: The light dāl can be a noun coordinated with māl; al-Kashshāf says a person of ʿadad has helpers and resources. Al-Rāzī connects this to pride. Al-Mahdawī prefers the noun analysis over poetic verbal de-gemination; al-Samīn identifies relatives. Al-Farrāʾ's gloss of the light reading includes clan.
  104:2/q08/p5 He prepared the wealth for his children to inherit. — reasons: Al-Māwardī gives this possibility; al-Qurṭubī attributes it to al-Ḍaḥḥāk.
  104:2/q08/p6 He boasted about the amount he owned. — reasons: Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī report this alternative; al-Iṣlāḥī describes calculation among flatterers.
104:2/q09 What is the force of counting the wealth? [وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether attention falls on the hoarder's attachment and fear of loss or on the error of treating wealth as protection.)
  104:2/q09/p1 Repeated counting shows possessive love, obsessive calculation, and fear that the hoard will diminish. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he guards the sum and withholds charity; al-Suddī depicts daytime preoccupation and nighttime concealment; Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb depicts daylong piling and corpse-like sleep. Al-Ālūsī, Quṭb, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Iṣlāḥī stress pleasure or anxious calculation.
  104:2/q09/p2 Counting signals ignorance: wealth cannot protect against the calamities that divine wisdom uses to disperse it. — reasons: Al-Qāshānī and Bursawī connect counting with ignorance; Bursawī adds that accumulating reflects appetitive power.
104:2/q10 What does this description condemn? [ٱلَّذِى جَمَعَ مَالًۭا وَعَدَّدَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Which moral consequence of accumulation is foregrounded: unpaid duties, social arrogance, false security, or the warning's response.)
  104:2/q10/p1 Hoarding wealth, withholding its due and refusing charitable spending or obedience. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says he fails to spend in God's path or pay God's due. Al-Qurṭubī invokes 50:25 or 68:12 and 70:18; Ibn Kathīr invokes 70:18. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the stingy mock people pressing God's and others' rights and compares 9:79's mocker
