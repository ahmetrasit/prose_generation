  83:4/q14/p1 The people stand for three hundred worldly years without an order. — reasons: Rāzī transmits this as a Prophetic report.
  83:4/q14/p2 They stand for forty years and then are addressed. — reasons: Rāzī transmits this from Ibn Masʿūd.
  83:4/q14/p3 For believers the standing lasts about the time needed to finish a prayer. — reasons: Rāzī transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās; it explicitly concerns believers.

  83:4/q16/p1 He wept so intensely that he could not continue reciting. — reasons: Thalabī adds that he fell; Rāzī also reports the weeping and inability to continue.
83:4/q17 Where are the opening verses placed in revelation? [* · sciences] (turns on: Whether the passage or the whole sūrah is dated to Medina, Mecca, or both.)
  83:4/q17/p1 The opening warning has a Medinan setting, with one account placing the first four verses just after the Prophet arrived. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya infers a Medinan setting involving believers from the resurrection rebuke. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the address as to Muslims, distinguishing sincere Muslims from hypocrites. Asad cites traditions mentioned by Ṭabarī, Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr for the first four verses soon after arrival. Study Quran reports an account putting verses 5–28 in Medina and the last eight among the last Makkan revelations.
  83:4/q17/p2 The sūrah may be late Makkan, early Medinan, or combine both periods. — reasons: Study Quran states these alternatives without choosing among them.
83:4/q18 How does this warning connect with earlier passages? [* · links]
  83:4/q18/p1 It develops al-Infiṭār's theme of moral accounting and the division of righteous and defiant people. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī says al-Infiṭār reasons from divine attributes shown in the human body, while this sūrah reasons from human moral nature. Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by Biqāʿī, links fraud's warning to the record of every deed in 82:10 and the exact weighing of deeds in 21:47: small fraud is a major crime.
83:4/q19 What inward application is drawn from the warning? [* · inward] (turns on: The two inward readings apply the warning to different hidden realities: unveiled character at judgment or the self's relation to the spirit.)
  83:4/q19/p1 At judgment hidden virtues and vices become visible, exposing any feigned goodness. — reasons: Qāshānī says the failure to expect resurrection leaves people unprepared for this unveiling and its shame and consequence.
  83:4/q19/p2 The limited self shortchanges the spirit in essential monotheism, though formal monotheism is its due. — reasons: Bursevī says essential tawḥīd has no counterpart and should not be weighed; the self cannot fully provide it because of its limited capacity.
83:4/q20 What does the surrounding rebuke particle كلا do? [* · rhetoric]
  83:4/q20/p1 It rebukes the cheats for their conduct. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān explicitly gives this function.
83:4/q21 What force does the cited Qur'anic no-oath formula have? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether lā cancels the oath or denies the need to swear.)
  83:4/q21/p1 It denies God's need to swear and thereby confirms the message through its evident context. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares ordinary Arabic 'do not swear', 'I need no oath', and 'I need not urge you', which strengthen assurance or instruction. She says her study retains the early generations' exegetical service as a resource for later generations.
  83:4/q21/p2 The lā negates the oath itself. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this interpretation from some scholars and rejects it in favor of denying the need for an oath.
