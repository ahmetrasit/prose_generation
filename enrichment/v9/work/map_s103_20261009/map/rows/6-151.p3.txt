[RAZI-FULL:v13p189#2/r4] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The apparent problem is that the list under what God prohibited includes avoiding shirk and doing good to parents, which are obligations rather than prohibitions.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p189#2/r5] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | One answer understands taḥrīm as God’s clearly fixed and delineated prescription, removing the objection about obligatory acts in the list.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p189#2/r6] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A second answer changes the clause boundary: the sentence may end at recite what God prohibited, after which a new clause begins with you must not associate, or it may end after prohibited you and begin with ألا تشركوا as lialla.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p189#2/r7] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | A third answer takes أن as explanatory, effectively saying: recite what God prohibited, namely, do not associate anything with Him.
[RAZI-FULL:v13p189#2/r8] RAZI-FULL, d. 606 · author | reports | The excerpt then raises an unresolved objection: because kindness to parents is coordinated with the explanatory prohibition of shirk, the same reading appears to make parental kindness forbidden.
