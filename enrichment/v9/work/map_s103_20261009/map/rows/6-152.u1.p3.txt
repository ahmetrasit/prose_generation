6:152/q36 How is tadhakkarun read? [تَذَكَّرُونَ · readings] (turns on: Assimilation of the second ta into dhal versus deletion of a ta for ease; reports differ over the deleted ta and related readings elsewhere.)
  6:152/q36/p1 It is read with a doubled dhal through assimilation of the underlying tatadhakkarun. — reasons: Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr double related forms throughout; Nafi, Abu Bakr from Asim, and Ibn Amir generally do so but lighten 19:67. Ibn Ashur adds Abu Jafar and Yaqub; Farisi and Ibn Mujahid detail further differences at 25:62, 17:41, 25:50, and 74:56, where all lighten and Nafi has ta versus others' ya. Qiraat-ER says the heavier sound may echo active remembrance; meaning stays the same.
  6:152/q36/p2 It is read with a light dhal by deleting one ta from tatadhakkarun. — reasons: Hamza, Kisai, Hafs, and in later listing Khalaf use the light form; traditions disagree over which ta is omitted. Farisi and Ibn Mujahid distinguish ya and ta forms and exceptions in Furqan and Isra. Wahidi considers deletion possibly lighter; the sense does not change.
6:152/q37 What are the form and sense of the remembrance verb? [تَذَكَّرُونَ · grammar]
  6:152/q37/p1 It is a responsive or reflexive form meaning repeated recollection or taking heed. — reasons: Wahidi calls tadhakkur repeated remembering; Farisi reports Sibawayh that simple dhakara takes one object, doubled dhakkara two, and Form V is its responsive form, citing Qur'an and poetry. Tabrisī likewise notes dhikr and dhakar as equivalent, with a poetic illustration. Farisi also surveys iddikar, dhikra, the plural al-dhikr, assimilated dal pronunciation, and transitive Form VIII, citing 73:8, 12:45, 54:15, 11:54; he compares grateful recollection in 8:26 and 7:86.
6:152/q38 How are these commands connected to the surrounding passage? [* · links] (turns on: Whether the connected sequence is explained chiefly as public duties or as a scheme of inward virtues.)
  6:152/q38/p1 They form the second group of linked divine duties, dealing especially with interpersonal, economic, and civic order. — reasons: Razi counts five clearer duties in the previous verse and four subtler here. Biqai says three groups each close with a testament and progress from reason to remembrance to taqwa; Ibn Ashur describes social, interpersonal, and straight-path sections, each closed by a repeated charge. Islahi ties them to Abraham's religion against Quraysh's invented food taboos and compares 17:22-38 and 16:90; Kuran Yolu counts nine main commands and a final encompassing straight-path duty. Kashshaf and Islahi say positive and negative forms imply their opposites. Ibn Ashur begins the second group with vulnerable property and mutual trust; Islahi says justice marks Abrahamic and Islamic conduct.
  6:152/q38/p2 The sequence is an inward scheme of vices, virtues, and the comprehensive virtue of justice. — reasons: Qashani says three vice classes entail injustice, while virtues entail justice; four listed vices are followed by four corresponding virtues. He calls the balanced middle difficult and encompassing all vice and virtue. Bursevi reads lifetime and divine rights as inward measure and scale, supported by a Persian verse about each person's wings and strength.
6:152/q39 Are these commandments universal and abrogated? [* · sciences]
  6:152/q39/p1 They are clear, universal commands preserved across scriptures and never abrogated. — reasons: Ibn Abbas calls them Mother of the Book, with Paradise for practitioners and Hell for abandoners; Baghawi, Thalabi, Tabrisī, Alusi, Qashani, and Bursevi transmit or affirm this. Qashani says scriptural communities agree. Itqan places three clear verses at qul ta'alaw, and another report adds three at 17:23.
6:152/q40 Where were these verses revealed? [* · sciences]
  6:152/q40/p1 These three verses were revealed in Medina although al-An'am otherwise came in Mecca. — reasons: Durr transmits Ibn Abbas and Shahr ibn Hawshab; Shahr says the sura descended all at once accompanied by ranks of angels, with 6:151-153 excepted.
