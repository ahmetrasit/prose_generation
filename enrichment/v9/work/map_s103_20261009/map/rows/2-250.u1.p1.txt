2:250/q18 What practice does this prayer teach believers? [* · interpretation]
  2:250/q18/p1 Believers should ask God for perseverance and aid, relying on Him in all affairs. — reasons: Al-Jishumi says seeking God's help and the ability to persevere is praiseworthy and that recounting earlier believers teaches later believers to rely on God. Al-Razi says the Prophet regularly prayed for help in battle, including at Badr for God's promise and for protection from enemy harm; al-Qurtubi recounts his battle prayer and urgent Badr supplication until his cloak fell.
2:250/q19 How does this prayer connect with the surrounding account and other verses? [* · links]
  2:250/q19/p1 It echoes earlier believers' battle prayer and anticipates God's help to the steadfast. — reasons: Al-Razi and al-Qurtubi cite 3:146–147. Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Hasan's gloss of 'by God's permission' as God's help and says God's being with the steadfast means victory and assistance; al-Jishumi compares angelic help at Badr in 3:123.
2:250/q20 What response to the prayer is understood before the next verse's victory? [* · rhetoric]
  2:250/q20/p1 An unstated divine answer granted patience and victory, after which they routed the enemy by God's leave and David killed Jalut. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi supplies the implied response linking the supplication to the following narrative.
2:250/q21 Why is the alif omitted in the written form of 'barazū' here? [بَرَزُوا۟ · rhetoric]
  2:250/q21/p1 Its omission portrays the encounter as spiritual and elevated, while the spelling at David's physical killing of Jalut retains alif. — reasons: Al-Burhan compares 2:251 and adduces parallel spellings: spiritual versus tangible gates in 39:73, 38:50 and 15:44; locusts and frogs at 7:133; varied amthāl in 43:17, 52:24, 17:48 and 25:9; trumpet blast and earth's crushing at 69:13 and 89:21; 'my book' and 'my account' at 69:19–20, and 69:25,28. For subḥān at 17:93 he prefers omission at a disputed place because the Prophet's elevated presence is not distracted by worldly movement.
2:250/q22 How was David identified and prepared as Jalut's challenger? [* · reports] (turns on: The accounts differ in David's place among Jesse's sons and in the signs, volunteer episode, and arms that led to his duel.)
  2:250/q22/p1 Samuel received signs identifying Jesse's youngest shepherd son, David, and David went to fight with trust in God. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi says Jalut challenged Talut, Samuel prayed, a horn of oil and iron furnace marked the warrior, twelve tall sons failed, and God chose by hearts rather than bodies. Jesse initially hid slight David among sheep; Samuel saw his mercy carrying sheep across floodwater and anointed him as oil overflowed. David cited rescues from beasts, then declined Talut's horse, armor and weapons as useless without God. Ibn Abi Hatim's al-Suddi adds his sling feats, submission of beasts and mountains, and armor fitting him.
  2:250/q22/p2 David was Jesse's seventh and youngest son in Talut's army; revelation told Samuel he would kill Jalut. — reasons: Al-Kashshaf calls David a shepherd and says three stones later called to him. Its seventh-son count differs from al-Thaʿlabi's twelve older sons.
  2:250/q22/p3 David came from his flock and volunteered despite his brothers' scorn, after which Talut armed him. — reasons: Al-Tabari transmits Ibn Jurayj, who places this after the river crossing and Talut's offer of half his kingdom.
2:250/q23 What do the accounts say of the stones and David's defeat of Jalut? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports differ in whose stones they were, what they did, and whether the blow or a later beheading killed Jalut.)
  2:250/q23/p1 Three speaking stones associated with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob joined, and their shot pierced Jalut's head and routed his army. — reasons: Al-Tabari's narrative names the patriarchs; his report from Ibn Jurayj through Mujahid says the stones recalled earlier victories, promised help, foretold Jalut's death and joined. Details of the stones' speech vary within this account.
  2:250/q23/p2 Three stones associated with Aaron, Moses, and one God reserved for David joined and killed Jalut. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi says the stones entered David's bag and spoke; David invoked the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, the fused stone flew on a God-driven wind through a helmet of three hundred iron pieces and the brain, killing thirty men behind Jalut. Jalut had mocked the sling and threatened David. David dragged his body to Talut.
  2:250/q23/p3 David used a sling and stones, hit Jalut between the eyes, routed the army and then beheaded him. — reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim transmits Wahb's account: David refused Talut's armor and fought after a wager over daughter and wealth.
  2:250/q23/p4 The young shepherd David killed Jalut with a sling stone; other accounts say three stones called to him or merged. — reasons: The Study Quran points to the familiar account in 1 Samuel 17; al-Kashshaf mentions three calling stones, and al-Suddi's report in Ibn Abi Hatim has merging stones along with earlier signs of David.
2:250/q24 What reward was promised for defeating Jalut? [* · reports] (turns on: Different speakers make offers in the reports, and Talut's promised share is described differently.)
  2:250/q24/p1 Talut promised his daughter and half his kingdom to the victor. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi reports this before David's duel and again when David volunteered; Ibn Jurayj in al-Tabari reports half the kingdom after the river crossing, without naming the daughter in that report.
  2:250/q24/p2 The wager promised Talut's daughter and rule over his wealth. — reasons: Ibn Abi Hatim transmits Wahb's version of the duel and its reward.
  2:250/q24/p3 Jalut offered his own kingdom if Talut's champion killed him. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi reports this as Jalut's challenge to Talut; it is a separate offer from Talut's reward.
2:250/q25 What followed the army's victory and David's marriage? [* · reports]
  2:250/q25/p1 The army returned safe and wealthy; David became acclaimed and married Talut's daughter. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi reports public praise, then Talut's demand for two hundred enemy foreskins as a dowry, which David brought strung together; Talut gave him his daughter and royal signet. Al-Kashshaf also says Talut married his daughter to David after Jalut's death.
2:250/q26 How did Talut treat David after David became popular? [* · reports]
  2:250/q26/p1 Talut grew jealous, tried to kill David, pursued him, and David repeatedly escaped while sparing Talut. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi says Talut's daughter warned David; a wineskin in his bed took the blow. Talut then sealed his doors. God blinded guards on two visits: David left arrows once, then took a water jug and cup, beard hairs and garment piece. Spies pursued him; a spider web concealed his cave, after which he worshiped in mountains. Al-Kashshaf also reports envy and an attempt on David's life, followed by repentance.
2:250/q27 What do reports say of Talut's repentance and death? [* · reports]
  2:250/q27/p1 After persecuting scholars, Talut sought repentance and died fighting after his ten sons died before him. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi relates killings of critical scholars and worshipers, the sparing of a woman versed in God's greatest name, graves that rebuked Talut, and the executioner's rooster parable. The saved woman prayed at Samuel's grave; he rose and prescribed renouncing the throne and death in God's cause for Talut and his sons. Talut wept, asked his sons to ransom him from the Fire, and they all died in battle. Al-Kashshaf briefly reports repentance.
2:250/q28 What happened to David's rule after Talut died? [* · reports]
  2:250/q28/p1 David became Israel's king and inherited the earlier royal authority. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabi says David beheaded the man who reported killing Talut, received Talut's treasuries and was made king by Israel; he says Israel united under no other one king and supplies David's genealogy through Jacob, Isaac and Abraham. Talut ruled forty years; al-Dahhak and al-Kalbi give David sixty-nine years after Jalut. The Study Quran transmits al-Tabari that David's wisdom meant Samuel's prophethood and sovereignty meant Saul's kingship, and points to 38:17–29 for David's later gifts.
2:250/q29 What inward response does the commentator attach to these spiritually realized people? [* · inward]
  2:250/q29/p1 The spiritually realized are hidden and nameless, united in outward law and inward truth; the commentator prays to join them. — reasons: Al-Bursevi cites preceding Persian verses for their hiddenness and unity, then asks God for peace, mercy and inclusion among them.
