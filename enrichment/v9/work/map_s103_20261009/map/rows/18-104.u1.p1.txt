18:104/q11 Why do they believe they are doing good? [وَهُمْ يَحْسَبُونَ أَنَّهُمْ يُحْسِنُونَ صُنْعًا · interpretation] (turns on: The notes variously stress mistaken moral judgment, confident idolatry, and the bare contrast between self-assessment and failed deeds.)
  18:104/q11/p1 Vanity, weak judgment, and investment in their work make their deeds seem proper. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī cites conviction and self-admiration. Al-Biqāʿī says weak judgment overlooks visible failure. Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī say they admire acts they labored to perform. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī names desire, arrogance, and inherited zeal as veils; Study Quran compares 6:108, where deeds are made fair to wrongdoers.
  18:104/q11/p2 They mistake worship of idols or other beings for obedience and divine nearness. — reasons: Al-Jishumī says they assume such worship benefits them; Ibn ʿĀshūr says idol worshippers thought their acts would fulfill their aims and count as good; Kur'an Yolu says they regarded shirk as obedience and idols as a path to God.
  18:104/q11/p3 They take their acts to be obedience and virtue, despite their failure. — reasons: Muqātil, al-Suddī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Ibn Kathīr describe the discrepancy. Study Quran notes parallels for disbelievers and wrongdoers whose deeds appear fair to them.
18:104/q12 Can someone do wrong without recognizing it? [يَحْسَبُونَ · theology] (turns on: Whether the mistaken actor is ignorant of the wrong or knowingly rejects truth; Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes leaders from followers.)
  18:104/q12/p1 Yes; a person can be sinful while unaware the act is sinful. — reasons: Al-Jishumī makes this explicit. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the worst loss is unseen loss, which cannot be corrected like an acknowledged mistake; desires, pride, and inherited zeal hide truth.
  18:104/q12/p2 Some leaders or rejecters know the truth inwardly yet persist in falsehood. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī says leaders protect status while followers imitate without evidence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 27:14 and 2:206 for arrogant, desirous, self-satisfied rejection despite inward knowledge.
18:104/q13 How is يحسبون read? [يَحْسَبُونَ · readings]
  18:104/q13/p1 Both yaḥsabu and yaḥsibu are transmitted; kasra on the sīn is a canonical dialectal variant. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj notes two readings. Qiraat-ER associates yaḥsabu with Hijazi usage and yaḥsibu with Tamimi usage.
18:104/q14 What wordplay joins يحسبون and يحسنون? [يَحْسَبُونَ يُحْسِنُونَ · rhetoric]
  18:104/q14/p1 They form paronomasia by near-identical written forms distinguished by dots or letter transposition. — reasons: Abū Ḥayyān calls it tajnīs al-taṣḥīf and adduces al-Buḥturī plus a deliberately mispointed poem restored as 'you watered me, my Lord …'. Al-Samīn calls it tajnīs al-taṣḥīf or tajnīs al-khaṭṭ and adds al-Buḥturī's غرور/عز line, another misread poem, and a letter example with قبل قبل يدك ثراك and عبد عند رخاك رجاك. Al-Ālūsī says the letters transpose and cites al-Buḥturī. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it dot-based paronomasia and elsewhere cites it as Qur'anic rhetorical ornament.
18:104/q15 What does يحسنون صنعا mean? [يُحْسِنُونَ صُنْعًا · interpretation]
  18:104/q15/p1 They suppose they perform their work properly, with skill, obedience, or virtue. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī glosses doing a deed ḥasanan as performing it suitably. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Bayḍāwī stress imagined obedience or good work; al-Sāmarrāʾī says iḥsān implies mastery and ṣunʿ is finer intentional work by a rational agent than fiʿl or ʿamal.
18:104/q16 What does صنعا mean and suggest? [صُنْعًا · meaning]
  18:104/q16/p1 It means work or doing, especially an intentional or well-fashioned deed. — reasons: Abū ʿUbayda equates it with ṣinʿa and ṣanīʿ, illustrating fashioned work by a ṣanīʿ horse. Al-Wāḥidī calls it a verbal noun of doing a favor, used most often for good works. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes it from general fiʿl and work that need not be skilled making.
18:104/q17 How does this description develop the preceding 'greatest losers in deeds'? [* · links]
  18:104/q17/p1 It specifies and intensifies the losers' failure: they strive yet mistake loss for success. — reasons: Ibn Abī ʿAṭiyya says the preceding question taunts and rebukes disbelievers. Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls al-akhsarīn a comparative superlative ranking the losers and says the passage's repeated deeds language, including 18:30, makes loss of work fitting; he says this is the Qur'an's only such attribution. Al-Qurṭubī parses the preceding aʿmālan as an accusative specification. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says unrecognized loss is worse than an ordinary recoverable failed undertaking. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the appositive description heightens suspense.
18:104/q18 How does this verse connect with the surrounding sūra? [* · links]
  18:104/q18/p1 It concludes the sūra's warnings about worldly seduction and reliance on false protectors. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says six verses gather the sūra's themes of worldly seduction, false patrons, blindness, and bad outcomes before 18:110. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage answers idol worshippers seeking powerless patrons' favor; Kur'an Yolu contrasts efforts aimed at divine pleasure and the hereafter with pursuit of worldly gain. Iṣlāḥī says it humbles mockers of revelation who thought themselves superior.
18:104/q19 What becomes of those who persist in this failed effort? [* · interpretation]
  18:104/q19/p1 Their deeds carry no weight and they face fire or painful punishment in the hereafter. — reasons: Al-Jishumī says worshippers of others exhaust themselves and end in Fire. Al-Qushayrī says lack of verification leaves no next-life weight and describes present denial and separation becoming future rejection and burning, against the elect's present union and future grace. Iṣlāḥī speaks of humiliation and Hell; al-Ālūsī describes exposure, expulsion, and painful punishment for ostentatious lodge dwellers.
18:104/q20 What inward warning is drawn from their vain effort? [* · inward]
  18:104/q20/p1 Deeds done for display, gratitude, or self-regard are spiritually ruined even when they look good. — reasons: Abū Bakr al-Warrāq names reproach, expected thanks, and reputation; al-Qushayrī names work for other than God, ostentation, self-admiration, and lack of verification. Al-Būrsawī warns innovators and the ostentatious that hidden shirk cancels deeds, citing 39:65. Al-Ālūsī applies the warning to attention-seeking lodge dwellers and their schemes.
18:104/q21 What reply is reported when Ibn al-Kawwāʾ asked ʿAlī about the greatest losers? [* · reports] (turns on: The transmitted replies differ in which people ʿAlī named and whether Ḥarūrāʾ is a literal identification or a warning by comparison.)
  18:104/q21/p1 ʿAlī answered: the people of Ḥarūrāʾ. — reasons: Yaḥyā, al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Ālūsī transmit this identification; Ibn Kathīr also names al-Ḍaḥḥāk. Al-Ālūsī favors treating it as severe allusion rather than literal restriction.
  18:104/q21/p2 ʿAlī answered: People of the Book who disbelieved and innovated; the river people were close to them. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite the report through al-ʿAyyāshī; al-Ālūsī says it supports a broad reconciliation.
  18:104/q21/p3 ʿAlī answered: the sinful people of Quraysh. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this variant report.
18:104/q22 What did Saʿd say when asked whether they were the Ḥarūriyya? [* · reports]
  18:104/q22/p1 He answered Jews and Christians and separately condemned the Ḥarūriyya. — reasons: In the Bukhārī report quoted by Ibn Kathīr, Saʿd says Jews rejected Muḥammad and Christians denied food and drink in Paradise; he says the Ḥarūriyya break God's pledged covenant and calls them profligates.
18:104/q23 What reward is reported for one who acts by the Quran? [* · reports]
  18:104/q23/p1 The Quran accompanies and intercedes for its practitioner, who receives honor. — reasons: Kaʿb describes a beautiful, fragrant companion that soothes terror and identifies itself as the person's deeds. It asks God to intercede, recalling thirsty days and wakeful nights, after which the practitioner is crowned and robed.
18:104/q24 What general linguistic rule is noted in the material accompanying this verse? [* · grammar]
  18:104/q24/p1 Irregularly reshaped Arabic nisba forms retain their usage; complete forms follow analogy. — reasons: Sībawayh reports al-Khalīl's rule on nisba formation, without connecting it explicitly to a word of this verse.
18:104/q25 What broader claims about Quranic eloquence accompany this rhetorical example? [* · rhetoric] (turns on: The accompanying notes range from Ibn ʿĀshūr's literary account to a later dispute over a book about Qur'anic eloquence.)
  18:104/q25/p1 The Quran combines varied expression, sound patterning, and truth with distinctive rhetorical ornament. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts rare boastful public performances with Qur'anic dialogue, preaching, argument, maxims, story, description, and narration; he says fitting diction and patterned endings spread rapidly, and truth gives spiritual force. He says ornament, especially paronomasia exemplified here, is common; he also cites 22:4 for antithesis and Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ's book on ornament.
  18:104/q25/p2 A later debate about a book on Quranic eloquence drew praise and a polemical defense. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ records Saʿd Zaghlūl's praise of al-Rāfiʿī's book and Yaʿqūb Ṣarrūf's recommendation, while judging neither an authority; she quotes al-Rāfiʿī's third-edition preface accusing critics of ignorant, malicious imitation and corrupt character.
