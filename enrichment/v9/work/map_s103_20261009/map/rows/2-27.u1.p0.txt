## THE CURRENT MAP
2:27/q01 Who are the covenant breakers? [ٱلَّذِينَ · referent] (turns on: Whether the immediate scriptural or hypocritical audience limits a general description.)
  2:27/q01/p1 Jewish scholars and People of the Book who concealed or denied Muhammad. — reasons: Muqatil includes rejection of Jesus and selective belief. Tabari cites the prior disbelievers and hypocrites and following address to Israel at 2:40; Ibn Atiyya sees this as a particular application in a broad context.
  2:27/q01/p2 All who share the conduct, including unbelievers, polytheists, and hypocrites. — reasons: Tabari extends the warning beyond its first audience; Abu Hayyan and Alusi prefer the general wording over a restricted occasion. Razi and Ibn Kathir favor wide application.
  2:27/q01/p3 Hypocrites whose six traits appear when they hold power. — reasons: Abu al-Aliya and Rabi list lying, broken promises, betrayal, covenant breach, severance, and corruption; the first three alone appear when weak.
  2:27/q01/p4 The Haruriyya or Kharijites exemplify the traits. — reasons: Sa'd applies both covenant breach and severance to them. Ibn Kathir says they arose after revelation, so the report, if sound, applies the verse rather than predicting them.
  2:27/q01/p5 People who believed in the Prophet and then disbelieved. — reasons: Qatada describes the reversal; Abu Hayyan reports apostasy after faith.
2:27/q02 How is ٱلَّذِينَ parsed? [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar] (turns on: Attachment to the preceding fāsiqīn or a separate condemnatory sentence.)
  2:27/q02/p1 It is accusative as an adjective of the preceding fāsiqīn. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur prefer syntactic continuity; Baydawi says the description confirms their transgression.
  2:27/q02/p2 It is a new subject whose predicate is 'those are the losers'. — reasons: Alusi calls this almost parenthetical; al-Samin gives the resulting final-clause parse.
  2:27/q02/p3 It is detached for censure or predicate of an omitted pronoun. — reasons: Nahhas and Qurtubi supply huwa/hum; Abu Hayyan also allows accusative censure.
2:27/q03 What does 'breaking' mean? [يَنقُضُونَ · meaning]
  2:27/q03/p1 Undoing a firmly secured bond, then abandoning or opposing its terms. — reasons: The sources compare untying rope and dismantling a building. Wahidi cites counter-poetry by Jarir and Farazdaq; Jishumi extends it to refuting proof. Ibn Ashur says untying conveys deeper destruction than cutting; Tabatabai notes a preceding bond.
2:27/q04 Why describe breach as untying? [يَنقُضُونَ عَهْدَ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
  2:27/q04/p1 The covenant is an imagined cord binding two parties; breach loosens its strands. — reasons: Baydawi and Bursevi explain the image. Alusi distinguishes explicit from implicit metaphor. Ibn Ashur discusses representational and imaginative features, citing Abu Firas, Labid, and poetry on severed bonds; classifications can overlap.
2:27/q05 What can ʿahd mean in Arabic? [عَهْدَ · meaning] (turns on: The word has several attested senses; the verse's referent is a separate question.)
  2:27/q05/p1 A charge, instruction, testament, or entrusted duty. — reasons: Ibn Jawzi and Ibn Faris cite the charge in 36:60 and revelation to Abraham and Ishmael in 2:125; some group revelation with instruction.
  2:27/q05/p2 A binding pact, security guarantee, oath, or agreement. — reasons: Ibn Faris gives binding security; 9:4 illustrates treaty protection, while Ibn Qutayba reads 16:91 as oath and another reading calls it agreement.
  2:27/q05/p3 In other uses it means keeping faith, safeguarding, monotheism, revelation, or prophethood. — reasons: Ibn Jawzi cites 7:102, 19:87, 2:125, and 2:124; a prophetic saying makes faithful keeping part of faith.
  2:27/q05/p4 Related forms name a period, first rain, refuge, covenanting person, sale document, unresolved matter, or renewed care. — reasons: Ibn Faris catalogs the uses; Ibn Jawzi distinguishes one-sided taʿahhad from reciprocal taʿāhad.
2:27/q06 Which covenant is broken here? [عَهْدَ ٱللَّهِ · referent] (turns on: The rival grounds are transmitted obligations, rational proof, primordial testimony, or a specific historical pledge.)
  2:27/q06/p1 God's general commands and prohibitions transmitted in revelation and through messengers. — reasons: Breaking means not acting on them. Qurtubi prefers this inclusive view despite an unbeliever context. Asad includes the moral duty to use endowed gifts as intended.
  2:27/q06/p2 The Torah or prior scriptures' duty to affirm Muhammad, follow him, and disclose his description. — reasons: Muqatil also names belief in Jesus and all prophets. Tabari cites 3:187; Wahidi prefers an obligation made known by revelation. Ibn Kathir transmits Ibn Jarir and Muqatil ibn Hayyan favoring this.
  2:27/q06/p3 The primordial pledge of Adam's descendants to acknowledge God as Lord and worship Him alone. — reasons: The supporters cite 7:172. Wahidi says revelation made an unremembered pledge known; Ibn Jawzi invokes truthful report. Jishumi, Tabarsi, and Razi's theologians object that forgotten obligations cannot fairly bind.
  2:27/q06/p4 Rational and innate evidence of God and prophecy in creation, reason, miracles, and scripture. — reasons: Zajjaj grounds obligation in discerning a Creator; Ibn Atiyya cites cosmic signs. Razi favors repeated accessible proofs; Asad reasons from human dependence. Ibn Kathir and Alusi encompass all unbelievers.
  2:27/q06/p5 The pledge of prophets and followers to affirm and support a confirming messenger. — reasons: Zajjaj cites 3:81 and glosses iṣr as God's covenant; Wahidi favors this known proof over the unremembered Adam pledge.
  2:27/q06/p6 Three covenants: primordial lordship, prophetic conveyance and unity, and scholars' disclosure of truth. — reasons: Razi cites 3:81 and 3:187; Nasafi adds 33:7.
  2:27/q06/p7 The Quranic charge accepted before its recipients disbelieved. — reasons: Al-Suddi reports acceptance followed by repudiation.
  2:27/q06/p8 An oath to follow a warner if one came. — reasons: Razi and Study Quran cite 35:42; Razi objects that it only binds oath makers.
  2:27/q06/p9 Israel's pledge not to kill, expel, oppress, or sever one another. — reasons: Abu Hayyan cites 2:84 for Israelite addressees.
  2:27/q06/p10 The trust humanity carried after heaven and earth declined it. — reasons: Reported by Abu Hayyan and Alusi.
  2:27/q06/p11 Any confirmed pledge made in worldly life, including one invoked in God's name. — reasons: Kuranyolu pairs such promises with the primordial pledge; Ibn Ashur cites 16:91 and includes prior or subsequent confirmation.
2:27/q07 What duty does this verse establish toward covenants? [عَهْدَ ٱللَّهِ · law]
  2:27/q07/p1 A lawful covenant must be fulfilled; feared betrayal must be openly declared instead of secretly committed. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya includes Muslim agreements. Qurtubi cites 5:1 and 8:58. Qatada stresses threat. Prophetic sayings connect covenant and trust to faith and religion. Malik ibn Dinar's story and the Mathnawi warn that repeated breach incurs rebuke or curse.
2:27/q08 How does مِنۢ بَعْدِ work? [مِنۢ بَعْدِ · grammar] (turns on: Temporal starting point or emphatic particle.)
  2:27/q08/p1 Min marks the start of breach after confirmation, suggesting swift disregard. — reasons: Abu Hayyan attaches it to yanqudun; Samin and Alusi reject emptiness. Baydawi and others allow this force.
  2:27/q08/p2 Min is extra or merely emphatic after baʿd. — reasons: Wahidi calls its mim emphatic; others report an extra particle.
2:27/q09 Why use بَعْدِ here? [بَعْدِ · rhetoric]
  2:27/q09/p1 It locates breach after the covenant in time and cannot be replaced by khalfa. — reasons: Samarrai compares 2:52, 2:64, 2:109, 2:120, 2:230, and 3:8.
2:27/q10 What is مِيثَٰق and how is it formed? [مِيثَٰقِهِۦ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the form names the confirming thing or functions as a verbal noun.)
  2:27/q10/p1 A firm confirmation, bond, or undertaking securing a covenant. — reasons: Wahidi traces yāʾ to wāw; Farra and others give plurals miyāthiq and mawāthiq with poetry. Baydawi includes signs, books, and acceptance. Abu Hayyan notes this instance need not signify an oath.
  2:27/q10/p2 A noun substituting for a verbal noun of binding or confirming. — reasons: Akhfash compares nabat/inbat and ata/iʿta, citing 71:17; Nahhas cites Ibn Kaysan and Qutami; Ibn Atiyya uses a line of Amr ibn Shayyim. Abu Hayyan and Samin find substitution unnecessary.
  2:27/q10/p3 A verbal or source noun expressing the act of confirmation. — reasons: Tabari says tawaththuq; Abu Hayyan compares miʿad and milad and notes missing mifʿal from Ibn al-Hajj and Ibn Malik's lists. Ibn Ashur favors this over Khafaji's intensive form.
  2:27/q10/p4 An intensive mifʿal form. — reasons: Khafaji's account is reported, but Ibn Ashur favors a source noun here.
2:27/q11 What is the antecedent of the pronoun in مِيثَٰقِهِۦ? [مِيثَٰقِهِۦ · grammar] (turns on: Whether confirmation is annexed to its object, agent, or undertaking.)
  2:27/q11/p1 The covenant: its own ratification. — reasons: Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur favor the topical antecedent; Abu al-Baqa makes the verbal noun object-related. Salikuti objects to a divine antecedent.
  2:27/q11/p2 God, who confirmed and bound His covenant. — reasons: Tabari chooses this. Abu al-Baqa makes the verbal noun agent-related; Alusi replies that such genitives are common.
  2:27/q11/p3 The accepted undertaking or its ratification. — reasons: Razi reports Zamakhshari's three alternatives, including the covenant undertaken and God as confirmer through books and signs.
2:27/q12 What does يَقْطَعُونَ mean lexically? [يَقْطَعُونَ · meaning]
  2:27/q12/p1 Severing or dividing, extended to crossing, bird migration, waning water, and broken breath. — reasons: Nahhas and Qurtubi list the uses; Jishumi says a doubled form can intensify or repeat cutting. Tabarsi extends physical cutting to speech and abstract matters.
