[ABUHAYYAN:101:9/r1] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Hawiya is a level of Hell; umm here means a dwelling or refuge, as the earth is called humanity's mother because it houses people; Utba ibn Abi Sufyan uses the same image in battle.
[ABUHAYYAN:101:9/r2] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · قتادة وأبو صالح وغيره | reports | Qatada, Abu Salih, and others say his head is in the abyss at the bottom of Hell because he is thrown in headfirst.
[ABUHAYYAN:101:9/r3] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | Alternatively, the phrase is an ill omen: people invoke destruction by saying “his mother fell,” since his fall and death make his mother fall into grief.
[ABUHAYYAN:101:9/r4] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · author | holds | An unnamed poet illustrates the idiom with a line beginning “His mother fell”; the verse provides no poet attribution.
[ABUHAYYAN:101:9/r5] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · الجمهور وطلحة | reports | Most readers pronounce the hamza of أُمُّهُ with ḍamma; Talha reads it with kasra.
[ABUHAYYAN:101:9/r6] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · ابن خالويه وابن دريد | reports | Ibn Khalawayh reports Ibn Durayd's statement that the kasra is a dialect form.
[ABUHAYYAN:101:9/r7] ABUHAYYAN, d. 745 · النحويون | reports | Grammarians say the hamza cannot be kasra unless preceded by a kasra or a yāʾ.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p533/r4] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | holds | Hāwiya is a level of Hell; umm means a place of refuge or dwelling, just as earth is called humanity's mother.

[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p533/r6] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · author | reports | Another view hears an ill omen: when people curse someone with his mother's ruin, they imagine her falling into grief when he falls and dies.
[ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p533/r7] ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 · الجمهور | reports | The majority pronounce the hamza of ummuhu with ḍamma; Ṭalḥa uses kasra, which Ibn Khalawayh cites as a dialect but grammarians restrict after a preceding kasra or yāʾ.
[SAMIN-DURR:101:9/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | هاوية means doomed or destroyed and is a figurative expression: people say هوت أمه of someone who perished because his mother collapses in grief.
[SAMIN-DURR:101:9/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A poet uses the idiom هوت أمه in a line about the morning traveler and the returning night.
[SAMIN-DURR:101:9/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · طلحة | reports | Ṭalḥa reads فإمه with kasra on the hamza; Ibn Durayd, as transmitted by Ibn Khalawayh, identifies this as a dialect.
[SAMIN-DURR:101:9/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · النحويون | reports | Grammarians permit this kasra only when preceded by a kasra or yāʾ; the fuller discussion of the reading differences appears in Sūrat al-Nisāʾ, 4:11. | mentions 4:11
