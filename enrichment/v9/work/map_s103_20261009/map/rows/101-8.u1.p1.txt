101:8/q10 What principles govern the moral weight and recompense of deeds? [* · theology]
  101:8/q10/p1 Faithful obedience and good deeds have standing; disbelief and sins have none. — reasons: Tabatabai says faith and obedience bear weight before God and lead to felicity, while disbelief and sins lack weight and lead to misery. Islahi makes truth or good deeds alone weighty and attributes the light scale to denial of revelations (7:8–9).
  101:8/q10/p2 Good deeds can erase evil and are rewarded tenfold, while evil is repaid only in kind. — reasons: Study Quran cites 41:34 and 11:114 for overcoming and erasing evil and 6:160 for the unequal recompense.
  101:8/q10/p3 Only deeds help at judgment, and the just balance calls for responsible belief and conduct. — reasons: Islahi says God weighs deeds in a just balance, with eternal bliss or Hell according to which side prevails. Kuranyolu calls the scene illustrative imagery that teaches just judgment and responsible conduct and belief.
  101:8/q10/p4 A deed's intention affects its value. — reasons: Study Quran reports the Prophet's saying that deeds are judged by intentions; Islahi says ill intent and heretical belief nullify otherwise good deeds.
  101:8/q10/p5 Divine mercy is needed for entry to Paradise even when deeds are weighed. — reasons: Study Quran reports the Prophet saying that no one enters through deeds alone, including himself; only God's mercy shelters him.
101:8/q11 What inward meaning is given to light and heavy scales? [خَفَّتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · inward] (turns on: The inward notes apply weight to spiritual ascent and lasting traits and lightness to veiling, appetite, and descent.)
  101:8/q11/p1 Spiritual ascent and enduring righteousness are heavy; descent into perishable sensual pleasures is light. — reasons: Qashani says this pattern rules out his earlier annihilation reading, for God's scale counts rising and ascending as weight and descent as lightness. Bursevi also says God's scale reverses ordinary scales: enduring good works rise, whereas passing sensual pleasures have no value.
  101:8/q11/p2 Light scales are psychic vices and evil deeds, ending in the hell of bodily nature and spiritual veiling. — reasons: Qashani makes bodily nature's abyss the owner's home. The Najmi interpretations cited by Bursevi describe a hot fire of ignorance, blindness, ego, passion, Satan, and the world.
  101:8/q11/p3 Godly attributes and character bring rest from the consequences of those qualities. — reasons: Bursevi reports this from the Najmi interpretations for the person whose weighed qualities are divine attributes and godly traits.
101:8/q12 How does the verse fit the surrounding passage and sūra? [* · links]
  101:8/q12/p1 The Resurrection scene divides humanity into heavy and light scale groups with opposed outcomes. — reasons: Razi says God divides people after describing Resurrection; Alusi says the passage first describes what all share and then each group's state. Study Quran says the final six verses contrast good-deed-heavy and light scales. Tabatabai says the Meccan sura warns and promises about resurrection, with warning dominant.
  101:8/q12/p2 The ending with the wretched prepares the next sūra's warning about the cause of misery. — reasons: Biqai says al-Qaria first establishes the Hour and divides happy and wretched, ends with the wretched, and then the next sura opens with what caused that misery (101:1, 101:8–9; 102:1).
  101:8/q12/p3 The passage uses a teasing question and delayed explanation to create rhetorical tension. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum compares the teaching questions of 101:1–11 with 90:12–16, 97:2–4, and 104:5–9, and with oath sequences such as Q 100.
  101:8/q12/p4 The opposed destinies are expressed with antithetical omitted clauses. — reasons: Biqai points to antithetical ellipsis in the clauses around the sinner's refuge.
101:8/q13 What other Qur'anic passages are linked to this verse's weighing and outcome? [* · links]
  101:8/q13/p1 The light scales and abyss are paralleled in 7:9 and 23:103. — reasons: Study Quran names both parallels; Islahi cites 7:8–9 for the balance in which truth has weight and deniers lose.
  101:8/q13/p2 Earlier passages explain the weighing, and 69:21 describes the pleasing life of the other group. — reasons: Abu Hayyan points back to discussion at 7:8 and to Sura al-Haqqa's pleasing life at 69:21. Tabatabai likewise says the scale has been explained under earlier suras.
101:8/q14 When was the sūra containing this verse revealed? [* · sciences]
  101:8/q14/p1 It is a Meccan sūra. — reasons: Tabatabai describes its Meccan setting and its dominant resurrection warning; Asad calls it early Meccan and tentatively places it after al-Tin (sura 95).
  101:8/q14/p2 It was probably revealed after Sūrat al-Tīn. — reasons: Asad gives this tentative placement for the early Meccan sura, citing sura 95.
