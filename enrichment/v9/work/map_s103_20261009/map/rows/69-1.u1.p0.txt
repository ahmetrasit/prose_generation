## THE CURRENT MAP
69:1/q01 What does al-Ḥāqqah refer to, and what does its name convey? [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · meaning] (turns on: The explanations select different aspects of ḥaqq: certainty, disclosure, recompense, fulfillment, overcoming, or impending punishment.)
  69:1/q01/p1 It names the Resurrection or Hour of Judgment. — reasons: ʿIkrima, Ibn ʿAbbās and others identify it thus. Al-Māwardī calls this the majority view; al-Rāzī says all agree, and Study Quran says commentators agree on the referent while differing on the aspect. Elmalılı reports that it may be a proper name without further intended sense.
  69:1/q01/p2 It is the certain, established and unavoidable Hour. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī stress certainty; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an active participle from ḥaqqa, a real existent, citing 39:71. Al-Rāzī cites al-Layth's comparison with 56:2, an event with no lie in its occurrence; Elmalılı also cites it for an unfailing calamity. Islahi grounds its necessity in common sense and morality; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives it from established fact.
  69:1/q01/p3 It is the Hour in which the realities of deeds or other things are disclosed. — reasons: Muqātil says believers' and unbelievers' deeds become manifest. Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted by al-Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya; al-Māwardī cites al-Kalbī, Ibn al-Jawzī al-Farrāʾ, and al-Qurṭubī al-Ṭabarī. Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī say knowledge is figuratively assigned to the Hour, though people learn. Al-Ālūsī applies this to disclosure and realization, but al-Kashf limits it to disclosure. Elmalılı cites 68:42. Asad cites 37:19, 39:68, 50:21–22; Study Quran cites 67:11.
  69:1/q01/p4 It is the Hour when deeds, reckoning, reward and punishment become real or due. — reasons: ʿAbd al-Razzāq reports Qatāda on deeds falling due; al-Māwardī reports Qatāda and Yaḥyā ibn Sallām on reward and punishment. Al-Durr reports Ibn Jurayj's believer's faith and hypocrite's hypocrisy. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī cite 39:71 for ḥaqq meaning 'became due.' Al-Rāzī reports al-Zajjāj that outcomes cease being awaited. Al-Ālūsī allows figurative attribution; al-Kashf calls the Hour 'possessor of realities.' Al-Qurṭubī adds assignment to Paradise or Hell.
  69:1/q01/p5 It is the Day of Truth. — reasons: Al-Kisāʾī is cited by al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī; the first and last also cite al-Muʾarrij and an Arabic idiom about recognizing an adversary's truth.
  69:1/q01/p6 It realizes God's promise and threat. — reasons: Al-Māwardī cites Ibn Baḥr. Al-Jishumī cites Abū Muslim, whose derivation from the Lord's word becoming due is also given by al-Rāzī and Elmalılı; the latter cites 10:33 and 40:10. Islahi says divine promises and messengers' warnings prove true.
  69:1/q01/p7 It overcomes false opponents of God's religion. — reasons: Al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı transmit al-Azharī's derivation from prevailing in a dispute. Al-Jishumī reports that the day overpowers unbelievers; al-Biqāʿī says it makes truth prevail.
  69:1/q01/p8 It is the appointed event or calamity due to befall a people. — reasons: Al-Rāzī and Elmalılı report this; Elmalılı calls it close to the inevitable Hour.
  69:1/q01/p9 It points to each person's particular due right. — reasons: Al-Rāzī reports a more specific and compelling form of right; Elmalılı cites 'this is my own right.'
  69:1/q01/p10 It is named for believers' rightful fear of it. — reasons: Al-Māwardī offers this explanation.
  69:1/q01/p11 It also includes inevitable punishment of peoples rejecting their messenger. — reasons: Islahi says punishment follows after a messenger conclusively conveys truth, and anticipates punishment in the Hereafter.
  69:1/q01/p12 It can refer to Quraysh's crushing defeat for opposing the Prophet. — reasons: Kur'an Yolu reports Ateş's view, prompted by the ensuing punished peoples.
  69:1/q01/p13 It realizes everything unbelievers rejected in earthly life. — reasons: Study Quran reports this view from JJ.
69:1/q02 What form is al-Ḥāqqah? [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · grammar] (turns on: The sources differ over participial, verbal-noun, elliptical-adjective and proper-name analyses.)
  69:1/q02/p1 It is an active participle or emphatic derivative of ḥaqq. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says active participle from ḥaqqa, a real existent; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report participial analyses. Al-Rāzī and Elmalılı report Abū Muslim's derivation from the Lord's word becoming due. Study Quran calls the form emphatic.
  69:1/q02/p2 It is a verbal noun like al-ʿāqibah or al-ʿāfiyah. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says possessor of truth; Elmalılı gives pure reality itself; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite the verbal-noun parallels.
  69:1/q02/p3 It is an adjective with its described noun omitted. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says the quality is unmistakable.
  69:1/q02/p4 It is a proper name for Judgment Day. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī gives this analysis, and Elmalılı reports no additional intended meaning.
  69:1/q02/p5 Al-Ḥaqqah and al-Ḥaqq are equivalent forms. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī lists two equivalent forms; al-Qurṭubī lists three.
69:1/q03 How does the opening noun fit the following question syntactically? [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · grammar] (turns on: Whether the following question predicates the opening subject or explains it as an exclamation.)
  69:1/q03/p1 The first al-Ḥāqqah is nominative subject and the following question its predicate. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj and al-Samīn parse mā as second subject and the repeated noun as its predicate; the whole clause predicates the first. Al-Naḥḥās confirms the nominative. Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī reconstruct 'what is it?' with the noun replacing a pronoun.
  69:1/q03/p2 The second occurrence explains the first in an exclamation of wonder. — reasons: Al-Thaʿlabī compares 'What is Zayd?' and cites 101:1 and 56:27.
69:1/q04 What effect does the abrupt opening and questioning create? [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · rhetoric] (turns on: The notes identify repetition and questioning, and the isolated name, as different devices.)
  69:1/q04/p1 It magnifies the event and evokes awe at what exceeds human knowledge. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī say repeating the noun instead of a pronoun heightens awe, and no imagination encompasses the Hour. Al-Durr reports Qatāda on magnification. Elmalılı says unaided reason cannot grasp it; Asad stresses its unimaginable reality.
  69:1/q04/p2 The isolated name stands for a whole sentence and jolts the indifferent listener through ambiguity. — reasons: Islahi says the truncated wording focuses attention on the subject by arousing uncertainty.
69:1/q05 How is al-Ḥāqqah related to al-Qāriʿah? [ٱلْحَآقَّةُ · links]
  69:1/q05/p1 Both designate Resurrection, while al-Qāriʿah evokes its terrifying blow. — reasons: Muqātil cites 69:4 and 101:1; al-Durr transmits Qatāda and Ibn Jurayj. Al-Kashshāf evokes terror, split sky, scattered mountains and earth, extinguished stars, and a noun replacing a pronoun for intensity. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the connected clauses make Thamūd and ʿĀd deniers of Resurrection, and the synonym conveys terror. Al-Rāzī reports scholars who find the al-Qāriʿah formula rhetorically stronger as warning intensifies, though al-Ḥāqqah is stronger semantically as justice.
69:1/q06 How does the opening connect to surrounding sūras and this sūra's argument? [* · links] (turns on: The notes emphasize al-Qalam, al-Wāqiʿah, or this sūra's sequence and theme.)
  69:1/q06/p1 It continues al-Qalam's warning about Resurrection and denied judgment. — reasons: Al-Jishumī links al-Qalam's warning and command to endure abuse. Abū Ḥayyān cites 68:44, the fortunate and wretched, and deniers. Al-Biqāʿī cites the refusal to equate wrongdoers and righteous and Jonah as proof of power. Islahi says the theme is shared but arguments differ. Study Quran says it answers accusations of possession (68:2, 68:51).
  69:1/q06/p2 Destroyed deniers warn of punishment and introduce this sūra's account of Judgment. — reasons: Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān say this warns Meccans. Islahi says verses 1–12 show worldly and later punishment after messengers' warnings. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran trace the sequence through Resurrection, fates or books of deeds, and confirmation of the Qur'an.
  69:1/q06/p3 The title states disclosure of truth, defeat of falsehood and just distinction of people. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra displays knowledge, power and justice, and calls the Day of Gathering the Qur'an's decisive reminder.
  69:1/q06/p4 It resembles al-Wāqiʿah in affirming Resurrection and depicting its terrors. — reasons: Islahi compares al-Wāqiʿah; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists it among names for Resurrection.
  69:1/q06/p5 Other sūras explain rational and moral grounds for Judgment's necessity. — reasons: Islahi says earlier sūras detail them, and this and later sūras allude to them.
69:1/q07 Where and when was the sūra revealed? [* · sciences]
  69:1/q07/p1 It was revealed in Mecca. — reasons: Al-Durr cites Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī infers the Meccan setting from its style.
  69:1/q07/p2 It dates shortly after al-Mulk, about three or four years before the migration. — reasons: Asad gives the estimate and mentions Sūra 67; Study Quran calls it late Makkan and generally dates it shortly after al-Mulk.
69:1/q08 How many verses does the sūra contain? [* · sciences]
  69:1/q08/p1 It contains fifty-two verses.
69:1/q09 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah? [* · reports]
  69:1/q09/p1 The reciter receives an easy reckoning from God. — reasons: Al-Jishumī transmits this from Ubayy ibn Kaʿb through the Prophet.
69:1/q10 What is reported about the Prophet's recitation of the sūra? [* · reports]
  69:1/q10/p1 He recited it and similar chapters in the dawn prayer. — reasons: Al-Durr transmits Abū Barzah.
