101:6/q12 Who undergoes weighing? [مَن مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · theology] (turns on: Whether the weighing includes all people or leaves classes who receive reward or punishment without it.)
  101:6/q12/p1 The weighing extends to hypocrites and believing and unbelieving jinn as well as humans. — reasons: Al-Alusi says hypocrites apparently fall with unbelievers but their deeds are weighed under the plain sense of many verses and hadiths; the intended weighing is beneficial weighing. Jinn follow the human rule.
  101:6/q12/p2 Some people are exempt; God weighs only those He wills. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi cites a sound hadith that some of Muhammad's community enter without reckoning, and 55:41 for criminals recognized by marks and seized by forelocks and feet. The passage's two groups are the weighed people.
  101:6/q12/p3 People of patience receive reward without being weighed. — reasons: Qadi Mundhir ibn Sa'id al-Baluti says their reward is poured out.
101:6/q13 When does the weighing occur? [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · reports]
  101:6/q13/p1 It follows the flight and receipt of records, questioning, and reckoning. — reasons: Al-Alusi attributes this order to al-Wahidi and others; Elmalili repeats it.
101:6/q14 How certainly can the weighing and its physical details be known? [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · theology] (turns on: Whether revelation requires belief in an actual measure, supports a particular physical account, or warrants leaving mechanics unspecified.)
  101:6/q14/p1 The weighing is real and must be believed, while its exact nature remains unknown. — reasons: Al-Alusi says denial alone does not make a person an unbeliever. Elmalili says accidents such as heat and cold can be measured appropriately, deeds have bodily effects, and 42:17 and 7:8 support a true divine standard; detailed mechanism is not required. Ibn Atiyya says most scholars, jurists and hadith scholars affirm a literal balance because it gives a familiar, trusted form of judgment.
  101:6/q14/p2 No sound hadith establishes the balance's physical nature; no deed is lost, however God effects that. — reasons: Ibn al-Arabi says God could ensure complete accounting through an instrument, angels, or another means.
  101:6/q14/p3 One should avoid speculation and verbal disputes over the mechanics. — reasons: Qutb says such disputes distract from the Qur'an's felt meaning; Kur'an Yolu says the unseen afterlife exceeds what can be asserted beyond revelation.
101:6/q15 Why is the weighing displayed? [مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether its stated purpose is just accounting or public disclosure of the outcome.)
  101:6/q15/p1 It manifests justice and ensures that no deed is lost. — reasons: Elmalili calls the just scales of 21:47 a true law that resolves the gathering's upheaval; Islahi invokes 21:47. Al-Bursevi says records show justice and remove excuses. Ibn al-Arabi says no deed is lost regardless of instrument.
  101:6/q15/p2 It publicly reveals each person's standing, bringing joy to the righteous and humiliation to evildoers. — reasons: Al-Razi says this is the purpose of exposing the outcome before all creatures.
101:6/q16 What happens to the person with heavy balances? [* · interpretation]
  101:6/q16/p1 The person receives a pleasing life in Paradise or lasting bliss. — reasons: Al-Tha'labi, al-Qushayri, Ibn Kathir and Qatada explicitly name Paradise. Al-Biqa'i describes lasting consistent pleasure, and al-Bursevi explains that the life causes or possesses satisfaction. Islahi says the bliss exceeds even imaginable wishes; Kur'an Yolu speaks of richly provisioned Gardens.
101:6/q17 What does the weighing teach about deeds and salvation? [* · interpretation] (turns on: How the weight of deeds relates to reward, intention, moral quality and divine mercy.)
  101:6/q17/p1 Deeds determine the person's honor, humiliation, bliss or doom according to their weight. — reasons: Ibn Kathir links honor and humiliation to what people do. Islahi says only deeds matter on that Day; the balance grants bliss when good outweighs evil and Hell otherwise. Tabataba'i says faith and obedience have weight, disbelief and sins none.
  101:6/q17/p2 Good and evil deeds are unequal in recompense. — reasons: Study Quran cites 41:34 and 11:114 for good removing evil, and 6:160 for tenfold good against one-for-one evil.
  101:6/q17/p3 Good character is especially weighty. — reasons: Study Quran cites a hadith that nothing placed in the Balance outweighs good character.
  101:6/q17/p4 Intentions govern the judgment of deeds. — reasons: Study Quran cites the Prophet's hadith that each receives what they intended.
  101:6/q17/p5 Entry to Paradise ultimately depends on God's mercy. — reasons: Study Quran cites the Prophet saying no one, including himself, enters Paradise through deeds alone except when God's mercy shelters him.
101:6/q18 How does this verse fit the sura's sequence? [* · links] (turns on: How the contrast of heavy and light balances relates to the sura's earlier gathering and overall warning.)
  101:6/q18/p1 It divides the gathered people into two outcomes after their common Resurrection scene. — reasons: Al-Razi says God divides people by heavy or light balances; al-Biqa'i says the Day is described by its events before outcomes. Al-Alusi contrasts the groups' particular states; Ibn Ashur says this details the people scattered like moths in 101:4. Study Quran notes that the final six verses contrast their recompense.
  101:6/q18/p2 The sura warns of Resurrection while also giving glad tidings. — reasons: Tabataba'i calls warning its dominant tone and the sura Meccan.
  101:6/q18/p3 The sura's teaching questions form a riddle that creates rhetorical tension. — reasons: Corpus Coranicum compares 101:1-11 to oath sequences in Q 100 and teaching questions in 90:12-16, 97:2-4, and 104:5-9.
  101:6/q18/p4 The lesser-resurrection reading sets the scene at bodily death and dissolution. — reasons: Al-Qashani describes separation from bodies, confused movement toward the light-world, and bodily members disintegrating like fluffed wool.
101:6/q19 When was this sura revealed? [* · sciences]
  101:6/q19/p1 It is an early Meccan sura, probably after Surat al-Tin. — reasons: Asad Notes gives the tentative relative order; Tabataba'i also calls it Meccan.
101:6/q20 What other Quranic passages illuminate these scales? [* · links]
  101:6/q20/p1 Earlier balance passages address weighing, and al-Haqqa describes the ensuing pleasing life. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi refers to al-A'raf 7:8, al-Kahf 18:105, al-Anbiya' 21:47 and his Tadhkira. Abu Hayyan refers to al-A'raf 7:8-9 and al-Haqqa 69:21; Tabataba'i says he has discussed the scale earlier. Study Quran adds 7:8 and 23:102 for heavy good deeds.
101:6/q21 What does the light-balance contrast imply for the heavy-balance group? [* · interpretation]
  101:6/q21/p1 The light side has too little good and loses by rejecting God's revelations. — reasons: Al-Qummi glosses light balances as little good; Islahi links the loss to denial of revelation.
