Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reward answers how believers fare after sarcastic glad tidings to the damned.   84:25/q07/p2 The close continues al-Muṭaffifīn's contrast of rewarded allies and punished enemies who denied resurrection and judgment. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī relates the sūra to the close of al-Muṭaffifīn and mentions 83:1, identifying their Creator-King as judge.
  84:25/q07/p3 The sūra moves from Judgment Day events through contrasting reckonings and oaths to the two groups' final ends. — reasons: The Study Quran gives this outline of the sūra.
  84:25/q07/p4 The nearby statement that God sees the denier answers his belief that he will not return: he will return and be requited. — reasons: Al-Ṭabrisī gives two formulations: God sees and requites his deeds, or God sees his state in the Hereafter and recompenses him.
84:25/q08 Where does the reward phrase or its root recur in the Qur'an? [غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ · links]
  84:25/q08/p1 The reward phrase recurs in al-Qalam, Fuṣṣilat, al-Inshiqāq, and al-Tīn, with related uses of the root elsewhere. — reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists those reward passages, counts sixteen occurrences of the root mann, and notes r-rayb al-manūn at 52:30. Al-Samīn directs the reader to his explanation at 41:8; al-Dāmghānī and the Study Quran connect this verse to 41:8 and 95:6.
84:25/q09 What Prophetic virtue is reported for reading al-Inshiqāq? [* · sciences]
  84:25/q09/p1 Its reader will be spared receiving their book from behind their back. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this as a Prophetic report.
