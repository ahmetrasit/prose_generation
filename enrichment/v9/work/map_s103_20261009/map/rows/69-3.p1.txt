[ELMALILI:69:2-5/r2] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Grammatically, the first ma is an interrogative subject, adra-ka is its predicate, the addressed pronoun is the first object, and the question what the Haqqa is forms the second object.
[ELMALILI:69:2-5/r3] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | No created being can comprehend the Haqqa's severity by estimation; its actual occurrence will disclose it, while beforehand it is known only through divine report and faith in that report cannot fully encompass its reality.
[ELMALILI:69:2-5/r4] ELMALILI, d. 1361 · author | holds | Those who disbelieve the transmitted report because reason cannot grasp the event dismiss it as superstition, fable, poetry, invention, or deception.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p113/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The sentence may coordinate with “What is the Reality?” The second interrogative “what” conveys that people cannot comprehend the Reality's true nature because an extraordinary event is difficult to imagine.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p113/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The addressee of “what made you know” is indefinite: the Reality is so great that its true nature is beyond your grasp.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p113/r3] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | “What made you know” is a fixed idiom built from interrogative mā and causative adrā, whose verb takes three objects; the first object's underlying role shifts under causation, while the second is suspended by the interrogative.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p113/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The first mā magnifies by a rhetorical association between asking about extraordinary things and their greatness; it may instead be a question denying anyone knowledge, but both readings create awe.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p113/r5] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | The third mā suspends adrā from governing its two objects, and the masculine singular address is generic rather than directed to one person.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p113/r6] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, Sufyān ibn ʿUyayna, and Yaḥyā ibn Sallām say every “what made you know” is later explained, unlike “what makes you know”; if sound, the former promises an outcome while the latter denies knowledge. | mentions 33:63, 42:17
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p114/r1] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | reports | Al-Rāghib observes that Qur'anic “what made you know” formulas are followed by an explanation; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes this as clarifying the report from Ibn ʿAbbās and others. | mentions 101:10, 101:11, 97:2, 97:3, 82:18, 82:19
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p114/r2] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Ibn ʿĀshūr says grammarians have not adequately explained this construction, and some omit it altogether.
[IBNASHUR-FULL:v29p114/r4] IBNASHUR-FULL, d. 1393 · author | holds | Alternatively, if the clauses connect, Thamūd and ʿĀd denied the Resurrection; “the Striker” is a synonym for the Reality, brought forward instead of a pronoun to explain its terrifying force.
[TABATABAI:69:1-5/r5] TABATABAI, d. 1402 · ابن عباس | reports | The author suggests Ibn Abbas's distinction may be intended: what made you know signifies God informed the Prophet, while what makes you know signifies something withheld.
[ASAD-NOTES:69:1#n2/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | The ultimate reality will be beyond anticipation or imagination, which is why the rhetorical question receives no answer.
[ASAD-NOTES:69:1/r1] ASAD-NOTES, d. 1412 · Asad | holds | Asad renders al-haqqah as the laying-bare of truth, emphasizes its awe, and says its reality exceeds human conception.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p447/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | rejects | Treating mā al-Ḥāqqah as the predicate is an erroneous grammatical analysis.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p447/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The truncated, ambiguous wording jolts an indifferent addressee by focusing attention on the opening subject and drawing attention through uncertainty.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p448/r5] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Mā al-Ḥāqqah heightens dread: who can know what this inevitable event is and what will befall its stubborn deniers?
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-8-english:p448/r6] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The same startling mode of address appears in al-Qāriʿah, where the author says he will explain it further.
[STUDYQURAN:69:1/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Ḥāqqah is an emphatic form of ḥaqq, meaning truth or reality; here it evokes the overwhelming, inevitable reality none can avoid.
[STUDYQURAN:69:1/r5] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Repeating the question, as in 101:2–3, emphasizes that the Reality exceeds mere rational comprehension. | mentions 101:2, 101:3
[STUDYQURAN:69:intro/r1] STUDYQURAN · author | holds | Al-Ḥāqqah is a late Makkan sūrah, generally dated shortly after al-Mulk, and named for the Day of Judgment in its opening verses.
