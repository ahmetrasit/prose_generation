## THE CURRENT MAP
18:28/q01 What occasion prompted the command to remain with the worshippers? [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ · reports] (turns on: The reports differ over Meccan chiefs, later influential converts, and the People of the Bench.)
  18:28/q01/p1 Quraysh leaders demanded that poor believers be dismissed before they would listen or sit with the Prophet. — reasons: Accounts list Bilal, Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, Salman and Ibn Masud, and mention shabby or smelly clothes and separate meeting times. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan prefer this occasion because the sura is Meccan; Ibn Kathir cites Sad's six companions and Muslim's transmission. Kashshaf compares Noah's opponents (26:111).
  18:28/q01/p2 Uyayna and al-Aqra, with other influential converts, asked for the poor wool-clad believers to be moved so they could sit near the Prophet. — reasons: Salman's report names the pair and poor Muslims including Salman and Abu Dharr. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say this implies a Medinan setting; Alusi thinks most reports support it. Some reports say the chiefs were not yet Muslim.
  18:28/q01/p3 Uyayna alone requested separation, complaining of Salman's smell and promising elite acceptance. — reasons: Muqatil lists Bilal, Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, Amir ibn Fuhayra, Mihja, Ayman, Abu Hurayra and Ibn Masud, and calls Mihja Badr's first martyr. Ibn Jurayj says Uyayna was then unconverted. Tabatabai objects to Qummi's Medinan variant.
  18:28/q01/p4 The verse concerned seven hundred poor People of the Bench who stayed in the mosque praying and waiting for prayer. — reasons: Qatada says they had neither trade nor farmland.
  18:28/q01/p5 Khabbab reports that the Prophet had left poor believers when he stood, before this instruction came. — reasons: Al-Suyuti transmits Khabbab's account of the sequence.
18:28/q02 Was the verse revealed in Mecca or Medina? [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ · sciences] (turns on: Which occasion report fits the sura's context.)
  18:28/q02/p1 It was revealed in Mecca in response to Quraysh. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan favor this from the sura's Meccan setting; Tabatabai says Medinan reports conflict with the passage's context.
  18:28/q02/p2 It was a Medinan exception to the Meccan sura. — reasons: Qatada places revelation in Medina; Ibn Abbas and Qatada identify an exception. Alusi argues most reports and the following verses favor Medina.
18:28/q03 How did the Prophet respond to the command? [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ · reports] (turns on: The reports describe distinct encounters.)
  18:28/q03/p1 He found poor worshippers remembering God, sat with them, and praised God for commanding their company in life and death. — reasons: Reports vary on whether he found them behind the mosque or in rough clothing, and whether he was first at home. Ibn Kathir notes Tabarani's transmission and Abd al-Rahman ibn Sahl's Companion status.
  18:28/q03/p2 He identified a Quran recitation or reminder gathering as the assembly he had been commanded to stay with. — reasons: Reports name a man reciting al-Kahf or al-Hijr, or Abdullah ibn Rawaha reminding companions. Ibn Kathir notes connected and disconnected routes.
  18:28/q03/p3 He urged companions to continue remembrance when they stopped at his arrival, wishing to share the descending mercy. — reasons: Thabit transmits this account.
  18:28/q03/p4 He turned from inattentive Umayya and found Salman remembering God. — reasons: Al-Rabi says Umayya was distracted while the Prophet spoke.
18:28/q04 What does 'restrain yourself with them' mean? [وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether read as a public command, inward account, or implication for others.)
  18:28/q04/p1 Remain steadfastly in the believers' company and keep oneself from leaving them. — reasons: Sabr's root sense is restraint: Ibn Atiyya, Razi and Abu Hayyan cite the prohibition on tying an animal as a target; Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan and Samin cite Abu Dhuayb's poetry. Ibn Ashur says ma'a conveys company; Alusi distinguishes literal restraint from extended steadfastness.
  18:28/q04/p2 Inwardly, patience is stable presence through God without attention turned elsewhere. — reasons: Ibn Ata describes patient self, heart and spirit in divine presence; Qashani's stripped poor seek only God.
  18:28/q04/p3 The wording specifies the Prophet's self because his heart was already with God. — reasons: Qushayri and Alusi distinguish public conduct from the heart privately reserved for God.
  18:28/q04/p4 For others the phrase hints at the difficulty and merit of company with the poor. — reasons: Alusi says this implication applies to others, not to the Prophet; reports urge humility and say such company teaches gratitude and contentment.
18:28/q05 Who are those calling on their Lord? [ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · referent] (turns on: Whether the description specifies prayer, broader worship, learning, or the historical companions.)
  18:28/q05/p1 They are people performing the obligatory prayers, including the five daily and congregational prayers. — reasons: Mujahid, Ibrahim, Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas and Kab identify prescribed prayer; Hasan rejects story-preachers in favor of congregational worship. Ibn Kathir says poor or rich, strong or weak may qualify.
  18:28/q05/p2 They perform the dawn and afternoon prayers. — reasons: Qatada says prescribed prayers began one year before the emigration.
  18:28/q05/p3 They continually worship and remember God through supplication, praise, glorification, prayer and good works. — reasons: Tabari and Ibn Kathir include several acts; Tabarsi says they begin and end each day in worship. Ibn Atiyya includes religious knowledge; Study Quran lists prayer and invocation.
  18:28/q05/p4 They are Quran reciters or pupils whom the Prophet taught to recite. — reasons: Abu Jafar says the Prophet taught them; Ubayd Allah names reciters.
  18:28/q05/p5 They discuss distinctions in lawful and unlawful matters. — reasons: Abu Hashim and Said ibn Jubayr are cited.
  18:28/q05/p6 They are the poor companions whom elites wanted expelled. — reasons: Lists vary, including Salman, Abu Dharr, Suhayb, Ammar, Khabbab, Bilal and Ibn Masud. Alusi says their devotion warrants company.
18:28/q06 What does 'morning and evening' signify? [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the hours are literal, an idiom of continuity, or spiritual symbols.)
  18:28/q06/p1 It denotes continual devotion across all times. — reasons: Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan take both ends for the whole; Abu Hayyan likens it to front and back meaning the whole body. Ibn Ashur says prayer spans day and night. Tabatabai calls recurring times a metonym and rejects restriction to named prayers; Study Quran cites 33:42, 48:9 and 76:25.
  18:28/q06/p2 It marks actual morning and evening worship. — reasons: Mawardi describes seeking success at morning and pardon at evening; Alusi says work distracts people at these hours and favors the literal times. Ibn Ashur defines morning as dawn to sunrise; Qiraat-Er contrasts a single ghudwa act with ghadat as a period.
  18:28/q06/p3 Waking and sleeping resemble return to life and return to death, inviting remembrance. — reasons: Razi says the wise thank God at these transitions.
  18:28/q06/p4 Inwardly, morning and evening signify annihilation and abiding. — reasons: Qashani applies the symbols to stripped seekers.
18:28/q07 How is the morning word read? [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ · readings] (turns on: The reader transmission, grammar of ghudwa with the article, and value of codex spelling.)
  18:28/q07/p1 Most readers have bil-ghadati, the standard and grammatically clearer form. — reasons: Tabari follows reader consensus; Zajjaj says ghudwa is already definite. Nahhas says written waw proves no pronunciation, comparing salat and hayat. Farisi calls this form clearer.
  18:28/q07/p2 Ibn Amir and some early readers have bil-ghudwati with waw. — reasons: Farra attributes it to Abu Abd al-Rahman alone; others add Nasr ibn Asim and Malik ibn Dinar. Defenders generalize or make indefinite the normally definite time word; Alusi compares explanations of Sibawayh, Khalil and Radi. Orthographic waw is disputed.
  18:28/q07/p3 Ibn Abi Abla reads plural morning and evening forms. — reasons: Jishumi explains the original waw turning to alif after fatḥa.
  18:28/q07/p4 Abu Abd al-Rahman is reported to read al-ghudu without final h. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya records the variant.
18:28/q08 What do worshippers seek by 'His face'? [يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether face denotes approval, essence, attributes or vision.)
  18:28/q08/p1 They seek God's pleasure, favor and approved obedience rather than display, reputation or worldly gain. — reasons: Bursevi and Suhayli explain the face metaphor from where approval and anger show or the turn toward one favored; Alusi prefers this. Ibn Ashur says devotion makes them worthy company; Study Quran compares 2:272, 13:22, 30:38-39, 76:9 and 92:18-20.
  18:28/q08/p2 They seek God's essence or nearness by turning to Him. — reasons: Alusi reports essence with an omitted genitive and nearness as alternatives; Qashani's stripped seekers seek divine essence without worldly or afterlife needs.
  18:28/q08/p3 God's face means His beautiful names and attributes through which He is addressed. — reasons: Tabatabai says the essence cannot be reached directly: seeking active attributes means receiving mercy and grace; seeking knowledge, power and majesty calls for humility, weakness and ignorance. He rejects pleasure, omitted essence and mere nearness.

  18:28/q08/p5 They seek God's face through supplication. — reasons: Al-Mawardi reports supplication as one account of how they seek Him.
