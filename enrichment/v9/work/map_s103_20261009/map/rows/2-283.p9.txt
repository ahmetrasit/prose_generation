[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Amānatihi may mean the entrusted property and be the direct object of ‘let him repay.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Alternatively it is a verbal noun with an omitted possessive construction: let him repay the debt of his trust.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | rejects | It cannot be an absolute object formed from the verb iʾtaman.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The pronoun in ‘his trust’ can refer either to the creditor or to the person who was trusted.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | In ‘his heart is sinful,’ the pronoun may be a pronoun of the matter explained by the following clause, or may refer to ‘whoever’; the latter is apparent.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The clearest syntax makes ‘whoever’ the referent, ‘sinful’ the predicate, and ‘his heart’ the subject of the active participle; this is incompatible with the Basran analysis of a pronoun of the matter.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري وأبو البقاء | reports | Al-Zamakhshari, Abu al-Baqa, and others make ‘sinful’ a fronted predicate and ‘his heart’ a delayed subject; the author notes a Kufi objection about a pronoun referring to a later noun.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another parse makes ‘sinful’ the predicate with an implicit pronoun and ‘his heart’ a partitive apposition to that pronoun.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#5/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya makes ‘sinful’ a subject and ‘his heart’ the active participle’s subject filling the predicate position; Basrans reject such unlicensed participle government, while al-Farraʾ and al-Akhfash allow it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن أبي عبلة | reports | Ibn Abi ʿAbla reads qalbahu in the accusative, as transmitted by Ibn Atiyya; one parse makes it a partitive apposition to the pronoun in inna.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second accusative analysis treats qalbahu as resembling a direct object, as in ‘a man whose face is handsome’; grammarians permit it in prose and verse, reject it entirely, or restrict it to poetry.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سيبويه | reports | Sibawayh permits the object-like accusative in poetry but not prose and adduces the cited poem as evidence.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · مكي | reports | Makki and others offer a third parse, accusative of specification; Basrans object that tamyiz must be indefinite, while Kufans allow definite examples such as 2:130 and 28:85. | mentions 2:130, 28:85
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن أبي عبلة | reports | Ibn Abi ʿAbla also reads athima qalbuhu as a past verb with a hidden subject and ‘his heart’ as object: he made his heart sinful, using heart for the whole person.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبد الرحمن | reports | Abu Abd al-Rahman reads ‘they do not conceal’ with third-person yāʾ, following the preceding mention of scribe and witness; singular wording refers to a group.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The passage uses contrasting wordplay in ‘you transact a debt,’ matching wordplay in ‘conceal the testimony / whoever conceals it,’ and antithesis in ‘err/remember’ and ‘small/large.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283#6/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · السلمي | reports | Al-Sulami likewise reads ‘God knows what you do’ with third-person yāʾ, consistently with his other third-person readings.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The common reading kātiban is an active participle, ‘a scribe.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abu Amr cites Qanab’s poem as evidence for ruhan and says he chose it to distinguish ordinary pledges from horse-racing wagers, following transmitted reading.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبي ومجاهد وأبو العالية | reports | Ubayy, Mujahid, and Abu al-Aliyya read kitāban, which can mean writing as a verbal noun or be a plural of kātib.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Al-Zamakhshari attributes kitāban only to Ubayy and Ibn Abbas; Ibn Abbas asks what if a scribe exists but no paper or ink, favoring ‘writing’ over ‘scribe.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عباس والضحاك | reports | Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak read kitāban as a plural, reasoning that each case has its own scribe.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو العالية | reports | Abu al-Aliyya reads kutuban, plural of kitāb, in view of the many separate cases.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Al-Zamakhshari’s quotation from Ibn Abbas supports that reading and shows the verse means the written record rather than a scribe.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Rihān may be a result clause with an omitted verb, a subject with an omitted predicate, or a predicate with an omitted subject such as ‘the document.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr read ruhan with ḍamma; the others read rihānan with kasra and a long alif, with a reported hāʾ-sukūn variant.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:283/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Ibn Kathir’s form is a plural of rahn on the pattern fuʿul, like saqf/suqūf; al-Akhfash supplies other examples though the pattern is rare.

[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p727#2/r2] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · الشافعي والجمهور | reports | They infer from 'pledges taken' that a pledge is not binding until it is taken into possession.
[IBNKATHIR-FULL:v1p727#2/r3] IBNKATHIR-FULL, d. 774 · رواية عن أحمد وطائفة | reports | They infer that the pledge must be held by the creditor, the pledgee.
