## THE CURRENT MAP
30:60/q01 What patience does the Prophet's command require? [فَٱصْبِرْ · interpretation] (turns on: The explanations emphasize different objects and practices of patience: hostile conduct, continued proclamation, restraint from haste, or the inner experience of affliction.)
  30:60/q01/p1 Endure the opponents' rejection, abuse, and obstinacy while trusting the promised outcome. — reasons: Yaḥyā ties endurance to victory and the religion's triumph; al-Ṭabarī adds bearing abuse and delivering the message, with establishment for the Prophet and followers in the land. Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect patience to rejection after sealed hearts or persistent unbelief; al-Biqāʿī says even hostility falls within God's will. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies taunts; al-Nasafī hostility, al-Bursawī verbal and physical abuse, and al-Iṣlāḥī stubborn rejection. The Turkish commentary includes both Prophet and believers.
  30:60/q01/p2 Keep inviting people to faith and proclaiming the message firmly. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says silence would let unbelievers claim the Prophet changed position and lacked firmness. Al-Biqāʿī forbids slackening or compromise; al-Ṭabarī requires carrying out God's command to deliver the message. Kur'an Yolu extends the instruction to the caller, who must withstand belittlement, doubt, and anxiety.
  30:60/q01/p3 Do not let opponents provoke haste, anger, or premature action. — reasons: Al-Jubbāʾī says anger could make the Prophet violate the command to patience and gentleness; al-Nasafī mentions rushing to ask for punishment. Al-Biqāʿī mentions hastening victory; Derveze relates the warning to preparing for migration before conditions matured.
  30:60/q01/p4 Patience is giving up complaint. — reasons: Al-Sulamī transmits Ruwaym's definition.
  30:60/q01/p5 Patience is exposing oneself to the arrows of affliction. — reasons: Al-Sulamī transmits al-Ḥārith al-Muḥāsibī's image.
  30:60/q01/p6 Confidence in the promised reward makes affliction easy to endure and removes its pain. — reasons: Al-Sulamī transmits Abū ʿUthmān's explanation concerning the generous reward for the patient.
30:60/q02 What is God's true promise here? [وَعْدَ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation] (turns on: The sources supply different promised outcomes; al-Māwardī explicitly offers support and victory or vengeance, while others combine them.)
  30:60/q02/p1 God will support the Prophet, give him victory over enemies, and establish or manifest his religion. — reasons: Yaḥyā, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī give victory or religious triumph; al-Ṭabarī includes followers established in the land. Al-Nasafī says Islam will triumph over every religion. Al-Biqāʿī adds victory for followers of originally revealed scripture over those without scripture and says time will disclose the promise. Ibn Kathīr adds the good end; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 30:47, with help promised at both ends of the sūrah. Study Quran reports this from IJ, Ṭ, and Z.
  30:60/q02/p2 God will punish or avenge the Prophet's enemies. — reasons: Al-Māwardī gives vengeance as an alternative to support and victory; al-Ṭabrisī combines punishment of enemies with support for Prophet and religion; Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions retribution alongside victory.
  30:60/q02/p3 The Prophet's truthfulness will become clear. — reasons: Al-Rāzī says the certain promise consoles the Prophet by disclosing his veracity; Study Quran reports the interpretation from R.
  30:60/q02/p4 The patient will receive generous reward. — reasons: Abū ʿUthmān, transmitted by al-Sulamī, says confidence in that reward eases hardship and removes its pain.
  30:60/q02/p5 For a sincere seeker, whoever seeks God will find Him. — reasons: Al-Bursawī transmits Najm al-Dīn's inward application of the promise.
30:60/q03 What does ḥaqq say about the promise? [حَقٌّۭ · meaning]
  30:60/q03/p1 It is established and certain, expressed without exaggeration. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr treats al-ḥaqq as the substantive of ḥaqqa yaḥuqqu. Study Quran links the promise's truth to 30:6 and cross-references 4:122, 10:4, 10:55, 18:21, 28:13, 31:33, 35:5, 40:55, 40:77, 45:32, and 46:17.
30:60/q04 What does 'do not let them yastakhiffannaka' mean? [وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · meaning] (turns on: The root's lightness is applied to agitation or haste, and some glosses extend its effect to foolish error or departure from religion.)
  30:60/q04/p1 Do not let them unsettle, distress, agitate, or dislodge your steadiness. — reasons: Al-Azharī, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, names judgment and forbearance; al-Baghawī reports judgment and patience; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says remain unmoved despite lack of insight. Al-Nasafī cites what opponents say and do; al-Rāghib, via al-Bursawī, says their doubts must not dislodge belief. Al-Ālūsī mentions denial and abuse, including charges that messengers lie; Kur'an Yolu adds belittlement, doubt, and anxiety. Study Quran translates the verb 'disquiet.'
  30:60/q04/p2 Do not let them hurry or provoke you into haste or angry reaction. — reasons: Ibn Shajara gives 'make you hurry'; Yaḥyā 'provoke.' Al-Jushumī explains istikhfāf as seeking lightness: making a people light prompts haste, while overwhelming delight impairs judgment. Al-Jubbāʾī warns anger would violate patience and gentleness. Al-Nasafī mentions a premature request for punishment; Ibn ʿĀshūr says figurative lightness denotes panic or visible anger. Study Quran also gives rushing and unsteadiness.
  30:60/q04/p3 Do not let them lead you into foolishness, error, or transgression. — reasons: Al-Azharī says istakhaffa can mean treating someone as foolish and inducing error; al-Baghawī gives that gloss as well as impaired judgment. Al-Qurṭubī says making someone foolish enough to follow error; Study Quran reports from Q a sense of making someone heedless or ignorant, leading to error and transgression.
  30:60/q04/p4 Do not let them draw you down from your position or away from your religion. — reasons: Al-Naqqāsh gives the first gloss; al-Zajjāj and his later transmitters explain the warning as preventing opponents from provoking or unsettling the Prophet away from religion. Yaḥyā says he must not follow their call to abandon it.
30:60/q05 How is yastakhiffannaka read? [يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · readings] (turns on: The variants change the root from istikhfāf to istihqāq, or change the emphatic nūn's weight while retaining the prohibition.)
  30:60/q05/p1 The common reading has khāʾ and fāʾ, from istikhfāf, with an emphatic heavy nūn. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts the majority form with istihqāq; al-Samīn derives it from istikhfāf. Al-Biqāʿī says its emphatic prohibition is stronger than a bare prohibition and guards the foundations of religion against conceding to opponents. Al-Ālūsī says the majority geminate the nūn. The Qirāʾāt note specifies a third-person plural subject.
  30:60/q05/p2 The nūn is light or unvowelled, while the prohibition keeps its meaning. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the two alternative readers also transmit silent nūn in the istikhfāf form. Ibn al-Jawzī assigns sukūn to Yaʿqūb except Rawḥ and Zayd; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī assign it to Yaʿqūb. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī name Ibn Abī ʿAbla and Yaʿqūb; Qirāʾāt says lightening reduces emphasis. Al-Nashr points to its earlier treatment of Ruways's reading at Āl ʿImrān.
  30:60/q05/p3 An alternative reads yastaḥiqqannaka with ḥāʾ and qāf, from istihqāq. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Ibn Abī Isḥāq and Yaʿqūb; al-Samīn agrees. Al-Bayḍāwī glosses it as not letting opponents turn the addressee away. Al-Biqāʿī attributes it to Ruways from Yaʿqūb, connects ḥaqq with not oneself seeking a just settlement from opponents, and warns that worldly concessions invite demands for favoritism. Al-Ālūsī says not to let them win the addressee over until they are more entitled to him than believers; he applies this reading to the community because the Prophet is protected.
30:60/q06 How does the prohibition work grammatically? [وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · grammar]
  30:60/q06/p1 It is a prohibition with an emphatic nūn, formally directed at the opponents but functionally telling the Prophet not to be affected. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say the jussive prohibition with heavy nūn is built on fatḥa. Al-Samīn compares the idiom 'do not let me see you here.' Al-Bursawī says the apparent ban on unbelievers indirectly forbids the Prophet from yielding to contempt; al-Ālūsī similarly invokes the idiom and glosses it as not grieving fearfully over them. Al-Biqāʿī says emphasis strengthens the ban beyond a bare prohibition.
30:60/q07 What is the case and form of alladhīna? [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar]
  30:60/q07/p1 Alladhīna is nominative; some Arabs use alladhūna in the nominative. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī state the case; al-Naḥḥās reports the Arab usage الذون, also given by al-Qurṭubī as اللذون.
30:60/q08 Who lack certainty, and what do they doubt? [ٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُوقِنُونَ · referent] (turns on: The sources variously locate the missing certainty in revelation generally, the afterlife, imminent worldly punishment, divine help, or the promise itself; al-Qurṭubī also reports a named individual.)
  30:60/q08/p1 They are unbelievers or polytheists who reject the Prophet's message and remain doubtful or astray. — reasons: Yaḥyā calls them polytheists urging the Prophet to leave his religion. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, says they disbelieve what the Prophet brought. Al-Ālūsī names their accusations that messengers lie; al-Biqāʿī includes wavering doubters.
