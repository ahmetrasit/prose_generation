2:133/q37 How does Jacob's testament connect to Abraham and the surrounding verses? [* · links] (turns on: How ancestral testimony bears on present claims.)
  2:133/q37/p1 Jacob renews Abraham's bequest of monotheism and refutes later exclusive Jewish and Christian claims. — reasons: Razi says it strengthens Abraham's proof; Biqaʿi relates 2:112 and 2:134 to religious kinship. Kuranyolu and Tabatabaʾi call Islam inherited; Study Quran calls it primordial monotheism.
  2:133/q37/p2 Ancestral deeds and lineage do not save a person; one must obey God oneself. — reasons: Biqaʿi cites 2:134 and places religious kinship over blood. Abu Hayyan cites a warning to Banu Hashim and Fatima.
  2:133/q37/p3 Jacob's story is developed in Surah 12 and prophet lists. — reasons: Study Quran cites 12:38, 2:136 and 4:163.
2:133/q38 What do adjacent reports say about the tribes and revelation? [* · links] (turns on: Two related topics recorded alongside this verse.)
  2:133/q38/p1 Believers must affirm every prophet and revelation, including Jesus. — reasons: Tabari cites Qatada and Ibn ʿAbbas's report of Jews rejecting Jesus; versions name Rafiʿ or Nafiʿ ibn Abi Rafiʿ, and 5:59 is invoked.
  2:133/q38/p2 Jacob's twelve sons founded the tribes. — reasons: Qatada and Rabiʿ report twelve peoples; al-Suddi lists eight named sons; Ibn Ishaq gives wives and genealogies, citing 7:160 and leaving full knowledge to God.
2:133/q39 Can ancestor testimony alone ground knowledge of God? [* · theology] (turns on: How the sons acquired their faith.)
  2:133/q39/p1 The sons' reference to forefathers shows imitation suffices. — reasons: Abu Hayyan reports the traditionalists' inference; Razi says the verse itself proves neither imitation nor reasoning.
  2:133/q39/p2 God is known only by instruction from a prophet or imam. — reasons: Abu Hayyan reports the Taʿlimiyya's inference; Razi and Abu Hayyan deny that the verse establishes it.
  2:133/q39/p3 The sons' faith rested on reasoning, though the verse only records confession. — reasons: Razi argues that exclusive imitation or instruction is disproved; Abu Hayyan says the confession alone gives no acquisition method.
2:133/q40 Does a grandfather exclude siblings in inheritance? [ءَابَآئِكَ · law] (turns on: Whether extended kinship language entails the father's legal rule.)
  2:133/q40/p1 Yes; he stands as father and excludes siblings. — reasons: Abu Hanifa cites this verse and 12:38; Ibn ʿAbbas swears at the Black Stone and compares 4:11. Razi lists Abu Bakr and Aisha; Ibn Kathir adds Bukhari's Abu Bakr report and Abu Yusuf and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan's later choice.
  2:133/q40/p2 No; he shares with siblings, receiving the better of a share or one-third. — reasons: Razi lists Shafiʿi, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Ibn Masʿud, Zayd, Malik, Abu Yusuf and Muhammad. Zayd and Shafiʿi give the better share or third; Ibn Kathir includes Ahmad among non-exclusion advocates.
  2:133/q40/p3 No; he counts as a sibling but receives at least one-sixth. — reasons: Razi reports it; Abu Hayyan attributes it to Ali and Ibn Abi Layla.
  2:133/q40/p4 Calling him father in this verse does not settle his literal or legal status. — reasons: Shafiʿi argues generational rank, ordinary speech, Companion disagreement, grandmother inheritance and 4:11's grandson case against literal identity. Razi says Ibn ʿAbbas may mean legal status only.
2:133/q41 What levels of Islam and faith are described? [وَنَحْنُ لَهُۥ مُسْلِمُونَ · theology] (turns on: Degrees of outward profession and inward surrender.)
  2:133/q41/p1 Outward Islam professes God's oneness and the Messenger, while faith adds inward guidance. — reasons: Al-Sadiq compares Islam to the sanctuary and faith to the Kaaba inside it; outward profession governs life, marriage and inheritance. Tabatabaʾi calls this a first level.
  2:133/q41/p2 Deeper Islam is surrender with certainty and without inward objection. — reasons: Ali defines surrender as certainty; al-Sadiq says ritual acts with inward objection can still be association. Tabatabaʾi assigns this to a third level.
  2:133/q41/p3 A fourth level is continual remembrance, choosing God's will and an illuminated heart. — reasons: Miʿraj and Haritha reports describe divine love, hidden knowledge, visions and eased judgment. Tabatabaʾi says each level has a matching subtler unbelief or association; he relates 50:35 and unimaginable reward.
2:133/q42 What does the verse imply about human acts? [* · theology] (turns on: The relation of divine creation to human capacity.)
  2:133/q42/p1 Humans are compelled without choice. — reasons: Abu Hayyan reports the Jabriyya's moved-branch analogy; Ibn ʿAtiyya says the verse refutes denial of acquisition.
  2:133/q42/p2 Humans can bring their own acts into existence without compulsion. — reasons: Abu Hayyan reports the Qadariyya position.
  2:133/q42/p3 God creates capacity before the act, leaving a choice to do or omit it. — reasons: Abu Hayyan attributes this to the Muʿtazila.
  2:133/q42/p4 God creates capacity alongside the act, which the servant performs and acquires without compulsion. — reasons: Abu Hayyan presents the Sunni view. Abu al-Hasan defines acquisition as relation to capacity; Baqillani locates the act's moral quality in created capacity; Isfarayini joins divine and created capacities.
2:133/q43 How is Jacob's question applied inwardly? [مَا تَعْبُدُونَ · inward]
  2:133/q43/p1 It applies to the Qaʾim from Muhammad's family, whose members ask children the same question at death. — reasons: Al-Baqir states the application; al-Safi explains it. Tabatabaʾi allows inner applications beyond the outward verse.
2:133/q44 What do earlier scriptures add to the argument? [* · links]
  2:133/q44/p1 Elmalılı reads preserved scriptural promises as foretelling Muhammad, the culminating Ishmaelite prophet whom earlier communities had to follow. — reasons: He identifies Israel's 'brothers' with Ishmaelites; Habakkuk's Paran with Mecca; Isaiah's visitors and new praise with pilgrimage and talbiya; Torah's twelve princes with Muhammad's community. Greek Torah, Psalms, Gospel and Paraclete reports are cited, including Faraklit as intercessor or truth separator. Ibn ʿAbbas's Torah report promises two rewards; another names Mecca, Tayba, Syria and Hammādūn. He says Jews and Christians conceal or distort signs and links 2:40–41 and 2:121.
2:133/q45 What does the following verse's 'that community passed away' mean? [* · links]
  2:133/q45/p1 It refers to Abraham, Jacob and monotheistic descendants, a community now dead and separate. — reasons: Bursevi derives umma from an intended goal and followed group; 'passed away' evokes becoming empty.
2:133/q46 What spiritual rank has Jacob's family? [* · inward]
  2:133/q46/p1 They inherited a single monotheistic path as purified people near God, each with an assigned rank. — reasons: Qushayri states the inherited path and different shares of felicity.
2:133/q47 What indirect command is discussed beside the witness challenge? [* · rhetoric]
  2:133/q47/p1 'Do not let me see you here' forbids the presence causing the sight and conveys anger. — reasons: Abu Hayyan explains the indirect expression.
