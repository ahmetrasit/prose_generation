30:60/q09 Who receives the closing warning? [وَلَا يَسْتَخِفَّنَّكَ · referent] (turns on: The prophetic address can be taken as direct, extended to the community, or applied to the community particularly under the istihqāq reading.)
  30:60/q09/p1 It directly addresses the Prophet Muhammad. — reasons: Most explanations address his patience and steadiness amid opponents; al-Ālūsī says the prohibition, although formally cast with a plural subject, is directed to him.
  30:60/q09/p2 The address to the Prophet also intends his community. — reasons: Al-Qurṭubī says the warning is worded for him but intended for the community; Study Quran reports this interpretation from Q. Kur'an Yolu extends the closing encouragement to believers and callers.
  30:60/q09/p3 On the istihqāq reading it addresses the community, because the Prophet is protected. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī explains that the alternate reading warns against opponents becoming more entitled to the addressee than believers.
30:60/q10 How does the conclusion connect to the sūrah's argument? [* · links] (turns on: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly favors the sūrah-wide promise of victory over a reading centered on distress at rejected signs.)
  30:60/q10/p1 The close follows obstinate rejection and sealed hearts, so the Prophet must persevere. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī says God has just reported their sealed hearts. Ibn Kathīr cites the many examples given to clarify truth, the dismissal of signs as magic including the moon's splitting, and 10:96–97 on those who would not believe even after every sign. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the omitted object of patience as their obstinacy after 30:58. Al-Ālūsī and Study Quran likewise connect the exhortation to sealed hearts; al-Iṣlāḥī says only people of intellect receive the historical proofs in 46–60.
  30:60/q10/p2 The close returns to the sūrah's promise of divine help and victory. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 30:47 and says the sūrah begins and ends with promised help; he rejects making distress at denied signs the main reason for the patience command. Al-Biqāʿī connects the Romans' promised victory, which exposed doubters' error, with the promised Hour's justice for wrongdoers and reward for the virtuous. Al-Iṣlāḥī reads 46–60 as historical proofs of help. Study Quran links the promise's truth to 30:6 and lists 4:122, 10:4, 10:55, 18:21, 28:13, 31:33, 35:5, 40:55, 40:77, 45:32, and 46:17.
  30:60/q10/p3 The patience command responds chiefly to the Prophet's distress when disbelievers deny clear signs. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this explanation only to reject it, appealing instead to the victory promise at both ends of the sūrah.
30:60/q11 What happened when ʿAlī recited this verse in prayer? [* · reports]
  30:60/q11/p1 A Kharijite insinuated ʿAlī's shirk by reciting 39:65 during dawn prayer, and ʿAlī answered with 30:60. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī says Qatāda also transmits the episode. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says ʿAlī recognized the insinuation. Al-Ālūsī names Ibn Abī Shayba and others as transmitters and judges the response apt from the Prophet's brother and gate of the city of knowledge. Al-Māwardī, al-Durr, and al-Itqān likewise report the prayer exchange.
30:60/q12 What occasion involving al-Naḍr is reported? [* · reports]
  30:60/q12/p1 After al-Naḍr demanded immediate punishment, Muhammad was told not to be provoked; al-Naḍr was later killed at Badr. — reasons: Muqātil says ʿAlī beheaded al-Naḍr and the dead are displayed to the Fire morning and evening. Al-Wāḥidī transmits Muqātil's identification of doubters in worldly punishment with those punished at Badr; al-Qurṭubī reports naming al-Naḍr as the one lacking certainty.
30:60/q13 What other experiences illustrate endurance of opposition? [* · reports]
  30:60/q13/p1 After Abū Ṭālib died, abuse intensified; someone threw dust on the Prophet, and he assured his weeping daughter of God's protection. — reasons: Al-Bursawī says the daughter wiped the dust from his head and adds that the Companions also endured abuse and ultimately prevailed.
30:60/q14 What is reported about reciting Sūrat al-Rūm in dawn prayer? [* · reports]
  30:60/q14/p1 The Prophet stumbled during its recitation and attributed the confusion to some worshipers' poor ablution. — reasons: Ibn Kathīr reports his instruction to perform ablution well, grades the chain and text ḥasan, and infers that a follower's prayer is connected to the imam's.
30:60/q15 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Rūm? [* · sciences]
  30:60/q15/p1 The reciter receives ten rewards per angel praising God between heaven and earth, compensating for missed deeds by day and night. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this as a prophetic report.
30:60/q16 Was this verse abrogated? [* · sciences]
  30:60/q16/p1 Some exegetes say it was abrogated. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzī reports the claim without further argument in this note.
30:60/q17 May Qur'anic wording be used in other speech, as in ʿAlī's reply? [* · law] (turns on: The jurists differ over whether adapting Qur'anic wording into other expression is disliked or permitted.)
  30:60/q17/p1 Using Qur'anic expressions in poetry or other writing is permissible. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī states this without doubt in al-Itqān, alongside the report of ʿAlī answering a Kharijite with the verse during prayer.
  30:60/q17/p2 Some jurists dislike making parables from Qur'anic wording. — reasons: Al-Itqān transmits this from al-ʿImād al-Bayhaqī through al-Ṣalāḥ.
30:60/q18 How does the Qur'an present proof to deniers? [* · rhetoric]
  30:60/q18/p1 It uses every kind of clear proof, argument, distinction, and warning in familiar Arab speech. — reasons: Al-Suyūṭī says plain arguments convince ordinary listeners without obscure technical disputation while teaching specialists too; Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ counters al-Jāḥiẓ's claim that kalām argument is absent from the Qur'an and defines such argument as evidence decisively defeating an opponent.
30:60/q19 How is the verse applied to seekers on the inward path? [* · inward] (turns on: The inward notes apply patience both to enduring critics and to the seeker's own self-discipline and detachment.)
  30:60/q19/p1 Seekers must endure mockery from people who belittle the quest for God. — reasons: Al-Bursawī says even holders of inherited faith may lack certainty, deny that seeking God is obligatory, and scorn those who leave worldly life, family, and children. Al-Ālūsī applies the warning to heirs of the Muhammadan way and spiritual guides whose states, words, and deeds are rejected by veiled deniers.
  30:60/q19/p2 The seeker patiently detaches from worldly ties and inward attachment until the self gives way to union. — reasons: Al-Bursawī gives outward detachment then inward separation from happiness in either world, citing 64:14 and the saying of two steps followed by arrival. He reports ʿAṭṭār's clod dissolving in the sea and the Mathnawī's grammarian and boatman: grammar cannot prevent drowning, whereas self-effacement lets the sea carry the dead. Najm al-Dīn defines patience as disciplining desire, guarding the heart from ego traits, and surrendering the spirit's separate existence to divine generosity; Ḥāfiẓ urges steadfast love because it is rewarded.
