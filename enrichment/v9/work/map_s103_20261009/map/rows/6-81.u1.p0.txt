## THE CURRENT MAP
6:81/q01 What does Abraham deny fearing, and why? [وَكَيْفَ أَخَافُ مَآ أَشْرَكْتُمْ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the feared object is the idols' alleged power or punishment for the people's shirk.)
  6:81/q01/p1 He denies fearing their powerless idols, while their association with the God who controls harm and benefit is what warrants fear. — reasons: Al-Tabari, Ibn Ishaq, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, and others contrast inert idols with God's power; al-Baghawi says they neither see nor hear. Ibn ʿAtiyya calls them stone and wood. Al-Biqaʿi says fear of harm from idols is the danger denied (7:89). Al-Bursevi describes associating created things with the incomparable God as the greatest danger. Ibn ʿAshur says they acknowledge God; Islahi says their error corrupts monotheism rather than denying God.
  6:81/q01/p2 He need not fear divine punishment for his people's association, because he is free of their shirk. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi reports this as another interpretation of Abraham's question.
6:81/q02 What force does 'How should I fear?' have? [وَكَيْفَ أَخَافُ · rhetoric]
  6:81/q02/p1 It is a rebuking denial and challenge: fear of idols has no basis, and their failure to fear God is more astonishing. — reasons: Al-Wahidi says it challenges them to prove his fear justified. Al-Alusi says a question about the manner of fearing intensifies denial of fear itself and answers their own assumption. Ibn ʿAshur says it reverses their challenge and denies both their proposed fear and their misplaced fearlessness; Abu Hayyan calls it astonishment. Al-Qurtubi calls it rebuke.
6:81/q03 What is 'mā ashraktum'? [مَآ أَشْرَكْتُمْ · referent] (turns on: Whether mā names the objects, describes an indefinite object, or names the act of association.)
  6:81/q03/p1 It denotes the idols or other powerless objects they associate with God; mā may be a relative pronoun with an understood complement. — reasons: Al-Wahidi and Ibn ʿAbbas identify idols. Abu Hayyan says mā fits non-rational objects. Ibn ʿAshur supplies the omitted 'with which you associate' from the preceding clause and makes mā the object of ashraktum. Al-Samin and al-Alusi also allow a relative mā with omission.
  6:81/q03/p2 Mā may be an indefinite noun qualified by an omitted description or complement. — reasons: Al-Samin and al-Alusi give this syntactic alternative alongside the relative analysis.
  6:81/q03/p3 Mā may be a verbal noun, referring to their act of associating others with God. — reasons: Al-Samin gives the paraphrase 'your association'; al-Alusi also lists the verbal noun analysis.
6:81/q04 How is 'you do not fear' joined to Abraham's question? [وَلَا تَخَافُونَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether wāw coordinates two clauses in the question or introduces a circumstance.)
  6:81/q04/p1 It is coordinated with 'I fear,' so both failures of fear remain within the question and rebuke. — reasons: Abu Hayyan says the conjunction places both under one rebuke. Al-Samin lists this analysis. Ibn ʿAshur says it condemns both his alleged fear and their actual lack of fear.
  6:81/q04/p2 It is a circumstantial clause: how could he fear while they do not fear the consequences of their shirk? — reasons: Al-Samin says an implied subject can precede the present verb negated by lā, while wāw can itself link the circumstance without a resumptive pronoun. Al-Alusi says the circumstance sharpens the contrast between safety and real danger. Ibn ʿAshur prefers this force, comparing 2:44.
6:81/q05 Why does the verse contrast fear of their objects with fear of their association? [أَخَافُ مَآ أَشْرَكْتُمْ تَخَافُونَ أَنَّكُمْ أَشْرَكْتُم بِٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
  6:81/q05/p1 It avoids treating God and idols as comparable objects of fear while exposing their inverted judgment. — reasons: Abu Hayyan says the changed objects avoid equating God with idols. Al-Samin says Abraham fears their idols whereas they should fear their own act of associating with God. Al-Alusi contrasts their fearless conduct in actual danger with Abraham's safety.
6:81/q06 Why is God named explicitly in the second fear clause? [بِٱللَّهِ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the divine name serves emphasis and reverence or a grammatical necessity.)
  6:81/q06/p1 His name magnifies the offense of associating with Him and reflects their failure to honor Him; it was already mentioned in the preceding verse. — reasons: Al-Alusi reports that the first clause omits the name because 6:80 supplied it and to avoid even suggesting Abraham's association with God. He rejects the proposal that the second mention merely provides a pronoun antecedent or marks a different scope of denial; the antecedent was already available and the clauses do not share the proposed negation.
  6:81/q06/p2 The explicit name is needed as the antecedent of the following pronoun, or for a different scope of denial. — reasons: Al-Alusi reports and rejects these explanations: an antecedent was already available, and the two clauses do not combine under one negation.
6:81/q07 What do they fail to fear in 'that you associated with God'? [تَخَافُونَ أَنَّكُمْ أَشْرَكْتُم بِٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
  6:81/q07/p1 They fail to fear the danger and consequences of their own association of partners with God. — reasons: Ibn ʿAshur says min is omitted after 'fear' before an, as often happens; they knew and feared God yet failed to fear their act of association. Ibn Zayd contrasts fearing creatures without fearing God against fearing God alone; Ibn Kathir and al-Tabari stress that God can harm or benefit.
6:81/q08 What does the 'mā' in 'what He did not send down' refer to? [مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ سُلْطَٰنًۭا · grammar] (turns on: Whether mā is relative or indefinite; a verbal noun is excluded by sense.)
  6:81/q08/p1 It is a relative noun, the object of 'associate': a partner for which God sent no warrant. — reasons: Al-Samin allows a relative noun and rejects the verbal noun here because that would distort the sense.
  6:81/q08/p2 It is an indefinite noun describing a purported partner without divine warrant. — reasons: Al-Samin gives this as the other permissible construction.
6:81/q09 How do the prepositions with 'sent down' attach? [بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar] (turns on: Whether 'upon you' modifies the verb or the noun 'authority'.)
  6:81/q09/p1 Both 'with it' and 'upon you' modify 'sent down'; 'with it' may express accompaniment or cause. — reasons: Al-Samin attaches both phrases to 'send down.' Al-Tabatabai gives accompaniment and causation as senses of the preposition in 'with it.'
  6:81/q09/p2 'Upon you' can instead be circumstantial from 'authority.' — reasons: Al-Samin lists this as an alternative attachment.
6:81/q10 What does sulṭān mean here? [سُلْطَٰنًۭا · meaning] (turns on: Whether the word denotes evidentiary authority in this verse or its broader sense of power.)
  6:81/q10/p1 It is proof, warrant, or authoritative argument for the alleged partners, which they have not received. — reasons: Abu ʿUbayda also offers excuse; al-Zajjaj says clear proof. Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn ʿAbbas and seven other authorities saying every Qur'anic sulṭān is proof. Ibn al-Jawzi cites 6:81 and 11:96, 17:33, 30:35, 27:21, 55:33. Ibn ʿAshur says proof prevails over an opponent's mind. Al-Wahidi and al-Jishumi also allow power enabling one to prevail; al-Samin's entry supplies no separate definition.
  6:81/q10/p2 In other Qur'anic contexts sulṭān can mean kingship or coercive power. — reasons: Ibn al-Jawzi derives it from salāṭa, forceful extension or dominance, on the pattern faʿlān; he cites Satan's denial of power in 14:22 and 34:21. Al-Wahidi and al-Jishumi note the broader sense of enabling power even while discussing this verse.
6:81/q11 What does the lack of revealed authority establish, and could partners ever receive it? [مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ سُلْطَٰنًۭا · theology] (turns on: Whether the denial is solely the lack of disclosed warrant, rules out partnership in principle, or addresses images as a possible prayer direction.)
  6:81/q11/p1 Their claimed partners have no divine warrant; the absence of proof defeats their worship and fear. — reasons: Al-Tabari, al-Baydawi and Ibn ʿAshur specify no scripture or command authorizes worship. Abu Hayyan says rational refutation is joined by denial of revealed evidence. Ibn Kathir compares denials of invented religion in 42:21 and 53:23; Study Quran cites 7:33, 7:71, 12:40, 22:71, 53:23. Al-Tabatabai says any genuine partnership would require revelation or outward signs, and neither exists; if a warrant existed fear would be justified. Al-Biqaʿi says none shields them from God's anger.
  6:81/q11/p2 No authorization of genuine divine partners is rationally possible. — reasons: Al-Razi presents this implication and compares 23:117. Al-Alusi chooses it, dismissing the Imam's analogy of treating idols as a prayer direction as outside the question of partnership. Al-Tabatabai says the negative wording does not imply God might authorize partners.
  6:81/q11/p3 The wording denies a revealed command to worship images, while directing prayer toward them is not rationally impossible. — reasons: Al-Razi reports this alternative; al-Alusi says the Imam's prayer-direction analogy does not address actual divine partnership.
6:81/q12 What principle about religious claims follows from the missing warrant? [مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِۦ عَلَيْكُمْ سُلْطَٰنًۭا · interpretation]
  6:81/q12/p1 A religious claim needs evidence from God; an unsupported claim is wrong. — reasons: Al-Tabrisi states the general rule. Al-Bursevi and al-Alusi call the nonexistent 'authority' sarcastic, emphasizing evidence revealed by God. Al-Tabatabai says the absence of evidence itself settles the issue without needing to label the qualification sarcastic or inherent in false partners. Ibn Kathir links this to unauthorized religious law in 42:21 and 53:23.
