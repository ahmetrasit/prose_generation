## THE CURRENT MAP
83:2/q01 How does the relative clause beginning الذين relate to the condemned defrauders? [ٱلَّذِينَ · grammar] (turns on: Whether الذين qualifies المطففين in the genitive or is set in the accusative for condemnation; the clause may define the group or describe its conduct.)
  83:2/q01/p1 It is a genitive adjective identifying the defrauders by their conduct. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās gives the genitive adjectival analysis; al-Ālūsī says the clause can define which defrauders are intended; al-Būrsawī says it explains how they defraud and merit condemnation.
  83:2/q01/p2 It describes the defrauders' characteristic conduct. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī offers description as an alternative to defining the class; al-Būrsawī connects their taking in full and more with their condemnation.
  83:2/q01/p3 It is accusative by way of condemnation. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās offers this parsing and says it better suits the verse than the genitive adjective.
83:2/q02 What does اكتالوا mean here? [ٱكْتَالُوا۟ · meaning]
  83:2/q02/p1 They take or buy goods by measure from other people. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī and al-Būrsawī compare iktiyāl, taking by measure, with ittizān, taking by weight. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the measured object is omitted because the verb supplies it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies measured goods received from people.
83:2/q03 What does على الناس mean and what does على attach to? [عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar] (turns on: Whether على simply substitutes for من, conveys an owed claim or harm and superiority, or belongs syntactically to يستوفون; sources also differ over whether the two prepositional constructions are synonymous.)
  83:2/q03/p1 It attaches to اكتالوا and means taking measure from people; على can stand for من here. — reasons: Al-Farrāʾ and those reporting him ground the alternation in collecting what someone owes. Ibn Qutayba adduces Ṣakhr al-Ghayy's على أقطارها glossed من أقطارها. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī and others give the from-people gloss. Al-Samīn calls this analysis clearer; Abū Ḥayyān prefers attaching the phrase directly to اكتالوا. Al-Farrāʾ's two descriptions distinguish an amount owed from the act of receiving it; al-Samīn reports a reversed formulation. Al-Ālūsī notes objections that ordinary purchases need no prior debt, then suggests debts acquired in trade as the usual case.
  83:2/q03/p2 على implies that taking from people burdens, harms, or dominates them. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn connect the choice of على over من with injury and oppression. Al-Bayḍāwī allows either a due owed or oppressive taking. Al-Biqāʿī sees superiority over customers and exploitation of anyone, especially the weak. Al-Būrsawī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and al-Sāmarrāʾī describe domination or seizure; al-Ālūsī cites compacting and shaking the measure as means of harm. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise reads harm in على.
  83:2/q03/p3 على attaches to يستوفون; its fronting emphasizes that they take fully from others in particular. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī proposes this attachment and fronting; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān transmit it. Al-Samīn calls it good. Al-Ālūsī reports a criticism that taking fully from oneself is an implausible contrast, and a defense that the contrast is harm to others rather than themselves; he remains unconvinced.
  83:2/q03/p4 على here means مع, 'with the people' or 'at the people.' — reasons: Ibn Qutayba explicitly glosses it مع الناس; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Ṭabarī's 'at' or 'with' gloss.
  83:2/q03/p5 اكتال عليه expresses taking what is owed, whereas اكتال منه expresses receiving full measure; the constructions are distinct. — reasons: Al-Naḥḥās explicitly denies synonymy. Al-Farrāʾ as reported by several sources distinguishes taking what is due from receiving the full measure, although the reports differ in which expression receives which gloss.
83:2/q04 How can the verb take على if its ordinary complement is من? [ٱكْتَالُوا۟ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ · grammar]
  83:2/q04/p1 The verb carries an implied sense of imposing or bearing down on others, which licenses على. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī as quoted in al-Burhān supplies تحاملوا. Al-Burhān says a verb's preposition can follow an implied meaning; its examples construe الرفث as الإفضاء in 4:187 and drinking by a spring as being quenched by it in 76:6. Al-Qāḍī Abū Bakr gives a broader account of تضمين as unstated meaning supplied by form or semantic necessity: معلوم entails an عالم, and ضارب a مضروب; he calls it concise expression and illustrates it with the basmala's implied instruction to begin acts in God's name for reverence or blessing.
83:2/q05 Why is measuring, but not weighing, named for what they take from others? [ٱكْتَالُوا۟ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether measurement stands for both trading modes, reflects opportunities for fraud, or describes the actual goods and practices of the first traders.)
  83:2/q05/p1 Mentioning measurement suffices to imply weighing in trade. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj says buying and selling use both measured and weighed goods, so either term can indicate the other; al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Ṭabrisī transmit or accept this. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the reasoning applies equally to omitting measure and is arbitrary.
  83:2/q05/p2 Buying by measure offered more scope than buying by weight to take deliberate extra fullness. — reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says selling short is possible by either measure or weight, while acquiring extra fullness is easier by measure.
  83:2/q05/p3 The buyers usually purchased produce by measure and paid with weighed coin. — reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr uses the different treatment of buying and selling to identify the fraud as a merchants' practice.
  83:2/q05/p4 Dealers commonly bought grain in bulk and resold smaller measured portions. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this proposed explanation and calls it weak.
  83:2/q05/p5 The original cheaters bought only by measure but sold by both measure and weight. — reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this explanation and rejects it as unsupported.
83:2/q06 What does يستوفون say they take? [يَسْتَوْفُونَ · meaning] (turns on: Whether taking in full names exact receipt, whose blame arises from the contrasting shortfall, or also names taking excess through force and manipulation.)
  83:2/q06/p1 They insist on receiving the full amount due to themselves when buying. — reasons: Al-Zajjāj extends the demand to weight; al-Wāḥidī's commentators, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Baghawī's al-Zajjāj, Ibn al-Jawzī's al-Zajjāj, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad contrast this with shorting others. Ibn ʿĀshūr parses يستوفون as the answer to إذا and says the added letters intensify taking the full amount. Al-Ālūsī reports the objection that full receipt is ordinarily proper, then answers that the unequal paired conduct makes it blameworthy.
  83:2/q06/p2 They take more than their right or use tricks to obtain an abundant measure. — reasons: Al-Māwardī says the cheater takes more than owed; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Zajjāj's extra-for-self gloss. Al-Biqāʿī says full or more than full, and al-Būrsawī lists packing, shaking and taking from rims or scale tongues; al-Ālūsī also mentions compaction and shaking. Al-Būrsawī explicitly denies that this is merely fair receipt of an exact right.
83:2/q07 How is the next clause's pronoun read and connected with الناس? [ٱلنَّاسِ · readings] (turns on: Whether the following pronoun is the object of a joined verb or begins a new predication; the written alif, idiomatic construction and antecedent are cited.)
  83:2/q07/p1 The joined reading makes the pronoun the object: they measure for those people and give them short measure. — reasons: Al-Jishumī says the common reading is transitive; he prefers it because the written form lacks an alif, Arabic permits 'I measured your due' for 'for you,' and readers unanimously use it. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports al-Farrāʾ and al-Akhfash on dropping the preposition before the object, and Abū Zayd's poetic example. Al-Ṭabarī makes hum the object of the people shortchanged. Al-Burhān points to الناس as the pronoun's antecedent and the missing alif against the rival analysis. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this direct-object construction is Hijazi and Qurʾanic, while other Arabs use a preposition.
  83:2/q07/p2 A pause after 'they measure' makes the following pronoun a subject: 'they are defrauders.' — reasons: Al-Jishumī attributes this reading to ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and Ḥamzah. He and al-Burhān oppose it on the written form's lack of alif; al-Burhān also invokes الناس as antecedent of the object pronoun.
83:2/q08 What is the actual pattern of trade described by this verse and its next clause? [* · interpretation] (turns on: Whether the notes state the general unequal transaction or report the specific device of two sets of measures and scales.)
  83:2/q08/p1 The traders require full measure or weight when receiving goods but give less when supplying others. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī's commentators, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī's al-Zajjāj, al-Qurṭubī's al-Zajjāj, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad state the two-sided pattern. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reference to measure and weight on the selling side shows merchants' conduct. Al-Ālūsī says the combination explains why a normally lawful full receipt is censured.
  83:2/q08/p2 Some traders used two measures and two scales, changing them between buying and selling. — reasons: Muqātil reports this practice of a pre-Islamic market: buyers received extra while sellers delivered short.
