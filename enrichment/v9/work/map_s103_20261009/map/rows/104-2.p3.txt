[SAMIN-DURR:104:2/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A second interpretation takes عدده as the number of the man’s clan and relatives, whom he gathered around himself.
[SAMIN-DURR:104:2/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | A third analysis reads عدده as a past verb meaning عده, though that form is irregular in its expression.

[BIQAI:104:2/r1] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The wealthy who needlessly hoard are especially blameworthy in a culture that treats accumulation as success and boasts of profit; this description exposes the true loser. | mentions 104:1

[BIQAI:104:2/r3] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | holds | The unaugmented form of collecting inherently indicates abundance, unlike counting, which can refer to a small number.
[BIQAI:104:2/r4] BIQAI, d. 885 · author | reports | All readers agree on doubling ʿaddada because the undoubled form can refer to counting only a few things.

[BIQAI-FULL:v8p525/r4] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | The description singles out a wealthy person who needs no hoarding yet collects money, because people regard profit as success and boast of it; collecting wealth is the first sign of his loss.

[BIQAI-FULL:v8p526/r1] BIQAI-FULL, d. 885 · author | holds | The collected wealth is substantial; he counts it until enumeration would take a long time, or stores and hoards it for future worldly needs.

[BURSEVI:v10p391#2/r7] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | Accumulating wealth explains the mockery: self-admiration over money makes a person imagine wealth confers honor and superiority, so he belittles others; the phrase is appositional rather than a grammatical adjective of the indefinite ‘every.’
[BURSEVI:v10p391#2/r8] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | He repeatedly counts and hoards wealth without discharging God’s due; an alternate reading with the doubled consonant separated supports the sense ‘counted it,’ while another reading makes it provisions for future calamities.
[BURSEVI:v10p391#2/r9] BURSEVI, d. 1127 · author | holds | The accumulation signals appetitive power and repeated counting signals ignorance: wealth itself attracts the calamities through which divine wisdom disperses it, so it cannot ward them off.
