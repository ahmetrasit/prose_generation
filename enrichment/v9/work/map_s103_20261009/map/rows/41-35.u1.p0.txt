## THE CURRENT MAP
41:35/q01 What does yulaqqaha mean here? [يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the verb emphasizes reception of a granted quality or the person's effort to cultivate it.)
  41:35/q01/p1 To receive or be granted the virtue, and thus accept and practice it. — reasons: Jishumi explains talaqqi as receiving a report from another person and cites al-Mu'arrij's gloss 'accepting.' Bursevi says talqiya can mean bringing something before someone; here it is receiving the virtue that restrains revenge. Ibn Kathir stresses overcoming the soul's resistance to accept and practice difficult counsel.
  41:35/q01/p2 To make oneself attain the virtue, as though seeking and meeting someone. — reasons: Ibn Ashur takes the meeting as a metaphor for cultivation and compares 76:11.
41:35/q02 What does the feminine pronoun in yulaqqaha refer to? [يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · referent] (turns on: Which preceding idea supplies the feminine antecedent; the conduct in 41:34 is explicit in context, whereas some alternatives require a more remote referent.)
  41:35/q02/p1 The virtue or better response of repelling evil with good, described in the previous verse. — reasons: Zajjaj allows the act itself. Jishumi calls it the stated virtue or way of life; Bagha wi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi's report from Zajjaj, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and al-Alusi identify the conduct or disposition. Al-Alusi also allows the better response itself. Ibn Ashur says ordinary good deeds need no such patience, so the antecedent is specifically best conduct in repelling. Islahi says the antecedent is unstated, a common Arabic construction, and compares 2:45.
  41:35/q02/p2 The recompense for the virtuous response. — reasons: Zajjaj offers receiving the reward for the act as an alternative to receiving the act itself.
  41:35/q02/p3 Righteous deeds. — reasons: Jishumi transmits this alternative without further grounds.
  41:35/q02/p4 Paradise itself. — reasons: Al-Qurtubi says the sense remains close because the patient attain Paradise. Al-Samin lists the Garden among possible antecedents; al-Alusi reports but dismisses it.
  41:35/q02/p5 The testimony that there is no god but God. — reasons: Makki gives this explanation in Abu Hayyan, who finds it far-fetched. Al-Samin lists the testimony of divine unity; al-Alusi reports it as construing the testimony as the best response but finds it unpersuasive.
  41:35/q02/p6 The word mentioned previously. — reasons: Al-Samin lists 'the word' alongside the quality, Garden and testimony, without further identification.
41:35/q03 How is yulaqqaha read? [يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · readings] (turns on: Whether the verb is the passive of talqiya or derives from mulaqat, meeting.)
  41:35/q03/p1 The common passive reading means that the trait is received or granted. — reasons: Abu Hayyan attributes this to the majority; al-Samin calls it the passive of talqiya.
  41:35/q03/p2 Talha ibn Musarrif and Ibn Kathir in one transmission read yulaqaha, meaning to meet or encounter it. — reasons: Abu Hayyan gives the meaning and names both readers; al-Samin derives it from mulaqat; al-Alusi reports the same variant.
41:35/q04 What is the syntax of the clause beginning wa-ma yulaqqaha? [وَمَا يُلَقَّىٰهَآ · grammar] (turns on: How this clause attaches grammatically to the command in 41:34.)
  41:35/q04/p1 It is coordinated with the preceding command. — reasons: Ibn Ashur gives this as one grammatical analysis and restricts the pronoun to the best way of repelling, since good deeds in general need not require patience.
  41:35/q04/p2 It is circumstantial to 'that which is best' in the preceding command. — reasons: Ibn Ashur gives this as his other grammatical analysis.
41:35/q05 Who are the patient people able to receive this response, and why? [ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا۟ · interpretation] (turns on: The notes specify patience as control of anger and harm, wider obedience and desire-restraint, inward endurance, or a strong soul; Biqa'i stresses divine enabling.)
  41:35/q05/p1 Those who endure harm, restrain anger and forgo retaliation can answer evil with good. — reasons: Zajjaj and Ibn al-Jawzi stress restraining anger; al-Zajjaj as reported by al-Razi adds swallowing distress, enduring hardship and leaving revenge. Al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursevi, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur and Study Quran connect patience with bearing injury and controlling the urge to retaliate. Al-Tabrisi reports Abu Abd Allah's formulation as enduring worldly harm. Ibn Ashur says this must be trained with resolve and links the patient exception to 103:2-3. Islahi calls patience necessary to acquire enduring wisdom; Kuran Yolu calls it the first condition of a high ethical aim. Ibn Kathir says the soul resists this difficult counsel. Baghawi, Tha'labi, Biqa'i and Tabataba'i likewise restrict this virtue to patient people.
  41:35/q05/p2 Those steadfast in obedience and in turning away from desires receive it; patience encompasses many good qualities. — reasons: Al-Mawardi offers this as an alternative under the first restriction, connecting their patience with Paradise. Ibn Atiyya says patience in obedience and restraint from desires gathers goodness; Ibn Abbas as reported by Abu Hayyan describes the same steadfastness and calls the verse praise.
  41:35/q05/p3 Those whose souls are strong and pure can bear external harm without seeking revenge. — reasons: Al-Razi reasons that a strong inner substance is not shaken or hurt by events; Bursevi calls revenge a sign of weakness, says purification makes sweetness and bitterness alike, and regards inner strength as making injury easy to bear. Elmalili describes a high soul and spiritual virtue.
  41:35/q05/p4 Those patient with God, trusting and imitating His forbearance, receive the trait. — reasons: Qashani describes remaining unchanged by enemies' slips, trusting God, taking on His forbearance or obeying His command, and calls the station a great gift.
  41:35/q05/p5 Those who endure the hardships of spiritual knowledge without self-regard enter its company. — reasons: Al-Sulami transmits this inward explanation, which makes endurance of spiritual knowledge and lack of self-valuation the qualifications.
  41:35/q05/p6 God grants the difficult virtue to those already patient. — reasons: Biqa'i draws this from the repeated passive 'is granted,' says the better response is contrary to human disposition and should be sought from God, and ranks it beyond forgiveness and forbearance. Junayd in Bursevi describes the station as special divine care.
41:35/q06 What does a great share mean here? [حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍۢ · interpretation] (turns on: Whether hazz names the present person's qualities and fortune, a future reward, Paradise specifically, or an inward allotment.)
  41:35/q06/p1 Paradise, or a destined share of it. — reasons: Qatada is reported by Abd al-Razzaq, al-Tabari, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Tabrisi and others; Ibn Atiyya says Qatada chooses it. Zajjaj says only those to whom Paradise is due receive it; Ibn Abbas in al-Tabari speaks of people prepared for Paradise; al-Hasan in al-Mawardi, al-Qurtubi and al-Nasafi says no share exceeds Paradise. Mujahid is named with Qatada by al-Qurtubi. Al-Baydawi, al-Alusi, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran also report the view.
  41:35/q06/p2 A great share of goodness, good deeds, virtue, or general prosperity. — reasons: Al-Tabari adds a prior record of good deeds; Zajjaj and Ibn Abbas in al-Mawardi and al-Qurtubi speak of abundant goodness. Tha'labi and Baghawi add reward; al-Mawardi also offers excellent character. Al-Baydawi adds perfection of soul; Ibn Ashur specifies virtue, right guidance and piety, with patience necessary but only part of the qualification. Tabataba'i calls it human perfection and good qualities, especially belonging to the patient; Biqa'i adds standing with God and people in this life and the next. Ibn Ashur says the 'possessor' construction presents a settled disposition. Kuran Yolu says supporting virtues adorn the patient. Study Quran reports virtue and inner purity.
  41:35/q06/p3 A large share of afterlife reward, making the clause a promise. — reasons: Al-Razi contrasts this with the first clause's praise of patience. Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi and Kuran Yolu report the reward reading; Qatada is named by Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi, while Ibn Abbas in al-Nasafi calls it reward. Ibn Kathir describes abundant happiness in this life and the next.
  41:35/q06/p4 Intelligence, reason, and insight. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya allows great intelligence and merit; al-Tabrisi says abundant insight and reason. Abu Hayyan reports intelligence; Kuran Yolu relays Ibn Atiyya's view; Study Quran reports abundant intellect and vision.
  41:35/q06/p5 Good fortune or prosperity. — reasons: Al-Suddi is reported by al-Tabari and Ibn al-Jawzi; al-Mawardi renders it great prosperity. Study Quran reports the good-fortune gloss.
  41:35/q06/p6 Noble character, spiritual strength, or a perfected soul. — reasons: Al-Mawardi suggests excellent character; Abu Hayyan reports good character. Ibn Abbas in al-Alusi says excellent traits and a perfected soul, so the clause praises the person. Bursevi and Elmalili explain the allotment through inner strength and a high soul; Study Quran reports elevated virtue, strength and purity.
  41:35/q06/p7 Simply a share, without identifying its object. — reasons: Tha'labi transmits the bare gloss 'a person with a share.' Ibn Ashur defines hazz lexically as a share, sometimes particularly a share of good.
  41:35/q06/p8 Knowledge of God and His days, or special divine favor. — reasons: Ibn Ata in Bursevi identifies the person by knowledge of God and His days; Junayd says only someone favored by God's special care receives the station.
