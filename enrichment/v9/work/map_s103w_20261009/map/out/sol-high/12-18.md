# 12:18: 30 questions

## 12:18/q01 Why use 'came' here?  [وَجَآءُو · rhetoric]
- 12:18/q01/p1 It describes bringing a concrete item. — Suyuti contrasts jāʾa for concrete things with atā for meanings and times, citing God's command. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 12:18/q02 What did the brothers do to Joseph's shirt?  [عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in details of its preparation and presentation.
- 12:18/q02/p1 They stripped it, stained it with slaughtered animal blood, and brought it intact as evidence. — Muqatil says they stripped it after casting him in the well; Study Quran says before. Ibn Kathir says the blood reinforced their plot. Genesis is cited for the special robe. [28 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(السدي,الشعبي); ZAJJAJ(يروى); IBNABIHATIM(الشعبي,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(IṬ)]
- 12:18/q02/p2 The bloodstained shirt was fabricated evidence. — Tabatabai notes scant blood and the intact shirt gave it little force. [2 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 12:18/q04 How did the shirt expose the brothers' story?  [عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Versions add different clues and episodes.
- 12:18/q04/p1 A wolf would have torn the garment or left marks. — Jacob found the shirt intact and ironically called the wolf gentle, merciful, or patient. Samarrai notes that 'ate' avoids the implication of torn clothing in 'preyed on'. [37 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الشعبي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي,الشعبي,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن؛ سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI(الحسن,الشعبي); RAZI-FULL(السدي,سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(IṬ, Ṭ)]
- 12:18/q04/p2 When robbers were blamed, Jacob asked why they left the useful shirt. — Their incompatible accounts heightened suspicion, according to Wahidi, Tabarsi, Razi, Qurtubi and Alusi. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي عن ابن عباس); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:18/q04/p3 The brothers presented a bloodied wolf, which spoke and denied eating Joseph. — Ibn Atiyya says its mouth was smeared; Baghawi says it came to visit kin. ## 12:18/q05 What three signs are attached to Joseph's shirt?  [عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ · links]
turns on: Whether these signs involve one garment or several.
- 12:18/q05/p1 False blood, a tear from behind proving innocence, and restoration of Jacob's sight. — Sha'bi, Shafi'i and many sources connect 12:18 with 12:25 and 12:93/96. Samarrai frames the shirts as marking grief's beginning and end and three stages of Joseph's story. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عامر الشعبي); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشافعي); TABRISI(الشعبي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 12:18/q05/p2 The three signs concern different shirts. — Qurtubi rejects Mawardi's formulation and notes a variant identification of the torn garment with the herald's. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); MAWARDI-FULL-]

## 12:18/q06 How is 'on his shirt' grammatically attached?  [عَلَىٰ قَمِيصِهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: Dispute concerns adverbial sense and advance of a circumstance before its genitive.
- 12:18/q06/p1 It is a circumstance of the blood placed before its noun. — Abu al-Baqa restores the order 'blood on his shirt'; Ibn Ashur agrees. Al-Samin cites grammarians and poetry allowing the advance; Abu Hayyan finds its sense suitable. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:18/q06/p2 It means 'above the shirt' as an adverb. — Zamakhshari rules out a fronted circumstance from a genitive. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin object that arriving people cannot be above a shirt. Alusi prefers blood placed above it. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 12:18/q06/p3 It attaches to 'they came'. — Al-Hawfi offers this; al-Samin and Abu Hayyan object that arrival cannot literally be on a shirt. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 12:18/q06/p4 They arrived possessing the shirt, the blood being circumstantial. — Alusi reports this minor alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:18/q07 What does 'false blood' mean?  [بِدَمٍۢ كَذِبٍۢ · meaning]

- 12:18/q07/p1 It was actual blood falsely claimed to be Joseph's. — The lie was the brothers', not the blood's. Akhfash and Ibn Qutayba compare inanimate attributions and 2:16; Nahhas compares 'ask the village' (12:82). Burhan notes literal lying is speech. [26 notes: AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء؛ أبو العباس؛ الزجاج؛ ابن الأنباري؛ الأخفش); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:18/q07/p2 The verbal noun 'lie' intensively describes the blood as falsehood itself. — Ibn Atiyya prefers this, rejecting Tabari's poetic evidence; Kashshaf calls it hyperbole; Tabatabai treats the blood as actively lying. Wahidi cites 2:16, 67:30, 68:6. [24 notes: FARRA(أبو ثروان); TAB-FULL(الراعي); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء؛ أبو العباس؛ الزجاج؛ ابن الأنباري؛ الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 12:18/q07/p3 'Lie' is a verbal noun with passive sense: falsified blood. — Farra cites ma'qul, majlud and poetry; Abu Tharwan uses makdhuba. Tabari's Kufan analysis agrees. Tabrisi says Sibawayh rejects the analysis and supplies ellipsis. [18 notes: FARRA(أبو ثروان); TAB-FULL(الراعي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء؛ أبو العباس؛ الزجاج؛ ابن الأنباري؛ الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 12:18/q07/p4 An omitted noun such as 'possessing a lie' explains the attribution. — The Basran grammarian and Nahhas compare other figurative attribution of a property to a noun. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء؛ أبو العباس؛ الزجاج؛ ابن الأنباري؛ الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]

## 12:18/q08 How is the word for 'false' read?  [بِدَمٍۢ كَذِبٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Variants change consonant or case.
- 12:18/q08/p1 The common reading has dotted dhāl, kadhib. — Jishumi explicitly contrasts this with Aisha's dāl reading. [2 notes: JISHUMI(عائشة); TABRISI]
- 12:18/q08/p2 An undotted dāl gives kadb/kadib. — Attributed to Aisha, Hasan, Ibn Abbas and Abu al-Aliya; glosses vary among fresh, murky, dry and discolored. Ibn Jinni derives a patterned mark from white nail spots. Ibn Atiyya calls it weak. - 12:18/q08/p3 The accusative kadhaban is read. — Farra allows adverbial 'came falsely'; others parse a circumstance of brothers or blood, or a causal object. Burhan compares 100:1; Alusi notes an indefinite circumstantial head is disfavored. [8 notes: FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); BURHAN(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:18/q11 What matter did their souls devise?  [أَمْرًۭا · referent]

- 12:18/q11/p1 An ugly act against Joseph, not the wolf attack. — Tabari says an act concerning Joseph; Ibn Ashur allows killing, sale, or banishment and says indefiniteness sounds ominous. ## 12:18/q12 How did Jacob recognize the lie?  [قَالَ بَلْ · interpretation]
turns on: Sources offer inference from signs, revelation, promise or intuition.
- 12:18/q12/p1 He inferred it from intact shirt, envy, or incompatible claims. — Jacob knew the brothers' envy; a wolf should leave marks and robbers would take a shirt. [20 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن؛ سعيد بن جبير,الكلبي عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:18/q12/p2 God disclosed it by revelation or an earlier promise that Joseph would survive. — Mawardi and Tabarsi list revelation; Razi cites God's choice of Joseph in 12:6; Tabatabai's report invokes the truthful dream. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين عليه السلام)]
- 12:18/q12/p3 A sound intuition perceived it. — Mawardi and Tabarsi allow intuition; Qushayri generalizes insight of truthful hearts. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI]

## 12:18/q13 How is 'beautiful patience' parsed and read?  [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · grammar]

- 12:18/q13/p1 Nominative: 'my affair or settled practice is beautiful patience'. — Sibawayh compares raised Arabic answers; Khalil and Zajjaj prefer this. ## 12:18/q14 What is beautiful patience?  [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · interpretation]

- 12:18/q14/p1 Endurance without distress, despair or complaint to people, though complaint to God is allowed. — Mujahid, prophetic reports and many exegetes give this sense. Alusi rejects this as far from Jacob's apparent meaning. [7 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL(وقيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 12:18/q14/p3 Bearing Joseph's loss and the grief of separation. — Mawardi gives this alternative; Qatada applies patience to their false account. [5 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:18/q14/p4 Contented acceptance of God's wise decree for His sake. — Tustari cites God being with the patient (2:153), remembered ease and honey beneath the bitter cup. Murtada calls for dutiful endurance. Razi cites divine ownership, wisdom, mercy and unchanging love. Tirmidhi describes surrender with true knowledge. [13 notes: TUSTARI; TABRISI(المرتضى); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الترمذي); TABATABAI]
- 12:18/q14/p5 Upright steadiness preserves deliberation and relies on God for recovery. — Tabatabai calls patience a defensive fortress, with God the further cause of recovery; it permits resistance to harm. Ibn Ashur cites the sound hadith on patience at first shock. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(الشبلي)]
- 12:18/q14/p6 It shows no visible change and meets affliction with an open heart. — Wahidi's people of meanings prohibit a frown; Yahya ibn Mu'adh describes a cheerful face. Alusi rejects construing this as Jacob's promise to face the sons cheerfully. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); ALUSI-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 12:18/q15 Did Jacob's grief conflict with his patience?  [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · theology]
turns on: Complaint to God and reports of rebuke are distinguished.
- 12:18/q15/p1 Grief and complaint to God can coexist with patience. — Hasan and Razi invoke 12:86. Tabatabai says Jacob's love and grief were for God's sake. Study Quran cites hope after hardship at 70:5. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 12:18/q15/p2 A report has God question Jacob's long lament and Jacob ask forgiveness. — Habib ibn Abi Thabit/Thabit says Jacob's brows fell over his eyes from grief; versions describe a cloth or bandage and differ in wording. [6 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(حبيب بن أبي ثابت); TAB-FULL(يعقوب); THALABI(حبيب بن ثابت); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(حبيب بن أبي ثابت); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:18/q15/p3 He cried and could neither sleep nor eat. — Muqatil links this to Jewish Torah wailing; Islahi contrasts the Qur'anic portrait with Torah's sackcloth and mourning. [2 notes: MUQATIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 12:18/q16 Why did Jacob not search or seek justice immediately?  [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · theology]
turns on: Razi asks why patience would justify leaving injustice unremedied; sources propose several replies.
- 12:18/q16/p1 God barred investigation to heighten his trial. — Razi calls this the decisive answer; Alusi transmits it. Ibn Ishaq and Tabarsi stress the test of Jacob's old age and Joseph's youth. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); TABRISI]
- 12:18/q16/p2 The sons could obstruct or harm him, or inquiry could worsen matters. — Razi and Alusi give this possibility. Biqai says asking for remains could prompt worse harm. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 12:18/q16/p3 He trusted God's protection and Joseph's future. — Razi points to 12:6; Tabatabai's report says Jacob trusted the truthful dream. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين عليه السلام)]
- 12:18/q16/p4 He avoided disgracing or punishing his sons. — Alusi cites a poem about a father wounded whether he punishes, pardons or strikes both sons. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرازي)]
- 12:18/q16/p5 Old age and lack of allies made him dependent on the suspected sons until he later sent them to search. — Ibn Ashur and Biqai cite 12:87; Tabatabai says his only powerful helpers were the brothers. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 12:18/q16/p6 Patience permits action against wrongdoing when possible. — Razi insists on removing harm to others; Tabatabai describes inner steadiness with resistance as able. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 12:18/q17 For what does Jacob seek God's help?  [وَٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: He may seek endurance, disclosure or both.
- 12:18/q17/p1 Help enduring their lies, alleged loss and grief. — Tabari and Qatada stress their lies; Mawardi lists patience, their account and its falsity. Nahhas parses a nominal sentence and calls ʿalā mā taṣifūn figurative. Razi and Alusi connect patience and help to 1:5. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي وأبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 12:18/q17/p2 Help exposing the lie and reuniting with Joseph. — Bursevi and Alusi speak of vindication; Alusi cites 12:83. Tabatabai describes exclusive divine agency and omission of Jacob's self. Ibn Ashur allows a prayer to free Joseph. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:18/q17/p3 Help bearing the trial and bitter patience. — Jishumi and Tabarsi report this alternative; Ibn Ashur allows prayer or statement of reliance. [3 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي وأبو مسلم); TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:18/q18 What is 'what you describe'?  [وَٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ · referent]
turns on: Jacob may mean their claim or the actual unknown harm.
- 12:18/q18/p1 The false claim that Joseph died by a wolf. — Qatada calls it lies; Ibn Ashur says that is the sons' intended referent. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 12:18/q18/p2 The unknown injury done to Joseph. — Ibn Ashur says Jacob knew the sons lied but did not know how they harmed him; the ambiguity resembles 37:180. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI]

## 12:18/q19 What legal lesson is drawn from the intact shirt?  [* · law]
- 12:18/q19/p1 Circumstantial signs may be weighed and acted on, including in qasama. — Ibn Atiyya attributes this to Malik's school; Qurtubi cites Ibn al-Arabi's claim of consensus on such indications. Jishumi says tears alone do not establish a claimant and the brothers' race was permissible since uncondemned. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 12:18/q20 What is learned about prophetic knowledge and the brothers' status?  [* · theology]
turns on: These are distinct theological inferences recorded with the episode.
- 12:18/q20/p1 Prophets may temporarily believe a liar before revelation. — Ibn Atiyya cites the Prophet's initial belief of Abd Allah b. Ubayy over Zayd b. Arqam and says prophets judge by what they hear. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 12:18/q20/p2 Jacob had truthful insight before the details were clear. — Qushayri says truthful hearts sense outcomes generally. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 12:18/q20/p3 The brothers' crime preceded prophethood, if they were prophets. — Baydawi qualifies the claim; Bursevi questions their commission because prophets are protected even before mission from repellent acts. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(البيضاوي)]
- 12:18/q20/p4 A single lie and betrayal did not make the brothers hypocrites. — Biqai says hypocrisy requires habitual or predominant breach of trust. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 12:18/q21 What lessons does this scene convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:18/q21/p1 Falsehood exposes itself through conflicting consequences. — Tabatabai says connected events cannot all be disguised; the intact shirt betrays the lie, citing 39:3, 40:28, 16:116 and 50:5. Qushayri says failure to tear it punished the brothers. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABATABAI]
- 12:18/q21/p2 Patience in trial must be joined to reliance on God. — Jishumi states this; Wahidi and Tabatabai connect the two. Asad views Joseph's story as divine direction of events, citing 2:216. [5 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:18/q21/p3 God tested Jacob and Joseph through grief and enslavement, then brought relief. — Ibn Ishaq says their resolve was tested; Tabarsi says this occurred under divine watch. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); TABRISI]

## 12:18/q22 Is there an inward reading?  [* · inward]
- 12:18/q22/p1 The senses scheme while the faith-lit spirit sees and accepts God's decree. — Bursevi gives this analogy; Ta'wilat Najmiyya reads the appeal as asking for patience with decree. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 12:18/q23 How did Aisha invoke this verse?  [* · reports]
- 12:18/q23/p1 During the slander ordeal she compared herself to Jacob, invoked beautiful patience and God's help, and was then vindicated. — Alusi says her accusers would accept neither oath nor excuse. Ibn Hisham and Muslim report Aisha refusing to confess or repent for a false accusation; Ibn Hisham adds that she expected her denial would not be believed. Bukhari reports witness testimony supporting her innocence and identifies the vindication as ten revealed verses, 24:11–20. Muslim reports that she invoked Jacob as Abu Yusuf. [8 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); ALUSI-FULL(عائشة); IBNHISHAM(عائشة); BUKHARI(عائشة); MUSLIM(أبو يوسف)]

## 12:18/q24 How old was Joseph when put in the well?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ, and Abu Hayyan prefers youth.
- 12:18/q24/p1 Seven, possibly six. — Abu Hayyan favors seven: wolf fear, play, a passerby's 'boy', and possible adoption all suggest a child. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 12:18/q24/p2 Ten. — Thalabi reports it. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 12:18/q24/p3 Twelve. — Hasan is cited by Abu Hayyan, who doubts the older estimates. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 12:18/q24/p4 Seventeen. — Thalabi and Ibn al-Sa'ib report it; Abu Hayyan doubts it. [2 notes: THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 12:18/q24/p5 Eighteen. — Thalabi and Hasan report it; Abu Hayyan doubts it. [2 notes: THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 12:18/q25 How long was Joseph in the well?  [* · reports]
- 12:18/q25/p1 Three days. — Thalabi, Baghawi and Tabarsi report this. [3 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 12:18/q26 What associated reports counsel response to calamity?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports give distinct counsel.
- 12:18/q26/p1 Recalling a calamity and saying resignation earns the original reward again. — A Prophetic report in Abd al-Razzaq. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 12:18/q26/p2 Hamna grieved especially for her husband despite words of resignation for several deaths. — The Prophet named Abdullah, Hamza and Mus'ab, showing a spouse's special place. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 12:18/q26/p3 Full patience occurs at the first shock. — Ibn Ashur calls sound the hadith of a grieving woman who later learned the one advising her was the Prophet. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 12:18/q27 What additional context do the extracted sources give?  [* · sciences]
turns on: These are distinct ancillary discussions outside the verse's direct wording.
- 12:18/q27/p1 Metaphor and tense must be read by Arabic usage. — Ibn Qutayba rejects critics who call figurative inanimate attributions false; he says 'God was' never means God began to exist. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الطاعنون على القرآن بالمجاز)]
- 12:18/q27/p2 Tha'labi's commentary combines twenty-four fields and cites earlier works; Ibn Abbas received a prophetic prayer for interpretation. — Fields include readings, Arabic, law, mystical allusions, virtues and reports; listed sources let him omit repeated chains. [3 notes: THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 12:18/q27/p3 Seven canonical readings gained consensus, while anomalous ones are transmitted but not recited in prayer. — Ibn Atiyya discusses regional transmission, the undotted and unvowelled early codex, and unreliable attributions. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 12:18/q27/p4 The Qur'an guards and witnesses over previous scripture. — Ibn Kathir reports Ibn Abbas and others on al-Muhaymin at 5:48 and explains haymana as guarding; he also reports a ten-year Meccan and ten-year Medinan revelation chronology and orders sura virtues before commentary. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,عائشة وابن عباس,مجاهد والسدي وقتادة وابن جريج والحسن البصري)]
- 12:18/q27/p5 Qur'an recitation should be beautiful, sorrowful and humble. — Ibn Kathir cites the voice hadith, calls its chain good, discusses transmitter reliability and Abu Ubayd's caution about invented tunes, rejects withholding sound reports, and cites Abu Musa. [8 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو عبيد,أبو موسى,البراء بن عازب,السلف,يحيى بن سعيد القطان)]
- 12:18/q27/p6 Teaching memorized Qur'an may serve as a marriage dower. — Ibn Kathir cites the man with no iron ring, favors Ahmad's understanding, and notes disputes about fees and specificity. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل,سهل بن سعد)]
- 12:18/q27/p7 Qur'anic inward and outward meanings call for learning and piety. — Burhan cites seven ahruf, Abu al-Darda's multiple aspects, and levels of hearing from reciter, Prophet, Gabriel and God; Yahya b. Mu'adh likens mouths to shops. [6 notes: BURHAN(أبو الدرداء,رسول الله,يحيى ابن معاذ الرازي)]
- 12:18/q27/p8 Separated madd has various short-duration routes. — Al-Nashr names Abu Ja'far, Ibn Kathir, Qalun, Warsh's Asbahani route and Abu Amr. [4 notes: NASHR]
- 12:18/q27/p9 Other source-context distinctions concern diction, judgment and punishment. — Itqan distinguishes zann, khashya from khawf, and shuhh from bukhl. Burhan says punishments fit offenses, the world is not universal recompense, and God protects threatened believers, exemplified by Ibrahim and Muhammad. Biqai notes wasila as rank, need and access. [10 notes: ITQAN(الراغب,الزركشي); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(القزاز)]

## 12:18/q28 What further detail is reported about Jacob's immediate reaction?  [* · reports]
- 12:18/q28/p1 He kissed or wept over the shirt until his face was stained; another account has him collapse and revive near dawn. — Alusi transmits the variant accounts; in the latter Judah checked Jacob's breath and lamented the loss of brother and father. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:18/q29 What caution is drawn from Jacob's repeated suspicion of his sons?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:18/q29/p1 Even prudent people should question their own judgment. — Ibn Abi Rifaʿa says Jacob rightly suspected them about Joseph, but repeated the same charge when they reported Benjamin's theft, where his suspicion failed; Qurtubi cites 12:17 and 12:81. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن أبي رفاعة)]

## 12:18/q30 What does the wording 'beautiful patience' evoke?  [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the implied quality is grandeur or bitterness.
- 12:18/q30/p1 Its indefinite form may magnify the grandeur of patience. — Tabatabai says the phrase praises patience by naming the cause for its result; an omitted description may suggest grandeur. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 12:18/q30/p2 Its indefinite form may suggest the bitterness and difficulty of patience. — Tabatabai offers this as the other implication of the indefinite form and omitted description. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
