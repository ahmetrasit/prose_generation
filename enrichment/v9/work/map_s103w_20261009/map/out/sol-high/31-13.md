# 31:13: 32 questions

## 31:13/q01 Who was Luqmān?  [لُقْمَٰنُ · reports]
turns on: Reports about his status, occupation, appearance, and identification differ; Ibn ʿĀshūr weighs narrative wording and report chains.
- 31:13/q01/p1 He was a wise, pious man, not a prophet. — Ibn ʿUmar transmits a Prophetic description of a reflective, convinced servant loved by God and given wisdom. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the majority and Mālik's title al-ḥakīm; the story mentions neither revelation nor angels and presents counsel rather than legislation. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عمر,الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 31:13/q01/p2 He was a prophet. — ʿIkrima and al-Shaʿbī argue that wisdom can mean prophethood, as with David and 2:269; God's instruction concerning parents appears amid his counsel (31:14). [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(عكرمة والشعبي); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 31:13/q01/p3 He worked as a tailor. — Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib supplies this occupation. [1 notes: MUJAHID(سعيد بن المسيب)]
- 31:13/q01/p4 He was flat-nosed and Abyssinian, or Black from Nubia or Abyssinia. — Qatāda gives the flat-nosed Abyssinian report; Hudhayl says he did not hear Muqātil transmit that description. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls reports of Blackness and Nubian or Abyssinian origin weak in chain. [2 notes: MUQATIL(قتادة بن دعامة); IBNASHUR-FULL; MUQATIL(الهذيل)-]
- 31:13/q01/p5 The Qurʾānic Luqmān is distinct from Luqmān ibn ʿĀd and from Balaam. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the latter identifications arose from confusion about names, fathers, or translated names; Balaam is the figure of Numbers 22–23. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:13/q02 What is the name of Luqmān's son?  [لِٱبْنِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: The transmitted names vary, with no criterion in these notes to settle them.
- 31:13/q02/p1 Anʿam or Anam. — Muqātil calls him Anʿum; Bursevī says Luqmān was known as Abū Anʿam. The slight vocalization differences are reported together. [9 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش); BAYDAWI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري)]
- 31:13/q02/p2 Mishkam or Mashkam. — The minor spelling and vocalization forms are transmitted by al-Kalbī and other reports. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري)]
- 31:13/q02/p3 Thārān, Tārān, Dārān, Barān, or Tharan. — Al-Suhaylī attributes Thārān to al-Ṭabarī and al-Qutbī; al-Ālūsī reports Ṭabarī and Qutaybī as Tārān. The forms vary in transmission. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(السهيلي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري)]
- 31:13/q02/p4 Ashkam. — Al-Kalbī gives this form; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī list it alongside other reported names. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري)]
- 31:13/q02/p5 Bābān. — Al-Māwardī lists this among the alternatives. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 31:13/q02/p6 Mathān. — Al-Ālūsī lists this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطبري)]

## 31:13/q03 What was the son's religious state, and what became of him?  [لِٱبْنِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Whether the warning addressed existing unbelief or prevented a future lapse, and how long the counsel continued.
- 31:13/q03/p1 He and Luqmān's wife were unbelievers and accepted Islam after repeated admonition. — Muqātil and later reports give the conversion; Bursevī contrasts Noah's son and wife and Lot's wife with Lot's believing daughters. [8 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:13/q03/p2 The son and wife were already Muslim; the warning guarded against future shirk. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:13/q03/p3 Luqmān continued advising his son until the son died. — Al-Ālūsī cites al-Faḍl al-Raqqāshī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:13/q04 How was Luqmān's son described?  [لِٱبْنِهِۦ · reports]
- 31:13/q04/p1 He was described as barr, meaning beneficent or thankful. — Abū Ḥayyān records both explanations of the descriptor. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 31:13/q05 How does وَإِذْ relate to the preceding wording?  [وَإِذْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an omitted imperative, the earlier grant of wisdom, or an extra particle accounts for idh; al-Naḥḥās objects to the wisdom link on word order and the wāw.
- 31:13/q05/p1 An omitted command, 'remember,' governs idh. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī prefer the implied command; al-Naḥḥās rejects attachment to the grant of wisdom because of the intervening wāw and order. Al-Ṭabarī and others take God to tell Muḥammad to recall the counsel. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 31:13/q05/p2 Idh attaches to God's giving Luqmān wisdom: the admonition was itself wisdom. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq place the gift at the time of the counsel; Abū Muslim coordinates it with the command to thank God. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ālūsī note omitted wording supplied by context. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 31:13/q05/p3 Idh is extra, leaving the sense 'and He said.' — Ibn Qutayba compares 2:30 and cites Ibn Mayyāda's line beginning idh lā yazāl as poetic evidence for extra idh. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 31:13/q06 What is the syntax of وَهُوَ يَعِظُهُۥ?  [وَهُوَ يَعِظُهُۥ · grammar]
- 31:13/q06/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing Luqmān as admonishing his son while speaking. — Abū Muslim, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī identify the ḥāl; al-Samarrāʾī infers sustained counsel and a receptive moment from the construction. [4 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:13/q07 What does waʿẓ mean here?  [يَعِظُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether waʿẓ is characterized chiefly by warning or by a heart-softening reminder of good; Elmalılı expressly favors al-Khalīl's definition.
- 31:13/q07/p1 Admonition or deterrence joined to warning and rebuke. — Rāghib gives deterrence with warning or fear; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī repeat this sense. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Râgıb)]
- 31:13/q07/p2 A reminder of good that softens the heart. — Al-Khalīl's definition is cited by Bursevī and al-Ālūsī; Elmalılı judges it better. Al-Biqāʿī adds benefit, discipline, reverence, and justice. [4 notes: BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(İmam Halil)+]
- 31:13/q07/p3 Calling people to the Real and His way. — Sulamī transmits the definition from Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī. Ibn ʿAbbās says Luqmān's admonition concerns God. [2 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)]

## 31:13/q08 What manner and occasion of admonition does the wording suggest?  [وَهُوَ يَعِظُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The precise occasion is inferred by Iṣlāḥī; other notes draw continuity and care from the wording.
- 31:13/q08/p1 He gave sustained, careful counsel, with repeated attention to his son. — Al-Biqāʿī hears repetition; al-Samarrāʾī takes the circumstantial wording as continuity and recommends ongoing parental guidance. Al-Ālūsī's mustard-seed story depicts many lessons and the son's tears. Iṣlāḥī sees a deliberate, elaborate occasion. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(حفص بن عمر الكندي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 31:13/q08/p2 He counseled the son when the son was being crowned the next tribal chief. — Iṣlāḥī infers this specific occasion. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:13/q09 Why use the diminutive address يَٰبُنَىَّ?  [يَٰبُنَىَّ · rhetoric]
- 31:13/q09/p1 It expresses tenderness, love, and concern, making the son receptive to advice. — Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad say it need not mean literal youth or smallness; al-Ālūsī cites two poems. Al-Samarrāʾī likens gentle address to 20:44 and gives the example of a parent sitting beside a child and touching his head or shoulder; he favors gentle, noncoercive counsel and recounts his own son's response. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:13/q10 How is يَٰبُنَىَّ formed grammatically?  [يَٰبُنَىَّ · grammar]
- 31:13/q10/p1 It is the diminutive of ibn with a possessive yāʾ, whose reduction explains the surviving final yāʾ and its vowel. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes restored and merged root letters and omission of the vocative possessive yāʾ. Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say kasra signals an omitted yāʾ and fatḥa eases pronunciation. Abū ʿAlī compares kasra to yā ghulāmi aqbil and fatḥa to an added yāʾ changed to alif, as in yā abati; al-Fārisī cites Abū ʿUthmān and compares possessive yāʾ to tanwīn. Al-Wāḥidī explains deletion after three yāʾs and treats an underlying final yāʾ as still present, citing poetry and the plural; he prefers other accounts of the lightening and says it better suits verse-ending rhyme. Qirāʾāt-ER calls the silent form shorter and compressed and the kasra form more explicitly possessive. [11 notes: NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(حفص عن عاصم); TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; WAHIDI-BASIT+]

## 31:13/q11 How is the final yāʾ of يَٰبُنَىَّ read here?  [يَٰبُنَىَّ · readings]
turns on: Transmission by reader and route, and whether a pause form may be sounded in connected speech.
- 31:13/q11/p1 With kasra and a doubled yāʾ. — The majority and named readers Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim use kasra; Ibn Kathīr has it in the middle occurrence. Al-Fārisī calls kasra the normal vocative after dropping the possessive yāʾ; Qirāʾāt-ER says it retains clearer possession. [18 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير,حفص عن عاصم); JISHUMI(ابن كثير,حفص عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن فليح,ابن كثير برواية البزي,القواس,حفص); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 31:13/q11/p2 With fatḥa and a doubled yāʾ. — Ḥafṣ and al-Mufaḍḍal from ʿĀṣim have fatḥa in the three Luqmān occurrences; Ḥafṣ has it throughout the Qurʾān. The underlying form is explained as yā bunayā with an elided or substituted alif; al-Fārisī and Abū ʿUthmān compare yā abati. Ibn Kathīr's al-Bazzī transmission has fatḥa in the third Luqmān address; Abū Bakr agrees at the Hūd occurrence. [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(المفضل عن عاصم,حفص عن عاصم); JISHUMI(حفص عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل,نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن فليح,ابن كثير برواية البزي,حفص); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(حفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حفص والمفضل عن عاصم); NASHR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:13/q11/p3 With sukūn and a lightened yāʾ. — Ibn Kathīr, including al-Bazzī, Ibn Abī Bazzā, Ibn Fulayḥ and Qunbul transmissions, has sukūn in the first Luqmān address; routes differ in the second and third. Makkī is also reported with sukūn. Al-Fārisī and Abū ʿAlī say this carries a pause form into connected recitation, as poetry does; al-Wāḥidī cites poetic precedents but judges other explanations preferable and says the light form is suited to rhyme. Qirāʾāt-ER calls it compressed. [19 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(ابن كثير,حفص عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي برة عن ابن كثير وقنبل); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن فليح,ابن كثير برواية البزي,القواس); NASAFI-FULL(مكي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(البزي); NASHR(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 31:13/q12 Can بِٱللَّهِ begin a new oath after a pause at لَا تُشْرِكْ?  [لَا تُشْرِكْ بِٱللَّهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the bāʾ can introduce an oath with an omitted verb, and whether stopping there preserves the sense.
- 31:13/q12/p1 Yes: stop after 'do not associate,' then take 'by God' as an oath before the statement about shirk. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Burhān and al-Ālūsī report the parse; it requires an understood oath verb, while al-Burhān says omitted-verb oaths more often use wāw. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; NASHR-; ITQAN(غيره)-]
- 31:13/q12/p2 No: 'with God' belongs to the prohibition against associating partners. — Ibn al-Jazarī calls the proposed pause forced and corrupting of meaning; al-Itqān cites a grammarian that Qurʾānic oaths with omitted verb normally use wāw, while bāʾ occurs with the verb expressed. His general rule is to seek coherent meaning and sound stopping. [5 notes: NASHR; ITQAN(ابن الجزري); TABRISI; ITQAN(غيره)+; NASHR+]

## 31:13/q13 What does shirk mean here?  [ٱلشِّرْكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression is explained by formal worship alone or also by subtler inner association.
- 31:13/q13/p1 Assigning God a partner in worship. — Ibn Qutayba calls shirk a verbal noun for sharing; Ibn al-Jawzī cites that explanation and a report of Muʿādh allowing human partnerships in Yemen, with sharecropping as another human example. With God, the partner is assigned to Him; al-Ṭabrisī and Bursevī specify worship. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,معاذ); TABRISI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:13/q13/p2 It includes outward idolatry and hidden attribution of events or inner attention to created beings. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes overt and hidden shirk and transmits a definition of affirming another while witnessing the unseen. Al-Biqāʿī says both manifest and hidden forms are covered. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 31:13/q14 Why does Luqmān begin his counsel by prohibiting shirk?  [لَا تُشْرِكْ · interpretation]
- 31:13/q14/p1 Sound belief and God's oneness are the foundation of action, worship, gratitude, and wisdom. — Al-Rāzī calls it the most important counsel; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr put correct belief before moral growth. Iṣlāḥī says gratitude to all others depends on thanks to the giver of all favors. Samarrāʾī says worship is not accepted with shirk and the ban applies before and after maturity; teaching tawḥīd early roots belief, while ritual duties await legal maturity. Kuran Yolu calls shirk the head and cause of other evils. Al-Qurṭubī sees the prohibition as evidence that Luqmān was instructing his son. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:13/q15 Whose statement is إِنَّ ٱلشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌۭ?  [إِنَّ ٱلشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the statement remains within the quotation; the hadith linking this verse to 6:82 is interpreted in both directions.
- 31:13/q15/p1 It is Luqmān's own speech, explaining his prohibition. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent reading and cites a report in Muslim; al-Ālūsī prefers it for the same reason. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God's quoting a wise servant could still reassure the Companions about 6:82. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 31:13/q15/p2 It is a divine declaration appended to Luqmān's speech. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say the 6:82 report can favor this, since God's own clarification would calm the Companions; both note that quoting a wise servant could do so too. Al-Ālūsī reports it as an alternative. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:13/q16 What does ẓulm mean as an Arabic word?  [ظُلْمٌ · meaning]
turns on: The word's range of Arabic usage; the explanations may coexist.
- 31:13/q16/p1 Putting something outside its proper place. — Ibn Qutayba gives idioms for resemblance, premature drinking, needless slaughter, wrongly dug land and leaving a road; al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī state the same base sense. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 31:13/q16/p2 Diminution or withholding. — Ibn Qutayba cites crops, rights, and souls in 2:57, 18:33, 19:60 and 36:54 for what is not diminished; al-Ṭabrisī applies withholding to God's due. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TABRISI]
- 31:13/q16/p3 Denial. — Ibn Qutayba cites Thamūd's denial of the she-camel's divine origin and denial of God's signs in 17:59 and 7:9. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 31:13/q17 Why is shirk called grave injustice?  [ٱلشِّرْكَ ظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌۭ · theology]
turns on: Which violated right or harmed party explains the label: God's exclusive right, the worshipper's self, the heart, or speech; several sources give more than one ground.
- 31:13/q17/p1 It assigns God's unique due, worship, or blessings to another and equates the sole giver with one who gives nothing. — Ibn Qutayba says lordship is misplaced and links 6:82; al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq name life, death, provision and blessings as God's alone. Al-Kashshāf calls the enormity beyond conception. Al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Biqāʿī, Elmalılı, Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu frame it as misplacing worship, denying God's right, or violating justice as giving each its due. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts the dependent creature with the Necessary Being. Samarrāʾī compares unfairly equating a capable person with an incompetent one; Creator and creature differ far more. Ibn ʿAbbās calls it the greatest sin; Elmalılı says divinity cannot be transferred as property can. Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that offense scales with the offended one's greatness and the unrestricted 'great' signals unparalleled gravity. [19 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 31:13/q17/p2 The idolater wrongs and degrades himself by worshipping lower beings and risking ruin. — Yaḥyā cites al-Ḥasan on diminishing the self and al-Suddī on great sin; al-Māwardī calls it self-wrong. Al-Rāzī and Elmalılı cite the human honor of 17:70; al-Ṭabrisī says the self is ruined. Bursevī cites Kashf al-asrār that this wrong is never forgiven. Samarrāʾī adds worship of an equal or lower being and eternal Hell. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 31:13/q17/p3 Shirk wrongs the heart, beyond the forgivable wrongs of the self. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes heart from self and says the former has no path to forgiveness; Yaḥyā's Prophetic report distinguishes unforgiven shirk, possibly forgiven sins against God, and wrongs between people requiring redress. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 31:13/q17/p4 It is a grave wrong in speech. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it a grave verbal error. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 31:13/q18 How does 31:13 clarify the injustice in 6:82?  [إِنَّ ٱلشِّرْكَ لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌۭ · links]
- 31:13/q18/p1 The Prophet cited Luqmān to explain that the injustice in 6:82 means shirk. — Ibn Masʿūd and other reports say the Companions feared any wrong would disqualify faith; the Prophet's citation relieved them. Ibn Kathīr cites Bukhārī and Muslim, al-Biqāʿī calls the Bukhārī report sound, and al-Burhān says both Ṣaḥīḥ collections contain it. Al-Itqān observes that broad wording can have a specific intended referent and warns against extending polytheist threats to sinful Muslims, as Ibn Sīrīn says the Khārijites did. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مسعود); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); BURHAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن سيرين)]

## 31:13/q19 What kinds of wrongdoing does the reported Prophetic teaching distinguish?  [ظُلْمٌ · theology]
- 31:13/q19/p1 Shirk is unforgiven; sins between servant and God may be forgiven; human wrongs require reciprocal redress. — Yaḥyā transmits the Prophet's threefold distinction. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 31:13/q20 Why say injustice is 'great' and explain the prohibition?  [لَظُلْمٌ عَظِيمٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 31:13/q20/p1 Its magnitude matches the supreme right it violates, and the explicit reason persuades the listener. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the unqualified description marks incomparable gravity; Samarrāʾī says people naturally dislike injustice, and a parent should explain the reason for a command so a child understands and accepts it. [3 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:13/q21 How is this counsel related to Luqmān's wisdom and gratitude?  [* · links]
- 31:13/q21/p1 His instruction embodies the wisdom and gratitude given him, especially through rejecting shirk. — Al-Zajjāj says the admonition is wisdom; al-Biqāʿī takes it as gratitude enacted and as an example of patience and perseverance. Elmalılı says it shows how Luqmān expressed gratitude; Iṣlāḥī makes avoidance of polytheism the essence of gratitude. Samarrāʾī says he practiced what he taught, giving advice force. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:13/q22 What conduct does Luqmān's example teach parents and advisers?  [* · interpretation]
- 31:13/q22/p1 Guide children steadily, personally, gently, and with reasons; live by the advice given. — Samarrāʾī says parents should not abandon upbringing to bad teachers or streets, that advisers' contrary conduct weakens words, and that repeated care, receptive timing, tenderness, and explaining reasons matter. Iṣlāḥī asks the audience to compare the wise father's teaching with its own treatment of children; Ibn ʿĀshūr outlines the counsel's progression from monotheism and parents to prayer, right conduct, patience, humility and measured speech. [14 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:13/q23 What inward reading is drawn from the warning against shirk?  [لَا تُشْرِكْ بِٱللَّهِ · inward]
- 31:13/q23/p1 Give God alone service of the self, attention of the heart, and vision of the spirit. — Sulamī transmits this as singling out the Real entirely; Bursevī says admonition turns the self away from what is below God and calls it individuation within unity. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BURSEVI]

## 31:13/q24 How does the passage address its first audience?  [* · interpretation]
- 31:13/q24/p1 It warns Quraysh and all listeners through a wise father's counsel and contrasts wisdom with blind ancestral imitation. — Al-Biqāʿī says the counsel promises security and guidance; Iṣlāḥī says Arab sages taught the Qurʾān's message, unlike forefathers who followed Satan, and condemns punishing children for what Luqmān taught. The Study Quran joins avoiding shirk with honoring parents as protection against two greatest sins, citing a Prophetic listing of them. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]

## 31:13/q25 What occasion is reported for the sūra or its nearby warning?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reported occasions concern the sūra as a whole and a nearby warning.
- 31:13/q25/p1 Quraysh asked about Luqmān and filial duty, prompting the sūra. — Al-Ālūsī reports this from al-Baḥr. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:13/q25/p2 The warning about arguing over God without knowledge concerned al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith. — Al-Thaʿlabī says he called angels God's daughters. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 31:13/q26 Where and how many verses was Sūrat Luqmān revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Reported Medinan exceptions and verse-count systems; al-Ālūsī challenges the prayer-and-zakat dating argument.
- 31:13/q26/p1 It is wholly Meccan. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this report. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 31:13/q26/p2 It is Meccan except verses 31:27–29, revealed in Medina after a challenge by Jewish rabbis. — Another Ibn ʿAbbās report connects the exception to the comparison of human knowledge with the Torah. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 31:13/q26/p3 It is Meccan except the two verses starting with the trees-and-pens parable. — ʿAṭāʾ gives the report, also attributed by al-Dānī and Abū Ḥayyān to Qatāda. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عطاء)]
- 31:13/q26/p4 A proposed Medinan exception is the verse about prayer and zakat. — Al-Ālūsī reports the argument that both obligations began in Medina, but objects that prayer was prescribed at the Meccan Night Journey and at least enjoined there; an alternative account distinguishes the Meccan obligation of zakat from Medinan thresholds. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:13/q26/p5 Its verse count is thirty-three in Meccan and Medinan systems, thirty-four in other systems. — Al-Ālūsī records the counting difference. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:13/q27 How does the sūra connect to the preceding sūras?  [* · links]
- 31:13/q27/p1 It resumes themes and contrasts from preceding sūras: guidance, signs, wise conduct, pleasure, resurrection, and shirk. — Al-Ālūsī cites al-Baḥr on parables and rejected signs (29:43, 29:50, 31:7), al-Suyūṭī on shared opening letters and the believers of 29:49, and contrasts rejoicing in the garden (30:15) with idle song (31:6), Roman-Persian struggle for worldly prize with a wise enslaved man counseling patience and peace, two accounts of easy resurrection (30:27; 31:28), and responses to hardship and shirk (30:33; 31:32). [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السيوطي)]

## 31:13/q28 How is monotheism in the counsel linked to honoring parents?  [* · links]
- 31:13/q28/p1 The command to worship God alone is paired with kindness to parents, as elsewhere in the Qurʾān. — Ibn Kathīr cites 17:23; the Study Quran calls shirk and disrespect to parents two gravest sins and cites a Prophetic report. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]

## 31:13/q29 What other wisdom or counsel is attributed to Luqmān in reports accompanying this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Distinct transmitted sayings and episodes, rather than a dispute over one saying.
- 31:13/q29/p1 He gave detailed travel counsel about prayer, companions, rest, animals, food, safety, and conduct. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Luqmān on praying promptly and in congregation, resting and feeding a mount, choosing grassy camps and praying on arrival and departure, giving food, reciting while riding, praising God, solitary prayer, avoiding all-night travel and loudness; Abū ʿAbdillāh adds gear, medicine, consultation, generosity, verifying advice, heeding elders and caution with lone strangers. [5 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,لقمان)]
- 31:13/q29/p2 He likened the world to a deep sea: faith is the ship, trust its sail, and piety its provisions. — Al-Ṭabrisī attributes this to Man lā yaḥḍuruhu al-faqīh; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports al-Ṣādiq adding that many learned people perish, survival is mercy and drowning follows sin. [2 notes: TABRISI(لقمان); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 31:13/q29/p3 The heart and tongue are the best morsels when good and the worst when bad. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Luqmān bringing them when his master asked for a sheep's two best morsels. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 31:13/q29/p4 He warned against lingering on the toilet and described physical harm. — Al-Ṭabrisī says he named liver harm, hemorrhoids and head heat, advised gentle sitting and rising, and his master wrote it on the privy door. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 31:13/q29/p5 He answered family deaths with statements about changed responsibility and loss. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn Dīnār reports Luqmān's reactions to his father, wife, sister and brother dying: control of his affairs, a renewed bed, covered nakedness and lost support. [1 notes: TABRISI(عبد الله بن دينار)]
- 31:13/q29/p6 The worst person is untroubled by being seen doing wrong. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Luqmān's answer to a question. [1 notes: TABRISI(لقمان)]
- 31:13/q29/p7 He answered criticism of his face by asking whether it targeted the design or its designer. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports the exchange. [1 notes: TABRISI(لقمان)]
- 31:13/q29/p8 He praised David's armor and said silence is rare wisdom. — Al-Ṭabrisī says David affirmed that Luqmān deserved his name. [1 notes: TABRISI(لقمان)]
- 31:13/q29/p9 He urged nearness to scholars, restraint in the world, fasting desire, and keeping prayer. — Al-Ṣādiq says not to dispute scholars, take only enough from worldly life, and not let fasting desire obstruct prayer, which God loves more. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 31:13/q29/p10 He urged sustained learning and avoidance of bad company and disputation. — Reports compare early manners to later fruit and knowledge to an inherited habit; they counsel daily learning, guarding knowledge, avoiding quarrels with obstinate people or jurists, hostile rulers, tyrants and the wicked. Another likens bad company to tar, disputation to insult, suspicious places to accusation, and an uncontrolled tongue to regret; share knowledge with seekers. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,لقمان)]
- 31:13/q29/p11 He taught balanced fear and hope, sincerity, obedience, and love of God. — Al-Ṣādiq says fear God even with all humanity's good deeds and hope even with all its sins; faith, sincerity, obedience, fear and love mutually confirm one another and culminate in God's pleasure and Paradise. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 31:13/q29/p12 He warned against reliance on worldly life and urged preparation for judgment. — Al-Ṣādiq says worldly pleasure is no reward for the obedient and hardship no punishment for sinners, likens the world to a bridge rather than a field for fattening, and says one will answer for youth, lifespan and wealth. Another report likens a person accumulating for heirs to a hired worker who must do his assigned task. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 31:13/q29/p13 His wisdom's core was to leave to God what was handled for him and fulfill what was entrusted to him. — Luqmān gives this answer when asked. [1 notes: TABATABAI(لقمان)]
- 31:13/q29/p14 Sleep and waking show that the self is under another's control. — Abū Jaʿfar reports Luqmān comparing sleep with death and waking with resurrection. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]
- 31:13/q29/p15 He taught measured social closeness, friendship, trustworthiness, and cheerful patience. — A saying warns against becoming too close or too distant; another urges friends over enemies, religion, good manners, service and trusts, while avoiding hidden sin. Al-Ṣādiq adds calm, kindness to kin, a cheerful face, contentment and freedom from greed. [3 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,لقمان)]
- 31:13/q29/p16 His forceful counsel reminded his son that the afterlife draws nearer than the world left behind. — Al-Ṣādiq says the counsel nearly split the son apart. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 31:13/q30 What other wording is reported in his exhortation?  [لِٱبْنِهِۦ يَعِظُهُۥ · reports]
- 31:13/q30/p1 Anʿim or ushkur, 'receive favor' or 'give thanks,' is reported as an alternate wording or explanation. — Al-Nasafī records the phrase without settling whether it is wording or interpretation. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 31:13/q31 What broad interpretive principles are drawn from this verse and its use elsewhere?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes present complementary interpretive principles.
- 31:13/q31/p1 Qurʾānic wording must be read for its intended scope and contextual meaning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says not to narrow broad wording, broaden specific wording, or absolutize restricted wording; he explains the Qurʾān's guidance, legislation and ethical principles as concise and transmissible, allowing scholarly derivation, with 5:3 on completed favor. Al-Itqān uses the Prophet's specification of ẓulm in 6:82 and Ibn ʿAbbās to show broad wording can intend a specific referent; Ibn Sīrīn says the Khārijites wrongly applied polytheist threat verses to Muslims. [6 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن سيرين)]
- 31:13/q31/p2 Revelation answering events displays spontaneous miraculous fitness and rebuts the 'ancient tales' charge. — Ibn ʿĀshūr states this principle while discussing occasions of revelation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 31:13/q31/p3 Some verses explain themselves; others require explanation. — Al-Burhān lists examples of the first category and elsewhere cites Prophetic explanation of 6:82 by 31:13. [2 notes: BURHAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 31:13/q32 What general principles about Qurʾānic oaths accompany discussion of the proposed oath here?  [بِٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 31:13/q32/p1 God swears by Himself or His signs, and oaths confirm central beliefs or commands. — Ibn al-Qayyim says oaths by created things display their greatness as signs and confirm revelation, messengership, judgment, promise, warning and the human condition; the response may be stated or understood and an oath can accompany a declarative statement or imperative (51:23; 15:92–93). [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن القيم)]
