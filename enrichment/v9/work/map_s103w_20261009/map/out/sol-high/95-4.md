# 95:4: 17 questions

## 95:4/q01 What statement answers the opening oath?  [لَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا · rhetoric]
- 95:4/q01/p1 God's declaration that He created humanity in the best constitution answers the oath. — Al-Tabari and Qatadah identify the oath's answer; al-Tabari cites the fig and olive. Ibn Ashur says the following coordinated clauses join it; the emphatic oath invites reflection on the hidden constitution and return. Al-Kalbi calls it an oath about God's blessing; al-Samarrai connects the places of revelation with human origin and end. [19 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 95:4/q02 Why is God explicitly named as the creator?  [خَلَقْنَا · rhetoric]
- 95:4/q02/p1 The active divine subject displays God's power, artistry, blessing, and authority to guide the humans He made. — Al-Samarrai compares active wording in 7:181 and 36:71–72 with passive creation statements about weakness in 4:28, 21:37, and 70:19–21; the Creator knows the fitting way of life. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 95:4/q03 What does God's creating humanity describe here?  [خَلَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the focus is origination of the species, each person's formation, or the act of divine determination.
- 95:4/q03/p1 God brought the human species into being through its first origin and the causes of reproduction. — Ibn Ashur cites 38:75 and 7:11 for first creation and subsequent shaping; al-Biqa'i stresses divine might. [4 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 95:4/q03/p2 The verse most likely describes each person's formation in the womb. — Corpus Coranicum cites human developmental language in 86:5–6, 80:18–19, 75:38, 77:20, and 70:39. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM+]

## 95:4/q04 Whom does 'the human' denote?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite singular is generic, names the original human essence, or points to a particular person.
- 95:4/q04/p1 Humanity as a species, including men and women, believers and unbelievers, or Adam and his descendants. — Al-Mawardi says the singular can be collective; al-Alusi cites the connected exception in 95:6. Ibn Ashur also allows the original human essence apart from individual changes, comparing 70:19, a proverb, and poetry. Wahidi and others name Adam and his descendants. [26 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(إبراهيم); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI+; QURTUBI-]
- 95:4/q04/p2 Kulda or Kaldah ibn Asid. — Ibn Abbas is named in al-Mawardi and Ibn al-Jawzi; al-Qurtubi also reports this identification. Spelling varies. [5 notes: MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 95:4/q04/p3 Al-Walid ibn al-Mughira. — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes this to Ata; al-Qurtubi connects it with an unbelieving human. [5 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 95:4/q04/p4 An unbeliever, as the subsequent lowering concerns deniers of resurrection. — Al-Qurtubi explicitly relates the reading to resurrection denial. Al-Tabataba'i reports a view that makes the exception disconnected, with the return to Hell or reversal of bodily form. [3 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(unnamed view)]
- 95:4/q04/p5 Abu Jahl ibn Hisham. — Al-Mawardi attributes this to Muqatil; Ibn al-Jawzi also records it. [3 notes: MAWARDI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 95:4/q04/p6 The Messenger of God, Muhammad. — Al-Mawardi reports this identification; an explicitly weak report in al-Durr allegorizes the human as Muhammad. [3 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 95:4/q04/p7 Hisham ibn al-Mughira. — Al-Damghani and Ibn al-Jawzi transmit this alternative. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]
- 95:4/q04/p8 Utba or Shayba. — Al-Mawardi's alternatives are transmitted by Ibn al-Jawzi; the two names remain distinct reported possibilities. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي)]
- 95:4/q04/p9 One particular person whose described quality is shared by people generally. — Al-Mawardi gives this as a way to understand the singular expression without naming the person. [1 notes: MAWARDI]

## 95:4/q05 What does taqwīm mean?  [تَقْوِيمٍۢ · meaning]
- 95:4/q05/p1 Shaping, straightening, ordering, or adjusting something into its fitting, balanced condition. — Al-Razi and Ibn Ashur define it as giving a thing the form it ought to have. Bursevi derives it from qama, standing or soundness, and qawwama, straightening. Corpus Coranicum calls it partly synonymous with creating; Islahi says it comes to mean fitting for purpose. Elmalili adds strengthening or valuing. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 95:4/q06 How does 'in the best constitution' work grammatically?  [فِىٓ أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a noun or construct is supplied, or fī is taken as extra.
- 95:4/q06/p1 A circumstantial phrase describes the human in the best configuration, with an understood noun after the superlative. — Al-Tabari, al-Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, and al-Samin understand an omitted 'formation' or 'form': the best formation. Ibn Atiyya says the adjective must modify a stated or understood noun; al-Samin sees no need for more complex analyses. Elmalili calls it a stable state. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM; NAHHAS+; TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
- 95:4/q06/p2 An omitted construct yields 'best uprightness of form' or a similar expression. — Abu al-Baqa allows it; al-Khafaji supplies 'stature' or 'one made upright.' Al-Samin regards the complication as unnecessary. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI(الخفاجي); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)]
- 95:4/q06/p3 Fī is extra, leaving an absolute object: God shaped him with the best shaping. — Abu al-Baqa and a reported parsing in al-Alusi allow it; al-Samin says it is unnecessary. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي)]
- 95:4/q06/p4 Fī figuratively conveys secure attachment or possession of the excellent constitution. — Ibn Ashur says it works like bāʾ of accompaniment or lām of possession, lending force concisely. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 95:4/q06/p5 The phrase is a circumstantial description; taqwīm denotes the resulting constitution, rather than God's act of forming. — Abu al-Baqa distinguishes the creature's state from the Creator's action. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 95:4/q07 What is the 'best constitution'?  [أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether excellence is primarily bodily, youthful, intellectual and moral, or a union of outward and inward capacities.
- 95:4/q07/p1 A comprehensive excellence of body, senses, intellect, spirit, and capacities suited to human purpose. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan prefer a combination. Al-Baydawi sees properties of other beings gathered in humanity; al-Qurtubi details head, chest, belly, organs and limbs; philosophers call humans a microcosm. Al-Alusi and Elmalili cite lines attributed to Ali about the greater world within a small body. Asad says each person's endowments suit their purpose, though unequal. Qutb sees God's care in body, intellect and spirit. Study Quran relates physical posture to inner comportment. ## 95:4/q08 How broadly does 'best' rank humanity?  [أَحْسَنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it asserts absolute superiority to all created forms or fitness relative to humanity's purpose.
- 95:4/q08/p1 Humanity is the most excellent and beautiful of creatures. — Some scholars universalize the comparison; al-Qurtubi says no created thing is more beautiful and al-Islahi and Kur'an Yolu stress humanity's high rank. Philosophers call the human a microcosm. Yahya ibn Aktham's moon ruling rests on this absolute reading. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA(بعض العلماء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء); QURTUBI(ابن العربي,الفلاسفة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 95:4/q08/p2 It is the most fitting human constitution; another creature may excel in a particular respect. — Ibn Atiyya gives the sun as a counterexample to absolute comparison. Asad says unequal individuals can still make the best use of their own endowments and environment. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 95:4/q09 What does the verse imply about God's form and Adam being made in His image?  [أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍۢ · theology]
turns on: How to understand shared attributes without making God bodily.
- 95:4/q09/p1 God has no bodily form; the Adam-image report refers to attributes or meanings such as life, knowledge, power, will, speech, hearing, and sight. — Al-Qushayri reasons that if form were shared with creation, its perfection would belong to God; yet the verse calls human form best. Ibn al-Arabi reads the version 'image of the Merciful' through meanings, not particular bodily form. Al-Alusi notes that the variant rules out taking the pronoun only as Adam's own physical image; Yaḥyā ibn Mu'adh's self-knowledge saying is not established as a hadith. [18 notes: QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI(ابن العربي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); BIQAI(الأستاذ أبو القاسم القشيري); BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI(رسول الله); ALUSI(يحيى بن معاذ الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ الرازي); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 95:4/q10 Does the verse imply no more excellent world could be created?  [أَحْسَنِ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'best' limits God's possible acts.
- 95:4/q10/p1 No; God's possible creations are unlimited and another world could differ in size or excellence. — Al-Biqa'i rejects a maxim attributed to elders that this is the best possible world. He follows al-Ash'ari and al-Ghazali against philosophers who deny divine will, explaining possibility by God's power and contradiction as the limit of impossibility. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 95:4/q11 What does the verse teach about human potential and decline?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How initial excellence relates to later choice, corruption, and divine reward.
- 95:4/q11/p1 Humans are made capable of truth and ascent, yet desire, pride, and rejecting guidance can bring them low; believers retain the reward of faith and good action. — Al-Biqa'i says reason can lead from Hell to Paradise while appetite and anger explain corruption (2:30); human form alone cannot save. Elmalili says people are born able to grow, not already complete. Islahi presents a trial against Satan. Ibn Ashur traces decline to desire, fear, imitation, illness, and intoxicants. Al-Tabataba'i links the connected exception to moral ascent; the Study Quran compares 103:2–3. [43 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); BAGHAWI; RAZI(بعض الصالحين); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]

## 95:4/q12 What reports about the comparison with the moon and a conditional divorce are given?  [أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse proves the wife is more beautiful than the moon, and how the oath's condition is framed.
- 95:4/q12/p1 The conditional divorce did not take effect, since the verse calls the human the best or most beautiful creation. — Ibn Atiyya reports some jurists; al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Bursevi, Elmalili, and al-Alusi name Yahya ibn Aktham and some Hanafis. In al-Razi, all other jurists ruled the opposite; al-Mansur accepts the Hanafi ruling in al-Qurtubi. Al-Alusi notes poetic comparisons to the moon and calls the distinction obvious. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI(يحيى بن أكثم); RAZI-FULL(يحيى بن أكثم); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(يحيى بن أكثم); ALUSI-FULL(يحيى بن أكثم وبعض الحنفية); ELMALILI(قاضي يحيى بن أكثم، الحنفية)]
- 95:4/q12/p2 Other jurists found the husband in breach. — Al-Razi and Bursevi say all or nearly all jurists except Yahya ibn Aktham ruled this way. [2 notes: RAZI(يحيى بن أكثم); BURSEVI(يحيى بن أكثم)]
- 95:4/q12/p3 A report in al-Alusi says the divorce did take effect, although its condition is worded differently. — Al-Alusi's shorter note attributes this to Yahya ibn Aktham and some Hanafis, whereas his fuller note gives the opposite result; the notes preserve the inconsistency. [1 notes: ALUSI(يحيى بن أكثم)]

## 95:4/q13 How is the verse linked to the rest of the surah and neighboring surahs?  [* · links]
- 95:4/q13/p1 The oath, the human capacity for truth, the fall, and the believers' exception form a moral sequence. — Al-Samarrai says revelation's places point to guidance suited to human nature and that creation and decline support resurrection and judgment. Ibn Ashur says the sacred places suggest stages of divine law and shared foundations; the surah warns rejecters and recalls God's gift. Kur'an Yolu sees fig and olive as food, Sinai as revelation, Mecca as security. Study Quran says sites of revelation support reading human stature as receptivity to revelation. Al-Gharnati praises formation from semen and readiness for revelation. Al-Wahidi notes that the secure city's protection extends to residents and visitors. Al-Samarrai reads thumma as delay and rank contrast and compares al-Inshiqaq's toil with al-Tin's honoring of humanity. [26 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN(others); GHARNATI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]
- 95:4/q13/p2 The surah follows al-Sharh's account of Muhammad by considering the human kind and its possible rise or fall. — Al-Biqa'i names Muhammad as the finest human, Abraham the noblest root, and Mecca the best land; Abu Hayyan describes opponents being lowered while mankind is prepared for truth. Al-Alusi says the previous surah showed the Prophet's perfected creation and character. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 95:4/q13/p3 The surah's account of creation and religion summarizes its predecessors and prepares for the next surah's command to read. — Elmalili relates the transition to true religion and God as best judge. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 95:4/q13/p4 The oath evokes Sinai, Seir, and Paran and a summary of Torah history. — Al-Biqa'i links ideal creation, the righteous, and the fallen with the Torah's beginning, laws, and later history. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 95:4/q13/p5 The believers' reward amid old age connects this surah with the account in 103:3. — Al-Baghawi relates Ibrahim's explanation that elderly believers retain credit for good works to 95:4–6. [1 notes: BAGHAWI]

## 95:4/q14 What ancillary reports about this surah's setting and recitation occur?  [* · sciences]
- 95:4/q14/p1 Most call the surah Meccan, while reports from Ibn Abbas and Qatadah call it Medinan. — Al-Alusi argues that the secure city as Mecca supports the majority, says the name is also al-Tin, and reports agreement on eight verses; Study Quran calls it early-middle Meccan after surah 85; Asad also places it after surah 85. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 95:4/q14/p2 The Prophet recited Surat al-Tin in one of the two evening prayer rak'ahs while traveling. — Ibn Hibban transmits the report from al-Bara ibn Azib. [1 notes: IBNHIBBAN(البراء بن عازب)]

## 95:4/q15 What other details do combined reports give about the oath's places and the subsequent verses?  [* · reports]
- 95:4/q15/p1 Combined reports vary on the fig and olive as fruits, mosques, cities, or mountains, while Mecca is the secure city; they differ on the lowest state as old age or Hell. — Mujahid and Ikrima take fruits; Ibn Abbas variously names the Sacred Mosque and al-Aqsa or Noah's mosque and Jerusalem; Qatadah names Damascus and Jerusalem. Muhammad ibn Kab names the Companions of the Cave's mosque and Elijah's. Ka'b and Abu Habib list sacred mountains. Jabir transmits a Prophetic identification of Mecca. A weak Ibn Abbas report allegorizes Muhammad, idolaters, and four caliphs. Follower reports variously preserve reward in age or identify Hell. [24 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,مجاهد); DURR(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,أبو حبيب الحارث بن محمد,ابن عباس,الحسن,الحكم,جابر بن عبد الله,زيد بن ميسرة,عكرمة,قتادة,كعب الأحبار,مجاهد,محمد بن كعب); MUJAHID(ابن عباس); FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)]

## 95:4/q16 What ancillary claims about Qur'anic eloquence are recorded?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether excellence lies in meaning-led expression, composition, rare diction, sound pattern, or broader content.
- 95:4/q16/p1 Qur'anic eloquence is meaningful expression in the best verbal form, with harmonious wording and arrangement. — Al-Rummani ranks its eloquence at the highest miraculous level and lists ten branches; al-Khattabi describes word, meaning, and their organizing bond and says the Qur'an perfects all three across creed, law, ethics, and reports. Bint al-Shati records their differing accounts of levels, and al-Baqillani's combination of meanings and wording. Al-Jurjani says trained judgment detects subtle compositional merit, illustrated by al-Buhturi's reports and Ibn al-Mu'tazz's verse. [26 notes: RUMMANI; KHATTABI; JURJANI-DALAIL(البحتري,الشاعر); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الباقلاني,الخطابي,الرماني)]
- 95:4/q16/p2 Qur'anic endings are eloquent when meaning leads the sound; forced saj' reverses that order. — Al-Rummani contrasts fawasil with saj', giving the soothsayer and Musaylima as poor examples, and allows identical or nearby ending letters when context keeps meanings clear. Bint al-Shati prefers his account, questions al-Baqillani's clause-length distinction, and says allowing saj' does not entail sarfah. [13 notes: RUMMANI(بعض الكهان,مسيلمة الكذاب); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الباقلاني); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(علي بن عيسى الرماني)+]
- 95:4/q16/p3 Rare vocabulary, mere correctness, and pronunciation do not explain the Qur'an's inimitability. — Al-Jurjani says people familiar with rare words could answer in kind, while challenging others would be like asking Arabs to speak Turkish. Umar's praise of Zuhayr and anecdotes about al-Hajjaj, Yahya ibn Ya'mar, and poetic transmission illustrate the limits of obscurity and routine parsing. Metaphor works by asserting the borrowed quality itself, rather than merely substituting a label; he cites 12:31 and lion, king, and musk examples. [13 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(الجاحظ,عمر رضي الله عنه)]
- 95:4/q16/p4 Some locate the miracle in the Qur'an's values, parables, and laws; Bint al-Shati questions whether this accounts for the early challenge. — She observes that the Meccan challenge preceded the completion of legislation and records al-Khattabi and al-Baqillani linking meanings with wording. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الباقلاني,الخطابي,قوم)]

## 95:4/q17 What other linguistic and textual observations are included?  [* · sciences]
- 95:4/q17/p1 The codices were checked, and reports of scribal error can refer to dialect or choice among allowed ahruf. — Al-Suyuti reconciles reports about Umar, Uthman, and Aisha by proposing an early correction of non-Quraysh spelling such as al-tabuwah for al-tabut. Ibn Ashtah and Ibn Jabara explain Aisha's 'error' as selection among seven ahruf; Sa'id ibn Jubayr treats a scribe's grammatical error as a dialectal reading. [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن أشته وابن جبارة,سعيد بن جبير,سوار بن شبيب,عبد الأعلى بن عبد الله بن عامر)]
- 95:4/q17/p2 The basic sense of bāʾ is attachment, literal or figurative. — Al-Zarkashi gives joining two things and passing by a place as examples. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 95:4/q17/p3 A singular expression may distribute over only part of a referent. — Al-Farra illustrates this with the verse about Abu Bakr's giving, 92:18. [1 notes: FARRA]
