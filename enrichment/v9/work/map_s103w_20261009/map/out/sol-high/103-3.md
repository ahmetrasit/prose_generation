# 103:3: 25 questions

## 103:3/q01 Who belongs to the exception?  [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the names limit the exception or exemplify it.
- 103:3/q01/p1 All people meeting the qualities. — Ṭabarī, Jishumī and Qurṭubī make the exception general; al-Ālūsī calls named persons foremost examples. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); JISHUMI+]
- 103:3/q01/p2 ʿAlī and Salmān are named among its believers. — Ibn ʿAbbās names them; al-Ālūsī denies an exclusive limit. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 103:3/q01/p3 Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān and ʿAlī represent the four successive qualities. — Ubayy transmits a Prophetic gloss; Ibn ʿAbbās repeated it from the pulpit. Some reports name Abū Jahl as the person in loss. [10 notes: THALABI(النبي,علي بن عبد الله بن عباس); QUSHAYRI(بعض التفاسير); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; JISHUMI(ابن علي بن عبد الله بن عباس); QURTUBI(أبي بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس); BURSEVI(رسول الله); JISHUMI-]
- 103:3/q01/p4 God's chosen people, with faith applied to ʿAlī's wilayah. — Al-Qummī reports this from al-Ṣādiq; counsel teaches descendants and successors. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 103:3/q02 Who is the person in loss from whom believers are excepted?  [إِلَّا · referent]
turns on: Whether the generic warning is applied to one opponent or several.
- 103:3/q02/p1 The warning concerns humanity generally, including named opponents. — Wāḥidī treats individual unbelievers as included under the general wording, since believers are excepted. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 103:3/q02/p2 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, al-ʿĀṣ ibn Wāʾil and al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib are named. — ʿAṭāʾ transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس)]
- 103:3/q02/p3 Abū Lahab is named. — Wāḥidī transmits this from Muqātil. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 103:3/q02/p4 Abū Jahl is named. — A report attributed to the Prophet names him; the four-Companion interpretation likewise uses him. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; THALABI(النبي); WAHIDI-QT]

## 103:3/q03 What human subject does this exception address?  [إِلَّا · interpretation]
- 103:3/q03/p1 A morally responsible human, individually and socially accountable. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys Qurʾānic al-insān as tested, articulate and entrusted with duty; she accepts al-Rāghib's sociability derivation but stresses obligations beyond mere membership of humankind. [4 notes: BINTSHATI(الراغب الأصفهاني)]

## 103:3/q04 Why can singular al-insān take this plural exception?  [إِلَّا · grammar]
- 103:3/q04/p1 It denotes humanity collectively despite singular form. — Majāz cites 70:19–22, 69:47 and al-Nābighah's poem; Ṭabarī, Qurṭubī and Burhān agree. [10 notes: MAJAZ; TAB; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 103:3/q05 What loss remains for a believer lacking some qualities?  [إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ · theology]
turns on: Whether khusr necessarily entails eternal punishment.
- 103:3/q05/p1 Full exemption requires all four; omission entails some loss. — Muḥammad b. Kaʿb says faith alone is insufficient; Rāzī and Elmalılı require both personal and communal duties. Elmalılı rejects the Murjiʾite denial of loss from inaction. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ELMALILI(Mürcienin)+]
- 103:3/q05/p2 Grave sinners outside the exception suffer eternal loss. — Rāzī reports threat-focused interpreters; some Muʿtazilites draw the same conclusion from the restricted exception. [5 notes: RAZI(القاطعون بوعيد الفساق); RAZI-FULL(القاطعون بوعيد الفساق); BIQAI(حديث صحيح); ALUSI(بعض المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المعتزلة); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 103:3/q05/p3 Loss has degrees: an unbeliever's is eternal; a sinner may be punished or lose higher reward. — Al-Ālūsī and Bursawī distinguish pardoned and unpardoned sinners; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 11:114; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds intercession. Al-Māturīdī's further replies seem strained to al-Ālūsī. [14 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI(الماتريدي); ALUSI-FULL(الماتريدى); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 103:3/q05/p4 Omitted extra counsel may lose extra reward without utter ruin. — Al-Tibyān contrasts the stricter exception here with another; enjoining may be individual, communal or recommended. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 103:3/q06 What does faith mean here?  [ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 103:3/q06/p1 Assent to God, revelation, messengers and the Last Day. — Ṭabarī adds duty to obey; Biqāʿī names angels and scriptures; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says denial of a messenger or Last Day contradicts faith. [8 notes: TAB; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 103:3/q06/p2 A living connection to God directing moral life. — Quṭb describes liberation, equality, dignity, calm, responsibility and lasting reward, citing 40:19 and 83:26. [40 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 103:3/q06/p3 Certain knowledge of God as sole agent, beyond time's veil. — Qāshānī and Bursawī describe lasting virtue and inward light. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 103:3/q07 How are faith and deeds related?  [ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · theology]
turns on: Whether coordination separates concepts or highlights a manifestation.
- 103:3/q07/p1 Deeds are outside faith's definition, or the coordination repeats faith. — Rāzī's first group rejects analogy with 33:7 and 2:98; repetition is possible but not the default. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also cites 3:81. [7 notes: RAZI(الأولون,من قال : العمل غير داخل في مسمى الإيمان); RAZI-FULL(الأولون,من قال إن العمل غير داخل في مسمى الإيمان); BINTSHATI]
- 103:3/q07/p2 Deeds express or overlap faith; mentioning them emphasizes action. — Al-Ḥalīmī allows repetition because deeds include faith. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls it specification; Iṣlāḥī says faith is foundation. Quṭb cites 14:18 and 24:39 for fruitless works without faith. [31 notes: RAZI(الحليمي); RAZI-FULL(الحليمي); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 103:3/q08 What are righteous deeds?  [ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · interpretation]
- 103:3/q08/p1 All prescribed good, including duties, avoiding sin, and benefit to others. — Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿĀshūr read the class comprehensively; Biqāʿī mentions worship and commerce; Ibn ʿĀshūr repentance; Elmalılı charity and juristic sources. [24 notes: TUSTARI; TAB; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 103:3/q09 What makes deeds righteous?  [ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ · theology]
turns on: Whether the adjective gives a ground independent of command.
- 103:3/q09/p1 Their goodness lies in inherent benefit. — Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilite inference. [3 notes: RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); BINTSHATI]
- 103:3/q09/p2 The wording leaves open inherent benefit versus divine command. — Rāzī reports the Ashʿarite reply. [3 notes: RAZI(الأشعرية); RAZI-FULL(الأشعرية); BINTSHATI]

## 103:3/q10 What does tawāṣaw imply?  [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ · meaning]
- 103:3/q10/p1 Reciprocal, regular advice and support, practiced by those offering it. — Thaʿlabī and Baghawī say urge; Qurṭubī adds love; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces binding and bequest. Ibn ʿĀshūr recalls 2:44 and 89:18; Biqāʿī emphasizes gentleness. [9 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 103:3/q11 Why use past-tense tawāṣaw?  [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ · grammar]
- 103:3/q11/p1 It describes and praises a continuing practice coordinated with earlier verbs. — Rāzī sees praise and persistence; Nasafī coordination; Biqāʿī encouragement to enter the faith. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 103:3/q12 What is the truth urged in mutual counsel?  [ٱلْحَقِّ · interpretation]

- 103:3/q12/p1 The Qurʾān or God's Book. — Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda often give this gloss; Ṭabarī explains its commands and prohibitions. ## 103:3/q13 Why add counsel in truth after personal deeds?  [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْحَقِّ · interpretation]
- 103:3/q13/p1 Believers must complete others by teaching, enjoining good and opposing wrong. — Rāzī cites 66:6; Biqāʿī invokes prophets and community; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 3:104, 9:71, 5:78–79. Quṭb stresses solidarity against ego and tyranny. [50 notes: RAZI(عمر); RAZI-FULL(عمر); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Im ām Far āhī); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 103:3/q14 What does patience encompass?  [ٱلصَّبْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it points to duties, comprehensive endurance, inward acceptance or active resolve.
- 103:3/q14/p1 Persevering in obedience and required duties. — Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda say obedience; Hishām b. Ḥassān says prescribed duties; Wahīdī includes striving. [21 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TUSTARI; TAB(الحسن,قتادة); TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI(قتادة,هشام بن حسان); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,هشام بن حسان); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 103:3/q14/p2 Obedience, restraint from sin, and endurance of trials, decree and harm. — Zamakhsharī gives all three; Ibn Kathīr adds harm from those corrected. Tustarī cites 16:126–127; Elmalılı cites 3:200, 2:153 and 39:10; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the unstated object broadens scope. [57 notes: MUJAHID(كعب); TUSTARI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن، قتادة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الإمام الطبري,الفخر الرازي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); BINTSHATI+]
- 103:3/q14/p3 Self-restraint and moral training against desire and hardship. — Jishumī defines restraint; Ibn ʿĀshūr excludes effort spent in sinful pleasure. Tustarī contrasts praiseworthy afterlife patience with worldly striving; Farāhī cites Ḥātim's verse. [20 notes: TUSTARI; JISHUMI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(علي بن أبي طالب); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Im ām Far āhī)]
- 103:3/q14/p4 Inward and outward contentment with God's acts, in ease and trial. — Qushayrī calls patience in well-being fullest and patience with God difficult; al-Ālūsī gives the rank of servitude; Sūlamī quotes a poem about concealed affliction. [7 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 103:3/q14/p5 Courageous perseverance in good, never passive acceptance of wrong. — Elmalılı cites the hadith of changing wrong by hand, speech or heart; 6:68 and 4:140 demand leaving mockery, and 12:108 insightful action. [8 notes: ELMALILI]

## 103:3/q15 Why repeat patience after truth?  [وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the second clause emphasizes an included virtue or distinguishes work.
- 103:3/q15/p1 Patience is part of truth but singled out because defending truth is hard. — Rāzī cites 31:17; Biqāʿī notes hostility; Ṭabāṭabāʾī points to the repeated verb; Quṭb stresses opposition and slow progress. [23 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI]
- 103:3/q15/p2 The clauses mark complementary domains: truth to know or practice, patience to sustain it. — Rāzī gives calling versus steadfastness; al-Ālūsī worship versus servitude; Farāhī truth as goal and patience as drive. [8 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Im ām Far āhī); STUDYQURAN(Sh)]

## 103:3/q16 What reading of biṣ-ṣabr is reported?  [بِٱلصَّبْرِ · readings]
- 103:3/q16/p1 Bāʾ can take slight kasra or ishām, generally explained as pause or transferred vowel. — Abū ʿAmr, Salām, Hārūn and Ibn Mūsā are named. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Aḥmad b. ## 103:3/q17 What expanded readings occur near the exception?  [إِلَّا · readings]

## 103:3/q18 Why state profit's cause but leave loss's cause vague?  [* · rhetoric]
- 103:3/q18/p1 Loss may arise through sin or omitted obedience, while profit needs action; the focus suits divine generosity. — Rāzī gives both explanations; Bayḍāwī says other pursuits lose or have reduced shares; Bursawī stresses eternal success. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 103:3/q19 What gain does the exception describe?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether afterlife profit, continued credit or inward perfection is stressed.
- 103:3/q19/p1 Believers exchange transient life for lasting reward. — Ibn Qutayba says reward is not diminished; Zamakhsharī and later sources use trade language. Rāzī consoles believers for lost youth. Elmalılı calls life loaned capital, citing 53:39 and 52:21. [31 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 103:3/q19/p2 Old or sick believers keep credit for deeds from youth and health. — Ibrāhīm's report parallels 95:4–6. [4 notes: BAGHAWI(إبراهيم); BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم عن ابن عون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(إبراهيم)]
- 103:3/q19/p3 Inwardly, escape yields perfected light and nearness beyond Paradise. — Qāshānī describes passage beyond time; Biqāʿī distinguishes outward Paradise and inner light. [4 notes: QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 103:3/q21 How does the exception connect to other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Which neighbouring or parallel passage is used.
- 103:3/q21/p1 It completes the warning of loss with the qualities that reverse it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the relative clause as explanatory; Study Quran compares 95:5–6 and Sura 102's rivalry. [8 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]
- 103:3/q21/p2 It parallels al-Balad's pairing of faith and counsel in patience and compassion. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 90:11–17; Ṭabarī's patience citation is 90:17. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 103:3/q21/p3 After Sura 102, it distinguishes believers from people distracted by rivalry. — Jishumī and Abū Ḥayyān give this link; Elmalılı relates counsel to 104:1. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 103:3/q21/p4 Another exception after descent to Hell parallels this escape from loss. — Ṭabarī transmits this from Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد)]

## 103:3/q22 What overall life does the verse commend?  [* · interpretation]
- 103:3/q22/p1 Faith and personal good joined to communal care for truth, justice and endurance. — Iṣlāḥī stresses individual and collective duties; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ social responsibility; Kur'an Yolu God's and others' rights. Quṭb invokes al-Nadwī's model of revealed, accountable, egalitarian leadership. Quṭb laments nationalist replacement of Islam's banner. Al-Nadwī praises early Muslim leadership: revealed law (6:122, 5:8), moral training, office as a trust (28:83, 4:58, 6:165), liberation from human servitude, equal dignity (49:13), ʿUmar's redress for an Egyptian, shared learning across peoples, and balanced spiritual and material civilization. Rabīʿ b. ʿĀmir's address to Yazdegerd illustrates its mission; Quṭb regards the Rightly Guided Caliphate as embodying the verse. [58 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الغزالي); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL(أبو الحسن الندوي,ربعي بن عامر,عمر بن الخطاب); BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Mehmet Âkif Ersoy)]
- 103:3/q22/p2 The four qualities form a complete path to salvation and summarize Qurʾānic guidance. — Quṭb says every other path loses; al-Shāfiʿī's reported saying supports sufficiency; Elmalılı sees life as capital. [8 notes: ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 103:3/q22/p3 The verse also calls for inward transformation and valuing the present. — Elmalılı reports the Sufi 'child of the moment'; Qāshānī sees affliction as divine discipline. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 103:3/q23 What historical allusions are drawn from its counts?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which count and event is correlated.
- 103:3/q23/p1 Fourteen words indicate year fourteen of the mission and permission for jihad. — Biqāʿī identifies Hijra and eventual triumph at Badr. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 103:3/q23/p2 Four basmala words added yield eighteen and evoke year five AH's campaigns. — Biqāʿī cites 3:173 on enemy warnings and 33:10 on Confederate terror and the Prophet's subsequent declaration. [6 notes: BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL]
- 103:3/q23/p3 Four pronouns point to year nine AH and Tabūk's hardship. — Biqāʿī links patience to ensuing delegations and honor. [4 notes: QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 103:3/q24 What is said about setting and verse division?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The evidence concerns chronology and textual division.
- 103:3/q24/p1 The sura is generally Meccan, though Madinan attribution exists. — Abū Ḥayyān names Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn al-Zubayr and most authorities for Meccan; Mujāhid, Qatāda and Muqātil for Madinan. Ṭabāṭabāʾī favors Meccan; Asad and Study Quran place it after Sura 94. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 103:3/q24/p2 A divider after al-ḥaqq yields rhyme but separates parallel clauses. — Medina II proposes it; Neuwirth objects. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Medina II); CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth)-]
- 103:3/q24/p3 Verse 3 may be a later addition, explaining its unusual length. — Corpus Coranicum notes phraseology characteristic of late Meccan and Medinan texts. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 103:3/q25 How is loss outside the exception understood?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How literally to apply the commercial metaphor and the force of exemption.
- 103:3/q25/p1 Finite life expires without afterlife profit, culminating in reckoning. — Ibn Qutayba glosses deficiency; Elmalılı likens life to loaned capital; Quṭb stresses Paradise and divine pleasure (78:40); Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 13:26, 21:35, 53:39–41. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-]
- 103:3/q25/p2 The explicit exception means qualified believers are never in loss, limiting universal trade claims. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects Zamakhsharī's trade metaphor and Rāzī's claim that permissible acts or imperfect worship always lose. She traces commercial and religious khusr (83:3, 55:9, 26:181) and rejects al-Rāghib's exclusively religious gloss. [12 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]
