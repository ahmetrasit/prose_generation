# 100:6: 16 questions

## 100:6/q01 Who is 'the human' in this verse?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the generic definite describes the species' disposition, only some humans, unbelievers, or a named occasion.
- 100:6/q01/p1 Humankind generally is naturally prone to ingratitude, though divine protection, vigilance, and righteousness allow exceptions. — Biqaʿi treats rare sincere people as exceptions and links insān to familiarity with self and forgetfulness. Rāzī allows a general reading only as unprotected disposition. ʿIṣām al-Dīn prefers this reading because it praises warriors who overcome nature. Ibn ʿĀshūr grounds the tendency in self-preference, excepting prophets and the most righteous; Qurʾān 70:19, 21:37, 96:6–7 provide parallels. Elmalılı and Kuran Yolu report a broad human tendency. [16 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); BIQAI; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI(çokları); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); ALUSI(عصام الدين)+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR+]
- 100:6/q01/p2 The generic wording applies in judgment to some humans, especially the unguided, rather than every individual. — Al-Ālūsī distinguishes generic wording from the limited class judged. Elmalılı allows some members. Kuran Yolu attributes a focus on those without guidance to Rāzī. [5 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); BURSEVI]
- 100:6/q01/p3 The human is the unbeliever or a particular unbeliever. — Ibn ʿAbbās reads the generic word as the unbeliever; al-Qurṭubī likens him to sterile earth. Rāzī argues that the mention of raised graves in 100:9 suits denial of resurrection; al-Wāḥidī calls the human an unbeliever. [9 notes: QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 100:6/q01/p4 The verse concerns Qurṭ or Qurāẓ ibn ʿAbd Allāh. — Muqātil says he ate alone, starved his servant, and gave nothing to his people; names vary as Qurṭ, Qurāẓ, and Qurṭah, with differing patronymics. Rāzī cites Ibn ʿAbbās for the occasion and the resurrection context. Al-Ālūsī questions both the identification and the argument. [6 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); RAZI(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR(قيل); RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI(ابن عباس)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 100:6/q01/p5 The verse concerns al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmits this occasion; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports it as a proposed identification. [5 notes: MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNASHUR(قيل)]
- 100:6/q01/p6 The raided people are the ungrateful ones, since they rejected Islam. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls Islam God's greatest blessing, bringing good life and eternal happiness. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 100:6/q02 What does 'his Lord' emphasize about the one wronged?  [لِرَبِّهِۦ · interpretation]
- 100:6/q02/p1 He is the benefactor who created, preserves, sustains, governs, and nurtures humanity, making ingratitude especially grave. — Biqaʿi lists God's care; al-Zamakhsharī compares even the greatest human gift, parents, with God's smallest favor and says human benefaction is more immediately visible. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the object is the one most deserving thanks. [5 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR]

## 100:6/q03 How does 'to his Lord' attach grammatically?  [لِرَبِّهِۦ لَكَنُودٌۭ · grammar]
- 100:6/q03/p1 It attaches to kanūd as its object, with lām marking the relation despite the intervening emphatic lām. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the derived adjective is not inherently transitive and regularly takes an object with lām; Ibn Hishām says the predicate lām need not occupy initial position. Al-Ālūsī likewise attaches the phrase to the predicate. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR(ابن هشام)]

## 100:6/q04 Why is 'to his Lord' put before kanūd?  [لِرَبِّهِۦ لَكَنُودٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources offer compatible or alternative motives: verse ending, special blame, or restriction.
- 100:6/q04/p1 Its placement preserves the verse ending or rhyme. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī explicitly cite the line ending; Bursevī says it also fits the rhyme. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 100:6/q04/p2 Its placement spotlights the enormity of ingratitude toward God. — Al-Ālūsī calls ingratitude for God's favor especially blameworthy; Ibn ʿĀshūr says God is the one most deserving thanks and the predicate lām adds astonished emphasis. [4 notes: ALUSI; IBNASHUR; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 100:6/q04/p3 Its placement provides emphasis or restrictive focus. — Bursevī allows emphasis or restriction; al-Ālūsī reports emphatic restriction. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل)]

## 100:6/q05 What does kanūd mean or describe here?  [لَكَنُودٌۭ · meaning]

## 100:6/q07 What does the form kanūd convey?  [لَكَنُودٌۭ · grammar]
- 100:6/q07/p1 It is an intensive adjective conveying deep or habitual ingratitude. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls it intensive; Biqāʿī and Elmalılı link the form to scorn for small favors and loss of gratitude after hardship. Al-Qurṭubī notes kanūd as masculine and kanūd or kand for a woman. ## 100:6/q09 How does the horse oath support the accusation of ingratitude?  [* · links]
turns on: The oath can contrast noble animal service with human conduct, portray acquisitive raiding, or act as evidence through horses' obedience.
- 100:6/q09/p1 Noble horses and fighters contrast with the ungrateful human. — Biqāʿī compares charging horses to galloping human thoughts, which can serve truth or falsehood; he says raiding often seeks unjust goods and even jihad may lack sincerity, citing a qudsī report about remembering God in combat. Bursevī sees either known expedition horses or all horses in God's cause and says fighters have still greater merit. Asad's classical commentators treat believing chargers as laudable. [7 notes: BIQAI(حديث قدسي); BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 100:6/q09/p2 Horses' obedient service to humans exposes humans' failure to serve God. — Iṣlāḥī says animals know their master's rights, whereas humans benefit from horses yet ignore God. He cites warhorses' clan value and Ḥamāsī poetry, extends the example to camels, and says God subjected animals to people. He rejects the warrior-horse interpretation as leaving the oath unrelated to verse 6. [8 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 100:6/q09/p3 Charging horses symbolize appetites through which people forget God and responsibility. — Asad links the charge to surrender to appetites and cites 14:48 and 20:105–107 for the judgment setting. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 100:6/q09/p4 The oath by horses provides evidence that humans are ungrateful. — Iṣlāḥī states the evidentiary role explicitly; Biqāʿī says the oath rebukes neglect of judgment and thanklessness. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 100:6/q10 How does verse 6 relate to what follows in the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize different links: continued accusations, a parenthetic witness clause, and eschatological consequences.
- 100:6/q10/p1 It begins the central sequence of ingratitude, self-witness, and love of wealth before judgment. — Al-Ṭabarī says the intended sequence is ingratitude and strong love of wealth, with God's witnessing in verse 7 fronted as a parenthesis. Corpus Coranicum describes verses 6–8 as the only nominal sentences and structural center, with the oath delaying eschatological resolution. Quṭb describes a sudden shift from horses to human selfishness; Ibn ʿĀshūr details shirk, disobedience, and misuse of blessings. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM; IBNASHUR; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع)]
- 100:6/q10/p2 The accusation leads into a warning of resurrection and reckoning. — Rāzī reads 100:9 as an argument for a disbeliever referent; Biqāʿī links the coming upheaval to fleeting wealth and status; Corpus Coranicum compares the sequence to Q104 and a doom-prophecy form. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 100:6/q11 How does this accusation connect with other passages or the sūra's design?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw different literary and thematic connections.
- 100:6/q11/p1 It continues earlier warnings about evil deeds, wealth, human loss, and final reckoning. — Biqāʿī links the sūra to al-Zalzalah and cites 99:7–8, 103:2 and 104:3. Ibn ʿĀshūr says pagans and hypocrites most exhibit the condemned traits while Muslims are also warned. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 22:66; Corpus Coranicum compares 80:17 and Q104. [8 notes: BIQAI(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 100:6/q11/p2 The rapid oath and delayed answer create tension, while verses 6–8 form a still center. — Corpus Coranicum contrasts the center's nominal sentences with dynamic surrounding clauses, comparing riddling oath structures to Q90, Q97, Q101 and Q104. Quṭb says the surah's rapid jumps and rough rhythms suit the horse charge and the forceful exposure of graves and hearts. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 100:6/q11/p3 The failings illustrate pre-Islamic human-centered values. — Neuwirth is reported to read the traits this way; Corpus Coranicum says the text does not explicitly criticize particular pre-Islamic virtues. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); CORPUSCORANICUM-]

## 100:6/q12 What occasion of revelation is reported for the sūra or this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports name different individuals or expeditions.
- 100:6/q12/p1 It was revealed concerning Qurṭ ibn ʿAbd Allāh. — Muqātil specifies eating alone, starving a servant, and denying his people; Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by Rāzī, while al-Ālūsī doubts the report and its contextual argument. - 100:6/q12/p2 It was revealed concerning al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk supplies the report. [4 notes: MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 100:6/q12/p3 It disclosed the safety and raid of a delayed expedition against Kināna. — Bursevī says a month without news led hypocrites to claim the party was killed; Ṭabāṭabāʾī attributes to Muqātil a company led by al-Mundhir ibn ʿAmr and says the surah revealed their charge. ## 100:6/q15 What further context do the notes give about the sūra?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Different sources describe its emphasis or adjacent material.
- 100:6/q15/p1 It warns against preferring fleeting wealth and status over the next life. — Biqāʿī says the oath and answer disclose the sūra's purpose, while Ibn al-Zubayr treats wealth acquisition and spending as accountable under 99:7–8. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]
- 100:6/q15/p2 The following Sūrat al-Qāriʿah is Meccan. — Al-Māwardī reports agreement among the authorities he mentions. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 100:6/q15/p3 Generosity and stinginess are illustrated by named exemplars. — Bursevī names Ḥātim al-Ṭāʾī, Abū Ḥabāḥib, and Ashʿab, with anecdotes about Ashʿab expecting food or gifts. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 100:6/q16 What pilgrimage scene is reported for the preceding oath?  [* · reports]
- 100:6/q16/p1 The oath depicts pilgrims departing ʿArafāt, lighting fires, leaving Muzdalifah at dawn, crossing the valley floor, and reaching Minā's Jamʿ. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī gives this sequence. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
