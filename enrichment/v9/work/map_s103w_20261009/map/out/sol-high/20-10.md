# 20:10: 31 questions

## 20:10/q01 How does the time clause 'when he saw' connect grammatically to the account?  [إِذْ رَءَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preceding account has enough event sense to govern idh, or an unspoken command or event must be supplied.
- 20:10/q01/p1 It modifies the preceding account or report of Moses. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the implicit action in ḥadīth; al-Ṭabrisī calls it an accusative circumstance; al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān treat the account as an event; al-Sharīf says story and report imply occurrence; al-Ālūsī prefers attachment to the preceding revealed account, since a bare substantive cannot govern the clause. Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly treats idh as an adverb attached to the story. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الشريف); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:10/q01/p2 It is the object of an implicit command to remember the time. — Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Zamakhsharī are cited for the implied udhkur; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow it. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:10/q01/p3 It depends on an omitted following event or verb. — Al-Zamakhsharī is cited by al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī; al-Nasafī permits an omitted event. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:10/q02 What journey and circumstances led Moses to see the fire?  [إِذْ رَءَا نَارًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in how Moses lost the road and in the journey's particular circumstances.
- 20:10/q02/p1 Returning from Midian toward Egypt with his family, Moses lost the way on a cold dark night and could not kindle fire. — Muqātil and others specify winter, sometimes Friday, snow, pregnancy or a son's birth, dispersed livestock, no water, and fire to the left or on a mountain near Ṭuwā. Wahb says Shuʿayb allowed a visit to Moses' mother; some mention Aaron, the completed term, a flock and more than ten years away. Ibn Kathīr cites 28:29; al-Kuranyolu gives eight or ten years. The reports vary in these details. Ibn ʿAbbās, as transmitted in al-Durr, specifies winter and loss of the road. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers darkness and lack of means to kindle fire from the request for a qabas; he also reports that Moses' flint failed to strike a spark and that another explanation has Moses wishing to travel by night through intense darkness. [39 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,وهب بن منبه اليماني); WAHIDI-BASIT(وهب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل,وهب بن منبه); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 20:10/q02/p2 His protectiveness toward his wife led him to separate from companions and lose the route. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says he avoided a group lest they see her; al-Baghawī says he joined caravans at night and separated by day; al-Qurṭubī says God decreed the separation. Some add detouring from Syrian rulers. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:10/q02/p3 The fire episode belongs to Moses' wanderings after fleeing Egypt. — Asad cites the sequence beginning at 28:14 and infers from 27:7 and 28:29 that Moses was lost in the desert. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:10/q03 What did Moses actually see when the verse calls it fire?  [نَارًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether 'fire' names a physical fire, divine light perceived as fire, a small visible flame, or an allegorical image; al-Rāzī and Bursevī appeal to prophetic truthfulness.
- 20:10/q03/p1 He saw divine light that appeared to him to be fire. — Al-Suddī, Ibn ʿAbbās and most exegetes distinguish Moses' perception from God's light; some call it the Lord's light. Al-Kuranyolu says the light drew him into God's presence. Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān object that making it merely imagined fire threatens the truth of a prophet's report. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل التفسير,ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماوردي); BURSEVI(أكثر المفسرين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); RAZI-FULL-; BURSEVI(الإمام)-]
- 20:10/q03/p2 It was real fire. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr calls it one of God's veils and cites Abū Mūsā's report that the unveiled fire would burn creation within God's sight. Al-Rāzī and Bursevī prefer actual fire because prophets cannot lie; al-Rāzī says the verse does not establish that it was in a tree. Classifications distinguish Moses' fire as consuming neither food nor drink, or as luminous without burning, against worldly and infernal fires; 36:80 is cited. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(الإمام)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:10/q03/p3 He saw a brief shimmering flame, perhaps visible only to him. — Iṣlāḥī reasons that a large blaze would have prompted a different description; Ṭabāṭabāʾ takes the emphatic report to imply that only Moses perceived it. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 20:10/q03/p4 The fire is an allegory of the burning bush. — Asad refers to the biblical burning bush and his note on 27:7–8. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:10/q04 What was reported about the tree and fire as Moses approached?  [نَارًۭا · reports]
turns on: Reports give different tree species and details of its behavior; al-Rāzī questions whether the Qurʾānic wording establishes any tree.
- 20:10/q04/p1 The fire appeared in a green tree and did not consume it. — Al-Baghawī describes brilliant white fire surrounding the tree from bottom to top without altering its green color; Ibn ʿAbbās marveled at its unchanged beauty. Al-Mahdawī says it receded as Moses approached, then drew near after fear. Al-Qushayrī says the tree burned end to end but yielded no flame and illuminated hearts, while al-Rāzī notes the Qurʾān does not establish the tree detail. Wahb ibn Munabbih reports a great fire growing within the lush green tree without consuming it; when Moses attempted to take a flame, it retreated and went out. [9 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,المهدوي); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وعبد الله ووهب وقتادة ومقاتل والكلبي); DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 20:10/q04/p2 The tree was a jujube or ʿanāb. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported for the jujube identification; Abū Ḥayyān's collected variants include ʿanāb. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وعبد الله ووهب وقتادة ومقاتل والكلبي)]
- 20:10/q04/p3 The tree was al-ʿawsaj, a thorn tree. — Qatāda, Muqātil and al-Kalbī are named by al-Baghawī; al-Mahdawī says a thorn tree. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وعبد الله ووهب وقتادة ومقاتل والكلبي)]
- 20:10/q04/p4 The tree was a green samura. — Al-Baghawī attributes this to Ibn Masʿūd; Abū Ḥayyān lists it among variants. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وعبد الله ووهب وقتادة ومقاتل والكلبي)]
- 20:10/q04/p5 The tree was al-ʿaliq or ʿalīqah. — Al-Baghawī reports Wahb's identification; Abū Ḥayyān lists the variant. Wahb ibn Munabbih identifies the green tree containing the fire as al-ʿaliq. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وعبد الله ووهب وقتادة ومقاتل والكلبي); DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 20:10/q04/p6 Moses thought the distant light was a shepherd's fire. — Al-Suddī and Bursevī connect the expectation of a firebrand to shepherds. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI]

## 20:10/q05 Who was Moses' 'family' whom he addressed?  [لِأَهْلِهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether ahl includes children and a servant, or denotes the wife alone with plural wording.
- 20:10/q05/p1 His wife, children, and possibly a servant. — Al-Rāzī gives wife, child and servant; Abū Ḥayyān specifies wife, two sons and servant. Al-Ālūsī allows wife, child and servant. Ṭabāṭabāʾ infers others were present from the instruction to stay. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the plural imperative as confirmation that Moses' wife and children accompanied him; he also cites Exodus' account of the wife and sons traveling on donkeys toward Egypt. [6 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:10/q05/p2 His wife alone, Shuʿayb's daughter. — Al-Wāḥidī explains the plural by ahl's collective outward form; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī add respectful plural address. Al-Suhaylī names her Ṣafūriyya. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(السهيلي)]

## 20:10/q06 What does 'stay' mean here?  [ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · meaning]
- 20:10/q06/p1 Remain here temporarily. — Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī distinguish makth from iqāma, which implies continuing residence; other sources gloss it as stay or remain in place. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:10/q07 Why did Moses tell his family to stay?  [فَقَالَ لِأَهْلِهِ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the departure is explained by deliberate care for the family or an overwhelming response to the sight.
- 20:10/q07/p1 He wanted them to remain while he went to seek fire or directions. — Al-Ālūsī says he prevented them following him toward the fire, as people commonly do; Ṭabāṭabāʾ supplies an omitted promise to go and return. Al-Qushayrī stresses his care for them despite the valley's danger. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers from the sight of fire at night that Moses needed it, explaining why he told his family to wait. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:10/q07/p2 The sight drew him out in an agitation he could not resist. — Al-Qushayrī gives this as another account. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:10/q08 How is the pronoun vowel in 'to his family' read?  [لِأَهْلِهِ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · readings]
turns on: Pronoun dialect and phonological explanation; the sources agree that both vocalizations are transmitted.
- 20:10/q08/p1 The hāʾ is read with ḍamma. — Hamza is consistently named, also for 28:29; Ibn Mujāhid adds Ibn Saʿdān from Isḥāq al-Musayyabī, while Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī add al-Aʿmash, Ṭalḥa and a Nāfiʿ transmission. Al-Naḥḥās calls it original wāw retention and a dialect like marartu bihu; al-Jishumī describes transfer of the hamza vowel; al-Samīn calls it an original Hijazi dialect and reports an assimilation explanation. Qirāʾāt-Er offers dialectal leveling or syntax. Ibn ʿĀshūr also names Khalaf alongside Ḥamza for ḍamma and explains it as assimilation to the following hamzat al-waṣl. [15 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، ابن سعدان عن إسحق المسيبي); NAHHAS(حمزة); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI(حمزة); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(حمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,حمزة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش وطلحة وحمزة ونافع); SAMIN-DURR(حمزة); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش); QIRAAT-ER; IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة وخلف)]
- 20:10/q08/p2 The hāʾ is read with kasra. — The other readers or majority are named by Ibn Mujāhid, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān; al-Jishumī explains it through adjacent sukūns. Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes the kasra to the majority. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، ابن سعدان عن إسحق المسيبي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; JISHUMI(حمزة); TABRISI(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش وطلحة وحمزة ونافع); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة وخلف)]

## 20:10/q09 What does 'ānastu' convey?  [ءَانَسْتُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb highlights clear reassuring sight in this scene or the broader act of finding or sensing.
- 20:10/q09/p1 I saw distinctly or perceived clearly. — Ibn Qutayba cites 4:6 for the related sense recognized; al-Ṭabarī derives it from uns and cites an Arab proverb; al-Mubarrad describes distant reassuring sight and a hunting bird noticing prey; al-ʿAjjāj and al-Ḥārith ibn Ḥilliza furnish poetic usage. Al-Rāzī contrasts visible human beings with hidden jinn. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ counts four Moses-fire uses and a fifth at 4:6, and argues for familiarity or comfort rather than neutral sight; Iṣlāḥī says attentive observation. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that īnās denotes clear perception without uncertainty. [25 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(العجاج,المبرد,المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحارث بن حلزة); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:10/q09/p2 I found or sensed something, potentially by a sense other than sight. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Farrāʾ gloss found; al-Māwardī allows sensing something by a fire. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say perception is broader than sight and cite a verse about faint sound; dictionaries cited by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ include hearing. Qatāda glosses Moses' statement as sensing a fire. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 20:10/q10 Why does Moses emphasize that he has seen a fire?  [إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ نَارًۭا · rhetoric]
- 20:10/q10/p1 He reports certain perception to reassure his family while leaving the hoped-for benefits uncertain. — Al-Bayḍāwī contrasts certain sight with merely hoped-for fire and guidance; al-Ālūsī says inna confirms the sight even though the family expressed no doubt. Ṭabāṭabāʾ takes the singular emphatic report to imply the others did not perceive the fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr says inna marks the importance of this welcome news to the family in the darkness. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:10/q11 How is the first-person yāʾ of 'innī' read?  [إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the possessive yāʾ is opened in connection or kept quiescent.
- 20:10/q11/p1 It is read open, innīya. — Ibn al-Jazarī names the two Medinese, Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr among five yāʾs in this passage; Qirāʾāt-Er says the change affects prosody without changing meaning. Ibn Mujāhid reports that Nāfiʿ opens the listed first-person yāʾs except in walī fīhā and akhī ushdud; Abū ʿAmr excepts walī fīhā and ḥashartanī aʿmā; Ibn Kathīr excepts walī fīhā, yassir lī amrī, lidhikrī in, ʿalā ʿaynī idh, and biraʾsī innī. Each opens the remaining listed yāʾs. [5 notes: NASHR(المدنيان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER; IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير,نافع)+]
- 20:10/q11/p2 It is read quiescent, innī. — Qirāʾāt-Er presents the quiescent form as the contrasting reading. Ibn Mujāhid reports that Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ibn ʿĀmir, and ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr keep all the listed first-person yāʾs quiescent. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي، ابن عامر، عاصم عن أبي بكر)+]

## 20:10/q12 Why does Moses say 'perhaps'?  [لَّعَلِّىٓ · rhetoric]
- 20:10/q12/p1 A firebrand or guide was hoped for, not guaranteed. — Al-Rāzī says Moses would not promise what he could not guarantee, contrasting a claim that Abraham lied for a good purpose. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī distinguish the uncertain outcome from the certain sight. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 20:10/q12/p2 The hope concerns finding a guide; Moses was certain he could take a flame. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says taking a flame is certain, while meeting and accompanying someone who knows the route remains possible. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:10/q13 How is the yāʾ of 'laʿallī' read?  [لَّعَلِّىٓ ءَاتِيكُم · readings]
turns on: Opening or quiescence of the first-person yāʾ.
- 20:10/q13/p1 It is read with fatḥ, laʿalliya. — Ibn al-Jazarī names Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and Abū Jaʿfar across six occurrences; Qirāʾāt-Er says meaning is unchanged. Ibn Mujāhid says Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, and Ibn Kathīr open the listed first-person yāʾs apart from their individually named exceptions; laʿallī is not among those exceptions. [5 notes: NASHR(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER; IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير,نافع)+]
- 20:10/q13/p2 It is read quiescent, laʿallī. — Ibn al-Jazarī names the Kufans and Yaʿqūb; Qirāʾāt-Er contrasts it with open yāʾ. Ibn Mujāhid reports that Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ibn ʿĀmir, and ʿĀṣim through Abū Bakr keep all listed first-person yāʾs quiescent. [3 notes: NASHR(الكوفيون ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة، الكسائي، ابن عامر، عاصم عن أبي بكر)+]

## 20:10/q14 What is the 'qabas' Moses hopes to bring?  [بِقَبَسٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether qabas is specifically a flaming brand, as al-Ālūsī insists, or may also be called an ember.
- 20:10/q14/p1 A flame or burning brand taken from a larger fire, carried on a stick or wick for warmth. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and al-Zajjāj specify the tip of a stick or reed, and al-Zajjāj allows a wick; al-Samīn calls it a passive-sense verbal noun, a glowing end taken up. Al-Ālūsī equates it with the jadwah of 28:29 and distinguishes it from an ember. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts its guiding warmth with hostile meteors and notes iqtabasa at 57:13; a report from al-Bāqir says the family needed warmth. Ibn ʿĀshūr describes a flame taken from a burning source, such as a firebrand, and infers darkness and no means of kindling their own fire. [24 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; STUDYQURAN(Q); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:10/q14/p2 An ember or piece of fire to carry back. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow an ember, and Ibn Kathīr cites 28:29 for a glowing brand and warmth; Iṣlāḥī likewise speaks of an ember against cold. Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly includes an ember or coal among what can be taken as a qabas. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:10/q15 How do the related verbs for taking or giving fire and knowledge differ?  [بِقَبَسٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether usage assigns separate domains to the simple and prefixed forms or allows both in both domains.
- 20:10/q15/p1 The simple form concerns fire; the prefixed form concerns seeking or imparting knowledge. — Abū Zayd distinguishes bringing fire from seeking it; al-Mubarrad and al-Samīn distinguish giving fire from giving knowledge. These reports differ in their precise formulation. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(المبرد)]
- 20:10/q15/p2 Both forms can be used for fire or knowledge. — Al-Kisāʾī allows both; al-Mubarrad says their underlying sense is one because both fire and knowledge illuminate. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكسائي,المبرد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي)]

## 20:10/q16 How is 'from it' attached grammatically?  [مِّنْهَا بِقَبَسٍ · grammar]
turns on: Direct verbal attachment or an omitted circumstance describing the brand.
- 20:10/q16/p1 It attaches directly to the verb 'bring'. — Al-Samīn and Abū al-Baqāʾ, as cited by al-Ālūsī, permit this attachment. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 20:10/q16/p2 It is an omitted circumstantial qualification of the firebrand. — Al-Samīn and Abū al-Baqāʾ supply the omitted circumstance. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 20:10/q17 Do the two hopes joined by 'or' exclude one another?  [أَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the alternatives are merely listed as possibilities or explicitly compatible.
- 20:10/q17/p1 They are alternative hoped-for outcomes but need not exclude obtaining both. — Bursevī explicitly allows both; al-Ālūsī says aw prevents exclusion and presents two anticipated possibilities. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:10/q17/p2 They express two possible outcomes, neither yet certain. — Al-Ālūsī treats aw in the hope clause as the expression of two anticipated possibilities. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 20:10/q17/p3 The alternatives are a certain flame and the possible added benefit of a guide. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls aw a choice and distinguishes the certainty of taking a flame from the possibility of finding and accompanying someone who knows the route. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:10/q18 What does 'upon the fire' mean?  [عَلَى ٱلنَّارِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ʿalā retains spatial elevation, stands for another local preposition, or describes an implicit guide.
- 20:10/q18/p1 On or above a nearby elevated place by the fire. — Al-Rāzī says people around a fire stand on overlooking ground; Abū Ḥayyān keeps the ordinary elevation sense, and al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow elevated ground. Al-Ālūsī says elevation becomes an idiom for a nearby place, citing Sībawayh's account of bi as contact with a nearby place. Ibn ʿĀshūr interprets ʿalā as figurative spatial nearness to someone tending the fire for light or warmth, and cites a verse by al-Aʿshā for this use; al-Aʿshā's usage is also separately reported. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL(الأعشى); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:10/q18/p2 It means 'at,' 'with,' or 'by' the fire. — Ibn al-Anbārī offers these prepositional senses; al-Ālūsī says substitution is unnecessary. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأنباري)-]
- 20:10/q18/p3 It is a circumstance of an implied guide located near the fire. — Some grammarians move a description before its noun and take the result to mean someone near the fire who could help the family warm itself. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:10/q19 What guidance did Moses hope to find by the fire?  [هُدًۭى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the family's lost route and need for water call for practical directions, or the phrase suggests religious guidance; the latter is challenged using the parallel at 28:29.
- 20:10/q19/p1 A person or people to guide him along the lost road. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Suddī and others give this sense. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Wāḥidī call hudā a verbal noun in place of hādī; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī allow ellipsis 'one possessing guidance' or metonymy. Al-Suddī reasons people must tend a fire. Al-Ṭabarī permits spoken information about the route; Wahb seeks knowledge from an account. Qatāda transmits Aylah as a place attribution; al-Māwardī reports the family later stayed three days before a shepherd found them and then lived in a village forty years. Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid, as transmitted in al-Durr, identify a person who could guide or point out the road. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that Moses hoped for someone who knew the route and was traveling in the same direction, so they could accompany one another. [41 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,وهب بن منبه); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الزجاج,السدي,الفراء,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,الفراء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:10/q19/p2 A sign or source of direction revealing the route. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī explicitly allow a sign, and al-Ṭabrisī says hudā names both guidance and what guides. Al-Rāzī gives a source or sign; al-Ṭabarī and Wahb allow route information. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:10/q19/p3 A person to lead him to water or to both water and the road. — Al-Zajjāj is cited for lost water; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report the water variant; al-Ālūsī's travel account says there was no water. ʿIkrima, as transmitted in al-Durr, specifies a guide to water. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج ومجاهد وقتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 20:10/q19/p4 Religious guidance. — Al-Bayḍāwī lists it as an alternative; Abū Ḥayyān attributes it to Mujāhid and Qatāda but calls it remote. Al-Ālūsī says their minds remained on religion, but rejects the fit with the family's needs and 28:29. Asad sees a possible symbol of Moses' dawning spiritual need. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God inspired Moses to say hudā, anticipating the great guidance he would find at the fire and the benefit he would bring his people. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج ومجاهد وقتادة)-; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)-]

## 20:10/q20 How is 'hudā' read at a pause?  [هُدًۭى · readings]
turns on: Whether the final alif is merely a pause substitute for tanwīn or can be treated as a lexical ending permitting imāla.
- 20:10/q20/p1 Pause without imāla, preserving the alif replacing tanwīn. — Al-Samīn calls this better; Abū al-Baqāʾ prefers alif without inclination. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:10/q20/p2 Pause with imāla. — Al-Ālūsī reports explanations that tanwīn resembles a lexical ending, that the ending remains without tanwīn, or that a pausal form is used in all cases. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]

## 20:10/q21 What grammatical role can the clause of hope play?  [لَّعَلِّىٓ ءَاتِيكُم أَوْ أَجِدُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the hope clause explains the command, describes Moses, or modifies the fire; al-Ālūsī rejects the last.
- 20:10/q21/p1 It gives the reason for telling the family to stay. — Al-Ālūsī lists this as one analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:10/q21/p2 It describes Moses' state as he went toward the fire. — Al-Ālūsī lists a circumstantial analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:10/q21/p3 It is an adjective describing the fire. — Al-Ālūsī reports a defense from a poetic comparison, but calls the analysis flawed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:10/q22 Why repeat 'the fire' instead of using a pronoun?  [عَلَى ٱلنَّارِ · rhetoric]
- 20:10/q22/p1 Naming the fire explains why someone offering guidance might be found there. — Al-Ālūsī says people commonly gather around a fire; al-Suddī makes the same practical inference. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); TABRISI(السدي)]

## 20:10/q23 How does this telling relate to other tellings of Moses' fire?  [* · links]
- 20:10/q23/p1 Parallel passages vary their wording while recounting the same episode. — Al-Bāqillānī cites Ṭā Hā, al-Naml and al-Qaṣaṣ as varied expression that broadens the challenge to produce comparable discourse, citing 27:8, 28:29 and 52:34; he says paraphrase reveals the loss of Qurʾānic wording. Al-Burhān notes Ṭā Hā's atāhā against al-Naml's jāʾahā. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ distinguishes Moses' shihāb qabs in 27:7 and 28:29 from hostile meteors in 15:18, 37:10 and 72:9; she records al-Rāghib's bright-flame sense and Arabic applications to stars, snow mountains and white streaks. [9 notes: BAQILLANI; BURHAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]
- 20:10/q23/p2 The parallel passages clarify that Moses sought warmth and a glowing brand. — Al-Yaḥyā cites 27:7; Ibn Kathīr and others cite 28:29 for darkness, cold and the brand. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 20:10/q24 How does the episode serve the surrounding sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize consolation, literary and narrative sequence, or the beginning of the prophetic call.
- 20:10/q24/p1 It consoles Muhammad through Moses' trials and God's support. — Al-Ṭabarī says the episode amid Quraysh's persecution promises Muhammad's eventual ascendancy and weakening of unbelievers' schemes, urging endurance in worship and effort. Al-Biqāʿī prefers a coherence reading in which all creation belongs to God and divine aid overcomes difficulty; Moses' rescue, Pharaoh's ruin and Israel's emergence exemplify it. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 20:10/q24/p2 Moses follows mention of Muhammad because God used the apparent fire to draw him from his family. — Al-Qushayrī says the fire was a means rather than the ultimate aim; al-Bāqillānī praises the exact ordering and smooth transitions between stories. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAQILLANI]
- 20:10/q24/p3 The episode begins Moses' call to prophethood in the sacred valley. — Iṣlāḥī places the return from Midian at Ṭuwā, where Moses receives prophethood; Kuranyolu calls the light an entry into divine presence. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]

## 20:10/q25 What inward meaning is given to the fire and Moses' hopes?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different inward readings identify the fire with the Holy Spirit, divine manifestation, or spiritual awakening.
- 20:10/q25/p1 The fire is the Holy Spirit illuminating souls, and the family are Moses' faculties. — Al-Qāshānī says the faculties remain still as sensory activity quiets; the firebrand is a luminous benefit to them, while finding a guide is gaining knowledge and gnosis leading to truth. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:10/q25/p2 God appeared in the desired form of fire to draw Moses toward Him. — Bursevī transmits some Sufis' explanation that another form might have made Moses turn away; his poem says Moses did not yet recognize the divine manifestation in light and speech. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]
- 20:10/q25/p3 The fire symbolizes rising spiritual certainty. — The Study Quran adds the mountain of God, Horeb, from Exodus; Asad treats the lost road as a possible symbol of spiritual need and the fire as a burning-bush allegory. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the search for light in darkness as a subtle divine symbol of revelation illuminating people with true religion after misguidance and false belief. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:10/q26 How did Moses recognize the divine call at the fire?  [* · theology]
turns on: Inference from the character of the speech versus direct divinely given recognition.
- 20:10/q26/p1 He inferred it from the speech's ordered structure and composition. — Al-Qushayrī says this recognition came when he tried to take a flame. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 20:10/q26/p2 God made His speech known through special inspiration without inference. — Al-Qushayrī reports this alternative. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:10/q27 Why did Moses remove his sandals, according to related reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports appeal to the sandals' material, the valley's blessing, or symbolic humility.
- 20:10/q27/p1 They were made from donkey hide. — Al-Thaʿlabī transmits Ibn Masʿūd's report from the Prophet specifying a dead donkey and a variant specifying untanned hide; al-Ḥasan contrasts this with Muhammad's prayer in sandals. [2 notes: THALABI(الحسن,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 20:10/q27/p2 His bare feet could touch and receive the blessed ground. — ʿIkrima and Mujāhid call it twice sanctified; another explanation singles out his heels. [2 notes: THALABI(عكرمة ومجاهد)]
- 20:10/q27/p3 Barefootedness expresses humility or detachment from family. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports these explanations, adding that shoes in dreams signify a wife. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 20:10/q27/p4 Removing sandals was associated with the sacred valley. — Abū al-Aḥwaṣ reports Ibn Masʿūd joking to Abū Mūsā, who had removed sandals before prayer, 'Are you in the sacred valley?' [1 notes: THALABI(أبو الأحوص)]

## 20:10/q28 How are other nearby words and the preceding question explained?  [* · meaning]
turns on: These are separate contextual observations included in the collected notes.
- 20:10/q28/p1 The preceding hal keeps its ordinary confirmatory interrogative sense. — Al-Samīn also reports proposed readings as qad or as negation. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 20:10/q28/p2 Akhfaytu can mean concealed or revealed, while khafaytu means revealed only. — Al-Ṭabrisī records the distinction and also glosses radā and taraddā as destruction. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 20:10/q28/p3 Saʿy means swift movement or action, and radā and ardā concern perishing and destroying. — Abū Ḥayyān cites Durayd ibn al-Ṣimma's poetry for ardā. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:10/q28/p4 The king's dream in Joseph was true inspiration, not the confused dreams claimed by his court. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 12:43–44 in her wider discussion of distinctive Qurʾānic diction. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 20:10/q29 What view of divine istiwāʾ does the source favor in the preceding context?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether istiwāʾ is explained as power or as a distinct act or attribute.
- 20:10/q29/p1 It means divine power. — Al-Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's explanation and calls the Kullābī account of a separate act or attribute named istiwāʾ unintelligible. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]
- 20:10/q29/p2 It is an act or attribute called istiwāʾ. — Al-Jishumī reports the Kullābī account solely to dispute its intelligibility. [0 notes: JISHUMI-]

## 20:10/q30 What suspense does the report of seeing a fire create?  [إِذْ رَءَا نَارًۭا · rhetoric]
- 20:10/q30/p1 The sight admits many possible outcomes and heightens suspense about what will happen. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sight of fire allows many scenarios, thereby intensifying suspense. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:10/q31 Why was Moses led to say 'guidance' at the fire?  [هُدًۭى · rhetoric]
- 20:10/q31/p1 God inspired the word in anticipation of the great guidance Moses would find and bring to his people. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses was divinely inspired to say hudā, foreshadowing the guidance at the fire and its benefit for his people. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
