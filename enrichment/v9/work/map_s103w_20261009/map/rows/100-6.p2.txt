[QURTUBI:100:6/r1] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | This is the answer to the oath: people are disposed to be ungrateful for blessings.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r10] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الشاعر | reports | A poetic usage glosses kanūd as ungrateful toward people’s favors.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r11] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قيل | reports | Another gloss says the kanūd rejects small favors and fails to thank for much.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r12] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قيل | reports | Another view defines kanūd as one who denies the truth.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r13] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قيل | reports | A wordplay says the tribe Kinda was named so because it denied its father.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r14] QURTUBI, d. 671 · إبراهيم بن هرمة | reports | Ibrāhīm b. Hirma’s verse uses kanūd for a miserly woman whose stinginess is remembered.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r15] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Another etymology derives kanūd from cutting: the ungrateful person cuts off the thanks that should be maintained; kand al-ḥabl means to cut a rope.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r16] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿshā’s line uses kannādah in a context of cutting, evidence for the root’s sense of severing.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r17] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | holds | Kand yaknud means to deny a blessing; kanūd is the masculine form, and kanūd or kand can also describe a woman.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r18] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الأعشى | reports | Al-Aʿshā’s verse calls a woman kanūd toward the regular visitor’s affection, ungrateful for continued connection.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r19] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās takes “the human” here to mean the unbeliever and compares him to barren ground that grows nothing.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r2] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās defines kanūd as very ungrateful and denying God’s blessings.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r20] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says the verse was revealed about al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r21] QURTUBI, d. 671 · المبرد | reports | Al-Mubarrad defines kanūd as one who withholds what is due and cites a poem about being kanūd toward a visitor’s affection.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r22] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو بكر الواسطي | reports | Abū Bakr al-Wāsiṭī defines the kanūd as one who spends God’s blessings in disobedience to God.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r23] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو بكر الوراق | reports | Abū Bakr al-Warrāq says the kanūd attributes blessings to himself and his helpers.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r24] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الترمذي | reports | Al-Tirmidhī defines the kanūd as one who sees the blessing but not the Benefactor.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r25] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ذو النون المصري | reports | Dhū al-Nūn al-Miṣrī says both the halūʿ and kanūd see the blessing but not its giver.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r26] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قيل | reports | Another interpretation calls the kanūd resentful and envious.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r27] QURTUBI, d. 671 · قيل | reports | Another view calls him ignorant of his own worth; a maxim says that one who fails to know his worth exposes his veil.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r28] QURTUBI, d. 671 · author | prefers | All the views return to ingratitude and denial; if sound, the Prophet’s interpretation through blameworthy traits is the highest and settles the matter.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r3] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says the kanūd recalls calamities and forgets blessings; a poet restates this contrast.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r4] QURTUBI, d. 671 · الشاعر | reports | The poem illustrates ingratitude as complaining of calamities while forgetting blessings.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r5] QURTUBI, d. 671 · أبو أمامة الباهلي | reports | The Prophet defines the kanūd as one who eats alone, withholds his gifts, and beats his servant; al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm transmits it in Nawādir al-Uṣūl without a grading stated here.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r6] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Another Prophetic report calls the worst person one who lives alone, withholds his gift, and lashes his servant; al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm transmits it in Nawādir al-Uṣūl.
[QURTUBI:100:6/r7] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says kanūd means disobedient in Kinda and Ḥaḍramawt dialects.

[QURTUBI:100:6/r9] QURTUBI, d. 671 · ابن عباس ومقاتل | reports | In the Kināna dialect kanūd means miserly and bad in one’s treatment of dependents; Muqātil gives this view too.
