[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#15/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The infinitive clause is the direct object of ‘tire,’ which is directly transitive; a poetic line supports this construction.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#15/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another analysis supplies the preposition min before the infinitive; a second poem shows ‘tire’ used with min.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#15/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Saʾam and saʾāma mean weariness, boredom, and annoyance with something.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#15/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The pronoun in ‘write it’ may refer to the debt or the right; the latter is nearer, though it also means the debt.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | The phrase ‘until its term’ most clearly modifies an implied phrase: the debt remains established in liability until it falls due.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa attaches ‘until its term’ directly to ‘write it.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh rejects attachment to ‘write’: writing ends quickly and does not continue until the debt is due.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | A third analysis makes the phrase a circumstantial clause referring to the pronoun in ‘write it.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | ‘That’ may refer only to writing, to writing and testimony, or to all the preceding instructions; the broadest reference is best.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | One derivation takes ‘more equitable’ from quadriliteral aqsaṭ, ‘act justly’; this makes the hamza privative but requires an anomalous comparative from a quadriliteral.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | Zamakhshari says the comparatives may derive from aqsaṭ and aqāma, or from nisba forms meaning ‘possessing equity’ and ‘upright.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الشيخ | rejects | The Shaykh says Sibawayh did not explicitly state that comparatives derive from quadrilaterals; this is inferred from his rules for the exclamative form.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#16/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Grammarians differ among unrestricted permission, unrestricted prohibition, and permission only when the hamza is not causative; the last reading explains Sibawayh.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn Atiyya derives aqsaṭ from the triliteral qasuṭ with ḍamma, like akram from karuma.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Aqwam may come from transitive aqāma with its extra hamza removed, meaning firmer for establishing testimony; the analogy is 18:12. | mentions 18:12
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa derives aqwam from intransitive qāma, meaning that testimony itself is more firmly established.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The lām in ‘for testimony’ attaches to aqwam and is semantically an object; it is extra if aqwam means ‘establish,’ but not if it means ‘be established.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r13] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The lām cannot normally be dropped to make its complement an accusative after a comparative, except under poetic necessity.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | Another derivation takes it from qist, ‘justice,’ a noun without a verb; this avoids deriving a comparative from ifʿāl.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | These etymologies assume the triliteral means injustice and the quadriliteral means justice.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · سعيد بن جبير | reports | When al-Hajjaj asked his judgment, Saʿid b. Jubayr called him ‘qāsiṭ, ʿādil,’ making a pointed double-edged description. | mentions 72:15, 6:1
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | If q-s-ṭ can mean both justice and injustice, the ambiguity is straightforward; Ibn al-Qattaʿ records both opposite senses.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو عبيدة | reports | Abu Ubayda, as cited through Ibn al-Sikkit, distinguishes qasaṭ as injustice or justice by vocalization, while aqsaṭ always means justice.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#17/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الراغب الأصفهاني | reports | Al-Raghib says taking another’s share is qist and injustice, while giving that share is iqsaṭ and fairness.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#18/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | ‘So that you do not doubt’ has a deleted preposition; proposed choices are lām, ilā, or min, but min does not fit the sense.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#18/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Tartābū is form VIII from rayba; its original yāʾ becomes alif because it is vowelled after an open syllable.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#18/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The comparison’s second term is omitted because context supplies it: writing is more equitable, stable, and doubt-free than leaving the debt undocumented.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#18/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Omission is smoother when the comparative is a predicate than when it is an adjective or circumstantial phrase.
