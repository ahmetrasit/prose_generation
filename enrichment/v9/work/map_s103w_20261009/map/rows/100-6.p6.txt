[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p416/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The oath sworn by horses is evidence for the later premise that man is ungrateful to his Lord.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p417/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Kanūd means ungrateful: a person who witnesses horses' obedience and benefits from their sacrifice yet ignores his own duty to God is more ungrateful than animals that know a master's rights.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p417/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Horses exemplify service found throughout animals God placed at man's disposal; Qur'anic mentions, especially of camels, should awaken gratitude and teach submission, diligence, and patience.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p418/r1] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Horses are singled out because clans needed high-pedigree warhorses for attack, defense, and family protection; their importance also made them common in classical poetry.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p418/r2] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | The author cites a Ḥamāsī poet's verse about spending on a thoroughbred horse as a house sentinel and keeping a servant for it, illustrating horses' esteem.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p418/r3] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | People understand a servant's value from its service, yet man disobeys his Lord and still expects the greatest afterlife reward; horses serve only because God subjected them to man.
[ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p418/r4] ISLAHI-TADABBUR · author | holds | Unlike horses, man remains indifferent to worshipping and serving the Creator who made him, all useful animals, and every means of his sustenance.
