[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Salam is a sale of a specified, described commodity in liability for present capital or its equivalent, due at a known term.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Requiring liability excludes unknown goods and specified individual orchards whose fruit may fail; full description excludes unspecified kinds or qualities of dates, cloth, or fish.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Present capital excludes debt-for-debt; treating it as present permits a two- or three-day delay in paying salam capital, which Malik allows, while al-Shafiʿi and Kufans forbid any delay after contract and separation by analogy with currency exchange.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes salam from currency exchange: exchange has many strict conditions, while salam more closely resembles ordinary transactions and tolerates more flexibility.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | A known term excludes spot salam, which is impermissible on the commonly held view, and excludes the pre-Islamic unknown terms.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Salam and salaf refer to the same transaction, but salam is its specific name because salaf can also mean a loan; salam is a unanimously permitted sale excepted from the ban on selling what one does not possess.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | Salam is licensed to meet mutual needs: the buyer needs the harvest and the grower needs money before it ripens; it is a sale for the needy, and an immediate sale would defeat that purpose.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The agreed conditions are six for the goods—liability, description, measurement, deferral, known term, and availability at maturity—and three for capital—known kind, specified measure, and cash payment, with cash status disputed.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#2/r9] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن العربي | reports | He says the first requirement, liability, is self-evident because salam is a debt transaction sought for benefit and accommodation.
