  12:18/q28/p1 He kissed or wept over the shirt until his face was stained; another account has him collapse and revive near dawn. — reasons: Alusi transmits the variant accounts; in the latter Judah checked Jacob's breath and lamented the loss of brother and father.
12:18/q29 What caution is drawn from Jacob's repeated suspicion of his sons? [* · interpretation]
  12:18/q29/p1 Even prudent people should question their own judgment. — reasons: Ibn Abi Rifaʿa says Jacob rightly suspected them about Joseph, but repeated the same charge when they reported Benjamin's theft, where his suspicion failed; Qurtubi cites 12:17 and 12:81.
12:18/q30 What does the wording 'beautiful patience' evoke? [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the implied quality is grandeur or bitterness.)
  12:18/q30/p1 Its indefinite form may magnify the grandeur of patience. — reasons: Tabatabai says the phrase praises patience by naming the cause for its result; an omitted description may suggest grandeur.
  12:18/q30/p2 Its indefinite form may suggest the bitterness and difficulty of patience. — reasons: Tabatabai offers this as the other implication of the indefinite form and omitted description.
