[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | ‘When you trade’ may be conditional, answered by a preceding clause or an omitted ‘then bear witness’; alternatively it is purely temporal.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r10] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The readings and explanations alternate because the verse permits both interpretations.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r11] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو جعفر وعمرو بن عبيد | reports | Abu Jaʿfar and ʿAmr b. ʿUbayd read the doubled rāʾ sākin in connected speech; the author notes the three-consonant cluster is weak, tolerated by lengthening and by analogy to pause.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r12] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima reads the form with both rāʾs separated and the first kasra; the implied subject is the rights-holder, who must not coerce the scribe or witness.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | On the temporal reading, the instruction is to witness at the time of trading.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The common reading has fatḥ on the final rāʾ in the jussive prohibition; lā is prohibitive, and the vowel follows the rule seen in the reading of ‘if one errs.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزجاج | prefers | On the active reading, the scribe and witness are forbidden to harm the beneficiary by altering or withholding documentation; al-Zajjaj prefers it because such conduct is clearly sinful.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عباس ومجاهد وطاووس | reports | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Tawus are reported to explain the active reading as the scribe or witness harming the party entitled to the record.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عمر وابن عباس ومجاهد وابن أبي إسحاق | reports | Al-Dani reports these authorities reading the first rāʾ with kasra, supporting the active interpretation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | On the passive reading, no one may harm the scribe or witness; its context concerns those receiving the writing and testimony, and the consequence would otherwise address two people.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r8] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عباس ومجاهد وطاووس | reports | The passive interpretation is also transmitted in tafsir from Ibn Abbas and the other named authorities.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#21/r9] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عمر وابن عباس ومجاهد وابن أبي إسحاق | reports | Al-Dani reports these authorities reading the first rāʾ with fatḥa, matching the passive interpretation.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#22/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن محيصن | reports | Ibn Muhaysin reads the rāʾ nominative as a negation whose report-like form conveys a prohibition, as in 2:197. | mentions 2:197
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#22/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · عكرمة | reports | In a report from Miqsam, ʿIkrima reads doubled rāʾ with kasra, reflecting the original meeting of two sukūns.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#22/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | The related grammatical and reading issues were discussed under the prohibition against a mother being harmed in 2:233. | mentions 2:233
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#22/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | ‘If you do’ means any act forbidden by God; its object is omitted because context supplies it.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#22/r5] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | The pronoun in ‘it is’ may refer to refusal or to harm.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#22/r6] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · أبو البقاء | reports | Abu al-Baqa supplies ‘incumbent upon you’ after ‘upon you’; the author instead supplies an unrestricted state as an adjective of ‘transgression.’
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#22/r7] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | prefers | ‘God teaches you’ is most likely an independent sentence; Abu al-Baqa also allows a circumstantial clause, but the author objects that a positive present verb does not directly follow the circumstantial wāw.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#3/r1] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | أن يكتب is the direct object of لا يأب. كما علمه الله may modify that writing or attach to the later فليكتب; the Sheikh finds the latter strained by word order and فاء.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#3/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · الزمخشري | reports | If كما علمه الله attaches to أن يكتب, refusal of the specified writing is forbidden and that writing is commanded; if to فليكتب, refusal in general is forbidden, then qualified writing commanded.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#3/r3] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · ابن عطية | reports | كما may attach to لا يأب causally: since God favored him with literacy, he should not refuse but write generously; the Sheikh calls this unlikely.
[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#3/r4] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | holds | Even if كما attaches to فليكتب, its كاف can still be causal: he should write because God taught him.

[SAMIN-DURR:2:282#4/r2] SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 · author | reports | أمل and أملى both mean dictating; some call them dialects, others derive the yāʾ as a replacement for a doubled consonant, with repetition underlying both forms. | mentions 5:54
