## THE CURRENT MAP
20:10/q01 How does the time clause 'when he saw' connect grammatically to the account? [إِذْ رَءَا · grammar] (turns on: Whether the preceding account has enough event sense to govern idh, or an unspoken command or event must be supplied.)
  20:10/q01/p1 It modifies the preceding account or report of Moses. — reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the implicit action in ḥadīth; al-Ṭabrisī calls it an accusative circumstance; al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān treat the account as an event; al-Sharīf says story and report imply occurrence; al-Ālūsī prefers attachment to the preceding revealed account, since a bare substantive cannot govern the clause.
  20:10/q01/p2 It is the object of an implicit command to remember the time. — reasons: Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Zamakhsharī are cited for the implied udhkur; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow it.
  20:10/q01/p3 It depends on an omitted following event or verb. — reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī is cited by al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī; al-Nasafī permits an omitted event.
20:10/q02 What journey and circumstances led Moses to see the fire? [إِذْ رَءَا نَارًۭا · reports] (turns on: The reports differ in how Moses lost the road and in the journey's particular circumstances.)
  20:10/q02/p1 Returning from Midian toward Egypt with his family, Moses lost the way on a cold dark night and could not kindle fire. — reasons: Muqātil and others specify winter, sometimes Friday, snow, pregnancy or a son's birth, dispersed livestock, no water, and fire to the left or on a mountain near Ṭuwā. Wahb says Shuʿayb allowed a visit to Moses' mother; some mention Aaron, the completed term, a flock and more than ten years away. Ibn Kathīr cites 28:29; al-Kuranyolu gives eight or ten years. The reports vary in these details.
  20:10/q02/p2 His protectiveness toward his wife led him to separate from companions and lose the route. — reasons: ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says he avoided a group lest they see her; al-Baghawī says he joined caravans at night and separated by day; al-Qurṭubī says God decreed the separation. Some add detouring from Syrian rulers.
  20:10/q02/p3 The fire episode belongs to Moses' wanderings after fleeing Egypt. — reasons: Asad cites the sequence beginning at 28:14 and infers from 27:7 and 28:29 that Moses was lost in the desert.
20:10/q03 What did Moses actually see when the verse calls it fire? [نَارًۭا · referent] (turns on: Whether 'fire' names a physical fire, divine light perceived as fire, a small visible flame, or an allegorical image; al-Rāzī and Bursevī appeal to prophetic truthfulness.)
  20:10/q03/p1 He saw divine light that appeared to him to be fire. — reasons: Al-Suddī, Ibn ʿAbbās and most exegetes distinguish Moses' perception from God's light; some call it the Lord's light. Al-Kuranyolu says the light drew him into God's presence. Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān object that making it merely imagined fire threatens the truth of a prophet's report.
  20:10/q03/p2 It was real fire. — reasons: Saʿīd ibn Jubayr calls it one of God's veils and cites Abū Mūsā's report that the unveiled fire would burn creation within God's sight. Al-Rāzī and Bursevī prefer actual fire because prophets cannot lie; al-Rāzī says the verse does not establish that it was in a tree. Classifications distinguish Moses' fire as consuming neither food nor drink, or as luminous without burning, against worldly and infernal fires; 36:80 is cited.
  20:10/q03/p3 He saw a brief shimmering flame, perhaps visible only to him. — reasons: Iṣlāḥī reasons that a large blaze would have prompted a different description; Ṭabāṭabāʾ takes the emphatic report to imply that only Moses perceived it.
  20:10/q03/p4 The fire is an allegory of the burning bush. — reasons: Asad refers to the biblical burning bush and his note on 27:7–8.
20:10/q04 What was reported about the tree and fire as Moses approached? [نَارًۭا · reports] (turns on: Reports give different tree species and details of its behavior; al-Rāzī questions whether the Qurʾānic wording establishes any tree.)
  20:10/q04/p1 The fire appeared in a green tree and did not consume it. — reasons: Al-Baghawī describes brilliant white fire surrounding the tree from bottom to top without altering its green color; Ibn ʿAbbās marveled at its unchanged beauty. Al-Mahdawī says it receded as Moses approached, then drew near after fear. Al-Qushayrī says the tree burned end to end but yielded no flame and illuminated hearts, while al-Rāzī notes the Qurʾān does not establish the tree detail.
  20:10/q04/p2 The tree was a jujube or ʿanāb. — reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās is reported for the jujube identification; Abū Ḥayyān's collected variants include ʿanāb.
  20:10/q04/p3 The tree was al-ʿawsaj, a thorn tree. — reasons: Qatāda, Muqātil and al-Kalbī are named by al-Baghawī; al-Mahdawī says a thorn tree.
  20:10/q04/p4 The tree was a green samura. — reasons: Al-Baghawī attributes this to Ibn Masʿūd; Abū Ḥayyān lists it among variants.
  20:10/q04/p5 The tree was al-ʿaliq or ʿalīqah. — reasons: Al-Baghawī reports Wahb's identification; Abū Ḥayyān lists the variant.
  20:10/q04/p6 Moses thought the distant light was a shepherd's fire. — reasons: Al-Suddī and Bursevī connect the expectation of a firebrand to shepherds.
20:10/q05 Who was Moses' 'family' whom he addressed? [لِأَهْلِهِ · referent] (turns on: Whether ahl includes children and a servant, or denotes the wife alone with plural wording.)
  20:10/q05/p1 His wife, children, and possibly a servant. — reasons: Al-Rāzī gives wife, child and servant; Abū Ḥayyān specifies wife, two sons and servant. Al-Ālūsī allows wife, child and servant. Ṭabāṭabāʾ infers others were present from the instruction to stay.
  20:10/q05/p2 His wife alone, Shuʿayb's daughter. — reasons: Al-Wāḥidī explains the plural by ahl's collective outward form; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī add respectful plural address. Al-Suhaylī names her Ṣafūriyya.
20:10/q06 What does 'stay' mean here? [ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · meaning]
  20:10/q06/p1 Remain here temporarily. — reasons: Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī distinguish makth from iqāma, which implies continuing residence; other sources gloss it as stay or remain in place.
20:10/q07 Why did Moses tell his family to stay? [فَقَالَ لِأَهْلِهِ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · rhetoric] (turns on: Whether the departure is explained by deliberate care for the family or an overwhelming response to the sight.)
  20:10/q07/p1 He wanted them to remain while he went to seek fire or directions. — reasons: Al-Ālūsī says he prevented them following him toward the fire, as people commonly do; Ṭabāṭabāʾ supplies an omitted promise to go and return. Al-Qushayrī stresses his care for them despite the valley's danger.
  20:10/q07/p2 The sight drew him out in an agitation he could not resist. — reasons: Al-Qushayrī gives this as another account.
20:10/q08 How is the pronoun vowel in 'to his family' read? [لِأَهْلِهِ ٱمْكُثُوٓا۟ · readings] (turns on: Pronoun dialect and phonological explanation; the sources agree that both vocalizations are transmitted.)
  20:10/q08/p1 The hāʾ is read with ḍamma. — reasons: Hamza is consistently named, also for 28:29; Ibn Mujāhid adds Ibn Saʿdān from Isḥāq al-Musayyabī, while Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī add al-Aʿmash, Ṭalḥa and a Nāfiʿ transmission. Al-Naḥḥās calls it original wāw retention and a dialect like marartu bihu; al-Jishumī describes transfer of the hamza vowel; al-Samīn calls it an original Hijazi dialect and reports an assimilation explanation. Qirāʾāt-Er offers dialectal leveling or syntax.
  20:10/q08/p2 The hāʾ is read with kasra. — reasons: The other readers or majority are named by Ibn Mujāhid, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān; al-Jishumī explains it through adjacent sukūns.
20:10/q09 What does 'ānastu' convey? [ءَانَسْتُ · meaning] (turns on: Whether the verb highlights clear reassuring sight in this scene or the broader act of finding or sensing.)
  20:10/q09/p1 I saw distinctly or perceived clearly. — reasons: Ibn Qutayba cites 4:6 for the related sense recognized; al-Ṭabarī derives it from uns and cites an Arab proverb; al-Mubarrad describes distant reassuring sight and a hunting bird noticing prey; al-ʿAjjāj and al-Ḥārith ibn Ḥilliza furnish poetic usage. Al-Rāzī contrasts visible human beings with hidden jinn. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ counts four Moses-fire uses and a fifth at 4:6, and argues for familiarity or comfort rather than neutral sight; Iṣlāḥī says attentive observation.
  20:10/q09/p2 I found or sensed something, potentially by a sense other than sight. — reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and al-Farrāʾ gloss found; al-Māwardī allows sensing something by a fire. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say perception is broader than sight and cite a verse about faint sound; dictionaries cited by Bint al-Shāṭiʾ include hearing.
20:10/q10 Why does Moses emphasize that he has seen a fire? [إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ نَارًۭا · rhetoric]
  20:10/q10/p1 He reports certain perception to reassure his family while leaving the hoped-for benefits uncertain. — reasons: Al-Bayḍāwī contrasts certain sight with merely hoped-for fire and guidance; al-Ālūsī says inna confirms the sight even though the family expressed no doubt. Ṭabāṭabāʾ takes the singular emphatic report to imply the others did not perceive the fire.
20:10/q11 How is the first-person yāʾ of 'innī' read? [إِنِّىٓ ءَانَسْتُ · readings] (turns on: Whether the possessive yāʾ is opened in connection or kept quiescent.)
  20:10/q11/p1 It is read open, innīya. — reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī names the two Medinese, Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr among five yāʾs in this passage; Qirāʾāt-Er says the change affects prosody without changing meaning. Ibn Mujāhid reports that Nāfiʿ opens the listed first-person yāʾs except in walī fīhā and akhī ushdud; Abū ʿAmr excepts walī fīhā and ḥashartanī aʿmā; Ibn Kathīr excepts walī fīhā, yassir lī amrī, lidhikrī in, ʿalā ʿaynī idh, and biraʾsī innī.
