[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r20] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | The author regards Khuli's position as an early call for a new kalām: an age-responsive effort to interpret and justify belief, protecting faith from extremism and stagnation as well as defending it.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r21] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | The author interprets Khuli's historicity as the view that ideas resemble and arise from their environments: intellectually fertile settings generate complex thought, while poorer settings produce simpler ideas.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r22] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | Khuli insisted that renewal begins by thoroughly understanding the old and changing our role from guarding and sanctifying heritage to studying it critically.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r23] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | The author reads Khuli's 'killing' of the old as discernment, not destruction: identify inert inherited elements and understand how their concepts and questions restrict present life and future movement.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r24] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | Khuli allowed renewal to find new insight by drawing on existing old material, as well as by independent reasoning that produces something previously unknown.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r25] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | The author says Khuli sought to breathe new life into old material after accreted misunderstanding had hardened it; he did not advocate discarding the entire past.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r26] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | Khuli differed from reformers who repackaged old content in new forms and treated heritage as an exhaustive storehouse; he called for genuinely novel work.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r27] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | Khuli made evolution comprehensive across fundamentals, beliefs, worship, and transactions, extending reform beyond the commonly accepted scope of legal dealings; the author says including worship may be his distinctive position.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r28] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | The author notes that worship is conventionally treated as fixed by revelation, unlike transactions, but Khuli still urged reconsidering legal and theological works in light of changing realities.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r29] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | Khuli argued that new events absent from old fiqh books cannot halt the world's course; rigidity risks alienating ordinary people from a religion unable to address their lives.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r3] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | Khuli fashioned a different path for understanding religion and interpreting its texts, beyond inherited foundations and principles.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r30] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | In a discussion with the Shaykh al-Azhar, Khuli rejected restricting change to a few worship rulings and said Islamic law selects what is easier to practice and best suited to endure.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r31] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | Khuli raised whether worshippers could follow congregational Friday and festival prayers through televised broadcasts, seeing and hearing the imam when local mosques are crowded.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r32] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | For Khuli, transactions turn on public benefit wherever it exists; evolving the presentation of beliefs, worship, and transactions should keep religious life connected to ordinary life.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r33] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | The author says Khuli did not fully explain the basis or applications of evolving worship rulings and cautiously reads him as seeking easier performance of obligations through a broader use of hardship relief and secondary rulings.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r34] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | The author judges general calls to adapt fiqh insufficient unless scholars rebuild the underlying principles and inference rules so they can yield alternative rulings responsive to changing times and places.

[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r6] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | Khuli regarded renewal at the head of each century as a major revolutionary work the community needs, almost a periodic social revolution.
[RIFAI-KHULI:c2/r7] RIFAI-KHULI · أمين الخولي | reports | He extended evolution across theology, law, language, and all moral, intellectual, social, and spiritual dimensions of human life, treating it as a universal law rather than biology's specialty.

[RIFAI-KHULI:c6/r11] RIFAI-KHULI · author | holds | Al-Rifai stresses that even Qur'an-by-Qur'an exegesis is shaped by hidden assumptions when an interpreter chooses one verse or word to explain another.
