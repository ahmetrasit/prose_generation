[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عمر بن الخطاب وابن مسعود ومجاهد | reports | They read the verb with the letters separated and the first rāʾ fatḥa, meaning a claimant may initiate harm to the scribe or witness; al-Ṭabarī reports and explains this reading accordingly.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو عمرو الداني | reports | Al-Dānī reports both kasra and fatḥa for the first rāʾ from ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Abī Isḥāq, and Mujāhid; separating the consonants is Ḥijāzī usage, while assimilation is Tamīmī.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · أبو جعفر بن القعقاع وعمرو بن عبيد | reports | They read the geminated rāʾ with sukūn in the jussive; Abū al-Fatḥ calls it questionable but explains it as treating connected speech like a pause.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عكرمة | reports | ʿIkrima reads the first rāʾ with kasra and makes “scribe” and “witness” accusative, so that the rights-holder is forbidden to begin harming them.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · ابن محيصن | reports | Ibn Muḥayṣin reads the geminated verb with rafʿ; after Ibn Mujāhid questions it, Abū al-Fatḥ explains it as a negative statement meaning the scribe ought not to be harmed, supported by poetry. Ibn ʿAṭiyya finds it close to the earlier analysis.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | If harm means falsification by a writer or witness, fisq is the legal commission of grave sins and invalidating rights; if it means mistreatment or refusal, fisq means departing from what is right, though disobeying God's command approaches the legal sense too.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p385/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Bikum implies “a state of fisq upon you”; the close of the verse admonishes and recounts God's favor, and another interpretation promises that piety teaches a person what is good.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · حذيفة بن اليمان | reports | Ḥudhayfa reports that, after transmitters and non-Arab readers spread across regions, Iraqi and Syrian groups disputed their readings in the Armenia campaign; some disavowed others’ recitations.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · حذيفة بن اليمان | reports | On returning to Medina, Ḥudhayfa urged ʿUthmān to act on the Book before the community split like Jews and Christians; ʿUthmān agreed to intervene.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عثمان بن عفان | reports | ʿUthmān appointed capable, learned, eloquent men to prepare a codex, choosing among transmitted variants the best-known Prophetic readings and most eloquent language.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · عثمان بن عفان | reports | ʿUthmān instructed the scribes to use Quraysh’s language when they differed.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains that if you differ concerns differences in transmitted readings; every one of the seven language forms in the Qurʾān must derive from the Prophet and have been recited to him.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The community followed the selected codex and set aside variant codices to prevent further division and preserve unity.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Ibn Masʿūd resisted replacing his codex, so it was left; scholars nevertheless barred reciting from it to prevent confusion, since explanatory notes were reportedly mistaken for Qurʾān.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p47/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | No Qurʾānic meaning was lost when noncanonical wording was left out, because the meanings remain in the established text.
[TABRISI:2:282#2/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · سيبويه | reports | A third analysis supplies an omitted predicate for the subject فرجل وامرأتان, understood as فرجل وامرأتان يشهدون. | mentions 5:95
[TABRISI:2:282#2/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | On this analysis يشهدون governs أن; its omission is placed before the conditional with fatḥ and after it with kasra.
[TABRISI:2:282#2/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The particle أن is accusative here, with the sense “because one woman may forget, then the other remind her.”
[TABRISI:2:282#2/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · سيبويه | reports | Sībawayh says testimony is requested so one woman may remind the other; possible forgetfulness is the reason for reminding, like shoring a wall because it may lean.
[TABRISI:2:282#2/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | With the nominative reading, كان means “occur” or “happen,” so the phrase means that a present trade occurs.
[TABRISI:2:282#2/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One analysis derives لا يضار from doubled ر, with the writer and witness forbidden to depart from the truth.
[TABRISI:2:282#2/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | The first interpretation of لا يضار is clearer; Abū Jaʿfar's doubled quiescent rāʾ is unusual and explained by treating connected recitation like pause.
[TABRISI:2:282#3/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | داينت and تداينت mean transacting in debt, either by borrowing or lending; the cited poem shows this usage.
[TABRISI:2:282#3/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The verb دنت means borrowing while أدنت means lending; أمل and أملى are both used for dictating.
[TABRISI:2:282#3/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | بخس is unjust diminution; safīh means ignorant or light-minded, named for the lightness of reason.
[TABRISI:2:282#3/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The otherwise unusual pattern أَبَى يَأبى is explained by the hamza behaving like a guttural consonant.
[TABRISI:2:282#3/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Ḍalāl originally means loss or going where something cannot be found; saʾam is weariness, and أقسط means acted justly while قسط means acted unjustly. | mentions 54:47
[TABRISI:2:282#3/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The opening continues the previous direction to defer an insolvent debtor by explaining rules for deferred claims and debt transactions.
[TABRISI:2:282#3/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | بدين may reinforce the meaning of تداينتم; alternatively it distinguishes debt transactions from تداينتم understood as mutual requital. | mentions 6:38
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الشعبي وجماعة من المفسرين | reports | Al-Shaʿbī and other commentators treat writing as a communal duty; al-Rummānī and al-Jubbāʾī choose that view.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r10] TABRISI, d. 548 · القاضي | reports | Al-Qāḍī differentiates the three: the foolish person squanders, the weak person is an adolescent, and the one unable to dictate is insane.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r11] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The debtor's guardian may dictate; alternatively, Ibn ʿAbbās says the guardian here is the creditor, who knows the debt best.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r12] TABRISI, d. 548 · مجاهد | reports | Mujāhid limits the two male witnesses to free, adult, Muslim men; al-Ṭabrisī rejects freedom as a condition, requiring Islam and probity instead.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · الجبائي | reports | Al-Jubbāʾī allows a scribe and witness to take payment, while al-Ṭūsī says their school does not permit it; paper costs belong to the creditor.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · السدي | reports | Al-Suddī says a scribe must write when free of other work.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · مجاهد وعطاء | reports | Mujāhid and ʿAṭāʾ say writing becomes obligatory when the scribe is asked.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن | reports | Al-Ḥasan says refusal is obligatory to avoid only when there is no alternative scribe and refusal would harm the creditor; otherwise the scribe has latitude.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · الضحاك | reports | Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says writing was originally obligatory but was abrogated by the ban on harming a scribe or witness.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The command is emphasized because scribes were scarce in the Prophet's time; combining a command with a prohibition against neglect makes compliance more likely.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The debtor dictates and must fear God, reducing neither the amount nor the quality of what is owed.
[TABRISI:2:282#4/r9] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The debtor may be a fool through ignorance, childhood, or impaired understanding; weak may mean intellectual disability or senility, and inability may mean madness, stammering, or muteness.
[TABRISI:2:282#5/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | If two men are unavailable, the witness set is one man and two women whose outward probity and trustworthiness the parties accept; this makes justice a condition of testimony.
[TABRISI:2:282#5/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The wording requires witnesses acceptable to the parties, since people cannot know whom God approves except from outward reliability and integrity.
