[MUJAHID:v1p118#2/r1] MUJAHID, d. 104 · مجاهد | reports | The witnesses must not refuse when called if they had already witnessed the transaction.
[MUQATIL:2:282#2/r1] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Here “more likely” means more suitable: written evidence is more likely to bring testimony in 5:108 and to comfort the wives in 33:51. | mentions 5:108, 33:51
[MUQATIL:2:282#2/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | An immediate cash trade with no term may go unwritten; sales should be witnessed, but a scribe or witness should not be pressured when occupied if another can be found.
[MUQATIL:2:282#2/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Wronging a scribe or witness is sinful; God commands the parties to fear Him and knows all their deeds.
[MUQATIL:2:282/r1] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | A deferred debt’s amount and due date should be written; a fair scribe writes between buyer and seller without increasing the debtor’s amount or reducing the creditor’s right; scribes were few in Muhammad’s time.
[MUQATIL:2:282/r2] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | The debtor must dictate the record, fear God, and omit none of what is owed; the warning against shortchanging also appears in 7:85. | mentions 7:85
[MUQATIL:2:282/r3] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | If the debtor is ignorant, mentally impaired, weak, unable to dictate, or cannot speak, the creditor’s guardian dictates fairly, neither adding nor subtracting.
[MUQATIL:2:282/r4] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | The right-holder’s representative dictates for an incapable debtor and must be fair, matching the same no-increase, no-decrease standard imposed on the debtor.
[MUQATIL:2:282/r5] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | For a debt, use two acceptable male witnesses or one man and two women; if one woman forgets, the other reminds her, and a summoned witness should serve when free.
[MUQATIL:2:282/r6] MUQATIL, d. 150 · author | holds | Do not tire of recording even a small amount through its due date: writing better records the term and protects property, is just, reliable for testimony, and less likely to create doubt.

[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Qusī and thudī yield qaswī and thadwī because they are fuʿūl forms; when the following kasra disappears, the original pattern returns.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With marma and marmawwiyyah, delete the first two yāʾs and keep the nisba yāʾs; those saying ḥānawī also say marmwī.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · يونس | reports | Yūnus reports that some Arabs retain the yāʾ in Umayyī, analogizing to Ṭayyiʾī; ʿAdiyyī is heavier because of the kasra among yāʾs.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nisba from ḥayyah becomes ḥayawī to avoid consecutive yāʾs; the yāʾ is vocalized so wāw can remain after it.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The tribal name Ḥayy ibn Bahdalah supplies evidence for ḥayawī and for vocalizing yāʾ before wāw.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nisba from layyah becomes lawawī by restoring the underlying form after moving yāʾ; speakers who say Umayyī also say ḥayyī.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أبو عمرو | reports | Abū ʿAmr used ḥayyī and layyī, including layyah from the phrase for twisting someone's hand.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nisba from ʿaduww and kuwwah remains ʿaduwwī and kuwwī because there are not enough consecutive yāʾs to require substitution.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Marmawī is analogized to bakhtī because its ending has the same sequence of yāʾs and kasra; maghzawī keeps its form when no cluster arises.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالإضافةإلىفعيلوفعيلمنبناتالياءوالواوالتيالياءات#560/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nisba from ʿadwah becomes ʿadawī because the feminine tāʾ triggers the same treatment as in shanrah/shanʾī.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Gemination is heavy, so Arabic favors distinct consonants and assimilation into one tongue movement; when the final consonant is quiescent, Hijaz preserves the form while Tamim quiesces the first and vowels the second.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The form radd stays unassimilated in faʿala because quiescing the ending would still leave gemination, while the first radical is inherently quiescent.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nouns longer than three letters follow doubled-consonant rules of four-letter verbs: shift a vowel onto a preceding quiescent consonant, retain an existing vowel, and leave preceding alif unchanged; afʿal forms assimilate too.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A dual nūn can drop and therefore differs from the inseparable doubled consonant after alif; separated doubled consonants do not undergo the same alteration.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil analyzes ṣabb as faʿala, supported by ṣababtu and ṣabāba, parallel to qaniʿa and qanāʿa.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Forms such as ṭabb and khāf follow the three-letter verbal pattern; the lack of an unassimilated tabbab confirms that faʿal is assimilated.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Three-letter nominals distinguish themselves from verbal forms: weak-verb-like nouns can retain original forms, other nonverbs preserve doubled patterns, and some simplify, as in ʿamīma to ʿamm and thunā.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · قوم | reports | Some speakers preserve the unassimilated faʿil form, as in ḍafaf, because this pattern can be sound in the qult class.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابالتضعيف)#800/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | prefers | Sībawayh prefers ḍaff and ḍafū al-ḥāl over the reported ḍafaf and ḍafafū.
