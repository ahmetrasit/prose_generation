[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Sībawayh cites al-Nuʿmān b. Bashīr's line as a poetic example following the rule for adjusting vowels together.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Alif-lām identifies nouns and behaves like qad or sawfa; Ghaylan's line uses bi-dhal and bi-jal, and Sībawayh says the lām is not broken once the form is complete.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalil treats the article as separable like qad and sawfa but meaningful; its initial fatḥa distinguishes it, and it remains before an interrogative alif to distinguish question from statement.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | An unnamed poet's līman Allāh attests the connected onset in the oath expression.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايجريفيهبعضماذكرناإذاكسرللجمععلىالأصل)#785/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Broken plurals on the fiʿāl pattern can yield expanded forms such as diyāwīr and qiyāwīm.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايجريفيهبعضماذكرناإذاكسرللجمععلىالأصل)#785/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | ʿAwār forms ʿawāwīr without hamza, unlike the hamza-bearing pattern; its morphology follows a different base form.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايجريفيهبعضماذكرناإذاكسرللجمععلىالأصل)#785/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Weak yāʾ and wāw immediately after alif are treated as word-final radicals, like in siqāʾ and qiḍāʾ; an intervening consonant restores the regular form.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايجريفيهبعضماذكرناإذاكسرللجمععلىالأصل)#785/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When separation from the word ending preserves the weak radical, forms such as al-shaqāwa and al-ghiwāya retain the underlying wāw or yāʾ.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايجريفيهبعضماذكرناإذاكسرللجمععلىالأصل)#785/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The farther weak radicals stand from the word end, the stronger preservation of the base: zawwār and ṣawwām retain wāw, as do akhwa and abwa.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايكسرمماكسرللجمعومالايكسرمنأبنيةالجمعإذاجعلته#590/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Plural patterns such as masājid and mafātīḥ resist further broken pluralization as endpoint patterns; as male names they take sound masculine plurals and as female names sound feminine plurals.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايكسرمماكسرللجمعومالايكسرمنأبنيةالجمعإذاجعلته#590/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Plural patterns that already have broken plural forms can be broken again as names: examples include aʿdāl to aʿādīl and aqwāl to aqāwīl, alongside abyāt, anʿām, and ajriba.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايكسرمماكسرللجمعومالايكسرمنأبنيةالجمعإذاجعلته#590/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A plural-derived name such as aʿbud can take both a diminutive and a broken plural, yielding al-aʿābid, because its pattern permits these processes like a singular.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايكسرمماكسرللجمعومالايكسرمنأبنيةالجمعإذاجعلته#590/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A three-consonant plural pattern used as a name follows the broken plural expected of a singular with that form, since it can shift into singular-like patterns and diminutives.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايكسرمماكسرللجمعومالايكسرمنأبنيةالجمعإذاجعلته#590/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A man named on the fuʿūl pattern may form faʿāʾil, since fuʿūl can also be singular and behaves like an existing plural pattern.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايكسرمماكسرللجمعومالايكسرمنأبنيةالجمعإذاجعلته#590/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A man named Jamāl on a pattern like singular jirāb takes the broken plural expected of a singular of that form.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايكسرمماكسرللجمعومالايكسرمنأبنيةالجمعإذاجعلته#590/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Tamra used as a name has shifted from its original meaning and behaves like qaṣʿa, allowing tamār just as qaṣʿa allows qiṣāʿ.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايمالمنالحروفالتيليسبعدهاألفإذاكانتالراءبعدها#721/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A following kasra on ر can make a preceding open vowel incline toward kasra: the ر is like two kasra-bearing consonants, so Sibawayh analogizes its effect to ي.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايمالمنالحروفالتيليسبعدهاألفإذاكانتالراءبعدها#721/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Imala is blocked when an open vowel follows the alif or when an emphatic consonant resists it, as in المحاذر and حاضر.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايمالمنالحروفالتيليسبعدهاألفإذاكانتالراءبعدها#721/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With ابن مذعور, the speaker approaches kasra after ر but does not incline the و, since it does not resemble ي and tilting it would also tilt what precedes.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايمالمنالحروفالتيليسبعدهاألفإذاكانتالراءبعدها#721/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A following separate word with ر and kasra can cause imala across the word boundary, as in خبط رياح and قفا رياح.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايمالمنالحروفالتيليسبعدهاألفإذاكانتالراءبعدها#721/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Other speakers leave forms uninclined when the conditioning kasra is too far away across a word boundary, as in مال قاسم, خبط رياح, and قفا رياح.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايمالمنالحروفالتيليسبعدهاألفإذاكانتالراءبعدها#721/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some speakers incline before ر in من عمرو but not من الشرق because the latter ر is followed by an emphatic consonant; this also blocks the pattern in مارق.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمضاعفالفعلواختلافالعربفيه)#646/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A doubled verb has two identical consonants at its end, as in رددت and وددت; this is the morphological class of the verse's مضارّ form.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمضاعفالفعلواختلافالعربفيه)#646/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | When both final consonants are vowelled, Arabs agree on assimilation; al-Khalil says it is especially apt because repeating the same articulation is burdensome.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمضاعفالفعلواختلافالعربفيه)#646/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أهل الحجاز | reports | The Hijaz dialect keeps the doubled consonants separate when the verb ending is quiescent, moving the preceding consonant to avoid two sukūns together.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمضاعفالفعلواختلافالعربفيه)#646/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · بنو تميم | reports | Banu Tamim assimilate the jussive as well as fully vowelled forms; Sibawayh says many other Arabs share this dialect pattern.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمضاعفالفعلواختلافالعربفيه)#646/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | If the consonant before the first of the doubled pair is quiescent, it takes that consonant’s vowel; a now-unneeded alif of connection is deleted.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمضاعفالفعلواختلافالعربفيه)#646/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With an alif before the doubled consonants and no alif of connection, the form remains unchanged; لا تضار is one of Sibawayh’s examples.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In ‘إني مما أفعل,’ ما can combine with من as one unit, as in ربما; Abū Ḥayya al-Numayrī’s line illustrates ربما.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Nouns such as أهل and مخافة can be annexed directly to an أن-clause, with the sense of أهل لأن يفعل and مخافة لأن يفعل.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some Arabs say ‘لحق أنه ذاهب’ to mean ‘يقين أنه ذاهب,’ but this usage is not common to all Arabs.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | خليق accepts either لأن يفعل or أن يفعل; عسيت أن تفعل similarly follows near-approach verbs such as قاربت and دنوت.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | عسى and اخلولقت take an أن-clause without a verbal-noun alternative here; Arabs do not say ‘عسيت الفعل’ or ‘عسيت للفعل.’
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | عسى is usually invariant across singular, plural, and feminine subjects, though some Arabs inflect it; with inflected forms, أن is accusative as in عسيت أن.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r16] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some Arabs use عسى يفعل like كاد يفعل, with a finite verb replacing the accusative noun in the proverb ‘عسى الغوير أبؤسا’; poetry attests this use.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r17] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | كاد and كرب normally take a finite verb without أن; poetry permits كاد أن by analogy with عسى, and لعل أن by analogy with عسيت أن.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r18] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | يوشك أن تجيء attaches أن to يوشك; توشك أن تجيء is like قاربت أن تفعل, while poetry also uses يوشك يجيء by analogy with عسى.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In ‘أن تضل إحداهما,’ the preposition is omitted; the clause is treated like a verbal noun, as if it said ‘لأن تضل إحداهما.’
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r20] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl explains al-Farazdaq’s ‘إن أذنا قتيبة حزتا’ by avoiding separation of أن from its verb, as with كي; it follows إن, which permits nouns before verbs.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | After temporal بعد, أن plus a verb stands for a verbal noun; likewise ‘أما أن أسير’ means ‘أما السيرورة.’
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمنأبوابأنالتيتكونوالفعلبمنزلةمصدر)#510/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | ‘ما منعك أن تأتينا’ means ‘what prevented you from coming to us’: من is omitted before the أن-clause.
