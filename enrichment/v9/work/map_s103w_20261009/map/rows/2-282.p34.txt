2:282#22/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشافعي | reports | Al-Shāfiʿī classifies sales as documented with witnesses, secured by a pledge, or based on trust, reading the verse as evidence for these forms.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#22/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عمر | reports | Ibn ʿUmar would obtain witnesses for cash sales and write down deferred sales.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#22/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الطبري | reports | Al-Ṭabarī reads the witnessing command as covering small and large sales; Abū Mūsā, Ibn ʿUmar, and others hold it obligatory, and ʿAṭāʾ demands witnesses for tiny purchases.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#22/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الشعبي والحسن | reports | Al-Shaʿbī and al-Ḥasan treat witnessing as recommendation and guidance rather than a binding duty; Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, and other jurists are also said to hold this.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#22/r8] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن العربي | prefers | Ibn al-ʿArabī says the recommendation is the view of all jurists and is correct.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#23/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن العربي | reports | Ibn al-ʿArabī says only al-Ḍaḥḥāk is reported to require witnessing; the Prophet made and recorded sales without witnesses and pledged his armor without them.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#23/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | rejects | Al-Qurṭubī notes that the obligation was attributed to several authorities besides al-Ḍaḥḥāk, correcting Ibn al-ʿArabī's account.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#23/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عطية | reports | Ibn ʿAṭiyya finds mandatory witnessing impractical for small sales and sometimes socially awkward; he treats it as recommended for its usual benefit unless an excuse intervenes.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#23/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النحاس | reports | Al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥakam, and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd are reported to say that the mutual-trust clause abrogated witnessing sales. | mentions 2:283
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#23/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الطبري | rejects | Al-Ṭabarī rejects abrogation: the pledge clause addresses inability to find a scribe and an unrequired pledge, a different case from writing and witnessing. | mentions 2:283, 4:43, 5:6
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#24/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | rejects | Later rules for different situations do not abrogate earlier rules; some note both clauses may have been revealed together, since abrogation cannot occur simultaneously. | mentions 5:6, 4:92, 58:4
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#24/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the debt verse is established, not abrogated, and writing, pledges, and witnesses are alternative means of securing debt; witnessing is recommended like pledging. | mentions 2:283
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#24/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | prefers | Al-Qurṭubī says explicit Sunnah showing the Prophet accepted a sale without witnesses is an even stronger argument against mandatory witnessing.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#24/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · طارق بن عبد الله المحاربي | reports | Ṭāriq ibn ʿAbd Allāh recounts selling a camel to a man later identified as the Prophet; the Prophet sent word for the sellers to take dates and eat until satisfied, without witnesses at the sale.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#25/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عمارة بن خزيمة | reports | ʿUmāra ibn Khuzayma reports that his Companion uncle witnessed the Prophet's purchase of a horse from a Bedouin, and Khuzayma ibn Thābit testified from trust in the Prophet.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#25/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · خزيمة بن ثابت | reports | The Prophet made Khuzayma's testimony count as that of two men because he testified on the basis of believing the Prophet.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#25/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الحسن وقتادة وطاوس وابن زيد | reports | Al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, Ṭāwūs, and Ibn Zayd say a scribe must write only what is dictated and a witness must neither add to nor subtract from testimony.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#25/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس ومجاهد وعطاء | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, and ʿAṭāʾ interpret the prohibition as forbidding a scribe or witness from refusing when asked to serve.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#26/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · النحاس | reports | Al-Naḥḥās reports Abū Isḥāq's preference for the reading that forbids falsifying a record or testimony, since the warning of sin fits dishonesty better than burdening a busy witness.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#26/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مجاهد والضحاك وطاوس والسدي | reports | Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ṭāwūs, and al-Suddī understand the verse to forbid harassing a busy scribe or witness who gives a reasonable excuse; Ibn Masʿūd has a similar reading.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#26/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The reciprocal form encompasses both kinds of harm: dishonest additions and omissions wrongfully affect people and property, while imposing on busy witnesses violates God's command.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#26/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · سفيان الثوري | reports | Sufyān al-Thawrī explains 'it is wrongdoing by you' as meaning a sinful act; falsifying testimony and imposing on busy witnesses are both disobedience.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#26/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | God promises that reverent people receive understanding and discernment to distinguish truth from falsehood, sometimes placed directly in the heart. | mentions 8:29
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#27/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The promise that piety gives believers discernment is linked to the verse promising a criterion by which to distinguish right from wrong. | mentions 8:29
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#3/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | He alone allows salam in a designated item under two conditions: it comes from a secure village, and collection has begun, as with milk from a sheep or ripe fruit from a palm.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#3/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | prefers | He accepts Malik's exceptions: a secure location makes non-delivery unlikely enough to tolerate slight uncertainty, and daily collection of milk or dates meets a recurring need when buyers lack cash or prices fluctuate and sellers need money.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#4/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The Prophetic wording إلى أجل معلوم settles the requirement for a known term; al-Qurṭubī allows one to three days when prices differ between towns, but not within one town where the price is stable.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#4/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | There is consensus that the term must be known; Malik alone also accepts harvest or fruit-picking as a sufficiently known deadline, discussed under the verse about lunar phases. | mentions 2:189
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#4/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | There is consensus that the goods must exist at maturity; if they fail to exist then through God's decree, the contract is dissolved according to all scholars.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#4/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The seller need not own the goods throughout the contract; reports from Ibn Abi Awfa and Ibn Abza say the Companions did not ask Syrian sellers whether they farmed the grain or oil they promised.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#4/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو حنيفة | reports | He requires the goods to exist continuously from contract through maturity, fearing that the promised goods may be unavailable when demanded; other jurists require existence only at maturity.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#4/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الكوفيون والثوري | reports | They require the delivery location for goods involving transport and invalidate salam if it is omitted; al-Awzāʿī calls omission disliked.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#4/r7] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | prefers | If the place is unstated, the contract remains valid and delivery defaults to the contract location, as held by Ahmad, Ishaq, and hadith scholars; the Ibn ʿAbbās report omits the place, although it specifies measure, weight, and term.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r1] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · أبو سعيد الخدري | reports | A report through ʿAṭiyya says the Prophet forbade redirecting an advance-purchase contract to a different commodity; ʿAbd al-Ḥaqq says the transmitter al-ʿAwfī is not an authority, despite prominent scholars transmitting from him.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r10] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الجمهور | prefers | The majority treat documentation as recommended protection against lost property and doubt; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this correct because a person may waive a financial claim, so there is no abrogation.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r11] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · عطاء وغيره | reports | They say the writer must write when asked if no other writer is available; al-Shaʿbī gives the same ruling.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r2] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · مالك | reports | If a salam seller cannot deliver at maturity and the buyer releases him, the buyer may take only the original currency or exact capital; exchanging it for another good before receipt would sell food before possession, which the Prophet forbade.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r3] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | فاكتبوه commands recording the debt and its term; some say it also implies witnesses, since writing without witnesses is not legal proof, and it prevents forgetting.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r4] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · ابن عباس | reports | A report says Adam first denied a written gift: God showed him David among his descendants, allowed forty years of Adam's thousand-year life to be added to David's sixty, recorded and witnessed the gift, then produced the document when Adam denied it; David received a hundred years and Adam completed his thousand.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r5] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · author | holds | The command to write calls for recording every detail that identifies the debt, preventing disputes and enabling a judge to decide if the parties later disagree.
[QURTUBI-FULL:2:282#5/r6] QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 · الطبري | reports | He holds that documenting every debt, whether sale or loan, is obligatory to prevent forgetfulness or denial.
