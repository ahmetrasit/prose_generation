[IBNASHUR:103:3/r1] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The exception completes the warning by dividing people into two groups: those who incur loss and those who escape it.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r10] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Faith applies uniformly to its particulars, while good deeds admit degrees; accordingly, freedom from encompassing loss varies with the amount of good done.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r11] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | The exception implies that the rest of humanity loses through lacking faith and good action; the relative clause identifies faith and good action as preventing loss from engulfing a person.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r12] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Exhorting one another to truth is singled out from good deeds for emphasis, since people may wrongly think righteous action benefits only oneself.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r13] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Mutual counsel in truth includes teaching guidance and sound beliefs, and training oneself to understand them by doing good and avoiding wrong.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r14] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Patience is also singled out as a particular of good deeds, specifically the endurance required to establish truth and bear harm in doing so.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r15] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Patience is restraining oneself from a desired thing or its pursuit, either to avoid resulting harm or to gain a benefit; examples include jihad, pilgrimage, and arduous work for reward, wealth, or reputation.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r16] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Patience includes enduring the displeasure or verbal abuse one may face when enjoining what is right.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r17] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Enduring the effort involved in sinful pleasures is not patience, because desire for that effort outweighs aversion to the effort of leaving the sin.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r18] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | These two exhortations encompass the religious good: Islamic beliefs and ethics fall under truth, while good works and avoiding sins fall under patience.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r19] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Patience underpins the virtues: moral discipline requires resisting many desires until good character becomes a settled trait.
[IBNASHUR:103:3/r2] IBNASHUR, d. 1393 · author | holds | Believers who do good avoid loss so long as they do not omit good acts by committing their opposites, sins.
