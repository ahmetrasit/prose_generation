[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتلحقهالهاءلتبينالحركةمنغيرماذكرنامنبناتالياء#730/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | At pause, hāʾ can make audible the final vowel on noninflectional dual and plural nūns, whose preceding consonant is still; the examples include ضاربانه and مسلمونه.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتلحقهالهاءلتبينالحركةمنغيرماذكرنامنبناتالياء#730/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The same pause hāʾ appears in أينه and ثمه because their nūn or final consonant is uninflected, preceded by a still consonant, and phonetically hidden; هلمه is another example.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتلحقهالهاءلتبينالحركةمنغيرماذكرنامنبناتالياء#730/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Many Arab speakers do not add hāʾ at pause when no segment has been deleted; whenever it is added, it disappears before continuing speech.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتلحقهالهاءلتبينالحركةمنغيرماذكرنامنبناتالياء#730/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Similar forms include إنه for إن, اعلمنه with an extra noninflectional nūn, and كيفه, ليته, and لعله; their endings are treated alike in pause.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتلحقهالهاءلتبينالحركةمنغيرماذكرنامنبناتالياء#730/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl reports انطلقته for انطلقت because its tāʾ is not an inflectional ending and is preceded by a still consonant.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتلحقهالهاءلتبينالحركةمنغيرماذكرنامنبناتالياء#730/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Possessive yāʾ suffixes after alif or yāʾ also take pause hāʾ, since the yāʾ is hidden and follows a still consonant, as in غلامايه and عصايه.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | An alif inclines toward imāla when followed by kasra, drawing pronunciation toward yāʾ for ease.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In roots with underlying yāʾ, an open alif is inclined because it occupies the place of yāʾ or replaces it.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الفرزدق | reports | Al-Farazdaq's verse with yashumm supports the yāʾ-directed pronunciation used as evidence for imāla.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In wāw roots, imāla follows because final wāw turns to yāʾ after three letters, whereas yāʾ does not turn to wāw; yāʾ is lighter.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some preserve the wāw quality and avoid imāla in short three-letter wāw-root nouns such as qafā, ʿaṣā, and al-qaṭā.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Verb alifs from wāw roots incline consistently because the final consonant changes toward yāʾ across inflection; nouns do not undergo that same change.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r15] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Many speakers, including many Tamimis, leave open the alif of four-or-more-letter nouns from wāw roots.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r16] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | An added final alif is often inclined because it behaves like the alif of a yāʾ-root.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r17] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Many speakers leave the feminine or other added final alif open, saying ḥublā and maʿzā.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r18] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some Hijazis incline alif in weak-root forms when the first vowel of faʿaltu is kasra; ordinary speakers do not.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r19] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · ابن أبي إسحاق | reports | Ibn Abī Isḥāq reportedly heard Kathīr ʿAzzah say ṣāra; some recited khāfa with imāla, illustrating the restricted middle-wāw case.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Imāla is an articulatory approximation, like moving ṣād toward zāy in ṣadr to ease the tongue's movement.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r20] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | With medial wāw, imāla is limited to forms beginning with kasra; qāma and dāra do not incline.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r21] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some say māta while others say mittu; speakers who say ṣāra and khāfa also use this variation.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r22] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The alif in kiyāl and bayyāʿ is inclined because a preceding yāʾ functions like a kasra; many Arabs leave it open.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r23] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Speakers who do not incline kiyāl also leave open shaybān, Qays ʿAylān, and Ghaylān, while others incline them for the yāʾ.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r24] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In a genitive position, some incline alif as in bābihi and mālihi by analogy with kātib and sājid, but the imāla is weaker because the kasra is not fixed.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r25] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some extend imāla to accusative zaydan by analogy with Ghaylān, but it is weaker; they do not incline ʿabdan or kaslān because those lack yāʾ.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r26] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some incline qazḥan and ʿalaman by treating their kasra as though it were yāʾ, and use the same analogy for al-najjādain.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r27] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | In genitive forms such as ʿajlānik and mālin kathīr, speakers incline by analogy with bābik and with māshin and dāʿin.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r28] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Speakers differ at pause: some keep imāla, while others open the alif because pausing removes the supporting kasra.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r29] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some incline ʿimādan by analogy with imāla after kasra, while others keep ʿalaman and ʿimādan open without preceding yāʾ or kasra.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A single intervening mobile consonant does not block imāla from an initial kasra, as in ʿimād.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r30] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · العرب | reports | Some pause with bimālin min ʿindi llāh or li-Zaydin māl, analogizing to ʿimād; this is weaker than a connected kasra.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r31] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | They do not say dhā māl this way because a medial alif resembles that of fāʿil; in ʿimādan the second alif follows the first in imāla.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r32] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The second alif in ʿimādan is inclined by the imāla of the first.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In longer nouns from a wāw root, the final alif may incline toward yāʾ once the form has developed a yāʾ, the relevant pattern for musammā.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Two intervening consonants still permit imāla when the first is quiescent, since a sākin is a weak barrier.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أهل الحجاز | reports | Hijazis do not apply imāla to the described forms such as sirbāl, shamāl, ʿimād, and kilāb.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | No imāla occurs when the following vowel is fatḥa or ḍamma: fatḥa is closer to alif, while rounding toward wāw would change the letter.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A preceding fatḥa or ḍamma also prevents imāla, as in ribāb, jimād, and al-balbāl.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابماتمالفيهالألفات)#715/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Some incline the alif of as-siwidād because widād behaves like kilāb, a form that accepts imāla.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Hamzat al-waṣl is prefixed so speech can begin before a quiescent consonant; it is an added, vowelled onset.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Sībawayh reports Yunus's account that some speakers say īm Allāh with kasra, then līm Allāh by analogy with the waṣl in ibn.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Waṣl occurs in imperatives of form I when the next consonant is quiescent, in forms such as infaʿala, ifʿalla, and iftaʿala, and in five further derived patterns including istafʿala.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The alif in afʿala is formative rather than an added onset for a quiescent consonant, so its conjugation behaves like a four-letter verb.
[SIBAWAYH:(هذابابمايتقدمأولالحروفوهيزائدةقدمتلإسكانأولالحروف)#723/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | A waṣl alif drops after preceding speech because earlier words remove the need for an onset; Sībawayh compares this to dropping hāʾ in vocative speech.
