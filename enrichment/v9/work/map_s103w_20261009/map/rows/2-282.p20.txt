[JURJANI-DALAIL:v1p64/r1] JURJANI-DALAIL, d. 471 · author | holds | The source of eloquence and inimitability is the arrangement of meanings, not the order of bare words; a word-focused account of the Qur’an’s merit collapses.
[JURJANI-DALAIL:v1p64/r2] JURJANI-DALAIL, d. 471 · author | holds | Fasāḥa is attributed to a word because it names the quality by which that word signals rhetorical merit; the meaning itself is not directly described as eloquent.
[JURJANI-DALAIL:v1p64/r3] JURJANI-DALAIL, d. 471 · author | holds | Older critics’ ‘noble’ or ‘restless’ word is shorthand for semantic fit with neighboring meanings: wording is called settled or awkward because of what it conveys in the composition.
[JURJANI-DALAIL:v1p76/r1] JURJANI-DALAIL, d. 471 · author | holds | Jurjānī says scholars agree that nazm is indispensable to eloquent speech; without it, unusual meanings alone confer no rank or completeness.
[JURJANI-DALAIL:v1p76/r2] JURJANI-DALAIL, d. 471 · author | holds | Nazm means arranging speech as grammar requires, following its rules and distinctions rather than merely placing words in sequence.
[JURJANI-DALAIL:v1p76/r3] JURJANI-DALAIL, d. 471 · author | holds | The composer must consider the different forms available within each grammatical category, such as alternative word orders and predicate forms.
