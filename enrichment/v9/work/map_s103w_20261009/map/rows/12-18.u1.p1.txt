  12:18/q14/p2 Forgiving the brothers and treating them graciously without a gloomy face. — reasons: Thalabi, Kashshaf, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Wahidi report a composed social manner. Alusi rejects this as far from Jacob's apparent meaning.
  12:18/q14/p3 Bearing Joseph's loss and the grief of separation. — reasons: Mawardi gives this alternative; Qatada applies patience to their false account.
  12:18/q14/p4 Contented acceptance of God's wise decree for His sake. — reasons: Tustari cites God being with the patient (2:153), remembered ease and honey beneath the bitter cup. Murtada calls for dutiful endurance. Razi cites divine ownership, wisdom, mercy and unchanging love. Tirmidhi describes surrender with true knowledge.
  12:18/q14/p5 Upright steadiness preserves deliberation and relies on God for recovery. — reasons: Tabatabai calls patience a defensive fortress, with God the further cause of recovery; it permits resistance to harm. Ibn Ashur cites the sound hadith on patience at first shock.
  12:18/q14/p6 It shows no visible change and meets affliction with an open heart. — reasons: Wahidi's people of meanings prohibit a frown; Yahya ibn Mu'adh describes a cheerful face. Alusi rejects construing this as Jacob's promise to face the sons cheerfully.
12:18/q15 Did Jacob's grief conflict with his patience? [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · theology] (turns on: Complaint to God and reports of rebuke are distinguished.)
  12:18/q15/p1 Grief and complaint to God can coexist with patience. — reasons: Hasan and Razi invoke 12:86. Tabatabai says Jacob's love and grief were for God's sake. Study Quran cites hope after hardship at 70:5.
  12:18/q15/p2 A report has God question Jacob's long lament and Jacob ask forgiveness. — reasons: Habib ibn Abi Thabit/Thabit says Jacob's brows fell over his eyes from grief; versions describe a cloth or bandage and differ in wording.
  12:18/q15/p3 He cried and could neither sleep nor eat. — reasons: Muqatil links this to Jewish Torah wailing; Islahi contrasts the Qur'anic portrait with Torah's sackcloth and mourning.
12:18/q16 Why did Jacob not search or seek justice immediately? [فَصَبْرٌۭ جَمِيلٌۭ · theology] (turns on: Razi asks why patience would justify leaving injustice unremedied; sources propose several replies.)
  12:18/q16/p1 God barred investigation to heighten his trial. — reasons: Razi calls this the decisive answer; Alusi transmits it. Ibn Ishaq and Tabarsi stress the test of Jacob's old age and Joseph's youth.
  12:18/q16/p2 The sons could obstruct or harm him, or inquiry could worsen matters. — reasons: Razi and Alusi give this possibility. Biqai says asking for remains could prompt worse harm.
  12:18/q16/p3 He trusted God's protection and Joseph's future. — reasons: Razi points to 12:6; Tabatabai's report says Jacob trusted the truthful dream.
  12:18/q16/p4 He avoided disgracing or punishing his sons. — reasons: Alusi cites a poem about a father wounded whether he punishes, pardons or strikes both sons.
  12:18/q16/p5 Old age and lack of allies made him dependent on the suspected sons until he later sent them to search. — reasons: Ibn Ashur and Biqai cite 12:87; Tabatabai says his only powerful helpers were the brothers.
  12:18/q16/p6 Patience permits action against wrongdoing when possible. — reasons: Razi insists on removing harm to others; Tabatabai describes inner steadiness with resistance as able.
12:18/q17 For what does Jacob seek God's help? [وَٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ · interpretation] (turns on: He may seek endurance, disclosure or both.)
  12:18/q17/p1 Help enduring their lies, alleged loss and grief. — reasons: Tabari and Qatada stress their lies; Mawardi lists patience, their account and its falsity. Nahhas parses a nominal sentence and calls ʿalā mā taṣifūn figurative. Razi and Alusi connect patience and help to 1:5.
  12:18/q17/p2 Help exposing the lie and reuniting with Joseph. — reasons: Bursevi and Alusi speak of vindication; Alusi cites 12:83. Tabatabai describes exclusive divine agency and omission of Jacob's self. Ibn Ashur allows a prayer to free Joseph.
  12:18/q17/p3 Help bearing the trial and bitter patience. — reasons: Jishumi and Tabarsi report this alternative; Ibn Ashur allows prayer or statement of reliance.
12:18/q18 What is 'what you describe'? [وَٱللَّهُ ٱلْمُسْتَعَانُ عَلَىٰ مَا تَصِفُونَ · referent] (turns on: Jacob may mean their claim or the actual unknown harm.)
  12:18/q18/p1 The false claim that Joseph died by a wolf. — reasons: Qatada calls it lies; Ibn Ashur says that is the sons' intended referent.
  12:18/q18/p2 The unknown injury done to Joseph. — reasons: Ibn Ashur says Jacob knew the sons lied but did not know how they harmed him; the ambiguity resembles 37:180.
12:18/q19 What legal lesson is drawn from the intact shirt? [* · law]
  12:18/q19/p1 Circumstantial signs may be weighed and acted on, including in qasama. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya attributes this to Malik's school; Qurtubi cites Ibn al-Arabi's claim of consensus on such indications. Jishumi says tears alone do not establish a claimant and the brothers' race was permissible since uncondemned.
12:18/q20 What is learned about prophetic knowledge and the brothers' status? [* · theology] (turns on: These are distinct theological inferences recorded with the episode.)
  12:18/q20/p1 Prophets may temporarily believe a liar before revelation. — reasons: Ibn Atiyya cites the Prophet's initial belief of Abd Allah b. Ubayy over Zayd b. Arqam and says prophets judge by what they hear.
  12:18/q20/p2 Jacob had truthful insight before the details were clear. — reasons: Qushayri says truthful hearts sense outcomes generally.
  12:18/q20/p3 The brothers' crime preceded prophethood, if they were prophets. — reasons: Baydawi qualifies the claim; Bursevi questions their commission because prophets are protected even before mission from repellent acts.
  12:18/q20/p4 A single lie and betrayal did not make the brothers hypocrites. — reasons: Biqai says hypocrisy requires habitual or predominant breach of trust.
12:18/q21 What lessons does this scene convey? [* · interpretation]
  12:18/q21/p1 Falsehood exposes itself through conflicting consequences. — reasons: Tabatabai says connected events cannot all be disguised; the intact shirt betrays the lie, citing 39:3, 40:28, 16:116 and 50:5. Qushayri says failure to tear it punished the brothers.
  12:18/q21/p2 Patience in trial must be joined to reliance on God. — reasons: Jishumi states this; Wahidi and Tabatabai connect the two. Asad views Joseph's story as divine direction of events, citing 2:216.
  12:18/q21/p3 God tested Jacob and Joseph through grief and enslavement, then brought relief. — reasons: Ibn Ishaq says their resolve was tested; Tabarsi says this occurred under divine watch.
12:18/q22 Is there an inward reading? [* · inward]
  12:18/q22/p1 The senses scheme while the faith-lit spirit sees and accepts God's decree. — reasons: Bursevi gives this analogy; Ta'wilat Najmiyya reads the appeal as asking for patience with decree.
12:18/q23 How did Aisha invoke this verse? [* · reports]
  12:18/q23/p1 During the slander ordeal she compared herself to Jacob, invoked beautiful patience and God's help, and was then vindicated. — reasons: Alusi says her accusers would accept neither oath nor excuse.

12:18/q26 What associated reports counsel response to calamity? [* · reports] (turns on: The reports give distinct counsel.)
  12:18/q26/p1 Recalling a calamity and saying resignation earns the original reward again. — reasons: A Prophetic report in Abd al-Razzaq.
  12:18/q26/p2 Hamna grieved especially for her husband despite words of resignation for several deaths. — reasons: The Prophet named Abdullah, Hamza and Mus'ab, showing a spouse's special place.
  12:18/q26/p3 Full patience occurs at the first shock. — reasons: Ibn Ashur calls sound the hadith of a grieving woman who later learned the one advising her was the Prophet.
