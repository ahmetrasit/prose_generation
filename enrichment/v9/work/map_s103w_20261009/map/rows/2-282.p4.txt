[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنحتىتنصبعلىوجهينPageV03P016فأحدهماأنتجعلالدخولغاية#471/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Indicative حتى can also introduce a present result after an earlier completed action, as in illness that has now made recovery unlikely; this is not a continuous endpoint.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنحتىتنصبعلىوجهينPageV03P016فأحدهماأنتجعلالدخولغاية#471/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Both indicative constructions are particles of inception with the same basic sense; they differ in whether the later action occurred together with the earlier one or is a present result of it.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنحتىتنصبعلىوجهينPageV03P016فأحدهماأنتجعلالدخولغاية#471/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | The Farazdaq's حتى كليب and Ḥassān ibn Thābit's حتى لا تهر support the inceptive use of حتى; the camel example likewise describes a present event after the clause.
[SIBAWAYH:اعلمأنحتىتنصبعلىوجهينPageV03P016فأحدهماأنتجعلالدخولغاية#471/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | ʿAlqama ibn ʿAbada's verse illustrates a connected sequence: the later riding and the earlier departure both belong to the past, and neither occurs without the other.
[SIBAWAYH:شبهوهبمايشبهمنالأسماءبالمصادرنحوقولكفاهms167إلىفىوليسبالفاعل#190/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Al-awwalu fa-l-awwalu resembles source-like nouns without matching them in every respect, functioning like wāḥidan fa-wāḥidan or rajulan rajulan.
[SIBAWAYH:شبهوهبمايشبهمنالأسماءبالمصادرنحوقولكفاهms167إلىفىوليسبالفاعل#190/r3] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In an imperative, accusative al-awwalu fa-l-awwalu is standard; it cannot be a badal or adjective because the corresponding readings are invalid.
[SIBAWAYH:شبهوهبمايشبهمنالأسماءبالمصادرنحوقولكفاهms167إلىفىوليسبالفاعل#190/r4] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · عيسى | reports | ʿĪsā explains the imperative as meaning li-yadkhul, “let the first enter,” and parses the phrase according to that underlying meaning.
[SIBAWAYH:شبهوهبمايشبهمنالأسماءبالمصادرنحوقولكفاهms167إلىفىوليسبالفاعل#190/r5] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Adding wa-l-ṣaghīru wa-l-kabīru permits nominative because the phrase means “all of them.”
[SIBAWAYH:شبهوهبمايشبهمنالأسماءبالمصادرنحوقولكفاهms167إلىفىوليسبالفاعل#190/r6] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | When the words are intended as direct adjectives, fa cannot join them; wa is acceptable, as supported by Umayyah ibn Abī ʿĀʾidh's verse.
[SIBAWAYH:شبهوهبمايشبهمنالأسماءبالمصادرنحوقولكفاهms167إلىفىوليسبالفاعل#190/r7] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أمية بن أبي عائذ | reports | Umayyah's poem with wa-shaʿthin supports coordination by wa; substituting fa before shaʿthin would be poor usage.
[SIBAWAYH:شبهوهبمايشبهمنالأسماءبالمصادرنحوقولكفاهms167إلىفىوليسبالفاعل#190/r8] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · الخليل | reports | Al-Khalīl allows al-awwalu fa-l-awwalu wa-l-awsaṭu wa-l-ākhiru by treating the sequence as a badal construction like kullukum.
[SIBAWAYH:فالحروفالتيتشركالواووالفاءوثموأووذلكقولكأريدأنتأتينيثم#480/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Mentioning possible error explains the reason for the reminder, as preparing support for a wall explains the cause without intending it to lean.
[SIBAWAYH:فالحروفالتيتشركالواووالفاءوثموأووذلكقولكأريدأنتأتينيثم#480/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · أهل الكوفة | reports | The Kufans read فتذكر with indicative mood.
[SIBAWAYH:فالحروفالتيتشركالواووالفاءوثموأووذلكقولكأريدأنتأتينيثم#480/r9] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In 2:282, فتذكر is subjunctive because witnessing is commanded so that one woman may remind the other; error is a cause, not the intended aim.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r1] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Tāʾ and dāl share a place and strong articulation; ṭāʾ may assimilate to dāl, but speakers can preserve its emphatic quality because it is audible.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r10] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Ṭāʾ, tāʾ, and dāl can assimilate into ḍād because its articulation approaches lām and the tooth roots; clarity is easier with its looser articulation.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r11] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Ḍād resists assimilation into ṣād, sīn, and zāy because its extended articulation acts as a barrier, much as shīn does.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r12] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Ṭāʾ, dāl, and tāʾ assimilate into shīn due to its extended articulation; the related dental group can also assimilate, though clear pronunciation is good.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r13] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Assimilation possible with a quiescent first consonant is also possible when it is moving, as with geminates; adjacent consonants within one word are heavier than those across a boundary.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r14] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | In derived forms, proximity can prompt substitution or assimilation: ṣabara yields muṣṭabir or muṣbir, while forms such as muzdān and mismiʿ show related adjustments.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r16] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Before tāʾ, ẓāʾ can retain emphasis or assimilate after substitution with ṭāʾ; Sībawayh cites Zuhayr's yẓlim / yṭlim rhyme and prefers the regular assimilation direction.

[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r18] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Dhāl before tāʾ becomes dāl in mudhakkir; the Qur'anic mudakkir exemplifies this, while *mudhdakkir is barred because the letters assimilate reciprocally when separated. | mentions 54:17
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r19] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Ḍād behaves like ṣād when combined with tāʾ: forms may show partial or complete assimilation, and the common word-internal pattern makes this stronger than across words.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r2] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · بعض العرب | reports | Some Arabs remove ṭāʾ's emphatic quality and make it like dāl when they fully assimilate it; assimilation also proceeds in the reverse direction to ṭāʾ.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r20] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | reports | Some speakers use ṭāʾ in muṭjaʿ; Sībawayh explains its assimilation through the combination of emphaticness, proximity, and frequent co-occurrence within a word.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r21] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | prefers | With the inflectional suffix tāʾ, the clearest and best Arabic leaves the suffix unchanged; unlike the fixed augment in iftaʿala, it marks a grammatical person and is separable.
[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r22] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · بعضهم | reports | Some speakers nevertheless assimilate the inflectional tāʾ, saying ʿaddahu for ʿadattahu and naqadahu for naqdattahu by analogy with other forms.

[SIBAWAYH:هذابابالإدغامفيحروفطرفاللسانوالثنايا#809/r24] SIBAWAYH, d. 180 · author | holds | Hijazis preserve the two consonants in forms such as arدد, while Tamim assimilate them; inflection can move the letters in the latter pattern, unlike the relevant close-consonant forms.
