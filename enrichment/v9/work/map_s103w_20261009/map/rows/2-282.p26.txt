[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p381/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Because the verse speaks of witnesses whom people approve, it indicates that people are not presumed upright until their reliability is established.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p381/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · حمزة | reports | Hamza alone reads inna tadilla with kasra on inna and fatḥa then kasra on the verb, and fataḏakkara with fatḥa on the ذ and nominative rāʾ; al-Aʿmash shares this reading.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p381/r8] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الباقون | reports | The other readers use an open an in an taḍilla and make fataḏakkara accusative; Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr lighten its ذ and ك, while the others geminate them.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p381/r9] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The purpose clause does not mean that testimony is taken in order for a woman to err; two women are appointed so one can remind the other if she forgets. Sibawayh compares this construction to preparing a prop for a wall if it leans.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382#2/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · قتادة والربيع وغيرهما | reports | They read the summons as one to give initial testimony. The verse was revealed when a claimant sought witnesses among many people, who hesitated to come forward.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382#2/r2] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · الحسن بن أبي الحسن وابن عباس | reports | They say the verse includes both accepting a request to establish testimony and accepting one to deliver testimony already held.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382#2/r3] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · مجاهد | reports | Mujahid limits the command to delivering testimony already acquired; al-Naqqash ascribes this interpretation to the Prophet. Initial witnessing remains voluntary, a view also reported from Lahik ibn Hamid, Ata, Ibrahim, Ibn Jubayr, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd, and others.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382#2/r4] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | prefers | He prefers al-Hasan’s inclusive reading: the verse recommends both forms of assistance. With ample witnesses and no danger to the right, a summoned person may decline for even a slight excuse; otherwise declining without excuse earns neither sin nor reward.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382#2/r5] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | When necessity or a real risk of losing a right arises, the recommendation approaches obligation; if delay will certainly destroy the right, testimony is obligatory, especially when it was already acquired and the summons is to deliver it, as a trust owed.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382#2/r6] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | La tasʾamu means ‘do not tire’; ṣaghīran and kabīran are circumstantial descriptions of the object, with the small item mentioned first for emphasis. Repeated debt recording could make people weary of writing.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382#2/r7] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Aqsaṭ means ‘more just’; its comparative form is anomalous if derived from the quadriliteral, but may follow aqram from karam, with aqsaṭ meaning just and qasiṭ meaning unjust.
[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p382/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | The clause puts the cause before the intended result because listeners naturally seek the cause of events; this anticipatory order is an especially eloquent, concise alternative to a follow-up question and answer.

[IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p383#2/r1] IBNATIYYA-FULL, d. 542 · author | holds | Because documentation can be burdensome, God removes blame for leaving immediate cash transactions unwritten; such dealings are usually small purchases rather than property.
