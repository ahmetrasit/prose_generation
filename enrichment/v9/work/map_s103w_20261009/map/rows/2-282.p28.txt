[TABRISI:2:282#6/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · قتادة والربيع | reports | Qatāda and al-Rabīʿ say the witnesses must not refuse when summoned to establish and carry the testimony.
[TABRISI:2:282#6/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس والحسن | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḥasan understand the summons as covering both bearing and delivering testimony; the author prefers this broader reading.
[TABRISI:2:282#6/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Do not weary of recording a debt, small or large, until its term; this is fairer, steadier testimony, and less likely to leave doubts about amount or term.
[TABRISI:2:282#6/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The command may address witnesses and mean not to tire of recording testimony; the stronger referent of its term is the debt's due date, not the witness's allowable period.
[TABRISI:2:282#6/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Writing the debt or testimony is more just before God, more reliable for testimony, and closer to preventing doubt; قسط means fairness and الأقسط means acting justly.
[TABRISI:2:282#6/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | A spot transaction exchanged hand to hand can be left unwritten because documentation is needed for deferred payment, not cash trade.
[TABRISI:2:282#7/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن وجميع الفقهاء | reports | Al-Ḥasan and the jurists regard witnessing sales as recommended; the Ẓāhirīs make it obligatory.
[TABRISI:2:282#7/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · الحسن وقتادة وعطاء وابن زيد | reports | With kasra on the first rāʾ, the prohibition addresses the writer and witness: do not change dictated terms, testify to matters not requested, or refuse to testify.
[TABRISI:2:282#7/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن مسعود ومجاهد | reports | With fatḥ on the first rāʾ, the prohibition tells people not to impose writing on a busy scribe or summon a witness when excused, or to mistreat either.
[TABRISI:2:282#7/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · الزجاج | prefers | Al-Zajjāj says the first reading is clearer because deliberate falsification makes someone a wrongdoer more plausibly than summoning a busy witness does.
[TABRISI:2:282#7/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | The verse threatens that harming a scribe or witness in writing or testimony is a departure from God's commands; fear God, who teaches what is needed and knows all.
[TABRISI:2:282#7/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · علي بن إبراهيم بن هاشم | reports | ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm b. Hāshim's tafsīr counts five hundred legal rulings in al-Baqara and fifteen in this verse alone.
[TABRISI:2:282/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · حمزة | reports | Ḥamza alone reads إن تضل with kasra, as a conditional particle whose جواب is فتذكر.
[TABRISI:2:282/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن كثير وأبو عمرو وقتيبة | reports | Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, and Qutayba read فتذكر lightly and in the subjunctive.
[TABRISI:2:282/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · حمزة | reports | Ḥamza reads فتذكر with doubled dhāl and indicative mood.
[TABRISI:2:282/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · الباقون | reports | The remaining readers read فتذكر with doubled dhāl and subjunctive mood.
[TABRISI:2:282/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · عاصم | reports | ʿĀṣim alone reads تجارة حاضرة in the accusative; the others read it nominative.
[TABRISI:2:282/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو جعفر | reports | Abū Jaʿfar reads لا يضار with a doubled, quiescent rāʾ; the remaining readers use the doubled rāʾ in the accusative form.
[TABRISI:2:282/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | For Ḥamza's conditional reading, رجل وامرأتان forms a nominative description of the two women, while ممن ترضون refers back to that nominative pair rather than accusative شهيدين.
[TABRISI:2:283/r1] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | One analysis takes تداينتم as mutual requital, while another takes it as dealing in debt; adding بدين resolves this lexical ambiguity.
[TABRISI:2:283/r2] TABRISI, d. 548 · ابن عباس | reports | Ibn ʿAbbās says the verse was revealed specifically concerning forward sale and testifies that God permitted a guaranteed sale to a known term.
[TABRISI:2:283/r3] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | prefers | Although Ibn ʿAbbās specifies forward sale, the verse's apparent scope includes every deferred debt, which commentators and jurists accept.
[TABRISI:2:283/r4] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | Writing the debt protects against forgetting or denial and secures the rights of creditor, debtor, and witnesses for the reasons explained.
[TABRISI:2:283/r5] TABRISI, d. 548 · أبو سعيد الخدري والحسن والشعبي | reports | Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, al-Ḥasan, and al-Shaʿbī say writing the debt is recommended, the majority's preferred view.
[TABRISI:2:283/r6] TABRISI, d. 548 · الربيع وكعب | reports | Al-Rabīʿ and Kaʿb regard writing as obligatory; the author cites the permission to rely on trust as evidence against obligation.
[TABRISI:2:283/r7] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | holds | A just scribe records the debt or sale accurately, without changing its terms, amount, or anything that harms either party without consent.
[TABRISI:2:283/r8] TABRISI, d. 548 · author | reports | The scribe must not refuse to write; كما علمه الله may mean writing justly as God taught, or that the scribe must not hoard the skill God gave him.
