# 1:6: 30 questions

## 1:6/q01 What does “guide us” ask God to do?  [ٱهْدِنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance here means direction, enabling, continuance, increase, future or final arrival.
- 1:6/q01/p1 Keep us firm in guidance and obedience already received. — ʿAlī and Ubayy compare it with asking a standing person to remain standing; 3:8 supports fear of deviation. [50 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(علي بن أبي طالب); MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI(علي وأبي بن كعب); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF(علي وأبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(عبد الله,علي وأبي); IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,علي وأبي); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير); ALUSI(يعسوب المؤمنين وأبيّ); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(البحر المحيط,روح المعاني); STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وأبي); ABUHAYYAN; JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; TAB-FULL(علي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(الصادق,علي); WAHIDI-BASIT(some scholars); BURSEVI(التيسير); DURR(أبو العالية الرياحي)]
- 1:6/q01/p2 Show and direct us to the right way. — Al-Akhfash glosses it as making known. Ibn ʿAtiyya reads the path wording as practical wayfinding. [14 notes: AKHFASH; MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QUTB-ZILAL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ثابت البناني)]
- 1:6/q01/p3 Increase guidance and higher degrees of insight. — 47:17 promises more guidance to the guided; al-Ṭabarī accepts increased aid for future deeds but rejects more exposition of already clarified duties. [13 notes: TAB(بعضهم); TAB-FULL; SULAMI; MAWARDI(unattributed); BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI; ALUSI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]
- 1:6/q01/p4 Grant success, inspiration, and effective divine aid. — Al-Ṭabarī cites Arabic poems where guidance enables success; Ibn ʿAbbās reports Gabriel's gloss as inspiration. [18 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس,الله); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TAB+]
- 1:6/q01/p5 Lead us to Paradise as reward. — Some cite 37:23 and idioms for leading to a destination; al-Ṭabarī objects from 1:5's concern with present worship. [7 notes: TAB(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA(أبو المعالي); ALUSI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني); TAB-; TAB-FULL-]
- 1:6/q01/p6 Guide us in the future as in the past. — Al-Ṭabrisī says prayer for a future act is worship and dependence. [4 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI; ALUSI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]
- 1:6/q01/p7 Express desire for guidance and seek the reward of supplication. — Al-Māwardī lists this as a reason to request already-given guidance. [3 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 1:6/q02 What does guidance mean in Arabic and Qur'anic usage?  [ٱهْدِنَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the lexical sense requires arrival and which root it reflects.
- 1:6/q02/p1 Direction or indication of a way, often gentle and beneficial. — Al-Wāḥidī cites leading animals and poetry; Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr call it gentle direction. Other contextual uses include clarification, invitation and inspiration. [82 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي); IBNATIYYA(ابن سيده,اللحياني); THALABI(الحسن بن الفضل,السدي,رسول الله); BAYDAWI; ABUHAYYAN(المفسرون); SAMIN-DURR(الراغب,امرؤ القيس); IBNASHUR; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(عبد الحكيم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو المعالي,اللحياني,المفسرون,النقاش); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الفضيل بن عياض,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض العلماء,عاصم الأحول عن أبي العالية,محمد بن الحنفية); IBNKATHIR; TABRISI]
- 1:6/q02/p2 Successful arrival belongs to the definition of guidance. — A group including al-Zamakhsharī requires this to distinguish guidance from misguidance; al-Ālūsī cites Thamūd against it. [4 notes: ALUSI(فريق); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المتأخرين); IBNASHUR(جماعة منهم الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 1:6/q02/p3 Showing the way is guidance even if the recipient rejects it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr endorses the majority and Ashʿarite account, citing Thamūd at 41:17. [5 notes: ALUSI(آخرون); IBNASHUR(عبد الحكيم); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR(الجمهور)+]
- 1:6/q02/p4 The root suggests inclining hearts toward truth. — Al-Qurṭubī connects it with turning and swaying; al-Samīn objects that this is a different root. [2 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 1:6/q03 What form and speech act is ihdinā?  [ٱهْدِنَا · grammar]
turns on: How imperative form is analyzed and the relative rank of speaker and addressee.
- 1:6/q03/p1 The same request form functions as command, prayer, or request according to the speakers' ranks. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Samīn and Almalılı distinguish a superior's command, subordinate's prayer, and request between equals. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL(علي وأبي); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN; ELMALILI]
- 1:6/q03/p2 The verb is an imperative expressing a subordinate's supplication to God. — Al-Farrāʾ calls it jussive; Basrans call it indeclinable. The initial hamza is waṣl and its vowel follows the imperative rules; the weak final yāʾ is dropped. [11 notes: NAHHAS(البصريون,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(البصريون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; AKHFASH]

## 1:6/q04 What alternate readings change the request verb?  [ٱهْدِنَا · readings]
turns on: Which transmitted wording expresses the petition.
- 1:6/q04/p1 Ibn Masʿūd reads arshidnā, “direct us rightly.” — Muqātil and al-Zamakhsharī report the substitution, a close gloss of guidance. [3 notes: MUQATIL(ابن مسعود); KASHSHAF(عبد الله); KASHSHAF-FULL(عبد الله)]
- 1:6/q04/p2 Thābit al-Bunānī reads “show us” or “make us see” the path. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report the wording. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA(ثابت البناني); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ثابت البناني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثابت البناني)]
- 1:6/q04/p3 ʿAlī is reported to recite the verbs as raised forms. — Al-Durr also reports his hamza, length and shadda in iyyāka. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(علي)]

## 1:6/q05 How does the verb govern “us” and “the path”?  [ٱهْدِنَا ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether syntax simply varies or signals a stage or different embedded sense.
- 1:6/q05/p1 “Us” is the first object and “the path” the second; the destination may be direct or introduced by ilā or lām. — Al-Ṭabarī cites Quranic and poetic parallels. Others analyze ordinary alternation, analogy to ikhtāra, or omission of a preposition. [32 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(أبي علي وأبي بن كعب); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)]
- 1:6/q05/p2 The constructions do not impose a fixed difference in guidance's basic sense. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the split a usage tendency; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the direct object in 40:38. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR+]
- 1:6/q05/p3 The constructions mark stages: ilā brings an outsider to the route, the direct object covers those on or off it, and lām marks the goal. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares Abraham's father, hypocrites, prophets and Muhammad; a path is a means, not the goal. [33 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(أبو عمرو,ابن بري,ابن كثير,اللسان,قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 1:6/q06 Why do already guided worshippers ask for guidance?  [ٱهْدِنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the need is preservation, further acquisition, or a new kind of guidance.
- 1:6/q06/p1 They need continuing divine aid against doubt and future error. — Al-Wāḥidī notes renewed doubts; al-Ṭabarī rejects the Qadarī claim that every commanded person already has all aid needed. [9 notes: TAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(some of our companions); BAGHAWI; IBNATIYYA; IBNKATHIR(أبو بكر الصديق); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 1:6/q06/p2 They may seek further knowledge, conduct and spiritual rank. — Rāzī cites innumerable signs in creation and moral moderation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes a final law from achieved personal rank. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); TABATABAI; BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 1:6/q06/p3 God has ordered kinds of guidance: faculties, proofs, revelation, and unveiled higher realities. — Bayḍāwī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Almalılı each give the fourfold scheme, with prophets and saints especially receiving the highest kind. [29 notes: BAYDAWI; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 1:6/q07 Why is the request plural?  [ٱهْدِنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether read as communal petition or grammatical pronoun form.
- 1:6/q07/p1 The worshipper includes the community, matching the preceding plural worship and reliance. — Rāzī says collective prayer is likelier answered; al-Sāmarrāʾī says it counters selfishness and strengthens fellow travelers. [20 notes: RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الرازي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 1:6/q07/p2 The suffix can denote speaker with others or a plural of majesty. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn describe its form and case. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 1:6/q08 What does ṣirāṭ mean and where does the word come from?  [ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether Arabic derivation or a foreign loan best explains the word.
- 1:6/q08/p1 It is a clear, broad road, extended metaphorically to religion and conduct. — Al-Ṭabarī cites Jarīr, Abū Dhuʾayb and rajaz; others cite 7:86. [28 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI(unattributed); BAGHAWI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QURTUBI(جرير,عامر بن الطفيل); ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير,جرير بن عطية); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; DURR(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR]
- 1:6/q08/p2 The road swallows travelers, or travelers consume its length. — Al-Māwardī connects the root with food passing the throat; al-Ālūsī cites desert and poetic uses of eating. [20 notes: MAWARDI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(readers of sīn); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; JURJANI-ASRAR; BURSEVI]
- 1:6/q08/p3 The word is Arabic; the claim of a Roman source is weak. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr reject al-Naqqāsh's proposal. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA(النقاش); QURTUBI(ابن عطية,النقاش); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(النقاش); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 1:6/q08/p4 It is a Latin loan from strata via, evoking paved Roman military roads. — Corpus Coranicum says the metaphor was likely already conventional. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 1:6/q09 What are ṣirāṭ's gender and plural forms?  [ٱلصِّرَٰطَ · grammar]
- 1:6/q09/p1 It can be feminine in the Hijaz and masculine in Tamīm, with different plural patterns. — Al-Akhfash compares other path nouns; al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish ṣuruṭ, aṣriṭa and a feminine afʿul pattern. ## 1:6/q11 What are the readings of the phrase's definiteness and construction?  [ٱلصِّرَٰطَ ٱلْمُسْتَقِيمَ · readings]
turns on: Whether definite, indefinite or annexed.
- 1:6/q11/p1 An indefinite “a straight path” is transmitted from al-Ḥasan, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Zayd. — Ibn Jinnī hears humility and an ongoing plea; Abū Ḥayyān analyzes a following definite apposition. [14 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الحسن); IBNATIYYA(الحسن والضحاك); ABUHAYYAN(زيد بن علي، والضحاك، ونصر بن علي عن الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن والضحاك,زيد بن علي، والضحاك، ونصر بن علي، عن الحسن); ALUSI(الحسن والضحاك وزيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن والضحاك وزيد بن علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والضحاك,ثابت البناني,جعفر بن محمد الصادق)]
- 1:6/q11/p2 The common reading makes both words definite, with mustaqīm an adjective. — Al-Ālūsī calls this mutawātir. [3 notes: ALUSI; NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]
- 1:6/q11/p3 Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq reads an annexation, “the path of the upright.” — Abū Ḥayyān glosses it as the upright religion. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA(جعفر بن محمد الصادق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جعفر الصادق); ALUSI(جعفر الصادق)]
