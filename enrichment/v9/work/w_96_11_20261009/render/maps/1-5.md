## 1:5/q02 What is the grammatical makeup of إِيَّاكَ?  [إِيَّاكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether iyyā, its suffix, or the whole form is pronominal, and how the suffix is governed
- 1:5/q02/p1 Iyyā is the independent accusative pronoun with a person marker. — KASHSHAF (الأخفش والمحققون): Al-Akhfash and careful grammarians hold that suffixes are not independent pronouns, like the kاف in araʾaytaka. KASHSHAF-FULL (الأخفش): Al-Akhfash and the verified grammarians treat the attached kāf, hā, and yā on iyya as markers of address, absence, or speech, not inflected pronouns. IBNATIYYA (بعضهم): Another analysis treats kāf, hāʾ, and yāʾ as the pronouns, unable to stand alone; iyā supports them before verbs while suffixes attach directly after them. IBNATIYYA (بعضهم): A further view takes iyā as an ambiguous accusative pronoun base; its suffixes distinguish addressee, absent person, and speaker and have no syntactic case of their own. IBNATIYYA (الخليل): Al-Khalīl analyzes iyā as a pronoun with the following suffix added for clarification, not to make it definite. IBNATIYYA (المبرد): Al-Mubarrad calls iyā an indefinite, ambiguous noun specified by the following suffix rather than made definite. IBNATIYYA-FULL (الخليل): Al-Khalil analyzes iyyā as an independent pronoun compounded with the following element for clarification rather than identification. IBNATIYYA-FULL (المبرد): Al-Mubarrad calls iyyā an indefinite pronoun made specific, not definite, by what follows. IBNATIYYA-FULL (بعض الكوفيين): Some Kufans treat the complete iyyāka as the pronoun, an exceptional one whose ending changes. IBNATIYYA-FULL (بعضهم): A further view treats ayyā as an obscure form for the accusative, with suffixes distinguishing addressee, absent person, and speaker but carrying no case role. TABRISI (author): Another explanation treats the repetition as emphasis, though the author questions that account because the second iyyaka is governed by a second verb. TABRISI (المبرد عن الأخفش): Al-Akhfash, transmitted by al-Mubarrad, treats iyyā as a singular pronoun whose ending changes with person and number; kaf marks address and has no case role. - 1:5/q10/p1 Seeking help means requesting aid; the form derives from the root ʿawn with regular sound changes. — IBNATIYYA (author): In nastaʿīnu, the vowel of the original wāw moves to ʿayn, and the wāw becomes yāʾ because the preceding consonant has kasra. IBNATIYYA (author): The verbal noun istiʿāna derives from istiʿwānan: the wāw's vowel moves to ʿayn, then the wāw becomes alif after fatḥa. IBNATIYYA-FULL (author): Nastaʿīn derives from nastaʿūn by moving the wāw's vowel to the ʿayn and changing wāw to yāʾ after kasra. QURTUBI (author): The form nastaʿīn derives from nastaʿwinu: the vowel of wāw transfers to ʿayn and turns to yāʾ; the verbal noun derives from istaʿwana, where transferred wāw becomes alif and one of two meeting alifs is deleted, with disagreement over which, while tāʾ compensates. BAYDAWI (author): Istiʿāna is seeking help. Necessary assistance includes capacity, conceiving the act, tools, and material; when these obtain, one can be morally commanded. ABUHAYYAN (author): Istiʿāna means seeking aid; the form istifʿala has twelve uses, including request, transformation, treating something as something, and several forms of equivalence or substitution. ABUHAYYAN (author): Istiʿāna means asking for help, as in other verbs of request. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): Istiʿāna means requesting help, and requesting is one of twelve meanings of the verb pattern istifʿāl, alongside the cited related semantic functions. ABUHAYYAN-FULL (author): Istiʿāna is derived from the root for help, with regular vowel changes across forms, and takes either a direct object or the preposition bāʾ. SAMIN-DURR (author): Nastaʿīnu derives from nastaʿwinu: a preceding kasra changes the quiescent wāw to yāʾ, as in mīzān and mīqāt. ALUSI (author): The same kasra on the imperfect prefix occurs in comparable verbs such as nastaʿīnu, except where the following sound makes the kasra difficult; some also say yijillu for yajalu.
