# 20:48: 27 questions

## 20:48/q01 Whose speech is this warning, and where does the dictated message end?  [إِنَّا قَدْ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْنَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the messengers' continuing speech after the peace formula is still God's dictated instruction.
- 20:48/q01/p1 It continues the speech God instructed Moses and Aaron to deliver; the peace and warning are part of the dictated message. — Yahya says the revelation statement continues the preceding speech. Ibn Ashur cites the transition to Pharaoh's question and says this stretch was absent from earlier suras. Alusi argues that their speech continues after the peace formula, so both sentences are dictated and the latter explains the warning. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:48/q01/p2 The dictated message ends with the sign; Moses and Aaron add the promise and warning themselves. — Alusi reports this as the view of some, with reference to 20:47. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:48/q02 Why introduce the warning as revelation to the messengers?  [إِنَّا قَدْ أُوحِىَ إِلَيْنَآ · rhetoric]
- 20:48/q02/p1 It delivers a general, indirect warning to Pharaoh gently, without making the messengers seem to threaten him personally. — Ibn Ashur says it warns before Pharaoh's disbelief and lets the message be completed before his response. Islahi says they report God's warning rather than impose punishment themselves, preventing a claim of personal affront. Baydawi says the explicit emphatic threat is useful and important at the start and suits what follows; Bursevi calls the threat gentle. Tabatabai says the required message remains truthful without flattery or deference to Pharaoh's false power and pride. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 20:48/q03 What does revelation mean here, and how did it reach Moses and Aaron?  [أُوحِىَ إِلَيْنَآ · meaning]
- 20:48/q03/p1 Waḥy is swift communication; here Moses received it through Gabriel, and Aaron through Gabriel and Moses. — Bursevi gives the original sense as a swift sign or communication and lists secret speech through Gabriel, inspiration, and dream as possible forms of waḥy. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:48/q04 Why is the revealer unnamed in the passive أُوحِيَ?  [أُوحِىَ · rhetoric]
- 20:48/q04/p1 Omitting God's name forestalls Pharaoh's rash irreverence; it honors God and belittles the addressee. — Abu Hayyan expects Pharaoh to respond irreverently if God is named; al-Samin adds that the omission honors God while diminishing Pharaoh. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:48/q05 What grammatical role does أَنَّ الْعَذَابَ عَلَىٰ مَنْ كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ have?  [أَنَّ ٱلْعَذَابَ عَلَىٰ مَن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · grammar]
- 20:48/q05/p1 The أن clause is nominative as the نائب فاعل of passive أُوحِيَ. — Al-Samin says it replaces the subject suppressed by the passive verb. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:48/q06 What does the word الْعَذَابَ mean in Arabic?  [ٱلْعَذَابَ · meaning]
turns on: The first account defines the threatened suffering; Ibn Faris gives the word's original sense.
- 20:48/q06/p1 It is pain whose suffering persists, here the punishment threatened against deniers who turn away. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives the continuing-pain sense and applies it to this verse. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 20:48/q06/p2 Its original Arabic sense is striking or beating. — Ibn al-Jawzi transmits this etymology from Ibn Faris. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس)]

## 20:48/q07 Which punishment does the warning encompass?  [ٱلْعَذَابَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether punishment is read as worldly and afterlife retribution or as an inward condition.
- 20:48/q07/p1 It includes punishment in this world and the next, with everlasting punishment in the next life. — Qurtubi specifies worldly ruin and eternal Hell; Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi, Study Quran, and Ibn Ashur include this world and the afterlife. Bursevi says finite suffering is negligible beside everlasting suffering and compares the scope with all peace; Ibn Ashur cites 79:25–26. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:48/q07/p2 It is punishment in nature's hell and matter's abyss. — Qashani gives this inward interpretation for whoever opposes and turns away. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:48/q08 How broad is the definite expression الْعَذَابَ, and can believers be punished?  [ٱلْعَذَابَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the definite article covers every instance of punishment, everlasting punishment, or simply punishment generically; whether finite punishment counts beside everlasting safety.
- 20:48/q08/p1 The expression confines all punishment to deniers, so believers receive no punishment at all. — Wahidi takes the unqualified generic word to imply that the believing affirmer has no punishment. Alusi reports the Murji'ah inference that only unbelievers are punished; Alusi challenges that inference. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:48/q08/p2 The generic or universal expression rules out everlasting punishment for believers, although finite punishment can occur. — Razi says the definite article may be generic or universal and that everlasting safety renders a finite punishment negligible. Bursevi likewise compares finite and everlasting suffering. Alusi says the article may designate a particular or everlasting punishment, or use hyperbolic generality compatible with finite punishment before endless safety; he rejects the inference that believers can never be punished. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:48/q08/p3 The definite article is generic, effectively saying that punishment awaits whoever denies. — Ibn Ashur compares the definite expression to an indefinite phrase and makes no categorical claim here about believers' finite punishment. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:48/q09 Who is threatened, and what do denying and turning away involve?  [مَن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:48/q09/p1 The warning targets whoever rejects God's signs, prophets, message, or faith and turns away from belief or obedience. — Muqatil specifies denial of God's oneness and turning from it. Qatada, via Yahya, Tabari, and Durr, specifies denial of God's signs or Book and abandonment of obedience. Tabari adds the call to oneness, obedience, and answering messengers; turning away is from truth. Tha'labi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Alusi, and Study Quran identify the denied prophets, their message, signs, or faith. Ibn Kathir distinguishes inward denial from outward turning and cites 79:37–39, 92:14–16, and 75:31–32. Bursevi says denial may be by speech or deed, and turning away rejects signs and follows desire. Islahi supplies the omitted objects as their prophethood and guidance; Tabatabai includes signs and the true call. Biqai stresses that the wording covers every denier and deserter, its nominal construction making the punishment established and enduring. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 20:48/q10 How does تَوَلَّىٰ express turning away?  [وَتَوَلَّىٰ · grammar]
- 20:48/q10/p1 It can be transitive or take an understood ʿan, expressing withdrawal and abandonment of closeness. — Bursevi says the act consists in refusing the signs and following desire. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:48/q11 Why are the objects of كَذَّبَ and تَوَلَّىٰ unstated?  [كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:48/q11/p1 They are understood as the messengers' prophethood and guidance; leaving them unstated avoids wounding Pharaoh's ego. — Islahi connects the omission with the generally phrased warning. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:48/q12 Why is the warning phrased generally rather than addressed to Pharaoh?  [عَلَىٰ مَن كَذَّبَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:48/q12/p1 It lets the warning reach Pharaoh without a direct insult, while implying his error and modeling proper address. — Islahi says a general formulation spares Pharaoh's ego. Biqai says shifting speech away from him signals that he is astray and a denier and models etiquette. Tabatabai says neither peace nor threat directly addresses him, undermining his self-importance before God's encompassing might, with 20:46 in view. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:48/q13 How does the warning relate to the preceding peace for the guided?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the preceding salām is a separate greeting or part of the report of revealed safety for the guided.
- 20:48/q13/p1 It explains that the guided are safe because punishment falls on those who deny and turn away. — Tabrisi says the following statement clarifies that guided followers escape punishment; Tabatabai says the revelation explains the limitation of peace to the guided. Abu Hayyan says the following threat supports reading peace as safety; al-Zajjaj, reported by Tabrisi, reads peace as deliverance from divine punishment. Ibn Atiyya and a view reported by Abu Hayyan attach the peace phrase to the revelation statement, and take ʿalā as 'for' or 'belonging to.' [7 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:48/q13/p2 The preceding peace is a separate salutation after addressing followers of guidance, implicitly reproaching Pharaoh. — Abu Hayyan prefers the separate greeting. He reports al-Zamakhshari's picture of Paradise's angels greeting the guided as Hell's wardens reproach deniers, but calls that interpretation strange. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 20:48/q14 Why has this verse been called especially hopeful for believers?  [* · theology]
- 20:48/q14/p1 It assigns punishment to deniers who turn away, leaving monotheists or believers safe from that threat. — Tha'labi cites an unnamed tafsir, Wahidi and Nasafi call it among the most hopeful verses, Qurṭubi and Abu Hayyan transmit Ibn Abbas's judgment, Burhan reports another proposed hopeful verse, and Itqan cites al-Kirmani. Nasafi says peace is assigned to believers and punishment to deniers, with no third category; Ibn Abbas says believers neither deny nor turn away. Wahidi stresses safety for followers of the message. [8 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; ITQAN(الكرماني)]

## 20:48/q15 What task and signs frame Moses and Aaron's delivery of the warning?  [* · reports]
- 20:48/q15/p1 God supported their mission with signs, and they went to Pharaoh to announce that they were God's messengers. — Qushayri says a messenger needs a clear sign proving the prophetic claim, although signs only strengthened proof against a spiritually blind audience. Tha'labi says Moses' hand emerged from his shirt bright as the sun and Pharaoh did not see the staff until the festival. Abu Hayyan names staff and hand as the signs and explains their attribution to both messengers although one performed them. Durr reports Moses' fear over a killing and speech impediment, his request for Aaron's support, Aaron's summons, their wait outside Pharaoh's court, and Pharaoh's question about their Lord. Tabrisi notes the narrative omits the transition in which they went and conveyed God's command. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL; TABRISI]

## 20:48/q16 What other warnings or moral lessons are placed beside this verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: These are separate surrounding moral observations rather than competing interpretations.
- 20:48/q16/p1 The messengers' commands and prohibitions are paired with promise and threat. — Jishumi states this pairing. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:48/q16/p2 Distorting scripture or religion and promoting falsehood is a grave sin. — Jishumi warns against following that path. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:48/q16/p3 Preferring worldly life to religion is condemned. — Jishumi warns against acting likewise. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:48/q16/p4 A dying Muʿawiya urged detachment from worldly life after condemning corruption and contrasting wrongdoers with persecuted believers. — Baqillani transmits his sermon after Quraysh rejoiced at his impending death; he distinguished powerless wrongdoers, armed predators, religious hypocrites, and false ascetics. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(معاوية بن أبي سفيان)]

## 20:48/q17 What is the broader approach to knowing a verse's occasion of revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 20:48/q17/p1 Its story and circumstances are needed for interpretation, and occasion claims require reliable witness reports and caution. — Wahidi says the Quranic sciences are vast, his earlier compilations cover much of them, and he wrote a beginner's guide because people neglect them. He requires reports from witnesses who knew and investigated the circumstances, notes predecessors' caution, cites the Prophet through Ibn Abbas warning that uninformed Quran claims or deliberate lies about him merit Fire, and cites Ubayda telling Ibn Sirin to fear God because the witnesses had passed away. [7 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن ابن عباس,عبيدة)]

## 20:48/q18 How may individual Arabic consonants and letter names be pronounced?  [* · grammar]
- 20:48/q18/p1 A consonant uttered alone takes a supporting sound, and letter names are expanded into pronounceable noun forms. — Sibawayh reports al-Khalil's supporting hāʾ for a moving consonant, removed in connected speech. An alif can play the same role: ألا تا and بلى فا stand for longer commands, supported by rajaz. An initial silent consonant needs connecting alif, as in ابن and اسم. Once a supported consonant is a name, hāʾ drops and an underlying deleted letter supplies a triliteral pattern; kasra expands with yāʾ as in في, ḍamma with waw as in لو، هو، أو, and fatḥa with alif as in ما، لا. Conventional قاف and باء name letters in noun forms, unlike sound imitations غاق and قب. For bāʾ as a man's name, he gives إب in pause and آب in connection, allowing the connecting alif to fall. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 20:48/q19 How should a letter named from a word be formed?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether the letter's source or the preceding vowel governs its name.
- 20:48/q19/p1 Its name follows its source word. — Al-Khalil gives ضاء from ضرب, ضي from ضراب, and ضو from ضحى; Sibawayh also records some speakers calling the bāʾ from ضرب رب while intending ʿayn. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,بعضهم)]
- 20:48/q19/p2 Its name follows the vowel of the immediately preceding letter. — Sibawayh reports this from opponents of al-Khalil. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(مخالفو الخليل)]

## 20:48/q20 What is the status of alif-lām and connecting alif in Arabic?  [* · grammar]
- 20:48/q20/p1 Alif-lām is one connected particle, and connecting alif can be distinguished from interrogative alif. — Al-Khalil compares the article with قد and أن rather than the separate interrogative hamza; it can detach from a noun like هل، قد، سوف, with a poetic recollection of قد as evidence. Sibawayh shows connecting alif in أيم الله from إيم الله and ليم الله, while interrogative آلرجل takes an open alif to avoid confusion. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 20:48/q21 How do articulated consonants and vowels work, and can sounded letters be eternal?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the succession of audible letters entails origination or the letters' essences and divine perfection establish eternity.
- 20:48/q21/p1 Sounds and letters are sequential, and speech made of them is originated rather than eternal. — Razi distinguishes a moving consonant from bodily motion: it follows a silent articulation with a sound. Vowels are reducible portions of voiced sound; prolonging them yields sound. A consonant precedes its dependent vowel, avoiding circularity. In a word earlier letters cease before later ones arise; simultaneous triliteral letters could not choose among six orders. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:48/q21/p2 Letters and God's speech are eternal. — Razi reports proponents arguing that letters have distinct essences which cannot vanish, that God's speech is a perfection whose absence would imply deficiency and a transition to perfection, and that oath law ties hearing God's speech to hearing these letters. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بقدم الحروف)]

## 20:48/q22 Can jinn know the unseen?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the Solomon report rules out jinn knowledge or they may acquire it through heavenly hearing or unknown means; Razi questions the certainty of these proposals.
- 20:48/q22/p1 They cannot know it. — Razi cites their remaining under Solomon after his death without knowing that he had died. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:48/q22/p2 They can know it by approaching the heavens and overhearing angels. — Razi reports this explanation from some. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:48/q22/p3 They can know it by other means unknown to humans. — Razi reports this separate proposal. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:48/q22/p4 The realities of such questions are known only to God. — Razi cautions that proposed answers are conjecture and speculation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:48/q23 What harms are included in seeking refuge from God?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:48/q23/p1 It covers countless false beliefs, sins, bodily harms, fears, and worldly afflictions; one should seek God for protection from all of them. — Razi divides spiritual and bodily evils. False beliefs are without number; he counts seventy-two deviant Muslim sects and at least seven hundred outside. Bodily and religious harms include every prohibition, illness, fire, drowning, poverty, disability, and blindness. He summarizes ignorance, sin, disliked affliction, and fear, urges one to survey these unbounded needs, recognize creaturely inability, and turn to God. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:48/q24 What does إنما add to the statement about those who remember?  [* · rhetoric]
- 20:48/q24/p1 It implies that those without understanding do not remember, where a bare statement only praises the intelligent. — Jurjani cites 13:19 and says negation after affirmation requires a contrast mentioned or signaled by the wording; إنما supplies that signal. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 20:48/q25 What implied meanings do Jurjani's cited love and means poems convey?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These are distinct poetic illustrations of implied meaning, not rival readings.
- 20:48/q25/p1 The love poem indirectly advises abandoning hope of union, while saying only a lover excuses lovers excludes blame from those who do not know love's affliction. — Jurjani explains both inferences from the love poem. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 20:48/q25/p2 The speaker expects success because he chose a strong means, and a doctor analogy commends reliance on the proper helper. — Jurjani says people turn to a doctor when sick and to the right source when in need. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 20:48/q26 What does Hudhayfa's prayer report say about the Prophet's recitation?  [* · reports]
- 20:48/q26/p1 In night prayer the Prophet recited al-Baqara, then al-Nisaʾ, then Aal ʿImran before bowing. — Gharnati cites Hudhayfa's report from Sahih Muslim; Hudhayfa repeatedly expected the bow. [1 notes: GHARNATI(حذيفة)]

## 20:48/q27 How do endings and themes of other suras compare?  [* · links]
turns on: These observations address different comparisons among suras.
- 20:48/q27/p1 Sura endings suit their themes, with different endings emphasizing threat, mercy, worship, kinship, consolation, praise, and proclamation. — Burhan says al-Anʿam ends with swift punishment and mercy; al-Aʿraf with angels and worship; al-Anfal with jihad and kinship; Baraʾa with honor of the Messenger and divine oneness; Yunus and Hud console; Yusuf, Ibrahim, and al-Hijr praise the Quran or counsel the Prophet; al-Raʿd answers deniers; al-Nahl consoles and promises; al-Isra praises; al-Kahf calls for the message and transcendence. He says examples through the Quran's first half model study of the rest. [6 notes: BURHAN]
- 20:48/q27/p2 Earlier suras recount destroyed peoples and saved messengers; these suras concentrate on prophets even without stating their peoples' fate. — Burhan makes this contrast of narrative focus. [1 notes: BURHAN]
