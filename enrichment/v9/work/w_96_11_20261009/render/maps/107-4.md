# 107:4: 23 questions

## 107:4/q01 What does the opening fāʾ do?  [فَوَيْلٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the subsequent praying people are the earlier denier and whether the clauses further identify a named orphan-pusher.
- 107:4/q01/p1 It marks the warning as a consequence of the preceding faults and connects the two passages. — Al-Samīn says the shameful traits lead to woe; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say the sequence escalates from orphan abuse to neglect of prayer, ostentation and withheld aid or zakāt. Ibn ʿĀshūr says even later attached revelation can be worded to fit the earlier context; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī applies denial of requital to outwardly praying people. [8 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR(ابن وهب وأشهب عن مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 107:4/q01/p2 It is causal: woe is theirs, and on one account the praying people are the same group as the earlier deniers. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives the paraphrase 'so woe is theirs.' Al-Ālūsī reports that some derive a single group from the preceding coordination and identify them as hypocrites, citing Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Mālik and the following ostentation clause. He reports Abū Ḥayyān's objection that the coordination and demonstrative are awkward. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI(أبو حيان)-]
- 107:4/q01/p3 The later clauses are a digression prompted by the orphan-pushing description. — The Kashf author says that if the earlier orphan-pusher is one named person, prayer neglect and ostentation fit poorly as further identifying descriptions of him. [1 notes: ALUSI(صاحب الكشف)]

## 107:4/q02 How is the threat of wayl understood?  [فَوَيْلٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether wayl is explained as the punishment generally or as a particular valley in Hell.
- 107:4/q02/p1 It announces punishment or ruin for the condemned worshippers. — Al-Qurṭubī calls it punishment; al-Burṣawī infers severe punishment from their treatment of the orphan and poor. Al-Rāzī adduces warnings for fraud, wrongful writing and slander at 83:1, 2:79 and 104:1 to show its use for grave crime; Biqāʿī calls it the strongest expression of humiliation. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and Study Quran direct readers to earlier discussions, including 104:1 and 2:79; Corpus Coranicum says emphatic wail opens the catalogue of vices. [10 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 107:4/q02/p2 It is a valley flowing with the pus of Hell, threatening hypocritical worshippers. — Al-Ṭabarī connects the valley to people who pray without seeking God and are heedless while praying. [2 notes: TAB]

## 107:4/q03 What is the grammatical role of the lām in 'to the worshippers'?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · grammar]
- 107:4/q03/p1 The prepositional phrase with lām is the predicate of fa-wayl. — Al-Samīn gives this parsing alongside the consequential force of fāʾ. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 107:4/q04 Why does the verse say 'worshippers' instead of using a pronoun?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the description replaces a pronoun for the same denier, broadens a specific case, mocks nonpraying people, or defines a standing class liable to prayer.
- 107:4/q04/p1 The explicit descriptive noun exposes another vice of the same people and heightens the warning. — Al-Zamakhsharī's analysis makes their description replace a pronoun for the deniers, who also neglect prayer, show off and withhold alms. Al-Samīn defends this as a coherent unified description against the Shaykh. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it avoids a monotonous sequence of six parallel traits and that placing wayl before the noun makes all preceding vices grounds for punishment. Al-Bayḍāwī says it highlights offenses against Creator and creatures. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh)-; ALUSI(قيل)-]
- 107:4/q04/p2 The plural descriptive noun broadens the warning to anyone with these traits, including an earlier named individual. — Biqāʿī says it blocks complacency in someone who outwardly prays and warns that scorning the weak leads to neglect of unseen duties. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the denier may be a generic class or one person included among many; al-Qāshānī also treats the denier as a generic type. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QASHANI]
- 107:4/q04/p3 The title 'worshippers' is ironic because the intended people do not truly pray. — Ibn ʿĀshūr applies this to a wholly Meccan reading and compares the disbelievers' admission at 74:43–44 that they did not pray. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 107:4/q04/p4 The noun denotes people liable to pray and marked by a persistent trait, rather than merely people praying at this moment. — Biqāʿī says the descriptive form ties woe to an enduring characteristic and cites the prophetic report about a menstruating woman's prayer requiring a head covering to support reference to people bound by prayer. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 107:4/q05 How can the plural worshippers relate grammatically to the earlier singular denier?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical treatment of fa-dhālika, the omitted answer of araʾayta, and whether a generic singular can take a descriptive plural in place of a pronoun.
- 107:4/q05/p1 The earlier singular denotes a generic class and so can be resumed by the plural worshippers. — Al-Zamakhsharī joins fa-dhālika to the denier, omits araʾayta's answer as supplied by the following warning, and reads the descriptive plural for a pronoun. Al-Samīn regards the unified description as coherent; Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Qāshānī likewise allow the denier to be a class. [10 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QASHANI; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh)-; ALUSI(أبو حيان,قيل)-]
- 107:4/q05/p2 The plain construction treats the worshippers as a separate plural group; the proposed singular-to-plural substitution is strained. — Abū Ḥayyān says the earlier demonstrative is naturally nominative; an accusative would require an awkward repeated verb. He and the Shaykh object that a plural noun cannot naturally replace a singular pronoun, and urge the apparent syntax. Al-Ālūsī reports objections to the unnecessary demonstrative and to a pronoun covering two groups. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(the Shaykh); ALUSI(أبو حيان,قيل); ABUHAYYAN+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 107:4/q05/p3 The demonstrative is an emphatic insertion conveying the person's extreme distance in evil. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative treatment of the preceding demonstrative. [1 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]

## 107:4/q06 Who are the worshippers threatened here?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the latter verses address the earlier deniers or another group, when they were revealed, and whether their outward prayer is hypocritical Islamic prayer or an older distorted ritual.
- 107:4/q06/p1 They are hypocrites who pray publicly for show but neglect or abandon prayer in private. — Ibn ʿAbbās adds withholding borrowed household goods out of spite; al-Ḥasan says they expect no reward for timely prayer and fear no penalty for lateness, or feel no regret on missing it. Al-Nasafī says they lack conviction of its obligation and seek neither nearness nor duty. Al-Rāzī prefers this limitation and links it to the extra penalty of unbelievers. Abū Ḥayyān says only the outward postures are established; Mālik's transmitted view ties this to Medinan revelation. Ostentation is cited as evidence by al-Ālūsī and Study Quran. [27 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(ابن عباس); TAB(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس ومجاهد والإمام مالك,بعضهم); IBNASHUR(ابن وهب وأشهب عن مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك); STUDYQURAN(majority of commentators); ABUHAYYAN+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 107:4/q06/p2 They are prayer performers broadly, outwardly observant yet heedless of its time, substance or duties. — Ibn Kathīr says they are committed to prayer but heedless of it. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī qualify the threat with the following verses on heedlessness, ostentation and withheld aid. Elmalılı includes professed believers who perform a few prayers smugly and neglect charity; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says they really do pray but lack the conscience prayer should form. Quṭb says they perform prayer without establishing it. Study Quran reports people who gain no benefit from prayer and fear no cost for omitting it. [22 notes: THALABI(عطاء بن دينار); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN(majority of commentators,some commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 107:4/q06/p3 They are the same deniers of judgment who push away orphans and fail to feed the poor. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this alternative but says the apparent reading has a separate group. Al-Samīn describes one class combining all six vices. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes the identification conditional on a wholly Meccan sura; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says people profess religion while practically denying requital. Al-Ālūsī's causal reading unifies the groups. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 107:4/q06/p4 They are a separate group from the earlier denier of judgment. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent reading and connects it to the transition from denial of requital to worship owed to the Creator. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ABUHAYYAN+]
- 107:4/q06/p5 They include disbelievers performing a distorted prayer at the House, such as Quraysh's custodians. — Study Quran cites 8:35's whistling and clapping. Iṣlāḥī says the custodians' public ritual was pretence: Abraham established the House for prayer (14:37), Ishmael enjoined prayer and zakāt (19:55), and later ritual became whistling and clapping (8:35). Ibn ʿĀshūr's Meccan reading calls the label 'worshippers' mockery, citing 74:43–44. [12 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 107:4/q06/p6 The warning may cover all legally accountable people, including non-hypocrite disbelievers who abandon prayer. — Al-Ālūsī reports this on the unified-group reading; it entails that disbelievers are bound by subsidiary legal duties. [1 notes: ALUSI(قيل)]

## 107:4/q07 Which fault in or about prayer makes these worshippers blameworthy?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether heedlessness chiefly concerns prayer's time and occurrence, its sincerity, its performance, or its ethical purpose; several sources combine these faults.
- 107:4/q07/p1 They neglect prayer itself or its appointed time, sometimes abandoning it entirely. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says they abandon prayer; al-Wāḥidī and the report from Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ explain delay beyond the time. Al-Ṭabarī includes omission and distraction; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes missing all or some prayers or delaying beyond the preferred time, and reports ʿAlī on taking times lightly. Elmalılı adds absence of regret, while Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes that omission or delay exhausts the verse. [15 notes: FARRA(ابن عباس); THALABI(الضحاك); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Sa‘d b. Ebî Vakkas); TABATABAI(علي عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)+; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الطبري)-]
- 107:4/q07/p2 They perform prayer for display, without sincerity or concern for its duty and reward. — Ibn ʿAbbās describes public performance and private abandonment; al-Ḥasan says they feel no regret on missing prayer, or seek no reward and fear no penalty. The report from Abū Barza calls the warning better than the world and describes lack of hope and fear. Elmalılı speaks of worldly aims and amusement; Quṭb says they pray without establishing prayer. Study Quran reports a prophetic description of a late, hastily pecked prayer with little remembrance as hypocritical. [22 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); ELMALILI(Ebû Berzete’l-Eslemî); QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN(majority of commentators)]
- 107:4/q07/p3 They lack reverence and attention, or hurry through the prayer and its required movements. — Abū al-ʿĀliya mentions incomplete bows and prostrations, and looking around during prostration; al-Zamakhsharī describes pecked prayer unlike the Prophet and early Muslims, without humility or avoidance of disliked acts. Al-Ālūsī gives the same hurried and inattentive performance; Study Quran also reports incomplete movements or proper outward movements without contemplation and remembrance. [9 notes: THALABI(أبو العالية); ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الزمخشري); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); QASHANI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]
- 107:4/q07/p4 They miss prayer's moral purpose: standing humbly before God and caring for others. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says ʿan points away from prayer's purpose, not to lapses within it: reverence, restraint of wrongdoing and care for orphans and the poor, citing 90:11–17 and the following ostentation verse. She says even timely prayer can be formal hypocrisy for an audience. Elmalılı cites 29:45 on prayer restraining evil; Küran Yolu stresses inward faith, intention, sincerity, reverence and piety alongside outward forms, with 1:5 and 3:64. Iṣlāḥī calls the custodians' worship empty and pretentious; Corpus Coranicum compares the root s-h-w to religious ghafla and 51:11. [14 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber); CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 107:4/q08 Does the warning include ordinary forgetfulness during prayer?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · interpretation]
- 107:4/q08/p1 No; the wording condemns heedlessness about or away from prayer, not an involuntary lapse within it. — ʿAṭāʾ ibn Dīnār distinguishes 'of/from prayer' from 'during prayer'; Elmalılı says human beings cannot fully avoid such mistakes. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ relies on the preposition ʿan and notes that accidental forgetfulness is no sin and may be remedied by prostration for forgetfulness or voluntary prayers. [6 notes: THALABI(عطاء بن دينار); ELMALILI(Atâ b. Dînâr); BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 107:4/q09 What is the relation of this warning to neglecting prayer and showing off in the following verses?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · interpretation]
- 107:4/q09/p1 The following descriptions qualify the worshippers who receive woe: they neglect prayer, show off and withhold aid. — Al-Māwardī treats the qualification as an ellipsis completed by the next verse; al-Nasafī extends it through verses 5–7, including alms or useful aid. Al-Rāzī counts these three sins and notes their severity needs explanation since the acts alone do not make a Muslim a hypocrite. Quṭb and Küran Yolu likewise link outward prayer with ostentation and obstructing help. [10 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 107:4/q10 What does the warning imply about the legal duties of unbelievers?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · law]
- 107:4/q10/p1 Unbelievers are also addressed by subsidiary laws and may be punished additionally for omitting them. — Al-Rāzī prefers identifying hypocritical unbelievers and says their extra penalty for forbidden acts and neglected duties supports al-Shāfiʿī's position. Al-Ālūsī reports a reading covering even non-hypocrite disbelievers who wholly abandon prayer and draws the same legal entailment. [2 notes: ALUSI(قيل); RAZI-FULL+]

## 107:4/q11 When is displaying prayer or worship itself blameworthy?  [لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · law]
- 107:4/q11/p1 Public obligatory worship is proper; voluntary worship is better concealed unless display encourages others, while showing off for praise is blameworthy. — Al-Nasafī derives riyāʾ from reciprocal showing: the performer displays his act and people respond with admiration. He distinguishes required public duties from voluntary worship. Küran Yolu connects sincere intention with divine unity and verses 1:5 and 3:64. [5 notes: NASAFI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 107:4/q12 How does this warning connect to the preceding abuse of orphans and the poor?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is parallel failures toward people and God or an escalating warning against outwardly devout pretenders; the explanations can coexist.
- 107:4/q12/p1 The passage moves from failures toward people to a failure of worship toward the Creator; both expose the same ruined heart. — Al-Rāzī gives three linked explanations: prayer without humility is stronger evidence of hypocrisy; ostentatious prayer cannot restrain wrongdoing; and compassion for creatures together with reverence for God completes the moral test. Abū Ḥayyān contrasts denial of judgment with worship owed to the Creator; Biqāʿī, Küran Yolu and Elmalılı stress the paired duties. Corpus Coranicum sees cross correspondence: religious omission at verse 1 and verses 4–6, social omission at verses 2–3 and 7. [21 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; CORPUSCORANICUM; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 107:4/q12/p2 The latter warning intensifies the blame: apparent worshippers who neglect its purpose and withhold aid are more astonishing than outright deniers. — Al-Kashshāf says the preceding sins disclose weak faith; al-Bayḍāwī says prayer is religion's pillar, ostentation a branch of unbelief and zakāt Islam's bridge. Elmalılı says religious pretenders are more astonishingly blameworthy; Biqāʿī says disregard for the weak makes neglect of unseen duties likelier. Al-Ālūsī says the escalating catalogue increasingly exposes denial of religion. [6 notes: KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI]

## 107:4/q13 What larger lesson about worship and social duty does the sūra draw?  [* · interpretation]
- 107:4/q13/p1 Genuine faith joins prayer and inward devotion to care, charity and cooperation with other people. — Biqāʿī makes denial of resurrection and recompense the root of vices and says belief produces opposite conduct. Quṭb presents religion as integrated rites and social duties that purify hearts and sustain care. Elmalılı points to prayer and zakāt's pairing and to 9:18 and 29:45; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 2:177 on lived righteousness and says piety rather than an offering's meat or blood matters. Küran Yolu cites the prophetic account of worshipping God as if one sees Him and stresses sincere service to God and people. Study Quran notes hypocrisy need not be limited to Muslims, comparing 63:1–8. [18 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI]

## 107:4/q14 Where and when was the sūra or this warning revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether ostentation and hypocritical prayer require a Medinan setting, or can describe an older Meccan ritual and a thematically unified sūra.
- 107:4/q14/p1 The whole sūra was revealed in Mecca. — Al-Jishumī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima and Qatāda; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports a majority and gives a possible seventeenth place after al-Takāthur and before al-Kāfirūn. Asad dates it early, probably after sūra 102, and rejects a Medinan latter section for lack of historical or textual evidence. Iṣlāḥī rejects Medinan dating: prayer predated Islam at the House from Abraham and Ishmael and survived in distorted form. Study Quran reports the early Meccan majority. [6 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 107:4/q14/p2 The whole sūra was revealed in Medina. — Al-Jishumī cites al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Wāqidī; Ibn ʿĀshūr also reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda for this view. Quṭb leans this way because hypocrisy and ostentation were unfamiliar in Mecca, while allowing thematic attachment of later verses; Study Quran considers it possible because religious hypocrisy could gain political advantage in Medina. [4 notes: JISHUMI(الضحاك، الواقدي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; QUTB-ZILAL+]
- 107:4/q14/p3 The opening verses are Meccan and the warning from verse 4 onward is Medinan. — Al-Jishumī cites Muqātil: Meccan through the needy, then Medinan. Al-Burhān transmits Muqātil's exception for fa-wayl through the end. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports attribution to Ibn ʿAbbās and calls the split account most plausible because the latter verses address hypocrites. Iṣlāḥī reports the argument from ostentatious prayer and rejects it; Asad also rejects it for lacking evidence. [8 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); BURHAN(مقاتل بن سليمان); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); IBNASHUR-FULL(هبة الله الضرير)+; ASAD-NOTES(some commentators)-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 107:4/q14/p4 The available accounts leave the sūra's Meccan, Medinan or split setting open. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists all three possibilities without choosing one. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 107:4/q15 Which reported occasions or people are tied to this passage's revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports identify different persons and settings; the notes do not reconcile them.
- 107:4/q15/p1 It was revealed among Meccan people pressed into Islam who later yielded to persecution and sometimes prayed with Muslims. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports that they were not firm, then treated Muslims harshly and sometimes joined prayer out of caution or uncertainty. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA]
- 107:4/q15/p2 It was revealed after Abū Sufyān struck an orphan who sought meat from his weekly slaughtered camel. — Ibn Jurayj supplies the weekly camel, the orphan's request and the blow with a stick. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن جريج)]
- 107:4/q15/p3 The three latter verses were revealed about ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Hibat Allāh ibn Salāma's account in al-Nāsikh wa-l-Mansūkh. [1 notes: IBNASHUR(هبة الله بن سلامة)]

## 107:4/q16 How many verses are counted in the sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Regional verse-counting convention.
- 107:4/q16/p1 Six verses in the majority count and the Iraqi schools. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the majority, al-Ālūsī's Iraqi count and ʿAlī al-Nūrī al-Ṣafāqusī's report of six outside the Ḥimṣī count. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 107:4/q16/p2 Seven verses in the Ḥimṣī count. — Ibn ʿĀshūr relays ʿAlī al-Nūrī al-Ṣafāqusī; al-Jishumī refers to a seven-verse sūra. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة)]

## 107:4/q17 What names are reported for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 107:4/q17/p1 It bears names drawn from its themes and opening or closing words, including al-Māʿūn, al-Dīn, Araʾayta, al-Takdhīb and al-Yatīm. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists al-Māʿūn, Araʾayta, Araʾayta alladhī, a compound al-Māʿūn wa-l-Dīn wa-Araʾayta, al-Dīn, al-Takdhīb and al-Yatīm while describing them as six reported titles. Asad says the usual title comes from the final verse. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 107:4/q18 What virtue is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · reports]
- 107:4/q18/p1 A report promises God's forgiveness to a reciter who pays zakāt. — Al-Jishumī transmits this from Ubayy as a prophetic report and gives no grading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(النبي)]

## 107:4/q19 What response to this warning is reported from the people of Hell and its hearer?  [* · reports]
- 107:4/q19/p1 Each person in Hell laments according to his offense; a hearer should ask God to forgive him. — Al-Rāzī gives status seeking, tribal zeal and prayer as examples and recommends saying, 'Woe to me unless God forgives me.' [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 107:4/q20 May a reciter stop after 'woe to the worshippers'?  [فَوَيْلٌۭ لِّلْمُصَلِّينَ · readings]
- 107:4/q20/p1 Such a stop is misleading and prohibited or abhorrent, apart from an unavoidable interruption. — Ibn al-Jazarī says it distorts the sense and allows unavoidable breathlessness; he criticizes a reciter who stopped similarly after 'the hands of Abū Lahab' to display vowel length. Al-Suyūṭī calls the unfinished phrase one of the worst stops. Teachers reject written hypothetical breaks inside a genitive construction. ## 107:4/q22 How does al-Kawthar respond to this sūra's warning?  [* · links]
- 107:4/q22/p1 Its command to pray for the Lord counters neglected and ostentatious prayer, while its generosity and sacrifice counter miserliness and withheld aid. — Al-Burhān pairs 108:2's prayer with abandonment of prayer here, and 'to your Lord' with ostentation. Al-Itqān sets four correspondences: abundant giving against miserliness, prayer against neglect, direction to God against display, and sacrificial meat as charity against refusal of small kindnesses. Elmalılı also sees al-Kawthar as condensing preceding sūras, particularly from al-Ḍuḥā, as an account of the Prophet's rank. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ELMALILI]

## 107:4/q23 Which other scriptural passages illuminate prayer's place in this warning?  [* · links]
- 107:4/q23/p1 Other verses connect prayer with charity, sincere worship and restraint from wrongdoing. — Elmalılı invokes 9:18 for prayer with zakāt and fear of God and 29:45 for prayer restraining evil. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ invokes 2:177's combined virtues and a teaching that God receives piety, not meat or blood. Küran Yolu invokes 1:5 and 3:64 for sincere intention and divine unity. Study Quran points to 19:59 for the consequences of neglected prayer; Corpus Coranicum sends readers to its discussion of ṣallā and ṣalāh at 108:2. Iṣlāḥī cites 14:37, 19:55 and 8:35 for the House's original prayer and its distortion. [11 notes: ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
