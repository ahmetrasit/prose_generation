# 34:24: 29 questions

## 34:24/q01 Whom is the Prophet told to question about provision?  [مَن يَرْزُقُكُم · referent]
- 34:24/q01/p1 The Meccan polytheists or unbelievers, especially those worshipping angels or idols. — Muqatil specifies Meccans who worship angels; Yahya, al-Wahidi, and Ibn Abbas identify polytheists or people addressed by the Prophet. Islahi identifies the addressees as Makkan idolaters who regarded their deities as intercessors, not creators or providers. [5 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 34:24/q02 How is man yarzuqukum parsed?  [مَن يَرْزُقُكُم · grammar]
- 34:24/q02/p1 Man is a complete interrogative noun, nominative subject, with yarzuqukum as its predicate. — Al-Nahhas gives the case and predication. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 34:24/q03 What provision comes from the heavens and earth?  [مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the two realms name physical sources, God's decree and lawful acquisition, or also spiritual sustenance.
- 34:24/q03/p1 Rain comes from above and vegetation, crops, fruit, or food from the earth. — Muqatil, al-Kalbi in al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Bursevi, and Study Quran name rain and plants; al-Tabari adds sun, moon, stars, livelihoods, and food for humans and livestock; al-Qurtubi adds their benefits and earthly water. Islahi adds seasons to rain and growing crops as forms or means of livelihood from heaven and earth. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(IJ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 34:24/q03/p2 Heavenly provision is God's decree for His servants; earthly provision is what He permits them to take lawfully. — Al-Mawardi offers this as an alternative to rain and vegetation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 34:24/q03/p3 Provision includes bodily food and inward knowledge and unveilings for spirits. — Bursevi says inward provision is nobler and gives enduring life, while bodily food gives passing strength; God creates and distributes both more or less widely as He wills. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 34:24/q04 Why are the heavens plural here but earth singular, unlike the singular heaven in Yunus?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
- 34:24/q04/p1 The plural points beyond the visible rain-cloud sky to higher or varied heavenly provision, while people know only one earth. — Al-Suhayli says they admit rain from clouds but not what lies above; al-Burhan contrasts Yunus 10:31, whose singular rests on that admission, with Saba, which supplies the answer and stresses varied provision. Al-Biqa'i says all sustenance depends on both realms and they know no other earth. [3 notes: BURHAN(السهيلي); BIQAI-FULL]

## 34:24/q05 What does asking who provides establish?  [مَن يَرْزُقُكُم مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · theology]
turns on: Whether the question is read solely as proof of God's unique provision or also as a challenge about provision withheld.
- 34:24/q05/p1 God alone provides, so powerless idols have no claim to worship and the sole provider deserves thanks and exclusive worship. — Al-Wahidi cites 10:31; al-Kashshaf cites 10:31-32 and 13:16; al-Qurtubi lists rain and celestial and earthly benefits idols cannot make; Ibn Kathir links sole creation and provision to sole divinity; Ibn Ashur says divinity cannot be divided; Tabataba'i notes even idolaters recognize God alone creates provision. Al-Razi sets divine benefit here beside God's sole removal of harm in 34:22, 6:17, and 10:107; he contrasts ordinary worship for benefit with the elect's worship for God's greatness. Al-Baydawi links powerless false gods to 19:87; al-Biqa'i stresses that acknowledged provision had not produced exclusive worship. Islahi says God's command of heaven and earth demonstrates one powerful ruler; the Makkan idolaters treated their deities as intercessors rather than creators or providers. [23 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IJ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 34:24/q05/p2 The question also asks who could provide if God withheld His provision. — Study Quran cites 67:21 for this possible implication. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IJ)]

## 34:24/q06 Who supplies the answer Allah, and why is it said?  [قُلِ ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Allah is their actual reply, the Prophet's supplied reply to silence, or an answer demanded of them.
- 34:24/q06/p1 The Prophet answers on their behalf because God is the only possible answer, though opponents may stay silent, hesitate, or refuse its implication. — Al-Tabari says the obvious answer was first omitted and the Prophet supplies it if they profess ignorance; al-Wahidi says they can name no other provider. Al-Kashshaf, al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, al-Alusi, and Tabataba'i say they inwardly assent but may not speak from stubbornness or fear of having to worship God alone. Ibn Atiyya calls them stunned; al-Razi compares acknowledgment in distress and heedlessness in ease, citing 30:33. Ibn Ashur treats Allah as their anticipated answer as at 10:31; Ibn al-Jawzi says they would give no other answer. Islahi says God supplies the answer because the addressees cannot deny it: they already acknowledge God as provider and regard their deities only as intercessors. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 34:24/q06/p2 The Meccans themselves answer Allah, acknowledging that God provides. — Muqatil supplies the implied words 'we are provided' and cites 10:31. Al-Mawardi says they did not deny divine provision; al-Biqa'i and Ibn Ashur say the confession is already known. [5 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 34:24/q06/p3 They are ordered to answer Allah themselves so their admission becomes proof against them. — Al-Mawardi states this as an alternative account. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 34:24/q07 Why is the command qul repeated before Allah?  [قُلْ قُلِ · rhetoric]
- 34:24/q07/p1 The repetition heightens attention to the announced answer and the importance of proclaiming it. — Ibn Ashur says the first command already marked public proclamation as important. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:24/q08 Where does the discourse divide after qul Allah?  [قُلِ ٱللَّهُ وَإِنَّآ · grammar]
- 34:24/q08/p1 Allah closes the question and answer; an understood further 'then say' introduces the challenge over guidance. — Muqatil says the speech breaks there; Sahib al-Nazm in al-Wahidi makes the omitted command explicit; Ibn al-Jawzi treats Allah as the end of a sentence and the next statement as another command. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 34:24/q09 Can the qaf in yarzuqukum assimilate into the kaf?  [يَرْزُقُكُم · grammar]
- 34:24/q09/p1 Yes; assimilation turns the qaf into a kaf. — Al-Nahhas records the phonetic possibility. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 34:24/q10 What is the form of inna?  [وَإِنَّآ · grammar]
- 34:24/q10/p1 Inna is shortened from innana by removal of one nun. — Al-Nahhas gives this derivation. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 34:24/q11 Who are we and you in the challenge?  [وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ · referent]
- 34:24/q11/p1 We are Muhammad, his companions, or monotheists; you are the Meccan idolaters or polytheists. — Muqatil, al-Tha'labi, al-Mawardi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, and al-Alusi identify these opposed camps; al-Naqqash adds that God provides for both. Al-Baydawi says the former worship the provider with intrinsic power, while the latter worship lowly inanimate things. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 34:24/q12 How is iyyakum joined to inna?  [إِيَّاكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether coordination with inna's expressed subject or its syntactic position controls the scope of the guidance predicate.
- 34:24/q12/p1 Iyyakum is coordinated with the expressed subject of inna; coordination by syntactic position would use antum. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi distinguish iyyakum from 'aw antum'; al-Nahhas says the latter would restrict guidance to the first group. Al-Qurtubi says the former favors the first group for guidance, while al-Mubarrad allows that reading either way. Al-Samin and Bursevi also coordinate it with ism inna; al-Alusi mentions the detached pronoun after elision. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 34:24/q12/p2 The guidance phrase may apply to the first group in the coordinated construction. — Abu al-Abbas permits this and likens the challenge to saying that one of two people is mistaken while letting the listener identify him. [3 notes: NAHHAS(أبو العباس); QURTUBI-FULL; NAHHAS(أبو العباس)+]

## 34:24/q13 What force does aw have in the challenge?  [أَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle keeps genuine alternative syntax, functions like wa, has an added alif, or coordinates two conditional outcomes.
- 34:24/q13/p1 Aw retains its ordinary alternative force; the alternatives are apparently open, although the speaker knows the truth. — Al-Farra's dirham example permits one or two, never three; al-Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi report him against literal equivalence to wa. Basran grammarians say known facts can be stated disjunctively without doubt; Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi preserve normal disjunction. Al-Biqa'i calls the alternatives unspecified for unbiased judgment; Ibn Ashur calls the dilemma exhaustive. Al-Burhan and al-Itqan say the listener is left to identify the truth. [20 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة,بعض نحويي الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الكسائي والأخفش والمبرد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(البصريون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]
- 34:24/q13/p2 Aw has the force of coordinating wa, pairing our guidance with your error. — Abu Ubayda and others give the direct paraphrase; Jarir's line joins Thalaba and Riyah, and al-Qurtubi cites another poet joining Riyah and Rizam. Al-Nazzam cites 28:73 and Imru al-Qays; al-Alusi reports Abu Ubayda's bread-and-bone line. Al-Farra is reported in some sources as supporting the meaning but rejects literal grammatical identity in others; al-Wahidi says exegetes privileged intended meaning over surface syntax. Al-Alusi calls this far-fetched; Ibn Atiyya says Abu Ubayda's parse lacks support. [21 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(آخر منهم,بعض نحويي الكوفة); THALABI(جرير); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة والفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭ); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 34:24/q13/p3 The alif of aw is added, leaving a coordinating sense. — Muqatil compares the added alif to 25:44; Ibn Abbas via Ata and some cited by al-Baghawi give the same explanation. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 34:24/q13/p4 Aw means that both parties are guided if they believe, or both in error if they do not. — Bursevi reports this conditional reading from unnamed interpreters. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 34:24/q14 Does the challenge require an omitted predicate?  [وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى أَوْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the surface alternatives themselves form a complete predicate, an omitted counterpart must be supplied, or ordered pairing distributes the two predicates.
- 34:24/q14/p1 No: the combined alternative is the predicate, and one group occupies one of the two states. — Abu Hayyan uses 'Zayd or Amr is in the palace or mosque' and rejects an unnecessary deletion. Al-Alusi prefers the same reading with market or home; al-Bahr agrees no ellipsis is needed here, while noting a simpler 'Zayd or Amr is standing' would need it. Al-Samin reports al-Shaykh's account but says it interprets rather than parses. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 34:24/q14/p2 One predicate is omitted and recovered from the expressed predicate, whether the first or the second is stated. — Ibn Atiyya says one predicate suffices to signal the other. Al-Samin records both directions and refers to 9:62; al-Alusi reports the same alternatives. Al-Samin insists the syntactic account needs ellipsis, while warning neither parse literally expresses the speaker's known conviction. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 34:24/q14/p3 The expression is an ordered wrap and distribution: guidance is assigned to we, error to you. — Ibn Kathir calls it wrap-and-spread. Al-Alusi cites Imru al-Qays's ordered fresh and dried hearts; Ibn Ashur says order indirectly signals the allocation. Al-Alusi's note labels this view rejected, even as it states the analysis. [5 notes: TABRISI(امرؤ القيس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:24/q15 Which side is guided and which is astray?  [لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى أَوْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The distinction between the verse's tactful surface alternative and the conclusion supplied by its prior proof.
- 34:24/q15/p1 Muhammad and the believers are guided; the idolaters or Meccan unbelievers are in manifest error. — Ikrima, Ziyad, Abu Ubayda, Muqatil, and many commentators state the allocation directly. Al-Wahidi says Abu Ishaq rejects this as a literal grammatical gloss but accepts it as the conclusion established by the proof. Qurtubi identifies associating partners with the provider as the error. Ibn Kathir says both camps cannot share guidance or error; al-Nazzam's paired syntax and ordered distribution also yield this result. [32 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه); MAJAZ; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(عكرمة وزياد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة وأبو عبيدة وزياد بن أبي مريم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة,صاحب النظم,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة والفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة وزياد بن أبي مريم); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭ)]
- 34:24/q15/p2 The wording itself leaves the assignment open for hearers to resolve, though one party is guided and the other astray. — Zajjaj rejects a direct believer-guided gloss as literal Arabic; al-Mawardi reports Mujahid's refusal to name the parties directly. Al-Tabari, Qatada, al-Tha'labi, and Study Quran describe the shared two-way frame. Al-Wahidi distinguishes surface alternative from the known intended result. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭ)]
- 34:24/q15/p3 God will decide which party is guided and which is in manifest error when the audience disputes the acknowledged truth. — Islahi reads the alternatives as leaving judgment of the parties to God when argument over God's provision has become futile. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 34:24/q16 Why frame guidance and error as a balanced alternative?  [وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى أَوْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the balanced wording chiefly offers courteous deliberation, sharp mockery, or an instance of rhetorical amplification.
- 34:24/q16/p1 It is fair, gentle, indirect debate: a certain speaker lets opponents infer the rebuke themselves, reducing resistance and prompting reflection. — Ibn Mujahid's father relates the idiom to Muslims' weakness in Mecca. Ibn Qutayba and al-Farra compare 'one of us lies'; al-Farra adds softened curses and rebukes. Al-Tabari prefers gracious refutation. Abu al-Aswad's conditional poem voices known conviction without bluntness; Jarir, Hassan, and a Banū Asad poet offer further parallels. Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur, and Kuranyolu say fairness wins a hearing, avoids anger, and leads the interlocutor through accepted premises; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin call the staged argument istidraj. Al-Biqa'i cites 41:5 for their earlier refusal to consider proof; Study Quran calls it a gentle invitation. Al-Alusi rejects calling the expression dissimulation. [65 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه); MAJAZ(قوم); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة,بعض نحويي الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; HAMASA; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو الأسود,الكسائي والأخفش والمبرد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(حسان); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حسان); SAMIN-DURR(حسان); BURHAN(السكاكي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن الجوزي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî,müfessirlerin çoğunluğu); STUDYQURAN(JJ); BURSEVI+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 34:24/q16/p2 The seemingly uncertain wording is sarcastic or mocking and makes an oblique accusation sting more. — Al-Tha'labi and others cite Abu al-Aswad's conditional poem: he knew love for the Prophet's family was right. Abu Hayyan reports that an indirect rebuke can be more cutting than a direct one. [4 notes: MAJAZ(قوم); THALABI(أبو الأسود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 34:24/q16/p3 It exemplifies rhetorical hyperbole or amplified expression. — Al-Baqillani lists this verse among hyperbole examples; his surrounding discussion distinguishes pairing, verbal appropriateness, tasrif, implicit tadmin, and emphatic forms such as rahman, ghaffar, and qadīr. Al-Rummani explains intensified adjective patterns and says a disputant can speak in apparent uncertainty to strengthen a fair argument, comparing a conditional statement about a son. [9 notes: RUMMANI; BAQILLANI]
- 34:24/q16/p4 The alternatives express prophetic disavowal when dispute over an acknowledged truth has become futile, leaving judgment to God. — Islahi says the addressees acknowledge God as provider yet dispute the truth; the Prophet's wording withdraws from futile debate and entrusts the decision to God, rather than expressing tolerance. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 34:24/q17 What do ala for guidance and fi for error picture?  [لَعَلَىٰ هُدًى فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 34:24/q17/p1 Guidance is elevation, mastery, and clear sight, like riding a horse or standing at a beacon; error is immersion, darkness, confinement, and lost direction. — Al-Kashshaf starts the horse and darkness contrast; al-Razi adds an elevated lookout. Al-Baydawi gives minaret, horse, and pit; al-Biqa'i says the guided can avoid danger while one immersed in mistaken belief cannot see out. Abu Hayyan stresses firmness and mastery and notes the error preposition as 'an' in one report. Al-Alusi calls the imagery an implicit or derived metaphor; al-Samarrai cites 2:5, 27:79, 23:54, 9:45, 7:186, 19:75, 6:39, and 11:110 and adds that truth ascends toward God while error descends. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(السكاكي); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(التفسير القيم,الكشاف)]

## 34:24/q18 Why is error called manifest while guidance is not?  [هُدًى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether manifest distinguishes degrees of deviation or the contrast between outside observers and the deluded person.
- 34:24/q18/p1 Truth has one straight way, while error has many degrees, some more obvious than others. — Al-Razi explains the asymmetry this way. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 34:24/q18/p2 Manifest error is visible to observers even though one immersed in it cannot recognize it until stepping outside it. — Al-Biqa'i says the person then sees that his conduct lacked reason. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 34:24/q19 What does mubin modify?  [مُّبِينٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the adjective attaches only to its nearest noun or semantically to both alternatives.
- 34:24/q19/p1 It most naturally modifies error alone. — Al-Alusi calls this the apparent construction; Study Quran refers readers to its note on manifest error at 36:24. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 34:24/q19/p2 It can qualify both guidance and error despite the singular adjective and pronoun after aw. — Al-Alusi allows the wider scope. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 34:24/q20 Why does guidance precede error?  [هُدًى أَوْ فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 34:24/q20/p1 Guidance describes the believers named first as we, so its mention comes first. — Al-Razi ties predicate order to the earlier order of the two camps. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 34:24/q21 What variant reading is reported for the alternatives?  [أَوْ أَوْ · readings]
- 34:24/q21/p1 Ubayy reads imma, or amma in the expanded report, for the second alternative marker. — Al-Kashshaf reports imma in place of aw; al-Alusi gives the wording 'inna aw iyyakum amma ala hudan aw fi dalalin mubin.' [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبي)]

## 34:24/q22 What did the Meccans propose before the challenge?  [* · reports]
- 34:24/q22/p1 They proposed comparing worldly livelihoods and charged Muhammad and his companions with error. — Muqatil reports their proposal and accusation. [1 notes: MUQATIL(كفار مكة)]

## 34:24/q23 Who used the two-party formulation with polytheists?  [وَإِنَّآ أَوْ إِيَّاكُمْ · reports]
- 34:24/q23/p1 Muhammad's companions told the polytheists they were not on one course and one of the two parties was guided. — Qatada's report appears in al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, and al-Durr. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 34:24/q24 What change in judgment did Khalid and Amr report?  [* · reports]
- 34:24/q24/p1 They once marvelled that anyone entered Islam and later marvelled that anyone hesitated. — Al-Biqa'i reports this from Khalid ibn al-Walid and Amr ibn al-As. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(خالد بن الوليد وعمرو بن العاص)]

## 34:24/q25 What does lawful sustenance do to spiritual life?  [يَرْزُقُكُم · inward]
- 34:24/q25/p1 Lawful food illuminates the heart and yields wisdom and spiritual growth, while unlawful food breeds heedlessness and obstructs accepted worship. — Bursevi cites hadith making lawful livelihood an obligation after basic duties, illumination and flowing wisdom after forty days of lawful eating, and an angelic call from Jerusalem saying prayers of one eating unlawful food are not accepted. Rumi's verses contrast light, knowledge, love, service, and resolve with envy and ignorance. Bursevi adds gnosis, unveilings, and spiritual stations. [5 notes: BURSEVI(جلال الدين الرومي,حديث)]

## 34:24/q26 How does this verse connect to nearby challenges and parallel verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding argument or parallel passage is foregrounded.
- 34:24/q26/p1 It extends the surrounding case against powerless idols from harm and intercession to provision, while echoing questions in Yunus and al-Rad. — Al-Razi connects 34:22 and 34:24 as sole removal of harm and sole gift of benefit, citing 6:17 and 10:107. Al-Kashshaf cites the provider question and truth-versus-error at 10:31-32 and powerless patrons at 13:16. Ibn Ashur says the argument moves from idols' inability to help in this life and next to exclusive worship, and notes the opening has a precedent in al-Rad. Asad directs readers to his note on 10:31; Study Quran cites 35:3, 10:31, and 27:64. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(IJ)]
- 34:24/q26/p2 The challenge follows two verses on Iblis and belongs to a sequence of questions addressed to disbelievers. — Study Quran lists the sequence from 34:20-30, including this verse. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 34:24/q28 Can Quranic wording bear a further possible sense beyond its primary construction?  [* · interpretation]
- 34:24/q28/p1 Yes; prophetic explanation can awaken a possible sense within the wording that accords with its aims. — Ibn Ashur says prophetic prostrations at prostration passages may exemplify this; if revealed, they more strongly establish divine intent for such possible senses. ## 34:24/q29 How does Abu al-Aswad's conditional poem parallel this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 34:24/q29/p1 The poet phrases his conviction about loving the Prophet's family or Ali as a condition, though he knows that love is guidance. — Al-Tha'labi says the poet was certain; al-Wahidi reports his verse on Ali, al-Tabrisi describes love of the Prophet's family as known guidance, and al-Alusi identifies Qushayr and Ali. The example supports the verse's apparent uncertainty without actual doubt. [6 notes: MAJAZ(قوم); THALABI(أبو الأسود); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الأسود); TABRISI(أبو الأسود الدؤلي); ALUSI-FULL]
