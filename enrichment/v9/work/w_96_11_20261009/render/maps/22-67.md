# 22:67: 23 questions

## 22:67/q01 Which communities are meant by 'every community'?  [لِّكُلِّ أُمَّةٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the expression ranges over successive nations or contemporary religious groups.
- 22:67/q01/p1 Successive communities, past through the Prophet's community, each under its own messenger or law. — Al-Wāḥidī calls them past generations; Bursevī includes past and future nations sent messengers. Rāzī allows all nations whether traces survive; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says succession rather than simultaneous distinct laws because Muhammad's mission is universal. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 22:67/q01/p2 Religious communities, including people of other religions living with Muhammad. — Bayḍāwī defines an umma by religion; Rāzī reports the narrower view that only Jews and Christians with extant revealed law are meant. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(قوم)]
- 22:67/q01/p3 An umma is a group of forty to one hundred or more. — Abū al-Malīḥ supplies the numerical gloss. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو المليح)]

## 22:67/q02 What is the appointed mansak?  [مَنسَكًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether mansak denotes law or action, sacrifice, festival, or a ritual place or time; context and 'they perform it' are cited.
- 22:67/q02/p1 A distinct religious law or path prescribed for each community. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited for law; Rāzī prefers it via ʿAṭāʾ and al-Qaffāl, citing 5:48, the breadth of worship, all pilgrimage rites, and 'they practice it'. Alūsī and Bursevī infer exclusivity from fronting 'for every community'; Kuran Yolu adopts the broad sense because context does not require restriction. Islahi includes worship forms and principles; Asad points to 5:48. [22 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 22:67/q02/p2 A sacrifice or the shedding of sacrificial blood. — Muqātil describes festival bloodshed and cites 6:162 for nusuk. Mujāhid specifies blood in Mecca. Ṭabarī prefers offerings at Mina on Nahr days because the pagans disputed that rite; ordinary slaughter in al-Anʿām is no rite. Al-Ḥākim grades ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥusayn's slaughter report sound. Kuran Yolu observes verse 34 encourages this reading. [26 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وغيره); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,علي بن الحسين,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحاكم عن علي بن الحسن,عكرمة,مجاهد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 22:67/q02/p3 A prescribed rite comprising pilgrimage and sacrifice. — Qatāda gives both Hajj and slaughter; Ṭabarī refers also to 22:34. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 22:67/q02/p4 A festival or feast day, sometimes one with an animal sacrifice. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Qutayba are cited; Rāzī specifies a festival at which slaughter occurs. Kuran Yolu relays Ṭabarī's festival report. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 22:67/q02/p5 A customary place of worship or ritual, especially sacrifice or pilgrimage sites. — Ṭabarī derives it from a place habitually frequented for good or evil, and Hajj sites from repeated visits; Thaʿlabī, Jishumī, Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr and Biqāʿī repeat this. Al-Farrāʾ specifies Hajj and ʿumra sites; al-Ḍaḥḥāk a slaughter place; Ṭabrisī includes Mina or elsewhere. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes it as pilgrimage sites here but sacrifice site at 22:34, opposing idol-specific shrines; Kuran Yolu agrees. [28 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(مجاهد وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك,الفراء); JISHUMI(الشاعر); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد وقتادة); RAZI-FULL(قول ثالث); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL(ابن جرير); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN]
- 22:67/q02/p6 Worship or a ritual act, encompassing acts of devotion. — Al-Ḥasan equates nusuk with worship and nāsik with worshipper; Jishumī distinguishes nusuk, nāsik and nasīka. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ṭabāṭabāʾī invoke 'they perform it' for a verbal noun; Asad and Study Quran allow an act of worship. [12 notes: THALABI(غيرهم); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; QUSHAYRI]
- 22:67/q02/p7 An appointed time for worship. — Rāzī reports the time alternative alongside place; Alūsī records it among lexical proposals. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(قول ثالث); ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:67/q02/p8 Ironically, an obstinate nation's customary path, even a wrongful one. — Alūsī reports this as consolation to the Prophet; Biqāʿī says a community may gather around its desires against God's command. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 22:67/q03 How is mansak read and justified?  [مَنسَكًا · readings]
turns on: Whether the noun follows the regular verbal or place noun pattern or an attested exceptional/dialect form.
- 22:67/q03/p1 With fatḥa on the sīn: mansak. — The other readers use it at both occurrences. Al-Fārisī prefers it because verbal and place nouns of this pattern normally take fatḥa; Ibn Khālawayh gives the same pattern argument. Ṭabarī calls it the Asad dialect. Jishumī calls it a verbal noun for shedding sacrificial blood; Ibn ʿĀshūr a place noun with both mīm and sīn open. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); FARISI-HUJJA+]
- 22:67/q03/p2 With kasra on the sīn: mansik. — Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read it thus at both occurrences; al-Nashr adds Khalaf. Ṭabarī calls it a Hijaz form; Ibn Khālawayh derives it from a slaughter site. Al-Fārisī allows an exceptional mafʿil place noun like maṭliʿ or masjid if attested and suggests al-Kisāʾī heard it; Qiraat ER calls it a canonical place or time noun. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 22:67/q04 How does 'they perform it' attach to mansak?  [هُمْ نَاسِكُوهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether a direct object pronoun excludes a place noun or a locative may be governed directly.
- 22:67/q04/p1 It is an adjective clause for mansak and its direct object favors an act or verbal noun. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī argue a place would require 'worshippers in it'; Rāzī likewise says the construction fits worship rather than a specific place or time. Samīn identifies the adjective clause; Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses the phrase for the worship sense. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 22:67/q04/p2 It can describe a place: the locative is made a direct object by extension or omission of 'in'. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects Ibn ʿAṭiyya's necessity: active participles can govern locatives directly, illustrated by an anonymous poem's mashrab and travel over two farsakhs. Samīn cites poems with 'a day we witnessed' and 'a drinking place I drink'; Ibn ʿĀshūr analyzes omission of the preposition. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:67/q05 What does the clause say about those performing the rite?  [هُمْ نَاسِكُوهُ · interpretation]
- 22:67/q05/p1 Each designated community follows or performs its own assigned law or rite. — Bursevī and Alūsī take the pronoun and adjective clause to reinforce exclusive allocation, comparing separate garments; their sequence is Torah until Jesus, Gospel until Muhammad, then Qurʾān until Judgment. Nasafī simply says they perform the sacrifice; Bayḍāwī says they perform the worship. [10 notes: MUQATIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 22:67/q05/p2 Their performance occurs by God's decree and should not trouble the Prophet. — Ibn Kathīr compares the diversity to 2:148 and says God's will governs their rites. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 22:67/q06 What does the verse teach about changing religious laws?  [لِّكُلِّ أُمَّةٍۢ مَنسَكًا · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's shared object is the full law of successive communities or specifically a recurring sacrificial rite.
- 22:67/q06/p1 God assigns different laws to successive communities; earlier laws yield to Muhammad's universal law. — Rāzī says living adherents must follow Muhammad. Qurṭubī cites differences across eras. Bursevī and Alūsī detail the Torah–Gospel–Qurʾān sequence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says successors' greater readiness brought better rites. Islahi says change reforms invented practices and tests sincerity; Kuran Yolu says rulings suit circumstances and development. Ibn Kathīr also states that God appointed a rite for every community. [21 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 22:67/q06/p2 God prescribed sacrifice for every community, establishing it as a genuine ancient rite. — Nasafī uses this to refute those denying sacrifice is part of divine law; Alūsī reports that its antiquity answers pagan objection. Shaykh al-Islām objects that restricting the dispute to sacrifices would falsely treat carrion and pagan practices as divinely assigned rites. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)-]

## 22:67/q07 Who are the disputants?  [فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ · referent]
turns on: The reported occasion points to pagans and slaughter, while the law interpretation points to scriptural communities.
- 22:67/q07/p1 Polytheists, including Quraysh or Khuzāʿa, challenge Muhammad and the Muslims. — Muqātil names Khuzāʿa and others; Ibn al-Jawzī names Quraysh and Khuzāʿa. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers polytheists from the continuing pronouns to 22:72 and the mixed dating; he finds People of the Book less likely. Several reports supply named leaders. [20 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(يزيد بن ورقاء وجماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(المشركون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 22:67/q07/p2 People of earlier revealed scriptures argue from inherited Torah or Gospel. — Bursevī and Alūsī say their laws have been abrogated; Islahi says Jews and Christians sided with Quraysh and fueled debate. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:67/q07/p3 Adherents of other religions generally dispute with the Prophet. — Bayḍāwī describes them as ignorant or obstinate; Tabatabaʾī describes unbelievers denying the Prophet's authority. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 22:67/q08 What is the 'matter' under dispute?  [فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ · referent]
turns on: The topic varies with whether mansak is read as sacrifice, law, or pilgrimage place.
- 22:67/q08/p1 Slaughter, sacrificial animals, and whether carrion may be eaten. — Ṭabarī and Mujāhid connect it to sacrifice and the pagans' meat argument; Qatāda says not to avoid one's slaughtered meat. Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes this follow the sacrifice-site reading. Alūsī reports a view grounding the response in sacrifice's antiquity. [24 notes: MUQATIL(بديل بن ورقاء وبشر بن سفيان ويزيد بن الحلبس); TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); NASAFI-FULL(المشركون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler)]
- 22:67/q08/p2 Religion, the Prophet's message, or rites and law generally. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes religion or scripture follow the law reading. Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān allow religion; Bayḍāwī includes religious rites. Bursevī and Alūsī identify the inherited laws and their abrogation. Biqāʿī includes satanic objections to abrogation. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 22:67/q08/p3 Monotheism, including whether pagan pilgrimage proves pagan religion true. — Ibn ʿĀshūr infers monotheism from the surrounding evidence and distinguishes valid pilgrimage from later idol rites. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 22:67/q08/p4 The proper pilgrimage standing place: Quraysh at Muzdalifah versus others at Arafat. — Mālik identifies the contest; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites a Muwaṭṭaʾ occasion report. [2 notes: MALIK; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:67/q09 What occasion of revelation is reported?  [فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ · reports]
turns on: The reports identify different concrete disputes, meat or pilgrimage standing.
- 22:67/q09/p1 Khuzāʿī or Quraysh leaders asked why Muslims eat animals they slaughter but reject carrion, which they said God killed. — Muqātil names Badīl ibn Warqāʾ, Bishr ibn Sufyān, and Yazīd ibn al-Ḥalbas of Banū al-Ḥārith; Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, and Baghawī give Yazīd ibn al-Khunays/Khunays; Jishumī gives Yazīd ibn Waraqah. Some accounts name only Khuzāʿa or polytheists. The names vary; the challenge is shared. [18 notes: MUQATIL(بديل بن ورقاء وبشر بن سفيان ويزيد بن الحلبس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(يزيد بن ورقاء وجماعة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(المشركون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(rivayetler)]
- 22:67/q09/p2 A pilgrimage dispute between Quraysh at Qazah in Muzdalifah and other Arabs at Arafat occasioned it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr quotes the Muwaṭṭaʾ from people of knowledge; Mālik connects the standing-place dispute to this verse. Ibn al-Zubayr defines valid standing areas, except ʿUrana and Muḥassir valleys. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; MALIK(عبد الله بن الزبير)]

## 22:67/q10 When was the injunction or its occasion?  [فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ · sciences]
turns on: The dating depends on the inferred relation to fighting or the pilgrimage occasion.
- 22:67/q10/p1 The instruction preceded fighting. — Al-Zajjāj infers this from the following instruction about what to say if they argue; Wāḥidī relays him. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)]
- 22:67/q10/p2 On the pilgrimage-occasion account, it came after Hajj became obligatory and before polytheists were barred, in year nine. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Bājī following Rabīʿa, but says the stronger dating differs without specifying it in this note. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الباجي)]

## 22:67/q11 What does the verb of disputation mean lexically?  [يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ordinary mutual disputation or a special 'prevail in dispute' derivation is intended.
- 22:67/q11/p1 Mutual contention or argument. — Jishumī gives the direct gloss; Bursevī derives contention from pulling something from its place. Al-Zajjāj illustrates opposing and prevailing in nāzaʿanī fa-nazaʿtuhu and ʿāzzanī fa-ʿazaztuhu. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ZAJJAJ; SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج)]
- 22:67/q11/p2 Contending until one party prevails over the other. — Al-Zajjāj's derivation is reported by Samīn and Alūsī. Alūsī notes grammarians reject a claimed guttural exception in the verb pattern and cites Sībawayh that the prevailing pattern is not universal. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج,سيبويه)]

## 22:67/q12 How is 'let them not dispute with you' to be understood?  [فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: The reciprocal verb can aim at opponents, indirectly restrain the Prophet, or urge firmness; syntax and the next verse are cited.
- 22:67/q12/p1 A real prohibition to the opponents: they must not contest Muhammad's prescribed religion or rites. — Qurṭubī reasons laws differ by era. Alūsī prefers the apparent address to opponents; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening supplies its argument despite unbelievers denying his authority. Bayḍāwī says opponents are ignorant or obstinate. [13 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators (R)); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 22:67/q12/p2 An indirect command to the Prophet to avoid engaging in mutual argument. — Al-Zajjāj cites the following 'if they argue' and says a reciprocal verb can forbid either participant, unlike 'strike'; Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr repeat it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya relates a threat reading but prefers belittling the disputants; Qurṭubī compares not fighting back. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزجاج); STUDYQURAN(commentators); ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Baghawi)]
- 22:67/q12/p3 Do not heed or be drawn into their contentious debate; continue steadfast in religion. — Yahyā says polytheists must not turn him away; Ibn Jinnī says the common wording orders certainty and ignoring false claims. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers belittling those too insignificant for debate; Bayḍāwī says debate suits truth seekers, not obstinate people. Ibn Kathīr says their disputes must neither trouble nor divert him. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari and Baghawi); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 22:67/q12/p4 A rhetorical prohibition aimed at opponents through the Prophet: give them no occasion to dispute. — Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes the idiom 'do not let me find you doing this'; Abū Ḥayyān compares 'do not let me see you here'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the implied statement that they lack power to dispute. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 22:67/q12/p5 Keep debating resolutely so opponents do not defeat you in argument. — Alūsī draws this force from al-Zajjāj's proposed 'prevail' derivation, while reporting the grammatical objection to that derivation. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج,سيبويه)]

## 22:67/q13 What variant readings occur in the prohibition?  [فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ · readings]
turns on: The readings differ in the verb root, mutual contention versus pulling away, and reported nūn weight.
- 22:67/q13/p1 The common reading is fa-lā yunāziʿannaka, with a light nūn also reported. — Ibn Jinnī glosses the common reading as standing firm and ignoring falsehood, citing the 'do not let me see you' idiom and al-Nābigha. Samīn and Alūsī report light nūn; Abū Ḥayyān says this reading tells the Prophet to resist enticement. [4 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:67/q13/p2 Abū Majliz reads fa-lā yanziʿannaka, 'let them not pull you away' from your religion. — Al-Zajjāj also records the form; Ibn Jinnī relates it to selection by belief and knowledge, with 30:60 and 19:69, rather than physical pulling. Alūsī adds Lāḥiq ibn Ḥumayd and kasra on zāy; Qurṭubī describes being snatched or overpowered. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مجلز); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مجلز); SAMIN-DURR(أبو مجلز); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مجلز ولاحق بن حميد,ابن جني); RAZI-FULL]

## 22:67/q14 Why does the verse tell Muhammad to call to his Lord?  [وَٱدْعُ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: The call's content and audience follow whether the dispute is about law generally or slaughter particularly.
- 22:67/q14/p1 He must continue preaching after ending futile dispute, leaving opponents' response and judgment to God. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted object generalizes the invitation, which still benefits all despite stubbornness; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the instruction returns him to his task. Biqāʿī says to act without calculating outcomes; Alūsī says to leave ignorant disputants; Ibn Kathīr compares 28:87. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:67/q14/p2 The invitation is an assertion of God's oneness, faith, worship and His prescribed religion. — Muqātil says knowledge of God through tawḥīd; Yahyā says sincere worship; Wāḥidī combines faith and the prescribed law, quoting Ibn ʿAbbās on pure monotheism's ordinances. Others specify belief, religion, or worship. Yahyā transmits Abū Hurayra's report that the Prophet was commanded to fight until people professed God's oneness, after which blood and property are protected except by right and reckoning belongs to God. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل بن سليمان); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; DURR-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 22:67/q14/p3 The call includes all nations or people, with the disputants especially in view. — Rāzī explicitly excludes no nation; Bursevī likewise invites everyone; Alūsī says the disputants may be the immediate audience but all people are included; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the omitted object. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 22:67/q14/p4 The call is specifically to obey God's prescribed sacrificial slaughter. — Ṭabarī connects the invitation and straight path to the sacrificial rite assigned to Muhammad's community. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 22:67/q15 What is hudā in 'straight guidance'?  [هُدًۭى مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether hudā denotes the religion, its demonstrating proofs, or guidance generally.
- 22:67/q15/p1 Islam or the upright religion and law. — Yahyā says Islam keeps a person upright to Paradise; Ibn ʿAbbās says no created religion is more upright or beloved to God. Muqātil, Wāḥidī, Damghānī, Qurṭubī, Ṭabrisī and others identify the religion; Damghānī cites 2:120 and 3:73. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:67/q15/p2 The proofs that clearly establish the religion. — Rāzī prefers the proofs over the religion itself: the straight path demonstrates the faith, and opponents shift from examining proofs to custom and dispute. Alūsī allows the path to represent the proofs. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 22:67/q15/p3 Guidance or right direction, in its general sense. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān give the simple gloss. Ibn Qutayba equates guidance with clarification; Ibn al-Anbārī gives divine enabling as the root sense. Ibn al-Jawzī's lexical entry also records gifts, bridal procession, livestock sent to sanctuary, and supportive walking. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو بكر بن الأنباري,ابن قتيبة,بعض أهل العلم); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 22:67/q16 What is conveyed by the image of straight guidance?  [إِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ هُدًۭى مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 22:67/q16/p1 The Prophet stands firmly on a direct path to truth, supporting the command to continue calling. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ʿalā a metaphor for stable footing, guidance a road, and straightness swift arrival; Islam leads most easily to spiritual perfection. Alūsī analyzes the path and 'upon' as layered metaphors and a causal explanatory resumption. Biqāʿī grounds certainty in God's knowledge and power. [11 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 22:67/q16/p2 Straightness properly belongs to the path to which guidance leads, so its attribution to guidance is a rational metaphor. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī supplies this rhetorical analysis. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 22:67/q17 What is the legal or prophetic practice associated with sacrifice here?  [مَنسَكًا هُمْ نَاسِكُوهُ · reports]
- 22:67/q17/p1 The Prophet sacrificed two rams, one for his monotheist community and one for himself and his family. — Abū Rāfiʿ says he fed the poor, ate with his household, and Banū Hāshim abstained from offering their own sacrifices for two years because their costs were covered. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 22:67/q18 How does 22:67 connect with the earlier 'for every community' at 22:34 and the surrounding passage?  [لِّكُلِّ أُمَّةٍۢ · links]
turns on: Explanations differ on whether the opening resumes rites, marks distance, or belongs to the sūra's disputation theme.
- 22:67/q18/p1 It resumes the earlier discussion of prescribed rites and sacrifice, with intervening material separating the statements. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 22:34, 22:37–38 and says the relation is clear enough to omit a conjunction. Al-Kashf links prior pilgrims' benefits and remembrance in 22:28; al-Ṭībī links honoring rites in 22:32. Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī refer back to 22:34. Rāzī says the passage turns from mercy despite ingratitude to the favor of prescribed duties. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي,الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL]
- 22:67/q18/p2 The absent conjunction marks semantic distance from the earlier sacrificial context. — Rāzī says this independent sentence is not attached to what precedes. Al-Zamakhsharī contrasts 22:34, which had a conjunction because nearby verses related to sacrifice, with this more distant context; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn relay it. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]
- 22:67/q18/p3 The connection is complete because prior evidence establishes that disputing God's ruling is unbelief. — Biqāʿī cites 2:106 and reads the opening without conjunction accordingly. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:67/q18/p4 The verse belongs to a wider consolation and disputation sequence. — Alūsī reports a link back to unbelievers' doubt in 22:55 and divine judgment in 22:69; al-Kashf objects that 22:60 does not reinforce consolation; Alūsī rejects that link as far-fetched and instead points to the sūra's recurring argument with obstinate opponents beginning at 22:3. The sūra's close exposes shirk and urges worship and striving. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 22:67/q19 What other Qurʾānic passages explain the diversity of rites?  [لِّكُلِّ أُمَّةٍۢ مَنسَكًا · links]
turns on: The disagreement concerns what the parallel diversity passages imply for other communities.
- 22:67/q19/p1 The verse parallels 2:148 and 5:48 on distinct directions, rules or paths and competing in good deeds. — Ibn Kathīr cites 2:148 for divinely allotted practice; Rāzī and Asad point to 5:48. Islahi cites both, but rejects an interpretation that they teach tolerance toward Jews and Christians and instead reads aversion to their refusal of reform. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 22:67/q19/p2 The cited passages express tolerance toward Jews and Christians. — Islahi reports this view only to reject it. [0 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people)-]

## 22:67/q20 Does change in God's laws imply a change in divine knowledge?  [مَنسَكًا فِى ٱلْأَمْرِ · theology]
turns on: Whether a changed ruling entails a changed divine judgment or expresses foreknown legislation.
- 22:67/q20/p1 No: abrogation displays God's foreknowledge and full power to legislate. — Biqāʿī rejects the objection that changing law entails badāʾ; he says satanic doubts should not unsettle the Prophet. He associates obedience with faith and opposition with unbelief. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:67/q20/p2 The objectors claim abrogation would entail badāʾ or changed divine knowledge. — Biqāʿī reports this as an objection and rejects it. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL-]

## 22:67/q21 What inward counsel does the verse offer when disputes arise?  [فَلَا يُنَٰزِعُنَّكَ وَٱدْعُ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · inward]
- 22:67/q21/p1 Trust God, fulfill the duty given, leave disputants to Him, and do not lean on stratagems or peers. — Qushayrī says witness turns of destiny, stay within permitted spiritual means, and leave outsiders to their own reckoning, citing 17:14. His surrounding meditation says God merits praise whether giving or withholding, created signs bring certainty, and His presence replaces losses. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 22:67/q21/p2 Each spiritual station has fitting practitioners, as each community has its assigned rite and path. — Qushayrī names worship, striving, gnosis and love; Bursevī applies distinct obedience to each bodily member and prayer for steady service. Bursevī's couplet depicts God's table shared by friend and foe. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]

## 22:67/q22 What broad lesson does the verse give about religious practice and opposition?  [* · interpretation]
- 22:67/q22/p1 God assigns rites and the Prophet should continue the call despite stubborn objections. — Alūsī says the verse consoles and honors him; Islahi says traditionalists cling to inherited practices and leave their fate to God; Kuran Yolu says the issue is the message's divine source and right guidance. Study Quran notes the sūra's treatment of religions and Muslim history. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 22:67/q22/p2 All creatures receive divine bounty, and legitimate use of it is grace. — Qushayrī's adjacent comments describe permitted benefits, inspired shipbuilding, stable earth and sky, stars, spiritual death and life, and divine sufficiency; Bursevī portrays earth as a shared table and disciplined use of bodily faculties. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]

## 22:67/q23 How is the sūra's revelation dated or characterized?  [* · sciences]
