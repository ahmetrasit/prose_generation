# 34:25: 14 questions

## 34:25/q01 Why is قُل repeated here?  [قُل · rhetoric]
- 34:25/q01/p1 The renewed command gives this argument independent emphasis. — Ibn Ashur says the renewed 'say' makes this statement stand independently of the preceding argument. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:25/q02 How does this sentence stand in relation to the preceding speech?  [قُل لَّا تُسْـَٔلُونَ عَمَّآ أَجْرَمْنَا وَلَا نُسْـَٔلُ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ · grammar]
- 34:25/q02/p1 It is set off for separate consideration, as an initial resumption that also interrupts the argument. — Ibn Ashur gives both grammatical possibilities and says the separation lets listeners consider the clause on its own. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:25/q03 What does the mutual denial of being questioned mean?  [لَّا تُسْـَٔلُونَ عَمَّآ أَجْرَمْنَا وَلَا نُسْـَٔلُ عَمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'being questioned' is read as final reckoning, religious disengagement, or mutual demands for an account in the debate.
- 34:25/q03/p1 Each side bears its own deeds and recompense; neither bears the other's guilt. — Ibn Abbas, reported by al-Wahidi, specifies the opponents' disbelief and rejection. Tabrisi says each person is judged for his own acts and infers that no one is accountable for another's sin. Ibn al-Jawzi specifies the believers' sins versus the opponents' disbelief and denial. Qurtubi says both sides are recompensed. Ibn Ashur explains 'questioning' as reckoning for good and evil, citing Kaʿb ibn Zuhayr's poetic use of 'you are questioned.' Bursevi says each answers for his own work. Tabatabai stresses that especially evil consequences do not pass to others. Study Quran cites 6:164, 17:15, 35:18, 39:7, and 53:38 for the no-burden principle and reports IK's parallel with 10:41. Yahya ibn Sallam compares 11:35 and 10:41. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(كعب بن زهير); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 34:25/q03/p2 It marks reciprocal religious separation and disavowal between the groups. — Yahya ibn Sallam invokes 11:35 and 10:41. Al-Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas's analogy with 109:6, and al-Wahidi al-Wajiz repeats that analogy. Ibn al-Jawzi calls the wording a disavowal. Ibn Atiyya calls it a truce and disengagement. Ibn Kathir says acceptance of the invitation to exclusive worship brings fellowship, while rejection leaves mutual disavowal; he cites 10:41 and Surat al-Kafirun. Qurtubi compares 109:6. Islahi says the separation follows the addressees' stubborn denial of obvious facts, so further discussion has no purpose and each side continues on its own way; he explains it as acquittal and aversion, citing the parallel disavowals in 42:15 and 109:6. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 34:25/q03/p3 Neither side demands an account from the other, leaving each to follow the path made clear to it. — Al-Biqaʿi frames this as an answer to opponents who openly claim guidance for themselves and error for Muslims; believers neither answer to the opponents for their alleged errors nor interrogate the opponents' deeds. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 34:25/q04 What does أَجْرَمْنَا mean?  [أَجْرَمْنَا · meaning]
- 34:25/q04/p1 We committed or acquired a culpable deed, a sin. — Qurtubi gives 'acquired' or 'committed.' Bursevi says jurm, with damma, means sin, originally 'cutting'; citing al-Mufradat, he says it extended to acquiring anything disliked. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 34:25/q05 What are the speakers' 'crimes'?  [أَجْرَمْنَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the first-person 'crime' concedes believers' actual minor faults or adopts the opponents' allegation about their faith.
- 34:25/q05/p1 The believers' real but small lapses and slips, distinct from the Messenger. — Abu Hayyan reports a possible reading that assigns the crimes to the believers rather than the Messenger, since believers may commit minor sins. Bursevi says no believer avoids small lapses. Alusi likewise describes their slips as inevitable. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 34:25/q05/p2 The alleged guilt imputed to the believers by their polytheist opponents. — Abu Hayyan says the opponents call the believers' deed a crime although it earns reward. Ibn Ashur says the believers adopt the polytheists' taunt over rejection of idols and cites 83:32; the past tense treats that guilt as established only according to the opponents' claim. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:25/q06 Why call the speakers' acts 'crimes' but the audience's acts 'deeds'?  [أَجْرَمْنَا تَعْمَلُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the source explains the contrast chiefly as equitable, conciliatory naming or as an indirect allusion; the explanations can coexist.
- 34:25/q06/p1 The asymmetric wording is fair and gentle, conceding fault on the speakers' side while describing the opponents' acts neutrally. — Nasafi says it pairs censured wrongdoing on the speakers' side with praised, commanded deeds on the audience's side. Abu Hayyan says it adopts the opponents' description despite the believers' reward. Bursevi and Alusi say even small believer lapses are called crimes while the opponents' graver disbelief is called deeds. Ibn Ashur says it adopts an allegation and leaves opponents room to reflect. Tabatabai calls it good manners in debate. Samarrai says it avoids calling the other party liars, wins hearts, and keeps preaching open, in the invitation context of 34:22, 34:24, and 34:26. Al-Biqaʿi says this is fairer even if opponents insist they are guided and Muslims astray. [10 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 34:25/q06/p2 The tactful wording conveys the intended contrast indirectly. — Burhan contrasts it with blunt wording that would call the audience's acts crimes. Alusi reports that some identify an indirect allusion and says this does not compromise the formulation's fairness. [2 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 34:25/q07 Why is أَجْرَمْنَا past tense while تَعْمَلُونَ is present tense?  [أَجْرَمْنَا تَعْمَلُونَ · rhetoric]
- 34:25/q07/p1 The past treats the speakers' alleged guilt as established; the present leaves the audience's actions open. — Alusi says the past presents the believers' crimes as established, whereas the present does not presuppose the opponents' actions. Ibn Ashur specifies that establishment is according to the polytheists' claim; the present anticipates future deeds and hints that they may come to faith. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 34:25/q08 What are the audience's 'deeds'?  [تَعْمَلُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'what you do' identifies the audience's present disbelief or deliberately leaves their future actions unspecified.
- 34:25/q08/p1 Their disbelief, denial, and other grave sins. — Ibn Abbas, as reported by al-Wahidi, specifies disbelief and rejection; Ibn al-Jawzi says disbelief and denial. Abu Hayyan contrasts their disbelief and major sins with believers' minor sins; Bursevi and Alusi make the same contrast. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 34:25/q08/p2 Their actions are left broad, including future work and the possibility of faith. — Ibn Ashur says the present tense anticipates future action and hints at conversion; the broad description gives the audience space to reflect. Nasafi describes the audience's side in the wording as praised, commanded deeds. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 34:25/q09 What does this manner of addressing opponents seek to achieve?  [* · interpretation]
- 34:25/q09/p1 It invites the opponents toward faith through a fair, unprovocative exchange. — Qurtubi says the speaker seeks the audience's good rather than protection from any harm their disbelief might cause him. Ibn Kathir says the groups invite each other to God and exclusive worship, with acceptance bringing fellowship. Al-Biqaʿi lets each follow what becomes clear. Ibn Ashur says conciliation leaves room for thought instead of distracting debate. Samarrai says the Messenger avoids accusing them of lying and seeks to open hearts and keep preaching open. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 34:25/q09/p2 It closes discussion with stubborn deniers and expresses acquittal and aversion, leaving each side to continue its own way. — Islahi says the addressees stubbornly deny obvious facts, making further discussion fruitless; he reads declarations of separation in this context as acquittal and aversion, not tolerance or accommodation. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 34:25/q10 How does this verse connect to the surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the source explains the verse through the preceding challenge or its movement toward the following judgment; both links can hold.
- 34:25/q10/p1 It continues the challenge to the disbelievers and explains how to proceed if they reject the preceding argument. — Tabrisi says Muhammad gives this reply if unbelievers reject the argument. Al-Biqaʿi frames it as an answer to opponents claiming guidance. Ibn Ashur says it explains the preceding argument by moving toward God's true judgment. Samarrai places it in the invitation context of 34:22, 34:24, and 34:26, also mentioning 11:35. Study Quran describes 34:20-30 as challenges following two verses on Iblis. [5 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 34:25/q10/p2 It prepares for the next verse's gathering, divine judgment, and separation of the two sides. — Tabatabai says the differing good and evil deeds require gathering and separation for their due reward or punishment. Ibn Ashur says God will judge truly when He gathers them on the Resurrection Day they deny. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 34:25/q11 Which other verses are invoked to explain its reciprocal separation and responsibility?  [* · links]
- 34:25/q11/p1 Passages on each side's own deeds and religion, and on the nontransfer of guilt, illuminate the statement. — Yahya ibn Sallam cites 11:35 and 10:41 for innocence of the other's wrongdoing. Al-Wahidi, al-Wahidi al-Wajiz, Qurtubi, and Ibn Kathir compare the separation in 109:6; Ibn Kathir also cites 10:41 and 109:1-5. Study Quran reports IK's 10:41 parallel and cites 6:164, 17:15, 35:18, 39:7, and 53:38 for the rule that none bears another's burden. Islahi also cites 42:15, where each party keeps its deeds, and invokes 109:6 to explain the separation as acquittal and aversion rather than tolerance or accommodation. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 34:25/q12 Was this statement abrogated by the sword verse?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether the mutual disengagement is a temporary truce superseded by fighting, or a statement about separate responsibility unrelated to fighting.
- 34:25/q12/p1 Yes; its truce or disengagement was abrogated by the sword verse. — Ibn Atiyya applies abrogation both to the truce here and to the statement about God's later judgment. Ibn al-Jawzi reports this as the view of most exegetes. Qurtubi calls it a truce verse abrogated by the sword, while also noting another view that it preceded the sword verse. Abu Hayyan and Alusi report abrogation as a cited view. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أكثر المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 34:25/q12/p2 No; the statement remains valid and concerns something other than fighting. — Ibn al-Jawzi says the abrogation claim has no basis. Ibn Ashur says this verse does not concern participation in fighting and so is not abrogated by fighting verses. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 34:25/q13 When was the verse placed relative to the sword verse?  [* · sciences]
- 34:25/q13/p1 It came before the sword verse. — Al-Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas saying the disavowal predates the sword. Qurtubi mentions a view that it was revealed before the sword verse, alongside his statement of abrogation. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 34:25/q14 Where was Sūrat Sabaʾ revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 34:25/q14/p1 The sūra is Makkan. — Study Quran's introduction identifies Sūrat Sabaʾ as Makkan. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
