# 53:23: 38 questions

## 53:23/q01 What are the things called 'only names,' and why?  [إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّآ أَسْمَآءٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether هي points to physical idols, their three names, or attributed qualities; al-Alusi disputes the explanatory force of the names account.
- 53:23/q01/p1 The three idols are bare divine names, with no real godhood or power behind them. — Muqatil, Tabari, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqaʿi, al-Alusi, Elmalılı and others identify al-Lat, al-ʿUzza and Manat. Al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi and Ibn al-Jawzi stress that inert objects cannot harm or benefit; al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Samin and al-Alusi compare 12:40. Ibn ʿAshur notes that stones and shrines physically exist: it is their alleged divine reality that does not. Islahi adds that no beings answer to the imagined deities; Asad and Study Quran cite 12:40. Al-Najmiyya's inward account calls them imaginary forms seen as nonexistent by people of unveiling. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:23/q01/p2 The pronoun points to the three names themselves, invented to suggest that their bearers deserve worship. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Samin identify the names al-Lat, al-ʿUzza and Manat. Al-Razi prefers this antecedent. Al-Baydawi says the names imply worship-worthiness, power, or entitlement to offerings; al-Alusi reports this account but objects that the force lies in denying godhood. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 53:23/q01/p3 The invented descriptions of femininity, divine kinship, godhood, or intercession are empty labels. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Baydawi take the referent as attributed qualities. Al-Razi reports a reading that denies both female and divine status, and explains that pagans inferred daughters from feminine names after claiming angels came directly from God. Al-Tabrisi and Elmalılı stress that these descriptions have no corresponding qualities. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 53:23/q01/p4 In particular, the name al-ʿUzzā asserts might that the idol does not possess. — Al-Razi records this as a distinct explanation of the empty naming. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:23/q01/p5 In particular, calling the idols gods gives them a title they do not deserve. — Al-Razi records this explanation separately. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:23/q02 Why restate that they are names at this point?  [إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّآ أَسْمَآءٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 53:23/q02/p1 It returns from the earlier argument about daughters to the root denial of any divinity. — Ibn ʿAshur says this guards against taking the prior rebuke as denying only daughters or female angels. Elmalılı says the feminine pronoun first evokes asserted traits, then strips them away, also rebutting the gharaniq praise. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 53:23/q03 How does 'you named them' work?  [سَمَّيْتُمُوهَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the pronominal object is the bare names or the idols bearing them; the presence or absence of bi matters to al-Razi.
- 53:23/q03/p1 It means that you made the names themselves, so only empty names are recognized. — Al-Razi says omitting bi avoids granting an independent status beyond the names, unlike the two-object naming of Maryam in 3:36. Bursevi and al-Alusi say the object pronoun returns to names; al-Alusi distinguishes making a name from the name's perspective from assigning it to a referent. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:23/q03/p2 It means you assigned or called the idols by these names. — Al-Zamakhshari says Arabic permits direct naming and naming with bi, illustrated with Zayd. Al-Razi allows either placing names for them or calling them by them. Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi use the ordinary assignment sense. Abu al-Baqa argues that asmāʾ must signify named beings, because a name itself cannot be named. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 53:23/q04 Why mention both the people and their ancestors as namers?  [سَمَّيْتُمُوهَآ أَنتُمْ وَءَابَآؤُكُم · interpretation]
- 53:23/q04/p1 The labels originated in human invention and continued through ancestral imitation, which supplies no excuse or proof. — Tabari says the fathers shared the disbelief, with 6:116; al-Razi says each later user is like an originator because the first lacked proof. Al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqaʿi and Islahi trace practice to imitation and whim; Islahi says their ancestors had themselves imitated ancestors. Bursevi notes the fathers' false whims. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:23/q05 What is the 'authority' that God did not send?  [مَّآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ بِهَا مِن سُلْطَٰنٍ · meaning]
- 53:23/q05/p1 It is proof or evidence authorizing the divine names, status, or worship claimed for the idols. — Ibn Qutayba and Ibn ʿAshur gloss sultan as proof; Ibn ʿAshur treats failure to send it as absence of evidence. Muqatil specifies a book with proof, citing 37:156 and 30:35; al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Muqatil deny any scripture for calling them gods. Al-Wahidi reports an alternative emphasis on worship. Al-Razi and Islahi also demand rational warrant, invoking harm from attributing deficiency to God and evidence from nature, intellect, signs, or prophetic disclosure. Ibn ʿAshur says unseen beings need revelation or rational evidence and compares angels, jinn and devils. Al-Tabari cites 7:71; Abu Hayyan cites 11:18 and 7:71. Islahi says God could have explicitly endorsed their daughters and intercession but did not. [28 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 53:23/q06 Why say proof was 'sent down,' and how do the particles work?  [مَّآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ بِهَا مِن سُلْطَٰنٍ · rhetoric]
- 53:23/q06/p1 'Sent down' depicts revelation or God-given reasoning as guidance coming from above; the particles tighten the denial. — Ibn ʿAshur likens absent proof to a road without a marker; revelation comes from the upper world and reflective thought is created by God, with 48:4, 22:71 and 12:40. He calls anzala metaphorical for revelatory and rational evidence and says the extra min after negation denies any proof. Al-Razi takes bi as comitative, like departing with family and possessions. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:23/q07 Is there a variant vocalization of 'sultan'?  [سُلْطَٰنٍ · readings]
- 53:23/q07/p1 ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar reads it with ḍamma on the lām. — Ibn ʿAtiyya reports this vocalization. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر)]

## 53:23/q08 How is 'they follow' read?  [إِن يَتَّبِعُونَ · readings]
turns on: The choice of yāʾ for report about absentees or tāʾ for direct address, and how that address connects to the preceding discourse.
- 53:23/q08/p1 The common reading uses third-person plural yattabiʿūna, 'they follow.' — Al-Thaʿlabi, al-Jishumi, Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan call it the common or majority reading; Ibn ʿAtiyya names Abu ʿAmr, ʿAsim, Nafiʿ and al-Aʿmash. Al-Samin says the shift belittles the idolaters. [6 notes: THALABI(العامة); JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو وعاصم ونافع والأعمش والجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 53:23/q08/p2 A second-person plural reading, tattabiʿūna, directly addresses the people. — Al-Thaʿlabi names ʿIsa; Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin and al-Alusi give overlapping lists including Ibn Masʿud, Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn Waththab, Talha, al-Aʿmash, Ayyub and Ibn al-Sumayqaʿ. Al-Jishumi links it to earlier 'have you seen' and 'is it yours'; al-Razi says it matches 'you and your fathers,' or may address the fathers or be an aside to the Prophet. Al-Samin calls it contextually apt; Qiraat-Er calls it mutawatir. [12 notes: THALABI(عيسى); JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى وابن مسعود وابن عباس وابن وثاب وطلحة والأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله وابن عباس وطلحة وعيسى بن عمر وابن وثاب); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); QIRAAT-ER]

## 53:23/q09 Why change from direct address to third person?  [إِن يَتَّبِعُونَ · rhetoric]
- 53:23/q09/p1 The speaker turns away from the idolaters and reports their shameful conduct to others. — Al-Baghawi notes a return to declarative report. Al-Samin says this belittles them; Bursevi and al-Alusi say they are unworthy of address. Elmalılı and Islahi connect the shift to their intellectual decline despite guidance; Ibn ʿAshur calls it an explanatory resumption and identifies the same people as the subject. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:23/q10 Who follows conjecture, and what does the present tense convey?  [يَتَّبِعُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is restricted to the earlier addressees or generalized to disbelievers.
- 53:23/q10/p1 The addressed idolaters persist in following conjecture and desire. — Ibn ʿAshur identifies the third-person subject with the earlier addressees and says the imperfect implies past practice continuing into the future. Al-Razi compares the projected present in 18:18. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 53:23/q10/p2 The subject can be disbelievers generally. — Al-Razi gives this as a further possibility. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:23/q11 What is the conjecture they follow?  [ٱلظَّنَّ · interpretation]
- 53:23/q11/p1 It is unsupported belief that their idols are gods, intercessors, or a true religion. — Muqatil specifies the named goddesses; al-Nahhas adds the daughters claim. Al-Wahidi, al-Biqaʿi, al-Zamakhshari and al-Alusi mention supposed intercession and true religion; Ibn Kathir notes inherited approval and leadership interests. Ibn ʿAtiyya and Abu Hayyan define zann as inclination between rival beliefs without evidence. Al-Razi contrasts it with knowledge and adduces uncertain water and unverified suspicion. Ibn ʿAshur says it normally lacks certainty, can mean certainty at 2:46, and often means error at 6:116; he quotes the Prophet warning that suspicion is the falsest speech without grading it. Islahi cites Ṭarfah's line separating knowledge and conjecture, and 69:20 as a future discussion. Study Quran points to 53:28; Kuranyolu stresses unsound methods especially about faith. Islahi says innate guidance, intellect, or revelation could have supplied knowledge but did not support these claims. [32 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ṭarfah ibn al-‘Abd); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 53:23/q12 How are conjecture and desire related here?  [ٱلظَّنَّ وَمَا تَهْوَى ٱلْأَنفُسُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the conjunction distinguishes belief from worship, marks a downward progression, or shows desire causing assent to conjecture.
- 53:23/q12/p1 Conjecture governs belief while desire governs worship. — Al-Razi reports this division; belief needs certainty and worship should resist caprice. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:23/q12/p2 The pairing descends from conjecture to something even less grounded. — Al-Razi reports that passions pursue what offers no expected good. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:23/q12/p3 Desire drives the conjecture because the supposed religion suits established preferences. — Ibn ʿAshur reads the coordination causally. Islahi says the mythology promises nearness, paradise, worldly goods and exemption from sacrifice and accountability; Satan made the daughters and intercession story alluring. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:23/q13 How is 'what the souls desire' construed?  [وَمَا تَهْوَى ٱلْأَنفُسُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā refers to desired things or turns the following verb into a verbal noun.
- 53:23/q13/p1 Mā is relative: they follow whatever their souls desire. — Al-Razi distinguishes the desired object from desire itself. Al-Samin reports this possibility; al-Alusi says an antecedent is omitted and the article can be anaphoric, possessive or generic. Ibn ʿAshur reads the article as possessive, 'their own souls'; al-Tabatabaʾi also allows this parse. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 53:23/q13/p2 Mā is a verbal noun particle: they follow their souls' desiring. — Al-Razi says the verbal expression conveys continuing, changing appetite and newly devised worship, including replacing broken idols; al-Samin and al-Tabatabaʾi also report the parse. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]

## 53:23/q14 What desire or 'souls' does the verse mean?  [وَمَا تَهْوَى ٱلْأَنفُسُ · interpretation]
- 53:23/q14/p1 It is personal appetite and whim, especially the appealing practice of idol worship and hoped-for intercession. — Dāmghānī glosses hawā as lust, comparing 20:16, 28:50, 7:176, 25:43 and 46:23, and nufūs as hearts, comparing 12:53. Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi say Satan made the practice attractive. Ibn ʿAtiyya and Abu Hayyan distinguish pleasure from reason's good outcome; al-Biqaʿi says deficient souls pull downward and intellect upward. Al-Razi says plural souls are distributive. Bursevi calls them evil-commanding selves. Islahi calls the desired thing a polytheistic innovation that evades reality's demands; Kuranyolu sees subjective religious motives. Study Quran compares capricious wandering at 45:23 and the evil-commanding soul at 12:53, and notes the literal rendering 'what their souls fancy.' [22 notes: DAMGHANI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; NASAFI-FULL]

## 53:23/q15 Are all conjecture and desire condemned?  [ٱلظَّنَّ وَمَا تَهْوَى ٱلْأَنفُسُ · theology]
turns on: The availability of evidence and whether attraction is grounded in it; the notes distinguish creed, law, social judgment, and sound love.
- 53:23/q15/p1 Foundational religious claims require evidence and certainty; probable judgment may be proper elsewhere. — Al-Jishumi says conjecture in religion's foundations is unsound. Al-Razi cites 'I am as My servant thinks of Me' as a seeming counterexample, then allows preponderant probability when certainty cannot be reached; here guidance made certainty available. Al-Qushayri and al-Biqaʿi distinguish good opinion of God and uncertainty over one's fate from speculation about God, attributes and rulings. Ibn ʿAshur distinguishes foundational belief from branches of law, and praiseworthy from blameworthy social conjecture, citing 49:12 and a saying urging vigilance. [7 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:23/q15/p2 Love supported by evidence is not the appetite blamed here. — Ibn ʿAshur says the restriction to appetite alone carries the blame; evidence can deepen love of something true. He cites prophetic praise of mutual love for God and attachment to mosques, and the Prophet's delight in prayer. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 53:23/q16 How does the clause about guidance connect syntactically?  [وَلَقَدْ جَآءَهُم مِّن رَّبِّهِمُ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause describes their condition while following conjecture or interrupts the sentence before 53:24.
- 53:23/q16/p1 It is circumstantial: they follow conjecture even while guidance has come. — Al-Samin, al-Alusi, Ibn ʿAshur and al-Tabatabaʾi use this parse; the conflict intensifies blame and wonder. Bursevi says either parse exposes conjecture's falsity. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI]
- 53:23/q16/p2 It is parenthetical, interrupting the rebuke before the following question. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Samin describe an insertion; al-Samin says 53:24 resumes with disjunctive am understood as bal plus interrogative hamza. He reports al-Zamakhshari that the question denies people's possession of whatever they wish. Al-Alusi and Bursevi also allow the parenthesis. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 53:23/q17 What guidance came from their Lord?  [ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Which concrete medium of divine guidance is meant: Book, Messenger, both, or also miracles.
- 53:23/q17/p1 The Qur'an or revealed Book. — Muqatil, Dāmghānī, al-Burhan and al-Itqan give this gloss; Dāmghānī compares 18:55 and 17:94. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi list the Book as an alternative to the Messenger; al-Alusi says the Qur'an can be called the guide or emphatically 'guidance.' [6 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; BURHAN; ITQAN; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:23/q17/p2 The Messenger, Muhammad, and his religious law or clarification. — Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz, Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Qurtubi identify Muhammad or his teaching; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi offer the Messenger as an alternative. Al-Qurtubi specifies the clarification that idols are not gods. [5 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:23/q17/p3 The Book and Messenger together, clarifying exclusive worship of God. — Al-Tabari, al-Nahhas, al-Wahidi al-Basit, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Nasafi, al-Biqaʿi, Bursevi, Elmalılı and Islahi name both. Al-Tabari says God alone is worshipped, the One who prevails; al-Nahhas calls idol worship associationism and unbelief. Islahi includes Sunnah and links the surah's opening praise of revelation. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:23/q17/p4 The Qur'an, messengers, or miracles can each be meant. — Al-Razi gives this range of possible referents. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:23/q18 What is stressed by the wording about guidance from 'their Lord'?  [وَلَقَدْ جَآءَهُم مِّن رَّبِّهِمُ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
- 53:23/q18/p1 Clear, complete guidance makes their persistence especially astonishing and disobedient. — Al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Abu Hayyan express wonder that evidence did not stop worship. Ibn ʿAshur says the oath-like lām insists that convincing guidance truly came, 'their Lord' stresses rebellion against their Creator, and the definite article marks completeness and clarity. Al-Biqaʿi says reason required acceptance even through an enemy. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:23/q19 Did those addressed benefit from the guidance?  [وَلَقَدْ جَآءَهُم مِّن رَّبِّهِمُ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 53:23/q19/p1 No; they turned from clear guidance and continued worship, conjecture and wishful hope. — Ibn Zayd says they did not benefit. Al-Qushayri, al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi and Elmalılı say they abandoned revelation despite proof; Islahi compares following conjecture in daylight to leaping into death. Al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi call their persistence astonishing. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:23/q20 What does the passage teach about evidence, inquiry, and human responsibility?  [* · theology]
- 53:23/q20/p1 Religious claims must be argued from acquired knowledge, and God has removed excuse by giving guidance and capacity. — Al-Jishumi says the argument with unbelievers validates religious debate and shows knowledge is acquired. He says God furnished proof, explanation and empowerment; people turned away themselves, opposing the coercion claim that God created their unbelief and withheld capacity. Kuranyolu says claims about God's attributes and lordship need direct contact or an authorized messenger; pagans claimed neither and rational scrutiny reveals their failure. [7 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:23/q21 What limit does the verse place on naming God or divine beings?  [سَمَّيْتُمُوهَآ أَنتُمْ وَءَابَآؤُكُم مَّآ أَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ بِهَا مِن سُلْطَٰنٍ · theology]
- 53:23/q21/p1 People cannot invent divine names whose implications diminish God without revealed warrant. — Al-Razi answers the daughter claim: even if angels were considered directly from God, an offspring title implies deficiency. He says such names require revelation, citing 39:56; conventional naming is acceptable only when its benefit outweighs harm, and neither revelation nor reason supports these labels. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:23/q22 What is the verse's inward warning about conjectural knowledge?  [إِن يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا ٱلظَّنَّ · inward]
- 53:23/q22/p1 Spiritual seekers can mistake conjecture and mental images for direct knowledge of God. — Al-Sulami and Bursevi report Junayd seeing seventy gnostics perish through imagining they knew God; Bursevi adds 39:67 and the limits of human capacity. Al-Wasiti says even Abu Yazid died in this delusion. Al-Baqlī warns that conceited claimants misread egoistic and satanic images as unveilings or divine lights: the Real exceeds shapes, and true vision belongs to saints. Al-Qushayri urges certainty and verification until knowledge is immediate; Shibli says realizing the Real's reality is that reality. [7 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد,الشبلي); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(البقلي,الجنيد,الواسطي)]

## 53:23/q23 What do the inward readings say the idolaters fail to see?  [إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّآ أَسْمَآءٌۭ وَمَا تَهْوَى ٱلْأَنفُسُ · inward]
- 53:23/q23/p1 Their sensual selves and illusion-clouded minds mistake imagined forms for realities and miss divine manifestation in creation. — Al-Najmiyya says created things have no independent power to create, destroy, benefit or harm; beings manifest divine names and beauty or majesty, while the Perfect Human manifests these fully and is God's vicegerent. It says idolaters descend into animal desire and bodily darkness rather than perceiving spiritual realities, comparing 53:28. Bursevi says they squander innate readiness for the Prophet, Qur'an, miracles and signs by adopting animal and elemental traits. [5 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 53:23/q24 How does an inward account relate earthly guidance to pre-eternal light?  [وَلَقَدْ جَآءَهُم مِّن رَّبِّهِمُ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · inward]
- 53:23/q24/p1 Guidance comes in earthly life, while a person lacking God's pre-eternal light remains without light. — Bursevi makes this distinction in his inward reading. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:23/q25 Where was al-ʿUzzā's shrine, and who founded it?  [أَسْمَآءٌۭ · reports]
- 53:23/q25/p1 It stood at Hirād in Nakhlah al-Shāmiyya, opposite al-Ghumayr on the Iraq road, and Zālim ibn Asʿad first built it. — Al-Kalbi gives the location as nine miles beyond Dhat ʿIrq toward al-Bustan. [1 notes: KALBI-ASNAM]

## 53:23/q26 What were al-ʿUzzā's shrine building and slaughter site called?  [أَسْمَآءٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The notes identify different parts of the sanctuary, the shrine house and its sacrificial place.
- 53:23/q26/p1 The shrine building was a bas, meaning a house. — Al-Kalbi gives this gloss. [1 notes: KALBI-ASNAM]
- 53:23/q26/p2 Its slaughtering site was al-Ghabghab, where meat from offerings was shared. — Al-Kalbi says a Hudhali satire describes taking a bride there and dividing an offering; Nuhayka al-Fazari mentions pilgrims' camels bound for Mina and al-Ghabghab. [3 notes: KALBI-ASNAM]

## 53:23/q27 How did Quraysh and other Arabs honor al-ʿUzzā?  [أَسْمَآءٌۭ · reports]
- 53:23/q27/p1 They used its name, visited, sacrificed, made offerings and oaths, and treated its precinct as sacred. — Al-Kalbi says Quraysh counted it their greatest idol, named people ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā and heard a voice at its shrine. They guarded Siqām to imitate the Kaaba's sanctuary; Abu Jundub's poem attests its protected branches. Dirham ibn Zayd swears by al-ʿUzzā and God at Sarif. Al-Kalbi also reports the Prophet recalling a tawny camel offering while following his people's religion. [5 notes: KALBI-ASNAM(رسول الله)]

## 53:23/q28 What invocation of the goddesses is reported from pagan worship?  [أَسْمَآءٌۭ · reports]
- 53:23/q28/p1 Quraysh called al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā and Manāt exalted gharānīq, God's daughters and hoped-for intercessors while circumambulating. — Al-Kalbi reports the formula; Elmalılı cites Yaqut that it was pagan speech before Muhammad and describes a poetic comparison of white stones with high-flying birds. [3 notes: KALBI-ASNAM; ELMALILI]

## 53:23/q29 What does gharānīq mean in the reported formula?  [أَسْمَآءٌۭ · meaning]
- 53:23/q29/p1 It denotes high-flying white water birds, also figuratively a beautiful young woman or soft wind-stirred hair. — Elmalılı gives gurnûk/gurneyk, a long-necked bird larger than a goose; the alternate senses vary by context. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 53:23/q30 Was the gharānīq praise part of the Prophet's recitation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The report's transmission and whether pagan wording was mistakenly attributed to the Prophet.
- 53:23/q30/p1 Some exegetes report that Satan inserted it, and God subsequently removed it and confirmed revelation. — Elmalılı attributes this report to some exegetes including al-Tabari and al-Zamakhshari, linking it to 22:52. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Taberî ve Zemahşerî gibi bazı müfessirler); ELMALILI(Beyhâkī,Ebû Hayyân)-]
- 53:23/q30/p2 The insertion report is fabricated or unestablished. — Elmalılı reports Abu Hayyan's statement that Ibn Ishaq called it a heretical fabrication and wrote against it. He reports al-Bayhaqi's objections: criticized transmitters and absence from sound collections. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Ebû Hayyân); ELMALILI(Beyhâkī)+]

## 53:23/q31 If the disputed gharānīq words were spoken, how would this verse frame them?  [إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّآ أَسْمَآءٌۭ · interpretation]
- 53:23/q31/p1 They would be a quotation of pagan speech immediately refuted as empty names. — Elmalılı says placement after the rebuke would form a coherent quotation, whereas inserting the formula directly after Manat disrupts the cadence. He says 53:26 already rejects hoped-for intercession even without a quotation. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 53:23/q32 What are the reported pause forms of Manāt?  [أَسْمَآءٌۭ · readings]
turns on: Whether reports about spelling were mistaken for recitation, and whether the pause is with hāʾ or tāʾ.
- 53:23/q32/p1 Every reader pauses with hāʾ in accordance with the written form. — Ibn al-Jazari rejects al-Ahwazi's all-tāʾ report and states this universal hāʾ reading. [1 notes: NASHR(الأهوازي)+]
- 53:23/q32/p2 Al-Kisāʾī alone pauses with hāʾ; other readers use tāʾ. — Ibn al-Jazari rejects the Iraqi report as a confusion of Nusayr's spelling report with recitation. [0 notes: NASHR-]
- 53:23/q32/p3 All readers pause with tāʾ, and codices spell Manāt with wāw. — Al-Ahwazi is reported by Ibn al-Jazari, who rejects this and asserts hāʾ according to the writing. [0 notes: NASHR(الأهوازي)-]

## 53:23/q33 How does this verse connect to the surrounding argument about intercession?  [* · links]
- 53:23/q33/p1 It rejects imagined intercession and prepares the warning that wishes cannot override God's judgment. — Al-Wahidi says the following passage rebukes hoped-for idol intercession; Elmalılı points to 53:26 on angels' ineffectual intercession. Asad calls goddess and angel mediation wishful thinking and says analogous patterns persist in saint veneration and deification. Islahi says reward, punishment and God's control of heaven and earth are real, so wishes cannot save them; their imagined deities cannot avert Judgment. Al-Samin reports al-Zamakhshari that the next question denies obtaining whatever one wishes. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]

## 53:23/q34 What role does the verse play in the sūrah's larger argument?  [* · links]
- 53:23/q34/p1 It rebuts pagan worship after establishing revelation, and exposes ignorance and conjecture behind claims of intercession. — Al-Tabatabaʾi says this section follows proof of prophecy based on monotheism and leads toward resurrection and recompense. Study Quran calls it the second section's reproach of Quraysh. Islahi says self-claimed deities cannot avert Judgment. Kuranyolu says Quraysh's idols illustrate historic deviations that enslave people to created things. [4 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:23/q35 How do the preceding male and female argument and its wording lead into this verse?  [* · links]
- 53:23/q35/p1 The prior charge of an unjust division leads to a broader rejection of the names themselves. — Ibn ʿAshur says even imagined injustice reveals belief and conduct. He analyzes ḍīzā as perhaps noun or verbal noun, though adjective is likelier; Sībawayh says an initial-kasra fuʿlā adjective is unattested. He records the majority's quiescent yāʾ and Ibn Kathir's hamza, reflecting the original verb. He also offers an analysis where afaraʾaytum is suspended from taking an object by the following negative inna, with the intervening male/female clause parenthetical. Kuranyolu cites 16:58-59 and 43:17-19 for the contradiction of preferring sons while assigning despised daughters to God. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(سيبويه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:23/q36 What kind of concision does 'they follow only conjecture and desire' exemplify?  [إِن يَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا ٱلظَّنَّ وَمَا تَهْوَى ٱلْأَنفُسُ · rhetoric]
- 53:23/q36/p1 It exemplifies qasr, concise expression that compresses meaning without an omitted word. — Al-Rummani contrasts qasr with recoverable deletion and says judging where it works is subtler. He compares other compact Qur'anic statements about retribution as life, every shout, unattainable booty, desire, self-aggression and evil plotting. Bint al-Shati reports his distinction and his separate account that ellipsis can let the reader supply possibilities, citing 42:51 and 39:73. [5 notes: RUMMANI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الرماني)]

## 53:23/q37 What illustrations warn against taking impressive names or appearances for realities?  [أَسْمَآءٌۭ · interpretation]
- 53:23/q37/p1 A favorable name or human appearance can conceal the opposite reality. — Bursevi quotes Jami's satire of calling a ruined thing prosperity and mistaking a swelling for fat; another poem likens apparently human people to predators or wolves in clothing. [2 notes: BURSEVI(مولى الجامي)]

## 53:23/q38 How is 'guidance came to them' read?  [وَلَقَدْ جَآءَهُم · readings]
turns on: Whether the transmitted direct address is plural or singular.
- 53:23/q38/p1 Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās read 'guidance came to you' in the plural. — Ibn ʿAtiyya reports this direct-address variant. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عباس)]
- 53:23/q38/p2 Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reports their reading as singular 'guidance came to you.' — Ibn ʿAtiyya attributes the alternative singular wording to al-Ḍaḥḥāk's report of Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وابن عباس)]
