# 96:11/q05 Why is أَرَءَيْتَ repeated, and how do the questions build? [أَرَءَيْتَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the repeated challenges form separate escalating censures, a single ironic concession, or a sequence of lost benefits.
- 96:11/q05/p1 Repetition reinforces the challenge and sharpens wonder at the contrast between guided worshipper and denier.
  reasons: Al-Baghawī calls it reinforcement; al-Bayḍāwī an echo of the earlier question. Al-Māwardī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya see omitted completions gathered by the final threat; the latter says one concise conclusion suffices. Al-Biqāʿī says the listener asks about the forbider's state, not his identity. Al-ʿĀshūr says each detached hypothetical renews wonder about the same person.

# 96:11/q06 What does the particle إِن contribute? [إِن · grammar]
- 96:11/q06/p1 It frames the servant's guidance as a hypothetical from the forbider's viewpoint.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says in is used when the condition is uncertain, matching the stance of the man trying to stop the servant; it heightens the wonder of forbidding even in that case.

# 96:11/q07 Who is the unspoken subject of كَانَ? [كَانَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun returns to the nearby forbider or shifts back to the praying servant.
- 96:11/q07/p1 The praying servant, Muhammad, is the one on guidance.
