# 96:11/q14 Does this warning extend beyond Abu Jahl and his encounter? [* · law]
turns on: Whether the historical setting restricts the warning's reach.
- 96:11/q14/p1 It applies to anyone who obstructs prayer or other worship, including distraction by action.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī says the specific revelation about Abu Jahl does not narrow the warning; diversion from worship is practical prohibition. He distinguishes objections to prayer at usurped places or disliked times, which concern an accompanying attribute. Kuran Yolu favors a universal reading about tyrants obstructing religion's goodness, truth and justice and calls it unjust to stop guided worship or responsible religious duty. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ likewise allows general application despite a particular occasion.
  holders (6 notes): ALUSI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI; JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+
  notes: ALUSI:96:11/r33 ALUSI:96:11/r36 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:9/r2 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:9/r3 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3 JISHUMI:v10p7481/r11
- 96:11/q14/p2 The clause can describe any worshipper who calls others to right, not just Muhammad.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī expressly allows both the Prophet and every such worshipper as the one commanding piety; Kuran Yolu's universal application likewise concerns guided people who urge reverence and responsibility.
  holders (2 notes): JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7481/r11 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:96:9/r3

# 96:11/q15 How does this passage relate to the sūra's opening and its revelation? [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is chronological placement after a later occasion or the sūra's continuous portrayal of human conduct.
- 96:11/q15/p1 The later passage was placed after the opening although revealed afterward in the Abu Jahl setting.
  reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's first solution: the remaining sūra came later about Abu Jahl and the Prophet was directed to place it at the beginning. She thinks the passage followed public preaching and prayer.
  holders (2 notes): BINTSHATI
  notes: BINTSHATI:v2p27/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3
- 96:11/q15/p2 Generic 'man' links the opening blessings to human transgression and the need for guidance.
  reasons: Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Rāzī's second solution: humanity, despite creation and blessings, forgets its return, transgresses and forbids worship when it ought to follow guidance and enjoin piety. Al-Ālūsī says the passage from 96:9 recounts the consequences of human tyranny.
  holders (3 notes): BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); ALUSI
  notes: BINTSHATI:v2p27/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p27/r3 ALUSI:96:13/r1
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; BINTSHATI = ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419

# 96:11/q16 What warning is attached to commanding right while acting against it? [* · reports]
- 96:11/q16/p1 A hypocritical preacher is punished in Hell despite enjoining right.
  reasons: Usāma says he counsels an official privately to avoid public controversy and refuses to praise a ruler after hearing the Prophet warn of a man whose entrails spill as he circles like a donkey at a mill: he commanded right and forbade wrong but did the reverse.
  holders (2 notes): BUKHARI(أسامة، رسول الله)
  notes: BUKHARI:3267/r1 BUKHARI:3267/r2
sources: BUKHARI = BUKHARI

# 96:11/q20 What is omitted in the later أَرَءَيْتَ clauses? [أَرَءَيْتَ · grammar]

- 96:11/q20/p1 Their objects are omitted separately, and the pronouns can point to different participants.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān says the later questions require omitted objects, whose antecedents may be the forbider or, in the second instance, the servant. He rules out tanāzuʿ because whole clauses cannot be pronominalized.
