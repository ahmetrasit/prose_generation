# 87:1/q06 What does rabbika evoke? [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 87:1/q06/p1 The Lord is the creator, nurturer, benefactor, and governor; 'your' gives the Prophet a special personal address.
  reasons: Biqai says the Lord formed and nurtured the Prophet in noble qualities; Qutb emphasizes tender care; Ibn Ashur says Rabb rather than Allah calls up creation and governance and the possessive honors the Prophet.

# 87:1/q07 Who is commanded to glorify? [سَبِّحِ رَبِّكَ · referent]
- 87:1/q07/p1 The Prophet is directly addressed, with a charge that guides believers too.
  reasons: Ibn Ashur identifies the addressee; Biqai says the perfected Messenger understands how to glorify fully; Islahi reads the address as strengthening the Prophet amid his preaching fears.

# 87:1/q08 What does al-aʿla qualify? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · grammar]
turns on: Case and the attachment of the following relative clause 'who created' in 87:2.
- 87:1/q08/p1 It qualifies rabb, 'Lord,' in the genitive.
  reasons: Nahhas prefers this. Kashshaf, Alusi and Study Quran call it the more natural or majority parse. Abu Hayyan and Samin note that if 'who created' in 87:2 qualifies rabb, an intervening modifier of ism would separate them. Corpus Coranicum compares the Hebrew epithet following a divine title.
