# 87:1/q13 What glorification belongs in prostration? [سَبِّحِ ٱلْأَعْلَى · law]
- 87:1/q13/p1 Use 'Subhana rabbiya al-aʿla' in prostration; the Great Lord's glorification belongs in bowing.
  reasons: Reports from Uqba and others assign 87:1 to sujud after assigning 56:74, 56:96, or 69:52 to ruku. Abu Hurayra's report ties revelation to the Companions' question and specifies an odd number of repetitions; Study Quran describes three repetitions in each posture. Ibn Ashur says bodily humility joins spoken praise. Kashshaf and Baydawi also report 'O God, to You I bow/prostrate' supplications.

# 87:1/q14 What occasion is reported for this verse's revelation? [* · reports]
turns on: The reported triggering event: a human question about sujud or an angel's failed ascent and praise.
- 87:1/q14/p1 The Companions asked what to say in prostration; the verse descended and the Prophet supplied its glorification.
  reasons: Abu Hurayra's version adds an odd number of repetitions; Durr and its full account also transmit the question.
  holders (3 notes): MUJAHID(أبو هريرة); DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)
  notes: MUJAHID:v2p751/r1 DURR:87:1/r20 DURR-FULL:v8p481/r1
- 87:1/q14/p2 An angel failed to reach the Throne despite multiplying wings, then glorified the Most High, prompting the verse and prostration instruction.
  reasons: Qurtubi transmits the tale from Ja'far b. Muhammad through his forebears and gives no grading; the angel is called Hizqiyal in one account.

# 87:1/q16 Where and when was this sura revealed? [* · sciences]
turns on: Early transmission and thematic dating versus a later inference from possible references to Eid worship; chronology counts al-Fatiha differently.
- 87:1/q16/p1 The whole sura is Meccan, among the early revelations.
  reasons: Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr and Aisha are reported; al-Bara says it was taught in Medina before the Prophet arrived. Ibn Atiyya calls the contrary Dahhak report weak; Alusi and Ibn Ashur say Eid and alms references do not prove Medinan origin. Ibn Ashur cites verse 6 and early themes.
