# 87:1/q25 How is the verse understood inwardly? [* · inward]
turns on: Self-emptying through the Highest Name versus inward witnessing and remembrance.
- 87:1/q25/p1 The Highest Name encompasses God's essence and attributes; the soul's self-emptying lets divine perfections appear.
  reasons: Qashani calls this the special praise of annihilation and says each thing glorifies through a particular divine name; Bursevi transmits this and adds each creature's own mode of praise.
  holders (3 notes): QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)
  notes: QASHANI:87:1-4/r1 BURSEVI:v10p313/r1 BURSEVI:v10p313/r2
- 87:1/q25/p2 Glorification joins outward denial of rivals to inward remembrance and witnessing only God.
  reasons: Tustari locates this in prayer; Qushayri describes knowing the names, swimming inwardly in exaltedness, and intimacy that resists disobedience.
  holders (4 notes): TUSTARI; QUSHAYRI
  notes: TUSTARI:87:1/r1 QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r1 QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r2 QUSHAYRI:v3p414/r3
sources: BURSEVI = İsmāʿīl Ḥaqqī Bursevī, d. 1127; QASHANI = ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Qāshānī, d. 736; QUSHAYRI = al-Qushayrī, d. 465; TUSTARI = Sahl al-Tustarī, d. 283

# 87:1/q26 What earlier language or scripture does this wording evoke? [سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ ٱلْأَعْلَى · meaning]
turns on: The proposed immediate language of influence, Hebrew or Syriac, and the biblical parallel.
- 87:1/q26/p1 Tasbih's religious praise sense may have entered Arabic from Hebrew.
  reasons: Ibn Ashur proposes Hebrew borrowing and connects it to 2:30.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR
  notes: IBNASHUR:87:1/r22
- 87:1/q26/p2 The praise sense likely reflects Syriac shabbeh, itself translating Hebrew hillel and echoing psalmic hallelu-yah.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum says Arabic 'swim/glide' cannot explain the devotional sense, compares Syriac prayer terminology, psalmic praise of God's name, and the epithet Hebrew elyon; the closest biblical construction is YHWH elyon.
  holders (4 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1:anmerkung/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1:anmerkung/r2 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1:anmerkung/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:87:1:anmerkung/r5
sources: CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments); IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393

# 87:1/q27 What broader devotional or human purpose does the opening serve? [* · interpretation]
turns on: The spiritual effect of remembrance and the relation between voiced, enacted, and inward glorification.
- 87:1/q27/p1 Recognition and remembrance of the Most High give people a patron, exemplar, path, courage, and perseverance.
  reasons: Biqai says God, Muhammad and Islam meet these needs, citing the Prophet's confession, and that mercy enables obedience. Islahi says sound knowledge of God strengthens faith and conduct; remembrance comforts the heart in 13:28 and protects against Satan.

# 87:1/q28 How is this opening classified among sura opening types? [* · rhetoric]
- 87:1/q28/p1 It combines a command with praise, within the broader kinds of Quranic openings.
  reasons: Abu Shama calls 87:1 both command and praise and surveys openings by command, condition, vocative, oath, prayer, question and report; his causal class is exemplified by 106:1.
  holders (3 notes): BURHAN(أبو شامة، الشيخ شهاب الدين أبو شامة المقدسى)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p180#4/r1 BURHAN:v1p180#4/r2 BURHAN:v1p180#4/r3
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794

# 87:1/q29 What syntactic construction is elsewhere compared with 87:1? [سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ · grammar]
- 87:1/q29/p1 A verb of glorification may substitute for a verb of remembrance while a prepositional phrase precedes the subject.
  reasons: Burhan parses yusabbih as a substitute for yudhkar, with lahu fiha a fronted predicate and rijal the subject, citing 87:1 by analogy.
  holders (1 notes): BURHAN
  notes: BURHAN:v3p199/r8
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794

# 87:1/q30 What morphological pattern does al-aʿla illustrate? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · grammar]
- 87:1/q30/p1 It is an afʿal form with the definite article.
  reasons: Burhan lists three af'al patterns: construct, definite with al, and unmarked followed by min introducing the compared item, citing 63:8.
  holders (1 notes): BURHAN
  notes: BURHAN:v4p170/r6
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794

# 87:1/q31 How does sanctifying the name relate to knowing God's essence? [ٱسْمَ · theology]
- 87:1/q31/p1 Pure names express the essence's transcendence and are the human route to knowing it in this life.
  reasons: Elmalili says minds know God through names, attributes or effects, while direct manifestation belongs to the afterlife; he relates this to 87:14-17.
  holders (1 notes): ELMALILI
