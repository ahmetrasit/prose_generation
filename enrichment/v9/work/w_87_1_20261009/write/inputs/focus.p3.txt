# 87:1/q09 What kind of highness does al-aʿla express? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · theology]
turns on: Whether highness denotes spatial direction, absolute excellence and power, ungraspable greatness, or a non-comparative adjective.
- 87:1/q09/p1 Supremacy in power, sovereignty, perfection, and rank, without spatial location.
  reasons: Jishumi cites al-Farazdaq's poetry for honor and strength; Tabrisi compares al-akbar. Razi argues a finite or infinite corporeal deity is impossible and creation in 87:2 suits power, not distance. Ibn Ashur reads the superlative as absolute superiority and figurative enduring perfection, citing 79:24 and al-Ghazali's sensory versus intellectual ranks. Qutb evokes limitless horizons.

# 87:1/q10 Why is the Lord called al-aʿla here? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanatory role of the epithet and the extent to which verse-end cadence explains its form.
- 87:1/q10/p1 His superlative rank makes transcendence fitting, and the following acts establish His power and care.
  reasons: Biqai says rank is absolute. Elmalili says the title explains why sanctification is owed and verses 2-5 establish it; Ibn Ashur selects creation, guidance and provision as fitting attributes, and links divine highness to the Quran's dignity and the Prophet's reception.
  holders (7 notes): BIQAI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI
  notes: BIQAI:87:1/r11 ELMALILI:87:1#2/r23 IBNASHUR:87:1/r36 IBNASHUR:87:1/r37 IBNASHUR:87:1/r43 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p275/r1 TABATABAI:87:1-19/r2
- 87:1/q10/p2 The ending contributes to the sura's cadence, but its choice carries meaning beyond rhyme.
  reasons: Burhan compares the extra endings in al-A'la and al-'Alaq; Bint al-Shati rejects making cadence the sole reason for al-a'la rather than al-'ula, comparing 92:20. Qutb hears the opening's extended rhythm.
  holders (3 notes): BURHAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: BURHAN:v1p65/r2 BINTSHATI-IJAZ:p271/r2 QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r5
sources: BINTSHATI-IJAZ = ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419; BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; ELMALILI = Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361; IBNASHUR = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; IBNASHUR-FULL = Ibn ʿĀshūr, d. 1393; QUTB-ZILAL = Sayyid Quṭb, d. 1386; TABATABAI = Muḥammad Ḥusayn al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, d. 1402

# 87:1/q11 What variant wording or pronunciation is reported for this opening? [سَبِّحِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ ٱلْأَعْلَى · readings]
turns on: Whether transmitted subhana phrases are variant text or recitation responses, and how the final vowel is pronounced.
- 87:1/q11/p1 The opening is recited as 'Subhana rabbiya al-aʿla,' sometimes followed by 'who created and proportioned.'
  reasons: Ibn Umar, Ubayy, Ali, Abu Musa, Ibn al-Zubayr, Malik b. Dinar and others are named across sources. Reports vary between an actual alternate reading and a response after the canonical verse. Qurtubi denies the phrase is Quranic text; Corpus Coranicum retains the common imperative because similar imperatives open early Meccan passages.
