# 87:1/q20 What extra wording was rejected when reciting this sura? [* · reports]
- 87:1/q20/p1 An improvised line about pregnancy and birth was rebuked as an addition to the Quran.
  reasons: Razi recounts Aisha rebuking a Bedouin reciter and companions; Durr reports the Prophet telling Hadrami b. Amir that the Quranic text was sufficient and healing. The reports differ in speaker and response.

# 87:1/q22 Why does this sura open with an imperative of glorification? [سَبِّحِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The formal series of praise openings, the pedagogic order of transcendence, and the sura's consoling purpose.
- 87:1/q22/p1 It completes a Quranic sequence of tasbih openings: verbal noun, past, present, and imperative.
  reasons: The author of al-Ajaib and Biqai identify 17:1, 57:1, 59:1, 61:1, 62:1 and 64:1 as the earlier forms; Burhan describes fourteen praise openings and Abu Shama calls 87:1 both praise and command. Samarrai surveys verbal forms and object/preposition patterns.
  holders (9 notes): BURHAN(أبو شامة، ابن الفركاح، صاحب العجائب); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT
  notes: BURHAN:v1p164#2/r1 BURHAN:v1p164#2/r2 BURHAN:v1p164#2/r3 BURHAN:v1p180#4/r2 BURHAN:v1p180#4/r3 BURHAN:v1p245/r5 BIQAI:87:1/r13 BIQAI-FULL:v8p393#2/r10 SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT:p41/r4
- 87:1/q22/p2 The command starts with removing impossible notions before affirming attributes and teaching prophethood.
  reasons: Biqai says clearing away defects precedes positive virtues, and theology proceeds through essence, attributes, self-subsistence and acts before prophethood; this counters imitation and denial and leads to obedience.
  holders (3 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:1/r6 BIQAI-FULL:v8p393#2/r4 BIQAI-FULL:v8p393#2/r5
- 87:1/q22/p3 It grants the Prophet permission to praise God and prepares reassurance about revelation.
  reasons: Qutb calls the permission itself a favor; Ibn Ashur says the imperative foreshadows verse 6 and fits the sura's purpose of steadying the recipient of revelation.
  holders (3 notes): QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR

# 87:1/q23 Why are the following descriptions coordinated or separated in praise? [ٱلْأَعْلَى · grammar]
- 87:1/q23/p1 Distinct attributes may be independently coordinated; varied syntax expands and strengthens praise.
  reasons: Burhan says conjunction signals independent adjectives, citing Zamakhshari and Abu al-Baqa; separation is stronger when meanings differ widely. It compares 68:10 and 57:3. Abu Hayyan and Samin discuss whether 87:2's 'who created' can attach across al-a'la.

# 87:1/q24 What theological claims are drawn from complete glorification? [* · theology]
turns on: Razi contrasts sovereign ownership with Mu'tazilite moral goodness and human welfare.
- 87:1/q24/p1 God's essence and eternal attributes are free of composition and deficiency; His acts are those of the absolute owner.
  reasons: Razi says God is neither substance nor accident, His attributes are not originated or finite, and no creature may object to His decisions. Duties bring God no benefit and rest on sovereignty.
  holders (6 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL
  notes: RAZI:87:1/r14 RAZI:87:1/r15 RAZI:87:1/r16 RAZI:87:1/r20 RAZI-FULL:v31p124/r3 RAZI-FULL:v31p124/r5
- 87:1/q24/p2 All God's acts are good, and He neither does nor approves evil; duties serve people's interests.
  reasons: Razi attributes these formulations to the Mu'tazilites, contrasting his ownership account.
  holders (4 notes): RAZI(المعتزلة); RAZI-FULL
  notes: RAZI:87:1/r17 RAZI:87:1/r20 RAZI-FULL:v31p124/r4 RAZI-FU
