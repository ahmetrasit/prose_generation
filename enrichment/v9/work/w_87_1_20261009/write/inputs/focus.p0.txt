# 87:1/q01 What does sabbih command? [سَبِّحِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether tasbih names declaration, a particular utterance, prayer, vocal praise, or a comprehensive devotional act.
- 87:1/q01/p1 Declare God free of all defects, false claims, rivals, and unworthy attributes and acts.
  reasons: Muqatil makes this denial of shirk and affirmation of God's oneness. Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, Jishumi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur and others include evil, change, deficiency, idolatry, anthropomorphism, coercion, and claims of polytheists; Jishumi adds wisdom and goodness of divine acts. Elmalili includes spoken, inward, and practical worship; Qutb insists on inward awareness, not rote repetition. Ibn Ashur says the religious sense may be borrowed from Hebrew; Corpus Coranicum instead adduces Syriac shabbeh and the Hebrew psalmic imperative.

# 87:1/q02 What does ism mean here? [ٱسْمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the direct object is the divine word, an honorific stand-in for God, the act of naming, an attribute, or a broader sign.
- 87:1/q02/p1 God's name itself is the object of sanctification.
  reasons: Tabari, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Elmalili emphasize the direct object; the name must not be given to idols, misinterpreted, or used irreverently. Ibn Ashur says spoken reports and sacred attributes are applied to names designating God; purely inward contemplation concerns God directly. Elmalili says the clearest sense is revealed linguistic names and attributes, though signs can indicate Him more broadly.

  notes: TAB:87:1/r13 TAB-FULL:v24p309#2/r9 WAHIDI-BASIT:v23p429#2/r3 RAZI:87:1/r1 RAZI-FULL:v31p123#2/r1 ABUHAYYAN:87:1/r5 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p455/r1 BIQAI:87:1/r5 BIQAI-FULL:v8p393#2/r3 ALUSI:87:1/r18 ALUSI:87:2/r5 ALUSI-FULL:v30p102/r1 ELMALILI:87:1#2/r8 ELMALILI:87:1#2/r22 ELMALILI:87:1/r1 IBNASHUR:87:1/r24 IBNASHUR:87:1/r25 IBNASHUR:87:1/r26 IBNASHUR:87:1/r27 IBNASHUR:87:1/r28 IBNASHUR:87:1/r29 IBNASHUR:87:1/r30 IBNASHUR:87:1/r31 TABATABAI:87:14/r2 STUDYQURAN:87:1/r1
- 87:1/q02/p2 Ism is an honorific added expression, or stands for God, the one named.
  reasons: Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi use Labid's 'name of Peace' to illustrate the idiom; Razi says Arabic mentions an exalted person's name instead of the person. Abu Ali adduces 'the name of peace be upon you.' Baghowi and Qurtubi argue that worshippers say 'Glory to my Lord,' not to His name. Alusi conditionally favors insertion if sabbih means utter subhan; Elmalili says sanctification of the referent still proceeds through its name.
