# 20:68: 12 questions

## 20:68/q01 What events or means of delivery are reported around God's reassurance?  [قُلْنَا · reports]
turns on: The reports give different surrounding events or modes of communication.
- 20:68/q01/p1 Gabriel conveyed God's assurance to Moses. — Muqatil identifies Gabriel as its conveyor and compares the promised victory with 3:139 and 47:35. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 20:68/q01/p2 The reassurance came as immediate revelation. — Islahi says this revelation restored Moses' confidence despite the apparent depth of the magicians' spectacle. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:68/q01/p3 Moses disputed Gabriel's call for gentleness toward the magicians before Gabriel foretold their imminent conversion and Paradise. — Al-Qurtubi reports Moses' objection that the magicians sought to defeat the miracle and support Pharaoh's religion; Gabriel said they would be with Moses until afternoon prayer, then in Paradise. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:68/q01/p4 Moses wondered whether God's knowledge of his eventual state might differ from his present state, as with the magicians; God then reassured him. — Al-Qurtubi gives the change in the magicians' state as the comparison. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:68/q02 What fear of Moses does 'Do not fear' address?  [لَا تَخَفْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes locate the fear in apparent magic, concern for the people, a question about divine knowledge, or an inward encounter with divine power; some describe human fear without specifying its object.
- 20:68/q02/p1 He feared the magicians' impressive magic might seem to prevail or lead the public astray. — Al-Nahhas says the apparent confusion would be resolved by proof; al-Biqa'i says God could nullify their magic; al-Alusi describes an imagined danger; Ibn Ashur says he feared even a temporary public impression of defeat, despite confidence in his mission, citing 3:196–197 for possible temporary unbeliever success; the Ibn Atiyye report in Kuran Yolu says the spectacle briefly affected Moses and raised fear for the people; Islahi notes how profound the magic seemed. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)]
- 20:68/q02/p2 He feared for his people's loss of their share of God, rather than for himself. — Ibn Ata, as reported by al-Alusi, explains the fear as concern that the people would lose their divine share. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء)]
- 20:68/q02/p3 He feared the Creator manifested in the serpent's overpowering of magic, not his created staff. — The inward reading in al-Bursevi says Moses recognized God's overpowering attribute in the serpent swallowing the magic. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 20:68/q02/p4 He wondered whether God's knowledge of his destiny differed from his present state. — Al-Qurtubi compares the magicians' changed state; the reassurance followed this concern. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:68/q02/p5 He felt an inward human fear that required divine reassurance. — Yahya ibn Sallam says the fear was inward; the inward reading transmitted by al-Bursevi says fear belongs to human nature even in a prophet until God removes it by command. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:68/q03 What does the command 'Do not fear' do?  [لَا تَخَفْ · interpretation]
turns on: Al-Alusi contrasts a literal prohibition with the reported reading of the command as encouragement.
- 20:68/q03/p1 It literally tells Moses to stop the fear that had overtaken him. — Al-Alusi says the prohibition is literal and addresses the imagined danger. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:68/q03/p2 It heartens Moses and removes his fear. — Al-Mawardi says the reassurance steadied him; al-Qashani attributes the strengthening to the Holy Spirit; an opinion reported by al-Alusi reads the command as encouragement; al-Tabatabai says it supports him because the magicians' trick cannot harm one superior to them in every respect; Kuran Yolu says it raised Moses' morale. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)]

## 20:68/q04 Why can Moses be assured that he need not fear?  [لَا تَخَفْ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain the assurance through God's protective presence and through the promised outcome of the contest.
- 20:68/q04/p1 God accompanies, sees, protects, and empowers him against the magicians' merely material means. — Ibn Ata in al-Sulami invokes God's seeing and hearing and contrasts reliance on the Causer with reliance on causes; al-Biqa'i says God's presence enables Him to nullify the magic; al-Bursevi adds divine signs protecting Moses in every state; Ibn Ata in al-Alusi offers divine care as one explanation of 'uppermost'; al-Qashani describes strengthening through the Holy Spirit. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء)]
- 20:68/q04/p2 His superiority and coming victory make the magicians' trick powerless. — Al-Tabatabai says Moses is superior in every respect, so their trick cannot harm him; al-Razi says the assurance removes fear that his cause might fail to prevail; al-Baydawi and al-Alusi say the promise of victory explains the command. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:68/q05 What does 'you are the highest' say about Moses?  [إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations differ over whether 'highest' names contest victory alone, also heavenly rank or comprehensive superiority, divine protection, or Moses' rank above the staff.
- 20:68/q05/p1 Moses will prevail over the magicians or any opponent in the contest. — Muqatil compares 'uppermost' with victory in 3:139 and 47:35; al-Nahhas adds victory by proof after apparent confusion; Ibn Abbas, reported by al-Wahidi, glosses it as 'victor'; al-Wahidi adds triumph; Ibn Atiyya extends it to whoever opposes Moses in the contest; al-Tabrisi specifies victory and dominance over the magicians; Ibn al-Jawzi prefers this over an unnamed first explanation; al-Razi calls the victory manifest; al-Biqai says Moses alone will win despite the magic's impressive appearance; Ibn Ata in al-Alusi says such victory could bring the people to follow him. Yahya, al-Thalabi, al-Wahidi's Wajiz, al-Baghawi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Bursevi, al-Alusi and Islahi likewise describe victory or dominance. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 20:68/q05/p2 Moses will prevail here and hold the highest ranks in Paradise. — Al-Qurtubi joins worldly triumph over the magicians with the rank granted by prophethood and God's choosing Moses. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:68/q05/p3 Moses is superior to the magicians in every respect. — Al-Tabatabai says this comprehensive superiority makes their trick incapable of harming him. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:68/q05/p4 Moses is above his own created staff as God's messenger and chosen intimate. — The inward reading reported by al-Bursevi says Moses manifests divine gentleness and power, whereas the staff manifests only overpowering. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 20:68/q05/p5 Moses is under divine care and protection. — Ibn Ata, reported by al-Alusi, gives this as one of two readings, the other being victory that wins followers. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء)]

## 20:68/q06 How does the wording make the assurance emphatic?  [إِنَّكَ أَنتَ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:68/q06/p1 An independently asserted, layered affirmation makes Moses' superiority and victory certain. — Al-Razi cites inna, repeated pronoun, the definite article and 'higher'; al-Baydawi adds the independent assertion and superlative; al-Nasafi lists inna, anta, definiteness and superiority; Abu Hayyan adds the renewed clause; al-Biqai says the force counters the apparent might of the magic; al-Alusi describes a discourse response plus affirmation, pronoun, definite predicate and comparative; Ibn Ashur says inna, separating pronoun and definite al-a'la answer Moses' fear of apparent public defeat. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:68/q07 Does the comparative 'highest' imply that the magicians also had manifest superiority?  [ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether comparative form entails shared superiority, or serves to intensify the assertion.
- 20:68/q07/p1 No; the comparative intensifies Moses' superiority without crediting the magicians with it. — Al-Alusi prefers this reading and says comparing Moses with the onlookers is irrelevant. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:68/q07/p2 The comparison implies that the magicians also attained manifest superiority. — Al-Alusi argues against this implication. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:68/q08 How can Moses' fear coexist with his prophetic certainty?  [* · theology]
turns on: One explanation appeals to natural human fear; the other to possible temporary adversary success despite ultimate certainty.
- 20:68/q08/p1 Fear is part of human nature even for a prophet, until a divine command removes it. — The inward reading transmitted by al-Bursevi adds that Moses is too elevated to fear creatures instead of the Creator. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 20:68/q08/p2 Certainty in God's mission still allows temporary unbeliever success as a test of believers. — Ibn Ashur cites the warning about unbelievers' worldly roaming in 3:196–197 to explain why Moses feared a temporary public impression of defeat. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:68/q09 Must kalam be a complete sentence, or can one word count as speech?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether complete propositional meaning is required for the label kalam; al-Razi disputes the cited Sibawayh proof.
- 20:68/q09/p1 Kalam is a complete meaningful sentence, distinct from a single kalima. — Al-Razi reports most grammarians' distinction and Ibn Jinni's agreement; Ibn Jinni finds the theologians' view unlikely, citing a passage of Sibawayh. Al-Razi challenges the strength of that proof. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أكثر النحويين,ابن جني); RAZI-FULL-]
- 20:68/q09/p2 A single word can also be called kalam; kalima and kalam can each name simple or compound speech. — Al-Razi reports most legal theorists' view: a word contrasts with silence, conveys understanding, the root kalm suggests an effect or wound, ordinary usage calls it speaking, and 'incomplete speech' remains speech. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أكثر الأصوليين)]

## 20:68/q10 What legal case does the dispute about speech raise?  [* · law]
- 20:68/q10/p1 It determines the effect of a husband's conditional divorce utterance to his unconsummated wife. — Al-Razi says Muhammad ibn al-Hasan first discusses the case in al-Jami al-Kabir: the husband makes three divorces conditional on speaking to her. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:68/q11 When does the conditional divorce utterance take effect?  [* · law]
turns on: The moment at which a meaningful utterance fulfills the condition, and the effect of the repetition particle in the 'whenever' variant.
- 20:68/q11/p1 The second utterance of the conditional phrase effects one divorce, completed with the full sentence. — Al-Razi reports Abu Hanifa and his two companions' ruling; Abu Hanifa says the condition remains incomplete until 'if I speak to you, you are divorced' is fully spoken. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,أبو حنيفة وصاحباه)]
- 20:68/q11/p2 The second utterance effects no divorce because speech occurs before its divorce clause is spoken. — Al-Razi reports Zufar's view: 'if I speak to you' already fulfills the speech condition, and the subsequent divorce clause falls outside the marriage and has no effect. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(زفر)]
- 20:68/q11/p3 In a repeated 'whenever I speak to you' version, the single-word definition appears to yield three divorces. — Al-Razi says the repetition particle produces this result if the speaker stops at the second occurrence; he suggests Zufar may accept it. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:68/q12 How does this verse fit the sura's larger connection between passages?  [* · links]
- 20:68/q12/p1 Any genuine relation between passages suffices; the discourse affirms the Qur'an, urges acting on it and faith, then returns to its opening. — Al-Burhan cites 2:23 and says the relation need not be of one specified kind. [1 notes: BURHAN]
