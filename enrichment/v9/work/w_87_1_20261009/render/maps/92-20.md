# 92:20: 27 questions

## 92:20/q01 What does ibtighāʾ mean here?  [ٱبْتِغَآءَ · meaning]
- 92:20/q01/p1 Earnest, diligent seeking of a desired end. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says ibtighāʾ is stronger than baghy. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys Qurʾānic uses: in good contexts it is diligent pursuit, while negative contexts can imply aggression and excess (9:48; 3:7; 23:7; 70:31; 4:34; 13:17; 16:14; 17:12, 66; 28:73; 30:23, 46; 35:12; 45:12; 73:20; 62:10; 48:29; 60:1). Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses it simply as seeking. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]

## 92:20/q02 How is the exception introduced by illā construed?  [إِلَّا ٱبْتِغَآءَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether seeking is outside the class of favors, supplies an implied purpose of giving, or follows the preceding noun's grammatical position; the Arabist also shifts the preceding preposition.
- 92:20/q02/p1 It is a disconnected exception from the preceding favor: seeking God is outside the class of favors owed, and illā has the force of 'but.' — Abū ʿUbayda cites ordinary usage and 4:157; al-Farrāʾ calls the contrasting construction Ḥijāzī and invokes poetry and 4:157. Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī compare 'no one in the house except a donkey'; al-Nābigha and Bishr ibn Abī Ḥāzim supply poetic examples. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say seeking approval cannot itself be a repayable favor; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes the extra min intensifies the preceding negation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the analysis questionable despite giving it. Al-Biqāʿī says the formally connected particle makes the seeking seem itself God's gift. Al-Ṭabarī's Arabist and Jishumī explicitly give illā the sense 'but.' [33 notes: MAJAZ; TAB(بعض أهل العربية); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الفراء,النابغة); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF(بشر بن أبي حازم); KASHSHAF-FULL(القائل,بشر بن أبي حازم); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 92:20/q02/p2 It is a purpose accusative tied to implied giving or spending: he gives only in order to seek God's face. — Al-Farrāʾ supplies spending, citing 2:272; al-Ṭabarī treats it as an accusative from the implied motive of spending or by contrast after illā. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Qurṭubī derive the purpose from the sentence's sense. Al-Ālūsī also makes it a free exception from all motives: 92:18–19 and purification imply the requisite negative and exclude ostentation. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes that Qurʾānic usage commonly makes this form an object of purpose. [21 notes: TAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري,الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري والفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الفراء); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفراء والزمخشري)]
- 92:20/q02/p3 In the nominative construction, ibtighāʾ follows the underlying nominative position of niʿma after the extra min. — Al-Farrāʾ permits nominative by the prior phrase's sense and cites a land with only gazelles and white camels. Al-Naḥḥās calls it figurative, cites al-Nābigha, and says the nominative reading is unattested and remote; al-Samīn replies to Makkī's similar objection that the transmitted reading follows widespread Tamīm usage. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī describe apposition to niʿma; al-Ālūsī also permits subject or topic. Poetry is transmitted with both cases. [20 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الشاعر,النابغة الذبياني); KASHSHAF(يحيى بن وثاب); KASHSHAF-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); RAZI(يحيى بن وثاب); RAZI-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); QURTUBI(يحيى بن وثاب); ABUHAYYAN(ابن وثاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وثاب); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,يحيى); ALUSI(بشر بن أبي حازم,يحيى بن وثاب); ALUSI-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); BINTSHATI(البحر المحيط); NAHHAS-; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]
- 92:20/q02/p4 The preceding lām of li-aḥadin can be understood with ʿindahu despite its shifted surface position. — Al-Ṭabarī endorses the Arabist's construction: Arabic permits a particle away from its expected place when the relation remains clear; al-Nābigha's verse is his evidence. Another Arabist formulation allows future repayment. [4 notes: TAB(بعض أهل العربية); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); TAB+]

## 92:20/q03 How is ibtighāʾ read?  [ٱبْتِغَآءَ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted case and shortened form, and whether dialectal apposition after a negative licenses the nominative.
- 92:20/q03/p1 The majority read ibtighāʾa in the accusative. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the majority reading and parses it as disconnected exception; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also reports the majority. Al-Samīn calls it the common reading and cites poetry for the accusative. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(الجمهور,الفراء والزمخشري)]
- 92:20/q03/p2 Yaḥyā ibn Waththāb reads ibtighāʾu in the nominative. — The reading follows Tamīm usage in a disconnected exception after negation and is construed as apposition to the syntactically nominative niʿma; poetry, including Bishr ibn Abī Ḥāzim and the gazelles-and-camels line, supports it. Al-Qurṭubī also cites 4:66. Al-Naḥḥās says an attributed nominative reading is unattested and remote; Makkī calls the apposition remote, but al-Samīn objects that it is transmitted and widespread. [12 notes: KASHSHAF(يحيى بن وثاب); KASHSHAF-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); RAZI(يحيى بن وثاب); RAZI-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); QURTUBI(يحيى بن وثاب); ABUHAYYAN(ابن وثاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن وثاب); SAMIN-DURR(يحيى); ALUSI(يحيى بن وثاب); ALUSI-FULL(يحيى بن وثاب); BINTSHATI(البحر المحيط); NAHHAS-; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]
- 92:20/q03/p3 Ibn Abī ʿAbla reads a shortened form without the full hamza or final length, allowing either case analysis. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the hamza-less form; al-Samīn calls it a shortened final vowel; al-Ālūsī says its case can be accusative or nominative. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN(ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI(ابن أبي عبلة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة)]

## 92:20/q04 What does wajh mean in Arabic usage?  [وَجْهِ · meaning]
turns on: Which Arabic extension of the bodily face explains its use for God: self or intended direction.
- 92:20/q04/p1 Literally the facing part, especially the bodily face. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Joseph and Abraham's wife (12:93, 96; 51:29), and washing, wiping, and turning faces toward the qibla (5:6; 2:144). [2 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 92:20/q04/p2 By Arabic idiom, face can denote the person or self. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the face identifies its owner and can even stand for notable people; she favors the idiom 'face of truth' or 'face of the matter' without embodiment. She reports a view that in divine expressions it is expendable and simply means God, citing 2:115, 55:27 and 28:88. [3 notes: BINTSHATI(بعضهم); BINTSHATI+]
- 92:20/q04/p3 Face can mean aim or direction. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ derives this from the face being the body's foremost facing part; she reports objections to treating the word as expendable that instead retain purpose and orientation. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(بعضهم)]

## 92:20/q05 What is sought in seeking the Lord's face?  [وَجْهِ رَبِّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase aims at approval, reward, God's own essence, the sight of Him, sincerity, or disclosure through divine names; Rāzī identifies the dispute over loving God's essence rather than only His reward.
- 92:20/q05/p1 God's pleasure, approval or favor, and nearness to Him. — Al-Naḥḥās, Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī and others gloss the phrase with approval. Al-Ṭabrisī combines God Himself, pleasure and reward; Ibn ʿĀshūr says face denotes God's essence and seeking it means approval, citing 55:27. Al-Biqāʿī calls this the highest aim. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ observes that eight of eleven Qurʾānic references to God's face concern spending or worship directed to Him. [24 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 92:20/q05/p2 God's reward or honor. — Al-Wāḥidī repeatedly glosses the sought face as reward; one view reported by Rāzī insists essence cannot be sought and therefore supplies reward and honor. Al-Bāqillānī instead reads the verse as devotion without hope of reward or fear of punishment, unlike ʿAlī's fear of Judgment in 76:9–10. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(من الناس); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)-]
- 92:20/q05/p3 God Himself or His essence, loved as the final aim even beyond worldly or afterlife reward. — Jishumī says face refers to God Himself. Al-Biqāʿī says the giver sees all benefit as divine grace and seeks God's own essence, without worldly gain or even afterlife reward. Rāzī reports the rival view that God's essence can be loved without supplying an omitted reward, referring to the debate at 2:165; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes essence as the expression's referent but explains seeking it as seeking approval. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI(من الناس); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI(من الناس)-]
- 92:20/q05/p4 The hoped-for sight of the Most High Lord in the gardens of Paradise. — Ibn Kathīr identifies hope of seeing God in the Hereafter as the righteous giver's motive. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR]
- 92:20/q05/p5 Sincerity of intention for God's sake. — Ibn al-Labbān, as reported in al-Itqān, explains divine-face expressions through sincere intention, including feeding for God's sake. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن اللبان)]
- 92:20/q05/p6 In an inward reading, the highest divine face is disclosed through the all-encompassing Highest name. — Al-Qāshānī says each divine name has a face by which God manifests Himself to a worshiper prepared for that name; if 'Lord' is adjectival, the Lord here is the Highest name. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 92:20/q06 Why mention God's face?  [وَجْهِ · rhetoric]
- 92:20/q06/p1 The face expresses the honored, highest aim of devotion directed to God. — Al-Ṭabrisī says mentioning the face honors the expression. Al-Biqāʿī calls it the highest aim, God's own essence. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes the Arabic senses of self and direction and the predominance of spending and worship among the eleven divine-face occurrences. Corpus Coranicum calls it a biblical metaphor and compares 76:9, Psalms 27:8 and 105:4, 2 Samuel 21:1, and Hosea 5:15. [6 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:20/q07 Does divine face imply a bodily feature?  [وَجْهِ · theology]
turns on: Whether divine 'face' is read bodily or through established Arabic idiom.
- 92:20/q07/p1 Anthropomorphists appeal to this verse's word 'face' for their bodily reading. — Rāzī reports their appeal, without developing it here. [2 notes: RAZI(المجسمة); RAZI-FULL]
- 92:20/q07/p2 The Arabic idiom can mean God's self without implying embodiment. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sets aside theological disputes and prefers the ordinary idiom of the 'face of truth' or a person's self. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God's face denotes His essence and seeking it His approval, citing 55:27. [4 notes: BINTSHATI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI+]

## 92:20/q08 What does 'his Lord' contribute?  [رَبِّهِ · rhetoric]
- 92:20/q08/p1 It identifies the benefactor who created and nurtured the giver, so the giver recognizes all benefit as God's grace. — Al-Biqāʿī says this leaves no independent human benefaction that could repay God; the giver owns nothing except grace received from Him. [2 notes: BIQAI]

## 92:20/q09 What does al-aʿlā mean?  [ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether al-aʿlā describes absolute transcendence and authority, physical elevation, or an implied comparison with another lord.
- 92:20/q09/p1 Supremely exalted, powerful and dominant in attributes, authority and proof. — Al-Wāḥidī says incomparable power, dominance and manifest proof and might; Jishumī gives 'overpowers everything' or 'most exalted.' Al-Qurṭubī calls it an epithet of the Lord's real exaltedness. Al-Biqāʿī says absolute supremacy prevents any creature giving independently to repay God. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ treats it as an unbounded superlative reaching utmost elevation, also in 87:1. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BINTSHATI]
- 92:20/q09/p2 Exalted in dominion, status and proof, without spatial height. — Al-Nasafī says physical elevation would entail createdness; his full commentary likewise stresses authority and proof. [2 notes: NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 92:20/q09/p3 Some opponents infer that 'Most High Lord' presupposes another, lower lord. — Rāzī reports the atheists' inference. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports it from Rāzī and rejects it because the superlative is absolute rather than a comparison between gods. [3 notes: RAZI(الملحدة); RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الرازي); BINTSHATI-]

## 92:20/q10 Why choose al-aʿlā in this wording?  [ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 92:20/q10/p1 It expresses absolute exaltedness, beyond a comparison with a lesser god or a merely convenient verse ending. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects the lesser-lord inference and says the superlative reaches the utmost degree, as in 87:1; she separately denies that al-aʿlā was chosen over al-ʿulā merely to rhyme. Al-Biqāʿī connects absolute height with creatures' inability to offer God anything independently. [3 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BIQAI]

## 92:20/q11 How does the phrase refer to God in the third person?  [رَبِّهِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 92:20/q11/p1 The two divine attributes effect a shift from first-person divine speech to third-person reference. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the rhetorical shift through 'his Lord' and 'Most High.' [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 92:20/q12 What motive for giving does the verse affirm or deny?  [إِلَّا ٱبْتِغَآءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِ ٱلْأَعْلَىٰ · interpretation]
- 92:20/q12/p1 The giver seeks God alone, neither repaying a human favor nor seeking human recompense, thanks or advantage. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this from early reports; his Arabist allows expected future recompense as well as a past favor and Qatāda applies it to Abū Bakr. Rāzī says repaying an earlier favor merely settles a debt, whereas giving in answer to God's command earns reward. Al-Nasafī says the giver owes no one, and God rewards the deed. Al-Ṭabrisī, Qutb, Islahi, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran link sincere spending to purification; Islahi cites 76:9. Kuran Yolu says all blessings originate with God and rebuts the pagans' allegation of self-interest. Al-Bāqillānī sharpens sole devotion into no hope of reward or fear of punishment. Variants describe avoiding a person's benefit, ostentation, gratitude, or repayment. [45 notes: TAB(بعض أهل العربية,قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن عباس وابن مسعود,قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TAB+]

## 92:20/q13 Does the denial of repayment exclude virtuous reciprocal kindness?  [إِلَّا ٱبْتِغَآءَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether denying a repayable favor is mistakenly taken to make every return of kindness displeasing to God.
- 92:20/q13/p1 The preceding denial can say the recipient had done the giver no favor requiring repayment. — Islahi reports this rendering as linguistically possible, but rejects the inference that returning kindness therefore cannot please God. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some people)]
- 92:20/q13/p2 Returning kindness can itself please God; the verse praises sincere spending without expecting repayment or gratitude. — Islahi rejects an anti-reciprocity inference and prefers his own linguistically sound translation, which preserves the virtue of reciprocal kindness; his precise alternative wording is not in these notes. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 92:20/q14 Who is the righteous giver described here?  [* · referent]
turns on: Competing occasion reports about Abū Bakr and Abū al-Daḥdāḥ, the sūra's proposed early Meccan setting, and whether the wording is confined to an individual.
- 92:20/q14/p1 Abū Bakr, especially in freeing Bilāl and other persecuted believers. — Qatāda and the report from ʿĀmir ibn ʿAbd Allāh's father identify him; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Masʿūd mention Bilāl. Ibn Kathīr says many commentators give this occasion, some claim consensus, and Abū Bakr is foremost under the general wording. Al-Bayḍāwī says the corresponding wretched one is Abū Jahl or Umayya ibn Khalaf. Al-Ālūsī says verses 17–end concern him; al-Imām appeals to this context against the rival ʿAlī claim, though al-Ālūsī calls the debate disputable. [26 notes: TAB(قتادة,والد عامر بن عبد الله); TAB-FULL(ابن عبد الله,قتادة); MAWARDI(ابن عباس وابن مسعود); WAHIDI-ASBAB(عطاء عن ابن عباس); RAZI(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني); RAZI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني); QURTUBI(ابن عباس,سعيد بن المسيب); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عامر بن الزبير عن أبيه,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير عن أبيه); ALUSI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ELMALILI(Hâkim); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 92:20/q14/p2 Abū al-Daḥdāḥ, who gave the palm purchased from its owner. — ʿAṭāʾ's palm account makes him the God-fearing giver and the refusing owner the opposite figure. Al-Qushayrī's version names forty palms but does not name the man. Study Quran questions this as the historical occasion if the sūra is early Meccan. [12 notes: THALABI(عطاء); QURTUBI(عطاء وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء,عطاء، ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(several commentators); STUDYQURAN-]
- 92:20/q14/p3 The wording applies generally to pious, generous believers, with Abū Bakr its foremost example. — Ibn Kathīr explicitly maintains the general wording despite reports about Abū Bakr. Study Quran says an early Meccan date would favor a general setting over the later palm story. Kuran Yolu says the passage honors believers like Abū Bakr. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:20/q14/p4 A Shīʿī claim applies the passage to ʿAlī. — Al-Imām argues from the context that this claim fails; al-Ālūsī records his argument and notes it is open to dispute. The notes do not supply the claim's supporting evidence. [0 notes: ALUSI(الإمام)-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]

## 92:20/q15 What is reported about Bilāl's persecution and release?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports vary on the seller, purchase price or exchange, and the accompanying conversations.
- 92:20/q15/p1 Abū Bakr bought and freed Bilāl after persecution for his monotheism, prompting pagans to claim a prior favor or debt. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports Bilāl's defiling idols, subsequent torture, the Prophet's promise of rescue and notice to Abū Bakr, purchase with gold, and the pagans' allegation; Rāzī names his enslaver ʿAbd Allāh ibn Judʿān and sale to polytheists for camels. Al-Qurṭubī reports Bilāl's cry 'One, One.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes Quraysh's allegation. Study Quran repeats the idols, testimony and rescue account. [10 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA(قريش); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); ALUSI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 92:20/q15/p2 In one account Abū Bakr bought Bilāl from Umayya and Ubayy ibn Khalaf for a cloak and ten uqiyyas. — Al-Qurṭubī associates that account with the revelation of 92:4 and says Abū Bakr freed him for God. [1 notes: QURTUBI]
- 92:20/q15/p3 In Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib's account Abū Bakr exchanged his wealthy polytheist slave Nustās for Bilāl. — Umayya offered Bilāl for Nustās; Abū Bakr first offered conversion and transfer of property, but Nustās refused. The pagans then alleged Bilāl had done Abū Bakr a favor. [1 notes: QURTUBI(سعيد بن المسيب)]
- 92:20/q15/p4 Bilāl asked whether he was bought for service or for God's cause, and Abū Bakr answered God's cause and freed him. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits the exchange as Bilāl's testimony. [2 notes: QURTUBI(بلال); QURTUBI-FULL(بلال، أبو بكر)]
- 92:20/q15/p5 The Prophet praised Abū Bakr for freeing Bilāl, and ʿUmar called Bilāl the master freed by their master. — Abū Ḥayyān al-Taymī transmits through his father from ʿAlī the Prophet's prayer mentioning Abū Bakr's marriage connection, service during migration, and freeing Bilāl; ʿUmar's saying praises both men. [4 notes: QURTUBI(علي,عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حيان التيمي، علي,عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 92:20/q16 How many persecuted people did Abū Bakr free in these reports?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported count and the names in ʿUrwa's list.
- 92:20/q16/p1 Six or seven, including Bilāl and ʿĀmir ibn Fuhayra. — Qatāda gives the uncertain count while stressing that Abū Bakr sought neither repayment nor thanks. [2 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة)]
- 92:20/q16/p2 Seven, with Bilāl, ʿĀmir ibn Fuhayra, al-Nahdiyya and her daughter, and three other women. — ʿUrwa's lists name Dīnara or Dhanīra, Umm ʿĪsā or Umm ʿUbays, and a Banū al-Muʾammil or al-Muʾammal slave woman; the names vary between al-Ālūsī's notes. [2 notes: ALUSI(عروة); ALUSI-FULL(عروة)]
- 92:20/q16/p3 The accounts describe Abū Bakr freeing vulnerable enslaved believers without a fixed count. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī mention Bilāl and other persecuted people; Study Quran describes weak slaves. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 92:20/q17 What exchange with Abū Bakr's father is reported?  [* · reports]
- 92:20/q17/p1 Abū Quḥāfa urged him to free strong men who could defend him; Abū Bakr said he sought God's face or what is with God, and the passage was revealed. — The ʿĀmir ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr report through his father gives the exchange and revelation through the promise of satisfaction; al-Ḥākim grades it sound. Al-Ālūsī also cites an Ibn Jarīr and Ibn ʿAsākir version in which Abū Bakr says he seeks only what is with God. Study Quran says he sought no defense. [7 notes: DURR-FULL(عامر بن الزبير عن أبيه,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير عن أبيه); ALUSI(أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Hâkim); STUDYQURAN(others)]

## 92:20/q18 What is reported about the palm-tree occasion?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the donor is named, whether the price is an orchard or forty palms, and the feasibility of a Medinan occasion for an early Meccan sūra.
- 92:20/q18/p1 Abū al-Daḥdāḥ bought an overhanging palm with his orchard or garden and gave it away after its owner refused the Prophet's offer of a palm in Paradise. — ʿAṭāʾ says fallen dates reached a neighbor's children; the Prophet's initial offer was rejected. One narration calls the garden Ḥusnā and says Abū al-Daḥdāḥ gave the tree to the Prophet in return for a Paradise palm, who gave the garden to the neighbor; others say the tree went to the neighbor. The story maps the donor and owner to the sūra's opposed figures, with Paradise and Hell as their fates. Study Quran gives forty palms as the payment. [9 notes: THALABI(عطاء); QURTUBI(عطاء وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء,عطاء، ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(several commentators); STUDYQURAN-]
- 92:20/q18/p2 A version from Ibn ʿAbbās gives forty palms but does not name the donor. — Al-Qushayrī reports this variant, which al-Qurṭubī distinguishes from ʿAṭāʾ's named Abū al-Daḥdāḥ account. [2 notes: QURTUBI(عطاء وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري، ابن عباس)]

## 92:20/q19 Does this verse establish a rank between Abū Bakr and ʿAlī?  [* · theology]
turns on: The subject of the passage and whether its stated motive is purer than ʿAlī's motive in 76:9–10.
- 92:20/q19/p1 Al-Bāqillānī ranks Abū Bakr higher because the verse portrays devotion without hope of reward or fear of Judgment. — He compares this with ʿAlī's feeding for God's face alongside fear of the Day in 76:9–10; both act for God, but Abū Bakr's motive is described as purer. [2 notes: RAZI(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني); RAZI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)]
- 92:20/q19/p2 Al-Imām uses the passage's context to argue Abū Bakr was the community's best. — His argument excludes the Shīʿī application to ʿAlī; al-Ālūsī notes that the extended debate is open to dispute. [2 notes: ALUSI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]

## 92:20/q20 What reward follows the giver's sincere act?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:20/q20/p1 God rewards the giver with Paradise and satisfaction far exceeding what was spent. — Al-Māwardī and al-Nasafī connect seeking God with deserved divine reward; Jishumī says the full qualities earn Paradise. Al-Qurṭubī says the giver receives whatever pleases him in Paradise many times over his expenditure; the palm account likewise says Abū al-Daḥdāḥ will be satisfied on entering Paradise. Qutb says the promise is astonishing and builds suspense before it. [7 notes: MAWARDI(قتادة); JISHUMI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 92:20/q21 What wider moral teaching accompanies this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:20/q21/p1 Piety and sincere generosity, not bare affirmation, mark the saved; withholding an obligation is blameworthy miserliness. — Jishumī explicitly opposes the Murjiʾa on bare affirmation and defines miserliness legally as withholding an obligation; Kuran Yolu emphasizes purification and generosity, and the divine origin of all blessings. [4 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 92:20/q22 May one pause just before illā here?  [إِلَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether illā here begins an independent 'but' clause or is syntactically tied to what precedes.
- 92:20/q22/p1 A pause before illā is allowed when illā means 'but.' — Abū ʿAlī explicitly gives this verse along with 6:119 and 4:157; a general grammatical view lets one pause where understood sentence components support it, though fuller meaning is preferable. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو على,بعض النحويين)]
- 92:20/q22/p2 A pause between syntactically dependent elements is forbidden. — Ibn al-Anbārī lists genitive and complement, subject and predicate, conjunction and antecedent, and relative pronoun and clause as examples; the principle constrains pauses where the exception is construed dependently. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن الانبارى)]

## 92:20/q23 How should Qurʾānic pauses be evaluated?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether pause labels are useful judgments about syntax and meaning or inadmissible labels for Qurʾānic text.
- 92:20/q23/p1 Classify pauses by completeness and quality, using grammar, recitation, interpretation and Prophetic practice. — Al-Burhān reports the majority's seven grades: complete, near-complete, deficient, good, near-good, bad and near-bad; they also consider the Prophet's stopping at verse endings. [1 notes: BURHAN(الجمهور)]
- 92:20/q23/p2 Calling a Qurʾānic pause complete, deficient, good or bad is an innovation. — Abū Yūsuf reasons that the whole Qurʾān and each of its parts are complete and good. [1 notes: BURHAN(أبو يوسف القاضى)]
- 92:20/q23/p3 One may stop where understood sentence components allow, ideally at the fullest meaning. — Some grammarians allow flexible pauses but prefer the point of complete meaning or a fitting estimate of it. [1 notes: BURHAN(بعض النحويين)]

## 92:20/q24 How does the verse fit the sūra's contrasting portraits?  [* · links]
- 92:20/q24/p1 It completes the pious giver's portrait opposite the denier and links generosity, purification and final recompense. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the oaths of night, day and creation frame divergent efforts and fates. Corpus Coranicum sees two antitheses across verses 5–13 and 14–21, with ittaqā in verse 5 and atqā in verse 17; the final stanza reverses the saved/damned order and places warning and promise before worldly behavior. Islahi reads 15–21 as contrasting traits and reward. Kuran Yolu links the fire warning of verse 14 to the giving and protection of 17–20. Al-Qurṭubī's palm story maps ease to Paradise, hardship to Hell and death to wealth's loss. Qutb notes suspense before the promised satisfaction. [9 notes: BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QURTUBI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 92:20/q25 What other scripture illuminates the phrase or act?  [* · links]
- 92:20/q25/p1 Other Qurʾānic and biblical texts parallel giving for God's face without human recompense. — Islahi cites 76:9 for giving without expecting thanks; Corpus Coranicum calls seeking God's face a biblical motif, comparing 76:9, Psalms 27:8 and 105:4, 2 Samuel 21:1 and Hosea 5:15. Al-Farrāʾ's ellipsis argument uses 2:272, as reported by al-Wāḥidī and Rāzī. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء)]

## 92:20/q26 What virtue is reported for reciting this passage?  [* · sciences]
- 92:20/q26/p1 A report promises ample provision, relief from hardship and an easy path to its reciter. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits the promise from the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:20/q27 What related prophetic promise is reported about Abū Bakr's spending?  [* · reports]
- 92:20/q27/p1 The Prophet hoped Abū Bakr would be called from every gate of Paradise. — The reported saying promises a call from Paradise's gates to one who spends two kinds of wealth in God's path; asked whether anyone receives every call, the Prophet names Abū Bakr as a hoped-for instance. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
