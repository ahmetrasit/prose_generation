# 21:33: 28 questions

## 21:33/q01 What does God's creation of these signs establish?  [وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the signs establish God's sole agency or prophetic foreknowledge.
- 21:33/q01/p1 God alone creates and governs these signs, proving His power, wisdom, sole divinity and beneficence. — Al-Tabari relates alternation to livelihood and the hereafter; Bursevi says creation from nonexistence proves power; Ibn Ashur says the definite clause excludes idols. Islahi, Biqai and Kuranyolu stress one governing intelligence, obedience and power to recreate. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 21:33/q01/p2 The celestial order is a sign of Muhammad's prophethood. — Elmalılı argues that an unlettered Prophet announced independently moving celestial courses before Copernicus, Newton and Laplace; he couples this with 21:30. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 21:33/q02 Why does this verse say 'created'?  [خَلَقَ · rhetoric]
turns on: How khalaqa relates to jaʿala in the preceding verse.
- 21:33/q02/p1 It concerns creation of the entities themselves rather than their being made in a state. — Ibn Ashur contrasts the previous wording. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 21:33/q02/p2 Night and day are unlike substantial creatures, explaining 'created' rather than 'made'. — Al-Alusi reports this and finds it unconvincing. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(some commentators)-]

## 21:33/q03 What are night and day and what do they do?  [ٱلَّيْلَ ٱلنَّهَارَ · interpretation]
turns on: Physical light and shadow versus temporal periods.
- 21:33/q03/p1 Night is darkness and rest; day is light and the time for activity. — Ibn Kathir adds their alternating length; Bursevi and Ibn Ashur identify night with Earth's shadow and day with sunlight on its illuminated hemisphere; Kuranyolu stresses benefit to living beings. Al-Tabataba'i says these conditions depend on Earth and its atmosphere; the Sun and fixed stars shine intrinsically while Moon and planets reflect, so their day and night differ from ours. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 21:33/q03/p2 They are alternating periods measured by the sun and moon's motion. — Al-Tabari, reported by al-Burhan, cites Ibn Abbas and Abu Hurayra for Earth and light preceding these named periods. [2 notes: BURHAN(الطبري); TAB-FULL]

## 21:33/q04 Why does night precede day?  [ٱلَّيْلَ ٱلنَّهَارَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Convention or temporal priority.
- 21:33/q04/p1 Arabic convention puts night first, including Qur'anic phrases and dating by nights. — Al-Burhan cites 2:164, 34:33, 30:17 and notes day is masculine. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 21:33/q04/p2 Darkness or night was created first. — Al-Samarrai compares jinn before humans (15:27), drowsiness before sleep, and cites 24:44 and 6:1. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 21:33/q05 Why do sun and moon follow day and appear in that order?  [ٱلنَّهَارَ ٱلشَّمْسَ ٱلْقَمَرَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Natural origin, temporal order or exemplary signs.
- 21:33/q05/p1 Their order points to daylight's origin and to creation or spatial priority. — Ibn Ashur says sunlight produces day and the moon relieves darkness; al-Burhan places location before time; al-Samarrai says sun existed before moon. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 21:33/q05/p2 They are the greatest signs of day and night, implying other stars. — Al-Biqaʿi says naming them implies the rest; al-Alusi connects the preceding signs and a shift of address. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:33/q06 What benefits come from the sun and moon?  [ٱلشَّمْسَ ٱلْقَمَرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Different accounts of the beneficial motion.
- 21:33/q06/p1 They give light, timekeeping, heat and seasonal benefit. — Al-Qurtubi cites 17:12 for months, years and reckoning; Ibn Kathir distinguishes their lights, paths and measures; Bursevi mentions heat and cold; Kuranyolu notes reflected moonlight. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TAB-FULL]
- 21:33/q06/p2 Their measured latitude and timing spread effects without destructive extremes. — Razi says north-south inclination and paced cycles prevent permanent severe seasons; intellect grasps only a little. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 21:33/q06/p3 The sun comes closer in winter and rises in summer for welfare. — Al-Aʿmash says the changes help and prevent heat injury. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الأعمش)]

## 21:33/q07 How does the verse connect to the surrounding signs?  [* · links]
turns on: Surrounding signs, opponents' demand, or parallel verses.
- 21:33/q07/p1 It expands prior sky signs into sun, moon, day and night as gifts. — Razi says it answers neglect of heavenly signs; Baydawi calls it further explanation; Alusi points to 21:32; Study Quran points to 36:40 and cosmological discussion. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 21:33/q07/p2 The signs answer opponents' demand for proof and warn against celestial worship. — Islahi connects the challenge to 21:8 and says the bodies' obedience makes another sign needless. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:33/q07/p3 It parallels other accounts of calculated celestial motion. — Ibn Kathir cites 36:40 and 6:96; Study Quran directs readers to 36:40. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 21:33/q08 Who or what does 'each' include?  [كُلٌّۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether the nearby two suffice or plural and context expand the set.
- 21:33/q08/p1 The sun and moon are the immediate referents. — Al-Samin prefers them because night and day do not swim; Islahi says kull can gather two nouns; Nasafi treats them as classes; Bursevi takes each separately. [7 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 21:33/q08/p2 It includes night, day, sun and moon. — Al-Samin and Abu al-Baqa allow all four; Qurtubi adds stars and planets; Biqai and Tabatabai give the four courses, the latter night as a conical shadow; Asad says all float in space. [8 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 21:33/q08/p3 It includes sun, moon and unnamed stars or all rising bodies. — Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Razi infer stars from the plural; Tabrisi includes them because night is named; Elmalılı supplies an omitted conjunct; Ibn Ashur calls them sky signs. Al-Alusi also records stars as a referent and calls night-and-day inclusion unsuitable because they do not swim. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q08/p4 Sun and moon are generic classes embracing many bodies or risings. — Zamakhshari and Baydawi cite successive risings; Elmalılı prefers encompassing all stars, some moon-like; Nasafi calls them classes. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI+]
- 21:33/q08/p5 Previously mentioned Earth joins sun and moon. — Elmalılı reports the possibility that every body has its own orbit. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 21:33/q09 What does the tanwin of 'each' signify?  [كُلٌّۭ · grammar]
turns on: What is omitted and whether tanwin compensates.
- 21:33/q09/p1 It replaces an omitted genitive complement, 'each of them'. — Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Ibn Ashur explain; al-Burhan cites 2:253 and 30:4 for this rare omission. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 21:33/q09/p2 The complement is singular indefinite, 'each one'. — Alusi reports Zamakhshari; Ibn Hisham says the pronoun should then be singular; Abu Hayyan allows either agreement. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(ابن هشام)-]
- 21:33/q09/p3 The complement is plural definite, as the agreement shows. — Ibn Hisham derives it from the plural verb. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن هشام)]
- 21:33/q09/p4 The tanwin may be ordinary declension. — Al-Burhan reports this alternative to substitution. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 21:33/q10 How is 'in an orbit, they swim' parsed?  [كُلٌّۭ فِى فَلَكٍۢ يَسْبَحُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Predicate choice and sentence attachment.
- 21:33/q10/p1 Kull is subject and yasbahun predicate; fi falak attaches to the verb. — Al-Samin and Abu al-Baqa invoke agreement by sense; Baydawi and Alusi allow the parsing. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q10/p2 Kull is subject, fi falak predicate and yasbahun circumstantial. — Al-Samin, Abu al-Baqa, Bursevi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur take the locative as predicate; Alusi cites distributive singular falak. [7 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 21:33/q10/p3 The whole kull sentence is circumstantial of sun and moon. — Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi and al-Samin allow accusative; the Shaykh excludes night and day because they do not swim. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 21:33/q10/p4 The whole sentence is an independent resumption. — Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi allow no syntactic slot. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:33/q11 Does singular falak mean one common orbit or an orbit for each?  [فِى فَلَكٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether singular falak distributes under kull.
- 21:33/q11/p1 It distributes: each body has its own orbit. — Zamakhshari's prince clothing people in one robe means each gets one; Baydawi and Alusi repeat; Ibn Ashur says courses do not intersect. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 21:33/q11/p2 It can suggest all swim in one orbit. — Abu Hayyan calls this apparent while allowing separate orbits. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 21:33/q12 What is falak's Arabic range or origin?  [فَلَكٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether celestial usage is independently old.
- 21:33/q12/p1 Anything circular or revolving; whorls, mills, round breasts and raised land illustrate it. — Al-Kalbi gives any revolving thing; Ibn Qutayba, Wahidi, Hasan and others cite spindle whorls; al-Raʿi illustrates raised land; rajaz and revolving-falak poetry support rotation; plural is aflak. [31 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(آخرون,الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة، الأصمعي,الحسن,الراعي,السدي,مجاهد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو,ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الكلبي,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI(الراغب,محيى السنة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي)]
- 21:33/q12/p2 It can name the rotation of the sky. — Al-Layth gives the hadith usage; al-Kalbi speaks of sky roundness. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,الليث)]
- 21:33/q12/p3 Vocalized fulk, it means ship and may be singular or plural. — Al-Jishumi records the lexical distinction. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 21:33/q12/p4 The celestial sense may have arisen in Qur'anic usage. — Ibn Ashur finds no pre-Islamic attestation and suspects transfer from circular sea wave and spindle whorl. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 21:33/q13 What is falak in this verse?  [فَلَكٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Sky, body, wave, pole and trajectory differ; evidence for precision is disputed.
- 21:33/q13/p1 A circular course or orbit containing the celestial bodies. — Wahidi calls this the majority view and rejects a mere pole; Ibn Zayd places paths between heaven and Earth; Qatada sees a circle in fixed sky; Tabari refuses more specificity without proof. [28 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,أكثر المفسرين,ابن زيد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان,ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,حسان بن عطية,قتادة); BURSEVI(الراغب); ELMALILI(Dahhâk); STUDYQURAN(Ḍaḥḥāk)]
- 21:33/q13/p2 The sky itself or its circular form. — Ibn Abbas and Suddi identify heaven; Alusi favors their report and says a roof can be spherical; Kalbi names sky roundness. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي)+]
- 21:33/q13/p3 A revolving physical body or sphere carrying or surrounding stars. — Mawardi describes a carrying circle; Ibn Atiyya says its revolution completes day and night; Razi calls physical body majority and apparent; Qurtubi prefers nested spheres. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ,الفلاسفة,شرح التقويم); ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة); STUDYQURAN(Q and R); ELMALILI(Bazıları)]
- 21:33/q13/p4 An immaterial or mathematical trajectory, not a body. — Al-Dahhak gives course and speed; Elmalılı calls it mathematical; Study Quran relays the trajectory. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); ELMALILI(Dahhâk); STUDYQURAN(Ḍaḥḥāk)]
- 21:33/q13/p5 A contained wave or gathered water under heaven. — Al-Farra and later writers report it; al-Kalbi says swimming requires water, rejected by Wahidi and Razi using the running horse; Bursevi calls it transparent. [11 notes: FARRA(يقال); TAB-FULL(آخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي,بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 21:33/q13/p6 The celestial pole or axis around which stars turn. — Tabari cites rajaz; Wahidi says the word must denote the container and rejects the pole. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة)-]
- 21:33/q13/p7 A water-wheel-like sphere between sky and Earth with Earth central. — Abu Sulayman says bodies travel within it without the sphere rotating them. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان)]
- 21:33/q13/p8 Its exact physical shape is unspecified by decisive evidence. — Tabari allows circular motion only; Ibn Atiyya warns against mill-iron speculation; Razi requires transmission; Alusi treats detailed models as possible, unproven. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]

## 21:33/q14 Where is the orbit and how many spheres are proposed?  [فَلَكٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Location and counting depend on differing physical schemes and reports.
- 21:33/q14/p1 The orbit lies between heaven and Earth or below the sky. — Ibn Zayd cites 25:61; Awf describes a dome and unfastened bodies, Jerusalem twelve miles from heaven and al-Ubulla farthest; Hasan says attachment would prevent running. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,عوف البكالي); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,حسان بن عطية)]
- 21:33/q14/p2 Seven planetary spheres have further stellar or enclosing levels. — Wahidi reports seven planetary spheres; Qurtubi prefers seven below heavens and names zodiac and greatest as eighth and ninth; Alusi reports seven plus fixed-star and daily-motion spheres. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المنجمون); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q14/p3 There may be fewer or more than nine, with extra nested levels. — Alusi reports eight- and seven-sphere schemes, Avicenna's doubt about fixed stars, hidden motion differences such as Herschel's, and epicycles and lunar node spheres; similar movements do not prove one sphere. [10 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q14/p4 A planet's sphere may sit inside each heaven, with intervals described by reports. — Alusi prefers sound 500-year reports; allows an internal mover, fixed stars above the heavens or within seventh, doubts many cosmological reports and disputes astronomical distances. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 21:33/q14/p5 All stars may lie in the lowest heaven. — Alusi says separate deferent-like spheres fit 37:6. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q14/p6 The sun is in the fifth heaven in winter and seventh in summer. — Ibn Amr says it could not stay in the apparent lowest heaven. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن عمرو)]
- 21:33/q14/p7 Sun and moon face heaven and turn their backs toward Earth, lighting both. — Ibn Amr cites 71:15–16. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن عمرو)]

## 21:33/q15 What analogy describes the orbit's shape or movement?  [فَلَكٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Different analogies all illustrate circularity.
- 21:33/q15/p1 It resembles a millstone, mill iron or spindle whorl. — Mujahid, Ibn Abbas and Hasan offer forms; Wahidi describes stars about a pivot; Ibn Kathir says parts turn relative to each other; Mujahid connects 55:5; Ibn Atiyya warns not to make the analogy an exact mechanism. Qatada reports sun, moon, night and day moving in the heaven's orbit. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد)]
- 21:33/q15/p2 It resembles a ball's circular motion. — Al-Mawardi gives the comparison. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 21:33/q15/p3 It resembles a circling horse. — Ibn Masud's hadith compares such a horse and the starry sphere. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 21:33/q16 Do stars move intrinsically, with spheres, or both?  [فِى فَلَكٍۢ يَسْبَحُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Intrinsic versus carried movement.
- 21:33/q16/p1 Stars move themselves through resting spheres or courses. — Razi and Bursevi's Imam favor this apparent fish-in-water reading; Alusi says the verse suggests travel within sphere thickness yet is ambiguous about rest; Elmalılı says old carrier astronomy is overturned; Abu Sulayman has motion without the sphere rotating the stars. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 21:33/q16/p2 Moving spheres carry stationary embedded stars. — Philosophers reject independent travel as rupturing solid spheres; Bursevi likens signet and ring; Alusi challenges the proof and exclusivity. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(جمهور الفلاسفة وأصحاب الهيئة); BURSEVI(الفلاسفة,شرح التقويم); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة,بعضهم); RAZI-FULL-; BURSEVI(الإمام)-; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 21:33/q16/p3 Sphere and star can both move, same or opposed, at equal or differing speeds. — Razi, Ibn al-Shaykh and Alusi list the models as possible. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ,الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة,بعض المسلمين والشيخ الأكبر)]
- 21:33/q16/p4 A separate small ring or hollow track could carry a planet. — Alusi allows a ring as wide as the planet, or a hollow course in moving or resting sphere, admitting a vacuum against Aristotle. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q16/p5 Stars might be suspended from chains of light moved by angels. — Alusi doubts this report but affirms divine power. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q16/p6 The orbit itself may be assigned the running. — Qurtubi reports this grammatical alternative. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 21:33/q17 Can the spheres be pierced or altered?  [فَلَكٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Strength of philosophical proof.
- 21:33/q17/p1 Spheres are solid, simple and incapable of rupture or decay. — Razi reports philosophers; Suhrawardi argues living spheres would either lack sensation of parts or suffer endless pain if pierced; Alusi calls this rhetoric. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(جمهور الفلاسفة وأصحاب الهيئة); ALUSI-FULL(السهروردي); RAZI-FULL-; BURSEVI(الإمام)-; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 21:33/q17/p2 No proof bars rupture, rejoining, vacuum or hollow tracks. — Razi and Bursevi's Imam reject the premise; Alusi says the straight-motion proof at most concerns the outer sphere, and the simplicity proof is weak. Al-Razi, as quoted by Al-Alusi, says God moves the whole by choice and a sphere's imparted movement could be through its own power rather than physical contact. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإمام); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعضهم)]
- 21:33/q17/p3 Sphere properties require transmitted evidence. — Razi withholds physical certainty; Study Quran attributes this caution to Qurtubi and Razi. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī and al-Rāzī)]

## 21:33/q18 What directions of celestial motion are proposed?  [يَسْبَحُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Observed relative displacement and trajectories.
- 21:33/q18/p1 Fast daily east-to-west combines with slow west-to-east motion. — Razi reports conjunctions, moon moving away from sun and zodiacal fixed-star drift; Alusi reports outer-sphere carrying, latitude-90 qualification and the ant-on-mill illustration. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(جمهور الفلاسفة وأصحاب الهيئة); ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة والهيئيون)]
- 21:33/q18/p2 All motion can be east-to-west at different speeds. — Razi says opposite simultaneous motions demand two places at once; ancients cite nature and faster outer bodies. Alusi rejects the model from lunar and solar observed paths, meridian timing, day length and zodiacal order. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعض الأوائل); RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 21:33/q18/p3 Opposite movements coexist in enclosing spheres and epicycles. — Alusi cites Sun's lag behind an equatorial point; an epicycle's upper and lower parts move in opposite directions. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q18/p4 Bodies move north and south too. — Razi says latitude variation prevents extreme seasons. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 21:33/q18/p5 Earth's rotation has been offered to explain the motions. — Alusi reports the proposal under the opposite-motion objection and calls it false. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]

## 21:33/q19 What daily paths are reported for sun and moon?  [ٱلشَّمْسَ ٱلْقَمَرَ · reports]
turns on: Different descriptions of daily travel.
- 21:33/q19/p1 They pass west beneath Earth to east, then across sky to west. — Muqatil gives the circuit; Ibn Abbas likens the Sun and Moon to a waterwheel above and below Earth. [2 notes: MUQATIL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 21:33/q19/p2 The sun rises only when ordered. — Ikrima's drawn-bow likeness suggests compelled movement. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 21:33/q20 What does yasbahun mean?  [يَسْبَحُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the swimming image denotes rapid motion, floating or kinds of movement.
- 21:33/q20/p1 They run, revolve or move swiftly along their courses like swimmers. — Mujahid compares spindle and mill; Ibn Abbas and Ikrima say revolve; Wahidi and Qurtubi cite 79:3 and the running horse, including al-Aʿsha's verse; Ibn Ashur sees unimpeded paths that do not intersect. Al-Baydawi specifies swift movement across the orbit's surface, like a swimmer over water. [34 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه,الكلبي,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأعشى); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,عكرمة,قتادة,مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 21:33/q20/p2 They literally float through a contained wave or water. — The wave reading takes swimming in its water sense; Ibn Atiyya calls this literal; Wahidi and Razi challenge the water-only premise. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BURSEVI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 21:33/q20/p3 They traverse good and evil, hardship and ease. — Tabrisi transmits Ibn Abbas's interpretation. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 21:33/q20/p4 It describes bodies' intrinsic motion. — Alusi calls this apparent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q20/p5 It describes carried apparent motion. — Others prefer visible carried motion to unknown intrinsic motion. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 21:33/q20/p6 It can include both intrinsic and carried motion. — Alusi reports this inclusive view. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 21:33/q21 What variant reading replaces yasbahun?  [يَسْبَحُونَ · readings]

## 21:33/q22 Why the masculine rational plural yasbahun?  [يَسْبَحُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Personification, referent count, cadence or literal reason.
- 21:33/q22/p1 Swimming or obedience personifies nonhuman bodies and licenses rational plural agreement. — Sibawayh cites stars and ants, 12:4 and 27:18, with al-Nabigha's poem; Farra, Zajjaj and others compare 41:11, akaluni al-baraghith and Great Bear poetry. Razi and Bursevi say this does not prove rationality. [26 notes: SIBAWAYH(النابغة الجعدي); FARRA; ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); NAHHAS(الفراء,سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق، أبو عبيدة، الفراء، المبرد,الجعدي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه والفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); TABATABAI; TAB-FULL]
- 21:33/q22/p2 It includes unnamed stars with sun and moon. — Jishumi and Razi infer more than two; Abu Hayyan supplies a conjunct; Samin cites other verses. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI]
- 21:33/q22/p3 Sun and moon denote repeated risings or classes. — Zamakhshari and Baydawi give the successive-risings explanation; Elmalılı prefers inclusion of all stars, some moon-like. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 21:33/q22/p4 It follows the sense of kull or grammatical predominance. — Abu al-Baqa says each swimming entails all swimming; Biqai says predominance; Burhan objects to making each individual plural. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 21:33/q22/p5 The form suits the verse ending. — Kisai cites 54:44; Samin mentions cadence. Al-Alusi records honorific precedence as another rationale. [5 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:33/q22/p6 The bodies really are rational beings. — Avicenna argues from this form and Joseph's dream (12:4); Alusi reports some Muslims; Razi and Bursevi answer with personification. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو علي بن سينا); BURSEVI(ابن سينا); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; BURSEVI-]

## 21:33/q23 Are the celestial movers rational or living?  [يَسْبَحُونَ · theology]
turns on: Literal grammatical inference and nature of movers.
- 21:33/q23/p1 Planets or spheres are living rational beings. — Avicenna infers from agreement and 12:4; philosophers describe spheres as living, knowing and volitional; theologians object. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو علي بن سينا); BURSEVI(ابن سينا); ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة); RAZI-FULL-; BURSEVI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 21:33/q23/p2 Agreement does not prove literal rationality. — Razi and Bursevi cite personification; Bursevi invokes 27:18 and considers speech to proceed from the heart rather than tongue, mentioning angels, jinn and parrots. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 21:33/q23/p3 Immaterial rational souls move the celestial bodies. — Bursevi reports spiritual thinkers who distinguish movers from matter while linking them to spheres. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض أهل الحقيقة)]

## 21:33/q24 What did a Friday creation report say?  [ٱلَّيْلَ ٱلنَّهَارَ · reports]
- 21:33/q24/p1 God created night and day during two of Friday's hours. — Al-Durr reports a reply to Jews asking the Prophet about Friday. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 21:33/q25 What wordplay occurs in kull fi falak?  [كُلٌّۭ فِى فَلَكٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 21:33/q25/p1 It reads identically forward and backward, an even palindrome. — Itqan gives 74:3 as the other Qur'anic example; Ibn Ashur notes seven letters and Sakkaki's term al-maqlub al-mustawi. [3 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 21:33/q26 What inward meanings are assigned to the signs?  [* · inward]
turns on: Distinct spiritual correspondences.
- 21:33/q26/p1 Night and day are soul and intellect, lit by spirit and heart, ascending toward God. — Qashani gives spiritual ranks and stations. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 21:33/q26/p2 Night and day are the gnostic's contraction and expansion. — Kashifi links majesty to contraction and beauty and generosity to expansion. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 21:33/q26/p3 Sun is stable divine-unity realization; moon is waxing and waning states. — Kashifi contrasts a fixed witnessing presence with changing states between loss and full realization. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]

## 21:33/q27 How should cosmological claims be weighed?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Authority of reports, apparent wording and observation.
- 21:33/q27/p1 Stay with sound reports and avoid unwarranted precision. — Tabari refuses a shape without scripture or report; Razi requires transmission; Alusi says early generations had little practical reason to describe cosmology and doubts many reports; Study Quran attributes revelation-based caution to Qurtubi and Razi. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī and al-Rāzī)]
- 21:33/q27/p2 Observation supports a sphere and allows reports to be interpreted. — Ghazali sides with sensory evidence and reads 21:33 as suggesting circularity without explicit flatness. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الغزالي)+]
- 21:33/q27/p3 Scripture should guide reconciliation of scientific theories. — Elmalılı says science should be reconciled with Qur'an when they seem to conflict. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 21:33/q27/p4 Astronomical schemes remain possible but not exclusively proved. — Alusi questions numbers, distances and motion details, citing modern London and Ibn Arabi alternatives. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:33/q28 How is sabaha related to tasbih in Arab usage?  [يَسْبَحُونَ · meaning]
- 21:33/q28/p1 Tasbih means declaring God free of evil, prayer, or wonder; swimming and running are related terms. — Jishumi cites poetry on Alqama's boast for subhana and records the wonder reading. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
