# 76:25: 10 questions

## 76:25/q01 What practice does remembering the Lord's name call for?  [وَٱذْكُرِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imperative denotes prescribed prayer, comprehensive devotion, praise by heart and tongue, affirmation of oneness within prayer, or an inward realization.
- 76:25/q01/p1 It includes sustained remembrance in worship, supplication, proclamation, and other devotion, including prescribed and voluntary prayer. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it persistent remembrance, with night prostration, prayer or saying subḥān Allāh. Bayḍāwī and Bursawī allow remembrance alongside the prayer reading; Bursawī says morning to evening means all times. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes preaching, obligatory and voluntary worship, exhortation through fear and hope, supplication and seeking forgiveness; 11:114–115 calls prayer remembrance. Elmalılı includes invocation, the call to prayer and prayer, citing 2:153. Kur'an Yolu prefers broad worship and devotion, especially night prayer with glorification, supplication, repentance and forgiveness. Study Quran's Aj also allows remembrance every moment. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN(Aj, JJ, Ṭs); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 76:25/q01/p2 It is prayer or naming God in the daily prayers. — Ṭabarī has calling on God during dawn, noon and afternoon prayer. Baghawī takes night prostrations as Maghrib and Isha and long night glorification as voluntary prayer after the obligatory prayers. Biqāʿī calls prayer the best bodily worship because tongue, heart and limbs jointly affirm God's existence and oneness. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 11:114–115 for prayer as remembrance. Study Quran reports Aj, JJ and Ṭs for naming God in daily prayers. The Kashshāf author reads the command as maintaining the dawn and afternoon prayers. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj, JJ, Ṭs); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); RAZI-FULL(قوم); KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 76:25/q01/p3 It is verbal and inward praise of God throughout the day and night. — Rāzī says remembrance by heart and tongue at all times, citing 33:41–42. Kur'an Yolu reports Rāzī's second reading as verbal glorification and belief in God's transcendence throughout day and night. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 76:25/q01/p4 It is affirming God's oneness in prayer. — Muqātil specifies three takbīrs and testimony to God's oneness at each of Fajr and Maghrib, repudiating shirk. Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī likewise explain remembrance as affirming oneness in prayer. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 76:25/q01/p5 It is realizing one's Essence as God's greatest Name and manifesting its rights and perfections. — Qāshānī places this from primordial beginning to end. Bursawī reports Qāshānī's formulation from the rise of divine light to its setting and concealment. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 76:25/q02 What does the Lord's name point to in remembrance?  [ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the name denotes God's knowable attributes or the Supreme Name in an inward reading; the latter is expressed differently by Qāshānī and the Sufis reported by Study Quran.
- 76:25/q02/p1 God's knowable names and attributes, while His essence remains beyond created minds. — Rāzī connects the command to the gift of revelation and guidance in 76:23 and obedience to God alone. Asad says the attributes are manifested in creation; he follows Rāzī in distinguishing knowledge of God's existence and attributes from grasping the nature of His Reality. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 76:25/q02/p2 The greatest divine name, understood inwardly as one's own Essence or as Allāh. — Qāshānī speaks of realizing one's Essence and manifesting the Name's rights and perfections from beginning to end; Bursawī reports this as the rise and setting of divine light. Study Quran reports many Sufis taking the verse as invocation of God's Supreme Name, Allāh; these accounts differ in expression. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني); STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 76:25/q03 What time does bukratan cover?  [بُكْرَةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the morning term marks dawn specifically or extends as far as noon.
- 76:25/q03/p1 Dawn or early morning. — Muqātil, Ibn Zayd as reported by Ṭabarī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Biqāʿī, and the prayer interpretations use it for Fajr. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it the early part of the day. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 76:25/q03/p2 The early morning through noon. — Elmalılı gives this wider span and says the two time terms together cover the day and its morning, noon and afternoon prayers. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 76:25/q04 How is bukratan inflected here?  [بُكْرَةًۭ · grammar]
- 76:25/q04/p1 It is indefinite and declinable, matching the indefinite aṣīlan; it becomes definite only with the article. — Al-Naḥḥās grounds its declension in its indefinite use and pairing with aṣīlan. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 76:25/q05 What time does aṣīlan cover?  [أَصِيلًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether aṣīl begins at noon or only after Asr, and whether 'evening' is a broad or narrow time label.
- 76:25/q05/p1 The evening or late part of the day. — Al-Zajjāj glosses aṣīl as evening and supports it with aṣalnā, 'they entered the evening.' Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it late day; Ibn Kathīr describes remembrance at the beginning and end of day. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 76:25/q05/p2 It can extend from noon toward sunset, covering both Zuhr and Asr. — Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī and Bursawī explicitly say the term includes both later prayers. Bursawī gives noon onward as one possible span; Ālūsī says noon to sunset. Elmalılı places its start in the afternoon and its end in evening, together with bukra spanning the day. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 76:25/q05/p3 It can mean the period after Asr until sunset. — Bursawī offers this as an alternative to a span beginning at noon. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 76:25/q06 Which prayers are assigned to morning and evening remembrance?  [بُكْرَةًۭ أَصِيلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The span of aṣīl and whether the time references denote a subset of prescribed prayers or the five prayers together with the next verse.
- 76:25/q06/p1 Fajr, Zuhr and Asr; in a five prayer reading, Maghrib and Isha follow in the night command. — Ṭabarī, Māwardī, Wāḥidī reporting ʿAṭāʾ, al-Kalbī and Muqātil, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, Bursawī and Elmalılı assign the three daytime prayers. Ibn al-Jawzī gives noon and afternoon together as an alternative to Asr alone. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ibn Ḥabīb and others mapping the surrounding times to all five prayers; Rāzī reports a group doing likewise, with long night glorification as night vigil. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Zayd on five prescribed prayers, with evening including noon and glorification denoting night prayer. Kur'an Yolu reports Rāzī's five prayer account and says extended night worship is obligatory for the Prophet but recommended for his community. Baghawī says night prostrations are Maghrib and Isha, followed by voluntary worship. Biqāʿī says other prayers occur in the following phrase. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء والكلبي ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن حبيب وغيره); RAZI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس وابن زيد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 76:25/q06/p2 Fajr and Asr. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives Asr as one reading of aṣīl. Biqāʿī says dawn and afternoon prayers were the only early Islamic prescriptions and were two cycles each. The Kashshāf author assigns morning and evening remembrance to maintaining the dawn and afternoon prayers. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 76:25/q06/p3 Fajr and Zuhr. — Ṭabarī reports Ibn Zayd assigning al-aṣīl to Zuhr. Ālūsī allows Fajr and Zuhr, explaining that aṣīl can cover noon to sunset. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 76:25/q06/p4 Fajr and Maghrib. — Muqātil says each has three takbīrs and testimony to God's oneness, repudiating shirk. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 76:25/q06/p5 The two evening prayers. — Al-Thaʿlabī uses this designation for morning and evening remembrance; he separately assigns night prostration and long glorification to voluntary night prayer. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 76:25/q07 Why are morning and evening named together?  [بُكْرَةًۭ أَصِيلًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the pair chiefly marks continuous practice or also carries distinct reflections at each time.
- 76:25/q07/p1 The two ends of the day express recurring or continual remembrance throughout daylight, and potentially at all times. — Ibn Kathīr calls them the beginning and end of day. Biqāʿī says naming both signals continual remembrance. Bursawī says morning to evening means all times. Ibn ʿĀshūr says both ends stand for all daylight hours, so opposition should not deter repeated preaching; he also includes set prayers and other devotions. Rāzī's verbal and inward reading requires remembrance at all times, citing 33:41–42. Kur'an Yolu reports this as day and night glorification. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 76:25/q07/p2 Each time prompts a distinct spiritual reflection: waking recalls resurrection, and evening recalls the world's end. — Biqāʿī calls waking a minor death and takes morning and evening remembrance as reminders of resurrection and the world's ending respectively. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 76:25/q08 What is the command's legal force?  [وَٱذْكُرِ ٱسْمَ رَبِّكَ · sciences]
turns on: Whether the command was abrogated or remains recommended; the reported distinction between Prophet and community concerns the accompanying extended night prayer.
- 76:25/q08/p1 It was obligatory but was abrogated, leaving only the five daily prayers obligatory. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this from Ibn Zayd and others. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد وغيره)]
- 76:25/q08/p2 It remains recommended. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this from another group. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم)]
- 76:25/q08/p3 The extended night prayer in the accompanying command is obligatory for the Prophet and recommended for his community. — Kur'an Yolu reports this within Rāzī's five prayer interpretation of the passage. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]

## 76:25/q09 Does the prayer reading require dating this verse after the five prayers were prescribed?  [بُكْرَةًۭ أَصِيلًۭا · sciences]
turns on: Whether the verse was revealed after the Ascension, when the five prayers were prescribed; Bursawī says the verse's date is unknown.
- 76:25/q09/p1 The continuous remembrance reading is needed only if the five prayers had not yet been prescribed. — Bursawī reports Saʿdī al-Muftī's premise that the prayers were prescribed at the Ascension. [1 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); BURSEVI-]
- 76:25/q09/p2 The verse's unknown date prevents assuming that it followed the Ascension. — Bursawī says the Ascension was a year before the Hijra; Saʿdī's argument works only if the verse came afterward. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]

## 76:25/q10 How does the command relate to patience and opposition in its context?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize different aspects of the surrounding passage: spiritual support, preparation for worship, limits on obedience, divine guidance, or the opponents' motive.
- 76:25/q10/p1 Remembrance and worship support the Prophet's patience, resolve and persistence amid opponents' words and attacks. — Rāzī places remembrance after the prohibition on obeying wrongdoers and beside night prostration and glorification in 76:26. Elmalılı connects patient endurance and invocation with 2:153. Ibn ʿĀshūr says continued preaching and varied remembrance help him endure opponents; his parallels are 15:97–98 and 73:8,10. Iṣlāḥī urges trust in God and patience through prayer. Kur'an Yolu says devotion strengthens resistance to polytheists. Sāmarrāʾī links remembrance to crisis and cites 15:97–99, 8:45, Jonah's rescue through glorification in 37:143–144 and 21:87, and glorification's high worth. Study Quran reads it as counsel amid worldly tribulations, citing 15:97–98. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 76:25/q10/p2 Remembrance remedies spiritual harm and prepares for worship through knowledge of God. — Biqāʿī calls unhindered remembrance the remedy, says knowing God is worship's aim, and calls thought joined to worship its highest form. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 76:25/q10/p3 The preceding ban concerns sinful obedience, while requests involving neither sin nor disbelief may be accepted. — Biqāʿī distinguishes outward from inward sin and cites 6:120; this qualifies the preceding command not to obey unbelievers. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 76:25/q10/p4 The revelation and guidance call the Prophet to obey God alone. — Rāzī places the instruction after the gift of revelation in 76:23. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 76:25/q10/p5 Opponents resist because they prefer immediate gains to the certain Hereafter. — Iṣlāḥī says they fabricate objections despite the Hereafter's certainty and the ease with which God can recreate them. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
