# 37:143: 20 questions

## 37:143/q01 What does falawlā mean here?  [فَلَوْلَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the reported hallā generalization applies to this occurrence, especially to lawlā with prefixed fa; the notes question the generalization with other Qur'anic uses.
- 37:143/q01/p1 It introduces a counterfactual condition: without Jonah's glorification, the result in 37:144 would have followed. — Ibn Qutayba says the following answer states what would have happened but for the condition. Ibn al-Jawzi calls this an original conditional use. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu likewise make his being a glorifier the preventing condition. Al-Burhān says the predicate after lawlā is omitted and understood from the answer; affirmative answers normally take a lām, although it may be implicit. Suyūṭī cites other conditional lawlā constructions with omitted answers at 12:24, 28:82, and 28:10. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BURHAN; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ITQAN]
- 37:143/q01/p2 Al-Khalīl reportedly excepted Jonah's lawlā from a general Qur'anic sense of hallā, an exhortation; that generalization is disputed. — Ibn Barjān quotes al-Khalīl as saying all Qur'anic lawlā means hallā except Jonah's; al-Burhān questions the exception. Suyūṭī questions the wider generalization through other verses. Abū Mālik via al-Suddī excepts both 10:98 and 37:143 from the sense fa-hallā; Suyūṭī says this may clarify that al-Khalīl had prefixed fa in mind. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو مالك); BURHAN(الخليل)-; ITQAN(الخليل)-]

## 37:143/q02 How is the answer to lawlā construed?  [فَلَوْلَآ أَنَّهُۥ · grammar]
- 37:143/q02/p1 The lām in the following answer belongs to lawlā, not to anna. — Al-Kisāʾī states that the lām does not govern anna; al-Naḥḥās agrees it marks lawlā's answer. Al-Qurṭubī adds that anna therefore does not take kasra. Al-Burhān says affirmative answers ordinarily carry the lām, even if it is understood rather than expressed. [3 notes: NAHHAS(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي والنحاس); BURHAN]

## 37:143/q03 Does kāna carry an independent meaning here?  [كَانَ · grammar]
- 37:143/q03/p1 On the reading of verbal praise, kāna may be superfluous: the condition is simply that Jonah was among those glorifying. — Al-Qurṭubī presents this parsing specifically with the interpretation of spoken glorification. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 37:143/q04 What does being among al-musabbiḥīn mean here?  [ٱلْمُسَبِّحِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether tasbīḥ here is prayer, worship in general, remembrance and spoken praise, the particular plea of 21:87, or repentance; Ibn ʿAbbās's broad prayer gloss and the proper verbal sense are both cited.
- 37:143/q04/p1 Being among those who pray, including voluntary prayer. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, al-Suddī, Ibn Masʿūd and others gloss tasbīḥ as prayer; an unnamed report specifies voluntary prayer. Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī transmit Ibn ʿAbbās's broader assertion that every Qur'anic tasbīḥ means prayer. Al-Ālūsī warns that a technical sense needs no contextual clue only if it is the sole established sense; competing or figurative senses require a clue. The notes differ over whether this prayer occurred before or inside the fish, treated separately below. [34 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(غير مسمى); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,سعيد بن جبير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); NAHHAS(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة,قتادة وابن عباس وأبو العالية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير والضحاك وعطاء بن السائب والسدي والحسن وقتادة,بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI(سهل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,ابن عباس)]
- 37:143/q04/p2 Being a worshipper or one who fulfills God's due through established righteous deeds. — Wahb glosses the term as worshippers; Sahl says fulfillment of God's rights before affliction. Abū al-ʿĀliya and al-Rabīʿ point to prior righteous deeds, and Muqātil combines prayer and obedience. This is a broader description than a particular utterance. [13 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(وهب); TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية); NAHHAS(الربيع بن أنس); SULAMI(سهل); MAWARDI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد ووهب بن منبه); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(سهل); ALUSI-FULL(بعض آخر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî)]
- 37:143/q04/p3 Remembrance and verbal glorification, declaring God free of what is unfitting to Him. — Al-Ṭabarī also reports a view explicitly preferring glorification to prayer. Al-Ṭabrisī explains declaring God transcendent and remembering Him. Ibn Jubayr takes the proper sense to be stated words of glorification or equivalent; Ibn Jurayj and another Ibn Jubayr report give Subḥān Allāh. Al-Qurṭubī prefers speech joined to the heart, citing the report in which angels heard Jonah's voice. Al-Biqāʿī includes heart, tongue, and limbs. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,بعضهم); ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 37:143/q04/p4 Jonah's particular plea, 'There is no god but You; glory be to You; I was among the wrongdoers' (21:87). — Muqātil quotes the plea as a confession of God's oneness and purity and Jonah's wrongdoing. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and others identify the verse with it; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it includes repentance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads its praise as disavowing what Jonah's departure might seem to imply about God; Iṣlāḥī calls it a model confession and request for pardon. Al-Ālūsī attributes this identification to Abū Ḥayyān. [20 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وغيره); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK, Ṭ)]
- 37:143/q04/p5 Repentance, specifically, is what his glorification denotes. — Quṭrub glosses it as repentance; another report says Jonah repented in ease. Asad combines remembrance with repentance. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī connect repentance with the confession of 21:87. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قطرب,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:143/q05 When was Jonah among those who glorified God?  [كَانَ ٱلْمُسَبِّحِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kāna points to established worship before distress, the invocation inside the whale, both, or the reported prenatal period; the prior reading and al-Ḥasan's inside-only report are explicitly contested.
- 37:143/q05/p1 Before the whale and in times of ease; prior worship was already his practice. — Muqātil locates prayer before the fault. Qatāda repeatedly says frequent prayer in ease; Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jubayr, al-Rabīʿ, Mujāhid, Sahl and others identify earlier devotion or good deeds. Al-Ḥasan is also transmitted denying prayer inside the fish in this sense, although other reports from him say the reverse. The majority is reported to prefer the prior reading, and Ibn Jarīr prefers it; Ibn Kathīr says an additional report would support it if sound. Quṭrub's repentance-in-ease version gives another prior act. [43 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); NAHHAS(الربيع بن أنس,قتادة); SULAMI(بعضهم,سهل); MAWARDI-FULL(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,الربيع بن أنس,قتادة,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن,الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وابن عباس وأبو العالية); TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، وقتادة، وأبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس,سعيد بن أبي الحسن,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI(سهل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جمهور العلماء)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+; TABATABAI-]
- 37:143/q05/p2 Inside the whale, especially the prayer or glorification in the darkness. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās places the act inside; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr identifies the prayer of 21:87. Some al-Ḥasan transmissions say Jonah first prayed there, while another says the contrary. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this among three views and rejects the prior-only reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī link the act and deliverance to his immediate confession and plea. [29 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير,عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير,ابن جريج,الحسن بن أبي الحسن); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن,عمران القطان عن الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وغيره); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK, Ṭ); TABATABAI+; TAB-FULL(قتادة)-; DURR-FULL(قتادة)-]
- 37:143/q05/p3 Both before the crisis and inside the whale: the verse encompasses an enduring practice. — Al-Qurṭubī says Jonah worshipped and prayed before entering yet also glorified within. Al-Biqāʿī describes an innate lifelong habit with heart, tongue, and limbs in ease and hardship. Al-Ālūsī explicitly prefers both periods. Elmalılı likewise combines earlier frequent glorification with the prayer in darkness; al-Bayḍāwī gives a lifelong alternative to the fish prayer. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 37:143/q05/p4 Even while he was in his parents' wombs. — Ibn Kathīr transmits this as the specification of some interpreters. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 37:143/q06 What in the wording suggests frequent or settled glorification?  [ٱلْمُسَبِّحِينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether frequency is conveyed by the intensive verbal form or by being counted among a definite class of habitual glorifiers.
- 37:143/q06/p1 Membership in the definite plural 'the glorifiers' presents a settled, abundant habit. — Al-Khafājī says naming Jonah as one of the group conveys deeper rootedness than simply calling him a glorifier; al-Ālūsī says the definite plural may signal constancy, and reads Qatāda as suggesting abundance. Al-Biqāʿī describes a habit with heart, tongue and limbs. Elmalılı and al-Sāmarrāʾī read a frequent or settled trait. Al-Ālūsī rejects deriving repetition merely from the intensive verbal form, since one can say Subḥān Allāh once. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الخفاجي,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 37:143/q06/p2 The intensive verbal form alone does not prove repeated glorification. — Al-Ālūsī objects that the act itself can be a single utterance of Subḥān Allāh; he instead allows the definite plural as evidence of habit. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 37:143/q07 Why was Jonah released from distress?  [فَلَوْلَآ ٱلْمُسَبِّحِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ over the timing of the effective worship and whether glorification by itself can account for rescue apart from divine grace and decree.
- 37:143/q07/p1 His established worship, prayer, and good deeds in ease supported him in hardship. — Al-Ṭabarī says God remembered earlier worship and rescued him. Qatāda's maxim says righteous deeds lift and support a person after a fall; al-Rabīʿ likewise cites past good. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk contrasts Jonah's earlier remembrance with Pharaoh's rejected belief at drowning (10:90–91). Al-Ḥasan says past good was stored for need. The angels' recognition reports likewise stress prior accepted deeds. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Baghawī describe God's gratitude for prior obedience. [35 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,الضحاك بن قيس,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك بن قيس,قتادة,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس,قتادة,قتادة وابن عباس وأبو العالية); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جمهور العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، وقتادة، وأبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,الربيع بن أنس,الضحاك,سعيد بن أبي الحسن,قتادة); BURSEVI(الوسيط); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+]
- 37:143/q07/p2 His glorification and repentant supplication inside the fish brought God's answer and rescue. — The plea in 21:87 is quoted or cited by Muqātil, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Iṣlāḥī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others. Al-Qurṭubī says it immediately led the fish to expel Jonah; Ibn ʿĀshūr pairs glorification with repentance. Iṣlāḥī says he turned wholeheartedly on recognizing punishment. [18 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وغيره); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK, Ṭ); TABATABAI+]
- 37:143/q07/p3 Both the former habit and his glorification inside the fish contributed. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly prefers the combined account. Al-Qurṭubī says Jonah prayed and worshipped before the fish and glorified within; al-Biqāʿī describes the practice in both ease and hardship. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 37:143/q07/p4 Deliverance ultimately came through divine grace and prior decree, not the power of Jonah's praise alone. — Al-Wāsiṭī says glorification by itself could not save him. Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes God's prior decree alongside the glorification. Al-Biqāʿī calls rescue an astonishing manifestation of divine greatness. [3 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 37:143/q08 What fate would have followed without the condition?  [فَلَوْلَآ ٱلْمُسَبِّحِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'remaining' until resurrection entails death in the fish; the grave wording and Kur'an Yolu's explanation specify death.
- 37:143/q08/p1 Jonah would have remained imprisoned in the whale until resurrection. — Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Jawzi explain the lawlā counterfactual by 37:144; al-Ṭabarī says God rescued him instead of leaving him there. The relevant Qatāda, Mujāhid, al-Kalbī and later reports repeat this outcome. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الربيع بن أنس,مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); DURR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ)]
- 37:143/q08/p2 The whale's belly would have become his grave; he would have died inside it and remained until resurrection. — Qatāda calls it his grave. Kur'an Yolu expressly interprets the counterfactual as death inside the fish followed by remaining there until resurrection. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 37:143/q09 How long did Jonah remain in the fish?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted durations differ; the notes offer no decisive reconciliation, though Kāshifī says forty is better known.
- 37:143/q09/p1 Forty days or nights. — Ibn Jurayj gives forty mornings; al-Suddī, the two Muqātil and al-Kalbī reports give forty days. Saʿīd b. Abī al-Ḥasan says forty days and nights before release and repentance. Bursawī gives forty nights and says Kāshifī regarded forty as better known, connecting Jonah's sick condition on shore to the stay. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن جريج); TABRISI(السدي، مقاتل بن سليمان، الكلبي); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن أبي الحسن); BURSEVI; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)+]
- 37:143/q09/p2 Three days or three nights. — Muqātil b. Ḥayyān says three days. The translated Book of Jonah says he prayed after three days and nights; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives three nights. Kāshifī also reports three days. [4 notes: TABRISI(مقاتل بن حيان); BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 37:143/q09/p3 Seven days. — ʿAṭāʾ gives seven days; Kāshifī also transmits seven. [2 notes: TABRISI(عطاء); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 37:143/q09/p4 Twenty days. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is named as the transmitter. [1 notes: TABRISI(الضحاك)]
- 37:143/q09/p5 One day and one night. — Ibn ʿĀshūr records this alternative. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 37:143/q09/p6 A few hours, or from midday until that evening without a night. — Ibn ʿĀshūr records a few hours; al-Shaʿbī specifies swallowing at midday and release that evening. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(الشعبي)]
- 37:143/q09/p7 An unspecified span of days and nights. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God kept Jonah alive and well in the fish for days and nights, a trial he understood as caused by his action. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 37:143/q10 What did Jonah experience and do inside the whale?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports supply different details of Jonah's experience and bodily condition; the notes do not settle their relation.
- 37:143/q10/p1 He heard creatures glorify God and called upon Him from the darkness. — Muqātil says water and wind challenged his flight and their own worship; Jonah recalled his deeds until inspired to invoke God. Anas's Prophetic report says he heard earth glorify beneath the whale; the Abū Hurayra report says sea creatures glorified. The prayer is associated with 21:87. [5 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); TABATABAI]
- 37:143/q10/p2 Feeling near death, he found his legs still moved, then prostrated and thanked God for a place of worship no one had used. — Al-Ḥasan gives this account in Ibn Abī Ḥātim and later collections. In some versions he calls the location a mosque or sanctuary; al-Ḥasan elsewhere is transmitted as saying prayer began inside the whale. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 37:143/q10/p3 He lifted whale flesh and pledged to build a mosque in an unprecedented place. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's unnamed account says Jonah lifted the flesh, vowed the mosque, and prayed; Abū Ḥayyān transmits the promise. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قائل غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(روي)]
- 37:143/q10/p4 The whale was instructed to hold Jonah as a refuge and place of worship, without eating or harming him. — Al-Qurṭubī says God forbade injury to flesh or bone; Shahr b. Ḥawshab calls it his refuge and mosque. Bursawī says the fish held him in trust and was forbidden to break bone or sever a joint. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(شهر بن حوشب); BURSEVI]
- 37:143/q10/p5 The whale traveled with a ship or through seven seas while Jonah glorified God. — One Abū Ḥayyān report says it accompanied the ship and then released Jonah unharmed, after which the people embraced Islam. Kāshifī says it crossed seven seas and Jonah's flesh and skin grew transparent so he saw wonders while remembering God. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(روي); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 37:143/q10/p6 His body wasted in the whale, and he called on God before being cast out. — Ḥumayd b. Hilāl says the gourd afterward fed Jonah until his body recovered. This varies from the report in which the whale released him unharmed. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال)]

## 37:143/q11 What role did the angels play in Jonah's rescue?  [* · reports]
- 37:143/q11/p1 They recognized his familiar prayer or faint glorification, recalled his prior devotion, and appealed to God; God commanded the whale to release him. — Anas's accounts have the voice rise from the darkness beneath the Throne or from a distant place; al-Ṭabarī is unsure whether the report is raised to the Prophet, as is Ibn Kathīr. Abū Hurayra's account stresses the faint voice and past good deeds. In ʿIkrima's version Jonah himself asks what good deed he has to offer and angels recognize a known voice in an unfamiliar place. God identifies Jonah and orders release. [8 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(أنس بن مالك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة عن النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 37:143/q12 What happened after the fish released Jonah?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on Jonah's condition on reaching shore and on the means of recovery.
- 37:143/q12/p1 He was cast out sick, sheltered or fed by a gourd, and later grieved its withering; God contrasted his grief with indifference to a hundred thousand people. — Ṭāwūs gives the plant and divine rebuke in ʿAbd al-Razzāq and al-Durr. Ḥumayd says the plant nourished Jonah until his wasted body recovered. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God ordered the fish to cast him out sick and grew a gourd for shade. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(طاوس); DURR-FULL(حميد بن هلال,طاوس); TABATABAI]
- 37:143/q12/p2 A female gazelle nursed him at the root of a gourd until his flesh returned. — Abū Hurayra reports that the gazelle came morning and evening with milk. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(أبو هريرة)]
- 37:143/q12/p3 The fish cast him onto shore after the prayer, sick or unharmed in different reports. — Al-Qurṭubī's Abū Hurayra report says sick; Abū Ḥayyān's report says unharmed. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also says sick. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(روي); TABATABAI]

## 37:143/q13 What wider account of Jonah's flight is reported alongside this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: The sources give different narrative supplements to the brief Qur'anic account.
- 37:143/q13/p1 The Book of Jonah account sends him to Nineveh, has him flee by ship and be swallowed after a storm and lot, then returned to preach after prayer. — Al-Biqāʿī's translated Book of Jonah says he prayed after three days and nights in the fish. Elmalılı's People of the Book account specifies a route toward Tarshish via Jaffa. Kur'an Yolu says the Biblical account broadly matches the Qur'an and adds his recommission and Nineveh's repentance. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes)]
- 37:143/q13/p2 Water and wind challenged Jonah's flight and reminded him of their worship, prompting his recollection and prayer. — Muqātil reports that they worshipped the One worshipped in heaven and earth and feared His punishment. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 37:143/q14 What does Jonah's prayer declare about God and his own act?  [ٱلْمُسَبِّحِينَ · theology]
- 37:143/q14/p1 It affirms God's sole divinity, declares Him free of the implication of Jonah's flight, and admits Jonah's own wrongdoing. — Muqātil quotes the threefold prayer of 21:87. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Jonah's conduct displeased God and cannot be assigned to God's will; his trial prompted confession and his praise clears God of the implication that Jonah turned to another. Asad and Iṣlāḥī emphasize repentance and forgiveness. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:143/q15 What lessons are drawn from Jonah's rescue?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: These are distinct applications of the verse rather than competing reports.
- 37:143/q15/p1 Remember and pray to God in ease so that He remembers and helps you in hardship. — The Prophetic counsel says to know God in prosperity; al-Ḍaḥḥāk applies it to Jonah and contrasts Pharaoh's rejected profession at drowning (10:90–91). Qatāda and al-Ḥasan say prior good deeds or supplication give support after a fall; al-Biqāʿī and Bursawī urge abundant remembrance. Al-Shāfiʿī applies the verse to glorification as a remedy for plague because remembrance lifts affliction. [20 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس); SULAMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(الضحاك بن قيس,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس,قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، وقتادة، وأبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,الضحاك); BURSEVI(الشافعي,الوسيط); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك بن قيس); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet Muhammad); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 37:143/q15/p2 Keep a sincere good deed hidden for a day of need, and invoke God through such deeds. — Al-Qurṭubī draws the counsel; he cites a Prophetic instruction to keep a hidden deed and the Bukhārī-Muslim cave report in which three people trapped by a boulder invoke sincere deeds for deliverance. Al-Burhān links the cave account with Jonah and a request for Moses' prayer. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURHAN]
- 37:143/q15/p3 No one should claim superiority over Jonah; God chose him after his fault. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq reports this as a Prophetic saying. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 37:143/q15/p4 The supplication of Dhū al-Nūn is answered when a Muslim invokes it. — Al-Qurṭubī cites Abū Dāwūd's report from Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ, attributing the promise to the Prophet. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص)]
- 37:143/q15/p5 Jonah's story shows God preparing prophets for difficult work in calling to monotheism. — Kur'an Yolu says extraordinary events trained prophets for this struggle and reads the account as instructive rather than bare history. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 37:143/q15/p6 Jonah's plea is an exemplary confession of sin and request for pardon. — Iṣlāḥī says he understood the disaster as punishment and immediately turned wholeheartedly to God; the prayer of 21:87 is the best expression of this confession. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 37:143/q16 How does this telling connect with other scripture and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the terse verse with different parallel accounts and the sūra's wider pattern.
- 37:143/q16/p1 Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ supplies Jonah's omitted prayer and rescue in 21:87–88. — Abū Ḥayyān says the present narrative omits earlier events and Jonah's prayer; combining the two tellings fills the gaps. Ibn Kathīr notes that the story appeared earlier in al-Anbiyāʾ. Study Quran also cites 21:87 for the prayer. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj, IK, Ṭ)]
- 37:143/q16/p2 The Biblical book of Jonah provides a fuller parallel account, including Jonah's prayer. — Study Quran points to Jonah 2:1–9 for fuller prayer and repentance. Kur'an Yolu says the Bible's account broadly matches the Qur'an while adding the flight and recommission; al-Biqāʿī's translation and Elmalılı's People of the Book account likewise give the flight. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kitâb-ı Mukaddes); BIQAI-FULL(مترجمه); ELMALILI(Âlûsî)]
- 37:143/q16/p3 The prophet narratives reinforce the sūra's refrain of divine peace, mercy, support, and reward for good deeds. — Iṣlāḥī says this refrain summarizes the anecdotes, and notes that after Aaron the prophets are ordered by their attributes rather than chronology. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 37:143/q16/p4 Jonah's deliverance belongs with other examples of relief obtained through prayer or good deeds. — Al-Burhān associates the request for Moses' prayer, Jonah's deliverance, and the cave companions' appeal through their best deeds. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 37:143/q17 What inward reading is drawn from the glorification?  [* · inward]
turns on: The sources offer distinct inward applications of the whale and glorification.
- 37:143/q17/p1 In crisis Jonah lost attachment to self and all else, remaining in God's oneness through glorification. — Al-Biqāʿī says this inward state illuminates his rescue. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 37:143/q17/p2 The lower self is like a whale engulfing the heart; constant remembrance brings rescue. — Bursawī makes the whale a figure for the lower self, adding that turning to God in ease brings help in hardship and good works support the person who falls. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 37:143/q18 How is the exception after the counterfactual parsed?  [فَلَوْلَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the construction has an implicit negation capable of licensing the accusative exception.
- 37:143/q18/p1 The accusative exception can be explained by an implicit negation in the construction. — Al-Burhān answers Ibn al-Shajarī's objection by saying negation is implicit in the verses' positive-seeming construction. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن الشجري)]
- 37:143/q18/p2 After explicit negation, substitution is the strongest parsing of an exception, though accusative remains possible. — Ibn al-Shajarī objects that the accusative exception in both 37:143 and 37:144 points to a positive construction; al-Burhān replies with implicit negation. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن الشجري)]

## 37:143/q19 When does tasbīḥ require a contextual clue for a technical sense?  [ٱلْمُسَبِّحِينَ · meaning]
- 37:143/q19/p1 A sole established technical sense needs none; figurative or competing technical senses need a contextual clue. — Al-Ālūsī states this while discussing the gloss of tasbīḥ as prayer. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 37:143/q20 Is a particular recitation chain attested for this passage?  [* · readings]
- 37:143/q20/p1 Ibn al-Qaṣṣāʿ reliably transmitted that ʿAlī ibn Shujāʿ read al-Miṣbāḥ with al-Ghaznawī, whom he met and heard. — Ibn al-Jazarī affirms this chain for his ten-reading recitation; this note gives the chain but no variant wording for the verse. [1 notes: NASHR]
