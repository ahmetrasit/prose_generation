# 17:44: 22 questions

## 17:44/q01 How is تُسَبِّحُ read and what licenses its agreement?  [تُسَبِّحُ · readings]
turns on: Whether agreement follows the feminine plural heavens, masculine/default syntax with an intervening phrase, or a transmitted past form.
- 17:44/q01/p1 Feminine present tusabbiḥu agrees with the heavens. — Al-Farrāʾ cites the feminine past sabbaḥat attributed to ʿAbd Allāh; Ibn Khālawayh cites Ubayy. Reports differ in lists of reciters: Abū ʿAmr, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ḥafṣ, Yaʿqūb and Khalaf occur, with variant transmission. Nonrational plural and nonliteral femininity allow this agreement. [14 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI(الحسن وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وحمزة والكسائي وحفص); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وحفص ويعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وحفص وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(علي); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو والأخوان وحفص); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم ويعقوب وخلف)]
- 17:44/q01/p2 Masculine present yusabbiḥu is also valid. — Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn Khālawayh invoke the small plural and masculine/default agreement; al-Farrāʾ cites 28:23 and 69:6, Ibn Khālawayh 9:5 and 28:23. Other explanations cite the separation from the feminine subject, sabʿ, and the permissibility of masculine agreement with nonrational plurals. Abū ʿUbayd is reported to prefer the yāʾ form; reports differ on reciter lists. [17 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وحفص ويعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو والأخوان وحفص,الباقون); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم ويعقوب وخلف); QIRAAT-ER; THALABI(الحسن وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وحمزة والكسائي وحفص)+]
- 17:44/q01/p3 A feminine past sabbaḥat is transmitted. — Attributed variously to ʿAbd Allāh/Ibn Masʿūd, Ubayy, Ṭalḥa ibn Muṣarrif and al-Aʿmash; these attributions vary among the sources. [6 notes: FARRA; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله والأعمش); ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:44/q02 How do the related yāʾ and tāʾ readings across the surrounding three verbs vary?  [تُسَبِّحُ · readings]
turns on: The readings shift between direct address and narration in the preceding verbs, and between feminine and masculine agreement here.
- 17:44/q02/p1 Ibn Kathīr reads yāʾ in all three. — Ibn Mujāhid and al-Jishumī record the threefold pattern; al-Jishumī says the first two report polytheists' words and the last uses masculine agreement. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID; JISHUMI(ابن كثير); RAZI-FULL]
- 17:44/q02/p2 Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī read tāʾ in all three. — Ibn Mujāhid and al-Jishumī also include this pattern, with direct address in the first two and feminine agreement in the third; al-Jishumī adds Khalaf. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID; JISHUMI(حمزة والكسائي وخلف بن هشام); RAZI-FULL]
- 17:44/q02/p3 Nāfiʿ, Ibn ʿĀmir, and Abū Bakr use tāʾ then yāʾ then yāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid reports their first tāʾ at 17:42; al-Jishumī adds Abū Jaʿfar and explains address, narration, then masculine agreement because of separation. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID; JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن عامر وأبو بكر عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(أهل المدينة والشام وأبو بكر)]
- 17:44/q02/p4 Abū ʿAmr uses tāʾ, yāʾ, then tāʾ. — The grammarian's sequence is given by Ibn Mujāhid and summarized by al-Rāzī; al-Jishumī reports a corresponding Abū Jaʿfar and Yaʿqūb combination. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID; JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL; TABRISI(أهل المدينة والشام وأبو بكر)]
- 17:44/q02/p5 Ḥafṣ uses yāʾ, yāʾ, then tāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Jishumī and al-Rāzī agree on this sequence. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID; JISHUMI(حفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL]

## 17:44/q03 Why are the heavens explicitly called seven?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ ٱلسَّبْعُ · rhetoric]
- 17:44/q03/p1 The number underscores that the heavens themselves, with their own beings, glorify God. — Al-Burhān gives this reason for specifying seven; Asad and Kur'an Yolu refer readers to other discussions of the seven heavens without developing a further explanation here. [3 notes: BURHAN; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 17:44/q04 Who are 'those in them'?  [وَمَن فِيهِنَّ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase designates inhabitants capable of direct praise or the broader inhabitants before the following universal expression.
- 17:44/q04/p1 The phrase includes angels, humans and jinn within the heavens and earth. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies believers among these beings; al-Wāḥidī's literal-praise explanation excludes devils and idol worshippers as not truly praising, while al-Ālūsī says context may exclude deniers and the silent. Yaḥyā includes believers and every creature. [8 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:44/q04/p2 The phrase reaches the inhabitants broadly, including all creatures. — Muqātil names plants and animals; al-Iṣlāḥī speaks of all beings between heaven and earth. The broad phrase then gives way to 'every thing.' [4 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 17:44/q05 Why is the plural pronoun used for heaven and earth?  [وَمَن فِيهِنَّ تَسْبِيحَهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the pronoun demands personification or follows ordinary feminine plural reference.
- 17:44/q05/p1 Rational treatment reflects their rational action of glorifying. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī give this account; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn transmit it. Al-Ālūsī says universal life would remove a need for special explanation. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:44/q05/p2 The feminine pronoun hun can refer to feminine plurals generally. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects Ibn ʿAṭiyya's assumption that hun is limited to rational beings; al-Samīn makes the same objection, recalling 17:36. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)]

## 17:44/q06 How does the lām in 'for Him' work?  [لَهُ · grammar]
- 17:44/q06/p1 It marks the object of testimony or is a clarifying lām. — Ibn ʿĀshūr understands tasbīḥ as bearing witness to God's transcendence, or compares the clarifying lām in al-ḥamdu lillāhi laka. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:44/q07 What does tasbīḥ with praise signify here?  [يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِهِۦ · meaning]
turns on: Whether emphasis falls on God's transcendence with praise or on glorification as creation's prayer.
- 17:44/q07/p1 It declares God free of defects, partners, and contingent limits, while praise affirms His perfections. — Al-Jishumī defines tasbīḥ as declaring transcendence. Al-Thaʿlabī adds obedience, lordship, existence and oneness; Bursawī speaks of freedom from possibility and origination. Al-Iṣlāḥī distinguishes negating negative traits from affirming higher attributes when joined to praise. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says need and deficiency disclose transcendence, while existence and beauty praise God's gifts. [6 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 17:44/q07/p2 The glorification of creation is its prayer. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says every Qur'anic tasbīḥ is prayer and every sulṭān proof; Ibn ʿAmr's remembrance formula calls glorification creation's prayer. Noah's counsel and Mujāhid's report likewise identify prayer with glorification. [4 notes: FARRA(سعيد بن جبير); TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 17:44/q08 What is the nature and extent of every thing's glorification?  [وَإِن مِّن شَىْءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether glorification is conscious utterance, the objective testimony of createdness, obedient operation, or a mode embracing these; and whether 'every thing' has literal exceptions.
- 17:44/q08/p1 Every created thing actually glorifies God, including inanimate things. — Muqātil includes plants and animals; al-Nakhaʿī, Mujāhid, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, Sufis and others cite food, pebbles, wood, doors and water. Al-Qurṭubī prefers literal universality from reports and David's mountains; God can create life and speech. Bursawī calls this sounder if inanimate speech is possible. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it conscious disclosure without requiring phonemes; prophetic reports and 21:79, 38:18, 34:10 support it. Al-Ālūsī cites strong audible reports, yet finally reconciles it with state testimony. Abū Ḥayyān reports God granting speech to lifeless things; al-Rāzī records literal advocates' appeal to the otherwise incomprehensible praise. A damaged al-Thaʿlabī report has an unidentified thing praising for three hundred years; al-Durr reports one created thing praising whenever a servant does and ʿIkrima naming pillars and trees. [88 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,عكرمة); ZAJJAJ(قيل); NAHHAS(بعضهم); THALABI(إبراهيم,وهب); MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(إبراهيم النخعي); BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(some scholars,إبراهيم النخعي); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو ذر,إبراهيم,عبد الله بن مسعود,عكرمة); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو صالح,أبو غالب الشيباني,أنس,إبراهيم,الحسن,النخعي,خيثمة,سليمان بن المغيرة,عبد الله بن مسعود,عكرمة,مسعر); BURSEVI(أبو عثمان المغربي,التأويلات النجمية,السهيلي,الشيخ الأكبر,الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL(أنس,ابن مسعود,الحسن,الخواص,الراغب,السجاد,الشيخ الأكبر,بعض الظاهرية,بعضهم); ELMALILI(Muhyiddîn-i Arabî ve sâir birçok sûfiyye,bazı müfessirîn); TABATABAI(أبو الصباح,أبو هريرة وجابر الأنصاري وابن عباس وأبي بن كعب وزين العابدين,علقمة وابن مسعود,مسعدة بن صدقة); STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الخازن)+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI(علي السمرقندي)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-; TABATABAI-]
- 17:44/q08/p2 Only living, ensouled, or growing things literally glorify; cut or dead material ceases. — Reports from Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Qatāda say living or spirited beings, including trees and plants. ʿIkrima says a tree does but a column does not; al-Ḥasan says a table once did. Yaḥyā reports severed stone and wood stop. Ibn Kathīr connects this with the fresh grave branches; al-Rāzī objects that treating inanimate matter as speaking while cut wood cannot is inconsistent. Al-Naḥḥās reports an unspecified restriction. [37 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(الحسن,الضحاك والحسن,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس,الحسن,الحسن والضحاك,عكرمة,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن والضحاك,عكرمة,قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة,يزيد الرقاشي); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة والضحاك,عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن,بعض العلماء,قتادة); DURR-FULL(الحسن,السدي,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,السدي,قتادة); NAHHAS(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:44/q08/p3 Every thing glorifies through its created state as a sign of its Maker. — Al-Zajjāj's reported view and al-Wāḥidī say created things signify a wise, defect-free Creator; al-Zajjāj objects that pagans already admit the Creator. Al-Rāzī says inert things cannot know or speak, each apple part and attribute testifies to a determiner, and one verb cannot be literal and figurative at once. Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān similarly avoid double usage. Al-Biqāʿī cites origination, precision and passing; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that such evidence is already understood and misses conscious praise. Abū Ḥayyān reports the condition of inanimate things as metaphorical testimony; Ibn al-Jawzī calls the heavens' praise evidence of divine creation and power. [39 notes: ZAJJAJ(قوم); NAHHAS(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL(بعض أهل المعاني); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(some scholars); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(صاحب الفنون); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(çokları); IBNASHUR-FULL(فخر الدين); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العلماء); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ-; BAGHAWI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 17:44/q08/p4 Both verbal praise and testimony through created states are encompassed. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows a word to bear both senses; al-Biqāʿī assigns speech to capable creatures and signs to all. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī allow combined senses or a broad figure; al-Ālūsī prefers every thing's state and voice, with rebuke for missed testimony. Al-Rāzī and al-Kashshāf resist combining literal and figurative senses; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects dividing creation by mode. [9 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(çokları); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 17:44/q08/p5 Creation glorifies by obedient submission to divine laws. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports submission as one explanation for inanimate things; Kur'an Yolu describes the cosmos' ordered operation and Raghib's distinction between voluntary conscious worship and universal obedience. The form can include human faith, acts, worship and morals. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râgıb el-İsfahânî)]
- 17:44/q08/p6 Each being manifests its distinctive perfection and seeks completion from God. — Al-Qāshānī distinguishes skies' permanence and influence, earth's stability and nurture, angels' knowledge, and immaterial beings' transcendence. Bursawī transmits him; al-Ālūsī calls such state-praise accepted and repeats the examples. [5 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني); ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:44/q08/p7 A report excludes donkeys and dogs from universal praise. — Ibn ʿAbbās is named in al-Durr and al-Ālūsī; al-Ālūsī doubts the attribution and sees no ground for preferring the exception to the unqualified reports. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:44/q08/p8 At sunrise Satan and wealthy humans are excepted from all creatures' praise. — The report in al-Durr limits this exception to the sunrise event; it does not assert their exclusion at every time. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 17:44/q09 What words or sounds are described as creation's glorification?  [يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports convey a shared formula, a heaven-specific hymn, natural sounds, or a spiritual mode of speech.
- 17:44/q09/p1 Creation's prayer is 'Subḥān Allāh' or 'Subḥān Allāh wa-bi-ḥamdihi'. — Noah instructs his son or two sons to say it, and reports call it a means of provision; versions vary between formulas and deathbed setting. Ibn Kathīr says Jābir's chain through Mūsā ibn ʿUbayda is weak and a longer Aḥmad version is unique. Ibn ʿAmr's expanded remembrance has profession, praise, greatness, glorification and reliance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains provision through manifest need. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو); THALABI(جابر بن عبد الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جابر بن عبد الله,عبد الله بن عمرو,قتادة); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد,نوح); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر); TABATABAI(عبد الله بن عمر)]
- 17:44/q09/p2 Each heaven has its own hymn of praise. — Luṭ ibn Abī Luṭ's report assigns seven different formulas, from praise of the Most High in the lowest to majesty and dignity in the seventh. A Night Journey report has the Prophet hear the upper heavens praising the majestic exalted Lord; Ibn Kathīr gives no grade. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن قرط); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,لوط بن أبي لوط); ALUSI-FULL(لوط بن أبي لوط)]
- 17:44/q09/p3 A creature's characteristic sound or act can be its praise. — Reports name a rooster's cry and wing motion, frog's croak, sea sound and waves, a door's creak, flowing water, cracking timber, and bird calls at dawn asking provision. A heavenly caller prompts a rooster; ʿAlī and al-Bāqir describe birds seeking daily food. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes hearing these sounds from grasping their meaning. [12 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو غالب الشيباني,ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب,محمد بن علي بن الحسين); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر,علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(أبو الصباح,أبو حمزة,عبد الله بن عمر,مسعدة بن صدقة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 17:44/q09/p4 Every creature may praise in its own tongue, including a heavenly or spiritual tongue. — Abū ʿUthmān says different languages are heard by opened inner ears; al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says inanimate things use a heavenly tongue and each particle has a spiritual kingdom; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes real meaning from the words senses supply, like dream images. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان المغربي); BURSEVI(أبو عثمان المغربي,التأويلات النجمية); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:44/q10 When, if ever, does a creature cease glorifying?  [وَإِن مِّن شَىْءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ · reports]
turns on: Whether altered or dead matter stops praise, and how reports about cut or soiled things fit universal wording.
- 17:44/q10/p1 Glorification ceases when a thing loses life, original state, or freshness. — Al-Ḥasan says cut stone, wood, and a former tree-table cease; Qatāda and al-Suddī say original things destined to die praise. Al-Miqdām's details vary by report: soil wetness, a bead's movement, a leaf falling, water changing, clothes becoming dirty or old, and animals no longer vocalizing. ʿĀʾisha is told to wash soiled clothes. Ibn Kathīr connects this with moist palm branches, but leaves the mechanism to God. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); THALABI(المقداد بن معد يكرب); BAGHAWI-FULL(المقدام بن معد يكرب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(خالد بن معدان عن المقدام بن معدي كرب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض العلماء); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,السدي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,السدي,بعضهم); TABATABAI(أنس,عائشة)]
- 17:44/q10/p2 Birds, fish and hunted creatures lose praise before being caught or cut. — Reports link hunted game, caught fish, cut trees and branches to neglected glorification. Some specify that no fish or bird is taken before abandoning its prescribed praise. Abū Bakr remarks on the cause; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records Shiʿi and Sunni routes. A report says an animal or insect lives until its praise ends, when God takes its soul; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says other angels mediate rather than the Angel of Death. [15 notes: NASAFI-FULL(السدي عن النبي); DURR-FULL(أبو بكر,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء وابن مسعود,أبو بكر); TABATABAI(أنس,إسحاق بن عمار,ابن مسعود)]
- 17:44/q10/p3 Glorification persists even in inert or altered material. — Al-Ḥasan reports soil glorifying after becoming a wall; other reports describe food, dry wood, house creaks, and a cooking pot praising. The universal-literal view does not condition praise on freshness. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,عكرمة); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,إبراهيم,الحسن,النخعي,خيثمة,سليمان بن المغيرة,عكرمة); TABATABAI(أبو الصباح,مسعدة بن صدقة); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 17:44/q11 What reports show particular creatures praising or responding?  [وَإِن مِّن شَىْءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ · reports]
turns on: Which particular report is being cited; the narrations differ in creature, scene and wording.
- 17:44/q11/p1 Food, water, pebbles, vessels, a tree trunk and a stone were heard or seen responding near the Prophet or others. — Ibn Masʿūd reports food heard during the Prophet's meal and water from his fingers; al-Bukhārī contains the food account. Pebbles praise in hands of the Prophet and three caliphs, while variants differ over other Companions. Thaʿlabī reports grapes and pomegranates at a meal. Anas reports thārīd heard by several. Jābir ibn Samura says a Meccan stone greeted the Prophet before his mission, perhaps the Black Stone. Reports add a groaning trunk, a cooking pot and household vessels. [20 notes: THALABI(أنس بن مالك,جعفر بن محمد); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(جابر بن سمرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو ذر,عبد الله بن مسعود); BIQAI-FULL(البخاري عن عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(أنس,خيثمة,سليمان بن المغيرة,عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(أنس,ابن مسعود); TABATABAI(أبو هريرة وجابر الأنصاري وابن عباس وأبي بن كعب وزين العابدين,علقمة وابن مسعود); ELMALILI]
- 17:44/q11/p2 The Prophet heard the heavens praise during the Night Journey. — A report has him returning from Jerusalem and hearing the highest heavens glorify; Ibn Kathīr does not grade it. Another says every span of the weighty creaking sky holds an angel in prostration and praise. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن قرط); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 17:44/q11/p3 David heard or was rebuked by the praise of other creatures. — Accounts describe David's mountains and birds, a frog with words David had never used, seventy thousand frogs standing on one leg, a more diligent night-praying frog, and a tiny pearl that rebukes his self-regard. Abū Mūsā says the red worm praises most. Some versions say a frog's praise surpassed David's. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو موسى,أنس بن مالك,ابن عباس,شهر بن حوشب,صدقة بن يسار); ALUSI-FULL(أنس بن مالك,شهر بن حوشب); TABATABAI]
- 17:44/q11/p4 Animals and places answer, remember, or testify. — Ibn Masʿūd says mountains ask about human remembrance; Anas says regions of earth ask each other morning and evening, and creatures hearing the call to prayer testify. Reports describe ants praising and God rebuking a prophet who burned an anthill; frogs praise in croaks; birds ask daily provision. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites animals and stones testifying to prophecy. [12 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,علي بن أبي طالب,محمد بن علي بن الحسين); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر,علي بن أبي طالب); TABATABAI(أبو حمزة,أبو هريرة,عبد الله بن عمر)]

## 17:44/q12 What conduct toward creatures or graves follows from their praise?  [وَإِن مِّن شَىْءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse grounds etiquette toward praising creatures, benefit at graves, or reward for their caretakers.
- 17:44/q12/p1 Respect animals and avoid striking faces, overburdening mounts, and needless killing of frogs or ants. — ʿIkrima advises against insulting animals or garments. Prophetic and ʿUmar reports forbid face-striking; related reports forbid branding faces. Mounts may remember God more than riders, so riders should not use them as seats for conversation. Al-Sakūnī lists six mount rights including feeding and watering. Frogs' croaking is praise and ants form a praising community. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,عكرمة,عمر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر,معاذ بن أنس); TABATABAI(أبو هريرة,السكوني,عبد الله بن عمر,محمد بن مسلم,معاذ بن أنس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 17:44/q12/p2 Fresh branches and recitation may benefit the dead. — The two-graves report says punishment for tale-bearing or backbiting and failing to avoid urine was hoped to ease until the palm branches dried. Some infer their ongoing praise caused relief. Al-Qurṭubī also supports planting trees and reciting Qur'an at graves, saying dedicated reward reaches the dead; Ibn Kathīr leaves the branches' mechanism to God. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض العلماء)]
- 17:44/q12/p3 A crop's glorification benefits the one who planted it. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū Qubayl say the reward of a praising crop goes to its owner or planter. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو قبيل,ابن عباس)]

## 17:44/q13 Does inanimate glorification require life and awareness?  [وَإِن مِّن شَىْءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ · theology]
turns on: Whether knowledge and speech presuppose ordinary life, another fitting life, or simply a created sound.
- 17:44/q13/p1 Actual praise can occur because God grants fitting life and knowledge to all things. — Al-Qurṭubī says God creates life and speech; Bursawī quotes Ibn ʿArabī on life throughout the world, the mountain knowing God's greatness, and audible praise being veiled. Al-Ālūsī adduces reports about all things testifying to the call to prayer, fever addressed as conscious, the moon, Nile and earth; awareness need not resemble animal sense. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says knowledge accompanies existence in proportion to it and true speech need not be phonetic. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الشيخ الأكبر); ALUSI-FULL(الخواص,السجاد,الشيخ الأكبر,بعضهم); TABATABAI]
- 17:44/q13/p2 God can make a stone speak by creating sound without giving it life. — Bursawī transmits al-Suhaylī's alternatives: create life and intelligence, or merely create the sound. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهيلي)]
- 17:44/q13/p3 Inert matter lacks the life needed for knowledge and speech; its praise is evidential. — Al-Rāzī says otherwise the proof from knowledge and power to life would collapse; he also argues literal advocates inconsistently end a slaughtered animal's or broken branch's praise. Al-Ālūsī reports the Imam's parallel objection. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]

## 17:44/q14 Why do people fail to understand their glorification?  [وَلَٰكِن لَّا تَفْقَهُونَ تَسْبِيحَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether failure is due to a veiled language, neglected inference, the inexhaustible detail of signs, or a nonphonetic mode of conscious speech.
- 17:44/q14/p1 Its languages or speech differ from human language and are normally veiled. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Nasafī, al-Kawwāshī and others say humans do not know other beings' tongues. Al-Sulamī says opened hearts can hear them; al-Ḥasan says unveiling household praise would make normal living impossible. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes hearing birds, thunder and frogs from understanding them. Bursawī objects that language difference alone cannot account for soundless beings; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects conventional human-like hidden words. [16 notes: MUQATIL; SULAMI(أبو عثمان المغربي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,مسعر); BURSEVI(أبو عثمان المغربي,الكواشي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; NASAFI-FULL+; BURSEVI-; TABATABAI-]
- 17:44/q14/p2 People neglect reflection on creation's testimony to God. — Al-Zajjāj's report says disbelievers miss the inference, but he objects that they already acknowledge creation. Al-Kashshāf, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect failure to defective reasoning and shirk. Al-Rāzī points to hidden countless apple parts and overlooked proofs of unity, justice, prophecy and resurrection; al-Biqāʿī says the wording is 'understand' rather than 'hear' because reflection is at issue. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this contextual explanation. [29 notes: ZAJJAJ(قوم); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(صاحب الفنون); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أهل الحقيقة,الزمخشري والبيضاوي وأبو السعود); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); IBNASHUR-FULL(فخر الدين); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 17:44/q14/p3 God teaches its reality selectively, and human understanding remains incomplete. — Al-Zajjāj suggests God grants knowledge of praise; al-Baghawī entrusts the reality of inert things to God. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows that no person fully understands all details. Al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say uncounted parts and attributes each bear testimony; Kur'an Yolu likens the remaining unknown to the scale of atoms and genetic code. Al-Qāshānī says only an attentive living heart perceives the unseen. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL(فخر الدين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 17:44/q14/p4 Their conscious disclosure is real but need not take audible human words. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says true speech is any disclosure of inward meaning and that a hearer's senses may present it as words, like dream images. People already understand mere evidence of a Creator, so the verse points beyond it. [5 notes: TABATABAI]

## 17:44/q15 Whom does 'you do not understand' address?  [لَّا تَفْقَهُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the rebuke follows the preceding polytheist argument, universal human limits, or the closing forgiveness.
- 17:44/q15/p1 It addresses polytheists or unbelievers who fail to infer God's transcendence. — Al-Zajjāj's objection is that they already accept Him as Creator; al-Kashshāf says they fail to follow that admission to its implication of no partners. Al-Ālūsī ties the address to the preceding false attributions, rejecting Ibn al-Munīr's interruption argument. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows this reading, as does Ibn ʿAṭiyya for heedless disbelievers. [20 notes: ZAJJAJ(قوم,قيل); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الزمخشري والبيضاوي وأبو السعود); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)-; TABATABAI-]
- 17:44/q15/p2 It addresses people generally, because they cannot comprehend all praise. — Al-Naḥḥās reports the universal addressee; Ibn al-Jawzī makes it follow literal praise. Bursawī reports a reading applying to Muslims and polytheists alike. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows that no human fully comprehends; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī does not require full comprehension. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI(أهل الحقيقة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 17:44/q15/p3 It addresses believers who fail to hear inanimate speech. — Ibn al-Munīr argues that the closing forgiveness cannot address unrepeant polytheists, whose association God does not forgive; al-Ālūsī rejects this as disrupting passage coherence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:44/q16 What does fiqh add to the verb 'understand'?  [تَفْقَهُونَ · meaning]
- 17:44/q16/p1 Fiqh is understanding that goes beyond bare knowledge, especially subtle or inferred knowledge. — Al-Jishumī says common usage restricts fiqh to inferred religious law, so God is not called faqīh. Al-Burhān says it exceeds simple knowing; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its choice over taʿlamūn signals subtle detail. Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes that audible praise still needs interpretation. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 17:44/q17 Is another reading reported for 'you do not understand'?  [لَّا تَفْقَهُونَ · readings]
- 17:44/q17/p1 A passive intensive lā yufqahū is transmitted. — Al-Ālūsī records it without further argument. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:44/q18 What do ḥalīm and ghafūr mean in this closing?  [حَلِيمًا غَفُورًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether the names are read as general patience and repentance, a temporal division, or a lexical contrast.
- 17:44/q18/p1 God delays punishment for sin, shirk or heedlessness and forgives those who repent. — Muqātil says He overlooks partner claims until an appointed time; Qatāda contrasts divine patience with human haste. Ibn Kathīr cites 11:102, 22:48, 4:110 and 35:41–45: persistent rebels may be seized, repenters forgiven. The names also show His rule is undiminished and mercy costs Him nothing, says al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī. Kur'an Yolu defines ḥalīm as patient despite rebellion and relates it to other divine names. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 17:44/q18/p2 Forbearance is in this life and forgiveness is for believers in the next. — Al-Qurṭubī pairs worldly clemency with otherworldly forgiveness; al-Nasafī specifically says believers' sins are forgiven. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن وأبو عمرو ويعقوب وحفص وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); NASAFI-FULL]
- 17:44/q18/p3 Ḥalīm is the opposite of foolishness. — Al-Jishumī gives this lexical contrast. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 17:44/q19 How do the closing words work grammatically and rhetorically?  [إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ حَلِيمًا غَفُورًۭا · grammar]
- 17:44/q19/p1 Ḥalīman is the accusative predicate of kāna. — Al-Jishumī gives the parsing; Ibn ʿĀshūr says kāna presents both names as established and enduring attributes. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 17:44/q20 Why does the verse end with forbearance and forgiveness?  [إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ حَلِيمًا غَفُورًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the immediate reason is failure to grasp signs, human disobedience amid sinless creation, or the prior declaration of transcendence.
- 17:44/q20/p1 God spares people who neglect signs, reason badly, and ascribe partners. — Muqātil cites the preservation of heavens and earth at 35:41; Yaḥyā cites 16:61, that immediate punishment would bring ruin. Al-Rāzī says culpable failure to infer fits these names better than inability to hear unfamiliar sounds. Al-Biqāʿī says a clear testimony makes neglect deserving of punishment. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the grave claim could bring down the heavens. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURHAN(صاحب الفنون); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 17:44/q20/p2 Creation praises sinlessly while humans disobey, so the names offer respite and pardon. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān cite a report that grazing beasts, bowed elders and nursing infants ward off punishment. Al-Burhān also links this with angels praising and seeking forgiveness for earth's people at 42:5; another al-Itqān view makes the names cover shortcomings of those praising God. [7 notes: BURHAN(صاحب الفنون); ITQAN(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 17:44/q20/p3 The ending connects God's transcendence with His merciful restraint. — Al-Jishumī reports one reading attaching the names to 'transcendent' and another on respite despite lies; al-Ālūsī calls a proposed direct attachment weak. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the names show sovereign power and unharmed mercy. [4 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 17:44/q21 How does this verse connect to its context and other scripture?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is the local anti-shirk argument, the sūra's tasbīḥ wording, or supporting scriptural depictions.
- 17:44/q21/p1 It completes the preceding rejection of partners: all dependent creation attests to its one Lord. — Ibn Kathīr contrasts universal praise with the claim of a divine child and cites 19:90–92; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says all creation comes from, depends on and returns to God. Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran connect it to 17:42 and created things' submission; al-Qurṭubī cites 38:17–18, 2:74 and 19:90–91. Al-Ṭabrisī says repeated proofs remove excuses although unbelievers flee. [8 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 17:44/q21/p2 It joins the sūra's recurring tasbīḥ language and the preceding 'Glory be to Him'. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says opening subḥān is timeless and unbounded and fits the sūra's tasbīḥ theme. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the universal-praise clause circumstantial to subḥānahu. Study Quran reports that some read 'Glory be to Him' as all creatures' universal prayer. Al-Ṭabrisī gives two ways of coordinating the preceding 'above what they say.' [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABRISI]
- 17:44/q21/p3 Other scriptures and verses depict creation's praise and God's restraint. — Al-Biqāʿī cites Psalms 68, 85, 88, 95, 97, 148 and 150, ranging from heavens and seas to creatures, nations, stars and instruments; 148 lists their divinely set limits. He also says David's Psalms repeatedly commend reflection. Ibn Kathīr links 35:41–45 and 11:102 to delayed judgment. Muqātil cites thunder at 13:13; Study Quran cites 13:13, 13:15, 16:49–50 and 22:18. [13 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN]
- 17:44/q21/p4 Some related recitation and reflection notes concern the preceding verse's call to heed proofs. — Al-Ṭabrisī discusses light/geminated li-yadhdhakkarū and says its point is reflection, not recalled memory, citing 28:51 and 38:29; he says Qur'anic repetition should induce recognition. Al-Sāmarrāʾī's presenter says grammatical and rhetorical mastery is needed to grasp such discourse; modern readers often lack the earlier Arabs' fluency. [4 notes: TABRISI(أهل الكوفة غير عاصم والباقون); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المقدم)]

## 17:44/q22 Why does this verse use min/man with those who praise?  [وَمَن فِيهِنَّ · rhetoric]
- 17:44/q22/p1 Man marks those who glorify, while mā elsewhere gathers all things. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 24:41 and the formulas in 59:24, 62:1, 64:1 and 57:1 to explain the wording. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
