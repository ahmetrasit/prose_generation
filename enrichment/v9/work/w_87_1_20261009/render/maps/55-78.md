# 55:78: 23 questions

## 55:78/q01 What does tabāraka mean here?  [تَبَٰرَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether baraka here denotes elevation, plentiful good, or permanence; Elmalılı favors elevation from the paired closing epithet.
- 55:78/q01/p1 Exalted and raised in rank. — Tabrisi says only God merits eternal, divine, self-powered, self-knowing life. Razi specifies rank, not spatial height. Alusi connects elevation with being above denial of favors. Elmalılı prefers this sense in the closing epithet and compares 25:1. Sulami adds transcendence above unbelievers' false claims. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI+]
- 55:78/q01/p2 Abundant and abiding good or blessing. — Nahhas calls it stable favor. Razi says baraka is also used for good; invocation dispels evil and brings good and happiness. Tabrisi urges seeking blessing by mentioning the name. Biqai calls the beneficence immeasurable; Ibn Barjan says tabāraka is rarely used of God except with something wondrous. Alusi and Elmalılı cite seeking rain, mercy, rescue, victory and aid by the name; Tabatabai calls baraka abundant good issuing from Him and ties it to both worlds' favors. [12 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI(Âlûsî); TABATABAI]
- 55:78/q01/p3 Lasting, established and enduring. — Razi derives baraka from a camel settling and water collecting in a pool; one interpretation says God's name continues, including the remembrance of Paradise's inhabitants. Nahhas adds persistence and stability of favor. Biqai says God's endurance surpasses the mind's grasp. Qurtubi notes the tafaʿāla form from baraka. [6 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:78/q02 What does ism, the Lord's name, signify?  [ٱسْمُ · referent]
turns on: Whether ism denotes the uttered divine name, its bearer, an inserted idiom, or an attribute; the nominative reading is cited for more than one analysis.
- 55:78/q02/p1 God's actual name or names, especially al-Raḥmān. — Elmalılı allows any beautiful name but privileges al-Raḥmān, which opens the sūrah; Alusi and Tabatabai identify that opening name. Qurtubi also connects the ending to al-Raḥmān. Baydawi says the name applies to the essence; Razi distinguishes the name from its bearer. Tabrisi adds that His names are most beautiful and free of evil. [8 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 55:78/q02/p2 The name means the named Lord Himself. — Damghani, Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan state this directly. Alusi reports it. Wahidi says Ibn Amir's nominative shows it, because majesty belongs to God rather than an act of naming. Qurṭubī reports the same inference. Elmalılı reports that this is one way of construing the reading. Kuranyolu compares the earlier 'face' signifying God's being. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عامر); DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عامر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:78/q02/p3 Ism is an added expression; the meaning is that the Lord is blessed. — Tabrisi illustrates the idiom with Labid's verse; Baydawi and Alusi cite a line about the name of peace upon two people. Razi calls this common and compares the earlier 'face' and passages where tabāraka lacks 'name' (23:14; 67:1). Ibn Jawzi reports it; Abu Hayyan compares 55:27. Elmalılı says Ibn Amir's reading leads some to it. [7 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 55:78/q02/p4 Ism means a divine attribute, which identifies its bearer. — Baydawi proposes 'attribute' as an alternative; Alusi explains that an attribute marks the one bearing it. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 55:78/q02/p5 It is the Supreme Name through which seekers ascend to attainment. — Qashani describes the seekers' ascent from beginning to end. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:78/q03 Why is blessing attached to the Lord's name?  [تَبَٰرَكَ ٱسْمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give different grounds for naming the name: a stronger tribute to its bearer, benefits of invocation, its lasting remembrance, or metonymic magnification.
- 55:78/q03/p1 Exalting the name points still more strongly to the exaltation of God. — Razi uses royal protocol: elevating a king's name implies higher honor for the king. Baydawi says the name applies to the essence; Abu Hayyan and Elmalılı give the same inference. Biqai says honoring the name is more emphatic than honoring the named. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 55:78/q03/p2 Mentioning the name indicates blessing gained by invoking it. — Ibn al-Anbari calls ism integral: blessing is acquired by mentioning it. Nahhas urges abundant supplication with reverence. Tabrisi urges seeking blessing through its mention. Razi says invocation removes evil and Satan and increases good; Alusi cites rain and aid; Elmalılı cites mercy, rescue and victory. Ibn Ashur contrasts blessing by uttering the name with the enduring face and connects this to seven aḥruf. [7 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Âlûsî); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:78/q03/p3 The name itself remains in remembrance, especially in Paradise. — Razi says Paradise's people continue naming God, need nothing from one another and fear no one; their conversations invoke Him. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:78/q03/p4 The name is a reverent metonymy that magnifies blessings beyond expression. — Ibn Ashur illustrates the figure with a proverb about doubt not reaching the extremities and a poem about a house free from disgrace. He compares the distinction between reciting Qur'an without ablution and touching the muṣḥaf only with ablution. The expression covers divine acts and gifts words cannot enumerate and calls for fitting thanks. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:78/q04 Why does the verse say 'your Lord'?  [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 55:78/q04/p1 It joins sovereignty with nurture and honors the Prophet as the means of guidance and blessing. — Ibn Ashur says the possessive honors the Prophet and points to him as the channel through which guidance reached those who feared their Lord. Biqai calls the Lord the benefactor who sent the Qur'an and formed the Prophet to follow it. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:78/q05 What does al-jalāl mean?  [ٱلْجَلَٰلِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is glossed generally as greatness or specified by punitive or inward implications.
- 55:78/q05/p1 Greatness, grandeur and majesty. — Ibn Abbas gives greatness; Tabari, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur likewise gloss it as majesty or greatness. Abu Hayyan cites anonymous poetry. Ibn Ashur says this greatness encompasses all attributes of perfection. Al-Aṣmaʿī claimed jalāl is used only of God, but Farisi and Tabrisi counter with a poem applying it to another. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(الأصمعي); TABRISI(الأصمعي); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:78/q05/p2 Overwhelming majesty expressed in vengeance against enemies. — Biqai contrasts it with generosity and mercy for allies. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 55:78/q05/p3 Inwardly, complete independence from manifestations. — Alusi gives this esoteric interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:78/q06 What does al-ikrām mean?  [ٱلْإِكْرَامِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ikrām names God's generosity generally, His honoring of the faithful, or an inward account of divine effusion.
- 55:78/q06/p1 Generosity, honor and bestowal of good. — Muqatil says none is more generous; Nahhas calls God Generous. Ibn Ashur calls ikrām the giving of blessings and good, making God worthy of thanks. Qurtubi contrasts generous acts with God's inherent majesty; Biqai pairs beneficence and mercy toward allies with majesty and vengeance toward enemies. [5 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:78/q06/p2 God honors the people of His religion and allegiance. — Tabrisi reports al-Hasan's explanation; Nasafi says majesty and generosity are bestowed on faithful servants through beneficence. [2 notes: TABRISI(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL]
- 55:78/q06/p3 Inwardly, universal divine effusion received according to each receptacle's capacity. — Alusi gives this esoteric interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:78/q07 What does the paired title teach about God and His acts?  [ذِى ٱلْجَلَٰلِ وَٱلْإِكْرَامِ · theology]
turns on: The notes emphasize divine perfection, the way attributes govern acts, or their guarantee and bestowal of reward.
- 55:78/q07/p1 God alone possesses perfect majesty and generous action, meriting praise and reverence. — Muqatil says God praises Himself. Qurtubi distinguishes majesty in Himself from generosity in acts. Ibn Ashur says His perfect attributes and blessings merit thanks. Islahi stresses that only God is exalted and without partner. Ibn Kathir says this calls for reverence without disobedience, worship and thanks without ingratitude, and remembrance without forgetting. Alusi says the title describes the Lord and completes preceding purification and affirmation. [6 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:78/q07/p2 The attributes govern creation, wise order, reward and punishment. — Tabatabai says acts and all blessings bear the imprint of beautiful divine names and attributes, from creation to rewarding obedience and punishing wrongdoing. Biqai pairs majesty and vengeance against enemies with generosity and mercy to allies, reading reciprocal ellipsis in the paired words. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 55:78/q07/p3 God's majesty guarantees the promised favors and reaches the faithful through beneficence. — Islahi says the repeated closing title guarantees the rewards. Nasafi says majesty and generosity are bestowed on faithful servants. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:78/q08 How is the final dhī or dhū al-jalāl read and construed?  [ذِى ٱلْجَلَٰلِ · readings]
turns on: Case and syntactic attachment: genitive to rabbika, nominative usually to ism, with reported alternative nominative parses and codex evidence.
- 55:78/q08/p1 The majority read genitive dhī, modifying rabbika. — Farisi cites Ibn Masʿud's codex, genitive at both occurrences; Tabrisi and Ibn Atiyya also report this, with Ubayy included by Ibn Atiyya. Jishumi, Ibn Jawzi and others contrast Syrian with Hijazi and Iraqi codices. Al-Aṣmaʿī argued jalāl belongs only to God, but Farisi and Tabrisi answer with poetry applying it elsewhere. Razi says glory belongs to the Lord and distinguishes name from bearer. Qurtubi links the Lord to al-Raḥmān. Ibn Ashur says the sense is the same under either case. Study Quran compares its modification of 'face' at 55:27. [17 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الأصمعي,الجميع); THALABI; JISHUMI(قراءة الباقين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن عامر,ابن مسعود,الأصمعي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عامر); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:78/q08/p2 Ibn ʿĀmir and Syrians read nominative dhū, usually modifying ism. — Wahidi infers that the name is the named God because majesty describes Him. Syrian codices attest the form; Ibn Jawzi contrasts Hijazi and Iraqi codices. Ibn Atiyya says the Syrians also use nominative at the first occurrence. Abu Hayyan notes other transmitted forms. Alusi says majesty and generosity can aptly describe the name itself; Qiraat-ER explicitly takes that construal, unlike an adjective of rabb. Ibn Ashur says the meaning is the same under either case. Nashr unusually says the nominative is an adjective of the Lord, while also reporting Ibn Amir's codices. [21 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); THALABI(أبو بكرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عامر); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الشام); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI(ابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عامر); NASHR; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عامر); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر وأهل الشام); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:78/q08/p3 The nominative may begin a new nominal clause. — Farisi reports this grammatical parsing of the nominative. [1 notes: FARISI-HUJJA]
- 55:78/q08/p4 The nominative may be coordinated with the noun. — Tabrisi reports Ibn Jinni's analysis. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن جني)]
- 55:78/q08/p5 A reported Syrian prophetic recitation also joins nearby phrases with wāw. — Thalabi reports Abu Bakra's account of the Prophet reciting 'rafraf khudr wa-abqariyy hissan' and joining the final doxology, as in Syrian codices. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو بكرة)]

## 55:78/q09 Why does this occurrence differ in case from 55:27?  [ذِى ٱسْمُ · grammar]
turns on: How directly 'face' and 'name' signify the divine essence, and what the earlier nominative indicates.
- 55:78/q09/p1 The Lord is the bearer of glory, while 'face' denotes Him more directly than 'name'. — Razi says 55:27 has nominative dhū but the common reading here has genitive dhī because God's face is the Lord whereas the name is distinct from its bearer. Ibn Ashur argues the unanimous nominative at 55:27 suggests face signifies essence more directly than name; name can also signify blessing through utterance, a distinction he relates to seven aḥruf. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:78/q09/p2 At 55:27 nominative points to the divine Face believers will see and joyfully meet. — Qurtubi interprets the agreed nominative adjective in the earlier occurrence this way. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 55:78/q10 What invocation of the paired title is reported or recommended?  [ذِى ٱلْجَلَٰلِ وَٱلْإِكْرَامِ · reports]
turns on: The reports give a general instruction, one answered occasion, and a greatest-name explanation; Tirmidhi disputes one chain of the instruction.
- 55:78/q10/p1 Persist in saying 'O Possessor of Majesty and Honor' when calling on God. — Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Durr, Kuranyolu and Study Quran report or recommend the prophetic instruction. Ibn Kathir cites al-Nasa'i through Rabi'ah and says al-Jawhari glosses aliz as staying close, Ibn Masud as keeping to it; he takes both as continual insistence. Ibn Kathir also relays Tirmidhi's criticism of Muammil's rare attribution to Anas, giving a different chain through Hamad, Humayd and al-Hasan. Durr's Anas report calls the expression two of God's greatest names. Kuranyolu says scholars hope invocation aids acceptance. [16 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,ابن مسعود,الترمذي,الجوهري,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أنس,ابن عمر,ربيعة بن عامر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 55:78/q10/p2 A man's invocation by the title was immediately answered. — Durr reports that when a man said 'O Possessor of Majesty and Generosity,' the Prophet told him his request had been answered and to ask. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 55:78/q10/p3 A prayer including the title used God's greatest name, by which He answers and gives. — Durr reports a prophetic statement about a worshipper's prayer also mentioning God's praise, oneness, generosity and life. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 55:78/q11 What did the Prophet say after prayer using this title?  [ذِى ٱلْجَلَٰلِ وَٱلْإِكْرَامِ · reports]
- 55:78/q11/p1 He said God is Peace, peace comes from Him, and blessed is the Possessor of Majesty and Honor. — Baghawi, Ibn Kathir and Study Quran transmit ʿĀʾisha's report that he sat after salutation only long enough for this formula. Durr's report adds that he first sought forgiveness three times; wording varies between direct address and third-person reference. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(ʿĀʾishah)]

## 55:78/q12 What conduct follows from God's majesty?  [ٱلْجَلَٰلِ · interpretation]
- 55:78/q12/p1 Revere God through obedience, worship, thanks, remembrance and due honor to people. — Ibn Kathir demands reverence without disobedience, worship and gratitude without ingratitude, and remembrance without forgetfulness. His cited hadith counts honoring an aged Muslim, a person of authority and a balanced Qur'an bearer as reverence for God. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 55:78/q13 What does the verse's closing praise express?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the close is described generally as praise and thanks or specifically as the supreme delight of divine remembrance.
- 55:78/q13/p1 It glorifies God, confirms His favors and calls for praise and thanks. — Zajjaj and Wahidi say the close declares God worthy of glorification and exaltation; Ibn Ashur calls it praise. Muqatil says God praises Himself. Alusi says sanctification confirms favors throughout the sūrah; Ibn Ashur says immeasurable gifts require proportionate thanks. Tabatabai says both worlds overflow with mercy. Sulami says creaturely praise remains limited by understanding; the Prophet's supplication says no one can enumerate God's praise and He alone praises Himself fully. [10 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعضهم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 55:78/q13/p2 The final divine remembrance is the highest favor and pleasure, beyond the described Paradise gifts. — Razi says the surahs end by naming God and that remembrance of Him is the most complete delight. He observes that hearing was absent from garden pleasures: reclining on green couches, inhabitants hear God's remembrance. Kuranyolu says divine pleasure exceeds all afterlife blessings, citing 2:25, 43:68–73, 47:14–15, 78:31–36 and 83:22–28. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 55:78/q14 How does the closing name connect to the opening of al-Raḥmān?  [ٱسْمُ · links]
- 55:78/q14/p1 The close returns to the opening name al-Raḥmān, source of creation and mercy. — Qurtubi recounts creation by mercy, governance, Resurrection, Hell and Paradise before praise of the opening name. Alusi says it stands above denial of favors; Elmalılı, Tabatabai and Kuranyolu likewise connect the opening and end. Biqai reports Razi that the closing name encompasses Qur'anic teaching and punishment. Elmalılı adds that majesty and generosity lead into al-Wāqiʿa's opening. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 55:78/q15 How does the close relate to the earlier enduring Face and the sūrah's blessings?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes explain the paired endings through the contrast between worldly and afterlife blessings, or through the guarantee of the final rewards.
- 55:78/q15/p1 The enduring Face closes worldly blessings; the blessed Name closes afterlife blessings. — Razi says God alone subsists: worldly things perish in themselves and the hereafter lasts by His sustaining power; its residents delight in His name. He says calling the survivor 'Lord' might imply continued subjects, whereas 'face' states absolute permanence. Abu Hayyan says permanence suits the world's passing, while growth suits Paradise's increasing, enduring gifts. Study Quran sees 55:27 echoed at 55:78, with God transcending earthly and heavenly blessings, and the paired title ending both sets. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:78/q15/p2 The final blessing follows Paradise's rewards because divine majesty guarantees them. — Islahi says the Lord's name signifies His being, as 'face' did earlier. Biqai says this passage completes eight blessings for the right-hand garden and alludes to eight gates. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:78/q16 How does the ending connect al-Raḥmān to neighboring sūrahs?  [* · links]
turns on: One connection is shared closing praise; another is al-Wāqiʿa's fuller account of the destinies implied here.
- 55:78/q16/p1 Al-Qamar and al-Raḥmān end by naming God; al-Wāqiʿa likewise ends in glorification. — Razi compares al-Qamar's 'Sovereign, Powerful' (54:55) and al-Wāqiʿa's glorification after spirit, fragrance and garden delight (56:89, 96). Islahi says al-Qamar taught Quraysh through messengers' history rather than demanded punishment signs and that revelation is a favor. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:78/q16/p2 Al-Wāqiʿa expands the differing destinies and divine power announced in al-Raḥmān. — Biqai says it details the foremost and later allies and openly or secretly hostile humans and jinn. Different outcomes demonstrate freely chosen divine action and eloquent wisdom; equal outcomes would give naturalists grounds for doubt. He reads al-Raḥmān's ending as perfection and al-Wāqiʿa's as denying defect, finds purpose in the name al-Wāqiʿa and its grammar, and interprets the basmala as God assigning differing conditions. Al-Raḥmān grants expression broadly, but responses differ; al-Raḥīm brings His party near and gives them excellent words and deeds. Elmalılı notes the transition from closing majesty and generosity to al-Wāqiʿa's opening (56:1). [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 55:78/q17 How is the repeated refrain arranged before this close?  [* · links]
- 55:78/q17/p1 It occurs thirty-one times, grouped as eight creation signs, seven Hell descriptions and two sets of eight garden descriptions. — Nasafi ties the first eight to Paradise's and Hell's gates: belief and right action open Paradise's gates and close Hell's. Biqai says the last passage completes eight blessings for the right-hand garden and alludes to its eight gates. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:78/q18 How are the two pairs of gardens preceding the close understood?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the later pair signifies a lower rank of believers or a further image of limitless bliss.
- 55:78/q18/p1 They describe distinct rewards for those brought near and the people of the right. — Islahi distinguishes Paradise's qualities for the muqarrabīn and aṣḥāb al-yamīn. Nasafi counts eight descriptions for each pair. Asad explicitly rejects the claim that the further gardens belong to believers of lesser merit. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES-]
- 55:78/q18/p2 The juxtaposed garden pairs suggest endless gardens and varied symbols of supreme bliss. — Asad argues the lesser-merit view is weak and arbitrary, citing 56:22 and 56:34. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES+]

## 55:78/q19 How did the jinn respond to al-Raḥmān's refrain?  [* · reports]
- 55:78/q19/p1 They denied none of God's favors and praised and thanked Him each time. — Nasafi reports Jabir's account: the Prophet recited al-Raḥmān, rebuked silent human listeners, and said the jinn had answered better at every refrain. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(جابر)]

## 55:78/q20 What virtue is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Raḥmān?  [* · sciences]
- 55:78/q20/p1 Whoever recites it has fulfilled gratitude for God's blessings. — Baydawi transmits this prophetic report. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 55:78/q21 Was Sūrat al-Raḥmān revealed in Mecca or Medina?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The sūrah's tone, rhythm and whether any verse appears Madinan.
- 55:78/q21/p1 The entire sūrah is Makkan. — Study Quran attributes this to most scholars. Islahi argues that no verse appears Madinan and that one tone and rhythm run through the sūrah; its Makkan opponents demand a punishment sign before accepting the threatened afterlife humiliation. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(Most scholars); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 55:78/q21/p2 The entire sūrah is Madinan. — Study Quran attributes this to a minority; Islahi reports that some hold it but argues against it from the sūrah's unified tone and lack of apparently Madinan verses. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); STUDYQURAN(A minority); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 55:78/q22 What inward meaning is given to the closing name and paired attributes?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward readings track the seeker's ascent, the lover's perception, or the relation of divine independence to effusion.
- 55:78/q22/p1 Seekers rise through the Supreme Name until they attain it. — Qashani follows their ascent from beginning to end. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:78/q22/p2 For lovers subsisting after annihilation, majesty and beauty appear together without veiling each other. — Qashani contrasts this with the earlier stage in which each veiled the other before realized divine existence and return to the attributes. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:78/q22/p3 Majesty is independence from manifestations; generosity is effusion received according to capacity. — Alusi presents this esoteric account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:78/q23 Where does Ibn Jawzi direct the reader for earlier explanations of this wording?  [تَبَٰرَكَ ذِى ٱلْجَلَٰلِ وَٱلْإِكْرَامِ · links]
- 55:78/q23/p1 To tabāraka at 7:54 and the paired title at 55:27. — Ibn Jawzi explicitly refers back to those discussions without restating them. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
