# 96:16/q01 What does nāṣiya denote? [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · meaning]
- 96:16/q01/p1 The hair at the front of the head, or by extension the forehead, head, face, or person.
  reasons: Māwardī says the front hair may stand for the whole person, as in a 'blessed forelock'; Ṭabrisī derives the name from attachment or joining and cites an anonymous rajaz poet's tanāsiha; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also identifies front hair; Ibn al-Shaykh allows its growing place and says it stands here for face and head, noting Abū Jahl's careful grooming; Elmalılı extends it from head to person; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ adds anything foremost or the leading people of a community; Bursevī also defines it as front hair.

# 96:16/q02 Whose forelock is meant, and whom can the threat include? [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the historical target exhausts the warning or its indefinite wording extends it.
- 96:16/q02/p1 It is Abū Jahl's forelock and, by metonymy, Abū Jahl himself.
  reasons: Muqātil links the threatened forelock to Abū Jahl, whose denial is warned of in the preceding passage; Māwardī, Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr identify him, and Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the definite forelock to the man who forbids prayer in 96:9–10. Ibn Qutayba explains that seizure of the part means humiliation of its owner, citing 55:41.
  holders (9 notes): MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL

- 96:16/q02/p2 The wording also reaches the class of offenders who obstruct worship, beyond Abū Jahl.
  reasons: Qurṭubī says the initial threat concerns Abū Jahl but warns everyone who refuses or prevents obedience; Elmalılı says indefinite apposition generalizes it; Kur'an Yolu applies the eventual capture to self-satisfied tyrants and says it may occur here, hereafter, or both, citing Abū Jahl and others.

# 96:16/q03 How does the second nāṣiya relate grammatically to the first? [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Which prior expression is supplied as the appositive's antecedent.
- 96:16/q03/p1 It is a genitive appositive or substitute for the preceding forelock.
  reasons: Sībawayh cites the verse repeatedly for apposition, including his broader examples of noun and appositive and of badal among raised constructions; Ṭabarī says the genitive repeats the previous noun. Al-Farrāʾ calls repetition emphatic and compares 'a straight path, the path of God'; Naḥḥās reports this term. Abū ʿUbayda, al-Kalbī's later exegetical tradition, and many commentators call it badal. Burhān says such substitution names an antecedent again with specification, making its quality the reason for seizure, and cites 26:47; Samarrāʾī says badal can blame, as here, or praise, as in 'this secure city.' Biqāʿī says the form approaches definiteness; al-Islāḥī says the adjective makes it count as definite.

# 96:16/q04 What licenses an indefinite appositive after a definite noun here? [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether informative qualification and repeated wording are grammatical requirements or only features of this example.
- 96:16/q04/p1 Its qualifying adjectives make the indefinite informative enough; the Kufan formulation requires matching wording and a modifier.
  reasons: Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn Yaʿīsh and others make the added description the source of useful independent meaning. Al-Samīn calls this the Kufan rule; al-Ālūsī says Kufans require both matching wording and a descriptive modifier, whereas Basrans require informativeness. Al-Islāḥī says the adjective lends definiteness.

# 96:16/q05 Why is the repeated nāṣiya indefinite? [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether indefiniteness chiefly reveals this forelock's qualities or presents a general class.
- 96:16/q05/p1 It introduces newly disclosed qualities of a known forelock.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī says its physical identity is familiar but its false speech and sinful deeds are newly exposed; al-Farrāʾ calls the repetition emphasis, and Burhān says naming it twice specifies why it is seized.
