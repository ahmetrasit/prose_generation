# 96:16/q12 What does the threatened seizure of this forelock entail? [نَاصِيَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Which consequence or event the image of seizing the forelock evokes.
- 96:16/q12/p1 It signifies forceful capture, humiliation, and dragging the offender to punishment, especially Hell.
  reasons: Muqātil says angels drag Abū Jahl face-first into the Fire for sin; Ibn Qutayba says seizure raises and humiliates its owner and cites 55:41; Thaʿlabī specifies the front of the head. Bursevī says Arabs regarded forelock seizure as degrading subjugation and quotes ʿAyn al-Maʿānī on coercion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says grasping and pulling convey dragging to punishment and the prefixed bāʾ stresses contact. Quṭb says the raised, forward, conspicuous forehead of an arrogant tyrant deserves to be cast down. Biqāʿī says humiliating capture already implies death, making explicit face-first dragging to Hell unnecessary; al-Islāḥī says the forehead is a site of honor, especially the prostration mark, so its refusal merits Hell; Kur'an Yolu reports explanations of transport to Hell or disgrace.

# 96:16/q13 How does the warning connect to the preceding verses? [* · links]
- 96:16/q13/p1 It answers the prayer blocker with an oath-backed conditional threat of divine seizure if he does not desist.
  reasons: Muqātil links Abū Jahl's denial and turning away to the threatened seizure, and the earlier promise of return to God to Resurrection. Ṭabarī says the preceding 'No' denies Abū Jahl's claim as he prevents Muḥammad's prayer. Bursevī expands desisting to repentance and submission before death. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the lām begins the oath, 'we will seize' answers it, the conditional answer is omitted, and the second kallā answers the first after suspense. Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain that the light emphatic nūn of the oath is written with alif for its pause pronunciation.

# 96:16/q14 What was reported to occasion the threat passage? [* · reports]
turns on: Which response by Abū Jahl the report highlights after he forbade prayer.
- 96:16/q14/p1 Abū Jahl forbade the Prophet's prayer and was rebuked, whereupon he boasted of the valley's largest council.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Wāḥidī says the rebuke and boast occasioned the threat; Ṭabrisī relates the confrontation, boast, rebuke and revelation of the passage about calling his gathering. Al-Suyūṭī's Ibn ʿAbbās report says the prohibition of prayer prompted the sequence through the lying, sinful forelock.
