# 69:10: 12 questions

## 69:10/q01 Who disobeyed whom as “the messenger of their Lord”?  [فَعَصَوْا۟ رَسُولَ رَبِّهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Which preceding peoples the plural pronoun includes, whether singular rasūl distributes across their prophets, and whether rasūl denotes a prophet, a message, or inward guidance.
- 69:10/q01/p1 The peoples mentioned disobeyed the messenger sent to each of them; the singular applies distributively or collectively. — Al-Wāḥidī includes Moses and Lot because both peoples precede this verse and compares 26:16; al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī relay that comparison. Ibn Kathīr cites 50:14 and 26:105, 123, 141: rejecting one messenger amounts to rejecting all. Al-Biqāʿī says their missions shared one purpose. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿAṭiyya take rasūl collectively. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes Pharaoh, earlier peoples and overturned towns as a possible antecedent; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes all three. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Kuranyolu distribute the messenger to each community. [21 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R); TABRISI(أبو مسلم)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 69:10/q01/p2 The messenger is Moses, and the disobedient are Pharaoh and his people, or Pharaoh and earlier peoples. — Al-Kalbī identifies Moses. Al-Rāzī makes this depend on taking the pronoun as Pharaoh and those before him. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Pharaoh may stand for his people; the majority reading supplies the contextual clue, and Abū ʿAmr's and al-Kisāʾī's readings make it clearer. Abū Ḥayyān notes Moses occurs earlier in the context. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عمرو والكسائي); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 69:10/q01/p3 The messenger is Lot, and the disobedient are the people of the overturned towns. — Al-Rāzī makes this depend on the pronoun referring to the overturned towns. Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān cite Lot's proximity in the passage. [8 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(غيره في كتاب الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 69:10/q01/p4 Rasūl means God's message, so the disobedience is rejection of the message. — Al-Māwardī, Abū Muslim as reported by al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Qurṭubī cite a poem using rasūl for a message. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this possibility; Study Quran says it allows reference to any disbelieving people mentioned in the sūra. Al-Ṭabrisī says the plain sense of the messenger himself is stronger. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(أبو مسلم)-]
- 69:10/q01/p5 The messenger is the intellect guiding people toward truth. — Al-Qāshānī gives an inward reading: their punishment submerges them in prime matter and disorders and ruins the body's temperament. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:10/q02 What did their disobedience involve?  [فَعَصَوْا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes name denial, disobeying commands, and the preceding sinful conduct as aspects of the offense.
- 69:10/q02/p1 They denied or accused their messengers of lying. — Al-Zajjāj says denial was included in their disobedience; al-Kuranyolu says each people accused its own prophet of lying. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:10/q02/p2 They opposed the messengers' commands and prohibitions and committed the reprehensible act already mentioned. — Al-Ṭabrisī specifies what the messenger commanded. Al-Ālūsī says the messenger forbade their reprehensible practices and that disobedience explains their preceding sinful act. Al-Biqāʿī adds that their beneficent Lord had sent them guidance. [5 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:10/q03 Why is “messenger” singular for multiple communities?  [رَسُولَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether singular rasūl is distributive or collective for prophets, or is a metonym for the message.
- 69:10/q03/p1 The singular denotes each community's own messenger or the messengers collectively. — Al-Bursawī says the noun form can remain invariant in gender and number and the possessive distributes across communities. Al-Ālūsī says it may be a literal singular for each people, or plural in force because rasūl began as a verbal noun; their message was one. Al-Biqāʿī says the messengers had one aim. Ibn Kathīr says rejecting one rejects all and cites 50:14 and 26:105, 123, 141. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the singular is more elegant and avoids three successive plurals. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI(أبو مسلم)+]
- 69:10/q03/p2 The singular messenger stands metonymically for the message. — Al-Māwardī introduces a poem and then explains its use of rasūl for a message; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī cite the same usage. Al-Ṭabrisī prefers the plain sense of a messenger. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABRISI(أبو مسلم)-]

## 69:10/q04 What does calling Him “their Lord” convey?  [رَبِّهِمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes point to either the Lord's benefaction or the contrast with Pharaoh's claimed lordship.
- 69:10/q04/p1 It makes disobedience culpable because He created, provided for, and guided them. — Al-Biqāʿī says He gave them faculties and provision and sent guidance, while they wrongly supposed their benefactor could not harm them. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 69:10/q04/p2 It exposes their error in treating Pharaoh as their lord. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the possessive hints at their worship of Pharaoh and their treating him as a god. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:10/q05 How does the disobedience clause relate to the preceding account?  [فَعَصَوْا۟ · links]
- 69:10/q05/p1 It elaborates the preceding statement of their sin. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the elaboration of Noah's people's sin in 54:9. Al-Ālūsī says disobedience explains their coming with the sinful deed just mentioned. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:10/q06 Who seized them?  [فَأَخَذَهُمْ · referent]
- 69:10/q06/p1 God seized the disobedient communities. — Al-Bursawī says God seized each community with punishment. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:10/q07 What does the taking mean, and what happened to the peoples?  [فَأَخَذَهُمْ أَخْذَةًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously explain the event as destruction of the peoples or stress the seizure's final, unbearable character.
- 69:10/q07/p1 God destroyed them completely with punishments suited to each people. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'taking' means destruction, comparing 6:44, and says Pharaoh's taking was drowning, comparing 54:42. Al-Biqāʿī says no denier escaped God's reach, unlike a human pursuer, showing His knowledge and power: Pharaoh's army was drowned; Noah's deniers perished except those aboard the ark; Lot's towns were uprooted, overturned, stoned and flooded. Al-Bursawī similarly lists drowning Noah's people, overturning and stoning Lot's towns then burying them in foul water, and drowning Pharaoh's troops in the Red Sea or Nile; each met fitting recompense. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that total destruction left none alive and compares 25:14. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:10/q07/p2 The seizure is an unbearable, final punishment rather than a temporary warning. — Iṣlāḥī calls it indefensible and unbearable, and distinguishes it from punishment sent to warn and remind. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 69:10/q08 How does the singular “taking” apply to those seized?  [أَخْذَةًۭ · grammar]
- 69:10/q08/p1 It is one taking: Pharaoh and his people's drowning, or one taking distributed to each nation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives both according to whether the antecedent is Pharaoh's people alone or includes the other peoples, and compares 54:42. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:10/q09 How is rābiya formed?  [رَّابِيَةً · grammar]
- 69:10/q09/p1 It is the active participle of rabā-yarbū, 'to increase,' with wāw changed to yāʾ after a kasra. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the morphological derivation and sound change. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 69:10/q10 What does rābiya say about the seizure?  [أَخْذَةًۭ رَّابِيَةً · meaning]
turns on: Whether increase describes intensity, comparison with other punishments, an extraordinary event, its destructive or lasting effect, or its continuation into the Hereafter.
- 69:10/q10/p1 It was severe, great, elevated, or increasing in severity. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss it as severe in al-Ṭabarī; Mujāhid repeats in al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr. Al-Mubarrad derives severity and greatness from increase; al-Naẓm calls it extreme in severity and explains rabā as increase and multiplication. Al-Majāz, al-Rāzī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī derive it from growth; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares wealth, usury and 22:5, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bursawī compare the increment of usury. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the image of increase is borrowed for severity. Ibn Zayd says increase can be good or evil, citing guidance in 47:17 and punishment in 16:88. Al-Itqān calls 'severe' a Himyarī usage. Al-Elmalılı says the grip rises with disobedience. [42 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,مجاهد); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,الضحاك,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد,صاحب النظم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والسدي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 69:10/q10/p2 It exceeded other peoples' or ordinary punishments. — Al-Zajjāj says the seizure exceeded other afflictions; al-Wāḥidī reports it surpassed other peoples' punishment, and al-Zajjāj says it increased beyond events. Al-Jishumī says it surpassed every other blow while denying that it exceeded what was deserved. Al-Baghawī reports it surpassed earlier nations' punishments. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī connect greater severity to especially ugly deeds. Abū Ḥayyān's Mujāhid contrasts drowning and the overturning of towns. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this as an alternative to increased severity. [22 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]
- 69:10/q10/p3 It was an extraordinary, overwhelming punishment outside the ordinary course. — Ibn Qutayba calls it high, overwhelming and much spoken of. Al-Jishumī calls it extraordinarily overwhelming; al-Ṭabrisī reports it as high, notorious and extraordinary. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the extraordinary character as a separate alternative. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 69:10/q10/p4 It was a destructive punishment. — Al-Suddī gives this gloss, as transmitted by al-Māwardī and Ibn Kathīr. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والسدي)]
- 69:10/q10/p5 It means remaining in God's punishment forever. — Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī gives this explanation in al-Māwardī. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني)]
- 69:10/q10/p6 The punishment grew from worldly destruction into punishment in the Hereafter. — Al-Rāzī says Pharaoh's worldly punishment continued into the afterlife and cites 71:25, drowning followed by fire. Study Quran applies the same sequence to Noah's people, with the Hereafter still more severe. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 69:10/q11 What does this seizure teach about deserved punishment?  [* · theology]
- 69:10/q11/p1 Punishment is earned by people's deeds, and rebellion against a divine envoy deserves a rebel's punishment. — Al-Jishumī says the seizure proves deserved punishment against the Mujabbiṣa and says even a blow greater than others is not more than deserved. Iṣlāḥī likens the messenger to the universe's King's envoy. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 69:10/q12 What role does the verse play in the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 69:10/q12/p1 It warns later deniers and awakens discerning readers through the earlier peoples' fate. — Al-Bursawī names Quraysh as the warning's immediate audience and people of understanding as its wider audience. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 69:10/q12/p2 It shifts the examples from pre-Islamic Arabian disbelievers to opponents of prophets in the Judeo-Christian tradition. — Study Quran describes this movement in the passage. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
