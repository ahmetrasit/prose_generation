# 40:26: 31 questions

## 40:26/q01 What does Pharaoh's command “leave me” mean?  [ذَرُونِىٓ · meaning]
- 40:26/q01/p1 Leave me alone to act against Moses. — Bursevī notes that the verb has no used past, verbal noun, or active participle; Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the audience as the council. [2 notes: BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 40:26/q02 What is the reading of the possessive yāʾ in ذَرُونِىٓ?  [ذَرُونِىٓ · readings]
turns on: Vocalization of the first-person suffix before أقتل.
- 40:26/q02/p1 Ibn Kathīr and al-Aṣbahānī from Warsh open the final yāʾ. — Rāzī names Ibn Kathīr; Nashr adds al-Aṣbahānī; Qirāʾāt-ER says -iya is fuller pronunciation without syntactic change. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); NASHR(ابن كثير والأصبهاني,ابن كثير والأصبهاني عن ورش); QIRAAT-ER]
- 40:26/q02/p2 The other readers quiesce the final yāʾ. — Samīn al-Ḥalabī contrasts them with Ibn Kathīr. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير)]

## 40:26/q03 Who, if anyone, was restraining Pharaoh from killing Moses, and why?  [ذَرُونِىٓ أَقْتُلْ مُوسَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the request reflects actual courtiers' restraint, an idiom of desire, or Pharaoh's fear concealed as restraint.
- 40:26/q03/p1 His courtiers or close associates restrained him. — Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Study Quran say they feared Moses would destroy them; Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ālūsī report they called him a weak magician and feared killing him would suggest Pharaoh could not answer his proofs. Ṭabrisī says they wished to delay for the magicians; Abū Ḥayyān also mentions a believer or their self-protection. [14 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭs); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:26/q03/p2 Believers among Pharaoh's people prevented him. — Māwardī explicitly gives believing Egyptians as the obstruction; Abū Ḥayyān also allows that one restrainer believed Moses. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 40:26/q03/p3 No one actually restrained him; his fear of Moses or a miracle held him back. — Rāzī says he feared an obstructing miracle would reveal his fear. Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Biqāʿī say he feigned being held back while fearing sudden destruction; Bayḍāwī suggests prophetic knowledge or perceived impossibility. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 40:26/q03/p4 The idiom expresses desire for action even without a real opponent. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Ṭarafa's دعني for possible restraint and al-Suhaylī's poem for usage without an opponent; it also makes the killing sound dangerous and difficult. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو القاسم السهيلي,طرفة)]
- 40:26/q03/p5 Pharaoh sought advice or authorization concerning the killing. — Ibn Ziyād says the people had advised against it because Moses would stop them; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes awaited approval. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زياد)]
- 40:26/q03/p6 Pharaoh meant to kill Moses himself because his people would refuse to execute the order. — Māwardī presents this as another reading. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 40:26/q04 What does Pharaoh's talk of killing Moses reveal about his power and motive?  [ذَرُونِىٓ أَقْتُلْ مُوسَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: How far Pharaoh's expressed resolve matches his actual ability or intention.
- 40:26/q04/p1 Moses' signs had weakened Pharaoh, who needed his council's help or approval. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the language is unlike that of a tyrant sure of obedience; Abū Ḥayyān reports him; Elmalılı says the miracles broke his despotism and an intervention prompted his outburst; Iṣlāḥī says Moses had become too powerful to seize without chiefs. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:26/q04/p2 Pharaoh blustered to conceal his private fear and inability to reach Moses. — Rāzī envisages a miracle stopping him. Biqāʿī says the claim preempted an accusation of powerlessness. Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ālūsī connect his pretence with fear of immediate destruction. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:26/q04/p3 Pharaoh resolved to kill Moses to protect his rule and end Moses' movement. — Ibn Kathīr calls his stance defiant obstinacy; Bursevī says he claimed killing was necessary for rule; Ibn ʿĀshūr says he later chose Moses alone as a surer target than believers' sons; Kuranyolu links the decision with killing male followers. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 40:26/q05 How are أَقْتُلْ and وَلْيَدْعُ parsed?  [أَقْتُلْ وَلْيَدْعُ · grammar]
- 40:26/q05/p1 أقتل is jussive as the response to the imperative, and وليدع is a jussive command. — Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī add that ذروني is built or indeclinable although it resembles a jussive. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 40:26/q06 What does Pharaoh mean by telling Moses to call on his Lord?  [وَلْيَدْعُ رَبَّهُۥٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the challenge is read chiefly as derision, as a sign of hidden fear, or both.
- 40:26/q06/p1 He taunts Moses to seek divine rescue, professing that the prayer will not save him. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, Bursevī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe the challenge. Māwardī distinguishes no answer from no help; Qurṭubī says he denied any real Lord and claimed supreme lordship; Asad marks sarcasm with 'alleged'. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 40:26/q06/p2 The challenge's show of indifference betrays Pharaoh's fear of Moses' prayer. — Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ālūsī see inward dread. Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī report a warning that Moses' prayer might be answered. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the imperative lām signals indifference; Biqāʿī says Pharaoh implied his gods restrained Moses. [8 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:26/q07 What sense can “call” have here?  [وَلْيَدْعُ · meaning]
- 40:26/q07/p1 It can mean seeking help or refuge. — Dāmghānī adduces Pharaoh's words here together with 10:38 and 11:13. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 40:26/q08 What is the reading of the possessive yāʾ in إِنِّىٓ?  [إِنِّىٓ · readings]
- 40:26/q08/p1 Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr, or the two Medinans with them, open it. — Rāzī lists the first three; Nashr adds the two Medinans and counts this among three occurrences; Qirāʾāt-ER explains the fuller suffix pronunciation does not change syntax. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); NASHR(المدنيان وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER]

## 40:26/q09 What does Pharaoh's expression “I fear” do here?  [إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ · rhetoric]
- 40:26/q09/p1 It presents strong apprehension as the reason for killing Moses. — Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses fear as strong apprehension or belief; Biqāʿī says the emphatic assertion reflects the gravity of the matter and counters courtiers minimizing Moses. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 40:26/q10 Why is no من expressed after أَخَافُ?  [أَخَافُ أَن · grammar]
- 40:26/q10/p1 The preposition normally required by أخاف is omitted before أن. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this syntactic explanation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:26/q11 What is the basic sense of يُبَدِّلَ?  [يُبَدِّلَ · meaning]
- 40:26/q11/p1 To change or replace one thing with another. — Baghawī glosses change; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines tabdīl as replacement. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:26/q12 What does “change your religion” mean, and what is their religion?  [يُبَدِّلَ دِينَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dīn names worship, the broader established order, or political sovereignty.
- 40:26/q12/p1 Moses would replace their worship of Pharaoh and idols with worship of his Lord alone. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī specify worship of Pharaoh; Bayḍāwī cites 7:127; Bursevī and Ālūsī say idols were treated as intercessors; Ibn ʿĀshūr says tabdīl replaces a thing and Pharaoh inferred that denial of his divinity would alter fundamentals and derived laws. [15 notes: MUQATIL(فرعون); TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IJ, Ṭs); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:26/q12/p2 Dīn is Pharaoh's political authority or sovereignty, which Moses threatens to overturn. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Zuhayr's dīn ʿAmr; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī transmit this; Iṣlāḥī explains that monotheism challenged divine kingship. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 40:26/q12/p3 Moses would change the people's existing way of life, customs or religious condition. — Qatāda glosses their dīn as the state they follow; Ibn Kathīr calls it customs and practices; Biqāʿī characterizes their religion as assigning events to nature; Wāḥidī says Moses would abolish or corrupt it. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 40:26/q12/p4 Religious change would end Pharaoh's kingship. — Māwardī says each new religion removes a kingdom. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 40:26/q13 Why does Pharaoh say “your religion” and mention religion first?  [دِينَكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 40:26/q13/p1 He casts his own interest as theirs to incite them, knowing they prize religion highly. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 7:127 for incitement. Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān say people value religion above wealth; Nasafī says Pharaoh posed as protector of religious and worldly life; Elmalılı says he presented himself as pious. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 40:26/q14 Is the second feared outcome joined by “and” or “or,” and with what force?  [أَوْ أَن · readings]
turns on: The written alif and whether conjunction expresses both feared things or alternatives; Abū ʿUbayd's equation of aw with wa is disputed.
- 40:26/q14/p1 Wāw joins two feared outcomes, so Pharaoh professes fear of both. — Medinan, Syrian, Meccan and Basran readers, including Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and Ibn ʿĀmir, transmit wa-an; some reports add Abū Jaʿfar. Regional non-Iraqi codices support it. Zajjāj, Naḥḥās, Ibn Khālawayh, Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī and later commentators explain combined fear; Ibn Khālawayh prefers it. Some give wa-in vocalization. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء المدينة والشام والبصرة); ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); NAHHAS(المدنيون وأبو عبد الرحمن وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); THALABI(أبو عمرو وأهل المدينة وأهل الشام وأهل مكة); BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب وأهل الكوفة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر؛ عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو,ابن كثير وابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(نافع وابن عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(المدنيون وأبو عبد الرحمن السلمي وابن عامر وأبو عمرو,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأكثرون); ALUSI-FULL(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر؛ غيرهم); QIRAAT-ER; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+]
- 40:26/q14/p2 Aw gives alternatives: religious change or corruption, sometimes the latter if change fails. — Kufan readers including ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza and Kisāʾī, and Yaʿqūb in some reports, transmit it; Iraqi codices have the alif. Zajjāj, Naḥḥās, Rāzī, Biqāʿī and others explain alternatives; Fārisī says one kind of feared thing may include both, as in bread or dates. Burhān notes this spelling in Hūd, Banī Isrāʾīl, Ṭāhā and al-Muʾmin. [32 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); NAHHAS(المدنيون وأبو عبد الرحمن وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); THALABI(الكوفيون وبعض البصريين); BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب وأهل الكوفة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر؛ عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أبو علي,الباقون,حفص ويعقوب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(الذين قرأوا بصيغة أو); QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(آخرون); BIQAI-FULL(الكوفيون ويعقوب); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر؛ غيرهم); BURHAN; QIRAAT-ER; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+; THALABI(أبو عبيد)+]
- 40:26/q14/p3 Aw can carry the force of wa, though wa cannot carry aw's force. — Abū ʿUbayd favors the extra alif and appeals to this one-way interchange; Naḥḥās objects that it erases the distinction between fear of both and fear of either outcome. [3 notes: NAHHAS(المدنيون وأبو عبد الرحمن وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)+; THALABI(أبو عبيد)+; NAHHAS-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-]

## 40:26/q15 How is يُظْهِرَ read and what role does ٱلْفَسَادَ take?  [يُظْهِرَ ٱلْفَسَادَ · readings]
turns on: Verb form and voice determine whether Moses acts on corruption or corruption is the grammatical subject; source lists of transmitters vary.
- 40:26/q15/p1 Form IV yuẓhira is active and transitive; Moses causes corruption to appear, with al-fasād accusative. — Generally assigned to Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥafṣ and Medinan/Basran readers; other reports add Abū Jaʿfar, Yaʿqūb or Abū Bakr. It parallels yubaddila, which Fārisī, Abū ʿUbayd and Nasafī prefer. The records disagree on a few transmitter assignments. [22 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم,نافع وأبو عمرو); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم,نافع وأبو عمرو); THALABI(أهل المدينة والبصرة وحفص عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والبصرة وحفص); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم والحسن وقتادة والجحدري وأبو رجاء ومجاهد وسعيد بن المسيب ومالك بن أنس); TABRISI(أبو علي,أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو,حفص ويعقوب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وأبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(قُرِئ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(نافع وأبو عمرو وحفص); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وأبو بكر عن عاصم وأبو جعفر); FARISI-HUJJA+; NASAFI-FULL+; THALABI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم)+]
- 40:26/q15/p2 Form I yaẓhar is intransitive; corruption itself appears, as nominative subject. — Generally assigned to Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza, Kisāʾī and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim; some reports add al-Aʿraj, ʿĪsā, Aʿmash and Ibn Waththāb. Abū Ḥātim prefers it; Ibn ʿĀshūr says corruption would emerge through followers or people emboldened by weakened authority. Some reports diverge on Ḥafṣ and Abū Bakr. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء الكوفة); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير وابن عامر,عاصم فى رواية أبى بكر وحمزة والكسائى); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وابن عامر,عاصم في رواية أبي بكر وحمزة والكسائي); THALABI(أبو عبيد وأبو حاتم); BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم والأعرج وعيسى والأعمش وابن وثاب); TABRISI(أبو علي,ابن كثير وابن عامر,الباقون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وأبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر والكوفيون غير حفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(باقي القراء); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأعرج والأعمش وابن وثاب وعيسى وابن كثير وابن عامر والكوفيون غير حفص); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم ويعقوب وخلف); QIRAAT-ER; THALABI(الآخرون)+]
- 40:26/q15/p3 Mujāhid reads an assimilated, doubled verb, with al-fasād as subject. — Samīn derives it from yataẓahhar with tāʾ assimilated into ẓāʾ; Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī also report the doubled consonants. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 40:26/q15/p4 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads the verb passive, with al-fasād nominative as deputy subject. — Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī report it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]
- 40:26/q15/p5 A further report contrasts al-Aʿmash's raised rāʾ with Ibn Masʿūd's open rāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya preserves this vocalization detail. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعمش وابن مسعود)]

## 40:26/q16 What does it mean for corruption to “appear”?  [يُظْهِرَ · meaning]
- 40:26/q16/p1 It becomes manifest or spreads through the land. — Dāmghānī contrasts manifest things with visible adornment and livelihood; Ibn al-Jawzī says ẓuhūr opposes hiddenness and cites 24:31 and 30:41; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it figuratively describes propagation. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:26/q17 What other senses of the root ẓ-h-r are listed in the attached lexical survey?  [يُظْهِرَ · meaning]
turns on: These are separate uses of the root in other contexts.
- 40:26/q17/p1 It can mean discovering, prevailing on someone, or disclosure. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 18:20, 66:3 and 72:26. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 40:26/q17/p2 It can mean climbing over something. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 18:97 and 43:33. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 40:26/q17/p3 It can mean dominance or elevation. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 9:33, 40:29 and 61:14; his general introduction contrasts ẓuhūr with concealment. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 40:26/q17/p4 It can mean apparent but invalid speech. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 13:33. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 40:26/q17/p5 It can denote backs or putting something behind oneself and neglecting it. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 3:187, 11:92 and 94:3 and al-Farazdaq's bi-ẓahr. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,الفرزدق)]
- 40:26/q17/p6 It can mean entering noon. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 24:58 and 30:18, including afternoon prayer. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 40:26/q18 What land is intended by “in the land”?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
- 40:26/q18/p1 Egypt, Pharaoh's kingdom. — Muqātil specifies Egypt; Ibn al-Jawzī groups this verse with Pharaoh's rule and Joseph's office; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the familiar land is Pharaoh's realm. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 40:26/q19 What other referents of “the land” are listed in the attached lexical survey?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: These are other Qur'anic uses in a lexical survey, not competing identifications of the land in 40:26.
- 40:26/q19/p1 It may denote the western land or a land of Gog and Magog identified by some with China. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 18:94. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 40:26/q19/p2 It may denote the seven earths. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites the provision of every creature in 11:6. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 40:26/q19/p3 It may denote the land of Islam. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites the punishment of exile in 5:33. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 40:26/q19/p4 It may denote the grave. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites disbelievers wishing to be leveled with earth in 4:42. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 40:26/q20 What “corruption” did Pharaoh allege Moses would cause?  [ٱلْفَسَادَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh's accusation targets Moses' religious reform, expected political violence, or reciprocal killing.
- 40:26/q20/p1 Changing their religion or obeying God was what Pharaoh called corruption. — Qatāda repeatedly says obedience to God was so labeled; Ṭabarī equates open worship of Moses' Lord with it. Saʿīd b. Abī ʿArūba likewise says Moses would bring obedience. Thaʿlabī calls it declining affairs and another worship; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Pharaoh judged by habit and benefit; Biqāʿī calls reform mislabeled corruption. [13 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن أبي عروبة); THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:26/q20/p2 Moses' followers would provoke unrest, fighting and damage to livelihoods. — Ibn ʿĪsā envisages fighting Pharaoh with believers; Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī describe conflict or rebellion. Nasafī names lost security, farming, trade and life; Biqāʿī adds bloodshed, captivity and plunder. Qurṭubī treats it as the alternative if religious change fails. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:26/q20/p3 Moses would kill Egyptian sons and spare their women, mirroring Egypt's acts against Israelites. — Muqātil and Ibn Jurayj give the explicit reversal; Ibn al-Jawzī also reports it. [3 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل)]

## 40:26/q21 How does Pharaoh's charge relate to the rest of the passage and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Different links concern preceding plots, the council scene, recurring corruption rhetoric, or the sūra's narrative focus.
- 40:26/q21/p1 It continues the pattern of peoples plotting against messengers and being destroyed. — Elmalılı links it to 40:5; Iṣlāḥī sees a warning to Quraysh and encouragement to Muhammad and companions amid trials, with the believer in Pharaoh's family as an example; Study Quran says 40:10–20 prepares that account. [4 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 40:26/q21/p2 The verse marks a separate council occasion, followed by Moses' response elsewhere. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says its initial wāw is not a reply to the previous proposal to kill believers' sons; Pharaoh spoke before his council while Moses was absent, and Moses later told his people he sought refuge (40:27), unlike the direct dialogue at 26:18–31. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:26/q21/p3 The corruption charge echoes Pharaoh's notables in 7:127 and the wider theme of corrupters claiming righteousness. — Study Quran cites 7:127 and compares 2:11 and 30:41. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 40:26/q21/p4 Ghafir foregrounds Pharaoh because Moses was sent to him and the sūra follows him. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the place of Qārūn in another context and cites 40:36. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 40:26/q22 What does this episode teach about Pharaoh, Moses and divine help?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes draw different theological lessons from Pharaoh's claim and Moses' survival.
- 40:26/q22/p1 Pharaoh's judgment was deluded: he called reform corruption and overlooked his own coming ruin. — Ibn Kathīr notes the irony in the proverb that Pharaoh became a preacher; Bursevī says Pharaoh foresaw religious change and disorder but not his and his people's destruction; Ibn ʿĀshūr says habit and near benefit were his only tests. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 40:26/q22/p2 Moses trusted God to aid believers who persevere against despots. — Kuranyolu says denial of the afterlife emboldens despots to think deeds go unpunished. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 40:26/q22/p3 Pharaoh's restraint reflected prudence under a rule that prophets and descendants are killed only by offspring of fornication. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits this from Imam al-Ṣādiq in al-ʿIlal. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 40:26/q22/p4 Pharaoh's supposed god may have been his sovereignty. — Elmalılı offers this as a conjecture. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 40:26/q23 What general rules of diminutive formation occur in the attached philological notes?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Radical position, whether the wāw is original or added, mobility and plural survival.
- 40:26/q23/p1 Second-radical root wāw is retained in diminutives. — Sībawayh says it is mobile and gives lawza, jawza and qawla. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 40:26/q23/p2 Third-radical root wāw normally changes to yāʾ, though some Arabs retain it. — Sībawayh prefers yāʾ because the preceding diminutive yāʾ attracts wāw; some Arabs keep wāw, but not in maqām and maqāl as maqyūm and maqyūl, where expected weakening applies. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض العرب); SIBAWAYH+]
- 40:26/q23/p3 An added third-radical wāw can remain, while final root wāw weakens to yāʾ. — Sībawayh cites jadwal and qaswar, whose plurals retain wāw, and al-Farazdaq's qasāwir; final wāw weakens in ghazwa, Raḍwā and ʿashwāʾ. The lengthening wāw of ʿajūz and jazūr disappears even in plurals; Muʿāwiya retains root wāw because it was mobile and persists in plural. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 40:26/q24 How do the attached notes treat lexicalized metathesis?  [* · grammar]
- 40:26/q24/p1 Established transposed forms retain their changed order in diminutives and derivatives. — Sībawayh gives qāʾil, al-ʿAjjāj's lāth from lāʾith, Ṭarīf's shāk from shāʾik, aynuq from anūq, and qasī from qawūs. Al-Khalīl derives masāʾīk from masāʾa; Kaʿb and Kuthayyir supply verse examples. Another analysis of rāʾanī moves hamza, and Abū al-Khaṭṭāb's rāʾa for rāya and Ḥassān's line show hamza/alif exchange. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 40:26/q25 How do the attached notes decline unusual proper names?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether names preserve dual endings, plural tanwīn, or weak letters, and whether sound counterparts determine defective inflection.
- 40:26/q25/p1 A dual-form name preferably retains dual inflection. — Sībawayh prefers nominative rajulān and oblique rajulayn; al-Khalīl analogizes the latter to sinīn, Filasṭīn and Qansarīn and denies nominative rajulayn for lack of parallel. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); SIBAWAYH+]
- 40:26/q25/p2 Some grammarians treat the dual-form name rajulān like ʿUthmān. — Sībawayh records this rival treatment. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(من النحويين)]
- 40:26/q25/p3 Names ending in feminine plural -āt remain declinable, though some Arabs omit tanwīn. — Sībawayh compares oblique tāʾ to Muslimīn's yāʾ and tanwīn to its nūn; ʿArafāt is definite without article. Some omit tanwīn from Adhruʿāt by analogy with feminine -ah, citing Imruʾ al-Qays and treating intervening alif as weak barrier. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض العرب)]
- 40:26/q25/p4 Defective names retain or restore endings under rules for weak nouns. — Sībawayh says final radical yāʾ/wāw after kasra/ḍamma disappears with compensating tanwīn in nominative/genitive; accusative depends on the sound counterpart. He discusses qāḍ, jawār, adl, aʿmā, yarمي, ghazw and imperative-derived names, restoring original radicals but not extraneous segments; poetic necessity may restore endings. [26 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); SIBAWAYH(الخليل)+]
- 40:26/q25/p5 Yūnus judges defective names by their sound definite counterparts, retaining jawārī. — Sībawayh records Yūnus's jawārī and marartu bi-jawārī; al-Khalīl objects that the rule would also force other nominative, genitive and accusative behaviors and maintains tanwīn as substitute for deleted yāʾ. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس); SIBAWAYH(الخليل)-]

## 40:26/q26 What other general Arabic morphosyntax do the attached notes give?  [* · grammar]
turns on: These notes concern different grammatical environments.
- 40:26/q26/p1 Certain nouns take initial hamzat al-waṣl, generally lost in connected speech. — Sībawayh lists ibn, ithnān, imruʾ, ibnm, ism and ist, with feminine forms; initial kasra persists despite unstable third-vowel ḍamma. Article hamza in questions and ayman in oaths resist deletion to avoid ambiguity; poetry restarts hamza after pauses. Frequent clitics may suppress vowels in huwa, hiya and imperative lām. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 40:26/q26/p2 Ordinary action verbs require a separate reflexive such as اقتل نفسك. — Sībawayh contrasts belief and judgment verbs, which allow reflexive clitics through subject-predicate complements; physical raʾā behaves like action verbs, mental raʾā like judgment verbs. The verse's separate object Moses is not reflexive. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 40:26/q26/p3 Some attached yāʾs on jussive verbs are quiescent before separate alif. — Ibn Mujāhid includes فاذكروني أذكركم and رب فأنظرني. [1 notes: IBNMUJAHID]
- 40:26/q26/p4 Nine possessive yāʾs are agreed to be quiescent. — Nashr includes Joseph's يدعونني إليه; weight of consecutive letters is one explanation among others. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 40:26/q27 How is āmīn explained in the attached lexical notes?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Short and long initial vowel and competing derivations.
- 40:26/q27/p1 Short-alif amīn is preferred by language scholars. — Ibn Qutayba says it stands apart from an implicit vocative and means 'so may God do'; Faṭḥal's verse supports it. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(فطحل); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أصحاب اللغة)+]
- 40:26/q27/p2 Long-alif āmīn is also reported from eloquent speakers. — Ibn Qutayba derives it from yā amīn, with yāʾ omitted in frequent use like yā Zayd becoming ā Zayd. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قوم فصحاء)]
- 40:26/q27/p3 Āmīn is a divine name and prayer response after al-Fātiḥa. — Some exegetes derive it from shortened 'O God, answer me' with the vocative omitted. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 40:26/q28 What rules for easing hamza at pause appear in the attached recitation notes?  [* · readings]
turns on: The segment immediately before hamza and whether it is original, extra or only pause-silent.
- 40:26/q28/p1 Hamza after silent alif can become alif at pause. — Nashr says quiescent hamza permits replacement after fatḥa, with treatment before a consonant discussed later. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 40:26/q28/p2 After extra silent yāʾ or wāw, hamza becomes a matching letter and assimilates. — Nashr cites al-nasīʾ, barīʾ and qurūʾ. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 40:26/q28/p3 After another silent consonant, hamza drops and transfers its vowel backward. — Nashr includes sound consonants and original yāʾ/wāw, long or lin, with seven sound-consonant examples; a consonant before moving hamza is only temporarily silent at pause, while rawm and rasm are treated elsewhere. [3 notes: NASHR]

## 40:26/q29 What accounts of the seven forms of Qur'anic recitation appear in the attached notes?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether variant wording is prophetically received or independently substituted.
- 40:26/q29/p1 The Prophet received seven forms from Gabriel, which he transmitted to companions. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Bukhārī's report of successive increase to seven, the ʿUmar–Hishām difference over al-Furqān as revelation at different times, and Ubayy and Ibn Masʿūd as recipients; the dispensation eased the community while preserving inimitable wording. Anas's synonymous alternatives were transmitted from the Prophet, as 15:9's preservation promise requires. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أنس بن مالك,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 40:26/q29/p2 Companions were free to replace revealed words independently with dialect synonyms. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects this: it would erase inimitability, allow alteration of revelation and violate preservation. [0 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 40:26/q29/p3 Arabic dialect examples clarify otherwise obscure Qur'anic expressions. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives usage for 'Originator of heavens and earth,' afātiḥuka as 'litigate' for 7:89, a youth's yatakhawwafunī and ʿUmar's acceptance for 16:47, and Quṭba b. Mālik's report of the Prophet reciting 'lofty date palms' in dawn prayer, recorded by Muslim. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب,فتى,قطبة بن مالك)]

## 40:26/q30 What literary judgments accompany the attached excerpts?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Different rhetorical examples and judgments, none offered as a competing gloss of 40:26.
- 40:26/q30/p1 Al-Bāqillānī faults five lines of Imruʾ al-Qays as obscene, weak or incoherent. — He calls the sexual boast repellent; the beloved's refusal badly woven; the address to Fāṭima limp despite a proposed feminine-style defense; adjacent hemistichs mismatched; and her rule over the heart inconsistent with the prior pose. [5 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 40:26/q30/p2 Moon imagery can represent waxing, waning and changing renown, but must fit the phenomenon. — Jurjānī faults a verse that treats waning as absence before conjunction; Ibn Bābak likens the moon to veiling itself. Ibn Nubāta likens past kings' faded renown and a patron's praise to moon and sun; another image gives a patron light wherever viewed. Jurjānī limits the analysis to conceptual, not visual, resemblance. [6 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(ابن بابك,ابن نباتة)]
- 40:26/q30/p3 Reverse comparison can praise indirectly by making the lesser thing the derived likeness. — Jurjānī says reversal fails if exaggeration depends on the usual superiority; Muḥammad b. Wahb makes dawn resemble the caliph's face. Indirect praise treats such resemblance as an honor for dawn and gives delight like a gift without reminder of favor. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(محمد بن وهيب)]
- 40:26/q30/p4 A broad subject can have distinct crafted semantic images. — Jurjānī contrasts the Kharijite and Abū Tammām and al-Buḥturī and Mutanabbī; he cites Jāḥiẓ's poetry-as-craft analogy. If repetition preserved identical meaning, judgments of improvement, failure and hidden borrowing would make no sense. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 40:26/q31 Where do the attached discussions locate the Qur'an's inimitability?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether miraculous force lies chiefly in literary composition, factual knowledge, divine diversion, or their combination.
- 40:26/q31/p1 Its unsurpassed eloquence, arrangement, composition and meanings make it inimitable. — Itqān reports Qāḍī Abū Bakr on unprecedented arrangement, Rāzī on eloquence and lack of defects, Zamalkānī on balanced composition, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya on divinely chosen wording. Ālūsī describes five ranks of speech, Qur'anic order beyond letters, sermons or sajʿ, and combined grandeur and sweetness. Ibn ʿĀshūr adopts this view and notes Bāqillānī's defense. [21 notes: ITQAN(ابن عطية,الزملكاني,القاضي أبو بكر,فخر الدين الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الآمدي وغيره,الوليد); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو بكر الباقلاني,مذهب المحققين)+]
- 40:26/q31/p2 Its truthful knowledge of past peoples, hidden things and future events is miraculous. — Itqān reports the eyewitness quality of ancient narratives. Ālūsī stresses Muhammad's unlettered access to exact past laws and hidden thoughts. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the Romans' victory in year seven and resulting conversions, 24:55, future transport in 16:8, the conquest announced in 48:1, entry in 48:27, and the unmet challenge of 2:23; he says prediction is uncommon and adds little to textual eloquence. [11 notes: ITQAN(آخرون); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 40:26/q31/p3 God diverted capable Arabs from matching the Qur'an (ṣarfah). — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes this to al-Ḥasan al-Ashʿarī, al-Naẓẓām, some Muʿtazilites and Ibn Ḥazm. Itqān says Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects diversion; Ālūsī objects that lost skill would have been reported, 17:88 presumes capacity, earlier Arabic could otherwise match it, and challenge to incapable people would be empty. He rebuts objections about ordinary speakers, brief eloquence, companions' disputes, demands for proof and audience training. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الحسن الأشعري، النظام، وطائفة من المعتزلة، وابن حزم); ITQAN(ابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 40:26/q31/p4 The miracle lies in the whole Qur'an's combined order, eloquence and reports of the unseen. — Ālūsī attributes this to al-Āmidī and others, noting wide acceptance. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الآمدي وغيره)]
