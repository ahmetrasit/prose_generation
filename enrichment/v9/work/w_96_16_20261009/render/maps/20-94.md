## 20:94/q01 Who is speaking?  [قَالَ · referent]
- 20:94/q01/p1 Aaron speaks to Moses after Moses returns to the calf worship. — Muqātil identifies the brothers; Yaḥyā places the rebuke on Moses' return. [2 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 20:94/q02 How is يَبْنَؤُمَّ read and analyzed?  [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · readings]
turns on: The mīm vowel, compounding and omission of a possessive ending.
- 20:94/q02/p1 The mīm has fatḥa, explained as a fused vocative or shortened yā ibn ummā. — Ibn Mujāhid and Fārisī list Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and Ḥafṣ; Jishumī adds Abū Jaʿfar. Naḥḥās compares a fused noun; Fārisī, Jishumī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya compare khamsata ʿashar or lost alif; Bursevī traces ummī through ummā. [10 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); BURSEVI]
- 20:94/q02/p2 The mīm has kasra, a genitive construction with omitted possessive yāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid and Fārisī assign this to Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, Kisāʾī and Ibn ʿĀmir; Naṣafī says Syrian and Kufan readers except Ḥafṣ. Fārisī also permits a compound; Jishumī cites ibn ʿamm and yā ghulām; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the yāʾ loss irregular. QIRAAT-ER notes overt genitive inflection. [16 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم في رواية أبي بكر وحمزة والكسائي وابن عامر); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وحفص عن عاصم,الشاعر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير عن عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); BURSEVI; QIRAAT-ER(vw)]
- 20:94/q02/p3 A form with explicit possessive yāʾ is also attested. — Abū Isḥāq permits non-Quranic yā ibna ummī; QIRAAT-ER records a possessive variant. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ)]

## 20:94/q03 Why does Aaron say 'son of my mother'?  [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 20:94/q03/p1 He appeals to kinship and maternal tenderness to calm Moses. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give this motive; Biqāʿī notes maternal pain. Burhān and Itqān call it affectionate address; Burhān links the renewed vocative to emotional distance. [13 notes: THALABI(الكلبي وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 20:94/q03/p2 In an inward reading it recalls mercy and Moses' Sinai encounter. — Bursevī reports Sufi masters linking maternal nurture to Aaron's merciful prophethood (19:53); Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya describes Moses' longing, collapse and angels dragging his head, with Ḥāfiẓ's Sīmurgh image of a lofty station. [4 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الحافظ,بعض الكبار)]

## 20:94/q04 What was the brothers' parentage?  [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · referent]
turns on: Literal genealogy versus rhetorical maternal appeal.
- 20:94/q04/p1 They shared both parents. — Often called the majority view; the maternal address is then tender rather than a literal distinction. [7 notes: THALABI(الكلبي وغيره); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:94/q04/p2 They shared only a mother. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī and Bayḍāwī report it; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it weak and Ālūsī rejects it as the explanation. [4 notes: THALABI(الكلبي وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:94/q04/p3 They shared a father but not a mother. — Māwardī reports this contradictory alternative. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:94/q04/p4 Motherhood is literal, fatherhood a legal attribution. — Thaʿlabī transmits this further account. [1 notes: THALABI(الكلبي وغيره)]

## 20:94/q05 What is notable about the written vocative and its parallel?  [يَبْنَؤُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Orthography and degree of narrative detail.
- 20:94/q05/p1 The connected spelling varies in retained alifs. — Itqān describes joined ibn umm, hamza written as wāw and omitted ibn hamza; Nashr says al-Dānī saw both alifs lost while the author saw codices retaining one. [2 notes: ITQAN; NASHR]
- 20:94/q05/p2 The fuller Ṭāhā wording uses a vocative particle for narrative detail or emphasis. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the concise al-Aʿrāf report; his notes phrase the direction inconsistently but cite clarity, detail and emphasis. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:94/q06 What do the beard and head designate, and how is 'beard' read?  [بِلِحْيَتِى بِرَأْسِىٓ · meaning]
turns on: Anatomical reference and dialectal vocalization.
- 20:94/q06/p1 Beard means chin hair; head means its hair or forelock. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī define beard; Thaʿlabī calls these honored parts; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Biqāʿī identify head hair. Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn note variant plural vowels. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 20:94/q06/p2 Head can stand for Aaron's ear. — Māwardī reports a view that Moses held ear and beard. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:94/q06/p3 The beard's lām has the usual kasra. — Samīn calls it the eloquent majority form. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]
- 20:94/q06/p4 The beard's lām has fatḥa in a Hijazi reading. — Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī attribute it to ʿĪsā ibn Sulaymān. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى بن سليمان الحجازي); SAMIN-DURR(عيسى بن سليمان الحجازي); ALUSI-FULL(عيسى بن سليمان الحجازي)]

## 20:94/q07 What is the bāʾ in 'by my beard' doing?  [بِلِحْيَتِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle is grammatically extra.
- 20:94/q07/p1 It is extra for emphasis. — Jishumī compares bi-junūdihi and 17:1; Samīn rejects this as overreach, comparing 2:195. [1 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:94/q07/p2 It contributes to the construction, or follows an omitted object 'me'. — Samīn supplies both analyses and rejects the extra-bāʾ view. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 20:94/q08 Did Moses seize Aaron, and for what purpose?  [لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَا بِرَأْسِىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: What occurred and what Moses intended.
- 20:94/q08/p1 He grasped head hair and beard in anger over idolatry or Aaron's apparent lapse. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Kalbī and reports specify right hand on hair and left on beard; Ibn Kathīr says he cast tablets and dragged Aaron. Māwardī prefers reproach for insufficient condemnation; Ibn al-Anbārī says Moses thought Aaron disobeyed; Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī and Islāḥī emphasize religious zeal. Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests an intended strike. [23 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف,من الناس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; MAWARDI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 20:94/q08/p2 He drew Aaron near without contempt, as one handles oneself in agitation. — Wāḥidī and Qurṭubī compare holding one's own beard; Rāzī compares twisting fingers and stresses brotherly identification. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:94/q08/p3 He wished to whisper about the tablets or investigate privately. — Māwardī reports the whispering account; Rāzī says Israel's suspicion, even an accusation that Moses killed Aaron, framed the investigation. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 20:94/q08/p4 He suspected Aaron supported the calf worshipers. — Māwardī reports and rejects this as impermissible of prophets. [0 notes: MAWARDI-FULL-]
- 20:94/q08/p5 The prohibition alone does not prove a completed seizure. — Rāzī cites prohibitions at 33:48 and 39:65; he distinguishes the separate report of head-pulling. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:94/q09 Why did Aaron ask Moses to stop?  [لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَا بِرَأْسِىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: How the gesture appeared to Aaron and Israel.
- 20:94/q09/p1 Observers might mistake it for contempt, punishment or rejection. — Naḥḥās, Qurṭubī's Ibn ʿAbbās and Rāzī cite possible misreading by a suspicious people. [4 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL]
- 20:94/q09/p2 He sought to end an angry or threatening gesture. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bayḍāwī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe angry seizure; Ṭabāṭabāʾī suggests a blow. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:94/q09/p3 The local custom treated beard-taking like taking a hand. — Wāḥidī says it was not then humiliating and that custom determines assessment. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 20:94/q10 Does the episode entail sin by Moses or Aaron?  [لَا تَأْخُذْ بِلِحْيَتِى وَلَمْ تَرْقُبْ قَوْلِى · theology]
turns on: Prophetic immunity and the reading of Moses' motive.
- 20:94/q10/p1 No: an ambiguous episode can reflect differing judgments about the better course or a conditional order. — Rāzī lists dilemmas over reproach, seizure and prohibition, cites prophetic immunity at 2:36, allows leaving the best course and conditions following on avoiding disorder. He rejects anger alone as an excuse. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:94/q10/p2 Moses' religiously motivated discipline is compatible with infallibility. — Ālūsī rejects Rāzī's anger objection and sees Aaron's apparent lapse as disciplinable; Māwardī rejects the thought that Moses suspected Aaron of idolatry. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)+]
- 20:94/q10/p3 If minor prophetic sins are allowed, the objection does not arise. — Rāzī distinguishes this theological starting point. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:94/q11 What division did Aaron fear?  [فَرَّقْتَ بَيْنَ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether departure, fighting or existing belief was the decisive cause.
- 20:94/q11/p1 Leaving with believers for Moses would separate them from the calf's remaining followers. — Ibn Zayd and Ibn ʿAbbās report this; Ṭabarī prefers it because Aaron urged the people and they refused (20:90–91). Naḥḥās, Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr give similar accounts; Islāḥī adds Sāmirī's sway and Aaron's fear. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)+]
- 20:94/q11/p2 Forceful confrontation would split factions and cause killing. — Muqātil, Ibn Jurayj and many later authors give this; Wāḥidī describes believers, calf followers and Sāmirī's party; Biqāʿī cites few weak allies and retaliation; Ālūsī fears lasting separation. Qatāda recalls earlier righteous people disliking division. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:94/q11/p3 Different beliefs had already divided the people. — Māwardī reports this account. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:94/q11/p4 Moses would himself be blamed for splitting them through fighting or separate followings. — Māwardī mentions fighting; Nasafī's wording attributes separation to Moses' action. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 20:94/q12 What was Moses' earlier instruction?  [قَوْلِى · interpretation]
turns on: Departure versus stewardship under changed circumstances.
- 20:94/q12/p1 Remain deputy, reform, preserve unity and await Moses' return. — Muqātil and later authors invoke 7:142; Biqāʿī says it did not require the sword and compares gentle resistance; Ālūsī cites Aaron's near killing (7:150). Rāzī makes following conditional on avoiding disorder; Asad reports Zamakhsharī on unity. Durr says Aaron stayed among the believers, listening and obeying, because he feared Moses would accuse him of dividing Israel and failing to observe his instruction. [15 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); DURR-FULL]
- 20:94/q12/p2 Follow Moses with believers. — Ṭabarī prefers this account of Moses' reproach; Aaron feared abandoning the rest. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)+]

## 20:94/q13 What does 'you did not heed my word' mean?  [وَلَمْ تَرْقُبْ قَوْلِى · meaning]
turns on: Preserving versus awaiting, and whose words are meant.
- 20:94/q13/p1 You did not observe, preserve or enact my instruction. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Ṭabarī derive it from watchful attention; Muqātil and others tie it to care for the people. Jishumī notes raqīb and mirqab; Asad glosses qawlī as what Moses said; Nasafī sees scope for juristic reasoning. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; JISHUMI]
- 20:94/q13/p2 You did not wait for my instruction, return or decision. — Abū ʿUbayda, Ibn Jurayj and al-ʿUyūn give waiting; Taʾwīlāt says Aaron waited obediently; al-ʿUyūn also offers consideration of Moses' situation. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,العيون)]
- 20:94/q13/p3 Moses did not await Aaron's own explanation. — Ālūsī transmits Ibn Jurayj's shifted implied speaker. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج)]

## 20:94/q14 How is the final clause construed and read?  [وَلَمْ تَرْقُبْ قَوْلِى · grammar]
turns on: Coordination versus circumstance; a separate variant changes the verb form.
- 20:94/q14/p1 It coordinates with farraqta as a second feared accusation. — Samīn calls it accusative; Ālūsī reports the same. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:94/q14/p2 It is circumstantial: division happened without awaiting Moses' instruction. — Ālūsī reports this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:94/q14/p3 It is circumstantial with Aaron's own speech implied. — Ālūsī cites Ibn Jurayj: Moses spoke without awaiting Aaron's clarification. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 20:94/q14/p4 Abū Jaʿfar reads turqib from arqaba. — Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī specify ḍamma on the prefix and kasra on qāf. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر)]

## 20:94/q15 What wider narrative and lessons are attached to the episode?  [* · links]
- 20:94/q15/p1 Al-Aʿrāf supplies the parallel seizure, Aaron's weakness and later reconciliation. — Qurṭubī cites 7:150; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes Moses' prayer for both; Asad points to 7:150. - 20:94/q15/p2 Aaron's restraint was a plausible effort to keep cohesion. — Wāḥidī calls it reasoned judgment; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī identify accommodating reform; Bursevī classifies denial, explanation and repentance as excuses; Islāḥī notes danger of bloodshed. - 20:94/q15/p4 Aaron was beloved and personally deferential to Moses. — Muqātil says seventy thousand children were named for Aaron; Ibn ʿAbbās says he stood in awe of Moses; Bursevī relates his love to patience and cites ʿAlī, Socrates, Buzurjmihr, Aristotle, prophetic gentleness and the Mathnawī. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(أرسطو,بزرجمهر,سقراط,علي)]

- 20:94/q15/p6 The wording carries further rhetoric and orthography. — Itqān cites farraqta as letter reversal; Ālūsī says Children of Israel evokes common descent; QIRAAT-ER notes a hamza-alif spelling variant in Israel. [3 notes: ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(HzT)]
