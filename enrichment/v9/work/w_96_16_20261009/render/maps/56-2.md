# 56:2: 7 questions

## 56:2/q01 What does waqʿatihā denote?  [لِوَقْعَتِهَا · meaning]
- 56:2/q01/p1 The Resurrection's occurrence or rising, conceived as a single forceful, momentous event. — Al-Dāmghānī glosses waqaʿa as arising or coming to pass and identifies the Occurrence as Resurrection. Al-Rāzī stresses one occurrence, all at once. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı trace waqʿa from a forceful fall to a great event or crisis, sometimes specifically war; al-Ālūsī identifies this instance as Resurrection. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 56:2/q02 What does the lām in li-waqʿatihā express?  [لِوَقْعَتِهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām locates the denial in time, gives the occurrence as its cause, or relates denial to the Event as its object.
- 56:2/q02/p1 It marks the time when the Event occurs. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī compare 89:24. Al-Ālūsī and al-Kashf take it temporally in the denial and self-encouragement readings. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives this temporal use from specification and compares 17:78, 65:1, and 7:143; he cites the Prophet's statement that prayer at its proper time is the best deed, without grading, and distinguishes 88:6. Bursawī gives this as one option. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 56:2/q02/p2 It gives the cause: the Event's overwhelming occurrence leaves no room for lying or false report. — Al-Rāzī says its overwhelming occurrence stops lying or false denial, unlike denial before it. Bursawī gives a causal alternative in which nothing reported of the Event is false. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 56:2/q02/p3 It marks the object or relation of the lying: no one falsifies the Event in reporting it. — Al-Rāzī explicitly contrasts this relational reading with the causal lām. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 56:2/q03 How is kādhibatun grammatically formed and construed?  [كَاذِبَةٌ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form functions as a verbal noun, modifies an omitted person or state, intensifies the noun, or carries a passive sense in active form.
- 56:2/q03/p1 It is an abstract or verbal noun for lying, denial, or falsehood, despite its participial shape. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qushayrī, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compare ʿāqiba and ʿāfiya; some add nāzila. Al-Kisāʾī compares lāghiya in 88:11 and khāʾina; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī cite qāʾiman for qiyāman and a woman's rhyme, and al-Qurṭubī adds ʿāʾidhan bi-llāh. Al-Burhān and al-Itqān state the general agent-or-object noun for action rule, the latter comparing al-maftūn and saying its bāʾ is not redundant. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Samīn give takdhīb and compare khāʾinat al-aʿyun. Al-Ālūsī notes a reservation: fāʿila is rare as a verbal noun. Al-Naḥḥās makes kādhibatun the subject of laysa. [30 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(الفراء,الكسائي); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والحسن); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 56:2/q03/p2 It is an active participle qualifying an omitted soul or person who lies. — Al-Rāzī and al-Zamakhsharī supply nafs; al-Samīn calls li-waqʿatihā the fronted predicate and kādhiba the delayed subject. Al-Ālūsī prefers nafs to an omitted maqāla because a person is more ordinarily called a liar than a statement; Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the form from intransitive kadhiba. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī compare the lām in 89:24. [16 notes: RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 56:2/q03/p3 It qualifies an omitted state, case, report, or statement rather than a person. — Al-Jishumī gives an omitted noun meaning no false case. Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies an omitted ḥāl, with either a true report or an occurrence that cannot fail; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report this circumstantial analysis. Al-Qurṭubī allows state or person, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report or case. Al-Ālūsī considers maqāla less idiomatic than nafs. [9 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:2/q03/p4 The feminine ending intensifies the expression. — Al-Rāzī relates this possibility to his account of the ending in al-wāqiʿa. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 56:2/q03/p5 An active participle shape can carry a passive sense while keeping active participle agreement. — Al-Fārisī says the passive meaning does not alter the pronoun's raising because the form follows the active participle pattern. [1 notes: BURHAN(الفارسي)]

## 56:2/q04 What does the verse say cannot occur at the Event?  [لَيْسَ لِوَقْعَتِهَا كَاذِبَةٌ · interpretation]
turns on: The senses of kādhiba range from retreat or failure, to denial by a person, to falsehood in reports or circumstances, to self-deception; the omitted noun and the lām affect which applies.
- 56:2/q04/p1 Nothing can repel, reverse, or recall the Event once it comes. — Muqātil and Qatāda also deny a second occurrence or return; al-Māwardī reports Qatāda's addition that there is no consultation. Al-Farrāʾ and Ibn Qutayba cite Abū Tharwān's Banū Numayr idiom for absence of rebuttal; Ibn Qutayba adds that an attacker who does not 'lie' does not retreat. Al-Zajjāj compares an unretreating charge. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī cite the assault idiom and Zuhayr's lion line for not faltering. Ibn Kathīr cites 42:47, 70:1–2, and 6:73 for God's irresistible will. Al-Ālūsī presents this discouragement or holding-back sense but prefers the Event's certain truth. [27 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(أبو ثروان); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو ثروان); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والحسن وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,زهير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن، قتادة,زهير)]
- 56:2/q04/p2 No one can deny the Event's occurrence when it becomes manifest. — Ibn Kāmil explicitly includes believer and unbeliever. Al-Zamakhsharī contrasts present denial with affirmation then; al-Thaʿlabī reports Sībawayh's 'no denier.' Al-Rāzī gives both personal and verbal-noun construals. Al-Qāshānī says each witnesses his own happiness or misery. Elmalılı adds that no one escapes its impact. The Study Quran says it overwhelms former deniers. Al-Ālūsī also pictures a futile question addressed to the Hour as representational metaphor, imagined or literal. [23 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(سيبويه); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن كامل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الثوري,الكسائي); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة والحسن); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 56:2/q04/p3 No soul will then lie about God or deny the unseen; all will acknowledge the truth. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān cite professions of faith upon seeing punishment (40:84), delayed belief (26:201), and persistent doubt until the Hour (22:55). Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī compare 89:24. Bursawī specifies that claims of God's partners, child, or spouse and denial of raising the dead end then. Abū Ḥayyān criticizes al-Zamakhsharī's extensive treatment. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 56:2/q04/p4 Its occurrence and the report of it are true; no false case or falsehood belongs to it. — Al-Jishumī and al-Kisāʾī say the Event contains no falsehood. Al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī compare the absence of idle speech in 88:11. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes the report's truth from unfailing occurrence. Al-Ṭabrisī invokes transmitted evidence and reason. Al-Biqāʿī extends the certainty to every promised event arriving and every denied event staying absent. Bursawī offers a causal lām. Al-Ālūsī prefers this sense as clearer, while noting the rarity of fāʿila as a verbal noun. [22 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قول آخر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المواردي); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(المجمع); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 56:2/q04/p5 The Event's occurrence itself cannot fail; it must come to pass. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya illustrates a person who does not fail when carrying out a task; al-Samīn compares a promiser who does not lie. Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb says the Event must happen. Ibn ʿĀshūr, citing Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī, explains metaphorical 'lying' of a failed cause through Arabic attributions of speech to non-speaking things: Abū al-Najm's speaking girths and 'lying' garments. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي)]
- 56:2/q04/p6 No self can falsely reassure its owner that the Event's terror is bearable. — Al-Zamakhsharī invokes the idiom of a soul emboldening someone before danger and compares people to scattered moths in 101:4. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Rāzī, and al-Ālūsī give the great-ordeal idiom; al-Kashf explains the lām as temporal. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the self-deception as transitive kadhdhaba and compares an eye deceiving its owner about what it saw. Abū Ḥayyān calls al-Zamakhsharī's elaboration excessive. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 56:2/q04/p7 The Day's terror prevents even a great liar from lying. — Al-Rāzī calls this the intensive construal and explicitly prefers it for conveying the Day's horror. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 56:2/q04/p8 Anyone denying the Event then would be an immense liar. — Al-Rāzī presents this as an alternative to the construal in which terror silences even a great liar, and says the latter better expresses the Day's horror. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 56:2/q04/p9 No one lies about anything at that time. — Al-Rāzī says the overwhelming Event prevents lying generally. Al-Ālūsī says this depends on accepting that no one lies then and explaining the deniers' oath in 6:23. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 56:2/q04/p10 The Resurrection is wholly serious, without jest. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as another explanation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 56:2/q05 How does this clause function in the opening sentence?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether verse 2 answers the opening idhā, interrupts a condition and its answer, describes the Event circumstantially, or opens independently; analyses differ on what governs idhā.
- 56:2/q05/p1 It supplies the answer to 'when the Event occurs' in verse 1. — Elmalılı prefers this apparent construction. The Study Quran reads idhā and verse 2 continuously as an adverbial clause. Al-Zamakhsharī's reported alternative allows the clause as a conditional answer; Abū Ḥayyān rejects making laysa itself govern idhā because laysa has no event or time, citing the Friday example where the preposed predicate governs the temporal phrase. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 56:2/q05/p2 It is a parenthesis or interpolation between the condition and its response. — Abū Ḥayyān adopts this analysis. Al-Samīn says it has no syntactic position and the answer is omitted. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, as reported by al-Ālūsī, also allows a parenthesis confirming the Event's certainty. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 56:2/q05/p3 It is a circumstantial clause describing the Event. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives it accusative circumstantial status. Abū al-Faḍl al-Rāzī makes al-wāqiʿa its circumstantial referent, governed by waqaʿat, giving three states of one referent; if all clauses are circumstantial, the governor of idhā is omitted, for example yuḥāsabūn. Abū Ḥayyān notes that grammarians dispute the number and classification of these circumstantial clauses and predicates. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الفضل الرازي,ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفضل,ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 56:2/q05/p4 It stands as an independent opening sentence with no syntactic position. — Al-Zamakhsharī says this follows if idhā is governed by laysa, but not if the clause is the conditional answer or idhā is the object of an implied udhkur. Al-Samīn also allows an opening without syntactic position. Abū Ḥayyān rejects laysa governing a temporal adverb because laysa has no event or time. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 56:2/q06 How is the following 'lowering, raising' phrase attached?  [* · links]
- 56:2/q06/p1 It attaches to the earlier statement, not to the immediately later clause. — Abū ʿUbayda says attachment to the later clause would require the accusative; Arabic may shift repeated predication from accusative to nominative, as in 70:15–17 and an anonymous rajaz line. [1 notes: MAJAZ]

## 56:2/q07 What does the Event reveal about people's states?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct consequences: exposure of sincerity, witnessed felicity or misery, and disgrace for former deniers.
- 56:2/q07/p1 It exposes pretence and distinguishes the safe and honored from those who meet regret and loss. — Al-Qushayrī says soundness and uprightness lead to safety and honor, while deviation leads to regret and loss; he cites a poem in which tears on faces distinguish a true mourner from a pretender. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 56:2/q07/p2 Each person witnesses happiness or misery as the Event lowers the wretched and raises the fortunate. — Al-Qāshānī links that direct witnessing to the impossibility of denying the Event. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 56:2/q07/p3 It proves the deniers wrong and leaves them disgraced and foolish before the gathered. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes this a warning to those who deny Resurrection before it occurs. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
