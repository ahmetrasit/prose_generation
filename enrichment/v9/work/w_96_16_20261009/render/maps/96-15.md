# 96:15: 23 questions

## 96:15/q01 What does kallā say here?  [كَلَّا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ over whether kallā directly rebukes conduct, declares ignorance of divine sight, or reverses Abū Jahl's boast.
- 96:15/q01/p1 It rebukes and restrains the man who forbids worship. — Al-Ṭabarī says it denies Abū Jahl's boast that he can trample Muḥammad's neck; al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī and al-Rāzī connect it to forbidding God's worship and commanding al-Lāt or idols. Abū Ḥayyān extends the rebuke to similar preventers; al-Biqāʿī calls the offender's blindness chosen, and Quṭb calls this the passage's decisive threat against tyranny. [23 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ABUHAYYAN+; ALUSI+; BAYDAWI+; NASAFI+]
- 96:15/q01/p2 It means that he does not know that God sees him. — Muqātil's gloss is transmitted by al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī; al-Rāzī adds that knowledge without acting on it counts as ignorance. Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī give the same sense. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); RAZI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; TABRISI+]
- 96:15/q01/p3 It denies that Abū Jahl can kill or trample Muḥammad; a follower of Muḥammad will instead kill him and trample his chest. — Al-Rāzī gives this as an alternative answer to Abū Jahl's boast. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 96:15/q02 Why is kallā placed here?  [كَلَّا · rhetoric]
- 96:15/q02/p1 This later kallā reinforces an earlier rebuke; its placement resolves suspense created by the earlier one. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the closing position is the natural place for this counterpart; Corpus Coranicum observes that kallā also opens the rebuke in 96:6–8 and the final call in 96:19. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 96:15/q03 What must the offender stop?  [لَّمْ يَنتَهِ · interpretation]
- 96:15/q03/p1 He must stop forbidding prayer, denying, insulting, opposing or harming Muḥammad. — Muqātil names denial and turning away; al-Wāḥidī adds insult and harm; al-Biqāʿī says the conditional leaves room for stopping and repentance while preserving responsibility. Bursevī specifies repentance and submission before death. Al-Ṭabarī links the repeated 'Have you seen?' clauses in 96:9,11,13 to the same forbider who denies and turns away. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:15/q04 How does the oath and condition work?  [لَئِن لَّمْ يَنتَهِ لَنَسْفَعًۢا · grammar]
turns on: The disagreement concerns the function of the lām before in.
- 96:15/q04/p1 The lām in la-in signals an implied oath; la-nasfaʿan answers it, supplying the omitted answer to the condition. — Al-Zarkashī calls the lām preparatory to the oath's answer and compares 59:12 and 3:12; al-Wāḥidī calls it an oath lām and notes that an initial lām attaches only to a noun or noun-like form. Ibn ʿĀshūr states that the condition's own apodosis is omitted; the light emphatic nūn commonly follows affirmative oaths. [10 notes: BURHAN(الواحدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN+; IBNASHUR+; BURHAN(أثيرالدين)-]
- 96:15/q04/p2 The lām is simply an emphatic lām. — Athīr al-Dīn gives this analysis; al-Zarkashī rejects it in favor of an oath introduction. [1 notes: BURHAN(أثيرالدين); BURHAN-]

## 96:15/q05 Why does yantahi lack its final yāʾ?  [يَنتَهِ · grammar]
- 96:15/q05/p1 The jussive drops the yāʾ. — Al-Naḥḥās says the noncanonical retained-yāʾ form assumes that yāʾ is mobile. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 96:15/q06 What does nasfaʿ mean?  [لَنَسْفَعًۢا · meaning]
turns on: Arabic usage cited for seizure and pulling, burning and darkening, striking, branding, or humiliation; some commentators combine these effects.
- 96:15/q06/p1 To seize, grasp and pull forcefully. — Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba compare taking someone by the hand; Muʾarrij calls it Qurashī usage. Al-Mubarrad stresses a painful tug, and al-Farrāʾ includes crushing or bending the forelock. Al-Azharī, ʿAmr ibn Maʿdīkarib and other cited poets describe grabbing a horse or man's forelock. Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima and al-Ḥasan gloss it as taking hold; 55:41 supplies a Qurʾānic parallel. Al-Biqāʿī gathers dragging, pushing and striking in the violent act; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes the verb occurs only here in the Qurʾān. - 96:15/q06/p2 To blacken, scorch or disfigure by fire or sun. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite a line about dark hearthstones; al-Khalīl describes a light scorch changing skin color. Al-Ṭabrisī mentions people scorched by fire, and al-Samīn relates marks on a horse, tripods, an angry face, falcons and a dark-cheeked widow. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says flame and poisonous heat commonly use this verb. [27 notes: FARRA; TAB(قيل); TAB-FULL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI(الخليل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN(التبريزي,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BINTSHATI]
- 96:15/q06/p3 To strike or slap the face. — Al-Rāzī reports this gloss; al-Qurṭubī says blows can accompany the seizure and dragging. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares a bird smiting its prey with its wings. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); BINTSHATI]
- 96:15/q06/p4 To brand or mark him. — Ibn ʿAbbās connects Abū Jahl to the branded snout in 68:16; a modern Turkish commentary also records stamping his forehead as a figurative explanation. [3 notes: RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 96:15/q06/p5 To humiliate or subjugate him. — Al-Rāzī records this as a separate gloss; ʿAyn al-Maʿānī treats seizure of the forelock as coercion, and modern commentators list disgrace and humiliation among the figurative explanations. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 96:15/q07 What event does the forelock threat foretell?  [لَنَسْفَعًۢا بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes dispute whether the scene belongs to Judgment, worldly defeat at Badr, or an unspecified act, and whether seizure or scorching is foregrounded.
- 96:15/q07/p1 Abū Jahl will be seized by the forelock and dragged to Hell, especially at the Resurrection. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī and others compare 55:41, where sinners are seized by forelocks and feet. Al-Qurṭubī says he may be folded with his feet and thrown into Hell. Muqātil and Bursevī specify angels dragging him face-first; Ibn Kathīr alternatively allows the forelock to be blackened on Judgment Day. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ hears in the wording both dragging and scorching. [41 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB(قيل); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو علي); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(المبرد,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:15/q07/p2 He will be seized, restrained and humiliated; the threat may be worldly or unspecified. — Al-Ṭabarī emphasizes restraint and abasement; al-Rāzī explicitly allows this life or the afterlife. Al-Qurṭubī says the forms of seizure and blows converge. Kurdish/Turkish commentary says tyrants may receive punishment in this world, the next or both. [16 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 96:15/q07/p3 It predicts Abū Jahl's defeat and seizure by Muslims at Badr. — Al-Rāzī says God would place his forelock in Muslim hands if he resumed hindering prayer; Ibn Masʿūd's killing and dragging of Abū Jahl is taken as fulfillment. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī repeat the worldly reading. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:15/q07/p4 His face or forelock will be blackened, burnt or marked as damned. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī understand the forelock as the face's front; Ibn Kathīr places the blackening on Judgment Day. Al-Jishumī adds the image of a black face and blue eyes. [12 notes: FARRA; TAB(قيل); TAB-FULL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(التبريزي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي)]

## 96:15/q08 How is lanasfaʿan read?  [لَنَسْفَعًۢا · readings]
turns on: The readings vary in nūn weight and, for Ibn Masʿūd, in the verb's person and agency.
- 96:15/q08/p1 Most read the first-person plural with a light emphatic nūn. — Sībawayh uses this verse as an example; al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Zarkashī, al-Suyūṭī and Ibn ʿĀshūr discuss this rare Qurʾānic light nūn. Al-Zarkashī says only two instances, comparing 12:32; al-Suyūṭī distinguishes the heavy nūn there. [14 notes: SIBAWAYH; TABRISI; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:15/q08/p2 A first-person plural form is read with a doubled, heavy emphatic nūn. — Reported from Abū ʿAmr through Maḥbūb and Hārūn. Al-Rāzī says the plural makes God and angels agents, comparing 66:4; al-Zarkashī connects emphatic nūns with inna and lām. [15 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(أبو عمرو في رواية هارون); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI(قرئ); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(محبوب وهارون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(محبوب وهارون); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI(محبوب وهارون); ALUSI-FULL(محبوب وهارون)]
- 96:15/q08/p3 Ibn Masʿūd reads la-asfaʿanna in God's first-person singular. — Al-Rāzī explains that God Himself undertakes the humiliation and compares 8:62 and 48:4. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports his codex's following phrase as 'nāṣiya kādhiba fājira.' [9 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(ابن مسعود); RAZI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI(قرئ); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI(محبوب وهارون); ALUSI-FULL(محبوب وهارون)]

## 96:15/q09 Why is the final emphatic nūn written as alif?  [لَنَسْفَعًۢا · grammar]
- 96:15/q09/p1 The light nūn becomes alif at pause, and muṣḥaf spelling reflects that pronunciation. — Al-Naḥḥās, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī cite poetic pause or rhyme forms; al-Samīn compares tanwīn and says nūn is dropped after ḍamma or kasra. Al-Ṭabrisī says Basrans favor alif for pause, while Kufans favor nūn for the underlying consonant. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the muṣḥaf writes alif in place of nūn. Al-Suyūṭī lists related written light-nūn forms (yakūnā, wa-idhā), contrasting kāyin, amid broader alif, wāw, yāʾ and tāʾ spelling examples. [27 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:15/q10 What grammatical comparison with lan appears beside this oath?  [لَئِن لَنَسْفَعًۢا · grammar]
- 96:15/q10/p1 The negative particle lan strengthens future denial and can answer a claim introduced by sa. — Al-Zarkashī contrasts lā abraḥ with lan abraḥ; he reports Sībawayh's account of lan as an answer to someone saying sa-yafʿal. These are comparative remarks about lan, distinct from the oath lām of la-in and emphatic la-nasfaʿan. [2 notes: BURHAN(سيبويه)]

## 96:15/q11 Who is threatened by the forelock phrase?  [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · referent]
- 96:15/q11/p1 The immediate offender is Abū Jahl, the man who forbade Muḥammad's prayer. — Muqātil identifies him and links his forelock to the lying, sinful forelock of 96:16. Al-Māwardī names him directly; the conditional refers back to the man who forbids in 96:9–10. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; MAWARDI+]
- 96:15/q11/p2 The warning also embraces anyone who refuses obedience or prevents worship. — Al-Qurṭubī states this extension explicitly; Abū Ḥayyān rebukes Abū Jahl and those like him; Kur'an Yolu applies eventual punishment to self-satisfied tyrants generally. [5 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 96:15/q12 What does nāṣiya denote?  [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · meaning]
turns on: The literal referent is front hair or its place; the figurative extent ranges from forehead and face to the whole person.
- 96:15/q12/p1 The hair or forelock at the front of the head. — Al-Jishumī cites rajaz for the term; Ibn al-Shaykh says the word may also name where the hair grows. Later writers extend it to the forehead or front of the head. [18 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI(مؤرج); ALUSI-FULL(مؤرج); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI]
- 96:15/q12/p2 It may mean the forehead or the place where forehead hair grows. — Al-Rāzī, Ibn al-Shaykh and Muʾarrij give the growth place; Asad reports al-Rāzī's metonymy. Islahi identifies forehead and hair together. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI(مؤرج); ALUSI-FULL(مؤرج); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:15/q12/p3 The forelock can stand for the whole person. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī use the idiom 'blessed forelock' for a person; Abū Ḥayyān reads the threat as dragging the person to Hell. Al-Ālūsī explicitly calls this whole-person reading weak, though he accepts the forelock as a metonym for dragging. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; ALUSI(أبو حيان)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 96:15/q12/p4 It stands for the face and head, or for the face's front. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī use this to explain blackening; Ibn ʿAṭiyya uses it for the burning gloss. Al-Rāzī says Abū Jahl groomed and perfumed it. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ also notes figurative senses 'foremost thing' and leaders of a community. [12 notes: FARRA; TAB(قيل); TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(التبريزي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI]

## 96:15/q13 Why seize the forelock in particular?  [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources give humiliation, Abū Jahl's grooming, and the forehead's relation to pride and prostration as rationales.
- 96:15/q13/p1 Taking the forelock displays extreme humiliation and mastery. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Khansāʾ's verse support its humiliating force among Arabs; al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī call it an especially honored part. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī liken the display of control to handling animals; Asad notes the old Arabian idiom and compares 11:56. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says it signals the seizer's power. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمرو بن معد يكرب); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI]
- 96:15/q13/p2 Abū Jahl specially groomed and perfumed it, so its blackening or seizure reverses his pride. — Al-Rāzī suggests darkening the groomed forelock and face; Ibn al-Shaykh, al-Ālūsī and others repeat the grooming explanation. [5 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:15/q13/p3 The raised forehead displays arrogant tyranny; dishonoring it answers refusal of prostration. — Quṭb calls it the tyrant's conspicuous forward part; Islahi says the forehead bears the highest honor in prostration and the preventer deserves Hell. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 96:15/q14 Why is the forelock definite without a possessive?  [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · grammar]
turns on: The referent is agreed; the grammatical dispute is whether the article replaces a genitive possessor or accompanies an implicit attachment.
- 96:15/q14/p1 The article identifies the known offender's forelock, making a possessive suffix unnecessary. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī say the owner is understood; Ibn ʿĀshūr points back to the forbider in 96:9–10. Al-Rāzī says its physical identity is known while its lying and sinful traits are newly revealed. [14 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(مؤرج); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR+]
- 96:15/q14/p2 The Kufan analysis calls the article a substitute for the genitive possessor. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this terminology for the understood 'his forelock.' [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR+]
- 96:15/q14/p3 The Basran analysis supplies an omitted complement instead of calling the article a substitute. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the Basran objection to the Kufan label. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:15/q15 What does the bāʾ add to the seizure?  [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · rhetoric]
- 96:15/q15/p1 It intensifies firm contact and attachment in the grasp. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an added bāʾ on the object. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 96:15/q16 How is the following 'a lying, sinful forelock' attached to this forelock?  [بِٱلنَّاصِيَةِ · grammar]
- 96:15/q16/p1 The indefinite, qualified second forelock is an appositive to the first, adding blame. — Sībawayh uses 96:15–16 for apposition; al-Farrāʾ calls repetition emphasis and compares 'a straight path, the path of God.' Al-Zarkashī and Ibn Yaʿīsh explain that an indefinite substitute for a definite needs qualification. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls its lying and sin a rhetorical attribution to its owner's speech and acts; al-Sāmarrāʾī says badal can serve blame, contrasting praise in 'this secure town.' [9 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; THALABI; BURHAN(ابن يعيش); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 96:15/q16/p2 Comparable raised expressions can be parsed by an omitted pronoun, as multiple predicates, by coordination, by negation or as an echo of quoted speech. — Al-Khalīl gives the omitted demonstrative/pronoun and combined-predicate analyses; Sībawayh allows coordination and apposition. In discussion of al-Akhṭal's line, al-Khalīl rejects an omitted anā that would license an invalid omitted huwa, and also gives reported-speech analysis with al-Rabīʿ al-Asadī's verse. Some read the line as negation, which Sībawayh prefers as easier. These are comparison constructions in his discussion, not alternative readings of this verse. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,بعضهم); SIBAWAYH+]

## 96:15/q17 What reports describe Abū Jahl's attempt to stop prayer?  [* · reports]
turns on: The accounts differ in the apparition or force that stopped Abū Jahl, while one reports his boast instead.
- 96:15/q17/p1 He threatened Muḥammad at prayer, then recoiled before a fiery trench, awe and wings; angels would have torn him apart. — Abū Hurayra transmits the Prophet's account with the surrounding sequence through 96:19. Al-Rāzī also tells a version in which Gabriel had instructed Muḥammad to recite and prostrate, and Abū Jahl saw a gaping stallion. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 96:15/q17/p2 When he threatened to kill Muḥammad at the prayer place, dark battalions stood between them. — Ibn ʿAbbās says angels would have seized Abū Jahl; another Ibn ʿAbbās report says he came upon the Prophet praying and forbade him, occasioning the passage. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 96:15/q17/p3 Gabriel and Michael appeared on Muḥammad's shoulders as lions, frightening Abū Jahl away. — Al-Rāzī records this variant beside the fiery trench or open-mouthed beast. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 96:15/q17/p4 Abū Jahl boasted of his numerous clan, cavalry and armed men after Muḥammad reproached him for forbidding prayer. — Al-Baghawī transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās, preparing the following call to his assembly. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 96:15/q18 What reports connect the threat to Ibn Masʿūd and Badr?  [* · reports]
- 96:15/q18/p1 Ibn Masʿūd recited al-Raḥmān before Quraysh, and Abū Jahl struck him and split his ear. — Al-Rāzī says Ibn Masʿūd was first discouraged because he was small and weak; al-Ālūsī repeats the recitation and injury. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:15/q18/p2 At Badr Ibn Masʿūd found Abū Jahl wounded, stabbed his snout, and heard his defiant last words. — Al-Rāzī suggests the snout wound may fulfill 68:16. Abū Jahl mocked Ibn Masʿūd as a little shepherd; Ibn Masʿūd replied that Islam rises and is not overcome. [3 notes: RAZI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 96:15/q18/p3 Abū Jahl asked to be beheaded with his own sharper sword; Ibn Masʿūd dragged the severed head by a thread through the split ear. — Al-Rāzī gives three possible meanings of his inability to carry it: Abū Jahl deserved dragging like a dog, the ear invited requital, and the dragging fulfilled this verse. Gabriel quipped 'ear for ear' with the head extra. Al-Rāzī cautions that he gives the report's meaning, not exact wording. [7 notes: RAZI(جبريل); RAZI-FULL(أبو جهل); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:15/q18/p4 After Abū Jahl's final hatred, the Prophet called him worse than Pharaoh. — The comparison says Pharaoh at least professed belief, as in 10:90, while Abū Jahl remained defiant. [2 notes: RAZI(النبي); RAZI-FULL(محمد)]

## 96:15/q19 What broader warning does the verse teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 96:15/q19/p1 It warns every opponent of worship and shows that tyrannical power is subject to God. — Al-Qurṭubī extends the lesson beyond Abū Jahl; al-Biqāʿī says reliance on followers overlooks heavenly and earthly forces, and that the conditional leaves a real chance to desist. Quṭb hears a decisive threat against tyranny; Islahi connects punishment to blocking prostration; Kur'an Yolu says tyrants may be left free temporarily but face ultimate punishment. [11 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TAB-FULL]

## 96:15/q20 How does this threat develop the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
- 96:15/q20/p1 Questioning turns into an explicit divine threat and a preview of Judgment. — Corpus Coranicum calls 96:15–18 the fourth stanza and the first unequivocal divine first-person plural. Al-Ṭabarī links the three elliptical 'Have you seen?' clauses to the forbider, who also denies and knows God sees. Muqātil connects the earlier warning of return to God with this named threat. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees the second kallā resolving earlier suspense. [7 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:15/q20/p2 The sound and accumulated force of the words convey anger and violent seizure. — Quṭb points to the severe sound; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ hears the greatest threat, both dragging toward Fire and scorching. Islahi hears anger and sees the offender's refusal of prostration answered by humiliation. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 96:15/q20/p3 The forelock wording participates in a wider set of Qurʾānic metaphors. — Al-Zarkashī lists it with images of enclosure, marking a nose, startled donkeys, joining limbs and return to creation; his example of gray hair spreading like flame explains how transferred imagery can intensify an idea. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 96:15/q21 Where do some verse counts end this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 96:15/q21/p1 Meccan and Medinan counts end a verse after lam yantahi. — Corpus Coranicum reports Neuwirth's objection that this breaks the rhyme and separates protasis from apodosis. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 96:15/q22 What merit report accompanies this passage?  [* · sciences]
- 96:15/q22/p1 A report promises the reciter reward as though all the shorter sūras had been recited. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this report concerning the sūra. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 96:15/q23 What adjacent reading does al-Farrāʾ attribute to ʿAbd Allāh?  [* · readings]
- 96:15/q23/p1 In the following call, ʿAbd Allāh adds ilā before nādiyah and reads fa-sa-adʿū. — Al-Farrāʾ records this adjacent reading with his discussion of the forelock threat. [1 notes: FARRA(عبد الله)]
