# 55:41: 26 questions

## 55:41/q01 Who needs the signs to recognize them?  [يُعْرَفُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ بِسِيمَٰهُمْ · interpretation]
- 55:41/q01/p1 Ordinary people and some angels need signs; angels who kept records or are stern already know them. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes the groups by whether recognition requires a mark. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 55:41/q02 How does recognition relate to the preceding statement that none is questioned?  [يُعْرَفُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ بِسِيمَٰهُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether the new sentence is independent explanatory speech or an adverbial qualification of 55:39.
- 55:41/q02/p1 It is a fresh explanatory sentence: their signs make inquiry unnecessary. — Al-Zajjāj juxtaposes the order to question people in 37:24; al-Rāzī says this explains the reading that no one asks who sinned, and notes an objection to the intercession reading with a grammatical and interpretive reply elsewhere. Al-Biqāʿī restricts the general wording by context. Al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ treat the sentence as a resumption, the latter answering the implied question of what happens if none is questioned. Ibn Kathīr places this after the order to the Fire; KURANYOLU cites written deeds and testimony of tongues, hands, and feet. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 55:41/q02/p2 It may be an adverbial clause modifying 55:39: they are not questioned while their marks identify them. — The Study Quran presents this syntactic possibility. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 55:41/q03 What does the passive wording emphasize or omit?  [يُعْرَفُ فَيُؤْخَذُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give complementary reasons for singular impersonal capture and omitted agent.
- 55:41/q03/p1 The agent is obvious, or leaving it unnamed signals God's greatness. — Al-Ālūsī reports both explanations; Abū Ḥayyān observes omission of agent and object in the passive. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 55:41/q03/p2 It shifts focus from universal recognition to the humiliating manner of capture. — Al-Rāzī contrasts universal 'they are recognized' with impersonal 'is seized': listeners await how capture occurs; a completed 'they are seized' would subordinate the detail. He also distinguishes particular captors from universal recognition. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:41/q03/p3 It makes seizure seem effortless, with any appointed captor. — Al-Biqāʿī says the passive suggests easy capture by whoever God appoints. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 55:41/q04 Who are the criminals named here?  [ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wording refers generally to the condemned, to named opponents, or inwardly to people ruled by vices.
- 55:41/q04/p1 The guilty or unbelievers among humans and jinn. — Muqātil places recognition after judgment; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the immediate threat addresses the unbelieving majority of both kinds at revelation; al-Ālūsī says the explicit noun singles out sinners among humans and jinn. [7 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 55:41/q04/p2 Abū Jahl, al-Aswad, al-Walīd, and al-Naḍr. — Al-Wāḥidī transmits this identification from ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس)]
- 55:41/q04/p3 Those dominated by entrenched bodily vices. — Al-Qāshānī says acquired dispositions and vices show outwardly. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:41/q05 Why say 'criminals' explicitly here?  [ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · rhetoric]
- 55:41/q05/p1 The explicit noun specifies sinners among humans and jinn in place of an expected pronoun. — Al-Ālūsī reports this explanation and mentions 28:78. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:41/q06 What does sīmāhum mean?  [بِسِيمَٰهُمْ · meaning]
- 55:41/q06/p1 Their identifying signs or distinguishing marks. — Majāz and al-Naḥḥās gloss sīmā as a mark; Ibn Qutayba gives black faces and blue eyes as examples; al-Bursawī notes sīmā and sīmiyāʾ with kasra, shortened or lengthened; al-Rāzī derives it from original sawmā, like ḍayzā in form; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ locates the conspicuous mark on the face. [7 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 55:41/q07 What signs identify the criminals?  [بِسِيمَٰهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Which visible condition counts as the identifying sign.
- 55:41/q07/p1 Blackened faces and blue eyes. — Muqātil places these signs after judgment; al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn Jurayj and many exegetes report them. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī cite 3:106 and 20:102; al-Zajjāj also connects 37:24; Ibn Kathīr contrasts believers' ablution radiance. [24 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,الضحاك); BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL]
- 55:41/q07/p2 Other disfigurements, including blindness, muteness, deafness, and walking on their faces. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows other disfigurement; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī name blindness, muteness and deafness; al-Biqāʿī includes walking on faces with blue eyes and black faces. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:41/q07/p3 Gloom, grief, dust, or sorrow visible on them. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī name gloom and sorrow or grief; al-Rāzī proposes dust and gloom; al-Ālūsī reports grief and gloom. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:41/q07/p4 Brands on their foreheads. — Al-Rāzī links this possibility to the branding of foreheads in 9:35. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 55:41/q07/p5 The visible effects of acquired vices. — Al-Qāshānī says entrenched bodily dispositions become outward signs. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 55:41/q07/p6 Signs of disgrace. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this explanation without specifying a physical mark. [1 notes: TABRISI(قول آخر)]

## 55:41/q08 How is sīmāhum read?  [بِسِيمَٰهُمْ · readings]
turns on: The length of the vowel, with route specific evidence.
- 55:41/q08/p1 A lengthened reading of بسيمائهم is transmitted from Ḥammād ibn Abī Sulaymān. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī transmit the reading. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(حماد بن أبي سليمان); ALUSI-FULL(حماد بن سليمان)]
- 55:41/q08/p2 Qunbul has a short reading on specified routes. — Ibn al-Jazarī prefers only short outside Ibn Mujāhid's and al-Zaynabī's routes, as Ibn Shānabūdh, Abū Rabīʿa and Ibn al-Ṣabbāḥ transmit without objection. Through Ibn Mujāhid, Ṣāliḥ al-Muʾaddib, Bakkār, al-Muṭawwiʿ and al-Shanabūdhī transmit short; its performance transmission is stronger. [3 notes: NASHR(ابن مجاهد عن قنبل); NASHR+]
- 55:41/q08/p3 Qunbul has a long reading on specified routes. — Through al-Zaynabī long is the only face; through Ibn Mujāhid, Abū al-Ḥasan al-Muʿaddil, Abū Ṭāhir and al-Kattānī transmit long. Ibn al-Jazarī says textual reports support long more strongly and adopts both faces where both are attested. [3 notes: NASHR(ابن مجاهد عن قنبل,الزينبي عن قنبل); NASHR+]

## 55:41/q09 How are the criminals seized and taken to the Fire?  [فَيُؤْخَذُ بِٱلنَّوَٰصِى وَٱلْأَقْدَامِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the two body parts are bound together, seized together, or used in alternate seizures; reports vary on posture, crushing, and the bridge.
- 55:41/q09/p1 Forelocks and feet are bound together behind the back, then the criminal is dragged or hurled into Hell. — Muqātil says keepers bind hands to necks and drive victims face-first; al-Ḍaḥḥāk specifies a chain, sometimes breaking the back; al-Suddī describes a twisted back. Al-Biqāʿī says the helpless reversal mirrors their joining what God separated. Al-Rāzī prefers the joined-body account, including this posture. [22 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش والضحاك); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك); STUDYQURAN(IK, Q); RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:41/q09/p2 Angels seize both the forelock and feet and cast or drive the person into Hell. — Al-Kalbī locates this on the bridge; al-Qurṭubī says front hair and feet. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bursawī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ, Elmalılı and KURANYOLU describe the direct seizure; al-Rāzī says the forelock is a means to seize its owner. Elmalılı likens it to catching by collar and tail. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); TABRISI(قول آخر); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL]
- 55:41/q09/p3 They are grasped by both forelock and feet, crushed or folded like firewood, and cast into the Fire. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports gathering and breaking them like firewood in an oven; reports differ between crushing, trampling and folding. Ibn ʿAṭiyya records al-Naqqāsh and al-Ḍaḥḥāk saying the fold is over the back; Islāḥī says the image implies throwing logs into Hell. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأعمش عن رجل عن ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,النقاش والضحاك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 55:41/q09/p4 Different people are dragged by different parts: some by forelock, others by feet. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to a group in al-Thaʿlabī; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī report it. Al-Bursawī allows dragging by the forelock or binding. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم في كتاب الثعلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q); BURSEVI]
- 55:41/q09/p5 The same person is seized at one time by the forelock and another time by the feet. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān report alternation; al-Qurṭubī gives face-first dragging by the forelock or head-first by the feet. Al-Ālūsī rejects this distributive reading as departing from the apparent sense. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 55:41/q09/p6 Forelock and feet may be joined in front, the head bent to the knees or folded over the face. — Al-Rāzī includes a frontward form of the account he finds clearer; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a faceward folding alternative to the hunched posture. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون); RAZI-FULL+]
- 55:41/q09/p7 Inwardly, forelock capture signifies ignorance and false belief; foot capture signifies practical bodily vices. — Al-Qāshānī locates forelock punishment above, as confinement through compounded ignorance and entrenched false beliefs, and foot punishment below, as face-down dragging for greed, stinginess, lust and anger. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:41/q10 What is the force of the bā in 'bi-l-nawāṣī'?  [فَيُؤْخَذُ بِٱلنَّوَٰصِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether bā marks an instrument or object, or reflects an implied verb of dragging or pushing.
- 55:41/q10/p1 It marks the physical means of capturing the person or an associated object. — Al-Rāzī distinguishes direct أخذ when the thing itself is intended from ب when it is the means, citing 55:41, 20:21, 4:102, 7:154, 20:94 and 57:15. Al-Bursawī gives أخذَه versus أخذ به, citing 4:71 and 20:94; al-Ālūsī compares an animal's bridle. Makkī says direct and prepositional forms coexist for a forelock and bridle but not taking an animal by its forelock. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:41/q10/p2 It reflects a verb of dragging, or bā itself marks the object. — Al-Samīn reports the Shaykh's taḍmīn of يسحب; al-Ālūsī transmits Abū Ḥayyān's object analysis with an implied verb such as dragging. Al-Samīn objects that يسحب takes على in 54:48. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 55:41/q10/p3 It reflects a verb of pushing. — Al-Samīn proposes دفع as a better implied meaning after objecting to يسحب on the evidence of 54:48. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR+]
- 55:41/q10/p4 The construction cannot supply an additional object in the way proposed as 'each one by the forelocks'. — Makkī rejects كل واحد بالنواصي because أخذ cannot govern two such objects with ب. He allows a second object with another preposition, as in taking a garment from Zayd, and causal ب; al-Samīn counters with taking a garment for a dirham, where ب is not causal. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(مكي); SAMIN-DURR(مكي)+]

## 55:41/q11 What is the passive subject of yuʾkhadhu, and why is it singular?  [فَيُؤْخَذُ بِٱلنَّوَٰصِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase serves as passive subject or the understood plural criminals do.
- 55:41/q11/p1 The prepositional phrase 'by the forelocks' occupies the passive subject position. — Al-Naḥḥās offers this explanation; al-Rāzī uses it to account for singular yuʾkhadhu, and al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ adopt it. [5 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 55:41/q11/p2 An implicit subject is understood, effectively 'they are seized by the forelocks'. — Al-Naḥḥās allows an unexpressed passive subject; al-Rāzī allows an implied plural verb. [2 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL]

## 55:41/q12 What does seizure by forelock and feet convey?  [فَيُؤْخَذُ بِٱلنَّوَٰصِى وَٱلْأَقْدَامِ · rhetoric]
- 55:41/q12/p1 Utter subjection, disgrace, and intensified punishment. — Al-Rummānī calls the paired recognition and seizure the strongest image of humiliation. Al-Ṭabrisī links dragging by the forelock to 96:15–16; Asad notes the Arab idiom of another holding one's forelock. Al-Qurṭubī says folding intensifies punishment and disfigurement; al-Zajjāj says binding behind does so. Ibn Qutayba notes the forelock names the bearer, citing 96:15–16. [8 notes: RUMMANI; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:41/q13 What are the nawāṣī?  [بِٱلنَّوَٰصِى · meaning]
- 55:41/q13/p1 Forelocks: the front of the head, especially its hair. — Al-Wāḥidī calls nawāṣī the plural of nāṣiya, forehead hair. Al-Samīn calls nāṣiya the front of the head, cites ʿĀʾisha's question about extending the forelock of the dead, and notes that nāṣiya can figuratively name a useful person or leader, perhaps from good pasture called naṣī. Al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ give the front or its hair. [8 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; SAMIN-DURR(عائشة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 55:41/q14 How does the forelock image connect to another Quranic verse?  [بِٱلنَّوَٰصِى · links]
- 55:41/q14/p1 It recalls 11:56, where God holds every crawling creature by its forelock. — The Study Quran cites 11:56. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 55:41/q15 Does the definite article in 'the forelocks and feet' stand for a possessive pronoun?  [بِٱلنَّوَٰصِى وَٱلْأَقْدَامِ · grammar]
turns on: The Kufan substitution analysis versus the Basran supplied-pronoun analysis.
- 55:41/q15/p1 Yes; the article replaces a possessive pronoun. — Al-Samīn reports the Kufan analysis. Al-Ālūsī says the dispute matters here only if a resumptive pronoun is required. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون); ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:41/q15/p2 No; an omitted 'of them' is supplied. — Al-Samīn reports the Basran estimate بالنواصي منهم. Al-Ālūsī says the dispute matters here only if a resumptive pronoun is required. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(البصريون); ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:41/q16 What are the aqdām?  [وَٱلْأَقْدَامِ · meaning]
- 55:41/q16/p1 Literal bodily feet. — Al-Dāmghānī compares the feet of believers at Badr and guilty people seized here. Ibn al-Jawzī defines the foot from ankle to toes below the leg and notes metaphorical uses elsewhere; al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ identify this plural as ordinary feet. [5 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 55:41/q17 What was said to the condemned as they reached the Fire?  [* · reports]
- 55:41/q17/p1 The keepers identified it as the fire they had denied. — Muqātil echoes 52:14. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 55:41/q18 What happened when this verse was recited during dawn prayer?  [* · reports]
- 55:41/q18/p1 ʿAlī ibn al-Fuḍayl fainted and later said the warning occupied him despite the following verse about houris. — Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-Ṣāʾigh's report. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(علي بن الفضيل بن عياض)]

## 55:41/q19 What does the report of ʿĀʾisha say about the bridge and this seizure?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted report versus Ibn Kathīr's explicit objection to its reliability.
- 55:41/q19/p1 At the bridge, hypocrites are hooked by forelock and feet into Hell while believers cross safely. — Ibn Kathīr reports that the Prophet would not intercede at placement of the bridge, judgment of faces, or the bridge; it is sharp as a sword and hot as an ember, and the hypocrite falls fifty years. Al-Durr's version from a man of Kinda adds a weight equal to five pregnant camels. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 55:41/q19/p2 The report cannot serve as proof. — Ibn Kathīr calls it very anomalous, with objectionable words attributed to the Prophet and an unnamed transmitter. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 55:41/q20 What does Anas's reported Prophetic statement say about Hell's angels?  [* · reports]
- 55:41/q20/p1 They were created a thousand years before Hell, grow stronger daily, and seize victims by forelocks and feet. — Al-Ālūsī transmits Anas's report without giving a grading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله)]

## 55:41/q21 How does the verse's warning count as a divine favor?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the favor is warning and deterrence, rescue by mercy, or benefit to those spared.
- 55:41/q21/p1 The terrifying warning deters sin and encourages obedience. — Al-Baghawī calls the admonitions and restraints from 55:26 onward blessings; al-Qurṭubī says the terror encourages obedience; al-Bursawī identifies the refrain's favor with admonition and deterrence. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 55:41/q21/p2 The description offers rescue through God's mercy and grace. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls the warning itself a kindness. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 55:41/q21/p3 The seizure is a favor to those who are not criminals, or the refrain is spoken as criminals are seized. — Elmalılı gives these alternative ways the refrain applies. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 55:41/q22 How does this scene fit the passage's movement to judgment?  [* · links]
- 55:41/q22/p1 It follows God's worldly signs and depicts judgment of humans and jinn, then their entry into Fire. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the preceding signs establish God's sole claim to worship, then the passage threatens polytheists and jinn who led them astray; God turns to both kinds in the afterlife. KURANYOLU traces resurrection, no questioning, hauling to Hell, entry and exit from fire, then boiling water without relief. Islāḥī stresses no escape on the certain Day; al-Biqāʿī contrasts sinners' signs with righteous white, smiling faces and ghurra and taḥjīl. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 55:41/q23 What signs distinguish the righteous in contrast?  [* · interpretation]
- 55:41/q23/p1 Radiant white faces and limbs, smiles, ablution marks, and a prostration mark. — Al-Biqāʿī names white smiling faces and ghurra and taḥjīl; Ibn Kathīr describes ablution radiance and whiteness; the Study Quran cites ablution light and the prostration mark in 48:29. Al-Bursawī contrasts signs of grief with their opposites. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); STUDYQURAN(IK); BURSEVI(قيل)]

## 55:41/q24 What does the cited Arabic guidance require of an exegete?  [* · sciences]
- 55:41/q24/p1 Knowledge of Arabic vocabulary and grammar, supported by lexicons, poetry, and sound reports. — Al-Zarkashī says particles belong to grammar and nouns and verbs to lexicons; he lists major noun dictionaries and Ibn Sīda's hundred-volume lexicon, and verb works by Ibn al-Qūṭṭiyya, Ibn Ṭarīf, al-Saraqusṭī and Ibn al-Qaṭṭāʿ. Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ defines ahl al-maʿānī as authors of Quranic meanings, and al-Wāḥidī names al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj and Ibn al-Anbārī. Mālik warns against interpretation without Arabic; Mujāhid forbids it; Ibn ʿAbbās recommends poetry to explain وَسَقَ as gathering in 84:17. Al-Suyūṭī prioritizes sound Ibn ʿAbbās reports, especially Ibn Abī Ṭalḥa's route used by al-Bukhārī. [10 notes: BURHAN(أبو عمرو ابن الصلاح,ابن عباس,الواحدي,مجاهد,يحيى بن نضلة المدني); ITQAN(الزركشي)]

## 55:41/q25 What do the cited authorities say about the scope of accepted readings?  [* · readings]
- 55:41/q25/p1 The seven selected readings and three additional readings are mutawātir, not limited to a seven-reader canon. — Al-Subkī says all ten certainly reached the Prophet and even ordinary Muslims share that certainty. Al-Qurab says restricting practice to seven has no transmitted or Prophetic warrant; a later famous book created confusion, other books preserve more transmissions, limiting an imam to one narration is unacceptable, and the seven aḥruf hadith cannot mean seven later readers. [5 notes: NASHR(إسماعيل بن إبراهيم بن محمد القراب,عبد الوهاب بن السبكي الشافعي)]

## 55:41/q26 What imāla and opening variants are reported in the cited reading survey?  [* · readings]
turns on: Transmission routes and whether apparent wording in reference books supports opening.
- 55:41/q26/p1 Al-Azraq has opening on all verse-final forms except those with rāʾ through al-Tajrīd's author. — Ibn al-Jazarī says this route is unique and applies to both wāwī and yāʾī forms, unlike other transmitters. [1 notes: NASHR(صاحب التجريد)]
- 55:41/q26/p2 Al-Azraq has intermediate imāla on non-final yāʾī forms. — Abū Ṭāhir ibn Khalaf, ʿAbd al-Jabbār al-Ṭarsūsī, Abū al-Fatḥ Fāris and Abū al-Qāsim Khalaf report it, matching al-Dānī's Taysīr; al-Dānī's al-Jāmiʿ, al-Shāṭibī and al-Ṣafrawī also allow it. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو طاهر بن خلف وعبد الجبار الطرسوسي وأبو الفتح فارس بن أحمد وأبو القاسم خلف بن خاقان,الداbني والشاطبي والصفراوي)]
- 55:41/q26/p3 Al-Azraq has full opening on non-final yāʾī forms. — Ṭāhir ibn Ghalbūn, his father and Makkī report it; al-Dānī in al-Jāmiʿ, al-Shāṭibī and al-Ṣafrawī allow both this and intermediate imāla, both sound. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو الحسن طاهر بن غلبون وأبوه وأبو محمد مكي,الداbني والشاطبي والصفراوي)]
- 55:41/q26/p4 Qālūn has intermediate imāla on all listed forms, according to al-Mubhij. — Ibn al-Jazarī calls this universal assignment unique and contrary to other accounts; the known report is only through Ismāʿīl al-Qāḍī. [1 notes: NASHR(صاحب المبهج)]
- 55:41/q26/p5 Warsh has intermediate imāla in the listed words. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects an apparent opening inference from Taysīr for hudāy, muḥyāy and mathwāy because al-Dānī's other books explicitly say intermediate imāla; he similarly rejects opening inferred from al-ʿUnwān for marsāhā and al-sūʾā. [2 notes: NASHR+]
