# 33:5: 34 questions

## 33:5/q01 How should adoptees be named?  [ٱدْعُوهُمْ لِءَابَآئِهِمْ · interpretation]
- 33:5/q01/p1 By their biological fathers rather than their adopters. — Muqatil and Tabari name Zayd son of Haritha; Ibn Abbas says the men who begot them; Tabarsi allows the father of the marital bed. Elmalili and Asad emphasize true identity and public welfare. Alusi notes the wording covers girls too. Qurtubi defines an adopted claimant as one called another man's son and notes daʿwa with kasra; unknown lineage means patronage and religious brotherhood. [28 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; NASAI(الله); TIRMIDHI(عائشة); IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; MUQATIL+; TAB-FULL+]

## 33:5/q02 What does the lām before 'fathers' convey?  [لِءَابَآئِهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: Naming usage, descent, or specification.
- 33:5/q02/p1 It follows Arabic usage for being called after someone. — Akhfash cites huwa yudʿā li-fulān. [1 notes: AKHFASH]
- 33:5/q02/p2 It marks descent or affiliation. — Ibn Ashur adduces poetry and the report of Umama daughter of Zaynab and Abu al-As; 'daughter of' can be implicit. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو قتادة)]
- 33:5/q02/p3 It specifies the fathers as those to whom they are attributed. — Tabatabai states this reading. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 33:5/q03 What happened around the verse's revelation?  [ٱدْعُوهُمْ لِءَابَآئِهِمْ · reports]
turns on: Reports vary in persons named and narrative detail.
- 33:5/q03/p1 Zayd was called Muhammad's son until the verse restored his father's name. — Ibn Umar's report is noted as transmitted by Bukhari; Durr and Alusi give Haritha ibn Shurahbil or Sharahil. Muqatil says Zayd affirmed his known descent. Tirmidhi grades Aisha's longer narrative gharīb and notes a shorter route through Masruq. [15 notes: MUQATIL(زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,عبد الله بن عمر); WAHIDI-ASBAB(عبد الله بن عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); TABRISI(ابن عمر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عمر); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عمر); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عمر); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar); TIRMIDHI]
- 33:5/q03/p2 Zayd's adoption and the Prophet's marriage to Zaynab prompted accusations that he married his son's former wife. — Thaʿlabi and Wahidi name Jews and hypocrites; Ibn Kathir links 33:37 and says 4:23 concerns sons from one's loins. [4 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 33:5/q03/p3 Zayd was captured and sold, passed through Khadija to Muhammad, then chose Muhammad over his birth family. — Qurtubi places capture in Syria and describes the ransom party; Durr names Ukaz as the sale place; Alusi and Kuranyolu recount his emancipation and adoption. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك وغيره); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Tefsir kitapları)]
- 33:5/q03/p4 The verse concerned Zayd, Salim, Amir ibn Rabi'a and al-Miqdad. — Durr transmits al-Hasan ibn Uthman's report. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الحسن بن عثمان)]
- 33:5/q03/p5 Salim was adopted by Abu Hudhayfa and treated as a son and heir until the verse. — Aisha or Urwa reports the old inheritance custom; Nasai says Abu Hudhayfa arranged Salim's marriage; Bursevi says he later bore a client name. Ibn Atiyya says people cited both Zayd and Salim as adoptively named. [8 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(عائشة); IBNHIBBAN(عروة بن الزبير); THALABI(عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة); BURSEVI; MALIK(عروة بن الزبير); NASAI(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الناس)]
- 33:5/q03/p6 Al-Miqdad, Sufyan and Salim show correction or persistence of earlier affiliation. — Mawardi gives their distinct lineages; Qurtubi observes continuing names; Ibn Ashur disputes inferring deliberate intent from old usage. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(القرطبي)]

## 33:5/q04 What legal effects can adoption or a sonship claim have?  [ٱدْعُوهُمْ لِءَابَآئِهِمْ · law]
turns on: Adoption alone versus a possible claim of paternity, and its effect on an enslaved person.
- 33:5/q04/p1 Adoption itself creates no biological lineage, inheritance, or mahram status. — Qurtubi and Nasafi describe the older inheritance custom; Ibn Ashur limits an express bequest to one-third; Kuranyolu lists marriage, covering, rights and duties, while citing a Prophetic reward for orphan care. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber); STUDYQURAN]
- 33:5/q04/p2 A plausible paternity claim for a younger person of unknown lineage establishes filiation and emancipates that person if enslaved. — Abu Hanifa's view appears in Jishumi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Ibn Ashur and Alusi. Jishumi presumes truthful conformity to the marital bed. [7 notes: JISHUMI(أبو حنيفة); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); NASAFI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:5/q04/p3 An impossible paternity claim establishes no lineage but, for Abu Hanifa, frees an enslaved person. — Known lineage or older age blocks filiation; Bursevi contrasts denial of an adoptee with denial of a biological child, liʿan and hadd. [8 notes: NASAFI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:5/q04/p4 An impossible claim is void and does not free an enslaved person. — Bursevi and Ibn Ashur attribute this to Abu Hanifa's two companions. [2 notes: BURSEVI(صاحبا أبي حنيفة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:5/q04/p5 Adoption has no legal effect, including no emancipation. — Baydawi gives his school view; Alusi attributes it to Shafiʿi. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 33:5/q04/p6 The verse distinguishes error and intent in lineage rulings. — Jishumi draws this legal inference. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 33:5/q05 On what basis is lineage established?  [لِءَابَآئِهِمْ · law]
turns on: Marital bed versus presumption and proof.
- 33:5/q05/p1 The established marital bed grounds lineage. — Jishumi offers this answer and Tabarsi mentions men whose marital beds children were born in. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]
- 33:5/q05/p2 Presumption governs unless truthful report, evidence or necessity establishes descent. — Jishumi cites the necessarily known descent to Adnan and many Companions' lineages. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 33:5/q06 What does 'more just before God' mean?  [هُوَ أَقْسَطُ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 33:5/q06/p1 True paternal naming is more just, true and correct than false adoptive lineage. — Muqatil, Qatada and Zajjaj gloss aqsaṭ as more just; Mawardi adds just in speech and judgment; Ibn Ashur says the contrary erases fathers' and adoptees' rights. Bursevi cites Kashf al-asrar for greater justice and truthfulness. [25 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; TIRMIDHI(عائشة)]

## 33:5/q07 What does 'it' refer to?  [هُوَ · grammar]
- 33:5/q07/p1 The verbal noun implied by the command: calling them by their fathers' names. — Samin, Alusi and Tabatabai compare 5:8; Baydawi and Ibn Ashur identify the naming act. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 33:5/q08 Is aqsaṭ an absolute or a comparative expression?  [أَقْسَطُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Full justice versus explicit comparison with prior adoptive naming.
- 33:5/q08/p1 It expresses full or absolute justice. — Razi compares 'God is greater'; Alusi derives it from qisṭ; Ibn Ashur says the false alternative is no justice. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:5/q08/p2 It compares true attribution with the prior false name. — Razi supplies the old practice as comparator; Alusi calls the comparison ironic. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 33:5/q09 Why state that paternal naming is more just?  [هُوَ أَقْسَطُ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 33:5/q09/p1 It justifies the command and reassures those attached to the old custom. — Ibn Ashur calls it explanatory resumption linked to 33:4; Baydawi connects the prior 'statements of truth'; Biqai says it prepares later events. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 33:5/q10 What if an adoptee's father is unknown?  [فَإِن لَّمْ تَعْلَمُوٓا۟ ءَابَآءَهُمْ فَإِخْوَٰنُكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ وَمَوَٰلِيكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the fallback is religious brotherhood, clientage, or both according to status.
- 33:5/q10/p1 Do not invent adoptive lineage; treat the person as a brother in faith and, when applicable, a client or ally. — Tabari excludes the adopter; Mawardi distinguishes free brothers and freed clients; Ibn Ashur says either designation preserves closeness. Mawardi reports Salman naming himself son of Islam and Umar affirming it. Zajjaj says to call a believing unknown-father person brother. [56 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(أبو بكرة,قتادة); NAHHAS; THALABI(أبو بكر); MAWARDI-FULL(محمد بن المنكدر,يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو بكرة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكرة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو بكرة,ابن جريج,سالم بن أبي الجعد,قتادة,مجاهد,مقاتل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NASAI(الله); STUDYQURAN; TIRMIDHI(عائشة); ZAJJAJ]
- 33:5/q10/p2 Use a conventional patronal name such as son of Abdullah or Abd al-Rahman. — Farra and Muqatil ibn Hayyan give examples; Durr reports Muqatil on brother and patron names. [3 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); DURR-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 33:5/q11 How does 'if you do not know' work grammatically?  [فَإِن لَّمْ تَعْلَمُوٓا۟ ءَابَآءَهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Past-negating lam under a conditional.
- 33:5/q11/p1 It means not knowing or recognizing the fathers, so paternal attribution is unavailable. — Ibn al-Jawzi and Alusi explain this. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 33:5/q11/p2 In can be understood as idh, a past-time particle. — Bursevi reports this analysis for an impossible condition. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 33:5/q11/p3 Lam turns the present verb into a past form the conditional governs. — Bursevi reports Baydawi's explanation and cites 2:24. [1 notes: BURSEVI(البيضاوي)]

## 33:5/q12 What is the syntax of 'your brothers and clients'?  [فَإِخْوَٰنُكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ وَمَوَٰلِيكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Nominative predicates or accusatives under an omitted imperative.
- 33:5/q12/p1 They are nominative predicates of an omitted 'they'. — Farra says an implied 'they are' licenses this; Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Ashur supply the subject; Nahhas coordinates mawālī with ikhwa. [5 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:5/q12/p2 They may be accusative under an implicit 'call them'. — Farra contrasts cases continuing an earlier verb and gives examples of food and clothing. [2 notes: FARRA]

## 33:5/q13 What does 'in religion' convey?  [فِى ٱلدِّينِ · grammar]
- 33:5/q13/p1 Brotherhood belongs to or is caused by their shared faith. — Majaz says religious community; Tabari compares 9:11; Ibn Ashur gives figurative containment or cause, citing 29:10 and 49:10. [4 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:5/q14 Who are the mawālī here?  [وَمَوَٰلِيكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Freed clients, paternal cousins, or allies.
- 33:5/q14/p1 Freed clients of emancipators. — Al-Suddi and Muqatil use freedmen; Wahidi cites Zayd; Tabarsi says their walā belongs to emancipators; Salim illustrates this. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); DAMGHANI; TABRISI; MAWARDI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI]
- 33:5/q14/p2 Paternal cousins. — Majaz gives this alongside patrons; Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas, Mubarrad and Zajjaj with a poetic line; Tabarsi accepts it. [7 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، المبرد والزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 33:5/q14/p3 Allies or protectors in religion or by pact. — Zajjaj says religious allies; Tabarsi mentions mutual support; Ibn Ashur says pact allies specifically rather than freedmen. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 33:5/q14/p4 A broad relationship term including heirs, Master, protector and friend. — Study Quran cites 4:33, 19:5, 2:286, 3:150, 6:62 and 8:40 and notes its relation to walī. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 33:5/q15 May one call a sinner 'my brother' or 'my client'?  [فَإِخْوَٰنُكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ وَمَوَٰلِيكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the honorific is restricted for a sinner.
- 33:5/q15/p1 The two forms of address are equally permitted, perhaps subject to the person not being sinful. — Alusi suggests this qualification. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:5/q15/p2 Calling a sinner 'my client' is forbidden where it honors him; the same reason could cover 'brother'. — Alusi reports a hadith-based or honor-based restriction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 33:5/q16 What does 'no blame for what you erred in' cover?  [وَلَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌۭ فِيمَآ أَخْطَأْتُم بِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Earlier custom, mistaken belief, slips, or a general rule; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan reject calling lawful prior conduct error.
- 33:5/q16/p1 Earlier adoption and misnaming before prohibition are pardoned. — Mujahid contrasts before and after clarification; Study Quran says adopters then had no intent to oppose God. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد ومقاتل); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 33:5/q16/p2 Honest error about who a father is is excused, even after inquiry. — Qatada supplies the believed-father case; Ibn Kathir cites 2:286, God's answer in Muslim, and the mistaken judge's reward. [19 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة,قتادة وغيره); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمرو بن العاص); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 33:5/q16/p3 Forgetfulness, habitual slips, and affectionate nonliteral address are excused. — Yahya applies it after prohibition; Habib emphasizes forgetfulness; Razi likens it to inadvertent oath; Ibn Ashur rejects inferring intent from a persistent old name. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL(حبيب بن أبي ثابت); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حبيب بن أبي ثابت); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 33:5/q16/p4 The negation establishes a broad exemption for mistakes, forgetfulness and compulsion. — Nahhas and Qurtubi give mistaken payment and greeting under oaths; Ibn Kathir, Alusi, Ibn Ashur and Study Quran cite the Prophetic lifting of three things, with 2:286; Biqai includes zihar. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء وكثير من العلماء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمرو بن العاص); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 33:5/q16/p5 The exemption is specific to adoptive attribution. — Alusi offers this as the narrower reading. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:5/q16/p6 Conduct before a prohibition is not properly called an error. — Ibn Atiyya defines khataʾ as deviation and distinguishes knowing khāṭiʾ from unaware mukhtiʾ, citing 69:37; Abu Hayyan agrees. [4 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)+]

## 33:5/q17 What does junāḥ mean and where might it come from?  [جُنَاحٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Arabic derivation versus a proposed Persian loan.
- 33:5/q17/p1 It means sin, linked to inclining from truth. — Bursevi compares a listing ship; Ibn Ashur uses the sense to infer obligation. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:5/q17/p2 It may derive from Persian gunāh. — Bursevi reports sound substitutions and compares jawhar from gohar. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 33:5/q18 What does akhta'tum imply in form and usage?  [أَخْطَأْتُم · meaning]
- 33:5/q18/p1 It indicates deviation without knowledge, unlike knowingly doing wrong. — Ibn Atiyya contrasts mukhtiʾ and khāṭiʾ, with usage examples and 69:37. [2 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عطية)]
- 33:5/q18/p2 Its hamza is pronounced because form IV takes hamzat al-qaṭʿ. — Bursevi states the morphology. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 33:5/q20 How is the second mā parsed?  [مَّا تَعَمَّدَتْ · grammar]
turns on: Genitive coordination versus nominative subject with an omitted predicate.
- 33:5/q20/p1 It is genitive, coordinated with mā in fīmā. — Farra and Tabari supply the construction; Alusi answers the objection about separation from a genitive governor by coordinating each relative with its clause. [13 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:5/q20/p2 It is a nominative subject with an omitted predicate assigning liability to intention. — Nahhas, Qurtubi, Samin, Razi, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi recover liability from context. [8 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 33:5/q21 What warning attaches to knowingly claiming false descent?  [وَلَٰكِن مَّا تَعَمَّدَتْ قُلُوبُكُمْ · law]
turns on: The reports express exclusion from Paradise, curse, or kufr.
- 33:5/q21/p1 Paradise is barred to one who knowingly claims another father. — Saʿd and Abu Bakra transmit the Prophetic warning; Qurtubi names the Sahih collections and Alusi notes major-sin classification. ## 33:5/q22 May nonliteral family terms or old adoptive names still be used?  [ٱدْعُوهُمْ لِءَابَآئِهِمْ · law]
turns on: The scope of the prohibition and the risk of lineage confusion.
- 33:5/q22/p1 Affectionate 'my son' or respectful 'my father' is permitted absent a lineage claim. — Razi and Alusi distinguish affection from pagan adoption. Ibn Ashur distinguishes grandchildren, nonadopted children, and clear honorifics, citing the Prophet's use, poetry, and the later Farewell Pilgrimage address. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الشاعر,النمر بن تولب); ASAD-NOTES]
- 33:5/q22/p2 Such address is discouraged when it creates suspicion or imitates unbelievers. — Ibn Ashur cautions against casual use. Al-Khafaji and a report of Ibn Hajar call it disliked; Alusi prefers an imitation rationale while rejecting its fit to this verse's direct ban. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إبن حجر,الخفاجي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:5/q22/p3 Old adoptive names sometimes persisted, but deliberate false naming of Zayd is sinful. — Qurtubi cites al-Miqdad and Salim; Ibn Ashur objects that continued use cannot prove intent. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(القرطبي)]

## 33:5/q23 What does 'but' do here?  [وَلَٰكِن · grammar]
- 33:5/q23/p1 It states the contrary to the exemption or qualifies a general negation. — Ibn Ashur compares its use in 33:40. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:5/q24 Why name the heart as agent of intention?  [قُلُوبُكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 33:5/q24/p1 Assigning intent to hearts is familiar Quranic wording. — Alusi compares 2:283; Biqai reads the feminine verb as hinting at feminine softness and the abundant plural as widening deliberate guilt. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 33:5/q25 What do divine forgiveness and mercy address?  [وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ غَفُورًۭا رَّحِيمًا · interpretation]
turns on: Earlier custom, innocent error, and repentance.
- 33:5/q25/p1 They forgive earlier adoptive speech and conduct. — Muqatil names 'Zayd son of Muhammad'; Mawardi adds pre-Islamic ignorance and repentance; Ibn Atiyya says the attributes also apply generally. [6 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 33:5/q25/p2 God lifts blame from mistakes and forgives deliberate sinners who repent. — Qurtubi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai, Alusi and Ibn Ashur distribute the attributes across error and repentance. Bursevi adds negligent causes of error and Umar's call to seek forgiveness for sins. [12 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(عمر); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 33:5/q25/p3 Forgiveness covers the subordinate's wrong; mercy helps the helpless; their order reflects covering then provision. — Razi excludes fearful concealment and self-interested kindness; he describes the converse order as favor then withheld punishment. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 33:5/q26 What happened to Salim after adoption ended, and how was nursing understood?  [فَإِخْوَٰنُكُمْ فِى ٱلدِّينِ وَمَوَٰلِيكُمْ · reports]
turns on: General precedent versus a special dispensation.
- 33:5/q26/p1 The Prophet told Sahla to nurse adult Salim five times, establishing milk kinship. — Aisha and Urwa report that his earlier adoptive status had allowed entry to Abu Hudhayfa's household. [4 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله); IBNHIBBAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(عائشة); MALIK(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 33:5/q26/p2 Aisha applied the instruction to other men she wished to admit. — Ibn Hibban and Malik say her sister and nieces nursed them. [2 notes: IBNHIBBAN(عائشة); MALIK(عائشة أم المؤمنين)]
- 33:5/q26/p3 The other wives regarded the instruction as a concession for Salim alone. — They refused to admit others on this basis. [2 notes: IBNHIBBAN(سائر أزواج رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MALIK(سائر أزواج النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 33:5/q27 What examples illuminate unknown fathers or corrected affiliation?  [ءَابَآءَهُمْ · reports]
- 33:5/q27/p1 Abu Bakra said his father was unknown, so he was a religious brother or ally. — The donkey remark varies between his father and narrator; Qurtubi says hadith transmitters name him Nufay ibn al-Harith; Thaʿlabi's variant names Abu Bakr. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو بكرة,أبو بكرة عن أبيه); THALABI(أبو بكر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو بكرة,الراوي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكرة); DURR-FULL(أبو بكرة)]
- 33:5/q27/p2 Salim's father was unknown and he was Abu Hudhayfa's client. — Durr cites Salim ibn Abi al-Ja'd that he was only their ally. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سالم بن أبي الجعد)]
- 33:5/q27/p3 The Prophet called Zayd their brother and client in the custody dispute over Hamza's daughter. — Ali, Zayd and Jaʿfar competed; the Prophet awarded the girl to her maternal aunt Asma bint Umays. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 33:5/q28 How did this verse change prior adoption practice?  [ٱدْعُوهُمْ لِءَابَآئِهِمْ · sciences]
turns on: Ending prior custom versus describing Quranic abrogation of earlier Sunnah.
- 33:5/q28/p1 It ended pre-Islamic and early Islamic adoptive filiation. — Ibn Kathir says early Islam allowed it; Qurtubi, Nasafi and Study Quran describe prior sonship and inheritance; Kuranyolu calls the change social reform. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 33:5/q28/p2 Quran abrogated a prior Prophetic practice or tacit Sunnah. — Nahhas names this Quran abrogating Sunnah, with 49:10; Ibn Ashur clarifies that practical and tacit Sunnah are meant. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:5/q29 How does this verse connect with nearby statements?  [* · links]
- 33:5/q29/p1 The one-heart and zihar examples in 33:4 prepare the correction of fictive sonship. — Ibn Kathir cites 58:3; Kuranyolu treats one heart as grounding the impossibility of two biological fathers and criticism of pagan customs; Biqai connects later events. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 33:5/q29/p2 The command elaborates God's truth and straight path. — Al-Tibi reads the preceding declaration through this command and the sura's end; Ibn Ashur says the reason reassures hearers of 33:4. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:5/q29/p3 Clause connection and separation signal emphasis, resumption and contrast. — Nasafi notes the imperative, declarative, nominal, imperative pattern; Al-Tibi discusses through 33:6. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 33:5/q30 How does the no-blame clause relate to the command?  [وَلَيْسَ عَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌۭ فِيمَآ أَخْطَأْتُم بِهِۦ · rhetoric]
