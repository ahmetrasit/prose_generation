# 12:97: 15 questions

## 12:97/q01 How is the brothers' speech situated in the surrounding dialogue?  [قَالُوا۟ · links]
- 12:97/q01/p1 The brothers speak after returning from Egypt, though the narrative omits their journey; Jacob's nearby replies continue his exchange with the sons who entered. — STUDYQURAN Q places the exchange after their return. QURTUBI says the omitted return supports identifying Jacob's earlier critics as relatives or grandchildren, since the sons were away. IBNASHUR says Jacob's reply answers the good news in dialogue style, with uncoordinated قال, and restates what he had said when his sons objected to his continued remembrance of Joseph. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 12:97/q02 Why do the brothers address Jacob as 'our father'?  [يَٰٓأَبَانَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the paternal appeal implies that their fate depends on Jacob's intercession; ALUSI rejects that added claim.
- 12:97/q02/p1 They invoke his fatherhood to stir paternal affection and compassion, confident of his pardon. — ALUSI says their confession makes pardon and intercession fitting and their confidence explains why they ask directly for his prayer. He also reports that the alternative explanation appeals to paternal compassion, but rejects its further claim that they would be lost without his intercession. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 12:97/q02/p2 They call him father because without his intercession they would be lost. — ALUSI reports this explanation from others and calls it unconvincing. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 12:97/q03 What do the sons ask Jacob to do?  [ٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَآ · interpretation]
- 12:97/q03/p1 They ask their father to seek God's forgiveness for their sins, acknowledging wrongdoing and repenting. — MUQATIL specifies wrongdoing concerning Joseph; JISHUMI and NASAFI include the injury to Jacob. BURSEVI adds Joseph and Benjamin and says they appeal to Jacob's compassion. ELMALILI stresses real guilt and remorse. IBNASHUR identifies the request as repentance and admission; BIQAI says the pronouns make the speakers and their sins plain. KURANYOLU says Jacob remained hurt and delayed praying. [9 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 12:97/q04 Why seek Jacob's prayer after Joseph's words of pardon?  [ٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Joseph had already pardoned them, whose right his pardon covered, whether God's pardon was still needed, and what they knew of Joseph's prophethood.
- 12:97/q04/p1 Jacob was their prophet father, whose prayer was expected to be answered. — MAWARDI says they sought the prayer of their prophet father. IBNJAWZI-ZAD gives his answered prayers as their reason for asking. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 12:97/q04/p2 Joseph's pardon did not remove the wrong done to Jacob through the grief they caused him. — MAWARDI says they could answer for Jacob's grief only by honoring him, and separately explains that Joseph forgave only his own right. QURTUBI says Jacob's own pardon was needed to clear the grief inflicted on him. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:97/q04/p3 Even the pardons of Joseph and Jacob could not replace God's forgiveness. — IBNATIYYA reports that the brothers realized this after Joseph had forgiven them, and therefore sought Jacob's prayer while acknowledging their wrongdoing. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 12:97/q04/p4 Joseph's earlier words promised pardon in the future; they did not announce a pardon already granted. — MAWARDI offers this as one answer concerning Joseph's words at 12:92. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 12:97/q04/p5 They trusted their prophet father's response but did not know Joseph was a prophet. — MAWARDI offers this as a third answer to why they appealed to Jacob despite Joseph's words at 12:92. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 12:97/q05 For which wrongdoing do the sons ask forgiveness?  [ذُنُوبَنَآ · referent]
turns on: Which people the sources identify as injured by the brothers' sins.
- 12:97/q05/p1 Their treatment of Joseph. — MUQATIL identifies the wrongdoing as concerning Joseph; BIQAI calls it intentional wrongdoing against him. ISLAHI says the brothers confess to Jacob as they had confessed to Joseph at 12:91 after recognizing their folly. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL(النبي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:97/q05/p2 The grief and injury they caused Jacob. — MAWARDI says they could answer for this only by honoring Jacob; JISHUMI identifies his grief; QURTUBI says his pardon alone could clear the injury. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:97/q05/p3 Their wrongdoing against both Jacob and Joseph. — NASAFI names both the father and his son, along with the brothers' admission and repentance. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 12:97/q05/p4 Their deeds against Joseph and Benjamin. — TABATABAI names both brothers and notes that Joseph had already forgiven them at 12:92. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 12:97/q05/p5 Their deliberate sins against Jacob, Joseph, and Benjamin. — BURSEVI names all three and says the brothers also appeal to Jacob's compassion. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 12:97/q06 When did Jacob seek forgiveness for his sons, and why did he delay?  [ٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَنَا · reports]
turns on: The transmitted timing differs among dawn, Friday, repeated Friday nights, and twenty years; other sources propose his hurt or a test of sincerity as the reason for delay.
- 12:97/q06/p1 He waited until the night prayer or predawn, when prayer was unobstructed, and then prayed at dawn. — THALABI says most commentators specify predawn because prayer then is not veiled from God; he reports Jacob praying at dawn for his own grief and impatience over Joseph and for his sons' acts. MUHARIB IBN DITHAR reports Ibn Masud invoking forgiveness at dawn and citing Jacob's delay; DURR also transmits Ibn Masud's predawn account. [4 notes: THALABI(محارب بن دثار); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 12:97/q06/p2 He delayed because he remained hurt, perhaps waiting until dawn or reconciliation, or signaling his grievance. — KURANYOLU presents these as possible explanations for his delay. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:97/q06/p3 He waited until Friday. — THALABI transmits through Ikrima from Ibn Abbas a Prophetic explanation of Jacob's promise as waiting until Friday. [1 notes: THALABI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 12:97/q06/p4 He sought forgiveness every Friday night for more than twenty years. — THALABI reports this from Wahb. [1 notes: THALABI(وهب)]
- 12:97/q06/p5 He waited until the predawn of Friday, which coincided with Ashura. — THALABI reports this from Tawus. [1 notes: THALABI(طاوس)]
- 12:97/q06/p6 The brothers and Jacob sought forgiveness for twenty years before Gabriel taught Jacob a supplication and they received an answer. — IBNABIHATIM's report says their petitions for the treatment of Joseph were not accepted until Gabriel taught a prayer invoking God as the hope, aid, and helper of believers and beloved of penitents. MAWARDI transmits from al-Layth through Ibn Wahb the twenty years and Gabriel's teaching. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(يعقوب); MAWARDI-FULL(الليث بن سعد)]
- 12:97/q06/p7 He delayed to test whether his sons were sincere and truthful. — STUDYQURAN reports this as a possible explanation for his delay. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q, R)]

## 12:97/q07 What do the reports say about the eventual forgiveness?  [ٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَنَا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over when pardon was announced and whether its announcement included a covenant of future prophethood.
- 12:97/q07/p1 At dawn, Jacob prayed for himself and his sons and God revealed that all were forgiven. — THALABI says Jacob included his own grief and impatience over Joseph and his sons' acts in the dawn prayer. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 12:97/q07/p2 After twenty years of seeking pardon, Gabriel taught Jacob a supplication and their petition was answered. — IBNABIHATIM gives the supplication's appeals to God as believers' hope, aid, and helper and penitents' beloved. MAWARDI transmits the account from al-Layth through Ibn Wahb. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(يعقوب); MAWARDI-FULL(الليث بن سعد)]
- 12:97/q07/p3 The brothers feared their parents' pardon was insufficient and requested God's pardon; twenty years later revelation announced forgiveness and a covenant of future prophethood among them. — TAB-FULL transmits this in a long report from Anas ibn Malik. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(أنس بن مالك)]

## 12:97/q08 What must a person who wrongs someone else do to obtain release?  [ٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَآ · law]
turns on: Whether the victim needs to know the nature and extent of the wrong to give meaningful pardon.
- 12:97/q08/p1 The offender must ask the injured person for release and disclose the wrong and its extent. — QURTUBI applies this to wrongs to person or property and rejects a vague pardon because knowing the details could change the injured person's willingness to forgive. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 12:97/q08/p2 A general, unspecified pardon is sufficient. — QURTUBI reports this view only to reject it, because the injured person might judge differently after learning the details. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 12:97/q09 Why does the wording use these pronouns without repeating the sons as subject?  [لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا · grammar]
- 12:97/q09/p1 Each pronoun clearly points back to its own speaker, so the subject need not be repeated. — BIQAI says the pronoun references are plain from the context. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 12:97/q10 Why is the admission to Jacob explicit and emphatic?  [ٱسْتَغْفِرْ لَنَا ذُنُوبَنَآ إِنَّا كُنَّا خَٰطِـِٔينَ · rhetoric]
- 12:97/q10/p1 They explicitly request forgiveness and use emphatic إنّا before Jacob, unlike their lighter إن and unnamed offense before Joseph. — SAMARRAI contrasts the brothers' speech to Joseph, where they neither asked him to forgive them nor named their offense, with their explicit plea and admission to their father; he also points to 12:98. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 12:97/q11 What does the sons' confession signify for repentance and pardon?  [إِنَّا كُنَّا خَٰطِـِٔينَ · interpretation]
- 12:97/q11/p1 It is a genuine admission of guilt and repentance, making forgiveness and intercession fitting. — BAGHAWI says they call themselves sinners. IBNATIYYA says they acknowledged wrongdoing even after Joseph's pardon. BAYDAWI says one who confesses sin is entitled to pardon and to ask forgiveness. BIQAI cites a Prophetic report that God accepts a servant who confesses and repents. ALUSI links admission to fitting pardon and intercession. ELMALILI stresses remorse; IBNASHUR calls the plea repentance; ISLAHI says they recognized their folly after events unfolded, as in their confession to Joseph at 12:91. [9 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NASAFI-FULL]

## 12:97/q12 What does 'khāṭiʾīn' mean?  [خَٰطِـِٔينَ · meaning]
- 12:97/q12/p1 It means sinners or wrongdoers; the sin can fall short of shirk. — BAGHAWI and JISHUMI gloss the admission as calling themselves sinners. DAMGHANI says khāṭiʾūn can mean sinners whose sin is not shirk, and IBNJAWZI-NUZHA says khaṭaʾ can have that sense. BIQAI and BURSEVI specify deliberate wrongdoing. ZAJJAJ distinguishes khaṭiʾa/yakhṭaʾu from akhṭaʾa/yukhṭiʾu and cites an Imruʾ al-Qays line using khaṭiʾna. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BIQAI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI]

## 12:97/q13 How is 'khāṭiʾīn' read in the canonical readings?  [خَٰطِـِٔينَ · readings]
- 12:97/q13/p1 The ten canonical readings elide the hamza while preserving the root and meaning. — QIRAAT-ER gives this as the ten-reading form. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 12:97/q14 What inward meaning does the family's gathering carry?  [* · inward]
- 12:97/q14/p1 Gathering the family figures the gathering of all faculties into moderation under the heart's mediation. — QASHANI says the heart mediates between higher and lower faculties and restrains bodily pleasure-seeking. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 12:97/q15 What does the wider Joseph story teach about how God directs events?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:97/q15/p1 God directs human affairs in ways people cannot foresee; they may dislike what is good or like what is bad for them. — ASAD contrasts this lesson of the Qur'anic story with Genesis' romantic account of Joseph's fortunes and cites 2:216. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
