# 96:8: 16 questions

## 96:8/q01 Why is the return asserted with inna?  [إِنَّ · rhetoric]
- 96:8/q01/p1 It reinforces a warning that tyrants behave as though they deny their return. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says their thorough forgetfulness warrants treating them as deniers and strengthening the indirect warning. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 96:8/q02 Who is addressed by 'your Lord,' and what does the address do?  [رَبِّكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the rhetorical shift addresses the human directly or the sequence of preceding addresses continues to address the Prophet.
- 96:8/q02/p1 The human being, especially the transgressor, is addressed directly as a warning. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, and Nasafī call the turn to direct address a threat about transgression; ʿAlūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report this as an emphatic shift, although they prefer the Prophet as addressee. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ALUSI(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 96:8/q02/p2 The Prophet is addressed, while the transgressor is warned and the Prophet consoled. — ʿAlūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefer this because preceding addresses are to the Prophet. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the verse a parenthesis between the introduction and main subject, consoling him with the tyrant's return and punishment; Elmalılı says the chief aim remains to warn self-sufficient transgressors. [6 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; TABATABAI+]

## 96:8/q03 What does placing 'to your Lord' first convey?  [إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the advance is explained as exclusivity or emphasis; the notes offer no argument that these effects exclude each other.
- 96:8/q03/p1 It restricts the return to God alone, with no independent or shared recourse elsewhere. — ʿAlūsī applies the restriction to all creation through death and resurrection; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ joins it to God's sole possession of the first and last, citing 92:13. Bursawī says the tyrant then sees his transgression's outcome. [5 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 96:8/q03/p2 It gives the phrase emphasis. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly names emphasis as the effect of advancing ilā rabbika. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 96:8/q04 What does al-rujʿā mean?  [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verbal expression names the act of return or its endpoint; several sources allow both.
- 96:8/q04/p1 The act of returning. — Abū ʿUbayda and al-Akhfash are reported by Wāḥidī; Wāḥidī equates it with rujūʿan and marjiʿan. Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabrisī, Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurtubī, Rāzī, Bursawī, ʿAlūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Abū Ḥayyān give the same basic gloss. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports this majority gloss. [26 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الأخفش); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(أكثر المفسرين)]
- 96:8/q04/p2 The place, endpoint, or ultimate destination of return. — Abū ʿUbayda allows the place as well as the act. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, as transmitted by Māwardī, calls it the final destination; Ibn al-Jawzī and Rāzī also give destination or place. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers an unrestricted endpoint and notes that this Quranic form is unique, citing later reminders at 37:68 and 89:28. [8 notes: MAJAZ; MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]

## 96:8/q05 What is the form and syntactic case of al-rujʿā?  [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: The notes treat form and case as compatible analyses, while differing in their reported pattern labels.
- 96:8/q05/p1 It is a verbal noun on a fuʿlā or faʿlā type pattern, with a feminine final alif. — Wāḥidī calls it faʿlā; Baghawī and Qurṭubī give a faʿlā/faʿla description. Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give fuʿlā; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares ʿuqbā and Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, ʿAlūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr compare bushrā. Abū Ḥayyān and Bursawī identify the feminine alif. The sources' pattern labels vary while sharing a verbal-noun analysis. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(أكثر المفسرين)]
- 96:8/q05/p2 Its syntactic position is accusative, although the final alif hides the case ending. — Al-Naḥḥās states the underlying case and the reason it cannot be heard. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 96:8/q06 What general Arabic form material accompanies the final alif and weak letters in these notes?  [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · grammar]
- 96:8/q06/p1 The supplied grammatical observations describe wider Arabic weak-letter, alif, diminutive, plural, and declension patterns; they do not directly analyze this verse's word. — Sībawayh describes possessive-yāʾ assimilation (#596), diminutive choices and root-like augments (#628 and #606), emphatic nūn with weak verbs (#644), hamza/yāʾ/wāw alternations in broken plurals (#792), derived nouns of weak verbs (#777), weak-initial noun forms (#700), types of final alif and their declension (#529), and loss or preservation of weak initials (#681). These are supplied as separate general observations rather than as a stated derivation of al-rujʿā. [73 notes: SIBAWAYH(أكثر العرب,الخليل,بعض العرب,بعضهم,من أثق به,ناس,ناس من العرب,يونس)]

## 96:8/q07 What reading and codex-spelling observations are supplied?  [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · readings]
turns on: Whether atypical codex forms have sound orthographic reasons or are writing mistakes; none of these notes ties an alternative spelling to this verse.
- 96:8/q07/p1 One reported choice concerns a hāʾ's vowel: both forms are sound, but ḍamma is preferred as more regular. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports al-Taysīr accepting both and al-Kāfī preferring ḍamma; he also prefers ḍamma because Ḥamza uses it where preceding yāʾ derives from alif. The note does not assert that this hāʾ occurs in 96:8. [2 notes: NASHR(صاحب التيسير وصاحب الكافي); NASHR+]
- 96:8/q07/p2 Several observations explain unusual codex spellings of bāyidin, bāyīkum, bāyiyah, and bāyātinā, without identifying a variant of 96:8. — Ibn al-Jazarī says the apparent extra yāʾ is a hamza spelling after an extra alif; al-Sakhāwī reports two yāʾs in Iraqi and Syrian codices and explains imālah, but Ibn al-Jazarī queries his wording or a copying omission. He regards most orthographic departures as purposeful or analogical. A report attributes to ʿUthmān the claim that Arabs would correct errors orally; al-Dānī rejects it because ʿUthmān would not leave baseless errors to repair. [5 notes: NASHR(السخاوي,بعض الناس); NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني)-]
- 96:8/q07/p3 The reported attribution to ʿUthmān that Arabs would correct codex errors orally is rejected. — Al-Dānī says leaving such errors would make writing pointless and burdensome; Ibn al-Jazarī instead expects a sound rationale in early spelling. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني); NASHR(بعض الناس)-]

## 96:8/q08 When and in what sense does the return to God occur?  [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether return is restricted to the afterlife, includes reversals and dependence in worldly life, also evokes origin, or is read inwardly as self-annihilation.
- 96:8/q08/p1 It is the return after death for resurrection, gathering, judgment, and recompense. — Ṭabarī says Muhammad's opponent will taste intolerable punishment; Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī, and Nasafī stress requital for tyranny. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says resurrection and gathering diminish worldly wealth; Abū Ḥayyān adds reckoning. Rāzī cites 14:42–43 for deferred punishment and says the warning benefits the steadfast more than pleasure-seekers. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 78:21–22 for Hell and 84:6 for universal return. Islahi says neglected lordship and divine power will become plain. Kurdish? No: Kur'an Yolu calls it the first Hereafter allusion if this is the first sūra, citing 2:177 and 4:136 for later explicit accountability. [36 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bu görüşü benimseyenler)]
- 96:8/q08/p2 God also returns people in this life from maturity, wealth, and strength to weakness, poverty, decline, and death. — Māwardī mentions maturity to decline and life to death; Rāzī makes the reversal symmetrical with God's earlier raising from inertness to life, poverty to wealth, and lowliness to honor. [4 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 96:8/q08/p3 Return embraces present dependence on God's decree as well as the final judgment after death. — Biqāʿī says no one acts in worldly life except by God's decree and all deeds receive fitting reward or punishment later, citing 95:7–8 in the full version. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that wealth never removes dependence on God or knowledge of one's fate; return can metaphorically suggest need of the one returned to. [5 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI]
- 96:8/q08/p4 All existence returns to God as its source as well as returning for judgment. — Asad gives both senses; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects human origin in a clot with God as beginning and end and cites 92:13. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI]
- 96:8/q08/p5 Inwardly, return is the annihilation of the self's essence and attributes before the true divine actor. — Qāshānī relates this to the Prophet's humble statement that he is not the reader: God is the true reader. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 96:8/q09 Who returns to God?  [ٱلرُّجْعَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the local threat is read through the Abū Jahl reports or through the generic human described in the preceding creation and transgression verses; universal return is also asserted.
- 96:8/q09/p1 All people or all creation return, whatever their wealth, conduct, or rank. — Ṭabrisī and Bursawī say all return at death and resurrection. Quṭb lists righteous and wicked, obedient and disobedient, rich and poor; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 84:6. Biqāʿī says all are dependent in life and death. [11 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bu görüşü benimseyenler)]
- 96:8/q09/p2 The immediate warning targets the self-sufficient transgressor, exemplified by Abū Jahl. — Muqātil identifies Abū Jahl using 96:15–16's forelock; Ṭabarī calls him Muhammad's opponent. Elmalılı mentions the arrogant unbeliever of the revelation occasion; ʿAlūsī says the Prophet can be addressed while the tyrant is warned. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; QURTUBI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR]
- 96:8/q09/p3 The surface context concerns the generic human, though reports point more readily to Abū Jahl. — Rāzī prefers humanity generally: after creation, blessing, and enrichment, people sin and pursue appetite, yet face judgment. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports his solution and extends the pattern to forgetting return and forbidding worship. Ibn ʿĀshūr assigns the general transgression statement through 96:8 a preparatory role for the specific Abū Jahl episode from 96:9. [4 notes: BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]

## 96:8/q10 What warning and moral point does the verse teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 96:8/q10/p1 The inescapable return and reckoning check tyranny, pride, and the illusion of self-sufficiency. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, ʿAlūsī, and Quṭb call it a threat. Biqāʿī calls remembered recompense tyranny's remedy and temporary wealth humbling; Tabrisī says God can destroy and punish the wealthy tyrant. Ibn Kathīr applies judgment to how wealth was acquired and spent. Elmalılı says none may claim sufficiency. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds consolation to the Prophet and that arrogant people do not know their fate; Asad says responsibility before supreme power grounds moral distinctions. Kur'an Yolu calls for preparation for judgment; its preferred translation stresses that God owns possessions people mistake as their own. Bursawī's Persian couplet says deeds last after wealth passes. [30 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bu görüşü benimseyenler); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 96:8/q11 How does the verse connect to its surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes offer complementary links: nearby transgression, the sūra's knowledge and wealth contrast, and the wider creation-to-return arc.
- 96:8/q11/p1 It carries the warning from human enrichment and transgression into the specific prayer-forbidding tyrant episode. — Muqātil links the warning about Abū Jahl to the forelock in 96:15–16. Rāzī prefers humanity generally in 96:6–8 despite the Abū Jahl reports. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 96:6–8 prepare for the episode beginning at 96:9; he calls 96:8 a parenthesis before the main subject. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الفخر الرازي); RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 96:8/q11/p2 The ending balances the opening's praise of knowledge with a warning against worldly wealth and urges learning and religion. — Bursawī gives this sequence; ʿAlūsī reports it and his full version accepts the woven warning. Bursawī also reports a prayer for refuge from wealth breeding tyranny and poverty bringing forgetfulness. [4 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 96:8/q11/p3 It completes an arc from creation and divine teaching to final return, with God as both beginning and end. — Quṭb connects creation, honor, teaching, and return without a partner. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ links creation from a clot to 92:13 and cites 37:68 and 89:28 for later return language. Study Quran calls return a central Quranic theme and cites 2:156. [5 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]

## 96:8/q12 What is reported about Abū Jahl's request for Mecca's mountains?  [* · reports]
- 96:8/q12/p1 Abū Jahl proposed turning the mountains to gold and silver; Gabriel offered it with a severe warning, and the Prophet withheld the prayer to spare them. — Rāzī's two versions say riches would make the Quraysh transgress, leave their religion, and follow Muhammad; they differ in citing 5:115 and 5:112 for the table punishment. Bursawī divides the request and Gabriel's warning between two notes. The request is sarcastic in Rāzī's full account. [4 notes: RAZI(أبو جهل); RAZI-FULL(أبو جهل); BURSEVI(أبو جهل)]

## 96:8/q13 What other transmitted sayings illustrate knowledge, wealth, and return?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes transmit two different sayings for the same moral contrast.
- 96:8/q13/p1 Two seekers are never satisfied: the seeker of knowledge and the seeker of worldly gain. — Ibn Kathīr says it was transmitted as a Prophetic report; Bursawī attributes it to Ibn Masʿūd and adds that knowledge increases divine favor whereas world-seeking increases rebellion. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود)]
- 96:8/q13/p2 The Prophet sought refuge from wealth that causes tyranny and poverty that causes forgetfulness. — Bursawī reports this prayer as part of the knowledge and wealth contrast. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 96:8/q14 What inward error makes people imagine themselves independent of the Lord?  [* · inward]
- 96:8/q14/p1 People mistake entrusted divine names and attributes manifested within them for their own possessions and become proud. — Bursawī says these manifestations should instead be recognized as signs of their Creator and of continued dependence on Him. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 96:8/q15 What sūra facts and chronology are reported?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The chronology turns on whether the prayer passage belongs immediately after the first five revelations, given the reported later start of obligatory prayer.
- 96:8/q15/p1 The sūra is Meccan and has seventeen verses. — Al-Jishumī states both particulars. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 96:8/q15/p2 Whether these verses followed the first five immediately is disputed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports that mention of prayer seems difficult if obligatory prayer began years later at the Night Journey; some place intervening revelation and later attachment, while others deny the first five were the earliest revelation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 96:8/q15/p3 If al-ʿAlaq was the first sūra, this is the Quran's first allusion to the Hereafter. — Kur'an Yolu attributes this conditional conclusion to those accepting the first-sūra premise and contrasts later explicit statements of accountability, citing 2:177 and 4:136. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bu görüşü benimseyenler)]
- 96:8/q15/p4 Reciting this sūra earns a reward like reciting the whole set of shorter sūras. — Bayḍāwī transmits this as a Prophetic report. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 96:8/q16 How is the earlier rebuke kalla related to this warning?  [* · links]
- 96:8/q16/p1 Kalla anticipates and rebukes the later description of one who forbids a servant's prayer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says kalla normally follows the speech it rejects; its initial placement here points forward to the target in 96:9 and following, and he compares an initial negative particle in Imru al-Qays. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
