# 39:21: 26 questions

## 39:21/q01 Whom does the opening call to observe?  [أَلَمْ تَرَ · referent]
- 39:21/q01/p1 The Prophet is addressed, and every observer is invited to consider the sign. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya extends the address to everyone; al-Bursevī allows Muḥammad or anyone. Abū Ḥayyān says it directs the listener to divine acts and the passing world. Al-Biqāʿī says addressing the Prophet, the best of creation, intensifies reflection on recurring evidence for restoration. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:21/q02 How does the opening question work?  [أَلَمْ تَرَ · rhetoric]
- 39:21/q02/p1 It calls for recognition of divine action through a jussive question. — Al-Jishumī parses tarā as jussive after lam, losing final yāʾ while rāʾ keeps its fatḥa. Al-Jurjānī distinguishes questions about occurrence from questions about agency with 21:62–63, 17:40, 37:153 and 10:59; his broader account grounds word order in relations of predicate, object, time and purpose, illustrated by Abū Tammām. [7 notes: JISHUMI; JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 39:21/q03 What is the water's origin and its relation to springs?  [ٱلسَّمَآءِ مَآءًۭ يَنَٰبِيعَ · interpretation]
turns on: The accounts differ over current rainfall, a primordial supply, or a physical source of spring water; reports and seasonal observations are used.
- 39:21/q03/p1 It is rain or other precipitation stored in earth and led into springs. — Al-Ṭabarī and others infer rain because springs swell with rain and dry without it (Ibn ʿAṭiyya); Abū Ḥayyān and al-Bursevī describe gradual release. Ibn Kathīr cites 25:48 and says God apportions differently sized springs; Saʿīd specifies mountain snow. Al-Ālūsī prefers rain and snow while allowing some condensation. Al-Mībudhī says seasonal evidence rules out vapor as sole source. Al-Biqāʿī stresses measured descent and reemergence. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري وغيره); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البركات البغدادي,الميبدي); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 39:21/q03/p2 All earthly water originally descended from heaven, sometimes reaching a rock before distribution. — Al-Shaʿbī, Saʿīd, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Kalbī transmit the universal claim. Ibn ʿAbbās says underground veins alter its taste; ascending would sweeten salt water. Abū Hurayra's prophetic report places sweet waters and pollinating winds beneath Jerusalem's rock; al-Bursevī says clouds convey water there. Al-Ālūsī allows the Ibn ʿAbbās report literally if authentic and marfūʿ; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the rain and spring explanations close. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(الشعبي); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الشعبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن بن مسلم بن يناق,الشعبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الشعبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الشعبي والضحاك); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير، وعامر الشعبي); BIQAI-FULL(الشعبي والضحاك); DURR-FULL(الشعبي,الكبي); BURSEVI(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,الزمخشري); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 39:21/q03/p3 At creation water descended from the Sea of the Throne onto a waterless earth. — Al-Ālūsī reports and rejects it: no sound report supports it, and the general invitation to see could not mean unwitnessed creation without forcing an implausibly exclusive address to the Prophet. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:21/q03/p4 The water comes from underground veins rather than descending from the sky. — Al-Durr's wording attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbās, with the ascent challenge; other transmissions of Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly say the opposite. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:21/q03/p5 Underground vapor condenses and bursts through earth as springs. — Al-Ālūsī reports the philosophers. Sharḥ al-Mawāqif allows vapor but prefers accumulated water because springs rise with snow and rain. Abū al-Barakāt rejects vapor as a sole cause since it predicts the opposite seasonal pattern; al-Mībudhī says this does not exclude partial contribution. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الفلاسفة,شرح المواقف); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البركات البغدادي)-]
- 39:21/q03/p6 Scattered water matter deep underground joins and forms spring water. — Sharḥ al-Mawāqif finds this stronger than condensed vapor on the evidence of well and spring levels responding to snow and rain. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شرح المواقف)]

## 39:21/q04 What does 'sky' denote?  [ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Whether it names rain's immediate direction or remote celestial causes.
- 39:21/q04/p1 It is the direction above. — Al-Ālūsī rejects direct fall from the familiar celestial sphere: clouds and heavy rain may lie below a mountain observer. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:21/q04/p2 It names celestial bodies as remote causes of rain. — Al-Ālūsī reports the sun drawing vapor upward and changing positions leading to cloud formation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 39:21/q05 What does 'salakahu' mean?  [فَسَلَكَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb emphasizes insertion and underground passage or the ensuing outflow.
- 39:21/q05/p1 God inserts, conducts and orders water through earth into springs, wells and channels. — Ibn Qutayba describes entry into earth; Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites poetry for driving through; al-Jishumī compares threading a needle and distinguishes salakahu fīhi from salaka fī al-Islām. Al-Bursevī distinguishes salaka, leading in, from aslaka, inserting. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and others compare courses to bodily veins; al-Qurṭubī cites 23:18 for settled water. [19 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:21/q05/p2 God makes water gush forth as springs. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives this as an alternative to sending water into the ground; al-Biqāʿī emphasizes its recurrence after being hidden in the depths. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:21/q06 What does 'yanābīʿ' mean and how is it formed?  [يَنَٰبِيعَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the plural names outlets or their flowing contents.
- 39:21/q06/p1 It is the plural of yanbūʿ, a spring or outlet where water wells up. — Al-Zajjāj gives the yafʿūl pattern from nabʿ; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the formation intensive and says an ʿayn is one kind. Ibn Kaysān, via al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī, explains poetic yanbāʿ by lengthening yanbaʿ's fatḥa; al-Naḥḥās records yanbaʿ as a singular. Al-Kisāʾī and al-Farrāʾ give three verb vocalizations via al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī. [16 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(ابن كيسان); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 39:21/q06/p2 It can denote the gushing water or a flowing thing. — Al-Bayḍāwī allows spring source or flowing water. Al-Ālūsī reports yānbiʿ, 'flowing thing', but finds its circumstantial parse awkward because min al-arḍ would be expected. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q07 How is 'yanābīʿ' parsed?  [يَنَٰبِيعَ · grammar]
turns on: The case is shared but its governing relation and implied words differ.
- 39:21/q07/p1 It is accusative after an omitted preposition: water is directed into springs. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī supply the preposition; al-Bursevī compares the explicit preposition in 28:32. Al-Ālūsī rejects a cognate accusative as requiring remote ellipsis. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:21/q07/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative for water becoming springs or flowing. — Al-Nasafī and al-Bayḍāwī allow a state construction; al-Ālūsī finds the flowing-water version awkward with fī rather than min al-arḍ. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:21/q07/p3 It is an adverbial accusative of place. — Al-Nasafī and al-Bayḍāwī allow a locative parse. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 39:21/q07/p4 It is a cognate accusative with an implied verbal noun or genitive. — Al-Ālūsī records and rejects both as distant ellipses. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 39:21/q08 What does 'in the earth' attach to?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
- 39:21/q08/p1 It specifies the location of the springs. — Al-Nasafī makes it a qualifier of yanābīʿ; al-Bursevī compares water being put in an irrigation channel in a garden. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 39:21/q09 Why use 'then' and the imperfect for crop growth?  [ثُمَّ يُخْرِجُ · rhetoric]
- 39:21/q09/p1 The sequence conveys delay between rain and growth, while the imperfect vividly presents the scene. — Al-Biqāʿī identifies the temporal interval; al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī allow delay in time or rank. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q10 What role does water play in crop production?  [بِهِۦ · theology]
turns on: Whether water is an instrument or merely accompanies God's act.
- 39:21/q10/p1 God brings crops forth by water as a divinely chosen means, without intrinsic necessity. — Al-Ālūsī distinguishes divine wisdom from water's independent necessity. Al-Nasafī says crops come forth by water; Abū Ḥayyān notes that rain sustains livelihood. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 39:21/q10/p2 Growth accompanies water, which has no causal role. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this account to the Ashʿarites. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q11 What does 'zarʿ' cover?  [زَرْعًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the defining feature is cultivation or lack of a trunk.
- 39:21/q11/p1 It covers cultivated crops generally. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān include all crops; al-Bursevī says the original verbal noun for sowing or growth names the product here; al-Qurṭubī says the generic singular embraces many kinds. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 39:21/q11/p2 It is trunkless vegetation, distinguished from trees. — Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguish shajar with trunk and branches, while nabāt includes both. [3 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]

## 39:21/q12 What differs in the crop's 'colors'?  [مُّخْتَلِفًا أَلْوَٰنُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'colors' denotes hues, kinds, or both with other properties.
- 39:21/q12/p1 The crop differs in visible hues. — Al-Zajjāj lists green, yellow, red and white; al-Qurṭubī adds blue and luminous or white; al-Baghawī gives red, yellow and green. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a group choosing qualities. Al-Rāghib, via al-Bursevī, places yellow between black and white and notes that it may signify black. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب)]
- 39:21/q12/p2 It differs in kinds or species. — Al-Ḥasan b. Muslim is transmitted with varying patronymics, Ibn Bayān and Ibn Yannāq; examples include wheat, barley, sesame, rice and maize, with 16:13 cited. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a group choosing kinds; Iṣlāḥī says alwān can mean types. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن بن مسلم بن بيان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:21/q12/p3 It embraces hues, species, and further qualities. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Bursevī name hues alongside crop kinds. Ibn Kathīr adds form, taste, scent and benefit; al-Biqāʿī adds temperament despite common water; al-Ālūsī extends it to all perceptible qualities, food and other crops. Al-Rāghib also uses 'colors' for speech and food kinds. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q13 What does 'yahīju' mean?  [يَهِيجُ · meaning]
turns on: The word is applied to different growth or decay stages; rising can follow full drying.
- 39:21/q13/p1 The plant dries, withers, or reaches the limit of drying. — Al-Aṣmaʿī speaks of complete drying and recession. Al-Jawharī includes dry or yellowed fields, wind drying crops, and anger rising or calming. Al-Jishumī also lists motion, turmoil, stirred blood, sexual arousal and longing. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī specify dryness's limit; Iṣlāḥī its beginning. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a report attributed to ʿAlī that piety prevents a community's crop or root-stock from withering. [26 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(الأصمعي); NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الأصمعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي رضي الله عنه); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأصمعي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,الجوهري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:21/q13/p2 The dried plant rises or loosens from its roots before dispersing. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursevī describe the stage after full drying; Abū Ḥayyān offers 'sprang up' as an alternative. Al-Ālūsī calls rising figurative because the plant is poised to detach. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الأصمعي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:21/q13/p3 The crop approaches fruiting. — Abū Ḥayyān gives this as another gloss beside drying. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 39:21/q13/p4 It means becoming green. — Al-Rāzī transmits al-Wāḥidī's lexical gloss. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)]

## 39:21/q14 What does seeing it yellow indicate?  [فَتَرَىٰهُ مُصْفَرًّۭا · interpretation]
- 39:21/q14/p1 Its former greenness and freshness have passed as it ages and dries. — Al-Nasafī and al-Bursevī describe yellowing after freshness; al-Biqāʿī places it after vigorous growth; Ibn Kathīr likens it to aging after youth; Abū Ḥayyān stresses lost freshness. [11 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q15 How is 'muṣfarran' also read?  [مُصْفَرًّۭا · readings]
- 39:21/q15/p1 A variant lengthens the standard muṣfarran to muṣfārran. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī report it; al-Ālūsī associates it with yellowing after freshness. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q16 What does 'ḥuṭām' mean?  [حُطَٰمًا · meaning]
- 39:21/q16/p1 It is dried vegetation broken and crumbled into fragments. — Abū ʿUbayda compares rufāt, al-Zajjāj adds darīn, Ibn Qutayba likens it to chaff, and al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī to dry wood. Al-Jishumī explains ḥaṭm as dry breaking and relates al-Ḥuṭama and al-Ḥaṭīm; al-Naḍr places the latter's broken stone near the Kaʿba spout. Muqātil glosses perishing with 27:18; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifies broken straw and grass. [22 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); JISHUMI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(النضر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:21/q17 Why say God makes the crop debris?  [ثُمَّ يَجْعَلُهُۥ حُطَٰمًا · rhetoric]
- 39:21/q17/p1 The forceful final change is attributed expressly to God's power. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts 57:20, which stresses worldly nothingness; al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say decay is God's effect just as growth is. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q18 How is 'thumma yajʿaluhu' read and parsed?  [ثُمَّ يَجْعَلُهُۥ · readings]
turns on: The disputed case ending requires either coordination or an omitted governor.
- 39:21/q18/p1 The common reading is indicative, coordinated with the preceding verb. — Al-Samīn gives this standard reading and parse. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 39:21/q18/p2 Abū Bishr reads the verb in the accusative, with fatḥa on the lām. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report it. Al-Hudhalī's al-Kāmil calls it weak; al-Samīn sees no apparent licenser, and al-Ālūsī agrees, citing the poetic parallel 'innī wa-qatlī Sulaykan thumma aʿqilahū'. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو بشر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو بشر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بشر); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:21/q18/p3 The accusative can supply an and coordinate with 'anna Allāha anzala'. — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers this first defense. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]
- 39:21/q18/p4 It can supply an after an implicit tarā: then you see it made debris. — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers this second defense. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 39:21/q19 What does 'in that' refer to?  [إِنَّ فِى ذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
- 39:21/q19/p1 It points to all the preceding details; the distant demonstrative marks their great significance. — Al-Ālūsī says the whole described sequence carries evidentiary force. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:21/q20 Who are 'people of understanding'?  [لِأُو۟لِى ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the description concerns reflective reasoning, spiritual purity, or apportioned intellect.
- 39:21/q20/p1 They use clear, sound intellect to recognize and benefit from the sign. — Al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī describe minds free of defect. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts discerners with people like animals; Ibn ʿĀshūr says beneficiaries reason and believe, citing 39:9. Al-Burhān notes lubb 'intellect' takes plural albāb and says revelation asks such people to ponder. [14 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:21/q20/p2 Inwardly, they are spiritual cores stripped of ego's shell. — Al-Qāshānī gives this reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 39:21/q20/p3 Intellect has one hundred parts: ninety-nine for the Prophet and one shared by believers. — Al-Sulamī reports that believers further differ over twenty portions of faith's realities. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 39:21/q21 How is 'ulī' related to singular and spelling?  [أُو۟لِى · grammar]
- 39:21/q21/p1 It is a collective with singular dhū; the written wāw distinguishes it from ilā. — Al-Naḥḥās attributes the orthographic explanation to some linguists. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 39:21/q22 What does the crop cycle teach about God's power and human duty?  [* · theology]
turns on: Different implications are emphasized: restoration, lordship, or gratitude and accountability.
- 39:21/q22/p1 God can restore the dead and fulfill His promise as He revives and changes plants. — Al-Ṭabarī says God can restore bodies and accidents in their former state; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Biqāʿī and Asad infer resurrection. Al-Qurṭubī adds the distinction of believer and unbeliever; al-Biqāʿī depicts human life from water and infancy to dust gathered again. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this an allusion; Study Quran cites 28:88 on universal perishing except God's Face. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:21/q22/p2 The ordered changes display God's oneness, wisdom, design and sole lordship. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī name oneness; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī stress design rather than neglect. Ibn al-Jawzī reports a power reading; al-Ṭabrisī notes varied plants from one water; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues exclusive lordship. Al-Ālūsī rejects a mere proof that a maker exists since God is already named as agent; Ibn ʿĀshūr nonetheless gives sole divinity as a structural reading, citing 39:5–6. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(غيره); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:21/q22/p3 People should thank God alone for sustenance and expect a just reckoning. — Abū Ḥayyān notes rain's livelihood benefit; Iṣlāḥī calls for gratitude to the Creator and anticipates reward and punishment. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:21/q23 What does the cycle teach about worldly and human life?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The applications stress transience, reception of revelation, or the general act of reflection.
- 39:21/q23/p1 Worldly beauty and human youth flourish briefly, then fade through old age to death. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī call it a worldly parable; al-Zamakhsharī cites 10:24 and 18:45, Ibn Kathīr 18:45, and Study Quran 18:45 and 57:20. Al-Rāzī applies decline to animals and people to lessen pleasure's lure; al-Bursevī quotes a Persian autumn-meadow poem. Qushayrī and Kashf al-Asrār trace childhood through death, and grain's harvest to human worth after arrival or self-emptying. Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh's success blinds them to its end. [23 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL(شرح المواقف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:21/q23/p2 It can represent the Qur'an's guidance and differing human responses. — Al-Qurṭubī reports rain entering believing hearts, while diseased hearts wither. Ibn ʿĀshūr maps rain to Qur'an, springs to its conveyance, varied crops to beneficial and harmful people, flourishing to believers' growth, and debris to everyone's death. He cites the Ṣaḥīḥ report comparing prophetic guidance to fertile, water-retaining and barren ground, and links 39:22–23. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 39:21/q23/p3 Its changing stages call sound minds to deliberate reflection. — Muqātil, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursevī and Iṣlāḥī stress remembrance. Al-Burhān says exegesis must track literal and figurative senses, grammar and context without omission or unwarranted addition. [6 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURHAN]

## 39:21/q24 How is the cycle read inwardly?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different correspondences use the image for spiritual cultivation, hypocrisy, annihilation, or fulfillment.
- 39:21/q24/p1 Spiritual water or knowledge enters inner faculties and produces varied deeds and morals. — Al-Qāshānī makes sky spirit, rain knowledge, springs wisdom in souls, and crops deeds and morals. Al-Tawīlāt al-Najmiyya via al-Bursevī makes the sky heart, springs wisdom in human nature, and crops prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage and jihad; al-Ālūsī reports God's presence or heart as sky and faculties as channels. [5 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 39:21/q24/p2 Ostentatious acts look green but wither through vanity into regret. — Al-Tawīlāt al-Najmiyya says apparently sound acts lose light and shatter by divine severity; al-Ālūsī applies yellowing and collapse to pretentious deeds and words. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:21/q24/p3 Spiritual advance dissolves earlier lights in divine knowing and unity. — Al-Qushayrī compares tawḥīd eclipsing insight to sunrise over stars. Al-Qāshānī reads withering as faculties and hearts annihilated by manifestations and fragments as established divine attributes. Al-Tawīlāt al-Najmiyya describes learning yielding to gnosis, then sciences being consumed by unity, with abiding after annihilation. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 39:21/q24/p4 Harvested grain represents realized human worth after arrival or self-emptying. — Al-Qushayrī says grain is the crop's purpose and cannot be gathered before drying; Kashf al-Asrār applies the life course to a person emptied of self. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]

## 39:21/q25 How does the verse relate to the sūra and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Contextual readings emphasize guidance, worldly transience, or promised rivers.
- 39:21/q25/p1 It resumes the guidance theme and prepares the guided and misguided responses of 39:22–23. — Ibn ʿĀshūr traces the theme past an excursus at 39:2; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links lordship and guidance. Iṣlāḥī sets it amid warning to arrogant deniers attached to affluence and a following attack on transient-world attachment; one aspect continues in the next verse. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:21/q25/p2 It balances the afterlife promise with a warning about the world's transience. — Al-Rāzī says afterlife hope precedes worldly aversion because it is primary; al-Ālūsī prefers this link and calls a bare proof of a maker remote from context. Asad hears an echo of God's unfailing promise in 39:20. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 39:21/q25/p3 Rain's governance supports the promised rivers beneath the chambers in 39:20. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative but prefers the transience reading. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:21/q25/p4 Its imagery recalls other water and mortality passages. — Study Quran links 6:99, 18:45, 57:20 and 28:88; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī invoke 23:18 on water settled in earth; Muqātil cites 27:18 for perishing. These are comparisons, not competing claims. [6 notes: STUDYQURAN; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; MUQATIL]

## 39:21/q26 What ancillary reports and lexical notes accompany the discussion?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate incidental reports, not competing versions of one event.
- 39:21/q26/p1 Companions wept and shivered at the Qur'an; Asmāʾ rejected listeners' fainting. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Asmāʾ's refuge-seeking on hearing of fainting. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أسماء)]
- 39:21/q26/p2 ʿAlī said he judged by the Qur'an alone when accused of unbelief and hypocrisy. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits his reply that he judged no created person. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(علي)]
- 39:21/q26/p3 A manuscript fragment vocalizes 'lā tablā', but its antecedent is missing. — Al-Bursevī mentions damma and fatḥa on the imperfect prefix and lām. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 39:21/q26/p4 A lexical excursus distinguishes bodily illness from metaphorical illness of hearts. — Ibn Fāris roots marad in departure from health; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says legal usages mean bodily illness and heart illness extends the sense. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن فارس)]
