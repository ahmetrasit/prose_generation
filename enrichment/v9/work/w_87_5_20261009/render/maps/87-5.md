# 87:5: 16 questions

## 87:5/q01 What does غُثَآءً mean?  [غُثَآءً · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names the final dried refuse or vigorous vegetation.
- 87:5/q01/p1 Dried, broken plant matter and mixed refuse, especially debris swept by floods to a valley edge. — Mujāhid compares it to flood debris; al-Mubarrad describes broken vegetation left at a valley edge; al-Suddī and Ibn Zayd stress flood transport. Al-Ṭabarī adds windblown remains. Al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı cite use for mixed scraps or mixed crowds; al-Qurṭubī also mentions floating foam and refuse. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah notes its figurative use for worthless people. Al-Biqāʿī describes abundant matter drying, breaking and collecting as froth. [68 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(السدي,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي,مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي، مقاتل,المبرد); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI(قطرب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR(امرؤ القيس); IBNKATHIR(ابن عباس,مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI(المجمع); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Âlûsî); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM(arabische Lexikographen); STUDYQURAN]
- 87:5/q01/p2 Luxuriant, fertile, densely layered vegetation. — Iṣlāḥī cites Qaṭṭāmī's valley couplet through al-Farāhī as evidence for dense undergrowth and rejects the usual meaning of decayed litter in this context. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 87:5/q02 What forms of غُثَآءً are attested?  [غُثَآءً · grammar]
- 87:5/q02/p1 The thāʾ may be light or doubled. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr attest both pronunciations. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; IBNASHUR]
- 87:5/q02/p2 Ghuthāʾah is given as a singular form. — Al-Rāzī transmits this from Quṭrub. [2 notes: RAZI(قطرب); RAZI-FULL]
- 87:5/q02/p3 Aghthāʾ or ighthāʾ is reported as a plural. — Al-Qurṭubī gives aghthāʾ. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn transmit al-Farrāʾ's form and call its afʿāl pattern unusual for a fiʿāl/faʿāl noun; al-Ālūsī agrees. [5 notes: QURTUBI; ABUHAYYAN(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:5/q04 What shade does أَحْوَىٰ denote?  [أَحْوَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the relevant darkness is a faded plant's color, luxuriant greenness, or another dark hue.
- 87:5/q04/p1 Black, or a dark shade tending to black. — Al-Jishumī and others derive it from ḥawah, blackness; al-Kisāʾī cites ḥawiya al-nabāt and rain-grown dark valleys. Dhū al-Rumma's dark lips and gums, al-Aʿshā's lip, a gazelle's striped back, and a dark-backed fawn are poetic examples. Al-Aʿlam says it tends to black; al-Qazzāz traces broader black usage from a horse-coat term. - 87:5/q04/p2 Intense green that looks almost black, the color of lush and well-watered growth. — Al-Farrāʾ, Abū ʿUbaydah and al-Zajjāj connect this to fresh growth; 55:64 calls two gardens dark green. Dhū al-Rumma's line illustrates the dark hue. Iṣlāḥī adduces Taʾabbaṭa Sharran's praise of a healthy youth and rejects decay-blackness. Al-Qurṭubī also describes a camel with green, black and yellow; Elmalılı includes sooty dark bay, and Corpus Coranicum notes lexicographers disagree about the exact hue. ## 87:5/q05 What does أَحْوَىٰ modify, and at what stage?  [أَحْوَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether dark color belongs to the final debris or the earlier pasture, and whether syntactic reordering is required.
- 87:5/q05/p1 It qualifies ghuthāʾ: the pasture becomes dark, dry refuse. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās prefer the direct order because inversion is needless when it makes sound sense. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call this the apparent syntax; Ibn Jarīr also cites transmitted exegetical explanations against inversion. Al-Samīn rejects Abū al-Baqāʾ's analysis as fronting part of a relative clause. Al-Ṭabrisī also describes a circumstantial parse for blackened pasture after drying. [41 notes: FARRA; TAB(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء، الكلبي، مقاتل); BAGHAWI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BURHAN(أحد القولين); ITQAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN+; NAHHAS+; SAMIN-DURR+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 87:5/q05/p2 It is circumstantial of the pasture as it emerges dark green, before becoming debris. — Al-Zajjāj, Qatādah, al-Farrāʾ, Abū ʿUbaydah and other grammarians give this parse. Al-Rāzī compares 18:1 for the order. Al-Ālūsī says the intervening coordinated clause does not break the circumstantial link, though he calls it less apparent. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās object that reordering is unnecessary; Ibn Jarīr invokes the early exegetes against it. [41 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة، الفراء، المبرد، الزجاج، أبو علي); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; RAZI(الفراء وأبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR(بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض أهل العلم بكلام العرب); BURHAN(الثاني); ITQAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TAB-; TAB-FULL-; NAHHAS-; IBNKATHIR(ابن جرير)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)-]
- 87:5/q05/p3 It qualifies ghuthāʾ as luxuriant blackish green vegetation. — Iṣlāḥī combines adjective syntax with the fresh, fertile lexical sense, adducing the two jāhilī poems and rejecting the decay-based construction. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ta’bbata Sharraṇ); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 87:5/q05/p4 It is circumstantial of pasture after drying, describing its blackened state. — Al-Ṭabrisī explicitly prefers this parse and also permits an adjective of ghuthāʾ. [1 notes: TABRISI+]

## 87:5/q06 Why is أَحْوَىٰ placed after غُثَآءً?  [غُثَآءً أَحْوَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give different rhetorical reasons for the same order.
- 87:5/q06/p1 Its delayed place suits the verse endings or rhyme. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burhān and al-Itqān give this reason for separating the dark-green description from pasture; al-Burhān also describes dark-green chaff. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 87:5/q06/p2 Its delayed place keeps both green life and dark death available in the wording. — Al-Biqāʿī says putting the color second avoids locking it to green alone and lets the two terms evoke both life and death. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:5/q07 What transition does فَجَعَلَهُۥ convey?  [فَجَعَلَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle conveys rapid succession or bare coordination.
- 87:5/q07/p1 The pasture's flourishing rapidly gives way to dry darkness. — Al-Ālūsī says the green state seems barely complete before debris follows, though that ordering is contrary to apparent syntax. Al-Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr see the brief span of worldly pleasure, comparing 10:24. Al-Ālūsī also observes a gradual nurturing through successive stages. [7 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:5/q07/p2 Fa may simply coordinate the successive actions. — Al-Burhān gives the coordination analysis for making vegetation dry and dark. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 87:5/q09 What makes the dried vegetation dark?  [أَحْوَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes propose natural drying, cold, deposited mud or dust, and later rain.
- 87:5/q09/p1 Severe drying, age, heat or burning darkens it. — Al-Farrāʾ describes darkening through age, Abū ʿUbaydah through burning and age, and al-Bursawī says either heat or cold can dry the pasture. Al-Biqāʿī allows severe drying. [9 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 87:5/q09/p2 Cold dries grass and thereby blackens dry matter. — Al-Rāzī says cold whitens wet things but blackens dry ones. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:5/q09/p3 Floodwater deposits muddy particles on it. — Al-Rāzī gives mud sticking to the refuse as a cause. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:5/q09/p4 Wind deposits abundant dust on it. — Al-Rāzī gives windblown dust as another cause. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:5/q09/p5 Rain makes old flood debris black and rotten. — Ibn ʿAbbās says old chaff or flood debris darkens and rots when rain reaches it. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 87:5/q10 What does the plant's change teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Different authors draw lessons about power, mortality, resurrection, warning, guidance, growth and spiritual attention.
- 87:5/q10/p1 It displays God's power, governance and free choice over life, death and opposing states. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts divine freedom with necessary natural effects, using fire as his example. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees God's power to create and end; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the stages governance. Asad calls decay a metonym for life and death. Corpus Coranicum reads the perfects aspectually, seeing recurring growth and decay as ongoing sustenance, comparing 53:43–49, 96:1–5 and 95:4–5. [10 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:5/q10/p2 Worldly freshness and pleasure are fleeting, and living things end. — Qatādah and al-Qurṭubī compare prosperity fading; al-Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the quick sequence, with 10:24's vanished harvest. Qutb links every living thing's end to the enduring afterlife, 87:16–17. Ibn ʿĀshūr also compares human weakness, strength and weakness in 30:54. [12 notes: MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:5/q10/p3 Recurring plant death and renewal recall resurrection. — Al-Biqāʿī says cultivated plants crumble to soil and return with water, like the dead restored; he explains why pasture is especially apt. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:5/q10/p4 The image warns disbelievers and those absorbed in bodily pleasure. — Qatādah likens unbelievers to dead growth; Elmalılı says schemers against truth and those living for bodily appetite face a worse fate as fuel for the great Fire, citing 47:12. [5 notes: MAWARDI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI; ELMALILI]
- 87:5/q10/p5 Pasture pictures revelation's guidance and law reaching maturity. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares law benefiting people to pasture benefiting animals. He cites the Prophetic rain report: receptive earth produces pasture, while water-retaining earth benefits people otherwise. [2 notes: IBNASHUR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 87:5/q10/p6 Created growth develops gradually under divine care. — Al-Ālūsī says the successive descriptions convey nurturing to completion; Iṣlāḥī includes verses 1–5 among signs that human physical and intellectual abilities mature gradually. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:5/q10/p7 Inwardly, worldly pasture must not distract from enduring perfection and special glorification. — Al-Qāshānī warns that occupation with perishing worldly pasture can conceal one's destined perfection. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 87:5/q10/p8 Both green and dry vegetation can feed creatures according to God's measure. — Qutb says each state has its place and purpose in life. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:5/q11 Where and how do coal deposits form in the geological reading?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The mechanism of accumulation: transport from afar or growth and deposition at the site.
- 87:5/q11/p1 Plants accumulated and decayed where they grew in marshy land hollows. — Elmalılı prefers local accumulation: deep transported floating masses are implausible. Rooted trees, fine leaf impressions, aquatic spores, separate basins and parallel seams indicate quiet marsh deposits. He reports that long times suffice and cites forest growth estimates; ancient carbon-rich air may have accelerated growth. The cited account describes repeated submergence, slight rot and fermentation, mineral gases and oils, then pressure and earth heat hardening lower seams; heated wood pressed in wet clay reportedly yielded coal-like material. [14 notes: ELMALILI(Keşfü’l-Esrâri’n-Nûrâniyyeti’l-Kur’âniyye); ELMALILI+; ELMALILI-]
- 87:5/q11/p2 Rivers or ancient sea waves transported masses of plants that were buried as coal seams. — Elmalılı reports this theory but rejects it: thick seams would require floating plant layers too deep for rivers or seas to carry. [1 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]

## 87:5/q12 What ancient setting and timescale does the coal account describe?  [* · reports]
- 87:5/q12/p1 A very long sequence of luxuriant ancient growth, submergence, decay and burial produced coal. — The cited Keşfü’l-Esrâr account imagines an initially flat water-covered earth, large early algae and sulfur-family plants, pre-animal coal flora and mountains burying plants. It invokes hot humid fog, rain and dim light, giant fern forests, and fossils across latitudes; reports roughly 250 fern and 120 gymnosperm species in European coal beds against about 50 and 25 living species. It proposes a thin elastic crust over fluid, lunar and solar tides and subsidence repeatedly submerging forests. Elmalılı compares the long timescale with shell-built limestone and cites preserved stems, leaves and trunks as evidence of plant origin. [14 notes: ELMALILI(Keşfü’l-Esrâri’n-Nûrâniyyeti’l-Kur’âniyye)]

## 87:5/q13 How does this verse fit its surrounding sūrah?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize divine governance, the surah's cosmic and afterlife themes, and a shift in audience.
- 87:5/q13/p1 It completes a display of creation and divine governance that grounds glorification. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the first four verses cover composed creation and establish absolute lordship; pasture's change exemplifies governance. Iṣlāḥī says 87:1–5 direct the Prophet to glorify the God whose attributes show gradual maturation. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:5/q13/p2 Its cosmic scene prepares the sūrah's ensuing message and enduring afterlife. — Qutb describes the whole cosmos as praising, with reassurance to the Prophet and themes of oneness, revelation and recompense, rooted in 87:18–19. He links withering to 87:16–17 and says the cosmic frame suits neighboring sūrahs. [4 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:5/q13/p3 The sūrah turns from persistent deniers toward reassuring the Prophet about preaching. — Iṣlāḥī says the previous sūrah allowed only temporary respite for deniers, while this and at least ten following sūrahs address the Prophet's fears. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:5/q14 What background and recitation reports are given for this sūrah?  [* · sciences]
- 87:5/q14/p1 It is Meccan, has nineteen verses, and was loved and recited by the Prophet. — Qutb cites ʿAlī for the Prophet's love of the sūrah and Muslim for his pairing it with al-Ghāshiyah at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes both on one day. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:5/q16 What glorification is reported for bowing?  [* · reports]
- 87:5/q16/p1 The Prophet instructed worshippers to put glorification of the Great in bowing. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports from al-Qummī a narration of ʿUqbah ibn ʿĀmir. [1 notes: TABATABAI(القمي)]
