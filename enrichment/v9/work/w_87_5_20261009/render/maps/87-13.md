# 87:13: 10 questions

## 87:13/q01 What does “then” add to the threat?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 87:13/q01/p1 It raises the threat to a worse degree: enduring between death and life is more terrible than entering and burning in the Fire. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr call this a progression in severity. Al-Samīn says it is rank rather than ordinary time. Al-Biqāʿī stresses the great interval or fluctuating degrees of torment, whose full severity God alone knows; al-Ālūsī says permanence there is worse than entering it. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that burning remains fully felt because death gives no relief. [18 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:13/q02 How does this clause connect grammatically to the preceding verse?  [ثُمَّ لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ · grammar]
- 87:13/q02/p1 It is coordinated with “he enters the greatest Fire” as a second description of the one punished there. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the earlier clause at 87:12. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]

## 87:13/q03 What condition do the denials of death and life describe?  [لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the denials describe continuing sensation without benefit, an Arabic distress idiom, a soul's physical state, figurative lack of escape, or inward spiritual states; Ibn ʿĀshūr disputes an inferred intermediate state and al-Ālūsī challenges the throat and deliverance readings.
- 87:13/q03/p1 The condemned remain alive and conscious of torment, unable to die for relief or to enjoy a beneficial, pleasant life. — Muqātil adds repeated burning and restoration, fire for clothing, flesh for food, and no mercy, satiety, or relief (69:36). Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite a poem of a soul unable to end torment or savor life. Al-Rāzī cites 35:36 and Arab usage for severe misery. Ibn Kathīr says continued consciousness allows pain, citing 43:77 and 35:36. Al-Biqāʿī relates lack of useful life to failure to purify oneself, believe, and pray. Al-Itqān says a thing may be denied when its defining benefit is absent. Al-Ālūsī stresses enduring hardship. Elmalılı calls it unsurpassable misery; Quṭb says they wish for death; Ibn ʿĀshūr says sensation never ceases. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says neither death nor blessed life ends punishment; Kur'an Yolu says persistence without positive life conveys eternal horror. [50 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; TAB(قيل); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BURHAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:13/q03/p2 The soul is caught at the throat, leaving neither to bring death nor returning to the body to restore life. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, Ibn al-Jawzī citing Ibn Jarīr, al-Rāzī, and al-Ālūsī transmit the account. Al-Ālūsī objects that it needs an unsupported qualification, such as complete life. [7 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:13/q03/p3 The phrase is an Arabic idiom for a person in extreme distress, who is called neither alive nor dead. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās give the idiom; al-Rāzī combines it with the lack of relief and cites 35:36. Study Quran calls it an idiom of great trials and cites 20:74, 74:28–29, and 35:36. Elmalılı reports the metaphor of enduring misery. [7 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(bazıları); STUDYQURAN]
- 87:13/q03/p4 They do not experience the breath or vitality of life. — Ibn ʿĪsā is reported by al-Māwardī; al-Ṭabrisī reports the same view. Al-Wāḥidī describes absence of life's spirit or vitality alongside the lack of relieving death. [5 notes: MAWARDI(ابن عيسى); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); TABRISI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 87:13/q03/p5 The wording describes a terrible state suspended between life and death. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī use this description to mark greater severity. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects construing the paired negations as a conventional assertion of an intermediate state, comparing 24:35 and sayings about heat and cold. [8 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 87:13/q03/p6 Figuratively, they cannot be saved from punishment, since deliverance by deeds belongs to earthly life where one dies and lives. — ʿIṣām al-Dīn proposes this and argues that a literal sense would more aptly use “neither dead nor alive.” Al-Ālūsī rejects that objection: the existing verbal wording fits the literal sense and preserves the verse endings; he finds the figurative rationale unclear. [5 notes: ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:13/q03/p7 Spiritually, there is no death ending the grief of separation and no life bringing the comfort of union. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ supplies this inward reading, reported by al-Sulamī, al-Thaʿlabī, and al-Bursawī. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]
- 87:13/q03/p8 The self cannot be annihilated to end the veil's punishment, and the heart cannot live through faith in the abode of recompense. — Al-Bursawī reports the Najmiyya reading. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النجمي)]
- 87:13/q03/p9 The spirit persists despite spiritual ruin and is repeatedly restored after burning, so it is neither absolutely dead nor truly alive. — Al-Qāshānī says the damned wish for death yet cannot cease to exist; al-Bursawī transmits this reading and adds that bodily and spiritual torment persist. For al-Bursawī, true human life follows death to the self, unlike animal life. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 87:13/q04 Why deny life after denying death?  [وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the added denial mainly excludes pleasant survival, implies no escape, or is explained as verbal antithesis.
- 87:13/q04/p1 It prevents survival in the Fire from sounding like comfortable life or an honor; their life has no benefit or relief from pain. — Al-Biqāʿī says immortality could otherwise sound like an extraordinary honor, while failure to purify, believe, and pray explains absence of beneficial life. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the second denial prevents imagining a mild, painless, nonfatal burning. Al-Itqān describes denying a thing when its defining benefit is absent; al-Burhān applies this to wholesome life. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN; BURHAN]
- 87:13/q04/p2 Denying life can also mean denying escape from the Fire. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this metonym: fire normally causes death, while life entails not dying. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says both death and a transition to blessed life would end punishment. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 87:13/q04/p3 The paired words form an antithesis. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the opposition of dying and living and rejects treating it merely as a standard double denial that establishes a middle quality, comparing 24:35. [3 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:13/q05 Whom does the verse's denial of death apply to?  [لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the warning is delimited by permanence in Hell, by the highest degree of wretchedness, or by the preceding description of those who reject the reminder.
- 87:13/q05/p1 It applies to permanent inhabitants of Hell, especially disbelievers; sinful believers who enter the Fire can die and later leave through intercession. — Abū Saʿīd's Prophetic report in Muslim draws the distinction; al-Farrāʾ calls the believers' place a lesser fire. Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, and Elmalılı cite the report. Elmalılı relates disbelievers' continuing punishment after resurrection to the two deaths and two lives of 40:11. [15 notes: QURTUBI(أبو سعيد الخدري); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(أبو سعيد عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); ELMALILI(Ebû Sa‘îd)]
- 87:13/q05/p2 The threat concerns the most wretched; others are wretched at lesser degrees. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this distinction as a way of limiting the severe warning. [2 notes: QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:13/q05/p3 It describes the wretched person who disregards the reminder and enters the great Fire. — Ibn Kathīr and Quṭb connect it to avoidance of admonition; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wretched prefer worldly to eternal life. Iṣlāḥī says their refusal comes from their own condition, not a failure of preacher or message, and they fear no afterlife. [8 notes: IBNKATHIR; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 87:13/q06 What do the reports say happens to sinful monotheists in the Fire and after intercession?  [لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا وَلَا يَحْيَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in how they describe the rescued people's revival and where it occurs; the Abū Saʿīd versions specify charcoal, intercession, and Paradise's rivers, while Abū Hurayra describes growth in the open and souls re-entering bodies.
- 87:13/q06/p1 They are burned and made dead like charcoal; intercessors bring them out, and they grow again by Paradise's rivers like flood-carried seed. — Abū Saʿīd transmits the Prophet's distinction from permanent inhabitants. Versions say God first mercies them, that they emerge in groups, and that water or the river of life restores them; one compares revival to a tree changing color. Aḥmad and Muslim transmit versions, including Aḥmad's route through al-Jarīrī; Ibn Kathīr notes Muslim's routes through Bishr ibn al-Mufaḍḍal and Shuʿba. Al-Qurṭubī also cites Muslim and al-Farrāʾ's “lesser fire.” [17 notes: QURTUBI(أبو سعيد الخدري); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(أبو سعيد الخدري عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,أبو سعيد عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد,أبو سعيد الخدري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); ELMALILI(Ebû Sa‘îd)]
- 87:13/q06/p2 People removed from Hell because they never associated anything with God grow like plants in the open and regain their souls. — Al-Ālūsī cites al-Bazzār's report raised from Abū Hurayra; it says these become the lowest Garden inhabitants and then ask God to turn their faces away from Hell. [3 notes: ALUSI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 87:13/q07 How is the reported death of sinful believers in Hell understood and timed?  [لَا يَمُوتُ فِيهَا · theology]
turns on: Whether the report's death separates soul and body, signifies torpor or sleep, when death occurs, and whether punishment can persist during death.
- 87:13/q07/p1 They truly die after suffering punishment proportionate to their sins, with souls leaving their bodies, and remain dead until intercession; delayed Paradise and confinement continue their penalty. — Ibn Rajab takes the Prophet's emphatic verbal noun as evidence for literal death. Al-Ālūsī prefers this account, likening it to beating followed by imprisonment; their confinement is not an unpunished life. He also says death may finish punishment or spare them the sight of the Fire. Al-Bazzār's Abū Hurayra report of souls returning supports real separation. [9 notes: ALUSI(ابن رجب,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن رجب); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 87:13/q07/p2 Their death is figurative, like torpor, stillness, or lifelessness; they remain alive in the Fire. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the claim that sinners are dead but interprets it metaphorically to retain the verse's denial of death. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNATIYYA+]
- 87:13/q07/p3 They may die as soon as they enter Hell; deprivation of Paradise during confinement itself punishes them. — Al-Qurṭubī offers this possibility, reported by al-Ālūsī. [2 notes: ALUSI(القرطبي); ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي)]
- 87:13/q07/p4 They may still feel pain while dead, though less than if alive. — Al-Qurṭubī compares this to an unbeliever's state after death and before resurrection. Al-Ālūsī treats the proposal skeptically. [2 notes: ALUSI(القرطبي); ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي)]
- 87:13/q07/p5 The reported death may mean sleep, a suspension of sensation, before they awake to torment when released. — Maṭāmiḥ al-Afhām invokes Qurʾānic usage that calls sleep death and another Prophetic report saying monotheists are killed in Hell and tormented as exit approaches; al-Ālūsī ultimately prefers literal death. [2 notes: ALUSI(مطامح الأفهام); ALUSI-FULL(مطامح الأفهام)]

## 87:13/q08 Which other Qurʾānic passages illuminate this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited passages serve different connections: repeated descriptions of Hell, a reckoning of deaths and lives, or continuation of the warning in the next sūrah.
- 87:13/q08/p1 Other passages repeat or support the denial of death and lasting punishment. — Ibn Kathīr cites 43:77, where Hell's people ask Mālik for death and are told they will remain, and 35:36, where they are neither killed nor relieved. Al-Rāzī also cites 35:36. Corpus Coranicum calls it a Qurʾānic topos, identifying the exact wording at 20:74 and related formulations at 35:36, 43:77, and 69:27; Study Quran adds 74:28–29. Asad relates 74:28–29 to the condemned's avoidance of reminders. [10 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 87:13/q08/p2 The two deaths and two lives of 40:11 explain why resurrected disbelievers have no further death or life in Hell. — Elmalılı applies 40:11 after resurrection for punishment. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 87:13/q08/p3 The succeeding sūrah al-Ghāshiyah develops the same great punishment. — Iṣlāḥī identifies the great torment of 88:21–26 with this passage's great Fire. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:13/q09 How does this warning fit the sūrah's surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace the warning's role at different scales: the immediate contrast of responses, the stanza's shift to public proclamation, or the sūrah's larger themes.
- 87:13/q09/p1 It is the fate of those who evade the Prophet's reminder, contrasted with the good who heed it. — Quṭb says the Prophet reminds and responses diverge. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wretched favor worldly life. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet should remind without forcing the obdurate, whose rejection reflects their own condition; evaders deny the Hereafter and wish for death in vain. Corpus Coranicum says verses 10–11 contrast responses, expanded by the warning in 12–13 and the good's fate in 14–15; the passage forms a chiasm like Q 92:5–11 and 14–21. Asad connects the fate to keeping away from divine reminders in 74:28–29. Elmalılı compares their fate to pasture reduced to black refuse. [9 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM; ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI]
- 87:13/q09/p2 The second stanza turns the Prophet's received revelation into a public admonition. — Corpus Coranicum says the Prophet first praises God, perhaps with close followers, then addresses a wider audience; this makes verse 6's revelation public in purpose. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:13/q09/p3 The sūrah frames the warning within God's oneness, revelation, recompense, and the Prophet's divinely eased mission. — Quṭb says creation becomes a sanctuary of praise, God preserves and eases the Prophet's mission, and these themes are rooted in earlier scriptures at 87:18–19. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 87:13/q10 What background about the sūrah is supplied with this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 87:13/q10/p1 Al-Aʿlā is Meccan and has nineteen verses; the Prophet loved and recited it with al-Ghāshiyah at Eid and Friday prayers. — Quṭb notes that the two sūrahs were sometimes recited on the same day. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
