# 87:16: 13 questions

## 87:16/q01 How does بل redirect the discourse here?  [بَلْ · links]
turns on: Whether bal cancels an implied conclusion or moves to a new explanation or address, and how the second- or third-person reading relates to the preceding verses.
- 87:16/q01/p1 It overturns the implied expectation that people will pursue the purification and success just described. — Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr connect it to 87:14: instead of following the path to success, people prefer the world. Elmalılı similarly contrasts worldly indulgence with purification, charity, remembrance, and prayer. [5 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; ELMALILI]
- 87:16/q01/p2 It moves from the contrast between those who heed or avoid the reminder to why the rebellious avoid it. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a clause after bal may change topic without cancelling the previous claim; this transition is especially clear with Abū ʿAmr's third-person reading. He cites 23:62–63 for such non-cancelling movement. [4 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 87:16/q01/p3 It turns from narration to direct rebuke, renewing the hearer's attention. — Ibn ʿĀshūr ties this to the majority second-person reading. Biqāʿī says the shift signals extreme anger. Al-Ālūsī says it heightens rebuke of unbelievers, and also admonishes Muslims if the address is general. Corpus Coranicum calls the turn abrupt and effective, confronting hearers with a choice and its consequences. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; BIQAI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:16/q01/p4 In general, بل redirects discourse; between clauses it may cancel the previous assertion or merely begin another report. — Al-Burhān distinguishes sentence-to-sentence transition from cancellation after an affirmative followed by a single word; al-Mubarrad also allows transfer after a negative or prohibition. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the two clause uses by context, citing 23:70 for cancellation and 23:62–63 for a simple move. [7 notes: BURHAN(المبرد); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:16/q02 How is the lām of بل pronounced before تُؤْثِرُونَ?  [بَلْ تُؤْثِرُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the adjacent lām and tāʾ are assimilated or kept distinct.
- 87:16/q02/p1 The lām is assimilated into the following tāʾ. — Sībawayh records the resulting batūthirūna. Al-Naḥḥās permits the assimilation; Ibn Khālawayh acknowledges assimilated articulation. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH; NAHHAS; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]
- 87:16/q02/p2 The lām is pronounced distinctly. — Ibn Khālawayh acknowledges clear as well as assimilated articulation, with arguments given earlier. [1 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 87:16/q04 What expanded readings of this clause are reported?  [تُؤْثِرُونَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا · readings]
turns on: The reported wording of the expansions and which reciter's wording is being cited.
- 87:16/q04/p1 Ubayy's wording explicitly adds 'you' (antum). — Al-Naḥḥās calls it explanatory rather than a competing recitation; many sources use it as evidence for second-person address. Al-Baghawī notes that al-ashqā is singular in form but generic and plural in reference. [17 notes: FARRA(أبي); TAB(أبيّ); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI(الآخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبي بن كعب); IBNATIYYA(الباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء والزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو ونصر بن عاصم)]
- 87:16/q04/p2 Ibn Masʿūd's wording explicitly adds 'over the Hereafter.' — Al-Durr and its full text transmit this expansion as an explicit contrast. [3 notes: DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 87:16/q04/p3 Ibn Masʿūd is also reported to read an explicit second-person 'you.' — Al-Kashshāf cites his expanded address in support of the direct-address reading, whereas other reports from Ibn Masʿūd give a third-person verb. [2 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 87:16/q05 What does تُؤْثِرُونَ mean?  [تُؤْثِرُونَ · meaning]
- 87:16/q05/p1 It means choosing or giving priority to one thing over alternatives. — Al-Biqāʿī glosses it as choosing and privileging; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines īthār as selection from several things and here as giving the world attention and concern. [2 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR]

## 87:16/q06 Whom does the rebuke address?  [تُؤْثِرُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the second-person speech is directed narrowly at disbelievers, more widely at human beings or Muslims, or takes its subject from the preceding 'most wretched'; some supply an implicit 'say'.
- 87:16/q06/p1 Unbelievers, especially the polytheists or Meccan disbelievers in context. — Al-Jishumī reads it after commands to obey; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says disbelievers deny an afterlife. Al-Ālūsī cites 10:7 for their satisfaction with this world. Elmalılı cites 10:7–8, 2:200 and 87:12–13. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they refuse reminders about the hereafter. Al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān identify unbelievers, and Iṣlāḥī singles out Quraysh's worldly interests. [20 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI(أبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL(أبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 87:16/q06/p2 Humanity generally, including believers and unbelievers. — Ikrima applies it to this community. Al-Ṭabrisī calls the preference a usual human pattern; Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes unbelief from believers' sin and desire. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī cite the predominance of worldly striving. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls attachment to worldly cultivation natural. Ibn ʿĀshūr says believers share the warning in proportion to neglect of salvation. [17 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(others); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:16/q06/p3 Muslims, whose worldly accumulation competes with the reward promised them. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī distinguish this from the unbeliever-directed interpretation. Al-Qurṭubī applies the common second-person reading to Muslims; Abū Ḥayyān's wider view says the righteous seek reward while the wicked act from desire. [6 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(قيل)]
- 87:16/q06/p4 The most wretched people just described. — This is the grammatical referent of the third-person reading according to al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr. Al-Bayḍāwī also allows the second-person address to turn toward the wicked. [14 notes: TAB(أبو عمرو); NAHHAS(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); IBNATIYYA(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو); RAZI(أبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI(أبو عمرو ونصر بن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو ونصر بن عاصم); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:16/q06/p5 The apparent address is an unspoken command to the Prophet to 'say' this to the audience. — Al-Bayḍāwī presents this as a possible construction; al-Ālūsī says it avoids a shift of address. [4 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:16/q07 What is preferred over what in this verse?  [تُؤْثِرُونَ ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: The omitted comparison can be stated as the Hereafter, its deeds and reward, God's rights, or spiritual purification; the sources develop different aspects of the same worldly priority.
- 87:16/q07/p1 People favor this present life over the Hereafter. — Al-Ṭabarī and Qatāda pair this with 87:17: the Hereafter is better in goodness and longer-lasting. Al-Qurṭubī's Abū Mūsā says seeing it would end wavering. Kur'an Yolu cites 7:169, 12:109 and 93:4. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the comparison is unstated because the world implies its alternative. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:16/q07/p2 People favor worldly actions and pursuits over deeds for the Hereafter and the means of success. — Al-Wāḥidī transmits al-Kalbī's contrast of actions; al-Rāzī contrasts worldly and afterlife work. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī say this neglects what brings success, happiness, or salvation. Al-Ṭabrisī says people work and build for this world. Elmalılı contrasts amusement and spending with purification, charity, remembrance and prayer. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:16/q07/p3 Worldly abundance is favored over an abundance of reward. — Al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī give this as the Muslim-directed sense. [5 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:16/q07/p4 Personal worldly interests are put before God's rights. — Al-Qushayrī frames the priority as one's own share against God's due; al-Sulamī reports al-Dīnawarī's higher ideal of preferring the Lord above both worlds. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري)]
- 87:16/q07/p5 Sensory life and its ornaments are preferred to spiritual purification and divine remembrance. — Al-Qāshānī says lack of purification leaves people neglecting remembrance and prayer; true spiritual life is better and enduring. Biqāʿī describes people absorbed like animals in appetite, sex and wealth. [4 notes: QASHANI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:16/q08 Why do people choose ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا?  [ٱلْحَيَوٰةَ ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: The sources point to immediacy and sense perception, denial of the next life, or desire and differing spiritual aspiration.
- 87:16/q08/p1 Its pleasures are immediate and visible, while the Hereafter is unseen and deferred. — Ibn Masʿūd names adornment, food, drink, women and pleasure; al-Ṭabarī and later transmitters report his explanation. Abū Mūsā says sight of the Hereafter would remove hesitation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds love of what is near and ignorance of what lasts. Biqāʿī calls sense-bound creatures captivated by immediacy. [19 notes: TAB(ابن مسعود); TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); RAZI(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI(أبو موسى,ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو موسى,ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(عرفجة الثقفي)]
- 87:16/q08/p2 Disbelievers deny or disregard the Hereafter and settle contentedly into this life. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to disbelief; al-Ālūsī cites 10:7, and Elmalılı cites 10:7–8 and 2:200. Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists dismiss reminders of a life beyond this one. Iṣlāḥī says Quraysh invent doubts to protect worldly interests. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:16/q08/p3 Believers may yield to sinful desire or neglect salvation when worldly interests conflict. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya exempts those God preserves. Elmalılı cites 2:201–202: believers can ask good in both lives yet sacrifice the next when interests collide. Ibn ʿĀshūr grades the warning by actual neglect of salvation. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR]
- 87:16/q08/p4 Low aspiration chooses the world's lowness; higher aspiration chooses the Hereafter, and the noblest state chooses the Lord above both. — Al-Sulamī transmits Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Dīnawarī's three levels of ambition. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري)]

## 87:16/q09 What does ٱلدُّنْيَا convey about this life?  [ٱلدُّنْيَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the description emphasizes nearness and lowness or the sensory life it denotes.
- 87:16/q09/p1 It is near or present, low and transient. — Al-Biqāʿī describes the low, present and perishable world. Quṭb says dunyā is named for nearness and lowness, baseness and transience. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:16/q09/p2 It denotes sensory life and its ornaments. — Al-Qāshānī contrasts sensory life with true spiritual life. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 87:16/q10 What conduct does the verse commend or condemn?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether worldly pursuit actually displaces salvation, and whether the point is renunciation, moral correction, or a conditional warning.
- 87:16/q10/p1 Worldly attachment diverts people from remembrance, purification and the deeds that bring success. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī connect preference with neglect of success. Biqāʿī says appetite and wealth consume time and lead to evil deeds. Quṭb calls worldly preference the root of neglect and urges accounting for the afterlife. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:16/q10/p2 Worldly benefits are permissible when pursuit of them does not neglect the means of salvation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 28:77 and says blame tracks neglect of one's afterlife responsibilities under the law. [1 notes: IBNASHUR]
- 87:16/q10/p3 The believer should renounce worldly dependence and seek God. — Al-Tustarī likens the believer to a person on a log at sea pleading for rescue; he says God sends a wise angel to plant wisdom in the heart of a renouncer. Al-Dīnawarī ranks choosing the Lord above both worlds as the noblest state. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو العباس الدينوري)]
- 87:16/q10/p4 The reproach is also a stage in the sūra's moral treatment, purging vices and urging lasting good. — Biqāʿī calls it a fourth treatment and says the pairing deters from the base world while attracting to the enduring Hereafter. Elmalılı contrasts festival pleasures with charity and prayer. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]

Ibn Kathīr gives the route through ʿArfaja al-Thaqafī and says Ibn Masʿūd's self-inclusion could be humility and self-reproach, or reference to humankind generally. - 87:16/q11/p8 Love of either world harms the other, so one should choose the enduring Hereafter. — Abū Mūsā reports this prophetic counsel. [3 notes: DURR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:16/q12 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on the immediate contrast of 87:14–17, the transition to earlier scriptures, or the sūra's broader address and mission.
- 87:16/q12/p1 It contrasts the earlier call to purification and prayer with preference for the world, then the better and lasting Hereafter. — Al-Ṭabarī takes 'this' in 87:18–19 to refer to nearby 87:14–17, including this verse, because the demonstrative points to the nearest passage; ṣuḥuf means Abraham's and Moses' books or scrolls. Biqāʿī describes an implicit contrast: rejecting the Hereafter follows from preferring the world, and 87:17 implies the world's inferiority. Corpus Coranicum points to the vocabulary of 93:4. [4 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:16/q12/p2 The worldly rebuke prepares a warning against abandoning purification outside the prophets' way. — Biqāʿī reads the subsequent development as warning against turning from purification by a path other than the prophets' method. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:16/q12/p3 The sūra shifts from addressing the Messenger to confronting hearers with the core choice and its consequences. — Corpus Coranicum links the abrupt rebuke with 102:1 and says an earlier contrast of good and bad now becomes a direct choice. It argues second-person rebukes and parallel growth of text and community make a presupposed formed community or follower-only devotional meetings unlikely. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 87:16/q12/p4 The sūra frames worldly preference within its themes of revelation, divine oneness and final recompense. — Quṭb calls al-Aʿlā Meccan with nineteen verses, loved and recited by the Prophet at Eid and Friday prayer alongside al-Ghāshiya, sometimes the same day. He describes cosmic praise, promised preservation and ease for the Messenger, earlier scriptures in 87:18–19, and the easy nature of the message and community. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:16/q12/p5 Attachment to transient interests explains why the Prophet's message does not take root among its hearers. — Iṣlāḥī says disbelievers cling to passing life despite prophetic continuity, and Quraysh invent doubts rather than risk worldly interests for eternal reward. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:16/q13 What does the direct address imply about human choice?  [تُؤْثِرُونَ · theology]
- 87:16/q13/p1 Choosing worldly life is an act of the human subjects addressed. — Al-Jishumī argues that the direct 'you prefer' attributes this choice to the people themselves. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
