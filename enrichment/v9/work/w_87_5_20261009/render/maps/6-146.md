# 6:146: 37 questions

## 6:146/q01 To whom and when did the clawed-animal restriction apply?  [وَعَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ هَادُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fronting the phrase restricts the ruling to Jews, and which historical generation first received it.
- 6:146/q01/p1 It was specific to the Jews, rather than a universal food ban. — Al-Rāzī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad cite the fronted prepositional phrase; al-Rāzī says naming Jews would otherwise be pointless. Tabrisī places imposition in Moses' time; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Deuteronomy 14 for camel, hare and hyrax. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:146/q01/p2 The restriction was imposed in Moses' time, during the wilderness period, for early Israelite sins. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes it from the later 4:160 penalties, whose listed offenses occurred after Moses; he mentions discontent with one food, refusal to fight and calf worship. [3 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:146/q01/p3 The restriction followed later Jewish offenses such as killing prophets and taking usury. — Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī and others parallel 4:160; this chronology differs from Ibn ʿĀshūr's Mosaic account. [3 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL]

## 6:146/q02 How is ظُفُرٍ read?  [ظُفُرٍۢ · readings]
turns on: Vowels on the ẓāʾ and fāʾ; some readers and transmitters vary.
- 6:146/q02/p1 The fāʾ is still, with ḍamma on ẓāʾ. — Al-Ḥasan, al-Aʿraj, al-Aʿmash, Ubayy and others are reported; Samīn calls it easing the ḍamma. [7 notes: NAHHAS(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور والحسن والأعرج وأبو السمال قعنب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن، الأعمش); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي الحسن والأعرج والحسن وأبو السمال قعنب); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن، أُبَيّ بن كعب، الأعرج); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 6:146/q02/p2 The ẓāʾ has kasra and the fāʾ is still. — Attributed variously to al-Ḥasan and Abū al-Sammāl; Abū Ḥātim rejects it, whereas al-Qurṭubī calls it a dialect. [6 notes: NAHHAS(أبو السمال); JISHUMI(عامة القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور والحسن والأعرج وأبو السمال قعنب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو السمال); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 6:146/q02/p3 Both consonants have kasra. — Al-Wāḥidī and Samīn attribute it to Abū al-Sammāl, Jishumī to Ibn al-Sammāl, Ālūsī to Abū al-Sammāk; the attributions vary. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(عامة القراء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو السمال); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السماك)]
- 6:146/q02/p4 The common reading has ḍamma on both ẓāʾ and fāʾ. — Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Samīn call it the majority or highest form; all forms are dialectal in Jishumī. [3 notes: JISHUMI(عامة القراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور والحسن والأعرج وأبو السمال قعنب); SAMIN-DURR(الجمهور)]

## 6:146/q03 What does ظُفُرٍ mean as a word?  [ظُفُرٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether hoof is a literal or figurative extension of nail.
- 6:146/q03/p1 It is a nail, claw or terminal digit; its forms and plurals vary. — Jishumī and Tabrisī explain aẓfar for long nails; Jishumī gives the idiom kalīl al-ẓufr. Al-Farrāʾ lists aẓāfīr, aẓāfira, aẓāfir and aẓfār; al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Anbārī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn record ẓufr, aẓfur/aẓfūr and plural variants, supported by anonymous poetry. Samīn distinguishes regular aẓāfīr from irregular aẓāfir. [19 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,شعراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري والنحاس والفراء وابن السكيت); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI(Kāmûs); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 6:146/q03/p2 A hoof can be called a nail figuratively. — Ibn Qutayba cites poets calling a hoof a foot and feet hooves, and al-Ḥuṭayʾa's human lips called camel mushāfir; al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Elmalılı report the analogy. Elmalılı compares broad Turkish tırnak. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الحطيئة,الشاعر الآخر,المفسرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,شاعر); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(القتبي والبلخي); ELMALILI(Kutebî); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 6:146/q03/p3 Hooves and talons are literally varieties of nails, differing by size and use. — Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm says both are soft bone grown from nourishment, can be cut, and serve digging or grasping. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي الحكيم)]

## 6:146/q04 Which animals are meant by كُلَّ ذِى ظُفُرٍ?  [كُلَّ ذِى ظُفُرٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the defining feature is unsplit toes, literal claws, metaphorical hooves, or a narrower named group.
- 6:146/q04/p1 All creatures with unsplit toes or undivided feet, notably camels and ostriches and often geese and ducks. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd, Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Suddī and others transmit this. Ibn Jurayj adds wild ass and contrasts chickens and sparrows; Qatāda also mentions fish in one report. Some wording calls these cloven-hoofed while examples and distinction mean undivided toes. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān reject camel-only as too narrow. Mujāhid's report also names al-warīna. [30 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي وقتادة,حنش الصنعاني,سعيد بن جبير ومجاهد وعكرمة وقتادة والضحاك وعطاء الخراساني ومقاتل بن حيان); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير، مجاهد، قتادة، السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جريج,قتادة); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، وسعيد بن جبير، ومجاهد، وقتادة، والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة ومجاهد والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، ابن جبير، مجاهد، قتادة، السدي); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وابن جبير وقتادة والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جريج عن مجاهد والقاسم بن أبي بزة,ابن جرير,ابن عباس عن علي بن أبي طلحة ومجاهد والسدي,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جرير وقتادة ومجاهد والسدي); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas, Mücâhid, İbn Cübeyr, Katâde ve Süddî); STUDYQURAN(commentators (JJ, Q, Ṭ))]
- 6:146/q04/p2 Every clawed bird and every hoofed beast. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Balkhī and others use nail figuratively for hoof, supported by poetry; al-Rāzī and Ālūsī object that it would wrongly include cattle and sheep. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(المفسرون); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن مسلم); JISHUMI(القتبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(القتيبي); TABRISI(القُتيبي والبلخي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القتبي); ALUSI-FULL(القتبي والبلخي); ELMALILI(Kelbî,Kutebî); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 6:146/q04/p3 Every animal or bird with a digit, broadly including camels, predators and birds. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Bursevī include formerly permitted creatures now barred; al-Biqāʿī emphasizes finger-like claws; Elmalılı favors a broad reading except cattle and sheep and says it excludes fish but includes swine, single-hoofed animals and birds. Ibn ʿĀshūr expressly includes rabbits and hyraxes. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI(Zemahşerî); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI+]
- 6:146/q04/p4 Camels alone. — Attributed to Ibn Zayd, Abū Zayd, and one report from Ibn ʿAbbās; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān object that this is too narrow. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس وابن زيد); JISHUMI(ابن زيد); TABRISI(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); ELMALILI(İbn Zeyd); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد)-]
- 6:146/q04/p5 Predators or hunting animals with claws and talons, including dogs, cats and birds of prey. — Al-Jubbāʾī and al-Rāzī favor literal claws; al-Rāzī objects that including hoofed cattle and sheep conflicts with their separate treatment. Study Quran sees a carnivore ban. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو علي); TABRISI(الجبائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,الجبائي); ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:146/q04/p6 Camels and ostriches specifically. — Al-Zajjāj favors these because ostriches have camel-like nails. Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid reports give the pair, sometimes as examples of the wider unsplit category. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس عن علي بن أبي طلحة ومجاهد والسدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ+]
- 6:146/q04/p7 Non-hunting nail-bearing creatures, distinguished from hunters with claws. — Thaʿlab and lexicographers distinguish ẓufr from mikhlab; al-Naqqāsh objects that lions also have nails. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثعلب); ELMALILI(Kāmûs,Sa‘leb); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش)-; ELMALILI(Nakkāş)-]
- 6:146/q04/p8 The rooster and other creatures with separated toes are included. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr gives a contrary report including a rooster; Ibn Kathīr contrasts it with his unsplit-toe definition. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 6:146/q04/p9 Clawed birds, hoofed quadrupeds, and fanged predators together. — Al-Kalbī gives this three-part animal-kind list. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي); ELMALILI(Kelbî)]
- 6:146/q04/p10 Camels, ostriches, and some birds and fish are included. — One Qatāda report adds fish, though another Qatāda report gives the ordinary unsplit-toe list. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 6:146/q04/p11 Ostriches and wild asses only. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk is reported thus; Abū Ḥayyān rejects the restriction as too narrow. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Dahhâk); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك)-]
- 6:146/q04/p12 Every clawed animal that had previously been lawful to the Jews. — Ālūsī allows this contextual qualification because some clawed animals were already prohibited before the punitive generalization. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:146/q04/p13 The pig is additionally included among animals forbidden by this expression. — Ḥanish al-Ṣanʿānī adds it in Ibn Abī Ḥātim's report. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(حنش الصنعاني)]
- 6:146/q04/p14 Animals with intact hooves, including camels and rabbits, were barred under Jewish law. — Islahi links the term to the Jewish split-hoof and cud-chewing criterion and contrasts Abrahamic permission. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:146/q05 What did the ban on each clawed animal cover?  [حَرَّمْنَا كُلَّ ذِى ظُفُرٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the object is the animal itself or an omitted prey item.
- 6:146/q05/p1 The whole animal, including meat and fat, was forbidden to Jews. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish this from cattle and sheep, whose meat remained lawful. Abū Ḥayyān says the entire animal is the apparent object. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 6:146/q05/p2 Only prey caught by an untrained clawed hunter was forbidden. — Some commentators supply an omitted object; Abū Ḥayyān rejects this as contrary to the apparent wording. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المفسرين)-]

## 6:146/q06 Are clawed predators and birds forbidden to Muslims by this verse?  [وَعَلَى ٱلَّذِينَ هَادُوا۟ حَرَّمْنَا كُلَّ ذِى ظُفُرٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether the Jewish-specific construction excludes a Prophetic prohibition for Muslims.
- 6:146/q06/p1 The verse supports permission for Muslims; a solitary report banning fanged predators and clawed birds cannot override it. — Al-Rāzī says the restriction is exclusive to Jews and supports Mālik; he rejects the reported Prophetic ban as contrary to the Book. Ālūsī relays and questions this argument. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 6:146/q06/p2 Prophetic Sunnah also forbids carnivorous beasts and birds of prey for Muslims. — Study Quran says the ban was incorporated into Islamic law through Sunnah; Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Rāzī's claimed conflict between the hadith and verse. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 6:146/q07 How does وَمِنَ ٱلْبَقَرِ وَٱلْغَنَمِ attach syntactically?  [وَمِنَ ٱلْبَقَرِ وَٱلْغَنَمِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether min depends on the following or preceding ḥarramnā, or is an inserted or partitive connector.
- 6:146/q07/p1 The fronted phrase attaches to the following حَرَّمْنَا and introduces the fats taken from cattle and sheep. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn answer Abū al-Baqāʾ's objection: a prepositional phrase and object are both complements and can exchange order; a pronoun can require the order, supported by a poetic fronted adverb. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an anticipatory 'as for' emphasis. [9 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:146/q07/p2 It coordinates with كُلَّ ذِى ظُفُرٍ and attaches to the first حَرَّمْنَا; the next clause explains what part is prohibited. — Abū al-Baqāʾ prefers this because he objects to attachment to the second verb; Abū Ḥayyān replies that order is possible. Al-Ālūsī says min then has a partitive sense. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 6:146/q07/p3 Min is partitive, selecting the forbidden fats from the two animals. — Al-Akhfash compares taking half from a house and striking part of a man's face; al-Zamakhsharī says it strengthens the relation. [2 notes: AKHFASH; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 6:146/q07/p4 Min is an idiomatic extra connector, so the sense is 'We forbade cattle and sheep to them.' — Al-Akhfash compares kāna min ḥadīth for kāna ḥadīth. [1 notes: AKHFASH]

## 6:146/q08 What do the surrounding notes say al-anʿām and bahīma include?  [ٱلْبَقَرِ وَٱلْغَنَمِ · meaning]
- 6:146/q08/p1 Al-anʿām includes cattle, sheep, goats and camels; bahīma is wider, and bahīmat al-anʿām can extend to similar edible ruminants. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls livestock a subset of living creatures without reason; some linguists include gazelles and wild cattle without fangs. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قسم من أهل اللغة)]

## 6:146/q09 Why say شُحُومَهُمَا after naming cattle and sheep?  [شُحُومَهُمَآ · rhetoric]
- 6:146/q09/p1 The dual possessive reinforces the fat's connection to the two species and the restriction's focus. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Samīn's Shaykh and Ālūsī say the simpler wording would already identify the fat. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:146/q10 What fats of cattle and sheep were forbidden?  [شُحُومَهُمَآ · referent]
turns on: The scope of 'fats' before the exceptions.
- 6:146/q10/p1 Chiefly the caul or omentum and kidney fat, including internal free fat. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Suddī, Ibn Zayd and many later sources identify these; Muqātil names thurūb; Ibn ʿAbbās calls them internal fats. Ibn Jurayj says other fat attached to bone was allowed. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines shuḥūm as fatty tissue associated with meat. [29 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي، ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي، ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL(ابن جريج); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة والسدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي وابن زيد,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:146/q10/p2 The ban covered cattle and sheep fat broadly, subject to the named exceptions. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran stress that their meat remained permissible. Leviticus 7:23 is compared by Asad; Islahi says an absolute Torah formulation appears to be a rabbinic addition. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:146/q10/p3 Any pure or free fat not on or mixed with bone was forbidden. — Ibn Jurayj and Ibn ʿAṭiyya define by what the exceptions leave out; al-Qurṭubī includes flank and tail because the tail lies on the coccyx. Kashshāf includes large pure and kidney fats. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 6:146/q10/p4 Only caul or omentum fat was forbidden. — Attributed to Qatāda; another report says omentum or rump fat. Al-Māwardī also reports Qatāda's 'omentum or rump' wording. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعض الناس); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 6:146/q11 Does the dual in شُحُومَهُمَا and ظُهُورُهُمَا refer to two collective kinds or many animals?  [شُحُومَهُمَآ ظُهُورُهُمَآ · grammar]
- 6:146/q11/p1 Cattle and sheep are treated as two collective referents; plural backs reflect the many animals within them. — Samīn compares 'your two hearts' at 66:4 and the plural heads of two rams; he says a dual backs would fail where the plural sense is intended. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:146/q12 May Muslims eat fat or animals that Jews regard as forbidden when Jews slaughter them?  [حَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ شُحُومَهُمَآ · law]
turns on: Whether Jewish intent at slaughter or Islam's later permission controls the food's status.
- 6:146/q12/p1 Such fat and slaughter are permitted to Muslims. — Abū Ḥanīfa, al-Shāfiʿī and most scholars say Islam lifted the restriction; Jewish mistaken belief has no legal force. At Khaybar Ibn Mughaffal kept a skin of fat and the Prophet smiled without forbidding eating it, which al-Qurṭubī uses against Mālik's prohibition. Ibn Wahb allows meat forbidden to Jews. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم,مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض الناس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة والشافعي وعامة العلماء,أصبغ وأشهب وابن القاسم,عبد الله بن مغفل,علماؤنا,مالك في سماع المبسوط وابن نافع); STUDYQURAN(Abū Ḥanīfah and al-Shāfiʿī,the Prophet); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر عن مالك)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 6:146/q12/p2 Fat and animals forbidden to Jews by scripture are unlawful from Jewish slaughter. — Mālik and senior Mālikīs say Jews do not intend lawful slaughter of such parts, so they resemble blood; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān read 5:5's 'food' as what Jews themselves eat and reject treating it as slaughter alone. Ibn Ḥabīb limits this to bans Muslims know from scripture, allowing bans known only from Jewish statements. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن المنذر عن مالك وغيره,ابن حبيب,قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن حبيب,مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن حبيب,ابن عباس وغيره,الشافعي,مالك في كتاب محمد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر عن مالك)-]
- 6:146/q12/p3 It is disliked rather than forbidden. — Another report from Mālik and Ibn al-Qāsim gives dislike due to scholarly dispute; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Mālik first eased it in al-Mudawwana then returned to prohibition. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم,مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة والشافعي وعامة العلماء,مالك في سماع المبسوط وابن نافع)]

## 6:146/q13 What do reports say Jews did with forbidden fats?  [حَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ شُحُومَهُمَآ · reports]
turns on: The reports' wording and occasion vary.
- 6:146/q13/p1 They melted or processed prohibited fats, sold them, and consumed the proceeds; the Prophet condemned or cursed them. — Jābir places a sale prohibition in the Conquest year, covering wine, carrion, pork and idols; the Prophet rejected the suggested uses of carrion fat. ʿUmar cites the Jewish precedent when condemning Samura's wine sale. Ibn ʿAbbās reports the Prophet near the Station or Black Stone, cursing them three times; Abū Hurayra and Usāma transmit similar reports, the latter during a sick visit. Ibn Kathīr notes major hadith collections and variant wordings. [14 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي,جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي هريرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أسامة بن زيد,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن جابر بن عبد الله,عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,أسامة بن زيد,ابن عباس,جابر بن عبد الله,عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 6:146/q14 What legal rule about selling forbidden food is drawn from these reports?  [حَرَّمْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ شُحُومَهُمَآ · law]
- 6:146/q14/p1 When God forbids a food, its sale price is also forbidden. — Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly states the principle in the reports; Jābir's Conquest report rejects sale despite practical uses of carrion fat, and ʿUmar applies the precedent to wine. ## 6:146/q15 What fat is meant by what their backs carry?  [إِلَّا مَا حَمَلَتْ ظُهُورُهُمَآ · referent]

## 6:146/q16 How is مَا حَمَلَتْ ظُهُورُهُمَا parsed?  [مَا حَمَلَتْ ظُهُورُهُمَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is a connected exception or an omitted-noun construction.
- 6:146/q16/p1 Mā is an accusative relative in a connected exception; ẓuhūr is nominative as subject of ḥamalat. — Sībawayh says an affirmative exception is governed by the preceding clause, unlike an appositional replacement after negation. Al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī and Samīn give the case analysis. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; NAHHAS; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 6:146/q16/p2 Mā is understood with an omitted 'fat' or with a semantic 'of the fat' qualification. — Samīn rejects an adjective analysis of the relative clause because his Shaykh says mā does not accept an adjective; the explicit 'of the fat' would be circumstantial. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 6:146/q17 Does eating back fat break an oath not to eat fat?  [إِلَّا مَا حَمَلَتْ ظُهُورُهُمَآ · law]
turns on: Whether the exception is classified as fat or meat in ordinary speech.
- 6:146/q17/p1 Yes: back fat counts as fat. — Al-Shāfiʿī and Abū Ḥanīfa's companions cite its inclusion in the verse and the fact that it melts in fire; al-Rāzī says white fatty meat adjoining red meat breaks the oath. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي); ALUSI-FULL(صاحباه); RAZI-FULL]
- 6:146/q17/p2 No: only abdominal fat breaks the oath; back fat counts as meat. — Abū Ḥanīfa treats the exception as disconnected. The Imam prefers this because it is cooked and eaten as meat, and its seller is a butcher, outweighing the presumption for a connected exception. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)+; ALUSI-FULL(صاحباه)-]

- 6:146/q26/p2 It is nominative as predicate of an omitted subject: 'the matter is that.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya states nominative, al-Ḥawfī, Makkī and Abū al-Baqāʾ supply an omitted subject; al-Qurṭubī gives this analysis. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي ومكي وأبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:146/q26/p3 It is an accusative cognate object, meaning 'that punishment.' — Al-Zamakhsharī analyzes it thus. Ibn Mālik and Abū Ḥayyān object that a demonstrative cannot stand for an unexpressed verbal noun, but Samīn and Ālūsī cite a poet's 'dhāk' to reject Ibn Mālik's rule. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان والجلبي,ابن مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(ابن مالك)-]
- 6:146/q26/p4 It is a nominative subject with the following clause as predicate and an omitted resumptive object. — Abū al-Baqāʾ offers it; Tabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī call it weak or confined to poetic necessity. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء)-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-; TABATABAI-]

## 6:146/q27 What is the transgression in بِبَغْيِهِمْ?  [بِبَغْيِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it denotes general wrongdoing or particular offenses and periods.
- 6:146/q27/p1 Their oppression and disobedience, especially killing prophets, obstructing God's path, taking usury and wrongfully consuming property. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī and many others cite 4:160; Jishumī adds seeking what was not theirs. Al-Ḥasan is reported to gloss baghy as unbelief. Bursevī and Ālūsī say each new sin brought another ban. [37 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي,الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Z,Ṭb); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL]
- 6:146/q27/p2 Israelite rulers oppressed the poor by withholding birds and fat. — A report attributed to ʿAlī ibn Ibrāhīm gives this account; Study Quran mentions needy people. [4 notes: TABRISI(علي بن إبراهيم); ALUSI-FULL(علي بن إبراهيم); TABATABAI(علي بن إبراهيم); STUDYQURAN(Ṭb)]
- 6:146/q27/p3 They wronged themselves by forbidding what God had made lawful. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī report this explanation, and Elmalılı includes reversing lawful and unlawful among their offenses. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ELMALILI]
- 6:146/q27/p4 They wronged Moses by making demands and disobeying him during the wilderness period. — Al-Māwardī gives the Moses explanation; Ibn ʿĀshūr points to refusing one food, refusing to fight and worshipping the calf, rather than 4:160's post-Mosaic offenses. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:146/q27/p5 Their Sabbath conduct was associated with the restriction. — Mujāhid makes the association; the note gives no further detail. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد)]
- 6:146/q27/p6 Their invoking an oath about what God had forbidden brought punishment. — Muqātil ibn Ḥayyān links the requital to this oath. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان)]

## 6:146/q28 How is the hāʾ in بِبَغْيِهِمْ read?  [بِبَغْيِهِمْ · readings]
- 6:146/q28/p1 Fāris ibn Aḥmad uniquely reports Yaʿqūb with ḍamma on the hāʾ; other readers use the usual kasra. — Ibn al-Jazarī's Nashr also compares ḥuliyyihim and Ibn Mihrān's report on min aydīhinna wa-arjulihinna. [3 notes: NASHR(فارس بن أحمد,فارس بن أحمد وابن مهران); ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:146/q29 Why was a food prohibition a recompense?  [ذَٰلِكَ جَزَيْنَٰهُم بِبَغْيِهِمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether a duty can count as punishment even though obedience can earn reward.
- 6:146/q29/p1 The ban was punishment and trial: it withdrew wholesome benefits because of prior wrongdoing, though compliance could still bring reward. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī call it trial and punishment. Tabrisī says grave sin required changing the benefit. Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān answer al-Qāḍī that withholding benefit can either increase reward or punish an earlier offense. Qatāda says the foods were not intrinsically impure; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they were originally permissible. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]
- 6:146/q29/p2 A prohibition cannot punish a previous offense because legal obligations are beneficent opportunities for reward. — Al-Qāḍī makes the objection; Tabrisī and al-Rāzī respond by distinguishing lost benefit from offered reward. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; TABRISI-]

## 6:146/q30 What wider lesson is drawn from the punitive restrictions?  [ذَٰلِكَ جَزَيْنَٰهُم بِبَغْيِهِمْ · interpretation]
- 6:146/q30/p1 Transgression can narrow lawful provision, whereas Islam's revealed law removes hardship and warns this community against the same conduct. — Al-Qurṭubī says restriction follows accountability; al-Biqāʿī contrasts Muslim concessions under necessity with Jewish deprivation and links a warning to 5:1. Islahi contrasts pagan invented prohibitions with Jewish punitive rules and invokes 7:157. Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests the ban could soften hard dispositions and display grace to Muslims. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 6:146/q31 Does divine recompense for chosen sins imply compulsion?  [جَزَيْنَٰهُم بِبَغْيِهِمْ · theology]
- 6:146/q31/p1 No. Commands, lawful status, reward and punishment concern voluntary acts; divine decree includes the servant's choice. — Elmalılı says God orders His will with the servant's choice rather than imposing pure compulsion. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 6:146/q32 What does وَإِنَّا لَصَٰدِقُونَ affirm?  [وَإِنَّا لَصَٰدِقُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate referent is the food ban, the Jews' wrongdoing, promise and threat, or all divine reports.
- 6:146/q32/p1 God truthfully reported the Jewish prohibitions and their punitive cause, refuting claims that Israel alone made them. — Muqātil says the revelation accurately distinguishes Muslim and Jewish bans. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn Jarīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr say it rebuts denial; Ibn ʿĀshūr links 3:93–95 and says Jacob's personal camel meat and milk abstention did not bind descendants. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī and others affirm both ban and wrongdoing. [20 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:146/q32/p2 God is truthful in His promises and threats, including the completed worldly punishment and future requital. — Al-Zamakhsharī says He does not break threats to sinners any more than promises to the obedient; Abū Ḥayyān calls this Muʿtazilite. Al-Tabrīzī and Samīn mention promised afterlife punishment after the present dietary penalty; al-Bayḍāwī gives promise and threat as an alternative. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(التبريزي,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:146/q32/p3 Truthfulness is intrinsic to God and covers every report, without supplying a specific omitted complement. — Tabrisī and al-Biqāʿī generalize to all divine speech; Samīn explicitly allows no complement. Ālūsī allows the immediate prohibition and cause while noting a promise-and-threat restriction. [5 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 6:146/q33 How is the emphatic closing clause constructed?  [وَإِنَّا لَصَٰدِقُونَ · grammar]
- 6:146/q33/p1 The phrase after inna is its predicate; the underlying pronoun form is innanā. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the grammatical analysis; Samīn discusses possible omitted complements to 'truthful.' [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:146/q34 Were these Jewish restrictions later lifted?  [حَرَّمْنَا جَزَيْنَٰهُم · sciences]
turns on: Whether the dietary restrictions continued in Islam.
- 6:146/q34/p1 They were temporary Jewish burdens, abrogated or lifted by Muḥammad's law. — Al-Qurṭubī lists Torah bans on carrion, blood, pork, unsplit-hoofed animals and scaleless fish and says Islam removed hardship. Tabrisī says earlier punishment benefited later generations until abrogation. Islahi invokes a promised final messenger restoring Abraham's natural religion and 7:157; Study Quran reports Muslim jurists permit the fat. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Abū Ḥanīfah and al-Shāfiʿī)]

## 6:146/q35 How does this verse connect with the surrounding dietary argument?  [* · links]
- 6:146/q35/p1 It contrasts revealed Muslim food law with punitive Jewish restrictions and refutes claims that God imposed no such Jewish bans or that Israel alone originated them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Islahi and Study Quran connect it to 6:145 and the Makkan invented taboos; Jishumī reports the claim that sāʾiba rules came from scripture. Ibn ʿĀshūr says pork is omitted because it is not a distinctively temporary Jewish ban; al-Biqāʿī sees warning to Muslims and a connection to hunting during consecration in 5:1. [19 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:146/q35/p2 The verse's detailed account is evidence of the Prophet's truth, since he knew earlier revelation without learning from Jewish scholars or books. — Al-Biqāʿī adds that the detail answers obstinate objections. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:146/q36 How do other scriptures or verses bear on these restrictions?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the Torah's wording is exact, broader, or from a different historical ban.
- 6:146/q36/p1 Qurʾānic 3:93 and 4:160 and Torah passages provide related but distinct contexts for Jewish food rules. — Ibn ʿĀshūr separates Mosaic restrictions here from later 4:160; Asad points to Leviticus 7:23 and 3:93; Study Quran says 3:93 places divine bans after Torah and that Leviticus 11 forbids pawed beasts, birds of prey and undivided hooves while sacrificial law burns backbone and entrail fat. Kur'an Yolu points to Leviticus and Deuteronomy. Islahi says Torah's absolute fat ban may reflect rabbinic addition; he lists fins and scales for sea creatures and broader bird bans. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 6:146/q36/p2 The phrase 'blood poured forth' sets the natural limit: residual blood attached to meat remains. — Islahi compares the nearby dietary wording with the natural limit on attached fat. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:146/q37 What incidental literary observations appear in the source excerpts?  [* · rhetoric]
- 6:146/q37/p1 Perception grasps a general impression first and discovers detail through closer or repeated attention; difficult similes exploit this. — Al-Jurjānī cites anemones as ruby flags on emerald spears and stars as pearls on blue carpet; repeated hearing or tasting reveals differences. A separate clipped Bint al-Shāṭiʾ excerpt tentatively concerns a proud nose restrained by shackles, but lacks context. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
