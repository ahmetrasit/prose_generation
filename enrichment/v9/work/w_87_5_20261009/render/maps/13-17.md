# 13:17: 57 questions

## 13:17/q01 What does 'from the sky' denote?  [ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: How literally the source location is taken.
- 13:17/q01/p1 From the clouds or direction of the visible sky. — Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī note observation; Nasafī chooses clouds; Samarrāʾī lists clouds, rain, open space, or a roof as general Qurʾānic senses. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BIQAI-FULL]
- 13:17/q01/p2 From the sky itself. — Bayḍāwī allows it; Ālūsī cites reports without ruling on their authenticity and also mentions heavenly causes of vapor. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 13:17/q02 What is the water sent down from the sky?  [مَآءًۭ · referent]
turns on: The literal rain scene and its symbolic counterparts are both stated; inward authors differ on the gift it depicts.
- 13:17/q02/p1 The Qurʾān or revelation as the water represented. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Farrāʾ, Rāzī, and later authors compare its life-giving descent to rain; Elmalılı contrasts unacquired revelation with knowledge gained through work. [41 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; SULAMI(الواسطي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري والفراء,ابن عباس في رواية عطاء,صاحب النظم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(مقاتل بن سليمان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عيينة); ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(آخرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 13:17/q02/p2 Rain, the literal scene. — Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī and others say rain. Bayḍāwī allows clouds, the sky's direction, or the sky itself; Nasafī takes sky as clouds. [9 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 13:17/q02/p3 Divine gifts, knowledge, light, love, or disclosure in inward readings. — Sulamī reports light, gifts, shyness, and certainty; Qāshānī has Holy Spirit knowledge; Bursevī's Najmī source distinguishes soul, heart, and secret; Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes divine outpouring measured by recipients. [10 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,بعضهم); QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); TABATABAI]

## 13:17/q03 How can the valleys be said to flow?  [فَسَالَتْ أَوْدِيَةٌۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether wādī here is the channel or the water in it.
- 13:17/q03/p1 The waters of the valleys flowed; the container stands for its contents or 'waters' is omitted. — Abū ʿAlī and Ibn al-Jawzī supply the genitive; Jurjānī calls attribution to the ravines a figure in the predication; Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares a flowing waterspout. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); JURJANI-DALAIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(السهروردي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 13:17/q03/p2 Wādī can itself mean flowing water, making the predication literal. — Qurtubī and Ālūsī record this sense. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 13:17/q04 What are the valleys?  [أَوْدِيَةٌۢ · meaning]
turns on: The word can name a physical channel or, by extension, its flowing contents.
- 13:17/q04/p1 Low channels or hollows where rainwater gathers and flows. — Most linguists say open ground between mountains or at their foot; Jishumī and Ṭabrisī mention the diya etymology; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a broad extended hollow; Asad calls it a normally dry riverbed. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة أهل اللغة); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(عامة أهل اللغة); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); ASAD-NOTES; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(شمر)+]
- 13:17/q04/p2 Awdiyah is the plural of wādin, with an unusual broken-plural pattern. — Abū ʿAlī derives it by overlap of fāʿil and faʿīl patterns; andiya is offered as parallel; al-Samīn counters a claim of uniqueness with jāʾiz/ajwiza and nāḥiya/anḥiya. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,غير أبي علي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,غيره); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الأخوان وحفص والباقون); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي)]
- 13:17/q04/p3 The flowing water itself by extension. — Shammar, Bayḍāwī, Qurtubī, and Ālūsī permit naming the water for its channel; Abū ʿAlī instead supplies the omitted 'waters of the valleys'. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(شمر); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 13:17/q05 Why are the valleys indefinite?  [أَوْدِيَةٌۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes offer complementary effects of indefiniteness.
- 13:17/q05/p1 Rain falls in different regions at different times, so only some valleys flow. — Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ālūsī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Asad give this explanation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds differences of length, breadth, and capacity. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari)]
- 13:17/q05/p2 The plural evokes valleys of differing sizes and receptivity. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī points to size, length, breadth, and capacity. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 13:17/q06 What does 'according to its measure' measure?  [بِقَدَرِهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether qadr relates to the valley's size, a divinely fixed beneficial allotment, or its water volume.
- 13:17/q06/p1 Each valley's physical capacity or size; small takes little and large much. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Qutayba, and many later authors use this account; Rāzī describes overflow; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses nondestructive fit. - 13:17/q06/p2 The amount God apportioned in wisdom, particularly what benefits without harm. — Zajjāj and Māwardī give the allotment; Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ālūsī, and Abū Ḥayyān connect it to beneficial rain rather than destructive excess; Ālūsī says it need not reach the rim. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أهل التفسير); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري والزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 13:17/q06/p3 The measure of each valley's water, by shifted reference of the suffix. — Abū ʿAlī rejects measuring valleys against themselves; Zarkashī gives this wording, and Ālūsī reports its grammatical basis. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو علي); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)]

## 13:17/q07 How is bi-qadariha read?  [بِقَدَرِهَا · readings]
turns on: Vocalization of the dāl; the notes generally retain the same sense.
- 13:17/q07/p1 With sukūn on dāl, bi-qadriha. — Reported for al-Ḥasan, al-Ashhab, Zayd b. ʿAlī, Abū ʿAmr in one transmission, and others; Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī call the forms dialectal and equivalent. [9 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وابن جبير وأبو العالية وأيوب وابن يعمر وأبو حاتم عن يعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي والحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي، زيد بن علي، أبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي والأشهب العقيلي وأبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي); TABATABAI(الحسن)]
- 13:17/q07/p2 With fatḥa on dāl, bi-qadariha. — The majority reading is reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي والأشهب العقيلي وأبو عمرو)]

## 13:17/q08 What does qadr mean here as a word?  [بِقَدَرِهَا · meaning]
turns on: The noun's measure sense and its verbal-noun sense support different construals of the phrase.
- 13:17/q08/p1 Amount or proportion measured in relation to a thing. — Wāḥidī cites quantities by weight; Ṭabrisī contrasts exact proportion with weight admitting excess or deficiency; Biqāʿī uses proportion without excess; Ibn ʿĀshūr says measurement. [10 notes: AKHFASH; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان)]
- 13:17/q08/p2 An apportioning, a verbal noun for what God determines. — Wāḥidī and Zajjāj explain the measured filling as God's assignment. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري والزجاج)]

## 13:17/q09 What is the grammatical relation of bi-qadariha?  [بِقَدَرِهَا · grammar]
turns on: The phrase's grammatical attachment.
- 13:17/q09/p1 It modifies or depends on sālat, describing how the valleys flowed. — Al-Ḥawfī gives the attachment; al-Samīn and Ālūsī report it. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي)]
- 13:17/q09/p2 It is an adjective or circumstantial qualifier of awdiya. — Abū al-Baqāʾ calls it an adjective; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a ḥāl; al-Samīn reports the option. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:17/q09/p3 It depends on anzala. — Ālūsī mentions this third parse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي)]

## 13:17/q10 What is al-sayl and why is it definite?  [ٱلسَّيْلُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether al-sayl resumes an implied event or denotes the resulting water.
- 13:17/q10/p1 The torrent or flowing water, made definite by the preceding 'flowed'. — Jishumī says sayl is both event noun and the water; Abū Ḥayyān says the verb introduces its verbal noun, which al-sayl resumes; Ālūsī records an objection that event and concrete torrent differ, then leaves the event sense open. [6 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر,الراغب)]
- 13:17/q10/p2 The flowing event itself, originally a verbal noun. — Al-Rāghib as quoted by Ālūsī cites its verbal-noun origin. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]

## 13:17/q11 What does 'the flood carried' mean?  [فَٱحْتَمَلَ ٱلسَّيْلُ · meaning]
- 13:17/q11/p1 The torrent lifted and carried its load. — Ṭabrisī explains iḥtimāl as carrying with strength; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and Ālūsī say iftaʿala has the simple ḥamala sense, comparing iqtidara/qadara. [5 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان)]

## 13:17/q12 What is the flood's zabad?  [زَبَدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Physical foam is the vehicle; sources vary in the particular spiritual or doctrinal residue it depicts.
- 13:17/q12/p1 Froth, scum, bubbles, and flood debris churned up and carried above water. — Mujāhid, Zajjāj, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī describe it; poetry about a river or Euphrates is cited. Al-Samīn also notes milk foam, sea foam, and a proverb of abundance. - 13:17/q12/p2 Doubt, disbelief, falsehood, satanic suggestions, or egoistic thoughts in the comparison. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies doubt and unbelief; Burhān lists doubts and desires; Nasafī and Bursevī report whispers; Biqāʿī includes idol worship and polemic; the inward readings identify self-regard. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); SULAMI(الواسطي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الجمهور); QASHANI; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي حاتم,ابن عباس); TABATABAI]

## 13:17/q13 What does rābiyan describe?  [رَّابِيًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The lexical glosses are compatible; the rhetorical question is why the height is stressed.
- 13:17/q13/p1 High, swollen, or floating above the water. — Abū ʿUbayda derives it from rabā 'to grow'; Ṭabarī cites 22:5 and rabwa, a mound; Rāzī records Zajjāj's height and another's swelling as close explanations. [26 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج وغيرهما); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,غيره); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 13:17/q13/p2 It emphasizes foam's apparent dominance without any lasting substance. — Ibn Qutayba, Biqāʿī, and Ālūsī explain that height makes falsehood appear to prevail; Ālūsī prefers 'swollen' over 'above it' so the foam's rise seems inherent. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 13:17/q14 What do rain and valleys represent in the parable?  [مَآءًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Different versions identify the descending gift and the recipients' faculty more narrowly; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān dispute the symbolic report's attribution.

- 13:17/q14/p2 The water is revealed law, and intelligent and foolish hearts each receive a share. — Attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās by Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān; both question its transmission and resist abandoning ordinary Arabic without cause, but allow a charitable sense if authentic. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 13:17/q14/p3 The valleys can stand for people's minds. — Ibn ʿUyayna says each mind carries the Qurʾān according to capacity. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عيينة)]
- 13:17/q14/p4 The water signifies faith or truth and the valleys believers' hearts. — The author of al-Naẓm gives faith and truth; Wāḥidī's other authorities call it guidance. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري والفراء,صاحب النظم)]

## 13:17/q16 What is heated over the fire?  [مِمَّا يُوقِدُونَ عَلَيْهِ فِى ٱلنَّارِ · referent]

## 13:17/q17 How does 'in the fire' attach?  [فِى ٱلنَّارِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase gives a distinct location, describes the object's state, or reinforces the action.

## 13:17/q41 What does the parable teach about truth's permanence?  [* · theology]
turns on: The general victory claim is elaborated either as a rule of history, a criterion of reality, or a rule of measured divine giving.
- 13:17/q41/p1 Truth endures and benefits while falsehood temporarily appears high and then vanishes by God's practice. — Ibn Qutayba, Ibn ʿAbbās, Biqāʿī, Iṣlāḥī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī use the physical processes to explain lasting truth, judgment, and the eventual triumph of its people; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 17:81 and 21:18. - 13:17/q41/p2 Truth is what matches reality or fulfills its end; falsehood lacks stability when examined. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī quotes al-Rāghib and develops the distinction for statements, acts, beliefs, and dependent beings; true realities cooperate toward God's ends, whereas defects frustrate them. [7 notes: TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 13:17/q41/p3 Recipients measure divine gift, yet their capacities remain subject to God. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 15:21, 39:6, 11:123 and 13:31, then says transient impurities emerge while mercy persists. [4 notes: TABATABAI]

## 13:17/q42 How do inward readings map the water, fire, and their foam?  [* · inward]
turns on: The inward sources differ over the divine influx, the faculty receiving it, and the impurity removed.
- 13:17/q42/p1 Qurʾān and faith refine hearts; whispers, ostentation, laziness, and spiritual defects are the foam. — Nasafī's majority reading makes adornment noble states and morals, goods sincere deeds; Qurtubī reports virtues as ornaments; Bursevī cites al-Kāshifī and Abū al-Layth. - 13:17/q42/p2 Divine light or gifts cleanse receptive hearts, leaving sincere states and certainty. — Sulamī reports al-Wāsiṭī's shy pearl and Qurʾānic generosity, Ibn ʿAṭāʾ's light removing heedlessness, and another account of guidance, gnosis, love, longing, and witnessing. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,بعضهم)]
- 13:17/q42/p3 Knowledge from the Holy Spirit floods heart valleys; love's fire refines gnosis and virtues; conceit is the foam. — Qāshānī maps the two processes to knowledge and love, with the soul's admiration and veiling dispersed while pure meanings remain; he cites 8:11. [5 notes: QASHANI]
- 13:17/q42/p4 Love, beauty, and disclosure water successive levels of soul, heart, and secret. — Bursevī's Najmī commentary pairs them with animal traits, spiritual selfhood, and figurative existence as three foams; the Mathnawī says eternal qualities burn transient ones. ## 13:17/q45 What general Arabic rhetoric is cited around the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited rhetoric distinguishes figures of comparison, attribution, and evaluative naming.
- 13:17/q45/p1 Metaphors transfer visible qualities to intellectual ones or move the figure into predication. — Jurjānī uses light for proof, faith, and knowledge, darkness for doubt, a balance for justice, and jāḥiẓ's strainer; his Dalāʾil analysis locates the figure in attribution, including ravines flowing. ## 13:17/q47 How was the Qurʾān collected according to cited reports?  [* · reports]

[6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(البخاري,الطبري,عثمان بن عفان)]
- 13:17/q47/p4 Al-Itqān reports ʿAlī also gathered the text after the Prophet's death. — Ibn Sīrīn transmits ʿAlī's vow to remain home except for Friday prayer until completion. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن سيرين)]

## 13:17/q48 How is Qurʾānic inimitability argued in the cited material?  [* · theology]
turns on: The minimum challenged unit and how its miraculous character is known.
- 13:17/q48/p1 Qurʾānic composition remains clear and consistent beyond imitable poetic devices. — Bāqillānī contrasts crafted horse similes and antithesis with the Qurʾān's sustained order; Ibn ʿAṭiyya praises its eloquence; Samarrāʾī stresses deliberate artistry.
