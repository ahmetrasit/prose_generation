# 23:41: 21 questions

## 23:41/q01 How does the seizure follow the messenger's plea and the surrounding account?  [فَأَخَذَتْهُمُ · links]
- 23:41/q01/p1 The initial fa marks the prompt answer to the messenger's prayer and the fulfillment of God's promise. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the fa as prompt response; al-Biqāʿī connects the destruction with Noah's prayer and God's promise to him. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:41/q02 Who were the people seized by the cry?  [فَأَخَذَتْهُمُ ٱلصَّيْحَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the cry and neighboring details identify Thamūd, the succession after Noah identifies ʿĀd, or the lack of names makes the account general; ʿĀd's wind destruction needs reconciliation with the cry.
- 23:41/q02/p1 They were Thamūd, Ṣāliḥ's people. — Mujāhid and al-Ṭabarī name Thamūd; al-Bayḍāwī names Ṣāliḥ's people. Ibn ʿĀshūr favors this from the cry or thunderbolt, morning regret and surviving ruins at al-Ḥijr (23:40; 15:83; 37:137–38). Al-Sāmarrāʾī takes the definite 'the people' here as Ṣāliḥ's nation, versus indefinite 'a people' in 23:44. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Bursawī relate the literal cry especially to Thamūd. [12 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Eski müfessirlerin bir kısmı); SAMARRAI-ASRAR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 23:41/q02/p2 They were ʿĀd, Hūd's people. — Ibn Kathīr reports the view that the generation succeeded Noah's people; Kuranyolu says most commentators identify ʿĀd and Hūd. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī address the difficulty that ʿĀd are said to have perished by wind: the wind may have carried a cry, or 'cry' may signify torment. Al-Bursawī also cites the heavenly cry that destroyed Shaddād and his family near Iram. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(çoğunluk); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:41/q02/p3 The generation is left unnamed to describe the recurring experience of messengers and rejecting peoples. — Kuranyolu reports Esed's general reading and adopts it. Ibn ʿĀshūr says unnamed generations illustrate the recurring fate of deniers, while the specific identity can still be inferred. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Muhammed Esed); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 23:41/q03 What was the cry that seized them?  [ٱلصَّيْحَةُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ṣayḥa names an audible destructive event, an earthquake or thunderbolt, or figuratively the punishment or death it brought; the reported wind destruction of ʿĀd affects the literal reading.
- 23:41/q03/p1 It was a literal deadly shout, usually Gabriel's single cry. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī and others say Gabriel's shout killed everyone, sometimes by bursting or splitting their hearts; al-Jishumī also reports an unnamed angel. Al-Qurṭubī pairs Gabriel's cry with wind; Ibn Kathīr reports a violent cold wind leaving houses (46:25); al-Ālūsī allows Gabriel's cry within wind. Al-Bursawī defines ṣayḥa as a raised voice. Rāzī prefers this literal reading. [19 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(جبريل); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(التفاسير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); RAZI-FULL+]
- 23:41/q03/p2 It was an earthquake or great quaking. — Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān transmit this from Ibn ʿAbbās; the Study Quran reports the quaking interpretation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 23:41/q03/p3 It was the sound of a thunderbolt. — Ibn ʿĀshūr favors this because the thunderbolt and cry descriptions of Thamūd fit together (69:5; 15:83). [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 23:41/q03/p4 The cry denotes punishment or overwhelming torment. — Al-Ḥasan, al-Baghawī, al-Jishumī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī report this explanation; a poetic cry against the Barmakids illustrates overwhelming disaster. Al-Jishumī and al-Baghawī also give destruction itself. Al-Biqāʿī allows figurative terrible punishment. One Abū Ḥayyān poetic line depicts a cry throwing people on their chins. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الجلالين); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 23:41/q03/p5 The cry denotes death itself. — Al-Ḥasan likens it to someone being called and answering; Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursawī and the Study Quran include death among the interpretations. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 23:41/q04 How does the cry 'take' them?  [فَأَخَذَتْهُمُ ٱلصَّيْحَةُ · rhetoric]
- 23:41/q04/p1 Taking them means destroying them, with the cry named as the cause or accompaniment of destruction. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls 'taking' a metaphor for destruction and the attribution to the cry a rational metaphor: the sound caused destruction or accompanied the air tearing when a thunderbolt fell. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:41/q05 What does 'in truth' say about their seizure?  [بِٱلْحَقِّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥaqq emphasizes moral justice, an unavoidable reality or decree, fulfillment of a true warning, divine command, or several of these senses together.
- 23:41/q05/p1 The seizure was just punishment earned by disbelief, denial, and wrongdoing. — Al-Ṭabarī says vengeance followed disbelief and denial of the messenger; Ibn ʿAbbās says they denied truth. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds their deeds and God's decree; Rāzī and al-Nasafī compare judging by truth to judging justly. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the bāʾ marks accompaniment, with justice rather than assault. Ibn Kathīr cites disbelief and tyranny; the Study Quran and Asad likewise stress justice. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 23:41/q05/p2 It was an established and irresistible reality or decree. — Al-Mufaḍḍal cites death's stupor arriving 'with truth' (50:19). Al-Biqāʿī calls it a judgment none but God could repel; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī attach the phrase to 'taking' and stress inescapability. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ allows the bāʾ as accompaniment or causation and an omitted 'true affair' or 'decree.' [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(المفضل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفضل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Righib)]
- 23:41/q05/p3 It fulfilled the messenger's true warning. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives the true promise as an option; al-Ālūsī relates it to the promised regret in 23:40. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:41/q05/p4 It occurred by God's command. — Al-Bursawī reports al-Jalālayn's gloss. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الجلالين)]
- 23:41/q05/p5 The phrase combines justice, wisdom, reality, inescapability, and suitability. — Asad reports Rāghib's compound explanation and says any single translation is approximate. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Righib)]

## 23:41/q06 What does ghuthāʾ mean?  [غُثَآءًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word itself names decayed plants, surface scum and refuse, or dead bodies; many descriptions combine vegetation and foam, while the bodies are often what the simile depicts.
- 23:41/q06/p1 It is flood-borne decayed vegetation and other debris, especially dried leaves, grass, reeds and twigs. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda describe decayed matter; al-Zajjāj specifies dead foliage mixed with foam. Quṭrub says fallen leaves that dry in water; Abū Zayd mentions dung, leaves and reeds, with the verb yaghthū. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Samīn cite Imruʾ al-Qays's flood-debris line. Al-Rāzī and Study Quran compare vegetation in 87:5. Some compare once-green vegetation crushed after greening; al-Kalbī and Muqātil stress its withering and wear. [39 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم,مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وقتادة,قطرب); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد,الكلبي,امرؤ القيس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:41/q06/p2 It is foam, scum or useless refuse carried on the surface of water. — Abū ʿUbayda, Quṭrub's counterparts Ibn Shajara and al-Akhfash, and Abū ʿUbayd emphasize froth, filth and uselessness. Al-Akhfash equates it with jufāʾ (13:17). Al-Samīn also reports useless matter taken by a flood or left in a cooking pot, which became proverbial. [16 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة والأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد,الأخفش,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,الزجاج); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 23:41/q06/p3 It can refer to dead bodies or corpses. — Al-Jishumī reports dead bodies as an alternative gloss; al-Wāḥidī reports commentators saying God made them corpses, and Ibn Kathīr uses the term for bodies cast down. [3 notes: JISHUMI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 23:41/q07 What does making them like ghuthāʾ convey?  [فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ غُثَآءًۭ · interpretation]
- 23:41/q07/p1 They were killed and reduced to lifeless, worthless remains like debris cast about by a flood. — Al-Ṭabarī says the debris has no use and Ibn Zayd calls this a divine simile; Ibn Qutayba notes it disperses. Al-Wāḥidī, Muqātil, al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī describe dry corpses. Ibn Kathīr stresses insignificance and uselessness; al-Biqāʿī likens them also to hollow palm trunks. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a compact state simile: they decayed and piled together, dying at once. Al-Bayḍāwī cites the idiom 'the valley swept him away' for perishing; al-Bursawī repeats it and al-Kāshifī stresses annihilation. [23 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,المفسرون,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:41/q08 How does jaʿalnāhum govern ghuthāʾ?  [فَجَعَلْنَٰهُمْ غُثَآءًۭ · grammar]
- 23:41/q08/p1 Jaʿala means 'turn into' here, and ghuthāʾ is its second object. — Al-Samīn gives the explicit parsing. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 23:41/q09 What are the form and inflection of ghuthāʾ?  [غُثَآءًۭ · grammar]
- 23:41/q09/p1 Its final root radical is wāw; its transmitted plural aghthāʾ is irregular. — Al-Samīn derives it from ghaṯā al-wādī and distinguishes the yāʾ-root nausea verb ghathiyat; he says expected plurals are aghthiya or ghithyān. Al-Wāḥidī gives regular aghthiya and aghthāʾ, while Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call aghthāʾ irregular. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:41/q10 How is the thāʾ in ghuthāʾ pronounced?  [غُثَآءًۭ · readings]
- 23:41/q10/p1 The thāʾ is transmitted both light and doubled. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ note both forms; Abū Ḥayyān reports both in the Imruʾ al-Qays line. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 23:41/q11 What does buʿdan mean for the wrongdoers?  [فَبُعْدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether remoteness is explained chiefly as death and destruction or as exclusion from divine mercy and good; several sources give both.
- 23:41/q11/p1 It means destruction, ruin or perdition. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, Rāzī and al-Nasafī gloss it as their perishing; al-Naqqāsh stresses increased ruin and punishment. Al-Biqāʿī includes expulsion; al-Ālūsī notes the second verb form for destruction. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the opposite idiom lā tabʿad, 'do not be lost,' in Mālik ibn al-Rayb's burial poem; al-Wāḥidī compares Midian's curse in 11:95. [14 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو بكر النقاش); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:41/q11/p2 It means distance from God's mercy, good or salvation, like a curse. — Ibn ʿĪsā, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Kāshifī specify exclusion from mercy. Rāzī adds scorn and greater exclusion from bliss in the Hereafter after worldly punishment; al-Ālūsī includes good and salvation. Al-Jishumī compares suḥqan and the opposite lā tabʿad in poetry. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 23:41/q12 Is fa-buʿdan a report or a curse?  [فَبُعْدًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form invokes distance upon them or declares the distance and ruin already realized.
- 23:41/q12/p1 It is an imprecation or denunciation against them. — Al-Jishumī treats it like suḥqan and contrasts lā tabʿad; al-Ṭabarī asks that God drive the deniers away. Rāzī says God uses it to humiliate and warn later generations; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is a curse and rebuke, not a literal prayer for people already dead. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ allows curse, denunciation and supplication. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:41/q12/p2 It reports or declares that their doom and distance have occurred. — Al-Wāḥidī says God imposed remoteness; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explicitly allow a statement of their ruin or distance as well as a curse. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:41/q13 How is buʿdan parsed and vocalized?  [فَبُعْدًۭا · grammar]
- 23:41/q13/p1 It is an accusative verbal noun replacing a verb whose governor is omitted. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn require the unspoken verb; Rāzī cites Sībawayh for buʿdan, suḥqan and damran. Al-Qurṭubī compares 'watering' and 'tending'; al-Bursawī says its expressed governor is rare. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares tabban and suḥqan. Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn note fatḥa or sukūn on ʿayn and analogous forms of rashada. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:41/q14 What does the lām in lil-qawmi attach to?  [لِّلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām has an unspoken explanatory attachment or is governed directly by buʿdan; al-Samīn disputes attestation for the latter.
- 23:41/q14/p1 It attaches to an omitted explanatory expression specifying those cursed. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this analysis; al-Samīn compares suqyā lahu and jadʿan lahu, and says fa-taʿsan lahum (47:8) gives no support for direct attachment. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:41/q14/p2 It attaches directly to buʿdan, indicating for whom distance is invoked. — Abū Ḥayyān transmits al-Ḥawfī's analysis. Al-Samīn reports and rejects it as unattested, arguing that fa-taʿsan lahum (47:8) has an omitted attachment too. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Bursawī describe the lām's identifying function without requiring this specific attachment. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR(الحوفي)-]

## 23:41/q15 Who are 'the wrongdoing people' and why this description?  [لِّلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is read with focus on the identified deniers in the episode or on the comprehensive warning that extends to people like them.
- 23:41/q15/p1 They are the polytheist deniers who wronged themselves and resisted their messenger. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī call them polytheists; Ibn ʿAbbās calls them deniers. Al-Ṭabarī specifies disbelief, disobedience and self-wronging; Ibn Kathīr cites defiance; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they associated partners (31:13) and accused Hūd of lying (23:38). Al-Bursawī lists denial of Creator and resurrection, worldly love and oppression. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:41/q15/p2 The description includes this destroyed people and, in its general wording, their like among wrongdoers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the definite plural is comprehensive and closes the account; al-Biqāʿī says the explicit quality generalizes the warning; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say it gives the reason for their curse. [8 notes: THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:41/q16 Why name the wrongdoing people instead of using a pronoun?  [لِّلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · rhetoric]
- 23:41/q16/p1 The explicit description identifies those condemned, gives their wrongdoing as the cause, and extends the warning. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the noun replaces their pronoun to supply the reason; al-Ālūsī agrees. Al-Biqāʿī makes it a general warning about powerful people who failed to support messengers; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the definite plural closes the account comprehensively. Al-Nasafī compares the identifying lām with hīta laka. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:41/q17 What wider warning or lesson does this destruction convey?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether attention falls on the recurring warning to rejecters or on the contrast between their end and believers' survival.
- 23:41/q17/p1 Deniers of messengers deserve destruction after proof; the account warns later rejecters and shows God's recurring pattern. — Al-Jishumī says sending messengers removes excuses before deserved punishment. Ibn Kathīr warns the present audience against denial (43:76); Rāzī says worldly exclusion warns of greater loss. Al-Bursawī applies it to those immersed in worldly life and opposing messengers; Kuranyolu says this passage warns of painful fate after denials answered elsewhere. Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects coincidence and the Study Quran notes recurring opposition of affluent and powerful people (7:59–62, 7:136). [8 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:41/q17/p2 The messenger and believers were saved and prospered, inheriting the land while the unbelievers perished. — Al-Biqāʿī makes this the counterpart to the destruction. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:41/q18 What theological point is drawn from sending the messenger before destruction?  [* · theology]
- 23:41/q18/p1 The sending of messengers establishes proof and human accountability, opposing the claim that God creates all human acts. — Al-Jishumī argues that if God created all acts, sending messengers would have no purpose; he applies this to compulsion, capacity and will. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 23:41/q19 What inward lesson about food and divine provision is drawn around this account?  [* · inward]
- 23:41/q19/p1 God's people eat and drink inwardly from divine provision, while messengers eat ordinary food without worldly excess. — Al-Bursawī speaks of the heart's mouth during nights with the Lord and says God's people avoid extravagance, unlike disbelievers said to eat sevenfold. He reports Aftāda that the Prophet received nourishment from divine disclosures; outward eating served the weak community, and the stone he bound on himself kept his subtle spirit present for guidance, rather than relieving hunger. [3 notes: BURSEVI(أفتاده أفندي)]

## 23:41/q20 How does this episode fit the sūra's sequence of prophetic stories?  [* · links]
- 23:41/q20/p1 It follows Noah's struggle and the ark, then exemplifies repeated rejection of messengers by powerful people. — The Study Quran's introduction links the post-blessings account to Noah's denial, rebuke and ark, and notes repeated opposition by affluent leaders elsewhere in the sūra and Quran. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the unnamed later generations show a recurring divine pattern. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:41/q21 What is suḥaq, the curse word compared with buʿdan?  [فَبُعْدًۭا · reports]
- 23:41/q21/p1 Suḥaq is a valley in Hell. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr calls it a valley in Hell and distinguishes Saʿīr as a valley of pus; al-Suyūṭī lists Suḥaq among several named valleys of Hell. [2 notes: ITQAN(سعيد بن جبير)]
