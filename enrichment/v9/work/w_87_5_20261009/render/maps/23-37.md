# 23:37: 12 questions

## 23:37/q01 What does هي refer to, and how is its referent understood?  [هِىَ حَيَاتُنَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the following predicate serves as a backward antecedent or clarifies a referent already present in context or thought; al-Samin also disputes a proposed technical classification of the former.
- 23:37/q01/p1 It refers to life, supplied or identified by the following predicate. — Al-Thalabi, al-Baghawi and al-Wahidi identify the referent as worldly life. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi reconstruct an underlying 'there is no life except our worldly life': the predicate identifies the replaced noun. Al-Razi and al-Baydawi cite the line 'hiya al-nafs ma hammaltaha tatahammal'; al-Alusi adds 'hiya al-Arab taqulu ma sha'at' as backward-reference parallels. Al-Baydawi says replacement avoids repetition and suggests the identity is evident. Al-Alusi answers an objection that the qualified predicate would make the reference tautological: the pronoun can refer to the noun without its qualification. Ibn Ashur describes deliberate initial vagueness followed by explanatory apposition, and rules out a pronoun of the story because a singular noun, rather than a clause, explains it and the construction precludes that analysis. [18 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الشاعر); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:37/q01/p2 It refers to life already understood from the context or as a familiar mental concept; the following words clarify it. — Abu Hayyan derives the referent from the preceding mockery of resurrection. Al-Samin says context explains the pronoun and rejects calling al-Zamakhshari's analysis explanation by what follows in both wording and syntax. Al-Alusi prefers the mentally understood genus of life; al-Kashf compares a familiar concept indicated by what follows to 'this is your brother'. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 23:37/q02 What claim does إِنْ هِىَ إِلَّا حَيَاتُنَا ٱلدُّنْيَا make?  [إِنْ هِىَ حَيَاتُنَا ٱلدُّنْيَا · interpretation]
- 23:37/q02/p1 The present worldly life is the only life; no life follows death. — Al-Wahidi calls it the near, present life; al-Tabrisi the familiar one. Al-Bursevi calls it near and perishing, and Ibn Ashur says dunya is the nearer life. Al-Biqai says their emphatic dismissal of resurrection leads to this claim. Elmalili reports their reasoning from the supposed impossibility of bodily resurrection from dust and bones to enjoying this low life. Asad says they also accuse the messenger of lying about God. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:37/q03 How does إِنْ form the negation here?  [إِنْ هِىَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle is described by its genus-negating force or by equivalence to negative ma; the notes do not present these descriptions as mutually exclusive.
- 23:37/q03/p1 The negative in applies to a pronoun denoting the genus of life, with the force of la negating the entire genus. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Razi, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi explain the genus-wide force; al-Alusi's preferred mental-genus analysis likewise yields no life except worldly life. Al-Samarrai points to earlier discussion of differences among negating particles while citing this and parallel formulations, without specifying a further analysis here. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 23:37/q03/p2 In is a negative particle meaning ma, making the clause a restriction to worldly life. — Al-Bursevi explicitly equates negative in with ma. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 23:37/q04 What does ٱلدُّنْيَا add to the description of life?  [ٱلدُّنْيَا · meaning]
- 23:37/q04/p1 It is the near, present worldly life, which passes away. — Al-Wahidi calls it near and present; al-Bursevi adds that it perishes; Ibn Ashur glosses it as nearer, present life. [3 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:37/q05 What does نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا mean, and how does its order work?  [نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dying and living refer to successive generations, coexisting people, the same person's ordinary life, prior lifelessness, a continued life through descendants, or return through transmigration; some accounts instead explain or alter the word order.
- 23:37/q05/p1 One generation dies and a new generation is born in its place. — Muqatil describes an endless cycle; Ibn Zayd compares generations to harvested and sprouting crops and cites deniers in 34:7 and 34:3. Yahya ibn Sallam says they die and are born. Al-Tabari, al-Zamakhshari, Ibn Atiyya, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Biqai, al-Alusi and al-Tabatabai likewise explain replacement. Al-Samin rejects using the word order as proof that waw lacks sequence, because successive replacement itself explains it. Al-Alusi says individual return would contradict their denial. Al-Tabatabai prefers this contextual reading to transmigration. Ibn Ashur allows that the living continue until their own deaths, alongside successive births. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); ELMALILI; TABATABAI+]
- 23:37/q05/p2 Fathers or ancestors die while their children or descendants live on. — Al-Wahidi reports Mujahid's sequence of children then descendants, with al-Kalbi and Muqatil agreeing; al-Mawardi and al-Tabrisi attribute the father-child detail to al-Kalbi. Al-Alusi regards the stronger suggestion that children's survival counts as their parents' continued life as far-fetched. [10 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 23:37/q05/p3 Some people die while others remain alive, without specifying new births or family descent. — Ibn Isa and the anonymous reports distinguish this general coexistence from specific generational and father-child explanations. Al-Razi says this excludes any individual's return; Ibn Ashur adds that survivors continue only until they too die. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:37/q05/p4 It describes each person's ordinary worldly life followed by death, with the verbal order reversed in meaning. — Ibn Shajara and others paraphrase it as 'we live and die.' Al-Zajjaj and Ibn Ashur say waw need not impose chronology. Al-Qurtubi cites the paired commands in 3:43; al-Alusi cites a proposed parallel in 3:55. Al-Bursevi and Study Quran's R describe only this world's life and death. Al-Samin objects to using this verse itself to prove waw lacks sequence, since successive generations already explain the surface order. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 23:37/q05/p5 Dying means an individual's prior lifelessness, followed by birth and worldly life. — Al-Zajjaj speaks of humans beginning as lifeless matter, then living and dying. Al-Qurtubi specifies lifeless drops of semen before life. Al-Alusi finds the explanation from prebirth nonexistence far-fetched. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:37/q05/p6 Their children staying alive is counted figuratively as the dead parents' continued life. — Al-Alusi reports this proposal but regards it as far-fetched. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:37/q05/p7 The speakers actually meant that they die and then themselves live again. — Al-Samin reports this interpretation of return to life after death without specifying a mechanism; al-Alusi says individual return conflicts with the explicit denial of resurrection. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 23:37/q05/p8 It can describe transmigration: souls leave dead bodies and enter newly formed bodies. — Al-Alusi does not find this proposal far-fetched. Al-Tabatabai says it was common among idolaters, could include human or nonhuman bodies and was called 'birth after birth,' but fits the context poorly. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 23:37/q05/p9 They may have actually said 'we live and die,' while the quoted order was changed to suit the preceding clause. — Al-Alusi distinguishes a change in the report of their speech from merely treating waw as temporally unordered. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:37/q06 Is there a reported variant order for نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا?  [نَمُوتُ وَنَحْيَا · readings]
- 23:37/q06/p1 Ubayy and Ibn Masud read 'we live and die' in the reverse order. — Al-Nasafi attributes the reversed reading to both Ubayy and Ibn Masud. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL(أبي وابن مسعود)]

## 23:37/q07 What do the speakers deny by وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَبْعُوثِينَ?  [وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَبْعُوثِينَ · theology]
- 23:37/q07/p1 They deny being raised after death for an afterlife and judgment. — Ibn Zayd compares their denial with that of opponents in 34:7 and 34:3. Al-Baghawi adds being spread abroad after death; Abu Hayyan specifies resurrection for judgment. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan associate the denial with the unbelief of the materialist eternalists. Al-Bursevi counters their blindness by saying restoration is easier than initial creation and the creator of existence and nonexistence can restore life; he attributes the claim to Hud's people. Elmalili says they consider bodily resurrection from dust and bones impossibly remote. Asad says they also accuse the messenger of inventing lies. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 23:37/q08 How does the wording strengthen the final denial of resurrection?  [وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَبْعُوثِينَ · rhetoric]
- 23:37/q08/p1 Its coordinated nominal form makes the denial sustained and emphatic. — Al-Alusi says the final clause makes the denial continuous and emphatic. Ibn Ashur says coordination follows the restriction to immediate life and death, while nominal wording strengthens the assertion. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:37/q09 How does this claim connect to the surrounding argument?  [* · links]
- 23:37/q09/p1 It explains their earlier dismissal of resurrection and leads to their charge that the messenger invents lies. — Ibn Ashur says the sentence is separated from the previous one because it supplies the reason for their rejection of promised resurrection. Al-Biqai says emphatic denial of its possibility leads to the claim that worldly life is all they can have. Al-Razi says they infer from no resurrection that the prophet fabricated a lie against God and they will not believe him; al-Zamakhshari includes both his commission and resurrection in the alleged fabrication. Al-Samin says the clause explains their claim of only this worldly cycle. Kuranyolu says this passage gives a warning of painful fate while the Qur'an answers objections to the prophet and afterlife elsewhere. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:37/q10 Which other Quranic passages are linked to this denial?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources cite different parallel passages or thematic connections.
- 23:37/q10/p1 It parallels the denial in 6:29 while adding 'we die and live.' — Al-Burhan explicitly notes the added words compared with al-An'am; Study Quran identifies the same resemblance to 6:29. [2 notes: BURHAN; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:37/q10/p2 Other denials of resurrection and the Hour in 34:7 and 34:3 parallel it. — Ibn Zayd cites both passages through al-Tabari. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 23:37/q10/p3 A similar denial appears in Surat al-Jathiya. — Muqatil notes this parallel without a verse number in the note. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 23:37/q10/p4 The close with resurrection follows naturally from life's sequence and development, as 39:31 helps show. — Al-Samarrai describes resurrection as the final link in that sequence and mentions 39:31. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 23:37/q11 What does the chiefs' rejection reveal about their stance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Al-Jishumi highlights inconsistency in their argument; Kuranyolu explains the social interest behind opposition.
- 23:37/q11/p1 Their objection is inconsistent because they call following a living messenger loss while worshipping powerless, inanimate idols. — Al-Jishumi contrasts the alleged loss in obeying the messenger with their unquestioned idol worship: idols neither help nor harm. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 23:37/q11/p2 Powerful, affluent opponents fear afterlife accountability and a just society that would threaten their interests. — Kuranyolu says they benefit from the prevailing order and fear public awareness of universal rights and justice. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:37/q12 How does the denial fit the surah's wider pattern?  [* · links]
- 23:37/q12/p1 It belongs to the surah's recurring accounts of prophets facing rejection, often from affluent and powerful opponents. — Study Quran says the surah moves from worldly blessings to Noah's denial and rebuke by his people and his ark journey. It also identifies recurrent opposition from wealthy and powerful people, citing 7:59-62 and 7:136. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
