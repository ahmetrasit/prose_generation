# 55:64: 10 questions

## 55:64/q01 What does mudhāmmatān mean as a description of the gardens?  [مُدْهَآمَّتَانِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'green' is a simple gloss, a saturated green approaching black, literal blackness, or an account of the gardens' felt quality.
- 55:64/q01/p1 The gardens are intensely deep green, darkening toward black through thick growth and plentiful moisture. — Mujāhid, Muqātil, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, Abū ʿUbayda, Abū Isḥāq, al-Kalbī, al-Zajjāj and others tie the dark cast to water, dense growth, freshness or blessing. Al-Layth says crops acquire idhāmm when luxuriance darkens them; al-Khalīl and others define dahma as blackness. Alūsī traces duhma to night blackness and dark horses; Elmalılı calls the form a feminine dual intensive participle. Al-Naḥḥās reports Abū Ḥātim's acceptance of both idham and idhāmm in ordinary speech. Ibn Qutayba and al-Wāḥidī cite Dhū al-Rumma's Abyssinian image; al-Wāḥidī also cites al-Quṭāmī's green night, Dhū al-Rumma's green shadow, a ḥawāʾ garden, the khadrāʾ idiom for blackness or abundance, and Abū ʿAlī's contrast of white barren years in Aws's verse. Al-Qurṭubī adds dark horses and camels, a still blacker jaww, Labīd's dark troops in armorlike covering, Iraq's Sawād, green night and the use of greenery for a multitude. Al-Rāzī says black is the terminal color, white accepts every color whereas black accepts none, kāfir applies to black, and the Prophet's saying about joining the great majority illustrates sawād as a large community; cultivated land is called black against white bare land. Ibn al-Zubayr invokes 87:5. Asad cites Tāj al-ʿArūs for abundant watering and Qurʾānic green as ever-fresh life (18:31, 76:21, 55:76). Ibn ʿĀshūr's color account and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's tree luxuriance are reflected in their glosses. [56 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(أبو حاتم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة,أبو علي,ابن الأعرابي,ابن عباس,الكلبي,الليث,ذو الرمة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الزبير); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(لبيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الخليل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو صالح,ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير وأبو صالح); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]
- 55:64/q01/p2 They are green or verdant; some glosses intensify the green without specifying blackness. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās are reported with the simple gloss; Ibn Kathīr records parallel reports from Abū Ayyūb, Ibn al-Zubayr, Ibn Abī Awfā, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ, ʿAṭiyya, al-Ḥasan, Yaḥyā ibn Rāfiʿ and Sufyān. Al-Durr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmit Abū Ayyūb's report that the Prophet answered 'green.' Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says two transmitted glosses support his prior account of two images, though their fit is unclear. Ibn Kathīr attributes freshness to luxuriant interwoven branches. Elmalılı likens the emphatic green to Turkish yemyeşil and gömgök. [26 notes: MUJAHID(سعيد بن جبير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وابن الزبير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,محمد بن كعب); DURR-FULL(أبو أيوب الأنصاري,ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن جبير,عبد الله بن الزبير,عطاء بن أبي رباح,عكرمة,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وابن جبير وعكرمة وعطاء بن أبي رباح,النبي صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم عن أبي أيوب); ELMALILI(Resûl-i Ekrem,İbn Abbas); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 55:64/q01/p3 They are black or very dark. — Al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid deriving the description from dahma, blackness, like a dark horse. Al-Qurṭubī also reports Mujāhid's black or very dark gloss. Al-Durr reports Mujāhid's black gloss and reports Mujāhid, ʿIkrima and al-Ḍaḥḥāk explaining the blackness by abundant moisture. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد,مجاهد وعكرمة)]
- 55:64/q01/p4 The two gardens are at the highest degree of joy, beauty and freshness. — Al-Qāshānī gives these qualities as the purport of the description. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 55:64/q02 How is the dual description construed grammatically?  [مُدْهَآمَّتَانِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the dual is attached as an adjective to the earlier noun or read as a predicate with an implicit subject.
- 55:64/q02/p1 It modifies the previously mentioned two gardens. — Al-Naḥḥās explicitly identifies the two gardens as its antecedent; al-Ālūsī gives this construction before noting an alternative. [2 notes: NAHHAS; ALUSI-FULL]
- 55:64/q02/p2 It predicates an omitted subject: 'they are intensely green.' — Al-Ālūsī gives this as an alternative; Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the implicit subject and compares al-Fajr, which implies an oath verb, 'I swear by the dawn.' [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 55:64/q03 Why is the description separated from the mention of the gardens?  [مُدْهَآمَّتَانِ · rhetoric]
- 55:64/q03/p1 The interruption makes denial of either the gardens or their attribute deserving of censure. — Al-Ālūsī explains the effect of the intervening wording this way. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:64/q04 How do these gardens compare with the earlier pair?  [مُدْهَآمَّتَانِ · links]
turns on: Whether deep green points to ground vegetation and lower rank, merely marks a different stated quality, or is prized enough to favor these gardens.
- 55:64/q04/p1 The earlier pair rank higher, with more trees, shade, fruit or broader blessings; this pair's deep green suggests lower ground vegetation. — Al-Zamakhsharī, reported by al-Rāzī, compares foliage (55:48), springs (55:50, 55:66), fruit (55:52, 55:68) and furnishings (55:54, 55:76). Al-Rāzī contrasts branching growth overhead with cultivated black ground below. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Aṣbahānī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran explain the present color as dominant groundcover or herbs against the first pair's trees and fruit; al-Ālūsī also mentions blossoms and less shade. Ibn Kathīr says the first pair's flowing springs surpass the present pair's gushing springs. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says afnān includes several kinds of blessing, making the first pair's wording superior. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الأصبهاني); ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 55:64/q04/p2 The wording contrasts the earlier pair's branches with this pair's deep green; that alone does not settle all botanical differences. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says every garden has branches, even one less verdant than these. Al-Qurṭubī says the later pair is described by color alone and further differences may remain unstated. Ibn Kathīr gives the earlier pair branching boughs or diverse pleasures and these abundant watering and dark green. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 55:64/q04/p3 The present gardens may be preferred for their lush greenery. — Al-Ālūsī reports the argument that greenery is especially prized in gardens and indicates ample water, care and protection from withering. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 55:64/q05 To whom do the two pairs of gardens belong?  [مُدْهَآمَّتَانِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the two pairs reward one group or distinguish two ranks of people.
- 55:64/q05/p1 All four gardens belong to those who fear their Lord's station. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Ibn ʿAbbās assigning all four to this group. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 55:64/q05/p2 The first pair is for the forerunners and this later pair for their followers or the people of the right. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports al-Ḥasan's distinction between forerunners and followers; Iṣlāḥī distinguishes the muqarrabīn from the aṣḥāb al-yamīn. [2 notes: TABRISI(الحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 55:64/q06 What establishes this single word as an āya?  [* · sciences]
- 55:64/q06/p1 Mudhāmmatān is a one-word verse, whose boundary is fixed by transmitted arrangement. — Al-Burhān cites Ibn al-Munīr and Abū ʿAmr al-Dānī for its unique one-word status. Elmalılı defines the specific Qurʾānic āya as a complete unit marked by a pause and says this word can be recited and paused on as a complete utterance. He compares the two attributes of 1:3, the short units of 74:21–22 and longer verses 2:255, 5:6 and 2:282; āya also means an evident sign or, by extension, a section conveying a divine ruling. He says varying verse length and final letters mean boundaries and order are tawqīfī, known by report, as with the separate Ḥā-Mīm and ʿAyn-Sīn-Qāf of 42:1–2. [12 notes: BURHAN(أبو عمرو الداني,ابن المنير); ELMALILI]

## 55:64/q07 Must this one-word verse be semantically self-contained?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Whether recitational completeness requires independent syntax or semantic independence.
- 55:64/q07/p1 It can be recited on its own with the force of a complete utterance. — Elmalılı says the word admits a pause and independent recitation. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies the implicit subject in its predication. [2 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:64/q07/p2 A verse ending need not itself be a complete semantic unit. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says meaning may attach a fāṣila to what precedes or follows it; his two discussions use examples including this verse. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 55:64/q08 Is mudhāmmatān the shortest verse?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The measure used for shortest: counted words, letters, or disjoint letters.
- 55:64/q08/p1 It is the shortest by word count; Ṭā Hā is shortest by disjoint letters. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the two measures. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 55:64/q08/p2 Al-Ḍuḥā and then al-Fajr are shorter candidates; mudhāmmatān and thumma naẓara are rejected under letter or word counts. — Al-Burhān gives the debt verse as longest at 128 words and 540 letters and uses counts to assess the shortest candidates. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 55:64/q09 What does this one-word verse imply for the scale of the Qurʾanic challenge?  [* · theology]
- 55:64/q09/p1 The challenge is at surah length because an individual verse can be only one word. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī adds that even the short surahs al-Kawthar and al-Ikhlāṣ are inimitable, so Qurʾanic speech at that length is miraculous (108:1, 112:1). [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 55:64/q10 What is reported after Jābir ibn Zayd recited this verse?  [* · reports]
- 55:64/q10/p1 He bowed. — Al-Durr transmits the report about Jābir ibn Zayd. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(جابر بن زيد)]
