# 87:5/q05 What does أَحْوَىٰ modify, and at what stage? [أَحْوَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether dark color belongs to the final debris or the earlier pasture, and whether syntactic reordering is required.
- 87:5/q05/p1 It qualifies ghuthāʾ: the pasture becomes dark, dry refuse.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabarī and al-Naḥḥās prefer the direct order because inversion is needless when it makes sound sense. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call this the apparent syntax; Ibn Jarīr also cites transmitted exegetical explanations against inversion. Al-Samīn rejects Abū al-Baqāʾ's analysis as fronting part of a relative clause. Al-Ṭabrisī also describes a circumstantial parse for blackened pasture after drying.

# 87:5/q06 Why is أَحْوَىٰ placed after غُثَآءً? [غُثَآءً أَحْوَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give different rhetorical reasons for the same order.
- 87:5/q06/p1 Its delayed place suits the verse endings or rhyme.
  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burhān and al-Itqān give this reason for separating the dark-green description from pasture; al-Burhān also describes dark-green chaff.
  holders (5 notes): ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN
  notes: ABUHAYYAN:87:5/r12 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p455/r16 BURHAN:v1p303/r4 BURHAN:v3p279/r7 ITQAN:v3p34/r2
- 87:5/q06/p2 Its delayed place keeps both green life and dark death available in the wording.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī says putting the color second avoids locking it to green alone and lets the two terms evoke both life and deat
