# 87:5/q10 What does the plant's change teach? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Different authors draw lessons about power, mortality, resurrection, warning, guidance, growth and spiritual attention.
- 87:5/q10/p1 It displays God's power, governance and free choice over life, death and opposing states.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī contrasts divine freedom with necessary natural effects, using fire as his example. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees God's power to create and end; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the stages governance. Asad calls decay a metonym for life and death. Corpus Coranicum reads the perfects aspectually, seeing recurring growth and decay as ongoing sustenance, comparing 53:43–49, 96:1–5 and 95:4–5.
  holders (10 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN

- 87:5/q10/p2 Worldly freshness and pleasure are fleeting, and living things end.
  reasons: Qatādah and al-Qurṭubī compare prosperity fading; al-Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress the quick sequence, with 10:24's vanished harvest. Qutb links every living thing's end to the enduring afterlife, 87:16–17. Ibn ʿĀshūr also compares human weakness, strength and weakness in 30:54.

# 87:5/q11 Where and how do coal deposits form in the geological reading? [* · interpretation]
turns on: The mechanism of accumulation: transport from afar or growth and deposition at the site.
- 87:5/q11/p1 Plants accumulated and decayed where they grew in marshy land hollows.
  reasons: Elmalılı prefers local accumulation: deep transported floating masses are implausible. Rooted trees, fine leaf impressions, aquatic spores, separate basins and parallel seams indicate quiet marsh deposits. He reports that long times suffice and cites forest growth estimates; ancient carbon-rich air may have accelerated growth. The cited account describes repeated submergence, slight rot and fermentation, mineral gases and oils, then pressure and earth heat hardening lower seams; heated wood pressed in wet clay reportedly yielded coal-like material.

# 87:5/q12 What ancient setting and timescale does the coal account describe? [* · reports]
- 87:5/q12/p1 A very long sequence of luxuriant ancient growth, submergence, decay and burial produced coal.
  reasons: The cited Keşfü’l-Esrâr account imagines an initially flat water-covered earth, large early algae and sulfur-family plants, pre-animal coal flora and mountains burying plants. It invokes hot humid fog, rain and dim light, giant fern forests, and fossils across latitudes; reports roughly 250 fern and 120 gymnosperm species in European coal beds against about 50 and 25 living species. It proposes a thin elastic crust over fluid, lunar and solar tides and subsidence repeatedly submerging forests. Elmalılı compares the long timescale with shell-built limestone and cites preserved stems, leaves and trunks as evidence of plant origin.

# 87:5/q13 How does this verse fit its surrounding sūrah? [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize divine governance, the surah's cosmic and afterlife themes, and a shift in audience.
- 87:5/q13/p1 It completes a display of creation and divine governance that grounds glorification.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the first four verses cover composed creation and establish absolute lordship; pasture's change exemplifies governance. Iṣlāḥī says 87:1–5 direct the Prophet to glorify the God whose attributes show gradual maturation.
  holders (3 notes): TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR

- 87:5/q13/p2 Its cosmic scene prepares the sūrah's ensuing message and enduring afterlife.
  reasons: Qutb describes the whole cosmos as praising, with reassurance to the Prophet and themes of oneness, revelation and recompense, rooted in 87:18–19. He links withering to 87:16–17 and says the cosmic frame suits neighboring sūrahs.
