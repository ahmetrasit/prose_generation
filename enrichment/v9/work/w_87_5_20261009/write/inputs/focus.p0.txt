# 87:5/q01 What does غُثَآءً mean? [غُثَآءً · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names the final dried refuse or vigorous vegetation.
- 87:5/q01/p1 Dried, broken plant matter and mixed refuse, especially debris swept by floods to a valley edge.
  reasons: Mujāhid compares it to flood debris; al-Mubarrad describes broken vegetation left at a valley edge; al-Suddī and Ibn Zayd stress flood transport. Al-Ṭabarī adds windblown remains. Al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı cite use for mixed scraps or mixed crowds; al-Qurṭubī also mentions floating foam and refuse. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah notes its figurative use for worthless people. Al-Biqāʿī describes abundant matter drying, breaking and collecting as froth.

  reasons: Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr attest both pronunciations.

# 87:5/q04 What shade does أَحْوَىٰ denote? [أَحْوَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the relevant darkness is a faded plant's color, luxuriant greenness, or another dark hue.
- 87:5/q04/p1 Black, or a dark shade tending to black.
  reasons: Al-Jishumī and others derive it from ḥawah, blackness; al-Kisāʾī cites ḥawiya al-nabāt and rain-grown dark valleys. Dhū al-Rumma's dark lips and gums, al-Aʿshā's lip, a gazelle's striped back, and a dark-backed fawn are poetic examples. Al-Aʿlam says it tends to black; al-Qazzāz traces broader black usage from a horse-coat term.
