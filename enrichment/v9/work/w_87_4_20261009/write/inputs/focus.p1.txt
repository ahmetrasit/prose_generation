# 87:4/q07 What do the perfect verbs imply about the time of this act? [أَخْرَجَ · grammar]
- 87:4/q07/p1 They present an ongoing, recurring act of creation and sustenance rather than an inherently past event.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum calls the perfects aspectual and points to the recurring growth and decay in verses 4–5. It compares 53:43–49, 96:1–5, and 95:4–5.
  holders (1 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:2-5:anmerkung/r1
sources: CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments)

# 87:4/q08 What does the producing of pasture demonstrate? [* · theology]
turns on: The notes draw different lessons from the same growth and change: divine exclusivity, resurrection, governance over life, or gradual nurturing.
- 87:4/q08/p1 God alone can produce it, whereas the idols cannot.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī uses the production of grass to contrast God's power with the worshipped idols; his fuller account says God alone brings forth pasture.
  holders (2 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL
  notes: RAZI:87:4/r2 RAZI-FULL:v31p127/r12
- 87:4/q08/p2 The yearly restoration of plants from soil demonstrates God's power to resurrect the dead.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī calls vegetation the clearest horizon sign of resurrection: pasture returns after breaking down into soil, and gathering the dead is no harder for God than restoring plants.
  holders (4 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:4/r1 BIQAI:87:4/r3 BIQAI-FULL:v8p395#2/r4 BIQAI-FULL:v8p395#2/r5
- 87:4/q08/p3 Its growth and change show divine governance and power over living creation.
  reasons: Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites growth and the transition to dark refuse as divine governance. Study Quran speaks of God's power over creation and destruction of living things; Kuran Yolu offers a metaphor of life followed by death.
  holders (3 notes): TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR
  notes: TABATABAI:87:1-19#2/r4 STUDYQURAN:87:4/r1 KURANYOLU-TEFSIR:87:1/r5
- 87:4/q08/p4 The successive stages display nurturing that brings creation to completion little by little.
  reasons: Al-Ālūsī reads the successive descriptions as gradual development. Iṣlāḥī says verses 1–5 show the gradual maturation of creation and of human physical and intellectual capacities.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: ALUSI:87:5/r11 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r3
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BIQAI-FULL = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; ISLAHI-TADABBUR = Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR = Hayreddin Karaman, Mustafa Çağrıcı, İbrahim Kâfi Dönmez, Sadrettin Gümüş; RAZI = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606; RAZI-FULL = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606; STUDYQURAN = Seyyed Hossein Nasr (ed.-in-chief), Caner K. # 87:4/q09 How does the verse connect with the preceding descriptions of God? [* · links]
turns on: Each source selects a different link in the surrounding sequence: beneficiaries, kinds of signs, scale of imagery, or relative-clause structure.
- 87:4/q09/p1 After blessings particular to humans, it mentions a blessing for other creatures.
  reasons: Al-Rāzī gives this shift in both his shorter and fuller accounts.
  holders (2 notes): RAZI; RAZI-FULL
  notes: RAZI:87:4/r1 RAZI-FULL:v31p127/r12
- 87:4/q09/p2 It moves from signs within the self to a visible sign across the horizons.
  reasons: Al-Biqāʿī identifies vegetation as the strongest horizon sign of resurrection after signs within the self.
  holders (2 notes): BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL
  notes: BIQAI:87:4/r1 BIQAI-FULL:v8p395#2/r4
- 87:4/q09/p3 It completes a survey of vast existence and glorification with an evocative plant image.
  reasons: Quṭb describes this movement from cosmic scope to plant life.
  holders (1 notes): QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:4/r1
- 87:4/q09/p4 The repeated relative pronoun highlights each divine attribute separately.
  reasons: Ibn ʿĀshūr says the repetition before measuring and producing pasture singles out each attribute, while fa-hadā marks guidance as the effect of measure.
  holders (1 notes): IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p276/r5

# 87:4/q10 How is this wording linked to Q 79:31? [* · links]
- 87:4/q10/p1 Both passages speak of God bringing out pasture; the other passage also names water and human and animal provisions.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum observes the verbal parallel with 79:31, which brings out water and pasture. Elmalılı cites 79:31–33 for plants, trees, and fruits used by humans and animals.
  holders (2 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM; ELMALILI
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:87:4:anmerkung/r1 ELMALILI:87:4-5/r1
sources: CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments); ELMALILI = Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi Yazır, d. 1361

# 87:4/q11 How does the wider sūra frame the verse? [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize different sūra-wide frames: cosmic praise, doctrinal foundations, or the change in addressee and reassurance.
- 87:4/q11/p1 The whole cosmos becomes a sanctuary of praise, and the Prophet receives assurance of preservation and ease.
  reasons: Quṭb describes the cosmic glorification and good news for the Prophet's mission and affairs.
  holders (1 notes): QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r3
- 87:4/q11/p2 Its foundations are God's oneness as creator, revelation, and afterlife recompense, rooted in earlier scripture.
  reasons: Quṭb adds the ease of message, messenger, and community and points to 87:18–19.
  holders (1 notes): QUTB-ZILAL
  notes: QUTB-ZILAL:87:1/r4
- 87:4/q11/p3 The sūra shifts from warning persistent deniers to reassuring the Prophet about preaching.
  reasons: Iṣlāḥī says the previous sūra urges patience because the deniers' respite is temporary and they cannot escape God. Here and for at least ten following sūras, the primary address turns from stubborn Quraysh to the Prophet and dispels his fears; verses 1–5 show gradual maturation.
  holders (3 notes): ISLAHI-TADABBUR
  notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r2 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p270/r3
sources: ISLAHI-TADABBUR = Amin Ahsan Islahi (d. 1997); QUTB-ZILAL = Sayyid Quṭb, d. 1386

# 87:4/q13 What is reported about the Prophet's attachment to and recitation of this sūra? [* · reports]
- 87:4/q13/p1 He loved it and recited it with al-Ghāshiyah at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes both on one day.
  reasons: Quṭb cites ʿAlī for the Prophet's love of the sūra and Muslim for the prayer recitations, linking al-Ghāshiyah to 88:1.
