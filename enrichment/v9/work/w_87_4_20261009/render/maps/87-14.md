# 87:14: 11 questions

## 87:14/q01 What does aflaha promise?  [أَفْلَحَ · meaning]
- 87:14/q01/p1 Attaining one's desired good and lasting felicity, including salvation and Paradise. — Al-Zajjāj and Ibn Jawzī explain enduring life and success; Wāḥidī specifies lasting residence in Paradise. Al-Ālūsī and Bursawī pair escape from feared harm with attainment of hoped-for good. Jishumī calls falāḥ attainment of one's goal; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes both victory and benefit, citing 2:5. Biqāʿī says every desired good; Quṭb distinguishes worldly sweetness of remembrance from afterlife rescue and bliss. [26 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:14/q02 Why use qad and the perfect tense for success?  [قَدْ أَفْلَحَ · rhetoric]
- 87:14/q02/p1 They present the promised success as certain and announce good news after the warning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 23:1 and 91:9 and says the wording encourages the wicked hearers to join those who fear God. Bursawī says qad prepares listeners for the good outcome and answers an implied question about the rememberer. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:14/q03 What does the form tazakkā convey?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · grammar]
- 87:14/q03/p1 It conveys deliberate effort to become pure and cultivate the self. — Ibn ʿĀshūr derives striving from the tafaʿʿala form, compares the purification and corruption of 91:9–10, and says the process begins with monotheism and includes good works and almsgiving. Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes cultivating the self toward good. [5 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 87:14/q04 Why name the purifier where the rememberer might be expected?  [مَن تَزَكَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 87:14/q04/p1 The wording describes the rememberer by the prior trait of purification. — Al-Ālūsī says the expected expression would identify the one who remembers; naming the purifier instead characterizes that person by the state that precedes remembrance. [1 notes: ALUSI]

## 87:14/q06 Who is the successful person in this verse?  [مَن · referent]
turns on: Whether man is read as a general description or linked by particular reports to ʿUthmān or Abū Bakr.
- 87:14/q06/p1 Any person who purifies himself and responds to God's reminder. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies this person with the one who fears and remembers in 87:9–10; Corpus Coranicum says the messenger exemplifies a general appeal to believers. Other commentators describe a general pattern of belief, worship and charitable action. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB; JISHUMI; BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:14/q06/p2 ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān. — A report through ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says ʿUthmān gave an orchard in exchange for a hypocrite's leaning palm whose fallen dates fed an Anṣārī family, after the hypocrite refused the Prophet's promise of a tree in Paradise; 87:11 concerns that hypocrite. Qurṭubī gives no grading. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI(عطاء عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس)]
- 87:14/q06/p3 Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk reports that the sūra or verse came down concerning him. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI(الضحاك)]

## 87:14/q07 How does purification lead into remembrance and prayer?  [تَزَكَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sequence expresses a spiritual progression, the timing of charity and prayer, or their combined effect.
- 87:14/q07/p1 Purification is the foundation; remembrance brings knowledge of God and prayer expresses obedience. — Rāzī, Bursawī and Ibn ʿĀshūr order the stages as clearing false belief, knowing or recalling God's names and perfections with fear and hope, then bodily service. Ibn ʿĀshūr says a pure soul accepts guidance and cites 29:45; the Study Quran adds heart, tongue and action. [9 notes: RAZI; JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الإمام); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:14/q07/p2 The sequence can describe almsgiving before prayer, especially fiṭra before Eid prayer. — Ibn Masʿūd and Abū al-Aḥwaṣ apply the order to giving before prayer; Ibn ʿUmar checks charity before leaving for Eid. Rāzī objects that the Qurʾān usually names prayer before alms; Biqāʿī and others maintain that fiṭra precedes Eid prayer in practice. Versions specify remembrance as Eid takbīrs or remembrance on the way to the prayer ground. [21 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو الأحوص,ابن مسعود); THALABI(ابن عمر,ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA(أبو سعيد الخدري وابن عمر وابن المسيب,قتادة وكثير من المتأولين); RAZI(عكرمة وأبو العالية وابن سيرين وابن عمر); QURTUBI(أبو سعيد الخدري وابن عمر,ابن عباس والضحاك,عكرمة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI(الرازي); BIQAI-FULL(الرازي في اللوامع); DURR(أبو الأحوص,ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(أبو الأحوص,ابن مسعود); STUDYQURAN(Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq); RAZI-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 87:14/q07/p3 Together these acts cleanse faults and bring the whole person into worship. — Biqāʿī says they strip self-admiration and defects, acquire perfections, and echo therapeutic stages and the sūra's opening; Abū Ḥayyān sees worship and charity together. Qutb describes a living bond with God here and bliss hereafter. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 87:14/q08 What does the verse teach about the path to success?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes vary in whether they emphasize faith and outward practice, inward discipline, or detachment from worldly desire.
- 87:14/q08/p1 Success follows purification, remembrance and prayer, with faith and obedient deeds. — Muqātil names testimony, alms and five prayers; Jishumī combines obligations with avoidance of grave sins and says the actions are human acts. Abū Ḥayyān joins worship and charity. Qutb gives success in this life and the next; Islahi ties it to heeding the reminder and God's pleasure. [9 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:14/q08/p2 The verse calls for fear of God privately and publicly and inward freedom from appetite and fixation. — Sahl and Tustarī stress fear in secret and public; al-Jurayrī names appetites, whim and unruly temperament. Qushayrī includes sins, faults and fixation on creatures; Qāshānī speaks of cleansing the self's qualities and bodily darkness after spiritual readiness. [5 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(الجريري,سهل); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 87:14/q08/p3 Purification includes detachment from worldly attachments and spending for God's sake. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects this with the contrast in 87:16; spending frees attachment to wealth and the earth, while ablution symbolizes removing traces of bodily acts. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 87:14/q09 Do these verses specifically concern Eid al-Fiṭr, despite the sūra's chronology?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether Eid reports date the verses themselves, apply a general earlier wording to a later practice, or conflict with a wholly Meccan date.
- 87:14/q09/p1 Yes; reports apply verses 14–15 to fiṭra and Eid prayer, and the ending may have been revealed in Medina. — Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās and reports from the Imams support the Eid application. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the opening is Meccan but the ending Medinan; Study Quran reports the same view. These distinguish the sura's opening from its later verses. [10 notes: IBNASHUR(ابن عمر وابن عباس); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); STUDYQURAN(family of the Prophet); QURTUBI(قتادة وعطاء وأبو العالية); DURR(ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر)]
- 87:14/q09/p2 The sūra is Meccan, so its original reference cannot require Eid or fiṭra, which did not yet exist there. — Thaʿlabī calls the Meccan dating a consensus; Ibn Jawzī prefers Ibn ʿAbbās's faith reading. Rāzī cites the chronology and the Qurʾān's usual prayer-before-zakāt order; Qurṭubī says the majority call the sūra Meccan. Kuran Yolu distinguishes general early financial duties from later formal zakāt. [7 notes: THALABI; RAZI(الثعلبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 87:14/q09/p3 A Meccan verse can praise or include a practice instituted later. — Baghawī compares predictions of the Prophet's lawful entry into Mecca at 90:2 and Badr at 54:45; Wāḥidī and Qushayrī say God knows future obedience. Biqāʿī appeals to the general wording and says revelation can precede the ruling. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI(الواحدي); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); QURTUBI(القشيري); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 87:14/q10 How does this promise relate to the sūra's warning and opening?  [* · links]
turns on: Which part of the sūra or other verse is taken as the key to this promise: the immediate warning, the opening command, or parallel accounts of purification and success.
- 87:14/q10/p1 It balances the warning about the most wretched with the reward of those who fear, heed the reminder and purify themselves. — Muqātil contrasts Meccan unbelievers who neither purify nor pray. Rāzī supplies unbelief as purification's object from the warning. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the verse a resumption after punishment and identifies the purified with those who fear and remember. Corpus Coranicum describes a balance, and a chiasm between responses in 10–11 and outcomes in 12–15, comparing 92:5–11 and 14–21; it says the reminder's implied content includes fear, purification, invocation and prayer. [12 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI; BIQAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:14/q10/p2 Purification and prayer reprise the opening command to glorify God and complete its account of guidance. — Biqāʿī sees paired omissions: fear implies its opposite and misery implies happiness; fire's opposite and purification's opposite are likewise implied. Al-Malawī describes the concision as exchanging cause and consequence. Biqāʿī says the end returns to 87:1's glorification and soul-cleansing, showing guidance is purposeful. Corpus Coranicum sees verse 15 recalling the opening's Creator praise and the public aim of verse 6's revelation. [12 notes: BIQAI(الملوي); BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 87:14/q10/p3 The purification here is linked to similar Qurʾānic statements about succeeding through piety and self-purification. — Rāzī cites 23:1–2 and 2:5 for success of humble believers and the God-conscious. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 91:9–10 for purified versus corrupted souls; Corpus Coranicum sends readers to 91:9. Al-Intiṣāf's author cites 87:14 to support God as subject of purification at 91:5, keeping oath pronouns consistent and reading purification as a passive-like result. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ALUSI(صاحب الانتصاف)]
- 87:14/q10/p4 The wider sūra frames revelation, divine unity, and afterlife recompense as a message to the messenger and wider audience. — Qutb says the Prophet loved this nineteen-verse Meccan sūra and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayers; its cosmos praises God and its revelation promises preservation and ease. Corpus Coranicum says the messenger's address can be exemplary for believers; the social ethic implied in purification is less explicit than Q 90 and Q 107. [6 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:14/q11 Does 'this' in the closing scripture statement include verse 14?  [* · links]
- 87:14/q11/p1 Yes; it refers to the nearby passage on purification, prayer, preference for this life and the lasting afterlife. — Ṭabarī prefers 87:14–17 as the nearest antecedent of the demonstrative and says ṣuḥuf means Abraham's and Moses' books. Abū Dharr's report has the Prophet recite these verses when asked about earlier scriptures; a further report says the Prophet recited the passage when asked what he received from those scrolls. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says that report supports the broader referent. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبي ذر); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); TAB-FULL+; TAB+]
