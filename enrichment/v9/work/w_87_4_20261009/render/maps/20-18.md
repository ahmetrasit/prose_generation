# 20:18: 29 questions

## 20:18/q01 Who speaks, and what does “it” refer to?  [قَالَ هِىَ · referent]
- 20:18/q01/p1 Moses speaks; “it” points back to the object God asked about. — Yaḥyā identifies Moses as speaker; al-Samīn identifies the antecedent as the thing asked about. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; SAMIN-DURR]

## 20:18/q02 Why does Moses call it “my staff”?  [عَصَاىَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the possessive simply identifies his relation to the staff, introduces its uses, or is spiritually reproached.
- 20:18/q02/p1 It identifies his possession and establishes why he carries it. — Al-Māwardī says “staff” alone would identify the object; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say “my” locates it in his right hand and prepares for the ensuing actions. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:18/q02/p2 The possessive claim is reproached because Moses does not truly own or control the staff. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ, Ibn al-Jawharī, and al-Qurṭubī’s report relate the later serpent and command to cast it down to this rebuke. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الجوهري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الجوهري)]

## 20:18/q03 Where did Moses' staff come from?  [عَصَاىَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on its source, transfer, and wood.
- 20:18/q03/p1 It descended from Paradise with Adam, passed through prophets to Shuʿayb, and came to Moses from him. — Tustarī calls it Paradise myrtle and mentions marriage; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it basil and a house of prophetic staffs; Jishumī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, Bursevī/Kāshifī, and Study Quran vary in details. Ibn Kathīr calls Adam provenance an apparent Israelite report. [9 notes: TUSTARI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); STUDYQURAN(traditional Islamic lore); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 20:18/q03/p2 Gabriel gave it to Moses at night on his way to Midian. — Muqātil supplies the time, messenger, and destination. [1 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل)]
- 20:18/q03/p3 It was made of buckthorn or thorn wood. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports buckthorn as an alternative; al-Ālūsī cites Wahb's thorn tree, ten cubits tall. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL(وهب)]
- 20:18/q03/p4 It was made of Paradise myrtle. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies myrtle; Tustarī and Bursevī also specify a Garden myrtle. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); TUSTARI; BURSEVI]
- 20:18/q03/p5 Its wood was nabʿa. — Al-Kashshāf reports the wood as nabʿa; Abū Ḥayyān also gives nabʿa as a name. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 20:18/q06 How is عَصَاىَ read?  [عَصَاىَ · readings]
turns on: The readings vary in the final alif and yāʾ, with dialect and hiatus offered as explanations.
- 20:18/q06/p1 With the ordinary final alif and open possessive yāʾ. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn call this the majority/common reading; al-Zajjāj prefers it. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says possessive yāʾ must open after a maqṣūr noun, comparing 6:162 and 2:38. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري، ابن أبي إسحاق); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ZAJJAJ+]
- 20:18/q06/p2 As ʿaṣī, replacing the alif with yāʾ and assimilating it. — Al-Zajjāj calls this Hudhayl usage, cites grammarians and Abū Dhuʾayb; Ibn Abī Isḥāq and al-Jaḥdarī are named by later sources; 12:19 is a parallel. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو ذؤيب); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL(قُرئ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق والجحدري); SAMIN-DURR(الجحدري، ابن أبي إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق والجحدري,شاعرهم)]
- 20:18/q06/p3 With kasra on the final yāʾ. — Al-Ḥasan, and in some transmissions Abū ʿAmr and Ibn Abī Isḥāq, resolve adjacent sukūns; sources compare 14:22 and غلامي. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وأبو عمرو); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 20:18/q06/p4 With a quiescent final yāʾ. — Ibn Abī Isḥāq, Abū ʿAmr, and al-Jaḥdarī are variously reported; al-Ālūsī explains the tolerated hiatus as pausal. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو إسحاق والجحدري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو، ابن أبي إسحاق); ALUSI-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق)]

## 20:18/q07 What grammatical properties of the word for staff are noted?  [عَصَاىَ · grammar]
- 20:18/q07/p1 ʿAṣā is grammatically feminine, with several plural forms. — Al-Ālūsī lists ʿiṣī, aʿṣ and aʿṣāʾ, and cites al-Farrāʾ against the solecistic ʿaṣātī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also calls the noun feminine. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); TABATABAI]
- 20:18/q07/p2 Its possessive suffix follows the special rule for a noun ending in alif. — Al-Zajjāj explains why ordinary pre-yāʾ kasra is impossible after alif, dual alif or existing yāʾ; Hudhayl substitutes yāʾ instead. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes forms that permit open or unvowelled yāʾ from mandatory opening after maqṣūr nouns. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:18/q08 How does أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ attach syntactically to the answer?  [أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ · grammar]

- 20:18/q08/p2 It is the predicate of an omitted-verb construction taking “my staff” as object. — Abū al-Baqāʾ reports this parse; al-Samīn says not to adopt it. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 20:18/q11 How is أَهُشُّ read?  [أَهُشُّ · readings]

Al-Biqāʿī cites Abū Ḥayyān for root softness; al-Qurṭubī distinguishes ḍamma in flock use from fatḥa in cheerful use, cites Shammar, al-Rāʿī’s verse and soft-person usage; al-Rāzī cites Thaʿlab on vowels in flock, man and bread expressions. ## 20:18/q12 How does the wording govern the flock and foliage?  [أَهُشُّ عَلَىٰ غَنَمِى · grammar]
turns on: The object may be omitted or the flock may be the direct object; the preposition is explained by the verb's sense.
- 20:18/q12/p1 The verb omits its foliage object while ʿalā marks the sheep who receive the leaves. — Al-Samīn, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī explicitly supply the omitted leaves or trees; al-Ṭabrisī compares verb alternations. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النخعي); ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 20:18/q12/p2 With the sīn reading, ʿalā follows the implied sense of driving toward or over the flock. — Al-Ṭabrisī notes that bihā ghanamī would be expected; al-Samīn calls it inclusion of a governing verb; al-Ālūsī imagines bending over the flock as if striking. [3 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الحسن، عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 20:18/q12/p3 A variant construction makes the flock the direct object. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī report it; al-Ālūsī cannot identify its verb reading. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL(جماعة)]

## 20:18/q13 How is غَنَمِى read?  [غَنَمِى · readings]
turns on: Readings differ over the nūn and the syntactic form.
- 20:18/q13/p1 The regular form leaves the nūn vocalized in the genitive construction. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a group reading “my sheep” in the genitive. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:18/q13/p2 Some make the nūn quiescent. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya knows no justification; al-Samīn says it is not productive by analogy; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also record it. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(جماعة)]
- 20:18/q13/p3 Another vocalization makes the flock the verb's direct object. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report this variant. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL(جماعة)]

[14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(يحيى); TUSTARI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(وهب); QURTUBI-FULL(أعرابي,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(وهب)]
- 20:18/q18/p3 It reached branches or drew water when a well rope was short. — Wahb's hooked or forked staff bends or breaks branches; Ibn ʿAbbās and other accounts say it lengthened a rope or acted as a bucket. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,وهب); THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أعرابي,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون,قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,وهب)]
- 20:18/q18/p4 It herded, defended, and guarded Moses' flock and himself. — Sources mention turning back distant sheep, warding off predators, enemies, snakes, scorpions and vermin; some accounts make the defense miraculous. [23 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,وهب); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(أعرابي,ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون,قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,وهب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(‘Ukbar ī)]
- 20:18/q18/p5 It lit the way, kindled fire, kept company, or marked prayer time. — Tustarī mentions prayer time; al-Māwardī mentions kindling; al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn ʿAbbās and others describe light, speech, or companionship. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:18/q18/p6 It produced food, water, fruit, or springs. — Accounts differ on whether it was planted, struck the earth, grew fruit, or released springs; Ibn ʿAbbās reports several forms. - 20:18/q18/p7 It could do other practical work such as gathering fodder or serving as a prayer aid. — Tustarī adds fodder; al-Māwardī mentions drawing water; al-Qurṭubī's Bedouin lists prayer, travel, warmth, crossing water and a substitute weapon. [3 notes: TUSTARI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أعرابي)]

## 20:18/q19 Were the staff's extraordinary uses established before the serpent sign?  [وَلِىَ فِيهَا مَـَٔارِبُ أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Ibn Kathīr questions the extraordinary preexisting-use reports because of Moses' fear.
- 20:18/q19/p1 Some accounts attribute extraordinary powers to it already. — These include light, autonomous guarding, fruit, water, movement and speech in Ibn ʿAbbās and other reports; al-Bayḍāwī says God creates the signs rather than the wood possessing inherent power. [11 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 20:18/q19/p2 Ibn Kathīr rejects those accounts as apparent Israelite stories. — He argues that if Moses knew such powers, he would not have fled when the staff became a serpent; he also groups the Adam and end-time beast claims with those stories. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الآخر,بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 20:18/q20 What previously hidden benefits does God reveal through the staff?  [مَـَٔارِبُ أُخْرَىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:18/q20/p1 Its later miracles include becoming a serpent and striking rock and sea. — Tustarī, Sahl and Study Quran say Moses did not yet know these uses; al-Qushayrī calls the serpent a proof of his truth; al-Qurṭubī notes the magicians believed through the signs. [7 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,سهل); QUSHAYRI(ويقال); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Tustarī)]
- 20:18/q20/p2 The staff's greatest benefit was occasioning God's address to Moses. — Jaʿfar calls that greater than all the other uses; al-Rāzī says the divine question itself disclosed an additional benefit. [2 notes: SULAMI(جعفر); RAZI-FULL]
- 20:18/q20/p3 The question exposes how little even prophets know of a present object. — Tustarī, Sahl, al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Study Quran take the unknown uses as evidence of the limits of human reason beside divine knowledge. [5 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(al-Tustarī)]

## 20:18/q21 How is the staff's later transformation understood?  [عَصَاىَ مَـَٔارِبُ أُخْرَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Sources differ between the wood's ordinary incapacity, divine creation of signs, and spiritual lessons.
- 20:18/q21/p1 The wood has no inherent power; God creates its signs and transforms it. — Al-Bayḍāwī explicitly denies inherent powers; Study Quran reports al-Rāzī's account that a harmless piece of wood becomes a mighty serpent. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 20:18/q21/p2 The humble stick displays God's power through a surprising miracle. — Al-Ālūsī reports an explanation that mā asks its description or kind and tilka minimizes it before the transformation; al-Kashshāf also contrasts ordinary uses with the great event. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 20:18/q21/p3 The serpent warns against attachment to anything besides God. — Al-Rāzī compares it with Abraham's disavowal of false gods at 26:77 and cites an ungraded hadith about unpaid zakat wealth returning as a serpent. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:18/q22 Why does Moses add uses after naming the staff?  [قَالَ هِىَ عَصَاىَ أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ عَلَيْهَا وَأَهُشُّ بِهَا عَلَىٰ غَنَمِى وَلِىَ فِيهَا مَـَٔارِبُ أُخْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources variously read the added details as affection, gratitude, an implied question, anticipation of a miracle, or a concise account.
- 20:18/q22/p1 He delights in God's address and wants the conversation to last. — Fāris, al-Qushayrī, al-Rāzī, al-Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Itqān and Islahi say this; a poet's wish to prolong reproach illustrates it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the explanation without preferring it. [15 notes: SULAMI(فارس); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشاعر); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:18/q22/p2 He gives thanks for the gift and shows the staff is useful rather than idle. — Abū Bakr al-Warrāq sees gratitude; al-Māwardī says Moses shows reliance on it; Ibn al-Jawzī repeats him; al-Nasafī combines gratitude with modest summary. [5 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:18/q22/p3 The uses answer a second explicit or implicit question about what he does with it. — Al-Wāḥidī posits a follow-up; Ibn ʿAbbās and Wahb, Jishumī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān describe implicit or dual questions; al-Ālūsī calls split-question proposals weak, and Abū Ḥayyān rejects treating بيمينك as a separate question. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ووهب); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:18/q22/p4 God's question elicits known ordinary uses before revealing a greater miracle. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Burhān, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī take the list as preparation; al-Ālūsī cites the distant demonstrative and vocative, while rejecting a proposal that this was merely to stop Moses fearing a snake. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:18/q22/p5 He knew God asked about its qualities, so his description answers directly. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this: obvious-object questions elsewhere at 6:78, 101:1–4 and 69:1–3 elicit description; al-Ālūsī reports a related description reading. [4 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 20:18/q22/p6 Awe made him begin in detail but end with a summary. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī report disrupted speech; al-Ālūsī says the repeated call consoles him and cites a report of Moses trembling and speechless. Al-Biqāʿī instead sees respectful brevity and eagerness to listen. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أحمد); BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:18/q22/p7 He names the principal benefits and summarizes or omits the rest. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls these his greatest uses; Ibn al-Jawzī offers three reasons for brevity: attention to God's speech, divine knowledge, or essential versus incidental uses. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī also note summary. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:18/q22/p8 He explained his need for it lest God tell him to cast it away. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr compares God's earlier command to remove his sandals. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 20:18/q22/p9 The fuller reply is an accepted way to add helpful information. — Al-Qurṭubī counts four extra details and compares Prophetic replies about seawater and pilgrimage; Abū Ḥayyān compares such expanded Prophetic answers. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 20:18/q23 Why are his own use and the flock's use mentioned in that order?  [أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ عَلَيْهَا وَأَهُشُّ بِهَا عَلَىٰ غَنَمِى · rhetoric]
- 20:18/q23/p1 He names his immediate personal benefit before the benefit to his dependents. — Abū Ḥayyān notes the sequence; al-Ālūsī suggests he had just leaned on it or that leaning best matched the right-hand question. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:18/q23/p2 The sequence can be contrasted with Muhammad's concern for his community. — Al-Rāzī invokes 8:33, a guidance prayer and an ungraded resurrection report; al-Ālūsī reports the comparison but warns it has slight basis. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:18/q24 What readings are reported for وَلِىَ فِيهَا?  [وَلِىَ فِيهَا · readings]
turns on: Readers differ between opening and quiescence of the possessive yāʾ.
- 20:18/q24/p1 Ḥafṣ and al-Azraq from Warsh open the yāʾ. — Ibn Mujāhid says this is Ḥafṣ's only opened form in his listed set; Ibn al-Jazarī counts it among al-Azraq's five open wa-lī places; al-Nasafī identifies Ḥafṣ's form. [3 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم); NASAFI-FULL(حفص); NASHR]
- 20:18/q24/p2 Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn Kathīr, and the listed Kūfan and Syrian readers use sukūn in their stated sets. — Ibn Mujāhid differentiates their other yāʾ forms: Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn Kathīr, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ibn ʿĀmir, and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,ابن كثير,حمزة، الكسائي، ابن عامر، عاصم عن أبي بكر,نافع); QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ)]
- 20:18/q24/p3 The difference is a licensed open versus unvowelled first-person yāʾ. — Qirāʾāt Encyclopedia describes pausal versus connected form; al-Sāmarrāʾī says vowel treatment depends on language and transmitted readings. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:18/q25 What does the staff symbolize in inward readings?  [عَصَاىَ أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ عَلَيْهَا وَأَهُشُّ بِهَا عَلَىٰ غَنَمِى · inward]
turns on: The inward readings identify the staff with different faculties and treat dependence on it differently.
- 20:18/q25/p1 It represents intellect used to gain spiritual perfection, and the sheep represent animal faculties. — Al-Qāshānī says Moses shakes useful sciences and practical wisdom from the tree of spirit and uses the staff for stations, states and disclosures. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:18/q25/p2 It represents the tranquil soul, while the flock represents limbs, senses and bodily powers. — Bursevī reports knowers' reading: discipline effaces illusions, brings hidden aims, and can turn disobedience to obedience, citing 25:70; the serpent can be receptive to faith, as in the Medina jinn report. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعض أهل المعرفة)]
- 20:18/q25/p3 The staff represents prophetic care for people who require guidance and defense. — Bursevī likens Satan and ego to wolves and a lion, calls prophets shepherds, and cites Ḥāfiẓ's couplet about service to Shuʿayb. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الحافظ)]
- 20:18/q25/p4 The staff's usefulness tests whether Moses attributes gifts to himself or relies on God. — Al-Qushayrī says a novice abandons means and the detailed list should make Moses trust God enough to cast it away; another reading says all benefits come from God, supported by poetry. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]

## 20:18/q26 What teaching is drawn from holding or leaning on a staff?  [عَصَاىَ أَتَوَكَّؤُا۟ عَلَيْهَا · law]
turns on: Reports and authors differ in the specific benefits and applications they emphasize.
- 20:18/q26/p1 Carrying a staff is prophetic or recommended even without physical need. — Maymūn reports Ibn ʿAbbās calling it a prophetic sunnah and sign of belief; al-Ḥasan lists six benefits, and al-Ālūsī recommends it even to someone who does not need bowstring support. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن البصري,قيل,ميمون بن مهران); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:18/q26/p2 It is useful in worship, sermons, and travel. — Al-Qurṭubī cites authentic reports of the Prophet's prayer barrier and walking stick, ʿUmar's eleven-rakʿa prayer with people leaning on staffs, Solomon and Ibn Masʿūd's preaching, consensus for preachers, Mālik's travel examples, and Arab oratory; he answers Shuʿūbiyya criticism. [13 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أعرابي,السائب بن يزيد,مالك)]
- 20:18/q26/p3 It can be used as a warning or corrective object in the household. — Al-Qurṭubī claims correcting wives with a staff can improve relations and cites reports about Abū Jahm, ʿUbāda's counsel, and a visible whip. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبادة بن الصامت)]
- 20:18/q26/p4 Its use can remind a believer that earthly life is a journey. — Al-Qurṭubī cites an ascetic and a poem saying a resident carries it by choice to remember travel rather than age or weakness. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:18/q26/p5 Tapping branches instead of stripping them conserves grazing. — Al-Ālūsī says it permits regrowth for others. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:18/q27 How do sources compare Moses' answer with Muhammad's conduct?  [* · links]
turns on: Some comparisons claim a difference in rank, while al-Ālūsī doubts their basis.
- 20:18/q27/p1 Some read Moses' mention of the staff as lesser attention than Muhammad's focus on God and his community. — Al-Rāzī contrasts created things with Muhammad's undistracted vision at 53:17, compares leaning with reliance on mercy at 8:64 and 5:67, cites a prayer of praise, and parses ومن اتبعك in 8:64 as included in God's sufficiency. Al-Ālūsī repeats the comparisons. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:18/q27/p2 Al-Ālūsī says those comparisons have only a very slight basis. — He reports them to warn readers against being impressed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:18/q28 How is this staff episode connected with other passages?  [* · links]
- 20:18/q28/p1 The staff signs recur elsewhere and are compared with earlier scriptural accounts. — Study Quran names 27:10, 28:31 and Exodus 4:2; it also connects the inherited-staff lore with 7:106–108. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(traditional Islamic lore)]
- 20:18/q28/p2 Other words and readings in the surrounding passage have their own parallels. — Al-Ṭabrisī contrasts Ibn ʿĀmir's readings of أشدد/أشركه with supplicatory readings and explains السيرة, الجناح, and جناح poetry; al-Farrāʾ glosses poetic لا نخفه as “do not reveal it.” [7 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبيدة,ابن عامر,الراجز); FARRA]
- 20:18/q28/p3 The choice of “staff” rather than minsaʾa fits its merciful use for sheep. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts 34:14. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:18/q29 What does the right hand in the preceding prompt denote?  [* · links]
- 20:18/q29/p1 It first means the bodily right hand, with extended senses of strength and oath. — Jishumī gives the lexical sequence for yamīn in God's prompt to Moses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
