# 43:9: 25 questions

## 43:9/q01 Who is addressed as the potential questioner?  [سَأَلْتَهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the surrounding consolation fixes a particular addressee or the conditional address is general.
- 43:9/q01/p1 The Prophet Muhammad. — Ibn ʿĀshūr relates this to the passage's consolatory address to him. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:9/q01/p2 Any potential questioner. — Ibn ʿĀshūr also allows the address to be general. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:9/q02 Who are the people denoted by 'them' and 'they'?  [سَأَلْتَهُم لَيَقُولُنَّ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent of the plural pronouns: the nearer unbelievers or the prophets.
- 43:9/q02/p1 The Meccan or Arab polytheists, especially Quraysh. — Al-Rāzī prefers the nearer antecedent, the unbelievers, while acknowledging a possible prophetic referent. Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Wāḥidī, and many commentators identify Muhammad's people; al-Biqāʿī says Quraysh knew the earlier peoples' histories. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 43:9/q02/p2 The prophets. — Al-Rāzī mentions the possibility but favors the nearer unbelieving antecedent. Some commentators reported by Study Quran take the following verses 43:10–13 as potentially continuing the prophets' answer. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 43:9/q03 How does this question connect to the preceding passage?  [وَلَئِن سَأَلْتَهُم · links]
turns on: The notes point to different contextual connections: an interrupted address, earlier peoples, or the monotheistic argument after 43:6.
- 43:9/q03/p1 It resumes the preceding address after an interruption about earlier messengers. — Al-Ālūsī says the verses beginning 'How many messengers We sent' (43:6) interrupt to affirm the point and console the Prophet. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 43:9/q03/p2 It builds on Quraysh's knowledge of earlier peoples. — Al-Biqāʿī says Quraysh heard their histories and would admit what they knew; God had made them generally truthful, preparing their admission about creation. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 43:9/q03/p3 It turns the passage into an argument for God's oneness against the Meccans. — Ibn ʿĀshūr relates it to the promise of victory in 43:6 and the contradiction of rejecting the Messenger's call to one God while admitting God created all worlds; their admission implicitly denies idols a right to worship. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:9/q04 What is the force of the emphatic conditional wording?  [وَلَئِن لَيَقُولُنَّ · rhetoric]
- 43:9/q04/p1 It makes their answer certain while dramatizing their incoherence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the prefatory oath lām, the answer lām, and the emphatic nūn; treating an undisputed fact as debatable heightens the contradiction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:9/q05 Why is their admission elicited through a staged question?  [سَأَلْتَهُم · rhetoric]
- 43:9/q05/p1 A direct question exposes a truth they disregard in conduct and prayer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they immediately answer Allah when asked, then return to association of partners. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 43:9/q06 What does مَّنْ mean here?  [مَّنْ · meaning]
- 43:9/q06/p1 It is the interrogative 'who'. — Bursevī explicitly glosses the interrogative. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:9/q07 What do the heavens and earth denote here?  [ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ · referent]
- 43:9/q07/p1 The upper and lower cosmic bodies. — Bursevī supplies this description of the two terms. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:9/q08 What does creating the heavens and earth mean?  [خَلَقَ · meaning]
- 43:9/q08/p1 Bringing the heavens and earth into existence. — Al-Rāzī understands 'created' as bringing into being; al-Qurṭubī likewise says God brought them into existence. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 43:9/q09 Why does the verse state creation explicitly?  [خَلَقَ · rhetoric]
- 43:9/q09/p1 The opponents appeared to deny origination, so their claim needed an explicit creation statement. — Al-Burhān describes them as apparently materialists or deniers for whom the heavens' creation had to be said outright. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 43:9/q10 Why is خَلَقَهُنَّ repeated in the answer?  [خَلَقَ خَلَقَهُنَّ · rhetoric]
- 43:9/q10/p1 It stresses the admission and answers the question in meaning, though a syntactically matching answer would be nominal. — Abū Ḥayyān says repetition intensifies emphasis and notes that مَّنْ is a subject, so literal syntactic correspondence would require a nominal answer. Al-Samīn illustrates that 'Who stood?' could be answered merely 'Zayd.' Al-Biqāʿī says the direct answer Allah would suffice, but repetition strengthens the rebuke. Al-Ālūsī follows Abū Ḥayyān's distinction between semantic and grammatical matching. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 43:9/q11 How can a short 'Allah' answer to a creator question be supplied grammatically?  [خَلَقَهُنَّ · grammar]
- 43:9/q11/p1 An omitted predicate can be understood from the explicit question. — Al-Burhān gives questions about who created heaven and earth or sent rain, with 'Allah' as the answer. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 43:9/q12 What does this answer show about the word Allah in 43:87?  [خَلَقَهُنَّ · links]
- 43:9/q12/p1 Allah is the agent of an understood verb, not a predicate starting a subjectless sentence. — Al-Samīn says the explicit verb here confirms nominative Allah as agent in 43:87 rather than an initial subject. Al-Itqān rejects the subjectless-predicate analysis on the strength of this parallel. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN]

## 43:9/q13 Whom do the respondents acknowledge as creator?  [خَلَقَهُنَّ ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · interpretation]
- 43:9/q13/p1 God alone, the mighty and knowing Creator. — Muqātil calls Him mighty in dominion and knowing of creation; al-Ṭabarī says mighty in power and vengeance and knowing all within creation. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Wāḥidī, says the people thereby affirm His justice and knowledge. Al-Ṭabrisī adds His knowledge of creatures' interests. Abū Ḥayyān observes that elsewhere their answer uses Allah, the comprehensive name for the supreme attributes. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares their answer in Luqmān 31:25; Study Quran cites 10:31, 23:84–85, 29:61, 29:63, 31:25, 39:38, and 43:87. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد,فتح الرحمن); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 43:9/q14 Did the respondents themselves say 'the Mighty, the Knowing'?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the titles are the speakers' own words, implicit in their acknowledgment, or part of the Qur'an's expanded presentation.
- 43:9/q14/p1 Yes, these titles can be their actual words. — Al-Bayḍāwī offers this reading. Abū Ḥayyān holds that their own wording may be these titles despite other verses giving 'Allah.' Bursevī reports it as another possibility. Ibn al-Munīr, reported by al-Ālūsī, parses the titles as their answer; al-Ālūsī prefers that as more apparent from the wording. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 43:9/q14/p2 The titles convey the truth of their attribution to God without requiring that they uttered them. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the attributes may be implicit in the content of their answer, effectively 'God.' Al-Irshād, reported by Bursevī, and al-Zamakhsharī, reported by al-Ālūsī, say they ascribe creation to the One who truly has these attributes without necessarily using or consciously describing Him by these titles; al-Ālūsī accepts this as sufficient. Elmalılı says admission of creation entails these and other divine attributes. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Meccans actually said Allah as in 31:25, and would also affirm that He is mighty and knowing if asked. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:9/q14/p3 The Qur'an gives an expanded divine formulation beyond an expected simple 'God' answer. — Fath al-Raḥmān, reported by Bursevī, says the expected answer was that God created them and the Qur'anic wording introduces God's following attributes, distinct from the reported answer. [1 notes: BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن)]

## 43:9/q15 Where does the reported answer end?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the answerers are the idolaters or prophets, and how the change to divine first-person speech is understood.
- 43:9/q15/p1 It ends by the divine titles, and the following description is God's speech. — The author of al-Naẓm, reported by al-Wāḥidī, points to the shift from the unbelievers' answer to 'for you' rather than their expected 'for us'; the attributes still name the same God. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Zamakhsharī say God's own description follows the answer. Al-Bayḍāwī proposes a new sentence after the actual answer. Abū Ḥātim, reported by al-Nasafī, normally keeps alladhī connected to al-ʿAlīm, but allows a pause by supplying huwa. Ibn al-Munīr and al-Ālūsī place God's continuation after the titles; al-Ālūsī finds 'We brought forth' in verse 11 natural after a speaker shift and compares Moses' speech followed by 'We brought forth' in 20:52–53. Elmalılı points to the third-to-first-person shift in the revival statement. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو حاتم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير); ELMALILI; BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 43:9/q15/p2 On a prophetic referent, verses 43:10–13 may continue the answerers' quotation. — Study Quran reports this possibility from some commentators. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 43:9/q16 What does 'the Mighty' signify?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ · meaning]
- 43:9/q16/p1 God has overwhelming, unconquerable power and dominion. — Muqātil stresses dominion; al-Ṭabarī adds power and vengeance. Al-Rāzī derives perfect power from the capacity to overpower. Al-Biqāʿī says He overcomes everything and nothing overcomes Him. Al-Ṭabrisī says He is almighty. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 43:9/q17 What does 'the Knowing' signify?  [ٱلْعَلِيمُ · meaning]
- 43:9/q17/p1 God's knowledge is complete and encompasses creation. — Muqātil says He knows His creation; al-Ṭabarī says He knows them and everything within heaven and earth. Al-Ṭabrisī adds that He knows creatures' interests. Al-Rāzī calls it perfect knowledge; al-Biqāʿī says it encompasses all things. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 43:9/q18 Why are might and knowledge highlighted together?  [ٱلْعَزِيزُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · rhetoric]
- 43:9/q18/p1 They display the Creator's power and knowledge against the idols' helplessness. — Al-Rāzī says complete knowledge and power make creation of every possible thing possible, before the passage details God's works. Al-Biqāʿī says the names entail unconquerable power and comprehensive knowledge. Elmalılı says their admission entails these and earlier attributes. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the names embarrass those who turn to weak, ignorant idols, which cannot even protect themselves from human hands. Kur'an Yolu says the idolaters never treated idols as God's equals in every quality and knew creation requires great power and knowledge. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:9/q19 What proof of God is drawn from the world's creation?  [* · theology]
- 43:9/q19/p1 The world's origination and God's agency provide the first proof of God. — Al-Rāzī says theologians make this the first divine attribute and first proof, taking creation as bringing the world into being. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 43:9/q20 What does their admission imply for worship and lordship?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on exclusive worthiness of worship or on exclusive governance of needs.
- 43:9/q20/p1 Only the Creator deserves worship; their idols and partners have no comparable claim. — Al-Kalbī says they contradict their confession by worshiping idols. Al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, and others expose the inconsistency. Ibn ʿĀshūr says idols are created stones and only the worshippers' Creator deserves worship. Al-Biqāʿī stresses the objects of worship lack power and knowledge and their own answer refutes their excuse. Ibn Kathīr says they nonetheless worship idols and rivals. Study Quran cites parallel admissions in 10:31, 23:84–85, 29:61, 29:63, 31:25, 39:38, and 43:87. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 43:9/q20/p2 God's creative acts also establish His sole governance of human needs. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 43:9–14 argue that God alone is Lord because His acts of creation govern human needs; the polytheists nonetheless assign governance to others. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 43:9/q21 What does this confession imply about resurrection?  [* · theology]
- 43:9/q21/p1 Denial of resurrection contradicts acknowledgment of God's creative power. — The scholars of meaning reported by al-Wāḥidī call this extreme ignorance. Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Rāzī say they deny His power to resurrect while admitting His creation. Study Quran reports the same denial and cites other confession passages. Islāḥī says cosmic signs support both divine oneness and the Day of Judgement. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 43:9/q22 Can their confession count as faith while they remain in shirk?  [* · theology]
- 43:9/q22/p1 Yes, faith can denote a partial profession that coexists with contradiction. — Al-Kalbī, reported by al-Wāḥidī, calls their confession a form of faith despite idol worship. Al-Dāmghānī says faith may mean professing God while remaining in shirk; he compares the People of the Book accepting some scripture while rejecting other parts and the messengers. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); DAMGHANI]

## 43:9/q23 How do the notes explain continued idol worship despite this confession?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources attribute the practice to ignorance, obstinacy, or hopes of intercession and benefit.
- 43:9/q23/p1 It exposes extreme ignorance or failure to live by their acknowledged truth. — The scholars of meaning reported by al-Wāḥidī call the combination the height of ignorance. Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, and Bursevī likewise call it ignorance; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they disregard the Creator in life and prayer and immediately return to shirk after answering. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 43:9/q23/p2 Their own admission reveals deliberate obstinacy. — Al-Biqāʿī says the idols have neither power nor knowledge, so the confession refutes the idolaters' excuse. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 43:9/q23/p3 They treat idols as intermediaries who can bring benefit, avert harm, and draw them closer to God. — Kur'an Yolu cites 10:18 and 39:3 for this explanation. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 43:9/q24 What grounds recognition of God's creative power in human beings?  [* · theology]
- 43:9/q24/p1 Primordial covenantal testimony planted recognition of divine lordship and creation. — Bursevī says the covenant's affirmation bears fruit in worldly recognition of creative power, citing 7:172; only God's gracious attraction opens the way to His majesty. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 43:9/q25 How does the argument continue in the following verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
- 43:9/q25/p1 It unfolds God's works to support exclusive lordship, resurrection, and the sūra's challenge to idolatry. — Ibn al-Jawzī says the next verse continues the rebuke (43:10). Al-Rāzī says the passage details the Creator's works after naming perfect power and knowledge. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 43:9–14 joins creation to governance of human needs. Islāḥī says 43:9–15 joins cosmic signs to tawḥīd and the Day of Judgement and treats the whole sūra as a harmonious unit. Study Quran says the sūra answers Makkan objections and forms of disbelief. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
