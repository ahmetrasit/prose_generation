# 28:23: 41 questions

## 28:23/q01 What does وَرَدَ mean here?  [وَرَدَ · meaning]
turns on: The range of wurūd in Arabic and other Quranic passages.
- 28:23/q01/p1 Moses reached or approached the water, without entering it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares the wider range with 19:71; al-Qurṭubī cites Zuhayr's poetry; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the arrival also marks entry into Midian's territory; al-Ālūsī excludes drinking. [19 notes: DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,علي بن عبيد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(زهير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن فارس); BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:23/q01/p2 Wurūd's primary sense is setting out to seek water; reaching, a water place, thirst, entering, and presence are extended uses. — ʿAlī b. ʿUbayd Allāh distinguishes the outward trip (wurūd) from return (ṣadr), and explains the senses of place and thirst; Ibn al-Jawzī cites 11:98, 19:71, 12:19 and 19:86 for other Quranic uses. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو عبد الرحمن اليزيدي,أهل التفسير,علي بن عبيد الله,قوم)]

## 28:23/q02 What was مَاءَ مَدْيَنَ?  [مَآءَ مَدْيَنَ · referent]
- 28:23/q02/p1 It was Midian's watering well or water source, where livestock were watered. — Ibn ʿAbbās compares it to a known local well; al-Qushayrī describes troughs; Bursawī places it three miles or less outside town; Iṣlāḥī cites the Torah's well and notes that a narrow spring would pose the same crowding problem. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains 'water' as a place name for the watering site and territory. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:23/q02/p2 The water was named for Midian, son of Abraham, or for his descendant tribe. — Muqātil gives the eponym; al-Qurṭubī calls Midian also a tribe descended from him and cites a poem. [3 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(شاعر غير مسمى)]

## 28:23/q03 How is مَدْيَنَ declined?  [مَدْيَنَ · grammar]
- 28:23/q03/p1 It is genitive in sense but diptote because it is a proper place name. — Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and al-Qurṭubī identify the town name as the reason. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:23/q04 What does لَمَّا do in this account?  [وَلَمَّا · grammar]
- 28:23/q04/p1 It marks when Moses' reaching the place was followed by finding the people there. — Ibn ʿĀshūr states the temporal relation; al-Zarkashī describes possible responses to lammā, including a present verb with past sense, and cites 17:67, 21:12, and 11:74. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 28:23/q05 What does أُمَّةً mean here?  [أُمَّةً · meaning]
- 28:23/q05/p1 A group or large crowd of people, chiefly shepherds. — Al-Suddī stresses its size; al-Kashshāf and al-Ālūsī describe varied people; al-Zarkashī lists other Quranic senses: leader, religion, and period (43:22; 12:45); Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 2:213. [25 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 28:23/q05/p2 The group numbered forty. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies this count. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 28:23/q06 Why does the verse say مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ?  [مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The force of the partitive phrase and its relation to the indefinite ummah.
- 28:23/q06/p1 It indicates the crowd comprised different kinds or classes of people. — Al-Ālūsī also reads the indefinite ummah as suggesting abundance. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:23/q06/p2 It disparages the crowd as merely low people, or clarifies that they were human. — Al-Ālūsī reports both alternatives. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 28:23/q07 What does وَجَدَ mean and how does its clause work?  [وَجَدَ · grammar]
- 28:23/q07/p1 It means encountered or came upon; تذودان describes the women rather than serving as another object. — Al-Dāmghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī compare the queen as another case of finding; al-Samīn specifies the participial clause's syntactic role. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); SAMIN-DURR]

## 28:23/q08 Where were the people in relation to the water?  [عَلَيْهِ · interpretation]
- 28:23/q08/p1 At the well's rim or watering place. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī interpret ʿalayhi spatially as its bank or edge. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:23/q09 What were the crowd watering?  [يَسْقُونَ · interpretation]
- 28:23/q09/p1 Their livestock, including sheep. — Ibn Isḥāq reports livestock and sheep; al-Ālūsī cautions that narrowing the animals requires a report. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:23/q09/p2 The verse leaves the species of the watered beings deliberately open. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Kashshāf and Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz: livestock or people are possible and attention rests on the women’s withdrawal. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف)]

## 28:23/q10 Why did Moses come to the water?  [مَآءَ مَدْيَنَ · reports]
- 28:23/q10/p1 He came seeking water or drink and a gathering where he could find help. — Al-Biqāʿī says he intended to drink; Ibn ʿĀshūr says a newcomer naturally goes to the water to meet companions and a host. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:23/q10/p2 He arrived after an exhausting eight-night walk from Egypt, hungry, barefoot, and without resources. — Ibn ʿAbbās says he ate leaves, lost his soles, and became so thin green matter was visible in his belly; Ibn Isḥāq adds fear, no servant, mount or provisions; Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq also reports the exhausting flight. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,محمد بن اسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 28:23/q11 Where were the two women relative to the crowd?  [مِن دُونِهِمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dūn marks lower ground or Moses' side of the crowd.
- 28:23/q11/p1 They were apart from the crowd, on Moses' approach side, so he encountered them first. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say they were on his side; al-Biqāʿī says nearer the approach. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives dūn from 'lower' extended to a farther place and discusses min as locative with 62:9 and 3:10. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:23/q11/p2 They stood below the people watering, at a lower place. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī take dūn spatially as lower; Abū Ḥayyān records Ibn ʿAṭiyya's competing approach-side gloss; al-Ālūsī allows below, nearby, or apart. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

Majority attribution is reported by al-Wāḥidī and al-Ālūsī. [13 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مجاهد والضحاك والسدي والحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد والضحاك والسدي والحسن); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ITQAN; BURSEVI(السهيلي); ALUSI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(tefsirler); STUDYQURAN(R)]

- 28:23/q12/p3 They were daughters of a righteous believer of Midian, with no established kinship to Shuʿayb. — Al-Baghawī says he believed in Shuʿayb; al-Ālūsī treats both unrelated and nephew possibilities as unproved; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls him unnamed. - 28:23/q12/p4 The flock owner and marriage arranger was their uncle, whom they called father. — Al-Ālūsī reports this explanation from al-Baḥr. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 28:23/q13 What names are given for the women?  [ٱمْرَأَتَيْنِ · reports]
turns on: Names vary among narrative reports.
- 28:23/q13/p1 They were twins Ṣabūra and ʿAbrā, with Ṣabūra older by half a day. — Muqātil supplies the names and age detail; Ibn al-Jawzī repeats them; Abū Ḥayyān attributes them to al-Farrāʾ. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)]

## 28:23/q14 What does تَذُودَانِ mean in Arabic?  [تَذُودَانِ · meaning]
- 28:23/q14/p1 To restrain, turn back, or drive away. — Al-Farrāʾ restricts the ordinary object to livestock; al-Ṭabarī disputes this with a Prophetic basin report and a poet's use for people. Jarīr, Saʿīd or Suwayd b. Kurāʿ, and other verse attest restraint and repulsion; al-Naḥḥās notes leading can count as restraining. Ibn ʿĀshūr says driving stock from water is literal, people figurative, and notes uncertainty about sheep in the strict lexicon. [28 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الشاعر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سويد بن كراع); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(قطرب); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(شاعر غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 28:23/q14/p2 A dhawd is a small group of camels, according to Abū ʿUbayd thirty to ninety females only. — Al-Jishumī also notes the related livestock term. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عبيد)]

## 28:23/q15 What were the women holding back, and why?  [تَذُودَانِ · interpretation]

## 28:23/q16 Is there an expanded reading of ٱمْرَأَتَيْنِ تَذُودَانِ?  [ٱمْرَأَتَيْنِ تَذُودَانِ · readings]
- 28:23/q16/p1 A reported reading supplies حابستين or حابستان, explicitly calling the women restrainers. — Al-Farrāʾ attributes ḥābistatān to ʿAbd Allāh; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī mention codices with ḥābistatayn. [3 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المصاحف); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:23/q17 Why are objects omitted from the watering and restraining verbs?  [يَسْقُونَ تَذُودَانِ لَا نَسْقِى يُصْدِرَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the unspoken object is intentionally generalized, recoverable by context, or elided for brevity.
- 28:23/q17/p1 The actions and agents matter; animal identity is immaterial to Moses' response. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī identifies four omissions, including Moses' later watering, and says naming sheep would wrongly narrow the lesson; his Buḥturī example shows how omission shifts focus. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Zarkashī agree; Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects reliance on Israelite material to supply sheep. [16 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB; JURJANI-DALAIL(البحتري); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشري); ITQAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الشيخان,الكشاف)]
- 28:23/q17/p2 The objects remain intended and are omitted for concise expression. — Ibn Qutayba and al-Wāḥidī supply sheep; al-Fārisī cites 18:2 and 41:13 and poetic ellipsis; al-Sakkākī counts five omitted objects with the causative reading. Al-Jazarī argues the weak sheep heighten compassion, which al-Zarkashī disputes. [12 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(الجزري,السكاكي); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(السكاكي)]
- 28:23/q17/p3 The object is left vague to prompt the listener or because context supplies it. — Al-Qurṭubī offers both rhetorical explanations; al-Biqāʿī says 'we do not water' has an understood object. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:23/q18 What did Moses ask with مَا خَطْبُكُمَا?  [مَا خَطْبُكُمَا · meaning]
turns on: The force of khaṭb and, in one rare reading, its vocalization.
- 28:23/q18/p1 He asked their situation, need, or purpose in standing apart and not watering. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Isḥāq connect it to their separation; the latter says he felt pity under crowd domination. Lexicographers cite a poem or Ruʾba's rajaz for khaṭb as affair; al-Māwardī and al-Jishumī say it connotes weight or importance; al-Kashshāf derives it from what one seeks, like shaʾn. [39 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,ابن عباس,الراجز); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,محمد بن اسحاق); NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,محمد بن إسحاق); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(رؤبة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 28:23/q18/p2 The question expressed concern and an offer to help. — Ibn Isḥāq describes compassion; Iṣlāḥī calls it concern rather than casual curiosity; al-Ālūsī says its gracious wording implicitly offered assistance; al-Kāshifī attributes prophetic gentleness. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:23/q18/p3 With Shammar's anomalous kasra of khāʾ, he asked who their husband was and why he did not water. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn call it rare; al-Ālūsī finds an objection to it unclear. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(شمر); SAMIN-DURR(شمر); ALUSI-FULL(شمر)]

## 28:23/q19 How is مَا خَطْبُكُمَا parsed?  [مَا خَطْبُكُمَا · grammar]
- 28:23/q19/p1 It is a subject and predicate question. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this parse; Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the speech as an explanatory substitution for the circumstance of finding the women apart. [2 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:23/q19/p2 Interrogative echo forms agree with the echoed noun in gender, number, and case. — Sībawayh gives ayya, ayyayn, ayya(t), and ayyāt; for a proper name he uses man ʿAbd Allāh rather than nominative ayy ʿAbd Allāh. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 28:23/q20 Why did the women wait before watering?  [لَا نَسْقِى حَتَّىٰ يُصْدِرَ ٱلرِّعَآءُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether practical weakness, social modesty, or both explain the delay; whether the statement is habitual.
- 28:23/q20/p1 They could not compete with men at the crowded water; after the shepherds departed they used leftover water in troughs. — Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Isḥāq and others describe their lack of strength or inability to jostle. Muqātil says the shepherds returned to pasture first. Al-Rāzī says Moses' help let them return early; al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī mention the remaining water. - 28:23/q31/p1 A man may speak with unrelated women for a real need when safe from temptation or harm. — Al-Jishumī states the condition; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī infer permission from Moses' question; Bursawī grounds Moses' safety in prophetic protection and compares a Prophet marrying without witnesses. ## 28:23/q32 What does the daughters' tending livestock teach about women's work and public conduct?  [ٱمْرَأَتَيْنِ وَأَبُونَا شَيْخٌۭ كَبِيرٌۭ · law]
turns on: How broadly to extend the conduct as a legal precedent and how custom and need affect it.
- 28:23/q32/p1 Their public work is permissible, especially from need, with modest conduct; custom affects propriety. — Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish Bedouin and settled customs; al-Ālūsī says a prophet's allowance is not forbidden; Ibn ʿĀshūr derives a woman's property management and public appearance while covering what must be covered from un-abrogated prior law. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:23/q32/p2 Their work was compelled by necessity and illustrates separate spheres for men and women. — Iṣlāḥī says they stayed apart and criticizes reformers advocating identical work spheres. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:23/q32/p3 The episode supplies precedents for livelihood, marriage guardianship, labor as dower, joining marriage and hire, and lawful hiring. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists these as rulings of prior prophetic law unless abrogated. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:23/q33 What duties and liabilities does a shepherd have?  [ٱلرِّعَآءُ · law]
turns on: Whether the shepherd acted as a trustworthy agent exercising judgment or exceeded authority.
- 28:23/q33/p1 A trustworthy shepherd is not liable for lost or stolen animals without evidence of betrayal. — Mālik and al-Muhallab treat shepherds and agents as trustees; al-Qurṭubī allows liability for a known corrupt shepherd; Bursawī cites the hadith that everyone is responsible for those in their care and distinguishes human from animal shepherds. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(المهلب,مالك); BURSEVI]
- 28:23/q33/p2 A shepherd who slaughters a dying sheep on reasonable judgment is not liable if he brings the slaughtered animal. — Ibn al-Qāsim's view; a report of a girl using a stone and the Prophet allowing its meat supports permissibility. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن القاسم,كعب بن مالك)]
- 28:23/q33/p3 He is liable for such slaughter until he proves its necessity. — Ibn al-Qāsim reports this competing view. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن القاسم)]
- 28:23/q33/p4 Breeding the owner's females without permission does not make the shepherd liable for loss. — Ibn al-Qāsim reasons that breeding develops the owner's property; al-Qurṭubī prefers latitude for conscientious judgment. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن القاسم)]
- 28:23/q33/p5 He is liable for losses caused by breeding without permission. — Ashhab takes the stricter view. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أشهب)]

## 28:23/q34 What marriage payment and delay rules are drawn from Moses' later service?  [* · law]
- 28:23/q34/p1 At least a quarter dinar should precede consummation, though the marriage remains valid without it. — Ibn al-Qāsim gives the threshold; later Mālikīs recommend advancing all or some dowry. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن القاسم)]
- 28:23/q34/p2 Beginning the agreed shepherd service counts as paying its dower. — Al-Qurṭubī applies this to a service dowry. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 28:23/q34/p3 Unconditioned consummation delay can last a lifetime; a stipulated delay needs a sound purpose. — Al-Qurṭubī gives wedding preparation or a young bride's readiness as examples. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:23/q35 What moral lessons does the Midian encounter teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:23/q35/p1 It commends helping the weak, generous cooperation, compassion, chastity, gratitude, modesty, and fidelity. — Al-Jishumī commends aiding the weak; al-Biqāʿī urges help despite one's own effort; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists Moses' virtues and Shuʿayb's hospitality, protection and gentleness, and urges emulation. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:23/q35/p2 One should flee with faith, rely on God, and trust deliverance and provision from oppression. — Al-Jishumī calls oppression a human act, so escape is a meaningful means to salvation. [4 notes: JISHUMI]
- 28:23/q35/p3 Hardship can preserve and raise righteous prophets and teach detachment from worldly ease. — Al-Biqāʿī compares the material hardship of two prophets. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:23/q35/p4 Prophets may go unrecognized by ordinary herdsmen; believers and knowledge of prophetic law are few. — Bursawī draws this lesson from the livestock. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 28:23/q35/p5 Moses' virtues before his mission invite comparison with Muhammad's known character before revelation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this can guide Meccan polytheists toward recognizing revelation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:23/q36 What inward meanings are assigned to the Midian water and its figures?  [* · inward]

- 28:23/q36/p1 Arrival at Midian's water is an inward arrival at intimacy, gnosis and divine converse. — Abū Bakr b. - 28:23/q36/p2 The water is unveiling; its drinkers are saints and seekers who nourish their faculties and disciples. — Al-Qāshānī gives this first symbolic identification. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 28:23/q36/p3 The water is unveiling; its drinkers are sacred intellects and disembodied spirits nourishing souls and the cosmos. — Al-Qāshānī presents this alternative. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 28:23/q36/p4 The women are theoretical and practical intellects below the spring; their flock is faculties held from unveiling. — Al-Qāshānī says they subsist on rational knowledge and practical philosophy until Moses as heart arrives; they then receive the higher beings' overflow. Their aged father symbolizes spirit. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 28:23/q37 What other phonetic variants are reported around this verse?  [يُصْدِرَ · readings]
- 28:23/q37/p1 The ṣād may be pronounced as zāy or an intermediate sound, a phonetic assimilation without changed meaning. — Ibn Khālawayh relates it to a quiescent ṣād before dāl; al-Ālūsī and Qirāʾāt ER record the variants. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(ibdāl)]
- 28:23/q37/p2 A hamza-softening class is cited by association with the passage, though no exact word occurs here. — Ibn al-Jazarī lists five readers and alternatives of between-between, wāw, and yāʾ realization. [1 notes: NASHR(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر ورويس)]

## 28:23/q38 How does this verse relate to its sūra and other passages?  [* · links]
- 28:23/q38/p1 The repeated Moses narrative uses differences of order, wording and detail to add a distinct nuance each time. — Al-Zarkashī compares the separate tellings with Joseph's gathered story and cites 28:3–43. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 28:23/q38/p2 The Midian story follows Moses' flight and leads to his marriage and service. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ and Study Quran summarize this narrative arc; the next verse 28:24 follows Moses' shade and prayer. [3 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر)]
- 28:23/q38/p3 The mention of Midian's water illustrates possible nearness to Hell without entering it in 19:71. — Al-Ālūsī reports ʿUbayd b. ʿUmayr's comparison. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عبيد بن عمير)]

## 28:23/q39 What is reported about the sūra's place of revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 28:23/q39/p1 All of al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes this to al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ, and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:23/q40 What special response style is illustrated near Moses' question?  [مَا خَطْبُكُمَا · rhetoric]
- 28:23/q40/p1 An answer may broaden, narrow, or redirect the question toward what ought to be asked. — Al-Sakkākī calls redirection a wise style; al-Suyūṭī notes that circumstances can require a broader or narrower reply. [2 notes: ITQAN(السكاكي)]

## 28:23/q41 What other lexical comparison is noted with this narrative?  [* · meaning]
- 28:23/q41/p1 Wa-ṭafiqa is glossed as intending or setting out, from Roman usage. — Al-Zarkashī reports the comparison; the note does not identify a word in 28:23. [1 notes: BURHAN]
