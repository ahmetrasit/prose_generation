# 79:33: 13 questions

## 79:33/q01 What does matāʿ mean here?  [مَتَٰعًۭا · meaning]
- 79:33/q01/p1 Benefit or enjoyment granted for use; the word can imply that the enjoyment lasts only a limited time. — Ibn Qutayba, the exegetes reported by Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn al-Jawzi himself, al-Qurtubi, and Ibn Abbas as reported by al-Suyuti gloss it as benefit. Ibn al-Jawzi's report illustrates benefit with uninhabited houses sheltering visitors from heat and cold. Ibn Ashur adds the limited duration of use and compares 7:24. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:33/q02 How is matāʿ read at this point?  [مَتَٰعًۭا · readings]
turns on: Whether the word is accusative in the common reading or nominative as a new clause.
- 79:33/q02/p1 A nominative matāʿ is also possible, beginning a new clause. — Al-Farra says matāʿ lakum may begin a new clause with an omitted nominative element and cites comparable wording at 46:35 and 16:117; al-Nahhas reports his allowance of the nominative. [2 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء)]
- 79:33/q02/p2 The common reading has accusative matāʿan. — Al-Samin calls the accusative the common reading and explains its two possible syntactic roles. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]

## 79:33/q03 What grammatical role does matāʿan have?  [مَتَٰعًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative marks purpose, depends on an implicit verb, or follows a suppressed preposition; al-Farra also allows a nominative clause.
- 79:33/q03/p1 It is an accusative expression of purpose: God made these things for people and livestock to enjoy. — Al-Zajjaj relates the purpose to bringing out water and pasture. Al-Samin gives the purpose object as one analysis of the common accusative. Al-Alusi reports it and says that an objection based on the deniers addressed is answered because the address generalizes to humankind; he questions the premise that only believers benefit. Al-Qurtubi's alternative with an omitted preposition likewise expresses 'for you to enjoy it,' but specifies a different construction. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 79:33/q03/p2 It is an accusative verbal noun governed by an omitted verb such as 'We provided you enjoyment.' — Al-Qurtubi supplies 'I provide enjoyment'; al-Samin supplies mattaʿakum; Ibn Ashur supplies 'We provided you with provision.' Al-Alusi reports either an emphatic verbal noun for an omitted verb or a verbal noun licensed by the preceding actions' meaning of providing enjoyment. Al-Alusi rejects the claim that this analysis by itself avoids the purpose objection, since it still states a purpose. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:33/q03/p3 Matāʿ is nominative in a fresh clause with an omitted nominative element. — Al-Farra accepts matāʿ lakum as a new clause, adducing 46:35 and 16:117; al-Nahhas transmits his nominative analysis. [2 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء)]
- 79:33/q03/p4 Its accusative follows an omitted preposition, yielding 'for you to enjoy it.' — Al-Qurtubi offers this as an alternative to construing the word as a verbal noun governed by an omitted verb. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 79:33/q04 Does 'you' limit the intended beneficiaries to the people directly addressed?  [لَّكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wording restricts benefit to believers or extends the addressed provision to all people.
- 79:33/q04/p1 The immediate address is to those present, but its provision extends generally to humankind. — Al-Alusi replies that a direct address can have a general ruling and questions the claim that only believers are intended beneficiaries. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)+]
- 79:33/q04/p2 Only believers are the intended beneficiaries, despite the address to resurrection-deniers. — Al-Alusi reports this as the premise of an objection to taking matāʿan as purpose; he counters that the ruling extends to humanity and calls the premise debatable. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 79:33/q05 Which animals are meant by 'your livestock'?  [وَلِأَنْعَٰمِكُمْ · referent]
- 79:33/q05/p1 Domestic livestock, including camels, cattle, and sheep. — Al-Qurtubi specifies these three kinds; Ibn Kathir adds that such animals are used for eating and riding. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 79:33/q06 What enjoyment is provided for people and livestock?  [مَتَٰعًۭا لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَٰمِكُمْ · interpretation]
- 79:33/q06/p1 God makes the ordered earth and its resources useful as food, shelter, and other benefits for people and livestock. — Muqatil identifies pasture and other livelihood. Al-Farra, al-Qushayri, al-Zamakhshari, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, and al-Nasafi explain the acts as providing enjoyment. Al-Zajjaj and al-Alusi name water and pasture; al-Nahhas transmits al-Farra's benefit gloss. Ibn Kathir details springs, hidden resources, rivers, crops, trees, fruit, stabilizing mountains, eating, riding, and use until the allotted lifespan. Al-Biqai describes consumption followed by renewed supply. Al-Bursevi and al-Utbi include grass, trees, grain, fruit, salt obtained from water, and fire from green wood. Elmalili says spreading the crust makes earth habitable. Ibn Ashur adds that earth and mountains furnish use, shelter from enemies, and safe pasture, while the two lams reinforce the verbal noun. Al-Tabatabai relates heaven and earth's ordering to human and animal sustenance. The Study Quran adds that God sustains all things and makes them serve humans, citing 80:32, 22:65, 31:20, and 45:13. [21 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(العتبي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:33/q07 How does provision for people and livestock explain the preceding mention of pasture?  [لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَٰمِكُمْ · links]
turns on: Whether pasture itself covers human food or human food is implied by an inference from God's care for animals.
- 79:33/q07/p1 Pasture broadly includes or stands for all earthly products consumed by people and animals. — Al-Razi says the later provision for both proves pasture means everything both eat. Al-Bursevi treats grazing figuratively as everything consumed from earth, even salt from water and fire from green wood. Al-Utbi, reported by al-Bursevi, calls water and pasture a comprehensive expression embracing grass, trees, grain, fruit, salt, and fire. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(العتبي)]
- 79:33/q07/p2 Pasture names animal provision, while human fruits and grain are understood by a stronger inference. — Ibn Ashur says that if God cares for animals, people deserve care all the more, and 79:33 signals the omission of explicit fruits and grain. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:33/q08 Why are people named before livestock?  [لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَٰمِكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 79:33/q08/p1 Rational beings take precedence over other creatures in the order of mention. — Al-Suyuti compares Gabriel's precedence over Michael and cites the ordering of creatures in glorification at 22:18. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 79:33/q09 What does the verse imply about the purpose of God's acts?  [* · theology]
- 79:33/q09/p1 Divine action has purposes and benefits; making a habitable world and providing life are wise acts. — Al-Razi reports that advocates of purposes behind God's acts and rulings cite this verse. Elmalili argues that making earth livable is purposeful rather than futile or accidental, and that life has wisdom and an end. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(من قال); ELMALILI]

## 79:33/q10 How do these provisions contribute to the proof of resurrection?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources use created power, observable renewal, and divine wisdom as grounds for resurrection.
- 79:33/q10/p1 Creating and ordering the greater world shows God's ability to recreate human beings after death. — Al-Jishumi says preserving created substances allows their restoration after destruction. Al-Razi says creation proves resurrection rationally before 79:34 announces it. Al-Biqai places this within the surah's purpose of establishing gathering and final fate, with decisive oaths after earlier surahs' exposition. Bint al-Shati says the heavens, earth, water, pasture, and mountains make resurrection of decayed humans no hardship. Al-Tabatabai adds that the greater creation removes difficulty. Islahi says creating the heavens, earth, night, day, and sustenance establishes divine power. Kuran Yolu points to the vast ordered cosmos and cites 40:57, then specifically adduces earth's habitability and provision. The Study Quran likewise identifies creation's wonders as evidence of resurrection. [9 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 79:33/q10/p2 Recurring renewal of earthly life and provisions illustrates restoration and resurrection. — Al-Biqai calls the daily return of night and day a demonstration of resurrection: restoring life is no harder than initial creation. He also points to the recurring cycle of consuming and replacing needed provision. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:33/q10/p3 The wise provision of human sustenance entails an end beyond present life. — Islahi says God's power makes re-creation possible and His wisdom requires it; Elmalili adds that useful, purposeful life has wisdom and an end. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]

## 79:33/q11 What response do God's provisions call for from people?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize gratitude and worship, and one also draws a duty to share from human vicegerency.
- 79:33/q11/p1 People should recognize, thank, fear, and worship the Lord who provides for them. — Ibn Ashur says gratitude is embedded in the proof and invites exclusive worship; he also identifies a blend-then-division figure. Al-Tabatabai says creation and provision obligate knowledge, fear, and thanks. Asad cites the food passage 80:24-32 for awareness of God as Provider. Bint al-Shati says divine gifts recall the heedless, deniers, and arrogant to gratitude. The Study Quran also calls for thanks. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]
- 79:33/q11/p2 People should share these resources with all creatures. — The Study Quran relates sharing to the human role as God's vicegerents and cites 2:30, 6:165, and 35:39. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 79:33/q12 How does mention of worldly enjoyment rebuke resurrection-deniers?  [* · rhetoric]
- 79:33/q12/p1 It likens deniers who enjoy this world and forget the next to cattle. — Al-Bursevi explains the rebuke through their absorption in worldly provision and neglect of the afterlife. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 79:33/q13 How does provision lead into the passage's warning of judgment?  [* · links]
- 79:33/q13/p1 The passage moves from the Creator's power and favor to the announcement of resurrection and final reckoning. — Al-Razi says the rational proof of resurrection is followed by the announcement at 79:34. Al-Biqai reads this as the surah's presentation of humanity's final fate after its decisive oaths. Asad says gratitude for provision leads into resurrection and judgment. Bint al-Shati describes a movement from power to bounty, paralleled by 80:17-32, and then an abrupt judgment warning: 80:33-42 speaks of the final blast and faces, while 79:34-36 speaks of the great calamity and reckoning. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI]
