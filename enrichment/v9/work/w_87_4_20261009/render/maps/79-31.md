# 79:31: 10 questions

## 79:31/q01 How is 'He brought forth' connected to the preceding account of spreading the earth?  [أَخْرَجَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the unjoined clause explains the preceding verb, supplies a circumstance with implied qad, or functions as a substitution of inclusion.
- 79:31/q01/p1 It explains or details what spreading the earth entails, so no conjunction is needed. — Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by al-Samīn, says spreading prepared earth for habitation and this clause details the water and provisions required for settlement. Rāzī adds food and stabilizing mountains; Bursevī calls the clause an explanation or completion of preparation. Ālūsī likewise says livelihood requires food and drink. Al-Kashshāf and Bayḍāwī present this as one of two analyses. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:31/q01/p2 It is an unjoined circumstantial clause, with an implicit qad: the earth was spread while its water and pasture were brought forth. — Rāzī states the simultaneous circumstance. Qurṭubī and al-Samīn cite the analogous construction at 4:90; al-Samīn calls supplying qad the majority analysis and says Kufans and al-Akhfash oppose it. Al-Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, and Ālūsī also give this analysis as an alternative. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:31/q01/p3 It is a substitution of inclusion (badal ishtimāl) from 'He spread it.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two verbs have different meanings and the earth's useful contents complete its spreading. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:31/q01/p4 The verb appears without a coordinating conjunction; this note gives no further construction. — Nasafī expressly observes the absence of a conjunction before akhraja. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 79:31/q02 What water is brought out of the earth?  [مَآءَهَا · referent]
turns on: Which forms of earthly water the general phrase specifies: springs, rivers, seas, their combination, or water issuing from within earth without further specification.
- 79:31/q02/p1 The earth's gushing springs. — Al-Kashshāf, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, and Nasafī describe springs issuing or gushing from the earth. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 79:31/q02/p2 Springs and flowing rivers. — Ālūsī says God opened springs and caused rivers to flow. Elmalılı also speaks of creation, gushing springs, and flowing rivers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī names released springs and rivers. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 79:31/q02/p3 Rivers made to flow. — Ṭabarī states this directly and transmits it from al-Ḍaḥḥāk; al-Suyūṭī transmits Ibn Jurayj's same gloss. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 79:31/q02/p4 Seas, rivers, springs, and all water. — Wāḥidī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās naming seas, rivers, springs, and all water. Ṭabrisī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās naming rivers, seas, and springs. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 79:31/q02/p5 Water belonging within the earth and emerging from it, without specifying a waterbody. — Biqāʿī infers an internal origin from the possessive attribution 'its water.' Bursevī says water is attributed to earth because it emerges from it. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 79:31/q02/p6 The earth's seas. — Muqātil identifies its water as seas, since they come from the earth. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 79:31/q03 Why is water mentioned before pasture?  [مَآءَهَا مَرْعَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
- 79:31/q03/p1 Water causes the pasture to grow. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports Abū Ḥayyān's explanation that the order follows the causal dependence of pasture on water. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)]

## 79:31/q04 What do water and pasture together stand for?  [مَآءَهَا مَرْعَىٰهَا · interpretation]
- 79:31/q04/p1 They encompass the earth's provisions and benefits for people and animals. — Ibn Qutayba and his later transmitters enumerate grass, trees, grain, fruit or dates, chaff or straw, leaves, wood or fuel, clothing, fire from wood, and salt from water; Rāzī also names medicine, says worldly pleasures stem from water and plants, and cites 21:30, 56:71–72, and 2:25. Ṭabrisī says water sustains animals, trees, fruit, grain, and springs, while pasture supplies livestock. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes cooking fuel and salt, and argues that mention of pasture suffices for fruit and grain because care for animals implies still greater care for people; he cites 79:33 for the ellipsis. Ālūsī says settlement requires food and drink. [12 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(القتيبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن مسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(القتبي); BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:31/q05 What does 'its pasture' denote here?  [مَرْعَىٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether marʿā here is limited to grazed fodder, includes human food too, or alludes to plant life generally; notes also discuss its verbal-noun, place, and time senses.
- 79:31/q05/p1 Food or vegetation eaten by both people and animals. — Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Kashshāf, Rāzī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Study Quran explicitly include both. Rāzī cites 16:10 and 80:25–32 and infers this scope from the later statement that it is provision for people and livestock. Al-Samīn treats marʿā as a verbal noun with passive-object sense applied metaphorically to people. Ālūsī reports restricting the literal base to animals before extending it to people. Asad reports Rāzī's metonymic sense of all edible herbal produce. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 79:31/q05/p2 Grazed vegetation, pasture, or fodder for livestock. — Jishumī derives the name from livestock grazing and eating it. Al-Kashshāf, Rāzī, and Bayḍāwī note an original sense of the grazing place. Biqāʿī includes what is grazed, its place, and its season. Ālūsī lists fodder, the act of grazing, and place or time as possible senses, reports an alleged original place sense, but judges fodder best here. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives marʿā from raʿā, takes a verbal noun for its grazed object, and says literal grazing is livestock eating pasture, grass, and fresh fodder. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes possible verbal-noun, time, and place forms, favors what is grazed, and compares 87:4. Bursevī glosses it as pasture. [12 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; BINTSHATI+]
- 79:31/q05/p3 Plants or vegetal growth generally. — Muqātil says earth's plants come from it. Ṭabarī names plants, transmitting the same from al-Ḍaḥḥāk; Ibn Jurayj, as transmitted in al-Durr, says all plants and other growth. Elmalılı adds grasses, pastures, and cultivated growth. Study Quran reports an allusion to all plant life. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 79:31/q06 How does grazing language apply to human provision?  [مَرْعَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the extension from livestock grazing to human food is metaphor, metonymy, or an a fortiori implication from provision for animals.
- 79:31/q06/p1 It is extended metaphorically from livestock grazing to people eating. — Al-Kashshāf and Rāzī compare 'roam/graze and play' and the nartaʿ reading at 12:12. Al-Samīn describes a verbal noun with passive-object sense used metaphorically of people. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says this usage is familiar Arabic, compares shepherd and flock, and cites 20:54. Ālūsī reports al-Ṭībī's explicit metaphor: resurrection-deniers are yoked with beasts in worldly enjoyment and heedlessness of the afterlife. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 79:31/q06/p2 The animal-pasture term extends to human food by metonymy. — Ālūsī reports this account as an alternative to explicit metaphor. Asad attributes to Rāzī a metonymic reference to all edible herbal produce for humans or animals. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 79:31/q06/p3 Human fruits and grain are left implicit because God's provision for animals implies greater care for humans. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 79:33 signals the ellipsis and explains the inference from animal pasture to human food. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:31/q07 How does bringing out water and pasture relate to the earth's spreading and the sequence of creation?  [* · links]
turns on: How daḥāhā in the preceding verse is understood, and when the emergence of water and pasture occurs relative to creation of heaven and earth.
- 79:31/q07/p1 Earth existed before the sky, but its spreading after the sky brought its latent contents into actuality. — Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn ʿAbbās's explanation and prefers the reading chosen by Ibn Jarīr. Ibn ʿAbbās says spreading brought forth water and pasture, split rivers, and placed mountains, sands, roads, and hills. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جرير)+]
- 79:31/q07/p2 Spreading means preparing earth to produce provisions, an aptitude attained only after heaven existed. — Rāzī reports this alternative interpretation, comparing heaven to a father and earth to a mother. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 79:31/q07/p3 After heaven and earth were created together and separated, spreading and bringing out water and pasture were later developmental stages. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes daḥāhā, spreading or expanding earth, from creating it; he says water, pasture, and mountains appeared in later stages, and cites 21:30 and 79:32. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 79:31/q07/p4 Some understand the adjacent 'spreading' as making earth spherical like an ostrich egg. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports this comparison without adopting it. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(من يقول أن الدحي هي بيضة النعام)]

## 79:31/q08 How are 'earth' and 'mountains' parsed in the surrounding construction?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The case reading of the surrounding nouns and whether later predicates explain omitted verbs.
- 79:31/q08/p1 Both are accusative through omitted verbs explained by later predicates. — Rāzī gives this parsing of the surrounding clauses. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 79:31/q08/p2 Both are nominative subjects. — Rāzī records al-Ḥasan's reading with both nouns nominative. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 79:31/q09 How do the earth's provisions serve the sūra's argument about resurrection?  [* · theology]
- 79:31/q09/p1 Making the earth habitable and providing for its creatures displays God's power to resurrect humanity. — Biqāʿī says the sūra establishes resurrection and gathering and humanity's final fate, after preceding sūras' exposition; its oaths mark a decisive climax. He also points to recurring night and day as daily demonstrations: restoring life is no harder than creating humanity from nothing. Kur'an Yolu says the ordered cosmos confronts resurrection-deniers and cites 40:57, which makes creation of heaven and earth greater than creation of humanity; furnishing earth with provision for people and animals reinforces the argument. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 79:31/q10 May Qur'anic writing or water used to wash it be consumed?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether contact with internal impurity bars drinking washed-off writing, and whether drinking wash-water, swallowing the paper, and eating inscribed food receive distinct treatment.
- 79:31/q10/p1 One may not drink water used to wash Qur'anic writing, because it meets internal impurity. — An unnamed shaykh gives the prohibition and its ground, as reported by al-Burhān. [1 notes: BURHAN(الشيخ); BURHAN-]
- 79:31/q10/p2 One may drink that wash-water; impurity in its original bodily location has no legal consequence. — Al-Burhān's author objects to the shaykh's impurity argument. Al-ʿImād al-Nihy permits washing a written Qur'anic scrap and drinking the water; the author saw this statement in Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ's hand. [2 notes: BURHAN(العماد النيهى)]
- 79:31/q10/p3 The written paper itself must not be swallowed. — Al-ʿImād al-Nihy distinguishes swallowing the paper from drinking water in which its writing was washed. [1 notes: BURHAN(العماد النيهى)]
- 79:31/q10/p4 Foods bearing some Qur'anic writing may be eaten. — Al-Burhān reports this allowance from al-Qāḍī al-Ḥusayn and al-Rāfiʿī. [1 notes: BURHAN(القاضى الحسين)]
- 79:31/q10/p5 A man ate a paper bearing the basmala out of reverence and then received wisdom in a dream. — Al-Bayhaqī transmits from al-Sulamī the story of Manṣūr ibn ʿAmmār; the note reports an event rather than a general legal ruling. [1 notes: BURHAN(البيهقى)]
