# 96:5/q01 What did God teach the human that was previously unknown? [عَلَّمَ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pen clause limits the teaching to writing or this covers broader knowledge, and whether the recipient is a named person.
- 96:5/q01/p1 Writing and script with the pen.
  reasons: Ibn Zayd and Qatāda explicitly gloss it as writing; al-Kalbī specifies writing in a book. Ibn al-Zubayr's explanatory reading names script; Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported to give this gloss.
  holders (15 notes): TAB(ابن زيد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI(قتادة وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF(ابن الزبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الزبير); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BINTSHATI(ابن عباس)

- 96:5/q01/p2 All formerly unknown knowledge, including writing, guidance, religion, crafts and insight.
  reasons: Ṭabarī adds other unknown things to writing. Al-Thaʿlabī and Baghawī name guidance and expression; Ṭabrisī adds religion, law and rulings. Qushayrī includes necessary and acquired knowledge; Ibn al-Jawzī writing and crafts; Bursawī universal, particular, manifest and hidden knowledge. Biqāʿī adds Scripture, Sunna, dealings, reasoning and immediate insight; ordinary learning alone would not explain unequal ability and learning time. Ālūsī includes teaching with and without writing; Qutb all discoveries.

# 96:5/q02 Who is 'the human' whom God taught? [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite noun denotes the species or a particular person.
- 96:5/q02/p1 Humanity as a species.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this clearer; Abū Ḥayyān calls it apparent. Qurṭubī cites 16:78: humans leave their mothers knowing nothing. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the generic noun broadens the teaching. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says it is unrestricted; Study Quran reports it as the majority.
