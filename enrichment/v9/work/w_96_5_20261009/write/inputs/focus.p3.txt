# 96:5/q20 How do these verses connect to the surah's next turn and Qur'anic themes? [* · links]
- 96:5/q20/p1 Teaching calls for gratitude, yet the human transgresses.
  reasons: Ālūsī supplies an implicit transition after verse 5. Biqāʿī says people cling to their present state and forget what God does not preserve; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ warns of arrogance in knowledge and status. Ibn Masʿūd contrasts knowledge, which increases divine approval (35:28), with wealth seeking, which deepens transgression (96:6).
  holders (4 notes): ALUSI(بعضهم); BIQAI; BINTSHATI; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)
  notes: ALUSI:96:6/r5 BIQAI:96:5/r2 BINTSHATI:v2p24/r4 DURR-FULL:v8p563/r5
- 96:5/q20/p2 The first five verses preview recitation, God's name, law, religion and divine teaching.
  reasons: Suyūṭī calls the opening a possible title for the Qur'an; Elmalılı says it joins rational and transmitted proof, reading, writing and knowledge.
  holders (2 notes): ITQAN; ELMALILI
  notes: ITQAN:v3p284/r3 ELMALILI:96:1-8#3/r2
- 96:5/q20/p3 Verse 5 closes the first unit; verse 6 opens a new topic.
  reasons: Abū Ḥātim uses the first five and later kalla at 96:6 to argue kalla opens speech and means alā. Ibn ʿĀshūr also marks verses 6–10 as a new subject.
  holders (2 notes): BURHAN(أبو حاتم); IBNASHUR-FULL
  notes: BURHAN:v4p315/r3 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p441/r6
sources: ALUSI = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; BINTSHATI = ʿĀʾisha ʿAbd al-Raḥmān (Bint al-Shāṭiʾ), d. 1419; BIQAI = al-Biqāʿī, d. 885; BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; DURR-FULL = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911;
