# 96:5/q10 Why is writing singled out in this passage? [عَلَّمَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ · interpretation]
- 96:5/q10/p1 It preserves and transmits knowledge, wisdom, reports and revelation, sustaining religious and worldly life.
  reasons: Zamakhsharī and Nasafī say affairs fail without writing. Qatāda says knowledge sustains religion and livelihood. Biqāʿī extends benefits to both worlds. Ālūsī calls pen and script a sign of wisdom and cites poems about their reach. Asad stresses cumulative learning; Kuryolu includes computers and information exchange.

# 96:5/q11 Who first learned or introduced writing, and how did scripts spread? [عَلَّمَ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ about first writers and the route of writing to Arabia.
- 96:5/q11/p1 Writing was divinely taught; Adam and other prophets may have learned it.
  reasons: Zarkashī cites pen teaching and 68:1; Ibn Fāris places letters among the names taught Adam. Ālūsī says the verse leaves the teacher unnamed and doubts alphabet invention claims.
  holders (4 notes): BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن فارس); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك، كعب)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p377/r2 BURHAN:v1p377/r3 ITQAN:v4p442/r5 ALUSI-FULL:v30p180#4/r7
- 96:5/q11/p2 Ishmael established Arabic writing first.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās says so; Zarkashī notes conflicting reports.
  holders (1 notes): BURHAN(ابن عباس)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p377/r1
- 96:5/q11/p3 Idris and Adam, or every writing prophet, were first writers.
  reasons: Ālūsī transmits al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Kaʿb but doubts alphabet invention claims.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك، كعب)
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p180#4/r7
- 96:5/q11/p4 Writing was old outside Arabia and later reached the Hijaz through Harb ibn Umayya.
  reasons: Al-Kalbī and al-Haytham trace Harb's learning near al-Anbar through Aslam ibn Sidra to Marāmir ibn Murra.
  holders (2 notes): ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي، الهيثم بن عدي)
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p181/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p181/r4
- 96:5/q11/p5 Himyar used a separate Musnad script taught with permission.
  reasons: Ālūsī allows twelve main script families only as origins because derivatives exceed counting.
  holders (1 notes): ALUSI-FULL
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p181/r5
sources: ALUSI-FULL = al-Ālūsī, d. 1270; BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; ITQAN = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911

# 96:5/q12 What does the history of writing imply for Qur'anic orthography? [عَلَّمَ · interpretation]
- 96:5/q12/p1 Early Muslims knew orthography and the first codex spelling should remain.
  reasons: Zarkashī cites wāw/yāʾ distinctions and hamza omission. Farrāʾ favors codex spelling with an Arabic basis; Mālik requires first spelling including extra written wāw or alif, with al-Dānī reporting no dissent.
  holders (3 notes): BURHAN(الفراء، مالك)
  notes: BURHAN:v1p377/r5 BURHAN:v1p377/r6 BURHAN:v1p377/r7
- 96:5/q12/p2 Grammar and prosody existed before their later codifiers.
  reasons: Zarkashī says Abū al-Aswad and al-Khalīl revived older sciences; he counters the claim ancient Arabs lacked letter names by citing the names taught Adam.
  holders (2 notes): BURHAN
  notes: BURHAN:v1p377/r3 BURHAN:v1p377/r4
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794

# 96:5/q15 Where and when did the teaching revelation arrive? [* · reports]
turns on: Reports locate it differently.
- 96:5/q15/p1 At Hira during Muhammad's retreat.
  reasons: ʿĀʾisha says truthful dreams and repeated seclusion with Khadīja's provisions preceded it. Ibn ʿĀshūr dates it Ramadan 17 in year 40 after the Elephant; Ibn Hishām says annual Ramadan retreat included feeding the poor and later circumambulation.
