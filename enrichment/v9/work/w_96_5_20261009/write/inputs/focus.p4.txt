# 96:5/q21 What did Muhammad's taḥannuth at Hira mean? [* · meaning]
- 96:5/q21/p1 Secluded worship.
  reasons: Bukhārī and Muslim gloss it as worship; ʿĀʾisha describes provisioning, Ibn Hishām a Ramadan retreat with feeding the poor and circumambulation after return.
  holders (5 notes): BUKHARI; MUSLIM; IBNHISHAM(عبيد); AZRAQI(عائشة)
  notes: BUKHARI:4953/r2 BUKHARI:6982/r2 MUSLIM:403/r2 IBNHISHAM:v1p235#2/r3 AZRAQI:v2p204/r2
- 96:5/q21/p2 A form of taḥannuf, meaning ḥanīfiyya.
  reasons: Ibn Hishām says Arabs replace th with f as in jadath/jadaf, citing Ruʾba; Abū ʿUbayda gives fam for thumma.
  holders (2 notes): IBNHISHAM(أبو عبيدة، ابن هشام)
  notes: IBNHISHAM:v1p235#2/r1 IBNHISHAM:v1p235#2/r2
sources: AZRAQI = al-Azraqī, d. 249; BUKHARI = BUKHARI; IBNHISHAM = Ibn Hishām, d. 213; MUSLIM = MUSLIM

# 96:5/q22 What knowledge and method qualify a person to interpret the Qur'an? [* · interpretation]
turns on: Directly taught unseen matters differ from disciplined inference and ungrounded opinion.
- 96:5/q22/p1 Some unseen and ambiguous passages require direct divine teaching.
  reasons: ʿĀʾisha says Muhammad explained only what Gabriel taught; Ibn ʿAṭiyya limits that to unseen matters. The Hour is inaccessible by inference, but wording can suggest trumpet blasts or creation sequence.
  holders (3 notes): IBNATIYYA-FULL(عائشة)
  notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r1 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r2 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r3
- 96:5/q22/p2 Disciplined interpretation is permitted; ungrounded opinion is condemned.
  reasons: A prophetic report warns even correct personal opinion errs. Ibn ʿAṭiyya targets disregard of precedent and grammar or legal theory, while trained linguists and jurists may reason; early scholars either abstained or preserved tafsīr. Suyūṭī requires rhetoric, readings, theology, law, occasions, abrogation, fiqh and hadith; these sciences are an endless ocean.
  holders (17 notes): IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم، سعيد بن المسيب); ITQAN(ابن أبي الدنيا، الزمخشري، غيره)
  notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r4 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r5 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r6 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r7 IBNATIYYA-FULL:v1p41/r8 ITQAN:v4p478/r1 ITQAN:v4p478/r2 ITQAN:v4p478/r3 ITQAN:v4p478/r4 ITQAN:v4p478/r5 ITQAN:v4p478/r6 ITQAN:v4p478/r7 ITQAN:v4p478/r8 ITQAN:v4p478/r9 ITQAN:v4p478/r10 ITQAN:v4p478/r12 ITQAN:v4p478/r13
- 96:5/q22/p3 ʿAlī and Ibn ʿAbbās were leading early tafsīr authorities.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAṭiyya ranks ʿAlī first, Ibn ʿAbbās next, then Mujāhid and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr; Ibn ʿAbbās learned from ʿAlī and was praised by ʿAlī and Ibn Masʿūd; the Prophet prayed for his understanding.
  holders (5 notes): IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس، رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم، عبد الله بن مسعود، علي بن أبي طالب)

# 96:5/q23 Which transmitted Qur'anic readings may readers use? [* · readings]
turns on: Established transmission differs from readings outside the codex.
- 96:5/q23/p1 Established readings beyond the seven may be recited when learned.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī cites Iraqi acceptance of ten or eleven and transmissions from al-Aʿmash, Yaʿqūb, Khalaf, Abū Jaʿfar and Shaybah; readers should not use unlearned readings or rebuke others. Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ's censure concerned anomalous readings outside the codex.
  holders (4 notes): NASHR(أئمة أهل العراق، القاضي عياض)
  notes: NASHR:v1p40/r1 NASHR:v1p40/r2 NASHR:v1p40/r3 NASHR:v1p40/r5
- 96:5/q23/p2 The seven named readings are distinct from the seven revealed aḥruf.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazarī reports agreement on the distinction; later scholars debated whether the codex with seven, ten and other readings represents one ḥarf.
  holders (2 notes): NASHR(المتأخرون)
  notes: NASHR:v1p40/r4 NASHR:v1p40/r6
sources: NASHR = Ibn al-Jazarī, d. 833

# 96:5/q24 What grammatical uses can 'mā' have in related constructions? [مَا · grammar]
- 96:5/q24/p1 It may be declarative or relative before laysa, lam or lā and after illā.
  reasons: Zarkashī gives general usage examples, without choosing the parsing here.

  notes: BURHAN:v4p400/r4

# 96:5/q25 What examples show surahs descending together or in portions? [* · sciences]
turns on: Reports conflict about whether al-Anʿām came wholly and about the number of angels.
- 96:5/q25/p1 Several short surahs descended whole; al-ʿAlaq's opening descended as a portion.
  reasons: Suyūṭī names al-Fātiḥa, al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Kawthar, Tabbat, al-Bayyina, al-Naṣr, the two Muʿawwidhatayn and al-Ṣaff. Ibn Masʿūd says al-Mursalāt descended in a cave but was unsure which of two verses ended what he heard; al-Ḍuḥā's first portion is another instance.
  holders (5 notes): ITQAN(ابن مسعود)
  notes: ITQAN:v1p109/r1 ITQAN:v1p109/r2 ITQAN:v1p109/r3 ITQAN:v1p109/r4 ITQAN:v1p109/r5
- 96:5/q25/p2 Al-Anʿām descended whole, with angels whose reported number varies.
  reasons: Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn ʿUmar, ʿAṭāʾ and Ubayy say seventy thousand; Mujāhid says five hundred; ʿAlī says a thousand with seventy from each heaven. Suyūṭī says the reports support one another, while noting an abandoned narrator in Ibn ʿUmar's chain and an unknown one in ʿAlī's.
  holders (7 notes): ITQAN(أبي بن كعب، ابن عباس، ابن عمر، عطاء، علي، مجاهد)
  notes: ITQAN:v1p109/r6 ITQAN:v1p109/r7 ITQAN:v1p109/r8 ITQAN:v1p109/r9 ITQAN:v1p109/r10 ITQAN:v1p109/r11 ITQAN:v1p109/r12
- 96:5/q25/p3 Al-Anʿām did not wholly descend at once, or no sound chain establishes that report.
  reasons: Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ cites three, six or another number of Medinan verses and found no sound chain for Ubayy's report.
  holders (2 notes): ITQAN(ابن الصلاح)
  notes: ITQAN:v1p109/r13 ITQAN:v1p109/r14
sources: ITQAN = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911

# 96:5/q26 How did Muhammad embody the opening instruction to act in God's name? [* · interpretation]
- 96:5/q26/p1 He filled ordinary acts with remembrance, prayer and reliance on God.
  reasons: Qutb states the opening's faith principle and cites Ibn al-Qayyim on remembrance in speech, silence and rulings. Reports detail waking, night prayer, Witr, leaving and entering home or mosque, dawn and evening, clothing, lavatory, ablution, moon sighting and eating; Qutb cites Bukhārī and Muslim and grades some sound or good. They vary by occasion and wording.
  holders (25 notes): QUTB-ZILAL(أبو بكر الصديق، أبو سعيد الخدري، ابن عباس، ابن قيم الجوزية، النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم، عائشة)
