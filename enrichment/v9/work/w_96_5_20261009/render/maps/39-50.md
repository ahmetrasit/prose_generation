# 39:50: 22 questions

## 39:50/q01 What does the pronoun in 'said it' refer to?  [قَالَهَا · referent]
- 39:50/q01/p1 The preceding boast that the blessing was received through one's own knowledge. — Kashshaf identifies 39:49 and Qarun's matching words in 28:78; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares a sentence treated as a word in 23:99–100. Bayḍāwī, Būrsawī, Ālūsī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī call it a saying, sentence, or utterance. Nasafī specifies the claim that favor came by one's knowledge. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:50/q02 Why is the pronoun in 'said it' feminine?  [قَالَهَا · grammar]
- 39:50/q02/p1 It agrees with an understood feminine 'word' or 'sentence' (kalimah). — Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī specify an implied kalimah; Kashshāf and Bayḍāwī construe the preceding claim as a word or sentence; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 23:99–100 for treating such an utterance as a word. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:50/q03 Is 'said it' also read with a masculine pronoun, and how is that construed?  [قَالَهَا · readings]
- 39:50/q03/p1 A variant reads qālahu; its masculine suffix refers to the saying or speech (qawl or kalām). — Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, and Ālūsī report the masculine reading and explain the referent as the statement or speech. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:50/q04 Who are the earlier people who made the claim?  [ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural denotes Qarun alone, Qarun with approving followers, earlier unbelieving nations generally, or an even broader class of prosperous people.
- 39:50/q04/p1 Earlier unbelievers or nations, including Qarun as an example. — Jishumī reports 'earlier unbelievers.' Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-Suddī's 'past nations.' Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Iṣlāḥī, and Study Quran cite Qarun's matching 28:78 claim; Ibn ʿĀshūr says God knows these irreligious predecessors. Ibn Kathīr calls them many peoples. Biqāʿī names Quraysh as the principal present audience while extending the pattern across times. Study Quran describes Korah as wealthy and in Pharaoh's court; Iṣlāḥī cites 28:76–78. Bursevī's broader good-or-evil proposal is kept separately. [17 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:50/q04/p2 Qarun and his people, who approved his statement and so share its attribution. — Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Bursevī explain the plural through the people's approval; Bursevī says Qarun claimed knowledge of his own worth. Ālūsī calls attribution to the group a rational metaphor; Biqāʿī recalls those who wished for Qarun's wealth. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:50/q04/p3 Qarun specifically made the earlier boast. — Muqātil says Qarun preceded Abū Ḥudhayfa; Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Baghawī citing Muqātil, and Ibn al-Jawzī citing Muqātil identify Qarun; several cite 28:78, where he credits his wealth to his own knowledge. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]
- 39:50/q04/p4 Any earlier people, good or evil, made arrogant by outward prosperity may be included. — Bursevī transmits this proposal from 'some,' explicitly extending the reference beyond Qarun. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 39:50/q05 How can a statement by one man be attributed to a group?  [قَالَهَا ٱلَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether collective attribution rests on followers' assent to Qarun or on circulation of the saying among them.
- 39:50/q05/p1 The group approved Qarun's claim, so the verse attributes it to them collectively. — Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Bursevī invoke the people's approval; Ālūsī terms this rational metaphor. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:50/q05/p2 The saying spread among the group, whether in these words or in equivalent words. — Ālūsī reports an analysis that treats the locative expression figuratively, denoting circulation of the claim among them. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:50/q06 How does the fā connect the failure of their earnings to the boast?  [فَمَآ · rhetoric]
- 39:50/q06/p1 It links their earnings' failure as a consequence of their boast, anticipating the next clause's grave harm. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says they would seek to ransom themselves from the harm but their possessions could not do so; the next clause explains the implied harm. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:50/q07 Is mā in 'what availed them' negative or interrogative?  [مَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā directly negates or asks a rhetorical question; Samīn favors direct negation to avoid figurative interpretation.
- 39:50/q07/p1 It directly negates any benefit from their earnings. — Qurṭubī gives the negative reading with wealth and children failing to protect them. Samīn prefers direct negation because it needs no figurative interpretation. Study Quran translates it as a negative; its r6 says both analyses emphasize worldly fragility. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 39:50/q07/p2 It asks rhetorically what their earnings could have benefited them, implying none. — Qurṭubī reports this alternative; Samīn accepts it as an interrogation interpreted as negation but prefers direct negation. Study Quran transmits it from Q and says both analyses emphasize worldly fragility. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 39:50/q08 What does aghnā ʿanhum mean?  [أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُم · meaning]
- 39:50/q08/p1 It means to suffice for them or be enough to help them. — Bursevī cites al-Mufradāt for the usage aghnā ʿanhu. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 39:50/q09 What did their earnings fail to do, and what befell them?  [فَمَآ أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُم مَّا كَانُوا۟ يَكْسِبُونَ · interpretation]
- 39:50/q09/p1 Their gains and deeds could not avert God's punishment or ruin. — Muqātil names being swallowed by the earth; Thaʿlabī says evil consequences overtook them. Zajjāj says their works were nullified. Ṭabrisī says wealth became a burden against them. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it could not ransom them from grave harm. Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that, had wealth come by their own power, effort would have preserved it. Iṣlāḥī says God seized Qarun and his treasures; knowledge and strategies failed. Study Quran cites 15:84 and 40:82 and extends failure to this life and the Hereafter. Qurtubī specifies wealth and children; others describe punishment, God's decree, or evil acquisitions. [24 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:50/q10 What are the 'earnings' that failed to help them?  [مَّا كَانُوا۟ يَكْسِبُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether kasb here designates material gains, disbelief, idol worship, or actions generally.
- 39:50/q10/p1 Their amassed worldly wealth, possessions, status, and other gains. — Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr specify wealth or possessions. Qurṭubī adds children; Biqāʿī adds status and repeated accumulation on a vast scale. Iṣlāḥī adds Qarun's knowledge and plans. Ṭabrisī says the wealth became a burden. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:50/q10/p2 Their earned disbelief. — Baghawī holds that acquired unbelief did not protect them at all; Ibn al-Jawzī reports it as one interpretation. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 39:50/q10/p3 Their worship of idols. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as a second interpretation. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 39:50/q10/p4 Their deeds or actions generally, whose evil consequences returned to them. — Zajjāj calls their works nullified; Thaʿlabī says consequences overtook them; Jishumī and Ibn al-Jawzī speak of earnings without narrowing them; Ṭabāṭabāʾī speaks of their effort; Kur'an Yolu calls evil human acts one's acquisitions. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:50/q11 What lesson does the earlier boast and its outcome teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes present complementary lessons: the vanity of self-crediting wealth and the relation of divine will to human responsibility.
- 39:50/q11/p1 Attributing blessings to one's own knowledge or planning is a false delusion; prosperity can be a test and cannot secure its possessor against God. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says predecessors' outcome clarifies that the blessing in 39:49 was a trial. Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues true self-generated bounty would be preserved by its earners. Abū Ḥayyān warns prosperity is gradual enticement. Ibn Kathīr calls the boast false; Iṣlāḥī points to God's seizure of Qarun and his treasures and calls worldly success transient. Study Quran says worldly pursuits are fragile before God discloses the unforeseen; it cites 39:47 and 26:88–89, when only a sound heart helps. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:50/q11/p2 Both adversity and blessings occur by God's will, while chosen human acts bring consequences for which people are responsible. — Kur'an Yolu says the passage rebukes those who blame God for trouble but credit themselves for blessings; it cites 4:78 for divine permission and relates human acquisitions to intention and choice. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 39:50/q12 How is this warning linked to the surrounding discourse?  [* · links]
turns on: The reported dispute is over whether a proposed causal link survives the intervening clauses; Abū Ḥayyān and the Sheikh connect the warning to the nearby threat.
- 39:50/q12/p1 It develops the threat of unexpected punishment: wrongdoers call on God in distress yet deny His part in prosperity. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this connection. Samīn reports the Sheikh's link to 39:47 and the contrast between seeking God in harm and withholding credit for blessings. Kur'an Yolu relates 39:51 to present deniers whose evil acquisitions will return to them. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 39:50/q12/p2 A causal fā links the scene to recoil at God's mention, joy at idols, then appeal to God in affliction. — Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī's comparison of fā here with noncausal wa at the sūra opening. He reports the Sheikh's objection, citing Abū ʿAlī's prohibition of interruption by two clauses, especially the many intervening clauses here. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 39:50/q12/p3 The warning passes from the return of good and evil deeds to mercy, with coherent transitions. — Bāqillānī cites 17:7–8 and says the compact account yields many lessons; unrelated insertion would break its order. Study Quran's sūra introduction says the death passage shows disbelievers' desperate end and verse 53 opens mercy even to those who squandered their souls. [4 notes: BAQILLANI; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:50/q13 What principles about authorized Qur'anic readings appear in the attached reports?  [* · readings]
- 39:50/q13/p1 Different authorized readings are sound and should not be a cause of quarrel or disdain. — Ṭabarī reports that the Prophet confirmed a disputed recitation, rebuked Satan's influence on ʿUmar three times, and allowed readings unless mercy and punishment were reversed; another Ibn ʿUmar report says seven sufficient sound forms were revealed. Ibn Masʿūd says the forms do not contradict law, limits, or duties, that the Prophet approved companions' recitations, and that spurning an authorized verse rejects the Qur'an as a whole. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 39:50/q13/p2 Ibn Masʿūd had direct, repeatedly affirmed knowledge of Qur'anic recitation. — He says he learned seventy sūras from the Prophet, sought anyone more knowledgeable, reviewed the Qur'an each Ramadan and twice in the last year, and received affirmation of his recitation. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]

## 39:50/q14 What does the attached literary discussion say about Qur'anic order and comparison with poetry?  [* · rhetoric]
- 39:50/q14/p1 The Qur'anic discourse compresses many lessons into a few verses and maintains a connected order across changing topics. — Bāqillānī says a longer account would be needed otherwise; he traces warning, return of deeds, and hope of mercy with 17:7–8, and says unrelated insertion would disrupt the order while continued study yields further wisdom. [3 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 39:50/q14/p2 Comparison with Imruʾ al-Qays's poetry fails because some poetic diction and lines are obscure, coarse, or inept. — Bāqillānī rejects the comparison; he reports al-Aṣmaʿī could not explain san, sannaq, and sanam, while another gloss makes sannaq a hill. [2 notes: BAQILLANI(الأصمعي); BAQILLANI+]

## 39:50/q15 What rhetorical images and relations are discussed in the attached poetry notes?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: These are separate poetic examples, with no dispute among the notes.
- 39:50/q15/p1 The dawn slapping a horse's brow and being avenged in its belly is the more skillfully compact image. — Jurjānī compares it favorably with a preceding poetic example. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 39:50/q15/p2 A chain image extends the familiar likeness of water ripples to armor rings. — Jurjānī says the current is then imagined carrying chains. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 39:50/q15/p3 An image linking speed to madness and slowness to reason works through familiar associations. — Jurjānī ties extreme speed to madness and deliberate pace to reason. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 39:50/q15/p4 A trembling sword is imagined as afraid of the warrior, perhaps borrowed by Ibn Bābak from Ibn al-Muʿtazz. — Jurjānī reports Ibn al-Muʿtazz's invented cause for a sword's shaking and conjectures that Ibn Bābak borrowed it. [2 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(ابن المعتز)]

## 39:50/q16 What does the attached discussion teach about anger and seeking refuge in God?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:50/q16/p1 Remembering God and seeking refuge from Satan can restrain anger, restore judgment, and prompt forgiveness. — Rāzī reports that a refuge formula would dispel two men's anger. He says anger impairs reason; uncertainty about who is right counsels entrusting the dispute to God; God's greater power and repeated pardon should inspire forgiveness. He derives recollection's effect from 7:201. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(غير مذكور في المقطع)]
- 39:50/q16/p2 Seeking refuge expresses human weakness, while al-Hashr's last verses express God's perfection; both establish full servanthood. — Rāzī gives this rational account of the practice accompanying recitation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 39:50/q17 What merits of reciting al-Hashr and the Musabbihat are reported?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports name differing recitations, times, and promised outcomes.
- 39:50/q17/p1 Reciting refuge and al-Hashr's last three verses morning or evening brings seventy thousand angels' prayers, and death then brings martyr status. — Rāzī transmits this prophetic report without grading; Itqān cites al-Tirmidhī's report from Maʿqil b. Yasār and identifies 59:22–24. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(معقل بن يسار)]
- 39:50/q17/p2 The Prophet advised al-Hashr before bed and promised martyrdom if the reciter died. — Itqān cites Ibn al-Sunnī from Anas. [1 notes: ITQAN(أنس)]
- 39:50/q17/p3 Reciting al-Hashr's closing verses by night or day and then dying brings Paradise. — Itqān cites al-Bayhaqī from Abū Umāmah and identifies 59:22–24. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو أمامة)]
- 39:50/q17/p4 The Prophet recited the Musabbihat before sleep; they contain a verse better than a thousand. — Itqān cites ʿIrbāḍ b. Sāriyah; Ibn Kathīr identifies the special verse as 57:3. [1 notes: ITQAN(عرباض بن سارية)]

## 39:50/q18 Which formulas for seeking refuge are transmitted in the attached reading notes?  [* · readings]
turns on: The transmitted wording and chains differ.
- 39:50/q18/p1 Aʿūdhu bi-llāhi l-ʿaẓīm min al-shayṭān al-rajīm. — Nashr says most Egyptian and Maghribi performance experts used it; reports routes from Qunbul, al-Zaynabī, Warsh, and Ibn Kathīr; al-Dānī lacked a performed Syrian transmission from Ibn ʿĀmir. [1 notes: NASHR(أهل مصر وسائر بلاد المغرب)]
- 39:50/q18/p2 A longer refuge formula adds 'inna llāha huwa l-samīʿu l-ʿalīm.' — Nashr cites al-Ahwāzī's routes from Abū ʿAmr, Abū Jaʿfar, Shayba, Nāfiʿ, Medinans, Ibn ʿĀmir, al-Kisāʾī, and Ḥamzah; another report names ʿUmar, Muslim b. Yasār, Ibn Sīrīn, and al-Thawrī. The author read it in al-Aʿmash's reading; al-Shanbudhī's route assimilates two hāʾs. [2 notes: NASHR(الأهوازي,عمر بن الخطاب ومسلم بن يسار وابن سيرين والثوري)]
- 39:50/q18/p3 Aʿūdhu bi-llāhi l-samīʿi l-ʿalīmi min al-shayṭān al-rajīm. — Nashr transmits it through al-Khuzāʿī from Hubayrah from Ḥafṣ, al-Hudhalī from Abū ʿAdī from Warsh, and a recalled Qunbul route. [1 notes: NASHR(الخزاعي)]
- 39:50/q18/p4 The formula using 'al-samīʿ al-ʿalīm' also adds 'inna llāha huwa l-samīʿu l-ʿalīm' after the invocation. — Nashr reports al-Hudhalī's transmission from al-Zaynabī from Ibn Kathīr. [1 notes: NASHR(الهذلي)]

## 39:50/q19 How do the attached notes classify Qur'anic content and word sciences?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The lists differ in how finely they divide Qur'anic content and language sciences.
- 39:50/q19/p1 Qur'anic content can be grouped as command, prohibition, report, and question, with other lists adding Sunna, promise, and threat. — Burhān reports these alternative short classifications. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 39:50/q19/p2 Thirty kinds of content may be enumerated, including warning, Paradise and Hell, divine attributes and favors, arguments, contrasts, wisdom, praise, and blame. — Burhān transmits ʿAlī b. ʿĪsā's list. [1 notes: BURHAN(علي بن عيسى)]
- 39:50/q19/p3 Al-Ṭabarī's three broad categories encompass the thirty and more, while Qur'anic wonders remain inexhaustible. — Abū al-Maʿālī prefers this, citing the verse on God's keys of the unseen, 6:59. [1 notes: BURHAN(القاضي أبو المعالي)+]
- 39:50/q19/p4 Four sciences of Qur'anic words are iʿrāb, naẓm, morphology, and iʿtibār; the last gauges the first three and permits inference. — Burhān reports this classification and illustrates morphology through aqsaṭa/qasaṭa (justice/injustice) and two forms of baʿuda (distance/perishing). [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 39:50/q20 When are oaths rhetorically apt according to an attached note?  [* · rhetoric]
- 39:50/q20/p1 They are especially apt in response to denial. — Burhān states this rhetorical principle. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 39:50/q21 How should differing interpretations and transmitted reports be assessed?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:50/q21/p1 Sound differing interpretations can coexist through repeated revelation, shared wording, or a general expression. — Itqān says sound reports need not conflict when both meanings are intended or the expression is general. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 39:50/q21/p2 Disagreements vary by whether they depend solely on transmission; soundness can be judged for some reports, not all. — Itqān states this distinction. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 39:50/q21/p3 Accept sound prophetic reports, but suspend affirmation and denial of details transmitted from People of the Book. — Itqān names Kaʿb and Wahb as examples. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 39:50/q22 What method of reading the Qur'an is described in the attached methodological notes?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:50/q22/p1 Read the Qur'an through its own wording and internal parallels, using induction and attention to exact rhetoric. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites the early maxim that the Qur'an explains itself, says its implications remain underused, and applies an inductive method to short, thematically unified mostly Meccan sūras. She says comparison within the Qur'an exposes strained external glosses (4:82), and no word, letter, vowel, or tone is interchangeable. Colleagues used the method in theses; she reports spiritual, intellectual, aesthetic, and interpretive gains. [7 notes: BINTSHATI(السلف الصالح)]
