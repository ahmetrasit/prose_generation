# 4:113: 35 questions

## 4:113/q01 How does this verse connect to the surrounding defense of the accused?  [وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · links]
turns on: Whether the immediate case exhausts the wording.
- 4:113/q01/p1 It continues the warning against defending traitors and explains that revelation saved the Prophet from their advocates. — Al-Tabari and Ibn Ashur link it to 4:105; Biqai says the warning now turns to God's favor; Study Quran invokes 4:105 and 4:111; Tirmidhi links the case to 4:114. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī); TIRMIDHI(الله تعالى)]
- 4:113/q01/p2 It broadens the particular case into a standing warning and promise of protection. — Ibn Atiyya makes the wording general beyond Tu'ma's case; Abu Hayyan likewise sees anyone among the people attempting deception. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 4:113/q02 What are God's opening favor and mercy to the Prophet?  [فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gifts name revelation in this case, prophetic office and protection, or a wider divine grace.
- 4:113/q02/p1 Revelation disclosed the plot and the true facts, protecting him from being misled. — Muqatil says the Quran clarified Tu'ma's case; Tabari says God guided him away from defending the traitor; Zajjaj and Nahhas stress the truth disclosed; Kashshaf adds gracious care; Baydawi specifies the addressee is the Messenger; Nasafi's wording about discovering the secret differs; Ibn Ashur ties the favor to correct judgment. Ibn Atiyya says the verse shows the degree of protection; Alusi adds safeguarding; Ibn al-Jawzi transmits Muqatil's case-based gloss; Tabarsi says the plot was turned away. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TIRMIDHI(الله تعالى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); TABRISI]
- 4:113/q02/p2 Favor is prophethood and mercy is protection from sin or judgment error. — Ibn Abbas via Ata, Razi, and Elmalili give the paired gloss; Kalbi specifies revelation as the aid; Study Quran reports special aid or isma and notes its broader prophetic doctrine. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs, Z)]
- 4:113/q02/p3 They are Islam and the Quran. — Ibn Abbas gives this paired gloss, transmitted by Tha'labi and Ibn al-Jawzi. [2 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 4:113/q02/p4 Favor is the Book's revelation; mercy is the Prophet's elevated cause and protection of his companions. — Biqai distinguishes the two gifts. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:113/q02/p5 Favor is subtle divine support and mercy is God's blessing. — Al-Jubba'i gives this distinction. [1 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي)]
- 4:113/q02/p6 They are special protection from neglecting God's rights and from people's schemes. — Qushayri describes encompassing divine preservation. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 4:113/q02/p7 Favor is enabling support that unfolds latent perfection, and mercy is the eternal gift of absolute perfection. — Qashani offers this inward account. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 4:113/q02/p8 The terms describe revealed guidance that also guards believers from serious error. — Study Quran cites 4:83 and 24:10,14,20,21, so the words need not name solely prophetic qualities. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 4:113/q03 How is the phrase after lawlā parsed?  [وَلَوْلَا فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · grammar]
- 4:113/q03/p1 It is a nominative subject with an understood predicate. — Sibawayh supplies the omitted predicate, as reported by Nahhas and Qurtubi. [2 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه)]

## 4:113/q04 Does 'a group would have intended' deny that the group actually tried?  [لَهَمَّت طَّآئِفَةٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lawlā negates intent itself, only effective intent, or has an omitted answer.
- 4:113/q04/p1 They did intend; the conditional negates the effect of their effort, or an effective resolve, rather than the historical attempt. — Farra reads an elided la-qad; Wahidi cites Farra and Zajjaj; Baydawi distinguishes attempt from success; Samīn explains an ineffective or religiously consequential intention; Bursevi expressly says only its effect is denied; Alusi reports the same problem and reads the denied intention as signaling no effect. [14 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 4:113/q04/p2 The lawlā answer is omitted; 'they certainly intended' is an independent or oath-like statement. — Abu al-Baqa supplies 'they would have misled you' and compares 24:10; Burhan similarly describes an oath-like preposed assertion. Samīn prefers the expressed answer instead. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 4:113/q04/p3 The defenders did not intend deception because they believed their claim was right. — Al-Raghib, reported by Alusi, says the Muslims thought their conduct sound; Ibn Ashur reports a Banu Zufr account in which the complainants believed Banu Ubayriq. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:113/q04/p4 The group did not even firmly intend the deception. — Ibn Ashur says the apparent wording denies the attempt itself: Muhammad's honesty deterred a confident lie; Banu Ubayriq's hopeful complaint lacked the resolve implied by hamm. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 4:113/q05 What does hamm mean here?  [لَهَمَّت · meaning]
- 4:113/q05/p1 It means intending or firmly resolving to do something. — Jishumi and Tabarsi cite himma as aspiration and hammām as a king of high aspiration; Baghawi says intent or resolve; Ibn Atiyya intensifies it as a consuming purpose; Ibn Ashur requires determination and confidence. [5 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

[34 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، الحسن، الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 4:113/q06/p2 They were Usayr or Asir ibn Urwa and companions. — Qatada ibn al-Nu'man gives the name and describes praise of Banu Ubayriq and reproach of his accusation; reports spell the name Usayr, Asir, or Usayd. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة بن النعمان); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة بن النعمان); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قتادة بن النعمان)]
- 4:113/q06/p3 They were the Thaqif delegation seeking religious concessions. — Ibn Abbas reports demands concerning al-Uzza; versions add exemption from conscription and tax, idols, or denial of resurrection. [7 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 4:113/q06/p4 They were hypocrites plotting to kill the Prophet. — Jishumi, Tabarsi and Abu Muslim report an unsuccessful assassination; 32:10 is offered for 'mislead' in the sense of destroy. [4 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 4:113/q06/p5 The wording can describe any would-be misleader among people. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan generalize the case. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:113/q06/p6 They were unbelievers unable to injure his religion or erase his prophethood. — Jishumi gives this further identification. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 4:113/q07 To whom does 'among them' refer?  [مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent adopted for the pronoun.
- 4:113/q07/p1 To Banu Zafar or the accused man's partisans. — Kashshaf says this follows if the faction is Banu Zafar; Abu Hayyan calls that apparent. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 4:113/q07/p2 To people generally. — Kashshaf associates this with the hypocrite reading; Nasafi and Alusi also report the broader antecedent. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:113/q08 What did the plotters seek to make the Prophet do?  [أَن يُضِلُّوكَ · interpretation]
turns on: The occasion and the range of ḍalāl, from mistaken judgment to physical destruction.
- 4:113/q08/p1 Give a false or unjust judgment by defending the thief and accusing an innocent person. — Farra and Zajjaj gloss an error of judgment; accounts say the armor was placed with or blamed on a Jew; Tabari says testimony and disguise of facts drove the plea; Al-Tabari in Study Quran stresses 4:105's warning. [23 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(بعضهم); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī)]
- 4:113/q08/p2 Turn him away from truth or revelation, including the revelation concerning idols. — Ibn al-Jawzi gives the broader 'turn from truth'; Alusi reports an idols-related reading; Damghani says ḍalāl need not imply unbelief and compares David in 38:26. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL; DAMGHANI]
- 4:113/q08/p3 Destroy or kill him. — Tabarsi compares 32:10 and 9:74; Alusi also cites the lost-in-the-earth usage of 32:10. [4 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 4:113/q09 What happened in the reported case behind this verse?  [طَّآئِفَةٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ أَن يُضِلُّوكَ · reports]
turns on: The reports assign different incidents and vary in the theft narrative's names and sequel.
- 4:113/q09/p1 Banu Ubayriq or Tu'ma stole armor, shifted blame to an innocent Jew, and enlisted advocates; revelation exposed the lie. — Mujahid specifies an iron coat; Qatada's versions name Usayr or Asir and say he rebuked Qatada; Durr reports Lubayd as falsely accused, and reports Tu'ma's flight to Mecca and apostasy; Ikrima adds later theft, expulsion and death after robbing travelers; Ibn Zayd instead says the thief died under a collapsing house. [14 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة بن النعمان); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة بن النعمان); DURR-FULL(عكرمة,قتادة); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,ابن زيد,قتادة بن النعمان); TIRMIDHI(الله تعالى)]
- 4:113/q09/p2 A Thaqif delegation sought allegiance conditioned on concessions; the Prophet refused. — Ibn Abbas versions differ: no resurrection and retention of idols, a year honoring al-Uzza, or exemption from conscription and tax. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 4:113/q09/p3 Hypocrites attempted to assassinate the Prophet, but God protected him. — Jishumi, Tabarsi and Abu Muslim transmit this account. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 4:113/q10 How is the infinitive 'to mislead you' governed?  [أَن يُضِلُّوكَ · grammar]
- 4:113/q10/p1 A governing bāʾ is omitted before the infinitive clause. — Samīn says the clause's case then follows a known grammatical dispute. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 4:113/q11 Why do the schemers mislead only themselves?  [وَمَا يُضِلُّونَ إِلَّآ أَنفُسَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reflexive error is explained by their deeds and consequences or a primordial disposition.
- 4:113/q11/p1 Their sinful collusion and false testimony constitute their own error, whose punishment and harm return to them. — Tabari cites the command against aiding sin in 4:105; Zajjaj and Nahhas call their conduct the acts of the misguided; Wahidi, Razi and Abu Hayyan specify collusion, false testimony, and slander; Tabarsi says invitation to error itself counts as misguidance and its consequence may be everlasting punishment; Bursevi cites al-Sa'ib's line that the oppressor suffers first; Study Quran compares 4:111, 3:69 and 39:41. Ibn Abbas says the plotters injure only themselves and cannot remove the Prophet's office. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الصائب); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; THALABI(ابن عباس)]
- 4:113/q11/p2 Their own primordial disposition toward wretchedness generates the misguidance; it cannot transfer to the Prophet. — Qashani gives the inward causal account. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 4:113/q11/p3 The different meaning of this phrase discussed at 3:69 also applies here. — Tabatabai mentions this earlier sense only to say it does not fit the present context. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 4:113/q12 How are the clauses about self-misguidance and harm joined?  [وَمَا يُضِلُّونَ إِلَّآ أَنفُسَهُمْ وَمَا يَضُرُّونَكَ مِن شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
- 4:113/q12/p1 The first statement is parenthetical and the denial of harm is coordinated with it. — Alusi describes this as the construction of the two clauses. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:113/q13 Why can they cause the Prophet no harm?  [وَمَا يَضُرُّونَكَ مِن شَىْءٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the assurance rests on divine preservation, valid reliance on outward evidence, or the false witness's own liability.
- 4:113/q13/p1 God protects, guides and supports him and exposes their plot, including in the future. — Tabari specifies divine disclosure and strengthening; Zajjaj adds support for true religion; Qushayri says divine might guards him; Qaffal and Abu Hayyan read a future promise; Biqai compares 5:67 and includes outward and inward safety; Alusi gives alternatives of protection from heretics or people. Ibn Abbas says God's choice of the Prophet for prophethood makes that office impossible for them to strip. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; THALABI(ابن عباس)]
- 4:113/q13/p2 A judgment on the outward evidence would not have harmed him because hidden facts were unknown. — Kashshaf and Nasafi excuse the Prophet's apparent thought; Razi says judging by appearances is what the law commands; Baydawi and Bursevi deny any bias; Elmalili states the judge is not liable when contrary facts are unknown. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 4:113/q13/p3 False testimony harms its giver, not the Prophet. — Wahidi identifies the false witness as the harmed party. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 4:113/q14 How does 'min shayʾ' intensify the denial of harm?  [مِن شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
- 4:113/q14/p1 The augmentative min makes the negation universal, excluding even the smallest harm. — Abu Hayyan and Samīn emphasize little or great; Alusi calls shayʾ an accusative cognate object in sense; Ibn Ashur says it is genitive after min but originally an absolute object; Baydawi describes cognate extent. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:113/q14/p2 Ḍarr can mean diminution or reduction. — Damghani compares 3:111. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 4:113/q15 What is the grammatical relation of 'God sent down the Book and Wisdom' to the preceding clause?  [وَأَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the waw starts a new favor, marks a circumstance, or supplies the protection's rationale.
- 4:113/q15/p1 The revelation clause supplies the reason that the plot cannot harm or mislead him. — Jishumi and Tabarsi invoke revelation of the Book and rulings; Razi connects scripture and conveyance of law with immunity from doubt; Ajhuri and Tabarsi are reported by Alusi; Abu Muslim rejects that explanatory link; Tabatabai grounds infallibility in revelation and teaching. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأجهوري والطبرسي); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)-]
- 4:113/q15/p2 It begins a new coordinated statement of God's favor. — Wahidi and Qurtubi give the independent reading; Ibn Ashur says coordination reinforces the favor and sees an opening and closing ring. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:113/q15/p3 The waw is circumstantial: they cannot harm him while God is sending revelation. — Wahidi supplies implicit qad and compares 4:90; Qurtubi cites Imru' al-Qays's birds-in-nests line; Tabatabai says it qualifies the apparently unrestricted denial of harm and notes grammarians' expected qad. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 4:113/q16 What is the Book sent to the Prophet?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'Book' is identified by its text, its adjudicating function, or an inward form of knowledge.
- 4:113/q16/p1 The Quran or recited scripture. — Tabari says it contains clarification, guidance and admonition; Ibn Atiyya calls it recited revelation; Elmalili says it is proof above other proofs; Kuranyolu says its identification is unanimous. - 4:113/q16/p3 It denotes comprehensive detailed knowledge after gifted existence. — Qashani gives the inward interpretation. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:113/q17 What does Wisdom designate in this verse?  [ٱلْحِكْمَةَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Wisdom is the Prophet's Sunna, Quranic judgment and rulings, a faculty, prophethood, or other divine teaching.
- 4:113/q17/p1 Judgment by revelation, its rulings, or its lawful and forbidden commands. — Muqatil says lawful and unlawful; Tabari expands to commands, prohibitions, promises and warnings and compares 2:129; Abu Sulayman adds clarification, inspiration and a sound answer in the heart; Biqai sees grasp of the Book's purposes and sound practice; Study Quran has religious understanding more broadly. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي,ابن عباس,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 4:113/q17/p2 It is the Sunna. — Jishumi presents the alternative; Ibn al-Jawzi's lexical survey cites 2:129, 2:231 and this verse; Nasafi, Ibn Kathir and Kuranyolu adopt or report it. [7 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:113/q17/p3 Book and Wisdom together designate the Quran, with Wisdom as its nonlegal or clarifying content. — Alusi reads both qualities together; Kuranyolu reports the Quran's nonlegal knowledge for religious and worldly life. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:113/q17/p4 It is a faculty to understand and apply revelation and judge well. — Ibn Atiyya says it includes native cultivated judgment so the Prophet does not speak from whim; Kuranyolu reports a distinctive mental capacity, comparing 2:269. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:113/q17/p5 It is prophethood. — Ibn Ashur distinguishes Wisdom from the Quran and the further Sunna and unseen knowledge. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:113/q17/p6 It is other divine teachings conveyed through revelation, beneficial in this world and the next. — Tabatabai rejects the Quran-rulings and Quran-Sunna identifications in favor of this broader category. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين,بعضهم)-]
- 4:113/q17/p7 It is detailed rulings and manifestations of divine attributes, joined to action. — Qashani's inward account combines knowing and doing. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 4:113/q18 What does ḥikma mean lexically and in other Quranic contexts?  [ٱلْحِكْمَةَ · meaning]
turns on: Lexical core versus context-specific Quranic usages.
- 4:113/q18/p1 Its root sense is restraint, and wisdom keeps its bearer from falsehood. — Ibn Faris gives ḥ-k-m as restraining a fool; Jarir's line asks Banu Hanifa to restrain fools; the horse's rein illustrates restraint; others define perfection of knowledge and action, or knowledge joined to action and justice. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,ابن قتيبة,بعض أهل العلم,جرير,غيره)]
- 4:113/q18/p2 In other verses it has several contextual senses: admonition, understanding, prophethood, Quran and Quranic sciences. — Ibn al-Jawzi lists admonition at 54:5, understanding at 6:89, 19:12, 21:79, 31:12, prophethood at 2:251 and 38:20, Quran at 16:125, and Quranic sciences at 2:269. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 4:113/q19 What did God teach the Prophet that he had not known?  [وَعَلَّمَكَ مَا لَمْ تَكُن تَعْلَمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the teaching denotes law, unseen information, all divinely given knowledge, personal rank, or special inward knowledge.
- 4:113/q19/p1 Religious law, ordinances, and knowledge of lawful and unlawful conduct. — Muqatil says scripture and religion; Qatada adds this world and the next as proof against creation; Ibn Abbas and Muqatil are repeatedly cited for the law; Qaffal cites 42:52 for prior lack of Book and faith. ## 4:113/q20 How was this teaching conveyed?  [وَأَنزَلَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيْكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ وَعَلَّمَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether divine teaching is angelic revelation alone or includes direct inspiration and unveiling.
- 4:113/q20/p1 By revelation, with God as ultimate teacher and Gabriel as the messenger. — Ibn Kathir cites 42:52–53 and 28:86; Tabatabai cites descent to the heart in 2:97 and 26:192–195 and further revelation texts 42:13, 4:163, 46:9 and 7:203; Samarrai says 53:2's 'strong in powers' identifies Gabriel, because ilham can also name lesser inspirations. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 4:113/q20/p2 Through divine encounter, unveiling and direct spiritual knowledge. — Al-Wasiti contrasts Muhammad's capacity to bear the Essence with Moses' bearing of the Attributes; Qashani describes unveiling, annihilation and restored existence. [2 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QASHANI]
- 4:113/q20/p3 Through both angelic revelation and direct inspiration cast into the heart. — Tabatabai distinguishes the two and cites reports, including the Holy Spirit supporting Prophet and Imam; he reads 42:52 as a guiding word cast to the Prophet and 21:73 as support of the Imam. [6 notes: TABATABAI(الروايات)]

## 4:113/q21 How is 'you had not known' parsed grammatically?  [مَا لَمْ تَكُن تَعْلَمُ · grammar]
- 4:113/q21/p1 Taʿlamu is the predicate of takun; the negative construction is jussive. — Nahhas and Qurtubi explain dropped final nūn ḍamma and wāw after two quiescent letters; Qurtubi calls taʿlam accusative as kāna's predicate. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 4:113/q22 What is the great favor at the verse's end?  [وَكَانَ فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ عَظِيمًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the culminating favor names one gift or the totality and force of divine benefaction.
- 4:113/q22/p1 Prophethood, especially the Prophet's universal mission and leadership. — Muqatil adds the Book; Damghani and Ibn al-Jawzi compare 17:87; Baydawi says nothing exceeds prophethood; Jishumi and Tabarsi add seal of prophets, master of messengers and intercession; Bursevi and Alusi add universal prophethood and supreme leadership. [10 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 4:113/q22/p2 All the singular gifts God gave the Prophet, beyond enumeration. — Jishumi dates favor from creation to mission; Abu Sulayman and Abu Hayyan include every special gift; Nasafi stresses what God taught and bestowed; Kuranyolu adds that humanity benefits from these qualities. [7 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 4:113/q22/p3 The knowledge God taught him. — Tabari calls historical and future knowledge favor; Razi and Study Quran infer knowledge's nobility from the repeated great-favor declaration; Qushayri says no gift rivals such knowledge. Bursevi cites the Prophet's prayer against useless knowledge and Muslim's report from Abu Hurayra that beneficial knowledge is among deeds whose reward continues after death. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); BURSEVI(أبو هريرة); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 4:113/q22/p4 Faith. — Tha'labi and Ibn al-Jawzi transmit Ibn Abbas's reading; Abu Hayyan also reports it. [3 notes: THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 4:113/q22/p5 The realized inward perfection bestowed on the Prophet. — Qashani attributes it to the practice bringing him to his attained state; Qushayri places the favor in divine pre-eternal regard. [2 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]
- 4:113/q22/p6 A declaration of benefaction after the account of protection and teaching. — Tabatabai says this is the phrase's rhetorical force; Ibn Ashur observes its echo of the opening. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:113/q22/p7 God Himself, understood as the Prophet's favor and mercy to the worlds. — Bursevi quotes the saying about the spheres being created for the Prophet. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 4:113/q23 What does the word faḍl mean in this passage and Arabic usage?  [فَضْلُ · meaning]
turns on: Lexical increase versus contextual benefaction or prophethood.
- 4:113/q23/p1 It may denote prophethood. — Damghani and Ibn al-Jawzi compare the Night Journey passage 17:87 with the concluding favor. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 4:113/q23/p2 Its original sense is increase, and here it denotes benefaction or blessing. — Ibn al-Jawzi says figurative uses depend on context and compares the opening to 4:83, 12:38, 24:10 and 24:22. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 4:113/q24 What does raḥma mean here and in other Quranic uses?  [وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: The contextual sense of the term across the cited verses.
- 4:113/q24/p1 Here it can mean favor or blessing. — Ibn al-Jawzi compares 18:65 and also lists grace or favor at 28:46. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 4:113/q24/p2 Elsewhere it can mean safety, deliverance, tenderness, forgiveness, relief, affection, or protection from wrongdoing. — Ibn al-Jawzi cites 39:38, 33:17, 57:27, 6:54, 2:178, 48:29 and 12:53 respectively. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 4:113/q25 What does ḍalāl mean in Arabic and other Quranic uses?  [يُضِلُّوكَ · meaning]
turns on: Which contextual shade of the root is in view.
- 4:113/q25/p1 Its base range includes confusion and departure from what is right. — Ibn al-Jawzi says ḍalāl and ḍalāla are synonymous, notes two verb forms, labels ḍalīl or muḍallal an erring person, and says aḍalla may mean bury the dead; Ibn al-Sikkit cites a missing camel and an unfound mosque or house; Jishumi describes turning from salvation to ruin. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن السكيت)]
- 4:113/q25/p2 Elsewhere it can signify misguidance, loss, misery, or futile nullification. — Ibn al-Jawzi cites 36:62 and 37:71 for misguidance; 12:8,95,97, 36:24, 40:25 for loss; 34:8 and 54:24 for misery; 18:103 and 47:1 for nullification. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 4:113/q25/p3 It can mean deviation from a matter without unbelief. — Damghani compares David in 38:26. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 4:113/q26 What does the verse teach about the Prophet's protection from error?  [وَمَا يَضُرُّونَكَ مِن شَىْءٍۢ وَعَلَّمَكَ مَا لَمْ تَكُن تَعْلَمُ · theology]
turns on: Whether protection is explained by revelation, a distinct unfailing knowledge, or proper judicial reliance on appearances.
- 4:113/q26/p1 God guards him in religious conveyance and judgment through revelation and teaching. — Razi ties his future protection to scripture and the law he conveys; Ibn Kathir cites 42:52–53; Elmalili describes inward and outward protection; Kuranyolu covers conveying, teaching and applying religion; Study Quran calls the disclosure preservation of isma; Tabatabai denies a consequential sin or blameworthy approach. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 4:113/q26/p2 The Prophet's apparent thought followed admissible evidence, so it involved no culpable bias or error. — Razi says revealed law itself requires outward evidence; Ibn Ashur's account says the opponents never gained confident intent; Elmalili says a judge cannot be liable for undisclosed contrary facts. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:113/q26/p3 Infallibility is a special divinely given knowledge that cannot be overpowered by desire. — Tabatabai compares a settled disposition to courage or chastity but distinguishes ordinary beneficial knowledge, which can lapse as faculties compete; the Prophet's special knowledge dominates all other causes and prevents sin and error absolutely. He invokes 12:33, 42:52, 21:73 and reports on the Holy Spirit. [10 notes: TABATABAI(الروايات)]
- 4:113/q26/p4 A favored prophet can stumble in a witnessed moment and then return after reproach to pre-eternal favor. — Sulami's anonymous voice compares 9:43. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 4:113/q27 What practical response does the verse require of the Prophet and believers?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The source's move from this protection to obedience, humility, and moral consequences.
- 4:113/q27/p1 The Prophet should thank God through obedience, adhere to revelation, and resist those diverting him. — Tabari says God will protect him in obedience while no one could save him if he followed their desire; the verse reminds him of his welfare and duty. [2 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 4:113/q27/p2 People should credit virtues and good deeds to divine favor, without self-admiration, while continuing to act. — Bursevi says even a lifetime of deeds cannot repay existence; Shah Shuja declines to prize fifty years of pilgrimage above one poor man's deed; al-Sa'di's verse attributes benefits to enabling grace. [5 notes: BURSEVI(السعدي,شاه شعاع الكرماني)]
- 4:113/q27/p3 Good and evil return to their actors. — Bursevi cites al-Sa'ib's poem and tales of a calf slaughterer and a charitable mother whose child was restored, illustrating consequences and mercy. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الصائب)]

## 4:113/q28 What legal rules follow from the theft and advocacy case?  [طَّآئِفَةٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ أَن يُضِلُّوكَ · law]
turns on: The distinction between dishonest advocacy, impartial adjudication, and evidentiary limits.
- 4:113/q28/p1 One must not advocate a right for or against a person without knowing the facts. — Bursevi attributes the rule to al-Haddadi; Tabari grounds the prohibition in 4:105 and the duty not to aid sin. [2 notes: BURSEVI; TAB-FULL]
- 4:113/q28/p2 A judge acting on apparent evidence is not liable for a hidden falsehood. — Elmalili states this legal implication; Razi says revealed law requires outward judgment. [2 notes: ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL]
- 4:113/q28/p3 A judge must treat Muslim and non-Muslim litigants without favoritism. — Bursevi reports al-Haddadi's rule. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 4:113/q28/p4 Possession of stolen property alone does not establish that its possessor stole it. — Bursevi reports al-Haddadi's evidentiary rule. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 4:113/q29 What inward readings are offered for the Prophet's favor and teaching?  [وَعَلَّمَكَ مَا لَمْ تَكُن تَعْلَمُ فَضْلُ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكَ · inward]
turns on: The different spiritual objects of teaching: rank, divine reality, servanthood, and perfected travel.
- 4:113/q29/p1 Divine encounter and eternal favor disclose divine realities and the Prophet's perfected state. — Al-Wasiti contrasts bearing the Essence and Attributes; Qushayri reports pre-eternal honor; Qashani speaks of latent perfection, annihilation, and restoration in divine knowledge. [9 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي,بعضهم,قيل); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]
- 4:113/q29/p2 The Prophet receives the fullest spiritual knowledge and rank. — Junayd divides knowledge into gnosis, expression, servanthood and service; Abu Yazid distinguishes explanatory knowledge and proof; Sahl ranks believers, scholars, and prophets by knowledge of God, His commands and His days; al-Jurayri contrasts scholars' outward, sages' inward, and elders' essential perception; another scheme gives four scholars different shares. Qushayri says all borrow the Prophet's light and those outside his banner miss nearness. [10 notes: SULAMI(أبو محمد الجريري,أبو يزيد,الجنيد,بعضهم,سهل); QUSHAYRI]
- 4:113/q29/p3 The Prophet learns servanthood, and human knowledge of God depends on obedience. — Qushayri reports manners of service, clarity about the Real, and knowledge of God's measure proportionate to following His command. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 4:113/q29/p4 No worldly or spiritual obstacle diverted him from arrival. — Bursevi names both kinds of obstacle and prays for protection and union with holy souls. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 4:113/q30 What rhetorical pattern in the passage reinforces its message?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The repeated forms and deliberately unspecified objects.
- 4:113/q30/p1 The repeated terms and balanced forms echo misguidance, teaching, and favor. — Abu Hayyan notices yudilluka/ma yudillun and allamaka/ta'lam, plus passage-wide travel and battle metaphors and changed complements; Ibn Ashur identifies the closing 'great favor' as an echo of the opening. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:113/q30/p2 The objects of seeing and knowing remain unspecified, creating meaningful ambiguity. — Abu Hayyan notes the indeterminacy of bima araka Allah and ma lam takun ta'lam and ellipses across the passage. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 4:113/q31 How is a paused hamza eased according to the reading tradition?  [شَىْءٍۢ · readings]
- 4:113/q31/p1 Hamza's pause follows the Uthmani spelling, using an Arabic-permitted eased form that matches the written shape. — Sulaim transmits Hamza's practice; al-Dani, Faris, Makki, Ibn Shurayh and al-Shatibi are cited. Ibn al-Jazari says 'without analogy' means without analogy detached from the codex and that grammar still constrains the form; eased hamza is represented by alif, ya or waw, while initial hamza remains alif. [4 notes: NASHR(جماعة من أهل الأداء,حمزة)]

## 4:113/q32 May a pause signal the vowel of the pronoun hāʾ?  [وَرَحْمَتُهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the pronoun's vowel is stable enough to signal, and whether surrounding sounds make signaling heavy.
- 4:113/q32/p1 Rūm or ishām may signal the pronoun vowel without restriction. — Many performance authorities and Ibn Mujahid permit this; al-Dani records it and calls it more analogical since the pronoun is like a fixed inflection. The general pause rule includes stable ḍamma except transferred or epenthetic vowels. [3 notes: NASHR(الداني,كثير من أهل الأداء)]
- 4:113/q32/p2 Neither signal is permitted on the pronoun hāʾ. — Other authorities reason its vowel is incidental; the apparent view of al-Shatibi supports this; al-Dani records the view as sound. [2 notes: NASHR(آخرون,الداني)]
- 4:113/q32/p3 It is barred after matching ḍamma, silent wāw, kasra, or silent yāʾ, but allowed elsewhere. — A group of precise authorities uses ease of pronunciation and clarification to distinguish contexts, citing examples such as minhu and hudahu. [1 notes: NASHR(جماعة من المحققين)]

## 4:113/q33 How does the verse relate to other statements about Book and Wisdom?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ وَٱلْحِكْمَةَ · links]
turns on: The cited intertext for the paired gifts and the mode of revelation.
- 4:113/q33/p1 The revelation and teaching here are God's guiding light and unexpected mercy. — Ibn Kathir compares 42:52–53 and 28:86; Tabatabai reads 42:52 as Spirit and guiding word. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:113/q33/p2 Other Quran verses also pair the Book and Wisdom as gifts to prophets. — Study Quran cites 2:129,151,231; 3:81,164; 4:54; 5:110; and 62:2. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 4:113/q34 What other sense of qawl is discussed in lexical notes adjacent to this passage?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The different lexical and contextual senses in the source's wider survey.
- 4:113/q34/p1 Qawl and kalām overlap in ordinary usage, though some restrict kalām to meaningful speech. — Ibn al-Jawzi notes maqūl for tongue and qawla/qawwāl for a frequent speaker; a reported view excludes uninformative 'stood, sat' from kalām; Arabic examples and two poems show qawl can mean think or suppose, and Abu Zakariyya describes its syntax. ## 4:113/q35 What biographical accounts illustrate divine care for the Prophet?  [* · reports]
turns on: The distinct biographical episodes given in the notes.
- 4:113/q35/p1 Khadija and Abu Talib supported him until their deaths in the same year, three years before the Hijra. — Ibn Ishaq says Khadija sustained his Islam and Abu Talib defended him. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق)]
- 4:113/q35/p2 After Abu Talib's death Quraysh abused him openly, yet he assured his daughter God would protect him. — Urwa recounts dirt thrown onto the Prophet, his daughter washing it away in tears, and his reassurance. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(عروة بن الزبير)]
- 4:113/q35/p3 He traveled alone to al-Taif seeking Thaqif's protection and acceptance of God's message. — Ibn Ishaq places the journey after Abu Talib's death and increased abuse. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق)]
