# 39:49: 21 questions

## 39:49/q01 How does the opening fāʾ connect this episode to its context?  [فَإِذَا · links]
turns on: Whether the fāʾ resumes 39:45 across parenthetical verses, develops the intervening punishment scene, or signals another causal link.
- 39:49/q01/p1 It resumes the recoil from God's name in 39:45 and exposes the polytheists' contradiction when they call on Him in distress. — Al-Zamakhsharī treats 39:46–47 as a reinforcing aside and contrasts the simple coordination at 39:8; al-Rāzī stresses immediate reversal; al-Nasafī calls the fāʾ causal and likens the unbeliever's appeal to a believer's; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Burhān, al-Bursevī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ālūsī explain the intervening verses as asides. Al-Ālūsī reports Abū Ḥayyān's and Abū ʿAlī's objections to that parenthesis and defends it; Abū Ḥayyān also allows this connection. [23 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:49/q01/p2 It branches from the preceding portrait of wrongdoers and their severe punishment, whose character the verse explains. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers the punishment connection over al-Zamakhsharī's long parenthesis. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes the verse an explanation of the preceding wrongdoers. Al-Biqāʿī also permits the preceding ransom scene as its starting point. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 39:49/q01/p3 The fāʾ is a causal connection in which a stated result implies its cause. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative but notes possible repetition of 39:50–51 unless one scene is worldly and the other in the afterlife. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:49/q02 Who is 'the human' here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular is generic, as the later plural suggests, or identifies the man named in the transmitted report.
- 39:49/q02/p1 Humanity generally, especially the characteristic conduct of polytheists or most people. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the human kind; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursevī limit the behavior to its prevalent pattern. Al-Ālūsī argues that the later plural 'most of them' makes a named individual strained. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the generic definite denotes the polytheist class and gives the repeated 39:8 passage a new point. Al-Biqāʿī sees a generalized human pattern. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 39:49/q02/p2 Abū Ḥudhayfa ibn al-Mughīra is the person meant or the reported occasion. — Muqātil names Abū Ḥudhayfa; Ibn al-Jawzī links that identification to 39:8. Some reports shorten the name to Ḥudhayfa. Al-Ālūsī reports it but argues that the later plural favors a generic subject. [6 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 39:49/q03 What does 'harm' mean here or in its cited Arabic uses?  [ضُرٌّۭ · meaning]
turns on: The notes collect broad hardship and several contextual or vowel-specific senses of the root.
- 39:49/q03/p1 Hardship or affliction broadly, including poverty, trial, and any injury. — Ibn Qutayba cites 6:17 and 2:177; Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes ḍurr with ḍamma from ḍarr with fatḥa. Al-Biqāʿī includes harm from an unexpected source. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī include illness. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:49/q03/p2 Illness is a specific sense, exemplified by Job's complaint and this verse. — Ibn Qutayba cites 21:83; Ibn al-Jawzī cites 21:83 and 39:49. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); TABATABAI]
- 39:49/q03/p3 Drought or scant rain is a specific hardship. — Ibn Qutayba reads the mercy after harm at 10:21 as rain after drought; Ibn al-Jawzī also cites 30:33. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 39:49/q03/p4 The terror of danger at sea is another use. — Both Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Jawzī cite 17:67, where other invoked powers vanish. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 39:49/q03/p5 With fatḥa, ḍarr denotes the opposite of benefit, harm or loss. — Ibn Qutayba cites 26:72–73 and 7:188 for inability to help or harm; Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts the vowel forms and cites 3:176 and 47:32 for inability to harm God. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 39:49/q03/p6 Need is a further Qur'anic sense. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 16:53, where need prompts an appeal to God. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 39:49/q03/p7 Hunger is a further Qur'anic sense. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites the hardship of Joseph's brothers in 12:88. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 39:49/q03/p8 Emaciation is an Arabic sense of the root. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports Ibn Fāris and adds related usages for a co-wife, a woman with co-wives, and someone impaired in sight or body. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس)]

## 39:49/q04 What does the person's calling God in distress show?  [دَعَانَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the focus is the sincere appeal itself or the reversal that follows it.
- 39:49/q04/p1 He turns sincerely to God alone, setting aside idols because only God can remove the harm. — Muqātil describes sincere monotheism; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say idolaters abandon idols and call the Creator; al-Ṭabrisī calls the distressed person sincere as a Muslim; al-Biqāʿī says distress makes them recognize God's greatness; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says other ties are cut off. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:49/q04/p2 His prayer and later boast expose a corrupt inconsistency between hardship and ease. — Al-Rāzī calls the concise sequence a fluent exposure; the Study Quran reports that the needy recognize God as protector yet credit gains to striving; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees desire and visible means as the source of delusion. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:49/q05 Why does the verse say 'then' before the gift?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 39:49/q05/p1 The delay signals severe or prolonged hardship, sharpening the blame for forgetting it so quickly. — Al-Biqāʿī makes the duration and the recipient's rapid ingratitude the point. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:49/q06 What does 'khawwalnāhu' mean?  [خَوَّلْنَٰهُ · meaning]
- 39:49/q06/p1 God granted or bestowed a gift, a favor given freely without compensation or as a reward. — Mujāhid glosses 'We gave him'; al-Zajjāj, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Bursevī stress gratuitous giving. Abū Ḥayyān cites Zuhayr's uses of istakhwalū/yukhwalū for wealth and Abū al-Najm's line about generous giving. Al-Bayḍāwī says this granting belongs particularly to God. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو النجم,زهير); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]

## 39:49/q07 What does the bestowed blessing include?  [نِعْمَةًۭ · referent]
- 39:49/q07/p1 Any beneficial relief or prosperity, including health, safety, ease, and wealth. — Al-Ṭabarī lists ease, wealth, health and well-being; Ibn ʿAṭiyya uses 39:52 to support broad scope; al-Ṭabrisī adds bodily health and abundant livelihood; Abū Ḥayyān includes removal of harm. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 39:49/q08 Why is the gift called a blessing 'from Us'?  [مِّنَّا · rhetoric]
- 39:49/q08/p1 The phrase assigns the favor wholly to God, although the recipient soon ignores its giver. — Al-Biqāʿī denies any other contributor; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts 'from Us' with the speaker's giver-free passive 'I was given it.' [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:49/q09 Where does the conditional clause end?  [مِّنَّا قَالَ · grammar]
- 39:49/q09/p1 The response to the second idhā is 'he says,' so one should not pause after 'from Us.' — Al-Nasafī identifies qāla as the apodosis. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 39:49/q10 What is the effect of the passive 'I was given it'?  [أُوتِيتُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 39:49/q10/p1 The passive obscures the giver and displays the speaker's self-importance and failure to thank God. — Al-Biqāʿī says the speaker attends to the gift's greatness; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts God's 'from Us' with the recipient's detachment of the bounty from its giver. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 39:49/q11 What does the masculine suffix in 'I was given it' refer to, and why masculine?  [أُوتِيتُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether innamā contains a relative pronoun or the suffix agrees with the blessing's intended masculine sense.
- 39:49/q11/p1 It resumes the blessing construed by meaning as a thing, wealth, a portion of blessing, or the act of bestowal. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī explain semantic masculine agreement; Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Baghawī invoke the act of conferring. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the visible gift like a demonstrative and compares 46:24; al-Biqāʿī sees magnification, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees denial that it is God's gift. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:49/q11/p2 It resumes relative mā in innamā. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Bursevī allow this parse; al-Ālūsī considers it unlikely because Qur'anic spelling joins innamā. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:49/q12 How is 'innamā ... ʿalā ʿilm' parsed?  [إِنَّمَآ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā neutralizes inna and restricts the claim, or is a relative pronoun governing its own clause.
- 39:49/q12/p1 Mā is a particle neutralizing inna and making an exclusive claim; ʿalā ʿilm states the cause or circumstance. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this as allowing a verbal clause; Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives ʿalā ʿilm an accusative circumstantial role; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares a mā...illā restriction and reads ʿalā causally; al-Ālūsī stresses exclusive attribution to knowledge. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:49/q12/p2 Mā is relative: 'that which I was given' is based on knowledge. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says mā is inna's noun with an omitted predicate, while al-Biqāʿī makes ʿalā ʿilm its predicate; al-Samīn notes the suffix returns to mā. Al-Ālūsī considers this reading unlikely because of the joined Qur'anic spelling. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:49/q13 What knowledge does the boast claim as the cause of the gift?  [عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whose knowledge is claimed—practical knowledge, foreknowledge, or God's knowledge—and whether the ground is skill, merit, or divine approval.
- 39:49/q13/p1 The speaker claims his own skill, planning, effort, trade, or knowledge of earning produced the benefit. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Asad and others compare Qarun's claim in 28:78; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes ordinary practical acumen here from Qarun's gold, silver and alchemy crafts. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes this as the contextual sense. Al-Ṭabrisī says it betrays ignorance about benefit and harm; al-Rāzī adds medical treatment. Al-Iṣlāḥī includes credit to another human intermediary, planning and science. [26 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI(الحسن، وأبو علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(الحسن والجبائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R); ELMALILI]
- 39:49/q13/p2 He claims God knew he had merit, goodness, or a special rank and therefore granted the blessing. — Al-Ṭabarī says God's knowledge of approved deeds is what he alleges, though 'in my view' marks his own estimation. Some formulate it as God's knowing a good quality in him, others as deserved rank; Qurtubī says worldly allotment is taken as evidence of rank. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this against the preceding account. [35 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة ومقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(IK, Q,IK, R); TABATABAI-]
- 39:49/q13/p3 He says he himself knew in advance that his merit entitled him to receive it. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq connect foreknowledge to entitlement; al-Naqqāsh reports simple advance knowledge; al-Ṭabarī records Qatāda's 'information I had.' Al-Jishumī's Qatāda report says more generally that he relied on information already in his possession. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, R); JISHUMI(قتادة)]
- 39:49/q13/p4 He attributes the gift to knowledge God had taught him. — Al-Ḥasan is reported for this distinct account; the Study Quran also transmits it. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 39:49/q13/p5 He says God's approval or pleasure with him explains the gift. — Ibn ʿĪsā, a reported view in al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī's cited alternative give this sense; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects it. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]

## 39:49/q14 What does 'rather, it is a trial' identify and say about the gift?  [بَلْ هِىَ فِتْنَةٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pronoun points to the bounty, the utterance, or knowledge, and whether the outcome is testing or punishment.
- 39:49/q14/p1 The blessing or its bestowal is a divine test of gratitude, obedience, patience, or ingratitude. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī and others frame the alternatives as thanks or disbelief; al-Rāzī likens it to gold in fire and adds patience on loss. Ibn Kathīr says God already knows the outcome; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the trial reveals response without God needing knowledge. Al-Jishumī adds God's rights, and al-Biqāʿī says the test establishes proof. Al-Baghawī and others include gradual enticement. Al-Ālūsī notes that calling the instrument itself 'trial' intensifies the expression. [34 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 39:49/q14/p2 The person's boastful utterance itself is the trial, for which he can be punished. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl and others point to the preceding speech; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows the inferred sentence and says it can cause a trial or arise from one, citing 5:8 and 23:99–100. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports but finds the account particularly far-fetched. [7 notes: THALABI(الحسين بن الفضل); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 39:49/q14/p3 The blessing becomes punishment when the person appropriates it to himself. — Al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī report this alternative; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it far-fetched. Al-Biqāʿī relates an adverse outcome to gradual entrapment. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 39:49/q14/p4 Knowledge itself is the test: action on it earns reward, and neglect turns it into evidence against its owner. — Al-Jishumī reports this further explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 39:49/q15 Why is the pronoun in 'rather, it' feminine?  [هِىَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement follows fitna, the form niʿma, or an implicit feminine referent.
- 39:49/q15/p1 It agrees with the feminine predicate 'trial.' — Al-Farrāʾ says masculine 'he/it is a trial' would also be grammatical, comparing 18:98, where masculine hādhā accompanies feminine raḥma; al-Zamakhsharī compares mā jāʾat ḥājatuka. Al-Nasafī and al-Bayḍāwī give the same predicate agreement. [7 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:49/q15/p2 It agrees with feminine niʿma after the prior masculine form considered its meaning. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī describe a shift from semantic to formal agreement; al-Thaʿlabī identifies the blessing as referent. Al-Biqāʿī gives a rhetorical explanation: masculine magnifies the gift, feminine belittles it against God's power and its deluding outcome. [6 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:49/q15/p3 It refers to the act of bestowing or the recipient's state, understood as feminine. — Al-Baghawī posits al-inʿām; al-Samīn also permits state or bestowal; al-Ālūsī lists the act of granting. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:49/q16 Is 'rather, it is a trial' read with a masculine pronoun?  [هِىَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the pronoun follows the feminine noun or the preceding masculine construal.
- 39:49/q16/p1 The transmitted wording uses feminine hiya. — Its agreement is explained through niʿma, fitna, or an implied feminine sense by the grammarians. [2 notes: FARRA; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 39:49/q16/p2 An alternate reading has masculine huwa fitna. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī say it follows the masculine construction of innamā ūtītuhu; al-Farrāʾ, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī allow the form, the last still referring to blessing or bestowal. [8 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قارئ); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:49/q17 What do most people fail to know?  [أَكْثَرَهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the missing knowledge concerns the test, its duties and consequences, or the true source of blessings.
- 39:49/q17/p1 They do not recognize that prosperity is a test, often a gradual enticement. — Al-Ṭabarī connects this ignorance to poor judgment; al-Nasafī, al-Baghawī, al-Bursevī and Abū Ḥayyān stress gradual entrapment. Al-Biqāʿī calls the speakers coarse and says even hypothetical rescue from Hell would be misattributed. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:49/q17/p2 They do not know the gratitude and duties blessings require. — Al-Jishumī distinguishes this failure to understand obligations from failure to understand the outcomes of their affairs. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 39:49/q17/p3 They do not grasp the consequences of their own conduct. — Al-Jishumī reports this as a separate formulation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 39:49/q17/p4 They cannot distinguish blessing from affliction or recognize God as the source even through human causes. — Al-Ṭabrisī supplies both explanations. [2 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]

## 39:49/q18 What does the verse teach about crediting worldly success?  [* · theology]
turns on: The sources emphasize divine agency, responsibility for human choices, or an inward assessment of harm and benefit.
- 39:49/q18/p1 God is the source of blessings, and claiming independent human agency or merit leads to ingratitude or shirk. — Al-Jishumī says the claim hides the true giver of benefits and harms. Al-Iṣlāḥī calls credit to oneself or another shirk: human beings are means, and their abilities and science are God's gifts dependent on His permission. A cited couplet denies Jupiter and Saturn control and attributes decree to the Lord of heaven. Al-Biqāʿī says pure beneficence meets unearned arrogance. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(الشاعر); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:49/q18/p2 Both prosperity and adversity occur under God's will, while chosen human actions incur their own consequences. — Kur'an Yolu cites 4:78 and says attribution to God or people depends on context; 39:52 shows provision's abundance and restriction follow divine decree. The test is passed by using the blessing according to God's commands. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:49/q18/p3 A knower sees outward affliction as mercy; real harm is a hardened, tarnished heart, and blessing is the heart's return to God. — Al-Sulamī reports al-Junayd, who says claiming to deserve a blessing denies that blessing. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]

## 39:49/q19 How does the verse's hardship-and-relief pattern connect with other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the comparison looks back to repeated hardship prayers or forward to consequences and mercy.
- 39:49/q19/p1 It repeats the pattern at 39:8 and elsewhere, now adding a pointed contrast with the surrounding passage. — Ibn al-Jawzī links the named person to 39:8; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr note the opening parallel. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī distinguish the earlier wāw from the present fāʾ. The Study Quran cites 10:12 and 30:33; al-Iṣlāḥī says the earlier account already explained the forgetting; Asad compares 7:189–190 and distinguishes Qarun's particular boast in 28:78 from humanity's general pattern here. ## 39:49/q20 What rhetorical point does the verse's rapid reversal make?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the rebuke concerns ingratitude in general or specifically reputation and pride.
- 39:49/q20/p1 It concisely censures a person who forgets prolonged distress and boasts after unearned relief. — Al-Rāzī praises the fluent brevity. Al-Biqāʿī calls repaying beneficence with evil strange and reads the masculine gift pronoun as magnifying it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says desire and visible means make people forget the divine test. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:49/q20/p2 The warning also addresses ostentation and pride in worldly success. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports Sufyān's 'Woe to the show-offs'; al-Iṣlāḥī says the transience of worldly success should be explained to those proud of it. [2 notes: THALABI(سفيان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:49/q21 What is implied by an unspoken feminine object in the related wording qālahā?  [قَالَ · grammar]
- 39:49/q21/p1 It means an implied feminine 'word' or utterance; masculine qālahu would also be correct. — Al-Farrāʾ supplies kalima as the implicit object. [1 notes: FARRA]
