THALABI: The verse answers an implicit plea about what the believers have been required to bear: God requires of a soul only what it can manage. THALABI reporting ابن عباس: Ibn Abbas transmits God's answer to the closing petitions: He has forgiven, excused, shown mercy, and granted victory, and will not burden people beyond their capacity. THALABI: In answer to whether a just God could impose beyond capacity, one view says God could do so because creation and command belong to Him, but He has promised not to. THALABI: Another answer stresses God's declared vastness, mercy, and denial of injustice, even though injustice could never be attributed to Him. QUSHAYRI: In answer to their plea, God replaced earlier peoples' destruction with forgiveness, their transformation with good deeds, and stones with mercy. WAHIDI-BASIT: The author answers that the same objection applies to foreknowledge: if God's prior knowledge says someone will die an unbeliever, that person cannot change it; al-Shafiʿi says the Qadari are defeated if they concede foreknowledge. WAHIDI-BASIT reporting ابن عباس: Ibn Abbas says God answers the prayer section by section: He will not hold them accountable or impose burdens, and has pardoned, forgiven, shown mercy, and granted victory. BAGHAWI-FULL reporting أبو هريرة: Abu Hurayra reports that when the verse about hidden thoughts was revealed, it distressed the Companions, who said they could bear prayer, fasting, jihad, and charity but not this. BAGHAWI-FULL reporting ابن عباس: A report from Ibn Abbas says the ruling specifically concerns believers: God has made their religious obligations easy and only requires what they can do, as affirmed by 2:185 and 22:78. BAGHAWI-FULL: They cannot attain good action except through obedience granted by God, nor leave disobedience except through His mercy.
