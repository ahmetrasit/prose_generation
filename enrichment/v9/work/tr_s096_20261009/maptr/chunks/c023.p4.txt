RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The Jabriyya argue that if humans created faith, the human would deserve more praise for this noblest act than God; therefore God creates faith. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The Jabriyya argue that Muslims praise God for the blessing of faith; if faith were a human act, praising God for it would wrongly credit Him with another’s act. | mentions 3:188 RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The Jabriyya argue that obligatory acts only avert blame and cannot earn praise, while voluntary grace would make God gain praise and thus be perfected by another. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): They further argue that if God is praised intrinsically, no act causes that entitlement, so no duty, compensation, or reward is owed; if it is not intrinsic, God would depend on another. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (الجبرية): The Jabriyya infer that faith too comes from God: all praise belongs to Him, praise follows blessing, and faith is the greatest blessing. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (الجبرية): They argue that God’s self-praise, though blameworthy among people, shows divine acts cannot be judged by human standards and challenges Muʿtazilī principles. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (المعتزلة): The Muʿtazila say absolute praise is deserved by one whose acts contain no evil, rulings no injustice, and judgments no oppression; they reject the Jabriyya picture of God creating disbelief then punishing it. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (المعتزلة): They argue that if a servant deserves God’s praise, the servant must be capable of action, contrary to determinism; if God praises Himself, that is also invalid, so the phrase only works on their doctrine. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): True praise is any act expressing reverence for a benefactor: inward belief in perfection, spoken words of praise, or bodily deeds that express it; saying al-ḥamdu lillāh merely reports praise. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): One group says God cannot command praise: if it repays a blessing, the command makes generosity transactional; if not, it is needless toil imposed on another. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The same group says praise is useless to a God complete in Himself and that punishing people eternally for omitting a useless act would not befit the generous Lord. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): Created nurturers seek their own profit, such as reward or praise, while God nurtures for the creatures’ benefit rather than His own. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The sequence assigns praise to God, then names Him Lord of the worlds: Allah signifies necessary perfection, while Lord of the worlds shows that all else flows from His generosity, making Him beyond completeness. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The seven Fatiha verses remove seven vices: praise dispels desire, Lord of the worlds greed, Judgment Day anger, worship and aid pride and vanity, guidance caprice, the favored path unbelief and doubt, and the final phrase innovation. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (الله تعالى): In a divine report transmitted by the Prophet, God responds to each Fatiha phrase: the opening remembers Him, praise praises Him, the mercy names magnify Him, Judgment Day glorifies Him or is entrusted to Him, worship belongs to Him, aid relies on Him, and guidance is the servant's request. RAZI-FULL, d. 606 (author): The report that God says “my servant praised Me” shows that praise is higher than remembrance.
