QURTUBI, d. 671 (الطبري): Al-Ṭabarī says God’s praise of Himself implicitly commands His servants to praise Him, as though the wording said, Say praise belongs to God. QURTUBI, d. 671 (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم): A report in Nawādir al-uṣūl from Anas says that if someone possessed the whole world and then praised God, the praise would be better than it. QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): Rabb can also mean the deity, as shown by a poet’s line about a lord upon whose head a fox urinates. QURTUBI, d. 671 (بعض العلماء): Some scholars call Rabb the greatest name of God because callers frequently invoke Him by it and it conveys the bond of care, mercy, and need between Lord and servant. QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): One derivation traces Rabb to tarbiya, since God manages and nurtures creation; the stepdaughter is called rabība because her mother’s husband raises her. In this sense it is an attribute of action, while owner or master denotes an attribute of essence. | mentions 4:23 QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): With the definite article, Rabb is specific to God; without it, the term can apply to God or a servant, but God alone owns owners and owned things, creates and provides, and cannot lose His dominion as human owners can. QURTUBI, d. 671 (قتادة): Qatāda says al-ʿālamīn is the plural of ʿālam and means every existing thing other than God, with no singular on this pattern, like rahṭ or qawm. QURTUBI, d. 671 (الفراء وأبو عبيدة): Al-Farrāʾ and Abū ʿUbayda restrict the term to rational beings: humans, jinn, angels, and devils, excluding animals because the plural form is for rational beings; al-Aʿshā’s verse is cited. QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): Abu ʿAbd Allah explains that the person first receives the world and then the enduring word of praise, which is better because the world perishes while the word remains among the righteous deeds. | mentions 18:46 QURTUBI, d. 671 (وهب بن منبه): Wahb ibn Munabbih says God has eighteen thousand worlds, of which this world is one. QURTUBI, d. 671 (أبو سعيد الخدري): Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī says God has forty thousand worlds, with the inhabited world from east to west counting as one. QURTUBI, d. 671 (الربيع بن أنس عن أبي العالية): Al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas transmits from Abū al-ʿĀliya that jinn and humans are worlds and that each of earth’s four corners contains 1,500 worlds created to worship God. QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): Qurtubi prefers the first view, that the worlds include every creature and existent thing, supported by Pharaoh asking about the Lord of the worlds and Moses answering that He is Lord of the heavens, earth, and all between them. | mentions 26:23, 26:24 QURTUBI, d. 671 (الزجاج والخليل): Al-Zajjāj derives ʿālam from knowledge or a sign: it is everything God created in this life and the next and it points to its maker and planner; al-Khalīl likewise says a sign or marker indicates a thing. QURTUBI, d. 671 (الجنيد): A man praised God before al-Junayd but stopped before saying Lord of the worlds; al-Junayd urged him to complete the verse, explaining that created things vanish when paired with the Eternal. QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): Rabb may be read in the nominative by syntactic disconnection, meaning He is Lord of the worlds, or in the accusative as a word of praise. QURTUBI, d. 671 (author): Another wording says that what the servant gave was more than what he received; this frames praise as the servant’s gift and the world as God’s gift, though both ultimately come from God. QURTUBI, d. 671 (رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم): Ibn ʿUmar reports that angels could not record a servant’s praise fitting God’s majesty, so God told them to write it as the servant had said until He rewards him. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (أبو جعفر الطبري): Al-Tabari and al-Mubarrad treat hamd and shukr as synonymous; al-Sulami also attributes this position to Jaʿfar al-Sadiq and Ibn ʿAtaʾ, who says praise is thanks for God teaching it. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (ابن عطية): Ibn ʿAtiyya says “praise to God, in thanks” restricts praise to a blessing and therefore argues against equating praise and gratitude. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (بعض العلماء): Some say gratitude is broader because it uses tongue, limbs, and heart, whereas praise is only verbal. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم): Abu Malik al-Ashʿari reports that purification is half of faith, praise fills the balance, and glorification with praise fills the space between heaven and earth. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (طائفة من العلماء): One group prefers alhamdulillah because it combines praise with the monotheism expressed by “There is no god but God.” QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (طائفة من العلماء): Another group prefers “There is no god but God” because it repels unbelief and shirk and is the profession for which people are fought. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (author): Hamd means complete praise; the definite article encompasses every kind of praise, all of which God deserves for His beautiful names and exalted attributes. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم): The Prophet admitted that he could not enumerate praise of God, illustrating the creature’s inability to fulfill God’s due praise. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (author): Other explanations say God praised Himself because servants cannot fully praise Him, or because abundant blessings exceed their capacity; divine praise removes the burden of gratitude. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (جعفر الصادق): Jaʿfar al-Sadiq offers an esoteric reading of the letters of hamd: unity, dominion, and permanence; knowing these attributes is the reality of praising God. QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (author): God praises Himself and opens His Book with praise, while scripture and the Prophet forbid human self-praise; human praise is compromised by motives, unlike God’s eternal self-praise. | mentions 53:32 QURTUBI-FULL, d. 671 (سيبويه): Sibawayh says the nominative attributes praise to the speaker and all creation, while the accusative attributes it to the speaker alone.
