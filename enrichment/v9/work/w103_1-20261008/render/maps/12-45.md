# 12:45: 29 questions

## 12:45/q01 Who is the one who survived from the two?  [ٱلَّذِى نَجَا مِنْهُمَا · referent]
- 12:45/q01/p1 The surviving man is the king's cupbearer or wine server, Joseph's former fellow prisoner. — Ibn Abbas identifies him as the servant who dreamed of pressing wine (Wahidi); Baghawi says he survived execution, and Tabari's Ibn Ishaq account and Ibn Abi Hatim's Mujahid name him Nabu. Others identify him through his earlier dream and Joseph's correct interpretation. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg)]

## 12:45/q02 What brought the survivor to remember Joseph, and what did he recall?  [وَٱدَّكَرَ · reports]
- 12:45/q02/p1 The failed attempt to explain the king's dream prompted the cupbearer to recall Joseph, his reliable interpretations, and his request to be mentioned. — Muqatil, Mujahid (Ibn Abi Hatim), Kalbi (Wahidi), Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Kashshaf, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi's Kashifi, Ibn Ashur, and Study Quran recall the accurate interpretations of the cupbearer and baker's dreams. Qurtubi and Study Quran connect the request to 12:42. The recollection occurs during the court discussion. [21 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس، الحسن، مجاهد، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg)]
- 12:45/q02/p2 The verse simply states that the survivor remembered Joseph or his request after a lapse, without a specified immediate trigger. — Ibn Kathir, Baydawi, Alusi and others give the delayed recollection as the event itself; Tabari's Ibn Ishaq account names Nabu. [13 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,قتادة); TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 12:45/q03 Whose earlier forgetting does this recollection indicate?  [وَٱدَّكَرَ · links]
turns on: Whether the form ادكر itself entails earlier forgetfulness and how 12:42 is read.
- 12:45/q03/p1 The cupbearer forgot Joseph or forgot to mention him to the king. — Ibn Atiyya calls the verb evidence, while leaving the issue open and comparing 18:63. Tabatabai says 12:45 confirms the cupbearer as the forgetter in 12:42. Tabatabai and Islahi specify forgetting to mention Joseph; Ibn Ashur says calling the period an ummah magnifies this delay. Ibn Qutayba says both readings of the phrase are true: Joseph was recalled after time and after having been forgotten. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 12:45/q03/p2 The verb can mean remembering or recounting without establishing prior forgetting by the cupbearer. — Ibn al-Anbari, reported by Wahidi and Razi, compares reflexive verbs with simple ones, including the use of اعتدى in 2:194. Ibn Jawzi notes that this allows the earlier forgetter to be Joseph. Islahi calls the words for remembrance synonymous. Ibn al-Sayyid prefers Joseph as the antecedent of the earlier forgetting, drawing on the rescued man's recollection only after a period. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURHAN(ابن السيد)+]

## 12:45/q04 Why did mention of Joseph wait until this moment?  [وَٱدَّكَرَ بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · reports]
turns on: Whether he forgot, deliberately withheld the information from fear, or both men forgot.
- 12:45/q04/p1 The cupbearer actually forgot; Joseph's continued imprisonment was God's decree. — Wahidi's Kalbi and Qurtubi report divine decree behind the delay. Tabatabai says he failed to mention Joseph to his lord for years; Ibn Ashur portrays a prolonged forgetting. Ibn Qutayba explicitly joins elapsed time and an actual lapse of memory: Joseph was remembered after a while and after being forgotten. [8 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 12:45/q04/p2 He remembered but concealed Joseph because mentioning the prison offense might remind the king of his own offense. — Wahidi's Kalbi reporting Ibn Abbas, Ibn Jawzi's Abu Salih reporting Ibn Abbas, Razi, and Qurtubi give this motive. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:45/q04/p3 Both Joseph and the cupbearer forgot. — Razi transmits this as an alternative to the fear of recalling the cupbearer's crime. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 12:45/q05 What does وَٱدَّكَرَ mean, and how is its form derived?  [وَٱدَّكَرَ · grammar]
- 12:45/q05/p1 It means he remembered; the form develops from a root of remembrance through substitution and assimilation ending in doubled dāl. — Majaz derives it from dhakartu. Akhfash explains the underlying اذتكر and preservation of voicing; Nahhas and Qurtubi say voiced dhāl cannot assimilate to unvoiced tāʾ, so tāʾ changes to dāl. Zajjaj, Tabrisi, Ibn Jawzi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Ibn Ashur detail the sequence. Ibn Ashur calls dāl lighter and more eloquent; Ibn Atiyya compares other Arabic forms and 54:15. The Prophet's recitation of مدكر at 54:15 is reported from the Sahih by Ibn Ashur. Wāhidi refers to that same passage. Bursevi gives idh-tadhakkara, and Islahi calls the remembrance forms synonymous. Burhan derives ادّكر from اذتكر, with dāl replacing dhāl and assimilation, and stresses that its root is ذ ك ر rather than an independent d-k-r root; it cites Ibn Faris on morphology's importance to understanding language. Samarrai compares مدّكر and explains that the افتعل tāʾ changes to dāl beside dāl or dhāl and assimilates, as it changes to ṭāʾ beside ṣād or ḍād. [23 notes: MAJAZ; AKHFASH; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURHAN(ابن فارس); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 12:45/q05/p2 It can mean he remembered and told or reported the matter, without implying an earlier lapse. — Ibn al-Anbari, cited by Wahidi and Razi, argues from Arabic reflexive forms that can equal simple verbs, with 2:194 as an example. Biqai parses it as seeking remembrance. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); BIQAI-FULL]
- 12:45/q05/p3 A reported analysis begins by replacing tāʾ with dhāl before assimilation. — Akhfash reports this alternative morphological route; his comparison with forms at 4:128 concerns the voicing and emphatic sound that govern assimilation. [2 notes: AKHFASH]

## 12:45/q06 How is وَٱدَّكَرَ read?  [وَٱدَّكَرَ · readings]
turns on: The consonant assimilation and the reported reciters' forms.
- 12:45/q06/p1 The prevalent dāl reading is وَٱدَّكَرَ. — Kashshaf, Razi, Nasafi and Alusi explicitly favor its eloquence; Samīn calls it common and compares مدكر at 54:15. The assimilation is described by Zajjaj and Ibn Ashur. [15 notes: MAJAZ; AKHFASH; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 12:45/q06/p2 Al-Hasan reads a form with dhāl rather than the usual dāl. — Kashshaf links wa-dhakara to original tadhakkara; Ibn Jawzi calls it a doubled dhāl. Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, Razi and Alusi give variant vocalizations of the form. Alusi and others regard the dāl form as more eloquent. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:45/q06/p3 Mujahid read the verb with a jussive ending and softened consonant. — Durr preserves this distinct report without further explanation. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 12:45/q07 How is the recollection clause connected syntactically?  [وَٱدَّكَرَ بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the remembrance supplies circumstance or another description of the survivor.
- 12:45/q07/p1 The recollection clause is circumstantial to the survivor, with بعد governed by ادكر. — Samīn prefers attachment to the relative pronoun or its referent, the subject of نجا. Abu Hayyan and Bursevi call the clause circumstantial; Samīn explicitly makes ادكر govern بعد. Alusi allows a circumstantial analysis with an understood قد. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 12:45/q07/p2 It may coordinate with نجا in the relative clause. — Samīn offers this as an alternative. Alusi rejects joining the later recollection to the relative clause, since it is new information rather than a known description. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 12:45/q08 What does بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ mean, including its reported readings?  [بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · meaning]
turns on: The transmitted vowel and consonant forms support time, forgetting, a community, or a favor; the favor's recipient is disputed.
- 12:45/q08/p1 It means after a period, usually a long span or years. — Ibn Qutayba and Bursevi specify seven years; Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn Abbas, Dahhak, Saʿid ibn Jubayr and Mujahid, with Dahhak leaving the term to God. Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas, Hasan, Mujahid, the majority, Ata and language scholars; its large-group sense underlies a span of time. Ibn Durustawayh requires an omitted noun, and Akhfash says ummah is formally singular but collective, with a dog-community hadith. Ibn Ashur explains a generation-length span and cites 3:110; Bursevi compares a collective of days and hours. Qurtubi mentions 11:8. Ibn Qutayba explains the period sense through the lifespan of a community and compares 11:8; Ibn Jawzi transmits the same generational analogy and reports the exegetes' reading of 12:45 and 11:8 as a period measured in years. Damghani calls it a measured period of years, and Itqan and Burhan include the time sense. [55 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,الحسن,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(ابن عباس، الحسن، مجاهد، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن درستويه,ابن عباس وغيره,الأخفش,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(almost all authorities); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Bg); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن قتيبة); BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 12:45/q08/p2 It means after forgetfulness, read with an open or light form, sometimes with hāʾ and sometimes a still mīm. — Farra, Majaz and Ibn Qutayba report the reading; Ibn Jinni distinguishes أمه from إمة, while Thaʿlabi's Abu Ubayda cites a poem with amihat. Jishumi's Abu Ubayd also cites poetry; Nahhas gives أمه يأمه, 'to forget.' Reports attribute variants to Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Ikrima, Mujahid, Dahhak, Qatada, Zayd ibn Ali, Abu Raja, Shibl and others. Alusi rejects dismissal of the reading. Majaz records a variant wording and the explanation 'after forgetfulness'; Ibn Qutayba reports Ibn Abbas's form alongside other reciters' form and says the forgetfulness and elapsed-time meanings both apply. Burhan attributes a light-mīm reading meaning forgetfulness to Ibn Amir. [40 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ(آخرون,بعضهم); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قراء); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس وابن عمر وعكرمة ومجاهد والضحاك وقتادة); THALABI(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس وعكرمة والضحاك,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); JISHUMI(أبو الهيثم,ابن عباس وعكرمة والضحاك,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد وشبل بن عزرة); TABRISI(ابن عباس، ابن عمر، الضحاك، قتادة، زيد بن علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة والضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد بن علي والضحاك وقتادة وأبو رجاء وشبيل بن عزرة وربيعة بن عمرو,الزمخشري,عكرمة ومجاهد وشبيل بن عزرة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس وزيد بن علي وقتادة والضحاك وأبو رجاء,الزمخشري,مجاهد وعكرمة وشبيل بن عزرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة والحسن وقتادة ومجاهد والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد بن علي); NAHHAS; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن عباس); BURHAN(ابن عامر)]
- 12:45/q08/p3 It means after a group or community of people had passed. — Al-Hasan's reading is transmitted by Ibn Abi Hatim, Mawardi and Durr. Tabrisi also gives a group united in purpose as a sense of ummah; Qurtubi's Akhfash and the dog hadith illustrate ummah as a community or species. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن درستويه,الأخفش,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 12:45/q08/p4 With a kasra reading, إمة means after a blessing or favor. — Ibn Jinni contrasts it with forgetting. Al-Ashhab al-Uqayli is named by Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn and Alusi. Kashshaf, Razi and Samīn cite ʿAdī ibn Zayd's poetry; Abu Hayyan also reports an open-hamza form for a favorable condition. Interpretations disagree on who received the favor. [16 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن عباس وابن عمر وعكرمة ومجاهد والضحاك وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي); TABRISI(الأشهب العقيلي); RAZI-FULL(الأشهب العقيلي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة والضحاك,الأشهب العقيلي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الأشهب العقيلي); SAMIN-DURR(الأشهب العقيلي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,الأشهب العقيلي)]

## 12:45/q09 Is the reading of forgetfulness with a still mīm accepted?  [أُمَّةٍ · readings]
turns on: The legitimacy of the transmitted still-mīm form versus the proposed regular verbal noun.
- 12:45/q09/p1 The still-mīm form is a transmitted dialectal or irregular verbal noun for forgetting. — Thaʿlabi's Mujahid, Jishumi's Mujahid, Ibn Atiyya's Mujahid and Shibl, Abu Hayyan's Ikrima/Mujahid/Shibl and Samīn's reports attest it. Jishumi's Abu al-Haytham prefers it and calls the mīm with fatḥa erroneous. Abu Hayyan criticizes dismissing readings; Samīn says an authentic reading cannot be wrong; Alusi likewise dismisses critics. [9 notes: THALABI(مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وشبل بن عزرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد وشبيل بن عزرة); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد وعكرمة وشبيل بن عزرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)+; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وزيد بن علي)+; JISHUMI(أبو الهيثم)+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+]
- 12:45/q09/p2 The still-mīm form is erroneous; the verbal noun has a vowel on mīm. — Zajjaj rejects the still-mīm form attributed to Abu Ubayda and requires fatḥa. Kashshaf calls still mīm an error; Abu Hayyan and Samīn discuss that judgment. Another grammarian in Jishumi accepts the open-mīm form, while Abu al-Haytham rejects it. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+; JISHUMI(أبو الهيثم)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-]

## 12:45/q10 How long was the interval before the cupbearer remembered?  [بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · reports]
turns on: The sources give different figures or leave the length unspecified.
- 12:45/q10/p1 The interval was seven years. — Ibn Qutayba and Bursevi state seven; Baghawi says Joseph had remained in prison seven years; Study Quran connects the seven years with 12:42. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Bg)]
- 12:45/q10/p2 The interval was four years after the two prisoners separated. — Mawardi transmits this from Ibn Abbas along with the report that the prison lay outside the city. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 12:45/q10/p3 The interval was two years. — Ibn Ashur attributes this figure to the Torah while explaining the rhetorical use of ummah. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:45/q10/p4 It was years or a long stretch without a fixed number in these reports. — Ibn Abi Hatim transmits 'years' from Saʿid ibn Jubayr, a long age from Mujahid, and an unspecified term known to God from Dahhak. Durr transmits Ibn Abbas and Saʿid ibn Jubayr on years. Damghani and the exegetes reported by Ibn Jawzi speak of a period measured in years without specifying a number. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES(almost all authorities); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 12:45/q11 What is the syntactic role of بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ in the sentence?  [بَعْدَ أُمَّةٍ · grammar]
- 12:45/q11/p1 It is part of an intervening or circumstantial recollection clause between 'said' and the cupbearer's quoted speech. — Baydawi calls the clause intervening. Abu Hayyan and Bursevi call it circumstantial; Alusi calls it parenthetical and allows a circumstantial construction with understood قد; Samīn makes بعد dependent on ادكر. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 12:45/q11/p2 The time sense can be understood through an ellipsis such as 'a span of time'. — Ibn Durustawayh, reported by Qurtubi, argues this because ummah also means a large community. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن درستويه)]

## 12:45/q12 How is أَنَا۠ recited here?  [أَنَا۠ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted length of the final vowel.
- 12:45/q12/p1 A canonical form has the short final vowel. — Qiraat-ER identifies this as the standard canonical recitation. Nashr says the other readers omit the alif of أنا in connected recitation across the following-vowel environments, while all retain it at pause. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR]
- 12:45/q12/p2 A canonical option extends the final ā with madd. — Qiraat-ER records this as another canonical option. Nashr identifies the two Medinese readers as retaining the alif of أنا before a following hamza with ḍamma or fatḥa, specifically including أنا أنبئكم. For other contexts before kasra, it records competing Qalun routes, naming transmitters of retention and deletion; the author accepts both through Abu Nashit and deletion through al-Hulwani, with both allowed in Abu Awn's route. [7 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(المدنيان)]

## 12:45/q13 Why does the cupbearer begin his offer with 'I'?  [أَنَا۠ · rhetoric]
- 12:45/q13/p1 Putting himself forward catches the king's attention and strengthens his surprising claim to explain the baffling dream. — Ibn Ashur says the king would be surprised that this man could explain what had baffled the court. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:45/q14 What does the cupbearer offer to do in أُنَبِّئُكُم بِتَأْوِيلِهِۦ?  [أُنَبِّئُكُم بِتَأْوِيلِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verb is 'inform' or the reported 'bring,' and whether his agency is direct or through Joseph.
- 12:45/q14/p1 He will report the king's dream interpretation obtained from Joseph, who possesses the knowledge. — Tabari, Mawardi, Kashshaf, Ibn Jawzi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Tabatabai and Ibn Ashur distinguish his report from Joseph's knowledge. Ibn Ashur calls attribution to himself figurative because he causes the explanation. Razi says he wants the court to see Joseph's learning and speaks cautiously after the others failed; Biqai instead describes his offer as confident. Wahidi's Hasan intended not to say it came from Joseph. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 12:45/q14/p2 In the variant wording, he says he will bring them the interpretation. — Al-Hasan and Ubayy transmit آتيكم; al-Hasan asks whether Joseph was their prophet or whether the cupbearer had already informed them, defending the sense of bringing back information. Zajjaj objects that it differs from the consonantal codex; Samīn calls its meaning close to 'inform'. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ALUSI-FULL(أبي,الحسن)]

## 12:45/q15 Which wording of 'I will inform you' is read?  [أُنَبِّئُكُم · readings]
turns on: The transmitted verb and its conformity to the written codex.
- 12:45/q15/p1 The majority wording is أُنَبِّئُكُم, 'I will inform you'. — Ibn Atiyya calls it the majority reading; Nahhas, cited by Qurtubi, says it means 'I will tell you when asked.' Zajjaj opposes the alternative because it departs from the written codex. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+]
- 12:45/q15/p2 Al-Hasan and Ubayy have آتيكم, 'I will bring you'. — Ibn Abi Hatim gives Hasan's challenge, 'Was Joseph their prophet?' Wahidi says he meant to report the answer without identifying Joseph. Ibn Atiyya and Alusi connect Ubayy's codex with the reading; Abu Hayyan also reports it as in the codex. Durr preserves Hasan's defense and another reader's similar question. Samīn says the meanings are near. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ALUSI-FULL(أبي,الحسن); ZAJJAJ(الحسن)-; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)-]

## 12:45/q16 Why does he speak as though he himself can supply the interpretation?  [أُنَبِّئُكُم · rhetoric]
- 12:45/q16/p1 He is the cause of obtaining Joseph's answer; speaking in the first person draws attention and can display Joseph's learning. — Ibn Ashur calls the claim figurative because he causes the explanation and says 'I' arouses surprise. Razi says he hoped people would recognize Joseph's learning, and cautioned his promise because court interpreters had failed. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 12:45/q16/p2 He confidently claims that he can report the answer once sent. — Biqai describes his assurance; the cupbearer has previously seen Joseph's successful interpretation, as Razi and Study Quran note. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 12:45/q17 Why does the cupbearer leave Joseph unnamed in his offer?  [أُنَبِّئُكُم بِتَأْوِيلِهِۦ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes offer surprise and fear as motives for withholding Joseph's name.
- 12:45/q17/p1 He leaves the source unnamed so the prisoner's unexpected skill and the explanation will surprise the court. — Ibn Ashur gives the effect of delaying Joseph's identification; Mawardi observes that he initially promises to identify someone who knows, without naming him. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:45/q17/p2 He avoids saying the information comes from Joseph. — Wahidi explains al-Hasan's variant this way; reports of fear of reopening the cupbearer's imprisonment provide a separate motive for withholding Joseph's story. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL]

## 12:45/q18 What does the cupbearer ask with فَأَرْسِلُونِ?  [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · interpretation]
- 12:45/q18/p1 He asks permission to go to Joseph, in prison, obtain the interpretation, and return with it. — Tabari and Thaʿlabi note that the dispatch and journey are omitted. Baghawi supplies 'send me to him'; Ibn Atiyya calls it permission to go; Baydawi allows 'to the knowledgeable person' or 'to prison.' Qurtubi says his return may be to explain the dream or display Joseph's knowledge and obtain his release. Ibn Ashur says he seeks leave from the king's presence; Alusi transmits a report that he knelt to ask. Abu Hayyan reports Ubayy's codex adding فأرسلون with this sense. Burhan expands the suppressed sequence: send me to Joseph to ask about the dream; they send him; he comes to Joseph. The failed court interpretations make the purpose clear. [22 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURHAN]
- 12:45/q18/p2 He asks the king's party to authorize him to find the knowledgeable interpreter, while initially leaving that person unnamed. — Mawardi stresses that Joseph was initially unnamed. Razi says the cupbearer speaks cautiously because the court has failed. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 12:45/q19 Who is addressed by the plural forms in 'inform you' and 'send me'?  [أُنَبِّئُكُم فَأَرْسِلُونِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the court is addressed with the king or the plural honors the king alone.
- 12:45/q19/p1 The plural addresses the king and the assembled courtiers or officials. — Wahidi's Abu Bakr, Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Tabatabai and Asad allow or prefer this. Tabatabai says officials managed state affairs and cites the later return to 'the people' at 12:46; Asad says the whole assembly. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES+; TABATABAI+]
- 12:45/q19/p2 It addresses the king alone as an honorific plural. — Wahidi's Abu Bakr explains that companions share the king's view and command. Ibn Jawzi, Razi, Qurtubi and Alusi allow the singular royal addressee; Ibn Ashur chooses it and compares Q 23:99. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 12:45/q20 How is the final first-person ending of فَأَرْسِلُونِ read?  [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the first-person yāʾ is retained, especially in pause.
- 12:45/q20/p1 The seven-reader or standard form omits the final first-person yāʾ at pause. — Qiraat-ER records elision in pause. Itqan says the written Qur'anic form omits the final yāʾ of فأرسلون in this category, without listing an exception. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; ITQAN]
- 12:45/q20/p2 Yaʿqub and the ten-reader form retain the full first-person yāʾ. — Ibn Jawzi reports Yaʿqub keeps the yāʾ both connected and paused, paralleling two other verses; Nasafi reports his form. Qiraat-ER describes the ten-reader option. Nashr counts فأرسلون among 81 first-person suffix yāʾs at verse endings and specifies that Yaqub retains its yāʾ in both connection and pause. Its surrounding inventories count other Yusuf suffixes and report disputed retention at other Yusuf words. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); QIRAAT-ER; NASHR]

## 12:45/q21 Where was Joseph's prison relative to the king's city?  [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted location reports differ; Ibn Ashur argues separately from what the court would have known.
- 12:45/q21/p1 It lay outside the king's city. — Ibn Abbas is cited by Tabari, Mawardi, Baghawi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi; Ibn Abi Hatim's Suddi also says so. This explains why the cupbearer sought dispatch. Mawardi adds a four-year separation. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:45/q21/p2 It lay within the city. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Alusi transmit this alternative without a stated argument. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:45/q21/p3 A place beside the Nile, eight miles from Fustat, was identified as the prison in the commentators' day. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan describe this contemporary identification; Alusi leaves the actual location to God. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:45/q21/p4 The cupbearer's certainty Joseph remained there suggests a private prison. — Ibn Ashur reasons that a release or death from another sort of prison would have reached the king and court. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:45/q22 What action is left unstated between this request and Joseph's reply?  [فَأَرْسِلُونِ · links]
- 12:45/q22/p1 The king or court sent the cupbearer to Joseph; he traveled to the prison and then relayed or received the interpretation. — Tabari and Thaʿlabi identify the ellipsis; Abu Hayyan specifies the dispatch and arrival, and Ibn Kathir says the king sent him. Kuranyolu says he got permission and brought the answer back. Burhan explicitly supplies the omitted dispatch, the cupbearer's question to Joseph about the dream, and his arrival before Joseph. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURHAN]
- 12:45/q22/p2 Joseph received the dream from the former prisoner without reproaching his failure to remember or demanding release first. — Ibn Kathir records this narrative detail after the dispatch. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 12:45/q23 What do the neighboring dream discussion and interpretation say about dreams?  [* · links]
- 12:45/q23/p1 Mixed, confused dreams and private thoughts are not treated like true visions, which can be investigated and interpreted. — Ibn Atiyya draws this distinction. Tabatabai reports the officials calling the king's vision a jumble and saying they could not interpret such dreams. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI(الملأ)]
- 12:45/q23/p2 The officials may mean that they cannot interpret dreams generally or know only how to interpret true dreams. — Tabatabai gives these alternative explanations of their reply. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الملأ)]
- 12:45/q23/p3 The ensuing interpretation predicts seven plentiful years, seven famine years, then rain. — Tabatabai's report from al-Qummi includes the king's seven fat cows devoured by seven lean cows and the green and dry ears, and says the cupbearer remembered after a time. Kuranyolu summarizes the abundance, famine and hardship. [2 notes: TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:45/q23/p4 The term يغماث in the later account may come from rescue and relief or from rain. — Abu Hayyan cites an Arab woman's request for rain as evidence for the rain derivation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 12:45/q24 How is Joseph characterized in the account that follows?  [* · links]
- 12:45/q24/p1 Joseph is exceptionally truthful, pious, and skilled in interpreting dreams. — Razi says the cupbearer praises Joseph's virtue, piety, learning and the accuracy of both prison interpretations. Tabrisi gives ṣiddīq as strongly affirming truth or especially truthful; Abu Hayyan calls it an intensive form grounded in long acquaintance and tested interpretations. Qurtubi notes the singular form. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 12:45/q25 What other reports connect this scene to Joseph's imprisonment and life?  [* · reports]
- 12:45/q25/p1 Gabriel told Joseph he would leave prison the next day and described Jacob's grief and reward. — Muqatil says Joseph asked after Jacob, whose grief had blinded him, and Gabriel said Jacob's reward equaled one hundred martyrs. [1 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل)]
- 12:45/q25/p2 Joseph entered the well at seventeen and later lived through eighty years of servitude, prison and kingship, then twenty-three more years after reunion. — Mawardi transmits this chronology from al-Hasan. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 12:45/q26 What broader point is drawn from Joseph's story?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:45/q26/p1 God directs human affairs in ways people cannot foresee; what they dislike may benefit them and what they favor may harm them. — Asad contrasts this Qur'anic point with Genesis' romantic account and connects it to 2:216. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 12:45/q27 What broader senses of أُمَّةٍ are recorded alongside its use for elapsed time here?  [أُمَّةٍ · meaning]
- 12:45/q27/p1 A human group or community. — Ibn Qutayba, as reported by Ibn Jawzi, gives a human kind or group as the basic sense; Damghani lists Muslim, unbelieving, and universal communities; Burhan includes group among Qur'anic senses. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة); BURHAN]
- 12:45/q27/p2 An exemplary leader or learned, God-fearing man. — Ibn Jawzi lists the leader and learned, God-fearing man; Burhan includes an exemplary leader. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BURHAN]
- 12:45/q27/p3 A religion. — Ibn Jawzi lists religion and cites al-Nabigha's line as poetic evidence; Burhan includes this Qur'anic sense. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(النابغة); BURHAN]
- 12:45/q27/p4 Stature or height. — Ibn Faris, reported by Ibn Jawzi, cites a poetic line describing people as tall. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس)]
- 12:45/q27/p5 Abundant blessing. — Ibn Faris, reported by Ibn Jawzi, gives this lexical sense. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس)]

## 12:45/q28 How is the vowel difference in readings of أُمَّةٍ classified?  [أُمَّةٍ · readings]
turns on: Whether seven is an exact count or a figure for abundance in the broader reading tradition.
- 12:45/q28/p1 It changes meaning through vowels while leaving the written form unchanged. — Ibn Qutayba uses أمة as an example of this class. Burhan says such a variation is accepted with sound transmission and Arabic compatibility. Nashr classifies the أمة variants the same way and transmits Ibn Qutayba's classification. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BURHAN; NASHR(ابن قتيبة)]
- 12:45/q28/p2 The verse's vowel variation belongs within a wider taxonomy of recitation changes, while pronunciation traits alone do not create different wording. — Nashr surveys seven kinds of variation, including vowels, letters, order, and addition or deletion, while distinguishing pronunciation traits such as assimilation, imala, and lengthening. It also reports al-Razi's seven groups and Ibn Qutayba's other classes, and cross-references comparable performance issues. [10 notes: NASHR(أبو الفضل الرازي,ابن قتيبة,ابن كثير)]
- 12:45/q28/p3 Seven in the account of the Quranic ahruf may signify abundance rather than an exact total. — Nashr reports that some interpret seven, seventy, and seven hundred figuratively and apply that view to the ahruf and the hadith about branches of faith. [1 notes: NASHR; NASHR-]
- 12:45/q28/p4 The seven Quranic ahruf are a literal count. — Nashr rejects the figurative count because reports describe Gabriel increasing from one letter to seven and Abu Bakra understanding Mika'il's silence as the final number. [1 notes: NASHR+]

## 12:45/q29 Why may readers explain the accessible Arabic meaning of this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:45/q29/p1 Understanding Arabic makes accessible verses intelligible and permits reflection and explanation of them. — Tabari relates Shuqayq's account of Ibn Abbas explaining Surat al-Nur and argues from commands to ponder verses that accessible meanings must be comprehensible. His Arabic-poem analogy says language must be understood before reflection, and he rejects opposition to explaining meanings God has not reserved. Damghani lists lessons, scripture, and commands or prohibitions among senses of ayat in the broader vocabulary of scriptural reflection. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(شقيق); DAMGHANI]
