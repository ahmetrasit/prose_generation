# 6:52: 27 questions

## 6:52/q01 What prompted the prohibition on expelling the worshippers?  [وَلَا تَطْرُدِ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · reports]
turns on: Which proposal the reports describe, and whether later Medinan incidents are causes or applications of a Meccan verse.
- 6:52/q01/p1 Quraysh or other Meccan notables demanded removal of poor believers from the Prophet's company as a condition of meeting or following him. — Ibn Masud, Qatada and many later transmitters name Suhayb, Ammar, Bilal, Khabbab and other poor followers; chiefs scorned their low status, dress or alleged dependence. Accounts vary on names, whether the Prophet refused, considered separate times, or a written pledge was begun. Ibn al-Anbari, Razi and Qurtubi say the Prophet hoped to win converts, not disparage believers. Reports of Sa'd put six companions there; lists differ. Khabbab also describes morning and evening teaching of Qur'an, religion and the afterlife. Ibn Zayd adds a man ashamed to be seen with Salman and Bilal, after which the Prophet was told to greet them with mercy; Baghawi transmits Mujahid's naming of Bilal and Ibn Umm Abd. Ibn Ishaq, as reported by Ibn Hisham, names Abu Fakihah Yasar alongside Khabbab, Ammar and Suhayb, and says Quraysh mocked the poor as unworthy of receiving guidance first; he connects the response to 6:52–54. [64 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الكلبي,سعيد,عبد الله بن مسعود,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الربيع بن أنس,سعد بن أبي وقاص,مجاهد); THALABI(جبير بن نفيل,سليمان وخباب بن الأرت,عبد الله بن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن مسعد,الربيع,خباب بن الأرت,سعد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL(رؤسا من المشركين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,سعد بن أبي وقاص); RAZI-FULL(عبدالله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(المشركون,سعد,سعد بن أبي وقاص); BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قريش); BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعد); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); BURSEVI(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(البيهقي,الواحدي,سعد بن أبي وقاص); TABATABAI(عبد الله بن مسعود); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭ, W); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق)]
- 6:52/q01/p2 Al-Aqra ibn Habis and Uyayna ibn Hisn requested an exclusive audience away from poor companions, sometimes before visiting Arab delegations; Gabriel's revelation interrupted a proposed written agreement. — Khabbab and Salman transmit this version. Accounts describe their embarrassment at wool clothes or slaves, the Prophet dropping or tearing the page, recalling the believers, sitting knee to knee, and staying until they rose; 18:28 follows in some accounts. Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Ashur question its use as a Meccan revelation occasion because these men appeared in Medina; Ibn Ashur allows a later analogous request. Khabbab's report in Ibn Majah says Gabriel revealed 6:52 when al-Aqra and Uyayna sought private access and dismissal of the poor; it also links 6:53–54 and 18:28 to their favor and continued companionship. [37 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(خباب,خباب بن الأرت); IBNABIHATIM(خباب); THALABI(الكلبي,النبي); WAHIDI-ASBAB(الأقرع بن حابس وعيينة بن حصن); WAHIDI-BASIT(خباب بن الأرت); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(سلمان وخباب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(خباب بن الأرت عن الطبري); TABRISI(النبي,سلمان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(خباب بن الأرت); RAZI-FULL(سلمان وخباب); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,خباب); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); IBNKATHIR-FULL(خباب); SUYUTI-LUBAB(خباب); ALUSI-FULL(خباب); ELMALILI(Selmân ve Habbâb); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن ماجة); TABATABAI(خباب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Habbâb); IBNMAJAH(خباب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن كثير)-; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية)-]
- 6:52/q01/p3 The nobles requested that poor worshippers move behind them in the prayer rows, rather than leave the Prophet's gathering. — Ibn Abbas's report makes precedence in prayer the dispute; Tabari expressly contrasts this with expulsion from the gathering. Reports differ about whether nobles promised belief. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العوفي عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 6:52/q01/p4 Abu Talib was approached by nobles, or himself proposed dismissing poor followers, and Umar suggested trying the request before revelation. — Muqatil attributes the suggestion to Abu Talib; Ikrima's account has Banu Abd Manaf petition him. Several reports say Umar apologized after the verse; names of freedmen and allies vary. Baydawi adds Umar's temporary suggestion and the arrival of Ali and a document just before revelation. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(عكرمة); THALABI(عمر بن الخطاب); WAHIDI-ASBAB(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); TABATABAI(عكرمة); BAYDAWI-FULL(عمر)]
- 6:52/q01/p5 The report about al-Aqra and Uyayna is chronologically doubtful as the verse is Meccan, though a similar incident may have happened later. — Abu Hayyan and Ibn Kathir note those men became known after the Hijra; Ibn Ashur says the original verse could later answer an analogous request. Tabataba'i treats the similar stories as applications, citing the sura's single Meccan descent and unreliable occasion reports; Kuranyolu records commentators' like objection and retains the universal lesson. [8 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirlerin çoğu); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن كثير)+]
- 6:52/q01/p6 A proposal to expel the Companions of the Suffa occasioned the verse. — Tabataba'i rejects it as a specific occasion: the sura was revealed in Mecca before the Hijra. [1 notes: TABATABAI(عمر بن عبد الله بن المهاجر); TABATABAI-]

## 6:52/q02 What does ṭard mean here?  [تَطْرُدِ · meaning]
- 6:52/q02/p1 It means to drive away or exclude, with a humiliating or disparaging force. — Jishumi gives banishment and expulsion; Abu Hayyan adds humiliation and related Arabic usage; Islahi says the harsh verb fits Quraysh's proposed contempt. [4 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:52/q03 Who are the people whom the Prophet must not drive away?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse is limited to the original companions or its wording applies to other believers.
- 6:52/q03/p1 The poor Muslim believers in the Prophet's company. — Mujahid names Bilal and Ibn Umm Abd, with one account saying Ibn Umm Ma'bad or Ibn Umm Maktum; other reports name Salman, Khabbab, Suhayb, Ammar, Ibn Masud, Sa'd, al-Miqdad, freedmen and allies. Hasan singles out congregational worshippers; Abu Ja'far describes Quran learners. Ibn Ashur says their public monotheism showed faith before Medinan hypocrisy emerged. Ibn Ishaq, as reported by Ibn Hisham, additionally names Abu Fakihah Yasar. Itqan's list explicitly includes Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Salman al-Farisi among Suhayb, Bilal, Ammar, Khabbab and Ibn Masud. Khabbab's Ibn Majah report identifies the poor believers whom the chiefs wanted dismissed. [17 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(أبو جعفر,الربيع بن أنس,سعد بن أبي وقاص,مجاهد); THALABI(الحسن,سليمان وخباب بن الأرت); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); ITQAN; IBNMAJAH(خباب)]
- 6:52/q03/p2 More broadly, they include anyone who believes in God and seeks His grace, even without full Qur'anic belief. — Asad distinguishes the verse's enduring wording from its historical occasion, links 6:51 and 6:53, and counsels patient explanation rather than repulsion. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 6:52/q04 What does calling upon their Lord morning and evening denote?  [يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether الدعاء names prescribed prayer, particular prayers, remembrance, Quran, supplication, or comprehensive worship.
- 6:52/q04/p1 The prescribed prayers, sometimes all five. — Ibn Abbas, Ibrahim, Mujahid, Hasan, Qatada, and others say obligatory prayer; some specify congregation. Ibrahim and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib oppose making it post-prayer storytelling. A minority identifies dawn and afternoon specifically; Muqatil and Hasan describe two Meccan daily prayers before five were prescribed; Amr ibn Shuaib identifies the evening as isha. These time-specific details remain distinct in the following positions. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد بن المسيب,عامر,عبد الرحمن بن أبي عمرة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(سعيد بن المسيب); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(إبراهيم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عمر وابن عباس,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عمر,بعض القصاص); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب ومجاهد والحسن وقتادة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 6:52/q04/p2 Specifically the dawn and afternoon prayers. — Mujahid, Qatada and Ibn Abbas identify the two times. Alusi says these prayers are singled out for their honor. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 6:52/q04/p3 The two daily prayers then practiced in Mecca, before five prayers were prescribed. — Muqatil specifies two units at dawn and two at evening; Abu Hayyan attributes this to Hasan and Muqatil. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن)]
- 6:52/q04/p4 The evening reference is the isha prayer. — Amr ibn Shuaib gives this identification. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عمرو بن شعيب)]
- 6:52/q04/p5 Remembrance of God or supplication. — Ibrahim gives remembrance; some describe literal supplication. Abu Hayyan emphasizes asking, refuge and desire; al-Zajjaj includes monotheism and sincerity. Mujahid sought a post-dawn remembrance gathering, but Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib rejected using this verse for storytellers. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم); IBNABIHATIM(إبراهيم); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي); WAHIDI-BASIT(إبراهيم); BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(إبراهيم النخعي,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(إبراهيم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 6:52/q04/p6 Quran recitation, learning or honoring the Quran. — Abu Ja'far describes the Prophet teaching them and their reciting for God's face; Khabbab's report adds instruction in Paradise, Hell, death and resurrection. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو جعفر); THALABI(أبو جعفر); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر,إبراهيم النخعي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); WAHIDI-ASBAB(خباب بن الأرت)]
- 6:52/q04/p7 Worship in a comprehensive sense, including prayer, remembrance, praise, and required and voluntary acts. — Tabari declines to narrow الدعاء: 40:60 calls worship supplication; al-Dahhak cites 40:43. Ibn Kathir likewise joins worship and asking. Several commentators allow all practices together. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 6:52/q04/p8 Inwardly, it is constant presence and orientation to God in every act. — Qashani speaks of presence of heart, spiritual witnessing and love without seeking reward; Tustari includes every motion and rest; Sulami's authorities urge constant remembrance and pure intention. [5 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان,النهرجوري); QASHANI]

## 6:52/q05 What do morning and evening contribute to the description?  [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the temporal pair is literal, exemplary of constant practice, or chosen because worship then is especially significant.
- 6:52/q05/p1 They express continual devotion across the day. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi and others read the endpoints as continuity. Ibn Jawzi says morning stands for day and evening for night; Qushayri distinguishes limited outward acts from unbroken inward desire; Ibn Ashur derives all-day practice from generic time words and the temporal ba. Bursevi says constant remembrance keeps one with God. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 6:52/q05/p2 They mark especially telling devotion during busy hours, or honor the prayers at those times. — Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan say people are ordinarily occupied then, so their worship is stronger evidence of devotion; Biqai says constancy proves sincerity rather than ostentation. Alusi says dawn and afternoon prayers are singled out for honor. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 6:52/q05/p3 They name the exact morning and evening acts or prayer times. — Ibn Ashur notes that prayer can stand for its time or place; Qiraat-ER contrasts an instance morning ghudwa with general ghadah, affecting a specific versus habitual sense. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]

## 6:52/q06 How is the morning expression read?  [بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the attested time noun can take the definite article, and the authority of oral transmission versus spelling.
- 6:52/q06/p1 The majority read بالغداة, al-ghadāh, with alif and an open ghayn. — Abu Ali favors this common reading because ghadāh can receive the article; ghudwa is usually a proper time name. Sibawayh, Yunus and Farra are cited about definiteness. Written waw does not establish sound: الصلاة and الزكاة also have written waw. Qiraat-ER distinguishes general morning from an instance. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد,ابن عامر,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيد,الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي)+; SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي الفارسي)+; TABRISI(أبو علي)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عامر)+]
- 6:52/q06/p2 Ibn Amir and other transmitted readers read بالغدوة, al-ghudwa, with waw and a rounded ghayn. — The reading occurs also at 18:28. Al-Khalil and Sibawayh attest indefinite ghudwa, which can take the article, or a temporal adverb like duhwa; Abu Ali and al-Wahidi explain generic use of a proper name, citing Ibn Mayada. Abu Ubayd attacked it as reliance on script, but Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi defend oral transmission and attested dialect. Manuscripts write waw throughout. [26 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,ابن عامر); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عامر,الخليل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو زيد,ابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر النحاس,أبو علي الفارسي,سيبويه والخليل,مكي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيد)-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيد)-]
- 6:52/q06/p3 Ibn Abi Abla reads plural بالغدوات والعشيات; another reported form drops the final tāʾ. — Jishumi and Samin explain waw becomes alif after an open dāl; Abu Hayyan also reports Abu Abd al-Rahman's form without final hāʾ, and Samin records بالغدو with doubled waw. [3 notes: JISHUMI(ابن أبي عبلة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:52/q07 What does al-ʿashiyy mean and what form is it?  [ٱلْعَشِىِّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the form names one evening or is collective/plural, and how wide a time it covers.
- 6:52/q07/p1 It is the late part of the day, usually a singular noun like ʿashiyya. — Abu Hayyan cites 38:31 for late afternoon; Samin favors synonymy with ʿashiyya because that verse means one evening; Alusi places it at day's end and derives it from a waw root. Ibn Ashur gives a wider noon-to-dawn span. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:52/q07/p2 It may be a collective or plural of ʿashiyya. — Abu Hayyan raises the collective analysis; Samin compares qamh/qamha; Alusi records but finds plural unlikely. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:52/q08 What is meant by seeking God's face?  [يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether face stands for God Himself, His pleasure/reward, His commanded path, or the inward goal of direct nearness.
- 6:52/q08/p1 They sincerely seek God Himself and His approval, without worldly aims. — Tustari, Zajjaj, Kashshaf and many later sources equate face with the divine self/essence as an honorific Arabic usage and infer sincerity. Ibn Abbas and others say reward, pardon, pleasure or favor through righteous deeds; Razi adds a lover's desire for the beloved's face, while StudyQuran records literal hope to see it. Ibn Ashur invokes 76:9 and 2:115; Bursawi cites a Persian couplet comparing worship without sincerity to grain without a kernel. Ibn Qutayba calls 'His face' an added expression for seeking God through supplication. Damghani identifies seeking God and His pleasure and says other cited uses of face likewise denote God. Ibn al-Jawzi reports the exegetes' sense as God's own self, grouped with 18:28, 28:88, 30:38 and 76:9. [38 notes: TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,المشهور,بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IK, R,R); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 6:52/q08/p2 They seek obedience to God, His commanded direction and the means of nearness. — Mawardi and the people of meanings use face for the aim or direction commanded; Wāhidi links 2:115, Alusi reports Zajjaj. Tabataba'i includes God's names, religion, good works and nearness as aspects through which creatures encounter God. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); TABATABAI]
- 6:52/q08/p3 In an inward reading they seek only the divine Essence beyond changing attributes, reward, and intermediaries. — Qashani describes annihilation of means and self; Qushayri says nothing worldly or of the afterlife occupies them; Tustari distinguishes the striving seeker from one enabled and sought by God. [5 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI; TUSTARI]

## 6:52/q09 Does 'His face' imply a bodily part?  [وَجْهَهُۥ · theology]
turns on: Whether face is read figuratively, as a divine attribute, or as a bodily part.
- 6:52/q09/p1 No; face is an Arabic metonym or metaphor for essence, aspect, direction, favor, or what brings one near God. — Jishumi notes bodily and figurative senses in Arabic. Razi answers the anthropomorphists with God's oneness in 112:1; Abu Hayyan rejects their inference. Tabataba'i denies body and material direction, takes 55:27's grammatical modifier with face, and uses 28:88 and other verses for enduring nearness of divine attributes and righteous realities. Qushayri and Alusi discuss the difficulty of willing God's eternal essence when will ordinarily attaches to originated or possible things. Ibn Qutayba treats 'His face' as an expression for seeking God in supplication; Damghani and the exegetes reported by Ibn al-Jawzi understand it as God Himself or His pleasure. [14 notes: JISHUMI; QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(المجسمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(المشهور); TABATABAI; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 6:52/q09/p2 It may be understood as God's possessed Face or literal sight of it. — Qurtubi records that God is described as possessing the Face, citing 55:27 and 13:22; StudyQuran reports Razi's lover analogy as literal hope to see it. Razi reports anthropomorphists' bodily argument only to refute it. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); RAZI-FULL(المجسمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 6:52/q10 How does 'seeking His face' attach grammatically?  [يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the circumstantial clause qualifies the worshippers or the object of their calling.
- 6:52/q10/p1 It is a circumstantial clause qualifying the callers' supplication and sincerity. — Qushayri, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur identify the ḥāl; Bursevi calls sincerity central. Samin prefers attachment to the subject of يدعون, with an implicit 'through their supplication at those times.' [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:52/q10/p2 It could be a circumstance of the object of يدعون. — Samin records this possible grammatical attachment but prefers the subject. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:52/q11 Whose account is meant by the reciprocal pronouns?  [حِسَابِهِم حِسَابِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the nearest antecedent governs both clauses or the clauses parenthetically address concern for the chiefs' conversion.
- 6:52/q11/p1 The poor worshippers' account and the Prophet's account. — The nearest explicit group and the later object 'expel them' favor this for Wahidi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Kuranyolu. Al-Mahdawi says all pronouns refer to believers; Alusi cites Ata and most commentators. ## 6:52/q12 What is the 'account' for which neither side is responsible?  [مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether حساب is moral reckoning, provision/sufficiency, or the disbelievers' responsibility for faith.
- 6:52/q12/p1 The reckoning of deeds, faith and hidden intentions belongs to each person and ultimately God. — Hasan and the majority take deeds; Noah's reply at 26:112–113 and the rule that no bearer bears another's burden support this. Zamakhshari, Razi, Biqai and Bursawi say the Prophet judges outward piety, even against a claim that the poor seek food and clothes. Abu Hayyan rejects positing actual inward blame after God's testimony; Ibn Ashur likewise finds no sound report of hypocrisy and rejects treating both clauses as merely divine accounting. Asad extends personal accountability to differing belief and conduct. Elmalılı says the Prophet should not expel them on a pretext of having to account for them. [39 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(المهدوي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müşrikler,one view); STUDYQURAN(R,Some commentators); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

Ibn Bahr and Biqai cite 78:36 for ample gift; Biqai adds 2:212, Abu Ubayd and Ibn Faris for aḥsabtuhu 'gave enough,' and a report on hospitality. Kuranyolu phrases the practical result as not sending worshippers away to earn a living. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); SAMIN-DURR(الطبري); BIQAI-FULL(أبو عبيد الهروي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(another interpretation)]
- 6:52/q12/p3 It is the chiefs' own response to faith, for which the Prophet is not liable. — Ibn Abbas's alternative and Ibn Ashur/Islahi say eagerness for conversion cannot cost existing believers their rights. Ibn Ashur also allows 'their account' to mean the chiefs' reckoning of the believers' poverty. ## 6:52/q13 Why are both reciprocal account clauses stated?  [مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether repetition supplies reciprocal completeness alone or also exposes the requesters' false rationale.
- 6:52/q13/p1 Together they state complete reciprocity: neither party bears the other's account. — Zamakhshari, Razi and others say both halves complete one point, invoking the no-burden rule. Bursawi reports Abu al-Suud that the uncontroversial reverse reinforces the first; Zajjaj says the second could be omitted structurally but confirms parallel phrasing. Ibn Ashur says five emphases, including two min, fronting, restriction and reciprocal completion, repel pressure from Quraysh; he also gives the first clause priority. Abu Hayyan and Samin record a wording objection to Zamakhshari's singular ṣāḥibihi and Samin's repair by supplying 'each one.' Burhan names the reciprocal account clauses an inversion, comparing wives as each other's garment, mutual unlawfulness and night entering day; Itqan calls the form aks, a reversal of the first statement's parts in the second, and compares 57:6, 30:19, 2:187 and 60:10. Burhan also describes interwoven premise–consequence pairs. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ); BURSEVI(أبو السعود); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 6:52/q13/p2 The second clause chiefly exposes the chiefs' pretense that poor believers obstructed their access to the Prophet. — Ibn Ashur says no believer should lose rights to satisfy another; if chiefs truly sought faith they could attend without judging others, as 6:55 clarifies. He rejects reducing the two clauses to one statement that God judges all, because that makes the second redundant in context. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 6:52/q14 How do the particles and fronted phrases in the account clauses work?  [مَا عَلَيْكَ مِنْ حِسَابِهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ وَمَا مِنْ حِسَابِكَ عَلَيْهِم مِّن شَىْءٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The function of min, the grammatical status of fronted phrases, and whether the clauses are an interruption.
- 6:52/q14/p1 The first min is partitive and the second min emphatically broadens the negation. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samin parse من حسابهم as partitive or circumstantial and من شيء as extra under negation. Samin lays out active or inactive ما, عليك as predicate or fronted predicate, and the possible raising of شيء. Abu Hayyan and Samin find treating من حسابك as a fronted circumstance weak because it precedes its semantic governor and owner; an explanatory analysis is possible. Ibn Ashur calls min extra for comprehensive denial. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:52/q14/p2 The fronting of عليك and عليهم may honor the Prophet, emphasize the lack of obligation, or mark restriction. — Abu Hayyan and Samin say putting the Prophet first twice is honor and reciprocal framing, رد الأعجاز على الصدور, supported by proverb and poem. Ibn Ashur says negated indefinite subjects permit fronting; Kashshaf favors restriction, implying only God accounts for believers, comparing 37:47, while Ibn Ashur also permits simple emphasis because restriction fits imperfectly. He explains على as burden and discusses whether the genitive means the Prophet's reckoning or chiefs' assessment. As general word-order background rather than a direct parsing of 6:52, Jurjani argues that a meaningful placement consistently carries its benefit and illustrates with questions: verb-first asks whether an act occurred, while noun-first asks who did it. [11 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 6:52/q14/p3 The two clauses may interrupt the prohibition as parenthetical explanation. — Alusi says the clause supports the ban; Ibn Ashur says it can give the reason or invalidate the rationale for expulsion. Samin rejects making parenthesis depend on reference to polytheists; Abu Hayyan identifies the interruption before the ban's result. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:52/q15 What governs the two subjunctive verbs after the account clauses?  [فَتَطْرُدَهُمْ فَتَكُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Which preceding negative or prohibition supplies the result, and whether the second verb answers that ban or coordinates with the first.
- 6:52/q15/p1 فتطردهم answers the preceding negation and فتكون answers the prohibition. — Akhfash, Tabari, Zajjaj, BaghaWi and others make the first consequence follow 'no account' and the second the ban. Abu Hayyan says the first denies an expulsion arising from that account; Samin rejects a reading where the visit occurs without conversation. Ibn Ashur allows the repeated expulsion clause to bridge the long interruption. Alusi rejects making the second denial govern if it would imply expulsion proper when accountability exists. Burhan describes two interwoven premise–consequence pairs: the ban on expulsion leads to avoiding injustice, and the reciprocal account clauses lead to the question of expelling them. [22 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو جعفر); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيان,مكي والواحدي وأبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 6:52/q15/p2 Both verbs follow the prohibition, warning that expulsion would make the Prophet unjust. — Akhfash reports this alternative; Abu Muslim parses both as consequences. Samin and Ibn Ashur note that repeated expulsion reconnects the warning after intervening clauses; Alusi objects to a literal 'do not expel and then expel' reading. [6 notes: AKHFASH; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الدر المصون); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:52/q15/p3 فتكون is coordinated with فتطردهم, so both expulsion and wrongdoing are negated as linked consequences. — Abu Hayyan and Samin prefer coordination; Zamakhshari and Razi allow the injustice to result causally from expulsion. Alusi answers that when expulsion is denied, its injustice ceases, though an objection worries this would imply expulsion could be just under a different accounting relation. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام والزمخشري,بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 6:52/q16 What wrong would expelling the worshippers constitute?  [فَتَكُونَ مِنَ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · interpretation]

- 6:52/q16/p1 It would wrong the believers who deserved welcome and honor. — Ibn Zayd calls their exclusion undeserved; Wahidi calls this sound. Qurtubi and Kuranyolu say humbling people God values for faith is injustice; Baydawi and Alusi make it a consequence of dismissal; Biqai says putting things out of place would not win pagan belief. turns on: Whether reported consideration became actual expulsion or culpable disobedience.
- 6:52/q17/p1 He did not expel them; he considered a separate audience to attract the chiefs, and the warning is conditional. — Qurtubi, Alusi, Razi, Ibn al-Anbari and StudyQuran stress his intent to win converts; Razi says any mistaken judgment was forgoing the better course, not violating a duty. ## 6:52/q18 How does this verse connect to its surrounding passage and other Quranic episodes?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether adjacent passages explain the command through context, analogous prophetic history, or later applications.
- 6:52/q18/p1 It follows the warning in 6:51 by commanding care for the devout, and continues through the trial and mercy statements in 6:53–54. — Kashshaf, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur mark the turn from warning the heedless to honoring worshippers. ## 6:52/q19 What ethical rule does the prohibition teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the enduring addressees are faithful Muslims in the Prophet's company or all sincere seekers of God.
- 6:52/q19/p1 Sincere faith and devotion give the poor a claim to company and honor regardless of rank, dress or wealth. — Zajjaj says religion takes priority over status and leaders' pretext is exposed. Qushayri says God champions vulnerable believers and tells the Prophet to see inward longing beyond ragged clothes. Qurtubi and Kuranyolu forbid elevating people for finery or lineage. Islahi says prophetic concern for wayward people has limits when loyal companions would be neglected; Jesus' lost-sheep parable illustrates concern for returnees but cannot override this duty. Biqai says the mission seeks the afterlife, with no worldly gain in replacing poor with wealthy. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(رسول الله); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Jesus (sws)); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:52/q19/p2 The enduring command also applies to people with incomplete beliefs: explain patiently rather than repel them. — Asad reads the wording beyond its occasion, applying it to anyone who believes in God and seeks grace; Kuranyolu says even a disputed occasion leaves a universal ethical lesson. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirlerin çoğu)]

## 6:52/q20 What inward instruction is drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different inward emphases on constant remembrance, self-effacement, and the disciplines of devotion.
- 6:52/q20/p1 Remain with sincere seekers, recognize inward rank, and cultivate uninterrupted remembrance. — Sulami transmits counsel to treat seekers gently and overlook slips, to keep the secret pure, and not distance those adorned with servanthood. Abu Bakr al-Farisi says sincere desire shows in continual remembrance and companionship with like seekers. Qushayri says God publicly attests to sincerity, while the seeker is driven by need and longing; a poem depicts crossing the wild without fear. Tustari describes intentions in every motion and stillness and distinctions between one who seeks and one whom God seeks, citing 42:38, 16:53 and angelic stations at 37:164. [12 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو بكر الفارسي,أبو عثمان,النهرجوري,بعضهم); QUSHAYRI]
- 6:52/q20/p2 Inward nearness may be beyond ordinary reward, fear, or intermediary accounts. — Qashani calls them complete people of unity whose hearts witness God, free of concern for reward, punishment, worldly status or even their own selves; expulsion interrupts this presence, while veiled observers see only poverty (70:23). Qushayri says reliance is on divine mercy rather than one's deeds, and their Master cares for those wholly devoted to Him. [7 notes: QASHANI; QUSHAYRI]
- 6:52/q20/p3 Purity of sustenance, command keeping, sincere treatment of creatures and gratitude describe the servant's path. — Tustari says the ascetic has pure food, the devout works hardest at commands and prohibitions, and the beloved is most sincere to creatures; he calls a squandered life wasted. Qushayri contrasts gratitude and patience and quotes poems grateful even for a beloved's blame. Bursevi uses Bahlul's cemetery and hunger sayings, a king's parable of hidden beloveds and a Persian couplet on servants' lowliness. [11 notes: TUSTARI; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(السري السقطي,النبي,بهلول)]

## 6:52/q25 What dialectal substitutions for final weak sounds are recorded alongside al-ʿashiyy?  [ٱلْعَشِىِّ · grammar]

- 6:52/q26/p1 Medial wāw may be lengthened, hamzaed, changed to yāʾ, or preserved according to pattern and proximity to the word's end. — Sibawayh gives ghuwuran and al-quwwul, sawm/saym and qawl/qil, contrasts retained wāw in zuwwar and siwwam, describes analogical alternations in mashub/mashib and hawr/hayr, and gives retained wāw in tawil/tuwal, augmented patterns and fiʿlaʾ. - 6:52/q26/p2 Root-initial yāʾ is usually retained but changes in specified environments and analogical forms. — Sibawayh contrasts root yāʾ in yaʾisa and yabisa with wāw, cites retained yāʾ in yayiʾasu and yaybasu, its change to wāw after ḍamma, isolated analogies in vocatives, its change to tāʾ in iftiʿal, and preservation in afʿal; he limits nonproductive cases and reports Abu ʿAmr's hamza-to-yāʾ reading. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو عمرو,بعضهم)]

## 6:52/q27 How can Arabic noun forms denote an act or a concrete thing in the accompanying lexical notes?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Sibawayh says the same outward pattern has several lexical functions that must be learned from usage.
- 6:52/q27/p1 A form's lexical meaning can be an action, an amount, its result, an agent or patient, or a collective member; the surface pattern alone does not decide. — Sibawayh discusses faʿul verbal nouns, waqud as burning or fuel, shibʿ and milʾ as amounts or acts, nouns with the same sense as their sources, unpredictable khars, kayla, qut and halab, mirya as milk quantity, laʿna and khalq as affected people or things, karaʿ and dhu tadruʾ as concrete referents, passive-result and agent senses, changed milk and pleasing usage, and collective members marked with feminine taʾ. These examples are ancillary and do not explicitly gloss a word in 6:52. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH]
