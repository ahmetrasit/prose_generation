# 12:36: 52 questions

## 12:36/q01 How does the verse indicate Joseph's imprisonment?  [وَدَخَلَ ٱلسِّجْنَ · rhetoric]
- 12:36/q01/p1 It leaves his imprisonment unstated and implies it through the youths' entry with him. — Al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Razi, Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi identify the omitted prior event, 'they imprisoned him'; Ibn al-Jawzi relays al-Zajjaj. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:36/q01/p2 The wording itself shows Joseph was already imprisoned. — Al-Zajjaj says entry into prison with him establishes his imprisonment; al-Wahidi reports the inference. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)]

## 12:36/q02 What does 'with him' say about when the youths entered?  [مَعَهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether maʿa requires concurrence when the entry began.
- 12:36/q02/p1 They entered prison together with Joseph at the same time. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi take maʿa as newly begun companionship; Abu Hayyan says it denotes a new shared event; al-Biqaʿi cites him. Al-Alusi prefers concurrence as the original sense, while comparing 27:44. Al-Suyūṭī gives shared time or place as a basic sense of maʿa, while analyzing it as a noun. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 12:36/q02/p2 The wording allows different times; it only establishes shared confinement. — Ibn ʿAtiyya allows simultaneous or separate admission. Al-Qurtubi allows before, after or together and says the verse only requires the same room. Al-Zarkashī says maʿa marks shared participation in an action or ruling without necessarily requiring simultaneity; al-Suyūṭī cites association apart from shared time or place. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 12:36/q03 Why place 'with him' before 'prison,' and what might the wording imply?  [مَعَهُ ٱلسِّجْنَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The force of maʿa and the emphasis conveyed by word order.
- 12:36/q03/p1 The order emphasizes companionship, with Joseph as the focus. — Al-Alusi says the object precedes the subject for emphasis and companionship precedes prison because it matters more; he cites 18:62. He distinguishes ongoing Islam from punctual entry and invokes 27:44. Al-Suyūṭī reports al-Rāghib's point that the complement of maʿa receives the intended focus. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN(الراغب)]
- 12:36/q03/p2 Their entry with him may have made imprisonment easier through his presence. — Al-Alusi reports this possibility. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:36/q03/p3 The expression associates Joseph with their prison state while leaving his choice of it unstated. — Al-Alusi reports this explanation and observes that maʿa normally implies concurrence from the action's beginning. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:36/q03/p4 'With' can express joining another in obedience rather than exact simultaneous action. — Al-Kashf's comparison is the Queen's 'I submitted with Solomon' (27:44): she enters submission to God and the Messenger following prophetic guidance, despite earlier religious self-understanding. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+]

## 12:36/q04 How is 'prison' pronounced and understood here?  [ٱلسِّجْنَ · readings]
turns on: The vocalization and whether the noun denotes place or act.
- 12:36/q04/p1 With kasra, it means the place of confinement. — Ibn ʿAshur says all readers use kasra because one enters a place, not the act of imprisoning. Ibn al-Jazarī says all readers agree on kasra at 12:36 and related prison mentions; the fatḥa of Yaʿqūb belongs to 12:33 and marks the verbal noun. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+; NASHR+]
- 12:36/q04/p2 With fatḥa, al-sajn denotes the prison location. — Al-Alusi gives this vocalization in explaining the clause after implied 'they imprisoned him.' [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; NASHR-]

## 12:36/q05 Why are the prisoners called 'two youths'?  [فَتَيَانِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the designation concerns age, service status or their mutual conduct.
- 12:36/q05/p1 Fatā can designate an enslaved man or servant regardless of age. — Al-Zajjaj allows young or old slaves; al-Mawardi says slaves are so called; al-Qushayri also permits free servants; Ibn ʿAtiyya allows literal youth, slave, or free servant. Ibn al-Anbari ties it to being owned. Al-Tabataba'i takes them as young male slaves. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري,الماوردي); TABATABAI]
- 12:36/q05/p2 They are called youths because neither tried to outdo the other's claim and each deferred to his companion. — Al-Tustari gives this ethical explanation. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 12:36/q05/p3 They are literally young men accompanying Joseph into prison. — Al-Dāmaghānī glosses the two fityān as the young men who entered alongside Joseph. [2 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 12:36/q06 What is the form of 'two youths'?  [فَتَيَانِ · grammar]
- 12:36/q06/p1 It is the dual of fatā, a weak-y noun. — Al-Nahhas identifies the dual; al-Qurtubi notes the irregular form al-futu. Sībawayh explains that the dual of fatā reveals its original yāʾ, hence fatayān; the wāw of futuwwa follows the preceding ḍamma rather than proving a wāw root. Related forms and imāla can expose the underlying consonant. [12 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SIBAWAYH(يونس وغيره)]

## 12:36/q07 Who were the two prisoners and whom did they serve?  [فَتَيَانِ · referent]
turns on: Whether external reports are used to fill the Quran's unnamed roles.
- 12:36/q07/p1 They were the Egyptian king's baker or food steward and cupbearer or wine steward. — Qatada, Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi and many commentators assign food and drink duties; the wine vision belongs to the cupbearer and bread vision to the baker. Ibn ʿAshur says the Quran leaves offences unspecified; Asad cites the Bible for the offices; Islahi stresses that the Quran does not identify them. Al-Razi says Joseph could infer royal service from their later use of rabb for their master in 12:41-42. Al-Dāmaghānī explicitly identifies the wine dreamer as the king's cupbearer. [34 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Torah); STUDYQURAN(Bg,Ṭ and JJ); DAMGHANI]
- 12:36/q07/p2 The Quran leaves their identities unnamed. — Islahi states this limitation explicitly; Asad says the Bible identifies the offices without conflict with the Quran. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]

- 12:36/q08/p4 They were Sharham and Sarham. — Al-Qurtubi cites al-Naqqash. Al-Suyūṭī also transmits the Sharham and Sarham naming. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش); ITQAN(قيل)]

## 12:36/q09 Why were the royal servants imprisoned?  [وَدَخَلَ مَعَهُ ٱلسِّجْنَ فَتَيَانِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the report describes suspicion alone, an actual poisoned meal, or reciprocal accusation.
- 12:36/q09/p1 The baker was suspected of trying to poison the king, and the cupbearer of complicity. — Al-Suddi reports suspicion, not proven execution; several later accounts repeat it. Asad says the Quran does not specify their offences; Ibn ʿAshur notes royal anger and an unnamed poisoning report. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R and Ṭ)]
- 12:36/q09/p2 Both were implicated in a planned poisoning, but the cupbearer withdrew while the baker accepted and poisoned food. — Al-Thaʿlabi and Baghawi describe bribery, warning, animal test and the poisoned meal; al-Qurtubi and Bursevi relay the same basic split. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI]
- 12:36/q09/p3 Conspirators pushed both to poison food and drink and the two accused one another. — Kuranyolu gives this report. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 12:36/q10 What happened to Joseph on entering prison?  [وَدَخَلَ مَعَهُ ٱلسِّجْنَ · reports]
turns on: Different reports emphasize public punishment or Joseph's reception inside prison.
- 12:36/q10/p1 He was paraded in chains on a donkey and chose this hardship over Hell. — Al-Qurtubi attributes the report to Wahb and others; it frames the punishment as a consequence of refusing his mistress. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(وهب وغيره)]
- 12:36/q10/p2 He encouraged hopeless prisoners, won their affection, and was offered favorable quarters. — Qatada and later reports describe counsel about patience, reward, purification and hope; the keeper could improve confinement but not free him. Al-Durr also reports a severe reply from Joseph to the keeper about the Fire's mountains. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة,يوسف); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI]

- 12:36/q12/p1 He named his lineage through Jacob, Isaac, and Abraham. — Qatada's account, al-Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan and al-Durr preserve the lineage; Abu Hayyan adds epithets 'God's chosen,' 'sacrificed one' and 'friend.' ## 12:36/q13 How did the two men come to ask Joseph for interpretation?  [قَالَ أَحَدُهُمَآ نَبِّئْنَا · reports]
turns on: Whether their approach followed a deliberate test or genuine distress and observed trust.
- 12:36/q13/p1 Joseph had announced or demonstrated that he could interpret dreams, and they tested him. — Al-Suddi, Ibn Masud and others say the men feigned dreams; al-Tabari says they first asked about his knowledge. Abu Hayyan says he told prisoners his skill, while al-Razi allows prior demonstration. Study Quran reports they had seen him interpret others. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,عبد الله); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود,المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(يوسف); STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 12:36/q13/p2 They were troubled or curious about unusual dreams and trusted his knowledge and piety. — Ibn Abbas reports troubled waking; al-Razi says they may have mentioned their sadness; Islahi says they had observed his knowledge and piety. Ibn ʿAshur says prisoners exchanged dreams for conversation and hoped for signs of release; Egyptian interpretation was familiar. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 12:36/q14 Did both prisoners actually have the dreams they recounted?  [إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰنِىٓ وَقَالَ ٱلْءَاخَرُ إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰنِىٓ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted accounts conflict; requests for interpretation and arānī are offered as evidence for genuine dreams.
- 12:36/q14/p1 Both truly dreamed. — Ibn Ishaq and Mujahid report actual dreams, and Ibn Abbas describes troubled waking; al-Zajjaj and al-Wahidi argue that requesting interpretation implies dreams because waking sight needs none. Al-Razi adds that arānī would otherwise be unnecessary. Ibn Kathir prefers this as the majority view. - 12:36/q14/p2 Neither dreamed; both invented accounts to test Joseph. — Ibn Masud, al-Suddi and al-Shaʿbi report fabrication; Mujahid also relates their later claim that they were joking, after which Joseph said the matter was settled. Al-Durr gives the subsequent interpretations and request to remember Joseph. Baghawi notes contrary reports. [22 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(السدي,عبد الله); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الشعبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود والسدي); RAZI-FULL(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود,ابن مسعود والسدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود والشعبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله); BIQAI-FULL(الشعبي); DURR-FULL(يوسف)]
- 12:36/q14/p3 Only the baker, later crucified, lied; the cupbearer dreamed truthfully. — Abu Mijlaz and an Imam report identify the crucified man as the liar; Bursevi repeats this resolution. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مجلز); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو مجلز); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو مجلز); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو مجلز); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام)]

## 12:36/q15 How does the first man's dream scene unfold?  [إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰنِىٓ أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا · reports]
turns on: Reports vary in whether the vine was planted and in details of its branches and clusters.
- 12:36/q15/p1 He pressed grapes from three clusters into the king's cup and served him. — Muqatil, Mujahid and later transmitters give three clusters, sometimes on a three-branched vine or in a garden; Ibn Abbas also reports the cup for the king. Bursevi describes the service. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(عكرمة); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الشعبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:36/q15/p2 He planted a vine, pressed its grapes, and served the king. — Ikrima adds that Joseph foretold release after three days and resumed wine service. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 12:36/q15/p3 The vision matches the cupbearer's ordinary occupation: pressing grapes for wine. — Al-Razi notes both visions mirror the men's professions; Study Quran connects this vision with the wine carrier and 12:41. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bg); DURR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 12:36/q16 How does 'I see myself' mark a dream rather than waking action?  [أَرَىٰنِىٓ · rhetoric]
- 12:36/q16/p1 It recounts a past dream scene; the sleep setting is understood. — Al-Zajjaj says sleep need not be named; Ibn al-Anbari says simple 'I press' would otherwise suffice. Al-Razi cites arānī plus the request for interpretation. Al-Tabataba'i notes the break before 'one of them said' separates the dream scene from prison entry. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن الأنباري); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 12:36/q17 How does the verb 'I see myself' take its objects?  [أَرَىٰنِىٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether aṣiru fills a second object slot or states the dreamer's circumstance.
- 12:36/q17/p1 As a cognition verb, it allows subject and object pronouns to refer to the same person. — Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi contrast ordinary verbs, which need a separate reflexive expression; al-Samin adds that causative hamza yields a third object, citing 8:43. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:36/q17/p2 'I press' is the second object of the dream cognition verb. — Al-Samin reports this parsing. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 12:36/q17/p3 'I press' is a circumstantial clause. — Al-Samin reports this alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

turns on: Length of the final yāʾ in recitation.
- 12:36/q19/p1 The standard form ends in a short yāʾ. — Qiraat-ER records this at both occurrences. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 12:36/q19/p2 A canonical recitational variant extends the yāʾ. — Qiraat-ER records this at both occurrences. Ibn al-Jazarī counts the two innī arānī expressions among the sūra's yāʾs before open hamza and records their opening among the Yūsuf forms. [4 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(أبو عمرو)]

## 12:36/q20 What does 'press' mean here?  [أَعْصِرُ · meaning]
- 12:36/q20/p1 It means squeezing something moist to extract its liquid. — Al-Layth distinguishes ʿaṣartu, doing it oneself, from iʿtaṣartu, having another do it; ʿuṣāra is extracted liquid. Al-Jishumi compares rain-bearing muʿṣirāt; Ibn ʿAshur explains ʿaṣīr as the product. Al-Biqaʿi likewise defines extraction. Ibn ʿAzīz glosses aʿṣiru as extract. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces ʿaṣr forms to pressure and extraction, comparing the vine, harvest year, rain clouds, whirlwind, and al-ʿAṣr. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الليث); JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN(ابن عزيز); BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 12:36/q21 What does 'wine' mean as the thing pressed, and how is the wording explained?  [أَعْصِرُ خَمْرًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether khamr directly names grapes, anticipates wine, abbreviates a construction, or names the intended product.
- 12:36/q21/p1 It means grapes in a dialect, especially Oman or Azd Oman. — Al-Asmaʿi's Bedouin called carried grapes khamr; al-Dahhak says the Quran uses several peoples' speech. Some name Ghassan and Azd Oman, while Ibn al-Qasim says Quraysh knew it. This explains the object directly. Al-Zarkashī relays Ibn Durayd through al-Fārisī on the Azd Oman lexical usage and a view denying metaphor on that basis; al-Suyūṭī reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk's Omani gloss. [31 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(الأصمعي); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,محمد بن الحنفية); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); THALABI(الأصمعي); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي,الضحاك,الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأصمعي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك والزجاج); RAZI-FULL(أبو صالح); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المعتمر); SAMIN-DURR(المعتمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); BURHAN(قيل); ITQAN(الضحاك)]
- 12:36/q21/p2 Grapes are called wine by anticipating what they will become. — Al-Zajjaj compares naming a thing by its eventual product; al-Wahidi cites cooking bricks from clay and molasses or juice. Ibn al-Anbari and many commentators take this as the common explanation. Al-Sijistani's separate evidence notes masculine khamr among eloquent speakers. Ibn Jinnī treats wine as the grapes' effect, named in place of its cause; al-Zarkashī describes the grapes by their future wine, and al-Suyūṭī compares other things named by their future state. [25 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أكثر المفسرين وابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; BURHAN(ابن جني); ITQAN]
- 12:36/q21/p3 An omitted noun is understood: grapes intended for wine. — Al-Zajjaj and later authors supply the missing grapes; al-Qurtubi calls it an omitted genitive relation and adds khamra/khamr/khumur inflection. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:36/q21/p4 Wine itself may be called pressed because grapes are pressed for its sake. — Ibn Qutayba compares saying 'I pressed oil' for olives; Ibn ʿAtiyya and Abu Hayyan permit the goal of pressing as its object. ## 12:36/q39 What does the verse establish about dreams and their interpretation?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether any dream can disclose reality, and which causes distinguish truthful from ordinary dreams.
- 12:36/q39/p1 Some dreams truly disclose future or hidden events and can be interpreted. — Al-Zajjaj says denying all true dreams contradicts scripture and a prophetic report of one-fortieth of prophecy. Al-Razi cites a report of one forty-sixth and explains the soul's sleep-time access to higher realities. Al-Tabataba'i argues correspondence to future events is not chance and distinguishes bodily and emotional dreams from sound dreams. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو هريرة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI]
- 12:36/q39/p2 Dreams include self-talk, satanic dreams and true dreams; many arise from bodily, emotional or chance causes. — Al-Razi accepts the prophetic threefold report as rationally sound. Al-Tabataba'i says imagination recombines sensory images, affected by body, temperament and emotions; all dreams have causes, though many do not foretell external events. He grants naturalists' account for some dreams. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي); TABATABAI]
- 12:36/q39/p3 All dreams are unreal. — Al-Tabataba'i rejects this inference from ordinary bodily and emotional causes; he says some dreams relate to external truth. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 12:36/q40 How can a true dream disclose reality, and when does it need interpretation?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the perception is direct or mediated by symbolic transformations.
- 12:36/q40/p1 The sleeping soul can reach nonphysical causes, seeing them directly or as symbolic images. — Al-Razi says bodily distraction lessens in sleep. Al-Tabataba'i distinguishes physical, imaginal and intellectual worlds; a sound soul may perceive causes clearly, while symbols or opposites such as crown/honor and darkness/ignorance need return to their meanings. Tangled symbolic chains resist interpretation. Ibn Sirin's Ramadan caller example illustrates decoding. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(ابن سيرين)]
- 12:36/q40/p2 Some dreams are clear without symbolic interpretation. — Al-Tabataba'i says clear dreams may reveal events directly and identifies these apart from interpretable symbols. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

- 12:36/q50/p1 The ancillary excerpts discuss divine signs, prophethood, and the Qur'an's lasting miraculous proof. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Bāqillānī, and al-Jurjānī give general arguments about revelation and inimitability, without directly treating 12:36. ## 12:36/q51 What wider recitation material accompanies the notes for this verse?  [* · readings]
- 12:36/q51/p1 The ancillary excerpts discuss the seven modes, preservation, and hamza practice outside this verse's specific readings. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr transmit seven-mode reports. Ibn al-Jazarī discusses broader hamza rules, other words, chains of transmission, and Qur'an preservation; these excerpts are ancillary to 12:36.
