# 103:1/q01 What does al-ʿaṣr in the oath designate? [ٱلْعَصْرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the unqualified word is inclusive or specifies a time of day, a prayer, a paired period, or a marked era; arguments use Arabic usage, poetry, report, context and analogous oaths.
- 103:1/q01/p1 Time or an age broadly, encompassing its changing periods and events.
  reasons: Al-Tabari declines restriction without textual proof; al-Nahhas and Elmalılı likewise allow several Arabic senses. Ibn Abbas and others identify an age; Imru al-Qays and Ibn al-Sikkit's poetry attest the usage. Farāhī and Asad distinguish fleeting ʿaṣr from dahr; Cuypers calls it a general span without further grounds. Ibn Kathir calls this the well-known view. Bint al-Shati follows al-Tabari, Ibn Qayyim and most exegetes; Kur'an Yolu finds this best suited to the sūra. Al-Razi questions treating a reported expanded wording as Qur'an because that would invalidate prayer. Elmalılı cites a poet likening unseen time's motion toward death to a ship. Al-Damghani glosses al-ʿaṣr as time or its passage; al-Suyuti includes time as a whole among the oath's possible referents.

- 103:1/q01/p2 Late afternoon, the day's declining period from after noon toward sunset.
  reasons: Al-Hasan and Qatada give the afternoon interval; al-Mawardi cites poetry, while Ibn Ashur defines it by shadow length and al-Harith ibn Hilliza's verse. The declining sun and approaching night signify divine power (Tabrisi, Nasafi, Qatada), and the closing market images judgment (Razi from Hasan). Robinson connects merchants' reckoning of earnings with verse 2's loss. Corpus Coranicum favors a specific daytime by analogy with other opening oaths, though observes late afternoon is unusual among early Meccan oaths. Ubayy's Prophetic report says God swears by day's end. Durr also reports Ibn Abbas and Qatada saying an hour of daytime; Tustari records 'afternoon or time itself'. Al-Suyuti also gives the day's latter part as the oath's referent.

- 103:1/q01/p3 The afternoon prayer, the middle prayer distinguished by special merit.
  reasons: Muqatil calls it the middle and best prayer. Hafsa's 2:238 recension and a reported reading of 5:106 are cited by Zamakhshari, Razi and others; Qurtubi cites an authentic identifying report. A Prophetic report likens missing it to losing family and property. Its hour conflicts with trade and livelihood, and Razi says it closes daily worship as repentance closes deeds. Razi also cites a report about a false oath after Asr and explains that God swears by His prescribed noble command. Alusi rejects Razi's sinful-woman story as unreliable. Neuwirth links prayer-time oaths to liturgical experience, though Corpus Coranicum disputes the proposed hymn tone.

- 103:1/q01/p4 The Prophet's era, honored by renewed prophethood and his community.
  reasons: Mawardi and Qurtubi cite renewed prophethood. Razi and Elmalılı invoke the workers' parable: the final workers labor from Asr to sunset and receive double pay; compare oaths by the Prophet's place and life (90:2; 15:72). Elmalılı describes the mission, Hijra, victory, and extension of Muhammad's community to the end of time; the afternoon of human history follows earlier ages. Tabataba'i favors this because the next verses treat truth and perseverance. Bursevi cites a report of a day or half-day for the community and Najmiyya reports of the Prophet's primordial status.
