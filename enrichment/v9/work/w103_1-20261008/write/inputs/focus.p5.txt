# 103:1/q16 What does the sūra's opening suggest about its literary form? [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the oath's prayer-time association produces a hymn-like effect.
- 103:1/q16/p1 It is a single-member oath followed by a rebuke, with three rhymes.
  reasons: Corpus Coranicum describes the structure; Neuwirth supports keeping the oath as an independent verse. Al-Zarkashi groups it with fifteen oath openings; al-Suyuti describes the specific oath-plus-human-condition pattern. Al-Zarkashi also notes the short opening's rhyme analogy with al-Fajr and al-Ḍuḥā.
  holders (6 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); BURHAN; ITQAN
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r7 BURHAN:v1p100/r8 BURHAN:v1p179#2/r1 ITQAN:v3p282/r1 ITQAN:v4p353/r5
- 103:1/q16/p2 Oaths by prayer times preserve liturgical experience and give a hymn-like tone.
  reasons: Neuwirth proposes this reading.
  holders (1 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); CORPUSCORANICUM-
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r2 | against: CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r3
- 103:1/q16/p3 The sūra has no hymn-like resonance.
  reasons: The Corpus Coranicum commentator objects to Neuwirth and refers to critical discussion at 93:1–2.
  holders (1 notes): CORPUSCORANICUM+
  notes: CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r3
- 103:1/q16/p4 Across its three verses, the sūra uniquely has ten wāws among three-verse sūras.
  reasons: Al-Suyuti cites Ibn Khalawayh's letter-count observation through the end of al-ʿAṣr.
  holders (1 notes): ITQAN(ابن خالويه)
  notes: ITQAN:v4p432/r1
sources: BURHAN = al-Zarkashī, d. 794; CORPUSCORANICUM = Nicolai Sinai (Frühmekkanische Suren), with Nora K. Schmid, using preparatory work by Angelika Neuwirth; later parts Dirk Hartwig, Angelika Neuwirth et al. (author line per sura in segments); ITQAN = al-Suyūṭī, d. 911

# 103:1/q20 How is the transition from al-ʿAṣr to the next sūra recited? [* · readings]
turns on: The transmitted practice of a reader who ordinarily joins or pauses between sūras.
- 103:1/q20/p1 Some readers who generally join sūras make a brief pause at this transition.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazari reports a special sakt among several Hamza, Abu ʿAmr, Ibn ʿAmir, Yaʿqub and Warsh readers. He explains that separating the ending wa-tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabr from the next opening wayl avoids a jarring joined phrase; special sakt presupposes joining elsewhere.
  holders (3 notes): NASHR
  notes: NASHR:v1p261#2/r1 NASHR:v1p261#2/r3 NASHR:v1p261#2/r6
- 103:1/q20/p2 Some readers who generally pause insert the basmala at this transition.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazari reports this practice from Ibn Ghalbun, the Hidaya author, Makki and others; it separates the same jarring phrase, and the special basmala presupposes pausing elsewhere.
  holders (3 notes): NASHR
  notes: NASHR:v1p261#2/r2 NASHR:v1p261#2/r3 NASHR:v1p261#2/r6
- 103:1/q20/p3 Most authorities give this transition no special treatment, and joiners do not insert an untransmitted basmala.
  reasons: Ibn al-Jazari names Faris ibn Ahmad, Ibn Sufyan, Abu al-Tahir, Iraqi authorities and al-Dani among those making no distinction. He says an optional basmala cannot override explicit transmission for readers who join and that special treatments follow the reader's general joining or pausing practice.
  holders (3 notes): NASHR; NASHR+
  notes: NASHR:v1p261#2/r4 NASHR:v1p261#2/r5 NASHR:v1p261#2/r6
sources: NASHR = Ibn al-Jazarī, d. 833
