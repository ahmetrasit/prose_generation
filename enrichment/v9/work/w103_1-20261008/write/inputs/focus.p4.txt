# 103:1/q10 Was the sūra revealed in Mecca or Medina? [* · sciences]
turns on: The authorities' differing attributions; the notes supply no decisive historical test.
- 103:1/q10/p1 It was revealed in Mecca.
  reasons: Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr and most authorities support Mecca (Abu Hayyan, Alusi); Tabataba'i says it seems more likely Meccan, while Study Quran also reports this. Al-Suyuti cites al-Bayhaqi's transmission from Ikrima and al-Hasan naming al-ʿAṣr among the Meccan sūras.

# 103:1/q11 How many verses does the sūra have, and where is its first break? [* · sciences]
turns on: The position of the first verse boundary; the reported total count is three.
- 103:1/q11/p1 It has three verses, with the oath as its own first verse.
  reasons: Alusi calls the three-verse count undisputed; Neuwirth favors an independent oath in line with other openings. Al-Zarkashi counts al-ʿAṣr as a one-word opening verse by analogy with al-Fajr and al-Ḍuḥā and their rhyme despite different lengths, and notes that a single utterance can form a verse. Al-Suyuti reports scholars adding wa-l-ʿaṣr to standalone verses; the counters other than the later Medinans count this opening separately.
  holders (8 notes): ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن الزبير والجمهور); QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); BURHAN; ITQAN(المدني الأخير والباقون، غيره)
  notes: ALUSI-FULL:v30p227/r1 QUTB-ZILAL:103:1/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r7 BURHAN:v1p100/r8 BURHAN:v1p267/r4 ITQAN:v1p180/r7 ITQAN:v1p183/r2
- 103:1/q11/p2 Medina II joins the oath to verse 2 in its division.
  reasons: Spitaler reports this verse division. Al-Suyuti reports that the later Medinan count instead makes wa-tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqq a separate verse while all still total three. Al-Mawsili's scheme distinguishes disagreements in internal breaks from disagreements in totals.

# 103:1/q13 What does the sūra as a whole teach? [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ in emphasis between the sūra's moral summary, its extraction imagery, decline, and inward lessons.
- 103:1/q13/p1 It compresses the Qur'an's path of salvation: faith, good works, truth and patience avert universal loss.
  reasons: Muhammad ibn Kaʿb says belief alone does not suffice; the four qualities follow in order. Sulami lists neglect of God's rights, desires and lack of fear as causes of loss. Qutb calls the sūra a concise Muslim constitution, while Biqai relays Ghazali's six Qur'anic themes and maps the clauses to them. Al-Shafi'i says reflection on this sūra would suffice; Akif's poem repeats its sequence.
