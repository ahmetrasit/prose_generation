# 103:1/q05 How is al-ʿaṣr read? [ٱلْعَصْرِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the transferred kasra is confined to pause or transmitted in connected recitation.
- 103:1/q05/p1 The received reading keeps ṣād vowelless in pause; Salām's reading gives ṣād kasra by vowel transfer.
  reasons: Ibn Atiyya limits Salām's al-ʿaṣri and al-ṣabri to pause; Razi attributes the sound to breath or another impediment. Abu al-Fadl says transfer marks inflection and avoids two vowelless letters; Samīn gives poetic examples and also ḍamma transfer. A rajaz line in fī al-ʿaṣr supports the form.
  holders (9 notes): IBNATIYYA-FULL(سلام أبو المنذر); RAZI-FULL(سلام بن المنذر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية، الراجز، الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفضل، الشاعر)
  notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL:v5p520#3/r3 RAZI-FULL:v32p85/r8 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p538/r7 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p538/r8 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p539/r1 SAMIN-DURR:103:1/r1 SAMIN-DURR:103:1/r4 SAMIN-DURR:103:1/r5 SAMIN-DURR:103:1/r6
- 103:1/q05/p2 Reports also allow the kasra in connected recitation.
  reasons: Al-Hudhali transmits Abu ʿAmr routes for al-ʿaṣr, al-ṣabr, al-fajr and al-watr in pause and liaison; Ibn Khalawayh does not restrict al-ṣabri to pause. Samīn records an imāla-like variant on bāʾ.
  holders (4 notes): SAMIN-DURR(ابن خالويه، الهذلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)
  notes: SAMIN-DURR:103:1/r1 SAMIN-DURR:103:1/r2 SAMIN-DURR:103:1/r3 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p538/r8
sources: ABUHAYYAN-FULL = Abū Ḥayyān al-Gharnāṭī, d. 745; IBNATIYYA-FULL = Ibn ʿAṭiyya, d. 542; RAZI-FULL = Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī, d. 606; SAMIN-DURR = al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī, d. 756

# 103:1/q06 What expanded recitations of the verse are reported? [وَٱلْعَصْرِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the additions are transmitted interpretations or canonical text, and which expanded wording is attributed to whom.
- 103:1/q06/p1 Ali's recitation names the vicissitudes of time and extends human loss until time's end.
  reasons: Tabari, Tha'labi and Elmalılı transmit the interpretive expansion with time's calamities; Razi refuses to treat an expanded report as Qur'anic wording because it would invalidate prayer.
  holders (4 notes): TAB-FULL(علي); THALABI(علي); ELMALILI
  notes: TAB-FULL:v24p612#2/r5 THALABI:v10p283#3/r15 ELMALILI:103:1-3/r15 ELMALILI:103:1-3/r16
- 103:1/q06/p2 A recitation attributed to Ali and Ibn Masʿud adds loss until time's end without naming vicissitudes.
  reasons: Tha'labi gives both attributions; the wording differs from his other Ali report.
  holders (2 notes): THALABI(ابن مسعود، علي)
  notes: THALABI:v10p283#3/r13 THALABI:v10p283#3/r14
- 103:1/q06/p3 The standard codices and general reciters have the sound reading.
  reasons: Tha'labi prefers it; Razi's prayer argument weighs against elevating the expanded wording to Qur'an.
  holders (2 notes): THALABI; RAZI-FULL

# 103:1/q07 How long is an oath not to speak for an ʿaṣr? [ٱلْعَصْرِ · law]

  reasons: Malik adopts the most expansive duration, consistent with his stricter handling of oaths.
  holders (1 notes): QURTUBI-FULL(مالك)

# 103:1/q08 Does the word al-ʿaṣr have a theological implication about who causes events? [ٱلْعَصْرِ · theology]
turns on: Whether the oath is read as correcting causal blame or as prompting inquiry into time's nature.
- 103:1/q08/p1 God acts through time; time itself does not cause calamity or loss.
  reasons: Qashani invokes 45:24 and the ungraded 'God is time' report; Bursevi also quotes the prohibition on reviling time. Razi, Alusi and Elmalılı put blame on human conduct rather than the age.

# 103:1/q09 How does this opening connect with other Qur'anic passages? [وَٱلْعَصْرِ · links]
turns on: The links draw on the preceding sūra, analogous oaths, parallels to the exception, and the sūra's own later clauses.
- 103:1/q09/p1 It follows al-Takāthur by exposing the loss caused by worldly distraction.
  reasons: Jishumi says the prior warning against abundance leads to the demand for deeds; Abu Hayyan distinguishes believer and unbeliever. Biqai and Alusi continue the warning of 102; Study Quran cites 102:1 and 102:8 about wasted time and accountable blessings.
  holders (7 notes): JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7529/r2 ABUHAYYAN-FULL:v10p538/r2 BIQAI-FULL:v8p522/r1 ALUSI-FULL:v30p227/r4 ELMALILI:103:1-3/r13 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p433/r1 STUDYQURAN:103:intro/r3
- 103:1/q09/p2 It complements oaths by the forenoon and other day and night phases.
  reasons: Zamakhshari, Nasafi and others pair afternoon with 93:1; Razi contrasts prior midmorning rising fortunes with afternoon decline. Bursevi cites oaths in 89, 91, 92 and 93; Corpus Coranicum observes this afternoon oath is unusual among early Meccan oaths.
