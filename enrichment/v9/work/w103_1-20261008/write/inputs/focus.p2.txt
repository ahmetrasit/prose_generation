# 103:1/q03 How does the opening oath work, and what does it emphasize? [وَٱلْعَصْرِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether wāw is explained as a conventional emphatic oath or as time's witness to the proposition.
- 103:1/q03/p1 The wāw introduces an oath whose answer affirms that humanity is in loss.
  reasons: Jishumi identifies the oath particle and its answer; Elmalılı compares 93:1. Ibn Ashur says an oath confirms the assertion and its object displays God's creation, power and knowledge. Bint al-Shati says wāw magnifies what is sworn by and moves from perceptible reality to moral truth. Corpus Coranicum notes a possible pre-Islamic seer formula, a connection not yet studied adequately. Al-Zarkashi and al-Suyuti count oath openings as the fifth of the sūra-opening modes and list fifteen such sūras; al-Suyuti further classifies al-ʿAṣr as an oath followed by a proposition about human conduct or condition, namely humanity's loss.
  holders (10 notes): JISHUMI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; BURHAN; ITQAN
  notes: JISHUMI:v10p7529/r9 ELMALILI:103:1-3/r2 IBNASHUR-FULL:v30p528/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p77/r1 BINTSHATI:v2p80/r4 CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1-3:kommentar/r1 CORPUSCORANICUM:103:1:anmerkung/r5 BURHAN:v1p179#2/r1 ITQAN:v3p282/r1 ITQAN:v4p353/r5
- 103:1/q03/p2 Time itself bears witness to humanity's loss.
  reasons: Islahi gives wāw this witness force: history shows recompense, while the rapidly spent lifespan makes a person's deeds profitable or ruinous.
  holders (4 notes): ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Im ām Far āhī)
  notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p435/r1 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p433/r3 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p436/r2 ISLAHI-TADABBUR:vol-9-english:p436/r3
- 103:1/q03/p3 A thematic link between an oath's object and its answer must be argued in each case, not assumed from other oaths.
  reasons: Bint al-Shati questions proposed links between night and day and differing human striving, and between the falling star and the Prophet's freedom from error; these analogies cannot by themselves establish the link in al-ʿAṣr.
  holders (1 notes): BINTSHATI-IJAZ

# 103:1/q04 Why is time or afternoon singled out for the oath? [وَٱلْعَصْرِ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations appeal to divine signs, the day's closure, the prayer's status, the Prophet's era, historical recompense, or the value and moral use of time.
- 103:1/q04/p1 Time displays divine power and wondrous changes, so its oath invites reflection.
  reasons: Alternating nights and days, shifting light and temperature, health and illness, wealth and poverty, and other events disclose God's power (Razi, Ibn Qayyim, Tabarsi). Razi cites 25:62 and 6:13. Bursevi relates the oath to dawn, sun, moon, night, day and forenoon oaths. Ibn Ashur adds the solar-earth cycle and daily social life. Bint al-Shati cautions against loading the verse with speculative philosophy and cites 28:71–72.
