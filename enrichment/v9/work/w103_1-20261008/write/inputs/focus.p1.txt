# 103:1/q02 What meanings and derivations does the word ʿaṣr have in Arabic? [ٱلْعَصْرِ · meaning]
turns on: The sources distinguish its concrete pressing root, wide Arabic uses, bounded temporal force and the definite article's scope.
- 103:1/q02/p1 Its root denotes squeezing or pressing, extended to time, confinement, and the extraction of an essence.
  reasons: Jishumi describes wringing cloth, grape extract, deferred prayer, rain-bearing clouds and a rising dust column. Tabarsi applies the pressed-events image to time. Bint al-Shati adds press equipment, lineage and the hardship that presses out a person's essence; she cites 12:36, 12:49, 78:14 and 2:266. Biqai connects the sūra name to extracting the saved essence. Bint al-Shati reads human life as pressed and refined by trials, exposing its essence; she cites the root's occurrences in Yusuf's dream and harvest year and the whirlwind in al-Baqara, calls this Qur'anic form unique, and rejects al-Raghib's derivation of temporal ʿaṣr from what remains after squeezing. Her criticism of proposed links in other oaths cautions against assuming a simple analogy for this image.
  holders (20 notes): JISHUMI; TABRISI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BINTSHATI-IJAZ+

- 103:1/q02/p3 Unlike dahr's vast or unlimited duration, ʿaṣr marks a bounded, passing period.
  reasons: Asad describes dahr as unbounded; Elmalılı cites Raghib's range from the universe's beginning to end, and Farāhī stresses swift passage, supported by Imru al-Qays and ʿUbayd ibn al-Abras. Bint al-Shati reports Razi's suggestion that 'asr avoids unbelievers' exalted dahr.
