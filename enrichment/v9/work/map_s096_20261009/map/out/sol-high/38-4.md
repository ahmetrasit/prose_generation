# 38:4: 19 questions

## 38:4/q01 Who are those who marveled?  [وَعَجِبُوٓا۟ · referent]
- 38:4/q01/p1 The Meccan unbelievers, especially Quraysh, are the people who marveled. — Muqatil and al-Wahidi identify the Meccans; al-Baghawi identifies them with the earlier disbelievers (38:2); Abu Hayyan identifies the pronoun with the unbelievers. Qatada and later commentators call them polytheists or Quraysh. [8 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 38:4/q02 Why did they marvel at a warner from among them?  [وَعَجِبُوٓا۟ مُّنذِرٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their objection focused on a human peer, his social rank, the message of one God, or their failure to recognize a credible messenger.
- 38:4/q02/p1 They found it implausible that a human of their own people and standing should be chosen as God's messenger. — Muqatil, Ibn Abbas as reported by al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hayyan and others identify the warner as one of them. Razi reports their appeal to shared appearance, lineage and inward qualities, and attributes their refusal to envy. Al-Baydawi and al-Alusi include his being unlettered and known to them. Al-Tabatabai says idolaters deny human messengers. Ibn Kathir compares 10:2; Study Quran compares 50:2, 7:69 and 10:2 and cites 25:7, 6:8-9, 11:12, 14:10, 17:94, 23:33, 36:15, 41:14, 54:24 and 64:6. Kuran Yolu says they expected an angel (6:8-9). Al-Alusi says their marvel expresses emphatic denial. Islahi connects the objection to the conceit of 38:2. [26 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الكفار); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 38:4/q02/p2 They objected to the chosen man's low social standing, expecting a wealthy or high-ranking messenger. — Islahi reports their question why a chief of Mecca or Ta'if was not chosen instead of a poor man and places this complaint in 38:8; Kuran Yolu says they expected one of the Arabs' wealthiest or highest-status people; Study Quran mentions lack of prior standing and 38:8. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 38:4/q02/p3 Their wonder exposes ignorance: his familiar character and message should have made his truth easier to recognize. — Razi reports that they knew he avoided lies while he called them to monotheism, honoring angels, the afterlife and detachment from worldly life; he cites 23:69. Biqai says an Arab and Qurayshi warner was better known, more credible and easier to hear. Bursevi says they treated their equal's mission as impossible while failing to wonder at carved idols. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 38:4/q02/p4 They also balked at the call to one God, thinking a single deity could not hear all their needs. — Qatada, reported by al-Durr, gives the objection to one God; al-Nasafi contrasts their wonder at monotheism with their lack of wonder at polytheism. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL]

## 38:4/q03 How does 'and they marveled' connect to the preceding verses?  [وَعَجِبُوٓا۟ · links]
turns on: Whether the opening conjunction resumes the description before the intervening destruction clause or opens a fresh statement.
- 38:4/q03/p1 It is coordinated with the earlier description of the disbelievers in pride and discord; the destruction statement intervenes parenthetically. — Al-Nazzam, reported by al-Wahidi, connects it to 'those who disbelieve are in pride and discord' and excludes 'How many We destroyed' from the accusative construction. Al-Qurtubi reports the same parsing. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 38:4/q03/p2 It begins a new statement, presenting their surprise as a sign of ignorance. — Al-Qurtubi reports this alternate reading of the passage. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 38:4/q04 How is 'that a warner came to them' construed?  [أَن جَآءَهُم · grammar]
- 38:4/q04/p1 The clause is accusative with an implied min: they marveled at the fact that a warner came. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi give the sense min an; al-Samin calls the ellipsis of min a disputed construction. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 38:4/q05 Who is the warner, and what is his mission?  [مُّنذِرٌۭ مِّنْهُمْ · referent]
- 38:4/q05/p1 He is Muhammad, a messenger from their people sent to warn them, including against sins and the Fire. — Muqatil and Ibn Abbas as reported by al-Wahidi identify their own messenger; al-Tabrisi specifies warning against sins and the Fire, and Ibn al-Jawzi specifies the Fire. Abu Hayyan says messengership entails warning and good news and compares the warning in 36:6. Elmalili calls him a prophet who awakens them. [11 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 38:4/q06 Why call him a warner in this form?  [مُّنذِرٌۭ · rhetoric]
- 38:4/q06/p1 The ordinary active participle fits their surprise at his warning itself, not at its intensity. — Biqai contrasts the chosen form with an intensive form. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 38:4/q07 Who are 'the disbelievers' here?  [ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ · referent]
- 38:4/q07/p1 They are the Meccan unbelievers who spoke against Muhammad. — Al-Wahidi explicitly identifies them as the people of Mecca; Muqatil calls them the Meccan unbelievers. Al-Baghawi relates them to the earlier disbelievers in 38:2. [3 notes: MUQATIL(الكافرون); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 38:4/q08 Why say 'the disbelievers' instead of 'they'?  [ٱلْكَٰفِرُونَ · rhetoric]
- 38:4/q08/p1 The explicit label rebukes and brands the speakers, attributing their accusation to entrenched disbelief. — Razi says it heightens astonishment and marks the speech as arising solely from complete disbelief; al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursevi and al-Alusi say only hardened unbelievers would dare say it. Abu Hayyan says the noun highlights their motivating trait. Al-Samin calls it testimony against their blameworthy character. Ibn Ashur says it brands them in reply to their branding Muhammad and sums up corrupt thought and deeds. Biqai says they knowingly concealed truth and stubbornly denied it, rather than being merely heedless. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 38:4/q09 Why is their accusation joined with waw?  [وَقَالَ · rhetoric]
- 38:4/q09/p1 The conjunction marks the sorcery accusation as a distinct charge, not a result of their surprise. — Biqai explicitly distinguishes the two acts. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 38:4/q10 Whom does 'this' indicate, and why this expression?  [هَٰذَا · referent]
- 38:4/q10/p1 It points contemptuously to Muhammad, made vivid by the speakers' recent encounter with him. — Ibn Ashur compares the contemptuous demonstrative in 21:36. He reports that they said this on leaving Abu Talib's gathering, so Muhammad seemed present to them. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 38:4/q11 What did they mean by calling him a sorcerer?  [سَٰحِرٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the allegation concerned separation of families, persuasive or strange speech, or signs and revelation they could not match.
- 38:4/q11/p1 They claimed he used magic to separate relatives and spouses. — Muqatil gives separation of people; al-Wahidi specifies relatives and spouses turned toward Muhammad and away from each other; al-Qurtubi reports parent from child and husband from wife. [3 notes: MUQATIL(الكافرون); WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 38:4/q11/p2 They explained the persuasive or unfamiliar speech, including monotheism and resurrection, as deceptive verbal magic. — Al-Qurtubi reports the charge of speech that tricks people. Ibn Ashur says claims of one God and resurrection sounded unintelligible to them and were classed with magic, comparing 2:102. Islahi says they could not refute the Qur'an's miraculous eloquence, so they called its sway word magic to keep the masses from accepting revelation; they also called it poetry and Muhammad a poet. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL-]
- 38:4/q11/p3 They labeled his unmatched signs, miracles or revealed words magic. — Al-Baydawi mentions the miracle he displayed; Biqai says his words and deeds attracted hearts, and they avoided an intensive magic charge that might attract people. Bursevi mentions wonders; al-Alusi says they could not match his signs; al-Tabatabai says they could not produce the like of the Qur'an. Study Quran compares sorcery accusations against Moses and Jesus (10:2, 51:52, 7:109, 26:34, 51:39, 5:110, 61:6). Ibn Kathir compares the objection in 10:2. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 38:4/q11/p4 They simply cast the messenger as a sorcerer in their rejection of him. — Al-Tabrisi, al-Baghawi, al-Nasafi, Elmalili, Kuran Yolu and Islahi record the accusation; Islahi traces it to arrogance and says it followed their inability to accept a messenger from their own people. Razi objects that sorcery would turn people from God to Satan, while Muhammad calls to God and reports rationally established truths including resurrection. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL-]

## 38:4/q12 What did they mean by calling him kadhdhab?  [كَذَّابٌ · interpretation]
- 38:4/q12/p1 They called him a liar, especially alleging that his claim to prophethood and revelation from God was false. — Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Qurtubi, al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi, Bursevi, al-Alusi, al-Tabatabai and Islahi connect the charge to his claimed commission or divine revelation; Ibn Ashur distinguishes this understandable but, to them, impossible claim from claims they deemed unintelligible and called sorcery. Islahi glosses kadhdhab as liar and braggart and says their real aversion was his prophethood. Biqai counters their intensive charge with their earlier name for him, al-Amin: only his increasingly truthful speech and moral excellence had changed. Razi calls the paired charges incoherent, as Muhammad reports truths supported by reason. [16 notes: MUQATIL(الكافرون); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; BIQAI-FULL-; NASAFI-FULL-]

## 38:4/q13 Why is the accusation kadhdhab in an intensive form?  [كَذَّابٌ · rhetoric]
- 38:4/q13/p1 The intensive form fits the verse's rhyme and their allegation that every Quranic verse was a lie, while wonders were less frequent. — Bursevi contrasts the pervasive alleged lying about God, which is especially grave, with the comparatively rare display of wonders. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 38:4/q14 What grammatical element is left unspoken in the quoted accusation?  [هَٰذَا سَٰحِرٌۭ كَذَّابٌ · grammar]
- 38:4/q14/p1 The subject pronoun 'he' is omitted before the reported predicate or accusation. — Al-Burhan notes this ellipsis in reports of opponents' speech. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 38:4/q15 What encounter with Quraysh is reported behind this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports place the encounter during Abu Talib's final illness or after Umar's conversion and give different details of the council and exchange.
- 38:4/q15/p1 During Abu Talib's final illness, Quraysh complained; Muhammad offered one confession yielding Arab obedience and foreign tribute, but they rejected God's oneness and clung to their gods. — Al-Zajjaj reports Abu Jahl mocking the offer. Elmalili specifies their complaint about attacks on their gods, Persian tribute, the testimony of God's oneness and their recoil, and says the passage was revealed then. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; ELMALILI]
- 38:4/q15/p2 After Umar's conversion, distressed Quraysh leaders sought Abu Talib's arbitration and demanded that Muhammad leave their idols alone in exchange for leaving him and his God alone. — Al-Tha'labi reports al-Walid convening twenty-five leaders: al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, Abu Jamil ibn Hisham, Ubayy and Umayya ibn Khalaf, Umar ibn Wahb, Utba and Shayba ibn Rabi'a, Abd Allah ibn Umayya, al-As ibn Wa'il, al-Harith and Adi ibn Qays, al-Nadr ibn al-Harith, Abu al-Bukhturi, Qart ibn Amr, Amir ibn Khalid, Makhrama ibn Nawfal, Zam'a ibn al-Aswad, Mut'im ibn Adi, al-Akhnas, Huwaytib, Nabih and Munabbih, al-Walid ibn Utba, Hisham and Suhayl. Al-Walid sent them to Abu Talib; Abu Talib summoned Muhammad and urged fairness. Al-Baydawi also reports the appeal after Umar's conversion. Ibn Ashur says the accusation was spoken as they left this gathering. [9 notes: THALABI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 38:4/q16 How does this response advance the sura's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is the sura's sequence of arrogant rejection and punishment, the contradiction in Quraysh's response, or its recurring human pattern.
- 38:4/q16/p1 Their surprise and accusations display the arrogance, opposition and polytheism named earlier, and anticipate the rejected warnings of earlier peoples. — Abu Hayyan links their accusations to arrogance and opposition; al-Alusi says their wonder is one false claim arising from these traits. Islahi says Sad centers on tawhid, verses 1-11 show rejection of the Quranic reminder, and Quraysh, like earlier nations, will believe only when too late. He relates the human-messenger objection here and poverty objection in 38:8 to the conceit in 38:2. Razi groups the opponents' attempted proofs under theology, prophethood and resurrection. Biqai says revelation states truth more clearly than creation and links arrogant rejection to the destroyed warners of 38:12 and 38:14. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 38:4/q16/p2 The verse exposes a perverse inversion: they reject a truthful, familiar warner while tolerating false idols, and their slander makes God's gift a cause for sorrow. — Al-Nasafi contrasts their marvel at one confirmed God with their failure to question false polytheism and calls their disbelief extreme. Bursevi contrasts their rejection of Muhammad with indifference to carved idols and says this inconsistency produced the accusations. Islahi says the style expresses sorrow at their mocking a native warner and a reminder-filled book. Razi argues the sorcerer charge conflicts with Muhammad's call to God and rational truths. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL]
- 38:4/q16/p3 The historical rejection also exemplifies a recurring refusal to accept inspiration in a human from one's own community. — Asad says the immediate reference is Quraysh but compares the recurring pattern in 50:2; Study Quran and Ibn Kathir likewise compare other verses about wonder at human messengers. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 38:4/q17 How is their objection to one God expressed in the following verse?  [* · links]
- 38:4/q17/p1 The next verse calls one God a wonder; ujab means ajib, an astonishing thing. — Al-Zajjaj supports the form by Arabic usage such as karim and karam for a man. Qatada reports their claim that one deity could not hear all their needs. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 38:4/q18 What inward lesson is drawn from mistaking revelation for sorcery?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is the perception distorted by estrangement or the divine aid and spiritual capacity needed for recognition.
- 38:4/q18/p1 Estranged or diseased hearts misread the light of revelation as magic and the truthful Prophet as a liar. — Bursevi reports al-Tawilat al-Najmiyya's account of distorted understanding and Kashifi's image of blind judgment; Kashifi's poem compares the denier to a bat unable to see the sun despite its world-filling light. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 38:4/q18/p2 Divine aid and detachment from other things enable recognition of prophets and saints; deprivation yields slander of the spiritual elect. — Bursevi says aided hearts turn from everything besides God. Junayd, as reported by Bursevi, calls affirmation of mystics' knowledge lesser sainthood, distinct from greater sainthood. Yafi'i's four grades are belief, knowledge and taste; belief and knowledge without taste; belief alone; and none, with the last slandering the elect through estrangement. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الجنيد,اليافعي)]

## 38:4/q19 What grounds should govern interpretation of this Quranic passage?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The scope of warnings against interpretation by opinion and the qualifications and evidence needed for reasoned or Sufi explanation.
- 38:4/q19/p1 Interpretation must use proper linguistic and transmitted sources and must avoid forcing doctrine, desire, or unsupported certainty onto the text. — Al-Madkhal, reported by al-Alusi, says a report against opinion-based exegesis, if authentic, directs meanings to linguists, abrogation to reports, and intent to the Prophet, allowing evidence-based reasoning where they give no answer. An alternative explanation condemns imposing doctrine or desire; another warns against claims about God-known ambiguities or categorical claims without proof. Al-Alusi reads a second hadith as warning against ignorant answers to hard Quranic questions or knowingly false interpretations. He rejects the capacity of those absorbed in Aristotelian speculation to grasp Quranic subtleties. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المدخل)]
- 38:4/q19/p2 Qualified evidence-based reasoning can reach deeper Quranic meanings while preserving the outward explanation. — Al-Alusi cites 4:83, 47:24 and 38:29 for referral to investigators and reflection; Ibn Abbas's multivalent-Quran report and the Prophet's prayer for his interpretation; and Ali's denial of secret scripture beyond text and understanding. Commentators disagree widely and few Prophetic explanations of constructions survive, so transmitted exegesis alone cannot settle them. Qualifications include Arabic, spiritual discernment, religious sciences and direct recognition of inimitability. He says Sufi allusions may accord with outward intent, but rejects esoteric abolition of outward sense and law; he says Sufi masters preserve outward exegesis. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
