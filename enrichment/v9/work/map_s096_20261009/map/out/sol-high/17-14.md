# 17:14: 28 questions

## 17:14/q01 How is the command to read introduced in the sentence?  [ٱقْرَأْ كِتَٰبَكَ · grammar]
- 17:14/q01/p1 An omitted saying introduces direct speech to the person. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Kisāʾī compare omitted speech in 40:46 and 3:106. Al-Ṭabarī identifies God as speaker and the angels' deed-register as object; al-Rāzī says God speaks through angels. Al-Samīn allows the clause to be adjectival or circumstantial; al-Ālūsī also allows an independent sentence. Ibn ʿĀshūr says context makes the speaker plain. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; FARRA+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+]

## 17:14/q02 How is the imperative ٱقْرَأْ formed or pronounced?  [ٱقْرَأْ · readings]
- 17:14/q02/p1 Its imperative drops the hamza's ḍamma; a light or replaced hamza is also reported. — Al-Naḥḥās adduces Arab replacement of hamza and Zuhayr's line وإلا يبد بالظلم يظلم; Ibn al-Jawzī reports Abū Jaʿfar's light hamza. [3 notes: NAHHAS(زهير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو جعفر); NAHHAS+]

## 17:14/q03 Can someone who was unable to read in life read the record then?  [ٱقْرَأْ · interpretation]
- 17:14/q03/p1 Yes; everyone reads the record at resurrection, including the formerly illiterate. — Qatāda is repeatedly transmitted; al-Ḥasan says literate and illiterate alike. Al-Biqāʿī says God grants the ability and each reads personally. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows miraculous understanding of inscriptions. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن,قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]

## 17:14/q04 What does reading one's book mean here?  [ٱقْرَأْ كِتَٰبَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether reading concerns a displayed register or the soul's disclosed knowledge.
- 17:14/q04/p1 The person reads or understands the displayed account of deeds. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it the angels' counted register; Ibn ʿĀshūr says reading can mean knowledge of recorded deeds or miraculous understanding of writing. Al-Biqāʿī stresses personal reading rather than another reader. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī report that the person recalls every deed as if performing it then, explaining 18:49. [7 notes: STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله); TABRISI(أبو عبد الله); BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 17:14/q04/p2 The intellect reads the soul's exposed impressions when bodily attachment ends. — Al-Rāzī says each deed leaves a lasting effect, like water drops boring stone; conventional letters signify sounds by agreement, whereas soul impressions are intrinsic. He cites 50:22 and affirms outward reports as true too. Al-Ālūsī calls this remote from the plain sense; Asad reports al-Rāzī's allegory of complete recollection. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); ASAD-NOTES(Razi); RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:14/q05 Why command the person to read the record?  [ٱقْرَأْ كِتَٰبَكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the imperative primarily elicits acknowledgment or sharpens rebuke.
- 17:14/q05/p1 It establishes proof by making the person recognize and confess the deeds, leaving no excuse. — Al-Māwardī says reading makes one acknowledge the deeds. Al-Jishumī says it completes proof and shows no injustice. Al-Ṭabrisī says every deed and recompense is seen. Ibn al-Jawzī adds that Hell follows sin while Paradise is grace. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a compelled challenge and says the person already knows the deeds. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 17:14/q05/p2 It heightens reproach and rebuke. — Al-Māwardī offers this as another purpose of making the person read. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 17:14/q06 What is the person's book?  [كِتَٰبَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'book' denotes the written register, deeds themselves, or a figurative record within the person.
- 17:14/q06/p1 It is the complete record of the person's deeds, displayed at resurrection. — Al-Ṭabarī says recording angels keep and count it; al-Ḥasan's report says two noble angels write it, roll it at death, and place it around the neck until resurrection. Study Quran cites 82:10–12 and lists parallels 17:71, 18:49, 69:19, 69:25, 84:7, 84:10. Al-Biqāʿī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say no deed is omitted; the latter says it was already prepared but is brought out from heedlessness. [11 notes: DURR-FULL(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(many commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; NASAFI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 17:14/q06/p2 The book is the actual deeds and their revealed realities, rather than ordinary written signs. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 3:30 and 50:22: deeds themselves appear with consequences, and the clinging 'bird' and book name the same deed. Their exposure confirms unavoidable recompense and removes concealment. [6 notes: TABATABAI+]
- 17:14/q06/p3 The book can be the soul inscribed with enduring traces of deeds. — Al-Rāzī likens habitual acts to drops wearing stone; soul impressions outlast conventional letters and become apparent on separation from the body. Study Quran reports this and cites bodily testimony at 24:24, 36:65, 41:20–22. Al-Ālūsī calls the reading remote but reports the view that outward and inward senses coexist. Al-Rāzī says the impressions reveal felicity or misery and explicitly affirms this spiritual sense alongside outward narrated events. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 17:14/q06/p4 The book can represent the person and full recollection of the past life. — Some commentators in Study Quran picture tongue as pen, saliva as ink, limbs as page and memory as dictated record; al-Qurṭubī gives tongue, saliva and limbs as a pious saying. Asad transmits al-Rāzī's allegory of complete comprehension, citing 37:19, 39:68 and 50:22. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]

## 17:14/q07 What does a report say happens when a believer receives and reads the book?  [كِتَٰبَكَ · reports]
- 17:14/q07/p1 A believer sees sins within and good deeds outside, then learns the sins were forgiven and rejoices. — Ubayy says the person led many to good, fears sins, finds them forgiven and good deeds doubled; his face brightens, he receives the book in his right hand, a crown, two garments, ornaments at every joint and Adam's sixty-cubit stature, then announces the reward to companions with 69:19–23. Bakr says the visible good deeds draw envy, and private forgiveness brings joy, citing 69:19 and, in al-Rāzī, 80:38–39. Al-Ālūsī's version begins with feared sins, then forgiven sins and increasing light and joy, citing 69:19–20. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبي بن كعب); WAHIDI-BASIT(بكر بن عبد الله); RAZI-FULL(بكر بن عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:14/q08 What does a report say happens when an unbeliever reads the book?  [كِتَٰبَكَ · reports]
- 17:14/q08/p1 The unbeliever sees good deeds rejected and then sins, with darkness and lament. — Al-Ālūsī's report cites 25:23 for rejected deeds and 69:25–26 for the lament. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:14/q09 What is the grammatical subject of كَفَىٰ and the function of ب in بِنَفْسِكَ?  [كَفَىٰ بِنَفْسِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the bāʾ introduces the overt subject or belongs to a different analysis of kafā.
- 17:14/q09/p1 Nafsika is the subject in nominative position; bāʾ is added for emphasis. — Al-Farrāʾ says the bāʾ can be omitted with a raised noun in praise or blame, unlike ordinary verbs, and adduces كفى الهدي. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī cite poetry without bāʾ; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 4:79. Al-Ālūsī calls this the majority parse. [16 notes: FARRA; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,الشاعر); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الشاعر); ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NAHHAS+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABATABAI+; ZAJJAJ+; BURHAN-]
- 17:14/q09/p2 The subject is an implicit notion of sufficiency, so bāʾ is not extra. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn mention this analysis; al-Ālūsī includes it among alternatives. Al-Burhān supplies kāna after kafā, with bi-nafsika its predicate, or a pronoun explained by accusative ḥasīban as in نعم رجلا زيد. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 17:14/q09/p3 Kafā is an imperative-like form with an implicit addressee, and bāʾ is not extra. — Al-Samīn finds the analysis weak because kafā accepts verbal inflections; al-Ālūsī says the majority prefers the ordinary past-verb parse. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the indeclinable imperative-like meaning اكتف. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 17:14/q10 Why is كَفَىٰ masculine although نَفْسِكَ is grammatically feminine?  [كَفَىٰ بِنَفْسِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether masculine agreement is allowed with the feminine noun nafs.
- 17:14/q10/p1 Masculine agreement is permitted because nafs is grammatically, not naturally, feminine. — Al-Samīn cites Qur'anic feminine agreement at 21:6 and 6:4 yet allows masculine for figurative gender; Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes the same point. Al-Ālūsī notes the difficulty and analogous verbs that do agree in feminine. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI+]
- 17:14/q10/p2 The lack of feminine agreement argues that bāʾ is not extra and nafs is not the subject. — Al-Burhān reasons that if bāʾ were extra, kafā would agree with normally feminine nafs; he offers omitted kāna or an explanatory pronoun as parses. [3 notes: BURHAN; BURHAN+; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:14/q11 What does بِنَفْسِكَ refer to as the sufficient witness?  [بِنَفْسِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether self-witnessing is the person's direct knowledge or testimony of bodily members.
- 17:14/q11/p1 It means the person's own self and knowledge of the deeds. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent sense and notes Abū ʿUbayda's emphasis on what the person knows; Ibn Kathīr says the person remembers and knows no injustice or alien deed was recorded. Al-Ālūsī calls the bodily-limbs reading contrary to the apparent meaning. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 17:14/q11/p2 It means the person's bodily limbs testifying if the person denies. — Muqātil compares the denial in 6:23 and testimony in 75:14, specifying self, tongue, hands and feet. Al-Biqāʿī says the tongue may deny but limbs confirm the precise record. Abū Ḥayyān transmits Muqātil; al-Ālūsī objects that this departs from the apparent meaning. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+; MUQATIL+; ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)-]

## 17:14/q12 What does ٱلْيَوْمَ modify?  [ٱلْيَوْمَ · grammar]
- 17:14/q12/p1 It is the time adverb governed by kafā. — Al-Samīn gives the attachment; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī call it the adverb. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 17:14/q13 What does حَسِيبًا mean?  [حَسِيبًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether ḥasīb is understood from counting and judgment or from sufficiency and testimony.
- 17:14/q13/p1 An accountant or reckoner who audits and calls to account. — Ibn Qutayba gives the active sense beside sufficient; al-Wāḥidī compares God's epithet in 33:39. Al-Zamakhsharī likens the form to occupational nouns; al-Kalbī and others give accountant or intensive reckoner. Al-Qurṭubī's pious saying pictures tongue as pen, saliva as ink and limbs as witnesses. [19 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن,الكلبي); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 17:14/q13/p2 A witness against oneself. — Muqātil says no better witness than oneself can be found; al-Zajjāj says the self suffices as witness. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī explain that a witness suffices a claimant in the matter at issue. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; DAMGHANI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; MUQATIL+; YAHYA-SALLAM+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 17:14/q13/p3 One who suffices, standing in place of a witness or accountant. — Ibn Qutayba offers sufficient as an alternative to reckoner; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī say the witness suffices the claimant. Al-Zajjāj allows non-Qur'anic feminine ḥasība. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]

## 17:14/q14 What is the syntactic role of حَسِيبًا?  [حَسِيبًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative specifies the sufficiency or describes the person's state.
- 17:14/q14/p1 It is an accusative specification, clarifying how the self suffices. — Al-Zajjāj states tamyīz. Al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī also mention a circumstantial alternative; al-Sarrāj specifically posits an omitted subject for that parse. [11 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 17:14/q14/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative. — Al-Naḥḥās allows ḥāl as well as specification; al-Ṭabrisī attributes this with an omitted subject to al-Sarrāj. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī report it too. [4 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:14/q15 Why is حَسِيبًا masculine with feminine نَفْس?  [بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement follows the referent's sense or the noun's formal shape.
- 17:14/q15/p1 Nafs means a person, so the description follows the person's sense. — Al-Zamakhsharī and others also point to male-associated roles such as witness, judge and trustee; al-Samīn cites a poem. Al-Bursawī takes nafs as person; al-Ālūsī also mentions the adjective pattern. Abū Isḥāq permits feminine ḥasība outside Qur'anic wording. [9 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأنباري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 17:14/q15/p2 Nafs lacks a visible feminine marker, allowing masculine description. — Ibn al-Anbārī compares sky and earth, citing 73:18; Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān cite poetry with masculine description of earth, including أبقل. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأنباري)]

## 17:14/q16 How does عَلَيْكَ connect syntactically and semantically?  [عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ḥasīb is construed as an accountant or a sufficient witness.
- 17:14/q16/p1 It depends on the sense of ḥasīb as one who reckons against a person. — Al-Nasafī cites the idiom 'reckoned this against him'; al-Samīn cites al-Zamakhsharī and examples from Sībawayh; al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī agree. Ibn ʿĀshūr says ʿalayka implies testimony against the person. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 17:14/q16/p2 It marks the matter about which a sufficient witness answers the claimant. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn give the witness construction as an alternative. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]

## 17:14/q17 What does it mean that the person suffices as their own reckoner?  [كَفَىٰ بِنَفْسِكَ عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sufficient self-testimony is direct self-accounting or bodily testimony.
- 17:14/q17/p1 One's own knowledge, record and confession suffice to account for and judge one's deeds, proving the judgment fair. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī say no outside witness or recorder is needed; al-Ḥasan repeatedly calls this justice. Al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Bursawī say it removes excuses and establishes proof. Ibn Kathīr says the person remembers the deeds and no alien deed appears; Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts Paradise by grace and Hell by sin. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 66:7 for unavoidable evidence. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; SULAMI(بعض السلف); THALABI(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; JISHUMI+; MAWARDI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 17:14/q17/p2 The person's own limbs testify when the tongue denies the deeds. — Muqātil illustrates this through 6:23 and 75:14; al-Biqāʿī says the record omits nothing and limbs answer denial. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+; MUQATIL+; ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل)-]

## 17:14/q18 Who is addressed by the command?  [ٱقْرَأْ بِنَفْسِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether a specific report about an unbeliever limits the general address.
- 17:14/q18/p1 Every person, believer and unbeliever, faces the command and account. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects restricting it to unbelievers merely because al-Suddī tells an unbeliever's story. Ibn ʿĀshūr points back to the person in 17:13; the believer and unbeliever reports cover both. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 17:14/q18/p2 In al-Suddī's reported episode, an unbeliever asks to audit himself and is told to read. — The unbeliever invokes God's declaration that He is not unjust to servants. Al-Ālūsī says this episode does not limit the verse's general address. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); RAZI-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 17:14/q19 What afterlife events does this verse establish?  [* · interpretation]
- 17:14/q19/p1 There will be a record, resurrection, precise reckoning and just recompense for one's own deeds. — Al-Jishumī explicitly names afterlife reckoning and records. Al-Ṭabarī says angels keep the register; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses exhaustive knowledge. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says deeds cling to their doer and their consequences inevitably appear. Iṣlāḥī says nobody bears another's burden; Kuranyolu says each sees the result; Study Quran cites 3:117, 10:44 and 29:40 for people wronging themselves. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABATABAI+]

## 17:14/q20 What does the command imply about who creates the act of reading?  [ٱقْرَأْ · theology]
- 17:14/q20/p1 The command would lose its function if God created the person's act of reading. — Al-Jishumī uses the imperative itself against the doctrine that God creates the commanded act. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 17:14/q21 What does self-accounting imply for responsibility and divine justice?  [كَفَىٰ بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · theology]
- 17:14/q21/p1 People are accountable for their deeds; reckoning proves God does not wrong them. — Al-Jishumī argues that determinism would make God accountable for the deed, and that creating disbelief while preventing faith would make accounting pointless and unjust. Al-Bursawī says the person's own confession establishes proof. Study Quran says actions lead to salvation or condemnation, while mercy remains beyond calculation. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI+; JISHUMI+]

## 17:14/q22 How should people prepare now for this self-accounting?  [بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the practical counsel concerns reviewing deeds or prioritizing current acts.
- 17:14/q22/p1 Audit and weigh one's deeds before the final presentation while amendment is possible. — ʿUmar's repeated counsel adds preparing for the great presentation; al-Bursawī stresses that tomorrow offers no repair. Al-Ḥasan urges a sound answer to questioning or a shroud for the Fire. Al-Sulamī contrasts righteous people reckoned now with wicked people reckoned later. Elmalılı urges daily examination. A Kashf al-Asrār report's king makes his self recount each evening's words, deeds, movements and stillness, which it finds hard to bear even for one day. [7 notes: TUSTARI(الحسن البصري); SULAMI(عمر بن الخطاب); BURSEVI(عمر,كشف الأسرار); ELMALILI+; TUSTARI(عمر)+]
- 17:14/q22/p2 In choosing among worthy acts, perform the one nearer to and weightier with God first. — Sahl's context concerns duties, sunnas and voluntary acts; Tustarī presents this as practical self-accounting. [1 notes: TUSTARI+]

## 17:14/q23 Where does self-accounting take place?  [بِنَفْسِكَ حَسِيبًا · interpretation]
- 17:14/q23/p1 It may be private between servant and God or public between the person and others. — Sahl gives both spheres as forms of self-accounting. [1 notes: TUSTARI(سهل)]

## 17:14/q24 Why can a person be said to have authored their book?  [كِتَٰبَكَ · interpretation]
- 17:14/q24/p1 The person's own acts dictated its contents. — Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh says people should read the book because they themselves dictated it; Study Quran's reported image makes memory dictate the record while tongue, saliva and limbs form its writing implements. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators); SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ)+]

## 17:14/q25 How does this account connect with the preceding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which preceding theme supplies the transition to each person's record and judgment.
- 17:14/q25/p1 Revelation and warning have supplied proof, leaving no excuse when records are judged. — Al-Rāzī says the completed account of signs and warning leads to the record; al-Ālūsī relays his proof of oneness, prophethood and resurrection. Kuranyolu likewise connects revelation, faith, reward and punishment to responsibility. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 17:14/q25/p2 God's worldly and religious benefits call for obedience, which is then examined. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī give this as a second link. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); RAZI-FULL+]
- 17:14/q25/p3 Creation is for worship, and the sun, moon, night and day aid obedience that judgment assesses. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī cite 51:56; the latter says judgment asks whether people obeyed or rebelled. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); RAZI-FULL+]
- 17:14/q25/p4 The passage moves from God's guidance-book to the person's exhaustive deed-book. — Al-Ālūsī cites 18:49: the personal book shows whether the first book was followed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 17:14/q26 How does this verse illustrate the seven revealed modes in a reported classification?  [* · sciences]
- 17:14/q26/p1 It exemplifies the mode concerning resurrection and accounting. — Al-Burhān reports a ninth explanation of the seven modes as knowledge types: creation, unity, transcendence, attributes, commands, mercy and punishment, resurrection and accounting, and prophethood; it gives this verse as the accounting example. Al-Qāḍī says all seven noncontradictory transmitted modes entered the codex except nonmutawātir material; al-Ṭabarī and most later scholars favored one, al-Shāṭibī distinguished Abū Bakr's and ʿUthmān's compilations, and al-Ṭaḥāwī viewed the modes as temporary recitation ease. [5 notes: BURHAN(أبو بكر القاضي,الطحاوي); BURHAN+]

## 17:14/q28 What ancillary rhetorical observations are included among the source notes?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes discuss separate rhetorical examples alongside the verse.
- 17:14/q28/p1 Verb-first interrogation can deny the action's occurrence or propriety. — Al-Jurjānī says such a question may challenge an impossible boast or condemn a proposal; 11:28 illustrates the latter. A figurative impossible-act question compares boast or hope to absurdity rather than asserting literal expectation. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL+]
- 17:14/q28/p2 Noun-first interrogation directs doubt to the alleged actor's identity or capacity. — Al-Jurjānī distinguishes it from action-focused questioning. In 'Would he kill me while the sword is beside me?', the sword is an impediment even though the actor could kill. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL+]
- 17:14/q28/p3 The aim of such interrogatives is to make the listener reconsider, feel shame, desist or fall silent. — Al-Jurjānī treats 'denial' as a label for this effect. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL+]
- 17:14/q28/p4 A cited Bedouin couplet and related images are praised for their figurative force. — Al-Jurjānī singles out خسأت, says dawn 'permits' sight because daylight removes night's hindrance, praises the al-Jāḥiẓ couplet and Bashshār's boil image, and finds 'toil as bridge to comfort' stronger than another bridge use, also citing Rabīʿa al-Raqqī. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL+]
