# 41:11: 34 questions

## 41:11/q01 What does “then” imply about the order of creating heaven and earth?  [ثُمَّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether thumma marks time or rank/discourse, and whether “creation” means initial matter, decree, formation, or later spreading.
- 41:11/q01/p1 Earth's body was created first, heaven afterward, and earth was spread and furnished after heaven. — Ibn Qutayba distinguishes creation from daha in 79:30 and counts two days for earth, two for heaven and two for earth's finishing; Mujahid also permits “after” for “with.” Tabrisi, Ibn Kathir, Suyuti's Ibn Abbas report, Bursevi and Elmalılı reconcile 79:27–33 the same way. Qurtubi takes thumma as smoke's transition to density. Ibn Abbas in Nasafi and Bursevi reports vapor and foam from a primordial water or jewel. Elmalılı prefers the majority temporal reading. Study Quran points to 2:29. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(مجاهد); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(عبد الله بن عباس); STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI(ekser müfessirîn; author)+; TABATABAI-]
- 41:11/q01/p2 “Then” orders the account or marks heaven's greater rank, without establishing that heaven was created after earth. — Baydawi prefers rank because of 79:30. Qashani says time did not yet exist. Abu Hayyan cites heaven as a more wondrous sign and analogies at 90:11, 90:17 and 6:151. Biqai stresses scale and care. Ibn Ashur explains more and greater worlds and equal attention to both subjects; Tabatabai finds 79:30 more explicitly temporal. Asad renders the conjunction “and”; Kuran Yolu renders “moreover” and agrees with Shawkani and Ibn Ashur. [12 notes: QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI(Râzî ve Kadı Beyzâvî); BAYDAWI-FULL+; ELMALILI-]
- 41:11/q01/p3 Heaven was created first; the apparent later mention is explained by an understood prior “had been” or by prior divine determination. — Muqatil's implied kana is reported by Razi and Abu Hayyan but rejected by Razi because thumma and anteriority conflict. Abu Hayyan reports Razi's preferred distinction between decree and actual creation, citing 3:59. Elmalılı reports Razi and Baydawi's rank sequence but worries it makes primal matter eternal. Ibn Ashur and Biqai also place smoky heaven before earth. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(Râzî ve Kadı Beyzâvî); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)+; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-; ELMALILI-]
- 41:11/q01/p4 Heaven and earth began together and were completed in integrated stages, so a strict first-created question is misplaced. — Islahi likens them to parts of a building, with attention moving between unfinished parts; Quranic order of mention varies. Biqai also says phases overlap. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 41:11/q02 What does istawā ilā al-samāʾ mean here?  [ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the subject of the turning is God's intentional act or His command.
- 41:11/q02/p1 God directed His will or attention toward creating and shaping heaven. — Ibn Qutayba, Zajjaj, Ibn Isa in Mawardi, Wahidi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Biqai, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalılı, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai and Islahi give this sense. Tabrisi derives direction from straightness; Baydawi and Bursevi stress undivided attention. Nasafi gives the idiom of finishing one task and beginning another. Raghib distinguishes ilā from ʿalā. Bursevi and Tabatabai exclude bodily movement; Abu Hayyan excludes physical influence. [34 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(الفراء والأشعري وجماعة أهل المعاني); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN(الفراء والأشعري وجماعة من أهل المعاني)+; ITQAN(إسماعيل الضرير)+]
- 41:11/q02/p2 God's command, rather than God spatially, turned toward heaven. — Al-Hasan's interpretation is transmitted by Mawardi, Jishumi and Tabrisi; Qurtubi cites Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas as well. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); JISHUMI(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 41:11/q03 Is istiwāʾ here an attribute of action or an eternal essential attribute?  [ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether the word names the creative act in this context or an eternal divine attribute.
- 41:11/q03/p1 It is an attribute of God's action in making and shaping heaven. — Thaʿlabi calls this the majority view; Qurtubi cites the subsequent making of seven heavens and 2:29. [3 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 41:11/q03/p2 God possessed istiwāʾ eternally as an additional essential attribute. — Qurtubi reports this position without endorsing it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(من قال إنه صفة ذاتية زائدة)]

## 41:11/q04 What does “heaven” designate here?  [ٱلسَّمَآءِ · referent]
turns on: Whether samāʾ denotes the forming cosmic heavens or the region immediately above earth.
- 41:11/q04/p1 The heaven being formed, initially a single undifferentiated smoky body and later seven heavens. — Tabatabai says the singular fits the undifferentiated state. Ibn Atiyya supplies an omitted completion before the command. Ibn Ashur says the effective act focused on heaven; Asad and Study Quran refer to 2:29 for its cosmic sense. [4 notes: TABATABAI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]
- 41:11/q04/p2 The upper region or air above earth. — Elmalılı compares verses about rain sent from heaven, 23:18, 25:48 and 31:10. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]

## 41:11/q05 What was the heaven's “smoke”?  [دُخَانٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether dukhān is literal combustion smoke, water vapor, dark formative matter, or modern gas.
- 41:11/q05/p1 Water vapor or a smoke-like moist cloud. — Thaʿlabi, Wahidi, Jishumi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Kathir, Qurtubi, Biqai and Bursevi give vapor; Ibn Abbas in Jishumi and Tabrisi says vapor from earth. Islahi describes dust and vapor or nebula. Ibn Ashur cites a Prophetic ʿamaʾ report as thin cloud or moisture, and Kuran Yolu cites Shawkani. Biqai says the vapor merely looked like smoke. [14 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس,قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 41:11/q05/p2 A dark, subtle primordial substance or constituent particles, described by resemblance to smoke. — Baydawi allows bulk matter or minute particles. Qashani contrasts subtle heavenly matter with dense earth. Bursevi says scattered, unlit and uncohesive; Raghib says smoke-like appearance indicates lack of solidity. Alusi rejects indivisible atoms. Ibn Ashur calls the simile emphatic and links ʿamaʾ to fine primal matter. Samin calls it visual simile. Tabrisi says this material was layered into seven heavens. [11 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 41:11/q05/p3 Gas, specifically hydrogen as a primal element. — Asad appeals to physicists' account of particles developing from hydrogen; Kuran Yolu prefers it in light of science. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 41:11/q05/p4 Darkness over the primordial deep. — Ibn Ashur reports the comparison with Genesis but rejects it as distant from the ʿamaʾ report. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 41:11/q05/p5 Smoke of fire or fine terrestrial matter raised by heat. — Ibn Jawzi reports fire sent on earth; Bursevi describes fine particles lifted by heat and Raghib distinguishes smoke accompanying flame from moist vapor. Elmalılı imagines an initially fiery earth and rising gases. Alusi objects that elemental fire follows earth and the philosophical fire sphere is unreal; meteors do not prove it. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI(الراغب); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 41:11/q06 What is the lexical and morphological sense of dukhān?  [دُخَانٌۭ · meaning]
- 41:11/q06/p1 Smoke rising from burning fuel, with an extended sense for visible vapor. — Samin adds the plural أدخنة for few and دخنان for many; the irregular دواخن is explained from metaphorical داخنة, with عثان and عواوثن as analogies. Raghib distinguishes flame smoke from vapor of moist water particles. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 41:11/q07 How did the smoke-like material arise according to the reports?  [وَهِىَ دُخَانٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The reported source and physical process by which earth and heaven emerged.
- 41:11/q07/p1 Wind, heat, or fire acting on primordial water raised foam for earth and vapor or smoke for heaven. — Ibn Jawzi reports wind over water. The Ibn Abbas report in Bursevi has the Throne over water and fire raising froth and vapor; Alusi reports heating water, and Elmalılı reports heat making curd and smoke. Abu Hayyan cites a Torah report of heat over water. Elmalılı transmits Razi's caution that the account is not Qur'anic and would need evidence. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(عبد الله بن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Fahr-i Râzî,قِيل)]
- 41:11/q07/p2 A primordial jewel or red ruby melted into water; its foam and smoke formed earth and heaven. — Nasafi attributes the jewel account to Ibn Abbas; Alusi reports the red ruby melting under God's gaze. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 41:11/q07/p3 Smoke rose from fire sent upon the already created earth. — Ibn Jawzi gives this as a second account; Elmalılı suggests a fiery first earth whose gases formed the upper region. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ELMALILI]
- 41:11/q07/p4 Fine ʿamaʾ solidified into heaven; water appeared and earth dried within it. — Ibn Ashur recounts this cosmology by reference to Genesis and takes ʿamaʾ as primordial fine matter. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 41:11/q07/p5 Dry land became earth and then divided into two earths. — Elmalılı reports Zamakhshari's unusual account and speculates that the second earth might be the Moon or America. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Zemahşerî)]

## 41:11/q08 How does wa hiya dukhān work grammatically?  [وَهِىَ دُخَانٌۭ · grammar]
- 41:11/q08/p1 The waw introduces a circumstantial clause; its feminine pronoun refers to heaven as it then was. — Bursevi explicitly parses the clause and notes that earth's existence and contents were already determined. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 41:11/q09 When was the command to heaven and earth given?  [فَقَالَ لَهَا وَلِلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command brings them into existence or directs entities already made.
- 41:11/q09/p1 After both had been created or prepared. — Mawardi cites the majority; Ibn Atiyya supplies omitted acts of creating and completing before the command. Alusi says a command to release benefits follows their preparation, though a proposed reordering to put seven-heaven completion before the command conflicts with the apparent order. Nuhhas places the event on Thursday. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(جمهور); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(بعض); NAHHAS]
- 41:11/q09/p2 Before their existence, as an expression of God's creative command. — Ibn Bahr equates “come” with “Be” at 16:40; Razi says no literal legal charge could address the nonexistent. Ibn Ashur directs it to formative smoke, with earth and heaven alike still to be created. Baydawi and Kuran Yolu accommodate separately timed creation or prior determination. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 41:11/q10 What does “come” command heaven and earth to do?  [ٱئْتِيَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imperative addresses existing entities' contents, obedience or coordination, or depicts their creation and formation.
- 41:11/q10/p1 Produce the benefits placed within them: celestial lights, water, rivers, fruit, plants and other provisions. — Tabari and Ibn Abbas detail sun, moon, stars, rivers and fruits; Qurtubi adds winds and clouds. Thaʿlabi, Wahidi, Naqqash in Mawardi, Baghawi, Razi's Ibn Abbas report, Ibn Kathir, Biqai and Alusi give the same reading. Biqai adds minerals, scheduled delivery and that this is subjection rather than legal duty. Razi rejects this as contrary to the later completion in 41:12; Tabatabai likewise says coming-to-be follows. Ibn Qutayba's al-Ajjaj line illustrates revelation as subjection. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,النقاش); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 41:11/q10/p2 Come into being or assume the created form and function God decreed. — Ibn Bahr says “Be”; Jishumi, Tabrisi, Nasafi, Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Tabatabai and Kuran Yolu treat it as creative will. Nasafi and Zamakhshari describe earth as dwelling, heaven as roof; Baydawi's mixed account assigns heaven's coming to creation and earth's to spreading, though he notes difficulty. Alusi reports Irshad's formation interpretation and earth-first exposition for human livelihood. Kuran Yolu says each was addressed when created. [18 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 41:11/q10/p3 Obey God's appointed course or give Him obedience, knowledge and remembrance. — Muqatil places this after an earlier rejected offer of reward and punishment; Saʿid ibn Jubayr in Nuhhas and Mawardi gives obedience, and Zajjaj and Ibn Kathir state doing God's command. Ibn Qutayba cites al-Ajjaj's line about the earth settling to illustrate directed subjection. [9 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(العجاج)]
- 41:11/q10/p4 Come together and cooperate in God's intended order. — Baydawi and Zamakhshari in Abu Hayyan explain mutual fit; Elmalılı describes a coordinated system whose parts adjust despite their natures; Tabatabai points to exchanges among parts of the visible world. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 41:11/q10/p5 Become exactly as decreed in physical qualities and accessibility. — Ibn Bahr lists hard or soft, rough or smooth, inaccessible or accessible, and explains their reply as becoming what God wished. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر)]

## 41:11/q11 Was the divine address and heaven and earth's reply literal speech?  [فَقَالَ قَالَتَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether inanimate or not-yet-existent entities could receive and answer a literal command; whether visible conformity can stand as speech.
- 41:11/q11/p1 They literally heard, understood and spoke after God granted them life or speech. — Ibn Atiyya prefers this as a fuller display of power; Qurtubi calls it the majority. Ibn Qutayba cites speaking limbs, mountains and birds and rejects evasions; Razi reports arguments from apparent wording, rational plural and the Trust in 33:72, but objects that nonexistent entities cannot receive commands. Islahi says created things knowingly worship and our hearing is limited, invoking Solomon's letter at 27:31. Study Quran cites analogous empowered particles and organs. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; RAZI-FULL(القائلون بهذا القول); QURTUBI-FULL(أكثر أهل العلم); ELMALILI(Bazıları); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Other commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)+; BURHAN(ابن عطية)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI-; RAZI-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 41:11/q11/p2 The dialogue figuratively portrays immediate, irresistible compliance with God's creative will. — Majaz treats inanimate actions as humanlike; Jishumi cites Arab idiom, full basin and answering eyes, and 2:117. Tabrisi and Baydawi compare “Be” in 36:82 and 2:117; Baydawi prefers the image. Zamakhshari, Nasafi, Qashani, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalılı, Ibn Ashur, Asad and Kuran Yolu give forms of this view. Ibn Ashur distinguishes individual metaphors for speech, coming and willingness from a composite representation and an implied personification, citing a rajaz basin line. Razi cites birds and poets' speaking eyes. Mawrdi and Qurtubi allow visible obedience to stand for speech. Ibn Atiyya reports but does not favor this. [29 notes: MAJAZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الراجز); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الراجز الذي لا يعرف تعيينه); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 41:11/q11/p3 God may have spoken aloud, or manifested power may have conveyed the address in place of words. — Mawardi offers both modes; Qurtubi repeats them. This addresses the divine side of the exchange independently of whether their answer was literal. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]

## 41:11/q12 Who actually spoke in qālatā?  [قَالَتَآ · referent]
turns on: Whether the dual names the two cosmic entities, two groups, localized speaking sites, or an implausibly reassigned speaker.
- 41:11/q12/p1 Heaven and earth themselves, as the dual antecedents. — Abu Hayyan and Samin reject a plural-collective explanation because the preceding nouns are singular and Ibn Atiyya's cited poem differs. Biqai says the dual does not prove simultaneous address. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BIQAI-FULL]
- 41:11/q12/p2 The two collectives of heavens and earths. — Ibn Atiyya cites a poem pairing sets of ropes and groups of people; Abu Hayyan and Samin report then object that one heaven and one earth were actually named. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(شاعر غير مسمى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 41:11/q12/p3 The Kaaba's site on earth and the corresponding place in heaven. — Abu al-Nasr al-Saksaki says God placed His sanctuary there; Mawardi, Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir transmit the view. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو النصر السكسكي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو نصر السكسكي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل)]
- 41:11/q12/p4 Al-Khiḍr is the subject of “they said.” — Kashshaf records and condemns this remote assignment as a distortion masquerading as a miraculous figurative reading. [0 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL-]

## 41:11/q13 How are iʾtiyā and ataynā read?  [ٱئْتِيَا أَتَيْنَا · readings]
turns on: The root and morphological pattern of the long-vowel variant and how many objects its interpretation must omit.
- 41:11/q13/p1 The common short-vowel forms derive from atā, “come,” with the answer “we came.” — Ibn Atiyya calls this the majority reading; Samin contrasts it with Ibn Abbas, Ibn Jubayr and Mujahid's lengthened forms. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR]
- 41:11/q13/p2 The lengthened ātiyā and ātaynā derive from giving and mean “give obedience” and “we gave it.” — Tabari's Tawus transmission, Ibn Abi Hatim, Thaʿlabi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan cite Ibn Abbas and others; Thaʿlabi says give from yourselves. Qurtubi says objects are omitted. Abu al-Fadl al-Razi and Zamakhshari object that the giving derivation requires two awkward omitted objects. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(طاووس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وعكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير ومجاهد,ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الفضل الرازي)-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفضل الرازي)-]
- 41:11/q13/p3 The lengthened forms derive from mutual agreement or haste: each complied with the other and they concurred. — Baydawi, Zamakhshari as reported by Abu Hayyan, and Samin link the form to cooperation; Abu al-Fadl al-Razi prefers it because only one object is omitted and allows haste as nuance. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الرازي والزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الفضل الرازي)+; QURTUBI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفضل الرازي)+]

## 41:11/q14 How is karhan read and what sense does its vowel suggest?  [كَرْهًۭا · readings]
turns on: The vowel of the first consonant and whether the variant carries a shade of hardship.
- 41:11/q14/p1 The verse's fatḥa form means imposed compulsion or reluctant hardship. — Samarrai contrasts it with voluntary action using 4:19 and 9:53. Abu Hayyan notes the other vowel often suggests hardship. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 41:11/q14/p2 Al-Aʿmash reads kurhan with ḍamma, a dialect variant often associated with hardship. — Abu Hayyan calls it dialectal; Samin refers to prior treatment under al-Nisāʾ. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش)]

## 41:11/q15 What does “willingly or unwillingly” convey?  [طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether willingness and reluctance are only the idiom of irresistible power, real potential states, or distributed between the two realms.
- 41:11/q15/p1 God's power is irresistible whether the two would wish to comply or refuse; the alternatives do not assert actual choice. — Razi likens this to a ruler ordering a subordinate; Baydawi, Nasafi, Zamakhshari, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, Asad and Kuran Yolu explain the idiom as inevitable creative will. Ibn Ashur treats it as proverbial metonymy and, in the composite reading, a feature only of the depicted scene. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 41:11/q15/p2 They genuinely have an alternative of willing or reluctant readiness, and their answer affirms willing acceptance. — Tabatabai takes the answer as evidence against a merely figurative alternative and suggests pre-existing readiness or its absence. Muqatil reports obedience without reward or punishment after a prior refusal through fear; Elmalılı says things accept effects contrary to their normal nature. [5 notes: TABATABAI; MUQATIL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 41:11/q15/p3 Heaven was willing and earth was compelled. — A reported view argues from heaven's orderly movement, obedient inhabitants and superior qualities, contrasted with earth's variation, darkness and density; it cites 16:50. Razi's own explanation says both answered willingly. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL-]

## 41:11/q16 How are ṭawʿan and karhan parsed?  [طَوْعًا كَرْهًۭا · grammar]
- 41:11/q16/p1 They are verbal nouns in the accusative functioning as circumstantial expressions: willingly or unwillingly. — Tabrisi, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Samin, Bursevi, Alusi and Ibn Ashur state the parse; Ibn Ashur attaches them to the imperative's dual subject. Ibn Atiyya supplies an ellipsis, “come willingly, or you will be brought unwillingly.” [10 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 41:11/q17 What does their reply “we came obedient” mean?  [قَالَتَآ أَتَيْنَا طَآئِعِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the reply describes willing compliance, actual formation, or the “giving” sense of a variant reading.
- 41:11/q17/p1 They willingly complied with God's command, fully submitting themselves and what they contain. — Muqatil says they offered obedience; Razi stresses willingly rather than unwillingly. Qurtubi supplies an omitted “to Your command.” Ibn Kathir includes future angels, humans and jinn; Biqai says nothing is withheld or changed. Study Quran compares universal submission at 2:116, 16:52 and 19:93. Tabari and Ibn Abbas specify compliance by releasing lights and produce. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 41:11/q17/p2 They became exactly what God wished or decreed. — Ibn Bahr makes the answer correspond to the command to assume specified qualities; Jishumi and Nasafi also describe unfailing creation. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL]
- 41:11/q17/p3 On the long reading, they gave the obedience requested. — Tawus and Ibn Abbas gloss ataynā as aʿṭaynā; Ibn Atiyya says they gave God the obedience He sought. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(طاووس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]

## 41:11/q18 Why is ṭāʾiʿīn masculine plural instead of a feminine dual?  [طَآئِعِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement follows personification, implied rational inhabitants, the first-person form, a real plurality of heavens, or the verse cadence.
- 41:11/q18/p1 The addressed heaven and earth are treated as rational speaking beings, so they take the human masculine plural. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Nuhhas, Thaʿlabi, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Ibn Kathir, Burhan, Biqai, Suyuti, Alusi, Tabatabai and Ibn Ashur make this explanation. Joseph's stars in 12:4, 21:33, 36:40 and sājidīn are cited as parallels. Tabrisi gives poems addressing an animal and a ruin as rational. Ibn Ashur adds that neither realm has real grammatical sex; Tabatabai says the plural can express humility by joining other obedient creatures. [26 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(آخرون); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 41:11/q18/p2 The response includes the rational inhabitants of heaven and earth; their masculine plural governs agreement. — Some Arab grammarians in Tabari and al-Kisaʾi in Nuhhas and Samin give this view; Qutrub says the inhabitants are brought along. Thaʿlabi and Baghawi combine this with rational treatment. Ibn Kathir takes the reply to include future inhabitants. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قطرب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 41:11/q18/p3 The masculine plural follows the male-form first-person plural in ataynā. — Tabari ties ṭāʾiʿīn to the alif and nun in ataynā. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 41:11/q18/p4 The plural refers to seven heavens, hence a plurality of obedient heavenly bodies. — Ibn Ashur cites the subsequent seven-heaven statement at 41:12. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 41:11/q18/p5 The form fits the verse-ending pattern. — Al-Kisaʾi's third explanation, reported by Nuhhas, is phonetic correspondence at the verse end. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 41:11/q19 What is the grammatical role or derivation of ṭāʾiʿīn?  [طَآئِعِينَ · grammar]
turns on: The syntactic state function and the verb from which the participle is derived.
- 41:11/q19/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial expression describing how they came. — Zajjaj states the parse, followed by Tabrisi and Ibn Jawzi. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]
- 41:11/q19/p2 It derives from ṭāʿnā, “we yielded,” in Arab usage. — Some grammarians cited by Burhan contrast this with a derivation from aṭāʿnā and cite a she-camel yielding willingly. [1 notes: BURHAN(بعض النحويين)]

## 41:11/q20 How does this creation scene connect with nearby verses and what does it teach?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the cited point is the passage's creation argument, creatures' obedience, or a broader linguistic and theological analogy.
- 41:11/q20/p1 It completes the account of staged creation and displays divine power, wisdom, oneness and purposeful providence. — Tabrisi says smoke was layered into seven adorned heavens to show God's power, knowledge and independence. Burhan resolves the apparent eight-day sum because the four earth days include the first two; Biqai says six days show free choice rather than natural necessity, calls six a perfect number, and notes the greater heaven's shorter duration. Islahi sees one wise ruler of roof and floor, not rival gods, and links purposive creation to judgment. Bursevi says mentioning human livelihood first calls for faith; Alusi says the same. Biqai reads earth's two periods as an allusion to shirk and heaven's signs as clearer unity. [14 notes: TABRISI; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 41:11/q20/p2 The heavens and earth are compelled to deliver what God lodged in them at their appointed times. — Biqai uses this verse as a parallel for the cosmos's awe and obedience, emphasizing release of benefits rather than bearing the trust itself. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 41:11/q20/p3 The verse exemplifies Quranic figures and constructions beyond this immediate creation account. — Majaz uses it for nonhuman agreement, human-style action and a dual with plural predicate; Itqan also cites plural for two. Jurjani's adjacent discussion distinguishes explicit new metaphors from established identifications and calls 41:28's “abode of eternity” a predicate rather than comparison. Razi uses smoky heaven to argue that fāṭir need not mean creation from nothing because forms may be made from prior matter. [10 notes: MAJAZ; ITQAN; JURJANI-ASRAR(أبو تمام); RAZI-FULL]

## 41:11/q21 What reports give the days and order of creation associated with this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The starting day and authority of the chronology, and whether passages about initial making and later spreading are being distinguished.
- 41:11/q21/p1 Creation ran Sunday through Friday: earth and provisions, then heaven, then Adam; nothing was created Saturday. — The report through Ikrima starts earth Sunday, provisions through Wednesday, heaven Thursday and Friday, Adam late Friday, and rebuts the Jews with 50:38. Jishumi's Prophetic report gives earth Sunday–Monday, mountains Tuesday, and plants, water and settlements Wednesday. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عكرمة); JISHUMI(النبي)]
- 41:11/q21/p2 Soil was created Saturday, followed by mountains Sunday, trees Monday, hardship Tuesday, light Wednesday, animals Thursday and Adam Friday. — Ibn Kathir reports Abu Hurayra's attribution to the Prophet in Muslim and Nasaʾi, calls it unusual, and cites Bukhari's correction that the report is more accurately from Kaʿb al-Ahbar. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(البخاري)-]
- 41:11/q21/p3 Ibn Abbas resolves apparent Quranic discrepancies by separating initial earth creation, heaven's formation, and later earth spreading. — Ibn Kathir records the questioner's wider list, including afterlife questioning, concealment and attributes; Itqan records Saʿid's question and Ibn Abbas's replies as differences of setting or meaning. Hakim authenticates the report, and Ibn Hajar identifies its four questions and an authenticated original. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن عباس,الحاكم,سعيد بن جبير)]

## 41:11/q22 What if heaven and earth had refused?  [طَوْعًا أَوْ كَرْهًۭا · reports]
turns on: The differing transmitted consequence of hypothetical refusal.
- 41:11/q22/p1 God would have commanded a creature to swallow them. — Thaʿlabi gives an unnamed prophet's question and says the creature was within God's knowledge; Qurtubi identifies the questioner as Moses and locates it in a divine meadow itself within His knowledge. [2 notes: THALABI(أحد الأنبياء); QURTUBI-FULL(موسى عليه الصلاة والسلام)]
- 41:11/q22/p2 God would have punished them with pain. — Ibn Kathir cites al-Hasan al-Basri through Ibn Abi Hatim. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري)]

## 41:11/q23 How is this verse used in debates over istiwāʾ in other passages?  [ٱسْتَوَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether the preposition ilā in this verse can fix the sense of a separate construction with ʿalā.
- 41:11/q23/p1 The ilā construction supports “turn toward” here, while istiwāʾ elsewhere has other senses depending on construction. — Ibn Jawzi distinguishes complete and incomplete uses, and lists settling at 11:44, embarking at 23:28 and 43:13, maturity at 28:14, equality at 6:50, 35:19 and 40:58, and elevation at 20:5. Study Quran contrasts “turned to” here with “mounted” for the Throne. Alusi and Tabatabai cite Raghib's ʿalā/ilā distinction. Suyuti rejects importing this sense to ʿalā in Throne passages because the preposition differs. [10 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI(الراغب); ITQAN]
- 41:11/q23/p2 By analogy with this verse, istiwāʾ over the Throne means intending or undertaking its creation. — Burhan endorses Farraʾ, Ashʿari and others to avoid denial and anthropomorphism; Itqan reports Ismaʿil al-Darir calling it correct. Suyuti objects that Throne verses use ʿalā, not ilā. [3 notes: ITQAN(الفراء والأشعري وجماعة أهل المعاني); BURHAN(الفراء والأشعري وجماعة من أهل المعاني)+; ITQAN(إسماعيل الضرير)+; ITQAN-]
- 41:11/q23/p3 The Throne expression means settling, control, ascent, justice, or affirmation without reinterpretation. — Itqan reports Ibn Abbas's “settled” via Muqatil and Kalbi but says corporeality requires reinterpretation; rejects istawla because dominion was already God's and conquest implies opposition; cites Ibn al-Aʿrabi's affirmation and Abu Ubayd's ascent but rejects ascent as spatial. Ibn al-Labban gives just balancing. Ismaʿil al-Darir's proposed verb reading of ʿalā fails orthography and canonical case; another clause break is rejected for disrupting sense and 20:5. [7 notes: ITQAN(أبو عبيد,إسماعيل الضرير,ابن الأعرابي,ابن اللبان,ابن عباس,قائلون); ITQAN-]

## 41:11/q24 What peripheral codex or recitation reports were attached to this verse's dossier?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which incidental report rather than a direct interpretation of this verse is preserved.
- 41:11/q24/p1 Uthman reportedly expected Arabs to correct a codex grammatical error in speech. — Ibn Qutayba records this as the objectors' report, without identifying a specific error in the note. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(عثمان)]
- 41:11/q24/p2 The creation passages were alleged to conflict about the order of earth and heaven. — Ibn Qutayba records the objection comparing 41:9–12 with 79:27–30; the staged-creation reconciliation is mapped under creation order. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(قالوا)]
- 41:11/q24/p3 Biographical chains of readers preserve an ʿĀṣim reading transmission. — Nashr gives Shuʿba's dates, learning and 18,000 recitations, including his sister's deathbed story; Hafs's dates, stepson relationship, precise command of ʿĀṣim and assessments by Yahya ibn Maʿin, Ibn al-Munadi and Dhahabi; and dates and judgments for Yahya ibn Adam, al-Ulaymi, Shuʿayb, Abu Hamdun, Abu Bakr al-Wasiti, Ibn Khaliʿ, al-Razzaz and Ubayd ibn al-Sabbah. [12 notes: NASHR(يحيى بن معين وابن المنادي والذهبي)]

## 41:11/q25 Why is earth addressed when the narrative turns specifically to heaven?  [لَهَا وَلِلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
- 41:11/q25/p1 Earth's body had been created, but its later spreading and formation remained to be done. — Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari's distinction between body and spreading at 79:30. Alusi notes that earth's existence and contents were already described, so heaven alone is named in the turning. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 41:11/q26 What stage of heaven is presupposed by the address?  [وَهِىَ دُخَانٌۭ فَقَالَ لَهَا · interpretation]
- 41:11/q26/p1 Heaven was already smoke when God addressed it. — Muqatil places the smoky state before the command; Mawardi says it was smoke as God turned to it. [2 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 41:11/q27 What can the imperative mean if its ordinary motion sense is impossible?  [ٱئْتِيَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the command addresses entities already made or depicts their making.
- 41:11/q27/p1 It pictures bringing into existence or creative realization. — Ibn Ashur says physical approach cannot apply to heaven and earth; Qurtubi compares the pre-creation subjection to “Be” in 16:40, unlike the post-creation majority view. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 41:11/q27/p2 It can mean manifesting the effects and varied beings already placed within them. — Baydawi reports this explanation. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 41:11/q28 What common point do the two readings of “come” share?  [ٱئْتِيَا أَتَيْنَا · interpretation]
- 41:11/q28/p1 Both express God's subjection of heaven and earth to their assigned functions. — Ibn Atiyya says the come and give readings converge in this point; Qurtubi permits the reading “we came” to include what was within them. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 41:11/q29 Can an obedient appearance stand in place of spoken reply?  [قَالَتَآ · rhetoric]
- 41:11/q29/p1 Yes; their visible obedience may itself serve as their speech. — Mawardi contrasts manifested compliance with literal utterance; Ibn Ashur cites the Prophet's warning to Musaylima for turning away as an idiom for disobedience, supporting the opposite metaphor of coming as compliance. ## 41:11/q30 How does the dialogue present God's effective creative will?  [فَقَالَ قَالَتَآ · rhetoric]
- 41:11/q30/p1 The exchange dramatizes realization of a will already described. — Abu Hayyan compares speech used to illustrate praise previously given. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 41:11/q31 What is the difference between ṭawʿan and ṭāʾiʿīn?  [طَوْعًا طَآئِعِينَ · meaning]
- 41:11/q31/p1 Ṭawʿan stresses willing spontaneity; ṭāʾiʿīn describes being obedient to God's will. — Samarrai distinguishes spontaneous willingness from obedient status. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 41:11/q32 What analogy illustrates the creatures' receptivity to divine command?  [أَتَيْنَا طَآئِعِينَ · interpretation]
- 41:11/q32/p1 The law of inertia illustrates how bodies receive effects that seem contrary to their ordinary nature. — Elmalılı uses it to explain natural-seeming celestial and terrestrial obedience. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 41:11/q33 How can “seven” in the next verse be parsed?  [* · grammar]
- 41:11/q33/p1 It may be a circumstantial accusative, a second object, or a specification of an ambiguous pronoun. — Abu Hayyan lists the three analyses of 41:12's sabʿ. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 41:11/q34 What does thumma add to the transition between earth and heaven?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
