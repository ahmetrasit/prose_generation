# 29:16: 13 questions

## 29:16/q01 How is إبراهيم read and syntactically governed?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · readings]
turns on: The case reading and, for the accusative, the governing verb; the surrounding sending formula supports the preferred coordination.
- 29:16/q01/p1 In the common accusative reading, it is coordinated with Noah: God also sent Abraham. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Naḥḥās and al-Kisāʾī supply 'We sent' from 29:14. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī connect it with Abraham's readiness to counsel. Al-Biqāʿī prefers this construction by the later Midian parallel; al-Ālūsī prefers it by 29:36. Al-Rāzī and al-Samīn identify the accusative as common. Al-Bursawī adds an implied command to recall the time of speech. Islahi explicitly describes Abraham as sent as a messenger to call his people, in parallel with Noah. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 29:16/q01/p2 In the accusative, an omitted 'Remember' governs Abraham. — Al-Zamakhsharī makes 'when he said' an inclusion apposition since the time contains the event. Al-Biqāʿī says recalling the exemplary forefather comforts the Prophet and instructs his people. Al-Ālūsī permits coordination of stories with different forms. [14 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:16/q01/p3 In the nominative reading, an omitted predicate gives 'Among the messengers was Abraham.' — Al-Kashshāf names al-Nakhaʿī and Abū Ḥanīfa as readers; Abū Ḥayyān adds Abū Jaʿfar and Ibrāhīm. Al-Samīn makes Abraham the subject. Al-Ālūsī favors this supplied predicate in light of 29:14–15. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي وأبو حنيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النخعي); SAMIN-DURR(النخعي وأبو جعفر وأبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة والنخعي وأبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 29:16/q01/p4 The accusative Abraham is coordinated with the object pronoun of 'We saved him' in 29:15. — Al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Kisāʾī and al-Samīn allow it. Al-Ālūsī reports Abū al-Baqāʾ and Ibn ʿAṭiyya offering it, but calls it poor. [4 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء وابن عطية)-]
- 29:16/q01/p5 The nominative may instead supply 'among those to be mentioned' or 'among those saved.' — Al-Ālūsī reports both estimates and prefers 'among the messengers' because of the surrounding verses. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:16/q02 What is the grammatical function of إذ قال?  [إِذْ قَالَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether idh modifies sending or belongs to an omitted 'remember'; Abū Ḥayyān disputes the appositional object analysis.
- 29:16/q02/p1 It marks the time of God's sending Abraham. — Al-Kashshāf says he could then counsel through age and knowledge; al-Bayḍāwī says judgment and recognition of truth had matured; al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī say he was ready to exhort and guide. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:16/q02/p2 It is an inclusion apposition to Abraham under an omitted 'Remember.' — Al-Zamakhsharī says the time contains the event; al-Samīn and al-Biqāʿī give the same parse. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow it with omitted 'remember.' Abū Ḥayyān objects that past-time idh is not a direct object, and al-Ālūsī reports his objection to making its indeclinable temporal form an object or substitution. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الزمخشري وابن عطية)-]
- 29:16/q02/p3 An implied command asks the audience to remember the time of his speech. — Al-Bursawī combines coordination of Abraham with Noah and this implicit recollection. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 29:16/q03 When was Abraham sent in relation to his call?  [إِذْ قَالَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'when he said' requires sending to coincide with speech, and whether 29:18 attests an already existing mission.
- 29:16/q03/p1 He was sent when mature enough in age, judgment and knowledge to counsel his people. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī make this the sense of the temporal phrase. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:16/q03/p2 He remained a messenger while speaking; sending is a continuing state. — Al-Rāzī raises the problem that preaching ordinarily follows sending and compares ongoing messengerhood to standing that lasts until a prince exits his house. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 29:16/q03/p3 He began calling people through divine guidance before formal sending, which happened while he preached. — Al-Rāzī offers this as another resolution of the order problem. Al-Ālūsī reports a similar proposal near adulthood but replies that Abraham's words at 29:18, if his, suggest prior sending; al-Saʿdiyya says the verse need not imply formal mission. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 29:16/q04 How does Abraham's account connect with Noah's and Quraysh?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · links]
- 29:16/q04/p1 It continues Noah's account by introducing another prophet God sent. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām links it to 29:14; al-Zajjāj supplies the sending verb. Al-Biqāʿī cites the later Midian parallel, and al-Ālūsī joins the stories despite differing sentence forms. Islahi makes the parallel with Noah explicit and calls Abraham's warning timely counsel for his people. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 29:16/q04/p2 It offers Quraysh a parallel and recalls their forefather's rejection of idols. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān make the analogy explicit. Al-Biqāʿī says remembering Abraham comforts the Prophet and instructs his people. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:16/q05 Who were Abraham's people in this account?  [لِقَوْمِهِ · referent]
- 29:16/q05/p1 His own people, identified as Nimrod and the idolaters of his city in Babylon. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān mention Nimrod and his idol-worshiping city; al-Bursawī names Babylon and includes Nimrod. Al-Biqāʿī stresses Abraham addressed his own people. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:16/q06 What is reported about Abraham before he called his people?  [وَإِبْرَٰهِيمَ · reports]
- 29:16/q06/p1 He grew unusually fast, then went out to call his people. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports al-Suddī: each day of growth equaled a week and each week a month. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي)]

## 29:16/q07 What does اعبدوا الله ask Abraham's people to do?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: The command is unpacked as exclusive monotheism, practical obedience or affirmation of God's existence; al-Rāzī explicitly gives alternative readings.
- 29:16/q07/p1 Worship God alone, affirming His oneness instead of idols or intermediaries. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā gloss worship as oneness; Ibn ʿAṭiyya sets the call among Nimrod's idolaters. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says exclusivity is implicit: idolaters worship angels and jinn as intermediaries to God. Kur'an Yolu asks whether idols have divine qualities. Al-Rāzī argues possible beings require a necessary existent, and two necessary beings would be composite through shared and distinguishing attributes; no partner can share God's status. Ibn Kathīr adds sincere worship, provision sought only from God, and thanks to the sole giver. Islahi says avoiding partners specifically concerns worship of God. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 29:16/q07/p2 Obey God by performing His worship and obligations. — Ibn ʿAbbās says 'obey'; al-Ṭabarī says to discharge duties. Al-Rāzī includes affirmation of God and rejection of shirk within obligations and prohibitions. Al-Biqāʿī says worship as God directs. Study Quran calls it a summary of appeals in 19:42, 19:48, 21:52, 21:66, 26:69, 26:76, 37:85 and 37:95. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 29:16/q07/p3 Affirm God's existence. — Al-Rāzī reports a paired reading: worship affirms God, while piety denies other gods; association with the sovereign is a grave offense. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 29:16/q08 What does وَٱتَّقُوهُ ask them to do?  [وَٱتَّقُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations specify fear itself, abstention from forbidden deeds, or especially avoidance of shirk.
- 29:16/q08/p1 Fear God and His punishment. — Al-Suddī, Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās say fear God. Al-Qurṭubī specifies His punishment and torment; al-Biqāʿī links association with punishment. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran speak of reverence alongside worship. Islahi explains fear of God as avoiding His wrath. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 29:16/q08/p2 Avoid sins and prohibited acts. — Al-Ṭabarī says to avoid sins while performing duties; al-Rāzī pairs obligations with abstention from prohibitions. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 29:16/q08/p3 Avoid associating anything with God. — Al-Rāzī says piety denies any other deity because a partner with the sovereign is a grave offense. Al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explicitly link fear to shunning shirk; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the pair a summons to monotheism and a warning. Islahi specifies that refraining from associating partners with God in worship avoids His wrath. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 29:16/q09 With what is ذَٰلِكُمْ خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ compared?  [ذَٰلِكُمْ خَيْرٌۭ لَّكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether khayr compares worship with idolatry, all goods, or nothing.
- 29:16/q09/p1 Worship and piety are better than their idolatry, unbelief or current practice. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bursawī contrast idols or unbelief; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī name current practice. Al-Bursawī says the comparative accommodates their false belief, though unbelief contains no good. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr glosses khayr as afḍal, 'better or more excellent.' Al-Jishumī and al-Baghawī state the advantage generally; Ibn Kathīr says it earns good and averts evil. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 29:16/q09/p2 Worship and reverent fear are better than everything beneficial. — Al-Biqāʿī says better than anything; al-Ālūsī reports comparison with all beneficial things. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:16/q09/p3 Khayr can mean simply 'good,' without comparison. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative alongside both comparative construals. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 29:16/q10 Why are worship and piety better for people?  [ذَٰلِكُمْ خَيْرٌۭ لَّكُمْ · interpretation]
- 29:16/q10/p1 They bring benefit and avert harm in this life and the hereafter. — Ibn Kathīr says worship, fear, seeking provision from God and thanking the giver bring good and repel evil in both lives. Kur'an Yolu says obedience benefits people; disbelief harms worldly life and prevents salvation in the hereafter. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 29:16/q10/p2 They draw a person near to sovereign God and raise that person's rank. — Al-Rāzī argues humans cannot own heaven and earth, so their highest rank is nearness to the sovereign. He cites 96:19 on prostration and two divine sayings about drawing near through obligations and continued worship. An atheist denies the sovereign, while an idolater posits inferior partners; uniqueness, worship and piety yield higher status to one who understands the proofs. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 29:16/q10/p3 Serving God protects human dignity and personality. — Kur'an Yolu says serving others damages these, whereas worship and obedience to God benefit the worshiper. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 29:16/q11 What does إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ require or imply?  [إِن كُنتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the condition specifies practical discernment, recognition of God as Lord, or censures their ignorance.
- 29:16/q11/p1 They must discern benefit from harm and good from evil with reasoned insight. — Al-Ṭabarī invokes recognizing good and harm, with 6:72 and 9:41. Al-Zamakhsharī contrasts discernment with blind ignorance; al-Bayḍāwī contrasts knowledge with ignorance. Al-Ālūsī says even any knowledge suffices to recognize the advantage. Al-Jishumī and al-Bursawī speak of understanding and discernment. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 29:16/q11/p2 They must know God is their Lord. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī's gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 29:16/q11/p3 The condition rebukes their actual ignorance. — Muqātil says they do not know; Ibn al-Jawzī says they fail to know good from bad. Al-Biqāʿī argues even animals seek their benefit, so people who miss this are below animals despite claims to reason. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL]

## 29:16/q12 Whose speech is the following passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the discourse remains Abraham's or interrupts it with an aside.
- 29:16/q12/p1 The verses continue Abraham's speech. — Al-Kashshāf reports this for the passage through the endpoint it names as 27:56. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 29:16/q12/p2 The verses include an aside about Muhammad and Quraysh within Abraham's story. — Al-Kashshāf reports this alternative for the same passage. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 29:16/q13 How does this call relate to other Qur'anic portrayals of Abraham and prophetic faith?  [* · links]
- 29:16/q13/p1 Islam renews Abraham's creed, which believers are told to follow. — Study Quran says the Qur'an treats Abraham in over two hundred verses and cites 6:161, 2:130, 3:95, 16:123, 2:135 and 3:67 for following and defining faith by his creed. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 29:16/q13/p2 This command summarizes Abraham's longer appeals elsewhere. — Study Quran cites 19:42, 19:48, 21:52, 21:66, 26:69, 26:76, 37:85 and 37:95. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 29:16/q13/p3 Calling people to the one God is a principle shared by all prophets. — Kur'an Yolu connects it with examining idols' supposed divinity and remembering earlier peoples who called messengers liars. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
