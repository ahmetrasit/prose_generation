# 27:8: 24 questions

## 27:8/q01 What does the pronoun in 'came to it' refer to?  [جَآءَهَا · referent]
turns on: The nearest named antecedent is the fire; another reading supplies the tree of the scene.
- 27:8/q01/p1 The fire Moses had seen or thought he saw. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī prefer the earlier explicit fire; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the feminine pronoun follows Moses' description of the appearance as fire. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:8/q01/p2 The tree bearing the light. — Abū Ḥayyān reports a green samur or ʿalayq tree which did not burn and seemed to recede; Ālūsī finds this antecedent implausible. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 27:8/q02 What was the apparent fire Moses approached?  [ٱلنَّارِ · referent]
turns on: Whether nār names Moses' initial impression, a physical veil, or spiritual light.
- 27:8/q02/p1 Divine light rather than ordinary fire. — Moses mistook light for fire, say Mawardī, the majority he reports, Ibn ʿAbbās and others, and many later sources. Qatāda calls it God's light; Muqātil the Lord's light. Ibn ʿĀshūr says only Moses saw this otherworldly light and his wife may have doubted him. The Study Quran notes Arabic nār can stand for nūr. [39 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن,قتادة); TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة,محمد بن كعب); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(أبو صخر,ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,عكرمة,قتادة,محمد بن كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(الجمهور); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة من أهل التفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر المفسرين,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومحمد بن كعب,وهب بن منبه); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وغيره); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,محمد بن كعب); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler); STUDYQURAN(some commentators,ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 27:8/q02/p2 A real fire, or fire containing light. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr lists God's veils of might, dominion, authority, fire, light, cloud and water, identifying this as the fire of the call. Baghawī and Biqāʿī connect it to the prophetic veil report; Mawardī records fire with light. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(أهل التأويل,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 27:8/q02/p3 Prophetic spiritual illumination. — Asad reads it as the light God bestows on prophets and, alternatively, as God's own light at the core of illumination. The Turkish commentary reports his metaphorical reading. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(some of the earliest commentators, quoted by Tabari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Esed)]

## 27:8/q03 What happened at the tree when Moses approached?  [جَآءَهَا ٱلنَّارِ · reports]
- 27:8/q03/p1 A luminous fire or light appeared in a green tree without consuming it and drew Moses near. — Wahb describes a green ʿalayq branch whose flame grew as its greenery increased; Moses tried to take a branch, recoiled when the flame leaned toward him, then heard the call. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the tree samur and says the light seemed to recede. Ibn Kathīr says its light reached heaven; Biqāʿī describes Moses following it into a ravine. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(وهب); MAWARDI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(وهب); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 27:8/q03/p2 Moses' sheep repeatedly fled before he approached God's light. — Abū Ṣakhr gives the repeated flight of the sheep as the lead up to the encounter. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو صخر)]

## 27:8/q04 Why does this account say jāʾahā rather than atāhā?  [جَآءَهَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the verbs imply differing difficulty in the narrated settings.
- 27:8/q04/p1 Jāʾa fits the more arduous scene, whereas atā suggests easier arrival. — Al-Rāghib distinguishes easy atyan from broader majīʾ. Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites derivatives implying ease and 23:27, 50:19, 18:71, 18:74 for harder jāʾa. Naml's firmer promise, shihāb qabas, and mission to Pharaoh's dispersed people make its scene harder than Qaṣaṣ or Ṭā Hā; frequency of the verbs in the sūras also fits. [9 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(الراغب الأصفهاني)]
- 27:8/q04/p2 The parallel accounts use different verbs for Moses' arrival. — Al-Burhān contrasts Ṭā Hā's atāhā; al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts Qaṣaṣ's atāhā and notes the broader comparison of parallel narratives. [5 notes: BURHAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:8/q05 Who was called, and why is the caller left unnamed here?  [نُودِىَ · rhetoric]
turns on: How the passive verb is supplied and whether its lack of an explicit caller serves suspense.
- 27:8/q05/p1 Moses was called by God; the passive first magnifies the call and creates suspense. — Biqāʿī says the unannounced caller creates suspense and the explanation signals the event's greatness; Iṣlāḥī says the passive magnifies the event. Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī prefer Moses as the passive subject. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Moses sought directions; Qurṭubī compares the right side of the mountain in 19:52. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:8/q05/p2 The passive subject is the phrase an būrika or an implicit 'calling'. — Zajjāj and Naḥḥās allow the whole phrase to be nominative passive subject; Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān also report an implied call explained by the following words. Al-Farrāʾ makes the case of an depend on whether Moses is supplied. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:8/q06 From where and in what manner did Moses hear the call?  [نُودِىَ · reports]
turns on: The reported source of the sound and the nature of divine speech.
- 27:8/q06/p1 God spoke and Moses heard speech from the direction of the tree, mountain, or fire. — Wahb says the call came from above; al-Suddī says God's speech came from heaven at the tree. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Baghawī and Bursevī describe an audible call from the tree or mountain; Ibn ʿAbbās says it came from the light. [8 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,وهب); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير والحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:8/q06/p2 God created speech in the tree, and Moses heard it from there. — Al-Naqqāsh and al-Jubbāʾī give created speech in the tree as the explanation; al-Jubbāʾī places the tree in the fire and angels around it. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)]
- 27:8/q06/p3 The call came from every direction and reached Moses through all his senses. — Biqāʿī reports this account without further proof. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 27:8/q06/p4 Moses heard God's letters and sound directly behind the fire's veil. — Ālūsī says divine wisdom allowed disclosure in the desired fire form while divine might remains unlimited; he rejects the claim that al-Ashʿarī said Moses actually heard inner speech. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الأشعري)]
- 27:8/q06/p5 Divine speech may be heard without ordinary letters or sound, even though the ordinary voice is also a reported account. — Rāzī reports Sunni explanations: necessary knowledge on hearing an extraordinary attribute or a voice from the tree. Ālūsī cites the sacred hadith and 42:11 for extraordinary hearing; he says al-Māturīdī permits hearing nonsound exceptionally and Ibn al-Humām's impossibility concerns ordinary experience. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأشعري,الباقلاني,الماتريدي)]

## 27:8/q07 How could Moses know the caller was God?  [نُودِىَ · theology]
turns on: Which sign can rule out a deceptive creature as caller.
- 27:8/q07/p1 A miraculous green tree burning without being consumed established the caller's identity. — Rāzī prefers this accompanying miracle. He rejects proofs from a human's inability to call from fire, since Satan could enter it, and from the voice's magnitude, since angelic or demonic capacities are unknown. The Transoxanian imams raise the epistemic question. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أئمة ما وراء النهر); RAZI-FULL+]
- 27:8/q07/p2 The extraordinary character of divine speech gave Moses necessary knowledge. — Rāzī reports this Sunni explanation: speech unlike letters or sounds is necessarily recognized as God's attribute. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 27:8/q07/p3 No human can call from the fire or tree. — Rāzī reports and rejects this first Transoxanian proof because Satan could enter either and call. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(أئمة ما وراء النهر)-]
- 27:8/q07/p4 The voice's magnitude proved its divine origin. — Rāzī rejects this second proof because the capacities of angels and devils are unknown. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(أئمة ما وراء النهر)-]

## 27:8/q08 How is an in the call parsed?  [أَنۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause explains a speech act, nominalizes a call with omitted bi, or contracts emphatic anna; the last hinges on the separator rule for supplication.
- 27:8/q08/p1 It is explanatory: the call conveys speech, then its content follows. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Nasafī, grammarians, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ālūsī use the speech sense in nūdiya; Ālūsī prefers it. Al-Raḍī says this is necessary if būrika is a prayer, but Ālūsī rejects the claimed consensus. Biqāʿī says it directs the listener to the clarification. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(النحاة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الرضى); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:8/q08/p2 It is a verbal noun particle, with an omitted preposition after nūdiya. — Zajjāj and Naḥḥās describe the resulting accusative construction; al-Farrāʾ relates case to whether Moses is implicit. Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Bursevī and Ālūsī give the omitted preposition analysis. Al-Kashf objects that an optative verbal noun would not announce good news, an objection Ālūsī questions. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:8/q08/p3 It is lightened from anna, with an implied pronoun of the affair. — Bayḍāwī and Nasafī say prayer permits the absent separator; Abū Ḥayyān replies to Zamakhsharī that qad is not used with a supplication. Samīn notes the difficulty of a prayer as its predicate. Ālūsī reports rule exceptions and disputes al-Raḍī's consensus. [9 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الرضى,بعضهم); KASHSHAF-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)-]

## 27:8/q09 How is the nūn in an būrika pronounced?  [أَنۢ بُورِكَ · readings]
- 27:8/q09/p1 Before bāʾ the silent nūn becomes a concealed mīm with nasalization, without assimilation. — Ibn al-Jazarī says there is no disagreement here; al-Dānī's reported dispute concerns original silent mīm before bāʾ. The sound matches the hidden mīm in yaʿtaṣim bi-llāh. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 27:8/q10 What does būrika mean?  [بُورِكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes abundant good, sanctity, or voices a prayer of blessing.
- 27:8/q10/p1 To be blessed with established and increasing good. — Al-Farrāʾ defines baraka as growing good established in something; Biqāʿī includes increase, purity and all good. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it abundant good; Naṣafī gives goodness and blessing; Ālūsī reads the verb as a blessing predicate. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); JISHUMI(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:8/q10/p2 To be sanctified, purified, or made holy. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī give sanctification; Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the sacred valley and removal of sandals in 20:12. Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Study Quran include purity alongside goodness. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والحسن وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(السدي); ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 27:8/q10/p3 It is an optative greeting: may the recipient be blessed. — Bursevī reports al-Kāshifī's gloss and some commentators' greeting interpretation; Biqāʿī gives supplication for the seeker of the fire. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL]

## 27:8/q11 What Arabic constructions govern the blessing formula?  [بُورِكَ · grammar]
- 27:8/q11/p1 Bāraka can take a direct object or fī, ʿalā, or li; the passive may omit an understood preposition. — Ṭabarī cites bārakaka Allāh and bāraka fīka; al-Kisāʾī, al-Farrāʾ, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr attest variants. Anonymous poems and one by ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr supply examples. Samīn identifies man fī al-nār as būrika's passive subject. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); NAHHAS(الكسائي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والثعلبي,الطبري,شاعر مجهول); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; JISHUMI(الفراء); STUDYQURAN]

## 27:8/q12 What readings of the blessing are transmitted?  [أَنۢ بُورِكَ مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا · readings]
turns on: Whether variant wording is recitation or explanatory report, and what it makes the subject of blessing.
- 27:8/q12/p1 Ubayy and others read 'the fire was blessed' (būrikat al-nār). — Reports attribute it to Ubayy, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and ʿIkrima. Thaʿlabī and others use it to support blessing the fire. Naḥḥās says Abū Ḥātim's chain is unsound and, if authentic, it is explanation rather than canonical recitation; Abū Ḥayyān also calls it commentary because it differs from the codex. Wāḥidī says this meaning does not fit the standard reading. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبي بن كعب); NAHHAS(أبي وابن عباس ومجاهد); THALABI(أبي,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وأبيّ); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبي بن كعب); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبي); RAZI-FULL(أبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبي وابن عباس ومجاهد); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,عكرمة); STUDYQURAN(some); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة)]
- 27:8/q12/p2 Ubayy read 'the earth and those around it were blessed'. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī and Ālūsī cite it for the blessed-place interpretation. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبي); RAZI-FULL(أبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبي,جماعة)]
- 27:8/q12/p3 An explanatory reading specifies the angels around the fire. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī report transmission from Ubayy, Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and ʿIkrima; both note it differs from the written codex and is generally classed as commentary. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:8/q13 Who or what is blessed as 'the one in the fire'?  [مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ · referent]
turns on: Whether man is personal or nonpersonal; whether 'in' is literal location, nearness, ellipsis for place or divine power, or a spiritual reading.
- 27:8/q13/p1 God, disclosed by His light and speech, without spatial enclosure. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jubayr, al-Ḥasan and others are reported to identify God; reports say His light was in the tree or that He called from light. Wāḥidī analogizes 'God came from Sinai' to being known there. Ibn al-Jawzī takes it as the holy caller, not indwelling; Qurṭubī and Ālūsī insist manifestation entails no location. Rāzī calls the Ibn ʿAbbās attribution fabricated; Abū Ḥayyān reinterprets sound reports as God's power. Baghawī cites a Torah description of God's coming from Sinai, Seir and Paran, associating them with Moses, Jesus and Muhammad and Paran with Mecca. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض أهل التأويل,محمد بن كعب); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة وابن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والحسن وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير والحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وسعيد بن جبير,ابن عباس ومحمد بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(النبي,بعض العارفين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)-]
- 27:8/q13/p2 Moses, present at or seeking the place of the fire. — Wāḥidī prefers an implied 'seeker of the fire'; nearness can count as being in it, says Abū ʿAlī. Thaʿlabī and Rāzī likewise read the phrase as God's greeting to Moses, with angels around him. Qurṭubī reports an omitted fī or nearby location. Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds Moses among those around the fire in the Ṭā Hā parallel, but reports his placement inside as another view. [22 notes: THALABI(آخرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم,قيل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل لموسى); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN(others); TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 27:8/q13/p3 Angels stationed in the fire or light. — Al-Suddī says angels were inside; Naḥḥās reports Qatāda naming both inner and outer groups angels. Ṭabrisī says they praised God aloud. Nasafī links their blessing to Moses' commissioning and miracles; some sources put Moses around them. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى)]
- 27:8/q13/p4 God's light or the luminous sign within the fire. — Qatāda, al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿĪsā and others name the light. Wāḥidī says man can mean mā and God created light in what Moses saw; Qurṭubī also reports blessing a sign placed in it. Study Quran distinguishes the light within the fire from fire itself. Samīn reports nonrational man for the light, coupled with surrounding places. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن,قتادة); TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة,محمد بن كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(جماعة من أهل التفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(قتادة والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,محمد بن كعب); TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى); STUDYQURAN(some); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى)]
- 27:8/q13/p5 The fire itself. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās report this repeatedly. Some treat min as mā and mā or min as extra; Ubayy's explanatory reading directly says the fire was blessed. Wāḥidī objects that the standard wording does not support it, while Thaʿlabī, Baghawī and others invoke the blessed spot of 28:30. [24 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد,مجاهد، ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,مجاهد); THALABI(أبي,ابن عباس,بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبي بن كعب,بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,عكرمة,مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(some); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وأبيّ)-]
- 27:8/q13/p6 The location of the fire, the blessed plot or valley; in its widest sense the Syrian region. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī take an implied place and sometimes all its inhabitants. They invoke 28:30's blessed plot and 21:71's Syrian land, home and burial place of prophets and revelation. God spoke, commissioned Moses and showed miracles there; Ubayy's earth reading supports it. Bursawī adds the fertility poem. [26 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل لموسى); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبي,جماعة); ELMALILI(Kâdî Beyzâvî); TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî); STUDYQURAN(some); ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+]
- 27:8/q13/p7 God's power, authority, dominion, or proof manifested there. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī and others supply an omitted noun; Abū Ḥayyān says sound God-referent reports must be read this way. The reading denies bodily presence. [8 notes: THALABI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); TABRISI(ابن عباس والحسن وسعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى); STUDYQURAN(still others)]
- 27:8/q13/p8 The unburned tree within the fire. — Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān report the tree itself as the blessed referent; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports it among interpretations. Ṭabāṭabāʾī judges most of the reported identifications forced, without specifying this one individually. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى); TABATABAI-]
- 27:8/q13/p9 Inwardly, Moses' heart reaching the fire of divine attributes. — Qāshānī says the heart gains true perfections and the prophetic station of intimate speech. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 27:8/q13/p10 Metaphorically, prophets' illumination, with Moses in it and believers around him. — Asad takes the light as divine illumination granted to prophets; the Turkish commentary reports his Moses-and-believers application. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(some of the earliest commentators, quoted by Tabari); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Esed)]

## 27:8/q14 Who or what is 'around it' and included in the blessing?  [وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether 'who' designates attending persons, nearby land, divine light, or inward faculties.
- 27:8/q14/p1 The angels. — Muqātil, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima and others repeatedly identify them; Wāḥidī says they stood around the light praising and sanctifying God. Some explanatory readings append 'of the angels'. [30 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(الحسن); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، الحسن); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الحسن,سعيد بن جبير,عكرمة,قتادة); NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس والحسن); RAZI-FULL(الجبائي,قتادة والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة وسعيد بن جبير والحسن وقتادة); DURR-FULL(أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 27:8/q14/p2 Moses together with the angels. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb connects the next verse's address to Moses by name; Wāḥidī calls this universal, and Thaʿlabī and Baghawī frame it as a greeting. The site and people may both receive blessing. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب); THALABI(آخرون); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(some); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(محمد بن كعب); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب); ELMALILI(Kâdî Beyzâvî); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 27:8/q14/p3 Moses alone, near the fire. — Abū Ṣakhr reports Moses around God's light; Baghawī and Ṭabrisī place him near but outside it, while angels are inside. Abū ʿAlī distinguishes nearby from entering using 9:101; Ibn al-Jawzī reports Moses alone. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو صخر); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو صخر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(قول غير مسمى)]
- 27:8/q14/p4 The surrounding places and blessed Syrian land, possibly with all angels. — Abū Ḥayyān allows nonrational man for places; Biqāʿī includes all angels and sacred lands across time; Ālūsī describes the valley and neighboring prophetic land. - 27:8/q14/p6 Inwardly, Moses' spiritual faculties and celestial angels. — Qāshānī says they receive unveiling, secret wisdom, sacred confirmations and states of spiritual tasting. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 27:8/q14/p7 Moses' believers in prophetic spiritual illumination. — Asad's metaphorical account is reported by Kur'an Yolu. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Esed)]

## 27:8/q15 Why is blessing announced at the start of the call?  [بُورِكَ مَن فِى ٱلنَّارِ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether blessing functions as a greeting, announces a sanctified event and place, or carries optative force.
- 27:8/q15/p1 It honors and greets Moses and the angels, reassuring him before his commission. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurṭubī compare the greeting of Abraham's household in 11:73. Abū Ḥayyān says it prepares Moses for conversation; Iṣlāḥī says it dispels fear of a voice in the dark valley. Ṭabāṭabāʾī links it to his purification and election in 20:11–14. [12 notes: THALABI(آخرون); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قوم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 27:8/q15/p2 It marks the place and people as blessed by God's speech, Moses' prophethood and the miracles. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī and Bursevī name the event's religious consequences and blessing spread through Syria. Bayḍāwī says the opening gives glad tidings of the great affair; Ālūsī adds the valley's prophetic history. Elmalılı calls a prophetic station sacred. [19 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,جماعة); ELMALILI]
- 27:8/q15/p3 The blessing is a prayer form used as a report. — Ṭabrisī says the prayer form intends a declarative statement; Biqāʿī describes a supplication for the fire seeker. [2 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 27:8/q16 Who says 'Glory be to God' here?  [وَسُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the doxology is in the divine call, Moses' response, the attendants' response, or a parenthetical address.
- 27:8/q16/p1 It is part of God's call to Moses. — Ibn Shajara says God speaks it and blesses the one who glorifies Him, but Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī find that expansion poorly supported. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī place the doxology within the call; Biqāʿī may coordinate it as a predicate. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شجرة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن شجرة); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن شجرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 27:8/q16/p2 Moses exclaimed it on hearing the call. — Al-Suddī says he sought God's aid and declared Him transcendent; Bayḍāwī says he expressed wonder. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers a doctrinal declaration over the mere wonder reading. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); ALUSI-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI-]
- 27:8/q16/p3 Those around the fire said it. — Qurṭubī reports an omitted speech verb: 'those around it said, Glory be to God.' [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 27:8/q16/p4 It is a parenthetical address to Muhammad. — Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī report a second-person address interrupting the two speeches. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:8/q17 What does the glorification assert or do?  [سُبْحَٰنَ ٱللَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether the primary force is divine transcendence, wonder at the event, or inward purification.
- 27:8/q17/p1 It declares God free of defect, evil, created likeness, embodiment and spatial limits. — Zajjāj and language scholars define subḥān as exalting God above evil; Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Abū ʿUbayd say imperfection. Ṭabrisī specifies no body, substrate or speech instrument; Rāzī says it protects divine essence and wisdom. Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Iṣlāḥī use it to block a localizing or humanizing inference from the fire and speech. [23 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+]
- 27:8/q17/p2 It expresses wonder and announces that the Lord of all worlds wills this momentous event. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı connect the phrase with the encounter's greatness; Biqāʿī says lordship reflects God's complete essence and acts, including His care for Moses as child and adult. Iṣlāḥī urges trust in the universal Lord's providence. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI-]
- 27:8/q17/p3 Inwardly, it transcends the essence by shedding psychic traits and bodily veils. — Qāshānī's inward reading includes relinquishing defects and faults. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:8/q18 What does calling God 'Lord of the worlds' contribute?  [رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the title explains this call's universal authority or the different emphases of parallel scenes.
- 27:8/q18/p1 It shows universal lordship, power, providence and fitness for glorification. — Biqāʿī connects the perfect Lord's acts with raising Moses. Iṣlāḥī says Moses can trust the Lord of all worlds; Kur'an Yolu stresses God is Lord of all humanity, not only Israel, and relates the next verse's Mighty and Wise names to Moses' struggle. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 27:8/q18/p2 In the parallel narratives, the divine names fit each scene's level of exaltation. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Naml also names the Lord of the worlds and adds Mighty, Wise in the next verse, whereas Qaṣaṣ uses Lord of the worlds in its direct identification. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:8/q19 What report about God's veil is linked to this fire?  [ٱلنَّارِ · reports]
- 27:8/q19/p1 God does not sleep, governs the balance and night and day deeds, and His veil of fire or light would burn all reached by His sight if removed. — Abū Mūsā transmits the Prophetic report, and Abū ʿUbayda recites 27:8 after it. Versions say fire, light, or light or fire; Muslim, Ibn Mājah and al-Bayhaqī are cited. Qurṭubī says Ibn Mājah supplies one wording and Muslim a fuller one. Abū ʿUbayd explains the radiances as the majesty of God's face; Ibn Kathīr locates the underlying report in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim through ʿAmr ibn Murra. Study Quran gives no grading. ## 27:8/q20 What other reports are attached to the call or its aftermath?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which supplementary report is being related.
- 27:8/q20/p1 A Bedouin learned glorification and other devotional words from the Prophet. — Saʿd reports monotheism, magnification, praise and the verse's glorification formula, then pleas for forgiveness, mercy, guidance and provision. Abū Ḥātim says a believer's request for guidance asks for steadfastness and increase. [2 notes: IBNHIBBAN(أبو حاتم,سعد بن أبي وقاص)]
- 27:8/q20/p2 Wahb says Moses did not touch a woman after God spoke to him. — Māwardī transmits Wahb's report. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 27:8/q20/p3 God's eschatological call is equally audible at every distance. — Ālūsī cites al-Bukhārī's report that God gathers people and calls: 'I am the King, the Judge.' [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البخاري)]

## 27:8/q21 How does this call compare with the parallel Moses narratives?  [* · links]
turns on: Which detail in the parallel telling carries each account's rhetorical emphasis.
- 27:8/q21/p1 Naml gives the blessing and glorification, while Qaṣaṣ specifies where the call came from. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says Naml's added formula suits its exalted setting; Qaṣaṣ supplies geographical detail for its fuller mode. He also notes Naml's short definite family promise versus Qaṣaṣ's tentative and detailed one. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:8/q21/p2 Naml's direct naming of Moses honors him and suits its weightier commission; Qaṣaṣ uses explanatory an. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the Qaṣaṣ call may convey meaning by words or sign, comparing 37:104–105 and 11:46. Its expansive mode permits an, while Naml's direct summons conveys honor; its divine names add exaltation. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:8/q21/p3 The narratives vary in the fire object, the hand sign, number of signs and addressees. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts shihāb qabas with jadwa, 'insert' with broader sulūk, nine signs with two proofs, Pharaoh's people with his elite, and Qaṣaṣ's wing gesture. He says shihāb suggests firmer benefit, idkhāl is narrower and harder, while sulūk can connote easy movement as in 16:69 and 39:21; recurring d-kh-l forms in Naml support its diction. [10 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 27:8/q21/p4 The accounts can be read together as Moses' encounter, commission and signs. — Al-Bāqillānī says the call flows without break into lordship and the staff and hand signs. Ibn Kathīr says Moses' prediction of news and light was fulfilled and the passage recalls his mission to Pharaoh's arrogant elite. Iṣlāḥī says these verses prepare him for the signs; Asad compares 20:17–20 and points to an earlier note on staff symbolism. [7 notes: BAQILLANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]

## 27:8/q22 How does this episode function in the sūra?  [* · links]
- 27:8/q22/p1 It begins the promised pattern of prophetic election and signs, later balanced by warnings. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the five stories illustrate the sūra's opening promise and warning: promise predominates in Moses, David and Solomon, warning in the final two. Ibn Kathīr recalls Moses' commission and Pharaoh's elite rejecting the signs; Iṣlāḥī says the encounter prepares Moses for his mission. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:8/q23 What inward significance is given to Moses' encounter?  [* · inward]
turns on: How visible light relates to spiritual disclosure and inward faculties.
- 27:8/q23/p1 The outward fire was a veil for divine lights that drew Moses from attachment into intimacy and prophetic speech. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says God removed attachment to the visible fire, restrained Moses' speech and singled him out among messengers. ʿArāʾis al-Bayān says tree and fire mirrored majesty and beauty in a humanly bearable disclosure. Bursevī adds that God may manifest through fire, tree or mountain without direction. [4 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(بعض العارفين,عرائس البيان)]
- 27:8/q23/p2 Moses' heart and faculties receive the fire of divine attributes, unveiling and wisdom. — Qāshānī calls the heart the blessed inner participant and the faculties, with celestial angels, its illuminated surroundings; glorification entails shedding bodily and psychic veils. [3 notes: QASHANI]
- 27:8/q23/p3 God's manifestation can be perceived in a form while His transcendence remains absolute. — Ālūsī invokes mystical disclosure without confinement and the sacred hadith of God becoming the servant's hearing, within 42:11. Bursevī invokes a hadith about God being seen in a form He wills. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 27:8/q24 What is said about tazkiya and zakāh in the ancillary note?  [* · meaning]
- 27:8/q24/p1 Tazkiya is the Messenger's taking alms; zakāh derives from growth and increase. — Ibn Qutayba cites 'he purifies you' at 2:151 and says alms grow wealth like blessed crops. This note offers an analogy for blessing language rather than an explanation of a word in 27:8. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]
