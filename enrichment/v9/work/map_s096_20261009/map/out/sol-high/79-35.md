# 79:35: 8 questions

## 79:35/q01 How is the day of remembrance related to the Great Calamity?  [يَوْمَ · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier expression governs or is restated by يوم, and whether the Calamity is merely timed by remembrance or identified with it.
- 79:35/q01/p1 It specifies the day or occasion when the Great Calamity comes, by apposition to the preceding clause or time expression. — Al-Zamakhshari makes it appositional to إذا جاءت; al-Nasafi to the coming Calamity; al-Samin allows substitution for إذا; al-Alusi allows apposition to all or part of the preceding clause and notes the Kufan analysis of a verbal clause; Ibn Ashur calls it inclusive apposition because that day includes resurrection and judgment. Al-Wahidi and al-Tabatabai describe it as the time of the Calamity's arrival; al-Bursawi says it explicates the Calamity. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 79:35/q01/p2 The day or its remembrance is appositional to the Great Calamity itself. — Al-Alusi reports الطامة الكبرى as a possible appositional antecedent; al-Elmalılı describes remembrance as appositional to the greatest catastrophe. This syntactic relation need not identify the catastrophe with remembrance itself. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 79:35/q01/p3 The day phrase is an adverb qualifying the coming of the Calamity. — Al-Alusi attributes this analysis of جاءت to al-Tabarsi. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي)]
- 79:35/q01/p4 An omitted verb, 'I mean,' governs the day as an explanatory accusative. — Al-Samin and al-Alusi report this analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي)]
- 79:35/q01/p5 The day phrase independently introduces what happens on that day. — Al-Samin gives this as another possibility. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 79:35/q01/p6 The Calamity itself is the recollection of deeds or the sight of Hell. — Al-Alusi reports this strained identification, motivated by the thought that recalling good and evil or seeing Hell overwhelms every worldly pleasure and pain, and rejects it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 79:35/q02 At what stage might the Great Calamity described by this day occur?  [يَوْمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which scene in the following verses is the decisive catastrophe: reading the record or dispatch to the final abodes.
- 79:35/q02/p1 At the reading of each person's record. — Al-Razi reports this possibility for the catastrophe and cites 17:13; al-Nahhas places recollection at reading the record and seeing one's place, while other commentators say seeing the written deeds prompts recollection. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS]
- 79:35/q02/p2 At the dispatch of people to Paradise and Hell. — Al-Razi reports this as another possible point of the catastrophe in the following verses. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون)]

## 79:35/q03 What does the human's remembrance of striving convey?  [يَتَذَكَّرُ مَا سَعَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether remembrance denotes literal recollection of worldly deeds, awareness of one's aims and outcomes, an inward spiritual journey, or the consequent requital.
- 79:35/q03/p1 People recall the good and evil deeds they performed in worldly life, including deeds they had forgotten. — Al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and others specify good and evil deeds; Ibn Kathir cites 89:23. Al-Nahhas adds reading the record and seeing one's place. Al-Jishumi says the record shows no deed, small or great, was lost. Al-Zamakhshari and al-Razi cite 58:6: God counted deeds their doers forgot. Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Bursawi and al-Alusi attribute forgetting to heedlessness or time; al-Alusi adds tribulation and the sheer number of deeds. Al-Biqa'i says the written record and recollection stress the terror of reckoning. Ibn Ashur says presentation of the record elicits acknowledgment, and calls tadhakkur the reflexive counterpart of reminding; al-Kuranyolu adds confession. Bint al-Shati reports al-Zamakhshari's comparison with 58:6. Islahi says present heedlessness and mockery cease when Judgment restores people's senses and shows their earnings. Study Quran says all deeds are recorded and cites 58:6, 10:21, 34:3, 36:12, 43:80, 45:29, 78:29 and 99:6–8. [23 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 79:35/q03/p2 The person recognizes the aims pursued and the profit or loss incurred, as well as good and evil deeds. — Al-Elmalılı extends the recalled striving to the aims pursued and the resulting gain or loss. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 79:35/q03/p3 People recall their journey from primordial nature through spiritual stages and stations to annihilation, and give thanks for what they attained. — Al-Qashani gives this inward account of striving and its grateful remembrance. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 79:35/q03/p4 Remembrance stands for the requital that follows being shown and acknowledging one's deeds. — Ibn Ashur calls remembrance a metonym for its consequence, requital, comparing the command to read one's book in 17:14. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:35/q04 Why does the wording say that the human remembers?  [يَتَذَكَّرُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the wording emphasizes forgotten knowledge returning, the dread of reckoning, or remembrance's consequence of requital.
- 79:35/q04/p1 The wording conveys recovery of deeds once known but forgotten. — Ibn Ashur says remembrance presupposes prior forgetting, the erasure of known information from memory. Al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi and Bint al-Shati's report of al-Zamakhshari link it to 58:6; al-Baydawi, al-Bursawi and al-Alusi specify heedlessness or elapsed time, and al-Alusi adds tribulation and many deeds. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)]
- 79:35/q04/p2 Mentioning remembrance brings out the terror of the approaching reckoning. — Al-Biqa'i connects remembrance of every good and evil deed in the written record with the terror of judgment. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:35/q04/p3 Remembrance names the requital that follows from admitting the deeds shown in one's record. — Ibn Ashur identifies a metonymic use and compares 17:14. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:35/q05 Who is 'the human' here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular human is generic, limited to disbelievers, or points to Abu Jahl.
- 79:35/q05/p1 Human beings generally, each person facing the record of worldly deeds. — Al-Tabari says people; al-Jishumi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Biqa'i, al-Bursawi, al-Alusi and Study Quran use every or each person; Ibn Ashur and other commentators describe the person shown his own record. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 79:35/q05/p2 The disbeliever. — Al-Wahidi transmits this identification from al-Kalbi. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 79:35/q05/p3 Abu Jahl in particular. — Al-Wahidi transmits this identification from Ata. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء)]

## 79:35/q06 What grammatical force does 'ما' have in 'ما سعى'?  [مَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether ما denotes the object of striving or turns سعى into a noun for the act of striving; Bint al-Shati finds the latter broader.
- 79:35/q06/p1 It is a relative pronoun: the things or deeds for which the person strove. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi allow this parsing alongside the verbal noun. Bint al-Shati reports Abu Hayyan's preference for it and al-Zamakhshari's acceptance of both. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان)+]
- 79:35/q06/p2 It is a verbal-noun particle: the person's striving or action itself. — Al-Zamakhshari, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi allow this parsing; Bint al-Shati reports al-Zamakhshari as leaving both open and prefers the verbal noun as broader, citing 53:39 that each person has only what he strove for. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); BINTSHATI+]

## 79:35/q07 What does 'سعى' mean here?  [سَعَىٰ · meaning]
- 79:35/q07/p1 It means serious action or striving. — Al-Tabatabai glosses striving as serious action. Bint al-Shati connects the person's striving with 53:39; other commentators specify the good and evil deeds pursued in worldly life. [4 notes: TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 79:35/q08 How does this verse serve the surah's account of resurrection and judgment?  [* · links]
- 79:35/q08/p1 It makes the gathering and final reckoning vivid by bringing every person's deeds and fate to mind. — Al-Biqa'i says the surah establishes resurrection, gathering and humanity's final fate after fuller exposition in preceding surahs, with the oaths bringing the matter to a decisive climax; he says remembrance of the written record heightens the terror of reckoning. Al-Jishumi says no deed, however small, is lost. Islahi contrasts present heedlessness and mockery of warnings with restored senses when Judgment arrives; Study Quran adduces passages about recording and displaying deeds, including 99:6–8. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
