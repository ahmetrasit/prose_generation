# 7:91: 17 questions

## 7:91/q01 How does the opening fāʾ connect the punishment to what preceded it?  [فَأَخَذَتْهُمُ · rhetoric]
- 7:91/q01/p1 It connects an immediate punishment to the people's preceding corruption and leaders' warning. — Jishumī calls it a connective fāʾ and the punishment the answer to grave corruption; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it marks immediate succession to the chiefs' warning. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:91/q02 What does al-rajfah mean here?  [ٱلرَّجْفَةُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether rajfah names the physical earthquake, the accompanying sound, a broader punishment, or the shaking's effects.
- 7:91/q02/p1 A violent earthquake or trembling of the earth. — Al-Zajjāj calls it severe; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says it makes a person shake and convulse; al-Rāzī calls it deadly and says it completed the earlier severe punishment. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī compare the Qur'anic cry; Ālūsī reports al-Kalbī. Asad adduces volcanic and earthquake traces in the region of Madyan, adjoining Thamūd's volcanic ḥarra. Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly keeps the earthquake alongside lightning. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي); ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 7:91/q02/p2 A cry or blast. — Mujāhid glosses the quake as a cry; al-Qurṭubī reports the blast as another explanation beside earthquake. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 7:91/q02/p3 Punishment, without specifying its physical form. — Muqātil gives this broad gloss. Iṣlāḥī observes that the identical wording in Ṣāliḥ's story, 7:78, does not itself specify the nature of the punishment. [2 notes: MUQATIL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:91/q02/p4 A tremendous shaking of hearts or dwellings. — Al-Biqāʿī allows either object of the shaking and either causal order between it and the cry. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:91/q03 What happened when the punishment struck?  [فَأَخَذَتْهُمُ ٱلرَّجْفَةُ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on the order and agents of destruction: fire, cry, earthquake, lightning, withheld wind, or a falling mountain.
- 7:91/q03/p1 Heat drove them beneath a cooling cloud, which burned them as the earth shook. — Ibn ʿAbbās and others describe unbearable heat, useless shelter or caves, a cool or fragrant cloud, then fire and earthquake, leaving ash or fried-locust-like bodies. Al-Baghawī specifies a gate of Hell; Bursawī's Ibn ʿAbbās report also includes Gabriel's cry. Study Quran adds a terrible cry. Al-Ālūsī explicitly places this report in Midian. [8 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس وغيره من المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI(ابن عباس وغيره من المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN]
- 7:91/q03/p2 After prolonged defiance and severe heat, a pleasant breeze and cloud drew them together; the cloud closed over and killed them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah's account says the heat came from a gate of Hell and that the cloud itself closed over them; it does not give the same ash-and-earthquake ending. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 7:91/q03/p3 Gabriel shouted; mountains and earth shook and the ground cast the lifeless people down. — Al-Durr describes the shout, the flight of souls, and the trembling ground. Muqātil also says they were dead and extinguished in their village after Gabriel's cry; Abū ʿAbdillāh reports a single fatal cry. [3 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI(أبو عبد الله ع); DURR-FULL]
- 7:91/q03/p4 Wind was withheld for seven days, then intense heat was imposed. — Al-Baghawī gives this as another account; Abū Ḥayyān likewise reports a seven-day lack of wind before heat. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 7:91/q03/p5 After seven days of heat or wind, they sheltered beneath a mountain with water, which fell on them. — Yazīd al-Jurayrī's reports vary: al-Baghawī says heat afflicted them seven days; Abū Ḥayyān says wind was sent seven days. Both locate a distant mountain with springs and streams. Al-Baghawī connects its fall with 26:189. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(يزيد الجريري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(يزيد الجريري)]
- 7:91/q03/p6 Lightning from a cloud struck Midian. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies this with the day of the shade in 26:189 and later combines the lightning with an earthquake. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 7:91/q03/p7 They burned beneath the cloud and became ash-like bodies on the ground. — Bursawī separately reports this concise account without specifying the preceding heat or quake. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 7:91/q04 How do the quake, cry, and Day of the Canopy accounts relate?  [ٱلرَّجْفَةُ · links]
turns on: Whether the Qur'anic descriptions are concurrent elements or causes of one disaster, or belong to distinct communities; al-Ālūsī tests the latter against 11:94.
- 7:91/q04/p1 The punishments happened together to the same people. — Ibn Kathīr links Hūd's blast (11:94), the mockery of Shuʿayb's prayer (11:87), their demand for a falling piece of heaven (26:187), and the day of shade (26:189). Ibn ʿAṭiyyah allows simultaneous cloud and earthquake. Iṣlāḥī says Hūd 11:94 supplies a shout and thunder, while 26:189 describes a canopy looking from afar like a column or mountain of dust or smoke; he treats the ḥāṣib as combining rajfah, ṣayḥah and ẓullah. Study Quran's report combines fire, cry, and quake. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:91/q04/p2 The cry preceded the quake, so the two descriptions name stages or causes of one event. — Al-Bayḍāwī cites al-Ḥijr 15:73 as calling it a cry and suggests onset; Bursawī cites Hūd's cry and calls it near versus remote cause. Al-Ālūsī makes the same suggestion from 11:94. Al-Biqāʿī allows the quake to result from the cry, or the reverse. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:91/q04/p3 The quake was an earthquake, and the cry was the thunderbolt accompanying lightning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues from Hūd 11:94 that the sound was a splitting burst from one thing launched against another, rather than the impact of an earthquake; he prefers simultaneous earthquake and lightning, consistent with erased traces. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 7:91/q04/p4 Different groups of Shuʿayb's people suffered the earthquake and the canopy. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah offers separate groups as an alternative to simultaneous events; al-Ṭabrisī reports a two-peoples version, one destroyed by quake and one by canopy; Abū Ḥayyān transmits Ibn ʿAṭiyyah. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 7:91/q04/p5 The accounts give distinct punishments to Midian and the people of the Thicket. — Qatāda assigns the cry of Gabriel to Midian and the canopy to the Thicket, as do ʿIkrima and al-Suddī, who say Shuʿayb was sent twice. Some reported by al-Ālūsī instead assign earthquake to Midian and cry to al-Aykah; he says this works only if Midian did not also suffer the cry. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة والسدي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 7:91/q05 Were Midian and the people of al-Aykah one people or two?  [ٱلرَّجْفَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether Shuʿayb's mission addressed one community or two, and whether a single people could receive both the cry and the quake.
- 7:91/q05/p1 Two peoples received Shuʿayb, with different modes of destruction. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports this possibility; Qatāda says the Thicket had the shade and Midian Gabriel's shout. ʿIkrima and al-Suddī say Shuʿayb was sent twice. Al-Ṭabrisī gives quake to one and canopy to the other. Al-Ālūsī notes that the alternate Midian-quake/al-Aykah-cry assignment must account for 11:94's cry for Midian. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(عكرمة والسدي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 7:91/q05/p2 They were one people described under both names. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says the reports differ and includes the one-people account; his simultaneous-earthquake-and-cloud alternative also permits one collective punishment. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 7:91/q06 What does aṣbaḥū indicate here?  [فَأَصْبَحُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether aṣbaḥa marks actual morning or simply entry into a state.
- 7:91/q06/p1 Morning came upon them in this state. — Jishumī says aṣbaḥa means to enter morning as amsā means to enter evening; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows that morning came upon them and points to the parallel account in 7:78. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI]
- 7:91/q06/p2 They became so, without necessarily specifying morning. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī offers this as the other sense and refers to his earlier explanation at Ṣāliḥ's parallel in 7:78. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 7:91/q07 What is meant by dārihim?  [فِى دَارِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether dār names individual homes, the collective settlement, or an unusual figurative sense.
- 7:91/q07/p1 Their dwellings or homes. — Abū al-ʿĀliyah gives this gloss; al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī describe motionless dead people in their dwellings. Al-Ṭabrisī says they lay face down there. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses that they lost even the land and possessions in which they had been established. [5 notes: THALABI(أبو العالية); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:91/q07/p2 Their town or village. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī give the town sense; Bursawī compares Hūd's plural fī diyārihim at 11:67. Muqātil places the dead in their village. Al-Biqāʿī notes a previously discussed rhetorical secret in the singular 'their abode' without spelling it out. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:91/q07/p3 Their destruction; by contrast diyārihim means their armies. — Muḥammad ibn Marwān states this unusual distinction for Qur'anic usage. [1 notes: THALABI(محمد بن مروان)]

## 7:91/q08 What does jāthimīn depict?  [جَٰثِمِينَ · meaning]
turns on: The glosses range from the resulting death and stillness to the particular bodily posture or the ash-like appearance.
- 7:91/q08/p1 They lay dead, collapsed and motionless. — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd simply glosses it 'dead'; al-Rāzī and al-Nasafī add motionlessness; al-Ālūsī points to an earlier parallel explanation. Al-Biqāʿī allows fixed stillness, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses their sudden loss of settled possession. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:91/q08/p2 They lay face down, on their faces and knees, fixed where they fell. — Al-Ṭabrisī has them face down in dwellings; al-Ḥaddādī places them under the cloud near their homes with no escape, linking this to 26:189. [2 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI(الحدادي)]
- 7:91/q08/p3 They crouched on their chests, legs folded, pressed to the ground. — Elmalılı compares sleeping rabbits or birds and says they could not move; al-Biqāʿī mentions collapse on their knees. [2 notes: ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:91/q08/p4 They lay as bodies cast to the ground, like settled ash. — Al-Zajjāj supplies the ash simile; the canopy reports also describe ash-like remains after burning. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; BURSEVI]

## 7:91/q09 Who warned of the cloud, and what did the warning say?  [* · reports]
- 7:91/q09/p1 ʿAmr ibn Jalḥāʾ of Midian warned that Shuʿayb was a messenger and the cloud signaled doom. — Al-Thaʿlabī writes ʿUmar ibn Jalḥāʾ, while Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Abū Ḥayyān have ʿAmr; the names vary trivially. Al-Ṭabarī's transmitted poem names Samīr/Simir and ʿImrān as soothsayers and al-Raqīm/al-Ruqaym as their dog, left to wander the passes. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمرو بن جلهاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري)]

## 7:91/q10 Who was Kulman, and what is reported about his death?  [* · reports]
- 7:91/q10/p1 Kulman or Kalman was a Midianite king who burned beneath the cloud and was lamented by his sister or daughter. — Al-Bajalī says the Abjad letter names belonged to kings and gives Kulman as ruler in Shuʿayb's time. The elegy says fire beneath the cloud consumed the settlement and that his death broke the mourner's support. Reports vary between sister and daughter, and between Kalmun, Kalman, Kulman and Kilmān. Al-Ālūsī suggests a believing daughter could have survived the general death, but also questions the report's chain; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah calls the royal identification speculative. [7 notes: THALABI(أبو عبد الله البجلي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبد الله البجلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبد الله البجلي,أخت كلمن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله البجلي,ابنة كلمن); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله البجلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 7:91/q11 What conduct did the punishment answer?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:91/q11/p1 The leaders' unbelief, defiance, threats and boast that Shuʿayb's followers would lose brought a fitting reversal. — Ibn Kathīr recalls their oath, their threats to expel Shuʿayb and his companions, their mockery of his prayer in 11:87 and demand in 26:187; he says they were shaken as they had shaken others and ties the answer to 26:189. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī observes that the people themselves lost the land and possessions they thought secure. Kur'an Yolu calls the elite unbelieving and oppressive and their disappearance deserved. Jishumī calls the quake the answer to grave corruption. [6 notes: JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:91/q12 How does this verse connect the chiefs' boast to the next verse?  [* · links]
- 7:91/q12/p1 It reverses their claim that Shuʿayb's followers would lose: the deniers were the ones destroyed and made losers. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this verse first leaves the victims implicit, then 7:92 names the deniers and answers the boast of 7:90. He also stresses their sudden loss of land and possessions. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ places punishment immediately after the chiefs' warning. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:91/q13 What earlier punishment narratives illuminate this one?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes compare the wording and fate with Ṣāliḥ's people or the combined calamity of Lot's people.
- 7:91/q13/p1 Ṣāliḥ's people are described with the same wording. — Ibn ʿĀshūr points to their punishment at 7:78; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī points back to its aṣbaḥa explanation. Iṣlāḥī says the phrase is identical and leaves the physical nature unspecified; Study Quran says Midian's destruction matches Thamūd's. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:91/q13/p2 Lot's nearby people suffered a comparable combined punishment. — Iṣlāḥī connects Shuʿayb's warning at Hūd 11:89 with Lot, calls Lot's punishment a stone-hurling wind, and says its ḥāṣib combines rajfah, ṣayḥah and ẓullah; he says both nearby peoples meet the same fate. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 7:91/q14 How does this destruction fit the sūra's sequence of messenger stories?  [* · links]
- 7:91/q14/p1 It concludes the series of rejected messengers and destroyed oppressors in 7:59–92 or 7:93. — Iṣlāḥī frames 7:59–92 as the continuing promised messenger mission and says Section VI uses Arabian nations' histories to warn Quraysh of judgment after authority, injustice, rejection and respite. He says the nations were known around Arabia and the accounts are historically ordered, beginning with Noah. Kur'an Yolu calls the five prior prophet accounts concise lessons in calls, responses and disasters, not history for its own sake. Study Quran says this narrative recounts destruction after rejection. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:91/q15 What lesson does the destruction carry for later peoples?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:91/q15/p1 After a messenger comes, rejecting reform brings destruction; Quraysh should recognize the warning and impending judgment. — Iṣlāḥī calls this God's law for every nation, says Quraysh knew vague traditions but missed their moral lessons, and sees the histories of once powerful unjust nations as Quraysh's own warning. Kur'an Yolu says calamities and hardships in the ensuing verses aim at reform and that Shuʿayb had done all he could to rescue his people, leaving no reason to pity them. The elite's deserved catastrophe is another example. [8 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 7:91/q16 How does the destructive sign compare with signs in Muhammad's time?  [* · interpretation]
- 7:91/q16/p1 The same sort of frightening angelic force accompanied Muhammad, the prophet of mercy, as a means of salvation. — Al-Biqāʿī compares reports of angels at Ḥunayn, unbelievers hearing a pebble-like sound within themselves, and an enemy feeling terror a month's journey in front and behind; here it destroyed, while with Muhammad it saved. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:91/q17 What inward meaning is given to their crouching death?  [جَٰثِمِينَ · inward]
- 7:91/q17/p1 Their bodily collapse mirrors spirits squatting in bodily abodes after stubbornness made truth look false and success look like loss. — Bursawī reports this as the Najmiyya interpretation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
