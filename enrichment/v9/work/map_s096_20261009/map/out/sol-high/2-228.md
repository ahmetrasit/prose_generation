# 2:228: 35 questions

## 2:228/q01 How does this verse relate to the preceding divorce discussion?  [* · links]
- 2:228/q01/p1 It follows the īlāʾ discussion by stating the waiting term after actual divorce. — Al-Tabari says an īlāʾ oath is not divorce and the term starts only after a divorce decision; Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan explain the placement after 2:226–227. Ibn Ashur links equal rights to the possibility that īlāʾ harms a wife. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI]

## 2:228/q02 What does 'divorced women' mean?  [وَٱلْمُطَلَّقَٰتُ · meaning]
- 2:228/q02/p1 Women released from the marital bond. — Tabari, Mawardi, Wahidi, Jishumi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Tabataba'i trace ṭalāq to untying or release; divorce differs from ṭalq for childbirth. Zajjaj and Sibawayh discuss the feminine forms ṭāliq and ṭāliqa. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); JISHUMI; MAWARDI-FULL; SIBAWAYH; TABATABAI; ZAJJAJ(سيبويه وأصحابه,قوم)]

## 2:228/q03 To whom does the three-cycle rule apply?  [وَٱلْمُطَلَّقَٰتُ · law]
turns on: Whether the general expression was originally universal and later abrogated, or was always specified by the nature of cycles and other texts.
- 2:228/q03/p1 Consummated, menstruating, nonpregnant divorcees, usually free women. — Tabari, Razi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Tabarsi, Ibn Kathir and later writers exempt unconsummated divorce by 33:49, pregnancy by childbirth under 65:4, and young or postmenopausal women by months. Most give enslaved women two cycles. [61 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الحنفية,الشافعية); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN; JISHUMI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 2:228/q03/p2 It first covered every divorced woman, and later verses abrogated exceptions. — Ibn Abbas and Qatada are reported for this account; Jishumi favors abrogation because he rejects delayed specification. Some sources extend the claim to the old unlimited revocation right. [7 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، وقتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، الضحاك وآخرون,قوم); TABRISI]
- 2:228/q03/p3 Its general wording remains operative and later evidence specifies cases. — Ibn al-Jawzi prefers specification; Abu Hayyan calls the abrogation account weak; Tabarsi and al-Itqan report specified exceptions. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قوم); TABRISI]

## 2:228/q04 What is the grammatical and rhetorical force of 'they wait'?  [يَتَرَبَّصْنَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether a declarative verb conveys command, reports a legal fact, or has an omitted imperative or governing noun.
- 2:228/q04/p1 A declarative sentence functions as an emphatic command. — Tha'labi, Wahidi, Razi, Baydawi, Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan, Burhan and others compare the command phrased as a report at 2:233. The nominal opening and apparent certainty strengthen prompt compliance. [44 notes: THALABI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ITQAN(ابن العربي); JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أهل اللسان); RAZI-FULL(الشاعر,صاحب الكشاف,عبد القاهر الجرجاني); SAMIN-DURR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)]
- 2:228/q04/p2 It literally reports the legal rule, so waiting counts without a conscious intention. — Ibn al-Arabi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi preserve the statement's declarative force; a woman who does not wait disobeys the legal rule rather than falsifying the report. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القرطبي)]
- 2:228/q04/p3 An imperative lām is omitted: 'let them wait'. — Kufan grammarians and Abu Zayd supply li-yatarabbaṣna; Wahidi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samin transmit it. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكوفيون); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون)]
- 2:228/q04/p4 A governing noun or clause is elided: 'God's rule for them is that they wait'. — Wahidi transmits the estimate; Abu Hayyan and Samin judge a heavier version of the ellipsis remote. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:228/q05 What does waiting require and why?  [يَتَرَبَّصْنَ · law]
- 2:228/q05/p1 She must restrain herself from remarriage until the appointed period ends. — Tabari glosses tarabbus as abstaining from marriage. Biqa'i includes avoiding signals of availability; Tabataba'i stresses preservation of lineage; Asad and Kuranyolu add time for reconsideration. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; MAWARDI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 2:228/q06 What does 'with themselves' add or mean?  [بِأَنفُسِهِنَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether bāʾ gives cause or emphasis and whether the phrase describes desire or personal agency.
- 2:228/q06/p1 It urges women to restrain their own desire and wait personally. — Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqa'i, Abu Hayyan and Alusi read an exhortation to master longing for marriage; Alusi contrasts 2:226 and sees anfus as hinting divorce should be rare. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:228/q06/p2 The bāʾ marks cause: they wait for or with regard to themselves. — Abu Hayyan and Samin attach the phrase to the waiting verb; Bursevi says they hold themselves in a state of waiting. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:228/q06/p3 The bāʾ is additional emphasis: they themselves wait. — Abu Hayyan and Samin compare emphatic nafs constructions and debate the need for an explicit detached pronoun. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:228/q07 How are 'three qurūʾ' construed grammatically?  [ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the numeral is a temporal accusative or the verb's direct object.
- 2:228/q07/p1 An accusative duration: they wait a time of three cycles. — Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi parse it as duration, often supplying an omitted object such as marriage or the passage of time. [10 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:228/q07/p2 A direct object: they wait for three cycles to pass. — Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Samin also report this parse; Nahhas paraphrases with counting their waiting period. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:228/q08 What is the Arabic meaning or root of qurʾ?  [ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the two uses are homonyms, or one shared meaning grounded in time, gathering, or transition.
- 2:228/q08/p1 It denotes a recurrent appointed time, covering menstruation and purity. — Ibn Qutayba, Tabari, Abu Amr, Mawardi and others cite stars rising or setting, winds in season and a due need; Hudhali poetry supports this temporal root. - 2:228/q08/p2 It means gathering, applicable to blood collected during purity or menstruation. — Zajjaj, Tha'labi, Mawardi, Razi and Tabarsi cite the womb gathering a fetus, the Qur'an gathering letters and water in a basin; Amr ibn Kulthum's poem supports gathering. Ibn Abd al-Barr objects that the water term lacks hamza. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); JISHUMI(الأصمعي والأخفش والفراء والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL(الأصمعي، الأخفش، الكسائي، الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر بن عبد البر,قوم); RAZI-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(عمرو بن كلثوم); TABRISI(عمرو بن كلثوم); THALABI(الزجاج,عمرو بن كلثوم,قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)]
- 2:228/q08/p3 It denotes a transition or boundary between the two states. — Abu Ubayda, Shafi'i, Raghib, Harali, Baydawi and others discuss a move between purity and bleeding; Jishumi calls Abu Ubayda's derivation weak. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشافعي); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب,الشافعي); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيد); SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:228/q08/p4 It can mean cessation of menses, the interval between two menses, or a combined cycle. — Akhfash, Abu Hayyan, Qurtubi and Samin transmit these narrower lexical definitions. [2 notes: AKHFASH(بعضهم)]
- 2:228/q08/p5 Arabic uses it for both menstruation and purity as opposite senses. — Yunus, Abu Amr, Abu Ubayd and many lexicographers treat it as a contronym; menstruation is witnessed by the Prophetic prayer report and purity by al-A'sha's campaign poem. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; SAMIN-DURR(الأصمعي,الأعشى,الشاعر,جمهور أهل اللسان); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأعشى)]

## 2:228/q10 When exactly does the waiting period end?  [ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether qurūʾ means purity or menses, whether an initial partial purity counts, and whether bleeding, cessation, bath, prayer or verified blood controls release.
- 2:228/q10/p1 At the onset of the third menstruation, counting the purity at divorce. — Aisha, Zayd, Ibn Umar, Malik and purity-view jurists count even a remaining moment of a lawful purity. Ashhab insists a spot be confirmed as menses; inheritance and revocation end at release. [29 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عمر وزيد بن ثابت,زيد بن ثابت,زيد بن ثابت وعلي بن أبي طالب,زيد بن ثابت ومعاوية بن أبي سفيان وابن عمر,عائشة,عائشة وزيد بن ثابت,عبد الله بن عمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أشهب,زيد، وابن عمر، وعائشة,مالك، والشافعي، وأحمد، وداود); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أشهب,ابن القاسم ومالك,زيد بن ثابت وغيره,عائشة وابن عمر ومالك وسليمان بن يسار); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,أشهب,ابن القاسم ومالك وجمهور أصحابه والشافعي وعلماء المدينة,الشافعي); WAHIDI-BASIT(من قال إن القرء الأطهار)]
- 2:228/q10/p2 Only after three complete purities, at the fourth menstruation. — Al-Zuhri discards the remainder of the first purity; Tabari insists on three complete purity units. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن شهاب); FARISI-HUJJA; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر,الزهري)]
- 2:228/q10/p3 After the third menstruation ends and she bathes or prayer becomes lawful. — Umar, Ibn Masud, Ali, Ubay and Abu Musa are cited; some retain revocation and inheritance until washing. Sharik would retain it despite a very long delay. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو قلابة وقتادة وأبو موسى الأشعري,أبي بن كعب وعثمان بن عفان,ابن مسعود وعمر بن الخطاب,الحسن وأبو موسى الأشعري,السدي,عبد الرحمن بن مهدي,عبد الله بن مسعود وعمر بن الخطاب,علي بن أبي طالب,عمر بن الخطاب,عمر بن الخطاب وأبو موسى الأشعري,عمر بن الخطاب وعبد الله بن مسعود,معبد الجهني,يونس بن جبير وعمر بن الخطاب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة,علي، وابن مسعود، وأبو موسى، وغيرهم من الصحابة); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,شريك); STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(من قال إن القرء حيض)]
- 2:228/q10/p4 When third menstrual bleeding stops, before ritual bathing. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr and others separate menstrual cessation from washing; Hanafi subcases depend on maximum or minimum bleeding, tayammum and prayer time. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وغيره); STUDYQURAN]
- 2:228/q10/p5 Revocation ends at third bleeding, but remarriage waits until the bath. — Ishaq ibn Rahawayh and a weakly reported Ibn Abbas view distinguish the two legal consequences; Qurtubi objects from 2:234. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(إسحاق بن راهويه,ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN]

## 2:228/q11 Why use qurūʾ, a plural of abundance, after three?  [ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ordinary morphology, many divorcees, or an omitted partitive explains the form.
- 2:228/q11/p1 Arabic permits the abundant plural instead of the expected paucity form. — Sibawayh attests 'three qurūʾ'; Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Alusi cite interchange or frequency. Ibn Malik calls the alternative anomalous. [63 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURHAN(أهل المعاني,ابن مالك,الحريري,المبرد); ITQAN; JISHUMI(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأصمعي)]

## 2:228/q12 What readings and pause forms are reported for qurūʾ?  [ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍۢ · readings]

- 2:228/q12/p2 Nāfiʿ or al-Zuhrī reads a doubled wāw without hamza. — The hamza is eased to wāw and assimilated; Abu Hayyan and Samin explain the phonology. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(نافع); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزهري); SAMIN-DURR(الزهري)]
- 2:228/q12/p3 Al-Ḥasan reads a light, unhamzated generic form. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Samin give the qāf vowel and tanwīn or light wāw details; numeral annexation indicates plurality. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR(الحسن)]

## 2:228/q13 Why is the numeral form feminine?  [ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether grammatical gender merely agrees with the masculine singular or favors one substantive interpretation.
- 2:228/q13/p1 It agrees with grammatically masculine qurʾ and proves no semantic choice. — Nahhas, Jishumi and Razi give the gender rule; Nahhas compares one thing bearing masculine and feminine names. [2 notes: NAHHAS]
- 2:228/q13/p2 It favors purity over the feminine word for menstruation. — Biqa'i makes the numeral a supporting argument, with al-Asbahani's distinct plural claim. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:228/q14 Which waiting period applies to an enslaved woman?  [ثَلَٰثَةَ قُرُوٓءٍۢ · law]

## 2:228/q16 What interests does the concealment ban protect?  [وَلَا يَحِلُّ لَهُنَّ أَن يَكْتُمْنَ · interpretation]
- 2:228/q16/p1 The husband's revocation right and the accuracy of the waiting period. — Ibn Abbas and many interpreters explain that false bleeding or pregnancy claims can shorten or prolong the term, defeat revocation, or impose unowed maintenance. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الزجاج,قتادة,قيل); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس,قتادة)]
- 2:228/q16/p2 The child's lineage and rights. — Qatada describes pre-Islamic women assigning a concealed child to a new husband; the prohibition protects paternity. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI(قتادة); QUSHAYRI]
- 2:228/q16/p3 Truthful disclosure of facts only the woman ordinarily knows. — Tabari, Wahidi, Razi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Kathir trust the woman's plausible report; Sulayman says people are not told to inspect private parts. ## 2:228/q17 When is a woman's claimed completion of the waiting period believed?  [وَلَا يَحِلُّ لَهُنَّ أَن يَكْتُمْنَ · law]
turns on: How far her privileged knowledge extends when the claimed duration is unusually short.
- 2:228/q17/p1 Her report is accepted if the time is plausibly sufficient. — Razi, Wahidi, Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan accept her own report about menstruation or miscarriage; Malik distinguishes ordinary from exceptional duration and sometimes requires corroborating women. - 2:228/q17/p3 A ten-day claim of three periods is not accepted absent a formed miscarriage. — Ibn al-Mundhir reports this boundary; Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan transmit it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المنذر)]

## 2:228/q18 Why invoke belief in God and the Last Day?  [إِن كُنَّ يُؤْمِنَّ بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْءَاخِرِ · interpretation]
- 2:228/q18/p1 It is a severe moral warning, not a legal exemption for unbelievers. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Razi, Tabarsi, Qurtubi and Alusi compare saying that a believer should not oppress; genuine faith and fear of reckoning should prevent betrayal. ## 2:228/q20 How is buʿūlatuhunna read?  [وَبُعُولَتُهُنَّ · readings]

## 2:228/q22 What does 'more entitled' compare or imply?  [أَحَقُّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it expresses priority over a rival or is a noncomparative intensive.
- 2:228/q22/p1 The first husband has priority over other suitors or over her postwaiting freedom. — Razi and Qurtubi explain that her control of herself begins after the waiting period; Tabataba'i compares other suitors; Kuranyolu cites family ties and shared history. - 2:228/q22/p2 It means simply entitled, since no rival can revoke during the term. — Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Alusi treat the comparative as emphasis: another suitor has no revocation right and she cannot veto a valid return. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:228/q22/p3 It can mean revocation is more beloved to God than divorce. — Alusi records this gloss and the report calling divorce the most detested lawful act. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 2:228/q24 Is intercourse permitted with a revocably divorced wife before revocation?  [بِرَدِّهِنَّ · law]

- 2:228/q24/p2 Yes; marriage rulings remain and return figuratively reverses impending separation. — Abu Hanifa and al-Layth say divorce reduces the remaining count but current marriage remains; Nasafi and Bursevi cite calling them husbands. - 2:228/q24/p3 Conduct such as seclusion, adornment, entry and travel is regulated differently by schools. — Malik restricts private access; Abu Hanifa allows seeing and adornment; Shafi'i treats her like an irrevocably divorced woman until spoken revocation. Qurtubi and Abu Hayyan transmit the detailed disagreements. ## 2:228/q25 To what does 'during that' refer?  [فِى ذَٰلِكَ · referent]

- 2:228/q25/p1 The waiting period of cycles, months or pregnancy. — Mujahid, Qatada, Tabari, Zajjaj, Baydawi and others say the right lasts until the term ends; Samin and Alusi discuss attachment to 'entitled' or 'return'. ## 2:228/q26 What does intending reconciliation require?  [إِنْ أَرَادُوٓا۟ إِصْلَٰحًۭا · law]

- 2:228/q26/p1 The husband should repair the estrangement and not prolong distress. — Tabari, Wahidi, Qushayri, Mawrdi and others cite the abusive practice of returning near the end of a term only to divorce again; 2:231 warns against harm. - 2:228/q26/p2 A harmful return is sinful but legally effective. — Tabari, Jishumi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Alusi and others distinguish inward intent from outward effect; Ibn al-Jawzi reasons 2:231 would not threaten an invalid act. - 2:228/q26/p3 Without sincere reform, return is inwardly illicit. — Biqa'i calls such a hidden motive inwardly adulterous, while leaving outward legal judgment to God. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 2:228/q27 What reciprocal rights do spouses have?  [وَلَهُنَّ مِثْلُ ٱلَّذِى عَلَيْهِنَّ · interpretation]

- 2:228/q27/p1 Each owes enforceable good treatment, though their duties need not be identical. — Muqatil, Tabari, Zajjaj, Baydawi, Nasafi, Tabarsi and later writers include maintenance, clothing, companionship, fidelity, obedience and freedom from harm; Prophetic reports forbid striking the face or insulting a wife. ## 2:228/q28 What is maʿrūf as the measure of rights?  [بِٱلْمَعْرُوفِ · meaning]
- 2:228/q28/p1 Good conduct recognized by revelation, sound reason, custom and noble character. — Jishumi, Harali, Tabataba'i and Kuranyolu give these tests. Kuranyolu says unfixed household roles may change with socially accepted practice while fixed texts remain. [3 notes: TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 2:228/q30 What does daraja mean as a word?  [وَلِلرِّجَالِ عَلَيْهِنَّ دَرَجَةٌۭ · meaning]

## 2:228/q31 What does the closing pair 'Mighty, Wise' teach?  [وَٱللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ · interpretation]

- 2:228/q31/p1 God can punish violations and legislates these duties wisely. — Tabari links the closing to commands from 2:221 onward; Zajjaj, Baydawi, Ibn Kathir, Alusi and others stress irresistible rule and wisdom in law. ## 2:228/q32 What occasion or historical practice is reported behind the verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reports identify different people or prior practices.
- 2:228/q32/p1 Asmāʾ bint Yazīd was the first divorcee for whom a waiting period was revealed. — Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Kathir, Durr and Tabataba'i report her divorce when no term existed; Ibn Kathir calls the chain strange. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أسماء بنت يزيد); DURR-FULL(أسماء بنت يزيد بن السكن الأنصارية,قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أسماء بنت يزيد بن السكن الأنصارية); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,أسماء بنت يزيد بن السكن الأنصارية,ربيعة الرأي)]
- 2:228/q32/p2 An Ismāʿīl or Mālik of Ghifār or the Anṣār reclaimed a pregnant wife who later died with her child. — Muqatil and Jishumi give variants of the man and wife and a later death; Durr reports the Ghifar version. [5 notes: MUQATIL; DURR-FULL(مقاتل بن حيان); JISHUMI(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(رجل من أشجع)]
- 2:228/q32/p3 Women concealed pregnancy to assign children to later husbands. — Qatada and other reports describe the pre-Islamic practice as prompting the ban. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); TAB-FULL]
- 2:228/q32/p4 Husbands repeatedly divorced and revoked near the term's end to harm wives. — Wahidi, Jishumi, Tabarsi, Ibn al-Jawzi and others describe the abusive cycle. [2 notes: JISHUMI(القاضي,مقاتل والكلبي)]

## 2:228/q33 What inward lesson is drawn from the waiting and return?  [* · inward]
- 2:228/q33/p1 The interval figures God's patience with servants and the possibility of repentance. — Bursevi says God delays turning away so love and repentance may return, and calls seekers to remain at His threshold; Study Quran applies reciprocal rights to soul and spirit. [4 notes: BURSEVI(أبو عبد الله الشيرازي); STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 2:228/q34 What broader social setting is used to explain the reciprocal-rights clause?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: How women's prior treatment and natural or social roles bear on the verse.
- 2:228/q34/p1 Earlier societies often denied women independent rights; Islam restored their human standing. — Tabataba'i surveys China, India, Egypt, Iran, Greece, Rome and pre-Islamic Arabia, then invokes 49:13, 3:195 and other verses for equal moral accountability and independent property. Kuranyolu cites Umar's recollection of change. [61 notes: TABATABAI(الآخر,القائل); BURSEVI(ابن عباس,السعدي,بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Ömer)]
- 2:228/q34/p2 Equal human worth coexists with differing family roles and responsibilities. — Tabataba'i argues from fitra, procreation, maintenance, inheritance and social capacity; Kuranyolu stresses balance and role-based duties while rejecting lesser humanity or inherent intellectual inferiority. [40 notes: TABATABAI(الرأي العام عندهم,معاوية)]
- 2:228/q34/p3 The verse's rights can adapt in details governed by maʿrūf. — Kuranyolu cites the Prophet's allocation of household and outside tasks to Fatima and Ali, and jurists who regard it as contingent rather than permanent. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
