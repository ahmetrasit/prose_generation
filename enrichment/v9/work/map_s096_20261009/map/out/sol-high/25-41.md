# 25:41: 24 questions

## 25:41/q01 Who sees and mocks the Prophet?  [رَأَوْكَ يَتَّخِذُونَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural denotes the unbelievers generally or a particular taunter and those with him.
- 25:41/q01/p1 The disbelievers or polytheists, especially the Meccan Quraysh, mock Muhammad. — Yaḥyā, al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identify the unbelievers; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the plural to those previously mentioned. Al-Bursawī names Quraysh; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the plural depicts groups in gatherings and meeting places. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 25:41/q01/p2 Abū Jahl, sometimes with companions or his assembly, is singled out as the speaker. — Muqātil, al-Baghawī, al-Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī and others transmit the attribution. Al-Jishumī and al-Baghawī place him passing the Prophet with companions; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them the people of his assembly. Abū Ḥayyān says the plural may reflect his group or magnify the ugliness of the act. [9 notes: MUQATIL(أبو جهل); JISHUMI(أبو جهل بن هشام); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جهل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جهل ومن معه); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو جهل وأهل ناديه)]

## 25:41/q02 What does taking the Prophet only as mockery describe?  [إِن يَتَّخِذُونَكَ إِلَّا هُزُوًا · interpretation]
- 25:41/q02/p1 They habitually make Muhammad an object of ridicule and belittle him whenever they see him. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and others paraphrase the construction as mocking him or treating him as one mocked. Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Bursawī say this goes beyond refusing belief or raising doubts. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the restriction excludes reflection on his words and call; the taunt is exchanged aloud. Ibn Kathīr compares 21:36 and cites 13:32 for earlier mocked messengers. [25 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 25:41/q03 How does the negation and exception construction work?  [إِن يَتَّخِذُونَكَ إِلَّا · grammar]
- 25:41/q03/p1 In negates the verb; the exception restricts their response to ridicule. — Al-Zamakhsharī contrasts this negative in with the lightened inna in the next verse, distinguished by its lām; al-Rāzī repeats him and al-Biqāʿī confirms the following inna. Al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Bursawī identify negation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the restriction excludes any thoughtful attention to the call. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:41/q04 What answers the conditional when they see you?  [وَإِذَا رَأَوْكَ إِن يَتَّخِذُونَكَ إِلَّا هُزُوًا أَهَٰذَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the negative clause itself completes idhā or interrupts before an understood speech response.
- 25:41/q04/p1 The negative clause, they take you only as mockery, is the response. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this parse. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn say idhā permits a negative response with in, mā or lā without fāʾ; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls idhā temporal with conditional force. Al-Qurṭubī also reports the parse. Al-Samīn makes the following implied saying circumstantial. [8 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 25:41/q04/p2 An omitted they said or say introduces the quoted question as the response; the mockery clause is parenthetical. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give this construction; al-Qurṭubī, al-Naḥḥās and al-Ālūsī report it. Abū Ḥayyān specifically describes the mockery clause as between idhā and its answer. [8 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 25:41/q05 What does huzʾ mean here?  [هُزُوًا · meaning]
- 25:41/q05/p1 It is mockery or derision, with the verbal noun applied to the person mocked. — Al-Jishumī defines mockery as displaying what differs from one's inward state to belittle someone. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives huzʾ from hazaʾa bihi and cites 2:67. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow object or place of ridicule, or a mocked person; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a verbal noun denoting its target. [8 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 25:41/q06 How is huzʾan read?  [هُزُوًا · readings]
turns on: Whether the medial vowel is pronounced.
- 25:41/q06/p1 The root-final hamza is retained and written explicitly as huzuʾan. — Qirāʾāt-Er records the explicit hamza. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 25:41/q06/p2 With sukūn on zāy, huzʾan drops the medial vowel into a compressed pronunciation. — Qirāʾāt-Er describes the result as one syllable. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 25:41/q07 Why is the verbal noun huzʾ used for the Prophet?  [هُزُوًا · rhetoric]
- 25:41/q07/p1 Making him mockery itself intensifies their treatment of him as wholly an object of ridicule. — Al-Biqāʿī stresses the extreme mockery despite his truthfulness, noble conduct and signs. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the verbal noun assigns him entirely to being mocked. Al-Ālūsī allows a verbal noun used passively for emphasis or an omitted genitive. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:41/q08 How is the mockers' quoted question introduced?  [أَهَٰذَا ٱلَّذِى بَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ رَسُولًا · grammar]
- 25:41/q08/p1 An omitted verb of saying introduces their quoted taunt. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī supply the verb; al-Ālūsī allows it to be circumstantial or a separate answer. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the quoted speech spells out the verbal form of their ridicule. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:41/q09 What does their question about God's messenger convey?  [أَهَٰذَا ٱلَّذِى بَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ رَسُولًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the clause presents God's sending as feigned assent or has an implied 'in their claim.'
- 25:41/q09/p1 It sarcastically denies Muhammad's mission and belittles him while feigning acceptance that God sent him. — Al-Jishumī says they use the title messenger despite inward rejection. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī say embedding God's sending as if conceded heightens sarcasm; direct denial or saying he merely claims it would be less pointed. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Itqān and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify the interrogative as derisive. Al-Burhān lists it as belittlement. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ(مشركي مكة); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)+]
- 25:41/q09/p2 Their wording can be understood as the one whom God allegedly sent, with 'in their claim' supplied. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative but prefers the reading that takes their feigned assertion at face value as part of the taunt. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 25:41/q10 What does the demonstrative hādhā add?  [أَهَٰذَا · rhetoric]
- 25:41/q10/p1 Pointing to this person signals contempt and belittlement. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr note contempt. Al-Ālūsī compares an expression about Ibn ʿUmar; al-Itqān notes nearby demonstratives can disparage a person and compares 21:36. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 25:41/q11 What object is understood after baʿatha?  [ٱلَّذِى بَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
- 25:41/q11/p1 An omitted object pronoun returns to alladhī: the one whom God sent. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows shortening in a relative clause; al-Ṭabrisī attributes the omission to the clause's length. Al-Burhān and al-Itqān cite the rule for clear relative referents; al-Itqān compares 4:95 and 2:48 and says similar omissions occur in adjective, predicate and circumstantial clauses. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL]

## 25:41/q12 What is the grammatical function of rasūlan?  [رَسُولًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether rasūlan directly describes the sent person or denotes a message through another construction.
- 25:41/q12/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial description of the omitted object: God sent him as messenger. — Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī attach it to the understood pronoun. Al-Samīn says this adjective functioning as a circumstantial accusative is the normal parse and finds the other parse strained. [6 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 25:41/q12/p2 It can be a verbal noun, meaning message or possessor of a message. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say baʿatha means dispatched and rasūl can mean message. Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies an omitted genitive, 'possessor of a message'; al-Samīn also considers an intensive or passive-agent sense but calls these routes strained, while al-Ālūsī finds the genitive unnecessary. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]

## 25:41/q13 How is the quoted nominal question parsed?  [أَهَٰذَا ٱلَّذِى بَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ رَسُولًا · grammar]
- 25:41/q13/p1 A-hādhā is the subject, alladhī its predicate, and God the nominative subject of baʿatha in the relative clause. — Al-Qurṭubī states this iʿrāb. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 25:41/q14 To whom is the messenger said to have been sent?  [بَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ رَسُولًا · interpretation]
- 25:41/q14/p1 He was sent to the mockers, or from among God's creatures; their question frames this in ridicule. — Al-Zajjāj, both al-Wāḥidī notes and al-Bursawī supply 'to us/them'; al-Ṭabarī says 'from among His creation.' Al-Bursawī adds that the sending establishes proof against them. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ(مشركي مكة); BURSEVI]

## 25:41/q15 What reports connect Abū Jahl with the passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports recount different utterances and occasions involving Abū Jahl.
- 25:41/q15/p1 He mocked Muhammad by asking whether this was the messenger God sent, sometimes while passing with companions. — Muqātil, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī connect the verse or its revelation to Abū Jahl; al-Jishumī and al-Baghawī specify passing with companions, and Ibn ʿĀshūr names his assembly. [8 notes: MUQATIL(أبو جهل); JISHUMI(أبو جهل بن هشام); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جهل); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جهل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جهل ومن معه); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو جهل وأهل ناديه)]
- 25:41/q15/p2 Abū Jahl said to Abū Sufyān, 'This is the prophet of Banū ʿAbd Manāf,' contemptuously. — Al-Bursawī reports the variant wording and addressee. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو جهل)]
- 25:41/q15/p3 Abū Jahl mocked the teaching that nineteen angels guard Hell and asked whether a hundred Quraysh could overpower one. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Ibn Isḥāq's report and connects it to 74:31, which identifies the guardians as angels and their number as a test. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو جهل)]

## 25:41/q16 Why did the disbelievers think Muhammad unfit for prophethood?  [أَهَٰذَا ٱلَّذِى بَعَثَ ٱللَّهُ رَسُولًا · interpretation]
turns on: Which feature of the Prophet or his selection provoked their contempt; al-Rāzī argues the proposed targets give no valid ground.
- 25:41/q16/p1 His modest clothes, conduct, companions and care for the weak did not match their expected status and leadership. — Ibn ʿĀshūr lists the social markers. Kur'an Yolu attributes the rejection to leaders' bigotry, stubbornness and denial, and says they wanted extraordinary signs, angels alongside him or a vision of God. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 25:41/q16/p2 They resented God's choice of Muhammad and his followers over themselves. — Study Quran compares 46:11 and 6:53. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 25:41/q16/p3 Their ridicule could target his appearance. — Al-Rāzī rejects the possibility as baseless: Muhammad was the best-looking among them and claimed superiority through proof, not looks. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Rāzī's discussion. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-]
- 25:41/q16/p4 Their ridicule could target his prophetic qualities. — Al-Rāzī rejects the objection because manifest miracles established his distinction and the deniers could not challenge his proof; Abū Ḥayyān transmits this. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-]

## 25:41/q17 What does the mockery reveal about the Prophet and the mockers?  [* · interpretation]
- 25:41/q17/p1 Their contempt is unjustified despite his truthful speech, noble conduct, signs and proof. — Al-Biqāʿī says his conduct alone made him an unlikely object of ridicule, even before the signs. Al-Rāzī says he was more handsome and possessed manifest miracles; the deniers deserve ridicule for reversing the matter. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي)-]
- 25:41/q17/p2 Their speech and the scene show obstinate refusal to reflect, beyond ordinary disbelief. — Al-Rāzī describes mockery as beyond denial of prophethood. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Bursawī say they belittle him in addition to disbelief; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they never combine it with consideration of his call. Kur'an Yolu describes bigotry and stubbornness. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 25:41/q18 What does their next claim that he nearly turned them from their gods imply?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which consequence is drawn from their admission in the following verse.
- 25:41/q18/p1 It shows Muhammad's sustained and effective effort to turn them from idols. — Al-Rāzī cites his arguments, replies to doubts and endurance of abuse. Abū Ḥayyān adds his use of every means and signs; by their own admission they nearly left their religion but stubbornly clung to idol worship. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 25:41/q18/p2 Their taunt and claim together expose an inconsistent judgment of him. — Al-Rāzī divides their speech into mockery followed by a claim that he nearly diverted them (25:42): ridicule treats him as ignorant and powerless, while their near-diversion concedes strong proof and reasoning. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 25:41/q18/p3 Their inability to answer his proofs shows reliance on imitation and stubborn denial. — Al-Rāzī says their own statement supplies no objection to his arguments and indicates they were overwhelmed by them. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 25:41/q18/p4 They believed idol worship true while ignorant of the proofs, yet were called unbelievers. — Al-Rāzī uses their fear of leaving the idols to reject the Aṣḥāb al-maʿārif view that disbelief occurs only after knowledge of the proofs. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 25:41/q19 What does the passage show about human agency in denial?  [* · theology]
- 25:41/q19/p1 Denial and refusal are acts originated by people, who therefore deserve punishment. — Al-Jishumī argues the acts and refusals mentioned require a choosing agent. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 25:41/q20 How does this scene fit the surrounding passage and other prophetic mockery?  [* · links]
- 25:41/q20/p1 It follows attacks on prophethood by showing conduct that goes beyond doubt and denial. — Al-Jishumī says the passage then warns them and gives proofs of divine unity. Al-Rāzī places the scene after their doubts; Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts face-to-face abuse with earlier insults made in the Prophet's absence. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 25:41/q20/p2 It belongs to a broader pattern in which disbelievers mock messengers and face warning or punishment. — Ibn Kathīr compares 21:36 and cites 13:32, where earlier mockers were seized. Study Quran cites 43:6-7, 2:14-15, 4:140, 5:57-58, 6:5, 31:6 and 45:9,35. Iṣlāḥī says the Moses, Noah and intervening peoples' narratives console the Prophet and warn his nation; the next sūra recounts them further. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 25:41/q20/p3 The passage gathers traits matching the disbelievers' objections. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists mocking the Messenger, following desire, worshipping powerless things and refusing to prostrate. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 25:41/q21 How does the inward reading explain their failure to recognize prophethood?  [* · inward]
- 25:41/q21/p1 Outward sight and resemblance cannot disclose prophecy; it needs God-illuminated insight, which the mockers lack. — Al-Bursawī transmits the Najmiyya reading: people of the senses fail to grasp prophetic speech and mock a human who needs food and drink. His Mathnawī citation contrasts a drawn lion with a real one and honey with dung to show the gap behind apparent resemblance. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,المثنوي)]

## 25:41/q22 Which rhetorical examples are adduced in the related discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The cited material illustrates different rhetorical devices rather than rival parses of this wording.
- 25:41/q22/p1 Negation followed by affirmation sets a denial against a positive reply. — Al-Bāqillānī cites a verse about rejecting one person's words and not another's. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 25:41/q22/p2 Metonymy and indirect expression convey an unstated sense. — Al-Bāqillānī cites the red woman whose sky is fertile and land barren, as well as laḥn al-qawl. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 25:41/q22/p3 Reversal and exchange transpose corresponding elements. — Al-Bāqillānī uses al-Ḥasan's paired maxims and a poem reversing jewel and beautiful face. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 25:41/q22/p4 Iltifāt interrupts an initial syntactic course and returns to it smoothly. — Al-Bāqillānī defines the device and cites Abū Tammām's turn to his tears; Jarīr's turns to a pigeon and to tents at Dhū Ṭulūḥ; al-Aṣmaʿī's account of Jarīr suddenly invoking rain on al-bashām; al-Nābigha's parenthetical 'they lie'; and Kathīr's line on generous givers learning delay from the beloved. [7 notes: BAQILLANI(الأصمعي)]

## 25:41/q23 Which other reading does al-Ṭabrisī refer readers to?  [* · links]
- 25:41/q23/p1 He refers to his earlier discussion of a variant of 'so that they may remember' in Sūrat Banī Isrāʾīl. — The note identifies 17:41 without describing the variant. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 25:41/q24 How is the sūra's place of revelation classified in a minority view?  [* · sciences]
- 25:41/q24/p1 Most of the sūra is Madinan, but verses 68–70 are Makkan. — Study Quran reports this as a minority classification. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(A minority)]
