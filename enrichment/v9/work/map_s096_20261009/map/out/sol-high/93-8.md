# 93:8: 11 questions

## 93:8/q01 What does 'found you' convey here?  [وَوَجَدَكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'finding' describes an encounter, divine selection, or an inwardly felt reassurance.
- 93:8/q01/p1 God encountered the Prophet in a state of need and aided him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats wajada as 'encountered,' with the needy states as circumstances; the rhetorical question invites the Prophet's assent. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:8/q01/p2 Finding conveys God's choosing a rare, precious person. — Elmalılı interprets the repeated 'finding' in the three verses as divine selection (iṣṭifāʾ). Sulamī's Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says finding follows seeking and the Prophet was the sought beloved. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); ELMALILI+]
- 93:8/q01/p3 The heart verb foregrounds the Prophet's inner reassurance. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says wajada evokes the Prophet's felt state and psychological consolation amid the remembered favors. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 93:8/q02 How does ʿāʾilan describe the Prophet's need?  [عَآئِلًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names material poverty, responsibility for dependents, or spiritual or communal need.
- 93:8/q02/p1 He was poor, needy, or without property. — Abū ʿUbayda/Majāz, Ṭabarī, Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, and others cite poetry using yaʿīl/ʿāla for becoming poor; Nāḥḥās and Ibn Qutayba distinguish aʿāla, having dependents. Q 9:28 pairs ʿayla with enrichment; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes the Qur'anic pattern. Ibn ʿAmr says Arab resources were scant, although Muhammad remained noble. [55 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB(سفيان); TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); NAHHAS; MAWARDI(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL(الشاعر,الفراء,جرير); WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(الجمهور); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,ابن قتيبة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI(ابن القطاع); BIQAI-FULL; DURR(سفيان,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); DURR-FULL(سفيان,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الأخفش,الفراء); ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; ELMALILI+]
- 93:8/q02/p2 He had dependents or people whose support fell to him. — Al-Akhfash invokes a Prophetic injunction to begin with those one supports; Māwardī cites a needy traveler in Jarīr's poetry. Rāzī adduces Q 4:3 and 9:28; Qurṭubī cites Q 2:273. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says dependents can cause poverty. Ālūsī accepts this lexical sense but says Muhammad had no dependents early in life. [16 notes: THALABI(الأخفش); MAWARDI(الأخفش); MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI(جرير); ELMALILI; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 93:8/q02/p3 His need was inward dependence on God, spiritual poverty, or lack of knowledge. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ and Tustarī describe a poor or yearning soul; Qāshānī describes annihilation of ego; Sulamī's voices variously specify scripture, gnosis's rulings, or proofs. Al-Wāsiṭī warns that ordinary categories of poverty and wealth do not apply to the Prophet in the usual way. [20 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,بعضهم); THALABI(ابن عطاء); IBNATIYYA(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QASHANI; QURTUBI(ابن عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 93:8/q02/p4 The dependents are his community, needy for guidance. — Rāzī says the community depended on him and was poor through ignorance; Elmalılı says needy companions gained through his teaching. ʿIṣām al-Dīn extends the allegory to lacking a teacher and knowledge; Ālūsī calls it strained. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين)-]

## 93:8/q03 What readings of ʿāʾilan and its clause are reported?  [عَآئِلًۭا فَأَغْنَىٰ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted form and whether the codex variant ends with shelter or enrichment.
- 93:8/q03/p1 The common reading is ʿāʾilan, 'poor or needy,' followed by 'so He enriched.' — Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān identify it as the common or majority reading; Jarīr's verse is cited for the poor person. The transmitted alternatives retain a related sense. [5 notes: JISHUMI(ابن السميقع); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 93:8/q03/p2 Ibn al-Sumayqaʿ or the Yemeni reader reads ʿayilan/ʿayylan with doubled, kasra-marked yāʾ and no alif. — Thaʿlabī compares the form with ṭayyib from ṭāba; Kashshāf compares sayiḥāt, while Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī compare sayyid. Jishumī says both readings mean poor. [16 notes: THALABI(ابن السميقع); JISHUMI(ابن السميقع); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA(اليماني); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن أبي السميفع); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(اليماني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(اليماني); ALUSI(اليماني); ALUSI-FULL]
- 93:8/q03/p3 A codex attributed to Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh has ʿadīman, 'destitute,' then 'He enriched.' — Wāḥidī, Rāzī, Bursevī and Ālūsī cite destitute as evidence for material need; al-Aʿmash transmits the wording with enrichment. Ibn ʿAṭiyya's full version also reports an Ibn Masʿūd wording 'needy.' [11 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(الأعمش); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 93:8/q03/p4 A codex attributed to Ibn Masʿūd or ʿAbd Allāh has 'destitute, so He sheltered.' — Sufyān's report and al-Durr's Ibn Masʿūd report place 'gave shelter' after 'destitute,' unlike the transmitted 'enriched' version. [3 notes: TAB(سفيان); DURR(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(سفيان)]

## 93:8/q04 How did God enrich the Prophet?  [فَأَغْنَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether enrichment denotes worldly resources, contentment, revelation and proof, community support, divine nearness, public recognition, or a response to prayer.
- 93:8/q04/p1 He gave material means, especially Khadīja's wealth. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī and others name Khadīja's resources; ʿAṭāʾ says they replaced dependence on Abū Ṭālib. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies a profit-sharing commercial partnership, and ʿAbduh says he inherited only a camel and maidservant. Reports of famine relief and Abū Bakr's gift add detail. [40 notes: TAB; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء,مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جماعة من المفسرين); RAZI(الصديق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI(أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده,مفسرون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Baghawī); ELMALILI+; BINTSHATI-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 93:8/q04/p2 He granted later war spoils and conquered wealth. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī and Biqāʿī append spoils after earlier support; Kashshāf cites the Prophet's report that provision lay beneath his spear. Rāzī and later commentators answer the chronology objection by saying future spoils were certain; Elmalılı relates them to the promise in 93:5. Qushayrī and Ālūsī object that this Meccan sūra preceded warfare. [24 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(مفسرون); QURTUBI(القشيري)-; QURTUBI-FULL-; ALUSI(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-; BINTSHATI-]
- 93:8/q04/p3 He made the Prophet content, inwardly rich and sufficient with his provision. — Muqātil and al-Farrāʾ identify enrichment with contentment; the latter prefers this over abundance. Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes patience and love of the modest state, with both poverty and wealth beloved; Qushayrī adds receiving before asking. Rāzī says gold and stone were alike, and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says this shielded his character from poverty's humiliating effects. The Prophet reportedly chose poverty when offered wealth. [43 notes: THALABI(مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI(مقاتل); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA(قيل,مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الكلبي,مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN(قيل,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل,مقاتل); BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(مفسرون); STUDYQURAN(others); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء)+; BAGHAWI(الفراء)+; BINTSHATI+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء)+; TABRISI(مقاتل، الفراء)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]
- 93:8/q04/p4 He provided trade earnings and other successive worldly supports. — Bayḍāwī names trade profits; Biqāʿī adds later gains of war; Ālūsī specifies the Syrian journey with Maysara, Khadīja and Abū Bakr. Rāzī and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ list Abū Ṭālib, Khadīja, Abū Bakr, the Anṣār and spoils, while ʿAbduh's shorter sequence avoids later events in this early sūra. [16 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI(الصديق); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI(الشيخ محمد عبده,مفسرون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 93:8/q04/p5 He enriched him with revelation, scripture, religious law, wisdom and knowledge. — Tustarī names Qur'an and wisdom; Sulamī's Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says he lacked scripture, revelation and knowledge of his rank. Another Sulamī report and Thaʿlabī say he had gnosis but lacked practical rulings. Kur'an Yolu says revelation supplied illuminating truths; Iṣlāḥī stresses faith and knowledge of God. [10 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,بعضهم); THALABI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 93:8/q04/p6 He supplied proofs and arguments by revealing the Qur'an. — Sulamī, Māwardī, Rāzī and Qurṭubī transmit this explanation; Rāzī says the Prophet was taught what he had not known. [7 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 93:8/q04/p7 He enriched him with companions, helpers and their strength. — Thaʿlabī says he had lacked brothers and helpers. Rāzī cites worshipping companions, Abū Bakr's means and ʿUmar's conversion; ʿUmar urged open worship and cited Q 8:64's sufficiency of God and believers. [5 notes: THALABI; RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر); RAZI-FULL]
- 93:8/q04/p8 God enriched the poor through the Prophet, especially by his knowledge and guidance. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says God sheltered, guided and enriched people through the Prophet. Māwardī pairs this with his reported threefold declaration that his Lord bestows and is worthy of bestowing; Rāzī and Elmalılı identify the needy as an ignorant community or companions. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); MAWARDI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 93:8/q04/p9 He enriched him through God Himself, freeing him from need of anything else. — Sulamī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya speak of need toward God answered by Him. Ālūsī cites a supplication asking for wealth through need of God and a poem praising poverty toward Him; Bursevī and Qāshānī frame it through relinquishing ego and abiding in the divine presence. [13 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI; IBNATIYYA(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,القشيري); ALUSI(النبي,شاعر,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 93:8/q04/p10 He taught contentment in place of wavering between patience and satisfaction. — An unnamed Sulamī voice describes a shift from the station of patience to full contentment. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 93:8/q04/p11 He made the Prophet's hidden worth and distinction manifest. — Sulamī reports that the Prophet was unrecognized among Meccans and then revealed as a prophet; another voice says God showed his worth through ascent, divine speech, vision and witnessing, and another says God disclosed favors and rank to his people. [3 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 93:8/q04/p12 He answered the Prophet's supplication. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this as a separate explanation without specifying the prayer or answer. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 93:8/q05 Why are the objects of 'sheltered,' 'guided,' and 'enriched' unspoken?  [فَأَغْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the ellipsis only preserves concise address and rhyme or widens the beneficiaries.
- 93:8/q05/p1 The understood object is 'you'; omitting it is concise and preserves the verse endings. — Farrāʾ, Thaʿlabī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say the preceding second-person pronoun supplies the object; the verse ending is maintained. Ibn ʿĀshūr also compares the omission after qalā and cites Q 33:35. [7 notes: FARRA; THALABI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:8/q05/p2 The open wording includes both Muhammad and people aided through his mission. — An alternative reported by Ālūsī sees expansive generosity, though he objects that the following fa-clauses resist it. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the open objects extend the three gifts to those helped through Muhammad. [1 notes: BINTSHATI; ALUSI(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 93:8/q06 What grammatical construction does 'found you needy' use?  [وَوَجَدَكَ عَآئِلًۭا · grammar]
- 93:8/q06/p1 Wajada means encountered, takes one object, and ʿāʾilan states the condition in which he was found. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads yātīman, ḍāllan and ʿāʾilan as circumstantial states in an image of help arriving to one in distress. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 93:8/q07 What reports place this favor in a conversation about earlier prophets' gifts?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the request was authorized and whether God's response can be cast as reproof.
- 93:8/q07/p1 God reminded Muhammad of shelter, guidance, enrichment and other favors when he asked about gifts to earlier prophets. — Versions in Muqātil, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Jishumī, Baghawī, Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, N ashr and Durr vary the earlier figures (Abraham, Moses, David, Solomon), the Prophet's regret or affirmation, and appended favors: opened breast, lifted burden, raised renown, al-Baqara's final verses and divine friendship. Gabriel conveys the reminder in Muqātil. Al-Qāḍī and Jishumī dispute the premise of an unpermitted request and a reproachful response. [13 notes: MUQATIL(جبريل عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); JISHUMI(ابن عباس عن النبي); BAGHAWI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASHR(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); JISHUMI-; RAZI(القاضي)-; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]
- 93:8/q07/p2 The story of the Prophet asking for special gifts is unsound or implausible. — Jishumī says Muhammad asks only with permission and a permitted request must be answered; al-Qāḍī says prophets would not ask this without permission and the purported divine reply sounds like rebuke. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI(القاضي); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]

## 93:8/q08 What is reported about the revelation pause connected with this sūra?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over cause and duration; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes two pauses.
- 93:8/q08/p1 A puppy in the Prophet's house delayed Gabriel's entry. — Jishumī reports Gabriel saying angels do not enter a house with a dog or image. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 93:8/q08/p2 The pause served a benefit or respite for revelation. — Jishumī prefers a beneficial purpose if the pause is established, rejecting neglect of the exception formula as sufficient to delay believers' interests. Ibn ʿĀshūr proposes that the second pause built capacity for revelation. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; JISHUMI+]
- 93:8/q08/p3 The pause lasted twelve days. — Ibn Jurayj gives twelve days; Ibn ʿĀshūr tentatively makes this the second pause, while distinguishing a first pause of forty days and this sūra as the third revelation. [3 notes: JISHUMI(ابن جريج); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:8/q08/p4 The pause lasted fifteen days. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies this duration. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس)]
- 93:8/q08/p5 The pause lasted twenty-five days. — Jishumī gives this anonymously. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 93:8/q08/p6 The pause lasted forty days. — Muqātil gives forty days; Ibn ʿĀshūr assigns a first pause forty days and a second about twelve in reconciling the reports. [2 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 93:8/q08/p7 Gabriel said he missed the Prophet but descends only on God's command. — Jishumī reports the exchange as part of the pause traditions. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 93:8/q09 What does the verse teach about true wealth and poverty?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether richness is material provision, sufficiency of soul, faith and knowledge, or mystical poverty.
- 93:8/q09/p1 True wealth is richness of heart or soul, expressed in contentment with sufficient provision. — A Prophetic report repeated by Tustarī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn Kathīr and Bursevī opposes inner richness to plentiful goods; Baghawī gives no grading, while Ibn Kathīr says both Ṣaḥīḥ collections transmit it. Another Prophetic report says success combines Islam, sufficient provision and God-given contentment; Ibn Kathīr attributes it to Muslim. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Q 2:273 and passages where wealth cannot avail its owners. [17 notes: TUSTARI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BAGHAWI(أبو هريرة,عبد الله بن عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة,عبد الله بن عمرو); IBNATIYYA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR(أبو هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(الراغب,رسول الله عليه السلام); BINTSHATI(مفسرون); STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 93:8/q09/p2 Faith, revealed guidance and knowledge of God make a heart rich even amid material poverty. — Iṣlāḥī says an unbelieving heart remains needy amid treasure, whereas revelation supplies true affluence; he treats merely material explanation as superficial and connects inner satisfaction with Sūrah al-Sharḥ. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:8/q09/p3 Qur'anic aghnā can mean avail, protect or suffice, so enrichment is not always increased money. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys Q 7:48, 8:19, 11:101, 12:67, 52:46 and many others; Q 64:6, 80:5 and 96:7 also imply independence. She contrasts divine al-Ghanī, named seventeen times, with the absence of al-Tharī as a divine name. [4 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 93:8/q09/p4 The Prophet's own simple life argues against interpreting enrichment as lasting material riches. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Qur'anic uses that allow material wealth but says Muhammad's life stayed simple after his mission and spoils; his character and preparation, not a filled treasury or restored father, manifest God's care. [3 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 93:8/q09/p5 True mystical poverty is surrendering everything besides God; purified states surpass possessions. — Bursevī quotes Qushayrī's contrast between possessions for ordinary people and purified states for the elect, and praises prophetic poverty as relinquishing the ego. Ālūsī cites Ibn Ḥajar's judgment that 'poverty is my pride' is fabricated, and rejects the saying that completed poverty is God. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,القشيري); ALUSI(ابن حجر)-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر,بعض المتصوفة)-]
- 93:8/q09/p6 The Prophet joined patient poverty to grateful wealth, although his rank transcends ordinary categories. — Ibn Kathīr says his circumstances joined both virtues; al-Wāsiṭī in Sulamī says the usual oppositions of poverty and wealth do not apply to the Prophet as to others. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 93:8/q10 How do the three remembered favors relate to the Prophet's life and subsequent duties?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes relate past favors to future reassurance, moral response, prophetic rank and neighboring passages.
- 93:8/q10/p1 They recall his precommission circumstances and reassure him that God will continue to favor him. — Qatāda calls orphanhood, wandering and poverty the Prophet's earlier conditions; Kashshāf sees childhood care as warrant for future favor. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues the long pattern cannot be coincidence, consoling believers and warning opponents; Elmalılı says each later state surpassed the former, and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the reminders answer fear of abandonment. [14 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNKATHIR(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı müfessirler); STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; DURR-FULL(مسلمة بن مخلد)]
- 93:8/q10/p2 Receiving shelter, guidance and sufficiency obliges care for orphans and people in need, guidance, and grateful speech. — Rāzī says benefaction is a lifelong debt impossible to repay fully, so serve others while recognizing God's enabling. Baghawī connects provision with 93:9's orphan command; Itqān matches recounting divine blessing to enrichment in a laf wa-nashr pattern. Qāshānī says grateful service repays the inward gift, and Study Quran describes the sūra's closing commands. [11 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAGHAWI; ITQAN; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:8/q10/p3 The remembrance prevents self-admiration and displays a miraculous rise beyond orphanhood and poverty. — Rāzī says the two conditions ordinarily diminish status, so the Prophet's great honor despite them overturns custom; recalling them makes him mindful of dependence and counters pride. [3 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 93:8/q10/p4 The favors direct attention to their Giver and support a promised greater future. — Ṭabrisī joins the recollection of need to divine love, lasting revelation and future rank; Elmalılı locates later conquest in the promised gifts of 93:5. N ashr says recounting favors makes the ending takbīr fitting. [3 notes: TABRISI; ELMALILI; NASHR]
- 93:8/q10/p5 The paired sūrahs share a pattern of divine relief and reassurance. — Study Quran says Q 94:1 repeats the construction implied in 93:6–8; Sūrah 93 addresses external needs and Sūrah 94 spiritual relief before exhortation. Iṣlāḥī explains the inner satisfaction of this verse with Sūrah al-Sharḥ. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 93:8/q10/p6 The three gifts distinguish four combinations of religion and worldly means. — Biqāʿī lists people with both, neither, faith without wealth, and wealth without faith. [2 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 93:8/q11 What merit is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 93:8/q11/p1 The reciter receives intercessory favor and rewards measured by orphans and petitioners. — Bayḍāwī transmits a report promising acceptance among those for whom Muhammad may intercede and deeds equal to the number of orphans and petitioners; no grading is given in the note. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
