# 31:11: 25 questions

## 31:11/q01 What does 'this' point to?  [هَٰذَا · referent]
- 31:11/q01/p1 The visible creation just described: the heavens, earth, and their contents. — Muqātil calls it God's work. Qatāda specifies heavens, earth, animals and plants and cites the preceding description (31:10); Bursevī adds mountains, and Ibn ʿĀshūr adds rain. Baghawī stresses that it is visible; Abu Ḥayyān says its display supplies evidence against unbelievers. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the singular demonstrative gathers the many listed things as one collective referent. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:11/q02 Why use a demonstrative and the singular 'this'?  [هَٰذَا · rhetoric]
- 31:11/q02/p1 The demonstrative presents creation vividly to the senses, while its singular form treats the listed works together. — Al-Suyūṭī says demonstratives distinguish their referent by making it present to the hearer's senses; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the singular gathers the enumerated works as one thing. [2 notes: ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:11/q03 What does 'God's creation' mean here?  [خَلْقُ ٱللَّهِ · meaning]
- 31:11/q03/p1 The things God has created, rather than the act of creating. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Ālūsī take khalq as the created work. Abū Ḥayyān compares 'a dirham minted by the prince'; Bursevī calls it a verbal noun used for its passive object. Al-Qurṭubī says this visible work was made without a partner. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:11/q04 How is 'this is God's creation' construed?  [هَٰذَا خَلْقُ ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
- 31:11/q04/p1 'This' is the subject and 'God's creation' its predicate. — Al-Qurṭubī explicitly gives the subject-predicate analysis and takes khalq as the visible created thing. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 31:11/q05 What does God's creation establish about divinity and worship?  [خَلْقُ ٱللَّهِ مَاذَا خَلَقَ ٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · theology]
- 31:11/q05/p1 God alone creates and therefore alone deserves divinity and worship; beings that create nothing have no claim to either. — Al-Ṭabarī infers exclusive divinity and worship from the listed creation. Al-Rāzī says everything besides God is itself created. Al-Biqāʿī says creation's order shows might and wisdom. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this evidence exposes rejection of the wise Book, makes attributing God's wisdom to idols untenable, and presents evidence for the evidence ('proof of proof'). Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds lordship: even if polytheists ascribe governance rather than creation to idols, a true governor would create what it governs. Elmalılı says human crafts and inventions also trace to God, who grants wisdom and deserves thanks. Al-Kāshifī contrasts powerless creatures with the all-powerful Creator. The Study Quran cites 52:35-36, 7:191 and 16:20 as parallel creation challenges. Qushayrī notes that the heavens and earth show divine majesty; Ibn Kathīr includes all between them and God's determination without a partner. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:11/q06 Why name God in the third person here?  [ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 31:11/q06/p1 The shift from first-person narration names the creator explicitly. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the shift makes God's identity as creator explicit, and the later return to third person maintains the frame established by 'God's creation'. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:11/q07 What does 'show me' ask for, and what kind of seeing is meant?  [فَأَرُونِى · meaning]
turns on: Whether ruʾya here demands visual perception or permits knowledge/information.
- 31:11/q07/p1 It demands a visible object made by the alleged gods. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says sensory seeing is the common Arab usage and intensifies the challenge: they must produce a creature; the command also incapacitates them. A line ascribed either to Ḥuṭā'iṭ ibn Yaʿfar or Ḥātim al-Ṭā'ī uses arinī of a horse said to die of starvation; Imruʾ al-Qays also uses raʾā for sight. Ibn ʿĀshūr adduces ordinary expressions about what one's eye saw and whether someone sees. Al-Ālūsī allows 'show'. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(امرؤ القيس,حطائط بن يعفر النهشلي وقيل حاتم الطائي)]
- 31:11/q07/p2 It can mean inform me or make me know. — Al-Ālūsī glosses arūnī as informing or showing. Ibn ʿĀshūr conditionally allows knowing or informing as the sense of ruʾya; in that case the interrogative suspends the command from taking an object. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:11/q08 Who speaks the imperative challenge?  [فَأَرُونِى · referent]
- 31:11/q08/p1 The Prophet speaks it. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this form of address cannot properly come from anyone else. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 31:11/q09 Who is challenged by 'show me'?  [فَأَرُونِى · referent]
- 31:11/q09/p1 The polytheists, especially the Meccan idolaters or Quraysh. — Muqātil specifies Meccan unbelievers; Bursevī names Quraysh. Yaḥyā, al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī and others call them polytheists or idolaters. Iṣlāḥī says they already acknowledge that God created the cited things. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:11/q10 Why challenge them to show what others created?  [فَأَرُونِى مَاذَا خَلَقَ · interpretation]
- 31:11/q10/p1 The taunting demand exposes their inability to name any creation by their objects of worship and thereby rebukes worship without proof. — Yaḥyā says idols create nothing and offer no proof. Al-Ṭabarī says they strayed, rather than worshipped on evidence of idol creation. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī contrast God's tremendous works with idols' failure. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they can show nothing. Al-Ṭabrisī says they cannot answer. Al-Biqāʿī says no rational being should submit to one that has done nothing to merit it. Al-Ālūsī supplies the implied condition 'once you know this'; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the order taunting and incapacitating; Iṣlāḥī says the hearers already concede God's authorship. The Study Quran says it tests the claim that idols participate in creation. [24 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:11/q11 How is 'mādhā khalaqa' parsed?  [مَاذَا خَلَقَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mādhā is one interrogative or relative word, or mā and dhā have separate syntactic roles; the proposed governor of mā also varies.
- 31:11/q11/p1 Mādhā is one interrogative word, the fronted object of khalaqa. — Al-Ṭabrisī takes the whole as an accusative noun meaning 'what thing', with the clause governed by arūnī. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī give this among alternatives; al-Ālūsī reports it. Al-Bayḍāwī says arūnī is suspended by the question. [4 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:11/q11/p2 Mā is an interrogative subject and dhā a relative predicate, with the object of khalaqa omitted. — Al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī give this analysis; al-Qurṭubī makes the resulting clause the object of arūnī, and al-Ālūsī its second object. Bursevī and al-Bayḍāwī say the question suspends arūnī. Abū Ḥayyān mentions an interrogative joined to a relative. [7 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:11/q11/p3 Mā is arūnī's object and dhā is a relative meaning 'that which'. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this alternative and says it still means 'what they created'. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 31:11/q11/p4 Mā is the object and dhā is redundant. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this alternative with mā as the object of khalaqa; al-Qurṭubī reports an alternative with mā as the object of arūnī and compares the sense 'What did you learn, grammar or poetry?' The two reports differ about which verb governs mā. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 31:11/q11/p5 The whole mādhā is a single relative pronoun. — Abū Ḥayyān cites Sībawayh for this uncommon usage and also mentions a relative object. Al-Ālūsī reports Abū Ḥayyān's rarer parse: it fills arūnī's second object slot and has an omitted antecedent. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 31:11/q11/p6 Mā is interrogative, without a fuller parse specified. — Al-Samīn refers to fuller treatment in his discussion of al-Baqara. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 31:11/q12 Who are 'those besides Him'?  [ٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the challenge names the idols ordinarily invoked or, in Muqātil's reading, the angels worshipped by Meccan unbelievers.
- 31:11/q12/p1 The idols worshipped and invoked by the polytheists. — Qatāda identifies the challenged rivals as idols. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Qurṭubī and others specify idols; Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows idols or other worshipped objects, and Elmalılı says anything worshipped besides God. Al-Ṭabarī says such idols created nothing. Bursevī calls them beings made partners in worship. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:11/q12/p2 The angels worshipped by the Meccan unbelievers. — Muqātil connects their angel worship to similar challenges in Saba (34:27) and al-Aḥqāf (46:4). [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 31:11/q13 Why use a rational relative pronoun for idols?  [ٱلَّذِينَ · rhetoric]
- 31:11/q13/p1 It accommodates the idolaters' belief that their idols are intelligent beings. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the person-directed relative pronoun as speaking according to the polytheists' conception. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:11/q14 What does 'min dūnihi' convey linguistically?  [مِن دُونِهِۦ · grammar]
- 31:11/q14/p1 Dūn stands metonymically for 'other'; min reinforces its connection to the circumstantial complement. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls min an added preposition before the locative noun dūn. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:11/q15 What does 'bal' do after the challenge?  [بَلِ · rhetoric]
- 31:11/q15/p1 It turns from demanding proof or rebuking the idolaters to declaring their manifest error. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī describe the shift from argument to judgment. Al-Biqāʿī says God answers with the stronger charge of entrenched injustice instead of merely saying idols create nothing. Al-Ālūsī says direct argument ends because they cannot grasp its premises or respond to refutation. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the move to setting aside one point in a debate for another; Iṣlāḥī hears indirect pity because they have no grounds for partners. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:11/q16 Who are the 'wrongdoers'?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ · referent]
- 31:11/q16/p1 The polytheists who assign worship or divine powers to what is besides God; the description also extends to peoples acting like Quraysh. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Fatḥ al-Raḥmān extend Quraysh's error to other nations who behave likewise. Al-Rāzī says they place worship incorrectly or put themselves in another's worship. Asad identifies those who attribute divine powers to beings or things besides God. Elmalılı says they distort the meaning of creation and divinity and falsely attribute creative power to creatures. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي,فتح الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 31:11/q17 Why say 'the wrongdoers' instead of a pronoun?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ · rhetoric]
- 31:11/q17/p1 The explicit label marks shirk as injustice and condemns its adherents. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the noun makes the injustice of shirk clear. Bursevī calls association with God their injustice. Al-Biqāʿī says they put things wrongly under desire. Al-Ālūsī says they exceed bounds and wrong themselves through eternal punishment. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 31:11/q18 How is 'the wrongdoers are in manifest error' construed?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · grammar]
- 31:11/q18/p1 'The wrongdoers' is the subject and 'in manifest error' its predicate. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this explicit subject-predicate analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 31:11/q19 What is their 'manifest error'?  [ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · interpretation]
- 31:11/q19/p1 Obvious loss and departure from truth through shirk and worship of powerless creatures. — Muqātil calls it manifest loss. Al-Ṭabarī says reflection and reason show departure from truth and uprightness. Al-Rāzī glosses mubīn as manifest or clear to a rational person. Al-Qurṭubī calls it evident loss; Ibn Kathīr manifest ignorance and blindness. Elmalılı says they depart from manifest truth and even worship things known to create nothing. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it grave disbelief: they reject Islam's true call and associate another in divinity. Iṣlāḥī says there is no ground for partnership; the Study Quran points to its discussion at 36:24. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(فتح الرحمن); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:11/q20 What does 'in error' suggest about their state?  [فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 31:11/q20/p1 Error surrounds them and clings to them in every state. — Ibn ʿĀshūr draws this image from the preposition fī. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 31:11/q21 How does the journey image distinguish degrees of misdirection?  [ضَلَٰلٍۢ · inward]
- 31:11/q21/p1 Worldly distractions delay a seeker of God, while turning away from God prevents arrival altogether. — Al-Rāzī likens a slight turn right or left to a lesser deviation than turning backward: the distracted seeker may arrive with hardship, the direct seeker quickly and easily, but one who turns away never reaches the destination despite long travel. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 31:11/q22 What do tawḥīd and shirk mean for worship and deeds?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمُونَ فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍۢ · theology]
- 31:11/q22/p1 Sincere tawḥīd guides and draws one near to God; shirk, including ostentation, corrupts deeds and causes loss. — Bursevī says escape from error requires guidance to tawḥīd and sincere worship. He calls tawḥīd the highest virtue and shirk the greatest sin: tawḥīd's light burns monotheists' sins, while shirk's fire consumes polytheists' good deeds. He says manifest and hidden shirk and works built on them are rejected; sincere knowledge and action are beloved. Ostentation and reputation seeking move between heaven and earth only to be thrown back against the doer's face. He adds that tawḥīd and remembrance, as the best worship and nearest approach, have no set times unlike fasting and prayer. [5 notes: BURSEVI]

## 31:11/q23 What prophetic report supports rejecting worship besides God?  [ٱلَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِۦ · reports]
- 31:11/q23/p1 Affirming God's oneness and rejecting other objects of worship protects life and property; God judges concealed sincerity later. — Bursevī transmits this prophetic report and distinguishes its worldly protection from God's judgment of what is hidden in the afterlife. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 31:11/q24 How is preference for lighter diction illustrated?  [* · rhetoric]
- 31:11/q24/p1 Al-Barizī illustrates it with shorter and lighter expressions in several Qurʾānic word choices. — As reported by al-Suyūṭī, his examples include āman over ṣaddaq, ātharaka Allāh over faḍḍalaka, atā over aʿṭā, andhar over khawwaf, khayr lakum over afḍal lakum, shorter nominal forms, and tankiḥ. [1 notes: ITQAN(البارزي)]

## 31:11/q25 How does this verse connect to the surrounding sūra and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the opening is read through the possible occasion of Quraysh's question or through the ongoing structure of the sūra.
- 31:11/q25/p1 It closes the opening creation argument, and the following Luqmān counsel and later signs continue its call to gratitude and exclusive worship. — Iṣlāḥī says the introduction ends at 31:11, Luqmān's intervening advice reinforces gratitude to God alone, and 31:20 resumes the appeal to surrounding signs. He says Luqmān's loving counsel to his son echoes the warning to fathers who pressure sons away from Islam in 29:8. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God's creative evidence explains why rejecters of His signs reject the wise Book. Al-Ṭabarī's Qatāda links the listed creation to 31:10; Muqātil invokes 34:27 and 46:4, and the Study Quran cites 52:35-36, 7:191 and 16:20 as parallels; its note on error points to 36:24. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:11/q25/p2 If Quraysh's question about Luqmān and his son occasioned the sūra, these verses through 31:12 introduce knowledge, wisdom and guidance that benefited them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr states this conditionally, tying the opening to the Luqmān story and 31:12. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
