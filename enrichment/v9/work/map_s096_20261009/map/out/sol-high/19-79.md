# 19:79: 16 questions

## 19:79/q01 What does kallā mean here, and what does it repudiate?  [كَلَّا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether kallā completes a rebuttal to the man's claim or opens and affirms the ensuing threat, and whether its negation targets the boast or the questions in 19:78.
- 19:79/q01/p1 It rebukes and negates the man's boast that he will have wealth and children in the next life. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Bursawī and others connect the rebuke to his false hope; al-Zajjāj says it makes him recognize his error. Al-Akhfash and Ibn Qutayba call it restraint and reproof; al-Sijistānī calls it lā and cites al-ʿAjjāj's verse; Ibn al-Jawzī counts fourteen comparable Qurʾānic uses. The Basran majority, named by al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī, regard rebuke as its fitting Qurʾānic force. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is a negative reply to reported speech as well as direct speech (26:61–62). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds that disbelief cannot produce the claimed prosperity; the result is punishment. Al-Bukhārī's Khabbāb report presents this as the answer to al-ʿĀṣ's boast. [29 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الأخفش,السجستاني,المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(جمهور البصريين,محمد بن الباهلي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BUKHARI(خباب); ALUSI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 19:79/q01/p2 It denies that the claimant knows the unseen or has made a covenant with God. — Ibn al-Anbārī, reported by al-Wāḥidī, permits a pause at ʿahdan followed by this negation; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī give it as an alternative to denying the boast. The Study Quran connects it directly to the two questions of 19:78. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 19:79/q01/p3 It introduces what follows affirmatively, as 'truly' or 'indeed.' — Al-Kisāʾī and others, reported by al-Samīn, gloss it ḥaqqan. Al-Qurṭubī gives 'indeed' as one of its two senses and begins here after ʿahdan: 'Indeed, We shall write.' Al-Wāḥidī says an analysis allows the sense alā, though Abū Ḥātim's opening-particle analysis does not. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي)]
- 19:79/q01/p4 It can function as an opening particle before the following threat. — Abū Ḥātim gives this analysis, as al-Samīn reports; al-Wāḥidī distinguishes it from taking kallā as alā. Al-Farrāʾ also allows a connective function like sawfa alongside its independent rebuttal function. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; SAMIN-DURR(أبو حاتم); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء)]
- 19:79/q01/p5 It affirmatively answers 'yes.' — Al-Naḍr b. Shumayl says this sense needs a preceding statement and can also occur in oaths. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(النضر بن شميل)]
- 19:79/q01/p6 It is a filler equivalent to ī. — Al-Samīn questions this proposal because ī is an answer particle restricted to oaths. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR-]

## 19:79/q02 Where may a reader pause at kallā?  [كَلَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether kallā completes the preceding rebuke or depends on the following speech, and whether 'pause' means a full stop or a recitational break.
- 19:79/q02/p1 One may pause after kallā here because the rebuke is complete. — Ibn al-Anbārī, reported by al-Wāḥidī, allows a pause because the claim is false. Al-Wāḥidī reports a broader rule allowing a pause at every kallā; another grammarian reported by al-Ālūsī allows this except at the oath in 74:32. Al-Qurṭubī allows it when kallā means 'no' and says sense determines the correct pause. Al-Farrāʾ's first of four groups, reported by al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr, includes this verse among places permitting both pause and restart; his other groups differ over pause and restart, with examples 26:14–15, 26:62, 80:11 and 102:4. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this permission as the majority view. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن سعدان,الفراء); ALUSI-FULL(الفراء,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء)]
- 19:79/q02/p2 One may pause before kallā and restart with it as the opening of the next statement. — Al-Wāḥidī reports a group that pauses before it, and Ibn al-Anbārī allows a pause at ʿahdan. Al-Qurṭubī says 'indeed' calls for this division and supplies 'Indeed, We shall write.' Al-Farrāʾ's first class allows restart here. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that kallā can precede the false proposition it rebuts, building anticipation, as in 74:32–35. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء)]
- 19:79/q02/p3 One should not pause after kallā, because its response is completed by what follows. — Abū al-ʿAbbās, reported by al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Anbārī, forbids a pause at any kallā; al-Ālūsī records the same rule. Al-Farrāʾ bars a pause when it is connective in an oath, such as 74:32. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Mubarrad's prohibition but judges his disagreement with al-Farrāʾ largely verbal, since a pause need not mean complete silence. Arabs ordinarily follow kallā with more speech, Ibn ʿĀshūr adds. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس أحمد بن يحيى); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر بن الأنباري,أبو جعفر محمد بن سعدان,الفراء); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:79/q03 How is the tanwīn reading of kallā understood?  [كَلَّا · readings]
turns on: Whether the unusual vocalization creates a noun with an implied governor or merely changes the form of the ordinary particle.
- 19:79/q03/p1 With fatḥa and tanwīn it is an accusative verbal noun from an implied verb meaning to tire or fail. — Abū Nuhayk is the reported reader; al-Samīn corrects a later attribution to Ibn Nuhayk. Abū Ḥayyān supplies an implied command to be weakened from worship or truth. Al-Samīn cites the idiom kalla al-sayf for a sword becoming dull, and allows 'exhausted or incapable.' [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو نهيك); SAMIN-DURR(أبو نهيك,الكسائي)]
- 19:79/q03/p2 Its tanwīn replaces the final alif of the ordinary rebuke particle. — Al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī's analogy with qawārīrā in 76:15, conditioned on the reading being sound, and lists this as a third parse. He also reports the Shaykh's objection: an indeclinable particle gives no reason for this exchange, whereas qawārīrā has ordinary tanwīn or a dialectal analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 19:79/q03/p3 The tanwīn form is an adjective describing the idols as exhausted or incapable. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the adjectival parse; al-Samīn finds its contextual meaning unsuitable but allows the exhausted or incapable sense. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)]
- 19:79/q03/p4 It is the object of an implied 'they carried,' meaning a burden. — Al-Samīn reports this parse from al-kall, 'burden,' and compares the Qurʾānic description of a person as a burden in 16:76. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:79/q04 How is sanaktubu read?  [سَنَكْتُبُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb explicitly names God as writer or leaves the writing in the passive voice; the reports also differ in the readers they name.
- 19:79/q04/p1 It is active first-person plural, 'We shall write.' — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes the nūn reading to Abū ʿAmr, al-Ḥasan and ʿĪsā; Abū Ḥayyān calls it the majority reading. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو والحسن وعيسى); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش)]
- 19:79/q04/p2 It is passive, 'it will be written,' with yāʾ and tāʾ forms reported. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names ʿĀṣim and al-Aʿmash for sayuktabu; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī also name al-Aʿmash. Ibn al-Jawzī names Abū al-ʿĀliya and Abū Rajāʾ for passive 'will be written' and the corresponding 'will inherit' in the following verse. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم والأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور والأعمش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية الرياحي وأبو رجاء العطاردي); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش)]

## 19:79/q05 What is included in 'what he says'?  [مَا يَقُولُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the concise phrase designates only the specific promise or also the unbelief and mockery bound up with it.
- 19:79/q05/p1 His boast that he will receive wealth and children and repay Khabbāb after resurrection. — Muqātil and al-Thaʿlabī specify the promised reward or wealth and children; the Khabbāb reports give the context of the boast. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the short phrase can stand for a longer statement, comparing 3:183. [8 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BUKHARI(خباب)]
- 19:79/q05/p2 The boast includes his disbelief, lies and mockery, all of which are held against him. — Al-Wāḥidī calls it unbelief and mockery; al-Bayḍāwī adds that every utterance is watched (50:18). Ibn Kathīr includes his demand, self-assigned fate and disbelief. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the expression abbreviates lengthy blasphemy and mockery along with the boast, comparing 3:183. Al-Bursawī calls them lies, disbelief and mockery. [5 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:79/q06 What does God writing the man's words mean?  [سَنَكْتُبُ مَا يَقُولُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether writing denotes the actual preserved record, its later disclosure, its penal result, or establishing the claim's consequence.
- 19:79/q06/p1 His statement is preserved as a record for later reckoning, with recording angels acting at God's command. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī explicitly assign the writing to angels; al-Baghawī allows either angelic writing or God's keeping it on record. Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī emphasize preservation for punishment or reproach. Al-Nasafī cites 50:18 and al-Ālūsī cites 50:18 and 43:80 as evidence for a watcher and God's writing messengers. Al-Jishumī infers that deeds are all preserved. [16 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 19:79/q06/p2 God will show him that his words have already been recorded. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī reconcile the future tense with an existing record by taking it as future disclosure. Al-Zamakhsharī compares a poem in which lineage reveals origins; al-Nasafī uses the same poetic point and 50:18. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī also give exposure of the record, the latter calling it an expressive metaphor; Abū Ḥayyān transmits al-Zamakhsharī's account. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:79/q06/p3 Writing is a threat of future vengeance or recompense for the words. — Al-Zamakhsharī takes it as a conventional threat despite delay; al-Rāzī says such future threat language emphasizes punishment even if it has begun. Abū Ḥayyān takes the writing as standing for its penal consequence. Al-Bayḍāwī compares retribution for a recorded crime. Al-Ālūsī treats the record as the cause named for its effect; he transmits Abū al-Suʿūd's related metaphorical threat interpretation while noting its obscurity. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود)]
- 19:79/q06/p4 God establishes the saying so that its consequence follows. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this to a ledger entry because, on the ledger reading, the next verse repeats the point without an evident purpose. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]

## 19:79/q07 Why is the writing expressed as a future act?  [سَنَكْتُبُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the future points to disclosure, delayed retribution, a merciful interval, or the certainty of a genuinely future event.
- 19:79/q07/p1 The man will later see and learn of an already completed record. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī expressly address the existing record; al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī read the future as exposure, supported by 50:18 and the poem about ancestry making origins known. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:79/q07/p2 The future is a conventional threat of coming punishment or revenge, even if delayed. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Abū Ḥayyān report this idiom. Al-Rāzī says it can intensify a threat even with punishment underway; Abū Ḥayyān connects the future sīn with punishment following the written boast. Al-Ālūsī gives eventual revenge as the record's consequence. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:79/q07/p3 The future allows the claimant time to repent before the record is made. — Al-Biqāʿī presents the delay as an expression of general divine mercy. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 19:79/q07/p4 The future particle assures that the threatened consequence will certainly occur. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 12:98. Al-Ālūsī insists the future promise is certain and rejects treating the particle as merely intensifying a present act. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:79/q07/p5 The future particle merely intensifies an act already in progress. — Al-Ālūsī rejects this proposal because it loses the actual future force of the promised threat; al-Rāzī reports a related idiom in which punishment has already begun. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 19:79/q08 What does madd mean as a word?  [وَنَمُدُّ مَدًّۭا · meaning]
- 19:79/q08/p1 Its basic sense is extending something to its furthest length; here it can mean continuance over time. — Ibn al-Jawzī states the root sense and notes metaphorical extensions; he reports duration or continuance as a second sense, comparing the everlasting shade of Paradise in 56:30. Al-Rāzī says both madda and amadda can convey extension. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL]

## 19:79/q09 Why use madd for punishment?  [وَنَمُدُّ مَدًّۭا · rhetoric]
- 19:79/q09/p1 Arab usage tends to reserve amdad for welcome provision and madd for painful extension. — Al-Rāghib, quoted by al-Burhān, contrasts blessing with punishment and cites this wording; al-Itqān reports the same distinction and compares 52:22. Al-Rāzī adds that madda and amadda can both mean to extend, so the contrast is a tendency rather than an absolute lexical division. [3 notes: BURHAN(الراغب); ITQAN(الراغب); RAZI-FULL]

## 19:79/q10 How is wa-namuddu lahu read?  [وَنَمُدُّ لَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: The reports specify different initial letters in the form attributed to ʿAlī.
- 19:79/q10/p1 A reading attributed to ʿAlī has ḍamma on the initial yāʾ of yamuddu lahu. — Al-Rāzī gives the yāʾ attribution. Al-Zamakhsharī also cites a ḍamma reading of ʿAlī and says it supports increasing and multiplying punishment, strengthened by the verbal noun. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 19:79/q10/p2 A reading attributed to ʿAlī has an explicit plural nūn, reported with ḍamma. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the explicit plural verb. Al-Ālūsī describes nūn with ḍamma and says it can be used with or without the lām; he takes the accompanying verbal noun to intensify the threat and anger. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(علي)]

## 19:79/q11 What does extending his punishment entail?  [وَنَمُدُّ لَهُۥ مِنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ مَدًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether extension describes duration, increased severity or repeated stages, or begins in worldly attachments that lead to later torment.
- 19:79/q11/p1 His torment is prolonged without end. — Muqātil and al-Suddī explicitly say it does not end; Yaḥyā b. Sallām relates this to 78:30, where more punishment is added. Ibn al-Jawzī connects madd with continuance and cites 56:30. Al-Ṭabrisī describes linked stages with no end. Al-Rāzī says both madda and amadda can mean extension; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the extension of respite in 19:75. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:79/q11/p2 More punishment is added, multiplied, intensified or made to follow punishment already due. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī specify addition beyond his existing due; Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Ṭabrisī describe one punishment following another. Al-Zamakhsharī adduces ʿAlī's ḍamma reading for multiplication. Al-Nasafī links increase to mounting slander and audacity. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares madd in 19:75. The Study Quran attributes the increase to added lies and vain claims. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 19:79/q11/p3 His beloved wealth and children already extend the causes of punishment in life and deepen the unbelief punished after death. — Al-Biqāʿī says labor and disputes over wealth and children gradually burden him and intensify the unbelief leading to later punishment. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 19:79/q12 What does the repeated verbal noun maddan add?  [مَدًّۭا · rhetoric]
- 19:79/q12/p1 It intensifies the threat of severe, multiplied punishment and divine anger. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the verbal noun intensifies like repetition; al-Zamakhsharī says it reinforces multiplication and the doubled punishment expresses severe anger. Al-Nasafī also ties the repeated infinitive to severity of anger; al-Ālūsī says it heightens the threat. [5 notes: TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(علي)]

## 19:79/q13 Why is punishment extended for this claimant?  [وَنَمُدُّ لَهُۥ مِنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ مَدًّۭا · interpretation]
- 19:79/q13/p1 It answers his disbelief, boast, lies and mockery; repeated slander and audacity deepen his liability. — Al-Qushayrī and al-Jishumī describe a reproach and threat for the boast. Al-Bayḍāwī singles out disbelief and mockery; al-Nasafī ties additional punishment to more slander and audacity. Ibn Kathīr, al-Bursawī and the Study Quran connect it with disbelief, lying or vain claims. Al-Zamakhsharī describes the severity of divine anger. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says disbelief produces punishment, not promised wealth and children; al-Biqāʿī says the claimed wealth and children deepen unbelief. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 19:79/q14 How does this punishment answer the man's promised increase in wealth and children?  [وَنَمُدُّ لَهُۥ مِنَ ٱلْعَذَابِ مَدًّۭا · links]
- 19:79/q14/p1 The only extension promised to him is punishment, replacing the prosperity he claimed. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly sets the two extensions against each other. Al-Bursawī says torment lengthens instead of wealth and children increasing. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the claimant's disbelief cannot bring the goods he expects; its consequence is extended punishment. Al-Biqāʿī goes further: the goods he loves themselves become gradual causes of harm. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 19:79/q15 What occasion for the passage is reported?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported mocking debt episode fits the context's serious reasoning, and whether the claimant is identified as al-ʿĀṣ or al-Walīd.
- 19:79/q15/p1 Al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil withheld a debt or wages from Khabbāb, demanded he disavow Muḥammad, then mockingly promised repayment from afterlife wealth and children. — Khabbāb's report is transmitted by Yaḥyā b. Sallām, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Bukhārī; al-Qurṭubī names Muslim and Bukhārī, while Ibn Kathīr notes several canonical routes. Al-Kalbī and Muqātil add that blacksmith Khabbāb made jewelry for al-ʿĀṣ, who mocked repayment in Paradise and claimed Khabbāb had no greater entitlement. These accounts vary between debt, wages and jewelry but describe the same withheld payment. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that a resurrection-denier's mockery does not fit the passage's serious reply. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(خباب بن الأرت); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل,خباب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); BUKHARI(خباب); TABATABAI(الأكثر); TABATABAI-]
- 19:79/q15/p2 The claimant may instead be al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra, named alongside al-ʿĀṣ in a majority explanation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the majority naming al-ʿĀṣ or al-Walīd and describing a claim of afterlife wealth and children, followed by God's questions about unseen knowledge or a covenant and by solitary return without possessions. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الأكثر)]
- 19:79/q15/p3 The statement should be understood as a serious claim, not a resurrection-denier's mockery. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the reported mocking occasion is inconsistent with the passage answering by a serious argument. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 19:79/q16 How does the preceding divine name al-Raḥmān sharpen the reply?  [* · links]
- 19:79/q16/p1 It ironically evokes the mercy the unbeliever assumed would guarantee his prosperity and needles Quraysh for rejecting that name. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the irony to Quraysh's response to the name in 25:60. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
