# 74:13: 14 questions

## 74:13/q01 Whose sons does the verse describe?  [وَبَنِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the portrait is restricted to the named notable or also describes a wider affluent mentality.
- 74:13/q01/p1 The sons of al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra al-Makhzūmī. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Saʿīd b. Jubayr's full identification; al-Jishumī says Ibn ʿĀmir identifies al-Walīd, a wealthy notable called al-Wāḥid; Kur'an Yolu calls him a wealthy Quraysh notable; Study Quran applies the verses to his wealth, sons, learning and eloquence. Iṣlāḥī allows this application while taking the portrait more broadly. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:13/q01/p2 The portrait describes the mentality of affluent, high-status people generally, while also applying to al-Walīd. — Iṣlāḥī links it to affluent leaders of Mecca and Ṭāʾif who thought wealth proved divine favor and expected more. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 74:13/q02 How many sons are reported?  [وَبَنِينَ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted counts and whether a six-name genealogy is an exhaustive count; one twelve-son report distributes seven births in Mecca and five in Ṭāʾif.
- 74:13/q02/p1 Ten sons. — Mujāhid, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī are among those cited for ten. Ibn Qutayba, al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī and others place them with their father in Mecca; al-Bayḍāwī says ten or more adult sons. Al-ʿĀlūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Study Quran also record alternative totals. [23 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد وقتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد، قتادة); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN]
- 74:13/q02/p2 Thirteen sons. — Saʿīd or Ibn Jubayr is repeatedly cited; Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Māwardī add that each was a household head. Ibn Kathīr also cites al-Suddī, Abū Mālik and ʿĀṣim b. ʿUmar b. Qatāda; al-Durr cites Abū Mālik. Some sources merely list thirteen alongside other reports. [15 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي وأبو مالك وعاصم بن عمر بن قتادة); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 74:13/q02/p3 Seven sons. — Muqātil gives seven and names them; several sources add that three later became Muslim. Al-Thaʿlabī's note is attributed to Muqātil but its claim names Mujāhid. Al-Rāzī lists al-Walīd b. al-Walīd, Khālid, ʿUmāra, Hishām, al-ʿĀṣ, Qays and ʿAbd Shams. [11 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN]
- 74:13/q02/p4 Twelve sons. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports twelve from al-Suddī; al-Qurṭubī attributes twelve to al-Suddī and al-Ḍaḥḥāk. Al-Māwardī transmits al-Ḍaḥḥāk's distribution of seven born in Mecca and five in Ṭāʾif; al-Qurṭubī repeats that distribution. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 74:13/q02/p5 Ibn Ḥazm's genealogy lists six sons. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the genealogy omits al-ʿĀṣ from the seven otherwise named; he describes a list of six, rather than explicitly declaring that no seventh son existed. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن حزم)]

## 74:13/q03 Which sons are identified by name?  [وَبَنِينَ · reports]
turns on: Whether al-ʿĀṣ appears in the genealogical list, and the transmitted seven-name list.
- 74:13/q03/p1 Seven are named: al-Walīd ibn al-Walīd, Khālid, ʿUmāra, Hishām, al-ʿĀṣ, Qays and ʿAbd Shams. — Al-Rāzī gives the full list; Abū Ḥayyān and Elmalılı repeat its names. Muqātil and sources transmitting him say seven were named, with slight forms of the names and different reports about which three converted. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts this seven-name list with Ibn Ḥazm's six. [10 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن حزم)]
- 74:13/q03/p2 Ibn Ḥazm names six of the seven, omitting al-ʿĀṣ. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Ibn Ḥazm's Genealogy with the seven-name list. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن حزم)]

## 74:13/q04 Which sons later embraced Islam?  [وَبَنِينَ · reports]
turns on: Whether ʿUmāra or al-Walīd ibn al-Walīd belongs in the three-name list; hadith reports and accounts of ʿUmāra's death are cited against his conversion.
- 74:13/q04/p1 Khālid, Hishām and ʿUmāra are named as the three converts. — Muqātil is cited for this trio by al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī; al-Thaʿlabī's note reports it with an internally varying attribution. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevî and Elmalılı also transmit it. Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı challenge ʿUmāra's inclusion: reports place his death in unbelief at Badr or Abyssinia, while hadith scholars affirm al-Walīd ibn al-Walīd's conversion. [10 notes: THALABI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المفسرون); ELMALILI(صاحب الكشاف); BURSEVI(المحدثون)-; ALUSI-FULL(الثعلبي)-; ELMALILI(آلوسي)-]
- 74:13/q04/p2 Khālid, Hishām and al-Walīd ibn al-Walīd are named as the three converts. — Al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and al-Suhaylī as quoted by Bursevî give this trio. Bursevî reports hadith scholars' affirmation of al-Walīd's conversion; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı say its omission in other lists is an error and place ʿUmāra's death in unbelief. Al-Ālūsī's rejection note calls the allegedly converted person al-Walīd, while Elmalılı describes the contested report as one about ʿUmāra. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI(السهيلي,المحدثون); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(آلوسي); ALUSI-FULL+; ELMALILI+]
- 74:13/q04/p3 Khālid is identified as a convert without a full list of the converts. — Muqātil names Khālid among seven sons; al-Biqāʿī says he later became the sword of God and His Messenger. [2 notes: MUQATIL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 74:13/q04/p4 Al-Walīd ibn al-Walīd converted, while ʿUmāra's conversion remains disputed. — Elmalılı gives this qualified conclusion and says the remaining sons beyond four are unknown; hadith scholars affirm al-Walīd's conversion, whereas accounts of ʿUmāra's death in unbelief conflict with lists naming him a convert. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]

## 74:13/q05 Where is ʿUmāra said to have died?  [وَبَنِينَ · reports]
turns on: The reported location of ʿUmāra's death; both accounts say he died without converting.
- 74:13/q05/p1 At Badr, as an unbeliever. — Bursevî quotes hadith scholars and Elmalılı cites al-Ālūsī; both also record the Abyssinia alternative. [2 notes: BURSEVI(المحدثون); ELMALILI(آلوسي)]
- 74:13/q05/p2 In Abyssinia at the Negus's hand, as an unbeliever. — Bursevî gives the Negus detail; Elmalılı says al-Ālūsī reports this alongside Badr, with both agreeing that ʿUmāra died an unbeliever. [2 notes: BURSEVI(المحدثون); ELMALILI(آلوسي)]

## 74:13/q06 What grammatical forms are noted for بنين in lineage expressions?  [وَبَنِينَ · grammar]
- 74:13/q06/p1 After movement the connecting alif drops; بنوي is used, and ابني is disputed. — Al-Naḥḥās says Sībawayh permits ابني while some Kufans forbid it. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 74:13/q07 In what sense are the sons شُهُودًا?  [شُهُودًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether shuhūd describes constant physical presence, public attendance or testimony, practical support, public association with the father, or witnessing his affection.
- 74:13/q07/p1 They remained present with their father, especially in Mecca, instead of going away to earn or conduct trade. — Bursevî and Elmalılı derive shuhūd as the plural of shāhid, 'present'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses constant availability. Muqātil locates their wealth and business in Mecca; al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī say wealth spared travel and let the father enjoy them. Ibn Kathīr's Mujāhid says servants and hired workers did the trading. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds raiding to the journeys avoided; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the father saw them and drew strength from them. [32 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 74:13/q07/p2 They attended public gatherings and assemblies with him, adding to his standing. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevî, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr give this alternative. Al-Biqāʿī calls them intelligent, capable and so prominent it was as though nobody else attended; Elmalılı says they were free to attend and consulted for opinions and knowledge. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:13/q07/p3 They could serve as witnesses whose testimony was heard in disputes. — Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān state the testimony interpretation; al-Ālūsī specifies legal disputes; Elmalılı mentions their testimony alongside opinions and knowledge. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 74:13/q07/p4 They were capable sons who stood by him and carried out his affairs when needed. — Al-Māwardī suggests they had become like their father in witnessing and managing affairs. Iṣlāḥī says they supported his many businesses and were alert enough to defend or help him; in tribal society numbers and defense mattered for status and survival. Al-Biqāʿī calls them intelligent and capable. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:13/q07/p5 Their public identity was bound to their father, so they were mentioned whenever he was. — Al-Māwardī transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 74:13/q07/p6 The children were present as witnesses of his affection. — Asad gives this explanation. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:13/q08 What happened to his wealth and children after revelation?  [* · reports]
- 74:13/q08/p1 They kept declining until al-Walīd died. — Al-Thaʿlabī's unnamed report, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī say the decline followed the passage; Abū Ḥayyān transmits it from Muqātil. Al-Ālūsī says reports differ over the manner of the father's death but agree that he died an unbeliever. [4 notes: THALABI(قالوا); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 74:13/q09 What account of al-Walīd's accusation against Muhammad accompanies the passage?  [* · reports]
- 74:13/q09/p1 At the pilgrimage gathering, he urged Quraysh to call Muhammad a sorcerer because his message divided families. — Ibn Hishām reports al-Walīd first rejected soothsayer, madman, poet and sorcerer as accurate descriptions, then chose sorcerer as a useful common charge; al-Jishumī similarly says he rejected soothsayer, poet and madman before choosing magician. [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(الوليد بن المغيرة); JISHUMI]

## 74:13/q10 How does the passage fit the Quran's account of opponents' charges?  [* · links]
- 74:13/q10/p1 It belongs to the Quran's recurring response to Meccan accusations that revelation was human invention or borrowed lore. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says opponents alleged fabrication, ancient stories, copying and dictation, and cites 68:10–15, 68:44–47, 25:4–10, 17:87–96, 6:7–9, 6:25–26 and 34:43–45 as passages recording or answering such charges. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 74:13/q11 What moral warning does this gift of sons and wealth convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:13/q11/p1 Abundant gifts call for gratitude; defiance after receiving them makes the coming judgment more severe. — Al-Jishumī says blessings heighten the seriousness of disobedience. Al-Biqāʿī calls male children a foremost worldly desire after wealth, and says presence adds enjoyment. Iṣlāḥī says the man became conceited instead of grateful, and affluent leaders treated wealth and rank as proof of divine favor and expected still more; he reads verses 11–17 as warning of severe Judgment Day. Kur'an Yolu calls gratitude a duty of reason and justice and says denial, polytheism and injustice aggravate ingratitude; al-Walīd suppressed Islam despite his gifts. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 74:13/q12 Are worldly goods given to unbelievers blessings from God?  [* · theology]
- 74:13/q12/p1 Yes; God grants worldly goods as blessings even to unbelievers. — Al-Jishumī expressly opposes the Mujbira on blessings to unbelievers and says worldly conditions and possessions come from God. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 74:13/q13 How does the verse connect to the surrounding wording and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: These are two links at different scales: the nearby sequence of gifts and warning, and the relationship to the preceding sūra.
- 74:13/q13/p1 The sons are coordinated with wealth as gifts that precede the warning against arrogant ingratitude. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the coordination with wealth; al-Biqāʿī treats male children as a major desire after abundant wealth; Iṣlāḥī reads verses 11–17 as a severe warning for those who expected more wealth and rank. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:13/q13/p2 Al-Muddaththir parallels the preceding al-Muzzammil in name meaning and central theme. — Iṣlāḥī identifies the two sūras as counterparts. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:13/q14 What inward reading is given to the man and his gifts?  [* · inward]
- 74:13/q14/p1 He figures the solitary evil soul, with wealth and followers as its sins, dark powers and prestige, overcome by spiritual light. — Bursevî transmits the Najmī allusion: Abū Bakr the hidden, ʿUmar the spirit, ʿUthmān the secret and ʿAlī the heart erase the soul's darkness, defeat its deeds and followers, and end its dominion. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
