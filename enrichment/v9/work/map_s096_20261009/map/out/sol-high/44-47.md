# 44:47: 14 questions

## 44:47/q01 Who is the one commanded to be seized?  [خُذُوهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the command is explained as the generic sinner's fate or tied to the report about Abū Jahl.
- 44:47/q01/p1 The condemned sinner or disbeliever. — Al-Ṭabarī places the seizure on Judgment Day; Ibn Kathīr calls him the condemned disbeliever. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the fire will envelop him; Study Quran names its guards the zabāniyah and cites 96:17–18. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,مجاهد ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 44:47/q01/p2 Abū Jahl is the particular person meant by the threat. — Muqātil says the guards push him face-first to Hell's middle, its sixth gate. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk likewise assigns this command to Abū Jahl. Qatādah says Abū Jahl boasted that none between Mecca's two mountains surpassed him in power or nobility; the later 'mighty, noble' address answers him ironically. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); NAHHAS(الضحاك)]

## 44:47/q02 To whom is the command addressed?  [خُذُوهُ · referent]
- 44:47/q02/p1 The angels who guard Hell are ordered to take the sinner. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq identify the angels; Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī and Iṣlāḥī identify Hell's wardens. Bursawī specifies Judgment Day and seizure by forelock and feet. Study Quran calls them zabāniyah and cites 96:17–18. Ibn Kathīr transmits a report that seventy thousand rush toward him when God commands them. [16 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 44:47/q03 What does the order 'take him' entail?  [خُذُوهُ · interpretation]
- 44:47/q03/p1 Seize the sinner coercively and deliver him into the guards' control. — Al-Ṭabarī says he is driven from behind on Judgment Day. Al-Biqāʿī stresses that he has no control over his own affairs. Bursawī describes taking him by forelock and feet; Ibn Kathīr says a report has seventy thousand keepers rushing to him. The subsequent dragging and, for Iṣlāḥī, boiling-water punishment follow the seizure. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 44:47/q04 What unspoken speech introduces the command?  [خُذُوهُ · rhetoric]
- 44:47/q04/p1 An implicit 'they said' or 'it was said' introduces the quoted order to the wardens. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the command presupposes an unspoken 'they said'; al-Ālūsī calls it an implied quotation. Iṣlāḥī says leaving the speaker unstated makes the direct pronouncement eloquent, then continues the order through dragging and pouring boiling water. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 44:47/q05 Why is the suffix wāw absent from 'take him' and 'drag him'?  [خُذُوهُ فَٱعْتِلُوهُ · grammar]
turns on: The phonological reason for the omitted original wāw; retention in connected speech is also permitted.
- 44:47/q05/p1 It was deleted because two long-vowel letters met. — Al-Naḥḥās reports Sībawayh's analysis that the wāw was originally present. Al-Khalīl and Sībawayh permit retaining it in connected speech as خذوهو and فاعتلوهو, although deletion is preferred. [2 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه); NAHHAS(الخليل وسيبويه)+]
- 44:47/q05/p2 It was removed to prevent two vowelless letters from meeting. — Al-Naḥḥās attributes this explanation to unnamed others. [1 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه)]

## 44:47/q06 How is the verb 'drag him' read?  [فَٱعْتِلُوهُ · readings]
turns on: The vowel and, in one report, the letter to which it is assigned; most sources contrast kasra and ḍamma on the tāʾ, while al-Biqāʿī attaches added force to ḍamma.
- 44:47/q06/p1 The tāʾ is read with kasra. — Al-Fārisī assigns this to most readers and records Abū ʿAmr with kasra or both vowels; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī name the Kūfans, Abū Jaʿfar and Abū ʿAmr. Al-Naḥḥās records the Kūfan form, calls both dialectal, and says analogy favors kasra, like ضربه يضربه. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah transmits both vowels also from al-Ḥasan, Qatādah and al-Aʿraj. Al-Ṭabarī accepts both as Arabic dialects; al-Wāḥidī compares يعكفون and يعرشون, while al-Fārisī compares paired يعكف forms. Ibn al-Jazarī lists all readers outside Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀmir and Yaʿqūb here. [20 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر); THALABI(أبو جعفر وأبو مرو وأهل الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو والباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر); TABRISI(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون، أبو عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL(الحجازيان وابن عامر ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر); NASHR(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر ويعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي والحجازيان وابن عامر ويعقوب); NAHHAS(أهل الكوفة)+]
- 44:47/q06/p2 The tāʾ is read with ḍamma. — Al-Fārisī names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir; Ibn al-Jawzī and Ibn al-Jazarī add Yaʿqūb. Al-Naḥḥās records the Medinan form; al-Ṭabarī mentions Medinan and Meccan readers. Al-Bayḍāwī calls them the two Ḥijāzīs, Ibn ʿĀmir and Yaʿqūb. Al-Ālūsī also transmits it from Zayd ibn ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan, Qatādah and al-Aʿraj. Al-Biqāʿī says this vowel conveys greater severity than kasra; other sources call both forms dialectal. Qiraat-ER describes an internal shift from i to u without changing the root or command. [22 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أهل المدينة); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر); THALABI(أبو جعفر وأبو مرو وأهل الكوفة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو والباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر); TABRISI(أهل الكوفة وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر ويعقوب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون، أبو عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL(الحجازيان وابن عامر ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL; NASHR(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر ويعقوب); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والأعرج,زيد بن علي والحجازيان وابن عامر ويعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]
- 44:47/q06/p3 A form with fatḥ is described as the majority reading by al-Ālūsī. — Al-Ālūsī pairs this with a ḍamma form and calls them dialectal patterns; his note names the vowel as fatḥ, unlike the many reports here of kasra. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي والحجازيان وابن عامر ويعقوب)]
- 44:47/q06/p4 Al-Samīn describes ḍamma on the ʿayn rather than on the tāʾ. — He ascribes this form to Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀmir and gives the others kasra; he calls both dialects of عتله, to drive roughly. His vowel location differs from the other reports. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن كثير وابن عامر)]

## 44:47/q07 What action does 'faʿtilūhu' describe?  [فَٱعْتِلُوهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root is glossed as dragging, pushing, simple leading, or breaking like firewood; al-Ālūsī objects to the last as needing syntactic supplementation.
- 44:47/q07/p1 Drag or drive him away by force, roughly and with humiliation. — Ibn Qutaybah compares forcibly bringing someone before a ruler; al-Layth describes taking a man's lapel and pulling him toward prison or calamity, or leading a camel by its reins. Ibn al-Sikkīt cites عتلته/أعتلته to prison and also عتنته for violent pushing. Al-Farazdaq's تعتل line, and another line likening the dragged man to a large slaughter camel, witness the force. Al-Qurṭubī adds a horse poem; al-Rāghib says gripping a garment is an example, not essential to the sense. Al-Biqāʿī quotes al-Rāzī's grip on the gathered chest garment and adds speed and helplessness; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah likens it to leading criminals. Bursawī cites Tāj al-Maṣādir and al-Qāmūs, the latter also defining مُعْتَل as one strong enough to drag. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn connect the root to an uncouth, coarse person. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr mention dragging from behind; Mujāhid is reported by al-Ṭabrisī as dragging him on his face. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes forceful shaking. [46 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI(الفرزدق); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة,الحسن,الفرزدق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن السكيت,الليث); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الشاعر,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(ابن السكيت,الليث); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن السكيت); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الفرزدق); BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); BURSEVI(القاموس,تاج المصادر); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش ومجاهد)+]
- 44:47/q07/p2 Push or shove him violently, in some reports face-down. — Mujāhid glosses the verb as pushing; al-Ḍaḥḥāk says take and push him into Hell's middle. Muqātil and Mujāhid are reported as pushing him face-down; Muqātil specifies Abū Jahl and Hell's sixth gate. Ibn al-Sikkīt says عتلته and عتنته both denote violent pushing. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes pushing among the verb's senses; Ibn Kathīr describes shoving him from behind. [15 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); NAHHAS(الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت,مجاهد ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); TABATABAI]
- 44:47/q07/p3 Lead him along toward Hell. — Al-Kalbī's short gloss says 'lead him along'; al-Wāḥidī reports him specifying the destination as Hell's middle and connecting it with 37:55. The gloss does not itself spell out the violence other sources give. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)]
- 44:47/q07/p4 Break, crush, or wrench him as one does firewood. — Al-Māwardī attributes the firewood comparison to al-Aʿmash; al-Durr gives it from Mujāhid. Al-Ālūsī reports it from both and says that this sense requires an implied element or attaching the prepositional phrase to 'take him'; he favors the familiar dragging sense. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأعمش); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش ومجاهد)-]

## 44:47/q08 Which commands does 'to the middle of Hell' modify?  [خُذُوهُ فَٱعْتِلُوهُ إِلَىٰ سَوَآءِ ٱلْجَحِيمِ · grammar]
- 44:47/q08/p1 It attaches to both 'take him' and 'drag him.' — Ibn ʿĀshūr says both verbs imply driving the sinner forcefully to that destination. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 44:47/q09 What is 'sawāʾ al-jaḥīm,' the destination?  [سَوَآءِ ٱلْجَحِيمِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'sawāʾ' emphasizes the center, the principal area reached by heat from every side, or its broader lexical senses.
- 44:47/q09/p1 The middle or center of Hellfire. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Qatādah and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr are cited for the center. Al-Ṭabrisī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī explain 'sawāʾ' by equal distance from every edge. Al-Kalbī links the middle with 37:55; Ibn al-Jawzī also cites 37:55 for 'sawāʾ.' Al-Nasafī and Bursawī call it the greatest or principal part as well. Al-Biqāʿī calls 'jaḥīm' the fiercely blazing Fire and the place where the sinner's food tree grows; Muqātil names its sixth gate. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says its central position lets Hell envelop him, linked by him to 29:54. Iṣlāḥī calls it Hell's depth. [28 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك وقتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك,سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 44:47/q09/p2 Hell's main part, where heat reaches the sinner from every side. — Al-Māwardī reports this as al-Ḥasan's alternative to the gloss 'middle'; al-Nasafī and Bursawī also call the central place its greatest or principal part. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 44:47/q09/p3 The word sawāʾ can also mean justice or evenness. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives these lexical senses alongside the middle sense; he does not explicitly replace the verse's destination with an abstract quality. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 44:47/q10 What report describes the number of guards at the seizure?  [* · reports]
- 44:47/q10/p1 Seventy thousand keepers rush toward the condemned person when God orders his seizure. — Ibn Kathīr transmits this as a report without a named transmitter in the note. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 44:47/q11 How does this scene relate to other Quranic passages?  [* · links]
turns on: These are different textual connections rather than competing explanations.
- 44:47/q11/p1 37:55 gives a related glimpse of someone in Hell's middle. — Al-Wāḥidī transmits al-Kalbī's comparison when specifying the sinner's destination; Ibn al-Jawzī also cites 37:55 for 'sawāʾ' as center. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 44:47/q11/p2 29:54 supports the image of Hell enveloping the sinner. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the central destination in 44:47 to the statement that Hell surrounds the unbelievers. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 44:47/q11/p3 52:13 likewise describes disbelievers violently thrust into Hell on Judgment Day. — Study Quran cites that passage as a parallel to the violent movement here. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 44:47/q11/p4 96:17–18 identifies the guards as the zabāniyah. — Study Quran cites these verses when naming the angels addressed by God's order. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 44:47/q11/p5 The tormenting food tree mentioned nearby is also connected with 37:62. — Kur'an Yolu says the zaqqūm tree of verse 43 grows in Hell for punishment and cites 37:62; al-Biqāʿī identifies the fiercely blazing Fire here as the place from which the sinner's food tree emerges. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 44:47/q12 How does the following boiling-water description intensify this scene?  [* · rhetoric]
- 44:47/q12/p1 It speaks of pouring torment from boiling water, strengthening the image beyond simply pouring water on the head. — Al-Rāzī compares the expected wording about pouring water from above and calls the actual wording a stronger metaphor, citing 2:250. Iṣlāḥī describes the wardens continuing from seizure and dragging to pouring boiling water over the sinner's head. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 44:47/q13 How does this punishment fit the surrounding account of judgment?  [* · links]
- 44:47/q13/p1 It depicts the disbelievers' fate after arguments for resurrection and just reward and punishment. — Iṣlāḥī says the passage first offers intellectual, historical and natural evidence, then contrasts believers' and disbelievers' fates; he identifies denial of afterlife as the source of Quraysh rebellion and disregard for the future. Kur'an Yolu says afterlife realities are described through worldly perceptions, desires and fears; Judgment Day separates and repays good and evil, innocent and guilty, oppressed and oppressor, and those destined for Paradise and Hell. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 44:47/q14 What inward torment does the scene signify?  [* · inward]
- 44:47/q14/p1 Regret, deprivation, and the burning ache of separation within the fires. — Bursawī adds this spiritual significance to the physical scene. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
