SAMIN-DURR (author): One explanation of the shīn form of the sneezer's prayer is a wish that God steady the sneezer as an animal's legs are steady. SAMIN-DURR (author): Another explanation treats the intensive form as privative: may God remove gloating from the sneezer; with sīn it means restoring him to his former condition after his disturbance. SAMIN-DURR (أبو بكر): Abū Bakr records the usages سمته and سمت عليه, including the hadith wording وسمت عليهما. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو بكر,أبو عبيد,ثعلب)]
- 7:150/q39/p19 Al-Zarkashī states broader rhetorical and exegetical principles. — BURHAN (author): The core of eloquence is placing each word in its most fitting position: replacing it either changes meaning or diminishes the speech’s beauty, since apparent synonyms retain distinct senses. BURHAN (author): A surah combines topics of different kinds to provide broader benefit; isolating each subject in its own surah would let an opponent see proof in only one kind at a time. BURHAN (author): A figurative or metonymic reading is used only when the wording cannot be carried on its literal sense; the passage rejects the preceding figurative reading. BURHAN (author): Whether metonymy requires a contextual indicator depends on the earlier dispute over whether metonymy counts as metaphor. BURHAN (عبد القادر الجرجاني): Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani states in al-Dala'il that metonymy necessarily has a contextual indicator. BURHAN (القاضي argues against): The Qadi rejects calling the Qur’an sajʿ: sajʿ belongs to ordinary speech and to soothsayers, so the label undermines inimitability and prophecy more than the rejected label of poetry. BURHAN (القاضي argues against): A cadence-like surface does not make Qur’anic speech sajʿ: in sajʿ wording follows meaning for rhyme, whereas the Qur’an’s wording follows its intended meaning. [5 notes: BURHAN(عبد القادر الجرجاني); BURHAN(القاضي)-]
- 7:150/q39/p20 Ibn al-Jazarī contrasts genuine and connective hamzas. — NASHR (author): هاؤم is built from هاء meaning take plus a connected plural pronoun, unlike the tanbih hāʾ in هؤلاء and ها أنتم; its hamza is genuine, while هاوم is softened between-between and can be paused on mīm. [1 notes: NASHR]
