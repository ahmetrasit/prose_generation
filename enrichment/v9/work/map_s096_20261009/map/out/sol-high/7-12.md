# 7:12: 19 questions

## 7:12/q01 Who speaks the opening question to Iblis?  [قَالَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the divine speech is direct or mediated, and whether direct speech itself marks prophetic honor.
- 7:12/q01/p1 God addresses Iblis directly. — Jishumi says the miracle signals divine speech; Ibn Ashur explains the shift from 'We said' to 'He said' as iltifāt. Razi asks how direct speech here relates to Moses' distinctive honor; the reported answer is an intermediary. [3 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 7:12/q01/p2 An angel conveys the question on God's behalf. — Abu Ali gives this account in Jishumi. Razi reports scholars who restrict direct speech to prophets and therefore posit an angelic intermediary. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء)]

## 7:12/q02 Why does God ask what prevented Iblis?  [مَا مَنَعَكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the interrogative is read primarily as disclosure and rebuke, an opportunity to answer, or a taunt about punishment.
- 7:12/q02/p1 The question rebukes Iblis and exposes his motive, defiance, and hidden pride. — Zajjaj and Abu Bakr say God already knows the cause; Qurtubi says the question draws out the inward refusal concealed before Adam's creation. Ibn Ashur says the angels learn Iblis's motive; Biqai says listeners learn why he is expelled. Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Jawzi call mā rebuking. Alusi says other suras give different reproaches for several sins. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو بكر); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:12/q02/p2 The question gives Iblis an opportunity to defend himself before expulsion. — Kuranyolu derives a general right of defense from the order of questioning and expulsion. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:12/q02/p3 The question taunts Iblis as though he thought himself safe from punishment. — Burhan rejects other proposed senses as linguistically or temporally unsuitable and understands a taunt about presumed impunity. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 7:12/q03 How is interrogative mā parsed?  [مَا · grammar]
- 7:12/q03/p1 Mā is a nominative interrogative subject, meaning 'what thing prevented you?'. — Farra gives an explicit matching answer, 'your stinginess prevented me'; Zajjaj says no explicit answer is supplied because the question conveys it. Jishumi makes an accusative as the object of manaʿa; Samin calls the following clause mā's predicate. [8 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 7:12/q04 How does 'allā tasjuda' express the reason for Iblis's refusal?  [أَلَّا تَسْجُدَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lā contributes negation, reinforces the rebuke, or is accommodated by ellipsis or a different sense of manaʿa.
- 7:12/q04/p1 Lā is an emphatic or connective addition; the question means 'what prevented you from prostrating?'. — Farra, Akhfash, Zajjaj and others compare 21:95, 57:29, 6:109, 75:1, 90:1, and 38:75, where the question lacks lā. Ibn Qutayba relates its addition to the prior denial; Kufans to 7:11. Zamakhshari says it confirms the act, Shihab says it marks the refused omission, Ibn Jinni likens it to repeating a clause. Samarrai says the stronger rebuke and more frequent prostration forms in Aʿraf call for it. Qurtubi supplies an elided min before an; Samin permits an accusative or genitive after min. Samin explains 'extra' as technical grammar, not meaningless divine speech. [67 notes: FARRA; MAJAZ; AKHFASH(الشاعر); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة,بعض نحويي الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(آخر,بعضهم,قال الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء,الكسائي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج,الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(الكسائي والفراء والزجاج والأكثرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الكسائي والفراء وأبو إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض النحاة); BURHAN(الشلوبين,قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن جني); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قائلون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكسائي، والفراء، والزجاج، والزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب)+; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)+; TAB-FULL-; RAZI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 7:12/q04/p2 An omitted second verb asks what prevented prostration and compelled or made Iblis need not to prostrate. — Tabari supplies 'and made you need not to'; Ibn Kathir calls that strong. The ellipsis is licensed by the prior statement that Iblis did not prostrate; Ibn Atiyya prefers a verb able to govern negation. Burhan describes general ellipsis from context. [12 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); SAMIN-DURR(جماعة); BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(الطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 7:12/q04/p3 Manaʿa means prompted, compelled, or diverted; the negation retains its force. — Jishumi says Iblis was not literally barred from obedience. Qadi, Sakkaki and Ibn Ashur infer the prompting of the opposite act from a deterrent; Tabatabai reads ḥamala or daʿā. Some call this figurative prevention, others compulsion. Alusi says the accounts differ little. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); THALABI(بعضهم); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(جماعة); BURHAN(السكاكي,قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(السكاكي); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ITQAN(السكاكي)+; SAMIN-DURR-; BURHAN-]
- 7:12/q04/p4 Manaʿa means forbade, ordered, or told; the question asks who told Iblis not to prostrate. — Some Basrans and Thaʿlab say speech can prevent action; Wāḥidī also attributes a version to Farrā. Ibn al-Sayyid explains prevention as ordering someone not to act. Tabari objects that Iblis could disobey and would not obey another after defying God. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); THALABI(أحمد بن يحيى ثعلب); WAHIDI-BASIT(أحمد بن يحيى,الفراء); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); SAMIN-DURR(جماعة); BURHAN(ابن السيد); TAB-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 7:12/q04/p5 The literal negation asks what kept Iblis from refraining from prostration. — Razi reports this accusatory question as implying that neither religion, reason nor shame restrained him, like asking an unjust assailant what stopped him. Raghib interprets protection from refusal. Tabari initially gives the surface wording but insists blame targets omission. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); RAZI-FULL+; BURHAN-]
- 7:12/q04/p6 The question asks what protected Iblis from punishment for failing to bow. — Burhan records this proposal before rejecting it as unsuited to the past-tense wording. [1 notes: BURHAN(قيل); BURHAN-]
- 7:12/q04/p7 A missing min before 'allā tasjuda' preserves ordinary manaʿa without making lā extra. — Burhan calls this closer than the other non-addition account. [1 notes: BURHAN(قيل)+]

## 7:12/q05 What do the cited poetic examples prove about lā?  [أَلَّا · grammar]
turns on: How the poetic lā is vocalized and parsed, and whether the line proves the Qurʾanic construction.
- 7:12/q05/p1 Poetic lā may be an extra connective, supporting an emphatic lā here. — Akhfash's unnamed poet is glossed as generosity refusing stinginess; Thaʿlabi gives two an lā examples. Ibn Atiyya and Samin cite 'a-fa-ʿanka lā barq' as yielding 'a-fa-ʿanka barq'. Abu Hayyan and Samin warn that the first poetic proof admits another parse. [13 notes: AKHFASH(الشاعر); ZAJJAJ(قالوا); THALABI(آخر,قال الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 7:12/q05/p2 In 'abā jawduhu lā al-bukhl', lā itself is an object and al-bukhl its accusative substitute. — Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq preserve a meaningful lā: generosity refused 'no', identified with miserliness. Abu Hayyan and Samin also report a causal-object parse, generosity refusing 'no' to avoid miserliness. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); ZAJJAJ+]
- 7:12/q05/p3 With genitive al-bukhl, lā is annexed to it and can name stinginess or generosity. — Yunus transmits Abu ʿAmr's genitive vocalization; 'no' can signify withholding or refusal to withhold. Abu ʿAmr also offers a line without the putative extra lā. This weakens use of the line as decisive evidence. [11 notes: AKHFASH(يونس); TAB-FULL(بعض من روى,يونس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو بن العلاء,يونس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(يونس عن أبي عمرو بن العلاء); TABRISI(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو,يونس); ZAJJAJ(أبو عمرو بن العلاء)+]
- 7:12/q05/p4 In another poem lā may coordinate with an omitted phrase. — Abu Hayyan and Samin's Shaykh supplies 'from you, not from another'; this prevents the line from proving redundancy. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)]

## 7:12/q06 What does 'when I commanded you' modify and emphasize?  [إِذْ أَمَرْتُكَ · grammar]
- 7:12/q06/p1 Idh modifies 'prostrate' and marks the time of God's command. — Samin and Alusi attach the adverb to tasjuda; Bursevi says it refers to God's order. Ibn Ashur infers from the time phrase that Iblis was included in the command. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:12/q06/p2 The wording recalls the command as the reason for sharper blame. — Samarrai contrasts Aʿraf's explicit 'when I commanded you' with Ṣād's 'what I created with My two hands'; Islahi says the honor was God's conferment, not Adam's intrinsic entitlement. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 7:12/q07 Was Iblis included in the prostration command, and what was he?  [أَمَرْتُكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether inclusion in the address establishes angelhood, and whether a separate command must be inferred.
- 7:12/q07/p1 Iblis was included in the command, without that proving he was an angel. — Razi says inclusion appears to suggest angelhood but earlier proofs say otherwise. Ibn Ashur says the time phrase establishes inclusion. Ibn Atiyya calls a connected exception stronger unless a distinct command is posited. Tabatabai cites al-Sadiq that angels mistakenly thought Iblis one of them; 18:50 identifies him as a jinn. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(غير الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL(المعتزلة وبعض الأشاعرة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:12/q07/p2 Iblis was one class of angels, made the progenitor of jinn and able to sin. — Ibn Ashur reports this as the Quran's apparent sense and a view of many jurists, citing 18:50; Zajjaj's detached exception still leaves Iblis commanded. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 7:12/q07/p3 Iblis received a separate specific command alongside the angels' general command. — Samarrai compares the angelic order in 2:34 with God's direct 'I commanded you' here, so membership among angels need not follow. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 7:12/q07/p4 Iblis was a jinn distinct from angels, though placed with them for the command. — Ibn Ashur reports the Muʿtazilite and some Ashʿarite view, with ʿAʾisha's light and fire report; he allows material fire as light mixed with matter. Tabatabai rejects unsupported consensus that angels and jinn are subtle shape-shifting bodies. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(المعتزلة وبعض الأشاعرة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]

## 7:12/q08 What legal force does the command and Iblis's rebuke show?  [أَمَرْتُكَ · law]
- 7:12/q08/p1 An unqualified divine command entails obligation. — Zajjaj distinguishes denying an obligation, which is unbelief, from Adam and Eve's confessed and repented sin; Iblis's defiance was disbelief. Razi says blame follows simply from God's commanding, and Qurtubi and others infer the same. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL(العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 7:12/q08/p2 The command requires immediate compliance. — Razi's proponents argue that delay could not have earned immediate blame otherwise. Baydawi and Abu Hayyan infer both duty and promptness; Alusi records replies locating immediacy in 'fall prostrate' or contesting the consequence particle. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(المحتجون بأن الأمر يفيد الفور); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:12/q09 How does 'I am better than him' answer the question?  [قَالَ أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌۭ مِّنْهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the comparison is treated as an elliptical answer or a diversion from the charge.
- 7:12/q09/p1 It answers indirectly: Iblis's presumed superiority is the reason he would not bow. — Farra contrasts a grammatically matched reply; Wahidi's Abu Bakr compares answering 'whose house?' with 'its owner is Zayd'. Tabari says Iblis boasts instead of directly naming the obstacle. Tabatabai says the fronted 'I' foregrounds ego; Burhan notes an objection that it answers a different question. [21 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,الزجاج,الفراء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 7:12/q09/p2 Iblis diverts to a separate comparison that cannot excuse his disobedience. — Burhan's reply to the objection says he shifted to an unanswerable issue; Elmalili says he argued back instead of confessing and apologizing. [2 notes: BURHAN; ELMALILI]

## 7:12/q10 Why did Iblis refuse to prostrate?  [أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌۭ مِّنْهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the refusal is explained by his stated material ranking, the pride beneath it, or his use of that ranking against command.
- 7:12/q10/p1 He believed himself superior because fire was nobler than Adam's clay and a superior should not bow to an inferior. — Muqatil says fire overcomes clay. Ibn Abbas reports Iblis also claimed to be older and stronger; fire is said to rise, shine, act and convey life, while clay is heavy, dark and receptive. Razi reports the analogy with leading jurists serving a lesser one. Elmalili calls the premises concealed sophistry. [38 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(إبليس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:12/q10/p2 Arrogance, envy, and self-regard made him refuse and exposed prior insincerity. — Qurtubi says the intent was concealed before Adam's formation and surfaced when angels bowed; Qushayri says earlier service was not sincere. Qatada calls pride and envy the beginning of sins. Islahi says jealousy at humanity's preference fueled later hostility; Elmalili says the first conflict of command and desire revealed self-service. [25 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,بعضهم,قيل); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:12/q10/p3 He reasoned from personal analogy against God's explicit command. — Wahidi and Qurtubi cite Ibn Abbas that obedience should precede analogy. Baydawi calls this the origin of arrogant rational moral judgment. Elmalili says a true material contrast was joined to false premises; Tabatabai says the real contest was with God's majesty. [26 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:12/q10/p4 In an inward reading, seeing one's own substance and worship displaced reliance on the Bestower. — Sulami's Wasiti says claiming 'I' through ritual practice itself is ruinous; Ibn Ata says self-regard veils reverence for God. Qushayri says merit is God's allotment and a claimed independent rank encroaches on lordship. [17 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,بعضهم,قيل); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); QASHANI]

## 7:12/q11 Does fire's origin make Iblis better than Adam?  [خَلَقْتَنِى مِن نَّارٍۢ وَخَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن طِينٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether raw elements rank created persons, whether earth has the better qualities, and what standard gives created beings merit.
- 7:12/q11/p1 Iblis claims fire's light, purity, force, subtlety and upward movement make him superior. — Muqatil adds that fire overcomes clay; Razi adds warmth ripening fruit and growth, against cold age. These are presented as Iblis's premise by the sources, not their verdict. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI(إبليس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, R, Ṭ)]
- 7:12/q11/p2 Clay or earth has stronger claims to excellence than fire. — Tabari, Qaffal and others contrast clay's gravity, patience, stability, modesty and receptivity to repentance with fire's rashness, pride and destructiveness; Tabari adds prior divine decree. Clay gathers and sustains plants, earth supports habitation, food and purification. Thaʿlabi and Qurtubi cite Paradise's musk soil, no reported Paradise fire or Hell soil, fire as punishment, and fire's need for earth as a place. Tabarsi defines this as practical benefit, not reward for inert matter; Qurtubi cites 39:16; Study Quran cites 4:43 and 5:6. Ibn Atiyya questions Tabari's trait inference. [36 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(الحكماء); BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن جرير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q,Q, R, Ṭ,R, Q, Ṭ); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 7:12/q11/p3 Material origin cannot by itself establish a creature's rank; God-given form, deeds, purpose and end decide it. — Razi says God brings believers from unbelievers and vice versa; a believing Abyssinian exceeds an unbelieving Qurashi. Ibn Atiyya says both materials are inert and Adam's spirit is not clay; Abu Hayyan and Alusi use similar arguments, the latter adding thorn-tree fruit and a bulb's flower. Biqai judges by end, Tabatabai by divine care; Ibn Ashur stresses arrangement and capacities. Elmalili says both are equally created; Kuranyolu calls disobedience unjustified. [41 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(الحكماء); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL(محمد بن جرير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض العلماء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]

## 7:12/q12 What special honor to Adam did Iblis's material comparison overlook?  [وَخَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن طِينٍۢ · interpretation]
- 7:12/q12/p1 God formed Adam specially, breathed His spirit into him, taught him, made him vicegerent, and commanded prostration in his honor. — Tabari lists creation by God's hand, spirit, names and angelic prostration; Baydawi adds form and purpose. Tabatabai says 'two hands' signals special care whatever its interpretation. Bursevi's Persian verse contrasts outward appearance with divine light. Islahi and Alusi say bowing at God's command was ultimately to God, not a shameful service to an inferior. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(ظافر الاسكندري); ELMALILI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:12/q12/p2 Adam's honor follows God's choice and wisdom, not any entitlement of his material. — Qushayri assigns superiority to God's allotment; Tabarsi says obedience follows divine wisdom and servants' interests. Bursavi quotes the Masnavi making piety the measure, not lineage. Islahi says God's command conferred the status. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 7:12/q13 What do the fire and clay origins imply about Iblis and created beings?  [خَلَقْتَنِى مِن نَّارٍۢ وَخَلَقْتَهُۥ مِن طِينٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether the stated materials are literal origins, dominant constituents, or read inwardly as faculties.
- 7:12/q13/p1 The origins are real; Iblis is a created jinn associated with fire, Adam with clay. — Mujahid says Iblis's descendants were made from water. Ibn Ashur says Iblis could learn his origin from God or witnessing angels; 55:14–15 and 18:50 confirm it. Tabatabai says later state and detailed creation are undisclosed and possibly differ from first existence; Asad points to 38:76. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة,عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو صالح); ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:12/q13/p2 The elements may be dominant constituents rather than the whole physical composition. — Baydawi and Alusi suggest clay and fire predominate in bodies made of several elements; Ibn Ashur says clay includes life-supporting water and fire has material rays. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:12/q13/p3 The passage supports generation, corruption and the bodily creation of Iblis and devils. — Baydawi and Alusi derive the changing composition of originated bodies, against pre-eternal spirits. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:12/q13/p4 The imaginative faculty is the inward Iblis, tied to the fiery animal spirit. — Qashani locates it in vaporous humors ascending from heart to brain; heat fosters elevation. The faculty can discern lower capacities but not higher human spiritual perfections, so it overreaches and refuses. [3 notes: QASHANI]

## 7:12/q14 What do reports say about the creation of angels, Iblis and Adam?  [خَلَقْتَنِى مِن نَّارٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted wording and attributed source of each creation report.
- 7:12/q14/p1 A report from ʿAʾisha says angels were made from light, Iblis or jinn from fire, and Adam as described in the Qur'an. — Ibn Kathir cites Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim's version for angels, Iblis and Adam; Ibn Mardawayh's variant has light of the Throne and jinn. A wording outside the sound collection adds houris from saffron. Ibn Ashur cites the report in discussing whether jinn differ from angels. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة,عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(المعتزلة وبعض الأشاعرة)]
- 7:12/q14/p2 Abu Salih says Iblis came from the fire of might and angels from the light of might. — Durr reports this distinct provenance. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو صالح)]

## 7:12/q15 What does this verse say about the origin and limits of analogy in religion?  [أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌۭ مِّنْهُ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported censure is universal or specifically about mistaken, self-directed analogy.
- 7:12/q15/p1 Iblis was the first to reason by analogy or personal religious opinion and erred. — Hasan, Ibn Sirin and Ibn Abbas are reported repeatedly; Ibn Sirin also says sun and moon worship came by analogy. Thaʿlabi and others transmit Ibn Abbas's warning that self-directed religious analogizers are joined with Iblis. Ibn Kathir calls Ibn Jarir's chains for Hasan and Ibn Sirin sound. Durr and Tabatabai transmit prophetic versions, and Jaʿfar says followers of such opinion join Iblis at resurrection. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن سيرين,الحسن,الحسن وابن سيرين); THALABI(ابن سيرين,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن سيرين,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وابن سيرين,الطبري); TABRISI(ابن سيرين,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن سيرين,ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس والحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن سيرين,الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن,جعفر بن محمد,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(جعفر بن محمد); TABATABAI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 7:12/q15/p2 The first-analogy reports target corrupt reasoning, not all principled inference. — Tabari reads Hasan and Ibn Sirin as condemning erroneous analogy; Ibn Atiyya says their words warned of Khārijite and other false analogies, not a blanket rejection. Wahidi says Ibn Abbas warns against personal opinion. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 7:12/q16 When is legal analogy valid in light of Iblis's example?  [أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌۭ مِّنْهُ · law]
turns on: Whether analogy is being used against a governing command, has legal foundations, and can restrict a general text.
- 7:12/q16/p1 Grounded analogy is valid or required where no text governs; analogy opposing text is invalid. — Wahidi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and Study Quran distinguish Iblis's opposition to command from scholarly inference. Qurtubi cites majority acceptance, Bukhari's sequence of scripture, Sunna, consensus then analogy, Tabari's binding duty of inference, consensus claimed by Abu Tammam, and examples: Ali's imamate and wine-penalty analogies, Abu Bakr on zakat and prayer, Umar's instruction to Abu Musa and reasoning about plague. Qaffal and Abu al-Husayn al-Basri call analogy rationally obligatory. Qurtubi calls baseless conjecture prohibited by 17:36. [25 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر,أبو تمام المالكي,ابن عباس,البخاري,الدارقطني,الطبري,القفال,علي,عمر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 7:12/q16/p2 Analogy is rationally and legally impossible or generally rejected. — Qurtubi reports Nazzam and some literalists; Study Quran notes Jaʿfarī rejection and that Hanbalis usually oppose or rarely use qiyās. Ibn Abbas's and Jaʿfar's reports are invoked against it. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النظام); DURR-FULL(جعفر بن محمد); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); QURTUBI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 7:12/q16/p3 A general scriptural command cannot be narrowed by analogy. — Razi reports opponents of specification: Iblis's fire-clay comparison exempted him from a command including him, and the severe blame condemns that move. Alusi reports the rival view that he accepted the command and objected to its wisdom; he says exemption may instead nullify the text outright. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(المحتجون بمنع تخصيص العموم بالقياس); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 7:12/q17 What wider moral pattern does Iblis's answer reveal?  [أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌۭ مِّنْهُ · interpretation]
- 7:12/q17/p1 Self-assertion and judging by lineage or matter conceal dependence on God. — Sulami's Wasiti says even the bare 'I' would ruin him; Qushayri says rank is God's gift. Bursevi's poems warn against outward appearance and analogy before certainty. Elmalili says learned material reduction repeats Iblis's error; Islahi calls lineage-based superiority satanic. [21 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,بعضهم,قيل); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:12/q17/p2 The contrast with Adam shows refusal versus confession, repentance and hope. — Zajjaj contrasts Iblis's persisted defiance with Adam and Eve in 7:23; Study Quran says Iblis seeks revenge while Adam accepts guilt; Tabari and Qaffal attribute the outcomes to their different tendencies under divine decree. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; TAB-FULL; THALABI(الحكماء); QURTUBI-FULL(القفال); NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:12/q18 How is this dialogue related to other tellings and to the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether differences among the accounts carry rhetorical force and how this scene serves the sūra.
- 7:12/q18/p1 Al-Aʿraf's shorter, stronger wording conveys greater divine displeasure than Ṣād's fuller exchange. — Samarrai compares omission of Iblis's name, added emphatic lā and explicit command here with Ṣād's named address and 'two hands'; Ṣād adds fa, 'Lord' and a set time to the respite exchange. Aʿraf's story has more emphatic forms and nine prostration derivatives versus Ṣād's three; its ending has angels prostrating. Its opening letters, especially alif and lām, suit the added lā; earlier scholars noted sūra-wide letter patterns and Jaʿfar counted alif-lām-rāʾ 220 times in its sūra. Burhan records the presence of lā in Aʿraf and Hijr but absence in Ṣād. Ibn Ashur cross-refers to the Aaron question in 20:92–93. [21 notes: BURHAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(القدامى,جعفر بن الزبير); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:12/q18/p2 The episode prepares the sūra's warning about Satan's lasting hostility and temptation. — Ibn Ashur links it to 7:27 and shirk; Islahi traces hostility to jealousy over preference for Adam and the challenge Satan seeks to prove. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:12/q18/p3 The repeated 'he said' carries the dialogue forward and marks a shift of address. — Alusi says the first resumes the story after an implied question and the second introduces Iblis's reply; Ibn Ashur calls the shift from 'We said' to 'He said' iltifāt. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:12/q18/p4 The narrative states real relations and a disobeyable command. — Tabatabai rejects treating the prostration and tree commands as creative or ontological, because such commands cannot be disobeyed; he records the objection distinguishing creative from legislative command. [3 notes: TABATABAI]
- 7:12/q18/p5 The parallel verses confirm Iblis's fire claim without endorsing his claim of merit. — Study Quran points to 15:33 and 38:76; Asad points to his note at 38:76; Tabatabai cites 18:50, 15:26–27 and 55:14–15 while stressing God's choice of Adam. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]

## 7:12/q19 What ancillary language and narrative notes accompany the source set?  [* · links]
turns on: These supplied rows concern separate examples or general principles alongside the verse notes.
- 7:12/q19/p1 Sibawayh surveys derivational patterns for ailments, emotions, qualities and related adjectives. — He groups faʿila/yafʿalu with faʿal and faʿil, alternatives faʿīl and faʿlān, semantic extensions from bodily affliction to fear, sorrow, defects and agitation, and afʿala pairings. Abū al-Khaṭṭāb equates ahyam and haymān. He also notes min omitted after fear verbs. These notes concern other lexical examples. [25 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو الخطاب)]
- 7:12/q19/p2 Sibawayh surveys verbs with one or two objects and limited omission of prepositions. — He uses 7:155 for ikhtāra, distinguishes naming from calling, and cites poetry by Farazdaq, ʿAmr b. Maʿdīkarib, Mutalammis and an unnamed poet. He notes that not all verbs permit omitted prepositions or extra objects. [13 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 7:12/q19/p3 Jurjani distinguishes verb-focused from object-focused negation and their compatible contrasts. — He contrasts 'mā ḍarabtu Zaydan' with 'mā Zaydan ḍarabtu'; fronted 'I' plus illā conflicts with the asserted exception. Object-fronting presupposes an act but changes its object. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 7:12/q19/p4 The Kashshaf excerpts discuss Pharaoh, Moses and Israel in 7:127–129. — They parse wa-yadharaka as coordinated, responsive, accusative with implicit an, nominative, jussive, or Anas's nūn reading; discuss wa-ilāhatika, Pharaoh's idols and renewed killing, Moses' reassurance, inheritance of Egypt or earth, the good end, Ubayy and Ibn Masʿud's accusative, and ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd's application to al-Manṣūr. [23 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبي وابن مسعود,أنس,الحسن,عمرو بن عبيد)]
- 7:12/q19/p5 Other side notes give general language or usage observations. — Majaz introduces concise expression without repeating material, though the excerpt stops before its example; Burhan gives a general rule for blame in divine questions and for ellipsis; Ibn Kathir glosses ablasa as despair of mercy; Samarrai corrects an apology construction. [5 notes: MAJAZ; BURHAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
