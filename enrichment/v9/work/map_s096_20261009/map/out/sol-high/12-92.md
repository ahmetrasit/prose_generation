# 12:92: 19 questions

## 12:92/q01 What does تثريب mean?  [تَثْرِيبَ · meaning]
turns on: The glosses range from ordinary verbal censure through the root's corruption or stripping image to concrete harm and aversion.
- 12:92/q01/p1 Reproach, blame, rebuke, shaming, taunting, or enumerating another's sins. — Ibn Qutayba, Tha'lab, al-Wahidi, al-Jishumi and al-Tabrisi trace it to naming or counting faults; Ibn al-A'rabi gives tharraba and athraba. Abu Ubayda, al-Jishumi and al-Wahidi cite a line about pardon without reproach; al-Qurtubi cites Bishr's mutathrib and al-Asma'i's synonym ta'rib. Ibn Qutayba, al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and Ibn al-Jawzi cite the hadith requiring an enslaved adulteress's penalty without later reproach. Abu Muslim, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Bursevi and al-Alusi derive the term from tharb, thin abdominal fat: blame strips covering, honor or beauty; Abu Muslim stresses intensified blame. Al-Raghib compares Yathrib (33:13), and al-Jishumi cites the hadith gloss samahiq al-shahm. Al-Biqa'i describes stripping off pardon and associations of ruin, persistence and confinement. [55 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن إسحاق,سفيان,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن إسحاق,سفيان بن عيينة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس,أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس ومحمد بن إسحاق وسفيان,الحديث,الكلبي,ثعلب عن ابن الأعرابي); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,ثعلب,سفيان، وأبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن الأعرابي، أبو عبيدة، ثعلب، أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,ثعلب); RAZI-FULL(الرسول عليه الصلاة والسلام); QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,بشر,سفيان الثوري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); SAMIN-DURR(الراغب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:92/q01/p2 Corruption, spoiling, disturbance or damage, given as an underlying or extended sense. — Abu Ubayda derives it from fasad; Ibn Qutayba gives the idiom tharraba alayna; al-Tha'labi and al-Zajjaj call the corruption sense Hijazi usage. Al-Biqa'i associates the root with ruin. Al-Majaz also includes clamor and confusion. [14 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة,أبو عبيدة، وأبو مسلم); TABRISI(ابن الأعرابي، أبو عبيدة، ثعلب، أبو مسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BIQAI-FULL]
- 12:92/q01/p3 Punishment or harm. — Al-Majaz and Abu Ubayda include punishment; al-Mawardi lists it as a fourth gloss, and Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan allow it. Al-Jishumi says harm would spoil the brothers' situation. [9 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج); JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة، وأبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 12:92/q01/p4 Aversion, resistance or objection. — Al-Mawardi attributes lack of aversion to Mujahid; al-Durr transmits a Mujahid gloss as lack of resistance or objection. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد)]

## 12:92/q02 What does Joseph promise with لا تثريب عليكم?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously take the negation as ending censure, preserving the relationship, removing concrete penalty, or denying aversion.
- 12:92/q02/p1 He pardons his brothers and will not blame, rebuke, taunt, publicize or recall their offense. — Al-Tabari and Ibn Ishaq locate the wrong against Joseph himself; al-Suddi and al-Kalbi stress that he will not mention it again. Qatadah says he neither broadcast nor reproached it. Al-Tha'labi calls his answer forbearing and guided; Ibn Atiyya calls it gracious, reporting Ikrima that God raised Joseph's renown for it. Ibn Abbas and Abu Salih make the promise permanent; al-Wahidi and al-Baghawi say it begins after that day. The brothers' confession precedes his pardon in al-Razi, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Jishumi; al-Tabataba'i stresses that they stood powerless. The Study Quran calls this a beautiful form of forgiveness. [41 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن إسحاق,ابنا عمران الجوني,سفيان,قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن إسحاق,سفيان بن عيينة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,ابن عباس,ابن عباس ومحمد بن إسحاق وسفيان,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(سفيان، وأبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,سفيان الثوري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق والثوري,السدي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 12:92/q02/p2 He will not alter or damage their brotherly bond and rights. — Al-Tabari explicitly names the rights of brotherhood and cites Sufyan for no change; al-Mawardi transmits Sufyan ibn Uyayna's no alteration gloss. Al-Qurtubi reports al-Zajjaj's kinship and pardon reading. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان); MAWARDI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 12:92/q02/p3 He will inflict no punishment or harm on them. — Al-Mawardi lists no punishment; al-Jishumi says no injury that spoils their condition; the Study Quran observes that reproach can include physical punishment. Al-Qushayri says their shame already sufficed as punishment. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة، وأبو مسلم); QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 12:92/q02/p4 He will neither investigate their deed nor rebuke them over it. — Al-Jishumi attributes this gloss to Sufyan and Abu Ali. [1 notes: JISHUMI(سفيان، وأبو علي)]
- 12:92/q02/p5 He bears no aversion or objection toward them. — Al-Mawardi reports Mujahid's gloss as no aversion to what they said; al-Durr reports Mujahid as no resistance or objection. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(عكرمة ومجاهد)]

## 12:92/q03 How is لا تثريب عليكم constructed grammatically?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ · grammar]
turns on: The parses differ over the omitted predicate, whether عليكم is its expression or an adjective, and what kind of generality the negative form conveys.
- 12:92/q03/p1 لا negates the entire genus, with indefinite تثريب built on fatḥa and an understood predicate; عليكم expresses the predicate or its relation. — Al-Tabrisi calls تثريب an indefinite singular noun built with lā; al-Zarkashi says lā can operate like inna and often omits a known predicate, citing 33:13, 16:62, 11:47 and 2:254. Al-Farisi and al-Samin bar direct government of the following complements by a built verbal noun. Ibn Ashur compares the self-contained idiom لا بأس and Q 75:11. [7 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; TABRISI; BURHAN; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:92/q03/p2 عليكم is an adjective to تثريب, while اليوم is the temporal predicate. — Al-Farisi allows an implied modifier; al-Hawfi gives this parse and Abu Hayyan calls it good; al-Samin states it directly. Al-Farisi also allows اليوم to modify the adjective of عليكم with an omitted predicate. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي); SAMIN-DURR]
- 12:92/q03/p3 عليكم is the predicate, with اليوم attached to it or to its implied stability. — Al-Farisi gives a parse with no separate adjective and also an omitted-predicate variant. Al-Samin says عليكم is the predicate; al-Radi says اليوم is governed by it, while the reverse is possible. Al-Alusi links عليكم to an omitted predicate and allows اليوم on that predicate or its adverbial phrase. [6 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الرضي); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 12:92/q03/p4 An omitted verb supplies 'no reproach will reproach you today.' — Abu Hayyan calls this strong because Arabic often omits a known predicate after lā. Al-Samin reports the parallel to لا عاصم اليوم من أمر الله (11:43), but objects that this verse would require two omissions and a metaphorical verb, where the parallel needs one omission and a literal verb. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 12:92/q03/p5 عليكم is an explanatory complement like لك in سقيا لك, or it and اليوم attach to an implied verb. — Al-Samin records both alternatives, the latter with lā's predicate omitted and a verb supplied from تثريب. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 12:92/q03/p6 لا تثريب is accusative as a verbal noun; اليوم may be accusative under negation or nominative under an implied ليس بتثريب. — Al-Jishumi gives both case analyses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 12:92/q03/p7 A non-governing لا can also negate the whole genus, though its built governing form signals generality more strongly. — Al-Zarkashi rejects al-Zamakhshari and al-Hariri's restriction to one instance; Ibn al-Hajib invokes the grammarians' agreement that indefinites in negation generalize. [2 notes: BURHAN(ابن الحاجب); BURHAN(الزمخشري والحريري)+]
- 12:92/q03/p8 A non-governing لا denies only one instance, rather than the entire genus. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Hariri are reported with this restriction; al-Zarkashi and Ibn al-Hajib object that indefinite negation can be general. [1 notes: BURHAN(الزمخشري والحريري); BURHAN(ابن الحاجب)-]
- 12:92/q03/p9 اليوم is the predicate of لا, while عليكم attaches to the predication implicit in that adverb. — Al-Samin presents this as another construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 12:92/q04 How does اليوم attach, and why specify today?  [ٱلْيَوْمَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The pause after عليكم or اليوم, the scope of today, and whether a built verbal noun may govern a separated adverb decide the readings.
- 12:92/q04/p1 It qualifies no reproach at the very moment blame or revenge would be expected, assuring no later reproach either. — Ibn al-Anbari calls it the first moment of pardon; al-Jishumi calls it the first revelation of Joseph's identity, when revenge was possible, or the end of earlier rebuke. Al-Wahidi says confession ended his previous reproach. Al-Razi argues that denying the whole kind of reproach now excludes every instance in every circumstance; al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, al-Biqa'i and al-Alusi infer still less blame later. Al-Tabataba'i adds that the brothers were powerless. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin attach today to an omitted stability predicate, and most readers pause after today. [26 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان الثوري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الرضي); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 12:92/q04/p2 Today means the present period, or the whole future span of his pardon. — Al-Wahidi cites Imru' al-Qays, and al-Tabrisi and al-Murtada cite poetic lines, to show that اليوم can mean an extended time. Ibn Ashur compares 5:3. Al-Jishumi also reports the whole-span interpretation. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,امرؤ القيس); JISHUMI; TABRISI(الشاعر); ALUSI-FULL(المرتضى); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:92/q04/p3 It begins the forgiveness clause: 'Today God forgives you,' as prayer, glad tidings, or declaration. — Al-Akhfash permits a pause after عليكم; al-Tabrisi reports his preference and analogy to لا ريب فيه. Al-Wahidi calls this weaker because a following prayer ordinarily cannot govern a prior adverb, though the indicative permits it. Al-Samin says the pause explains the reading; Ibn Ashur finds the preceding idiom ends at عليكم and its addition intensifies reassurance. Al-Murtada's attachment is challenged on prayer syntax. The declaration reading draws objection from Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi because certainty requires revelation; al-Alusi considers responses under the forgiveness question. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(المرتضى); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TABRISI(الأخفش)+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 12:92/q04/p4 It directly modifies the verbal noun تثريب. — Al-Zamakhshari lists this among three attachments. Al-Farisi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, the Itqan and Abu al-Baqa object: a built indefinite verbal noun cannot have its complement separated by عليكم, and quasi-annexation would demand inflection or tanwin. Al-Alusi qualifies the tanwin argument with grammatical texts and an agreed hadith where quasi-annexed nouns lack tanwin; authorities dispute whether these are built. Al-Samin recounts al-Farisi's poetry on كفران and Ibn Malik's analysis of لا صمت يوم إلى الليل. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الفارسي,جمال الدين بن مالك); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); FARISI-HUJJA-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-; ITQAN-]

## 12:92/q05 What is Joseph doing by saying يغفر الله لكم?  [يَغْفِرُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the indicative verb is a petition, a certain announcement, or a deferential statement about God's right; certainty raises the question of revelation and the brothers' later request to Jacob.
- 12:92/q05/p1 He prays that God forgive, conceal and spare them from the consequence of their wrong. — Al-Akhfash, al-Tabari, Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, al-Qurtubi, al-Alusi and al-Itqan understand a declarative or future verb functioning as supplication; al-Nasafi notes both past and present prayer formulas. Al-Tabari specifies their wrong against Joseph, and al-Razi seeks removal of afterlife punishment. Al-Biqa'i extends the prayer to past and possible future lapses; the imperfect inspires repentance and hope. Al-Tabataba'i says it embraces absent brothers too. Al-Alusi prefers this because most pause after اليوم and cites al-Tabari and Ibn Ishaq. [23 notes: MUQATIL; AKHFASH; AZRAQI(النبي); TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 12:92/q05/p2 He announces God's forgiveness or gives glad tidings that it has arrived or will soon arrive. — Al-Razi links forgiveness that day to repentance, shame and confession; al-Baydawi says Joseph pardoned and the brothers acknowledged the crime. Al-Nasafi calls it near glad tidings. Ibn Ashur invokes prior revealed laws under which repentance brings forgiveness; revelation is needed only to know the repentance is genuine. Al-Alusi reports that Joseph's pardon entails God's forgiveness under God's promise to accept repentance; he answers Ibn al-Munir's objection from the later request to Jacob (12:97): concealment from punishment is future, and a known promise does not make prayer pointless. The brothers may also have sought Jacob's pardon for offenses against him or not yet believed Joseph a prophet. Ibn Atiyya and al-Qurtubi object that definite forgiveness requires revelation. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(ابن المنير)-]
- 12:92/q05/p3 He respectfully leaves God's own due to God, who may forgive them. — Al-Jishumi offers this alongside the prayer interpretation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 12:92/q06 What grounds or scope do the notes give for divine forgiveness here?  [يَغْفِرُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمْ · theology]
turns on: The grounds named are the brothers' penitence and Joseph's waiver of his own claim; the notes also differ over how broadly the request extends.
- 12:92/q06/p1 Their repentance, confession, shame, regret and return to obedience invite God's forgiveness. — Al-Tabari puts Ibn Ishaq's prayer after admission and says God's mercy reaches those who repent and return. Al-Mawardi lists repentance, confession and regret; Ibn Abi Hatim's Ibn Ismail says Joseph promised forgiveness on acknowledgment. Al-Razi and al-Baydawi likewise join forgiveness to confession, and al-Islahi calls their regret sincere. Abu Hayyan notes that certainty on this ground can reflect a Mu'tazili premise that forgiveness requires repentance; Ibn Ashur places it under prior law. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسماعيل); MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 12:92/q06/p2 Joseph's pardon of his own claim allows or entails God's forgiveness of them. — Al-Mawardi reports that God forgives because Joseph made pardon permissible; al-Alusi reports an inference from Joseph's pardon and God's promise to accept repentance. Al-Bursevi distinguishes Joseph's waived personal claim from what God must forgive. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:92/q06/p3 The request includes all who wronged Joseph, even brothers absent at this meeting. — Al-Tabataba'i invokes a later statement at 12:95 for the absence of some brothers. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 12:92/q06/p4 The prayer reaches their past offenses and possible future lapses. — Al-Biqa'i takes the imperfect as fostering sincere repentance and hope in the Most Merciful. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 12:92/q07 Why call God أرحم الراحمين here?  [وَهُوَ أَرْحَمُ ٱلرَّٰحِمِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the closing mercy formula points chiefly to the brothers' hoped-for forgiveness or Joseph's own elevation.
- 12:92/q07/p1 God's greater mercy gives hope that He will accept Joseph's prayer and forgive repentant sinners. — Al-Mawardi also takes it as God's mercy in pardoning the brothers' earlier offense. Al-Tabari says God is most merciful to those who return to obedience; al-Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas that God is more merciful to devoted servants than parents to children. Abu Hayyan makes mercy the reason for Joseph's hope. Al-Nasafi contrasts Joseph's poor, miserly human mercy with the rich, forgiving God's; al-Baydawi says God forgives small and great sins. Al-Alusi says all created mercy comes from God, while God alone forgives every scale of sin and accepts penitents. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:92/q07/p2 It recalls God's mercy in making Joseph a king. — Al-Mawardi gives this as an alternative; al-Tabrisi likewise connects Joseph's pardon with God's elevation of him. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 12:92/q08 How was Joseph's pardon applied at the conquest of Mecca?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · reports]
- 12:92/q08/p1 The Prophet quoted Joseph's pardon to Quraysh after asking what they expected, and forgave or released them. — Al-Azraqi places the exchange after two rak'ahs in the Kaaba and includes a prayer for forgiveness. Al-Tha'labi transmits Ata from Ibn Abbas; al-Qurtubi's Ibn Abbas report says Umar was ashamed of his earlier wish for revenge. Al-Nasafi has Quraysh appeal to a noble kinsman; al-Durr preserves accounts from Amr ibn Shu'ayb, Ibn Abbas and Abu Hurayra, with variants in the quoted length. Al-Razi, al-Islahi and the Study Quran also transmit the precedent; the Study Quran says he freed them, and al-Islahi calls them former enemy leaders. [10 notes: AZRAQI(النبي); THALABI(عطاء); RAZI-FULL(الرسول عليه الصلاة والسلام); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,عمرو بن شعيب); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Prophet Muḥammad (sws)); STUDYQURAN(Prophet Muhammad)]

## 12:92/q09 What other proclamations accompany the conquest-day pardon report?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · reports]
turns on: These are distinct accompanying proclamations, rather than rival versions of one ruling.
- 12:92/q09/p1 The Prophet abolished pre-Islamic claims for usury, blood and property while preserving Kaaba custodianship and pilgrims' water service. — Al-Azraqi transmits this in the same conquest account. [1 notes: AZRAQI(النبي)]
- 12:92/q09/p2 He ended ancestral boasting, recalling descent from Adam and honor by piety. — Al-Azraqi connects this proclamation to 49:13. [1 notes: AZRAQI(النبي)]
- 12:92/q09/p3 He assigned aggravated blood payment of one hundred camels, forty pregnant, for a mistaken quasi-intentional killing with stick or whip. — Al-Azraqi records this legal detail. [1 notes: AZRAQI(النبي)]
- 12:92/q09/p4 He declared Mecca sacred from creation, with only a one-hour permission to him that day. — Al-Azraqi reports that its sanctity held before and after him. [1 notes: AZRAQI(النبي)]
- 12:92/q09/p5 He barred disturbing Mecca's game, cutting thorn trees or grass, and taking lost property except to announce it. — Al-Azraqi lists these restrictions. [1 notes: AZRAQI(النبي)]
- 12:92/q09/p6 He excepted idhkhir from the vegetation ban. — Al-Azraqi says al-Abbas requested it for smiths and roofs; the Prophet paused before allowing it. [1 notes: AZRAQI(النبي)]
- 12:92/q09/p7 A later herald forbade bequests to an heir. — Al-Azraqi says the herald was sent after leaving Mecca. [1 notes: AZRAQI(مناديا)]
- 12:92/q09/p8 The herald assigned the child to the marital bed and the stone to the adulterer. — Al-Azraqi transmits this proclamation. [1 notes: AZRAQI(مناديا)]
- 12:92/q09/p9 The herald said a woman may not give away her property without her husband's permission. — Al-Azraqi transmits this additional proclamation. [1 notes: AZRAQI(مناديا)]

## 12:92/q10 What is reported about invoking the brothers' words for Abu Sufyan and another petitioner?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · reports]
- 12:92/q10/p1 Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith was coached to approach the Prophet with the brothers' words, and the Prophet answered or prayed for forgiveness. — Ibn Atiyya says Ali coached Abu Sufyan and Abdullah ibn Abi Umayya, and the Prophet answered 'no reproach.' Al-Razi and al-Nasafi say al-Abbas coached Abu Sufyan, after which the Prophet prayed for him and the person who taught him. The accounts differ over the adviser and accompanying petitioner. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(العباس); NASAFI-FULL(العباس)]

## 12:92/q11 What pardon at Uhud is associated with Joseph's words?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ · reports]
- 12:92/q11/p1 Hudhayfa forgave Muslims who mistakenly killed his father Husayl and gave them the offered blood money. — Ibn Hisham reports Mahmud ibn Labid's account: Husayl ibn Jabir and Thabit ibn Waqsh left the fortifications seeking martyrdom; polytheists killed Thabit, while Muslims failed to recognize Husayl and killed him. Hudhayfa's pardon brought him further esteem from the Prophet. [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(حذيفة,محمود بن لبيد)]

## 12:92/q12 What happened when Joseph's brothers were ashamed to accept his hospitality?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ · reports]
- 12:92/q12/p1 Joseph reassured them that their presence and kinship to Abraham increased his standing among Egyptians. — Al-Razi and al-Baydawi say their visits honored him; al-Nasafi and al-Biqa'i add that Egyptians once saw him as a slave sold for twenty dirhams but now knew his Abrahamic descent. Al-Alusi reports the same dinner embarrassment and answer, with wording varying across reports. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(يوسف عليه السلام); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:92/q13 What is reported about Joseph sending his shirt to Jacob?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over what immediately prompted the shirt: news of Jacob's blindness or Jacob's letter.
- 12:92/q13/p1 After asking about Jacob and learning of his lost sight, Joseph gave his brothers his shirt. — Al-Baghawi places this immediately after Joseph identified himself. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 12:92/q13/p2 Jacob's letter pleading that Benjamin was no thief moved Joseph to tears and to send his shirt to restore Jacob's sight. — Al-Durr transmits this report from Wahb ibn Munabbih; the letter recounts the family's trials. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 12:92/q14 What other report shows Joseph's view of his ancestry?  [* · reports]
- 12:92/q14/p1 Joseph claimed greater grounds for pride as Abraham's, Isaac's and Jacob's descendant when the king limited his role. — Al-Alusi transmits Ibn Abbas's report that the king wanted Joseph involved in all matters except his household. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 12:92/q15 Why did Joseph forgive at once while Jacob deferred his prayer?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ يَغْفِرُ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations invoke age, affection, differing harm and benefit, delayed readiness to pray, or how the sons addressed each father and brother.
- 12:92/q15/p1 Young Joseph was easier to ask, forgiving at once; older Jacob promised to ask later. — Ata al-Khurasani gives this contrast through Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi and al-Durr. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عطاء الخراساني); RAZI-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); QURTUBI-FULL(عطاء الخراساني); DURR-FULL(عطاء الخراساني)]
- 12:92/q15/p2 Jacob loved them more and first reproached them; Joseph forgave promptly on their confession. — Al-Qushayri offers this contrast and notes the brothers' shame itself punished them. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 12:92/q15/p3 Joseph saw benefit and blessing from their act, whereas Jacob had borne continuing grief and blindness. — Al-Bursevi reports his shaykh that Joseph's gratitude led to immediate forgiveness while Jacob awaited sincere, divinely permitted intent. Al-Samarrai says the deed benefited Joseph but continued to hurt Jacob (12:91, 12:97). [3 notes: BURSEVI(شيخي وسندي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 12:92/q15/p4 Their requests differed: they did not explicitly ask Joseph for forgiveness, but emphatically confessed sin and requested it from Jacob. — Al-Samarrai contrasts their light in with their father-directed inna and cites 12:98. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 12:92/q16 What moral example does Joseph's pardon set?  [لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْيَوْمَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes explain the example through noble pardon or awareness of divine decree.
- 12:92/q16/p1 It is exceptional, generous forgiveness to emulate, especially when the forgiver has power and receives an apology. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports the sons of Imran al-Jawni had heard no pardon like it, and Malik ibn Dinar reports al-Hasan that Scripture preserves it as an example; al-Durr transmits Abu Imran's praise. Ibn Atiyya reports Ikrima that God raised Joseph's renown; al-Alusi says noble people accept apology and forgive when able. Al-Qushayri says their shame was punishment enough. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابنا عمران الجوني,مالك بن دينار); QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 12:92/q16/p2 A person who sees events within God's decree need not dwell on another's offense. — Al-Alusi transmits Shah al-Kirmani's explanation that Joseph excused his brothers because he understood destiny. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(شاه الكرماني)]

## 12:92/q17 What inward reading is given to the brothers' wrongdoing?  [* · inward]
- 12:92/q17/p1 Their original wrong became a means of Joseph's elevation and kingship, prompting his prayer for them. — Al-Bursevi transmits the Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya's inward reading. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 12:92/q18 How is Joseph's reply related to his brothers' confession and the surrounding story?  [* · links]
turns on: These notes link the reply to confession, the earlier prophecy, divine favor and the larger sūra theme.
- 12:92/q18/p1 Their admission and apology immediately elicit his pardon. — Al-Razi says he answers their admission of guilt; al-Tabari's Ibn Ishaq has the prayer after they acknowledge sin; Kuranyolu likewise narrates admission and pardon. Al-Samarrai contrasts this admission with the emphatic request later made to Jacob. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 12:92/q18/p2 Joseph's identification fulfills the earlier promise to tell his brothers of their deed while they did not know him. — Kuranyolu connects the recollection of what they did to Joseph and Benjamin with 12:15. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 12:92/q18/p3 The brothers' envy could not block God's decreed favor to Joseph. — Kuranyolu cites the Prophet's prayer that no one can withhold God's grant or grant what God withholds, and Ziya Pasha's couplet that power makes tyrants admit God's preference. The brothers acknowledge the preference. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah,Ziyâ Paşa)]
- 12:92/q18/p4 The Joseph story displays God's inscrutable direction: people may dislike what benefits them or love what harms them. — Asad contrasts the Qur'anic story with Genesis' romantic account and cites 2:216. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:92/q18/p5 The nearby word fand means corruption. — Abu Hayyan cites poetry illustrating the term and its verb. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 12:92/q19 What rhetorical force can a declaration of no reproach have?  [قَالَ لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ · rhetoric]
- 12:92/q19/p1 The declarative report serves in place of a request or command. — Al-Zarkashi includes Joseph's words among reports used with this force. [1 notes: BURHAN]
