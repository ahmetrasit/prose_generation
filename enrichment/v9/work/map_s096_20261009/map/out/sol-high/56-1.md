# 56:1: 29 questions

## 56:1/q01 What does idhā convey here?  [إِذَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether idhā retains conditional adverbial force, functions as a noun, or is treated as extra.
- 56:1/q01/p1 It marks the assured future occurrence of the Resurrection, with a conditional sense. — Naḥḥās distinguishes its specified time from open-ended in; al-Burhān contrasts certain idhā with ordinarily uncertain in, citing 56:1 and other assured events. Bursevī says the future event is worded as present or past because it is certain. [6 notes: NAHHAS; BURHAN; ELMALILI; BURSEVI]
- 56:1/q01/p2 It can function as a noun meaning the time when, with another idhā as its predicate. — Ibn Jinnī and Abū al-Faḍl al-Rāzī parse the two idhās as subject and predicate; Ibn Mālik adduces this as evidence for nominal idhā. Al-Ṭabrisī reports Abū al-Ḥasan's related genitive analysis after ḥattā and nominative clauses. Al-Itqān reports majority objections. [6 notes: TABRISI(أبو الحسن,ابن جني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جني وأبو الفضل الرازي,ابن مالك); ITQAN(ابن جني); TABRISI-; ITQAN(الجمهور)-]
- 56:1/q01/p3 It is a temporal adverb without conditional force. — Al-Zamakhsharī's Friday illustration, discussed by Abū Ḥayyān, illustrates the nonconditional usage; al-Samīn reports an adverb attached to laysa. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR]
- 56:1/q01/p4 It may be treated as an extra particle. — Al-Jurjānī compares the near-future wording of 54:1 and 16:1 and saying the fast has come when it is near. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجرجاني)]
- 56:1/q01/p5 Some Arabs give idhā a jussive force in poetry. — Sībawayh calls the conditional analogy strong, but Naḥḥās limits this usage to poetry. [1 notes: NAHHAS(سيبويه)]

## 56:1/q02 What governs idhā, and what answers its clause?  [إِذَا وَقَعَتِ · grammar]
turns on: Which word or omitted element governs the opening temporal expression, and whether an explicit later clause supplies its response.
- 56:1/q02/p1 An unspoken command to remember governs it. — Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī supply 'remember'; al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān note that this makes idhā an object rather than a temporal adverb. Al-Kashshāf and al-Samīn also give the analysis. [10 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 56:1/q02/p2 An omitted, unspecified answer governs it; the omission magnifies the Event. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn allow an implied 'such-and-such will happen.' Al-Biqāʿī, al-Kashf, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran emphasize awe; Ṭabāṭabāʾī understands believers prospering and unbelievers losing. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI(مفسرون كثيرون); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bazı müfessirler); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)+]
- 56:1/q02/p3 Laysa or the verbal sense of its negation governs it. — Al-Ṭabrisī invokes laysa's implied verbal sense; Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Samīn appeal to the negation's event meaning. Abū Ḥayyān objects that laysa lacks event meaning and that a conditional response needs fāʾ; al-Ālūsī replies that fāʾ is not the default with idhā. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 56:1/q02/p4 The following verb waqaʿat governs the conditional time adverb. — Naḥḥās says conditional likeness favors the following verb; Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh following Makkī defend it. Al-Ālūsī notes the dispute over annexing idhā to its clause. [5 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 56:1/q02/p5 The later account of people's groups or conditions supplies the answer. — Al-Rāzī points to 56:8, al-Qurṭubī to the people of the right, al-Samīn to their condition or the conditional response; the majority in al-Itqān infer division into three groups. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN(الجمهور)]
- 56:1/q02/p6 The later 'when the earth is shaken' explains or governs the first idhā. — Al-Samīn reports rajjat as governor, with the second idhā substituting or repeating; Ibn Jinnī in al-Burhān calls it apposition that tightens the construction. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the shaking clause appositional or explanatory. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; BURHAN(ابن جني); TABATABAI]
- 56:1/q02/p7 An omitted contextual verb, such as 'they are judged,' governs it. — Al-Ṭabrisī supplies believers winning and unbelievers losing; Abū al-Faḍl's example is 'they are judged.' Bursevī leaves the ensuing happenings beyond description. [4 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الفضل الرازي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو الفضل); BURSEVI]
- 56:1/q02/p8 The descriptions 'lowering, raising' govern it, or indicate its implied day. — Abū al-Baqāʾ takes idhā as their adverb; al-Rāzī supplies their day; Elmalılı allows tanāzuʿ between the two descriptions. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 56:1/q02/p9 The two idhā clauses form a nominal subject and predicate construction. — Ibn Jinnī and Abū al-Faḍl treat the first as subject and the second as predicate; the accusative 'lowering, raising' are circumstantial. Al-Ṭabrisī rejects attributing another proposed governor to Ibn Jinnī; most grammarians reject taking idhā out of its adverbial role. [7 notes: TABRISI(أبو الحسن,ابن جني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جني وأبو الفضل الرازي,ابن مالك); ITQAN(ابن جني); ITQAN(الجمهور)-]
- 56:1/q02/p10 The next verse supplies the answer: no one can deny its occurrence. — Elmalılı prefers the apparent continuation; Study Quran reads verse 1 continuously with verse 2. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI+]

## 56:1/q03 What does waqaʿat mean?  [وَقَعَتِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the physical falling image or the broader occurrence sense is being explained.
- 56:1/q03/p1 It means occurred, happened, or came to pass. — Al-Zajjāj says Arabic speaks of an expected thing as occurring when it comes. Al-Dāmghānī gives 'arose'; Ibn al-Jawzī cites 51:6, 52:7 and 77:7. Al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān describe the arrival of an awaited matter. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 56:1/q03/p2 Its basic physical image is falling from high to low. — Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes the original falling sense from a matter happening and cites places where rain falls; Jishumī describes an object appearing before an observer by falling. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; JISHUMI; STUDYQURAN]

## 56:1/q04 Why is the verb phrased as a past event?  [وَقَعَتِ · rhetoric]
- 56:1/q04/p1 The past form expresses the certainty of a future Resurrection. — Bursevī joins the past verb with the active participle and idhā; al-Ālūsī says the name implies occurrence already certain independently of the conditional. Islahi says Judgment cannot be escaped. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 56:1/q05 Why does waqaʿat have a kasra on its feminine ending?  [وَقَعَتِ · grammar]
- 56:1/q05/p1 The tāʾ takes kasra to avoid two adjacent sukūns. — Naḥḥās says its underlying vowel is sukūn. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 56:1/q06 What is al-Wāqiʿa here?  [ٱلْوَاقِعَةُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the name designates the Day as a whole, one trumpet blast, the falling rock, or an inward event.
- 56:1/q06/p1 The Resurrection, Last Hour, or Day of Judgment. — Ibn ʿAbbās and others call it one of the Resurrection's names beside al-Ṭāmma, al-Ṣākhkha and al-Āzifa; these names warn and convey terror. Sources also invoke 53:57 and 69:15; Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares al-Ḥāqqa, al-Qāriʿa and al-Ghāshiya. [39 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن,السدي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي,علي بن الحسين); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 56:1/q06/p2 The first blast or cry that begins the Hour. — Muqātil calls it the first blast; al-Ḍaḥḥāk's cry is explained as the trumpet blast. Al-Naḥḥās reports the first-blast view; Ibn al-Jawzī says the blast starts the Hour. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 56:1/q06/p3 The final or second blast, which brings resurrection and gathering. — Al-Thaʿlabī pairs Resurrection's dawn with the final blast; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī call it the final blast. Al-Biqāʿī says the second blast gathers all creatures for judgment under God's sole rule. [6 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 56:1/q06/p4 The rock of Jerusalem falling on Resurrection Day. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report this; al-Ālūsī calls it baseless. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 56:1/q06/p5 The seeker's clarified purpose or sudden spiritual realization. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says the seeker's intention becomes clear; Study Quran also allows a person's profound transformation. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); STUDYQURAN]
- 56:1/q06/p6 The minor resurrection. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 56:1/q07 Why is the Resurrection called al-Wāqiʿa?  [ٱلْوَاقِعَةُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the name stresses certainty, severity, its sound, or the force of its form.
- 56:1/q07/p1 Its occurrence is certain and inescapable. — Al-Kashshāf derives the name from inevitable occurrence; al-Qurṭubī says it will occur soon. Ibn Kathīr cites 69:15. Al-Ālūsī rejects treating name and verb as empty repetition; Islahi's verbal-noun comparison is from the following phrase. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 56:1/q07/p2 Its feminine, intensive form conveys gravity and terror. — Al-Rāzī compares a feminine ending that compresses emphasis into one letter and treats the implied event as terrifying; Ibn ʿAbbās's parallels are names that magnify the Day. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 56:1/q07/p3 Many ordeals and severe events happen during it. — Al-Māwardī cites its numerous ordeals; al-Ṭabrisī adds that its impact is exceptionally severe, while al-Qurṭubī reports many calamities. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قول آخر); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 56:1/q07/p4 The definite article presents it as the supreme occurrence. — Al-Biqāʿī says no event deserves the full name as completely as Resurrection; Elmalılı also notes the definite article. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 56:1/q07/p5 Its sound gives it the name. — Abū al-Layth's explanation is reported by Bursevī. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو الليث)]

## 56:1/q08 What noun or form underlies al-Wāqiʿa?  [ٱلْوَاقِعَةُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form is an adjective with an implicit head, a fixed name, or an ordinary noun built from the root.
- 56:1/q08/p1 It is a standalone established name for the Resurrection. — Al-Ālūsī calls it a conventional or transferred name; Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares other standalone Qur'anic names. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 56:1/q08/p2 Its root denotes an occurrence or severe falling. — Jishumī describes appearance through occurrence and the feminine wāqiʿa; al-Rāghib, cited by Bursevī, extends waqʿa to battle and any severe falling. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الراغب); STUDYQURAN]
- 56:1/q08/p3 It is a feminine descriptor of an omitted Resurrection, earthquake, event, or Hour. — Al-Rāzī suggests Resurrection or earthquake, or an unspecified terrifying event. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ordinary Arabic agreement with an implied feminine noun and compares 5:52 and 9:98. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 56:1/q09 What effect has the repetition of waqaʿat and al-Wāqiʿa?  [وَقَعَتِ ٱلْوَاقِعَةُ · rhetoric]
- 56:1/q09/p1 It makes a paronomastic pairing and intensifies the certainty of occurrence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr names the paronomasia; al-Ālūsī says the pairing is meaningful, since the name itself already assures occurrence. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 56:1/q10 What does the opening teach about the coming Judgment?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the opening is developed as an assurance, an account of judgment's effects, or an inward moral lesson.
- 56:1/q10/p1 It will certainly arrive, cannot be turned back, and calls people to prepare. — Ibn ʿAbbās says it cannot be repelled; al-Samīn praises the no-return explanation. Al-Ṭabrisī says the phrase urges preparation, and Islahi says deniers' objections have been answered and the warning is no false threat. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(قول آخر); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 56:1/q10/p2 It divides people by faith and deeds, lowering enemies and raising friends. — Al-Rāzī explains believers raised to Paradise and unbelievers lowered to Hell; ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥusayn likens the worlds to balancing scales, while al-Qummī applies lowering and raising to God's enemies and friends. Al-Biqāʿī connects distinct outcomes to divine power and wisdom. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي,علي بن الحسين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 56:1/q10/p3 It distinguishes sincere spiritual response from pretense. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts uprightness and safety with deviation and loss; his poem compares genuine and feigned mourners. Sahl says prophetic example leads to Truth whereas conjecture leads astray. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 56:1/q10/p4 God's name and sovereignty make His action irresistible. — Al-Qushayrī describes God as eternal, one, self-subsisting, gracious and capable of compelling obedience through affliction; al-Biqāʿī stresses His unshared rule at assembly. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 56:1/q10/p5 Its upheaval reverses height and depth, leveling land and mountains. — Al-Rāzī cites 15:74 and 56:4–5, compares Sodom and the reshaping of sandy ground by wind, and reads the shaking as a sign of severity. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 56:1/q11 What occasion is reported for this opening?  [* · reports]
- 56:1/q11/p1 It followed the polytheists' question about the promised event and victory. — Abū Sulaymān al-Dimashqī is reported by Ibn al-Jawzī. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سليمان الدمشقي)]

## 56:1/q12 How does this opening connect with the preceding sūrah and nearby verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which part of the previous sūrah or surrounding sequence supplies the opening's context.
- 56:1/q12/p1 It develops al-Raḥmān's promised rewards, punishments, and ranked groups. — Jishumī observes al-Raḥmān's close with promise and Paradise; al-Rāzī contrasts gratitude and denial, mercy and awe, and divine majesty. Abū Ḥayyān links its two garden pairs to believers' ranks; al-Biqāʿī and al-Baḥr connect the three groups and later outcomes. [11 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); STUDYQURAN]
- 56:1/q12/p2 The sūrah's title and structure disclose the theme of final judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī says the name signals its subject and its end affirms divine perfection. Ṭabāṭabāʾī summarizes upheaval, three groups, proofs, revelation and death; Study Quran likewise identifies five overlapping sections and the title's referent. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 56:1/q12/p3 It makes explicit the afterlife division prepared by al-Qamar and the preceding sūrahs. — Ibn al-Zubayr links al-Qamar's teaching that every event follows decree, citing 54:49 and 54:52, to this sūrah's explicit final division. Islahi says the earlier sūrahs supplied reason, nature and worldly evidence, while this final Makkan sūrah states the certain outcome. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 56:1/q12/p4 Its later shaking clause explains the opening occurrence. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats 'when the earth is shaken' as apposition or explanation; Ibn Jinnī says repeated idhā binds the two constructions. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BURHAN(ابن جني)]
- 56:1/q12/p5 The two sūrahs mirror or reverse their order of themes. — Some scholars align sky splitting in 55:37 with earth shaking here; al-Ālūsī lists the reversed sequences from Revelation and creation through Resurrection and recompense, and calls this sūrah al-Raḥmān's counterpart. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]

## 56:1/q13 Where was this sūrah revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether later reports except particular verses from the Makkan classification.
- 56:1/q13/p1 It is Makkan. — Al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima, ʿAṭāʾ and Jābir are cited by al-Māwardī; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr by al-Durr. Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues from the context; Study Quran reports broad agreement. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن وعكرمة وعطاء وجابر); JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن الزبير,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(all commentators)]
- 56:1/q13/p2 It is Makkan with some verses excepted as later revelations. — Al-Ālūsī reports exceptions for 56:39–40, 56:75–82 on Muslim's occasion report, or 56:82 from Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda. Study Quran reports exceptions of 56:81–82 or 56:83. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة,مسلم); STUDYQURAN(all commentators)]

## 56:1/q14 How many verses are counted in the sūrah?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Regional systems of verse counting.
- 56:1/q14/p1 Ninety-six. — Jishumī and al-Durr give 96 without regional qualification; al-Ālūsī assigns 96 to the Kufan count. [3 notes: JISHUMI; DURR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 56:1/q14/p2 Ninety-nine. — Bursevī gives 99; al-Ālūsī assigns it to the Hijazi and Syrian counts. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 56:1/q14/p3 Ninety-seven. — Al-Ālūsī assigns 97 to the Basran count. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 56:1/q15 What virtues or recitation reports are associated with the sūrah?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which distinct report or practice is at issue.
- 56:1/q15/p1 Nightly recitation is reported to prevent poverty. — Reports are attributed to the Prophet through Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās, and to Ibn Masʿūd directly; the cited excerpts state no grading. Ibn ʿAjībah says understanding its Hereafter teaching prompts preparation. [7 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAjībah,the Prophet)]
- 56:1/q15/p2 It is called the sūrah of wealth and recommended for teaching to children or women. — Anas transmits the Prophetic designation and teaching advice, with a variant concerning women; ʿĀʾisha urged women to recite it. The reports have no stated grades. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم)]
- 56:1/q15/p3 The Prophet is reported to have recited it in dawn prayer and among sūrahs that made him gray. — Jābir ibn Samura reports its dawn recitation; Ibn ʿAbbās reports Abū Bakr's question about the Prophet's graying and the answer concerning Hūd and sister sūrahs. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 56:1/q15/p4 It offers a compact account of past and future peoples and the Hereafter. — Masrūq reports Ibn Masʿūd advising recitation for knowledge of earlier and later peoples, Paradise and Hell; no grading is given. [1 notes: JISHUMI(مسروق)]
- 56:1/q15/p5 It has become a recitation at significant moments, especially near death. — Study Quran attributes the practice to the reported virtue and notes recitation for the dying or recently dead. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 56:1/q16 What other report is transmitted in the sūrah's material?  [* · reports]
- 56:1/q16/p1 A report explains first and last groups through Adam's and Muhammad's communities. — Jābir ibn ʿAbd Allāh reports ʿUmar's question and the Prophet's explanation that Adam and his community form groups, with his own community's group completed on seeking aid. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 56:1/q17 How is the next verse's denial understood?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the negation stresses irreversibility or the failure of false descriptions.
- 56:1/q17/p1 There is no turning back or denial once the Event occurs. — Qatāda glosses kādhiba with mathnūya; al-Samīn praises the no-return explanation. Islahi calls the warning true and urges preparation. [3 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); SAMIN-DURR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 56:1/q17/p2 No false report or interpretation can negate the Event or reverse its results. — Al-Rāzī coordinates 'lowering, raising' with 'false' and reads denial of any misleading report or undoing of the effects. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 56:1/q18 How are the following 'lowering' and 'raising' words read and parsed?  [* · readings]
turns on: The nominative predicate reading versus the accusative circumstantial reading.
- 56:1/q18/p1 Zayd ibn ʿAlī and others read them accusative as circumstantial clauses. — Al-Samīn names ʿĪsā, al-Ḥasan, Abū Ḥaywa, Ibn Muqsim and al-Yazīdī; he doubts a report that al-Kisāʾī wished to adopt it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū al-Faḍl attach both clauses to al-Wāqiʿa; al-Samīn corrects Abū al-Baqāʾ's pronoun wording. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي,زيد بن علي); ITQAN(ابن جني)]
- 56:1/q18/p2 The majority read them nominative, as predicates of an omitted subject. — Al-Samīn says either the Event lowers people to Hell and raises others to Paradise, or it itself bears both qualities. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 56:1/q19 What grammatical comparisons to idhā occur in the cited material?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The scope of the grammatical comparison being drawn.
- 56:1/q19/p1 Idhā can appear after ḥattā as a genitive or in a nominal analysis. — Al-Akhfash parses 17:18 after ḥattā as genitive; Abū al-Ḥasan's related analysis is reported by al-Ṭabrisī. The majority instead treat ḥattā as opening a full clause. [2 notes: ITQAN(الأخفش); TABRISI(أبو الحسن); ITQAN(الجمهور)-]
- 56:1/q19/p2 Seven sūrahs open with conditional forms. — Al-Burhān and Abū Shāma in al-Itqān classify sūrah openings this way. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(أبو شامة)]
- 56:1/q19/p3 Idhā may refer to present or past contexts as well as the future. — Al-Itqān cites night, day and a setting star for simultaneous present action, and trade, Dhū al-Qarnayn and the barrier for reports revealed after events. [2 notes: ITQAN]
- 56:1/q19/p4 Some Qur'anic idhā clauses are temporal rather than conditional. — Al-Itqān argues that the nominal responses in 42:37 and 42:39 would otherwise need fāʾ, and rejects forced ellipses. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 56:1/q19/p5 Both in and idhā ordinarily require verbal clauses. — Al-Burhān says an apparent noun after either is explained by an implicit verb, citing 4:128, 4:176, 9:6, 82:1 and 81:1. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 56:1/q19/p6 An adjective after a numbered construct may agree with either element. — Al-Itqān cites seven heavens in tiers and seven fat cows, 67:3 and 12:43. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 56:1/q20 What broader weak-root morphology examples are recorded?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Which noun pattern and original weak-radical state is being compared.
- 56:1/q20/p1 Some patterns retain their original wāw or yāʾ. — Sībawayh says patterns unlike verbs preserve weak radicals, aided by adjacent sukūn; examples include ʿawār, qawwāl, miqwāl, ʿāwir and ṣāyid. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 56:1/q20/p2 Abyanāʾ is a reported but irregular pronunciation. — Sībawayh attributes it to some Arabs: yāʾ is quiescent and bāʾ vowelled to avoid kasra on yāʾ. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعض العرب)]
- 56:1/q20/p3 Verb-derived nouns can follow their verbs in defectiveness. — Sībawayh gives iqāma, istiqāma and passive participle mafʿūl; independent triliteral nouns would retain fuller forms. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 56:1/q20/p4 Ṭawīl preserves the root as faʿīl with a passive sense. — Sībawayh says the noun directly based on yaṭūlu would be ṭāʾil and compares makhyūṭ. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 56:1/q20/p5 Original vowels or quiescence decide hamzation in plurals. — Sībawayh says maqāwil, maʿāyish, taqāwul and tabāyuʿ retain weak radicals because originally vowelled, whereas ʿajūz, risāla, ṣaḥīfa and analogous fawāʿil hamzate; heavy paired wāws also favor hamza. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 56:1/q20/p6 Mafʿal may preserve a shortened mifʿāl pattern. — Al-Khalīl compares maftaḥ and miftāḥ, which can alternate with shared adjectival meaning. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 56:1/q20/p7 Maṣāʾib is called an erroneous plural analysis. — Sībawayh says maṣība is mafʿila, not faʿīla, and also attests maṣāwib. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 56:1/q21 What poetic examples of indirect expression are cited?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Which poetic image carries the unstated quality.
- 56:1/q21/p1 A docile dog and thin calf indirectly signify a generous host. — Al-Jurjānī relates the dog's docility to habitual restraint and compares other dogs welcoming visitors; the thin calf is compared with Ibn Hurmā's line لا أمتع العوذ بالفصال. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 56:1/q21/p2 A tent or prison can locate a person's virtues by metonymy. — Al-Jurjānī says Ziyād's tent locates generosity and manliness, while Yazīd ibn al-Ḥakam's prison poem gives captive virtues an analogous setting. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 56:1/q22 What is said about Qur'anic inimitability in the cited rhetorical material?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Which feature of Qur'anic discourse explains the challenge.
- 56:1/q22/p1 Its inimitability lies beyond rhyme, easy letter sequences, meter, or verse endings. — Al-Jurjānī says Arabs could compose rhyme; he treats an alleged imitation anecdote with reserve, rejects difficult sounds as the decisive test, and cites Ibn Masʿūd's garden image and inexhaustible recitation as evidence for deeper structure. [5 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(ابن مسعود)]
- 56:1/q22/p2 A single sūrah's structure and expression would expose Arab orators' inability to match it. — Al-Jāḥiẓ is quoted by al-Jurjānī. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(الجاحظ)]

## 56:1/q23 How are madd lengths ranked in the cited reading traditions?  [* · readings]
turns on: The reader or transmission route and whether madd is within one word or across two.
- 56:1/q23/p1 Some routes shorten separated madd while allowing medium joined madd. — Al-Tadhkira lists Ibn Kathīr, Abū Shuʿayb, most Qālūn routes and Yaʿqūb; al-Dānī's Taysīr and Makkī's Tabṣira give overlapping short-reading lists, with Makkī's excerpt cut off. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني,أبو محمد مكي,أصحاب التذكرة)]
- 56:1/q23/p2 Other routes extend both joined and separated madd in ranked degrees. — Al-Tadhkira puts Warsh and Ḥamza longest, then ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir and al-Kisāʾī, then Abū Nashīṭ and al-Dūrī; al-Dānī's Taysīr and Jāmiʿ refine route-specific ranks. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو الداني,أصحاب التذكرة)]

## 56:1/q24 What stopping forms are allowed for a final hamza after a vowel or alif?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the residual vowel of rūm licenses eased hamza at pause.
- 56:1/q24/p1 Rūm with between-between easing is allowed. — Fāris, al-Dānī, al-Shāṭibī and others permit a partly voiced final ḍamma or kasra; Ibn al-Jazarī cites Ḥamza routes through Khalaf, Sulaym and Idrīs. He finds no explicit Sībawayh ruling for pause. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو الفتح فارس والداني وصاحب التجريد والشاطبي وأبو العلاء وسبط الخياط,سيبويه)]
- 56:1/q24/p2 Only replacement of the silent hamza is allowed. — Most readers and grammarians reason that pause silences the hamza, so it follows silent-hamza easing; al-Mahdawī and others agree. Al-Shāṭibī weakens this restrictive view; Ibn al-Jazarī still calls replacement analogical and undisputed. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو العباس المهدوي وأبو عبد الله بن سفيان وأبو الطاهر بن خلف وأبو العز القلانسي وابن الباذش,جمهور القراء وأكثر النحاة)]

## 56:1/q25 How is final rāʾ pronounced at pause in the cited reading discussion?  [* · readings]
turns on: The effect of preceding kasra, emphatic consonants, and imāla at pause.
- 56:1/q25/p1 It is emphatic after fatḥa or ḍamma and light after kasra, with contextual refinements. — Ibn al-Jazarī counts silent yāʾ and imāla like kasra, ishāmām like silence, and rūm like connected reading; he chooses emphatic Miṣr but light Qaṣr. He rejects emphasis on al-nār after imāla and lightens the second rāʾ of bisharar reciprocally. [4 notes: NASHR; NASHR+]
- 56:1/q25/p2 After a consonant preceded by kasra, an emphatic consonant can make final rāʾ emphatic. — Ibn Shurayḥ reports this; al-Dānī reports lightening, which Ibn al-Jazarī finds closer to readers' wider practice. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عبد الله بن شريح وأبو عمرو الداني)]
- 56:1/q25/p3 After that consonant, final rāʾ is lightened. — Al-Dānī's report is preferred by Ibn al-Jazarī as closer to broader practice. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عبد الله بن شريح وأبو عمرو الداني)]

## 56:1/q26 How is the divine name pronounced after a light rāʾ?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether light rāʾ changes the following vowel enough to parallel major imāla.
- 56:1/q26/p1 It remains emphatic after an un-kasraed light letter. — Al-Jaʿbarī and al-Wāsiṭī give yubashshir Allāh, distinguishing major imāla in narā Allāha, which permits either sound. Ibn al-Jazarī rejects analogy from a merely light rāʾ because its ḍamma does not turn toward kasra. [2 notes: NASHR(إبراهيم بن عمر الجعبري وعبد الله بن عبد المؤمن الواسطي)]
- 56:1/q26/p2 It may be lightened by analogy with imāla. — An unnamed performance authority argues even ḍamma can approach imāla; Ibn al-Jazarī says the proposed reading lacked teacher transmission and rejects the analogy. [1 notes: NASHR(بعض أهل الأداء من أهل عصرنا); NASHR-]

## 56:1/q27 What is the purpose and timing of seeking refuge in prayer?  [* · law]
turns on: Whether refuge attaches to recitation or the prayer as a whole.
- 56:1/q27/p1 It serves Qur'an recitation and is said when recitation begins. — Abū Ḥanīfa and Muḥammad say a follower who does not recite omits it, citing 16:98; in Eid prayer they place it after the extra takbīrs. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة ومحمد)]
- 56:1/q27/p2 It serves prayer itself and is said at prayer's start. — Abū Yūsuf says a follower recites it and places it before Eid takbīrs; he argues that it repeats with each prayer, not each recitation. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو يوسف)]

## 56:1/q28 What recitation practice and reward are mentioned in the cited material?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the note concerns manner of recitation or its promised ascent.
- 56:1/q28/p1 Measured recitation clarifies meaning and enables understanding. — Al-Rāzī calls clear articulation Sunnah and cites 73:4; he refers to an Abū Dāwūd report from al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib, whose wording is cut off. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 56:1/q28/p2 The Qur'an's companion is told to recite and ascend in Paradise. — An Ibn ʿUmar report has no stated grading; al-Khaṭṭābī relates Paradise's ranks to the number of verses recited and completion to the highest rank. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو سليمان الخطابي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
