# 19:43: 28 questions

## 19:43/q01 What does the form yā abati express and how is it constructed?  [يَٰٓأَبَتِ · grammar]
- 19:43/q01/p1 It is an affectionate address to the father; its tāʾ replaces the first-person yāʾ in direct address. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī restricts the substitution to vocatives. Kashshāf and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain the fatherly address as a plea for closeness and renewed attention. [3 notes: TABATABAI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:43/q02 How is the fatherly vocative read?  [يَٰٓأَبَتِ · readings]
turns on: The vocative particle and the final vowel.
- 19:43/q02/p1 Yā abati has a kasra on the final tāʾ. — Al-Samīn reports Ibn ʿĀmir's reading. Qirāʾāt ER contrasts the kasra, susceptible to imāla, with a regional prosodic fatḥa. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER]
- 19:43/q02/p2 Yā abata has a fatḥa on the final tāʾ. — Qirāʾāt ER relates the fatḥa to a different regional prosodic tradition. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 19:43/q02/p3 The codex of ʿAbd Allāh has wā abati, a lamenting address. — Al-Samīn identifies wā as the lamenting vocative particle. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن عامر)]

## 19:43/q03 Why is the address to the father repeated?  [يَٰٓأَبَتِ · rhetoric]
- 19:43/q03/p1 The fourfold address renews attention and expresses tender, persuasive concern. — Kashshāf calls each address a plea for closeness and compassion; Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts Noah's single address to his son in a concise setting and cites 31:13 and 11:42; Iṣlāḥī sees affectionate concern throughout the argument. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 19:43/q04 How do interrogative hamza and conditional in constructions cited with in work?  [إِنِّى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the interrogative hamza leaves an ensuing conditional fully operative.
- 19:43/q04/p1 The hamza may precede an intact conditional construction. — Sībawayh gives أإن تأتني آتك; من and متى need the hamza for interrogation. It does not change the clause's syntax, unlike هل. He treats أفإن مت (21:34) as genuinely conditional; reversal is also possible. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 19:43/q04/p2 أإن تأتني آتيك is awkward as a conditional, though acceptable as a question. — Sībawayh reports Yūnus's judgment. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس)]

## 19:43/q05 When may a noun separate a conditional particle from its verb?  [إِنِّى · grammar]
- 19:43/q05/p1 Ordinarily governing and especially jussive particles must touch their verbs, but conditional particles admit qualified separation and ellipsis. — Sībawayh says conditional particles resist a fronted subject by analogy with jussives, yet poetry permits one. With nonjussive إن separation occurs in ordinary speech; jussive separation is poetic. إن is the original conditional particle and allows shorthand إن خيرا فخير. ʿAdī ibn Zayd's متى and Kaʿb ibn Jaʿbal's أينما lines illustrate the license; a finite verb would be stronger. A raised verb needs an underlying verb or an independent topic-comment clause, clarified by فانا; without that analysis raising is poetic, as in Hishām al-Marmī. [9 notes: SIBAWAYH(عدي بن زيد,كعب بن جعبل)]

## 19:43/q06 When does a conditional noun retain its conditional force after another operator?  [إِنِّى · grammar]
- 19:43/q06/p1 It becomes relative-like if an earlier operator already governs; an explicit or understood pronoun can preserve a conditional clause. — Sībawayh says إن or كان cannot lack a syntactic role or double the condition; with no other governor من, إن, and أي remain conditional. An explicit pronoun in كنت من يأتني آته permits the conditional, unlike unexpressed addressee endings in كان or ليس. Al-Khalīl supplies إنه in al-Aʿshā; his and al-Rāʿī's lines, ʿAdī's أن كلانا, and lightened أن after علمت rely on understood هاء. Qur'an 20:89 parallels أن لا يقول, where لا can compensate for the lost marker. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 19:43/q07 When is a conditional response jussive after an intervening particle?  [إِنِّى · grammar]
- 19:43/q07/p1 Unchanged particles normally do not license a jussive response; an understood pronoun or poetic license can alter the analysis. — Sībawayh says إذ normally leaves the clause unchanged, though Labīd uses poetic jussive; a pronoun can intervene before من. After فإذا a hidden pronoun permits jussive, whereas إذا filling the conditional role excludes it. لا can be semantically light like ما in 3:159, leaving the clause unchanged; an ensuing إن supports jussive in a distinct construction. ولكن admits a conditional with recoverable subject, as Ṭarafa's متى line shows. In al-ʿUjayr's line ما is superfluous and متى governs أملك. The nonjussive Qur'anic إن كان in 56:90–91 is acceptable. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 19:43/q08 What knowledge came to Abraham?  [ٱلْعِلْمِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the knowledge names its subject matter, its revealed source or means, or the prophetic status it conveys; several sources combine these.
- 19:43/q08/p1 Knowledge and recognition of God, especially His oneness and exclusive right to worship. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls it certainty and recognition; Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī call it knowledge of God. Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān specify recognition and exclusive worship; Asad connects intellectual insight into God's existence and uniqueness with 6:74–82. Jishumī, Nasafī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī also report this sense; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds prior vision of the heavenly kingdom. Iṣlāḥī says nature, intellect, and the world witness to one God, but partners have no evidence. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; JISHUMI(غير مسمى); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:43/q08/p2 Knowledge by revelation from God. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq say the phrase signals revelation. Abū Ḥayyān specifies an angel; Bursevī calls it revelation brought to him. Jishumī reports revelation among the means of knowledge. Ibn ʿĀshūr's report of Kashshāf's traveler analogy identifies revealed knowledge and prophecy; Iṣlāḥī contrasts revelation with unsupported conjecture and says it alone supplies true knowledge. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; JISHUMI(غير مسمى); IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 19:43/q08/p3 Knowledge of the afterlife, resurrection, recompense, and punishment of false worship. — Muqātil identifies what follows death. Thaʿlabī adds clear proof of punishment for worship of another than God; Qurṭubī combines certainty about God and the afterlife. Abū Ḥayyān mentions recompense. Jishumī includes resurrection and God's promise, threat, and punishment; Ālūsī reports reward and punishment. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 19:43/q08/p4 Prophethood or prophetic spiritual knowledge. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām calls it prophethood; Jishumī includes prophethood among the means. Study Quran calls it prophetic knowledge of God's Reality and the Hereafter; Ibn ʿĀshūr's Kashshāf report says revealed knowledge and prophecy. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; JISHUMI(غير مسمى); STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف)]
- 19:43/q08/p5 Religious knowledge generally, or evidence and clear proof. — Jishumī reports religious knowledge and, separately, evidence as a means to knowledge; Thaʿlabī speaks of God-given knowledge and clear proof. [3 notes: JISHUMI(غير مسمى); THALABI]
- 19:43/q08/p6 Complete knowledge of what is necessary, impossible, and possible for God. — Ālūsī reports this as one interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 19:43/q08/p7 Knowledge encompassing both divine matters and the afterlife. — Ālūsī reports this combined interpretation; Qurṭubī speaks of certainty about God and the afterlife, and Study Quran joins God's Reality with the Hereafter. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:43/q08/p8 Inward knowledge of the unity of the divine essence. — Qāshānī gives this reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 19:43/q09 What does 'knowledge came to me' imply about its arrival and the time of the conversation?  [قَدْ جَآءَنِى · interpretation]
turns on: What temporal implication is drawn from 'came' and from Abraham's earlier knowledge.
- 19:43/q09/p1 It implies Abraham had already become a prophet when he spoke. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ālūsī infer this from the wording; Abū Ḥayyān says the exchange suggests the same. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 19:43/q09/p2 It presents the knowledge as newly acquired. — Kashshāf draws this implication from 'came'. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 19:43/q09/p3 Abraham already knew God and saw the heavenly kingdom before disputing with his father and people. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī places his knowledge before those arguments; he also says 'this is my lord' in 6:74–83 proposes a subordinate governor, not an equal creator, amid polytheist and Sabian beliefs. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 19:43/q10 Why use jāʾanī for the arrival of knowledge?  [جَآءَنِى · rhetoric]
- 19:43/q10/p1 Jāʾa suggests arrival involving difficulty, whereas atā suggests ease. — Sāmarrāʾī invokes the well-trodden-path term al-imtiyāʾ and cites 16:1; he also observes jāʾa only in the past tense in the Qur'an, while atā occurs in the imperfect, citing 76:1. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:43/q11 What force can min have under negation?  [مِنَ · grammar]
- 19:43/q11/p1 It can make a nonuniversal noun exhaustive or reinforce a noun already universal under negation. — Burhān distinguishes ما جاءني من رجل from من أحد, disagreeing with Sībawayh's equivalence. Abū Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr reports Sībawayh's exhaustive account: ما أتاني رجل without min could deny a strong man, one man, or all men; min denies all three. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 19:43/q12 How does negation scope over a quantified or qualified assertion?  [لَمْ · rhetoric]
- 19:43/q12/p1 Negation targets the restriction rather than necessarily denying the whole event. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī says 'not all came' implies some came, and 'not together' implies separately; affirmatively, 'all came' makes the totality the informative focus. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 19:43/q13 How is mā in mā lam yaʾtika analyzed?  [مَا لَمْ يَأْتِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is relative or descriptive.
- 19:43/q13/p1 Mā may be a relative pronoun. — Kashshāf gives this alongside a descriptive analysis and says the objects of 'hears' and 'sees' in the parallel phrase are omitted and not intended; Nasafī reports both alternatives. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 19:43/q13/p2 Mā may be descriptive. — Kashshāf and Nasafī report this alternative. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 19:43/q14 How can two verbs share government of a following noun?  [جَآءَنِى يَأْتِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Proximity to the noun, anticipatory pronouns, and whether a single noun may receive two governing effects.
- 19:43/q14/p1 The nearer second verb should govern. — Rāzī says the two verbs may have like or unlike effects and the noun may be singular, dual, or plural. Basrans cite proximity, including ما جاءني من أحد; neither ignoring both nor applying both is possible. With a dual or plural noun, the first verb then bears a matching subject suffix. They likewise choose the second for unlike effects on a singular noun. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(البصريون)+; RAZI-FULL(الكوفيون)-]
- 19:43/q14/p2 The first verb should govern. — Kufans say second-verb governance forces a pronoun before its referent, including with dual and plural nouns; they extend the point to singulars. The first encounters an ungoverned noun, whereas the second does not. They say Basran examples show only that second governance is possible. If the first governs, Rāzī says the second carries the suffix and the noun is accusative. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكوفيون); RAZI-FULL(الكوفيون)+; RAZI-FULL(البصريون)-]
- 19:43/q14/p3 Two verbs may both govern one singular noun. — Al-Farrāʾ allows قام وقعد زيد; the common objection is that one effect cannot have two causes. Rāzī says explaining two causes as one recognition does not settle the problem, because that recognition itself comes from the combined causes. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفراء); RAZI-FULL-]

## 19:43/q15 What does following Abraham require?  [فَٱتَّبِعْنِىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the invitation is explained as adherence to Abraham's monotheistic revelation or as a wider discipline of spiritual following.
- 19:43/q15/p1 Follow his God-given guidance, worship the one God, and enter the true religion. — Muqātil calls it following Abraham's Islam; al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's 'what came from his Lord'; Abū Ḥayyān specifies worship of one God. Ṭabarī, Nasafī, Baghawī, Biqāʿī, and Bursevī connect following with his received knowledge and straight path. Qushayrī says truth outweighs the son's younger age, while salvation follows the people of truth and ruin follows innovation. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TAB-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; QUSHAYRI]
- 19:43/q15/p2 Following is a graded spiritual practice, from scripture through the Messenger and Companions to God's friends and those learned in Him. — Al-Qāsim calls following the safest way to God; Sahl ibn ʿAbd Allāh says imitation is hardest on the ego because it affords no ease. [2 notes: SULAMI(القاسم,سهل بن عبد الله)]

## 19:43/q16 What kind of guidance does 'I shall guide you' promise?  [أَهْدِكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance here is restricted to showing the road or includes its saving result.
- 19:43/q16/p1 He will show the right way, not bring his father to the destination. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts this with an imam's destination-reaching guidance and cites Abraham's later appointment in 2:124. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 19:43/q16/p2 He offers guidance to beneficial safety through an upright religion. — Ibn Kathīr stresses the younger son benefiting his father; Muqātil, Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, Ṭabarī, and Qurṭubī describe the saving course and its destination. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:43/q17 Does Abraham's offer of guidance require an infallible prophet or imam to understand proofs?  [فَٱتَّبِعْنِىٓ أَهْدِكَ · theology]
turns on: Whether an offered aid to understanding proves an indispensable, binding intermediary.
- 19:43/q17/p1 A complete infallible prophet or imam is necessary to guide comprehension of proofs. — Rāzī reports the objection that Abraham's invitation concedes the need for such a guide. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 19:43/q17/p2 The invitation does not establish that necessity; it offers an easier path as advice. — Rāzī answers that a prophet is not necessary for understanding proofs and that 'follow me' is not a binding command, defeating the alleged circularity. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 19:43/q18 How is the yāʾ at the end of 'follow me' read before 'I shall guide you'?  [فَٱتَّبِعْنِىٓ أَهْدِكَ · readings]
- 19:43/q18/p1 All readers leave the yāʾ quiescent in fattabiʿnī ahdika. — Ibn al-Jazarī counts it among four agreed forms; parallelism after an agreed quiescent letter may explain it. He says the other 64 yāʾs follow general rules and contrasts forms such as ʿaṣāya atawakkaʾā and iyyāya atuhlikunā, opened to avoid two consecutive quiescents. Ibn Mujāhid also lists quiescent added yāʾs before a separate alif with a jussive, such as فاذكروني أذكركم and رب فأنظرني. [4 notes: NASHR(all readers); IBNMUJAHID]

## 19:43/q19 How is a fronted object or topic grammatically related to its following verb?  [فَٱتَّبِعْنِىٓ أَهْدِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fronted noun is a resumed topic or an accusative governed by an understood verb.
- 19:43/q19/p1 A fronted noun can be a nominative topic resumed by a pronoun. — Sībawayh calls ضربت زيدا the standard direct order and زيد ضربته a topic-predicate structure requiring the pronoun. The same patterns occur with أعطيت. A preposition or a related object makes direct accusative fronting more remote; زيد مررت به resembles زيد لقيت أخاه. He prefers nominative in زيدا لقيت أخاه and sometimes in transmitted poetry, though both cases occur in Bishr ibn Abī Khāzim; Dhū al-Rumma supplies another fronting example. [8 notes: SIBAWAYH; SIBAWAYH+]
- 19:43/q19/p2 A fronted accusative can depend on an omitted verb explained by the following verb. — Sībawayh allows زيدا ضربته with a suppressed first verb; زيدا ضربت is equally good to the normal order, with emphasis deciding. A related object can license accusative because acting on a relative resembles acting on the person; أيهم تر يأتك likewise uses understood repetition. He notes accusative transmission in Bishr's poem and variants with أعطيت. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 19:43/q20 What is the straight, sound path promised here?  [صِرَٰطًۭا سَوِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the path is identified as the religion followed or by its saving destination.
- 19:43/q20/p1 It is the upright religion of faith and monotheism, free of deviation. — Muqātil and Yaḥyā call it Islam; Ṭabarī God's unbent religion; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān faith, the latter exclusive worship. Jishumī calls it truth, and Ibn al-Jawzī cites the 'right path' at 20:135. Ṭabrisī calls it moderate, al-Zajjāj straight, Baghawī upright, Nasafī straight, and Study Quran the correct salvific religion. Iṣlāḥī says monotheism leads directly to God. Ālūsī says it reaches the highest aims and avoids ruin; Biqāʿī compares it to a road without crookedness. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); TABRISI; ZAJJAJ; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Quran commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; THALABI]
- 19:43/q20/p2 It is the road to Paradise or escape from Hell. — Jishumī reports both alternatives; Yaḥyā says upright Islam leads to Paradise, and Qurṭubī calls the path saving. [3 notes: JISHUMI; YAHYA-SALLAM; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:43/q21 What does sawiyyan mean?  [سَوِيًّۭا · meaning]
- 19:43/q21/p1 Even, upright, straight, or right. — Ibn al-Jawzī traces the shared sense of evenness and uprightness across bodily form, religion, and road; exegetes call it just or right and cite 20:135. Al-Zajjāj says straight, and Ṭabrisī says moderate. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ZAJJAJ; TABRISI]

## 19:43/q22 How is ṣirāṭ pronounced and explained?  [صِرَٰطًۭا · readings]
turns on: Articulation of the initial consonant.
- 19:43/q22/p1 With sīn instead of ṣād, associated with the root s-r-ṭ, 'to swallow'. — Qirāʾāt ER attributes the sound change to phonetic interchange and likens the road to one that swallows its traveler. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 19:43/q22/p2 With an intermediate articulation between ṣād and zāy. — Qirāʾāt ER describes the ṣād bearing a zayn marker. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 19:43/q23 Why does Abraham present his knowledge and invitation so gently?  [* · rhetoric]
- 19:43/q23/p1 He avoids insulting his father or boasting, presenting himself as a fellow traveler who knows the safe route. — Kashshāf says he claims only some guidance his father lacks; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī develop the traveler image. Abū Ḥayyān notes that the father cannot explain idol worship yet Abraham does not call him ignorant. Bursevī adds that revelation supplied the knowledge. Ibn ʿĀshūr quotes Kashshāf's analogy. Biqāʿī imagines warning a companion of road hazards; Ibn Kathīr stresses a younger son benefiting his father. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the indefinite 'a path' as implying the father's current crooked route. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف); BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 19:43/q24 What does a knowledgeable son guiding his father teach about authority and duty?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:43/q24/p1 Truth and knowledge warrant guidance regardless of age or lineage; those taught must explain religion to others. — Qushayrī says the younger son's truth is stronger than age; Study Quran says spiritual authority answers ancestral custom (2:170, 5:104), and points to aged Zachariah and infant Jesus (19:4–9, 19:30–33) as other cases where age does not limit God's will. Jishumī requires the learned to invite and clarify. Ibn Kathīr stresses the child's God-given knowledge benefiting his father. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 19:43/q25 What theological claim is drawn from the father's lack of Abraham's knowledge?  [* · theology]
- 19:43/q25/p1 The passage refutes the أصحاب المعارف view. — Jishumī argues that Abraham expressly possessed knowledge his father lacked. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 19:43/q26 Why did Abraham's repeated counsel fail with his father?  [* · theology]
- 19:43/q26/p1 A prior decree of exclusion was not undone by later effort. — Qushayrī says Abraham displayed every kind of compassion, but his repeated counsel did not help. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 19:43/q27 How does this invitation fit the sequence of Abraham's appeal?  [* · links]
- 19:43/q27/p1 The appeal moves from the idols' sensory uselessness to Abraham's knowledge, Satan's influence, and a warning of punishment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports his grandfather's account: the final warning is hypothetical and implies idols cannot punish their desertion. Bayḍāwī says idol worship obeys Satan, who disobeys the beneficent Lord; obedient followers share his fault. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 19:43/q28 How does Abraham's account serve the sūrah's audiences?  [* · links]
- 19:43/q28/p1 It shows Christians the earlier prophets taught Muhammad's message and warns Quraysh that their ancestral practice differs from Abraham's and Ishmael's true religion. — Iṣlāḥī describes the Qur'an's presentation of Abraham's true religion. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
