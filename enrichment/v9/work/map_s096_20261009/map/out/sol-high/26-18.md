# 26:18: 22 questions

## 26:18/q01 What happened before Pharaoh gave this reply?  [قَالَ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ over whether the audience followed a year's wait or a nighttime arrival; the commentators also identify speech omitted by the condensed narrative.
- 26:18/q01/p1 Moses and Aaron came to Pharaoh and delivered their message; the narrative leaves their arrival and speech implicit. — Al-Wāḥidī says they delivered the message; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān identify the omitted words as their going and speaking, supplied by context; Iṣlāḥī adds the courtiers, warning, and demand to release the Israelites. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:18/q01/p2 Moses and Aaron waited a year at Pharaoh's gate before he admitted them to mock their claim. — Abū Ḥayyān says Pharaoh initially withheld access; Qatāda, as cited by Bursawī, says he recognized Moses after hearing the message; al-Ālūsī adds the porter's announcement of a messenger from the Lord of the worlds. Al-Māwardī also reports a year's exclusion. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:18/q01/p3 They arrived at night; Pharaoh was startled by a knock and admitted Moses after calling him mad. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the porter relayed Moses' claim to be a messenger. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:18/q02 Whom did Pharaoh recognize and address?  [قَالَ · referent]
- 26:18/q02/p1 Pharaoh recognized Moses, whom he had raised, and addressed him personally. — Muqātil connects recognition to Moses' upbringing and flight after killing a man; Ibn ʿAbbās reports Pharaoh recognized him on entry and reproached his claimed prophethood; another Ibn ʿAbbās account says Moses first identified himself as son of ʿImrān. Al-Thaʿlabī says recognition preceded the reminder of thirty years' care. Al-Biqāʿī says Pharaoh singled Moses out as chief messenger and treated Aaron as his minister; Ṭabāṭabāʾī also says Pharaoh addressed Moses alone. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(قتادة); TABATABAI]

## 26:18/q03 What is Pharaoh trying to do with the question about raising Moses?  [أَلَمْ نُرَبِّكَ فِينَا وَلِيدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ over whether Pharaoh's primary aim was to claim gratitude, belittle Moses, challenge the mission, establish identity, or expose a moral defect; several commentators combine aims.
- 26:18/q03/p1 He claims a favor and demands gratitude for raising Moses. — Jishumī reports the favor reading; al-Ṭabrisī says Pharaoh claims credit for kindness; Ibn al-Jawzī and Ibn Kathīr connect the claim to Moses' later killing of an Egyptian, which Pharaoh calls repayment for housing, feeding, and protecting him. Al-Biqāʿī says Pharaoh's claimed guardianship should restrain Moses; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the negative question presses an acknowledgment presented as voluntary. Al-Ālūsī calls it boastful indebtedness; Ibn Kathīr says Pharaoh ignored the demand first. [12 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)]
- 26:18/q03/p2 He belittles and reproaches Moses for claiming authority after growing up under his care. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī say Pharaoh contrasts sparing Moses with killing boys of his age and asks when his mission arose; Abū Ḥayyān says he dismisses the sender and message. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows that Pharaoh treats Moses as denying his upbringing or supposing they forgot his deed. Iṣlāḥī attributes the arrogant reply to Pharaoh's inability to tolerate an Israelite's demand. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:18/q03/p3 He invokes their knowledge of Moses' past to deny or question his prophethood. — Jishumī reports this explanation. Ibn ʿAbbās says Pharaoh reproached Moses for the prophethood he now claimed. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says years of upbringing made Pharaoh know Moses' identity and history, prompting 'how can he be a messenger?' Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī ask when Moses acquired the claimed mission. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 26:18/q03/p4 He asks whether Moses really is the same person formerly raised there. — Jishumī records this as a further possibility, consistent with his account of Pharaoh asking if Moses was the child they raised. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 26:18/q03/p5 His recital of favors exposes a lack of chivalry. — Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī says a benefactor should not lord favors over someone; al-Ṭabrisī reports the alternative that Pharaoh disclosed his meanness by recounting them. [2 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن علي); TABRISI(قيل)]

## 26:18/q04 What does نُرَبِّكَ mean?  [نُرَبِّكَ · meaning]
- 26:18/q04/p1 It means to rear a child by caring for him through successive stages of growth. — Jishumī compares the verb with growing; al-Ṭabrisī explains staged nurturing from infancy; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines tarbiya as taking charge of a child's care and affairs. Qatāda says Pharaoh's household found Moses as an infant and raised him to manhood. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:18/q05 What place and people does فِينَا indicate?  [فِينَا · referent]
turns on: The phrase can point broadly to Pharaoh's people, specifically to the royal household, or to Moses' relationship with the particular ruler addressed.
- 26:18/q05/p1 It means in Pharaoh's household or royal Egyptian houses, where Moses was raised and honored. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī supply the implicit 'our houses'; Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the royal Egyptian household and says Moses was taken from the Nile. Al-Biqāʿī says Moses belonged to the household and appeared honored. Al-Māwardī calls him a foundling there; the Study Quran refers to 28:8–13. Kuranyolu says his mother set him afloat because Pharaoh killed Israelite newborn boys, then palace servants found him. [15 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 26:18/q05/p2 It means with or among Pharaoh and his people. — Al-Dāmghānī says fī can mean 'with/among' and compares Shuʿayb and Ṣāliḥ among their peoples at 11:91 and 11:62; al-Burhān says fī can mean ʿinda. The wording encompasses the people among whom Moses lived. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; BURHAN; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 26:18/q05/p3 Moses may have grown up with the speaking Pharaoh himself, as a foster brother. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies foster father Ramses II and the Pharaoh confronted as his son and successor Merneptah II, citing 28:14–15 in the surrounding account; he presents the brotherly upbringing as possible. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:18/q06 What does وَلِيدًا mean here?  [وَلِيدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The ordinary contextual gloss is a very young child; al-Rāghib gives a wider original range, al-Māwardī stresses foundling status, and al-Suddī also gives 'servant.'
- 26:18/q06/p1 It means a newborn or very young child, named for nearness to birth. — Al-Zajjāj specifies birth; Ibn ʿAbbās calls him a small child; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Kāshifī, Bursawī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain proximity to birth. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn give faʿīl with passive sense; al-Ālūsī calls it active form used passively, while 'some' infer recency from an intensive form because birth itself admits no degree. Al-Samīn notes the designation recalls a former state. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:18/q06/p2 It can originally designate a person whether birth is recent or remote, though usage drops the name as the person grows. — Al-Rāghib's explanation is reported by al-Ālūsī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الراغب)]
- 26:18/q06/p3 It describes Moses as a foundling in Pharaoh's house. — Al-Māwardī gives his status as a foundling as the reason Pharaoh calls him a child. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 26:18/q06/p4 It is glossed as a servant, then as a young child. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām reports al-Suddī's two glosses in that order. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي)]

## 26:18/q07 How is وَلِيدًا construed grammatically?  [وَلِيدًۭا · grammar]
- 26:18/q07/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative describing the object of 'we raised you.' — Al-Naḥḥās calls it circumstantial; al-Samīn identifies the object of nurabbika as its referent and adds the passive sense of faʿīl. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 26:18/q08 What sound change is possible in وَلَبِثْتَ?  [وَلَبِثْتَ · grammar]
- 26:18/q08/p1 The thāʾ can assimilate into the following tāʾ. — Al-Naḥḥās notes the assimilation while discussing the verse's grammatical forms. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 26:18/q09 How is عُمُرِكَ read and pronounced?  [عُمُرِكَ · readings]
turns on: The transmissions from Abū ʿAmr differ over whether the middle consonant retains its vowel; Hārūn says he accepted the full form too.
- 26:18/q09/p1 The standard readers use the full or heavy form with a moving mīm. — Ibn Mujāhid says all readers use the heavy form; al-Fārisī says all canonical readers do. Al-Samīn describes the contrasting lightened form, and Hārūn says Abū ʿAmr accepted this heavy alternative; ʿUbayd ibn ʿAqīl also transmits it from him. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says most readers have ḍamma on the mīm. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو (رواية عبيد بن عقيل),أبو عمرو (رواية هرون),كلهم); FARISI-HUJJA(الجميع); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 26:18/q09/p2 A transmission from Abū ʿAmr lightens the word by making the mīm still. — Ibn Mujāhid names ʿUbayd and al-Khaffāf; al-Fārisī specifies reports involving ʿUbayd, Hārūn, and al-Khaffāf. Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives the light form as ʿamruk; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and al-Ālūsī record a still mīm. Al-Naḥḥās says the heavy ḍamma may be dropped and notes Sībawayh's attestation of ʿamr with a still middle consonant in the oath. [8 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو (روايات عبيد وهرون والخفاف)); FARISI-HUJJA(عبيد عن هارون والخفاف عن أبي عمرو وعبيد عن أبي عمرو); NAHHAS(سيبويه); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو)]

## 26:18/q10 What does عُمُرِكَ denote, and what forms of the word are attested?  [عُمُرِكَ · meaning]
- 26:18/q10/p1 It denotes one's lifespan, the period during which the body is alive, however long or short. — Al-Rāghib defines the span as bodily habitation by life; Jishumī cites a poet contrasting life and ʿumr. Abū al-Fārisī quotes Ibn Muqbil's ʿumrī and Abū Zayd's ʿumran; Jishumī records ʿumar and ʿumr as language forms. Al-Zajjāj reports three ordinary vocalizations from Sībawayh, al-Khalīl, and the Basrans. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه والخليل وجميع البصريين); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد,ابن مقبل); JISHUMI; BURSEVI(الراغب)]

## 26:18/q11 How do Arabic oath and fixed source expressions involving عُمُر work?  [عُمُرِكَ · grammar]
turns on: The notes distinguish an oath by life from an accusative source formula with an understood verb; the other fixed formulas are grammatical analogies.
- 26:18/q11/p1 The oath expressions swear by life; in an oath the initial vowel is open and the mīm still. — Ibn Qutayba glosses laʿamruka and wa-laʿamr Allāh as oaths by your or God's life. Al-Zajjāj reports Sībawayh, al-Khalīl, and the Basrans restricting the oath form to laʿamr Allāh; Jishumī states its voweling, and al-Naḥḥās reports Sībawayh's explanation that fatḥa eases pronunciation. [4 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ(سيبويه والخليل وجميع البصريين); NAHHAS(سيبويه); JISHUMI]
- 26:18/q11/p2 ʿAmraka Allāh is an accusative fixed source expression standing for an omitted verb; similar expressions clarify its syntax. — Sībawayh says fixed maṣdars occupy one slot and lack ordinary article, nominative, and genitive uses, unlike free sources; ʿamraka Allāh and qaʿdaka Allāh are analogous to nashadtuka Allāh. Al-Khalīl says the proposed verb ʿammartuka is explanatory, not necessarily spoken; an unnamed poet and Ibn Aḥmar attest it. Sībawayh compares subḥān Allāh, with its omitted verb and definiteness from use (though Umayya's verse has tanwīn), and rayḥānah, meaning provision; Abū al-Khaṭṭāb glosses subḥān as God's freedom from evil, supported by al-Aʿshā's disavowal line. Ghufrānaka lā kufrānaka seeks forgiveness, not disbelief. Salāman can mean separation, as Abū al-Khaṭṭāb explains Abū Rabīʿa's wording; Umayya's poetry and some Arabs' raised salām bi-salām support the expression. Subbūḥan quddūsan are names with implied adhkuru; some Arabs raise them too. Khayr mā rudda fī ahl wa-māl patterns like khayr maqdam. Exclamatory karaman wa-ṣalafan omits a verb; Abū Murhab's karaman wa-ṭūl anf illustrates this. [23 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو الخطاب,أبو ربيعة,أبو مرهب,أمية بن أبي الصلت,ابن أحمر,الأعشى,الخليل,بعض العرب)]

## 26:18/q12 How is مِنْ عُمُرِكَ related to سِنِينَ?  [مِنْ عُمُرِكَ سِنِينَ · grammar]
- 26:18/q12/p1 The preposed phrase 'of your lifetime' is circumstantial and modifies 'years.' — Al-Samīn, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī explicitly attach the phrase to sinīna. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو عمرو)]

## 26:18/q13 How is سِنِينَ inflected?  [سِنِينَ · grammar]
- 26:18/q13/p1 It is a regular sound masculine plural, with an attested alternative that inflects its final nūn. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the alternative form sinīnan. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 26:18/q14 How many years did Moses live among Pharaoh's people?  [وَلَبِثْتَ فِينَا مِنْ عُمُرِكَ سِنِينَ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted chronologies give different lengths of residence or ages at departure: eighteen, thirty, forty, ten, and twelve.
- 26:18/q14/p1 Thirty years. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī's reports from Muqātil, al-Baghawī, Jishumī's unnamed report, al-Ṭabrisī's attribution to Mujāhid under a Muqātil speaker label, Ibn al-Jawzī's Muqātil report, and later chronologies all give thirty; al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz states it directly. [12 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:18/q14/p2 Eighteen years. — Ibn ʿAbbās gives this figure in al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī; al-Wāḥidī repeats the estimate alongside the gloss of walīd. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 26:18/q14/p3 Forty years, or approximately forty. — Al-Kalbī gives forty in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī; Ibn al-Jawzī names Ibn al-Sāʾib; Ibn ʿĀshūr says about forty, supported in his account by Moses leaving at about forty after the killing. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); IBNASHUR-FULL(التوراة)]
- 26:18/q14/p4 He left at ten years of age. — Al-Māwardī gives this within a chronology that also says he was kept from entry for a year, returned at thirty, and lived fifty years after Pharaoh drowned. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 26:18/q14/p5 He fled at twelve years of age. — Al-Ālūsī gives this in a different chronology, after Moses struck the Egyptian. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:18/q15 What wider chronology of Moses' life is reported?  [وَلَبِثْتَ فِينَا مِنْ عُمُرِكَ سِنِينَ · reports]
turns on: The reports assign different ages to Moses' departure and mission and differ in the intervals afterward.
- 26:18/q15/p1 He spent thirty years in Egypt, ten in Midian, thirty preaching after return, and fifty after the drowning, totaling 120. — Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī report the sequence; Bursawī explicitly totals 120. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:18/q15/p2 He left at ten, returned to summon Pharaoh at thirty, and lived fifty years after Pharaoh drowned. — Al-Māwardī also says Moses was kept from entering for one year. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 26:18/q15/p3 He fled at twelve, herded for Shuʿayb for ten years, and lived eighteen more years after marriage, reaching forty before his mission. — Al-Ālūsī links the flight to the blow against the Egyptian. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:18/q15/p4 He left Egypt at about forty after killing the Egyptian and was commissioned at eighty. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the Torah for the age of commissioning and points to 28:14–15 around the Egyptian's death. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(التوراة)]

## 26:18/q16 How does this speech connect to Pharaoh's next accusation and Moses' reply?  [* · links]
- 26:18/q16/p1 Pharaoh turns his claimed care into a charge of ingratitude for Moses' killing of an Egyptian. — Ibn al-Jawzī and Ibn Kathīr say Moses' killing is presented as repayment for being raised, housed, and fed; Ibn Kathīr cites Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd for 'disbelievers' as denial of favor and reports Ibn Jarīr's preference for that meaning. Kuranyolu says the Egyptian died from an unintended blow while Moses protected an Israelite, citing 28:16. Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes Moses acknowledges the deed and says he fled in fear in 26:20–21. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وعبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+]

## 26:18/q17 Why does Moses omit a claim that he seeks no wage?  [* · links]
- 26:18/q17/p1 He avoids speaking that way to Pharaoh because Pharaoh raised and supported him. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls a no-wage statement poor manners to a father or benefactor. Al-Kirmānī says Moses and Abraham modestly refrained from it despite innocence of seeking payment, comparing 26:69–70. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الكرماني)]

## 26:18/q18 What moral lesson is drawn from Pharaoh's reminder of his care?  [* · inward]
turns on: The two inward applications stress either the giver's duty not to boast or the recipient's duty to honor care, especially God's.
- 26:18/q18/p1 Reminding beneficiaries of one's favors and lording them over others violates chivalry. — Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī uses Pharaoh's appeal to Moses' upbringing as the example; al-Ṭabrisī similarly reports that recounting favors exposes his meanness. [2 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن علي); TABRISI(قيل)]
- 26:18/q18/p2 Even an enemy's upbringing creates a claim to respect, so God's true nurturing deserves still greater regard. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ draws the analogy from Moses' upbringing and points to 26:26. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء)]

## 26:18/q19 What ancillary meanings of sūrah are supplied with these notes?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The derivation depends on whether the noun is read with hamza; al-Māwardī favors the elevated-rank derivation.
- 26:18/q19/p1 Without hamza it denotes an elevated station or rank. — Al-Māwardī compares a city wall raised above its enclosure and cites al-Nābigha's verse; al-Ṭabarī explains that the poem grants its addressee rank beyond kings' stations. Al-Māwardī notes both hamzated and unhamzated forms and prefers this derivation. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL+]
- 26:18/q19/p2 With hamza it denotes a remainder left after something is consumed or taken. — Al-Ṭabarī compares a remainder of drink and quotes al-Aʿshā on lingering residue; al-Māwardī cites suʾr, a prophetic instruction to leave some drink in the vessel without a stated grading, and al-Aʿshā's poem about love left in a heart. Al-Māwardī reports but ranks the unhamzated derivation higher. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأعشى,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL-]

## 26:18/q20 What ancillary meanings of āyah are supplied with these notes?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The cited Arabic usage supports either a boundary sign or a narrative message.
- 26:18/q20/p1 It is a sign marking the end of a passage and the start of another. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Māwardī cite the heavenly table as a sign from God at 5:114; al-Māwardī also cites a line by ʿAbd Banū al-Ḥasḥās, while al-Ṭabarī cites a poem. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 26:18/q20/p2 It is a story or message, one narrative following another. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Māwardī cite Kaʿb ibn Zuhayr's poetic use of āyah as a report or message. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 26:18/q21 What report about the Quran's aḥruf accompanies the ancillary notes?  [* · reports]
- 26:18/q21/p1 The Quran came down in seven aḥruf; dispute over it is disbelief, and unfamiliar matters should be referred to learned authority. — Al-Māwardī reports Abū Hurayra's narration from the Prophet: the prohibition of dispute is repeated three times, people should act on what they know, and no grading is stated. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 26:18/q22 What bitter implication does Pharaoh's wording carry?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:18/q22/p1 Moses' long stay recalls Pharaoh's failed precautions against Israelite boys. — Al-Biqāʿī says Pharaoh's fear came true despite the precautions, making the years he mentions sound long and bitter to him. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
