# 69:6: 15 questions

## 69:6/q01 Who are ʿĀd?  [عَادٌۭ · referent]
turns on: Whether ʿĀd is read as the historical people or as an inward type of transgressor.
- 69:6/q01/p1 The historical people of the prophet Hūd. — Al-Biqāʿī identifies them as Hūd's people. Bursevī locates them in al-Aḥqāf, from Oman toward Ḥaḍramawt and Yemen, and describes them as imposing Arabs, reportedly sixty to a hundred cubits tall, with dome-like heads. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 69:6/q01/p2 In an inward reading, people who transgress religious law through heretical permissiveness in divine unity. — This is Qāshānī's reading of ʿĀd. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:6/q02 Why does the account of ʿĀd follow that of Thamūd?  [عَادٌۭ · links]
- 69:6/q02/p1 The order follows chronology and advances from lesser to deeper misguidance. — Bursevī gives both grounds for the order. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 69:6/q03 How were ʿĀd destroyed?  [فَأُهْلِكُوا۟ · interpretation]
- 69:6/q03/p1 They were punished and destroyed by a devastating wind or storm. — Muqātil glosses 'destroyed' as punished. Al-Ṭabarī, Jishumī, and al-Durr contrast their wind with Thamūd's destroying cry; al-Ṭabarī prefers an overpowering cry for Thamūd. Asad and Kuran Yolu give seven nights and eight days and bodies like hollow or uprooted palms; Kuran Yolu says their palaces were razed and their people vanished, citing 54:19–20 and attributing the punishment to persistent unbelief. Iṣlāḥī calls them tempestuous winter winds. Al-Durr's Qatāda says the wind pierced their hearts. [8 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TAB-FULL+]

## 69:6/q04 What is the destroying wind in this verse?  [بِرِيحٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wind is read as a historical storm or an inward force.
- 69:6/q04/p1 The physical storm that struck ʿĀd. — Asad describes its seven-night, eight-day duration and the bodies left like hollow palms; Kuran Yolu describes the destruction of people and buildings. The reports identify its westward source and its unusual force in other questions. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:6/q04/p2 In Qāshānī's inward reading, sudden egoic desire arising from a frozen nature and lack of ardor and love. — Qāshānī says this desire drives the transgressors to ruin. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:6/q05 From which direction did ʿĀd's wind come?  [بِرِيحٍۢ · reports]
- 69:6/q05/p1 It was the west wind. — Al-Māwardī, al-Nasafī, Bursevī, and al-Durr transmit the Prophetic contrast: the Prophet was aided by the east wind, whereas ʿĀd were destroyed by the west. Al-Durr's expanded report adds the ring-sized wind and the people mistaking it for rain, citing 46:24. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن عباس); NASAFI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 69:6/q06 What does the report about a ring-sized release say the wind did?  [بِرِيحٍۢ · reports]
- 69:6/q06/p1 Only a ring-sized portion was released; it carried people, herds, and property into the air and cast them onto a town whose residents thought it was rain. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Ibn ʿUmar's Prophetic account: it lifted desert people, livestock, and property and cast them on town residents. Al-Durr's Ibn ʿAbbās report describes herders and camps swept into a city and crushed, and cites 46:24; the reports vary in transmitter and wording of the affected groups. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 69:6/q07 Why is wind expressed in the singular here?  [بِرِيحٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 69:6/q07/p1 Qur'anic wind in punishment contexts is singular, whereas winds in mercy contexts are plural. — Al-Burhān cites ʿĀd's wind and 30:48, 15:22, 30:46, 33:9, 41:16, and 14:18 for the usage. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-rīḥ denotes punishment; even the apparently helpful wind at 10:22 becomes a destructive storm, and he cites 3:117 and 46:24. [2 notes: BURHAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 69:6/q08 What does ṣarṣar mean?  [صَرْصَرٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣarṣar is traced to cold, to the wind's sound, to force, or to scorching heat; some sources explicitly allow two senses together or as alternatives.
- 69:6/q08/p1 Bitterly or severely cold wind. — Muqātil, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and others give cold; Qatāda says it pierced hearts. Al-Zajjāj discusses whether doubling conveys repetition. Al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Kashshāf, and al-Nasafī derive it from ṣarr, cold; al-Kashshāf and al-Qurṭubī say repeated or intense cold burns. Al-Ṭabrisī pairs cold with sound that makes teeth chatter; al-Biqāʿī pairs burning cold with deafening sound. Al-Malwī allows severe cold or heat in ṣarr. Iṣlāḥī calls it a winter wind. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Elmalılı, and Study Quran also report the alternative sound sense. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك والحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(ابن عباس، قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة والربيع والسدي والثوري); BIQAI-FULL(الملوي); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 69:6/q08/p2 A loud, piercing, repeated roar or howl of the wind. — Al-Naḥḥās reports loud sound; Mujāhid in al-Māwardī calls it howling. Al-Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bursevī, and Elmalılı describe the harsh repeated noise, with Ibn ʿAṭiyya treating the word as sound imitation. Al-Ṭabrisī says the cold and noise made teeth chatter; al-Biqāʿī says the sound deafened. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī allow a root of loudness, Nasafī connecting it to ṣarra, a cry. Study Quran reports the alternative, and notes other verses call the wind howling. [13 notes: NAHHAS(غيره); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI(ابن عباس، قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(R); KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 69:6/q08/p3 A severe, violently blowing wind. — Ibn Zayd and Mujāhid gloss it as severe in al-Ṭabarī. Al-Ṭabrisī reports a wind blowing violently beyond its usual limit. Al-Rummānī calls the wind severe, while distinguishing ʿātiya as a stronger term. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); TABRISI; RUMMANI]
- 69:6/q08/p4 An intensely scorching or hot wind. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Mujāhid's scorching gloss. Al-Malwī, as cited by al-Biqāʿī, says the root ṣarr can signify severe heat as well as severe cold. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(الملوي)]

## 69:6/q09 What does the doubled form ṣarṣar add?  [صَرْصَرٍ · grammar]
- 69:6/q09/p1 Doubling can convey repeated action or intensity. — Al-Zajjāj raises repetition for ṣarṣar and compares qalqala, repeated lifting, with aqalla, lifting, and doubled sound forms ṣarṣar and ṣalṣal with ṣar and ṣal. Al-Kashshāf describes repeated sharp sound and, alternatively, repeated burning cold; Nasafī similarly says repeated cold burns. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ observes that this doubled epithet appears three times for ʿĀd's wind, including 41:16 and 54:19. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 69:6/q10 How does ṣarṣar function grammatically?  [صَرْصَرٍ · grammar]
- 69:6/q10/p1 It is an adjective describing the wind; its wind-specific use explains its lack of a feminine ending. — Al-Ālūsī identifies ṣarṣar as an adjective and says its explanation came earlier. Al-Burhān explains the absent feminine marking by its special use for wind, unlike ʿātiya, which can describe other feminine nouns. [2 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:6/q11 What does ʿātiya say about the wind?  [عَاتِيَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: The target of the wind's 'rebellion' may be its ordinary bounds or angelic keepers, or the people it overwhelmed; some explain the keeper language figuratively.
- 69:6/q11/p1 It was exceptionally violent, exceeding ordinary force, measure, or bounds. — Muqātil says it exceeded its stores without pity. Al-Zajjāj notes ʿāt or ʿātiya for something very great; al-Rummānī says it is stronger than severe and implies rebellious excess. Al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Burhān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and others stress excess beyond a proper limit. Al-Kashshāf, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī allow a figurative account of its violence; Ālūsī says no stratagem saved those it pulled from shelters. Iṣlāḥī parallels the uncontrollable ʿātiya with Thamūd's ṭāghiya. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; RUMMANI; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزهري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); ALUSI-FULL]
- 69:6/q11/p2 It defied or overwhelmed its angelic keepers, escaping their measured control by God's permission. — Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī, and later transmitters say wind and rain ordinarily came in measured allotments, sometimes each wind and drop measured by an angel; ʿĀd's wind and Noah's flood alone exceeded the keepers or their knowledge, with Noah's flood linked to 69:11. Al-Ṭabrisī's al-Zuhrī/Qubayṣa report says ʿĀd's wind overflowed their knowledge in God's anger; al-Durr repeats Qubayṣa's version. Al-Thaʿlabī's report says Noah's flood overwhelmed reservoirs and escaped human control. Al-Ālūsī says the Ṣaḥīḥayn and others corroborate ʿAlī's report. Al-Kashshāf also permits taking the report's wording as a figure for extraordinary force. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,علي بن أبي طالب); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب وابن عباس); TABRISI(الزهري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب,علي وغيره); BIQAI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب وابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,علي بن أبي طالب,قبيصة بن ذؤيب); ALUSI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب)]
- 69:6/q11/p3 It overpowered or rebelled against ʿĀd, leaving them no shelter, resistance, mercy, or blessing. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and Qatāda stress the assault without mercy or blessing, with Qatāda saying it pierced hearts. Ibn Zayd says it rebelled against and overcame them. Al-Māwardī gives sparing no one as an option and Ibn ʿAbbās's people-directed alternative. Al-Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Study Quran stress that refuge and resistance failed; Ālūsī says it pulled them from shelters. Ibn Kathīr transmits Qatāda and al-Ḍaḥḥāk's accounts. [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة والربيع والسدي والثوري); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(IK,R); ALUSI-FULL]

## 69:6/q12 How does calling the wind 'rebellious' work as a figure of speech?  [عَاتِيَةٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the figurative mechanism is direct metaphor, emphatic simile, or a representative image that can function metonymically.
- 69:6/q12/p1 Rebellion is a metaphor for the wind's extreme force and excess. — Al-Kashshāf says the report's language may express exceptional intensity; his gloss uses rebellion metaphorically. Al-Burhān calls it a transfer of insolence to wind. Ālūsī describes metaphorical boundary-breaking and reports al-Zamakhsharī's figurative reading. Abū Ḥayyān also allows metaphorical violence. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 69:6/q12/p2 It may be an emphatic simile likening the wind to one who rebels against obedience. — Ālūsī reports this further analysis without rejecting it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 69:6/q12/p3 It may be a representative metaphor, able to function as metonymy when the image conveys its point without recalling its original story. — Ālūsī reports al-Kashf's account of the figure. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)]

## 69:6/q13 How do the clauses about Thamūd and ʿĀd relate?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources identify different features of the pairing: cause, matched words for excess, and the surrounding day frame.
- 69:6/q13/p1 They name parallel causes of destruction: Thamūd's overpowering cry and ʿĀd's wind. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the cry as the calamity named for Thamūd, matching ʿĀd's wind here. Jishumī likewise contrasts blast and wind. Ālūsī reports al-Zamakhsharī's demand for parallel causes: assigning one group transgression and the other an instrumental wind would be unbalanced since both could be said to fall through transgression; Ālūsī says the issue is parallelism, not event versus substance. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TAB-FULL+]
- 69:6/q13/p2 The wording pairs Thamūd's ṭāghiya with ʿĀd's ʿātiya as uncontrollable excess. — Iṣlāḥī draws this verbal parallel. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 69:6/q13/p3 The surrounding account begins and ends with 'day.' — Jishumī notes this frame while comparing the two peoples' punishments. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 69:6/q14 How is this wind described or echoed elsewhere in the Qur'an?  [* · links]
- 69:6/q14/p1 Other verses describe ʿĀd's wind as howling and barren, and repeat its ṣarṣar epithet. — Study Quran points to howling at 41:16 and 54:19 and barren wind at 51:41. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ counts three uses of doubled ṣarṣar for ʿĀd's wind, including 41:16 and 54:19. Ibn al-Jawzī points back to 41:16 when explaining ʿātiya. Kuran Yolu cites 54:19–20 for the storm and its effect on people. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:6/q15 What does the destruction of ʿĀd teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Different but compatible lessons about divine agency, warning, and Resurrection.
- 69:6/q15/p1 God chooses and directs even extraordinary wind as punishment, showing divine agency beyond nature. — Al-Biqāʿī says the unprecedented force shows God's choice and direction, answering denial of His rule and of Resurrection. Iṣlāḥī says wind normally serves life but God turns it into death when people rebel. Ibn ʿAṭiyya explains taskhīr as using a thing through power over it. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 69:6/q15/p2 Their fate warns the Muslim community and proves that the power to destroy can bring about Resurrection. — Kuran Yolu draws the lesson and cites passages about ʿĀd at 7:65, 7:72, 11:50, and 11:60. Al-Biqāʿī also connects the punishment to denial of Resurrection. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
