# 92:9: 11 questions

## 92:9/q01 What is al-ḥusnā that the denier rejects?  [ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the feminine 'best/good' names God's promise and reward, a place or being, a confession or practice, or the prior verse's full range of senses.
- 92:9/q01/p1 God's promised recompense or reward, especially in the hereafter. — Al-Farra and Ibn Qutayba specify the reward of Paradise and denial of any reward; al-Tabari prefers recompense, with Ibn Abbas's gloss. Al-Qurtubi specifies recompense for giving; Ibn Kathir locates it in the next life. Al-Malili adds that denial treats generosity and restraint as fruitless pessimistically. Ibn Ashur lists an implied 'reward'; Study Quran allows the Garden's reward. [18 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB(ابن عباس); THALABI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TAB+]
- 92:9/q01/p2 God's promised replacement or compensation for what is spent. — Al-Tabari's full account prefers the promised replacement and transmits it from Ibn Abbas and Qatada; Ibn Abi Hatim transmits it from Ibn Abbas. Al-Razi says withholding wealth on this understanding betrays a bad opinion of God, citing a proverb about withholding. The similar broad 'compensation' gloss in al-Qurtubi may include this sense. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); RAZI; QURTUBI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]
- 92:9/q01/p3 God's good promise, with its particular benefit unspecified. — Qatada twice calls it God's good promise in al-Tabari. Ibn Abi Hatim reports Qatada likewise. Al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi link denial to withholding good spending; Qatada in al-Durr describes neglect of God's due and self-sufficiency. Al-Baydawi says the denier rejects the promise and what it signifies. Ibn Ashur includes an implied 'promise'. [13 notes: TAB(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:9/q01/p4 Paradise itself, the Garden promised as the beautiful reward. — Al-Tabari reports Mujahid; al-Qurtubi reports him through Ibn Abi Najih and again in the full account. Al-Nasafi gives this alternative. Study Quran cites 3:148 and 3:195, where Paradise is called the most beautiful reward; Ibn Ashur says this is a conventional use. [9 notes: TAB(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(others); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:9/q01/p5 The testimony that there is no god but God, or God's oneness. — Al-Tabari transmits it from Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak; his full account explicitly mentions God's oneness. Al-Qurtubi reports Mujahid's other transmission. Ibn Masud's report in al-Durr calls it the testimony of faith. Study Quran attributes this to most and adds belief in God as a related gloss. Ibn Ashur lists the testimony as a conventional use. [8 notes: TAB(ابن عباس,الضحاك); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس، الضحاك); QURTUBI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); STUDYQURAN(most); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:9/q01/p6 Islam, God's call and religion. — Al-Nasafi gives Islam as an alternative to Paradise; Qutb describes the denied good as good creed, God's call and religion. [4 notes: NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 92:9/q01/p7 God Himself, or God's Reality. — Study Quran says the Garden's reward, the Garden's reality and God's Reality can all be intended; its author cites God's Most Beautiful Names at 17:110, 20:8, 59:24 and 7:180. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 92:9/q01/p8 The highest good of spiritual perfection. — Al-Qashani says contentment with worldly life veils the unseen and afterlife and makes the person reject perfection. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 92:9/q01/p9 A better outcome or victory. — Ibn Ashur explains the feminine form of 'better' by an implicit feminine noun, possibly victory or outcome alongside reward and promise. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:9/q01/p10 Prayer or alms. — Ibn Ashur lists prayer and alms among conventional referents of al-ḥusnā; these are distinct acts in his list. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:9/q01/p11 The same range of seven explanations given for affirmation of al-ḥusnā in verse 6. — Al-Mawardi expressly carries over seven earlier interpretations; al-Wahidi says it is what Abu Bakr affirmed; Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Alusi refer back to 92:6. Corpus Coranicum also refers back to its note there without spelling out a separate sense. [7 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 92:9/q02 How is al-ḥusnā formed grammatically?  [ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · grammar]
- 92:9/q02/p1 It is the feminine form of 'the better/best,' with a feminine noun understood. — Ibn Ashur supplies possible nouns: reward, victory, promise or outcome; he also lists conventional referents such as Paradise, the testimony, prayer and alms. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:9/q03 What does the denial say about the denier's belief and conduct?  [وَكَذَّبَ بِٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of denial the source explains: refusal of truth, distrust expressed in withholding, or attachment to worldly life.
- 92:9/q03/p1 He rejects a truth deserving belief and loses hope in God's good promise. — Al-Biqa'i stresses denial of what deserves affirmation; al-Baydawi says he rejects the promise and what it signifies. Al-Malili says he assumes giving and restraint will bring no better result; Ibn Ashur says affirming the promise entails hope and action, so denial reverses both. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:9/q03/p2 Miserliness and self-sufficiency accompany denial of God's provision or reward. — Ibn Abi Hatim's Ibn Abbas says the miser relies on himself; al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi add withholding good spending. Al-Razi sees distrust of God's replacement. Al-Alusi reads disdain for God's provision or preference for worldly pleasure and dismisses the alternative gloss of withholding commanded acts. Qatada in al-Durr says he withholds God's due. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI; ALUSI; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:9/q03/p3 Treating worldly life as sufficient obscures the unseen and leads to moral ruin. — Al-Qashani says this veil blocks perfection and the afterlife; Qutb says withholding self and wealth while rejecting God's call carries the person toward extreme corruption. Islahi says worldly fixation removes resolve for the hereafter's reward. [4 notes: QASHANI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:9/q04 Who is the denier portrayed in this verse?  [وَكَذَّبَ بِٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the contrasted person is treated as an archetype or identified with a particular report about the palm or Bilal.
- 92:9/q04/p1 The miserly, self-sufficient person, understood as a general type. — Al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi describe a withholder of good spending; Qatada says one withholding God's due; Qutb makes this a broad rank among diverse human aims. Ibn Ashur says the three traits together characterize a polytheist. [11 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR]
- 92:9/q04/p2 The Ansari owner of the date palm who refused the Prophet's proposed exchange. — Al-Tha'labi's Ata account and al-Baghawi identify him against Abu al-Dahdah, who acquired and donated the palm; al-Qurtubi gives this as an alternative contextual account. Al-Tha'labi links the owner to the 'most wretched' in the Fire. [5 notes: THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:9/q04/p3 Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, contrasted with Abu Bakr. — Al-Kashshaf reports the pairing. Ibn Abbas's report through al-Kalbi and Abu Salih in al-Durr names Abu Sufyan as the miser and Abu Bakr as the giver. [7 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح)]
- 92:9/q04/p4 Umayya ibn Khalaf, in the report of Abu Bakr freeing Bilal; another report adds Ubayy. — Al-Baghawi's anonymous report says Abu Bakr bought Bilal from Umayya for a garment and ten coins. Ibn Masud's report in al-Durr contrasts Abu Bakr's effort with Umayya and Ubayy's. [2 notes: BAGHAWI(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 92:9/q05 What occasion or illustrative event is reported for these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the people, transaction and price, or the time of the angels' call.
- 92:9/q05/p1 A miserly palm owner denied fallen dates to a poor household; a believer exchanged forty palms for that tree and gave it to them. — Ibn Abi Hatim's Ibn Abbas report says the buyer was promised a palm in Paradise; it links the surah's revelation to this sale. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 92:9/q05/p2 A palm owner refused a Paradise palm in exchange; Abu al-Dahdah bought his tree and donated it to the needy neighbor. — Ata's accounts in al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi differ in purchase detail: al-Baghawi says he paid with his garden, while al-Tha'labi only says he bought it. Al-Qurtubi calls this an alternative setting; al-Baghawi's full account maps the giver and miser to the two men. [4 notes: THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:9/q05/p3 Abu Bakr bought and freed Bilal from Umayya, and the opposed efforts occasioned the passage. — An anonymous al-Baghawi report gives the price as a garment and ten coins. Ibn Masud's report in al-Durr says the release was for God and also names Ubayy among the opposed figures. [2 notes: BAGHAWI(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 92:9/q05/p4 The verses concern Abu Bakr and Abu Sufyan ibn Harb. — Al-Kashshaf reports this pairing; Ibn Abbas's transmission through al-Kalbi and Abu Salih in al-Durr identifies Abu Bakr with the giver and Abu Sufyan with the miser. [8 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
- 92:9/q05/p5 At sunset two angels pray for repayment to the spender and destruction of the hoarder. — Al-Tha'labi's Abu al-Darda report says the call is heard by creatures except humans and jinn and associates the passage's revelation with this contrast. [1 notes: THALABI(أبو الدرداء عن النبي)]
- 92:9/q05/p6 At sunrise two angels pray that God replace the spender's gift and ruin the hoarder's wealth. — Al-Mawardi's full account transmits this from Abu al-Darda through Khulayd and Qatada; it supplies no grading. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]

## 92:9/q06 What does this denial lead to in the passage?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether hardship is described as facilitated deeds and final punishment or as the corrupting course of the person's conduct.
- 92:9/q06/p1 God facilitates the denier toward evil deeds and finally the Fire. — Ibn Abbas in Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Durr says he is eased toward evil. Al-Tha'labi and al-Baghawi explain this as doing what displeases God until punishment is deserved; their palm account equates hardship with Hell. Islahi says God lets the person follow his chosen desires, seeming to prosper until death and then facing the steep hardship of 74:17. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:9/q06/p2 His rejection and withholding tend toward the greatest moral corruption. — Qutb says withholding self and wealth and denying God's call and religion carries the person there. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 92:9/q07 What did the Prophet say about decree and action when reciting this passage?  [* · reports]
- 92:9/q07/p1 People's destinations or deeds are decreed, but they must act; each is enabled for the work or path assigned to them. — Al-Tabari preserves several versions from Ali, al-Nazzal, Sa'd ibn Ubada, Jabir and Bashir; one cites 54:49 and another contrasts a settled decree with a new beginning. Ibn Hibban's Ali reports have Shu'ba's corroborating transmission through Mansur and al-A'mash. Al-Qurtubi places Ali's question at a Baqi funeral; his Tirmidhi wording is graded hasan sahih. Two young men respond that they will strive. Al-Durr repeats Ali and Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami, and gives Jabir's Suraqa version. Study Quran repeats Ali. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي ﷺ عن النزال بن سبرة,النبي ﷺ عن بشير بن كعب,النبي ﷺ عن جابر بن عبد الله,النبي ﷺ عن سعد بن عبيدة وأبي عبد الرحمن السلمي,النبي ﷺ عن علي); IBNHIBBAN(شعبة,علي بن أبي طالب); QURTUBI-FULL(الغلامان,علي عن النبي,غلامان شابان عن النبي); DURR(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]

## 92:9/q08 How are denial and affirmation arranged in the two paths?  [وَكَذَّبَ بِٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether one describes the paired structure as a whole or explains why its acts are placed first.
- 92:9/q08/p1 Denial is the negative counterpart of affirming al-ḥusnā in verse 6, one of four paired opposites. — Ibn Ashur calls the pattern joining with division and lists giving/withholding, piety/self-sufficiency, affirmation/denial and ease/hardship. Corpus Coranicum lists the same pairs and notes matching behavior-to-consequence sequences with a cross-rhyme attributed to Neuwirth. Al-Alusi also refers back to verse 6's senses. [9 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ALUSI-FULL; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI]
- 92:9/q08/p2 The giving and withholding pair comes first because it underlies the ensuing traits. — Al-Irshad, reported by al-Alusi, says the acts are lower in rank than the faith and piety or denial and self-sufficiency that follow, but foundational to them. [1 notes: ALUSI(الإرشاد)]

## 92:9/q09 How does this verse fit the passage and the sūra's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Which larger frame is emphasized: divergent human effort, the sūra's paired stanzas, or the preceding sūra.
- 92:9/q09/p1 The paired paths explain how differing striving reaches reward or punishment. — Al-Razi relates this to the contrast of human striving in 92:4 and the Fire warning in 92:14. Qutb says varied efforts converge in two ranks, each with its facilitation. Ibn Ashur offers either the ease/hardship groups or the two sets of three traits as a valid division; he also illustrates varied deeds through 99:6–8. Bint al-Shati links the oath's night, day and creation to earthly efforts and afterlife fates. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI]
- 92:9/q09/p2 The two opposed conduct lists are matched with divine promises of their final outcomes. — Corpus Coranicum describes antitheses in verses 5–13 and 14–21; first-person plural authorizes God's promise beside the oath, comparable to 93:1 and 94:1. It observes the final stanza reverses the order of saved and damned, with ittaqā at 92:5 echoed by atqā at 92:17; warning and promise precede conduct there. Neuwirth instead groups verse 11 with the theological assertions in 12–13. [6 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth)]
- 92:9/q09/p3 The traits here specify the success and failure announced in the preceding sūra. — Abu Hayyan connects them to 91:9–10, followed by the Fire warning and a distinction between those entering it and those spared. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 92:9/q10 What other scriptural wording is brought alongside this denial and its outcome?  [* · links]
turns on: The cited passages illuminate different words or themes: denial, ease, or promised reward.
- 92:9/q10/p1 Other uses of 'denied' are compared with this verse. — Corpus Coranicum compares kadhdhaba at 92:16, 95:7 and 73:11 and refers to its 92:6 note on al-ḥusnā. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:9/q10/p2 Ease and hardship here are compared with other promises of ease. — Corpus Coranicum compares 94:5–6 and distinguishes the promise addressed to the messenger in 87:8. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:9/q10/p3 The Prophet cited the verses of al-Layl to support the promise of Paradise in return for work. — Al-Burhan reports that he also cited 32:17 for unseen delights and 56:30 for the tree's extended shade. [1 notes: BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:9/q11 Does a specific reported occasion limit whom the verse addresses?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:9/q11/p1 The meaning remains general despite reports identifying individuals. — Al-Razi argues from 92:4, which says striving differs, and 92:14, which warns of the blazing Fire. Kur'an Yolu notes a view that verses 5–19 concern Abu Bakr but says the message and rulings remain general. [2 notes: RAZI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]
