# 31:26: 13 questions

## 31:26/q01 What does it mean that all in the heavens and earth belongs to God?  [لِلَّهِ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · interpretation]
- 31:26/q01/p1 Every being and thing, including idols and their worshippers, is God's creation, servant, and possession, subject to His sovereign disposal. — Muqātil calls creatures His servants within His dominion. Al-Ṭabarī explicitly includes idols and all things whether worshipped or not; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes idolaters and their idols. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ālūsī add unrestricted disposal and control. Al-Rāzī gives a rational proof: contingent created things depend ultimately on a necessary being, directly for Ahl al-Sunna or through an intermediary for others; he also compares ownership of all that emerges from owned land. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Qurṭubī ground ownership in creation; al-Biqāʿī invokes the disbelievers' admission that God created it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says creation entails governance and that absolute independence entails exclusive ownership and disposal. Iṣlāḥī and Kuranyolu add that everything is subject to and dependent on Him; Kuranyolu stresses His unrestricted freedom in creation and governance. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the idolaters' contrary statements lack reality. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 31:26/q02 Why is ما في and the preposition not repeated before earth?  [وَٱلْأَرْضِ · rhetoric]
- 31:26/q02/p1 The preceding admission of God's creation already establishes that earth is included, so repeating the expression is unnecessary. — Al-Biqāʿī points to the prior proof and admission of creation as sufficient for earth's inclusion. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 31:26/q03 What does God's ownership imply about worship and supposed partners?  [لِلَّهِ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · theology]
- 31:26/q03/p1 Only God deserves worship and has lordship; His possessions, including idols, cannot be independent rivals or claim worship or gratitude. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī infer exclusive worship from universal ownership. Al-Biqāʿī says the idolaters' admission of God's creation leaves no partner. Al-Ālūsī argues that a possession cannot be its owner's partner or claim its due. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says creation necessarily entails governance and disposal, establishing exclusive lordship and divinity, while no other can independently own or govern. Ibn ʿĀshūr says owning idolaters and their idols belittles their disbelief. Iṣlāḥī says none can share God's claim to gratitude because He alone has self-sufficiency and every praiseworthy attribute. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the idolaters' claims have no reality. Iṣlāḥī adds that the self-sufficient sovereign needs no intercessor to win His favour. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 31:26/q04 What force do هو and the definite epithets add?  [هُوَ ٱلْغَنِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ · rhetoric]
- 31:26/q04/p1 They emphatically restrict unmatched self-sufficiency and praise to God and exclude supposed gods. — Al-Biqāʿī says the explicit divine name and emphatic هو answer the implied denial of self-sufficiency in associating partners and affirm the attributes without limit. Bursevī says هو confines self-sufficiency to God, citing 47:38 on God's riches and human need. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls هو a separating pronoun restricting both attributes, with a reference to 31:12. Samarrāʾī contrasts the definite الغني الحميد after ownership with bare غني حميد where possession is unmentioned: the former marks unmatched wealth and praise. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:26/q05 How does the wording here compare with al-Ḥajj 22:64?  [هُوَ ٱلْغَنِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ · rhetoric]
- 31:26/q05/p1 This verse uses هو and definite epithets; 22:64 adds a lām before هو, giving further emphasis, though the cited excerpts leave its reason unanswered. — Both Samarrāʾī notes observe this contrast and explicitly pose, without answering, why the additional lām occurs in 22:64. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 31:26/q06 What does al-Ghanī mean here?  [ٱلْغَنِىُّ · meaning]
- 31:26/q06/p1 God is absolutely self-sufficient, needing nothing from creation, including its worship or praise, while creatures need Him. — Al-Jishumī glosses the word as the opposite of needy. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Bursevī say He needs nothing for existence or perfection; Bursevī extends this to before and after creation and to essence and attributes. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭībī specify independence from worshippers' praise or worship; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī and Iṣlāḥī contrast universal creaturely need. Al-Rāzī says God's ownership benefits Him in no way and unbelievers' lack of praise cannot diminish Him. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that the source of creation and giver of every perfection must possess all that beings need, or He could not originate or give it; His independence entails ownership without a competing independent governor. Bursevī cites 47:38. Samarrāʾī says the form after the ownership clause marks unmatched wealth and praise. Study Quran refers readers to its note on 31:12 for these names without giving a further gloss here. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 31:26/q07 What does al-Ḥamīd mean here?  [ٱلْحَمِيدُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name expresses worthiness for praise regardless of its occurrence, praise actually given, or the act of ascribing praiseworthy qualities.
- 31:26/q07/p1 God inherently deserves praise, whether anyone praises Him or not, for His perfections, beautiful acts, creation, and benefits. — Al-Jishumī makes the term the opposite of blameworthy. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Bursevī locate praiseworthiness in God's essence and attributes; Bursevī says He praises Himself even if no one else does. Al-Ṭabarī cites blessings and 14:1; al-Qurṭubī His acts; Abū Ḥayyān creation and gifts; Ibn Kathīr creation, law and all affairs; al-Biqāʿī absolute benefaction. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines ḥamd as praise for chosen beauty: all worldly beauty is God's, so all praise returns to Him; independent beautiful governance by another would contradict His absolute praiseworthiness. Iṣlāḥī says every praiseworthy attribute is His, so gratitude belongs to Him alone. Al-Ālūsī reports this as one sense. Study Quran refers to its note on 31:12 without adding a gloss here. [24 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 31:26/q07/p2 God is actually praised by creation, by its speech or state, for His sovereignty and acts. — Muqātil says He is praised among creatures in His sovereignty; Yaḥyā says creatures praise Him and are charged to do so. Al-Ṭabarī links praise to His blessings; al-Qurṭubī to His acts; Ibn Kathīr to creation, law and all affairs. Al-Biqāʿī says every tongue praises Him and even blameful speech is praise insofar as God caused it. Al-Ālūsī reports actual praise by every creature through its state as an alternative to mere deservingness. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 31:26/q07/p3 Al-Ḥamīd can denote one who describes Himself or His servants with praiseworthy qualities. — Al-Rāzī adds that a human ḥamīd can likewise mean one who describes himself or others with praiseworthy qualities, or a grateful worshipper. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 31:26/q08 How do God's self-sufficiency and praiseworthiness work together here?  [ٱلْغَنِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give three rationales for joining independence and praise: praise cannot benefit God, His beneficence benefits creatures, or absolute wealth grounds absolute praiseworthiness.
- 31:26/q08/p1 God needs no praise, yet deserves it regardless of whether creatures give it. — Al-Rāzī gives this as his second explanation: unbelievers' failure to praise cannot diminish God, while believers' praise is vindicated and completed. Al-Kashshāf, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭībī likewise contrast independence from praise with inherent worthiness. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Ṭībī's additional point that ownership belittles idolaters and removes any need for their worship. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]
- 31:26/q08/p2 God gains nothing from owning creation, while His creation and beneficence meet human needs and merit praise. — Al-Rāzī presents this as his first explanation. Al-Ālūsī says the second epithet signals beneficence and perfections while the first rules out owning things from need. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects God's giving every perfection with His independence and says all beauty returns praise to Him. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 31:26/q08/p3 Because needy creatures praise whoever supplies their needs, only the absolutely self-sufficient can be absolutely praiseworthy. — Al-Rāzī gives this as his third explanation of the pair. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 31:26/q09 Why are creatures obliged to praise or worship God if He needs neither?  [ٱلْغَنِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ · interpretation]
- 31:26/q09/p1 The obligation benefits creatures themselves; their worship and praise supply no need in God. — Yaḥyā says God made creatures duty-bound to praise Him. Al-Qurṭubī explains that His commands are for their own benefit. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Ṭībī stress that God has no need of polytheists' or other creatures' worship and praise. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي)]

## 31:26/q10 How does this verse follow the preceding admission about creation?  [* · links]
- 31:26/q10/p1 God's universal ownership follows from the admitted creation of heaven and earth, exposing the idolaters' disbelief and claims to partners. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the ownership sentence a result and inclusive apposition to what precedes, comparing the reasoning at 31:16; he says the stated conclusion belittles their disbelief because they and their idols belong to God. Al-Biqāʿī says the disbelievers' own admission of His creation leaves no room for a partner and renders repetition about earth unnecessary. Al-Ṭībī, reported by al-Ālūsī, says the ownership statement belittles idolaters and shows that God needs neither them nor their worship or praise. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 31:26/q11 How does the following verse relate to this ownership statement?  [* · links]
- 31:26/q11/p1 It counters an inference that God's dominion is finite by presenting boundless wonders of His power and knowledge. — Al-Rāzī says the preceding ownership statement could suggest a finite dominion, which the next verse corrects. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 31:26/q12 What reading of the following verse is reported?  [* · readings]
- 31:26/q12/p1 Abū ʿAmr and Yaʿqūb read al-baḥra in the accusative, coordinated with the syntactic position of mā, meaning 'and if the sea.' — Al-Jishumī reports the readers and gives coordination with the syntactic position of mā as the grammatical explanation. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب)]

## 31:26/q13 What spiritual practice or outcome is drawn from the verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: The notes offer different devotional applications: a broad path of sincerity and obedience, and persistence in a particular invocation.
- 31:26/q13/p1 Sincerity, reliance on God, tawḥīd, and obedience lead to Paradise and nearness; hypocrisy and shirk lead to loss and separation. — Bursevī commends entrusting oneself to God, turning from all else, sincerity and obedience, opposing them to disbelief, shirk, showing off and reputation-seeking, which lead to fire. He quotes ʿAṭṭār's couplet that worldly success helps in the end only with sincerity; otherwise the soul is lost. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الشيخ العطار)]
- 31:26/q13/p2 Persistence in an invocation praising God's acts and beneficence brings immeasurable wealth. — Bursevī reports an Idrīsian invocation calling God praiseworthy in action and beneficent to creation; al-Suhrawardī gives the promised result for persistence in it. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهروردي)]
