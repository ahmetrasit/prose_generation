# 28:21: 19 questions

## 28:21/q01 From where, and in what circumstances, did Moses leave?  [فَخَرَجَ مِنْهَا · reports]
- 28:21/q01/p1 He left Pharaoh's city in Egypt after warning of a plot to seize him. — Yaḥyā, Thaʿlabī, Qushayrī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bayḍāwī, Nāṣafī, and others identify the departure as from the city or Egypt. Biqāʿī says he acted promptly because the warning man's signs made him credible. Ālūsī says he obeyed that warning. Ibn Kathīr says he knew of Pharaoh's plot, left alone, and was unused to flight after comfort, luxury, and status. Kuranyolu says the Egyptian's killer became known, Pharaoh ordered his capture, and a well-wisher ran to warn him. Bursevī says he departed immediately without provision, mount, or companion. Thaʿlabī adds that he prayed during the flight. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:21/q02 How did Moses find his way toward Midian?  [فَخَرَجَ مِنْهَا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in whether the route was found through general divine guidance, an angel's direction, or travel on the main road while searchers took side routes; these details can partly coexist.
- 28:21/q02/p1 He did not know the way, and God guided or prepared the route for him. — Ibn ʿAbbās says he had never met a man there, knew no route, and trusted his Lord. Ibn Isḥāq says he had no provisions, servants, or mount; Wāḥidī reports him saying God prepared the way. Bursevī likewise says he had no provisions, mount, or companion. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن إسحاق); BURSEVI]
- 28:21/q02/p2 An angel guided him toward Midian. — Muqātil identifies Gabriel and says God also gave Moses a staff. Al-Suddī describes an angel on horseback with a spear meeting him in side paths, forbidding prostration, and directing him; al-Durr's version says Moses prostrated in fear before the angel led him. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Gabriel or another angel, with a staff perhaps already Moses's. These reports differ in the angel's identity and the prostration detail. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي ومقاتل); DURR-FULL(السدي)]
- 28:21/q02/p3 He took the main road to Midian and escaped the searchers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he got away unseen, travelled alone on an unfamiliar road, and trusted God. Biqāʿī says God directed him along the main road, while pursuers searched side routes because they assumed a fugitive would avoid it. Baghawī reports Pharaoh's order to search the mountain passes because Moses did not know the route; al-Durr says searchers used byways. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Midian was eight days from Egypt according to Ibn Jubayr and others, and ruled by someone other than Pharaoh. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير والناس); BIQAI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي)]

## 28:21/q03 Where did Moses obtain the staff mentioned in the flight reports?  [فَخَرَجَ مِنْهَا · reports]
turns on: Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares the angel-and-staff report with the more frequent account that Moses took the staff for later shepherding.
- 28:21/q03/p1 God gave him a staff through the angelic guidance. — Muqātil says God sent Gabriel to guide Moses and gave him the staff. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the angel account with a staff, while allowing it may have been Moses's own. [2 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي ومقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 28:21/q03/p2 He took the staff for shepherding in Midian. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this report sounder and more frequent than the angel-gift account. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 28:21/q04 Why was Moses afraid as he left?  [خَآئِفًۭا · interpretation]
- 28:21/q04/p1 He feared being pursued, captured, and killed after the Egyptian's killing. — Qatāda links his fear to the killing. Muqātil, ʿAbd al-Razzāq, Zajjāj, Rāzī, and Study Quran mention fear of death or seizure; Zajjāj adds that Moses watched for signs or tracks. Nāṣafī and Bursevī include attack on the road. Biqāʿī says he feared Pharaoh's people and looked back repeatedly. Ibn Kathīr says he looked around fearfully. Study Quran links this to the earlier fear after striking the Egyptian at 28:18. Others simply describe the fearful flight. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:21/q05 What was Moses watching or awaiting in يَتَرَقَّبُ?  [يَتَرَقَّبُ · meaning]
turns on: The object of his expectation: threatening pursuers or God's help.
- 28:21/q05/p1 He kept watch for pursuers, attack, or harm. — Ibn Qutayba glosses watchful fear and expectation of harm. Jishumī says taraqqub is waiting and keeping watch and relates it to raqīb. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it literally means waiting, derives from observing a thing's condition, and metaphorically conveys caution here. Zajjāj specifies signs or tracks; Biqāʿī describes repeated backward glances. Nāṣafī and Bursevī include attack on the road. The other sources specify pursuit or capture. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:21/q05/p2 He awaited God's sufficiency, guidance, and aid. — Qushayrī reports an alternative explanation that he watched for divine sufficiency and aid. Samīn says he awaited guidance and God's succor. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]

## 28:21/q06 How is Moses's prayer related grammatically to his watchfulness?  [يَتَرَقَّبُ قَالَ · grammar]
- 28:21/q06/p1 The prayer is a badal ishtimāl of his watchfulness. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says keeping watch includes praying to God for rescue. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:21/q07 Why does Moses address God as رَبِّ?  [رَبِّ · rhetoric]
- 28:21/q07/p1 He invokes the benefactor who created, nurtured, and favored him. — Biqāʿī grounds the address in God's many prior acts of kindness to Moses. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:21/q08 What does نَجِّنِى mean?  [نَجِّنِى · meaning]
- 28:21/q08/p1 Rescue or deliver me. — Biqāʿī derives the word from najwa, a high place inaccessible to everyone. Yaḥyā, Qurtubī, and others paraphrase the prayer as a request for rescue; Bayḍāwī and Bursevī add protection from being caught. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:21/q09 What does Moses's prayer reveal about his reliance on God?  [قَالَ رَبِّ نَجِّنِى · interpretation]
- 28:21/q09/p1 He recognizes God as his refuge and seeks divine rescue. — Rāzī says Moses knew there was no refuge except God. Study Quran reports commentators who say the prayer shows reliance on God alone. Yaḥyā, Qushayrī, Qurtubī, and others state the request for rescue; Bursevī says he sought freedom from Pharaoh's people and protection from their pursuit. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); YAHYA-SALLAM; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:21/q10 Who are the unjust people, and what makes them unjust?  [ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · referent]
- 28:21/q10/p1 They are Pharaoh's people in Egypt, including its polytheists and Pharaoh's elite. — Muqātil and Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by Wāḥidī, identify Egypt's polytheists; Zajjāj, Wāḥidī al-Wajīz, Nāṣafī, and others say Pharaoh's people. Baghawī calls them unbelievers; Ibn Kathīr names Pharaoh and his elite. Biqāʿī says they seek to kill one undeserving despite their power, reversing proper order. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they are polytheists and seek to kill Moses for an accidental killing, which reason and law do not warrant. Rāzī says they sought to kill him in retribution. [12 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:21/q11 How did God answer Moses's plea?  [رَبِّ نَجِّنِى مِنَ ٱلْقَوْمِ ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · reports]
- 28:21/q11/p1 God saved him by guiding him toward Midian, beyond the reach of the searchers. — Muqātil describes Gabriel's guidance and the staff. Ibn ʿAbbās says Moses trusted God despite not knowing the way; Ibn Isḥāq says God guided him without supplies or a mount. Wāḥidī says God prepared the route. Biqāʿī explains that the main road let him evade searchers in side routes. Bursevī says God answered and the later account explains the rescue. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن إسحاق); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 28:21/q12 What does the scene imply about Moses's killing of the Egyptian?  [ٱلظَّٰلِمِينَ · theology]
turns on: Rāzī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī infer lack of culpability from the unjust pursuers and the flight; Sāmarrāʾī identifies the act named in Moses's earlier confession while distinguishing it from unbelief.
- 28:21/q12/p1 Moses did not regard the accidental killing as a culpable sin. — Rāzī argues that if the killing were a sin Moses would be the oppressor, rather than those seeking to kill him in retribution. Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise treats his fearful flight as evidence he did not deem the act a crime for which he was culpable. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:21/q12/p2 His confession of wrong concerned the killing, while he was already a believer. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the Queen of Sheba, who left sun worship and submitted to God at 27:44, with Moses; his confession at 28:16 referred to killing the Egyptian, not conversion from unbelief. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 28:21/q13 What inward lesson is drawn from Moses's fearful flight?  [* · inward]
turns on: The sources develop either an inward reading of Moses's actions or a general lesson about trials leading a person to God.
- 28:21/q13/p1 It signifies spiritual struggle, vigilance, and refuge from oppressive dominance in God. — Qāshānī reads the departure as striving in God while maintaining presence and watchfulness; Moses fears the oppressors' dominance and seeks refuge in God. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 28:21/q13/p2 An ordeal can drive a servant to God and become the means of reaching divine beauty and kindness. — Bursevī reports some gnostics saying terror makes one flee from everything to God, where the Beauty of the All-Merciful is found. His Masnavi example has fear of a night watchman send a lovesick youth into the garden of his beloved. He adds that an apparent enemy may be medicine and guide; the cited snake analogy shows the same venom sustains a snake but kills a human, so adversity's effect varies by recipient. [5 notes: BURSEVI(بعض العارفين)]

## 28:21/q14 How does this departure fit the surrounding account?  [* · links]
- 28:21/q14/p1 It is part of the sequence from Moses's maturity to his arrival in Midian. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the section recounts events after Moses reached maturity that led him out of Egypt. Ibn al-Jawzī says his departure in fear had already been explained earlier in the sūra. Kuranyolu supplies the immediate link: discovery of the Egyptian's killer, Pharaoh's capture order, and the well-wisher's warning. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:21/q15 What larger pattern does this fearful flight contribute to in al-Qaṣaṣ?  [* · links]
- 28:21/q15/p1 Fear recurs through al-Qaṣaṣ, contrasting with al-Naml's briefer, more honorific treatment. — Sāmarrāʾī traces fear from Moses's mother's alarm at 28:7 and 28:10 through his fear after the killing and this flight, then his prayer, reassurance in Midian at 28:25, fear of Pharaoh's killing and denial at 28:33, and request for Aaron at 28:34. He says al-Naml mentions fear only at the staff miracle; his Ḥalaqāt account calls that setting more honorific. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:21/q16 Why is Moses's story retold with different details across the Quran?  [* · links]
- 28:21/q16/p1 Each telling has a nuance served by its wording, order, and detail. — Burhān compares the repeated Moses and Pharaoh accounts, including 28:3–43, with earlier scripture's separate single tellings and the gathering of Joseph's story in one sūra at 12:1. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 28:21/q17 How does Moses's escape connect to Muhammad and his audience?  [* · links]
turns on: Burhān and Ibn ʿĀshūr draw both a parallel in the prophets' escapes and an argument from the story to Muhammad's prophetic standing.
- 28:21/q17/p1 Moses's fearful escape foreshadows Muhammad's departure from Mecca and rescue from oppressors. — Burhān offers the parallel of the two departures. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the warning hints that Muhammad too will leave Mecca and God will save him. [2 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:21/q17/p2 The account offers signs of prophecy and invites Meccans to assess Muhammad's character. — Burhān links Moses's account after the Night Journey to Muhammad's visible signs and reports of past events as proofs of prophecy. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God chooses and prepares means for His messenger, whose upright conduct and love of truth reveal that care; the signs invite Meccans to consider Muhammad's character and their dealings with him, citing 10:16 and the earlier episode at 28:14. [3 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:21/q18 What is reported about the revelation setting of al-Qaṣaṣ?  [* · sciences]
- 28:21/q18/p1 All of al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this view from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ, and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:21/q19 What is reported about retaining the yāʾ in نَجِّنِى?  [نَجِّنِى · readings]
- 28:21/q19/p1 The yāʾ is written in the codices and retained by the readers. — NASHR reports that codices consistently write yāʾ in fifteen listed places and the readers agree to retain it; the list includes فإن الله يأتي بالشمس in al-Baqara. [1 notes: NASHR(القراء)]
