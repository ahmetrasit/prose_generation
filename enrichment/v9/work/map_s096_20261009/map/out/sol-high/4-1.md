# 4:1: 105 questions

## 4:1/q01 Whom does “O people” address?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the address is read by its general definite plural and shared rationale, or narrowed by its later reference to Arab appeals.
- 4:1/q01/p1 All accountable people addressed by the revelation, including women and later generations — Al-Rāzī cites the definite plural, universal creation, and universal duty of taqwā; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī tie the address to shared origin; al-Ālūsī excludes past generations unable to comply. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 4:1/q01/p2 The people of Mecca or Arabs who invoked God and kinship — Ibn ʿAbbās grounds the restriction in Arab invocation custom; al-Rāzī replies that a particular final clause need not narrow the opening. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL-]
- 4:1/q01/p3 Believers or unbelievers and hypocrites specifically — Saʿīd b. Jubayr calls the warning one to believers; Ibn ʿAbbās in Ibn Abī Ḥātim identifies unbelievers and hypocrites; Abū Ḥayyān cites believers' brotherhood. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]
- 4:1/q01/p4 All Adam's children, including earlier peoples — Al-Ālūsī reports an eternal-speech rationale but objects that the command's revealed legal force cannot bind earlier peoples. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 4:1/q02 How does the opening vocative work grammatically?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · grammar]
- 4:1/q02/p1 Yā calls, ayy is the singular vocative, hā alerts, and al-nās specifies or modifies ayy — Al-Naḥḥās notes yā can be omitted when the addressee is known; al-Akhfash instead treats ayy as a relative pronoun. [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 4:1/q03 What does the command to fear the Lord require?  [ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ · interpretation]
turns on: The breadth of taqwā in the command and whether it is read as general duty, social duty, or an inward state.
- 4:1/q03/p1 Obey God and avoid disobedience, sins, and His punishment — Al-Ṭabarī stresses commands and prohibitions; Ibn ʿAbbās glosses obedience, Muqātil fear, and al-Ṭabrisī adds avoiding neglect of God's due. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل,مقاتل); ELMALILI]
- 4:1/q03/p2 Protect mutual rights and maintain human ties — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī say common descent frames the sūra's obligations toward kin, orphans, women, and spouses. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:1/q03/p3 Inwardly, avoid all that diverts from God and purify intention — Al-Sulamī gives levels from avoiding shirk through leaving reliance on deeds; al-Qushayrī names the self as the first 'other'; al-Bursawī distinguishes three levels. [19 notes: SULAMI(أبو يزيد,الجريري,النصر آباذي,الواسطي,بعضهم,سهل); QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q04 Why is God called “your Lord” here?  [رَبَّكُمُ · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q04/p1 The title evokes ownership, creation, nurture, and benefaction, which warrant obedience — Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses rabb as owner; al-Rāzī argues lordship and servitude entail submission; al-Biqāʿī and Elmalılı emphasize nurture. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:1/q05 How is “who created you” related to the command?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَكُم · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q05/p1 Creation establishes God's power to punish and His blessings that demand grateful obedience — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Ālūsī give both power and favor; al-Rāzī adds that creation makes God master and enables obedience itself. [17 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:1/q05/p2 The creation account also grounds social care through shared origin — Al-Ṭabarī says all people owe sibling-like care; al-Rāzī applies it especially to the weak; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees protection of social unity. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 4:1/q06 How is “created you” parsed and what does creation mean?  [خَلَقَكُم · grammar]
- 4:1/q06/p1 The relative adjective qualifies “your Lord,” and min marks origin in creating the addressed people — Al-Naḥḥās gives the imperative and adjective case; al-Samīn calls min nafs an accusative complement and min the starting point. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 4:1/q06/p2 The verse does not establish that every creation requires prior matter — Al-Rāzī's theologians argue that creation of something distinct from matter is from nonexistence; al-Ālūsī says the verse states only human origins. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 4:1/q07 How is خلقكم pronounced at the qāf–kāf junction?  [خَلَقَكُم · readings]
- 4:1/q07/p1 Most pronounce qāf and kāf distinctly; some assimilate qāf into kāf — Al-Jishumī says their shared point of articulation allows assimilation. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 4:1/q08 Who or what is the one soul?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether nafs denotes a particular ancestor, a generic human origin, or an inward archetype.
- 4:1/q08/p1 Adam, the particular first human and common ancestor — Numerous early reports name Adam; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the two parents of 7:27 and Adam's children in 17:62, and says a generic reading loses the force of 'one' in 4:1. [30 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(the classical commentators); STUDYQURAN(most commentators); TABATABAI-]
- 4:1/q08/p2 Humanity or a living entity in a common-origin sense, without identifying an individual Adam — ʿAbduh and Asad seek a statement of brotherhood without commitment to the Biblical narrative; some exegetes take every person's parents generically, but al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the verse speaks of one original source. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين); ASAD-NOTES(Muhammad 'Abduh)+; TABATABAI-]
- 4:1/q08/p3 Inwardly, the universal rational soul or Muhammadan Reality — Al-Qāshānī calls it the world's heart and real Adam; al-Ālūsī calls it father of historical Adam; an unusual view reported by Abū Ḥayyān calls it spirit paired with body. [4 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن الفارض); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 4:1/q09 Why does wāḥidah have a feminine ending, and is wāḥid read?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · grammar]
- 4:1/q09/p1 Wāḥidah agrees with the feminine noun nafs though Adam is male; masculine wāḥid is possible by sense and is also read — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, and others cite semantic agreement and poetry; Ibn Abī ʿAblah's reading has wāḥid, and al-Samīn notes nafs can itself be masculine. [17 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة، ومجاهد، وقتادة وغيرهما); RAZI-FULL(الشاعر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي عبلة); STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]

## 4:1/q10 What does nafs mean as a word?  [نَّفْسٍۢ · meaning]
- 4:1/q10/p1 A person or thing itself; a human self can include body and spirit — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives the basic lexical sense; Kur'an Yolu also gives living element continuing after death and recounts views identifying or distinguishing nafs and spirit. [12 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı âlimler, filozoflar ve sûfîler,Bazıları,Gazzâlî,Şah Veliyyullah); QUSHAYRI]

## 4:1/q11 Does this verse imply one Adam or earlier human cycles?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · theology]
turns on: Whether transmitted reports, creation verses, or proposed scientific inferences establish prior human kinds or cycles.
- 4:1/q11/p1 This Adam is humanity's first ancestor, with no earlier humans like him — Al-Ālūsī states the mainstream Sunni view and rejects an eternal human species; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī holds that the current lineage begins with Adam and Eve. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(زين العرب); TABATABAI(زين العرب)]
- 4:1/q11/p2 Other Adams or earlier worlds may have preceded this Adam — Reports in Jāmiʿ al-Akhbār, al-Tawḥīd, and al-Khiṣāl give thirty or a million Adams and twelve thousand worlds; al-Ālūsī suggests other worlds need not mean prior humans here. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds the Qurʾān noncommittal about earlier cycles. [12 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عربي,الصادق,صاحب جامع الأخبار,محمد بن علي الباقر); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,الإمام الصادق عليه السلام,الصادق)]
- 4:1/q11/p3 Humanity arose by development from earlier life forms — Researchers cite increasing complexity and limited observed development; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says no experiment demonstrates change of species into humanity. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الباحثون عن طبيعة الإنسان); TABATABAI-]

## 4:1/q12 What can be inferred about the age of humanity?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · sciences]
turns on: How much chronological weight is assigned to traditional reports, demographic estimates, and ancient remains.
- 4:1/q12/p1 A seven-thousand-year chronology is reported — Al-Ālūsī reports a six-thousand-year span before the Prophet and a seven-thousand-year world; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī outlines Jewish tradition and a demographic model, including severe mortality. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:1/q12/p2 The chronology is unknown or much older evidence exists — Al-Ālūsī says reports conflict and God alone knows; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports geologists' ancient fossils but says they do not establish uninterrupted descent. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 4:1/q13 Who is the spouse, and what does zawj mean?  [زَوْجَهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse is read as a historical creation account or as an inward pairing.
- 4:1/q13/p1 Eve, Adam's wife; zawj can name either spouse or a paired companion — Ibn Qutayba gives 'companion'; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad say either human partner can be zawj; al-Aṣmaʿī objects to zawjah, while poetry and juristic usage support it. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الأصمعي,الفرزدق,ذو الرمة); ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI(الراغب); STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 4:1/q13/p2 Inwardly, the animal soul, nature, or body paired to a rational soul or spirit — Al-Qāshānī calls it the animal soul; al-Ālūsī allows nature; Abū Ḥayyān reports the soul–body interpretation as unusual. [3 notes: QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 4:1/q14 What was Eve created from?  [مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether min is bodily partitive, points to common kind or clay, or marks another proposed substance.
- 4:1/q14/p1 From Adam himself, especially a rib — Mujāhid specifies the short rib during sleep; al-Suddī places Adam alone in Paradise; Ibn Isḥāq specifies the left rib. Prophetic rib reports counsel gentle treatment; al-Qāḍī argues an independent Eve would imply two original souls. [33 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(النبي); TABRISI(أكثر المفسرين,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,تفسير علي بن إبراهيم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس، ومجاهد، والسدي، وقتادة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,الجمهور); RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(النبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(commentators); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)+; TABATABAI-]
- 4:1/q14/p2 From Adam's kind or the same human nature, without requiring a rib — Abū Muslim cites 16:72, 3:164, and 9:128; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites other same-kind spouse verses and says this verse supplies no rib proof; modern writers treat rib reports figuratively. [16 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن بحر); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم الأصفهاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن بحر وأبو مسلم); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Abu Muslim); STUDYQURAN(some modern commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 4:1/q14/p3 From the leftover clay of Adam's creation — Abū Jaʿfar al-Bāqir and a reported Prophetic answer reject the rib account and specify leftover or same superior clay. [4 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عن أبيه عن آبائه عن رسول الله); STUDYQURAN(Muḥammad al-Bāqir); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 4:1/q14/p4 A heavenly maiden of the same substance as houris — Al-Ālūsī reports and rejects this for lack of Qurʾānic or hadith support. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 4:1/q15 When was Eve created relative to entering Paradise?  [وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · reports]
turns on: The divergent reports about the place and sequence of Eve's creation.
- 4:1/q15/p1 After Adam entered Paradise, while he slept — Reports from Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, and others describe his sleep, rib removal, and waking recognition; some specify left, others right. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود وابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود,قتادة); BURSEVI]
- 4:1/q15/p2 Before Adam entered Paradise — Kaʿb al-Aḥbār, Wahb, and Ibn Isḥāq are named for this report. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(كعب الأحبار ووهب وابن إسحاق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وابن مسعود)]

## 4:1/q16 How are the creation clauses joined syntactically?  [وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether wāw joins the preceding finite verb directly or an understood clause or verbal sense.
- 4:1/q16/p1 They coordinate directly with “created you”; wāw does not require chronological order — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn reject a needed ellipsis; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the clauses as successive qualifications detailing one wondrous creation. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:1/q16/p2 An omitted “He created Adam” precedes “and created his spouse” — Al-Zamakhsharī inserts the clause to preserve the historical sequence; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī report it; Abū Ḥayyān says it is unnecessary. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 4:1/q16/p3 “Created her spouse” coordinates with wāḥidah understood as “made single” — Al-Samīn gives the verbal sense of wāḥidah and cites the verb waḥada as support. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 4:1/q17 What variant reads the creation and spreading verbs as participles?  [وَخَلَقَ وَبَثَّ · readings]
- 4:1/q17/p1 Khāliq and bāthth are read as participles with an omitted subject “He” — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Samīn parse them as predicates of huwa. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q18 What does baththa mean?  [وَبَثَّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether a contextual gloss states the act of creation or its dispersal.
- 4:1/q18/p1 To spread, scatter, or disperse widely; in context to propagate people — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj note abaththa as another form; 101:4 compares scattering moths, 88:16 spread carpets, and poetry uses the verb for disclosing secrets. [24 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن المظفر,الفراء والزجاج,ذو الرمة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(ابن المظفر,الفراء والزجاج,الواحدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 4:1/q18/p2 To create or bring forth, in al-Suddī's contextual gloss — Ibn Abī Ḥātim also attributes this gloss to Muqātil b. Ḥayyān. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي)]

## 4:1/q19 Who are the people spread from the pair, and how?  [مِنْهُمَا رِجَالًۭا كَثِيرًۭا وَنِسَآءًۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the dual source exhausts the ancestry of all presently living humans.
- 4:1/q19/p1 All human descendants arose through reproduction from Adam and Eve — Al-Ṭabarī and many others make the dual Adam and Eve; al-Rāzī notes descendants may be attributed to the original pair even through later generations; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies another contributing source. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 4:1/q19/p2 Human origins included separate parents or later admixture — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports color and blood arguments for four original couples and a geographic argument from the Americas, but counters that variation and geological change can account for both. [2 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI-]

## 4:1/q20 How did Adam and Eve's first children reproduce?  [وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا · reports]
turns on: Whether sole descent entails sibling marriage or leaves room for an unstated form of creation.
- 4:1/q20/p1 Their children married siblings under a temporary divine permission — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues a sole first pair entails this; al-Sajjād reports cross-pair sibling marriages and a later ban; the ruling is changeable when necessity ends. [11 notes: TABATABAI(السجاد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Tabâtabâî); TABATABAI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 4:1/q20/p2 They married houris or jinn spouses — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this alternative and rejects it in favor of sibling marriage. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]
- 4:1/q20/p3 The verse and sound reports leave the mechanism unknown — Kur'an Yolu notes varied miraculous creation modes for Adam, Jesus, and Yaḥyā and declines to infer sibling unions. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 4:1/q21 What do reports say about Adam and Eve's children?  [وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا · reports]
turns on: The number and details transmitted in the distinct reports.
- 4:1/q21/p1 Adam had forty children, twenty boys and twenty girls, in twenty paired pregnancies — Ibn Isḥāq names Cain, Abel, Seth, and several daughters; Ibn ʿAbbās gives the count. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن إسحاق)]
- 4:1/q21/p2 Eve bore Seth as Adam's third child after severe labor — Arṭāʾ says angels kept him forty days and taught him signs before returning him. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أرطاة بن المنذر)]

## 4:1/q22 Why mention many men and women in this form?  [رِجَالًۭا كَثِيرًۭا وَنِسَآءًۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the adjective is shared by ellipsis, intentionally restricted, or part of a hierarchy in ordering.
- 4:1/q22/p1 The wording conveys vast human multiplication; “many” after men also covers women — Ibn ʿĀshūr says baththa and the first adjective make women's abundance clear; al-Samīn reports ellipsis; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī say women may be more numerous. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الخطيب); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 4:1/q22/p2 Many qualifies men alone because men are publicly prominent while women are concealed — Al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Samīn report this; al-Biqāʿī also associates the order with visible strength. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 4:1/q22/p3 Men are named first as a mark of rank or prominence — Al-Burhān compares other Qurʾānic orderings; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives a distinct sex-symbolic inward reading. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q23 How does “many” work grammatically?  [كَثِيرًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether kathīr qualifies the expressed noun or an understood verbal noun.
- 4:1/q23/p1 It modifies rijāl and keeps one form with a plural noun — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 3:146; Abū al-Baqāʾ explains masculine agreement through the collective sense of men. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 4:1/q23/p2 It modifies an omitted verbal noun, meaning a great dispersal — Al-Samīn cites Sībawayh's circumstantial analysis; al-Ālūsī calls the analysis strained. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 4:1/q24 Do “men and women” exclude a third human sex?  [رِجَالًۭا كَثِيرًۭا وَنِسَآءًۭ · law]
- 4:1/q24/p1 An intersex person is not a third kind but belongs in reality to one of the two — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, and Abū Ḥayyān make the inference from the division into men and women. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 4:1/q25 How is tasāʾalūna read and formed?  [تَسَآءَلُونَ · readings]
turns on: The phonological treatment of the two tāʾs, and separate simple-verb forms.

[8 notes: JISHUMI(ابن كعب); DURR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,بعض أهل النبي,حذيفة,عائشة,واثلة بن الأسقع)]

## 4:1/q39 How does this opening connect with the previous sūra and al-Ḥajj?  [* · links]
- 4:1/q39/p1 It continues Āl ʿImrān's closing taqwā call and turns to shared human origin and care — Al-Jishumī notes the repeated command; Abū Ḥayyān connects prior promises to mutual care; al-Biqāʿī links Uhud's orphans and a shift from believers to all humanity. [9 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 4:1/q39/p2 Al-Nisāʾ and al-Ḥajj each open with “O people”: one grounds duty in creation, the other in resurrection — Al-Rāzī and later authors note each is fourth in a half of the Qurʾān and that beginning precedes return. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI]

## 4:1/q40 How does the opening frame Sūrat al-Nisāʾ and its ending?  [* · links]
- 4:1/q40/p1 Shared origin introduces laws of marriage, lineage, inheritance, and protection of women, children, orphans, and the weak — Al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and others trace the sūra's legal themes to common origin and kinship; al-Biqāʿī and al-Samarrāʾī relate first human generation to its ending on heirs and death. [20 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(الإمام); ITQAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 4:1/q40/p2 Creation wording links to other Qurʾānic accounts of origin, pairing, and return — Al-Thaʿlabī cites 7:189 and 39:6; al-Biqāʿī reads Adam, Eve, Jesus, and ordinary descent as four modes; al-Samarrāʾī compares the different creation verbs across sūras. [7 notes: THALABI; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]

## 4:1/q41 How does shared human origin encourage concord?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q41/p1 People should practice affection, humility, and mutual care because they share one human ancestor — Abū Ḥayyān stresses natural affinity and repudiation of pride; the common origin also supports the duties of the sūra. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 4:1/q42 What does a universal human address imply for women and enslaved people?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱتَّقُوا۟ · law]
- 4:1/q42/p1 The human address includes both sexes and, on the stronger legal analysis, enslaved people; the apparent reach of a masculine plural command is disputed — Al-Ālūsī reports the majority's grammatical distinction and the 33:35 and Umm Salama arguments, but here favors inclusion by predominance. He rebuts excluding slaves by examples of required prayer and specific exemptions. [13 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أم سلمة)]

## 4:1/q43 What signs of prophecy and unity does creation from one soul provide?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · theology]
- 4:1/q43/p1 The remarkable shared origin displays divine unity and power, while an unlettered Prophet's knowledge of it supports his prophethood — Al-Aṣamm says unaided reason cannot establish this unseen origin; Abū Ḥayyān notes the Prophet had no prior scriptural instruction and says branching descent defeats materialist claims. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأصم)]

## 4:1/q44 What does the verse teach about resurrection, pride, and human diversity?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q44/p1 Diverse humanity from one origin demonstrates divine power to resurrect and undermines pride and rivalry — Abū Ḥayyān follows al-Rāzī in arguing that re-creation is no harder than origination, and common ancestry calls for humility. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]

## 4:1/q45 How do the Adam and Eve reports bear on a literal or figurative rib?  [مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · reports]
- 4:1/q45/p1 A sound rib report is cited for bodily origin, while a figurative reading treats the rib as an image of temperamental difference — Abū Ḥayyān reports the Adam and Eve identification and Ibn ʿAṭiyya's sound hadith; he allows the figurative comparison, while al-Qāḍī argues the single origin favors a literal derivation. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والسدي,ابن عطية,القاضي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسلم)]

## 4:1/q46 Why might Eve's creation from Adam have taken this form?  [وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q46/p1 It displays creation of life from life, creates mutual desire and companionship, and prepares reproduction — Al-Ālūsī says the original part's identity ceased and reports Ibn ʿArabī's account of a void attracting Adam to Eve; he also says the pair may be unique among animals and the addressees' creation is fronted for exhortation. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر,الشيخ الأكبر)]

## 4:1/q47 What additional rhetorical figures occur in this passage?  [* · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q47/p1 The passage uses antithesis, ellipsis, and repetition — Abū Ḥayyān identifies contrasts of one and mate, rich and poor, few and many; he notes omitted elements and recurring fear, creation, women, orphans, inheritance, and guarding. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 4:1/q48 How does a first mention of taqwā relate to rights of others?  [ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q48/p1 It can be a general avoidance of punishment or a focused warning to protect others' rights — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts the readings; al-Ālūsī describes broad piety as avoiding unbelief, sin, and vice alongside divine and human rights. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q49 What does the appeal to God's name imply for obedience?  [ٱلَّذِى تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q49/p1 The appeal treats God's name as weighty, so people should obey the One invoked — Abū Ḥayyān notes appeals by God and kinship together; al-Ālūsī says such entreaty calls for keeping God's commands. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q50 What further reasons support care for kinship?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q50/p1 The command joins a specific human right to reverence for God, backed by scriptural parallels and the kinship bond's reality — Abū Ḥayyān sees a common sense of avoiding violation and cites 2:83 and 2:27; al-Ālūsī says the second exhortation can specify what the first includes. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q51 What further objections were made to coordinating al-arḥām with a bound pronoun?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · grammar]
- 4:1/q51/p1 Some grammarians object to the tight preposition–pronoun unit and to the implications for swearing — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's unit argument and Ibn ʿAṭiyya's Basran, poetic-exception, inseparability, exhortative-value, and oath objections. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري)]

## 4:1/q52 What did al-Ālūsī say about the scope and order of the creation wording?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · grammar]
- 4:1/q52/p1 The addressed people and their ancestors are included without splitting the pronouns; the clauses can join directly — Al-Ālūsī rejects requiring a separate antecedent audience or implied clause, and says the wording gives the addressees' creation precedence because it most directly prompts obedience. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q53 What further reports concern other Adams or esoteric cycles?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · theology]
- 4:1/q53/p1 Accounts of prior cycles and Adams are reported but their application to this world's humanity is disputed — Al-Ālūsī rejects a proposed universal-soul cycle of judgments as unsound creed; Ibn ʿUmar reports an analogous origin for Iblīs's wife. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 4:1/q54 How are other reading details in the verse explained?  [وَخَلَقَ تَسَآءَلُونَ رِجَالًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · readings]
- 4:1/q54/p1 A participial creation reading and assimilated asking form are reported; the nouns for the offspring can denote both sexes at all ages — Al-Ālūsī describes the two-tāʾ source of the ordinary form, its omission and assimilation, and the wording's picture of each descendant as a potential ancestor. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الواحدي)]

## 4:1/q55 What basic glosses does al-Baghawī give?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَبَثَّ تَسَآءَلُونَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q55/p1 The one soul and spouse are Adam and Eve; God spread their offspring; people ask by God and should keep kinship — Al-Baghawī connects the ordinary accusative to maintaining ties, and says the Kufans lighten the verb through deletion. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قراءة العامة)]

## 4:1/q56 What kinds of evidence follow a Qurʾānic address to all people?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q56/p1 A general address may be followed by proofs of lordship or reminders of known blessing — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts addresses to unbelievers, which often receive proofs of unity, with addresses to believers, which often recall favors; the opening belongs to this rhetorical pattern. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 4:1/q57 What further does al-Baghawī say about the kinship reading and final attribute?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ رَقِيبًۭا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q57/p1 The genitive represents invoking kinship, while the accusative is more eloquent; raqīb is a guardian — Al-Baghawī says the overt noun normally needs its preposition repeated, although the construction occasionally occurs. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة)]

## 4:1/q58 What additional syntactic and social points does al-Bayḍāwī make?  [تَسَآءَلُونَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ رِجَالًۭا كَثِيرًۭا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q58/p1 The doubled verb assimilates tāʾ, the light one deletes it; “many” is collective; pairing kinship with God honors family ties — Al-Bayḍāwī names ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī for deletion and says care for kin is owed for God's sake. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي)]

## 4:1/q59 How does al-Biqāʿī connect the sūra's opening to revelation and social repair?  [* · links]
- 4:1/q59/p1 The opening serves a divine arrangement that develops knowledge and courage into justice, chastity, and care after Uhud — Al-Biqāʿī cites ʿĀʾisha's account of gradual legislation, reads the basmala as joining scattered relations, and links Eve's origination, procreation, and kinship duty to social repair and the sūra's end. [10 notes: BIQAI-FULL(يوسف بن ماهك)]

## 4:1/q61 How do rhetorical order and omission help explain this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q61/p1 Order, specified terms, and contextual ellipsis guide emphasis and sense — Al-Burhān treats the single soul as an allusion to Adam, discusses a specified member after a general class, permissible conjunct attachment, deletion with sufficient contextual indication, and ordering by rank or cause; it includes related examples elsewhere in the Qurʾān. ## 4:1/q62 How should the two fear commands relate to each other?  [ٱتَّقُوا۟ رَبَّكُمُ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q62/p1 Both address the original audience, with the second confirming and specifying the first — Al-Ālūsī objects to making the second alone Arab-specific; the first stresses shared creation and the second God-invoked social obligations. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(حمزة)]

## 4:1/q63 What is the inward reading of God's name and kinship?  [ٱلَّذِى تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · inward]
- 4:1/q63/p1 Asking by God denies independent existence; true kinship binds saints and higher principles — Al-Ālūsī says loving saints preserves spiritual ties and prevents estrangement from the Real. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 4:1/q64 What grammatical analogies bear on coordination and agreement here?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · grammar]
- 4:1/q64/p1 Coordination to pronouns, deletion of conjunctions, and agreement by noun or sense have competing analyses — Al-Burhān compares nominative and genitive pronoun conjunctions, Ibn Mālik's clause analysis, al-Fārisī's repeated-preposition rule, and a disagreement over feminine agreement in a separate nafs verse; another ordering ranks unseen above seen. [12 notes: BURHAN(أبو البقاء,ابن مالك,الجمهور,الزمخشري,الفارسي,الكوفيون)]

## 4:1/q65 How does al-Bursawī explain common origin and kinship?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q65/p1 Common descent and faith create duties of mutual care, while the spouse and family bond support reproduction — Al-Bursawī says women, family, and ancestral descent call people to preserve rights; he distinguishes people of the revelation and later generations from earlier people, and adds a spiritual warning against neglecting divine watchfulness. [12 notes: BURSEVI(ذو النون المصري)]

## 4:1/q66 What do the two gender nouns denote?  [رِجَالًۭا وَنِسَآءًۭ · meaning]
- 4:1/q66/p1 They mark the male and female human kinds — Al-Dāmghānī distinguishes rijāl from women and cites 33:40. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 4:1/q67 What extra reports specify Eve's creation and its aftermath?  [مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · reports]
- 4:1/q67/p1 Eve is identified with Adam's rib in several reports, and a reported saying links woman's desire to man and man's desire to earth — Al-Durr transmits Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAmr, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, and Ibn ʿAbbās, including differing back-rib descriptions, Satan's wife's analogous origin, and the Nabataean term for woman. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عمرو,الضحاك,مجاهد)]

## 4:1/q68 How does Elmalılı reason from present reproduction to the first pair?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q68/p1 Successive generations can be traced back to one first pair; Eve's miraculous branching and the complementary pair display divine creation — Elmalılı calls fathers prior in fertilization but says neither can reproduce alone. He compares the first pair to a seed with differentiated complementary parts, combines transmitted teaching with rational inference, and treats woman as a divine gift. [22 notes: ELMALILI(Resûlullah)]

## 4:1/q69 What readings and interpretations of the kinship appeal does Elmalılı distinguish?  [تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · readings]
- 4:1/q69/p1 Ḥamza's genitive favors an appeal by God and kinship, while the accusative also supports a direct duty not to neglect kin — Elmalılı says the appeal interpretation works under both cases, while the explicit legal command requires the non-genitive analysis; the first brings law forward, the second moral mercy. [3 notes: ELMALILI(Hamza)]

## 4:1/q70 How does Elmalılı connect gender, rights, and the divine command?  [وَنِسَآءًۭ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q70/p1 Universal brotherhood and family bonds require protection of women's dignity, responsible sexuality, and compassion governed by taqwā — Elmalılı distinguishes the opening universal warning from the later family warning; he says selfish desire must not defeat divine commands and reports the Prophet's praise of a righteous spouse. [7 notes: ELMALILI]

## 4:1/q71 What broader duties does Elmalılı derive from human creation?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q71/p1 Creation from a single pair calls for brotherhood, marriage, procreation, nurturing offspring, and obedience to a Creator beyond nature — Elmalılı rejects autonomous nature as the explanation of diverse offspring, cites Iblīs's erroneous nature argument, and reports the Prophet's encouragement of marriage and procreation. [11 notes: ELMALILI(Fahreddîn Râzî)]

## 4:1/q72 What additional glosses does Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmit?  [وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · reports]
- 4:1/q72/p1 Created can mean made; a report interprets woman's derivation from man as an explanation of attraction — Al-Suddī supplies the lexical gloss, while Ibn ʿAbbās's report says woman desires men and man desires earth, then counsels restraint. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي)]

## 4:1/q73 What does Ibn ʿĀshūr see in the opening's creation language?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q73/p1 The wondrous creation supports a universal summons, reciprocal benefit between spouses, and laws founded on lineage — He says the Prophet has a special kinship claim on polytheists; the spouse recalls al-Baqara, and both sexes benefit through mutual pairing and offspring. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 4:1/q74 How does Ibn ʿĀshūr read the family-rights command?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q74/p1 The accusative commands regard for relatives' rights, while the genitive recalls Arabs' reverent appeal by kinship and reproves neglect of those very relatives — He cites ʿUtba's appeal, criticizes al-Mubarrad's categorical grammatical rejection, and connects the wording with harm to orphan kin and even to Muhammad despite his blood relation. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(المبرد)]

## 4:1/q75 What broader praise of Qurʾān study is transmitted near this verse?  [* · reports]
- 4:1/q75/p1 Learning, teaching, reciting, and following the Qurʾān are praised in reports collected by Ibn ʿAṭiyya — The reports include the Prophet's saying about learning and teaching, Ibn Masʿūd's preference for recitation, Jesus' praise of following scripture, signs of late moral reversal, and extraordinary recitation accounts. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أسيد بن حضير,ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض العلماء,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,عبد الله بن مسعود,عقبة بن عامر,علي الأثرم,عيسى ابن مريم,محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 4:1/q76 What additional language evidence does Ibn ʿAṭiyya cite?  [وَبَثَّ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ رَقِيبًۭا · meaning]
- 4:1/q76/p1 Bathth is dispersal; Basran grammarians object to genitive coordination; raqīb is close, verifying observation — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites 101:4 for bathth, Sībawayh's poetic-license rule and Abū ʿAlī's weak-analogy view, and a watcher in the arrow game with Abū Duʾād's poem. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو دؤاد,بعض النحويين، وأبو علي,رؤساء نحويي البصرة، والزجاج عن المازني,سيبويه)]

## 4:1/q77 What further exegetical glosses and critiques does Ibn al-Jawzī report?  [ٱتَّقُوا۟ نَّفْسٍۢ تَسَآءَلُونَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q77/p1 Taqwā can mean reverent fear, nafs here Adam, asking invokes rights, and genitive kinship remains grammatically contested — Ibn al-Jawzī reports al-Zajjāj's and al-Farrāʾ's objections, Ibn al-Anbārī's past-custom defense, Abū ʿAlī's weak-but-possible analysis, and an alternative rights-seeking interpretation. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,ابن الأنباري,الزجاج,الفراء)]

## 4:1/q78 What does Ibn Kathīr add about kinship and God's watching?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ رَقِيبًۭا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q78/p1 People invoke God and kinship; God sees all deeds and the worshipper should remain aware of that sight — Ibn Kathīr reports an appeal formula from Ibrāhīm, Mujāhid, and al-Ḥasan, a genitive reading, the parallel 85:9, and the sound ḥadīth of worship as though seeing God. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(إبراهيم ومجاهد والحسن,بعضهم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 4:1/q79 What additional earlier glosses distinguish the two cases of al-arḥām?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q79/p1 Accusative means fearing severance; genitive represents appealing or swearing by God and kinship — Ibn Khalawayh reports the first gloss; Ibn Qutayba gives both meanings explicitly. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA(بعضهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 4:1/q80 Which criteria establish an accepted Qurʾānic reading?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · sciences]
- 4:1/q80/p1 A reading is accepted when soundly transmitted, compatible with Arabic in some face, and compatible with the Uthmanic writing — Al-Suyūṭī prefers Ibn al-Jazarī's criteria over al-Balqīnī's fixed categories of seven, ten, and anomalous; Abū Shāma warns a famous reader alone cannot guarantee every transmission. [6 notes: ITQAN(أبو شامة,جلال الدين البلقيني); NASHR]

## 4:1/q81 What further links and readings does al-Jishumī note?  [تَسَآءَلُونَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · readings]
- 4:1/q81/p1 Light and doubled asking forms coexist; the genitive kinship reading is transmitted, though fatḥa is preferred for its command to maintain ties — Al-Jishumī names ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, and al-Kisāʾī for the light form; he cites poetic precedent for coordination but prefers the accusative. Al-Itqān also notes the apt order of Medinan sūras. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ITQAN]

## 4:1/q82 What does al-Zamakhsharī draw from the first couple and the kinship clause?  [مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q82/p1 Humanity branches from Adam and Eve, and invoking God and kinship should lead to honoring requests and avoiding severance — Al-Zamakhsharī includes Eve's creation from clay at Adam's rib, al-Ḥasan's direction to grant appeals, and 17:23 as a parallel for pairing God's right with parents' rights. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(الحسن)]

## 4:1/q83 What is the first act of maintaining kinship in a child's conception?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · law]
- 4:1/q83/p1 Choose a lawful and wholesome context for conceiving children — Al-Zamakhsharī cites the Prophet's exhortation to choose well for offspring and Ibn ʿUyayna's explanation that this concerns one's children and lawful placement; he adds preservation of lineage and health. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عيينة,النبي)]

## 4:1/q84 How does Kur'an Yolu connect the universal address to family obligations?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q84/p1 A shared origin gives all humans rights, while descent through wombs adds nearer obligations, especially toward women as mothers, sisters, and wives — The source takes these two ties as the basis for al-Nisāʾ's social laws and treats family care as obedience to God. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 4:1/q85 What extra early reports explain the creation story?  [مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · reports]
- 4:1/q85/p1 Woman's creation from man is connected with desire for man, while Adam called Eve a Nabataean word for woman — Mujāhid reports Adam's waking utterance; al-Māwardī transmits a Prophetic saying at revelation about woman's concern with man and man's concern with clay. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); MUJAHID(مجاهد)]

## 4:1/q86 What further grammatical notes does al-Naḥḥās give?  [وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · grammar]
- 4:1/q86/p1 The dual uses one marker for both genders, and the accusative kinship phrase joins the fear command — Al-Naḥḥās also reports Abū Isḥāq's rights-seeking gloss as leaving no basis for genitive case. [3 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق)]

## 4:1/q87 What additional details does al-Nasafī give on reading and audience?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ تَسَآءَلُونَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q87/p1 The Messenger's addressees and prior humanity descend from Adam; both forms of asking invoke God, and kinship must be maintained — Al-Nasafī reports the Prophetic saying that woman was made from man, explains assimilation and deletion, and judges the genitive coordination weak because the pronoun is bound. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL(حمزة)]

## 4:1/q88 What is the inward account of the soul, spouse, and wombs?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ زَوْجَهَا وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · inward]
- 4:1/q88/p1 The rational soul has an animal-soul mate; outward family ties mirror ties to higher spirits and saints — Al-Qāshānī reads the left rib as orientation toward creation, the appeal as asking through God rather than oneself, and divine watching as prevention of veiling by a residual self; the Prophet's longevity report signifies lasting subsistence. [6 notes: QASHANI(رسول الله,قيل)]

## 4:1/q89 What further grammatical and exegetical comments does al-Qurṭubī give?  [خَلَقَكُم نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا تَسَآءَلُونَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q89/p1 The verse points to the Creator and Adamic descent, and the kinship wording can be read as command or reported invocation — Al-Qurṭubī refers several lexical discussions to al-Baqara, names Mujāhid and Qatāda for Adam and the rib, notes the two asking pronunciations and genitive readers, and preserves the Arab formula of asking by God and kin. [10 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أهل الكوفة,أهل المدينة,إبراهيم النخعي، قتادة، الأعمش، حمزة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد,مجاهد، قتادة)]

## 4:1/q90 What inward readings of “people” and taqwā does al-Qushayrī give?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱتَّقُوا۟ · inward]
- 4:1/q90/p1 The call can summon forgetful people back to God, while true piety and remembrance raise the seeker beyond self and worldly aims — Al-Qushayrī records derivations of insān from visibility or forgetfulness, and a derivation of its name from elevation or a mark. He distinguishes worship, seeking, love, knowledge, nearness, abandonment of creation, and the heart's grief in separation. [14 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 4:1/q91 What further inward obligations arise from creation and divine watchfulness?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ رَقِيبًۭا · inward]
- 4:1/q91/p1 Common creation displays divine governance and calls the seeker to live before a Watcher who knows the hidden heart — Al-Qushayrī says creatures pair for continuity and warns that God is adversary to abusive orphan guardians; al-Sulamī reports creation under prior divine knowledge and urges watchfulness over inward thought. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,عمرو بن عثمان,قيل)]

## 4:1/q92 How does al-Rāzī use creation to argue for revelation and social mercy?  [خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q92/p1 The unseen origin supports the Prophet's truth, and shared descent should produce affection for relatives and the weak — Al-Aṣamm says an unlettered Prophet's report of an unseen fact proves prophecy; the saying about Fāṭima illustrates kin affection. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الأصم,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 4:1/q93 What names and distinctive benefits are associated with Adam and Eve?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ زَوْجَهَا · reports]
- 4:1/q93/p1 Adam's name is linked to the varied earth, and Eve's to her living origin; their pairing brings companionship and reproduction — Ibn ʿAbbās derives Adam from earth's surface; the commentary derives Hawwāʾ from life, and al-Rāzī asks why a rib origin was chosen when earth creation was possible. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 4:1/q94 Why does the verse say God spread men and women without repeating the source?  [وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا رِجَالًۭا كَثِيرًۭا وَنِسَآءًۭ · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q94/p1 The wording avoids suggesting Adam and Eve were themselves dispersed from the pair — Al-Rāzī contrasts the given phrasing with an explicit 'spread from both parents' after naming them. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 4:1/q95 What other analyses and transmitted readings of al-arḥām does al-Rāzī weigh?  [تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · readings]
- 4:1/q95/p1 Accusative can be an implied exhortation or coordinate with bihi's position; the genitive is challenged by an oath objection that al-Rāzī answers — Al-Rāzī cites al-Wāḥidī's 'preserve kinship' ellipsis, al-Zajjāj's swearing objection, the light and doubled tasāʾalūn readings, three case endings, and the Qāḍī's distinct-meaning objection, to which he replies with repeated utterance. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,القاضي,الواحدي,صاحب الكشاف,قيل)]

## 4:1/q96 How do verse wording and sūra structure correspond?  [* · rhetoric]
- 4:1/q96/p1 Small verbal choices suit their contexts, and beginnings and endings of sūras answer one another — Al-Samarrāʾī treats exact word and letter choice as deliberate and regards opening–ending correspondence as intentional. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 4:1/q97 What other analyses of al-arḥām does al-Samīn record?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · grammar]
- 4:1/q97/p1 The accusative singles out kinship under general fear of God; the genitive can be an oath or omit a prior preposition — Al-Samīn notes Abū al-Baqāʾ's implied 'Lord of kinship' and exaltation reading, al-Farrāʾ's uneasy acceptance, and that God can swear by creatures though the verse's sense is not an oath. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الزجاج,الفراء)]

## 4:1/q98 What does al-Samīn say about source, gender agreement, and the conjunction?  [مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · grammar]
- 4:1/q98/p1 Min denotes starting point; masculine wāḥid follows Adam or a masculine nafs, and wāw permits direct coordination without time order — Al-Samīn disputes al-Zamakhsharī's audience restriction and reports the same-kind or leftover-clay view only to reject it. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري)]

## 4:1/q99 What social and symbolic meanings does Study Quran find in single-soul creation?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا · interpretation]
- 4:1/q99/p1 A common parentage calls for affection and humility, while the singular–dual–many sequence and mixed gender references suggest reciprocity — Study Quran links marriage to 2:187 and 30:21 and says the rib report may be an oblique allusion to a primordial androgynic humanity. [5 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 4:1/q100 What inward calls are heard in “O people”?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · inward]
- 4:1/q100/p1 The call summons forgetful people to intimacy with God and to present attention rather than distraction — Al-Sulamī reports that piety gathers all good; al-Wāsiṭī addresses those absorbed in passing time, Ibn ʿAṭāʾ calls them to intimacy, and Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq to never neglect God. [6 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,الواسطي,بعضهم,جعفر الصادق)]

## 4:1/q101 What further language points does al-Ṭabarī add?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar]
- 4:1/q101/p1 A poetic conjunction is an exceptional analogue for the genitive; the final address uses second person when the report includes hearers and absent people — Al-Ṭabarī also says honoring God in speech must become obedience in action. [3 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 4:1/q102 What further social and historical issues does al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī raise?  [نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q102/p1 The shared lineage is a real social bond, while the first-generation marriage and ancestral identity remain debated through reports and creation verses — He notes natural-law objections to sibling marriage, a difference between Adam as individual and type, the Torah's name Eve, the womb as a real small community, and kinship's stronger effect than ordinary association. [13 notes: TABATABAI(بعض علماء الحقوق,بعضهم)]

## 4:1/q103 What other linguistic and interpretive details does al-Ṭabrisī give?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ مِّن نَّفْسٍۢ وَٰحِدَةٍۢ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · interpretation]
- 4:1/q103/p1 The address is broad, creation from one source displays wisdom, and both accusative and nominative kinship forms can make its duty clear — Al-Ṭabrisī compares earlier scriptural address, calls creation a sign of knowledge, says honoring God in speech requires obedience, and parses the accusative as care for family ties. [7 notes: TABRISI(الباقون)]

## 4:1/q104 What additional evidence and background does al-Thaʿlabī record?  [وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · reports]
- 4:1/q104/p1 Poetry gives exceptional genitive coordination, while pre-Islamic inheritance practice neglected women and children — Al-Thaʿlabī cites two unnamed poetic lines and Ibn Zayd's report that an eldest male inherited to the exclusion of women and children. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن زيد,شاعر)]

## 4:1/q105 What more does al-Wāḥidī say about the asking and kinship readings?  [تَسَآءَلُونَ وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ · readings]
- 4:1/q105/p1 The asking verb has light and doubled forms; Hamza's genitive kinship reading reflects customary paired invocation — Al-Wāḥidī reports grammarians' criticism of the coordination and distinguishes the spread of descendants from divine surveillance of their deeds. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(حمزة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
