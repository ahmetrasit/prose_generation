# 91:13: 25 questions

## 91:13/q01 How does this warning follow the denial and the wicked man's rising?  [فَقَالَ · links]
turns on: Whether fāʾ orders the narrated events or the exposition.
- 91:13/q01/p1 The fāʾ gives event sequence: an initial denial, the she-camel sign and Ṣāliḥ's warning, then rejection of his threat and hamstringing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wicked man's rising is mentioned early to show collective incitement although hamstringing followed the warning; prophetic signs normally follow initial rejection. He cites Hūd 11:64 and al-Aʿrāf 7:73. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR+]
- 91:13/q01/p2 The fāʾ may introduce detail after a summary of Thamūd's denial, without moving the wicked man's rising out of its chronological place. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites comparable summary-to-detail transitions at 2:36 and 7:4. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:13/q02 Who does 'them' in lahum refer to?  [لَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether ashqāhā names one man or a collective, and whether Thamūd is treated as a people.
- 91:13/q02/p1 Thamūd as a people. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn make this the referent if ashqāhā denotes an individual; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the plural returns when Thamūd is treated as a people. Al-Ālūsī takes this as his first reading. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; ALUSI+]
- 91:13/q02/p2 The most wretched offenders understood as a group. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the direct killer is most intensely wretched; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow a collective ashqāhā. Al-Ālūsī says this remains compatible with the plural reference to watering. [6 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 91:13/q03 Who is 'God's messenger' here?  [رَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
- 91:13/q03/p1 Ṣāliḥ, sent to Thamūd. — Bursevī gives the lineage Ṣāliḥ ibn ʿUbayd ibn Jābir ibn Thamūd ibn Aws ibn Iram. Elmalılı cites Hūd 11:61. ## 91:13/q04 What does calling Ṣāliḥ 'God's messenger' contribute?  [رَسُولُ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 91:13/q04/p1 It magnifies his authority and God's sign, making obedience obligatory and the people's refusal more grave. — Al-Biqāʿī says the office is to convey and warn, and honoring the messenger and camel honors their sender; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī stress the duty to obey and the depth of defiance. [6 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 91:13/q04/p2 Rasūl denotes a sent emissary; its use here is an early nonbiblical application. — Corpus Coranicum compares Greek apostolos and Syriac shlīḥā, tracks later referents Moses, Muḥammad, and an angel at 73:15–16, 81:19, 69:10, 69:40, 77:11, and traces arsala from birds in 105:3 to punishments and messengers. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:13/q05 What did Ṣāliḥ's cry about the she-camel require?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ وَسُقْيَٰهَا · interpretation]

- 91:13/q05/p1 They must leave God's she-camel unharmed and protect her allotted access to water; some state an explicit threat of punishment. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī relate the command to 7:73; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Jishumī cite 26:155 for the water share. Al-Kalbī says punishment was warned of but denied. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the threat of painful punishment is explicit at 7:73. Corpus Coranicum notes fuller commands at 26:155–156. - 91:13/q05/p2 The conjunction may mean to leave the she-camel together with her watering, without separating them. — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposal but calls it far-fetched; his fuller commentary likewise calls it far-fetched. [1 notes: ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 91:13/q05/p3 The implied command could be to make much of, cling to, or merely respect the camel. — Al-Ālūsī records this proposed estimate and rejects it as unsound. [0 notes: ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 91:13/q06 How is 'God's she-camel' read and grammatically construed?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the form is accusative warning or nominative, and which omitted governing expression is supplied.
- 91:13/q06/p1 The transmitted accusative is a warning construction, with an omitted verb such as 'beware'. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī state that Arabic warnings take the accusative; al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī and others compare 'the lion, the lion'. Abū Ḥayyān attributes this reading to the majority. Al-Samīn says coordination requires the warning verb's omission; al-Ālūsī cites Makkī on disputed conditions and says repetition is unnecessary. Iṣlāḥī says the style focuses listeners. [34 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; TABRISI(الفراء عن الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:13/q06/p2 The accusative is governed by an omitted positive command, 'leave' or 'spare' the camel. — Al-Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq cite the parallel command at 7:73; al-Biqāʿī allows either 'leave' or 'beware'. Al-Ālūsī objects to treating the construction as merely a positive leave/respect command. [10 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ITQAN; BIQAI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 91:13/q06/p3 The nominative can mean 'this is God's she-camel' as a warning. — Al-Farrāʾ adduces nominative Arabic cries such as 'the enemy!' and a poem calling 'weapons!'; al-Samīn attributes the nominative reading to Zayd ibn ʿAlī, with an omitted subject. Al-Naḥḥās rejects inventing a new reading. [4 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; NAHHAS-]
- 91:13/q06/p4 Zayd ibn ʿAlī's nominative directs attention to guarding the camel and her water. — Al-Ālūsī reports Zayd's reading and construes it as a direction not to slaughter her or monopolize her water. [2 notes: ALUSI(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]

## 91:13/q07 Why are commands left unspoken in this cry?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ وَسُقْيَٰهَا · rhetoric]
- 91:13/q07/p1 The context supplies the commands, yielding brevity, urgency, emphasis, and a forceful warning or inducement. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān say the situation makes omission intelligible and avoids redundancy; al-Itqān also lists shortage of time, magnification, or only one possible completion. Ḥāzim says strong contextual indication and tedious detail can invite wonder or terror. Al-Burhān cites Sībawayh's common ease-driven contractions. Al-Biqāʿī points to urgency and impending punishment; Iṣlāḥī to focusing attention. [12 notes: FARRA; BURHAN(سيبويه); BIQAI; ITQAN(حازم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:13/q08 Why is the she-camel called God's?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
- 91:13/q08/p1 The possessive honors a divinely given sign of God's power and unity and Ṣāliḥ's prophethood, making violation more grave. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says her miraculous emergence exceeded ordinary causes; al-Biqāʿī says repeating God's name heightens the warning, for violating God's sacred claim cannot go unpunished. [12 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:13/q08/p2 It evokes a consecrated camel protected from slaughter and use. — Corpus Coranicum points to reported pre-Islamic dedication of camels and later Islamic accounts of their exemption from slaughter and other use; it sees a cultic offense in the inherited story. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:13/q09 What is reported about the she-camel's appearance as a sign?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the additional rock-emergence detail is included.
- 91:13/q09/p1 After the people requested a sign, the she-camel emerged from a rock. — Al-Qurṭubī says they demanded the camel; Study Quran connects the rock-born camel to 54:23–30, 7:73 and 26:155. [3 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 91:13/q09/p2 She was given as the requested miracle and test, but her special physical nature is not specified. — Quṭb says God disclosed no details of her special nature; Iṣlāḥī cites 54:27–29 for the test and assigned drinking turn. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:13/q10 What does suqyāhā denote?  [وَسُقْيَٰهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verbal noun names drinking, the allotted water or turn, or the source itself.
- 91:13/q10/p1 Her allotted share or turn of drinking water. — Qatāda calls it God's apportioned share; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī and Asad connect it to 26:155. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its verbal noun and context indicate a water allotment; one estimate supplies 'share' or 'water source'. - 91:13/q10/p2 Her act of drinking, or what is given to her to drink. — Al-Wāḥidī lists her drinking and the supplied drink alongside the allotment. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls suqyā a verbal noun from saqā and allows the source word to name its object, the water itself. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 91:13/q10/p3 The well or water source assigned to her. — Al-Biqāʿī names the well; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports an omitted noun such as her water source. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 91:13/q11 How were the drinking days allocated and why did they matter?  [وَسُقْيَٰهَا · reports]
- 91:13/q11/p1 The camel and Thamūd or their livestock had alternating days, one reserved for her and the next for them; the arrangement burdened or tested them. — Al-Ṭabrisī and Elmalılı cite 26:155; al-Rāzī says the arrangement harmed their herds and helped provoke the plan to kill her. Al-Qurṭubī says her day was at their well. [12 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:13/q11/p2 Her day barred them from watering their own camels or taking her water. — Al-Baghawī says not to approach the water on her day; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Ālūsī forbid withholding or monopolizing her share; Ibn ʿĀshūr infers the prohibition from suqyā. ## 91:13/q12 What occasion and threat lie behind Ṣāliḥ's brief warning?  [فَقَالَ نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: The reported timing of the demand for a sign relative to the warning.
- 91:13/q12/p1 Seeing or learning their intention to harm her, Ṣāliḥ repeated a brief warning that harm or denial of her water would bring punishment. — Al-Rāzī says his repeated threats made the brief allusion sufficient; al-Biqāʿī says he sensed the plot. Al-Kalbī reports they denied that the threatened punishment would come. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the explicit painful-punishment warning at 7:73. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:13/q12/p2 After Ṣāliḥ's warning they demanded a sign; the camel and her water turn became a final test. — Iṣlāḥī gives this sequence and calls the verse Ṣāliḥ's final warning; he cites 54:27–29. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:13/q13 Who attacked the camel and how is the culprit described?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: The differing reported names and descriptions of the killer.
- 91:13/q13/p1 Qidār was the rebellious Thamūd leader who killed her. — Iṣlāḥī compares him with Qayl ibn ʿUmar of ʿĀd and cites a couplet naming Qidār; al-Afwah al-Awdī likens the two to corrupt, destructive figures resistant to guidance. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(al-Afwah al-Awdī)]
- 91:13/q13/p2 An unnamed report describes the killer as blond, blue-eyed, short, and thick-necked. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 91:13/q13/p3 Umayya's poem names the culprit Aḥmar. — Corpus Coranicum says Aḥmar is associated elsewhere with ʿĀd, perhaps reflecting overlap of tribal names or traditions. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:13/q14 What happened after Thamūd harmed the camel?  [* · reports]
- 91:13/q14/p1 They denied Ṣāliḥ and hamstrung the camel, and God destroyed the people for both offenses. — Al-Thaʿlabī ties the punishment to rejection of the messenger and harm to the camel. Iṣlāḥī says a three-day respite for repentance increased their arrogance before destruction. [2 notes: THALABI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 91:13/q14/p2 The destruction covered them all without exception. — Al-Thaʿlabī explains 'He made it even' as punishment leaving none untouched. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 91:13/q14/p3 An older poem's aftermath includes a lame servant who survived, reported the event, and died. — Corpus Coranicum says the Quran omits this aftermath because it does not serve the general lesson. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:13/q15 What does damdama mean in the punishment account?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The lexical evidence adduced by the reported authorities.
- 91:13/q15/p1 Annihilating destruction. — Al-Thaʿlabī attributes this to al-Murūj. [1 notes: THALABI(المروج)]
- 91:13/q15/p2 Persistence or continuity. — Some linguists cite an Arab expression for a fat, full camel. [1 notes: THALABI(بعض أهل اللغة)]

## 91:13/q16 Is the destruction verb read damdama or dahdama?  [* · readings]
- 91:13/q16/p1 ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Zubayr reads fa-dahdama with hāʾ, a dialectal equivalent. — Al-Thaʿlabī compares paired verbs for a changed complexion. [1 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن الزبير)]

## 91:13/q17 How is the following 'and he does not fear its outcome' read and understood?  [* · readings]
turns on: The conjunction's transmitted reading and the pronoun's subject.
- 91:13/q17/p1 Hijazi and Syrian codices read fa-lā; others read wa-lā, and ʿuqbāhā means its consequence. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports the regional codices and the lexical gloss. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 91:13/q17/p2 The subject is God, who fears no claim against Him for destroying Thamūd. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports al-Ḥasan's explanation. [1 notes: THALABI(الحسن)]
- 91:13/q17/p3 The subject is the camel-killer, who rose without fearing the consequences. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Suddī and al-Kalbī reordering the syntax. [1 notes: THALABI(الضحاك والسدي والكلبي)]

## 91:13/q18 Did Thamūd ever believe Ṣāliḥ before the attack?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether there was an initial period of following Ṣāliḥ.
- 91:13/q18/p1 The majority say they were unbelievers. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the majority. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 91:13/q18/p2 A report says they followed Ṣāliḥ for a time, then disbelieved and hamstrung the camel. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 91:13/q19 What inward meanings are given to the camel and her drink?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ وَسُقْيَٰهَا · inward]
turns on: Different allegorical correspondences.
- 91:13/q19/p1 The she-camel is the soul, a divine gift carrying a person through deeds; its water sustains remembrance and worship. — Al-Biqāʿī says the soul must be preserved and purified; neglecting and slaughtering this gift merits destruction. [2 notes: BIQAI]
- 91:13/q19/p2 The camel is the spiritual path or way of law. — Al-Ālūsī reports a larger scheme: sun as necessary existence or divine essence, brightness as first determination or existence, moon as fixed entities or first determination, day and night as manifestation or beauty and majesty, heaven and earth as subtle and dense worlds. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض آخر)]
- 91:13/q19/p3 The camel and its drink are longing and remembrance or reflection. — Al-Ālūsī reports a scheme with sun as divine essence, light as Muhammadan reality, moon as possible being, day as manifest world, night as contingent existence veiling the Real, heaven as intellect and earth as body. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل)]

## 91:13/q20 How does this account connect to other Quranic accounts of Thamūd?  [* · links]
- 91:13/q20/p1 The brief account presumes fuller Quranic treatments of the camel's water share, warning, and punishment. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the camel and drink have already been treated. Al-Qurṭubī points to al-Shuʿarāʾ and al-Qamar; Asad cites 26:155 and his 7:57 note. Corpus Coranicum lists fuller 54:23–31 and shorter 89, 69 and 51, with 85 and 89 linking Pharaoh and ʿĀd. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:13/q20/p2 This belongs to an early series of punishment allusions, later expanded into fuller legends. — Corpus Coranicum groups Q 91, 89 and 85 with Thamūd, ʿĀd and Pharaoh and places them alongside Q 105 as early narrative Quranic texts; it calls this the oldest genuine Quranic punishment legend. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:13/q21 How familiar was the Thamūd story to its first hearers?  [* · reports]
- 91:13/q21/p1 It was familiar and proverbial among Arabs and Quraysh, so a short allusion evoked the full history. — Asad says the camel legend was well known before Islam. Iṣlāḥī quotes Farāhī on Quraysh's inherited settlements and traditions; his cited Arabic couplet and al-Afwah's comparison of Qayl and Qidār attest poetic familiarity. He says this familiarity explains why Thamūd alone is mentioned here. [8 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(al-Afwah al-Awdī)]

## 91:13/q22 How is this Quranic account related to a pre-Islamic poem?  [* · links]
- 91:13/q22/p1 It recasts an older Thamūd story by adding a divine messenger and explicit command, turning consecrated-camel disrespect into a moral and theological warning. — Corpus Coranicum considers Umayya ibn Abī al-Ṣalt's poem likely authentic: it lacks a divine warner, the foal's curse brings destruction, and its culprit Aḥmar may reflect overlapping traditions. It says the Quran omits the poem's lame surviving servant and may mirror the Quranic preacher's rejection. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 91:13/q23 What lesson does the Thamūd warning give the audience?  [* · interpretation]
- 91:13/q23/p1 Quraysh are warned that collective arrogance and rejection after clear warning can bring divine destruction. — Iṣlāḥī says historical incidents can move ordinary hearers alongside cosmic and inward evidence for reflective thinkers; God allows a nation time and clear warning before its persistence makes destruction necessary. Kur'an Yolu treats Thamūd as a community choosing evil despite the capacity to choose good, citing al-Aʿrāf and Hūd. [7 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 91:13/q23/p2 Worldly national punishment is collective, whereas the Hereafter judges each person individually. — Iṣlāḥī distinguishes collective worldly rebellion from individual final reward or punishment. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 91:13/q24 What speech act does the bare address perform?  [نَاقَةَ ٱللَّهِ وَسُقْيَٰهَا · rhetoric]
- 91:13/q24/p1 It combines warning with exhortation, as a call can induce conduct while warning against violation. — Al-Zarkashī cites al-Zamakhsharī that Quranic address carries religious instruction through commands, prohibitions, admonitions, warnings or stories. He reports Abū al-Baqāʾ that 'O Zayd' is a call, not a truth-valued proposition, and Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī's minority view that 'O sinner' asserts a quality. [5 notes: BURHAN(أبو البقاء,أبو علي الفارسي,الزمخشري); ITQAN]

## 91:13/q25 What reward is reported for reciting Sūrat al-Shams?  [* · reports]
- 91:13/q25/p1 A report likens it to giving charity over everything lit by the sun and moon. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this from the Prophet. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
