# 87:2: 17 questions

## 87:2/q01 How does 'who created' attach grammatically to the preceding words?  [ٱلَّذِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause modifies Lord or name despite the intervening adjective and the problem of assigning creation to a name, or instead begins an implicit predication or praise.
- 87:2/q01/p1 It qualifies 'Lord' or 'Most High' as another adjective. — Al-Naḥḥās says both adjectives can share one referent and compares the idiom yā Yazīd al-karīm dhū al-jumma. Abū Ḥayyān reports the possible attachment to rabb, while al-Ālūsī objects that an adjective of another noun intervenes. [5 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN(قالوا); ALUSI; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:2/q01/p2 It qualifies 'name'; creation is predicated of the name through its named referent or creative attribute. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the grammatical possibility if al-aʿlā qualifies ism. Al-Ālūsī finds a name's creating implausible and questions the proposed metonymy. Elmalılı says creation may aptly be assigned to the Creator-name as the effect of its attribute of bringing into being, citing Sunni and Sufi formulations. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN(قالوا); ALUSI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 87:2/q01/p3 It stands independently as the predicate of an omitted subject or as an accusative of praise. — Abū Ḥayyān says this allows al-aʿlā to qualify ism. Al-Ālūsī prefers this solution to the intervening-adjective and name-as-creator difficulties; Elmalılı also prefers treating the clause independently. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; ELMALILI+]

## 87:2/q02 What does 'created' mean here?  [خَلَقَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes origination, an initial form, or the assembly of parts before their proportioning.
- 87:2/q02/p1 God brought things into existence from nonexistence. — Al-Biqāʿī says God can bring about whatever He wills without difficulty. Elmalılı says the Creator, as such, transcends creatures' origination, possibility, and need for a cause. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 87:2/q02/p2 Creation can mean making an initial sketch or form that is subsequently perfected. — Iṣlāḥī cites the bird example at 3:49 for the sense of making an effigy; for a human, the initial sketch passes through stages before completion. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:2/q02/p3 Creating gathers the parts of a composed creature. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes gathering parts in creation from placing them proportionately through fashioning. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 87:2/q03 Whom or what does God create and fashion, and in what way?  [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The unstated object of 'created' may be every creature, living beings, humanity, Adam, or composed beings; the accounts also differ over whether fashioning concerns form, useful capacities, equal rulings, understanding, worship, or stages of gestation. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the usual rule favoring a general omitted object; al-Ālūsī rejects unsupported restrictions.
- 87:2/q03/p1 God creates all things and fashions them in sound, balanced, precise order without flaw. — Al-Ṭabarī glosses taswiyah as adjustment; al-Naḥḥās adds that the results are good. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, and Abū Ḥayyān see harmony as evidence of a knowing, wise maker; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah says it displays power and oneness. Al-Rāzī attributes the order to God's knowledge and power. Al-Ālūsī says each essence and attribute is formed as wisdom requires, rejects narrower objects as unsupported, and notes that the omitted object gives general scope. Al-Qurṭubī recalls his explanation at 82:7; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites precise fingertips and 91:7 and 75:38. Q 67:3–4 is cited by Study Quran for the absence of flaw. Al-Ṭabrisī says creatures are alike in sound construction, and another account says God forms things according to His will and wisdom as testimony to His oneness; al-Biqāʿī says even opposed elements are harmonized. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the omitted object is general by the usual rule when context does not specify it, adducing the majority, ʿAṭāʾ, and 20:50. [32 notes: TAB; NAHHAS; THALABI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR(الجمهور وعطاء); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 87:2/q03/p2 God fashions every kind or creature with what it needs for its own completion, activity, benefit, and livelihood. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursawī stress perfection and sustenance; al-Nasafī adds benefit. Al-Biqāʿī says the balance of opposed elements shows knowledge, power, wisdom and free choice, and pairs striking with the hand, walking with the leg, hearing with the ear and sight with the eye. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the scorpion's bent defensive tail; Asad emphasizes advance adaptation to conditions of existence. Kuranyolu cites 15:29 for fitting form. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 87:2/q03/p3 God creates each creature in an excellent or beautiful form. — ʿAṭāʾ says creation is in its best form, though the preserved comment ends mid-sentence; Ibn ʿAbbās says God beautified what He created. Ibn Kathīr calls each creature's form the finest. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 87:2/q03/p4 God creates living beings and proportions their limbs and senses. — Al-Kalbī names hands, feet or legs, and eyes; al-Rāzī adds each animal's needed organs, tools and senses. Al-Ālūsī reports this reading but says nothing in the ellipsis requires restriction to living beings. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); TABRISI(الكلبي); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(الكلبي); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(الكلبي)-]
- 87:2/q03/p5 God creates the human being with upright, balanced, beautiful stature. — Al-Zajjāj contrasts upright humanity with inverted cattle and beasts; al-Rāzī links the beautiful form to 95:4. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Zajjāj's human object, while al-Ālūsī says this restriction lacks textual support. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحق); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI(الزجاج); IBNASHUR(الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزجاج); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج)-]
- 87:2/q03/p6 God brings humans into being and then perfects their form. — Al-Māwardī transmits this as one possibility. Iṣlāḥī says the human sketch matures gradually; Muqātil names the womb stages of drop, clot and morsel of flesh. Study Quran describes the first human's shaping after the divine Spirit, without irregularity, with measured form and nourishment. [5 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 87:2/q03/p7 God creates humans complete and makes each limb correspond proportionately to its counterpart. — Al-Māwardī reports the corresponding-limbs explanation separately from simple perfection. [2 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 87:2/q03/p8 God creates Adam and perfects or fashions his form. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk specifies Adam; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the pairing with fashioning suggests this and cites 15:29. [6 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNASHUR(الضحاك); IBNASHUR-FULL(الزجاج)]
- 87:2/q03/p9 God creates people in their fathers' loins and fashions them in their mothers' wombs. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī transmit this division of the two acts. [4 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:2/q03/p10 God creates bodies and fashions understanding. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī report this distinction between bodily and intellectual formation. [4 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:2/q03/p11 God creates people through beneficence and makes them equal in His rulings. — Al-Māwardī gives this as a third possibility; al-Ālūsī reports the broader assertion that creatures are alike in their rulings and perfection, with none perfected at another's expense. [5 notes: MAWARDI(unattributed); MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 87:2/q03/p12 God creates humanity and equips it for religious obligation and worship. — Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī give this as a human-specific explanation of proportioning. [4 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 87:2/q03/p13 God gives humans faculties and tools enabling them to perform the actions of other animals. — Al-Rāzī records this as a second human-specific explanation. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 87:2/q03/p14 God creates the human in balanced form, with innate disposition and testimony to His lordship. — Al-Zajjāj connects human formation with its natural disposition and testimony that God is its Lord. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 87:2/q03/p15 The clause concerns composed creatures: creation collects their parts and fashioning proportionately places them. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates this account to the first four verses' depiction of divine governance and absolute lordship. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 87:2/q04 What is the sense of 'fashioned' (sawwā) as a word?  [فَسَوَّىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The word's range includes equality, adjustment, straightening, improvement, and perfection; Iṣlāḥī says the context selects perfection.
- 87:2/q04/p1 It means making proportionate, adjusted, straight, or precise. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it adjustment; Jishumī says equality in form, rank, status, or any respect. Al-Ālūsī calls proportion its original sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses it as straightening and precise making, citing 91:7 and 75:38. Iṣlāḥī also notes senses of improving and flattening, while choosing perfection here. [7 notes: TAB; JISHUMI; ALUSI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:2/q04/p2 In this context it means completing or perfecting what is made. — Iṣlāḥī contrasts possible senses of improving or flattening and says context selects perfecting; he also pairs an initial sketch with its later completion. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah and Qutb speak of perfection suited to the creature. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 87:2/q05 What does the 'fa' between creating and fashioning express?  [فَسَوَّىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the ordered verbs mark a conceptual and causal sequence accompanying one act, or emphasize observable stages of human development.
- 87:2/q05/p1 It makes fashioning the consequence and chief point of creation, ordered in thought though simultaneous in occurrence. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Ibn Ziyāba's poem, where a raid, plunder, and escape provide the ordered groundwork for grief; creation likewise precedes fashioning in the observer's understanding. Al-Biqāʿī says creation requires proportion for balance. [7 notes: IBNASHUR(ابن زيابة); IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 87:2/q05/p2 The sequence reflects a gradual development from initial making to completed form. — Muqātil describes drop, clot, and morsel of flesh in the womb before proportionate formation; Iṣlāḥī says God's works unfold gradually in wisdom, and a human sketch passes through stages before completion. [4 notes: MUQATIL(مقاتل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:2/q06 Do the perfect verbs describe only past creation or continuing activity?  [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · grammar]
- 87:2/q06/p1 Their perfect form is aspectual; creation continues as ongoing world-sustaining activity. — Corpus Coranicum points to pasture's recurring growth and decay in 87:4–5 and compares 53:43–49, 96:1–5, and 95:4–5. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 87:2/q07 What divine claim does the clause make about human acts?  [خَلَقَ · theology]
turns on: Whether universal creation includes human acts without qualification, or allows mediated creation by God's granting human capacities.
- 87:2/q07/p1 God's universal creation includes human acts and challenges the claim that humans create their own acts. — Al-Ālūsī appeals to the verse's unrestricted generality against the Muʿtazilī claim. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]
- 87:2/q07/p2 God creates human acts directly or through enabling people and granting their capacities. — Al-Ālūsī suggests this as a possible way to understand al-Zamakhsharī's broad wording despite his Muʿtazilī commitments; he also allows that al-Zamakhsharī may avoid strict universality. [3 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 87:2/q08 How does creating relate God to creatures?  [خَلَقَ · theology]
- 87:2/q08/p1 As Creator, God is above created beings and free from their contingency, beginning, and need for a cause. — Elmalılı says creator and creature must not be confused in names or attributes, and the Lord is superior to those He nurtures. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 87:2/q09 How does this creation clause bear on God's exaltedness?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · theology]
turns on: How the surrounding acts establish divine worthiness of praise and what the superlative language does or does not imply.
- 87:2/q09/p1 It shows exalted power and lordship through creation and proportioning, in a way befitting God's majesty. — Al-Rāzī rejects a spatial reading because distance does not establish worthiness of praise, while these acts display power. Al-Qurṭubī's teacher says the clauses explain God's exaltedness above creatures. [2 notes: QURTUBI(بعض أشياخي); RAZI-FULL+]
- 87:2/q09/p2 The descriptions 'Great' and 'Highest' do not imply multiple gods or a comparison with another lord. — Al-Rāzī rejects that objection because God's oneness is already established and no other lord is mentioned. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 87:2/q10 What inward significance is given to creation and fashioning?  [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · inward]
turns on: Whether fashioning's spiritual consequence is distinctive guidance, readiness for the perfect spirit, or receptivity to an allotted effusion.
- 87:2/q10/p1 Humans receive a special gift of guidance; pride belongs in guidance and piety rather than created form. — Al-Sulamī cites 49:13; Bursawī reports a related view that creatures share basic creation but differ through special guidance. [2 notes: SULAMI; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 87:2/q10/p2 The outward human constitution is proportioned to receive the most complete spirit and every perfection. — Al-Qāshānī speaks of a particular temperament; Bursawī transmits the same reading as the body receiving the perfect spirit. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]
- 87:2/q10/p3 Each being is formed to receive its allotted divine effusion according to innate capacity. — Bursawī attributes this interpretation to the Najmiyya. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النجمي)]

## 87:2/q11 How do creation and fashioning support the command to glorify God?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · links]
turns on: Whether the clause's immediate lesson is the observable proof warranting glorification or the gradual divine ordering that calls for patience.
- 87:2/q11/p1 They answer why the Lord is known and worthy of glorification by displaying observable divine power, wisdom, and guidance. — Al-Rāzī calls creation and guidance a central prophetic proof: Abraham says his Creator guides him (26:78), Moses describes the giver of each thing's form and guidance (20:49–50), and the opening revelation joins creation and teaching (96:1–4). He says their abundant marvels are directly observable. Jishumī says the sūra answers the preceding warning with God's attributes and power. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn al-Zubayr connect the preceding servants' or unbelievers' plots with the command to glorify and the demonstration of power and wisdom. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the first four verses prove absolute lordship; Ibn ʿĀshūr says they indicate oneness through human creation and sustenance. [16 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI(الأستاذ أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:2/q11/p2 The ordered acts explain the gradual maturing of created physical and intellectual capacities and counsel patient obedience. — Iṣlāḥī says verses 1–5 address the Prophet's preaching fears; divine plans unfold by wisdom, so delays are wise and hardship can benefit people in both lives. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 87:2/q12 How does this clause relate to neighboring sūras and later verses of this sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes trace different links: the preceding sūra's warning and resurrection argument, the Prophet's later promises in this sūra, and the sūra's broader themes.
- 87:2/q12/p1 It carries al-Ṭāriq's account of human origin toward proof of resurrection and exposed secrets. — Al-Biqāʿī cites 86:5 and 86:8–9; Ibn al-Zubayr also ties the plot at 86:15 to the command at 87:1 and creation and guidance at 87:2–3 as evidence against slander. Iṣlāḥī says the prior sūra warned persistent deniers that their respite is temporary and that this sūra turns chiefly to the Prophet's fears. [4 notes: BIQAI(الأستاذ أبو جعفر ابن الزبير); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:2/q12/p2 God's fashioning and guidance anticipate the Prophet's capacity to retain revelation and receive an easy law. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 87:6 and 87:8 and says God equipped the Prophet for the burdens of mission; Iṣlāḥī describes a sequence of sūras dispelling his preaching fears. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 87:2/q12/p3 This creation account belongs to a sūra praising the Creator's oneness alongside revelation and afterlife recompense. — Qutb says the sūra makes the cosmos a sanctuary of praise, reassures the Prophet with divine preservation and ease, and roots its themes in earlier scriptures (87:18–19). Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'Most High' honors the Quran and steadies its reception as God's speech. [3 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 87:2/q13 Which other passages or expressions parallel the pairing of creating and fashioning?  [خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · links]
- 87:2/q13/p1 The two verbs also occur together at 82:7 and 75:38. — Corpus Coranicum names both parallels; Ibn al-Jawzī refers readers to his explanation at 82:7. Ibn ʿĀshūr also invokes 75:38 when explaining precise fashioning. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 87:2/q13/p2 Creation and determination express the specification that precedes and accompanies particular existence. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī groups 87:2–3 with 15:21, 54:49, 25:2, 57:22 and 64:11, saying things descend from undetermined plenitude into particular existence with determined relations to other existents. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 87:2/q14 What variant wording of the glorification including this verse is reported?  [ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · readings]
- 87:2/q14/p1 The wording 'Glory be to my Lord, the Most High, who created and fashioned' is read as the commanded glorification. — Al-Ṭabarī reports this as Ubayy ibn Kaʿb's reading. Al-Rāzī reports ʿAlī and Ibn ʿUmar reading the command as 'Glory be to my Lord, the Most High' followed by the relative clause; he says it supplies the words of glorification. [2 notes: TAB(أبي بن كعب); RAZI(علي)]

## 87:2/q15 What reported recitation or prayer events involve this passage or its sūra?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are separate reports about prostration, an addition to recitation, and the Prophet's customary sūra recitation.
- 87:2/q15/p1 The Prophet instructed 'Glory be to my Lord, the Most High' in prostration after this sūra's opening. — Al-Rāzī cites ʿUqba ibn ʿĀmir, who also reports 'Glory be to my Lord, the Great' in bowing after 56:74. Al-Durr reports Abū Hurayra's account that the Companions asked what to say in prostration and then the sūra was revealed. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 87:2/q15/p2 The Prophet told Ḥaḍramī to add nothing after he appended his own line about a pregnant woman to the recited verses. — Al-Durr reports Ḥaḍramī ibn ʿĀmir's recitation and the Prophet's judgment that it was sufficient as given. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(حضرمى بن عامر)]
- 87:2/q15/p3 The Prophet loved the sūra and recited it with al-Ghāshiya at Eid and Friday prayers, sometimes on one day. — Qutb cites ʿAlī on the Prophet's love and Muslim on the paired recitation with sūra 88; he also says al-Aʿlā is Meccan and has nineteen verses. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 87:2/q16 How does the following determination develop the account of creation?  [* · links]
- 87:2/q16/p1 The following verb can be read as 'measured' or as 'had power' or 'determined,' affirming God's direct power over what He created. — Study Quran reports qaddara and qadara as variant readings and explains the latter by God's continuing direct power over creation. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 87:2/q17 What guidance follows God's creating and fashioning?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether guidance denotes creatures' instinct for nourishment or human religious guidance by signs and proofs.
- 87:2/q17/p1 Creatures have instinctive guidance toward their allotted nourishment. — Study Quran reports this as one meaning of the following guidance. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 87:2/q17/p2 God leads people to religion and knowledge of His unity through signs and proofs. — Study Quran reports this as another meaning of the following guidance. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
