# 72:19: 15 questions

## 72:19/q01 How is the opening wa-annahu read and connected to the surrounding speech?  [وَأَنَّهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the hamza is governed by the revelation verb or begins a sentence, and whether the jinn's quotation has ended.
- 72:19/q01/p1 With fatḥa, the clause is governed by revelation and belongs to God's report. — Ibn Qutayba makes it accusative and coordinated with previous clauses; Sībawayh, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr relate it to 72:1, with some allowing an independent start. Ibn ʿĀshūr parses the pronoun as one of affair. Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr and most readers read fatḥa; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the jinn quotation had ended by 72:18. Al-Ṭabrisī also records Sībawayh's independent start after quoted speech and a conditional fāʾ, with examples at 5:115 and 72:23. [33 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(القائلون بهذا القول); IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر وحمزة والكسائى وحفص عن عاصم,ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر، حمزة، الكسائي، حفص,ابن كثير، أبو عمرو); TABRISI(سيبويه,قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر); NASHR(نافع، وأبو بكر); BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(بقية العشرة); QIRAAT-ER(in↔an); RAZI-FULL(الأكثرون)+]
- 72:19/q01/p2 With kasra, it opens an independent divine assertion or quoted command to say the assertion. — Nāfiʿ and Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim read kasra, with other transmitters Ibn Hurmuz and Ṭalḥa. Al-Ālūsī allows a fresh divine account; Elmalılı prefers it because an intervening verse makes continuity with jinn speech awkward. Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates with 'it was revealed to me' and supplies 'say'; he rejects continuing jinn speech after 72:18. [22 notes: IBNMUJAHID(عاصم فى رواية أبى بكر ونافع، والمفضل عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع، عاصم); TABRISI(سيبويه,نافع وعاصم برواية أبي بكر); BAGHAWI-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن هرمز وطلحة ونافع وأبو بكر); NASHR(نافع، وأبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر وابن هرمز وطلحة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وأبو بكر عن عاصم); QIRAAT-ER(in↔an); ELMALILI+]
- 72:19/q01/p3 With kasra, the words continue the jinn's report to their people. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr relate the jinn's description of the Companions. Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects Noah here as strained. Al-Wāḥidī argues third-person self-reference is awkward for the Prophet; al-Rāzī calls it possible and compares 19:85. Al-Kashf favors continuity if the mosques prohibition addressed the jinn; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Elmalılı object that the report was interrupted. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(أصحاب قول ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,نافع وأبو بكر وابن هرمز وطلحة); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+; ELMALILI-; IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 72:19/q02 Who is called God's servant?  [عَبْدُ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the title is read in the Prophet's historical scene, a remote report about Noah or Ibn Salām, or the inward scene.
- 72:19/q02/p1 Muhammad, the Prophet. — This is explicit or called unanimous by al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī; 72:20 confirms it for Ṭabāṭabāʾī. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates the title to 17:1. The setting is usually his prayer or proclamation. [27 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الجميع); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن هرمز وطلحة ونافع وأبو بكر); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Most commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 72:19/q02/p2 Noah, whose disbelieving people pressed upon him and nearly killed him. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this strained in the sequence. Abū Ḥayyān calls it remote; al-Ālūsī rejects it as remote. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 72:19/q02/p3 ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām. — Abū Ḥayyān calls this even farther fetched than Noah; al-Ālūsī finds it less credible. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 72:19/q02/p4 In an inward reading, the servant is the heart turned toward truth. — Al-Qāshānī describes the heart as humble, obedient, seeking divine light, and glorifying God. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 72:19/q03 What is conveyed by calling him 'God's servant'?  [عَبْدُ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes variously stress humility, honor, the reason for worship, the obviousness of serving God, or exact transmission.
- 72:19/q03/p1 It expresses the Prophet's humility and honored, complete servitude to God. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Rāzī discuss humble self-reference. Bursevī says ʿAbd Allāh is a beloved name, tied to the Night Journey (17:1), the Criterion (25:1), and unmatched servitude; a Persian couplet contrasts it with kings. Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Iṣlāḥī, Kuranyolu and Study Quran see honor or the highest spiritual station in this title; al-Sāmarrāʾī defines chosen servitude as devotion to God's pleasure. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(شاعر); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; STUDYQURAN]
- 72:19/q03/p2 The title shows that a servant worshipping his own Lord is natural and gives no cause for hostile amazement. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī contrast rational worship with the opposition it drew. Al-Biqāʿī says worship of another would be the astonishing act, and even an ordinary master's servant should not be treated this way. Iṣlāḥī notes opposition even in the house built for God's worship. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 72:19/q03/p3 The title identifies servanthood as the reason he stood to worship. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī make the title the cause of his standing; al-Ālūsī says it suits an obedient transmission of revelation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees preparation for 'I invoke my Lord' in the next verse. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 72:19/q03/p4 The Prophet conveys God's own third-person wording without inserting himself. — Al-Ālūsī says this removes any trace of personal intervention from the transmitted account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 72:19/q04 What does qāma mean here?  [قَامَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the standing denotes the observed prayer or rising to undertake public proclamation.
- 72:19/q04/p1 He stood or rose for prayer and worship. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the bodily upright posture as the root sense. Reports set the action at prayer, often the dawn prayer at Nakhlah. Al-Qāshānī gives the inward equivalent as the heart's turning toward truth. [13 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 72:19/q04/p2 He undertook the public call or mission to God's oneness. — Ibn al-Jawzī notes qāma for undertaking a call and also for an event's arising, like the Hour. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 18:14, al-Nābighah's line about the sons of Asad, and Juzʾ ibn Kulayb's verse about the Prophet's public worship. Al-Ḥasan places Arab opposition when he proclaimed monotheism. [9 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; TAB-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة,جزء بن كليب الفقعسي)]

## 72:19/q05 What does yadʿūhu mean, and whom was he addressing?  [يَدْعُوهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether yadʿūhu describes worship directed to God or preaching directed to an audience; the reported audience also varies.
- 72:19/q05/p1 He invoked and worshipped God, including through prayer and recitation. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Bayḍāwī explicitly gloss the verb as worship; al-Samīn describes invocation in monotheism. Al-Ālūsī and Bursevī make it circumstantial to his standing, at the dawn prayer in Nakhlah. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes 'I only call upon my Lord' in 72:20 as its explanation. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 72:19/q05/p2 He called people to God and proclaimed His oneness. — Ibn Qutayba says he called people to God; al-Ṭabarī and al-Ḥasan specify 'there is no god but God.' Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as calling people to Him; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes public monotheistic proclamation. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL(جزء بن كليب الفقعسي)]
- 72:19/q05/p3 He called the jinn to God. — Ibn Jurayj is reported by al-Qurṭubī; the verse's jinn scene gives the proposed addressees. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج)]
- 72:19/q05/p4 He called the Jews to God. — Al-Māwardī transmits this from Ibn Jurayj. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج)]

## 72:19/q06 How does yadʿūhu function grammatically?  [يَدْعُوهُ · grammar]
- 72:19/q06/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing the servant as he stood, invoking God. — Al-Samīn, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī give this parse; Bursevī links the action to Fajr prayer at Nakhlah. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 72:19/q07 Who are 'they,' and why did they nearly gather over him?  [كَادُوا۟ عَلَيْهِ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun's antecedent and the motive for gathering: listening, following in prayer, or hostile opposition.
- 72:19/q07/p1 The listening jinn crowded around Muhammad in eagerness, wonder, and love for the Qur'an and his worship. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Zubayr and others give this account. Some say they nearly climbed over him or one another, approached unseen until Gabriel disclosed them (72:1), or marveled at unfamiliar prayer and recitation. Al-Ālūsī says the image implies a larger audience than nine; Bursevī replies that seven or nine at Nakhlah make a crowd unlikely, though approach from one direction could explain it. [46 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الضحاك,عطية عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك ورواية عطية عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون,الحسن وقتادة); TABRISI(ابن عباس والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطية عن ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الزبير بن العوام,الضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,الزبير بن العوام); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس والزبير بن العوام); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Bg, Ṭ); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+; BURSEVI(ابن عباس)-; TABATABAI-]
- 72:19/q07/p2 The Prophet's Companions crowded near him, following his prayer and showing remarkable obedience. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr say they matched his bowing and prostration, and jinn told their people of it. Al-Ṭabrisī adds that each wanted to hear and be closest. Al-Biqāʿī compares an envoy's account of orderly Muslim rows impressing a Persian king. Bursevī says few companions, perhaps only Zayd, were with him and objects to a literal crowd. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(أصحاب قول ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير,ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جبير عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Bg); TIRMIDHI(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود)-; TABATABAI-]
- 72:19/q07/p3 Arab or Quraysh polytheists crowded around Muhammad in hostility to his monotheistic call. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this from the link with 72:18: opposition follows the ban on invoking another. Al-Ḥasan describes united Arabs, and Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the referent from 72:16–18; they gathered angrily to hear and intended harm. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers mockery and loud interruption. Asad broadens it to a recurring majority's hostility to unpopular moral truth. [32 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الحسن وقتادة وابن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة والحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Most commentators,Tabari); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Bg); IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]

- 72:19/q07/p5 Noah's disbelieving people nearly attacked him. — This depends on identifying the servant as Noah; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call that identification strained or remote. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)-]
- 72:19/q07/p6 In an inward reading, the faculties crowd the heart and veil it by their dominance. — Al-Qāshānī interprets the servant as the heart. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 72:19/q07/p7 Truth-deniers repeatedly mass against any individual or minority defending an unpopular moral truth. — Asad acknowledges Muhammad and his contemporaries as an initial setting, then generalizes the pattern. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ASAD-NOTES+]

## 72:19/q08 What setting and further events are reported for this scene?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted locations, prayer times, and numbers differ.
- 72:19/q08/p1 At Nakhlah, jinn heard Muhammad praying and reciting, then gathered to listen. — Al-Muqātil places him between Mecca and Ṭāʾif with nine jinn; al-Zubayr specifies evening prayer, while al-Zajjāj, al-Zamakhsharī and others specify dawn. Al-Biqāʿī says jinn sent toward Tihāmah by Iblīs heard his monotheism and believed. Ibn Kathīr says he did not see their approach until Gabriel informed him. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(الزبير); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 72:19/q08/p2 He prayed and recited in a valley of Mecca. — Al-Wāḥidī gives this location without specifying Nakhlah. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الجميع)]
- 72:19/q08/p3 At al-Ḥajūn, an Ibn Masʿūd account describes black men like the Zutt crowding around the Prophet after he drew Ibn Masʿūd a line. — Al-Durr transmits the account as before the Hijra; Bursevī says Ibn Masʿūd's event followed Nakhlah and objects that it was witnessed rather than revealed and too few Companions were present. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI(ابن مسعود)-]
- 72:19/q08/p4 Seventy thousand jinn pledged allegiance or followed the Prophet until daybreak. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits Burd from Makḥūl without grading; al-Biqāʿī cites Makḥūl from Abū Ḥayyān. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); BIQAI-FULL(مكحول)]

## 72:19/q09 What does libadan mean here?  [لِبَدًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The notes offer a physical crowd image, helpers, abundance, endurance, and other related lexical usages.
- 72:19/q09/p1 Dense, layered crowds or heaps packed tightly together. — The singular is libda/lubda; comparisons include felted wool, matted hair and a lion's mane, and a locust swarm. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī cite ʿAbd Manāf's locust verse and explain jābiya as consuming or gathering food; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also records jāni. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī cite Zuhayr's lion line; Dhū al-Rumma is also cited. Ibn Qutayba uses an accumulation of illicit gain. Al-Farrāʾ likens the form to plural human adjectives. Al-Naḥḥās favors the crowd sense over mere abundance and traces the root to accumulated, sticking material, including a mass of hair. [49 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ذو الرمة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة,أبو علي الفارسي,الفراء,الكسائي,المبرد,زهير,عبد مناف بن ربع الهذلي وأبو عبيدة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد بن مناف بن ربع); TABRISI(ابن عباس والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(زهير); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن عباس ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NAHHAS; NAHHAS(مجاهد)+]
- 72:19/q09/p2 Helpers or supporters. — Ibn ʿAbbās supplies this gloss in al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Naḥḥās, al-Māwardī and al-Durr; Abū Ḥayyān also reports it. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن عباس ومجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 72:19/q09/p3 Abundance or a great quantity. — Al-Naḥḥās reports Mujāhid's alternate abundance gloss but favors gathered crowds. Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī points to 90:6 and says the ḍamma form suggests numerous listeners, though kasra is more familiar here. Al-Mubarrad calls a similar form 'many' or a singular descriptive form. Al-Qurṭubī also cites abundant wealth. [6 notes: NAHHAS(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي,المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(عاصم)]
- 72:19/q09/p4 Enduring, like Luqmān's last vulture Lubad. — Al-Qurṭubī cites al-Nābighah's poetry and the lore of Luqmān choosing seven vultures instead of ewes for long life; al-Samīn mentions the name. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 72:19/q09/p5 In another usage, al-labad is a stay-at-home person. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Abū ʿUbayd's preferred transmitted variant in a line praising good judgment and large undertakings. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيد)]
- 72:19/q09/p6 A cited lion poem may refer to its thick mane, though the excerpt does not identify its lexical target. — Al-Naḥḥās gives an unattributed poem of a well-armed lion with mane and unclipped claws. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 72:19/q10 How is libadan read and formed?  [لِبَدًۭا · readings]
turns on: The vowel on lām, the vowel or doubling of bāʾ, and the related singular or plural pattern.
- 72:19/q10/p1 The common reading has kasra on lām and fatḥa on bāʾ, generally a plural of libda. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the city readers' form; Abū ʿUbayda and Abū Ḥātim also prefer it in al-Thaʿlabī. Nāfiʿ, the majority, and most transmissions use it. Al-Farrāʾ and later grammarians say its sense matches the ḍamma reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr instead calls it a collective noun. [20 notes: FARRA(بعضهم); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI(هشام بن عمار عن ابن عامر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون,الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وابن عباس ومجاهد); SAMIN-DURR(هشام); NASHR(الفضل بن شاذان,هشام); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وابن محيصن وابن عامر); IBNASHUR-FULL; DURR-FULL(عاصم); TAB-FULL(قراء الأمصار وابن محيصن)+; THALABI+]
- 72:19/q10/p2 With ḍamma on lām and a light, open bāʾ, it is a dialectal plural of lubda. — Attributed variously to Ibn Muḥayṣin, Mujāhid, Hishām from Ibn ʿĀmir, and in some accounts Ibn ʿĀmir. ## 72:19/q11 How does this statement connect with the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How the clause's speaker and crowd relate to 72:1, 72:18, and the commands that follow.
- 72:19/q11/p1 It continues the revelation or jinn report begun at 72:1. — Al-Ṭabarī connects a jinn listening account with 72:1. Ibn ʿAṭiyya coordinates the fatḥ reading with 72:1; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise connect it to revelation. Al-Biqāʿī sets both possibilities alongside each other. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(القائلون بهذا القول); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 72:19/q11/p2 It follows 72:18's command of exclusive worship by showing hostility to God's servant who obeyed it. — Al-Ṭabarī uses this link to prefer hostile Arabs; al-Ālūsī calls it an elegant transition. Ibn ʿĀshūr traces polytheists through 72:16–18; Iṣlāḥī notes tawḥīd was opposed in the house built for it. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 72:19/q11/p3 The following commands clarify the Prophet's call and God's protection. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 72:20 explains yadʿūhu. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads mission, oneness, resurrection and protection from error across these verses; Elmalılı says the commands fit their flow. Al-Ālūsī links 72:21–22 to bodily prostration points. Al-Fārisī compares qāla with imperative qul in 72:20, the latter fitting commands in 21–22. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 72:19/q11/p4 The passage informs the Prophet of unseen outward events and inward feelings. — Elmalılı gives this as a function shared by its interpretive alternatives. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 72:19/q11/p5 Asad connects its recurring opposition to 19:73–74. — He directs readers to those verses and their notes. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 72:19/q13 What do the cited passages say about literary clarity?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The surrounding literary discussion contrasts a clear image with obscure construction.
- 72:19/q13/p1 The cited verse pictures distinguished men admitted by their standing, while mere approach does not secure entry. — Al-Jurjānī uses the image of kings' gates. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 72:19/q13/p2 Obscure verbal order makes meaning needlessly indirect; effort pleases only when the discovered meaning merits it. — Al-Jurjānī likens unrewarding complexity to a miser's empty promises and faults Abū Tammām's strained diction. His line about a 'second' in the sky and two men in the cave illustrates the contrivance. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 72:19/q14 What grammatical and recitational principles appear in ancillary citations?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The citations concern distinct grammatical examples.
- 72:19/q14/p1 A full pause can separate utterances; a lesser pause permits only breath or no interval. — Al-Zarkashī gives the distinctions. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 72:19/q14/p2 A pronoun of affair is third-person nominative and may introduce a clause without an antecedent. — Al-Zarkashī reports grammarians parsing huwa with Allāh as second subject and aḥad as predicate; he distinguishes a separation pronoun, which may have any person and case. ## 72:19/q15 What ancillary reports address revelation and the seven revealed forms?  [* · reports]
turns on: The ancillary reports differ in topic and detail.
- 72:19/q15/p1 The Prophet showed physical effects when revelation came. — ʿĀʾishah describes his head drooping, face changing color, cold teeth, and pearl-like sweat. [1 notes: ITQAN(عائشة)]
- 72:19/q15/p2 Each of seven revealed forms is sufficient and should not be abandoned or disputed. — Ibn Masʿūd rejects abandoning one form out of disdain; Abū Bakrah permits any form so long as punishment and mercy are not interchanged; ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ calls disputes over forms unbelief. ʿUthmān confirms witnesses to the Prophet's declaration. [5 notes: NASHR(أبو بكرة,عبد الله بن مسعود,عثمان بن عفان,عمرو بن العاص); ITQAN(عثمان بن عفان)]
- 72:19/q15/p3 The seven-forms report is widely transmitted and raises questions of occasion, meaning, number, and variation. — Abū ʿUbayd calls it mutawātir. Ibn al-Jazarī traces nineteen Companions and al-Suyūṭī counts twenty-one; Ibn al-Jazarī lays out ten questions. [3 notes: NASHR(أبو عبيد القاسم بن سلام); ITQAN]
