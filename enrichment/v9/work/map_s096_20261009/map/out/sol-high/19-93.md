# 19:93: 33 questions

## 19:93/q01 What does إن mean in this construction?  [إِن · grammar]
- 19:93/q01/p1 It is a negative particle equivalent to mā, with the exception giving the affirmative sense. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī state the negative sense; al-Zarkashī adduces 19:93 and parallels at 6:29, 45:24, 13:7, 2:171, 86:4, 58:2, and 36:15. Al-Nasafī identifies the predicate after illā. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; NASAFI-FULL]

## 19:93/q02 How is من after كل parsed?  [كُلُّ مَن · grammar]
turns on: Whether man is the usual universal relative or the rarer descriptive indefinite after kull.
- 19:93/q02/p1 It is a relative pronoun covering all who are in the heavens and earth. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers this as the ordinary construction; al-Ālūsī reports that the descriptive use is rarer. Al-Samīn requires a universal, not individually known, relative, adducing the poetic kull alladhī ḥamalatnī and 39:33. Al-Zarkashī says the agreement analysis then combines singular form and plural sense. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 19:93/q02/p2 It is an indefinite noun describing the indefinite كل, with its location as a qualifier. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares man following rabb in poetry; al-Nasafī treats the following phrase as its description. Al-Samīn says Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Zamakhsharī appear to allow only this parse. Al-Zarkashī places the verse in a different agreement category under this parse. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء والزمخشري); BURHAN; NASAFI-FULL]

## 19:93/q03 Why are آتي and related pronouns singular with كل?  [كُلُّ ءَاتِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement follows the word's singular form, its collective meaning, or a rule tied to annexation and context.
- 19:93/q03/p1 Agreement follows the singular wording of كل; agreement with its collective meaning is also possible. — Al-Zawzanī contrasts singular-form kull with dual-sense kalā and cites 18:33 and 27:87. Al-Nasafī and al-Samīn identify ātī as predicate; al-Samīn says plural agreement would follow meaning and cites 21:33 and 27:87 as unannexed examples. Al-Zarkashī gives both patterns, notes an omitted plural genitive via Abū al-Fatḥ, and cites 2:285, 17:84, 38:14, 29:40, 43:76, 21:33, 2:116, 27:87. Al-Suyūṭī finds both here and in 19:94, and with detached kull at 17:84, 27:87, 9:70. [9 notes: MUALLAQAT; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(أبو الفتح); ITQAN]
- 19:93/q03/p2 Clause-initial كل annexed to a definite calls for a singular predicate, understood as كل واحد. — Al-Suhaylī's analysis is reported by al-Samīn; the Sheikh demands Arabic attestation for a plural predicate in this construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(السهيلي)]
- 19:93/q03/p3 Detached كل normally calls for a plural predicate as a collective, with contextual reasons for singular cases. — Al-Zarkashī reports al-Suhaylī's rule and his appeal to context in singular examples. [1 notes: BURHAN(السهيلي)]

## 19:93/q04 Who is included among those in the heavens and earth?  [مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
- 19:93/q04/p1 Every created being is included, notably angels, human beings, jinn, and the beings claimed as God's children or deities. — Muqātil names angels, ʿUzayr, Jesus, and Mary; al-Ṭabarī and al-Ālūsī explicitly add jinn; al-Wāḥidī distinguishes angels above and creatures below. Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burūsawī, and al-Rāzī stress the alleged divine beings. Kuranyolu cites 17:44; Study Quran cites 51:56. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(العيون); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 19:93/q05 How is coming to the Merciful understood?  [ءَاتِى ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the coming is an event at Judgment or a figurative description of enduring submission; al-Rāzī points to the absence of a time restriction.
- 19:93/q05/p1 It means appearing before God as a subdued servant at Judgment. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies the Day of Judgment and absence of kinship; Yaḥyā connects the counted, solitary appearance with 6:94. Al-Qurṭubī adduces 27:87. Ibn al-Jawzī names Jesus, Ezra, and angels. Al-Rāzī reports that some restrict the coming to that day. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(العيون); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 19:93/q05/p2 It figuratively means submission, confession of God's divinity, and dependence on Him throughout existence. — Al-Dāmghānī gives atā the sense acknowledge and submit. Al-Ālūsī explicitly rejects bodily arrival at the judgment place. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it figurative confession; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says no creature independently owns benefit, harm, life, death, or resurrection and compares the explicitly eschatological solitary coming. Al-Rāzī prefers an unrestricted sense because the verse states no time limit; al-Qāshānī grounds continuous dependence in divine effusion. [10 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)+]
- 19:93/q05/p3 The verb is the ordinary 'come,' with ātī its active participle. — Al-Ṭabarī derives it from ataytuhu, 'I came to him.' Ibn al-Jawzī distinguishes ityān, coming, from long-vowel ātā, giving, and notes istāʾtat for a camel seeking a mate. Al-Nasafī gives the participle future force. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; NASAFI-FULL]

## 19:93/q06 How is آتي الرحمن read and written?  [ءَاتِى ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · readings]
turns on: Annexation versus a retained tanwīn and accusative object, with a separately reported Ibn Masʿūd wording.
- 19:93/q06/p1 The common reading annexes آتي to الرحمن in the genitive. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān identify it as the common or majority reading; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī explain the written yāʾ and say original tanwīn was removed for ease before annexation. Al-Bayḍāwī contrasts a reading preserving the original form. [6 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وابن الزبير وأبو حيوة وطلحة وأبو بحرية وابن أبي عبلة ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله بن الزبير وأبو حيوة وطلحة)]
- 19:93/q06/p2 Some read آتٍ with tanwīn and الرحمن in the accusative. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Ṭalḥa; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī list ʿAbd Allāh, Ibn al-Zubayr, Abū Ḥaywa, Ṭalḥa, Abū Baḥriyya, Ibn Abī ʿAbla, and Yaʿqūb; al-Samīn names a subset. Al-Bayḍāwī calls this retention of the original form; al-Ālūsī's note spells it مات. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(طلحة بن مصرف); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وابن الزبير وأبو حيوة وطلحة وأبو بحرية وابن أبي عبلة ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله بن الزبير وأبو حيوة وطلحة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:93/q06/p3 Ibn Masʿūd reads لَمَّا أَتَى الرَّحْمَٰنَ or an original unannexed form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports lammā atā al-Raḥmān; al-Nasafī reports Ibn Masʿūd's آت الرحمن as the original form before annexation. The two source descriptions vary. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); NASAFI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 19:93/q07 Why is الرحمن repeated here?  [ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ · rhetoric]
- 19:93/q07/p1 Its repetition stresses that God alone merits this name because every original and subsequent blessing comes from Him. — Abū Ḥayyān gives this explanation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 19:93/q08 How is عبدا parsed and what state does it express?  [عَبْدًۭا · grammar]
- 19:93/q08/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative expressing the person's lowly, submissive, obedient state as God's servant. — Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr identify the ḥāl; al-Nasafī spells out humble and obedient. Al-Ālūsī says this holds under both readings of ātī. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وابن الزبير وأبو حيوة وطلحة وأبو بحرية وابن أبي عبلة ويعقوب); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:93/q09 What does universal servitude teach about God's creatures?  [عَبْدًۭا · theology]
- 19:93/q09/p1 All are owned by God, dependent on Him, subject to His will, and owe Him worship; no creature has divine rank. — Al-Baghawī and al-Bayḍāwī stress ownership. Al-Qāshānī says possible beings need divine effusion for existence and perfection and owe worship before and after existence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī denies independent control of benefit, harm, life, death, and resurrection. Asad cites 13:15, 16:48–49 for conscious or unconscious submission; Kuranyolu cites 17:44 and Study Quran 51:56. Al-Rāzī notes hope in God. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 19:93/q10 Does calling all creatures servants rule out divine offspring?  [عَبْدًۭا · theology]
turns on: Whether the verse's contrast of servitude and sonship licenses a general inference about children; Ibn ʿAṭiyya finds that inference remote.
- 19:93/q10/p1 Yes; sonship and servitude cannot coexist, so angels, Jesus, Ezra, and all other beings cannot be God's children. — Al-Jishumī argues God is not a body, whereas offspring belongs to bodies, and analogizes emancipation when a man owns his son. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, and Abū Yaʿlā use the same status conflict. Al-Biqāʿī adds that offspring requires a shared kind, impossible for the absolute Creator. Ṭabāṭabāʾī appeals to every being's need, full ownership, counting, assigned role, and final solitary appearance; he notes believers in one creator differ about multiple objects of worship and delegated lords. Al-Ṭabarī denies kinship; Asad denies shared divinity. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(العيون); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس)-]
- 19:93/q10/p2 Some infer that a child cannot be a servant. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this inference and calls it remote. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس)]

## 19:93/q11 Is every creature's servitude the same as chosen devotion?  [عَبْدًۭا · theology]
- 19:93/q11/p1 Compulsory servitude is universal, while freely chosen servitude is a high station of the righteous. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says universal forced servitude confers no merit and cites 19:94; he contrasts chosen service using 17:3, 38:44, and 72:19. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 19:93/q12 What inward approach to God does servanthood suggest?  [ءَاتِى ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ عَبْدًۭا · inward]
turns on: The notes develop distinct inner aspects: need and prayer, the servant's relation to divine qualities, and loss of selfhood.
- 19:93/q12/p1 Constant servanthood and confessed need are the best approach: they sustain service, refuge, prayer, and humble pleading. — Abū Bakr al-Warrāq is reported by al-Sulamī and al-Burūsawī. Al-Burūsawī also cites Ḥāfiẓ's Persian image of arriving poor and wounded with only prayer for mercy. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر الوراق); BURSEVI(أبو بكر الوراق,الحافظ)]
- 19:93/q12/p2 The arriving servant is poor and humbled by personal qualities, or noble through pointing to the qualities of the Real. — Al-Sulamī reports Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad's two-sided description. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر بن محمد)]
- 19:93/q12/p3 All arrive poor and humbled, stripped of selfhood before the Merciful. — Al-Ālūsī gives this inward description. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:93/q13 What legal effect does the verse imply for ownership between parent and child?  [عَبْدًۭا · law]
- 19:93/q13/p1 Buying or acquiring one's child or parent emancipates that person because parentage and servitude cannot coexist. — Abū Yaʿlā applies it to a parent buying a child; al-Qurṭubī extends it from father owning son to son owning father and cites Muslim's report that a child repays a parent only by buying and freeing an enslaved parent. Al-Shāfiʿī, reported by al-Zarkashī, infers automatic release of ascendants and descendants. Al-Biqāʿī says both parent and child are freed if purchased; al-Ālūsī affirms the son's release when acquired by the father. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN(الشافعي); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:93/q14 Does the word عبد include female slaves in the rule on freeing a share?  [عَبْدًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether عبد is generic across male and female slaves.
- 19:93/q14/p1 Yes; the word is generic, so emancipation extends to male and female slaves. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the majority's objection to Isḥāq and invokes this verse's inclusion of both sexes. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور)+]
- 19:93/q14/p2 No; the share-emancipation rule concerns male slaves only. — Al-Qurṭubī reports Isḥāq ibn Rāhwayh's distinction. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(إسحاق بن راهويه); QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور)-]

## 19:93/q15 How do the following statements about counting and arriving alone develop this verse?  [* · links]
- 19:93/q15/p1 God knows and counts every servant, then each arrives alone without protector or strength. — Yaḥyā cites 6:94. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says enumeration is restated and ʿaddan intensifies it; arriving alone leaves no supporter against God's will. Ibn Kathīr says the count includes every male and female, young and old, from creation to Resurrection. Ṭabāṭabāʾī folds counting, assigned roles, and solitary appearance into the proof of dependence. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]

- 19:93/q16/p2 Ubayy's codex reads that God counted them and enumerated them completely. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ubayy's wording. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي)]

turns on: The sources point either to the adjacent reassurance or to earlier discussion of the subject.
- 19:93/q17/p1 The preceding servant-and-solitary-arrival account prepares consolation for believers through love, honor, gifts, and signs of forgiveness. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya proposes this link to the next account of divine love. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 19:93/q17/p2 The topic had been discussed under al-Baqara and elsewhere, and repetition here is fitting. — Al-Qurṭubī makes this cross-reference. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:93/q18 What does the divine report say about denying resurrection and claiming God has a child?  [* · reports]
- 19:93/q18/p1 God calls denial of resurrection a false denial and the claim that He has a child an insult; He affirms oneness and no equal or offspring. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports Abū Hurayra and cites 112:1, 112:3. Al-Qurṭubī says al-Bukhārī transmits the divine saying through Abū Hurayra. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:93/q19 What does Abū Dharr's report promise sinful monotheists?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the promise guarantees immediate entry despite sins.
- 19:93/q19/p1 The Prophet would give away even a mountain of gold; the wealthy are few on Judgment Day, and monotheists enter Paradise even after sins. — Abū Dharr transmits the report in Ibn Ḥibbān. [1 notes: IBNHIBBAN(أبو ذر)]
- 19:93/q19/p2 Entry is conditional on pardon before entry or punishment before later entry. — Abū Ḥātim qualifies the promise and denies it is unconditional. [1 notes: IBNHIBBAN(أبو حاتم)+]

## 19:93/q20 What is reported about Quraysh's Ahmasī marriage practice?  [* · reports]
- 19:93/q20/p1 Quraysh drew in many Arab groups through marriage stipulations making offspring Ahmasī and adherents of their religion. — Al-Azraqī's source lists Kināna, Khuzāʿa, Aws, Khazraj, Thaqīf, Ghatafān, ʿAdwān, and Quḍāʿa. The example is al-Adram Taim ibn Ghālib marrying Majd daughter of Taim Rabīʿa ibn ʿĀmir on that condition; Labīd's verse names Majd, Numayr, and Hilāl in a watering line. [4 notes: AZRAQI(أبو صالح مولى أم هاني,قريش,لبيد بن ربيعة بن جعفر الكلابي)]

## 19:93/q21 How is the poetic image of earth and rain explained?  [* · meaning]
- 19:93/q21/p1 The earth is figured as female and rain as the male that fertilizes it to bear vegetation. — Ibn Qutayba says al-Aṣmaʿī and ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar did not know Umayya's line; later interpreters supply this explanation, and he extends the reproductive pairing to firesticks and their spark. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الأصمعي)]

## 19:93/q22 What do مسفد and the related firestick image mean?  [* · meaning]
- 19:93/q22/p1 مسفد means mated; the upper firestick is the father, the lower the mother, and the spark their offspring. — Ibn Qutayba compares safada/asfada with nakaḥa/ankaḥahu and cites Dhū al-Rumma, explaining his السقط as fire. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ذو الرمة)]

## 19:93/q23 What is the wording and sense of Umayya's cow-ritual line?  [* · meaning]
- 19:93/q23/p1 Read سلع ما rather than عسل ما; drought burdened cows with burning rain-making branches tied to their tails. — Ibn Qutayba corrects al-Aṣmaʿī's transmission, identifies al-Salʿ and al-ʿUshr as the trees, explains وعالت البيقورا and al-ʿāʾil as poor, and cites a second poet for سلع. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الأصمعي)+]

## 19:93/q24 How are Imruʾ al-Qays's obscure words about two stabs explained?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the line describes directions of two stabs or two uninterrupted stabs under Zayd's corrected wording.
- 19:93/q24/p1 سلكى is a straight stab and مخلوجة a stab veering right or left. — Al-Aṣmaʿī compares two arrows landing differently, though he had said interpreters of this line had disappeared. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الأصمعي)]
- 19:93/q24/p2 The correct line is كر كلامين على نابل: two rapid stabs like twice telling an archer to shoot. — Zayd ibn Kathwa says the line is commonly misquoted and prefers this wording and sense. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(زيد بن كثوة العنبري)+]

## 19:93/q25 What does العير denote in al-Ḥārith's line?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Which sense of العير is intended; all interpretations widen responsibility or alliance beyond the original offenders.
- 19:93/q25/p1 It is Kulayb ibn Wāʾil, a chief or lord. — Ibn Qutayba says the title is derived from the onager as the largest wild beast and cites the Prophet's saying to Abū Sufyān, 'All game is inside the ʿayr.' Al-Zawzanī says the poet's group then inherits feuds attached to killers or approvers of Kulayb. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(بعضهم,رسول الله); MUALLAQAT]
- 19:93/q25/p2 It is a tent peg. — Ibn Qutayba derives its name from protrusion like an arrowhead; al-Zawzanī explains that all tent dwellers are called allies, spreading guilt to Arabs at large. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(بعضهم); MUALLAQAT]
- 19:93/q25/p3 It is a mountain near Medina. — Ibn Qutayba invokes the Prophet's prohibition of the area between ʿAyr and Thawr; al-Zawzanī says everyone reaching it is counted as an ally. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(آخر); MUALLAQAT]
- 19:93/q25/p4 It is a donkey or wild ass. — Ibn Qutayba takes it literally as a donkey driven by anyone; al-Zawzanī uses hunters of wild asses. The particular acts differ, but both readings make the animal the referent and extend collective guilt. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(آخر); MUALLAQAT]
- 19:93/q25/p5 It is a mote stirred aside to clear water. — Al-Zawzanī reports this additional interpretation. [1 notes: MUALLAQAT]

## 19:93/q26 What is the point of al-Ḥārith's line whatever العير means?  [* · interpretation]
- 19:93/q26/p1 The poet's people are made allies of all who perform the named act and are saddled with others' guilt. — Ibn Qutayba summarizes the blame; al-Zawzanī says the line's ending has the same sweeping sense under every reading of العير. Al-Aṣmaʿī had reported that no one remained who understood the line. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MUALLAQAT]

## 19:93/q27 How are the other obscure cited poem expressions understood?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The two expressions come from distinct obscure lines; al-Aṣmaʿī's uncertainty is answered by Ibn al-Aʿrābī's glosses.
- 19:93/q27/p1 منغمس في الأهيغين means immersed in food and sex, like الأطيبان. — Ibn al-Aʿrābī gives both glosses; al-Aṣmaʿī had only guessed that الأهيغ suggested water. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن الأعرابي,الأصمعي)]
- 19:93/q27/p2 خذع denotes inclination as a sword-struck bull leans with its side hanging. — Ibn al-Aʿrābī gives this explanation for Ruʾba's line; al-Aṣmaʿī had said he could not explain it. [2 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(ابن الأعرابي,الأصمعي)]

## 19:93/q28 What does لِدّ mean in the nearby verse?  [* · meaning]
- 19:93/q28/p1 It means intensely contentious. — Al-Ṭabrisī cites 2:204 and a poem to illustrate the word and its plural. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 19:93/q29 What does al-Zawzanī's cited line say about كلا and كل?  [كُلُّ · grammar]
- 19:93/q29/p1 كلا is singular in form yet dual in sense; كل is singular in form although collective in sense. — Al-Zawzanī reads the cow as uncertain which space between her legs poses danger and which way to flee; he uses 19:93 for agreement by form and contrasts 18:33 and 27:87. [2 notes: MUALLAQAT]

## 19:93/q30 How do مثل and غير work in the cited rhetorical examples?  [* · rhetoric]
- 19:93/q30/p1 They can describe a type or deny a quality of the speaker without pointing to another actual person. — Al-Jurjānī explains fronted mithl as anyone matching the addressee, cites the reply to al-Ḥajjāj about an emir's conduct, and reads ghayrī as refusal of deceit or ingratitude. Abū Tammām's verse denies his own baseness, not another poet's conduct; he grounds the usage in instinct and ordinary speech. [5 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 19:93/q31 What makes a Quranic reading admissible?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether linguistic and written conformity suffice without a sound transmission.
- 19:93/q31/p1 It requires reliable received transmission and a valid Arabic basis; muṣḥaf conformity and Arabic possibility alone do not permit recitation. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects unsupported and unreliable readings, finds no relevant Ḥamza route, and calls untransmitted invention sinful. Abū Ṭāhir condemns the contrary proposal; al-Khaṭīb reports a Baghdad consensus, punishment, and repentance. Early authorities call recitation transmitted Sunnah; Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr restrained their own preferences. Ibn al-Jazarī allows analogy from consensus or reliable principles only when performance is unclear and explicit transmission absent. [7 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر الخطيب,أبو طاهر بن أبي هاشم,عمر بن الخطاب وزيد بن ثابت وابن المنكدر وعروة بن الزبير وعمر بن عبد العزيز وعامر الشعبي,نافع وأبو عمرو); NASHR+]
- 19:93/q31/p2 Any Arabic form conforming to the muṣḥaf may be recited, including in prayer. — Ibn Miqsam is reported to permit an untransmitted form; Abū Ṭāhir quotes a contemporary's broader claim. Ibn al-Jazarī rejects this practice and al-Khaṭīb reports a jurist-and-reader gathering forbidding it. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو طاهر بن أبي هاشم); NASHR(أبو بكر الخطيب)-]

## 19:93/q32 What is tafsir, and why do readers need it?  [* · sciences]
- 19:93/q32/p1 Tafsir explains revealed meanings and derives rulings and wisdom; concise or multivalent wording, omitted premises, and later readers' weaker Arabic knowledge make it needed. — Al-Zarkashī lists language, grammar, morphology, rhetoric, legal theory, readings, revelation context, and abrogation as tools. He and al-Suyūṭī say eloquent first Arabs knew surface meanings but subtleties emerged through inquiry and questions to the Prophet; commentaries expand concise expression and weigh senses. Al-Zarkashī notes exegetes' specialties: al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī rare vocabulary, al-Thaʿlabī stories, al-Zamakhsharī rhetoric, and al-Rāzī kalām. Al-Suyūṭī adds that later readers lack native command of Arabic. [8 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 19:93/q33 How many verses yield legal rulings, and how are rulings derived?  [* · sciences]
- 19:93/q33/p1 The cited figure of five hundred likely counts explicit legal verses; many more rulings are inferred from stories and parables or by combining verses. — Al-Zarkashī attributes five hundred to al-Ghazālī and others followed by al-Rāzī, directs readers to Ibn ʿAbd al-Salām, and distinguishes express rulings from inference from one verse or several. [2 notes: BURHAN(الغزالي)]
