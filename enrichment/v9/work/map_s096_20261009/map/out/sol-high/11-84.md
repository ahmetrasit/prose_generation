# 11:84: 27 questions

## 11:84/q01 Whom does Madyan name?  [مَدْيَنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the name designates an ancestor and his descendants or a place and its inhabitants; some sources allow both.
- 11:84/q01/p1 The tribe descended from Madyan, son of Abraham. — Muqātil gives Madyan as Abraham's son; Māwardī and Qurṭubī compare the eponym Muḍar. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the ancestry but disputes Abraham's direct paternity because Shuʿayb is Arab. Ibn Kathīr locates the Arab tribe near Maʿān. Ālūsī favors an eponymous tribe. [20 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش,قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 11:84/q01/p2 A town or region whose inhabitants are intended. — Abū ʿUbayda, Zajjāj, Wāḥidī and Rāzī supply omitted 'people of'; Wāḥidī cites a poem about Madyan's monks through Ibn al-Anbārī. Ṭabāṭabāʾī instead calls attribution to the town figurative, like 'the gutter ran.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya places the region in Syria; Kuran Yolu near the Gulf of Aqaba. [25 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج,كثير من المفسرين); JISHUMI(أبو مسلم,الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 11:84/q02 How is the name Madyan formed and inflected?  [مَدْيَنَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it is a city or tribal name, foreign or Arabic, and which root is proposed.
- 11:84/q02/p1 It is indeclinable as a feminine city name. — Abū ʿUbayda cites the feminine place name; Naḥḥās says the city name is diptote, as does Qurṭubī citing him. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it is indeclinable on either the place or personal name analysis. [4 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)]
- 11:84/q02/p2 It is diptote as a city or tribal name. — Zajjāj allows either city or tribe as the basis for diptote treatment. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
- 11:84/q02/p3 It may be a foreign name or an Arabic derivation. — Māwardī offers both origins: if a city, from madana 'reside' with extra yāʾ; if a man, from dānat 'rule or possess' with extra mīm. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 11:84/q03 What is reported about Shuʿayb's lineage and identity?  [شُعَيْبًۭا · reports]
turns on: The transmitted genealogies differ, as do identifications with Moses' father-in-law.
- 11:84/q03/p1 He is an Arab prophet, later associated with Moses as father-in-law. — Ibn Kathīr calls him of noble lineage; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls him the third Arab messenger and relates Moses' marriage and service. Study Quran reports Muslim identification with Jethro; Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the Torah calls Moses' Midianite father-in-law Reuel. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Muslim authorities)]
- 11:84/q03/p2 Shuʿayb descends through Mīkāʾīl and Yashjan or Yashjur to Midian. — Ibn Isḥāq gives his father Mīkāʾīl, while another genealogy says Malkāyān; Nawawī's manuscript and Bursevī give the line through Yashjan or Yashjur. Elmalılı calls him noble and the Orator of the Prophets. [4 notes: ITQAN(ابن إسحاق,النووي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 11:84/q03/p3 He was called the Orator of the Prophets. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this from the Prophet without grading; Nawawī's notice and Elmalılı repeat the title. Ṭabāṭabāʾī also records a report of his blindness after weeping from love of God and Moses' service. [4 notes: ITQAN(النووي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 11:84/q03/p4 Shuʿayb descends from Midian through Nuwayb. — Muqātil calls him son of Nuwayb, son of Midian, son of Abraham. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 11:84/q04 Why is Shuʿayb called their brother?  [أَخَاهُمْ · interpretation]
- 11:84/q04/p1 He belonged to them by kinship or peoplehood. — Muqātil excludes shared religion; Bursevī says lineage. Samarrai contrasts the People of al-Aykah in 26:177, who were not his kin, and adds Moses and Jesus as analogous address patterns. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 11:84/q05 Was Shuʿayb sent to Madyan and al-Aykah as one people or two?  [مَدْيَنَ شُعَيْبًۭا · reports]
turns on: The two stories share the measure and weight command, but reports distinguish the punishments.
- 11:84/q05/p1 They were two communities with separate missions. — Suddī and ʿIkrima report Madyan perished by the Cry and al-Aykah by the shade. A raised report from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr says two peoples, but Ibn Kathīr questions its attribution; others add a third, al-Rass. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); ITQAN(السدي وعكرمة,عبد الله بن عمرو); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ITQAN(ابن كثير)-]
- 11:84/q05/p2 They were one community. — Ibn Kathīr, cited by Suyūṭī, points to both accounts commanding full measure and weight. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن كثير)+]

## 11:84/q06 What does the name Shuʿayb signify?  [شُعَيْبًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The proposed meanings of its base shaʿb differ.
- 11:84/q06/p1 It is a diminutive of shaʿb, a mountain path. — Māwardī gives this derivation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 11:84/q06/p2 Its base shaʿb may mean a great tribe. — Māwardī reports this alternative. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 11:84/q06/p3 Its base may refer to repairing a broken vessel. — Māwardī reports this third proposal. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 11:84/q07 What is understood in the opening mission statement?  [وَإِلَىٰ مَدْيَنَ قَالَ · grammar]
- 11:84/q07/p1 The verb 'We sent' is understood before 'their brother Shuʿayb'; his speech then follows. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya supplies the omitted verb; Ālūsī says the form parallels the mission to Thamūd at 11:61. Bursevī takes qāla as an explanatory answer to an implicit question. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 11:84/q08 What does 'worship God; you have no god other than Him' require?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ مَا لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرُهُۥ · theology]
- 11:84/q08/p1 Affirm God's sole divinity, obey Him, and leave idolatry. — Muqātil stresses divine oneness; Ṭabarī adds humble obedience to commands and prohibitions. Ibn ʿAṭiyya infers their idol worship from 11:87. Qāshānī reads unity as release from the root of vice. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 11:84/q09 Why does Shuʿayb begin with monotheism?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
- 11:84/q09/p1 It is the foundation and chief duty before reform of conduct. — Jishumī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr say prophets begin here; Rāzī moves next to the most urgent social wrong. Biqāʿī argues agreement across distant prophets proves their truth; Suhrawardī says their fundamentals share one source while laws differ by community and age. [14 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(عمر بن محمد السهروردي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 11:84/q10 How is 'other than Him' read and construed?  [غَيْرُهُۥ · readings]
- 11:84/q10/p1 Ghayr is genitive, agreeing with ilāh as its adjective. — Qiraat Encyclopedia labels this CE; it means a god other than Him. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(CE)]

## 11:84/q11 What wrongdoing does the prohibition on measure and balance describe?  [وَلَا تَنقُصُوا۟ ٱلْمِكْيَالَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ · interpretation]
turns on: Notes distinguish giving customers less from manipulating measuring instruments, and some include taking excess.
- 11:84/q11/p1 They gave less than people's due in measure or weight. — Ṭabarī describes deprivation of due shares. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī say they took full or excess when buying and gave short when selling. Rāzī includes taking more than due, both reducing another's right. Bursevī says two sets of instruments were used. Ibn ʿAbbās in Durr says Shuʿayb singled out injustice. [37 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,خلف بن حوشب); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Some commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:84/q11/p2 The standard capacity or weights themselves were reduced or altered. — Wāḥidī says short measure is below customary agreement, and the shortfall in weight belongs to the weights rather than balance pans. Ālūsī prefers this over taking the words as goods. Biqāʿī includes reductions in instruments and measured quantities. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 11:84/q12 What do mikyāl and mīzān denote here?  [ٱلْمِكْيَالَ ٱلْمِيزَانَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the words directly name instruments or stand for the goods measured.
- 11:84/q12/p1 They name a measuring vessel and a weighing instrument. — Wāḥidī distinguishes measure from scale; Biqāʿī distinguishes quantity from weight or quality. Ṭabrisī defines weighing as adjustment for lightness or heaviness by an instrument. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 11:84/q12/p2 They stand for the measured and weighed goods or their due quantities. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls this rational metonymy: goods, not instruments, are deficient. Ālūsī reports naming container for contents, while Samīn allows the instrument's due to be understood. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]

## 11:84/q13 How does 'do not reduce' govern its object?  [تَنقُصُوا۟ ٱلْمِكْيَالَ · grammar]
- 11:84/q13/p1 It may take one object, two objects with the second introduced by min, or the measure with its due omitted. — Samīn says the omitted due emphasizes full measure. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 11:84/q14 Why is commercial fraud singled out after the call to worship?  [وَلَا تَنقُصُوا۟ ٱلْمِكْيَالَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ · interpretation]
- 11:84/q14/p1 It was habitual, prevalent, and especially destructive of others' rights. — Māwardī and Bayḍāwī pair shirk with cheating. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the custom combined theft and betrayal of trusting buyers. Elmalılı infers trade's centrality; Islahi says cheating became a professional skill. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes miscalculated needs, lost trust and social cooperation, relating 17:35. [25 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Some commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 11:84/q15 How does this prohibition relate to the command to give full measure in the next verse?  [وَلَا تَنقُصُوا۟ ٱلْمِكْيَالَ وَٱلْمِيزَانَ · links]
turns on: Whether a positive command adds a distinct point to the prohibition.
- 11:84/q15/p1 The positive command reinforces and clarifies lawful fair trade. — Rāzī says repetition answers persistence and the command is distinct: a ban alone could make some deem trade inseparable from fraud. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad note the positive restatement in 11:85. Ālūsī compares the 'measuring' wording of 7:85. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]

## 11:84/q16 What is the 'good' in which Shuʿayb sees them?  [بِخَيْرٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: The notes range from prices or material abundance to divine guidance or Shuʿayb's goodwill.
- 11:84/q16/p1 Ample wealth, provision, blessing, and worldly prosperity. — Ṭabarī prefers a broad worldly sense embracing adornment, low prices and wealth; Ibn Zayd cites 2:180 for wealth as khayr. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers wealth because it makes fraud indefensible. Qatāda says visible luxury; Wāḥidī mentions fertility; Rāzī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī also connect it to adequate resources. [45 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة وابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة المفسرين); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قتادة، وابن زيد، والضحاك,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وقتادة وابن زيد,الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 11:84/q16/p2 Low or favorable prices. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḥasan are repeatedly cited; Ibn al-Jawzī's lexical note gives this sense. Al-Farrāʾ combines cheap prices with wealth, arguing fraud is needless. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، والحسن); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن العباس والحسن ومجاهد,ابن عباس وقتادة وابن زيد,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABATABAI]
- 11:84/q16/p3 Fertile conditions, abundant crops and earnings. — Mujāhid is cited for fertility; Māwardī adds crops and earnings. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عامة المفسرين); JISHUMI(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI]
- 11:84/q16/p4 God's favor can include reason, guidance and provision. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this removes excuse for both idolatry and corrupt dealings; Sūlamī relates favor to the danger of failing gratitude. Qāshānī gives an inward reading of preparedness for perfection and guidance despite vice. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); QASHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); TABATABAI]
- 11:84/q16/p5 Shuʿayb looks on them with goodwill and desires their happiness. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the following warning explains his benevolent concern. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:84/q17 Why mention their present good before warning them?  [إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰكُم بِخَيْرٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: The good can remove need for fraud, call for gratitude, or be a state that fraud will destroy.
- 11:84/q17/p1 Prosperity removes any need to cheat. — Al-Farrāʾ, Rāzī, Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī, Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr make this inference; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it one of two deterrents. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:84/q17/p2 Blessing calls for gratitude, generosity, justice and better conduct. — Sūlamī fears falling short in gratitude; Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı urge good conduct. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Shuʿayb presents himself as witness to God's favor; Islahi says ingratitude can turn luxury to destruction. [13 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QASHANI]
- 11:84/q17/p3 Wrongdoing could destroy their current good. — Zamakhsharī compares the believing man's words in 40:29. Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Rāzī warn of lost blessing and rising prices unless they repent. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:84/q18 What does Shuʿayb mean by 'I fear'?  [أَخَافُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the threatened event is known as certain or depends on continued wrongdoing.
- 11:84/q18/p1 He genuinely fears it, since repentance may avert it. — Rāzī reports the alternative: they may quit fraud out of fear, so expectation rather than certain knowledge is appropriate. Elmalılı emphasizes compassion. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(آخرون); ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 11:84/q18/p2 He means 'I know' that punishment will occur. — Rāzī transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 11:84/q19 Which punishment is the 'encompassing day'?  [عَذَابَ يَوْمٍۢ مُّحِيطٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the warning names price rises, worldly destruction, the afterlife, or an inclusive threat.
- 11:84/q19/p1 Worldly destruction that overtakes all of them. — Muqātil, Māwardī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī and Qurṭubī give this reading; Biqāʿī says it reaches young, old, and property. Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the later cloud, cry and earthquake and salvation of believers. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 11:84/q19/p2 Ruinous high prices, drought, and loss of provision. — Ibn ʿAbbās links good low prices to threatened high prices; Mujāhid adds drought and famine. The Prophet is reported to say cheating brings withheld provision or high prices; Ibn Mājah's Ibn ʿUmar report adds hard years and unjust rulers, without grading. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، والحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 11:84/q19/p3 The Fire or punishment on Judgment Day. — Māwardī and Qurṭubī report the Fire; Rāzī says the Day of Resurrection is appointed for condemned people. Ibn Kathīr takes the afterlife. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 11:84/q19/p4 The threat includes worldly and afterlife punishment, or every kind of punishment. — Rāzī prefers the encompassing general sense, comparing a circle and 18:42; Ibn ʿĀshūr leaves both worlds open. Ālūsī notes either Resurrection or annihilation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows either and says no escape, helper, repentance or intercession. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+]

## 11:84/q20 What does muḥīṭ convey?  [مُّحِيطٍۢ · meaning]
- 11:84/q20/p1 It surrounds and reaches everyone, leaving no escape. — Jishumī likens it to a wall and notes the verb can mean containing with care. Zamakhsharī and Ālūsī invoke the ruined garden of 18:42 and an enemy's siege; Biqāʿī says punishment surrounds every side; the current surrounding blessings give way to surrounding harm. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(زمخشري); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 11:84/q21 Why is the day called encompassing, and how does the adjective attach?  [يَوْمٍۢ مُّحِيطٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the day itself figuratively bears its punishment's quality, the adjective attaches by proximity, or a noun is omitted.
- 11:84/q21/p1 The adjective grammatically describes the day but figuratively means its punishment encompasses them. — Ṭabarī compares 'a burned garment'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also allows the day's evil itself surrounding them. Rāzī compares 11:77; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the genitive phrase makes the figure clear. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:84/q21/p2 Calling the entire day encompassing intensifies the threat, since it contains every event and torment. — Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī and Ālūsī say this is stronger than an encompassing punishment; Ālūsī says one punishment may reach everyone, but an encompassing day gathers every kind, citing a poetic dome image. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين,زمخشري); KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 11:84/q21/p3 Muḥīṭ may modify ʿadhāb through attraction by adjacency. — Samīn reports this analysis; Ālūsī also reports it. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:84/q21/p4 Muḥīṭ may describe the day with an omitted reference to 'its punishment'. — Samīn reports this ellipsis; Abū al-Baqāʾ objects that an explicit pronoun would be needed, and Ālūsī rejects it as remote. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 11:84/q23 How does this story connect with nearby prophetic accounts and parallel verses?  [* · links]

## 11:84/q24 What happened to Shuʿayb's people after this warning?  [* · reports]
- 11:84/q24/p1 Most rejected him, persecuted believers, and were destroyed while he and the believers survived. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 7:86, 7:89, 26:187 and 7:93 for obstruction, threatened expulsion or stoning, his prayer, and cloud, cry and earthquake. Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports they challenged the warning; Khalaf says tiny cheating amounts were enough for ruin. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي,خلف بن حوشب); TABATABAI]

## 11:84/q25 What conduct toward God and people does the verse teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 11:84/q25/p1 Monotheism and fair dealing jointly require reform, gratitude, and respect for others' rights. — Asad calls them twin requirements and rejects a merely historical limit. Kuran Yolu says honesty toward people is bound to honesty toward God. Ṭabāṭabāʾī links reciprocal rights and social cooperation; Elmalılı says the account bears on contemporary civilization. ## 11:84/q26 What inward warning is drawn from their prosperity and threatened punishment?  [إِنِّىٓ أَرَىٰكُم بِخَيْرٍۢ عَذَابَ يَوْمٍۢ مُّحِيطٍۢ · inward]
