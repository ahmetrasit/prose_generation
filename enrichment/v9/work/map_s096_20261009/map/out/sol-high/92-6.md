# 92:6: 12 questions

## 92:6/q01 What does affirming mean here?  [وَصَدَّقَ · interpretation]
- 92:6/q01/p1 Assenting to the truth of God's promise also entails seeking it and acting accordingly. — Al-Biqāʿī defines taṣdīq as assent that makes the informant's statement true for the believer. Qatāda says one believes God's promise and works for it. Elmalılı requires practical, sincere pursuit of goodness; Ibn ʿĀshūr says acknowledgment implies desire to attain the promise and deeds leading to success, in contrast to denial in 92:9. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says truthfulness can concern words, deeds, intention and belief; Qur'anic affirmation commonly concerns God, His signs, messengers, words and meeting. [14 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI; RAZI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI; DURR(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 92:6/q02 What is the form and lexical sense of al-ḥusnā?  [ٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · meaning]
- 92:6/q02/p1 It is the feminine superlative of aḥsan, literally 'the best' or 'most beautiful,' with an understood feminine noun. — Jishumī calls it the supreme blessing for its excellent outcome. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows an omitted noun such as reward, victory, promise or outcome; Islāḥī supplies ʿāqibah, fate or outcome. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ distinguishes material beauty from moral good and cites al-Rāghib's distinction: ḥusn applies to objects and events, ḥasana as a noun to events, and ḥusnā only to events; she notes its opposition to sawʾa. [8 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI(الراغب); CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 92:6/q04 Why are giving and piety mentioned before affirmation?  [وَصَدَّقَ · rhetoric]
turns on: How the order relates to belief's logical priority, the practical acts, the verse ending and the paired opposite clause.
- 92:6/q04/p1 Affirmation follows piety as a specific instance of a general virtue; its position also preserves the verse ending. — Al-Ālūsī acknowledges faith's importance while explaining its later placement by the general-to-specific order and rhyme. [2 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:6/q04/p2 Giving and piety come first because practical deeds establish the affirmation and lead to its promised result. — Elmalılı says belief precedes deeds in the order of being; the text puts deeds first because verbal belief without them does not earn ease and because effects and recompense follow their causes. [3 notes: ELMALILI]
- 92:6/q04/p3 Giving heads the paired clauses because it is foundational for the faith and piety or denial and self-sufficiency that follow. — Al-Irshād notes that giving ranks lower than the later traits yet founds their paired descriptions. [1 notes: ALUSI(الإرشاد)]
- 92:6/q04/p4 Giving obedience and avoiding idolatry could be understood as acts that precede faith. — Al-Ālūsī rejects this explanation: 'giving' conventionally concerns money, especially when opposed to withholding, and the proposed sequence remains unexplained. He also rejects counting listening to learn monotheism as giving and avoidance of shirk as piety. [0 notes: ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 92:6/q05 What does this affirmation teach about conduct and its result?  [وَصَدَّقَ بِٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · interpretation]
- 92:6/q05/p1 Faith in God's good reward motivates giving, piety and perseverance toward the Hereafter. — Al-Rāzī says the paired paths lead to praiseworthy or blameworthy outcomes. Elmalılı says expectation of a better afterlife makes sacrifice joyful, citing 59:9 and 64:16. Islāḥī says faith in reward moves one to give for God rather than worldly gain, citing 107:1–3; the struggle against desire grows strength and leads to the company and Paradise of 89:29–30. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:6/q05/p2 The three traits express the person's effort toward purification, met by God's freely willed aid. — Sayyid Quṭb says success depends on God's aid despite the person's utmost effort. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]
- 92:6/q05/p3 Giving covers financial worship, piety covers obedience and avoidance of sin, and affirmation covers comprehensive faith. — Al-Ālūsī prefers the triad because together it covers all endeavors; al-Biqāʿī specifies giving what God commands and guarding oneself through obedience and avoidance. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]

## 92:6/q06 How is this verse read inwardly?  [وَصَدَّقَ بِٱلْحُسْنَىٰ · inward]
- 92:6/q06/p1 Abū Bakr's giving included self and wealth, refusal to settle into the natural self, and affirmation of monotheism. — Tustarī reports this from Abū Bakr. [1 notes: TUSTARI(أبو بكر الصديق)]
- 92:6/q06/p2 The generous renounce attachment to both abodes, avoid frivolity and doubt, and seek nearness to God. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 92:6/q06/p3 Certain faith in the rank of perfection makes ascent possible, and God eases the seeker by cutting attachments and strengthening certainty. — Qāshānī joins intellectual certainty to God's assistance on the easy path. [2 notes: QASHANI]

- 92:6/q09/p1 The passage's teaching is general despite specific occasion reports. — Al-Rāzī cites the general claim that human striving differs and the warning of blazing fire in 92:4 and 92:14. ## 92:6/q11 How does the passage connect to its surrounding verses and sūra?  [* · links]
- 92:6/q11/p1 Its opposed lists pair giving with stinginess, piety with self-sufficiency, affirmation with denial, and ease with hardship. — Corpus Coranicum notes the symmetrical behavior-to-consequence sequences, cross-rhyme from vowel variation and first-person divine promise authorized beside the oath; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the oath's night, daylight and creation frame opposed earthly efforts and afterlife fates. [7 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 92:6/q11/p2 The final stanza reverses the earlier saved-and-damned order while echoing its conduct and outcomes. — Corpus Coranicum points to ittaqā in 92:5 and atqā in 92:17; the first pair lists conduct before outcomes, while the final pair puts warning and promise before conduct. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:6/q11/p3 The sūra spells out traits of success and failure announced in the preceding sūra. — Abū Ḥayyān relates it to 91:9–10, then to the Fire and those spared it. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 92:6/q11/p4 The contrasting paths exemplify divine preparation for obedience and disbelief. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares easing toward obedience to saddling and bridling a horse and cites 6:125 on opening the heart to Islam. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
