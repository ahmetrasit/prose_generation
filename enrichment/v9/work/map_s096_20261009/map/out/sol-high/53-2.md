# 53:2: 20 questions

## 53:2/q01 Who is 'your companion,' and what does the description imply?  [صَاحِبُكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether sahib stresses shared association or Muhammad's standing as master.
- 53:2/q01/p1 Muhammad, their familiar close associate. — Muqatil, al-Zajjaj, al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Alusi, al-Tabataba'i and Study Quran identify him. Al-Damghani pairs 53:2 with 81:22. Ibn Ashur defines sahib as close associate, citing Abu Ma'bad's 'Quraysh's companion' after Umm Ma'bad's ewe miracle; al-Hajjaj's address to Khawarij likewise illustrates accusing people one knows. Al-Biqa'i, Bursevi, Elmalılı, Ibn Ashur, Kur'an Yolu and al-Samarra'i say Quraysh's long acquaintance with his honesty, sound mind, purity and care for them defeats its slander; Kur'an Yolu notes sohbet shares the root. Al-Samarra'i cites 34:46 and 81:22 as parallel uses. Asad directs readers to 7:184. Al-Nasafi and Abu Hayyan explicitly name Quraysh as addressee. [26 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو معبد الخزاعي,الحجاج); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; DAMGHANI+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; MAWARDI-FULL+; MUQATIL+; NASAFI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+; STUDYQURAN+; TABATABAI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 53:2/q01/p2 Muhammad as their master. — Razi allows sahib to mean master as well as companion. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:2/q02 What does 'did not stray' mean?  [ضَلَّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether dalal is taken as departure from guidance or as madness.
- 53:2/q02/p1 He did not lose the path of truth and guidance. — Al-Tabari says he remained on a straight, sound course. Al-Nahhas, al-Mawardi, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Bursevi and al-Alusi use path or truth imagery; Bursevi and al-Alusi call it the road of the Hereafter. Ibn Atiyya says the path God set him on. Razi says dalal may be broad enough to include any lost route. Elmalılı contrasts it with guidance and sound judgment; Ibn Ashur calls it failure to reach a goal, metaphorically a course contrary to truth; al-Tabataba'i calls it departure from the straight path. Al-Samarra'i says dalal may be intentional or accidental and can apply even to an animal (45:23, 18:104). [23 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; NAHHAS+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 53:2/q02/p2 He was not insane. — Razi says a madman can be called astray and cites 68:2–4, 26:109 and 10:72 to connect sanity with Muhammad's wise guidance. Al-Damghani links the same denial in 81:22; Elmalılı likewise denies loss of mind. [3 notes: DAMGHANI+; ELMALILI+; RAZI-FULL+]

## 53:2/q03 What does 'did not go astray' mean?  [غَوَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ghayy names wandering, chosen corruption, false creed, or failure, and what sought result failure concerns.
- 53:2/q03/p1 He did not stray from guidance. — Abu Ubayda derives ghawa from al-ghayy and calls the ghawi one who strays; al-Qurtubi makes ghayy the opposite of right guidance. Al-Tabrisi says this reinforces the preceding denial because ghawa likewise means straying. [3 notes: MAJAZ+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABRISI+]
- 53:2/q03/p2 He did not follow deliberate error or corrupt conduct. — Ibn Atiyya calls ghawa taking a crooked, corrupt path and contrasts intended ghayy with unintended dalal. Razi contrasts it with right conduct, including sinful conduct beneath unbelief, and compares it to corrupt existence (4:6). Ibn Kathir says the deviant knows truth but turns away. Elmalılı calls it loss of sound judgment; Ibn Ashur corrupt judgment attached to falsehood. Al-Samarra'i says it requires moral responsibility and deliberate persistence (7:146, 2:256, 20:79); Study Quran calls it following religion incorrectly. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(R); ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+; STUDYQURAN+]
- 53:2/q03/p3 He did not hold a false belief. — Al-Raghib, quoted by Bursevi and al-Tabataba'i, defines ghayy as ignorance founded on a false belief, unlike mere absence of belief; Bursevi says dalal also covers speech, action, character and belief. Al-Alusi says belief is singled out because it grounds action. Study Quran reports Is, R and Tb for the corrupt-creed sense. [6 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); TABATABAI(الراغب); STUDYQURAN(Iṣ, R, Ṭb,R); ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]
- 53:2/q03/p4 He did not fail or suffer loss. — Al-Jishumi and al-Tabrisi explain ghayy as failure or loss, reflected in ghawaya and ghawa; al-Qurtubi reports this sense. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 53:2/q03/p5 He did not fail to obtain mercy or right guidance. — Al-Nahhas reports mercy as the object sought; al-Tabrisi reports right guidance. [2 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI(غير مسمى)]
- 53:2/q03/p6 His effort did not fail: he receives God's reward and honor. — Al-Tabrisi reports this explanation. [1 notes: TABRISI(غير مسمى)]
- 53:2/q03/p7 He committed no error and did not end in disappointment. — Al-Mawardi gives this paired explanation of the two clauses. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 53:2/q04 What grammatical or dialectal form does ghawa have?  [غَوَىٰ · grammar]
- 53:2/q04/p1 It can be analyzed as غوى يغوي, like shaqiya yashqā. — Abu Ubayda reports the alternative dialectal analysis and notes the verb is also said of a calf made overfull or sick by milk. [1 notes: MAJAZ]

## 53:2/q05 Why negate both straying and erring?  [ضَلَّ غَوَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the two words are synonymous or distinguish kinds, degrees, or causes of error.
- 53:2/q05/p1 The second clause reinforces the first. — Al-Tabrisi says ghawa also means straying; Razi reports that most commentators do not distinguish the terms. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI+]
- 53:2/q05/p2 They deny distinct losses of guidance and right conduct. — Razi and al-Nasafi contrast guidance (hudā) with right conduct or direction (rushd), citing 7:146 and 2:256; Elmalılı and al-Samarra'i similarly distinguish path from sound judgment. Study Quran states the first lacks guidance and the second follows religion incorrectly. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(R,some commentators)]
- 53:2/q05/p3 They deny accidental error and knowingly chosen corruption. — Ibn Atiyya calls dalal unintentional and ghayy acquired and intended. Ibn Kathir contrasts ignorance with deliberately turning from known truth; al-Samarra'i qualifies that dalal itself can be deliberate or accidental, whereas ghawaya is deliberate. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]
- 53:2/q05/p4 They deny both unbelief and lesser sinful conduct. — Razi says dalal is broader: losing all routes to a destination, whereas ghayy lacks a straight route; the pair can deny unbelief and then sin, like nonexistence and corrupt existence (4:6). His separate formulation again ranks dalal as nonexistence and ghayy as corrupt existence. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:2/q05/p5 The narrower second term especially emphasizes sound belief. — Al-Raghib's definition, as quoted by Bursevi, makes ghayy false-belief ignorance while dalal includes speech, action, character and belief. Bursevi and al-Alusi say placing ghayy after dalal singles out the foundation of action, belief. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]

## 53:2/q06 What does repeating the negative particle achieve?  [مَا وَمَا · rhetoric]
- 53:2/q06/p1 It excludes either fault alone as well as the two together. — Al-Samarra'i says the repeated ma makes the denial distributive: neither error nor delusion occurred jointly or separately. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 53:2/q07 What does the past tense of the denials imply?  [ضَلَّ غَوَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 53:2/q07/p1 He had never strayed, even before his mission. — Razi applies the past tense to protected childhood and retreat, before his present revelation guides others. Al-Biqa'i says it meets premission accusations through the audience's knowledge of his prior life. Al-Samarra'i says an affirmative 'he was guided' could imply a limited period or earlier straying. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]

## 53:2/q08 Which reading of the verse endings is reported?  [غَوَىٰ · readings]
- 53:2/q08/p1 Readers vary between fatha, intermediate pronunciation, and imala. — Ibn Atiyya reports Ibn Kathir, Asim and Ibn Amir using fatha at all endings; Abu Bakr's transmission from Asim using imala at ra'a; Nafi and Abu Amr using an intermediate form; Hamza and al-Kisa'i using imala throughout; and Ubayd's transmission from Abu Amr using imala at al-a'la and tadalla. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي)]

## 53:2/q09 How does the cadence of the verse work?  [مَا ضَلَّ صَاحِبُكُمْ وَمَا غَوَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which cadence pattern is valued and how sound is kept subordinate to meaning.
- 53:2/q09/p1 Its balanced clauses show the acceptable longer second cadence. — Al-Burhan says equal clauses are finest because extra length after the expected endpoint feels heavy, yet 53:2 exemplifies a longer second clause; 69:30–31 illustrates a longer third. Itqan reports rhetoricians ranking equality first, then longer second, and Ibn al-Athir allowing a matching or slightly longer second; al-Khafaji forbids a shorter second. [5 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(أهل البديع,ابن الأثير,الخفاجي)]
- 53:2/q09/p2 Short cadence units can display the speaker's power. — Itqan reports another rule: two words is the minimum, more than ten is long, and intermediate units are medium, as in al-Qamar; it cites 74:1, 77:1, 51:1 and 100:1. [1 notes: ITQAN(قائلون)]
- 53:2/q09/p3 Cadence is eloquent when sense and sound remain coherent. — Itqan quotes al-Zamakhshari: sound alone does not make eloquence; fronting in 2:4 conveys exclusivity rather than merely matching an ending. [2 notes: ITQAN(الزمخشري)]
- 53:2/q09/p4 Pausal forms and long vowel sounds shape Quranic verse endings. — Itqan says pausal forms can make nominative and genitive endings match, citing 37:9–11, 54:11–13 and 13:11–12; it says long vowels and appended nun support melodic extension, as Sibawayh observed of Arabic singing. [2 notes: ITQAN]

## 53:2/q10 How does this verse answer the opening oath?  [* · grammar]
- 53:2/q10/p1 The denial that Muhammad strayed is the oath's answer. — Al-Farra, al-Zajjaj, al-Jishumi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, Ibn Kathir and Bursevi say so. Al-Jishumi says al-najm is genitive as oath object, or an omitted rabb is understood. Al-Biqa'i stresses that its past tense refutes charges based on earlier life. Elmalılı says if najm means Qur'an, each descent of revelation is the sworn moment. Bint al-Shati' says the witnessed setting star and its luminous apparent link between earth and sky make revelation's descent less disputable. Al-Samarra'i associates the setting/falling star with the moral fall of deviation and contrasts 'in' misguidance with 'upon' guidance (34:24). [14 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BAGHAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; ELMALILI+; FARRA+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; JISHUMI+; QURTUBI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; ZAJJAJ+]

## 53:2/q11 What accusations does the opening answer?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which contemporary allegation is in view: religious deviation, fabricated speech, madness or poetry, or false revelatory experience.
- 53:2/q11/p1 Muhammad had strayed from the true religion. — Al-Jishumi reports polytheists accusing him of straying; God swears against it. Al-Baydawi and Abu Hayyan say Quraysh accused him of error. Bursevi says they claimed he abandoned ancestral religion. Al-Biqa'i reports Ibn al-Zubayr that the oath answers accumulated charges. [5 notes: JISHUMI(المشركون); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير)]
- 53:2/q11/p2 He invented or spoke the Quran himself. — Muqatil gives the Meccan charge and says God swore to refute it; al-Wahidi reports Muqatil that he did not speak falsehood. Bursevi says they accused him of speaking on his own; Elmalılı says Quraysh accused him of inventing the Quran. Study Quran links the opening to 52:33. Al-Baydawi says it denies a false belief directed at him. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; BAYDAWI-FULL; MUQATIL+]
- 53:2/q11/p3 He was mad, a magician, or a poet. — Ibn Ashur says the verse refutes all these charges as well as fabrication: madness is misguidance, lies and magic both misguidance and error. He says familiar poetry misleads followers, citing 26:224. Al-Damghani compares 81:22; al-Samarra'i cites 34:46 and 81:22 for parallel denials of madness or deviation. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; DAMGHANI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:2/q11/p4 His revelation was soothsaying or a deceptive vision. — Islahi says 53:1–18 establish that the Quran came by a dependable angel, not divination, and that Muhammad's experience of Gabriel was not illusion. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:2/q12 What does the denial establish about Muhammad's guidance and protection?  [* · theology]
turns on: How broadly the verse's guidance extends across life, conduct, and revelation.
- 53:2/q12/p1 He remained guided and morally protected throughout his life. — Razi says one destined for prophethood is preserved from unbelief and grave youthful vices; he applies the past tense to Muhammad's childhood and retreat. Al-Biqa'i says he neither deviated slightly nor intended harm and was guarded from devils. Bursevi says he worshipped God, affirmed unity and avoided reprehensible conduct before and after revelation; his contrast is Adam's disobedience and ghawa in 20:121. Kur'an Yolu says revelation and reason govern him and God protects him from harmful influences. Ibn Kathir calls him righteous and guided and says his law differs from Christian deviation and Jewish concealment of known truth. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:2/q12/p2 His proclamation was free of personal whim or satanic insertion. — Study Quran says absence of caprice means he did not author revelation and his own speech is guided, affirming true religion and practice. Elmalılı says the later denial of speech from caprice excludes a satanic insertion in delivery. Razi explains hawa as lowly appetite: its letters suggest falling; 79:37–40 contrasts desire with restraint, and 38:26 cautions against desire leading one astray. Razi also orders the sequence as right path, straight path, then swift movement, or says freedom from desire prevents straying. [4 notes: ELMALILI+; RAZI-FULL+; STUDYQURAN+]
- 53:2/q12/p3 He attained human happiness through servitude to God and the path to it. — Al-Tabataba'i says he missed neither the destination nor the path, whether in belief or judgment. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 53:2/q13 How is this denial reconciled with 93:7, which says God found him astray?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 93:7 describes relative lack of later guidance, lack of knowledge, or moral conduct.
- 53:2/q13/p1 The earlier description is relative to guidance received with prophethood. — Ibn Atiyya says he was not astray on the path God set for him, though 93:7 describes him before prophethood relative to his later guidance. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 53:2/q13/p2 In 93:7 'astray' means not knowing. — Al-Biqa'i interprets 93:7 as lack of knowledge, rather than moral misguidance. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:2/q13/p3 He was always worshipping God and avoiding reprehensible conduct. — Bursevi says this denial holds before and after revelation despite the wording of 93:7. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 53:2/q14 Who responds to the accusation against Muhammad?  [* · links]
- 53:2/q14/p1 God defends Muhammad directly. — Al-Qushayri contrasts Noah and Hud answering their own accusers (7:61, 7:67), and Moses rebuking Pharaoh (17:102). Al-Biqa'i likewise says other prophets had to deny charges of error or foolishness themselves. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; QUSHAYRI+]

## 53:2/q15 What inward readings are given for the Prophet's freedom from error?  [* · inward]
turns on: Which spiritual loss is denied: departure from oneness, nearness, witnessing, or attachment to self or attributes.
- 53:2/q15/p1 He never left divine oneness or followed Satan. — Al-Tustari and Sahl, as reported by al-Sulami, explain the verse by unbroken tawhid and never following Satan; al-Qushayri likewise says he never strayed from monotheism. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); QUSHAYRI]
- 53:2/q15/p2 He never strayed from nearness to God, even momentarily. — Al-Sulami reports Ja'far's reading. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 53:2/q15/p3 He never lost witnessing of God, even momentarily. — Al-Sulami reports Ibn Ata' and al-Qushayri speaks of uninterrupted awareness of witnessing. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI]
- 53:2/q15/p4 He did not stop at or incline toward the self instead of the ultimate aim. — Al-Qashani gives this reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 53:2/q15/p5 He was not veiled by attributes or held at the heart's station. — Al-Qashani gives this second reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 53:2/q16 How does the verse connect to the following revelation and vision account?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the ensuing vision concerns Gabriel or God; the opening also establishes revelation's trustworthiness.
- 53:2/q16/p1 It introduces trustworthy revelation and guided prophetic speech. — Razi describes the sequence as right path, straight path, then swift pursuit, or says absence of desire prevents error. Study Quran says lack of caprice proves Muhammad did not author revelation. Al-Alusi alternatively says the Quran itself is guiding knowledge from which he did not stray. Islahi says 53:1–18 reject soothsaying and illusion, showing transmission by God's trusted angel. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:2/q16/p2 The subsequent passage recounts two visions of Gabriel in his true form. — Study Quran reports many commentators who distinguish these visions from Gabriel's usual human appearance. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 53:2/q16/p3 The subsequent passage concerns seeing God during the Night Journey and Ascension. — Study Quran reports others: some say by heart alone, others by eye and heart. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(many others)]

## 53:2/q17 How does this opening connect with the close of al-Tur?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is the Prophet and revelation, or the two surahs' common account of judgment.
- 53:2/q17/p1 The two surahs continue the theme of Muhammad and his deniers. — Al-Jishumi says al-Tur closes with the Prophet, opponents and patience (52:7, 52:48), while al-Najm opens with his truth and revelation. Razi notes the shared star wording (52:49; 53:1–2) and the move from night's end to successive dealings with opponents. Study Quran says the opening replies to the invention charge in 52:33. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:2/q17/p2 Both surahs establish reward and punishment, with al-Najm refuting false intercession. — Islahi says al-Tur stresses torment, while al-Najm closes the supposed escape of intercession. False belief makes threats ineffective; invented deities are mere names, and no intercessor could increase God's knowledge, change His just judgment, or turn falsehood into truth. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:2/q18 How does the oath introduce the rest of al-Najm?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the surah's progression is framed around Muhammad's vindication or the broader correction of intercession.
- 53:2/q18/p1 It first clears the Prophet, then unfolds his closeness to God and rebukes his deniers. — Al-Biqa'i reports Ibn al-Zubayr: the oath answers exhausted accusations; the surah then turns to the Prophet's closeness, 53:19, 53:42–43 and the concluding questions at 53:55–56. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر ابن الزبير)]
- 53:2/q18/p2 It opens a true and inevitable discourse that exposes false intercession. — Islahi says the surah joins monotheism with the Hereafter because belief in intercession weakens divine threats; its idols are invented names, and no intermediary can change God's just judgment. He says every verse is true and inevitable. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 53:2/q19 What reported incident is cited near this verse?  [* · reports]
- 53:2/q19/p1 An attacker of the Messenger was struck by a dog. — Al-Tabrisi reports Hassan's poem: the attacker threw at the Messenger; the poem calls the fate a lesson for leader and follower. [1 notes: TABRISI(حسان)]

## 53:2/q20 What is said about the opening's eloquence?  [* · rhetoric]
- 53:2/q20/p1 Its Quranic eloquence is recognizable to capable Arabic listeners. — Bint al-Shati' reports al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar: Arab experience let eloquent hearers recognize extraordinary eloquence without a fresh trial, and less eloquent listeners could recognize inimitability still more readily; examples include 53:1, 53:3–4, 11:44 and 56:27. Some teachers answered a Jewish denial by reciting such passages. Al-Qadi also answered attacks, esoteric displacement of apparent meaning, contradiction claims, and objections to repetition or length. Study Quran notes the surah's rhetorical power and disputed opening interpretation. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(القاضي عبد الجبار,بعض شيوخنا); STUDYQURAN]
