# 20:81: 25 questions

## 20:81/q01 Who receives the command to eat?  [كُلُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the command is direct recalled speech to Israel or a later address with an omitted speech formula.
- 20:81/q01/p1 The Israelites are addressed in the recalled event. — Ibn al-Jawzī says God told Israel to eat. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 20:81/q01/p2 Muhammad's contemporaries are addressed through an elliptical recollection of their ancestors. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the implied speech is 'it was said to them'; the rebuke recalls ancestral ingratitude, though he prefers the direct reading. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:81/q02 What force does the command to eat have?  [كُلُوا۟ · law]
- 20:81/q02/p1 It grants permission to eat. — Al-Ṭabrisī states permission; al-Rāzī compares permission to hunt after consecration (5:2); al-Biqāʿī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also read permissive force. [4 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:81/q03 Why is the command to eat and its provision phrased this way?  [كُلُوا۟ مِن طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 20:81/q03/p1 It recalls and completes a generous divine blessing. — Al-Wāḥidī calls it a reminder of blessings; al-Biqāʿī says good provision signals abundance and 'what We provided' magnifies the gift; al-Ālūsī says it opens a fresh permission and completes the blessing. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:81/q04 What are the good things provided?  [طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṭayyib refers to legal permissibility, agreeable taste, or both.
- 20:81/q04/p1 Lawful food, including the manna and quails. — Muqātil and al-Suddī specify manna and quails; al-Rāzī reports al-Kalbī and Muqātil: it came down untouched by human hands; al-Qurṭubī says it carried no doubtful human claim. Al-Rāghib, cited by al-Bursawī, requires permissible acquisition, amount, and place. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:81/q04/p2 Pleasant or delicious food, especially manna and quails. — Al-Shāfiʿī makes ṭayyib what people find agreeable; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes a juristic dispute about repulsive or impure animals. Al-Rāzī says manna and quails are pleasant; al-Bursawī describes natural taste. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشافعي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:81/q04/p3 Food that is both lawful and pleasant. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya expressly combines the senses; al-Rāzī says they share a common meaning; al-Ālūsī also allows both qualities. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:81/q05 How does the wording relate good things to all divine provision?  [طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · grammar]
- 20:81/q05/p1 The adjective and its noun describe provision as good, without dividing God's provision into good and bad. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī invokes 45:16 and takes the phrase as an adjective joined to its noun. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:81/q06 How do manna and quails explain this provision and connect to other passages?  [مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · links]
- 20:81/q06/p1 They are the freely given sustenance of Moses' followers, recalled also elsewhere. — Asad cites 2:57 and 7:160; philologists say mann is sweet desert resin or an unearned gift, and Qāmūs says salwā is quail or comfort after deprivation. Asad treats the pair metonymically; Study Quran connects 2:57, 2:60, and 7:160; al-Rāzī points to an earlier treatment in al-Baqara. [6 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Arab philologists,Qamus); STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL]

## 20:81/q07 How is divine person expressed in the provision and preceding verbs?  [رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Which divine first-person form the readers transmit and whether the gift's personal attribution is stressed.
- 20:81/q07/p1 First-person plural forms express the divine 'We'. — Ibn Mujāhid lists Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim; Abū ʿAmr omits an alif in waʿadnākum. Al-Rāzī calls this the other readers' usage; al-Nasafī says Kufans except ʿĀṣim use singular forms. [4 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(كوفي غير عاصم)]
- 20:81/q07/p2 First-person singular forms express the divine 'I'. — Ibn Mujāhid names Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī; al-Rāzī notes an exception in the manna clause; al-Bayḍāwī records variant promise forms. Qiraat-ER says the singular emphasizes God's personal gift. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(كوفي غير عاصم); QIRAAT-ER]

## 20:81/q08 What inward provision is meant by eating good things?  [كُلُوا۟ مِن طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقْنَٰكُمْ · inward]
turns on: The inward referent assigned to food and nourishment.
- 20:81/q08/p1 Wholesome knowledge feeds and revives the heart. — Al-Qāshānī makes receiving good knowledge the heart's eating. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:81/q08/p2 Provision received in conscious witness, gratitude, and spiritual realization is good. — Al-Qushayrī reports provision obtained without disobedience, received while witnessing the Sustainer, and producing gratitude; he distinguishes enabling for self, affirmation for heart, realization for spirit. God's elect privately taste now what others openly receive in the afterlife (51:16). [5 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 20:81/q08/p3 Manna and quails signify divine attributes and noble character to embody. — Al-Najmiyya, reported by al-Bursawī, maps manna to attributes and quail to gracious character; divine care rescues spirit and heart from the Pharaoh-like soul and supports this embodiment. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:81/q09 What does transgressing concerning provision mean?  [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which misuse of provision is emphasized: social injustice, ingratitude, unlawful use, hoarding, excess, or spiritual conceit.
- 20:81/q09/p1 Do not wrong other people by taking their share or oppressing them. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies interpersonal wrongdoing, specified by al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān as taking another's provision; al-Wāḥidī adds insolence over the blessing. Al-Suyūṭī glosses transgression as injustice. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN]
- 20:81/q09/p2 Do not exceed God's permitted bounds or disobey His commands. — Muqātil compares Pharaoh's transgression; al-Naḥḥās says abundance must not induce disobedience; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Muqātil include wronging oneself by exceeding permission; Ibn Kathīr includes exceeding need. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes Israel's reckless excess. [12 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); NAHHAS; THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك، مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:81/q09/p3 Do not deny the blessing or neglect gratitude. — Al-Kalbī says denial makes them transgressors; al-Kashshāf includes pleasure distracting from gratitude; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites their complaint of one food (2:61); Study Quran connects that request with ingratitude. [16 notes: THALABI(الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:81/q09/p4 Do not store more manna or quails than allowed. — Muqātil, al-Suddī and later commentators name hoarding; Qatāda allows a double gathering before the Sabbath when Israel did not work. Ibn ʿAbbās specifies the daily limit as a day and night. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:81/q09/p5 Do not cross from lawful food to unlawful food or consume it unlawfully. — Al-Qushayrī and al-Ṭabrisī state this crossing; al-Kalbī includes trading lawful things for forbidden ones. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); THALABI]
- 20:81/q09/p6 Do not use or spend God's provision to support disobedience. — Al-Māwardī and al-Ṭabrisī forbid making provision an aid to sin; al-Kashshāf lists sinful spending and withholding the poor's due; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī add waste, pride, and failure to pay obligations. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 20:81/q09/p7 Do not eat more than the minimum needed or eat heedlessly. — Sahl says excess intoxicates and distracts remembrance; al-Qushayrī separately reports taking beyond subsistence and eating without mindfulness. [3 notes: SULAMI(سهل); QUSHAYRI(ويقال)]
- 20:81/q09/p8 Do not exchange manna and quails for lesser food. — Al-Qurṭubī connects this answer to Israel's request at 2:61. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:81/q09/p9 Do not credit God's favors to one's own noble descent. — Asad identifies arrogance about descent from Abraham. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:81/q09/p10 The ego must not claim perfection, beauty, or lordship through divine gifts. — Al-Qāshānī describes self-display in spiritual light; al-Bursawī warns that ecstatic 'I am the Real' or 'Glory be to me' can reflect human nature, and temporary states do not establish stations. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 20:81/q10 How is the verb 'do not transgress' read?  [تَطْغَوْا۟ · readings]
- 20:81/q10/p1 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads the ghayn with ḍamma. — Al-Samīn derives it from ṭaghā yaṭghū and compares ghadā yaghdū; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit the reading. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]

## 20:81/q11 What happened when Israel stored the provision?  [وَلَا تَطْغَوْا۟ فِيهِ · reports]
- 20:81/q11/p1 Stored manna or food became wormy or spoiled, and without the storage food would not have spoiled. — Muqātil says it became wormy and foul; Ibn ʿAbbās says food, meat, or manna would not have spoiled absent the deed; Qatāda reports an exception before Sabbath, when enough was gathered for Friday and Saturday. One prophetic report couples this with Eve and marital betrayal; Muqātil also gives that comparison. [9 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(unnamed view); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 20:81/q12 What do the connected reports say of the calf?  [* · reports]
- 20:81/q12/p1 Al-Sāmirī blamed Moses' delay on borrowed ornaments and made a lowing calf from them. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq transmits Qatāda: al-Sāmirī used a horse hoof-trace and claimed the calf was their god and Moses' god. Al-Kalbī says Gabriel's horse bore life, and its trace made the calf low. Study Quran links Israel's idol request and worship to 7:138–141 and 20:87–88. [3 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي,قتادة); STUDYQURAN]

## 20:81/q13 How are the nearby words bi-mulkinā read and explained?  [* · readings]
- 20:81/q13/p1 Kasra means ownership, ḍamma authority, and fatḥa the verbal noun of malaka yamliku. — Ibn Khālawayh supports authority with 40:16, 'whose is the dominion today?' The word is in the following calf passage. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA]

## 20:81/q14 What grammatical form expresses the threatened consequence?  [فَيَحِلَّ عَلَيْكُمْ غَضَبِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fa marks a resulting subjunctive or coordinates a jussive prohibition.
- 20:81/q14/p1 The verb is subjunctive after consequential fa, with an omitted an. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Bursawī call it the answer to the prohibition; al-Samīn prefers the accusative analysis because the consequence is clear. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:81/q14/p2 The verb may be jussive, coordinated with 'do not transgress'. — Abū al-Baqāʾ allows it, but al-Samīn objects that it would wrongly prohibit wrath from descending. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 20:81/q15 How are the two verbs for wrath read, and what do their forms mean?  [فَيَحِلَّ يَحْلِلْ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowels and constructions, and whether the verb depicts a due claim, descent, or a caused descent.
- 20:81/q15/p1 Both verbs use kasra, conveying wrath becoming due, arriving, or overtaking. — The majority reading is reported by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Mujāhid, al-Fārisī and others. Al-Naḥḥās and Ibn Khālawayh prefer it by the parallel of a lasting punishment becoming due (cited as 22:4); al-Farrāʾ favors its fit with obligation; al-Qurṭubī cites 11:39. Al-Fārisī also says kasra evokes permissibility and arrival, citing 16:1, 29:54, 10:24, 11:39 and 20:86. Al-Wāḥidī says 'becomes due' describes lifted protection and ensuing punishment rather than a literal lexical definition; several compare a payable debt (2:196). [41 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قراء الحجاز والمدينة والبصرة والكوفة); IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي); NAHHAS(الكوفيون); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد,الباقون); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,قتادة); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الأعمش، الكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور الناس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الجمهور); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش، يحيى بن وثاب، الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(على والباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(الباقون); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء)+; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة، الفراء)+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+]
- 20:81/q15/p2 Both verbs use ḍamma, conveying wrath descending, alighting, or settling. — Al-Kisāʾī is repeatedly singled out, with al-Aʿmash, Yaḥyā b. Waththāb, Qatāda, Abū Ḥaywa and Ṭalḥa also transmitted. Al-Fārisī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Wāḥidī explain descent through punishment that follows anger (13:31); al-Biqāʿī notes forceful settling. Qiraat-ER distinguishes a root for settling from one for descending, contrary to sources that gloss ḍamma as descent; al-Miṣbāḥ allows both vocalizations for punishment. [42 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(جماعة من أهل الكوفة,قتادة); IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي); NAHHAS(الكوفيون); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد,الكسائي); THALABI(يحيى بن وثاب والأعمش والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL(الأعمش، الكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,الكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة، الفراء,الأعمش، يحيى بن وثاب، الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(على والباقون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي، قتادة، أبو حيوة، الأعمش، طلحة); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي); NASHR(الكسائي); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 20:81/q15/p3 Kasra and ḍamma are both sound and closely related in meaning. — Al-Ṭabarī accepts both famous readings because disobedience makes wrath both descend and become due; al-Qurṭubī calls them dialect forms, and al-Wāḥidī says protection lifts so punishment arrives. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة، الفراء,الأعمش، يحيى بن وثاب، الكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:81/q15/p4 A mixed kasra and ḍamma form is transmitted. — Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān attribute a mixed form to Ibn ʿUtayba; al-Rāzī reports al-Aʿmash from ʿAbd Allāh's companions with first kasra and second nominative; al-Ālūsī notes Ibn ʿUtba agrees in the latter vowel. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(ابن عتيبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي، قتادة، أبو حيوة، الأعمش، طلحة); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:81/q15/p5 A prohibitive lā with emphatic nūn warns them not to expose themselves to wrath. — Ibn Ghazwān reports this from Ṭalḥa; al-Samīn compares 'I must not see you here', and al-Ālūsī specifies fatḥa on lām and kasra on ḥāʾ. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن غزوان عن طلحة); SAMIN-DURR(طلحة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن غزوان عن طلحة)]
- 20:81/q15/p6 A causative reading makes transgression bring wrath down, or implies God as agent with punishment omitted. — Al-Lawāmiʿ reports Qatāda, ʿAbd Allāh b. Muslim b. Yasār, Ibn Waththāb and al-Aʿmash. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give the alternative omitted object. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة، عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار، ابن وثاب، الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(صاحب اللوامح); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة وعبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار وابن وثاب والأعمش)]

## 20:81/q16 What is the subject of the verb when wrath descends?  [فَيَحِلَّ عَلَيْكُمْ غَضَبِى · grammar]
turns on: Whether wrath functions as subject or object in the causative analysis.
- 20:81/q16/p1 My wrath is the subject in either ordinary reading. — Al-Samīn calls this the prevailing analysis and objects to al-Lawāmiʿ's exclusion; on a causative account, an omitted object is possible with wrath as subject. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:81/q16/p2 My wrath is the object, with transgression an omitted subject. — Al-Lawāmiʿ considers transgression obvious; al-Samīn calls that restriction awkward because causative iḥlāl takes one object and wrath can instead be subject with another object omitted. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(صاحب اللوامح); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة، عبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار، ابن وثاب، الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة وعبد الله بن مسلم بن يسار وابن وثاب والأعمش); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 20:81/q17 Why is one doubled verb assimilated and the other written distinctly?  [فَيَحِلَّ يَحْلِلْ · grammar]
- 20:81/q17/p1 Assimilation depends on adjacent lām consonants both moving; the other form keeps a still second lām after vowel transfer. — Ibn Khālawayh gives the consonant movement; Ibn Mālik says undoubled clarity such as yaḥlil is Hijazi usage, while assimilation is Tamimi. [2 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; BURHAN(ابن مالك)]

## 20:81/q18 What does God's wrath mean here?  [غَضَبِى · theology]
turns on: How language of human anger and physical descent applies to God's wrath.
- 20:81/q18/p1 It is divine punishment, vengeance, or torment, rather than bodily anger. — Muqātil and al-Qurṭubī equate anger with punishment; al-Bursawī excludes heart agitation. Al-Ālūsī cites a hadith describing human anger as heated blood, swollen veins and red eyes, then says God’s wrath suits His majesty and may mean punishment, vengeance, or willing either. Asad describes unavoidable retribution for deliberate rejection and injustice. [7 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(classical commentators)]
- 20:81/q18/p2 It is an active attribute realized by willing harm through causes after disobedience. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes anger's realization and compares wrath becoming payable to a debt. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:81/q18/p3 In inward reading, it is deprivation and abandonment after spiritual failure. — Al-Qushayrī connects abandonment to one sin following another, missing regret, or accepting a lower state; al-Qāshānī says deprivation follows egoic self-display. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); QASHANI]
- 20:81/q18/p4 Calling divine wrath a 'descent' may name an effect by the will that causes it. — Al-Intiṣāf, reported by al-Ālūsī, says a will of punishment cannot itself descend; one may interpret suitably, as with reports of divine descent, or speak of its effect. Al-Ālūsī says the verb can be literal or figurative by reading. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:81/q19 What does the warning teach about grave sin and spiritual loss?  [وَمَن يَحْلِلْ عَلَيْهِ غَضَبِى فَقَدْ هَوَىٰ · theology]
turns on: The doctrinal and inward implications drawn from falling under wrath.
- 20:81/q19/p1 A grave sinner deserves punishment, contrary to the Murjiʾa. — Al-Jishumī explicitly argues from the fall under divine anger against the Murjiʾa. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 20:81/q19/p2 Spiritual arrogance can nullify devotional dealings and cast the servant from nearness. — Al-Bursawī ties the warning to ecstatic claims of lordship, urges guidance through 1:6; al-Qāshānī says the spirit falls into the self's hell and is veiled from beauty. [3 notes: BURSEVI; QASHANI]

## 20:81/q20 What does 'he has fallen' mean?  [فَقَدْ هَوَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the fall names ruin generally, wretchedness, worldly loss, Hell's abyss, or spiritual exile.
- 20:81/q20/p1 He perished or was destroyed, an image from a fatal fall. — Ibn Qutayba cites hawat ummuhu; al-Wāḥidī cites al-Nābigha and Kaʿb b. Saʿd for death, while al-Kashshāf cites a poem on a crushing mountain fall. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites an eagle and falling bucket; Ibn al-Jawzī gives hawā yahwī and a pit. Abū Ḥayyān explains the euphemism as calamity after safety. ## 20:81/q21 What Hell images are reported for this fall?  [هَوَىٰ · reports]
- 20:81/q21/p1 A palace in Hell receives a falling unbeliever for forty years. — Shafī al-Aṣbaḥī's report pairs a palace named Hawā with a mountain named Suʿūd climbed for forty years and links 74:17. ## 20:81/q22 How is the warning situated among the surrounding blessings and verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding events and verse links illustrate the warning.
- 20:81/q22/p1 The passage moves from rescue, to revelation, to worldly food, to a ban and accepted repentance. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī put removal of harm first, religious benefit above worldly benefit, and provision last; al-Kuranyolu draws the universal lesson to enjoy without excess, seek forgiveness and never despair of mercy. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:81/q22/p2 Israel's desire for other foods and later idolatry show the warned ingratitude. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran invoke 2:61 for the food complaint; Study Quran adds 7:138–141 and 20:87–88 for the later idol episodes. [3 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 20:81/q23 Why can an omitted pronoun sharpen expression near the word for wrath?  [غَضَبِى · rhetoric]
- 20:81/q23/p1 Ellipsis can make a line more graceful than overtly restoring its subject. — Al-Jurjānī's general point uses Ibn al-Zubayr's omitted huwa before dāsiʿ nafsihi and Bakr b. al-Naṭṭāḥ's ghaḍbā omitting hiya before or after the adjective; pronouncing it spoils the effect. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 20:81/q24 What authority governs reports and interpretation of this warning?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The relationship of transmitted boundaries and reflective insight.
- 20:81/q24/p1 Its occasion and story require verified transmission, while interpretation must respect transmitted limits. — Al-Baghawī says tafsīr uncovers circumstances like a physician examining a urine sample; taʾwīl means returning a sense to its source. Recitation stays within written Qur'an and interpretation within transmitted knowledge, though God can open insight through contemplation. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 20:81/q24/p2 Sound understanding also requires study, sincerity, reverence, and reflective insight. — Al-Baghawī distinguishes learned recitation from understanding gained by inward preparation; he ties contemplation to 73:4 and 38:29. Abū al-Dardāʾ says one must discern multiple Qur'anic facets. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(أبو الدرداء)]

## 20:81/q25 How can a verse have an outer and inner aspect, a limit, and an opening?  [* · sciences]
turns on: How the transmitted outer/inner and boundary/opening terms are interpreted.
- 20:81/q25/p1 Each verse or letter has outer and inner aspects and a boundary with a point of ascent. — Al-Baghawī reports Ibn Masʿūd's seven-aḥruf wording, without grading; al-Suyūṭī gives a stopped Ibn Masʿūd report on each letter's boundary and opening. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk transmits Ibn ʿAbbās on inexhaustible branches and the need to study scholars rather than fools. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي); ITQAN(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود)]
- 20:81/q25/p2 Outer is the wording or recitation; inner is interpretation or understanding. — Al-Baghawī and al-Suyūṭī report this formulation; another says reflection compares inner sense to wording; a further version says recitation is as revealed and inner understanding follows contemplation (73:4; 38:29). Ibn al-Naqīb says inner secrets are disclosed to spiritual adepts. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(ابن النقيب,ابن عباس)]
- 20:81/q25/p3 Outer historical stories warn later readers inwardly not to repeat past disobedience. — Al-Baghawī gives this view; Abū ʿUbayd judges it the soundest; Ibn Masʿūd says each verse has been enacted before and will be enacted again. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(ابن مسعود); ITQAN(أبو عبيد)+]
- 20:81/q25/p4 A verse's limit may be intended meaning, lawful and unlawful rulings, or its deserved reward or punishment. — Al-Suyūṭī gives the alternatives; al-Baghawī calls each letter bounded in recitation and interpretation. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ITQAN]
- 20:81/q25/p5 Its opening may be a route to hidden meaning, understanding, or the unveiling of reward and punishment. — Al-Baghawī defines maṭlaʿ as ascent to knowledge; al-Suyūṭī gives routes to difficult senses and rulings, afterlife disclosure, and insight into promise and threat. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; ITQAN]
