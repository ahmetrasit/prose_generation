# 77:20: 8 questions

## 77:20/q01 How is the qāf of نَخْلُقكُّم pronounced?  [نَخْلُقكُّم · readings]
turns on: Whether the qāf is fully merged, retains its emphatic quality, or is audibly distinguished, and which transmitted readings and Arabic phonetic rules are accepted.
- 77:20/q01/p1 The qāf is fully assimilated into the kāf, without an audible qāf or its emphatic quality. — Naḥḥās permits assimilation; Ibn al-Jawzī attributes it to the other reciters besides Qālūn. The Nāshr reports all Abū ʿAmr transmitters agreeing on complete assimilation and al-Dānī's consensus for a pure kāf; Ibn Mihrān cites Ibn Shanabūdh's consensus, al-Hāshimī's ban on distinct articulation, and his teachers' practice across readings. The Nāshr gives the Arabic rule for adjacent identical or homorganic consonants, except long vowels and gutturals, and prefers full assimilation as better transmitted and stronger by analogy, especially for Abū ʿAmr. Its nearby discussion also records that most assimilating Abū ʿAmr transmitters keep imāla before assimilated rāʾ or lām because assimilation is incidental, while Ibn Ḥabash reports al-Sūsī opening it (2:201; 3:190), and that they assimilate nūn into lām or rāʾ without ghunna. [10 notes: NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الباقون); NASHR(ابن مهران,الداني,جمهور رواة الإدغام عن أبي عمرو,رواة الإدغام عن أبي عمرو); NASHR+]
- 77:20/q01/p2 Some transmissions pronounce the qāf distinctly rather than fully assimilating it. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports Qālūn from Nāfiʿ with distinct qāf. The Nāshr notes al-Hudhalī's unique report from Ibn Dhakwān through Ibn al-Akhram and a similar ascription to Qālūn; it itself calls pure unassimilated iẓhār impermissible. Ibn Mihrān reports al-Naqqāsh's iẓhār for Nāfiʿ and ʿĀṣim and al-Bukhārī's for Warsh; the separate claim that only Abū ʿAmr assimilates is attributed to Ibn Mujāhid and rejected by Ibn Mihrān. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قالون عن نافع); NASHR(ابن مهران,الهذلي); NASHR(ابن مهران,الداني)-]
- 77:20/q01/p3 A middle pronunciation assimilates while retaining the qāf's emphatic quality. — Ibn Mihrān recommends a middle reading to one avoiding complete assimilation, though he calls audible intermediate iẓhār ugly and unanimously impermissible. The Nāshr accepts retained istiʿlāʾ as sound in text and oral transmission but prefers full assimilation. [2 notes: NASHR; NASHR(ابن مهران)+]

## 77:20/q02 What fluid does مَّآءٍۢ refer to, and whose is it?  [مَّآءٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse's reference to semen identifies a male contribution alone or semen from both sexes; most glosses leave the contributor unspecified.
- 77:20/q02/p1 It is semen or a sperm drop, with no contributor specified. — Mujāhid and Qatāda's description of the fluid as weak or lowly is reported by al-Māwardī; Ibn Kāmil calls it flowing semen. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī cite 32:8 for the humble fluid; Kuran Yolu cites 75:37 and links the following secure place to the womb and the known term to pregnancy. Some sources call the drop base or filthy, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it low-value and minimally useful. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن كامل,مجاهد وقتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 77:20/q02/p2 The semen or fluid comes from both male and female. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the weak, contemptible water is semen from both sexes; Abū Ḥayyān likewise names the semen of man and woman while using first creation as evidence for resurrection. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 77:20/q02/p3 The embryo comes from the man's fluid alone. — Al-Qurṭubī reports that advocates of this view use this verse as proof; he reports their inference without endorsing it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 77:20/q03 What does مَّهِينٍۢ mean?  [مَّهِينٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: The reported glosses emphasize weakness, low status, filth, small amount, selection, or flowing quality.
- 77:20/q03/p1 Weak. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, and al-Durr report Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss; al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid and Qatāda. Al-Ṭabarī relates the phrase to 32:8. Several authors pair weakness with lowliness; al-Qāmūs, quoted by al-Biqāʿī, also lists it. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(القاموس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد)]
- 77:20/q03/p2 Lowly, contemptible, base, or insignificant. — Ibn Qutayba explicitly glosses it as lowly or contemptible; al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid and Qatāda, and al-Biqāʿī quotes al-Qāmūs. Some specify a dirty or base drop. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it of little value or use. Iṣlāḥī calls it trivial and ordinary, abundant in nature, and says this highlights the ease of recreating a human. Other renderings vary among lowly, humble, despised, and insignificant. [20 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 77:20/q03/p3 Dirty or filthy. — Al-Bayḍāwī calls the drop dirty and base; al-Biqāʿī calls it filthy, weak, and contemptible; al-Ālūsī calls it filthy but says this wording does not establish legal impurity. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 77:20/q03/p4 Scant. — Al-Biqāʿī quotes al-Qāmūs, which includes scant among the senses of mahīn. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(القاموس)]
- 77:20/q03/p5 The pure or selected part of the fluid. — Al-Māwardī reports this gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 77:20/q03/p6 Flowing semen. — Al-Māwardī reports this gloss from Ibn Kāmil. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن كامل)]

## 77:20/q04 Does calling the fluid lowly or filthy make it legally impure?  [مَّهِينٍۢ · law]
- 77:20/q04/p1 No; the description does not establish legal impurity. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly separates the word's lowly or filthy description from a ruling of ritual impurity. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 77:20/q05 What response does recalling this origin call for or expose?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw different, compatible lessons from the same reminder: humility, exposure of ingratitude, and obligation under divine lordship.
- 77:20/q05/p1 It should make humans reflect on their humble origin and restrain conceit. — Muqātil addresses deniers; al-Qushayrī says Adam's descendants claim more than other creatures despite their contemptible beginning. [2 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI]
- 77:20/q05/p2 It exposes ingratitude, disobedience, and defiant denial of God. — Al-Rāzī calls creation a blessing whose rejection makes ingratitude more heinous and punishment greater. Kuran Yolu contrasts God's priceless material and spiritual gifts with disobedience and the warning of woe at 77:18. Study Quran relates denial after creation to 16:4 and 36:77 and says deniers become adversaries to God and themselves. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the passage reproaches arrogance toward worship and revelation. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 77:20/q05/p3 God's sole lordship entails human submission and obligation. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues that creation and governance of human development prove sole lordship, and that lordship requires the creature's submission and obligation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 77:20/q06 What does the cited report say to a human who defies God?  [* · reports]
- 77:20/q06/p1 God challenges Adam's child for defying Him after creation from such a humble substance. — Ibn Kathīr recalls the report from Busr ibn Jahhash in his discussion of Yā-Sīn. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 77:20/q07 What does first creation establish about resurrection and judgment?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The first line of argument establishes God's ability to recreate; the second draws the necessity of judgment from wisdom, provision, lordship, and moral awareness.
- 77:20/q07/p1 God's first creation of humans from insignificant fluid proves He can recreate the dead. — Al-Qushayrī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Iṣlāḥī, Kuran Yolu, and Study Quran make the inference. Al-Rāzī says denial of this evident proof warrants woe; Ibn Kathīr stresses the origin's insignificance beside God's power. Iṣlāḥī answers the objection of decayed bones by citing the already evident creation from mud and fluid; the fluid is ordinary and abundant. Kuran Yolu points to stages in the womb, the resulting material and spiritual powers, and humans as Earth's most perfect beings. Study Quran cites 32:8, 32:7, and 32:11. [15 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 77:20/q07/p2 The creative power, wisdom, providence, lordship, and moral order shown in human creation require a Day of Judgment and recompense. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says sole lordship entails submission and obligation, hence recompense on the Day of Decision. Iṣlāḥī gives three further grounds: God's power, wisdom, and creativity in making humans; the elaborate provision system sustaining them; and innate awareness of good and evil. He also says human physical structure and stages of creation show power, providence, and wisdom, making resurrection certain and denial doomed. [6 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 77:20/q08 How does this reminder fit the surrounding passage and sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize either the local shift to creation as evidence or the wider alternation of reminders, warnings, and outcomes.
- 77:20/q08/p1 After warnings and historical evidence, the sūra turns to human creation as a stronger proof of resurrection. — Al-Rāzī calls this the third warning and relates its blessing to the gravity of unbelief. Al-Biqāʿī says initial creation follows destruction as an even clearer proof of power. Iṣlāḥī calls it a turn from historical evidence to creation and describes signs within humans and the external world before a vivid portrayal of unseen punishment. Sāmarrāʾī says the sūra moves from its oath and Judgment scenes to the blessing of origin (77:1, 77:8–11). [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 77:20/q08/p2 The creation reminder belongs to a sequence of warning, human signs, the deniers' fate, and the pious reward. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the passage's proofs of sole lordship and the Day of Decision, punishment for deniers, blessings for the pious, and reproach of arrogance. Sāmarrāʾī describes the sūra's alternation of Judgment scenes, reminders, the two outcomes, and a final admonition; the deniers' fate precedes reward for the pious (77:29–31, 77:41). Kuran Yolu connects the ingratitude after God's gifts to the woe formula from 77:18. [5 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
