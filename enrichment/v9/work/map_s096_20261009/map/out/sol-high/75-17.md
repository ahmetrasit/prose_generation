# 75:17: 21 questions

## 75:17/q01 Why does the promise begin with inna?  [إِنَّ · rhetoric]
- 75:17/q01/p1 It links the command against haste to God's guarantee and emphasizes that extraordinary guarantee. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the particle supplies a causal connection that would be lost without it, comparing 9:103; perfect retention and reproduction after hearing alone also call for emphasis. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:17/q02 What does 'upon Us' say about God's undertaking?  [عَلَيْنَا · theology]
turns on: Whether divine obligation rests on the promise or on the wisdom required for prophethood.
- 75:17/q02/p1 God undertakes and guarantees the gathering and recitation of revelation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls ʿalaynā a guarantee in both clauses; al-Rāzī and Jishumī observe that ʿalā conveys obligation. Jishumī links the Prophet's commission to his duty to explain enough to remove a responsible person's excuse. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:17/q02/p2 The obligation to preserve revelation follows from God's promise. — Al-Rāzī attributes this basis of obligation to his school. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 75:17/q02/p3 Divine wisdom requires preservation for prophethood to fulfill its purpose. — Al-Rāzī reports the Muʿtazilites' argument that revelation cannot serve its prophetic purpose if forgotten. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)]

## 75:17/q03 Why is 'upon Us' placed before 'its gathering'?  [عَلَيْنَا جَمْعَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 75:17/q03/p1 Fronting 'upon Us' restricts the guarantee to God alone. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says God alone can gather, preserve, and enable accurate complete recitation; later placement would remove exclusivity and change the sense. He illustrates ordinary fronting of prepositional phrases with 'Khalid explains this to you'; the present order also suits the verse ending. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:17/q04 What does the pronoun 'it' refer to?  [جَمْعَهُۥ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether 75:16–19 interrupts the resurrection account to address the Prophet receiving revelation, or continues that account; Elmalılı also considers a general human address.
- 75:17/q04/p1 The Qur'an or revelation being sent to the Prophet. — Al-Wāḥidī and Elmalılı allow an implicit antecedent from context, comparing 97:1; Elmalılı notes the passage later names the Qur'an and the address is specifically to the Prophet. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God gathers its parts and recites without omission; Abū Ḥayyān identifies the Qur'an as the referent of 'in your chest.' [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ELMALILI+]
- 75:17/q04/p2 The person's record of deeds at resurrection. — Al-Qaffāl relates the pronoun to the record shown after 75:13–15 and compares 17:14; al-Balkhī and al-Bayḍāwī describe the distressed reader of that record. Al-Balkhī and his followers infer the record as the pronoun's antecedent from 75:13, even though the record is only implied there. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(البلخي); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; TABATABAI-]
- 75:17/q04/p3 The person's inward knowledge or conscience. — Elmalılı says an address to humanity generally could supply this otherwise unexpressed antecedent. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 75:17/q04/p4 The coming Judgment and those destined for it. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the view that God gathers those destined for Judgment and reveals its description. Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative as referring the pronoun to the Day of Resurrection: God gathers creation, reveals the Day's conditions in the Qur'an, and manifests it at the blast. [3 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:17/q05 What is meant by God's gathering it?  [جَمْعَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether gathering concerns revelation retained in the Prophet, its assembled portions, or eschatological evidence and people.
- 75:17/q05/p1 God fixes the revelation in the Prophet's breast or heart for secure memory. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk describe placing it in his chest or establishing it in his heart. Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī add that no meaning escapes him; Abū Ḥayyān reports Gabriel's repetition until it settles there. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the heart-and-tongue explanation remote. [31 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK,Ibn ʿAbbās)]
- 75:17/q05/p2 God preserves and assembles the revelation, arranging its parts and recitation. — Qatāda repeatedly glosses the phrase as preservation and composition. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī specify ordering as revealed; Iṣlāḥī includes both the Prophet's memory and assembly of separate portions. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes collection of its parts without omission. [16 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); JISHUMI(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 75:17/q05/p3 God gathers the person's deeds and the evidence of them for judgment. — Al-Qaffāl, al-Balkhī, and al-Bayḍāwī connect it with the distressed reader of the record, who must submit or confess; al-Qaffāl calls the warning strong but acknowledges no supporting reports. Al-Qaffāl adds that God will have the person acknowledge or examine the assembled record of deeds before explaining its consequences and punishment, a severe threat of detailed reckoning. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); TABRISI(البلخي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); TABATABAI-]
- 75:17/q05/p4 God gathers those destined for Judgment. — The reported Judgment-day reading then makes recitation its revealed description and explanation the trumpet blast. In the alternative reported by al-Ālūsī, God gathers creation for the Day and manifests it at the blast. [2 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:17/q06 Why pair 'its gathering' with 'its qurʾān'?  [جَمْعَهُۥ قُرْءَانَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether qurʾān adds the act of reciting, specifies a kind of gathering, or itself means gathering.
- 75:17/q06/p1 The second term specifies or adds to the general act of gathering. — Al-Wāḥidī says general gathering can be paired with the specific Qur'an or recitation, unlike 'reading people'; the second term can add a sense as 'warn' adds to 'inform.' Ibn Kathīr describes successive preservation, recitation, and explanation; al-Zarkashī distinguishes heart, tongue, and hearing. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:17/q06/p2 Both can denote collection, with external existence and inward memorization distinguished. — Al-Rāzī offers this distinction to avoid redundancy if qurʾān also means collection. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 75:17/q06/p3 Both terms jointly refer to composition and collection. — Abū ʿUbayda treats the pairing figuratively as arrangement of parts; Ibn Qutayba and Abū ʿUbayda relate the root to joining sūras and the fetus idiom. [4 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 75:17/q07 What does 'its qurʾān' mean, and whose recitation is promised?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether qurʾān denotes recitation or collection, and on the recitation sense whether the immediate reciter is Muhammad, Gabriel, or God.
- 75:17/q07/p1 God enables the Prophet himself to recite the revelation reliably. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk call qurʾān a verbal noun of reading; the Prophet recites after it is fixed in his heart. Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī compare 87:6, distinguishing his recitation from Gabriel's on another reading. Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Ḥassān's verse; al-Ālūsī says he may recite whenever he wishes. Jishumī includes repeating and explaining it; al-Bursawī specifies ordered joining of letters and words. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس والضحاك); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس والضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās)]
- 75:17/q07/p2 Qurʾān means collection, composition, or arrangement of revelation. — Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba derive the name from joining sūras. The she-camel or woman carrying no fetus, ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's لم تقرأ جنينا, water collected in a basin and its maqrāh, and a gathered qarya attest qaraʾa as collecting; al-Zajjāj relates the name to collected speech. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī compare the verbal-noun forms rujḥān and nuqṣān. Al-Ṭabarī says both composition and clarification are valid Arabic, while each revealed verse bound the Prophet before full compilation. Al-Ālūsī questions the reported composition account, though he also records its etymology. [24 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(أبو عبيدة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة,قتادة); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو عبيدة,ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 75:17/q07/p3 God recites the revelation to the Prophet so that he understands or retains it. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī explicitly add understanding; Muqātil and al-Rāzī connect recitation with memorization and freedom from forgetting, the latter comparing 87:6. Ibn ʿAbbās's alternate report treats it as divine clarification through recitation, followed by the Prophet's following its contents; al-Ṭabarī says this is valid Arabic. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس والضحاك); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)+]
- 75:17/q07/p4 Gabriel repeats or recites it to the Prophet. — Al-Farrāʾ says Gabriel's repetition assures the Prophet's recitation; ʿAṭāʾ and al-Rāzī describe repetition until memorized. Al-Wāḥidī contrasts Gabriel as reciter with the Prophet on al-Zajjāj's interpretation. [5 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,عطاء); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 75:17/q07/p5 Qurʾān is the verbal noun 'recitation,' without identifying a single reciter here. — Al-Farrāʾ equates qirāʾa and qurʾān as verbal nouns; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite Ḥassān's nightlong recitation verse, as does Ibn ʿĀshūr. Al-Bursawī compares ghufrān; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds furqān; Elmalılı also allows the recited text as the noun's sense, and al-Nasafī compares 20:114. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(حسان); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:17/q07/p6 It means collecting the recitation in the Prophet's chest. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this alternate sense and calls the noun a verbal form like rajḥān and nuqṣān; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also reports collection and arrangement in the breast. [2 notes: THALABI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 75:17/q07/p7 It is God's reading of the person's deeds or record at resurrection. — The record interpretation makes the reader acknowledge its contents and face explanation through requital; al-Qaffāl calls it a strong warning but notes lack of reports. Al-Qaffāl says God reads the assembled deeds to their owner, who must acknowledge or consider them; the ensuing explanation is of their consequences and punishment. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); TABRISI(البلخي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); TABATABAI-]
- 75:17/q07/p8 It is God's revelation of Judgment's description. — A reported interpretation says the gathering is of Judgment's people and its explanation comes with the trumpet blast. The alternative reported by al-Ālūsī specifies revelation of the Day's conditions in the Qur'an before its manifestation at the blast. [2 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:17/q08 How does the verbal noun 'qurʾānahu' work grammatically?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · grammar]
- 75:17/q08/p1 It is a verbal noun annexed to its object, with its agent unexpressed. — Al-Wāḥidī says the pronoun is the object of 'our gathering and reciting it,' avoiding the absurdity of adding a named Qur'an to itself; al-Samīn paraphrases 'your reciting it.' Al-Ālūsī also understands an omitted annexed word on the recitation sense. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 75:17/q09 Is there a variant reading of 'qurʾānahu'?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · readings]
- 75:17/q09/p1 A reading eases the hamza, as in wa-qurānahu / wa-qarānahu. — The qirāʾāt note assigns hamza easing to Ḥamza while retaining the q-r-ʾ field; al-Samīn reports Abū al-ʿĀliya's جمعه وقرانه and فاتبع قرانه through Ibn ʿAṭiyya and relates it to Ibn Kathīr's earlier variant discussion. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER(hmz); SAMIN-DURR(أبو العالية)]

## 75:17/q10 Who is addressed by this assurance?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage shifts to the Prophet's reception of revelation or continues the surrounding account of resurrection.
- 75:17/q10/p1 The Prophet receiving Qur'anic revelation. — Ibn ʿAbbās's reports place the address during Gabriel's revelation; Elmalılı notes the explicit Qur'an later in the passage, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares 20:114. Al-Ṭabarī prefers the report in which he hurries before revelation is gathered, since memory review would follow gathering. Al-Ālūsī contrasts al-Qaffāl's record address with the authentic majority transmission addressing the Prophet. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ELMALILI+; TAB-FULL+]
- 75:17/q10/p2 A person facing the record of deeds at resurrection. — Al-Qaffāl, al-Balkhī, and al-Bayḍāwī infer this address from nearby self-witness and judgment verses; al-Qaffāl concedes the view has no report. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects it by treating the verses as an interruption and comparing 20:114. Al-Qaffāl says fear at being shown the record makes the person stumble and rush to read it. Al-Balkhī and his followers prefer this address as an implied saying at the book's presentation; al-Ālūsī contrasts it with the authentic majority transmission addressing the Prophet. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); TABRISI(البلخي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; TABATABAI-]
- 75:17/q10/p3 Humanity generally, in relation to inward knowledge. — Elmalılı offers a general addressee in which God gathers and voices the conscience a person should not rush to voice; he prefers the specific prophetic address. [2 notes: ELMALILI; ELMALILI-]
- 75:17/q10/p4 The person asking prematurely about the coming Judgment. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this additional view and himself calls it untenable. Al-Ālūsī reports that the Judgment-day prohibition can address the Prophet, a generic addressee, or any fitting listener who asks prematurely for the Day's date. [4 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:17/q11 What happened as the Prophet received revelation, and why did he hurry?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether haste chiefly reflected fear of forgetting during recitation, or also concern for understanding and receiving guidance for the mission.
- 75:17/q11/p1 He moved his tongue or lips during Gabriel's recitation, fearing loss or forgetting, then listened and recited after Gabriel left. — Ibn ʿAbbās describes the strain of revelation, and Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd demonstrate the movement. Bukhārī and Muslim transmit it by multiple routes; Bukhārī also has him lower his head as Gabriel arrived. Al-Tirmidhī is cited by Elmalılı. Qatāda's version also ascribes the movement to fear of forgetting. Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes hurried reception before gathering from later memory review, which he finds chronologically less fitting. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the fear account as obscuring the command to follow the completed recitation; he also calls the report's fit with the verses obscure. The Imam suggests the Prophet's haste may have been expressly permitted before the prohibition; al-Ālūsī finds that explanation problematic. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,البخاري ومسلم); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); TABATABAI(ابن عباس,بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Ibn ʿAbbās); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، مجاهد، قتادة، عطاء); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 75:17/q11/p2 He eagerly sought further revelation for his mission and guidance amid pressure from opponents, as well as fearing loss. — Iṣlāḥī says his opponents demanded answers and challenged piecemeal revelation; pauses had divine wisdom, yet the Prophet looked heavenward for help. Revelation renewed strength, guided his strategy and spiritual, moral, and intellectual needs, and enabled responses. Farāhī adds fear of responsibility for even one lost letter. Al-Zamakhsharī says he also hurried to ask about meaning; al-Rāzī contrasts reliance on repetition with turning to God, comparing 20:114. Al-Ālūsī favors al-Zamakhsharī's account over the Judgment-day alternative as better fitting the Qur'an's eloquence and subtle indications. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 75:17/q12 What should the assurance teach about receiving and studying revelation?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the instruction is applied specifically to the Prophet's reception or also to every later reader and listener.
- 75:17/q12/p1 The Prophet should listen patiently, trust God's preservation and timing, and attend to the revealed portion and its meaning. — Al-Rāzī says fear of forgetting is answered by divine aid; al-Jishumī stresses reflection and knowledge of meanings and rulings over mere memorization. Al-Bayḍāwī calls haste blameworthy even here. Iṣlāḥī links patience to gradual revelation and 20:114–15, the Prophet's noble motives, and his duty to convey and explain; al-Zamakhsharī says questions about meaning wait until recitation ends. [16 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI+]
- 75:17/q12/p2 Every Qur'an student should read attentively and grasp the whole message before hasty conclusions. — Asad extends the passage to anyone reading, listening, or studying, compares 20:114, warns against isolated verses, mechanical fluency, and attraction to sound without meaning, and sees one integrated message rather than detached maxims, narratives, or laws. Study Quran likewise calls for reflection on all teachings. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:17/q12/p3 Do not hurry to ask when the Day will come; God will disclose its conditions and manifest it in due course. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative as a prohibition on opening one's mouth to demand the Day's date; its timing rests on divine wisdom, while the Qur'an reveals its conditions and the blast manifests it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:17/q13 What does the promise imply about preservation of the received Qur'an?  [* · theology]
- 75:17/q13/p1 The Qur'an has been guarded against loss, addition, and textual alteration. — Al-Naḥḥās rejects a claim that some remained unrevealed; Sufyān contrasts God's direct care of the Qur'an (15:9) with Torah and Gospel entrusted to their people (5:44). Al-Zarkashī invokes 15:9 and communal agreement that the codex is certain and protected for practice. Farāhī cites 41:41–42 and 15:9 and reports communal agreement. Named Imāmī authorities reject disappearance; al-Qummī calls the codex precisely the revelation without an extra letter, and Murtaḍā says weak dissenting reports cannot defeat certainty. [11 notes: NAHHAS(سفيان بن عيينة); BURHAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī ibn Bābawayh al-Qummī,Sayyid Murtaḍā,Sayyid Murtaḍā, Muḥammad ibn Ḥasan al-Ṭūsī, Abū ʿAlī Ṭabarī, Ṭabrasī, and Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī ibn Bābawayh al-Qummī,Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]

## 75:17/q14 When and how were the Qur'an's parts collected and ordered?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The distinction between memorized and ordered revelation in the Prophet's lifetime and Abū Bakr's later gathering from existing materials.
- 75:17/q14/p1 The entire revelation was memorized and arranged during the Prophet's life, with divinely guided placement and final recitation. — Al-Zarkashī says the four famous Companion preservers were examples, not the only memorizers, while noting an exception concerning Barāʾa. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet instructed collectors where verses belonged and reviewed extant revelation with Gabriel each Ramadan, twice in the final Ramadan. Farāhī infers an ordered sequence from the instruction to follow the recital, says it matched the Guarded Tablet, cites whole sūrahs recited and memorized in order, and says agreed authentic reports make Gabriel's final recitation settle doubts. [8 notes: BURHAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ḥamīd al-Dīn Farāhī)]
- 75:17/q14/p2 After Yamāma's heavy losses, Abū Bakr commissioned Zayd to gather written and memorized Qur'an. — Al-Bukhārī's Zayd report says ʿUmar feared further deaths among reciters, persuaded a hesitant Abū Bakr despite the Prophet not having performed this collection, and Zayd, a trusted young revelation scribe, assembled material from writing media and memories after his own hesitation. [2 notes: BURHAN(زيد بن ثابت)]

## 75:17/q15 How does this verse fit its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether 75:16–19 form an instructional interruption or continue and echo the surrounding resurrection theme.
- 75:17/q15/p1 Verses 75:16–19 interrupt the resurrection account to instruct the Prophet about revelation. — Al-Khaṭṭābī reports the objection that they disrupt movement from self-witness (75:14–15) to love of worldly life (75:20–21). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts an interruption and compares 20:114, adding that an interruption needs no overt signs before and after it; al-Bayḍāwī sees a rebuke of haste even in revelation. [4 notes: KHATTABI; TABATABAI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 75:17/q15/p2 The revelation passage is integrated with the sūra's account of conscience, impatience, and gathering. — Elmalılı joins the preceding conscience discussion to the end of the passage; al-Sāmarrāʾī compares God's gathering of bones and of sun with moon at Judgment to earthly gathering and reciting of Qur'an. Al-Rāzī compares the unbeliever fleeing God with the Prophet's need to turn from repetition to God. Abū Ḥayyān links the passage to the contrast between a denier absorbed in sinful desires and one who preserves, studies, and presents God's signs in hope of acceptance; opposites illuminate one another. [4 notes: ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 75:17/q15/p3 The passage continues the resurrection scene through the reading of a person's record. — Al-Qaffāl points to 75:13–15 and 17:14 and calls it a strong warning without reported support; al-Balkhī and al-Bayḍāwī portray fear, confession, judgment, and requital. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this because the prophetic interruption is plausible and 20:114 supports it. Al-Qaffāl compares 75:13–14: fear of the record makes its recipient hurry and stumble; God then threatens to assemble, read, and explain the deeds. Al-Balkhī and his followers take the words as a circumstantial saying when the book is shown. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); TABRISI(البلخي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+; TABATABAI-]
- 75:17/q15/p4 The passage concerns haste to ask about Judgment and God's gathering of its people. — A further interpretation reported by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes recitation as revealing Judgment's description and explanation as the trumpet blast; he rejects it. Al-Ālūsī reports the alternative as an interruption of the threat that intensifies the Day's terror; asking for its date conflicts with the wisdom of God's timing. He judges the alternative seriously difficult. [4 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 75:17/q16 What inward sense is given to gathering and recitation?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward/outward division describes a secret deposit, effacement at unity, or reliance on God across all affairs.
- 75:17/q16/p1 The Qur'an is gathered inwardly and recited outwardly. — Al-Wāsiṭī describes the deposit in the innermost secrets and explanation in inward hearts, with recitation aloud in public; al-Bursawī summarizes his inward/outward distinction. [3 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); BURSEVI(الواسطي)]
- 75:17/q16/p2 God gathers it in the addressee at unity and recites through one effaced from self. — Al-Qāshānī describes absence of independent existence or trace at this station. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 75:17/q16/p3 The Prophet should rely on God for every affair, including inner retention and easy utterance. — An unnamed authority in al-Sulamī extends the promise from Qur'anic gathering in the breast and ease on the tongue to divine care for all affairs. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 75:17/q17 How is heard recitation related to divine speech?  [* · theology]
- 75:17/q17/p1 The listener hears created recitation and through it understands God's eternal speech. — Al-Zarkashī reports this from some of his colleagues. [1 notes: BURHAN(بعض أصحابنا)]

## 75:17/q18 Why is an alif written in Qur'an?  [قُرْءَانَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 75:17/q18/p1 Its written alif reflects clear, accessible revelation as compared with the comprehensive Book's higher aspect. — Al-Zarkashī says written alif marks a concrete, lower, knowable reality and omission a metaphysical or attributive higher one. The Qur'an details verses established in the Book and is nearer understanding; he contrasts its written alif with its omission in كتاب, citing 11:1, 41:3, and 75:17. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 75:17/q19 How do the divine plural forms here relate to singular forms elsewhere in the sūra?  [* · rhetoric]
- 75:17/q19/p1 The sūra uses both plural divine actions of gathering and ability and singular actions of creating and proportioning. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī juxtaposes 'We are able,' 'We gather,' and 'We shape' with 'He created' and 'He proportioned.' [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:17/q20 How is the record-reading address grammatically connected to the presentation of the book?  [* · grammar]
- 75:17/q20/p1 It is circumstantial speech, with an implied saying, as the person's book is presented. — Al-Balkhī and his followers, as reported by al-Ālūsī, read the sentence as a circumstantial clause introduced by an implied saying at the presentation of the record. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البلخي)+]

## 75:17/q21 Was the Prophet's haste permitted before the command against it?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:17/q21/p1 His haste may have been expressly permitted until the prohibition came. — The Imam suggests earlier explicit permission; al-Ālūsī calls the explanation problematic. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]
