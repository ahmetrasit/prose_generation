# 18:37: 28 questions

## 18:37/q01 Who is the companion who speaks?  [صَاحِبُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether sahib is a kinship term and which disputed personal name belongs to the believer.
- 18:37/q01/p1 He is the believing companion of the garden owner. — Muqatil, Yahya b. Sallam, al-Thaʿlabi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Kathir, and others identify the speaker by his faith and the addressee as the owner; Ibn ʿAshur says he had already admonished the rich man, who answered arrogantly. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:37/q01/p2 Sahib here means brother. — Ibn al-Jawzi attributes this gloss to some exegetes. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]
- 18:37/q01/p3 The believing companion was called Judah. — Al-Qurtubi gives Judah as one of the disputed names. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:37/q01/p4 The believing companion was called Tamlīkha. — Al-Qurtubi gives Tamlīkha as the alternative disputed name. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 18:37/q02 Why does the companion's speech begin without a conjunction?  [قَالَ · rhetoric]
- 18:37/q02/p1 The bare qāla introduces the answering turn in the dialogue. — Ibn ʿAshur treats the speech as a question-and-answer turn. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:37/q03 How are the hāʾ pronouns in this phrase used?  [لَهُۥ صَاحِبُهُۥ يُحَاوِرُهُۥٓ · grammar]
- 18:37/q03/p1 The hāʾ is an ordinary third-person pronoun occurring in genitive or accusative position. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān cite the companion's speech phrase as an example; the former notes both cases, and the latter specifically glosses 'his companion said to him.' [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 18:37/q04 What does yuḥāwiruhu mean here?  [يُحَاوِرُهُۥٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the conversation is described neutrally or as dispute; the dispute wording is reported as an explanatory reading.
- 18:37/q04/p1 He was speaking or conversing with him. — Abū ʿUbayda derives it from muḥāwara, conversation; al-Tabatabaʾi takes the repeated dialogue wording as showing the believer's steady composure and gentle speech. [2 notes: MAJAZ; TABATABAI]
- 18:37/q04/p2 He was disputing with him. — Ubayy's explanatory wording yukhāṣimuhu expresses this sense; Ibn ʿAtiyya reports it, while Abū Ḥayyān says it is explanatory and not transmitted recitation because it differs from the codex. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ALUSI-FULL(أبي)]

## 18:37/q05 What status has Ubayy's wording wa-huwa yukhāṣimuhu?  [وَهُوَ يُحَاوِرُهُۥٓ · readings]
turns on: Whether Ubayy's differing wording counts as recitation or explanation in light of the codex.
- 18:37/q05/p1 It is reported as Ubayy's wording describing a dispute. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Ālūsī report Ubayy's wording. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب); ALUSI-FULL(أبي)]
- 18:37/q05/p2 It is only explanatory, not a transmitted Qur'anic recitation. — Abū Ḥayyān says the received reading is yuḥāwiruhu and Ubayy's wording differs from the codex. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي)]

## 18:37/q06 What is the syntax of wa-huwa yuḥāwiruhu?  [وَهُوَ يُحَاوِرُهُۥٓ · grammar]
- 18:37/q06/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing the believing speaker. — Abū Ḥayyān identifies it as a ḥāl; al-Ālūsī calls it an independent circumstantial clause framing the exchange. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:37/q07 Why is their conversation explicitly mentioned?  [وَهُوَ يُحَاوِرُهُۥٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the dialogue phrase chiefly draws attention to the speech or characterizes the believer's manner.
- 18:37/q07/p1 It signals the importance and careful presentation of the ensuing exchange. — Al-Bursawī reports al-Irshād's explanation that it alerts the listener to the speech's importance; al-Ālūsī says it presents the exchange as conversation. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:37/q07/p2 Its repetition shows the believer's composure, gentle speech, and restraint. — Al-Tabatabaʾi says the believer keeps his faith steady, does not insult the man's children, and only hints that his garden may fail. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 18:37/q08 What force does the question akafarta have?  [أَكَفَرْتَ · rhetoric]
- 18:37/q08/p1 It rebukes and censures a disbelief already evident, rather than seeking information. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it an effective affirmation of disbelief; Ibn ʿAshur adds astonishment and says the speaker knew the man was a polytheist. Thābit's 'woe to you' addition makes the rebuke explicit. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 18:37/q09 What is reported in Thābit's expanded wording of the rebuke?  [أَكَفَرْتَ · readings]
- 18:37/q09/p1 Thābit adds waylaka, 'woe to you,' before akafarta. — Ibn ʿAtiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī report Thābit al-Bunānī's wording; Abū Ḥayyān calls it explanatory, not a Prophetic reading. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ثابت البناني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ثابت البناني); ALUSI-FULL(ثابت البناني)]

## 18:37/q10 What disbelief is charged by akafarta?  [أَكَفَرْتَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the charge targets resurrection doubt, idolatrous association, or the claim that self and natural causes have independent power.
- 18:37/q10/p1 He doubted or denied resurrection, and thereby God's power, truthfulness, or wisdom. — Al-Zajjāj links doubt after creation to disbelief and false sinful conjecture, citing 38:27; al-Zamakhsharī compares denying a Messenger; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Bursawī stress power; al-Ālūsī prefers this reading and cites 23:115; al-Biqāʿī says the man treated resurrection as a doubtful hypothetical. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 18:37/q10/p2 He associated other beings with God or worshipped idols. — Abū Ḥayyān calls the rebuke one of association; al-Ālūsī reports an idolatrous reading and notes that calling God 'Lord' does not rule it out; Ibn ʿAshur includes resurrection denial in shirk; al-Qurṭubī infers polytheism from the companion's later refusal to associate anyone. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 18:37/q10/p3 He claimed independence for himself and worldly causes, denying God's sole lordship. — Al-Tabatabaʾi says this shirk underlies his doubt about resurrection, though he calls God 'my Lord' and does not outright deny resurrection; human capacities, including mature powers, belong wholly to God. [4 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 18:37/q11 What does the question teach about doubt concerning resurrection?  [أَكَفَرْتَ · theology]
- 18:37/q11/p1 Doubting resurrection is disbelief in God. — Al-Zajjāj says false conjecture is sinful and cites the warning in 38:27 against treating creation as purposeless; al-Rāzī derives the ruling from the believer's question; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ṭabrisī, and others connect doubt to doubting God's power and attributes. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:37/q12 How was the addressee created from dust?  [خَلَقَكَ مِن تُرَابٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dust names Adam's creation, food-derived material of the man's own body, or soil allegedly placed into sperm by an angel.
- 18:37/q12/p1 God created Adam, the man's ancestor and origin, from dust, so Adam's descendant is said to be created from dust. — Ibn ʿAbbās says a son's origin follows his father; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī explain attribution from the created origin to its descendant; al-Ālūsī says Adam's constitution contained humankind's pattern. Al-Bayḍāwī gives this as one possibility. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 18:37/q12/p2 The man's own material came from soil through food that produces reproductive fluid. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports the food-to-fluid explanation; Abū Ḥayyān mentions the father's food-derived semen; al-Ālūsī calls this a figurative cause-to-effect attribution; Ibn ʿAshur cites 36:36. Al-Bayḍāwī allows his own source material as an alternative. [5 notes: TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:37/q12/p3 An angel puts soil into each sperm destined to become a human. — Al-Ālūsī recalls possible authentication but finds problems applying the report here; Abū Ḥayyān says this report needs a sound basis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 18:37/q13 What does min mark in the creation stages?  [مِن تُرَابٍۢ مِن نُّطْفَةٍۢ · grammar]
- 18:37/q13/p1 Min marks origin or source material. — Ibn ʿAshur applies the same origin sense to dust and the drop. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:37/q14 What does nuṭfa mean?  [نُّطْفَةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether nuṭfa is defined by a small drop, water of variable quantity, or its reproductive referent in this context.
- 18:37/q14/p1 It is a drop or small quantity of water, applied to semen. — Abū Ḥayyān says semen drips in drops; al-Samīn derives it from naṭafa, to drip, adducing a hadith about a dripping head and its 'poured' variant; al-Wāḥidī cites a little water in a waterskin and 75:37 for emitted semen. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 18:37/q14/p2 Arabic may use nuṭfa for either a little or much water. — Al-Wāḥidī and his cited authority al-Azharī give this wider range of quantity. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري)]
- 18:37/q14/p3 Here it is the man's reproductive fluid, his immediate material origin. — Ibn ʿAshur derives the term from flowing; al-Ālūsī distinguishes the nearer sperm origin from the earlier soil origin; al-Wāḥidī locates the drop in the mother's womb, and al-Bursawī similarly speaks of maternal sperm. [6 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 18:37/q15 What does thumma indicate between the stages?  [ثُمَّ · meaning]
- 18:37/q15/p1 With ḍamma, thumma joins stages in order with delay. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the conjunction in 18:37 and 2:28 with thamma, 'there,' with fatḥa in 26:64. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 18:37/q16 What does sawwāka describe?  [سَوَّىٰكَ · meaning]
- 18:37/q16/p1 God proportioned, completed, and perfected the person's form and growth. — Al-Kalbī includes sound limbs and senses; al-Wāḥidī prefers al-Zajjāj's 'completed' and invokes Arabic usage and Moses' maturity in 28:14; al-Ṭabrisī describes changes through successive states; al-Ālūsī stresses balanced form; Ibn ʿAshur adds proportionate function. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+]

## 18:37/q17 What does rajulan add to the description?  [رَجُلًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether rajulan emphasizes male adulthood, human personhood in general, or mature intellectual and religious capacity.
- 18:37/q17/p1 God made him a complete, upright, mature male human. — Al-Zamakhsharī includes proportion and adulthood; al-Qurṭubī adds sound limbs and male sex; al-Qāmūs defines rajul as a man, especially one past puberty; Abū Ḥayyān allows the male rather than female sense; al-Tabatabaʾi treats mature capacities as gifts. [16 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(القاموس); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي); TABATABAI]
- 18:37/q17/p2 It may simply mean a person or living being. — Ibn ʿAtiyya offers this beside al-Ṭabarī's view that male sex is itself a blessing; al-Ṭabrisī speaks more generally of balanced human stature. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI]
- 18:37/q17/p3 Mature personhood includes intelligence and religious responsibility. — The Study Quran reports this explanation with taklīf; al-Rāzī says rational adulthood prepares a person for obligation, making recompense fitting. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL]

## 18:37/q18 How is rajulan parsed?  [سَوَّىٰكَ رَجُلًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether sawwāka takes rajulan as a resulting state or as a second object with a 'make' sense.
- 18:37/q18/p1 It is a circumstantial accusative, a ḥāl. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit this parsing; al-Samīn defends it despite rajul being neither derived nor normally transient, since God could have made him otherwise, citing a giraffe example and a poetic comparison. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 18:37/q18/p2 It is the second object of sawwāka, meaning 'made you a man.' — Al-Ḥawfī's parsing is cited by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī; al-Samīn says it implies ṣayyaraka or jaʿalaka. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الحوفي)]

## 18:37/q19 What does the appeal to human creation establish?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the cited stages are used to demonstrate resurrection, God's existence and worship, dependence on Him, human lowliness, or final recompense.
- 18:37/q19/p1 The Creator who first made a human can resurrect him; the successive stages show His power. — Al-Rāzī states the initial-creation argument; al-Qurṭubī calls the first creation more extraordinary; Ibn ʿAshur cites 50:15 and 30:27; Abū Ḥayyān adds the messengers' truthful promise and says nothing is beyond God; al-Bayḍāwī says dust creation establishes re-creation from it. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:37/q19/p2 The stages establish a wise, pre-eternal Creator and His sole right to worship. — Al-Jishumī says the changes prove a wise pre-eternal Creator; Ibn Kathīr cites 2:280 and argues that one who did not exist cannot create himself or derive existence from another created thing; Ibn ʿAshur says people acknowledge their Creator, so worship belongs to Him alone. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:37/q19/p3 Human powers and accomplishments are God's gifts, leaving no basis for self-sufficiency. — Al-Tabatabaʾi extends the gift from dust and semen to mature capacities, denying independent ownership to persons and natural causes. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 18:37/q19/p4 Human origin from dust and a drop underscores lowliness before God. — The Study Quran connects the stages to 3:59, 30:20, 16:4, 23:13–14, 36:77, 75:37, 76:2, 80:19, 22:5, 35:11, and 40:67; Asad points readers to 3:59 and 23:12. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]
- 18:37/q19/p5 Purposeful creation entails worship, obligation, and eventual reward and punishment. — Al-Rāzī says God did not make humans in vain, and rational adulthood fits them for obligation; al-Zajjāj recalls 38:27's warning that imagining purposeless creation is disbelief; al-Biqāʿī says ending life at death would leave the obedient and sinful unrecompensed. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:37/q20 What does the companion's argument show about religious reasoning?  [* · theology]
- 18:37/q20/p1 Reasoned argument in religion is valid, and knowledge of God is not self-evident or necessary. — Al-Jishumī reports Abū ʿAlī's inference from the brother's argument and himself cites the companion's debating address as evidence. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي)]

## 18:37/q21 How does this exchange answer the rich man's boasts?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the exchange is read chiefly as a direct reply to the boasts or through the wider garden warning.
- 18:37/q21/p1 The believer addresses the man's assumed independence from God and his contempt for his poorer companion. — Al-Tabatabaʾi says 18:38 answers the first boast and the later comparison of gardens answers the second; Ibn ʿAshur says prior admonition met an arrogant reply. [2 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:37/q21/p2 The garden story warns that reliance on anything besides God ends in its loss. — Al-Biqāʿī describes two Israelites, one deceived by wealth and permanence and one reliant on God; the garden vanishes and leaves regret, since everything except God perishes. Iṣlāḥī says the parable mirrors Quraysh's arrogance and truth's contest with falsehood; the poor believer retains joy in God. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:37/q22 Is the garden dialogue presented as a historical event or a parable?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the unprefixed dialogue and later defeat are read as remembered history or the story as a parable.
- 18:37/q22/p1 It is more likely a historical event. — Ibn ʿAshur prefers this because the unprefixed dialogue and later account of the man's defeat resemble remembered events and sharpen the warning; he cites 18:37 and 18:43. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 18:37/q22/p2 It is treated as a parable reflecting Quraysh's arrogance. — Iṣlāḥī calls it a parable of outward and inward arrogance, with the poorer companion representing faith and joy in God. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:37/q23 How is the alif of lakinnā in the companion's following reply read?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the alif is permitted in connected recitation or functions as a pausal element only.
- 18:37/q23/p1 Some readers retain the alif both in connection and at pause. — Al-Ṭabrisī lists Ibn ʿĀmir, Ibn Fulayḥ, al-Barjamī, Yaʿqūb, and one form of Warsh; al-Qurṭubī lists Ibn ʿĀmir, al-Musayyilī from Nāfiʿ, and Ruways from Yaʿqūb, and reports poetic support. Abū Ḥātim calls ʿĀṣim's connected alif ungrammatical. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم,ابن عامر والمسيلي عن نافع ورويس عن يعقوب); TABRISI(أبو علي)-]
- 18:37/q23/p2 Most readers omit the alif in connection and keep it at pause. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports Qutaybah as an exception at pause; al-Qurṭubī says there is no disagreement about the pause alif. Abū ʿAlī rejects treating retained connected alif as a desirable replacement for lost hamza because it functions like a pausal letter. [3 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي)+]

## 18:37/q24 How is the form lakinnā in the following reply derived?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Which underlying word order and sound changes produce lakinnā, and whether its alif belongs to the pronoun or to pause.
- 18:37/q24/p1 It contracts lakin anā by removing hamza and assimilating the two nūns. — Al-Zajjāj derives it this way and notes some dialects retain anā's alif in connection; al-Naḥḥās attributes vowel transfer and hamza deletion to al-Kisāʾī, al-Farrāʾ, and al-Māzinī; Abū ʿUbayd also derives it from lakin anā, and al-Kisāʾī cites poetry. Al-Qurṭubī reports Ubayy's lakin anā huwa Allāhu rabbī. [5 notes: TABRISI(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم,أبو عبيد,النحاس)]
- 18:37/q24/p2 The underlying expression is reordered lakin Allāhu huwa rabbī anā, then contracted. — Al-Qurṭubī reports al-Kisāʾī's account: hamza and alif disappear and the two nūns assimilate. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي)]
- 18:37/q24/p3 The retained alif attaches to the pronoun as a fixed alif after nūn assimilation. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this as one grammatical account. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 18:37/q24/p4 The alif is a pausal extension comparable to a pausal hāʾ. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this alternative; Abū ʿAlī says the alif behaves like a pausal letter and disputes its use as compensation for a lost hamza in connected recitation, while al-Zajjāj offers that compensatory explanation. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)]

## 18:37/q25 What other forms of the companion's following reply are reported?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the reply expresses anā, huwa, or both, and whether lakinnā retains its long alif.
- 18:37/q25/p1 The reading lakin anā keeps the independent pronoun explicit. — Al-Ṭabrisī calls it anomalous and parses anā as sentence subject; al-Qurṭubī reports Ubayy's fuller lakin anā huwa Allāhu rabbī. [2 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حاتم)]
- 18:37/q25/p2 The reading lakin huwa Allāhu rabbī uses an explicit huwa. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports it as anomalous and explains huwa as the matter or story pronoun; al-Qurṭubī reports al-Kisāʾī's 'rather, the matter is: God is my Lord' with an understood noun. [2 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي)]
- 18:37/q25/p3 The reading lakinnā huwa Allāhu rabbī retains the long alif with huwa. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes it to Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī and Abū al-ʿĀliya. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي وأبو العالية)]

## 18:37/q26 What is the syntactic role of huwa in the following reply?  [* · grammar]
- 18:37/q26/p1 Huwa is a pronoun of matter or story; the statement means that God is my Lord. — Al-Ṭabrisī says rabbī supplies the return to the matter pronoun and cites 21:97 and 112:1; al-Qurṭubī compares 21:79 and the opening of al-Ikhlāṣ; al-Kisāʾī supplies an understood 'matter.' [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي)]

## 18:37/q27 What can the following refusal to associate anyone with God imply about the other man?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the refusal answers literal polytheism, mistaken attribution of wealth, or resurrection denial that diminishes divine power.
- 18:37/q27/p1 He was a polytheist. — Al-Qurṭubī infers this from the explicit refusal of shirk. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:37/q27/p2 He failed to recognize that both wealth and poverty come from God. — Al-Qurṭubī offers this as another implication of the reply. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:37/q27/p3 By denying resurrection he likened God to creation and implied divine powerlessness. — Al-Qurṭubī offers this as a further explanation of the contrast. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 18:37/q28 What other Qur'anic nūn form is noted with this material?  [* · sciences]
- 18:37/q28/p1 The light emphatic nūn is rare; one of its two Qur'anic occurrences is la-nasfaʿan bi-l-nāṣiya. — Al-Burhān states the count and cites this example. [1 notes: BURHAN]
