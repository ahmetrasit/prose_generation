# 68:43: 29 questions

## 68:43/q01 What is the state of their eyes?  [خَٰشِعَةً أَبْصَٰرُهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the description is outward abasement or an inward loss of perception.
- 68:43/q01/p1 Their gaze is downcast, humbled and unable to lift in shame or fear. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī link it to seeing the Fire and becoming sure of punishment; al-Jishumī adds fearful expectation; al-Kāshifī describes bowed heads; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a transfer from the posture of an abased person. Al-Biqāʿī says the eyes show the heart. [19 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشافي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:43/q01/p2 Their eyes are humbled and confused because the luminous faculty is lost. — Al-Qāshānī says they cannot look into the world of light or perceive its joyful radiance. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 68:43/q02 Why is humility assigned to the eyes?  [أَبْصَٰرُهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 68:43/q02/p1 The eyes visibly reveal the humility of the whole person. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn say all limbs are humbled; Bursawī says the heart is its source; al-Jishumī and al-Biqāʿī say the eyes show the inner condition. [8 notes: JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 68:43/q03 How is خَٰشِعَةً أَبْصَٰرُهُمْ constructed?  [خَٰشِعَةً أَبْصَٰرُهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether خاشعة is read as a circumstantial accusative or with أبصارهم as a nominal sentence.
- 68:43/q03/p1 خَٰشِعَةً is an accusative circumstantial qualifier, and أبصارهم is its nominative subject. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī attach the circumstance to their inability in 68:42; al-Nasafī, al-Samīn, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī relate it to those summoned. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts it as the first of two circumstances. [10 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 68:43/q03/p2 Both words may be nominative as subject and predicate. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this as an alternative to the accusative analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 68:43/q04 What does تَرْهَقُهُمْ mean?  [تَرْهَقُهُمْ · meaning]
- 68:43/q04/p1 It reaches, covers, overtakes or overwhelms them. — Bursawī explains rahq as one thing covering another; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds eager approach and compares 80:41. Al-Biqāʿī says the shame overpowers them. [15 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 68:43/q05 What is the humiliation that overtakes them?  [ذِلَّةٌۭ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the disgrace is described as outward shame, remorse, punishment, or inward attachment.
- 68:43/q05/p1 It is severe, visible disgrace and abasement. — Al-Jishumī says their submission becomes visible; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the soul's humiliation appears externally; Bursawī derives it from the root and dhalīl; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses that it is coercive. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 68:43/q05/p2 It is the shame of regret and grief at their refusal and inability. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies remorse; al-Rāzī likens them to a servant abandoned and shamed by his master; al-Biqāʿī traces shame to limbs spent on pleasures away from God; Ibn Kathīr calls it reversal of worldly arrogance. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 68:43/q05/p3 It comes from God's punishment when they see the Fire. — Al-Ṭabarī names divine punishment; Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī say their eyes lower on seeing the Fire or certainty of punishment. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل)]
- 68:43/q05/p4 It is inward attachment to lower things and bodily nature. — Al-Qāshānī describes stagnation in inferior passive states. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 68:43/q06 How is تَرْهَقُهُمْ ذِلَّةٌۭ related syntactically to the preceding description?  [تَرْهَقُهُمْ ذِلَّةٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is circumstantial or independent.
- 68:43/q06/p1 It is a second circumstantial clause describing those unable or summoned. — Al-Naḥḥās allows an accusative circumstantial clause; Ibn ʿĀshūr attaches it to the pronoun in 'they are unable'; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī counts two circumstantial clauses. [3 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 68:43/q06/p2 It can be detached from the preceding clause. — Al-Naḥḥās gives this alternative. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 68:43/q07 What happens to believers' and nonbelievers' faces after the prostration?  [خَٰشِعَةً أَبْصَٰرُهُمْ · reports]
- 68:43/q07/p1 Believers' faces shine white or bright; hypocrites' and disbelievers' faces blacken. — Al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī say whiter than snow and darker than pitch; al-Biqāʿī says brighter than the sun; Abū al-Layth includes Jews and Christians among those grieving with dark faces. [5 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو الليث)]

## 68:43/q08 Whom does the failed prostration expose?  [يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the failed test is aimed at hidden hypocrisy, open disbelief, or insincere worship.
- 68:43/q08/p1 It exposes hypocrites who stood among Muslims. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly prefers the allusion to hypocrites; reports repeatedly contrast true believers with hypocrites whose backs stiffen. Al-Qurṭubī says this report pattern is established in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim through Abū Saʿīd and others. [27 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود,قتادة); THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله); TABRISI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); ELMALILI(ابن مسعود); BINTSHATI(ابن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 68:43/q08/p2 It also includes unbelievers or polytheists who rejected God's summons. — Ibn ʿAbbās names unbelievers; Ibn Kathīr and the Turkish commentary name both disbelievers and hypocrites; al-Shawkānī's narration says even unbelievers wish to prostrate. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع بن أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî,Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN]
- 68:43/q08/p3 It includes people whose worldly prostrations were ostentatious or fearful. — Abū Saʿīd's Prophetic report contrasts willing, sincere prostration with fear or display; the Turkish commentary reports the ostentatious men and women cannot bend. [3 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 68:43/q09 What scene unfolds when people are summoned to prostrate on Judgment Day?  [يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in sequence, forty-year duration, accompanying signs, final destinations and named groups.
- 68:43/q09/p1 True worshippers recognize God at the disclosure and prostrate; hypocrites try but cannot. — Abū Saʿīd, Abū Hurayra, Ibn Masʿūd, Qatāda, and other transmitted accounts give the shared contrast; backs become rigid, flat, fused, or one bone. Al-Qurṭubī says its meaning occurs in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim; al-Dāraquṭnī and al-Ḥākim grade the Ibn Masʿūd account sound. [23 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله,قتادة); THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); TABRISI(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,الربيع بن أنس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); ELMALILI(Dârekutnî ve Hâkim,ابن مسعود); BINTSHATI(ابن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:43/q09/p2 After forty years of standing, worshippers follow false gods to Hell; sincere Muslims prostrate and hypocrites are sent to Hell. — Ibn Masʿūd and ʿAbdallāh report the forty years; al-Ṭabarī's accounts mention believers then enter Paradise and receive rewards; al-Thaʿlabī's version says the gathering is barefoot and naked; al-Qurṭubī's version includes a declaration of divine justice and compares 83:6. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود); THALABI(عبد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 68:43/q09/p3 After other worshippers are led away, believers recognize God by a sign; hypocrites fall backward, and survivors cross a bridge by varying lights. — Abū Hurayra names angels resembling ʿUzayr and Jesus and a prior settling of wrongs; his other report describes bridge speeds. Ibn Masʿūd's report says light varies by deeds and may flicker at the feet. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 68:43/q09/p4 Idol worshippers, Jews and Christians are separated before the remaining worshippers meet their Lord. — Abū Saʿīd's accounts say other groups follow their objects of worship; one al-Ṭabarī transmission merely recalls the coming described before; al-Ḥasan's report says Muslims remain to be tested. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 68:43/q09/p5 In Abū Mūsā's account, monotheists recognize the Lord when the veil lifts, and each is replaced by a Jew or Christian in Hell. — Abū Bardah swore three times his father transmitted it; ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz liked no report about monotheists more. Al-Qurṭubī states no grading there. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر بن عبد العزيز)]
- 68:43/q09/p6 Qatāda's account describes the unveiling as a horrendous event in which believers' prostration rebukes hypocrites. — Al-Ṭabarī also transmits a Prophetic form of the report; the specific stress is humiliation brought by believers' act. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 68:43/q10 Why can some people not prostrate then?  [يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether inability is bodily, the close of the time for worship, or a lost spiritual capacity.
- 68:43/q10/p1 Their backs or bodies are made rigid, fused or jointless, so they cannot bend. — Ibn Masʿūd says one bone without joints; Muqātil likens backs to iron spits; other reports say flat or joined spines and falling backward. Al-Rāzī says God removes their capacity to heighten regret. [30 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله,عبد الله بن مسعود); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,قتادة); ELMALILI(ابن مسعود); BINTSHATI(ابن مسعود); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BINTSHATI-]
- 68:43/q10/p2 The period of worship and obligation has ended, making the missed opportunity irreversible. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects accounts of lost joints, fused vertebrae, and rigidity after forty years as contrary to the text and context; she compares the belated wish in 89:23–25. [4 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 68:43/q10/p3 Their souls have lost the capacity to answer truth because of afflictions acquired after worldly life. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this as one account of what their former safety lacked. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 68:43/q11 Why are they called to prostrate on that Day?  [يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the scene is framed chiefly as test, rebuke, or permission for true worshippers.
- 68:43/q11/p1 The call tests and distinguishes sincere believers from hypocrites. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it honors the former and exposes the latter; Qatāda says believers' act humiliates hypocrites; al-Ḥasan says Muslims remain for testing. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); TABRISI(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:43/q11/p2 The call rebukes their earlier refusal and increases regret; it is no new legal duty. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Elmalılı and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ emphasize rebuke; Elmalılı rejects Abū Muslim's claim Resurrection is impossible for a summons because rebuke requires no legal command. Islahi says the summons exposes their lack of excuse. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 68:43/q11/p3 The summons gives sincere worshippers permission to prostrate. — The Prophetic report via Qatāda and Qatāda's own account say believers are allowed to prostrate; the rest cannot. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس)]

## 68:43/q12 When was the earlier summons to prostrate?  [وَقَدْ كَانُوا۟ يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the warning's immediate scene is Resurrection, life's end, or worldly incapacity.
- 68:43/q12/p1 It was during worldly life, when response was possible. — Al-Ṭabarī explicitly says both the summons and safety refer to worldly life; Ibn ʿAbbās contrasts former security with present fear. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the clause parenthetic recollection. Al-Qushayrī says recalling the old call makes proof decisive and regret sharper. [35 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع بن أنس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:43/q12/p2 The warning may also meet them at the end of life or beginning of the next. — Al-Qushayrī leaves both boundaries possible. Abū Muslim proposes the last day of a life, when angels bring no good news and late conversion fails, with 25:22 and 6:158; another explanation has a dying patient unable to rise. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; ELMALILI(أبو مسلم)]
- 68:43/q12/p3 It can describe illness, old age or incapacity while still in this life. — Abū Muslim proposes bodily incapacity following prior soundness; Elmalılı permits this worldly reading but rejects exclusion of Resurrection. [2 notes: ELMALILI(أبو مسلم)]

## 68:43/q13 What does the earlier call to prostrate ask of them?  [إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sujūd denotes bodily prostration, prayer, all obedience, or inward surrender.
- 68:43/q13/p1 It asks for the prescribed ritual prayers, represented by their prostration. — Ibrāhīm al-Taymī, al-Shaʿbī, al-Nakhaʿī and Qatāda identify obligatory prayer; some specify all five. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr identifies the summons to congregational prayer. Bursawī says prayer contains prostrations. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī dismisses this reading. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(إبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل وإبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي والشعبي,ابن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,كعب الحبر); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير,النخعي والشعبي); STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 68:43/q13/p2 It asks for literal prostration to God and worship of Him. — Al-Ṭabarī says no impediment then; the call was freely rejected despite ability. Some accounts make worldly sincere prostration the condition for prostration on Judgment Day. [20 notes: MUJAHID(أبو سعيد الخدري); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 68:43/q13/p3 Prostration stands for all acts of obedience or the summons of religious obligation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports some interpreters: prostration is greatest obedience and the later test. Al-Qurṭubī says the explanations are close; al-Ālūsī gives the wider obligation reading; Abū Jaʿfar and Abū ʿAbdallāh speak of obeying commands and avoiding prohibitions. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); TABRISI(أبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 68:43/q13/p4 It means inward surrender and readiness to receive the world of light. — Al-Qāshānī says their faculties and readiness were still present, allowing future happiness. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 68:43/q13/p5 It summons people to worship God alone in submission to His lordship. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the parenthesis recalls this; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links afterlife felicity to worldly acknowledgment of God's sole lordship. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 68:43/q14 How was the earlier summons delivered?  [يُدْعَوْنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wording points to the public prayer call, wider divine and prophetic commands, or the afterlife caller.
- 68:43/q14/p1 Through the adhān and iqāma, especially 'Come to success' or 'Come to prayer'. — Ibrāhīm al-Taymī names adhān and iqāma; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says they heard 'Come to success' yet did not come; Ibn ʿAbbās gives hearing and ignoring the call; Bursawī also cites scholars' invitations. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); THALABI(إبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل وإبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي,سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وابن جبير)]
- 68:43/q14/p2 Through God's command, the Prophet, prophets, religious teachers, or the imposed religious duty. — Bursawī reports divine commands to establish prayer and Prophetic invitation; the Prophet's instruction to pray five times, fast, give zakāt and obey promised Paradise. He cites the report that a servant is nearest God in prostration; al-Nasafī says prophets called; al-Biqāʿī says callers to God. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي,بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 68:43/q14/p3 By a caller who invites prostration on Resurrection Day. — Mujāhid and Qatāda describe that caller; the summons publicly exposes hypocrites. [1 notes: TABRISI(مجاهد)]

## 68:43/q15 What does سَٰلِمُونَ mean here?  [وَهُمْ سَٰلِمُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether safety refers to bodily health, freedom from fear, or inward capacity.
- 68:43/q15/p1 They were healthy, physically sound and able, without impediment to prostration. — Al-Ṭabarī says no obstacle; Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts their then flexible backs with later fused vertebrae; al-Biqāʿī says arrogance rather than weakness stopped them; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines salāma as absence of defects and disease. Elmalılı stresses safety and freedom. [32 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:43/q15/p2 They were free from fear, unlike the fearful summons now. — Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly contrasts worldly security with present fear; al-Ṭabarī's link to 11:20 describes their inability to heed in both lives. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI]
- 68:43/q15/p3 Their faculties and readiness for inward felicity were intact. — Al-Qāshānī says they were able to receive the influx of the world of light and gain happiness. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 68:43/q15/p4 They were free of the afflictions that later destroyed the soul's capacity to answer truth. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī offers this before the simpler physical-ability interpretation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 68:43/q16 What is the grammar of وَهُمْ سَٰلِمُونَ?  [وَهُمْ سَٰلِمُونَ · grammar]
- 68:43/q16/p1 It is a waw-marked circumstantial clause describing those summoned in worldly life. — Al-Samīn gives its subject as the subject of the second yudʿawn; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the waw marks the circumstance. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:43/q17 What warning about congregational prayer is drawn from the verse?  [إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · law]
turns on: Whether the warning targets attendance at congregation specifically or neglect of prescribed prayer generally.
- 68:43/q17/p1 The verse warns those who hear the congregational prayer call but fail to attend. — Kaʿb swears that it was revealed specifically about absentees; al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and Ibn al-Jawzī apply the warning. Al-Rabīʿ ibn Khuthaym came despite paralysis, urging even crawling; Ibn al-Musayyib answered despite a threat from Ṭāriq. Al-Qurṭubī points to his earlier discussion of congregational prayer's obligation. [18 notes: THALABI(كعب الأحبار); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(كعب الأحبار); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن المسيب,ابن جبير,فلج الربيع بن خثيم); TABRISI(الربيع بن خثيم,كعب الأحبار); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(كعب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن خيثم,سعيد بن المسيب,سعيد بن جبير,كعب الأحبار); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير)]
- 68:43/q17/p2 The verse concerns the five obligatory prayers when their call is made. — Kaʿb's version in al-Durr names five prayers; other authorities identify prescribed prayer and its call. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); THALABI(إبراهيم التيمي); BAGHAWI-FULL(إبراهيم التيمي); DURR-FULL(قتادة,كعب الحبر); BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 68:43/q18 Why repeat the summons to prostrate explicitly?  [وَقَدْ كَانُوا۟ يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · rhetoric]
- 68:43/q18/p1 The explicit wording in place of a pronoun emphasizes the earlier summons. — Bursawī also allows that the wording marks prostration as standing for prayer and its prostrations; al-Ālūsī agrees on emphasis. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 68:43/q19 Why single out prostration?  [ٱلسُّجُودِ · rhetoric]
- 68:43/q19/p1 It is the greatest act of obedience and serves as the decisive afterlife test. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes the figurative reading to some interpreters; Bursawī states the ranking directly. A Prophetic report cited by Bursawī says the servant is closest to the Lord while prostrating. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المتأولين); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 68:43/q20 What unspoken response follows the earlier summons?  [وَقَدْ كَانُوا۟ يُدْعَوْنَ إِلَى ٱلسُّجُودِ · rhetoric]
- 68:43/q20/p1 Their refusal to answer is omitted because the context makes it evident. — Al-Ālūsī states the ellipsis; many explanations explicitly say they refused though capable. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL]

## 68:43/q21 How do the two verses pair ability and inability?  [وَهُمْ سَٰلِمُونَ · rhetoric]
- 68:43/q21/p1 The passage uses iḥtibāk: present inability implies omitted former ability, and former safety implies omitted present unsafety. — Al-Biqāʿī states both halves of the correspondence. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 68:43/q22 What moral reversal does their condition teach?  [* · interpretation]
- 68:43/q22/p1 Their former arrogance and refusal return as helplessness, shame and regret. — Ibn Kathīr calls it punishment opposite to worldly crimes; Elmalılı says they thought sinners wiser than believers and now desire what they refused; Islahi says their own bodies testify to lack of excuse; Study Quran says some Arabs deemed prostration humiliating but meet greater humiliation. Al-Ṭabarī invokes 11:20 for inability in both lives. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:43/q22/p2 The disclosure brings joy and honor to Muslims but dread and humiliation to criminals. — Elmalılı stresses this opposed experience; the face-color reports portray the same reversal. [5 notes: ELMALILI; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 68:43/q23 What links this scene to other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes offer different scriptural parallels for the scene.
- 68:43/q23/p1 11:20 parallels their failure to hear or see in life and to prostrate now. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly draw the link. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 68:43/q23/p2 70:43–44 parallels emergence from graves with downcast eyes and ignominy. — Islahi says the parallel clarifies the exposed-shin Day as severe upheaval; Muqātil connects lowered eyes on seeing the Fire to al-Qalam. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; MUQATIL; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:43/q23/p3 89:23–25 parallels the sinner's useless late wish to have prepared for true life. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ connects that wish to the missed time for prostration. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 68:43/q23/p4 83:6 is invoked for the long standing before God. — The report to ʿUmar names forty years of standing. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 68:43/q24 How does this passage fit its surrounding argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources emphasize different surrounding themes.
- 68:43/q24/p1 It interrupts the argument about alleged partners to show that only worldly submission to God's sole lordship yields afterlife happiness. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the alleged partners cannot provide felicity or intercession at Resurrection. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 68:43/q24/p2 It depicts a cataclysmic Day that overturns dreams of worldly luxury. — Islahi places the refusal to prostrate within that reversal and reads 70:43–44 as its parallel. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 68:43/q25 What accounts for the Qur'an's inimitable expression?  [* · rhetoric]
- 68:43/q25/p1 Its eloquent wording, exact placement, sound meanings and ordered joining of diverse excellences exceed human capacity. — Al-Khaṭṭābī says substitution damages meaning or style; al-Burhān and al-Itqān enumerate doctrine, worship, law, ethics, exhortation, past and future reports, and proof joined to conclusion. They say only divine knowledge unites these qualities. [12 notes: KHATTABI; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 68:43/q26 How did opponents characterize the Qur'an despite its effect on them?  [* · reports]
- 68:43/q26/p1 They called it poetry, sorcery or dictated ancient legends while acknowledging its sweetness. — Al-Khaṭṭābī and later compilations say poetry was inferred from order and sorcery from inability to match it; the dictated-lore charge conflicted with knowledge that Muhammad was unlettered and had no nearby dictation helper, compared with 25:5. [5 notes: KHATTABI; BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 68:43/q27 What does the report about al-Walīd's deliberation say?  [* · reports]
- 68:43/q27/p1 After reflection, al-Walīd called the Qur'an human speech or sorcery. — Al-Khaṭṭābī tentatively identifies him; the Qur'an recounts his thought and claims in 74:18, 74:24–25. Al-Burhān says frustration and stubbornness drove the claim. [2 notes: KHATTABI; BURHAN(الوليد بن المغيرة المخزومي)]

## 68:43/q28 Why do near-synonyms matter in Qur'anic wording?  [* · meaning]
- 68:43/q28/p1 Words that overlap retain distinct meanings, so their exact placement matters. — Al-Khaṭṭābī lists knowledge/recognition, praise/gratitude, and stinginess/miserliness; he says maʿrifa takes one object, ʿilm two, and one says 'recognize God' but 'know God' with an attribute such as just or powerful. [4 notes: KHATTABI]

## 68:43/q29 How did the practice of combining readings in one completion develop?  [* · readings]
- 68:43/q29/p1 Early readers completed one transmission at a time; combining readings in one completion became established later. — Ibn al-Jazarī says earlier authorities largely omitted the topic; al-Ṣafrawī only hinted at it. He attributes omission to intensive repeated study: al-Ḥuṣrī reportedly made ninety completions over ten years, al-Kattānī was kept with ʿĀṣim by Ibn Mujāhid, and al-Wāsiṭī spent years on multiple completions. Some imams disliked combination as novel, but it became accepted. [7 notes: NASHR(أبو الحسن علي بن عبد الغني الحصري القيرواني,أبو الفتح فرج بن عمر الواسطي,أبو حفص الكتاني,بعض الأئمة)]
