# 50:16: 30 questions

## 50:16/q01 What does God's creation of the human mean?  [خَلَقْنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether خلقنا refers only to initial formation or to continuing dependence and renewal.
- 50:16/q01/p1 God brought the human into ordered, wise existence. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya defines creation as ordered, measured, wise bringing into being. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 50:16/q01/p2 Human creation and existence are continuously renewed and dependent on God. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes ongoing changing existence; the present knowledge clause and enduring nearness accompany it. Būrsawī says Sufis and theologians posit renewed substances and accidents; the Mathnawī images recurring waves and al-Shaʿrānī distinguishes changing attributes from entities. Būrsawī applies this to reports of the Kaaba turning toward Rābiʿa, whose truth requires unveiling. [7 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI(المثنوي)]

## 50:16/q02 Why is human creation stated so emphatically here?  [وَلَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the emphasis chiefly grounds knowledge of inward states or the resurrection argument.
- 50:16/q02/p1 The emphatic assertion supports the following claim to know hidden human states. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says lām and qad anticipate the coordinated knowledge clause: people acknowledge creation but fail to grasp God's knowledge. Elmalılı hears an oath by divine majesty and says creation grounds the inference to knowledge and guards the Creator–creature distinction. Islāhī argues that the Creator must know every detail of His creatures, including thoughts before words and deeds, since otherwise He could not arrange their protection and sustenance; he cites the question at 67:14. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 50:16/q02/p2 The reminder of first creation establishes the power to recreate people. — Al-Kāshifī says Meccan polytheists admitted the first creation without matter or aid, so resurrection from matter is possible despite Satanic doubt. Islāhī links creative power and knowledge to resurrection. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 50:16/q03 Who is ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ here?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular is generic or singles out Adam before generalization.
- 50:16/q03/p1 Humanity generally, especially the addressed polytheists and Adam's descendants. — Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī and Study Quran take a generic human; Ibn ʿĀshūr says polytheists are the immediate audience (50:19–22). Al-Dāmghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī compare other generic uses, including 76:2. Al-Jawzī says the word has many Qurʾanic referents. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 50:16/q03/p2 Adam specifically, with the lesson then extending to his descendants. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī report this alternative; Qurṭubī and Study Quran identify his inner prompting as eating from the forbidden tree. Būrsawī says Adam is included and Satan prompted the thought. Ibn al-Jawzī cites other Adam verses, 23:12, 55:14 and 76:1. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 50:16/q04 How is the word ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ explained linguistically?  [ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · meaning]
turns on: The competing etymologies appeal to familiarity, visibility, or forgetfulness and differ over morphology.
- 50:16/q04/p1 It is a singular noun for a human, with nās and anāsī as plurals. — Ibn al-Jawzī calls it indeclinable in his account. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 50:16/q04/p2 It derives from intimacy or familiarity with one's kind. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this proposed etymology. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 50:16/q04/p3 It derives from visibility or perception. — Ibn Qutayba connects it with anastu, 'I saw' (20:10); Basran grammarians analyze it as faʿlān and explain the diminutive's added yāʾ by analogy with layla. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,البصريون)]
- 50:16/q04/p4 It derives from forgetting a covenant. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to connect the name to a forgotten covenant; linguist exegetes point to the diminutive unaysiyān as restoring a dropped yāʾ. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن عباس,قوم من المفسرين من أهل اللغة)]

## 50:16/q05 What does نَفْسُهُۥ designate?  [نَفْسُهُۥ · referent]
- 50:16/q05/p1 The person's heart or inward self. — Muqātil explicitly calls the whispering self the heart; al-Dāmghānī gives 'heart' as a sense of nafs, comparing 17:25. Other glosses describe the heart speaking inwardly. [5 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 50:16/q06 What does تُوَسْوِسُ mean?  [تُوَسْوِسُ · meaning]
- 50:16/q06/p1 It is faint, hidden, inward speech or sound, extended to thoughts. — Māwardī calls it abundant indistinct secret inner talk and cites Ruʾba. Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares faint jewelry noise and says it often concerns what is not good; Qurṭubī cites al-Aʿshā's jewelry line. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Būrsawī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give the quiet-sound or jewelry sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr says metaphorical waswās and waswasa cover varied thoughts, estimates and intentions better than narrower terms. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]

## 50:16/q07 What is included in the soul's whisper here?  [مَا تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish the broad range of inner activity from specific moral, historical, and inward applications.
- 50:16/q07/p1 All private inward speech, thoughts, feelings, intentions and secrets. — Ṭabarī and al-Thaʿlabī say nothing in the heart is hidden. Zajjāj stresses concealment; al-Wāḥidī compares 7:20; Bayḍāwī and Nasafī include whatever comes to mind. Elmalılı lists feelings, imaginings, memories, fancies and decisions; Study Quran includes intentions behind acts and words even unknown to oneself, citing many knowledge verses. Ibn Kathīr includes good and evil promptings; Ibn ʿĀshūr's wording covers thoughts, estimates and intentions. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj,most commentators)]
- 50:16/q07/p2 It particularly includes appetites, corrupt traits and beliefs that trouble the heart. — Qushayrī lists display before people, bad character and rancor; Būrsawī adds appetite and corrupt belief. Qurṭubī treats the encompassing warning as against secret sin. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 50:16/q07/p3 In the setting of denied resurrection, it includes doubt that scattered remains can be raised. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī states the skeptic's doubt about dust; al-Biqāʿī says deniers inwardly know God's power, the Qurʾan's truth and the Messenger's veracity but speak denial from envy, rivalry, pride and ambition. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 50:16/q07/p4 On the Adam reading, it includes his inclination to eat from the tree. — Qurṭubī and Study Quran report this; Būrsawī attributes Adam's thought to Satanic prompting. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 50:16/q07/p5 One inward account assigns each person exactly 70,000 thoughts daily. — Būrsawī reports gnostics comparing them to the 70,000 angels entering the Frequented House: each thought yields a fitting angel that ascends and intercedes; hearts stay inhabited. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 50:16/q08 Why is whispering assigned to the self?  [تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the named self is read chiefly as an epistemic focus or in light of Satanic access to inner thoughts.
- 50:16/q08/p1 The subject is the human's innermost thought, which illustrates God's encompassing knowledge. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Satan also whispers, but the self is named because God's knowledge of the human is at issue. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 50:16/q08/p2 Prophetic inward selves are shielded from Satan even when he approaches outward senses. — Būrsawī reports gnostics that prophets do not act on such suggestions; he distinguishes saints, who may be protected from suggestions but whose hearts remain reachable, because prophets bear revealed law. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 50:16/q09 How do مَا and بِهِۦ work grammatically?  [مَا بِهِۦ · grammar]
turns on: The antecedent of بِهِۦ follows whether مَا denotes the whispered thing or the act of whispering.
- 50:16/q09/p1 Mā is relative; the pronoun in bihi returns to what the self whispers. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās allow the return to mā; Bayḍāwī likens bāʾ to 'a sound in something.' Ālūsī attaches it to tawaswasa and calls accompaniment or redundancy weak. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes bāʾ as extra for emphasis, comparing 5:6; Ṭabāṭabāʾī takes it as instrument or cause. [8 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 50:16/q09/p2 Mā is a verbal noun; the pronoun returns to the human. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Naḥḥās give the human alternative. Bayḍāwī says bāʾ transitivizes the verb; Būrsawī says it makes the person experience whispering and reports al-Kashshāf's idiom 'a person told himself' alongside 'his self told him.' Ālūsī cites Labīd's speaking-self line. [7 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكشاف); ALUSI-FULL(لبيد)]

## 50:16/q10 How can a pronoun be repeated or followed by another pronoun?  [بِهِۦ · grammar]
- 50:16/q10/p1 A separate pronoun may confirm or stand in apposition to an attached pronoun, subject to its case and construction. — Sībawayh gives بك أنت and رأيتك أنت, distinguishes this from ordinary adjectival description, and uses إياك/إياه for accusative and أنت/هو for nominative apposition. Pronouns do not adjectivally describe overt nouns; an overt noun may take pronominal apposition as an independent restatement. He finds مررت به وبزيد هما awkward and repeats the preposition: مررت به وبزيد بهما. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 50:16/q11 What does divine knowledge of the whisper establish?  [وَنَعْلَمُ مَا تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ over whether the emphasis merely establishes unlimited divine knowledge or also contrasts it with the angels' limited record.
- 50:16/q11/p1 No inward or outward state is hidden from the Creator, even before its thought emerges. — Ṭabarī, Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī and Ṭabrisī stress concealed thoughts. Al-Biqāʿī adds future thoughts before emergence; Būrsawī says God knows before creation essentially and after it actually, and creates thoughts; Ibn ʿĀshūr says knowledge of inner speech entails knowledge of what earth removes from corpses. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts God's direct knowledge with angelic knowledge of acts; Islāhī connects it to resurrection. Islāhī infers complete knowledge of human thoughts, words and deeds from God's creation of humans; he argues that provision and protection require such detailed knowledge and cites 67:14. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 50:16/q11/p2 God knows passing thoughts which recording angels cannot perceive or write. — Elmalılı says angels do not know these hidden thoughts, although God created them; the following context confirms this distinction. He distinguishes unsettled thoughts from intention, resolve and decision. [4 notes: ELMALILI]

## 50:16/q12 What is the significance of the shift from خَلَقْنَا to وَنَعْلَمُ?  [خَلَقْنَا وَنَعْلَمُ · rhetoric]
- 50:16/q12/p1 The past creation and present knowledge express continually renewed, unlimited knowledge and warn about hidden intentions. — Ibn ʿĀshūr states this contrast. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says creation itself continues through changing existence and the present clause indicates continuous knowledge. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 50:16/q13 Are unspoken whispers morally recorded or punished?  [مَا تُوَسْوِسُ بِهِۦ نَفْسُهُۥ · law]
- 50:16/q13/p1 Passing inward whispers are pardoned unless spoken or acted upon, and unsettled thoughts are not written. — Ibn Kathīr cites a sound prophetic report of pardon for the Muslim community. Elmalılı distinguishes passing thoughts from intention, resolve and decision in the angelic record. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 50:16/q14 What does God's being nearer than the jugular mean?  [وَنَحْنُ أَقْرَبُ إِلَيْهِ مِنْ حَبْلِ ٱلْوَرِيدِ · theology]
turns on: The dispute turns on whether the first-person plural names God's knowledge or power, both attributes, His angels, or a deeper nonspatial relation; the following angels and the prior knowledge clause are key contextual evidence.
- 50:16/q14/p1 God's knowledge and witness encompass the person more intimately than any physical closeness. — Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, Muqātil, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr make this reading. Bodily parts or flesh conceal one another or the vein, but nothing screens God. Bayḍāwī's close observer and Elmalılı's omitted 'whoever' explain the comparator; one possible observer is the person himself. Al-Itqān applies the rule of interpreting impossible literal attributes by their implication. The image is not spatial presence. [48 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(غير مسمى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(الإمام فخر الدين); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Bazıları,Fahr-ı Râzî,Kādî Beyzâvî ile Ebussuud,Sâhib-i Keşşâf); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 50:16/q14/p2 God has greater power, mastery and command over the person than the vein. — Reported by Arabic interpreters, Akhfash, Māwardī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī and Elmalılı. Ibn ʿAṭiyya stresses dominion and grasp without bodily proximity; Rāzī compares command in the person with blood moving through veins. Qurṭubī contrasts the person's control of the vein; al-Ḍaḥḥāk and an Abū Saʿīd prophetic report assert the comparison, the latter joining it to God intervening between heart and person and holding forelocks. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية); NAHHAS(الأخفش); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(غير مسمى); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك,رسول الله); ELMALILI(Bazıları,Fahr-ı Râzî)]
- 50:16/q14/p3 The clause conveys knowledge and power together, with care and effects such as kindness or wrath. — Thaʿlabī and Qushayrī join knowledge and power; Muqātil as quoted by Qurṭubī combines both. Elmalılı prefers the combination: power entails knowledge, and the added nearness clause goes beyond the preceding knowledge clause. He weighs the proposed bar on combining metaphors against context cues and says a representative metaphor can unite them. Islāhī says God's knowledge and power encompass each person, both seen and unseen. [12 notes: THALABI; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ELMALILI; ELMALILI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 50:16/q14/p4 The near ones are God's angels placed near the human, rather than divine indwelling. — Ibn Kathīr argues from 56:85, where unseen nearness to the dying is angelic, and 15:9, where 'We sent down' acts through angels. God enables angelic prompting alongside satanic prompting; the Prophet likened Satan's movement to blood. Study Quran reports this reading, while noting interpreters seek to avoid literal divine nearness. Ibn Kathīr says the wording does not demand a mere knowledge reading. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK)]
- 50:16/q14/p5 God is nearer in perception than the jugular would be if it perceived. — Ṭabrisī reports this further gloss. [1 notes: TABRISI(غير مسمى)]
- 50:16/q14/p6 The nearness is a real, abiding ontological relation in which each created self and faculty depends on God. — Qāshānī rejects spatial conjunction but says particular existence stands only through divine existence; he cites ʿAlī that God is with things without conjunction. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God mediates between soul and itself and actions (8:24), while the familiar image only approximates greater nearness. Ibn ʿArabī, as Elmalılı reports, places the Near One above bodily life channels, makes faculties dependent on God, and describes nearness with distance; devotional nearness through prescribed acts makes God the servant's hearing and sight without identity. Study Quran reads divine immanence throughout creation (2:115, 57:4). Elmalılı accepts essential and active nearness yet insists creation, divine acts, and the servant remain distinct, citing 37:96 and 8:17; unity language must not become union. He says God's encompassing cannot be spatial, and common exegesis uses attributes to prevent that impression. [34 notes: QASHANI(أمير المؤمنين عليه السلام); TABATABAI; ELMALILI(Âlûsî,Şeyh Muhyiddîn-i Arabî); STUDYQURAN]
- 50:16/q14/p7 God remains more powerful and knowledgeable than the two recording angels when they arrive. — Study Quran reports a reading tied to 50:17: God needs no angels to know acts. Elmalılı suggests the omitted close comparator could be the recording angels, whom God exceeds in nearness. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Z); ELMALILI]

## 50:16/q15 What is the jugular or warīd named in the comparison?  [حَبْلِ ٱلْوَرِيدِ · referent]
turns on: The sources locate the vessel in the neck, heart, or branching network, sometimes treating these as names for connected parts.
- 50:16/q15/p1 A vein or pair of veins in the throat or sides of the neck. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās identify throat or neck; Ibn Qutayba and Ṭabarī place it between throat and upper chest or collarbones. Zajjāj cites a poet's dual وريداه; al-Thaʿlabī cites a swelling vein in poetry and plural awrida. Abū al-Haytham describes pulsing vessels beside the throat notch and a life vessel carrying breath rather than blood; al-Layth says anger swells the pair. Farrāʾ, Abū ʿUbayda and Zajjāj locate it within the neck; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī note right and left veins. Zamakhsharī connects the pair to the watīn. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies an artery from the left heart chamber, the aorta and its carotid branches; Elmalılı gives competing accounts of venous and arterial blood, the two circulations, and says their colors are only general labels. Islāhī calls warīd the life vein. [47 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الهيثم,الفراء وأبو عبيدة والزجاج,الليث); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الفراء); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,العراء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); ELMALILI(Nizamüddîn Nîsâbûrî,Zemahşerî,İbn Esîr); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 50:16/q15/p2 A vessel connected to the heart, called the watīn or vital heart cord. — Al-Ḥasan and al-Aṣamm give the heart vessel; Muqātil says it reaches the heart. Ibn ʿAbbās is also reported as calling it the heart's vital cord. Rāghib describes a liver–heart vessel carrying spirit or blood, for which some compare God to a person's spirit. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن والأصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); ELMALILI(Râgıb)]
- 50:16/q15/p3 A blood-carrying vein or vessel that branches throughout the body. — Language scholars in Wāḥidī name upper arm, forearm and hand branches; Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe distribution. Al-Athram names watīn in the heart, abhar in the back, akḥal/nasā in arm/thigh and aslam or sulaym in the little finger. Elmalılı allows the generic singular to denote the entire vein network or vena cava and explains blood returning to the heart. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأثرم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض العلماء); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأثرم); SAMIN-DURR(الأثرم); ALUSI-FULL(الأثرم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 50:16/q16 Why is the vessel called ٱلْوَرِيدِ?  [ٱلْوَرِيدِ · meaning]
- 50:16/q16/p1 Its name relates to arrival or passage of life or spirit through it. — Bayḍāwī reports that spirit goes to it; Zamakhsharī says spirit arrives there from the head. Samīn and Ālūsī allow active or passive participle readings, the arriving vessel or the one arrived at. Elmalılı says the vessel gathers blood toward the heart. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 50:16/q17 What does حَبْلِ mean here and in cited usage?  [حَبْلِ · meaning]
turns on: Context selects the bodily cord here from the word's wider rope and bond senses.
- 50:16/q17/p1 Here it denotes a bodily cord or vessel resembling an ordinary rope. — Ibn al-Jawzī defines ordinary ḥabl as twisted fiber, cotton or wool and includes neck vein as a Qurʾanic sense; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes thick bodily vessels. Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say the vessel is called a cord by resemblance; Elmalılı gives rope, bond and vessel senses. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 50:16/q17/p2 Elsewhere ḥabl can denote a covenant, safe-conduct or the Qurʾan. — Ibn al-Jawzī says a covenant is a rope one holds to reach a goal (3:112); al-Aʿshā uses plural ḥibāl for guarantees or safety. The literal rope appears at 26:44 and 111:5; the Qurʾan as a bond appears at 3:103. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,الأعشى)]

## 50:16/q18 How does the annexation حَبْلِ ٱلْوَرِيدِ work?  [حَبْلِ ٱلْوَرِيدِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the genitive identifies the same vessel, specifies a member of a class, or relates neighboring cords.
- 50:16/q18/p1 It names one vessel twice; the second, specific name explains the first general name. — Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give identity or explanatory annexation. They compare differing names, 50:9's 'grain of harvest,' مسجد الجامع and shajar al-arāk; Elmalılı allows a rope-like simile. [14 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 50:16/q18/p2 Ḥabl is a broad category specified by the kind warīd, rather than self-annexation. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly rejects calling this a thing added to itself; Samīn reports a comparable specification of a broad term, as in بعير سانية. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 50:16/q18/p3 It can mean the cord belonging to or near the vein in the same neck region. — Samīn reports a possessive analysis like مسجد الجامع or a neck cord added to the vein because both share an organ. Abū Ḥayyān offers a shoulder cord in the jugular's region; Elmalılı reports Zamakhsharī's base-of-neck sense, allowing a lāmī genitive and neck or body metaphor. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI(Zemahşerî)]

## 50:16/q19 Why compare divine nearness to the jugular?  [مِنْ حَبْلِ ٱلْوَرِيدِ · rhetoric]
- 50:16/q19/p1 It is a familiar image of the closest bodily connection, made vivid by a hidden vessel. — Qushayrī calls it the nearest part to oneself; Bayḍāwī cites death nearer than the vein. Biqāʿī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı call it a proverbial image; Dhu al-Rumma uses it poetically. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the vessel is both intimate and unnoticed, making unseen knowledge intelligible, and judges it better than midwife-seat or belt images; he cites Zuhayr's hand-mouth and Ḥanẓala's sandal-strap comparisons. Elmalılı says it evokes the closest vessel or life itself; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the image approximates a greater nearness. Islāhī says the life vein is a common Arabic idiom for extreme nearness. [13 notes: QUSHAYRI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ذو الرمة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(حنظلة بن ثعلبة بن سيار العجلي,زهير); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 50:16/q20 What is the syntax of وَنَعْلَمُ and وَنَحْنُ أَقْرَبُ?  [وَنَعْلَمُ وَنَحْنُ أَقْرَبُ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical account of how wāw introduces the circumstantial clauses.
- 50:16/q20/p1 وَنَعْلَمُ has an omitted subject, 'and We know'; its nominal clause is circumstantial, as is the nearness clause. — Samīn says a positive imperfect directly joined to wāw cannot itself be the circumstantial clause; the nominal analysis supplies it. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 50:16/q20/p2 The nearness clause is circumstantial to the pronoun in وَنَعْلَمُ and reinforces continuous knowledge. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats it as a confirming ḥāl. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 50:16/q21 Why assign two recording angels if God knows everything?  [وَنَعْلَمُ وَنَحْنُ أَقْرَبُ · interpretation]
- 50:16/q21/p1 Their record establishes evidence against each person despite God's immediate knowledge. — Al-Wāḥidī and Ṭabrisī state this purpose; Islāhī says God secured the record of deeds and utterances. Elmalılı distinguishes thoughts unseen by angels from what they record; Study Quran says God remains capable of knowing acts without them. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Z)]

## 50:16/q22 How does the verse fit its neighboring argument?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the verse both to the resurrection proof and to the theological safeguard supplied by creation.
- 50:16/q22/p1 It answers resurrection deniers by moving from creation and God's knowledge to recording, death and requital. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says these verses prove resurrection and requital. Rāzī offers both a fresh proof after 50:15 (earlier creation as heavens) and completion of human first creation; he also sees a warning to recant hidden claims. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to 50:4 and the angels from 50:17, and to knowledge of what earth removes from the dead. Ṭabāṭabāʾī links 50:6 and 50:15; Islāhī links knowledge and creative power to resurrection and the record. Study Quran describes the sūra's arc from rejection through creation and past peoples to death, afterlife and reminder. Islāhī treats doubt about resurrection and doubt that hidden or open deeds can be recorded as two sides of one objection briefly answered at 50:4. He says creation and knowledge of thoughts answer the latter doubt; the ensuing passage moves through recording words and deeds to Judgment, punishment and the righteous person's blessings. [16 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 50:16/q22/p2 The reminder of creation keeps Creator and creature distinct when nearness is discussed. — Elmalılı says this is why creation precedes nearness; the created human and its acts remain God's work without becoming God. [5 notes: ELMALILI]

## 50:16/q23 What responses should awareness of divine nearness evoke?  [* · inward]
- 50:16/q23/p1 It produces fear and awe in some, tranquility and intimacy in others, and calls for obedience and remembrance. — Qushayrī gives the contrasting effects; Biqāʿī repeats him. Al-Sulamī reports that al-Kharrāz describes travelers reached by God without seeking or flight, and al-Wāsiṭī grounds nearness in creation, gathering and spirit and says self-knowledge comes through God in a measure humans can bear. Another report says awareness leads to obedience and remembrance in public and private. Study Quran says people can remain distant from God despite His constant nearness. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); SULAMI(أبو سعيد الخراز,الواسطي,بعضهم); STUDYQURAN]

## 50:16/q24 How is the vocabulary of nearness used beyond spatial distance?  [* · meaning]
- 50:16/q24/p1 Qurb can indicate time, relation, honor, benefit, care or power as well as place. — Elmalılı cites Rāghib's range across many verses, including 2:186, 34:37 and 7:56. He says the verse allows power and knowledge, while spatial interval would contradict divine encompassing. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Râgıb)]

## 50:16/q25 Why can a speaker call the near God with the distant vocative يَا?  [* · rhetoric]
- 50:16/q25/p1 The caller feels humility and remoteness from divine favor despite God's nearness. — Al-Burhān notes that يَا normally calls someone distant. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 50:16/q26 What does the cited love poem's figurative language mean?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The poetic alternatives turn on whether aʿshār evokes shattered pieces or the ten shares of gaming arrows.
- 50:16/q26/p1 Its command to the heart means a command to the speaker, not a command to a literally acting heart. — Bāqillānī rejects literal heart-command as impossible; he calls the first line weak because self-deprecation would justify abandonment despite the lover's claim to deserve attachment, while the second hemistich is unusually intimate. [3 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 50:16/q26/p2 The garment image can stand for the body, or for separation and abandonment. — Bāqillānī reports the body reading by comparison with 74:4. Abū ʿUbayda says tansal means to part. [2 notes: BAQILLANI(أبو عبيدة)]
- 50:16/q26/p3 Aʿshār means the speaker's heart is shattered into pieces. — Al-Aṣmaʿī compares a broken vessel called aʿshār; Bāqillānī finds this more likely. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(الأصمعي)+]
- 50:16/q26/p4 The two eyes are the muʿallā and raqīb arrows, holding seven and three camel shares, so she takes the whole heart. — Bāqillānī objects that if she already owns the whole heart, her tears cannot newly make it wholly hers. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(غيره); BAQILLANI-]

## 50:16/q27 What further feature marks humanity's creation?  [خَلَقْنَا ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ · interpretation]
- 50:16/q27/p1 The human is a wondrous, comprehensive creature combining opposing traits and watched in every state. — Al-Biqāʿī lists intimacy and arrogance, memory and forgetfulness, ignorance and knowledge, obedience and disobedience, and says God appoints guardians over every state. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 50:16/q28 How is the preceding phrase about a new creation worded?  [خَلَقْنَا · rhetoric]
- 50:16/q28/p1 Its indefinite noun magnifies new creation and presents it as beyond ordinary custom and worthy of inquiry. — Būrsawī explains the indefinite خلق in the preceding argument. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 50:16/q29 Who does the pronoun in إِلَيْهِ identify?  [إِلَيْهِ · referent]
- 50:16/q29/p1 It returns to the human being whom God created. — Būrsawī says God is nearer to that human than his jugular vein. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 50:16/q30 What other verses and uses are noted alongside this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes point to different cross-references: divine protection and a separate use of the word human.
- 50:16/q30/p1 The verse recalls God's protection in 5:67. — Al-Biqāʿī refers the reader to his earlier discussion of the same divine protection. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 50:16/q30/p2 The word al-insān can have another specific referent in 46:15. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports exegetes who say that verse about parents was revealed concerning Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
