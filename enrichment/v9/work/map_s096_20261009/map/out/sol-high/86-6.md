# 86:6: 27 questions

## 86:6/q01 How does this clause answer the preceding call to consider creation?  [خُلِقَ · links]
turns on: Whether the answer is syntactically resumed or directly governed by the previous command.
- 86:6/q01/p1 It begins a fresh clause answering the implied question, 'From what was he created?' — Al-Kashshāf, Bursawī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī identify the implied question raised by 86:5; al-Bayḍāwī says it answers that preceding question. Al-Biqāʿī says human weakness and distraction call for an explicit answer. Al-Ālūsī insists the clause is syntactically detached from the command, and rejects direct attachment. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 86:6/q01/p2 It attaches directly to the prior command to consider. — This is the analysis al-Ālūsī calls imprecise; he says the answer is syntactically detached. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 86:6/q02 What sense does min carry here?  [مِن · grammar]
- 86:6/q02/p1 It marks origin and may also be partitive. — Elmalılı relates the partitive sense to a sound hadith that a child comes from only some of the water, not all of it. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 86:6/q03 What is the water from which the human is created?  [مَّآءٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular material denotes both parents' mingled contributions, male semen, an unspecified seminal fluid, or an inward creative source.
- 86:6/q03/p1 It is the mingled fluids of man and woman. — Muqātil says they adhere and form the human. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī specify mingling in the womb. Ibn Kathīr says the two produce the child by God's permission. Al-Qushayrī mentions the father's loins and the mother's nurture. Elmalılı and Study Quran identify a female reproductive contribution; Study Quran calls it the egg and takes loins and pelvic arch as the respective male and female parts. Al-Ālūsī notes Jesus' creation without this mixture is extraordinary. [19 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(diğer bir kısım ulemâ); STUDYQURAN(Some); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 86:6/q03/p2 It is semen, described as emitted or poured into the womb; the number of parental fluids is left unspecified. — Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Ṭabrisī, says the child develops from semen poured into the womb. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī identify it as semen; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Qutb stress its forceful emission. Al-Qummī calls it forcefully emerging sperm, a report al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds elliptical and mursal. Al-Rāzī reports a prophetic saying about which parent's fluid predominates and resemblance. [9 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI(القمي); RAZI-FULL(النبي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI-]
- 86:6/q03/p3 The gushing fluid is the man's semen alone or primarily his material. — Al-Rāzī's second speaker argues that dāfiq and the following exit from between loins and chest bones describe male fluid. Ibn ʿĀshūr says usage most commonly denotes male semen. Some scholars quoted by Elmalılı cite the Qur'anic progression from clay to lowly water and drop, with no explicit female fluid. Al-Jishumī records this side of a dispute alongside the two-fluid view. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب القول الثاني); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI(bir kısım ulemâ); JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 86:6/q03/p4 Seminal components contribute to creation, while other components also join before life appears. — Al-Jishumī qualifies any simple identification of the whole formed child with seminal fluid alone. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 86:6/q03/p5 In the inward reading, the water is creative effusion from divine love. — The Najmī interpretation in Bursawī calls the human origin the moisture of the All-Merciful's Breath. It invokes a prophetic saying about a breath from Yemen and the divine saying of a hidden treasure that loved to be known. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,النبي,قوله تعالى)]

## 86:6/q04 Why does the verse call the reproductive fluids one water?  [مَّآءٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 86:6/q04/p1 They mix and unite in the womb as one material. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī say the child is formed from both. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī say their union begins human creation. Al-Biqāʿī emphasizes thorough mixing. Bursawī adds that a quality belonging to one part can describe the whole. [12 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; MUQATIL]

## 86:6/q05 Does the wording imply all of the fluid becomes the child?  [مِن مَّآءٍۢ · interpretation]
- 86:6/q05/p1 Only a portion of the fluid does. — Elmalılı cites a sound hadith that a child is not made from all water; he applies the point also to the woman's contribution, identifying its relevant part as the ovum. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 86:6/q06 What do the two parental contributions supply?  [مَّآءٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: How much formative material comes from each parent and whether one contribution is active, receptive, or already formed.
- 86:6/q06/p1 Both parental fluids contribute to forming the child. — Muqātil says the fluids adhere; Ibn Kathīr says God permits their combined production. Al-Aʿmash assigns bones and nerves to the man's fluid and flesh and blood to the woman's. Elmalılı cites resemblance patterns and the prophetic report that dominance of one fluid affects resemblance; he identifies the female material as the ovum. Elmalılı leaves open whether her fluid bears vital material or is auxiliary, and whether both act or one is receptive. [9 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(الأعمش); RAZI-FULL(النبي); ELMALILI(diğer bir kısım ulemâ)]
- 86:6/q06/p2 Male semen bears primary or living material, while the female contribution prepares or receives it. — Some scholars in Elmalılı point to Qur'anic wording that does not explicitly name female fluid. Elmalılı describes female secreted fluid as lacking the life in male semen, and a vessel-like female contribution; his cited reproductive account calls her role mainly preparatory. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî,bir kısım ulemâ)]
- 86:6/q06/p3 The woman's ovum already contains the whole potential being; male semen activates and imprints it. — The first theory quoted by Elmalılı compares an unfertilized hen's egg. It likens male influence to a seal impressing soft wax to account for resemblance and inherited disease; greater male effort supposedly increases paternal resemblance. [3 notes: ELMALILI(Birinci kavil)]
- 86:6/q06/p4 Male and female seed particles combine to generate the body. — Elmalılı reports the older Hippocratic and Galenic account: both sexes emit semen in the womb and particles from each parent's organs arrange about an inner bodily mold. An older seed variant attributed to Pythagoras, or to magnetism or semen, joins two seeds in the womb. [3 notes: ELMALILI(mütekaddimîn; Pythagoras,İkinci kavil,İkinci kavil; Hipokrat ve Calinos)]

## 86:6/q07 What does dafq or dāfiq describe in ordinary usage?  [دَافِقٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root names forceful pouring, mutual pushing, a fluid quality, or direction of origin.
- 86:6/q07/p1 Pouring or flowing out forcefully and swiftly. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites waves rising and falling; al-Ṭabrisī adds abundance. Al-Rāzī cites al-Khalīl's all-at-once pouring and a spilled vessel called dāfiq khayr as an omen, and Quṭrub's flow. Al-Layth, as reported by al-Ālūsī, uses an all-at-once intransitive sense, which al-Ālūsī says is unique in his cited lexicons. Al-Nasafī quotes linguists distinguishing transitive 'I poured water' from water flowing itself. Al-Rāzī calls the verb a single outpouring; al-Samīn says it is transitive. Elmalılı records al-Khalīl and Quṭrub's dāfiq or dufūq and gives a Persian gloss for pouring water. [17 notes: THALABI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الخليل,قطرب); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(بعض أهل اللغة); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الليث); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 86:6/q07/p2 It means fluid that pushes its own parts together. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the water's parts push each other; al-Ālūsī reports the image of a torrent whose portions ride over one another. Al-Sakkākī's reported explicit metaphor similarly pictures successive drops pushing. Abū Ḥayyān rejects Ibn ʿAṭiyya's derivation from tadaffaqa because established dafq means pouring rather than mutual pushing; al-Ālūsī reports his objection. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الخليل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,السكاكي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 86:6/q07/p3 It describes viscous or sticky fluid. — Ikrima transmits Ibn ʿAbbās's gloss; Abū Ḥayyān treats it as a quality of the fluid rather than a lexical definition. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 86:6/q07/p4 It means coming from above. — Al-Zajjāj gives this brief gloss. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 86:6/q08 How does the active form dāfiq describe the water?  [دَافِقٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether active morphology is passive in sense, relational, figuratively transferred, or a literal intransitive action.
- 86:6/q08/p1 It is active in form but passive in sense: water poured out. — Al-Farrāʾ appeals to Hijazi adjectival usage and surrounding verse endings; his examples include a concealing secret, sleeping night, pleasing life, and made-ready condition. Ibn Qutayba adds ʿāṣim (11:43), rāḍiya (69:21), mubṣira (17:12), and a line of Waʿila al-Jurmī describing a hard day; he also mentions faʿīl for mufʿil without an example. Other sources cite 29:67, 17:45, 21:32, and examples such as sīl mufʿam. Al-Qurṭubī bases the form on passive dufiq al-māʾ and records a death curse using the root. Al-Naḥḥās rejects this as non-analogical, arguing it would let ḍārib mean maḍrūb; al-Sāmarrāʾī prefers keeping active meaning. [26 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(وعلة الجرمي); NAHHAS(الكسائي والفراء); THALABI(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(كثير); TABRISI(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء والأخفش); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي); ELMALILI(Ferrâ); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المفسرون); NAHHAS-; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT-]
- 86:6/q08/p2 It is an attributive adjective meaning water possessed of an outpouring. — Al-Zajjāj credits Sībawayh's school; al-Khalīl and Sībawayh compare lābin and tāmir. The Basran analysis uses poetic examples with nāṣib and nibāl. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the water's parts push one another. Al-Ālūsī says the relational form can fit agent or patient. Bursawī treats the verbal source as attributed to the described water regardless of agency. Elmalılı attributes this explanation, with tāmir and laban, to al-Zajjāj from Sībawayh. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه وأصحابه); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الخليل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج وسيبويه وأصحابه); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل وسيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الخليل وسيبويه); ELMALILI(Zeccâc (Sîbeveyh’ten naklen)); NAHHAS(البصريون)+]
- 86:6/q08/p3 It is a figurative active attribution: the person emits, while water is depicted as doing so. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī assign the pouring strictly to the owner; al-Nasafī agrees. Al-Biqāʿī says the fluid seems to propel itself. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Sakkākī's implicit imaginative metaphor and another explicit metaphor of successive drops. Elmalılı says al-Farrāʾ treats the passive sense through figurative attribution. Al-Sāmarrāʾī prefers preserving the active surface meaning. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السكاكي,قيل); ELMALILI(Ferrâ); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]
- 86:6/q08/p4 The active form can describe the fluid's own literal flowing. — Al-Nasafī quotes linguists who use intransitive dafaqa al-māʾ; al-Layth gives that sense, though al-Ālūsī marks it unusual. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports some linguists' intransitive derivation but says most reject it and use the passive analysis. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says other accounts that retain active and passive forms can be sound. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL(بعض أهل اللغة); ALUSI-FULL(الليث); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(المفسرون); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]
- 86:6/q08/p5 The passive analysis is invalid because the active form cannot analogically name a passive object. — Al-Naḥḥās's counterexample is that otherwise ḍārib would mean maḍrūb. Al-Sāmarrāʾī separately favors the active form's own sense. [2 notes: NAHHAS+; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]

## 86:6/q09 Is a distinct reading of dāfiq reported?  [دَافِقٍۢ · readings]
- 86:6/q09/p1 Zayd ibn ʿAlī reads it as madfūq, 'poured out.' — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call this a passive-sense reading; al-Samīn says it may explain the intended sense. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي)]

## 86:6/q10 Why call the fluid dāfiq here?  [دَافِقٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether gushing characterizes the male element alone, both elements, or the whole by rhetorical transfer.
- 86:6/q10/p1 It vividly evokes the force and effort of emission at creation's beginning. — Elmalılı says the fluid must reach the womb, and the effortful motion prefigures human striving. Al-Biqāʿī describes the thrust as if the water propelled itself. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 86:6/q10/p2 The descriptor applies to the male component, or to the mingled whole by one part's quality. — Al-Ālūsī reports the male-only account; Bursawī says the whole takes the quality of one part, and Elmalılı says the same is possible. Elmalılı links the spurting emission during intercourse with the region between the woman's loins and breastbones. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 86:6/q10/p3 Both male and female components may themselves flow toward the womb. — Al-Ālūsī reports this possibility; Elmalılı offers it as an alternative to applying the male component's quality to the whole. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]

## 86:6/q11 What effect does the indefinite form of water have?  [مَّآءٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 86:6/q11/p1 It belittles the material as a lowly, insignificant fluid. — Elmalılı takes the indefiniteness as contemptuous. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

- 86:6/q12/p1 Pause between the identical consecutive words at the end of verse 5 and start of verse 6. — Al-Zarkashī gives 9:108 as another example of separating identical neighboring words. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 86:6/q13 What is wrong with an imitator's wording for the favor of pregnancy?  [خُلِقَ · rhetoric]
- 86:6/q13/p1 Using faʿala evokes punishment rather than the intended favor. — Al-Khaṭṭābī says language such as 'showed kindness' or 'bestowed favor' fits; he cites 105:1, 4:147 and 14:45. [1 notes: KHATTABI]

## 86:6/q14 What does the prophetic report about creation from water say?  [* · reports]
- 86:6/q14/p1 Everything was created from water, followed by instructions for peaceful entry into Paradise. — Two Abū Hurayra transmissions in Musnad Aḥmad give greeting others, keeping kin ties, and night prayer; one says kind speech, the other feeding people. [2 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله)]

## 86:6/q15 Why was sexual desire placed in human reproduction?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:6/q15/p1 An innate organ-based urge sustains procreation and thus the species. — Muḥammad b. Aḥmad al-Iskandarānī, quoted by Elmalılı, says reproduction cannot rely on will alone; desire is like hunger, depends on functioning organs, and is absent after childhood castration. He says stored semen may contribute, but abstinence is not an independent cause and habit matters; women also desire without emitting semen, while brain, cerebellum and imagination contribute. [5 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]

## 86:6/q16 How are the male and female reproductive materials brought together?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:6/q16/p1 Intercourse prepares and deposits both materials, while contractions expel male semen. — Al-Iskandarānī says male arousal brings blood into erectile tissue, excitation increases testicular secretion like salivation, vesicles contract, and ducts and muscles eject semen into the vagina. He describes female arousal, congestion and spasms affecting ovaries and tubes, and her role as mainly preparatory. He calls intercourse the sole reproductive act yet says it is preparatory to combining the materials. Elmalılı describes testes, fine ducts, vesicles and ejaculatory passages, and says female secretions awaken reproductive organs. [9 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]

## 86:6/q17 How does semen reach the site of conception?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The route from deposition in the reproductive tract to the proposed site of fertilization.
- 86:6/q17/p1 It enters the uterus and passes through its connecting tubes toward the ovaries. — Al-Iskandarānī prefers this route because intercourse sends semen into the uterus and he places conception at the ovary. He rejects vapor alone, cites tubes, observed eggs and engorged tube ends, and ectopic pregnancy. Ruviş and others report generative semen in tubes of women, dogs and cattle. The wider account mentions possible tubal or ectopic pregnancies and an embryo spanning tube and ovary but questions whether these are established. [6 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları,Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî,Ruviş ve bazıları); ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)+]
- 86:6/q17/p2 Semen is absorbed from the vagina into the circulation and travels to the ovary. — Elmalılı reports this as one theory, also among later seed theorists' proposed routes. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları,Müteahhirînin büyük bir kısmı)]
- 86:6/q17/p3 Semen enters the uterus and rises as vapor to the ovary. — Elmalılı reports this as a second route; al-Iskandarānī says presumed vapor alone cannot suffice. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları,Müteahhirînin büyük bir kısmı); ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)-]
- 86:6/q17/p4 It reaches the ovary by magnetism, electricity, or intercourse-induced disturbance. — Elmalılı lists these as later seed-theory proposals alongside absorption and vapor. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Müteahhirînin büyük bir kısmı)]

## 86:6/q18 Where and how is fertilization thought to occur?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The claimed site, role of ovum and animalcules, and how much the observations establish.
- 86:6/q18/p1 Fertilization completes at the ovary through semen contacting the female egg. — Al-Iskandarānī favors the ovarian site; he cites observed eggs and tube ends, ectopic pregnancy, and says semen contact is ordinarily necessary, though the actual vital mechanism remains unobserved and conjectural. He classifies it as a biological function depending on coordinated living organs rather than ordinary chemistry or physics. Later seed theorists largely locate it in the ovary but disagree on semen's route. [9 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî,Müteahhirînin büyük bir kısmı); ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)+]
- 86:6/q18/p2 Fertilization occurs in the uterus when male and female semen mix. — Elmalılı attributes the old account to Hippocrates and Galen and some later authors. Pythagoras's reported older seed view joins two seeds there through a subtle nervous element, or through magnetism or seminal fluid. [2 notes: ELMALILI(mütekaddimîn; Pythagoras,İkinci kavil; Hipokrat ve Calinos)]
- 86:6/q18/p3 Animalcules fertilize in the uterus without ovum participation. — One theory quoted by Elmalılı specifically excludes the ovum. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Kiminin re’yince)]
- 86:6/q18/p4 Animalcules draw ovarian follicles into the uterus for fertilization. — A second animalcule variant moves the follicles there. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Kiminin re’yince)]
- 86:6/q18/p5 An animalcule draws an ovum to the uterus, opens a passage and enters it. — A third animalcule variant retains the ovum but assigns transport and penetration to one animalcule. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Kimi)]
- 86:6/q18/p6 Fertilization takes place in the uterus after the ovum acquires a mucous coating. — Prikus and Domas cite animalcules observed in the uterus rather than tubes or ovaries and say ovarian ova need coating obtained on their passage. They cite failure of artificial fertilization of a freshly removed ovum; the account suggests instruments may have damaged those ova and compares the coating with frogs' eggs immersed in thick mucus. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Prikus ve Domas); ELMALILI(Ruviş ve bazıları)-]
- 86:6/q18/p7 The mechanism is still unknown despite observations and competing theories. — Al-Iskandarānī says fertilization is too minute to see; he summarizes conjectures, then limits the observed account to follicle rupture, ovum release and later development. Specialists call for renewed investigation of ovarian structures. [5 notes: ELMALILI(Bu fennin ulemâsı,Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]

## 86:6/q19 What are the female ovary and egg said to do?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether an ovum is newly produced and transported, already contains a preformed embryo, or its observed structures remain uncertain.
- 86:6/q19/p1 The ovaries produce eggs that enter a tube and move toward the womb. — Al-Iskandarānī compares ovaries with testes: both are needed for fertility; ovaries mature at puberty, form follicles and regress at menopause. Most specialists locate the ovum in small follicles, and experimenters report an egg growing, separating and entering a tube after conception. He compares internally deposited and externally laid eggs and calls the observed egg female in origin. Elmalılı describes paired ovaries with viscous follicles and fringed tubes carrying ovarian material to the womb; he cautiously calls follicles embryonic seeds. After a mature follicle swells and ruptures, al-Iskandarānī says its wound heals with a pitted mark; another observer reports varied large yellow masses and rupture cavities. [11 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları,Bu fennin ulemâsının kısm-ı a‘zamı,Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî,Tecribecilerin büyük bir kısmı)]
- 86:6/q19/p2 The embryo is already formed in the ovary as a potential being. — The first theory quoted by Elmalılı says the ovary forms it by its own action; male semen then enables it to live and imprints the soft ovum. [3 notes: ELMALILI(Birinci kavil)]
- 86:6/q19/p3 The competing descriptions of ovarian structures remain unsettled. — Specialists request renewed investigation; one observer finds varied yellow masses and sometimes more than one per egg. Al-Iskandarānī says the fine generative mechanism is not directly observable. [3 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları,Bu fennin ulemâsı,Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]

## 86:6/q20 What are male semen's composition and physical properties?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:6/q20/p1 It includes fluid attributed to the prostate and a thicker testicular component bearing life material. — Al-Iskandarānī distinguishes conception-aiding semen from madhy and wady, which only lubricate; Elmalılı describes semen from testes through fine ducts to vesicles and says spurting male semen contains many sperm cells, each potentially developing a world-like being and the nervous system. [4 notes: ELMALILI(Muhammed b. Ahmed el-İskenderânî)]
- 86:6/q20/p2 A reported analysis gives measured water, mucus, soda, minerals and other matter. — The reported 125-part analysis assigns 112.5 water, 7.5 animal mucus and 1.25 soda, plus 3.75 phosphate of lime, mucous and volatile matter and sulfur. [2 notes: ELMALILI(Tahkik ettiklerine göre)]
- 86:6/q20/p3 It changes with time, heat, air and water. — Elmalılı reports liquefaction after roughly 20–25 minutes for an unknown reason, decomposition and ammonia at stronger heat, calcium-phosphate crystals in hot dry air, acidic yellowing and mold in hot humid air, partial coagulation and cotton-like dispersion in water, and a heated filtrate with characteristic odor and pearly appearance whose residue resembles meat extract and whose ash contains soda. [4 notes: ELMALILI]

## 86:6/q21 How is female reproductive fluid described?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the female contribution is a non-spurting auxiliary secretion or itself shares the flowing description.
- 86:6/q21/p1 A thin, yellow, non-spurting secretion aids conception and ovum release. — Elmalılı describes a light transparent secretion from vaginal walls and labia, which he says lacks the life of male semen. He says its pleasurable release awakens reproductive organs; juristic descriptions contrast thin yellow female fluid with thick white spurting male semen. He also identifies a female ovarian egg as her reproductive material, and says only the ovum is relevant rather than all her fluid. [5 notes: ELMALILI]
- 86:6/q21/p2 The woman's fluid may itself effuse into the womb. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative, and Elmalılı considers it when explaining why dāfiq may describe both fluids. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]

## 86:6/q22 What stages and timings of embryo formation are reported?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Reported embryonic stages and conflicting observations about position and timing.
- 86:6/q22/p1 Development moves from drop to attached form, lump, bones, flesh and renewed creation. — Elmalılı compares the nine-stage account of 23:12–16, from clay extract through death and resurrection, with this passage's focus on the most immediately evident fluid stage. He calls it technically second after earth or clay yet foregrounded for reflection. He also describes the attached drop and seventh stage before birth. [4 notes: ELMALILI]
- 86:6/q22/p2 The dark original point adheres to the embryonic membranes. — Elmalılı reports scholars holding this placement in transparent embryonic fluid. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Ulemâ)]
- 86:6/q22/p3 The dark original point lies in the fluid's center. — Elmalılı reports this as the alternative. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları)]
- 86:6/q22/p4 A small transparent sphere forms after six days. — Hippocrates gives this timing. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Bukrat)]
- 86:6/q22/p5 The embryonic form becomes visible only after fifteen days. — Elmalılı reports some holding this view. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları)]
- 86:6/q22/p6 Implantation takes several days, but its duration across species is unsettled. — Elmalılı gives reports of three days for rabbits and six to eight for dogs. [1 notes: ELMALILI]
- 86:6/q22/p7 The human form starts after thirty-five days at about bee size. — Elmalılı reports this timing and notes other accounts exist. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Bazıları)]

## 86:6/q23 What does reflection on creation teach about God and human conduct?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: What creation from lowly fluid demonstrates, and in one objection what preserves identity through resurrection.
- 86:6/q23/p1 Creation from humble fluid proves God's power to restore and judge people. — Al-Ṭabarī addresses resurrection deniers and cites two poetic lines with gold, saffron, neck and chest imagery for tarāʾib. Al-Qushayrī adds divine knowledge and will. Abū Ḥayyān links every soul's guardian to reckoning and pleasing deeds. Bursawī argues from creation out of lifeless matter to easier restoration. Elmalılı stresses the body's transformation into a rational person, common observations of birth, and God's power to gather components and breathe in a soul again. Islahi calls the bodily fluid accessible to God's power, and Kuranyolu urges afterlife deniers to infer resurrection. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 86:6/q23/p2 Humble origin and eventual death should remove arrogance and prompt moral preparation. — Al-Jishumī names humility; al-Biqāʿī says attending to origin brings eternal happiness. Elmalılı connects surveillance, divine preservation, purification of secrets, resurrection and human striving. Qutb says first creation shows deliberate governance and that humans are not abandoned. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 86:6/q23/p3 The ordered world and growth of human capacities reveal God's creative provision. — Al-Jishumī lists water apportioned as rivers and springs, food for animals, desire sustaining life, varied organs from flowing water, and stages from milk and food to speech, sensation and reason. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 86:6/q23/p4 The sūra's larger claim is the Qur'an's true promise of judgment and exposed secrets. — Al-Biqāʿī reads its name al-Ṭāriq, oath and subject as signaling true reward and punishment, with hidden deeds exposed and recorded. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 86:6/q23/p5 The objection says power alone cannot establish resurrection of the identical person. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī presents this objection: a recreated body could be only similar. He answers that the preserved soul carries personal identity and, joined to a recreated body, makes the same person; he cites the Angel of Death and verses 32:11, 39:42 and 45:29 on taking and preserving souls and recording deeds. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 86:6/q24 How is the preceding interrogative mimma parsed?  [* · grammar]
- 86:6/q24/p1 Mimma contracts from min plus interrogative ma, whose alif is deleted. — Al-Naḥḥās says the interrogative is complete before the answer in this verse. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 86:6/q25 Why is the creation of humans described as easy?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:6/q25/p1 It follows God’s earlier creation of Adam from earth and Eve from his rib. — Al-Biqāʿī draws the comparison to show the ease of subsequent human creation. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 86:6/q26 Which generative theory does the cited account favor?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:6/q26/p1 The seed theory is called the best among the proposed accounts. — The account quoted by Elmalılı adds that seed theorists themselves differ about the mechanism. [1 notes: ELMALILI(bezriyyûn ‘tohumcular’)+]

## 86:6/q27 What general morphological comparison is made for dāfiq?  [* · grammar]
