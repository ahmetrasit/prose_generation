# 26:216: 12 questions

## 26:216/q01 Who are those who may disobey the Prophet?  [عَصَوْكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun carries forward the near relatives of 26:214, denotes unbelievers more generally, extends to believing followers, or applies universally; al-Samīn rejects the believers reading as remote.
- 26:216/q01/p1 His near relatives or clan, whom he has just been told to warn. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies the closest kin persisting in idols after his warning; Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies the assembled clan; al-Ṭabrisī says they reject his call. Al-Ālūsī prefers this reference because of the command to warn near relatives in 26:214. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Iṣlāḥī also take the conditional as concerning kin. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:216/q01/p2 Believing followers who violate Islamic rulings after accepting the Messenger. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report this as another reading, with the Prophet rejecting actions while retaining his intercession; al-Ālūsī also reports an objection that it would cost him intercession. Al-Samīn calls it extremely remote. Al-Biqāʿī argues that if even believers disobey, others are included more strongly; al-Nasafī's contrast with followers whose obedience elicits gentleness fits this reference. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 26:216/q01/p3 Unbelievers or polytheists generally, understood from the call to faith. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām calls them polytheists; al-Samīn specifies unbelievers disobeying the summons to monotheism. Al-Ālūsī reports this as the contextual alternative to near kin; Iṣlāḥī speaks of deniers rejecting the message. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 26:216/q01/p4 Specifically Banū Hāshim and Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. — Muqātil names these two groups as the nonbelieving kin; al-Wāḥidī transmits his identification. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 26:216/q01/p5 Any creature who disobeys him, regardless of relationship. — Ibn Kathīr makes the command universal while pairing gentleness toward believing followers with disavowal of disobedience. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:216/q02 What disobedience is meant by عَصَوْكَ?  [عَصَوْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the refusal is unbelief and idolatry, sinful breaches by believers, or more generally failure to follow and pledge obedience.
- 26:216/q02/p1 Refusing the prophetic warning or call, especially through unbelief and idolatry. — Muqātil says the named kin did not accept faith; al-Ṭabarī describes persisting in idol worship and associating partners after warning. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī specify worship of other than God and opposition to the call; al-Ālūsī says refusal after warning. Abū Ḥayyān describes idolatry and other-god worship; Iṣlāḥī calls it rejection of the message. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:216/q02/p2 Disobeying particular Islamic rulings while remaining a believer. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report this alternative as disobedience in subsidiary duties after faith; al-Samīn reports it but judges it extremely remote. Al-Biqāʿī speaks of believers' sinful conduct. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 26:216/q02/p3 Failure to obey or follow the Prophet, expressed as withholding allegiance. — Al-Bayḍāwī says they do not follow him; al-Nasafī contrasts obedience and nonfollowing; Bursevī says the clan leaves obedience; Elmalılı explains the disobedience as not pledging allegiance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says following means obedience. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 26:216/q03 How does this condition relate to the preceding command to lower the wing?  [فَإِنْ عَصَوْكَ · links]
- 26:216/q03/p1 The two conditions assign gentleness to obedient followers and disavowal to those who disobey. — Abū Ḥayyān calls it a division of outcomes; al-Nasafī says obedience brings lowering the wing and nonfollowing brings disavowal. Ibn Kathīr contrasts gentleness toward believers with disavowal of any disobedient creature; al-Biqāʿī says a disobedient believer loses that gentleness, and others are included a fortiori. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects following with obedience and nurture; Kur'an Yolu says leaders should be humble and compassionate toward followers but withhold support when they turn hostile. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:216/q04 How is the following command to trust God read and joined to this condition?  [فَإِنْ عَصَوْكَ · readings]
- 26:216/q04/p1 Abū Jaʿfar and Nāfiʿ read فتوكل with fāʾ, making it the answer to the condition. — Al-Jishumī says this reading follows the Medinan codices and the fāʾ stands in place of the conditional answer: if they disobey, then rely on God. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع)]

## 26:216/q05 What does the Prophet's declaration of being innocent of what they do entail?  [بَرِىٓءٌۭ مِّمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the object of disavowal is conduct alone, people together with conduct, the broader withdrawal of support expressed by barāʾa, or in the inward reading human power itself.
- 26:216/q05/p1 He repudiates their wrong deeds, especially unbelief and idol worship, without bearing responsibility for them or necessarily repudiating the people. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī name shirk, idol worship, unbelief or disobedience as the acts repudiated. Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Naḥḥās, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe rejection of conduct. Al-Ḥusayn, reported by al-Sulamī, says Muhammad unlike other prophets rejects deeds rather than kin themselves because of his rank and intercession, which lifts the darkness of sins; Abū Ḥayyān likewise preserves intercession for sinful believers. Bursevī distinguishes their worship from their persons and urges gentle counsel. Elmalılı and The Study Quran explain that he bears no responsibility for their acts; The Study Quran cites 6:19, 6:78, 10:41, 11:35, 11:54 and says 26:115 makes him responsible for warning and good news, not for their sins. Al-Biqāʿī calls it complete separation from disobedience already warned against by the Quran. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; SULAMI(الحسين); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 26:216/q05/p2 He disavows the disobedient people as well as their conduct. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes disavowal of the clan; al-Nasafī says to disavow them and their deeds, including shirk and other acts; Ibn Kathīr extends disavowal to any disobedient creature. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 26:216/q05/p3 Barāʾa means separation and distance, including withdrawal of support when it is needed. — Al-Jishumī roots the word in distance, gives its plural forms and says أنا منك براء works for singular and plural alike. His examples of recovery from illness, sharpening a pen away from the removed material, and releasing a debtor express separation or distancing. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 26:216/q05/p4 Inwardly, he disavows their power and his own while trusting God and seeing all acts under divine will. — Al-Qāshānī attributes disobedience to thickened veils, describes annihilation in God's acts and says no one acts outside His will. He describes God's might veiling and barring some from faith, and mercy illuminating the guided, through majesty and beauty; he cites 28:56. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 26:216/q06 What does disobeying the Prophet imply about obedience to God?  [عَصَوْكَ · theology]
- 26:216/q06/p1 Disobeying him is disobeying God, who disowns opposition to His approved command. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī reason that the Prophet commands only what God approves; al-Naḥḥās says God disowns whoever opposes Him, and al-Qurṭubī says God disowns whoever disowns the Prophet's command. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 26:216/q07 Does God's disavowal of their sins exclude His willing or creating them?  [مِّمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether disavowal means noncreation and nonwilling of sin, or God's prohibition of sin while willing its known occurrence.
- 26:216/q07/p1 Yes: God cannot will or create acts that He and His Messenger disavow. — Al-Jubbāʾī, as reported by al-Rāzī, argues that the Prophet's repudiation entails God's repudiation or God would contradict His Messenger; a God who disavows sins cannot create or will them. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 26:216/q07/p2 No: God disavows sin by forbidding it, yet wills its occurrence. — Al-Rāzī answers that God's knowledge makes the event necessary, and what is necessary cannot be willed not to occur; disavowal concerns prohibition, not divine will. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(الجبائي)-]

## 26:216/q08 What conduct toward disobedient kin or followers is drawn from this command?  [فَقُلْ بَرِىٓءٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether the command grounds a general ban on polytheist alliance, measured withdrawal of support with continuing counsel, or Jaʿfar's application to speech about the two Companions.
- 26:216/q08/p1 Alliance with a polytheist is forbidden even when the polytheist is a closest relative. — Al-Wāḥidī infers this general ban from God's commanding the Prophet to disavow even his nearest polytheist kin. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 26:216/q08/p2 Disapprove hostile or sinful conduct and withhold support, while continuing humble, compassionate counsel where appropriate. — Bursevī says to disavow idolatrous worship rather than people and counsel gently in hope of their return. Kur'an Yolu says leaders should treat followers well and humbly but not support them when hostile to faith. Bursevī reports his shaykh severed ties with his successors except by testament, while maintaining a duty to counsel everyone, especially them, in truth and patience, citing 103:3. [3 notes: BURSEVI(شيخي وسندي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:216/q08/p3 Disavow anyone who speaks ill of Abū Bakr or ʿUmar. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Jaʿfar ibn Muḥammad applying the command to those who mention either Companion except with good. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(جعفر بن محمد)]

## 26:216/q09 What reports illuminate the Prophet's warning and relations with his kin?  [فَقُلْ · reports]
turns on: Which reported warning, occasion, or saying is being recounted.
- 26:216/q09/p1 After this verse, the Prophet called his relatives clan by clan and warned that kinship could not protect them from God. — Al-Ḥasan, as reported by Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, says the Prophet addressed Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib and identified the God-fearing as his allies. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن)]
- 26:216/q09/p2 The Prophet warned his family against coming with worldly claims while others came with the afterlife. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām reports this as the Prophet's own warning. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله)]
- 26:216/q09/p3 The verse came when the Prophet's relatives persisted in hostility and spoke abusively to him. — Bursevī transmits this occasion from Kashf al-asrār. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]
- 26:216/q09/p4 The Prophet said prophets are most detached from this world and their own close relatives treat them most harshly. — Ibn Kathīr transmits Abū al-Dardāʾ's report of the Prophet's statement; Abū al-Dardāʾ adds that a scholar's family neglects his knowledge until he leaves. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]

## 26:216/q10 Was this command to disavow later abrogated?  [فَقُلْ إِنِّى بَرِىٓءٌۭ مِّمَّا تَعْمَلُونَ · sciences]
- 26:216/q10/p1 Yes: the truce-like instruction was replaced by the sword verse or the order to fight them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the conciliatory arrangement abrogated by the sword verse; Abū Ḥayyān calls it a truce-like instruction abrogated by 9:5. Ibn Zayd, transmitted by al-Durr and al-Ālūsī, says the subsequent fighting command abrogated it; al-Ālūsī also reports al-Baḥr's sword-verse explanation. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد,البحر)]

## 26:216/q11 Why does the next verse direct the Prophet to trust God after this disavowal?  [* · links]
- 26:216/q11/p1 Disavowing or being opposed by relatives may bring estrangement and hostility, so reliance on God steadies and protects him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links disavowal's estrangement to the instruction in 26:217 not to fear his kin. Kur'an Yolu says family ties may sour and leaders should disregard hostility and trust God's power and mercy to protect believers. Al-Qāshānī links trust to witnessing divine will, might and mercy behind veiling and guidance. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QASHANI]

## 26:216/q12 How does this warning fit the sūrah's concluding discourse?  [* · links]
- 26:216/q12/p1 It returns to the opening warning: rejection after the final warning brings the deniers toward punishment while the Prophet attends to believers. — Iṣlāḥī says the Qur'anic warnings are true, punishment signs may arrive, and prophetic histories already warn stubborn Quraysh. The deniers should not distract the Prophet from believers. He describes this as the final warning phase, followed by messenger punishment and follower migration, comparing Abraham before his father. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
