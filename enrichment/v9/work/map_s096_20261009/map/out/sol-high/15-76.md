# 15:76: 9 questions

## 15:76/q01 What does the pronoun in wa-innahā refer to?  [وَإِنَّهَا · referent]
turns on: Which earlier feminine antecedent the pronoun resumes: the destroyed settlement, its signs or stones, or the cry; Māwardī extends it to two punished communities.
- 15:76/q01/p1 Lot’s destroyed city or towns, including their visible ruins. — Wāḥidī points back to the city in 15:67. Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits Mujāhid, Qatāda and Ibn Zayd; Abū Ḥayyān transmits the same three. Rāzī stresses the visible, undimmed ruins. Qurṭubī notes the feminine pronoun for the towns. Bayḍāwī allows city or villages; Samīn calls this the apparent referent. Ibn Kathīr describes Sodom as overturned physically and morally, pelted with stones and become a foul lake. Nasafī specifies the towns’ visible, unerased remains; the singular/plural wording varies across sources. [20 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة، ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 15:76/q01/p2 The signs left by the destruction. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān offer this as an alternative; Samīn records it. Jishumī transmits al-Aṣamm and Abū ʿAlī on signs known and enduring, while his report from al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Mujāhid says the signs are clear to people who saw the remains or heard the reports. Ālūsī also lists the signs. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); JISHUMI(الأصم، وأبو علي,الحسن، وقتادة، والضحاك، ومجاهد)]
- 15:76/q01/p3 The punishment stones. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites a truncated report that punishment stones hang between heaven and earth; its full quotation is unavailable. Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī also record this antecedent. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 15:76/q01/p4 The cry of punishment, waiting for those who repeat the people’s deeds. — Abū Ḥayyān’s unnamed view invokes the statement that the punishment is not far from wrongdoers; his continuation quotes it. Ālūsī also gives this view and cites 11:83. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 15:76/q01/p5 The people of Lot’s towns and the people of the Thicket, Shuʿayb’s community. — Māwardī states this broader antecedent. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 15:76/q02 What is the sabīl on which it lies?  [لَبِسَبِيلٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sabīl denotes the traversed road by the ruins, a way of destruction, or al-Suddī’s scriptural register; Ibn Kathīr explicitly rejects the register reading.
- 15:76/q02/p1 A visible, established road traveled past the ruins. — Mujāhid calls it marked; Wāḥidī transmits Qatāda, Mujāhid and al-Ḍaḥḥāk that it is neither obliterated nor hidden and stays open to travelers. Baghawī’s Mujāhid says it cannot disappear; Ibn Kathīr’s Mujāhid and al-Ḍaḥḥāk call it recognizable, while his Qatāda calls it clear and open. Zajjāj, Thaʿlabī, Muqātil, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī stress a visible, enduring passage with observable traces; Study Quran reports a traveled road on which the vestiges can be seen. Iṣlāḥī glosses the expression as a commonly trodden way. Jishumī says both the settled route and signs are clear to observers and hearers. [29 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,قتادة ومجاهد والضحاك); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن، وقتادة، والضحاك، ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة وابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة، ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد والضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)+]
- 15:76/q02/p2 An enduring way to destruction. — Māwardī and Abū Ḥayyān report Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Durr transmits the same gloss, with slight wording variation between enduring destruction and a route to destruction. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 15:76/q02/p3 A manifest register. — Ibn Kathīr reports al-Suddī’s comparison with 36:12, then says that this is not the intended sense here. [0 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)-]

## 15:76/q03 Where is the road or ruined site located?  [لَبِسَبِيلٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The starting point and precision of the geographical description vary: Mecca or Hijaz, Medina, or only a single region.
- 15:76/q03/p1 On the route from Mecca or the Hijaz to Syria, beside the Dead Sea in Asad’s account. — Muqātil says Meccans and others travel it; Wāḥidī’s Ibn ʿAbbās calls it your people’s familiar Syrian road, and Wāḥidī’s Wajīz specifies Quraysh. Rāzī gives the Ḥijāz–Syria route; Qurṭubī says your people use it; Biqāʿī says Quraysh passed it on some Syrian journeys; Bursawī and Iṣlāḥī connect it to Quraysh’s Syria trade. Iṣlāḥī names Sodom and Gomorrah. Asad places it from northern Hijaz to Syria along the Dead Sea and cites aerial photographs published by the American School of Oriental Research as confirmation of its trace. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 15:76/q03/p2 The dwellings lie between Medina and Syria. — Jishumī transmits this location from Qatāda. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قتادة)]
- 15:76/q03/p3 The place is in one region of the earth. — Ibn Kathīr transmits this less specific statement from Qatāda. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 15:76/q04 What does muqīm add to the description?  [مُّقِيمٍ · meaning]
turns on: Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī preserve the alternative glosses ‘known’ and ‘lasting’; many descriptions combine both traits.
- 15:76/q04/p1 The road or signs are known, clear and recognizable. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī explicitly offer ‘known’ as one gloss. Mujāhid’s marked road is transmitted by Māwardī, Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr; Wāḥidī’s Qatāda, Mujāhid and al-Ḍaḥḥāk call it visible and unhidden. Jishumī transmits al-Aṣamm and Abū ʿAlī on enduring, known signs. Biqāʿī calls the corresponding imām a clear path people follow. [13 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة ومجاهد والضحاك); JISHUMI(الأصم، وأبو علي,الحسن، وقتادة، والضحاك، ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد والضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL]
- 15:76/q04/p2 The road or its traces endure, remaining fixed and continually used. — Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī explicitly give lasting as an alternative gloss, with Ālūsī adding continuous travel. Muqātil, Thaʿlabī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, Biqāʿī and Bursawī describe permanent roads or unerased traces. Iṣlāḥī’s ‘commonly trodden way’ stresses continued use. Biqāʿī says the corresponding imām is enduring and its traces have not vanished. [15 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الأصم، وأبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 15:76/q05 What lesson does the verse draw from the remains?  [* · interpretation]
- 15:76/q05/p1 Their visible persistence lets people reflect and take warning, especially Quraysh on its Syrian journeys. — Wāḥidī says persistent traces make a lesson possible; Jishumī says they are divine luṭf for us. Abū Ḥayyān says the road allows sight and reflection, and Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān warn Quraysh who pass the ruins morning and night; Ibn Kathīr links the passage to the same passing. Biqāʿī says the fixed clear road makes reflection easy; Bursawī urges Quraysh to take warning as the traces remain; Iṣlāḥī urges learning from ruined nations on the trade routes. Study Quran notes visible vestiges. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، قتادة، ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 15:76/q05/p2 The surviving path displays God’s greatness and His defense of His messengers. — Biqāʿī says the clear, enduring path people follow displays both, with traces that have not vanished. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 15:76/q06 What happened to Lot’s people and his believing household?  [* · interpretation]
- 15:76/q06/p1 Lot’s people were destroyed, while the believers in his household were saved. — Jishumī draws this from the surrounding verses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 15:76/q07 How is the warning linked to other Quranic verses?  [* · links]
- 15:76/q07/p1 It recalls the statement that people pass the destroyed towns morning and night. — Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn Kathīr connect the route and Quraysh’s warning to 37:137–138. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 15:76/q08 How do the descriptions of the two paths complement each other?  [لَبِسَبِيلٍۢ مُّقِيمٍ · rhetoric]
- 15:76/q08/p1 They form an iḥtibāk: ‘fixed’ implies an omitted ‘clear,’ while the later ‘clear’ implies an omitted ‘fixed.’ — Biqāʿī compares the fixed road here with the clear imām in the following description; each supplies the quality left unstated in the other. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 15:76/q09 What can be recovered from the report cited for the stones?  [وَإِنَّهَا · reports]
- 15:76/q09/p1 Its surviving fragment mentions earth, two thousand years, and sinners of the Prophet’s community; the full claim is unrecoverable. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya’s preceding note uses a report about punishment stones hanging between heaven and earth, but the quotation is cut off; the clipped continuation preserves only these fragments. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
