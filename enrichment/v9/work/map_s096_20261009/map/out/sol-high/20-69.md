# 20:69: 35 questions

## 20:69/q01 What does the command to cast mean?  [وَأَلْقِ · meaning]
- 20:69/q01/p1 Throw or cast the object away. — Jishumi equates ilqāʾ with ṭarḥ, nabdh, and ramy. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:69/q02 Who is addressed by the command?  [وَأَلْقِ · referent]
turns on: The implicit addressee may be Moses or the staff.
- 20:69/q02/p1 Moses is addressed. — Qurtubi gives Moses as one possible addressee; Ibn Kathir says God inspired him to cast the staff. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:69/q02/p2 The staff may be addressed. — Qurtubi gives this as another grammatical possibility. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:69/q03 What is in Moses' right hand?  [مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · referent]
- 20:69/q03/p1 His staff. — Muqatil says it became a serpent when cast; Biqai and Alusi relate it to God's earlier question at 20:17. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 20:69/q04 Why call the staff 'what is in your right hand'?  [مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanations turn on small appearance, extraordinary power, right-hand connotations, and prior mention.
- 20:69/q04/p1 The indirect wording magnifies its extraordinary power. — Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi contrast it with the many implements. Alusi says its earlier miraculous state supports this; Tabatabai stresses God's power to make it staff or serpent. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين)+; BURSEVI+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 20:69/q04/p2 It belittles the solitary small staff, reassuring Moses. — Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi and Abu Hayyan contrast a small rod with many ropes and staffs. Nasafi says it may belittle their implements. Alusi objects that this ignores the staff's prior extraordinary state. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:69/q04/p3 It recalls God's earlier question and encourages Moses. — Biqai and Alusi cite 20:17; Alusi says this wording suits removal of fear, while 7:117 gives the command by meaning. Ibn Ashur says mā recalls God's question at 20:17 and reassures Moses that the familiar object will again take on its miraculous state; this explains why the command does not simply name the staff. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 20:69/q04/p4 The right hand evokes blessing and good fortune. — Abu Hayyan proposes this; Alusi objects that the address to Moses was not in Arabic. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 20:69/q04/p5 It shows created things lack fixed intrinsic reality. — Tabatabai says God can make the same object a staff or living serpent. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:69/q05 Where did Moses' staff come from?  [مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · reports]
turns on: Mawardi transmits differing origin reports.
- 20:69/q05/p1 An ʿawsaj thorn tree. — Mawardi reports Wahb. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(وهب)]
- 20:69/q05/p2 Paradise. — Mawardi reports Ibn Abbas, who says Moses killed ʿUj ibn ʿAnaq with it. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 20:69/q06 What does تَلْقَفْ mean?  [تَلْقَفْ · meaning]
- 20:69/q06/p1 To seize swiftly or swallow, here by mouth. — Hasan specifies its mouth. Jishumi compares laqifa, talaqqafa and iltaqafa; Kisa'i says taking quickly. Qurtubi cites laqif thaqif for a deft person and also wall or basin collapse; Bursevi cites taking by hand or mouth. Razi stresses speed. Tha'labi also glosses it as gulping down and engulfing what the magicians made. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الكسائي); RAZI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(يعقوب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; THALABI]
- 20:69/q06/p2 The intensive form conveys eager or forceful devouring. — Qiraat-ER says Form V makes the staff eagerly snatch by itself; Biqai says omitted tāʾ signals speed; the doubled tāʾ evokes the effort of swallowing. [3 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Assim,Vr); BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:69/q07 How is تَلْقَفْ read and construed?  [تَلْقَفْ · readings]
turns on: The ending marks command consequence versus circumstance or assertion, while the consonants reflect different forms and routes.
- 20:69/q07/p1 Indicative with raised fāʾ and doubled qāf, as circumstance or new clause. — Ibn Amir, especially Ibn Dhakwan, is named. Zajjaj allows the syntax but says it was not established for his recitation. Al-Farisi allows Moses or staff as subject, using 8:17 and 5:95; Tabrisi cites 99:4. Qiraat-ER emphasizes independent assertion. Ibn Mujahid names Ibn Amir alone for the raised fāʾ and doubled qāf. Ibn Khalawayh derives the doubled form from tatalaqqafu with one tāʾ omitted; he allows an understood fāʾ ('and it will gulp') or a circumstantial clause describing mā, citing 94:6. [27 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الزجاج); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عامر); TABRISI(ابن ذكوان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر); RAZI-FULL(ابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن ذكوان وأبو حيوة الشامي ويحيى بن الحرث); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر برواية ابن ذكوان وحفص); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,ابن ذكوان); NASHR(ابن ذكوان وحفص والبزي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عامر); QIRAAT-ER(CE); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+; IBNMUJAHID(ابن عامر)+; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:69/q07/p2 Jussive with doubled qāf, answering the command. — Zajjaj and others supply an implied condition: if cast, it swallows. Al-Farisi says it can describe the staff or address Moses. Alusi derives the form from tatalaqqaf after one tāʾ is dropped. Ibn Mujahid says the readers other than Ibn Amir, including Abu Bakr from Asim, have the jussive. Ibn Khalawayh explains the doubled jussive as the consequence of the imperative. [22 notes: IBNMUJAHID(النبال عن ابن كثير وقنبل); FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الزجاج); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); TABRISI(ابن ذكوان); RAZI-FULL(العامة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(ابن ذكوان وحفص والبزي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+; IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم، الباقون وأبو بكر عن عاصم)+]
- 20:69/q07/p3 Jussive with light qāf and still lām, from laqifa. — Hafs is widely named; Qurtubi also names Sulami, Abu Hayyan adds Abu Jafar and Isma, and Ibn Mujahid and Farisi report Qunbul or al-Nabbal. Nasafi also names Ibn Dhakwan. Ibn Mujahid specifies that Hafs from Asim has still lām, light qāf and jussive fāʾ. Ibn Khalawayh derives the light form from laqifa yalqafu and treats it as the consequence of the imperative. [18 notes: IBNMUJAHID(النبال عن ابن كثير وقنبل); FARISI-HUJJA(النبال عن ابن كثير، والبزي وابن فليح,حفص عن عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); JISHUMI(ابن عامر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); TABRISI(ابن ذكوان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(السلمي وحفص); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن عامر برواية ابن ذكوان وحفص); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر، وحفص، وعصمة عن عاصم، وقنبل); SAMIN-DURR(حفص); NASHR(ابن ذكوان وحفص والبزي); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+; IBNMUJAHID(حفص عن عاصم، الباقون وأبو بكر عن عاصم)+]
- 20:69/q07/p4 Doubled initial tāʾ from tatalaqqaf. — Al-Bazzi and Ibn Fulayh transmit it from Ibn Kathir, in connected recitation. Ibn Atiyya notes no initial alif is then needed. Al-Farisi objects that hamzat al-wasl cannot enter imperfect verbs; Ahmad ibn Musa starts lightly. Tabrisi gives the same difficulty; NASHR lists 31 such verbs. Ibn Khalawayh reports Ibn Kathir's doubling of the initial tāʾ here and in more than thirty similar forms. [14 notes: IBNMUJAHID(البزي وابن فليح عن ابن كثير,النبال عن ابن كثير وقنبل); FARISI-HUJJA(أحمد بن موسى,النبال عن ابن كثير، والبزي وابن فليح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(البزي عن ابن كثير); TABRISI(ابن ذكوان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير); NASHR(ابن ذكوان وحفص والبزي,البزي); QIRAAT-ER(Assim); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+; FARISI-HUJJA-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي)-]
- 20:69/q07/p5 Talqamu, with mīm, is transmitted. — Ibn al-Jawzi names Ibn Masud, Ubayy, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Abu Raja, referring to 7:117; Qurtubi lists synonymous talaqqama. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب وسعيد بن جبير وأبو رجاء); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:69/q08 Who performs the swallowing in تَلْقَفْ?  [تَلْقَفْ مَا فِى يَمِينِكَ · grammar]
turns on: The feminine prefix may follow the staff by sense or indicate Moses as addressee.
- 20:69/q08/p1 The staff, with feminine agreement by sense. — Al-Farisi cites 33:31 and 6:160; Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Bursevi and Alusi say mā means feminine ʿaṣā. Abu Hayyan notes formal agreement would use yāʾ; Samin says that form was not read. Ibn Khalawayh allows the raised verb to describe mā, the object in Moses' hand, as a circumstantial clause. [12 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+]
- 20:69/q08/p2 Moses, the thrower, may be the subject. — Al-Farisi compares attribution through cause with 8:17; Abu al-Baqa permits Moses as subject of the indicative, but Samin finds this far-fetched. Tabrisi allows the human addressee. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR-]

## 20:69/q09 What did the staff swallow?  [تَلْقَفْ مَا صَنَعُوٓا۟ · referent]
- 20:69/q09/p1 The ropes and staffs that appeared to move like snakes. — Muqatil, Yahya, Qatada and later sources identify the props. Tabrisi distinguishes the physical objects from the illusion the magicians made. Qushayri says they only appeared serpentine; Bursevi and Alusi say the motion frightened Moses. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي,سعيد,وهب بن منبه); NAHHAS; THALABI(القاسم بن أبي برزة); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(رُوي); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:69/q09/p2 Their deceptive display or magical fabrication. — Baydawi, Nasafi, Tabatabai and Islahi describe the trick swallowed, stressing illusion's nullification. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:69/q10 What happened when Moses cast the staff?  [وَأَلْقِ تَلْقَفْ · reports]
- 20:69/q10/p1 It became a serpent, swallowed the props, and returned to a staff. — Sa'id says it was huge and Pharaoh threatened crosswise amputation and crucifixion; Suddi says magicians professed faith; Wahb says Moses feared the apparent snakes. Tabrisi says it circled the ranks. Ibn Kathir describes eyes, limbs, neck, head and fangs in daylight; Razi says its growth and return exceeded craft. Razi emphasizes that the huge animal appeared instantly before the public and then shrank, beyond human power to produce. [18 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي,سعيد,وهب بن منبه); THALABI(القاسم بن أبي برزة); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(رُوي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:69/q10/p2 The swallowed ropes and staffs equaled three hundred camel loads. — Nahhas says only God knows where they vanished; Mawardi and Ibn Atiyya give the same load. [3 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(رُوي)]
- 20:69/q10/p3 The growing serpent crossed a river or sea with its tail. — Ibn Atiyya's unnamed account says Pharaoh watched from an eighty-cubit pavilion, was frightened, and the magicians believed. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(رُوي)]
- 20:69/q10/p4 The magicians saw Paradise, Hell, and their rewards before prostrating. — Tha'labi also recounts Pharaoh's believing wife's vision of Paradise and death before a stone struck her body. [1 notes: THALABI(القاسم بن أبي برزة)]

## 20:69/q11 How many magicians or implements are reported?  [مَا صَنَعُوٓا۟ · reports]
turns on: The accounts vary in whether they give the number of magicians.
- 20:69/q11/p1 Seventy thousand magicians and three hundred camel loads of props. — Tha'labi's Qasim report gives the magician count; Ibn Atiyya adds the load, apparent motion and eighty-cubit pavilion. [3 notes: THALABI(القاسم بن أبي برزة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رُوي)]
- 20:69/q11/p2 Three hundred camel loads of props, with no magician count. — Nahhas and Mawardi give this quantity; Nahhas says only God knows the props' destination. [2 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 20:69/q12 What does صَنَعُوٓا۟ mean?  [صَنَعُوٓا۟ · meaning]
- 20:69/q12/p1 They fabricated or contrived the display. — Razi notes Arabic can call a lie manufactured speech; Baydawi likewise says fabricated. Biqai stresses long practice; Bursevi defines ṣunʿ as skillful action, distinct from mere action and normally not attributed to animals or inert things. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:69/q13 Why call the display 'what they made'?  [مَا صَنَعُوٓا۟ · rhetoric]
- 20:69/q13/p1 It exposes the work as human fabrication and belittles its illusion. — Bursevi and Alusi say the moving props deceived; Tabatabai contrasts unrestricted divine power with fabrication; Tabrisi says the actual ropes and staffs were not made by the magicians. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABRISI]
- 20:69/q13/p2 It stresses their extensive training. — Biqai calls the display a product of long practice. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:69/q14 How does مَا function in إِنَّمَا صَنَعُوا۟?  [إِنَّمَا صَنَعُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The status of mā determines whether kayd is predicate or object.
- 20:69/q14/p1 Relative pronoun: 'what they made' is a magician's plot. — Zajjaj, Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Samin, Ibn Ashur and others make mā the subject of inna and kayd its predicate; Tabrisi and Samin posit an omitted resumptive. Ibn Ashur says this simply reports their act, without restriction, despite written joining. [13 notes: MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; THALABI]
- 20:69/q14/p2 Extra or restrictive particle: kayd is the object of ṣanaʿū. — Zajjaj compares innamā ḍarabtu Zaydan; Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Baydawi and Alusi cite the associated accusative. Tabari rejects its uninflected siḥr form because the authoritative readers agree against it. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد، وحميد، وزيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(مجاهد وحميد وزيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد، حميد، زيد بن على); TAB-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 20:69/q14/p3 Infinitival particle: 'their making' is a plot. — Nahhas and Nasafi allow the nominalized action; Samin says this needs no omitted resumptive; Alusi lists it among three construals. [6 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:69/q14/p4 Indefinite descriptive mā: 'something they made'. — Bursevi and Alusi permit this construal. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:69/q15 How is the hamza of إِنَّمَا read?  [إِنَّمَا · readings]
turns on: The opened hamza makes the clause causal.
- 20:69/q15/p1 Opened hamza yields a causal clause: because their work is a trick. — Abu Ishaq allows it and Qurtubi reports it; Zajjaj says he does not know it as a reading here. [2 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; ZAJJAJ-]
- 20:69/q15/p2 Kasra introduces the explanation of their work. — Zajjaj says the known reading is stronger in sense. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]

## 20:69/q16 What does joined spelling of إِنَّمَا suggest?  [إِنَّمَا · rhetoric]
- 20:69/q16/p1 It may suit the magicians' completed deed. — Samarrai follows the mushaf and suggests a rhetorical pattern, comparing 8:41. Ibn Ashur says written joining does not reflect the relative distinction early scribes observed, comparing 2:173. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:69/q17 How is كَيْدُ سَٰحِرٍ read and understood?  [كَيْدُ سَٰحِرٍ · readings]
turns on: The variants differ in agent versus craft and nominative predicate versus accusative object.
- 20:69/q17/p1 Kaydu siḥr: nominative 'a plot of magic'. — Hamza, al-Kisai and Khalaf are named, with Kufans except Asim. It can mean possessor(s) of magic, magicians so steeped in it they are magic itself, or the kind of trick. Nahhas and Ibn al-Anbari compare cloth of silk; Alusi gives lām and min analyses of the explanatory genitive; Qiraat-ER makes the craft the scheming agent. - 20:69/q17/p2 Kaydu sāḥir: nominative 'a magician's plot'. — Ibn Mujahid names Ibn Kathir, Nafi, Abu Amr, Asim and Ibn Amir; others call it majority. Al-Farisi prefers attribution to the agent, supported by the following الساحر and al-A'mash; Abu Ubayda, Baghawi and Wahidi agree. Abu al-Baqa calls the annexation one of action to agent. Abu Ubayd, as reported by Tha'labi, prefers the reading with alif because the plot is more aptly attributed to a magician, while accepting both forms as Arabic. Zajjaj supplies the syntax. ## 20:69/q18 What does كَيْد mean here?  [كَيْدُ · meaning]
- 20:69/q18/p1 A magician's device, plot, or deceptive stratagem. — Muqatil contrasts it with Moses' act; Wahidi calls it an illusion without substance; Tabrisi gives device or stratagem; Qashani says deception and embellishment. Bursevi notes a scheme can be praiseworthy or blameworthy, though commonly blameworthy. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QASHANI; BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:69/q18/p2 A plot specifically of sorcery rather than another type. — Razi and Abu Hayyan say the modifier specifies its kind; Abu al-Baqa describes genus annexed to type. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو بحرية، والأعمش، وطلحة، وابن أبي ليلى، وخلف في اختياره، وابن عيسى الأصبهاني، وابن جبير الأنطاكي، وابن جرير، وحمزة، والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 20:69/q19 Why use singular indefinite سَٰحِرٍ for the group?  [سَٰحِرٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Generic class, belittlement, and avoidance of a specific person are proposed.
- 20:69/q19/p1 It denotes the class or one kind of magic, without stressing numbers. — Razi, Zamakhshari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan and Samin give generic force. Abu Hayyan says the group did one kind of magic. Kashshaf and Baydawi cite Umar and al-Ajjaj's line on worldly striving for indefinite annexation; Abu Hayyan calls the poetry necessity and questions the reported saying. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(عمر); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(العجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 20:69/q19/p2 It belittles the entire company's work. — Biqai says one or many amount to one insubstantial trick; Bursevi says the indefinite annexation diminishes it. Alusi objects that this implies ranks of magic and conflicts with 'great magic' at 7:116, despite apparent greatness and inner triviality being compatible. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:69/q19/p3 It avoids identifying a particular known sorcerer. — Alusi reports this proposal and says the context at 20:66 already identifies the magic's kind. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:69/q20 Why is the later ٱلسَّاحِرُ definite?  [ٱلسَّاحِرُ · rhetoric]
- 20:69/q20/p1 It denotes the familiar generic class and resumes the earlier indefinite. — Razi says first mention denotes one kind and second extends futility to all kinds; Abu Hayyan compares the messenger sent to Pharaoh and Pharaoh's disobedience to the messenger in 43:46; Ibn Ashur calls it a familiar class. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 20:69/q21 What does يُفْلِحُ mean?  [يُفْلِحُ · meaning]
- 20:69/q21/p1 To succeed, attain one's aim, or win. — Ibn Atiyya also gives remaining or surviving. Bursevi distinguishes worldly survival, wealth, power and honor from permanent afterlife life, wealth, honor and knowledge; his student-teacher story illustrates temporary prosperity amid ultimate loss. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI]
- 20:69/q21/p2 To remain or survive. — Ibn Atiyya offers this lexical nuance alongside attaining one's aim. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:69/q22 What does 'the magician does not prosper' assert?  [وَلَا يُفْلِحُ ٱلسَّاحِرُ · interpretation]
turns on: The interpretations differ between general failure and punitive failure; sources also discuss how far the denial extends.
- 20:69/q22/p1 No magician attains his aim through magic wherever he practices it. — Ibn Abbas and many commentators give the universal gloss. Tabrisi, Biqai, Tabatabai and Islahi ground it in unreality; Ibn Ashur limits denial to sorcery, not other trades, and says reflection exposes illusion. Razi raises whether the verse denies magic's good and evil effects; Asad cites Razi as including both and condemns every practice. [29 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABRISI]
- 20:69/q22/p2 It can mean a magician is killed wherever found. — Farra, Tabari, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan give this as the outcome of failure; jurists are said to share it. Jundub's Prophetic report is cited without grading by Wahidi and later sources; Ibn Kathir notes Companion and Prophetic transmissions. [11 notes: FARRA(التفسير); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)-]

## 20:69/q23 What does حَيْثُ أَتَىٰ mean?  [حَيْثُ أَتَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: The place may be one where magic is practiced or where the sorcerer is found.
- 20:69/q23/p1 Wherever the magician is, goes, comes, or works magic. — Yahya preserves both 'is' and 'comes'; Baghawi says wherever he attempts his trick; Kashshaf compares coming, going and being sent. Qurtubi includes wherever he comes from on earth; Ibn Ashur universalizes every place attended with magic. Tha'labi explicitly includes wherever the magician contrives his trick. [15 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,بعضهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; MAJAZ; THALABI]
- 20:69/q23/p2 Wherever he is found, he is unsafe and may be killed. — Jundub's reported tradition quotes the verse after an order to kill a caught sorcerer; Farra and jurists also take this sense. [8 notes: FARRA(التفسير); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)-]

## 20:69/q24 Is there a variant of حَيْثُ أَتَىٰ?  [حَيْثُ أَتَىٰ · readings]
- 20:69/q24/p1 أين أتى is transmitted instead. — Akhfash reports Ibn Masud and compares جئتك من أين لا تعلم and من حيث لا تعلم; Ibn Atiyya says the meanings are close. Tabari reports grammarians' discussion, including queries for water and pasture; Abu Hayyan and Alusi also list the variant. [5 notes: AKHFASH(ابن مسعود); TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL(فرقة)]

## 20:69/q25 Why use أَتَىٰ?  [أَتَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:69/q25/p1 It evokes magicians brought from across Egypt and preserves verse endings. — Ibn Ashur compares kāna and ḥalla. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:69/q26 How does the command connect to Moses' fear and the next verse?  [* · links]
- 20:69/q26/p1 It reassures Moses, explains the defeat of illusion, and leads to prostration. — Tabari supplies the omitted act of casting and swallowing. Sa'id, Suddi and Wahb recount prostration; Razi cites 20:70 to confirm the serpent, while Nasafi and Wahidi link swallowing to belief. Alusi says it completes the earlier prohibition of fear; a proposed inference about fear of public disbelief remains under consideration. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,سعيد,وهب بن منبه); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:69/q26/p2 It recalls the earlier question about Moses' staff. — Biqai and Alusi cite 20:17; Alusi says 7:117 gives the order by meaning. Ibn Ashur links the indirect wording to God's earlier question at 20:17 and to reassurance that the staff would again become miraculous. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:69/q27 What legal or reported consequence is attached to the sorcerer?  [وَلَا يُفْلِحُ ٱلسَّاحِرُ · law]
turns on: The issue is whether the failure formula itself supplies a killing rule.
- 20:69/q27/p1 A sorcerer caught or found should be killed. — Farra gives the exegesis, Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq attribute it to jurists, and Ibn Atiyya links punishment with failure. Jundub reports a Prophetic order followed by this verse in Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Itqan and Alusi; Wahidi and Alusi give no grading, while Ibn Kathir notes al-Tirmidhi's Companion and Prophetic transmissions. Al-Tabataba'i transmits Jundub al-Bajali's report that the Prophet ordered a caught sorcerer killed, then cited this verse. [10 notes: FARRA(التفسير); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,جندب بن عبد الله البجلي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(جندب بن عبد الله البجلي عن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 20:69/q27/p2 The verse states failure; the separate ruling is addressed elsewhere. — Qurtubi sends readers to al-Baqara for the legal ruling and meaning of magic; Tabari's primary gloss is universal failure before reporting the killing interpretation. Al-Tabataba'i says the reported application of this verse to a sorcerer's being safe nowhere is unclear in context. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TAB-FULL; TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]

## 20:69/q28 What distinguishes Moses' sign from the magicians' work?  [* · theology]
turns on: Sources appeal to visible miraculous reality or to prophets' wider character and impact.
- 20:69/q28/p1 The divine miracle is real and defeats illusory human magic. — Muqatil says Moses' act was not magic; Wahb's magicians know it exceeds their art; Qushayri says swallowing exposes their falsehood. Razi lists growth, organs, swallowing, and return as beyond craft; Ibn Kathir stresses public daylight proof. Tabatabai contrasts God's power with fabrication; Kuran Yolu says God nullified their skills. Razi argues that humans cannot instantly create a huge animal and shrink it; the public transformation thus shows a world-governing Creator. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:69/q28/p2 Discerning observers recognize prophets through character and effects. — Islahi says magicians briefly fool crowds but do not contribute to society as prophets do; he rejects theologians' logical distinction as unhelpful. Kuran Yolu says Moses' superiority exceeds winning a magic contest and cites 2:102 against the undertaking. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 20:69/q29 What general principle does the defeat of magic illustrate?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:69/q29/p1 Truth eventually erases falsehood; magic cannot overcome divine truth. — Tabatabai cites 13:17, 17:81, 42:24, 28:50 and 61:5. Islahi says magic deceives sight and imagination. Bursevi's Najmiyya citation contrasts God's making with human craft; Study Quran links Moses' words to 10:77. [8 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); STUDYQURAN]
- 20:69/q29/p2 Illusory magic cannot yield genuine lasting happiness. — Tabatabai calls its object unreal; Bursevi distinguishes temporary worldly advancement from lasting success, using the example of a materially successful student who remains a loser. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BURSEVI]

## 20:69/q30 What was the wider setting and purpose of the contest?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:69/q30/p1 Pharaoh used influential magic against Moses' leadership; the contest established his prophethood. — Kuran Yolu describes Israelite presence and return aspirations as political anxiety, elites' pressure and rewards for magicians, magic's social-religious force, and Pharaoh's expectation of victory. God's miracles supplied another test; Moses' superiority was more than a contest win. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:69/q31 How is this account connected to other passages?  [* · links]
- 20:69/q31/p1 It is compared with parallel Quranic and Biblical accounts. — Study Quran cites 7:117–119, 10:77 and 20:89; Kuran Yolu directs comparison with 7:103–126 and the Biblical account. Ibn al-Jawzi refers talqamu to 7:117; Alusi says 7:117 gives the command by meaning. [6 notes: STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود وأبي بن كعب وسعيد بن جبير وأبو رجاء); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:69/q32 What inward sense is given to the staff and magic?  [* · inward]
- 20:69/q32/p1 The staff is truth-lit intellect consuming decorated false doubts. — Qashani says the intellect is controlled and joined to holy light; sorcery is deceptive embellishment. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:69/q32/p2 God's making and firm design overcome human craft. — Bursevi reports al-Ta'wilat al-Najmiyya's contrast of the staff as God's making with the magicians' handiwork. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 20:69/q33 Is this verse reported as a remedy for sorcery?  [* · reports]
- 20:69/q33/p1 It is recited with other verses over water as a cure. — Layth says he heard that 10:81, 7:117–122 and 20:69 are recited over vessel water poured on a bewitched person's head. [1 notes: ITQAN(ليث)]

## 20:69/q34 What recitation and assimilation details are reported?  [* · readings]
- 20:69/q34/p1 كَيْدُ سَٰحِرٍ belongs to a sīn assimilation inventory. — Ibn al-Jazari says al-Dani omitted it by oversight. Authorities discuss showing movement in idgham through rawm or ishmam; Abu al-Karam cites this phrase. Abu Ahmad's mīm gesture is read as rawm. With a preceding weak quiescent letter madd lengths differ; with a sound one, both full idgham and ikhfa or rawm are valid, without obligatory izhar. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو أحمد عبد السلام بن الحسين,أبو الكرم,أبو طاهر بن سوار وأبو العز القلانسي وابن الفحام)]
- 20:69/q34/p2 Al-Bazzi doubles the initial tāʾ in connected recitation. — NASHR lists this among 31 future verbs and records Ibn Dhakwan's raised fāʾ and Hafs's light qāf here. Ibn Khalawayh also reports Ibn Kathir's doubled initial tāʾ here among more than thirty similar forms. [3 notes: NASHR(ابن ذكوان وحفص والبزي,البزي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+]
- 20:69/q34/p3 The al-Naba occurrence follows its verse-ending pattern. — NASHR mentions this nearby agreement. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 20:69/q35 How does the public miracle lead to knowledge of the Creator?  [* · theology]
- 20:69/q35/p1 Its instant creation and shrinking of a huge animal supply public causal premises pointing to a governing Creator; conviction also requires God to create the premises, their ordering, and the conclusion in people's hearts. — Razi says observers could infer a world-governing Creator because events require causes and humans cannot produce this transformation. He says deniers knew the premises but chose ignorance; reasoning succeeds only when God creates the premises, awareness of their order, and conclusions by divine decree. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
