# 2:2: 20 questions

## 2:2/q01 What does 'that Book' refer to?  [ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ · referent]
turns on: The referent and whether distance is literal, retrospective, or inward.
- 2:2/q01/p1 The Qur’an, present or already known to its first hearers. — Early authorities gloss 'that' as 'this'; Khafāf's poem supports this usage. Some specify earlier revealed sūras because al-Baqara is Medinan. Al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī prefer the Qur’an. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also lists the prior revealed portion. [51 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,بعضهم,عكرمة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(الأخفش وأبو عبيدة); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن وابن عباس,عكرمة وسعيد بن جبير والسدي ومقاتل بن حيان وزيد بن أسلم); THALABI(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة ومجاهد والضحاك ومقاتل,ابن كيسان); MAWARDI-FULL(الأصم); QUSHAYRI(قيل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، والحسن، وقتادة، ومجاهد، والضحاك، ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كيسان); TABRISI(الأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس ومجاهد وعكرمة والكسائي وأبو عبيدة والأخفش); RAZI-FULL(الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,خفاف بن ندبة,قيل,معمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كيسان وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:2/q01/p2 The Qur’an promised earlier to Muhammad or in prior scriptures. — Reported promises include an unwashable Book, a weighty word (73:5), covenant revelation, and a Book foretold by Moses and Jesus; ʿIyāḍ's Muslim report describes reciting it asleep and awake. Al-Nasafī gives both the earlier scriptural promise and the opening letters. [33 notes: ZAJJAJ(غيرهما من النحويين); THALABI(ابن عباس,الفراء,عطاء بن السائب,يمان بن رئاب); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج,يمان بن رباب); BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الفراء وأبو علي الجبائي,المبرد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد,عياض بن حمار,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن رئاب,ابن عباس,عطاء بن السائب); ALUSI-FULL(الواحدي,كعب); NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:2/q01/p3 The Preserved Tablet, heavenly Qur’an, or God's fixed decree. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr names the Tablet and al-Kisāʾī the Qur’an before descent; inward readings add primordial destiny and mercy written before creation. Abū Ḥayyān also reports the undescended Qur’an. [21 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الكسائي,قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,الكسائي,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن حبيب,قوم); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); SULAMI(قيل); QUSHAYRI(قيل)]
- 2:2/q01/p4 The just spoken opening letters or the path requested in al-Fātiḥa. — Al-Zamakhsharī and others point back to completed speech; Abū Jaʿfar ibn al-Zubayr identifies the requested path with the Book; Abū Ḥayyān favors that link, al-Ālūsī objects. Abū Ḥayyān adds the alphabet letters used in the challenge. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); ELMALILI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر بن إبراهيم بن الزبير)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:2/q01/p5 The Torah and Gospel themselves. — Some retain a distal reference to absent scriptures. Al-Ṭabrisī rejects this because the Qur’an describes their alteration, and Ibn Kathīr calls it unsupported. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); THALABI(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); TABRISI-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير وغيره)-; ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)-]

## 2:2/q02 Why use dhālika and how is it formed?  [ذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether distance is temporal, honorific, or literal, and the role of lām and kāf.
- 2:2/q02/p1 The form contains a demonstrative base, lām, and address kāf. — Basrans and Kufans dispute the base and lām's role: emphasis, distance, extra support or prevention of false annexation. Dual nūn retention proves kāf is not a genitive noun; the lām's kasra is also explained. Al-Rāzī explains masculine agreement by the named item rather than the feminine word sūra. [28 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق,البصريون,الكسائي والفراء,علي بن سليمان); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الفتح الموصلي,أبو الهيثم,الزجاج); THALABI(أبو عمرو); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء); RAZI-FULL]
- 2:2/q02/p2 Discourse and context license a distant form for what is already spoken or present. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and Ibn Mālik cite ordinary speech and Qur’anic alternations; Ibn ʿĀshūr says contextual purpose chooses near or far. Visible people remain distinct. [16 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن مالك,قيس بن الخطيم); ALUSI-FULL(الإمام الرازي,الجرجاني); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري)]
- 2:2/q02/p3 Distance elevates the Qur’an or recalls its descent, promise, and hidden depths. — Al-Sakkākī's honorific explanation is preferred by al-Ālūsī; al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr link lofty rank to the opponents' inability to imitate it. Other accounts cite descent or inaccessible sciences. [22 notes: RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرضي,السهيلي,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL(خفاف بن ندبة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 2:2/q02/p4 Dhālika retains its normal sense of distance. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects reinterpreting the remote form as 'this'; al-Qurṭubī reports this distal premise. Al-Raḍī distinguishes distant reference to absent objects, and al-Rāzī records the objection that the Book seems present. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL(الرضي); RAZI-FULL]

## 2:2/q03 What does kitāb signify here?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word names a written product or asserts perfect bookhood.
- 2:2/q03/p1 Written and composed revelation, rooted in joining or gathering. — Poetic and Arabic examples include sewing, strung beads, assembled troops and joined letters. Al-Wāḥidī prefers written revelation over a restricted sense of duties; Elmalılı insists revealed wording and meaning belong together. Al-Bayḍāwī takes kitāb as an emphatic verbal noun or a passive written-object form, used before actual writing. [52 notes: THALABI(ذو المرجة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الجعدي,ذو الرمة,شاعر); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجوهري); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT+; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)-]
- 2:2/q03/p2 The complete Book, uniquely deserving its definite title. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares 'the man' for complete virtue; al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr discuss rhetorical perfection rather than literal exclusion of other books. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عصفور); IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:2/q04 How is 'that Book' attached to the opening letters and what follows?  [ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the opening letters are a name or completed utterance.
- 2:2/q04/p1 The letters, demonstrative, and Book admit subject, predicate, adjective and apposition analyses. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Rāzī and al-Samīn enumerate parses depending on whether the letters name a sūra, sound, or complete utterance. Al-Ṭabarī rejects inconsistent Kufan combinations. Al-Ālūsī also records the variant 'tanẓīl al-kitāb'. Al-Itqān insists any parse respect each grammatical category's constraints, with disputed apposition and adverbial examples. [78 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض المتقدمين في العلم بالعربية من الكوفيين); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء,الكوفيون); WAHIDI-BASIT(من قال إنها أسماء للسور والقرآن); KASHSHAF-FULL(الذبياني,عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(عبد الله); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قوم); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ITQAN]
- 2:2/q04/p2 The letters end independently and dhālika al-kitāb starts a fresh clause. — Abū Ḥayyān favors this least forced analysis; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it apparent. Al-Zamakhsharī favors closely linked independent clauses. [23 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+]

## 2:2/q05 What do the opening letters contribute to 'that Book'?  [ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether their function is a marker, oath, challenge, or concealed secret.
- 2:2/q05/p1 They are an oath for the Qur’an, with the response supplied by 'that Book' or 'no doubt in it'. — Ibn Qutayba treats the letters as oath; Qāshānī says the answer is omitted and indicated by dhālika al-kitāb. Al-Qurṭubī prefers lā rayba fīhi as its answer. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; QASHANI; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:2/q05/p2 They mark the sūra's opening and draw attention to the following revelation. — Abū ʿUbayda says the letter names are indeclinable and cites Abū al-Najm; Ibn ʿArabī says their unusual sound arrests idle talk and invites reflection. [3 notes: MAJAZ; BURSEVI(الشيخ الأكبر)]
- 2:2/q05/p3 Their ultimate intended meaning is a hidden secret. — Al-Bursawī reports knowers who require divine unveiling; Jishumī rejects unknowability because revelation aims to be understood and early scholars interpreted the letters. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض العارفين,شيخي الأكمل); JISHUMI(قيل)-]

## 2:2/q06 What does rayb mean?  [رَيْبَ · meaning]
- 2:2/q06/p1 Doubt, often colored by suspicion or inward unease. — Saʿīda and Ibn al-Zibaʿrī witness doubt, Jamil accusation, a distressed gazelle and time's calamities disturbance; a rare poem gives 'need'. Sībawayh, Abū Zayd and Ibn ʿĀshūr disagree on rāba versus arāba. Al-Ṭabrisī adds severe doubt and distinguishes rābanī from arābanī. Al-Bārizī prefers rayb to shakk for a lighter sound sequence without heavy assimilation. ## 2:2/q07 What does 'no doubt in it' claim?  [لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the statement describes the Book objectively, commands the hearer, or has a restricted audience.
- 2:2/q07/p1 The Book itself leaves no rational ground for doubt despite actual doubters. — The 2:23 challenge tests its miraculousness; al-Ṭabrisī denies obscurity and contradiction, and Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes doubts in hearers from defects in the Book. Elmalılı adds certainty of revelation and trustworthy delivery. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties the denial to familiar letters' miraculous inability to be rivalled. [45 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المبرد,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:2/q07/p2 It functions as a prohibition: do not doubt it. — Supporters compare the prohibitive negation at 2:197; Abū Ḥayyān at one point finds this far-fetched. [12 notes: THALABI(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الخليل وابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:2/q07/p3 It is restricted to believers' freedom from doubt. — Al-Sulamī speaks of unveiled hearts; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān reject narrowing the general wording. [6 notes: SULAMI(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 2:2/q08 How does the negation work grammatically and rhetorically?  [لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ · grammar]
turns on: The particle's scope, the function of fīhi, and whether word order suggests contrast.
- 2:2/q08/p1 Lā negates the genus, building rayb accusative without tanwīn. — Sībawayh compares it to inna; Basran, Kufan and other accounts differ on government and omitted tanwīn. The common fatḥa categorically negates doubt; Abū al-Shaʿthāʾ's nominative reading has a different possible scope. Al-Ṭabrisī cites Sībawayh for lā governing the unnunated noun. Other grammarians call lā plus indefinite noun a compound; fīhi can be predicate or adjective. [50 notes: AKHFASH(قوم); NAHHAS(البصريون,الفراء,الكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو الشعثاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الشعثاء,الأخفش,الجمهور,سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش,سيبويه); ALUSI-FULL(سليم أبو الشعثاء); ITQAN; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(القرطبي); IBNASHUR-FULL(القراء المتواترون); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو الشعثاء); RAZI-FULL(أبو الشعثاء); TABRISI(سيبويه,غيره من حذاق النحويين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+; SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج)-]
- 2:2/q08/p2 The order foregrounds denial and avoids implying another scripture is doubtful. — Al-Zamakhsharī and followers contrast 37:47's Paradise wine; Abū Ḥayyān rejects the exclusivity inference, and al-Ālūsī replies. [14 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,الزمخشري)]
- 2:2/q08/p3 Fīhi is the predicate, or a modifier with an omitted predicate. — Al-Fārisī and al-Wāḥidī distinguish surface accusative and syntactic nominative; Abū Ḥayyān prefers a readily supplied predicate. Fī also has literal and figurative containment and other Arabic uses. Al-Burhān reports an independent fīhi hudā parse versus fīhi as lā's predicate with hudā as accusative circumstance. [21 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BURHAN]

## 2:2/q09 How are fīhi and the adjoining sounds recited?  [فِيهِ هُدًۭى · readings]
turns on: Reader transmission and whether a glide, assimilation, or nasalization is heard.
- 2:2/q09/p1 Ibn Kathīr lengthens hāʾ with a connecting yāʾ or wāw. — Ḥafṣ and Qutayba agree at specified exceptions; some readers restore original ḍamma. Sībawayh objects to a sequence of quiescent sounds. [13 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير); FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); TABRISI(ابن كثير,حفص وقتيبة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق,الزهري وابن محيصن ومسلم بن جندب وعبيد بن عمير); NASAFI-FULL(مكي); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير,حفص)]
- 2:2/q09/p2 The common performance uses a short or light hāʾ vowel. — Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and ʿĀṣim have light forms; Ibn al-Sarrī prefers kasra without yāʾ because it is usual and fits the rasm. Abū Ḥātim distinguishes concealment from true assimilation. Al-Akhfash also reports silent pronominal hāʾ in an Asad al-Sarāt poetic example. [29 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو وعاصم,نافع); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو بكر,أبو بكر محمد بن السري,أبو حاتم,أبو عمرو وعاصم,الأخفش,الكسائي عن إسماعيل عن نافع,بعض أصحابنا,حمزة والكسائي وابن عامر,نافع); WAHIDI-BASIT(الباقون); JISHUMI(ابن كثير); TABRISI(الباقون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير,الزهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق,الزهري وابن محيصن ومسلم بن جندب وعبيد بن عمير); ALUSI-FULL(الزهري وابن جندب); AKHFASH; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو بكر محمد بن السري)+; JISHUMI+]
- 2:2/q09/p3 Assimilation and nasalization vary by reader and sound. — Ibn al-Jazarī authenticates ghunnah or its omission before lām and rāʾ; Abū Jaʿfar conceals before khāʾ and ghayn. Abū ʿAmr has bāʾ-to-fāʾ assimilation; al-Kisāʾī pauses on hudā with yāʾ. Al-Akhfash reports assimilation of the adjacent hāʾs in fīhi hudā. [22 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر,أبو جعفر وابن كثير وحمزة والكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الطباع,أبو عمرو); NASHR(الدوري عن الكسائي، أبو عثمان الضرير,الكسائي,خلف عن حمزة,نافع، ابن كثير، أبو عمرو، ابن عامر، عاصم، أبو جعفر، يعقوب); ITQAN(ابن الجزري); BURHAN; AKHFASH(بعض القراء)]

## 2:2/q10 Where may recitation pause?  [لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ هُدًۭى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether fīhi closes the doubt clause and how 2:3 attaches.
- 2:2/q10/p1 After lā rayb, making fīhi hudan a new clause. — Nāfiʿ and ʿĀṣim are reported with it; a predicate for lā rayb is supplied. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains a concessive force for opponents. Abū Isḥāq pauses after lā rayb, interprets it as 'truly', and starts fīhi hudā. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(نافع وعاصم); RAZI-FULL(نافع); NASAFI-FULL(نافع); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض القراء); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج,نافع وعاصم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ITQAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); NASHR-; ITQAN-]
- 2:2/q10/p2 After fīhi, making the Book itself guidance. — Most pause here; al-Rāzī and Ibn Kathīr prefer the emphatic reading. Ibn al-Jazarī rejects the rival pause by 32:2. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(نافع وعاصم); NASAFI-FULL(نافع); NASHR; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:2/q10/p3 At the verse end, with quality depending on the next clause's grammar. — The stop is good, sufficient, or complete according to how alladhīna in 2:3 attaches. Ibn al-Jazarī permits it against Sajawandī and cites the verse-ending convention. [15 notes: BURHAN; NASHR(أبو الفضل الرازي,ابن عباس); ITQAN(ابن هشام)]

## 2:2/q11 What is hudā and what guidance does it describe?  [هُدًۭى · meaning]
turns on: Whether guidance requires a response or simply points the way.
- 2:2/q11/p1 It is direction, clarification, proof, light, and a way out of error. — Al-Shaʿbī gives leading away from error, al-Suddī light, Saʿīd clarification; al-Ṭabarī explains showing the way. Al-Fārisī discusses the verb's second object with ilā or lām. Ibn ʿAṭiyya glosses it as right guidance and clarification. [42 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(السدي، أبو مالك، أبو صالح، ابن عباس، مرة الهمداني، ابن مسعود، وناس من أصحاب النبي,الشعبي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,الشعبي,سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن,أبو عبيدة,أبو عمرو,ابن مقبل); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,الشعبي,قتادة); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 2:2/q11/p2 Guidance entails reaching the goal. — Al-Zamakhsharī argues from opposition to error, praise of the guided and ihtadā as responsive form; al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī counter each inference. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:2/q11/p3 Guidance can mean indication without attainment. — Al-Rāzī cites Thamūd in 41:17 and 'I guided him, but he was not guided'; al-Ālūsī distinguishes being shown from following. Elmalılı says the Qur’an as speech instructs while tawfīq is God's act. [21 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ELMALILI+]
- 2:2/q11/p4 The word accommodates direction and successful arrival. — Al-Ālūsī reports competing literal and figurative accounts, then includes both and averting destruction; Elmalılı distinguishes non-conveying and conveying guidance. [9 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 2:2/q12 How is hudan parsed and formed?  [هُدًۭى · grammar]
turns on: Its case, governor, source form, and gender.
- 2:2/q12/p1 It is accusative as a detached adjective or circumstance. — The Book, demonstrative or pronoun in fīhi can supply its referent; Ibn ʿAṭiyya weakens the last, while Abū Ḥayyān notes difficulty in qualifying an absence. The same note permits an accusative circumstance. He also permits accusative circumstance. [22 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:2/q12/p2 It is nominative as a predicate, new sentence, or praise. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and later grammarians offer predicate of dhālika, omitted huwa, or after fīhi; al-Ṭabarī critiques one Kufan combination. Al-Qurṭubī takes hudā as a nominative subject with preceding fīhi as predicate. Al-Bayḍāwī also permits hudā after a fronted fīhi predicate with a pause at rayb. Ibn Kathīr gives nominative description as an Arabic possibility. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(بعض المتقدمين في العلم بالعربية من الكوفيين); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:2/q12/p3 It is a rare verbal noun or source substitute, with disputed gender. — Sībawayh calls its pattern rare; Abū ʿAlī compares weak roots. Al-Liḥyānī treats it as masculine, while Banū Asad and Ibn ʿAṭiyya allow feminine; related derivatives include daylight, offerings and gifts. [31 notes: AKHFASH(العرب); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن,أبو علي,أحمد,أحمد بن يحيى,سيبويه); TABRISI(أبو علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن سيده,ابن عطية,الفراء,اللحياني,رضي الدين أبو عبد الله محمد بن علي بن يوسف الشاطبي); SAMIN-DURR(اللحياني); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية,الشاطبي,اللحياني والفراء,سيبويه); ITQAN; ELMALILI]

- 2:2/q13/p1 Those fulfilling obligations and avoiding prohibitions. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this broad definition; al-Ḥasan and ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz agree. [36 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن البصري); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(رسول الله,طلق بن حبيب,عمر بن عبد العزيز); BAGHAWI-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(بعضهم,قيل); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو الدرداء,ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,طلق بن حبيب); ELMALILI; TAB-FULL+]

[43 notes: TAB-FULL(الأعمش,الكلبي); IBNABIHATIM(عطية السعدي); THALABI(ابن المعتز,النبي,بكر بن عبد الله,شهر بن حوشب,عمر بن عبد العزيز,كعب الأحبار); BAGHAWI-FULL(شهر بن حوشب,عمر بن الخطاب); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض الناس,عمر بن الخطاب وكعب الأحبار,عمر بن عبد العزيز); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(آخرون,النبي ﷺ,بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن المعتز,عمر بن الخطاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,أبي بن كعب,ابن المعتز,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,أبو العفيف,أبو هريرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(النبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Hüreyre,hadith); KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 2:2/q13/p3 Believers who renounce shirk and affirm revelation. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī and Muʿādh give the basic faith threshold; al-Wāḥidī includes believers with major sins. Ibn ʿUmar says a pious person does not see himself above anyone. [50 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(أبو يزيد,الجنيد,الداراني,سهل); THALABI(أبو الدرداء,أبو القاسم الحكيم,أبو تراب,أبو زيد,ابن عمر,الثوري,الجنيد بن محمد,الحسن,السري بن المفلس,الشبلي,الفضيل,القاسم بن القاسم,بعض الحكماء,جعفر الصادق,سفيان الثوري والفضيل,سهل,مالك,محمد بن علي الترمذي,محمد بن يوسف المقري,ميمون بن مهران); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL(إبراهيم بن أدهم,الحسن,النبي ﷺ,الواقدي,علي بن أبي طالب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سليمان الداراني,أبو يزيد البسطامي); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عمر)]

## 2:2/q14 What is the root sense of muttaqīn?  [لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · meaning]
- 2:2/q14/p1 It means guarding oneself, from waqā and wiqāya. — Sources explain wāw-to-tāʾ change and plural morphology; armor, hand shielding and a thorny path illustrate protection. Asad emphasizes awareness of God's presence, while Elmalılı joins positive action with restraint. ## 2:2/q15 Why can the God-fearing adjective stand without an expressed noun?  [لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the adjective's implicit noun and plural scope are supplied by context.
- 2:2/q15/p1 The understood noun is 'people'; context permits omission in praise. — Al-Burhān says an adjective can stand alone when it uniquely identifies its noun or when praise centers it; thus the phrase means guidance for God-fearing people. [3 notes: BURHAN]
- 2:2/q15/p2 Plural forms have context-sensitive scope. — Al-Burhān says four broken paucity forms and two sound plurals inherently indicate few, while other plurals may denote few or many according to context. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 2:2/q16 Why is guidance specified for the God-fearing?  [هُدًۭى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrase restricts potential guidance, actual benefit, continuing guidance, or later attainment.
- 2:2/q16/p1 They benefit from and follow guidance available to everyone. — 2:185 names all people; 79:45 analogously singles out receptive hearers; 17:82 says wrongdoers increase in loss. Al-Ālūsī rejects adding an omitted 'and disbelievers'. [36 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعض المحققين); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ELMALILI; QASHANI]
- 2:2/q16/p2 Those already guided gain further or continuing guidance. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 1:6; Elmalılı says guidance continues at every rank, while al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes earlier innate and subsequent scriptural guidance. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 2:2/q16/p3 The term anticipates people about to become pious. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī cite names based on imminent state; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that hudā as a verbal noun has no fixed tense. [19 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أئمة الأصول); ITQAN(الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:2/q16/p4 An omitted 'and disbelievers' extends the audience. — Ibn al-Anbārī compares an omitted cold after garments protecting from heat; al-Ālūsī rejects this ellipsis. ## 2:2/q17 How does the verse link to its sūra and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is between clauses, with al-Fātiḥa, or with parallel sūras.
- 2:2/q17/p1 Four unjoined clauses advance from challenge to Book, certainty, and guidance. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī explain each clause's rhetorical force; Abū Ḥayyān prefers three after the letters. The denial anticipates the 2:23 challenge. [31 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 2:2/q17/p2 The Book answers al-Fātiḥa's prayer for a straight path. — Al-Biqāʿī connects the opening believer and rejecter groups to paths to follow and avoid; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī emphasizes the sūra's affirmation of revelation and law. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر بن إبراهيم بن الزبير)]
- 2:2/q17/p3 Opening titles and guidance terms vary with each sūra's purpose. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī counts Book and Qur’an uses across sūras and contrasts the God-fearing with beneficent people for whom mercy is added; al-Itqān compares Luqmān 31:3. [10 notes: ITQAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 2:2/q18 What reports or historical claims accompany the verse?  [* · reports]
- 2:2/q18/p1 Reports describe the opening groups, rehearsal, and recitation. — Mujāhid differs on the hypocrite count; al-Rabīʿ names confederate leaders. Ibn ʿAbbās reports Jibrīl's final rehearsal and a dawn-prayer recitation. [7 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع بن أنس,مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وجماعة,مجاهد); NASHR(ابن عباس)]
- 2:2/q18/p2 Jewish denial is reported as an occasion. — Muqātil names Kaʿb ibn al-Ashraf and Kaʿb ibn Asad; al-Thaʿlabī separately names Mālik ibn al-Ṣayf. [2 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI]
- 2:2/q18/p3 The promised Book and mercy were written or announced before this revelation. — ʿIyāḍ reports the unwashable Book, Abū Hurayra God's mercy preceding wrath, and Qāshānī cites Jesus on revelation and Mahdī's interpretation. - 2:2/q18/p4 Al-Baqara starts Medinan revelation and belongs among the long sūras. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ dates the opening after the Hijra; al-Itqān cites Ibn ʿAbbās on the seven long sūras. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ITQAN(ابن عباس)]

## 2:2/q19 What inward readings are offered?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether guidance is inward disclosure or a journey shaped by prior readiness.
- 2:2/q19/p1 Purified hearts realize the Book as certain guidance and divine intimacy. — Al-Sulamī describes love and knowledge inscribed inwardly; al-Qushayrī likens the Book to consoling letters from the Beloved; Tustarī centers surrender of independent power. [30 notes: SULAMI(أبو يزيد,الجنيد,الداراني,سهل,قيل); QUSHAYRI(أنشدوا,النبي,بعضهم,قيل); TUSTARI; BURSEVI(الشيخ الأكبر,بعض العارفين,شيخي الأكمل)]
- 2:2/q19/p2 The Book guides distinct classes of souls according to readiness. — Qāshānī's seven final classes include two veiled groups that cannot benefit and five guided classes from virtue to belovedness; lovers and beloveds need the Book at different stages. [17 notes: QASHANI(الحديث الرباني,عيسى عليه السلام)]

## 2:2/q20 What wider claims are drawn from its status as Book and guidance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which claim about proof, scope, knowledge, or scripture's nature is emphasized.
- 2:2/q20/p1 Inimitability and its many Qur’anic titles establish divine status and varied effects. — Al-Bāqillānī argues eloquent rivals would answer an achievable challenge; al-Rāzī catalogs proof, light, healing, mercy, criterion, reminder, truth and other titles with verses and reports. [53 notes: BAQILLANI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الخليل,المؤرخ,النبي,سفيان بن عيينة,عكرمة والسدي,قتادة,قيل); ELMALILI]
- 2:2/q20/p2 Its certainty and concise wording support knowledge, moral life, and multiple readings. — Elmalılı invokes observation, true report and inference against skepticism; Ibn ʿĀshūr includes ethics, law and public affairs and allows multiple contextually licensed meanings and pauses. [34 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أئمة الأصول,ابن الحاجب,الغزالي وأبو الحسين البصري,قوم,لبيد)]
- 2:2/q20/p3 Recitation and grammatical analysis require context-sensitive method. — Al-Nawawī recommends lowering the voice for verses voicing false claims about God; al-Itqān illustrates grammatical constraints with other Qur’anic passages. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī defers Book and guidance discussion to Sūrat al-Shūrā. [2 notes: ITQAN(النووي); TABATABAI]
- 2:2/q20/p4 It guides religious life while allowing scientific inquiry and renewal. — Al-Khūlī cites guidance for the God-fearing to separate Qur’anic purpose from natural science; the study develops debates on evolution and historically informed renewal of language, law, and theology. - 2:2/q20/p5 It is an uncreated divine attribute. — Al-Qurṭubī calls it true and revealed, yet neither created nor originated, despite unbelievers' doubts. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 2:2/q20/p6 Omitting names can serve clarity, generality, honor, or censure. — Al-Itqān says famous referents need no names, unhelpful identification may be omitted, anonymity can generalize a rule, and description may honor Abū Bakr or demean an enemy; a report contrasts Pharaoh and Nimrod. [6 notes: ITQAN(قيل)]
