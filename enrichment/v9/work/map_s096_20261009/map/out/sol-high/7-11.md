# 7:11: 24 questions

## 7:11/q01 What force does وَلَقَدْ add?  [وَلَقَدْ · rhetoric]
- 7:11/q01/p1 It emphatically asserts the creation account, with an implied oath. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies lām and qad as reinforcing the report like 7:10; al-Sāmarrāʾī also identifies an implied oath and notes al-Aʿrāf’s stronger opening than Ṣād. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; TABATABAI]

## 7:11/q02 What does خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ mean here?  [خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether creation denotes actual origination, initial design, or determination.
- 7:11/q02/p1 God brought into existence or prepared the first substance of humanity according to measure. — Jishumī gives measured origination and also creation without a prior model; Ṭabrisī links measure to wisdom; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines bringing into existence. Biqāʿī describes preparation of material with capacity for form; Elmalılı begins before any individual existed. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:11/q02/p2 It denotes design or determination before the final form. — Iṣlāḥī says khalaqa alone covers consequences but alongside taṣwīr narrows to initial design. Rāzī uses divine decree and will; his preferred account sets the forms in the Preserved Tablet before Adam. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL]
- 7:11/q02/p3 It denotes the first, unformed stage of bodily and species development. — Kur'an Yolu describes ordering chaos into form and embryonic, bodily, intellectual development; Asad describes embryonic and evolutionary development. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 7:11/q03 What does صَوَّرْنَٰكُمْ add to creation?  [صَوَّرْنَٰكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether form is bodily shape, final perfection, or prior potential.
- 7:11/q03/p1 God gave a thing its visible bodily structure and distinct human shape. — Jishumī and Ṭabrisī define taṣwīr as giving outward form; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares shaping clay. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes bringing into existence from giving particular human form; Qur'an Yolu says form and shape. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:11/q03/p2 It marks the final form and perfection following initial design. — Iṣlāḥī contrasts initial design with final perfection; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees imaging as a higher perfection involving refined perception and deliberation, comparing 23:14. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:11/q03/p3 It refers to a prepared capacity for a future form. — Biqāʿī compares clay able to grow a tree and reconciles Ṣād's actual formation with this verse's preparatory capacity. He reads a forty-two-night embryonic formation report as such preparation within the second forty-day period. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+]

## 7:11/q04 Who is created and fashioned, and where or when?  [خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ صَوَّرْنَٰكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural names Adam representatively, descendants at a primordial stage, womb development, or prior decree.
- 7:11/q04/p1 Both verbs concern Adam: God created him, then gave him form before commanding prostration. — Al-Zajjāj cites Adam's creation from dust (3:59); Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, and Ālūsī invoke the next command. Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr prefer this sequence; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the transition concise. Some supply 'your father'; al-Ḥasan and Yūsuf al-Naḥwī favor it. Adam was first unformed clay; Eve from his rib appears in al-Zajjāj’s account. [37 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(يونس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج وابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+; RAZI-FULL(الحسن ويوسف النخوي)+; TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 7:11/q04/p2 Adam was created and the descendants were fashioned in his back or loins before the command. — Mujāhid’s reports describe descendants as particles, sometimes connected with their eventual reward; Abū ʿUbayd prefers this due to the sequel. Ṭabrisī and Ālūsī report Ibn ʿAbbās and others. The primordial covenant in 7:172 is cited; variants phrase creation as occurring in Adam’s back. [27 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد والربيع وقتادة والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,النحاس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والربيع وقتادة والسدي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(ابن عباس، مجاهد، الربيع، قتادة، السدي); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Th); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد)+]
- 7:11/q04/p3 People were created in men's or fathers' loins and fashioned in women's or mothers' wombs. — ʿIkrima, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Aʿmash and others transmit this. Ibn al-Sāʾib specifies male and female reproductive fluids joining; one report from Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr was graded sound by al-Ḥākim, as Ibn Kathīr reports. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,الأعمش,عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي,عكرمة); TABRISI(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب,ابن عباس وعكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة والأعمش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن السائب,ابن جبير عن ابن عباس,الحارث عن ابن عباس,عكرمة وقتادة والضحاك والأعمش); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); TABATABAI(عكرمة)]
- 7:11/q04/p4 Adam was created, and his descendants were fashioned afterward in wombs or as successive generations. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Rabīʿ, al-Suddī and al-Ḍaḥḥāk are repeatedly transmitted. Qatāda specifies clay, clot, lump, bones, flesh and a new creation; Muqātil notes sex, color and bodily variation. Qurṭubī cites 23:12–13, 4:1 while allowing multiple views. [26 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الربيع,السدي,الضحاك,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس,قتادة والربيع والضحاك والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة والضحاك والسدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والربيع بن أنس وقتادة والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير,الربيع بن أنس والسدي وقتادة والضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Th); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT-; IBNKATHIR-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 7:11/q04/p5 Each person is created in the womb and then fashioned there with hearing, sight, fingers or other organs. — Al-Kalbī, Maʿmar, Yamān and other reports specify opening sensory faculties and fingers. This reading generates the question of how the ensuing command can follow descendant formation. [14 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(آخرون,عمن ذكره); THALABI(يمان); MAWARDI-FULL(معمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(يمان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(معمر بن راشد); TABRISI(يمان); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(معمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(معمر بن راشد); SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(الكلبي); ALUSI-FULL(يمان); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 7:11/q04/p6 People were created in Adam's back, then fashioned in wombs. — Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted through ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Ḥārith and elsewhere; ʿAṭāʾ also gives this allocation. It differs from placing both acts in Adam's back. [5 notes: THALABI(عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحارث عن ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)]
- 7:11/q04/p7 God created the spirits, then fashioned the bodies. — Qāḍī Abū Yaʿlā supplies omitted 'spirits' and 'bodies'; Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān also transmit this distinction. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القاضي أبو يعلى)]
- 7:11/q04/p8 The words describe the development of the human species and each individual from initial formless matter to completed form. — Elmalılı includes clay and seed in fathers’ loins, with Adam representing the species; Asad includes embryo and evolution; Kur'an Yolu prefers collective species development, including intellectual maturity. [9 notes: ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 7:11/q04/p9 People were created in Adam's back and fashioned as particles at the primordial covenant. — Bāghawī gives this view; Mujāhid’s account is judged best by al-Naḥḥās, who cites 7:172. Qurṭubī reports the covenant hadith. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)+]
- 7:11/q04/p10 Adam and Eve were created and then formed as the two parents. — Al-Ḥasan says Adam was from earth and Eve from his rib; al-Zajjāj also notes Eve's rib and shaping. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 7:11/q04/p11 God created your spouses, then fashioned your bodies. — Al-Samīn attributes this unusual interpretation to Qāḍī Abū Yaʿlā. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(القاضي أبو يعلى)]
- 7:11/q04/p12 Creation is God's prior determination, and fashioning is the inscription of future forms in the Preserved Tablet. — Rāzī calls this closer than alternatives: after decree and inscription God actually created Adam and commanded prostration. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 7:11/q04/p13 Adam was created and formed in Paradise, and descendants were formed in his loins at the covenant. — Abū Ḥayyān transmits this as a chronological report from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 7:11/q04/p14 God intended to create and fashion humanity, stated prospectively before Adam's actual creation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares prospective wording in the angels' report before Lot's departure (51:35). [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:11/q05 Why is humanity addressed in the plural when Adam is named?  [خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ صَوَّرْنَٰكُمْ لِءَادَمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether ancestry licenses representative address or the address literally includes the species.
- 7:11/q05/p1 An ancestor's creation and honor can be attributed to descendants, since Adam is their origin. — Ṭabarī compares 2:63; Ṭabrisī and Rāzī likewise cite ancestral covenant language. Ibn Kathīr compares 2:57; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 69:11. A tribal poem about Dhī Qār illustrates speaking to descendants about ancestors. Some explain plural by Adam as leader or an honorific plural. [28 notes: AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(يونس); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس والربيع بن أنس وقتادة والضحاك); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); THALABI(يونس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:11/q05/p2 The address directly encompasses the whole human race, with Adam its representative and bearer of its potential traits. — Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu make Adam the species’ exemplar; Asad says Adam symbolizes humanity. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects bare kinship as enough to transfer Adam’s creation to descendants, because the verse names Adam for prostration. [11 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:11/q05/p3 The immediate addressees are Quraysh or polytheists within a universal human address. — Iṣlāḥī specifies Quraysh and their ordained trials; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the address covers all humanity but especially the polytheists targeted by the sūra. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:11/q06 What does each ثُمَّ express, and how does the order work?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: How to reconcile human formation with the earlier command to prostrate.
- 7:11/q06/p1 Here ثم may function like و, coordinating without chronological delay. — Al-Akhfash adduces a father-and-mother poetic line; al-Zajjāj and Basran grammarians reject the analogy, while Bāghawī reports it for both particles. [15 notes: AKHFASH; THALABI(الأخفش,يونس); MAWARDI-FULL(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأخفش); TABRISI(الأخفش); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش); TABATABAI(الأخفش); TAB-FULL-; ZAJJAJ(الأخفش)-; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)-; TABRISI(الزجاج)-; TABATABAI(الزجاج)-]
- 7:11/q06/p2 It marks real temporal succession: Adam was created, formed, and then the angels were commanded. — Ṭabarī rejects metrical poetic ثم = و and unsupported inversion; al-Zajjāj appeals to al-Khalīl and Sībawayh. Zamakhsharī, Nasafī and others use the following command to identify Adam; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says early readings retain interval. Ālūsī distinguishes an earlier conditional order in 15:29 from the enacted command after formation. [21 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الحسن ويوسف النخوي); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش); TABATABAI(الحسن,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 7:11/q06/p3 The later ثم orders the telling: after describing humanity, God tells them about His prior command. — ʿAlī b. ʿĪsā and al-Sīrāfī endorse narrative order; Abū Ḥayyān calls it easier for an all-human address. Al-Samīn calls the version with both particles narrative order of little explanatory gain; Ibn ʿAṭiyya applies this to womb formation. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(علي بن عيسى والقاضي أبو سعيد السيرافي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(العوفي عن ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(جماعة من النحويين); TABATABAI(جماعة من النحويين); THALABI]
- 7:11/q06/p4 The statement is reordered: Adam was created, prostration commanded, and descendants then fashioned. — Al-Thaʿlabī, Māwardī, Baghawī and Qurṭubī report fronting and postponement; Ṭabarī rejects it without contextual evidence. [5 notes: THALABI(قوم); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(غير مسمى); TAB-FULL-]
- 7:11/q06/p5 ثم can mark rise in rank rather than elapsed time. — Al-Ṭībī ranks Adam’s honor through angelic prostration above his creation, connected with knowledge in 2:31; Ibn ʿĀshūr ranks both human imaging and prostration above preceding stages; al-Zarkashī allows non-temporal progression in qualities. [6 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(الطيبي); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 7:11/q06/p6 The sequence can include gradual initial and final formation across Adam and each person. — Al-Zarkashī reports Adam’s beginning in clay and every person's beginning in a drop (23:13). Biqāʿī reads preparation of a form before its realization; the embryo reports of forty and forty-two days are reconciled on that basis. [7 notes: BURHAN(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 7:11/q06/p7 The first ثم is chronological but the second marks narrative order. — Al-Samīn lists the mixed explanation; he also reports first-clause Adam and second-clause descendants within that family of readings. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 7:11/q07 How is مَلَٰٓئِكَةِ analyzed linguistically?  [لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ · grammar]
- 7:11/q07/p1 Its form is analyzed as مفاعلة or معافلة; malak commonly drops an original hamza. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya refers to earlier derivation in al-Baqara; Sībawayh cites malʾak, maʾlaka and malʾaka for 'message' while noting common hamza loss. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; SIBAWAYH]

## 7:11/q08 Which angels were commanded to bow?  [لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the definite plural denotes every angel, those present on earth, or another group.
- 7:11/q08/p1 All angels were addressed and bowed. — Bursevī says general wording lacks a restricting proof; Biqāʿī includes angels of heaven and earth; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 15:30. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows all but says the generic article alone does not require it. [4 notes: BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:11/q08/p2 The angels on earth or present where Adam was created were addressed. — Muqātil includes Iblis among the earthly group; Abū al-ʿĀliya reports earthly angels; some argue heavenly angels bow only to God. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿAbbās on those present. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı yorumcular); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:11/q08/p3 The angels of the heavens and Throne were addressed. — Rāzī records this side of the dispute without choosing. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 7:11/q08/p4 The angels represent forces of nature. — Kur'an Yolu reports Süleyman Ateş’s proposal. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Süleyman Ateş)]
- 7:11/q08/p5 The evidence does not fix which angels bowed. — Kur'an Yolu prefers restraint because this unseen matter has no sound transmitted proof. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]

## 7:11/q09 What readings or vowel analyses are noted around the command?  [صَوَّرْنَٰكُمْ لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ ٱسْجُدُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether proposed changes have transmitted or grammatical support.
- 7:11/q09/p1 The standard reading is unanimously reported for the indexed word. — Qiraat-ER records no canonical variant for its indexed word. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 7:11/q09/p2 A proposed nominative malāʾika would make it a nominal subject. — Qiraat-ER gives this hypothetical restructuring against its ordinary genitive governed by لِ. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vhr)]
- 7:11/q09/p3 Abū Jaʿfar read a ḍamma on the preceding hāʾ by transferring the imperative hamza's vowel. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports it as weak; Ibn Jinnī rejects transfer because hamzat al-waṣl disappears in connected speech. His discussion contrasts alphabetic openings, 3:1 and 68:1, normal transfer before a sound consonant, and rare anomalous cases; a pause before اسجدوا cannot complete قلنا للملائكة. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو جعفر بن القعقاع); IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو الفتح)-]

## 7:11/q10 What does ٱسْجُدُوا۟ mean?  [ٱسْجُدُوا۟ · meaning]
- 7:11/q10/p1 Its root means lowering oneself; ritual usage is placing the forehead on the ground. — Jishumī cites poetry about hillocks beneath hooves; Ṭabrisī gives the same basic and bodily senses. [2 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI]

## 7:11/q11 To whom, and for what purpose, was the prostration?  [ٱسْجُدُوا۟ لِءَادَمَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Adam was the object honored, a prayer direction, or a symbol of humanity.
- 7:11/q11/p1 It honored Adam while obeying and worshipping God. — Ibn ʿAbbās distinguishes direction to Adam from obedience to God. Al-Ḥasan says honor reflects God's knowledge and will. Joseph’s family bowing in 12:100 is a parallel; Bursevī says worshipful prostration belongs only to God. Ṭabāṭabāʾī grounds the honor in divine spirit and care, not clay. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الحسن); SULAMI(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 7:11/q11/p2 The prostration was to God, with Adam as the qibla or direction. — Māwardī, Rāzī and Study Quran report this solution to worship being due only to God; Ṭabāṭabāʾī extends Adam’s representative direction to all humanity and compares the Kaʿba. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI]
- 7:11/q11/p3 The command honored the entire human species through Adam. — Elmalılı stresses descendants, including future prophets, and regards Adam as a universal exemplar; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kur'an Yolu say his honor is a favor to humanity; Study Quran says primordial humanity may thereby stand above angels. [10 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:11/q11/p4 It was a gesture of reverence or symbolic honor, not worship of Adam. — Rāzī reports a distinction between reverence and a direct prostration to Adam; Asad reads the command allegorically. Study Quran notes symbolic readings. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R); ASAD-NOTES]
- 7:11/q11/p5 Adam himself was the one prostrated to. — Rāzī reports this as a third view; the verse's لآدم names the recipient. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 7:11/q11/p6 The angels were commanded to serve Adam with submission and compliance. — Elmalılı glosses the prostration as service and obedience. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 7:11/q12 When and how did the angels prostrate?  [فَسَجَدُوٓا۟ · interpretation]
- 7:11/q12/p1 They obeyed immediately and together, after Adam was complete. — Bursevī stresses no delay or hesitation; Ālūsī says all together; Biqāʿī places the command after Adam stood upright with a spirit. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 7:11/q13 Why is Adam called ءَادَمَ?  [لِءَادَمَ · meaning]
- 7:11/q13/p1 His name derives from adīm, the earth's surface. — Ibn ʿAbbās says his soil was red, white and black; Saʿīd b. Jubayr transmits the same etymology. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,ابن عباس؛ سعيد بن جبير)]

## 7:11/q14 What is Iblis's name or title and its derivation?  [إِبْلِيسَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name is Arabic-derived or Greek-derived.
- 7:11/q14/p1 Iblis is a title of the jinn rebel; his earlier name was ʿAzāzīl or another name. — Ibn ʿAbbās names ʿAzāzīl and says he had four wings and high status before falling; al-Ḥasan says he bore another name before disobedience. Iṣlāḥī calls Iblis the title of the jinn who deceived Adam. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:11/q14/p2 Iblis is associated with despair of good, from Arabic ablasa. — Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī give the despair derivation. Asad proposes that Greek diabolos Hellenized Arabic Iblis and argues against an unproven Greek borrowing by Arabs. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); ASAD-NOTES]
- 7:11/q14/p3 The name derives from Greek diabolos. — Asad reports that Western scholars commonly hold this; he disputes the alleged direction of borrowing. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Western scholars); ASAD-NOTES-]

## 7:11/q15 Was Iblis an angel or a jinn?  [إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ · theology]
turns on: Whether inclusion in the angelic command proves membership in their kind.
- 7:11/q15/p1 He was one of the angels before his fall. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls him honored and four-winged; some infer angelhood from his exception among them; al-Ṭayyār argues from inclusion in their command. Study Quran observes the apparent support but notes 18:50. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İblîs’in daha önce melekler arasında yer aldığı sonucunu çıkaranlar); TABATABAI(الطيار); STUDYQURAN]
- 7:11/q15/p2 Iblis was a jinn, distinct in kind from angels, though included in their company or command. — Al-Ḥasan cites his fiery creation versus angels' light and their obedience; 18:50 and 66:6 are cited by Kur'an Yolu. Al-Ṣādiq says angels mistook him for one of them; an inclusive address can include outsiders, like hypocrites under 'believers.' Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds one command including Iblis. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام,القائلون بالاستثناء المنفصل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 7:11/q15/p3 His classification is disputed or not settled by the exception alone. — Qurṭubī refers to his al-Baqara discussion; Rāzī reports both sides; Study Quran cites the tension between apparent inclusion and 18:50. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 7:11/q16 How does إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ work as an exception?  [إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether he belonged to the group excepted from or was separately included among those ordered.
- 7:11/q16/p1 It is a detached exception: Iblis is outside the angels' kind yet was commanded. — Al-Zajjāj cites 7:12 as proof he was commanded; Bursevī glosses إلا as لكن. An alternative posits a separate command, while Ṭabāṭabāʾī counters that 7:12 suggests a single command. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(القائلون بالاستثناء المنفصل)]
- 7:11/q16/p2 It is a connected exception from those commanded, or by predominance from the angelic company. — Ālūsī prefers this whether Iblis was an angel or a jinn counted among many angels; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says otherwise a separate command must be posited weakly. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the predominance view; Ibn ʿĀshūr credits al-Sakkākī. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(غير الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI(القائلون بالاستثناء المتصل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI(القائلون بالاستثناء المنفصل)-]
- 7:11/q16/p3 The exception alone does not establish membership of the angelic kind. — Al-Ṣādiq compares inclusive address to believers encompassing hypocrites; Ibn ʿĀshūr uses social mixing and predominance. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:11/q17 What is the syntax and force of لَمْ يَكُن مِّنَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ?  [لَمْ يَكُن مِّنَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause is independent or circumstantial, and whether it adds force to the exception.
- 7:11/q17/p1 It emphatically restates the exception and strengthens denial. — Al-Samīn reports emphatic restatement; Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'not among the prostrators' is stronger than 'did not prostrate' (6:56). The classical poem in al-Mufaḍḍaliyyāt illustrates the same negated-category form. [9 notes: MUFADDALIYYAT(عبد يغوث بن وقاص الحارثي); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 7:11/q17/p2 It declares that Iblis never joined the prostrators, with no expectation of later compliance. — Ālūsī records the stronger universal reading and a criticism of its reach; Ibn ʿĀshūr sees a lasting disposition to disobey and preparation for the next interrogation. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 7:11/q17/p3 It is an independent explanatory clause answering an implied question. — Al-Samīn places the pause after Iblis and compares أبى in 2:34; Abū Ḥayyān says it has no syntactic position and reinforces the exclusion. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:11/q17/p4 It is a circumstantial clause describing Iblis as refusing. — Abū al-Baqāʾ supplies this parse; Ibn ʿĀshūr also treats it as circumstantial and reinforcing. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:11/q17/p5 It makes Iblis's refusal explicit for public rebuke. — Ālūsī says indirect inference would be weak for the enormity charged. Ibn al-Kamāl ties the addition to a detached exception; Ālūsī rejects that because connectedness is not the only route to opposite meaning. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن الكمال); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 7:11/q18 What does an exception from a negative establish?  [إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ لَمْ يَكُن مِّنَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the opposite ruling is explicit, indicated, or inferred.
- 7:11/q18/p1 The opposite ruling follows by explicit wording. — Ālūsī attributes this to al-Shāfiʿī. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 7:11/q18/p2 It follows by indication. — Ālūsī attributes this to a group of Ḥanafīs. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(طائفة من الحنفية)]
- 7:11/q18/p3 The excluded case remains unspoken and is resolved by contrary implication. — Ālūsī reports this third account. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(آخرون)]
- 7:11/q18/p4 Its force may be explicit or by indication depending on context. — Ālūsī cites the author of al-Baḥr. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب البحر)]

## 7:11/q19 Why does ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ take a sound masculine plural?  [ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · grammar]
- 7:11/q19/p1 It is an adjective describing persons and may take the sound masculine plural in wāw and nūn. — Sībawayh gives الساجدين as an example of the plural pattern for human descriptive adjectives. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 7:11/q20 What does the angels' obedience and Iblis's refusal teach?  [فَسَجَدُوٓا۟ إِلَّآ إِبْلِيسَ · theology]
- 7:11/q20/p1 God's command tests obedience, exposing pride and rebellion. — Ṭabarī says the command distinguished obedient from disobedient; Jishumī contrasts praised angels and blamed Iblis. Rāzī says Iblis's claimed material superiority cannot set aside the command, especially since angels obeyed. Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees Iblis's arrogant refusal as the sin and describes the story as real relations in familiar social terms. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(المعترضون); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 7:11/q20/p2 The prostration exhibits Adam's great rank and human dignity. — Jishumī's Zaydī authorities infer descent-based virtue for the Prophet's family; Study Quran suggests primordial humanity may surpass angels. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu stress human dignity and gratitude. [6 notes: JISHUMI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:11/q20/p3 Angels are by nature obedient; Iblis's contrary disposition emerged at this command. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the angels' obedience is not a legislative test like Iblis's; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Iblis's latent tendency appeared when desire was crossed. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 7:11/q20/p4 God needs no worship; the command displays His independence. — Abū Ḥafṣ says worship adds nothing to God's kingdom before creation, after destruction, or at resurrection. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص)]

## 7:11/q21 What reports or ancillary legal issues accompany human formation?  [صَوَّرْنَٰكُمْ · reports]
- 7:11/q21/p1 Embryonic development proceeds through reported stages and angelic action. — Biqāʿī cites Ibn Masʿūd: forty days, then comparable clot and morsel stages before spirit; Muslim's forty-two-night report says an angel forms faculties and records sex and decree. Biqāʿī reconciles 'forms' as preparatory work. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 7:11/q21/p2 The embryo's protected status and permissibility of expulsion were disputed. — Biqāʿī notes the dispute without setting out positions. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 7:11/q22 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage and other Adam accounts?  [* · links]
turns on: Which theme in the sūra explains the presentation of creation and Iblis.
- 7:11/q22/p1 It recalls gifts to humanity and warns against ingratitude after earthly establishment. — Rāzī compares 2:28; Qurṭubī says the passage turns to beginnings; Biqāʿī joins provision, Adam's favor and Iblis's fall. Ibn ʿĀshūr links 7:10 and the warning in 7:27–28; Kur'an Yolu targets polytheist ingratitude; Elmalılı calls worship of another especially abasing after this honor. [20 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 7:11/q22/p2 This is one of several Qurʾānic tellings of Adam's creation and fall. — Rāzī lists seven locations: 2, 7, 15, 17, 18, 20, 38. Study Quran calls this the second in textual order and locates 7:11–27 as the account; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn refer to earlier discussion at 2:34. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; STUDYQURAN]
- 7:11/q22/p3 It explains the primordial hostility between Adam's descendants and Satan. — Ibn Kathīr urges caution toward humanity's envious enemy; Iṣlāḥī calls the episode the history and cause of that hostility. Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates prostration, Garden and descent in one story ending at 7:24. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 7:11/q22/p4 Al-Aʿrāf's punishment and ingratitude setting shapes its wording differently from al-Baqara and Ṣād. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Baqara's honor and knowledge test, its refusal/arrogance/unbelief formula and accepted repentance, with al-Aʿrāf's rebuke, creation opening, plea and angel/Iblis contrast; Ṣād begins differently. He relates Iblis's prediction about gratitude to 7:10,17. [14 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 7:11/q22/p5 The passage depicts humanity's creation and place on earth through one connected narrative. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says fifteen verses expand 7:10; he treats the social language as representing created relations and notes the expulsion from a rank or station. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the angels' presence cannot prove Adam's creation in their realm; he reports the apparent Paradise reading but calls the verse indecisive. [8 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 7:11/q23 What inward readings of creation and prostration are offered?  [* · inward]
- 7:11/q23/p1 Created forms have distinct modes of life, and devotion is ordered by sincerity and knowledge. — Sulamī reports hearts living by witnessing, selves by existence, elect by sincerity, the afterlife by knowledge, and worldly life by ignorance and delusion. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 7:11/q23/p2 The prostration obligation honors Adam while the command to depart humiliates Iblis. — Sulamī reports this paired reading, which also distinguishes obedience to God from honor to Adam. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 7:11/q24 Which supplied notes discuss other subjects without interpreting 7:11?  [* · links]
- 7:11/q24/p1 These notes concern separate Arabic grammar, history, hadith, rhetoric, and recitation topics; they make no claim about the meaning of this verse. — Sībawayh’s #623, #598, #788, #605, #608, and #603 treat weak-letter diminutives, hamza substitutions and inversions, plurals, deletions and analogical forms; #69 treats apposition, partitives, predicates, circumstances and object slots, with examples from 3:97, 7:75, 2:251, 8:37 and poetry. Majāz glosses extra lā in 7:12; al-Zajjāj disputes a hamza derivation of maṣāʾib. Ibn Hishām recounts Lakhniʿa and Dhū Nuwās, rival Quraysh genealogies and q-r-sh etymologies, and the Trench; al-Azraqī collects Black Stone origins, colors, healings and Last Day testimony. Ibn Ḥibbān records al-Rumayṣāʾ in Paradise and the virtues of Prophetic knowledge. Al-Bāqillānī debates the Quran's intrinsic inimitability against ṣarfa; al-Jurjānī analyzes transferred senses, exception and restriction, and whether new wording can claim an old meaning; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ critiques al-Bāqillānī’s method. Al-Nashr records hadith chains, hamza easing, reading routes, and the benefits of multiple readings; al-Itqān treats accepted reading conditions, the ten readers, and suitable recitation amounts. One Ṭabāṭabāʾī note disputes an unsupported consensus on jinn and angels as mutable airy bodies. None of these notes supplies an answer about the wording or events of 7:11.
