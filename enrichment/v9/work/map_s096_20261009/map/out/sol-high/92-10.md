# 92:10: 16 questions

## 92:10/q01 What does God’s facilitating this person involve?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether taysīr directly prepares deeds, withdraws help, guides to an end, or rhetorically attributes the outcome to its cause.
- 92:10/q01/p1 God prepares or enables the denier for evil deeds and a course ending in punishment. — Al-Ṭabarī says these are deeds God dislikes; Ibn ʿAbbās says God directs him to evil. Al-Baghawī and al-Biqāʿī make divine displeasure and Hell the end. Ibn Kathīr cites 6:110 on hearts and sight turned away; al-Tustarī calls these the deeds of Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ibn ʿAṭiyya describe gradual or continuing involvement in deeds, and al-Alūsī explicitly prefers preparation for obedience or disobedience with their dispositions. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this unfolds gradually, as fixed in prior knowledge. Sayyid Quṭb says the rejecter stumbles ever farther from guidance while imagining success. [56 notes: FARRA; TUSTARI; TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(author وابن مسعود); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA; QUTB-ZILAL; ALUSI+]
- 92:10/q01/p2 God withdraws assistance and leaves the person to himself, making obedience and faith difficult. — Al-Zamakhsharī cites 6:125, the constricted heart; al-Rāzī reports al-Qaffāl's account of withheld graces. ʿAṭāʾ identifies prevention of faith in God and His messenger. Muqātil says giving good becomes difficult. Islahi says apparent smooth progress lasts until death, when 74:17's steep hardship arrives. Abū Ḥayyān and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ flag the withholding-grace formulation as Muʿtazilī; al-Rāzī rejects al-Qaffāl's alternatives as departing from the apparent sense. [22 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء,مقاتل); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(القفال); QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن الضحاك,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-; ABUHAYYAN-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; BINTSHATI-]
- 92:10/q01/p3 God guides the person in the afterlife to Hell's path or hastens entry into Hell. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, and al-Alūsī give this as an alternative paired with guidance to Paradise. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the lām as purpose and cites 50:44 for swift entry. [10 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:10/q01/p4 God abandons the person to a downward inward course from humanity into nature. — Al-Qāshānī says the person sinks into the abyss of nature and frustrated desires. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 92:10/q01/p5 The wording is a divine judgment or an attribution to a cause, rather than direct enabling. — Al-Rāzī reports al-Qaffāl's analogies: idols are said to lead people astray at 14:36, and God reports the eventual judgment. Al-Rāzī rejects these as contrary to the apparent sense and decisive reason. [2 notes: RAZI; RAZI-]

## 92:10/q02 What does the verb yassara mean here?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb retains literal ease or extends to readiness for an outcome.
- 92:10/q02/p1 It means making easy or accessible. — Ibn ʿĀshūr defines the verb as making something easy to obtain and distinguishes what is made easy from its beneficiary; his literal reading can mean persistence in action. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:10/q02/p2 It means preparing or making ready. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Farrāʾ cite Arab speech about sheep giving birth or ready to give birth, with a poem using yassarat. Abū Ḥayyān says preparation works for both ease and hardship; al-Alūsī derives it from ease, and Ibn ʿĀshūr makes it metaphorical through preparation's connection with accessibility. [14 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 92:10/q03 What does the future marker fa-sa convey?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the future denotes emphasis, deferred judgment, ongoing conduct, or a possible change in conduct.
- 92:10/q03/p1 It emphasizes a certain and gentle divine enabling. — Al-Rāzī gives this as his first reading and compares the promise of becoming God-fearing in 2:21; al-Alūsī also permits emphasis. [4 notes: RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:10/q03/p2 It points to delayed recompense, chiefly in the afterlife. — Al-Rāzī says its time is known only to God; al-Alūsī says most promised requital is still future. [4 notes: RAZI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 92:10/q03/p3 It signals continuing facilitation from now until death. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Joseph 12:98 and observes that giving and withholding already precede this future facilitation, so their deeds continue and increase. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:10/q03/p4 It allows a future change of conduct, including a sinner's repentance or an obedient person's disobedience. — Al-Rāzī reports this as a second reading. [2 notes: RAZI]

## 92:10/q04 How does the verb's object relate grammatically to li-l-ʿusrā?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ لِلْعُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether lām expresses the purpose of metaphorical preparation or the clause uses inversion with the verb literal.
- 92:10/q04/p1 The person is the object and the lām gives purpose: God prepares him for the end. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the ordinary pattern makes the facilitated thing the object and the beneficiary the lām complement, citing 20:26 and 54:17; here he permits a figurative verb and purposive lām. [7 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:10/q04/p2 The construction reverses the expected roles to intensify facilitation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the idiom of presenting a she-camel to a watering trough and keeps the verb literal. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 92:10/q04/p3 The parallel harder clause can omit an expressed preposition. — Al-Ṭabarī notes the formal parallel with the preceding easing for the easier path. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 92:10/q05 What does al-ʿusrā denote?  [لِلْعُسْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the adjective names present deeds, obstructed obedience, the resulting condition, or the final destination.
- 92:10/q05/p1 The evil course: sin, disobedience, or deeds that bring divine anger. — Ibn ʿAbbās calls it evil from God; Ibn Kathīr cites 6:110. Al-Rāzī additionally reports shirk or a return to withholding financial rights. Al-Biqāʿī calls it the hardest trait leading to Hell. Al-Alūsī favors deeds and dispositions; Study Quran says evil deeds can open further evil until return is harder. [25 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(عكرمة عن ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL(author وابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI+]
- 92:10/q05/p2 Hell or the Fire as the destination or its route. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Ibn Masʿūd say the Fire; the palm and Bilāl accounts likewise locate the miser there. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, and al-Alūsī specify the afterlife route to it; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows Hell as a place of severe hardship. [28 notes: NAHHAS(الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI(ابن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(author وابن مسعود); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; DURR(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 92:10/q05/p3 A difficult or miserable condition and outcome in this life, the next, or both. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say a bad condition in both worlds; al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī explain the name by its difficult outcome. Al-Rāzī gives hardship among its senses. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 74:9–10 for the grievous Day, while Bint al-Shāṭiʾ traces Qur'anic ʿusr words to resurrection. [22 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 92:10/q05/p4 Difficulty in doing good, including faith, righteousness, and giving. — ʿAṭāʾ names faith in God and His messenger; Muqātil names giving good. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī speak of difficult causes of righteousness; al-Zamakhsharī cites 6:125 on a constricted heart. [18 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء,مقاتل); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس عن الضحاك,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 92:10/q06 What forms and readings of al-ʿusrā are noted?  [لِلْعُسْرَىٰ · readings]
turns on: The vowel on the sīn.
- 92:10/q06/p1 The standard noun form is read without a ḍamma on the sīn. — The Qirāʾāt source notes the ordinary form. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 92:10/q06/p2 A reading adds ḍamma to the sīn for a fuller vocalization of the same noun pattern. — The Qirāʾāt source presents it as vocalization of the same pattern. Al-Nashr attributes ḍamma on the sīn of al-yusr and al-ʿusr to Abū ʿAmr, including dhū ʿusra and the paired al-ʿusra/al-yusra forms, and to Abū Jaʿfar, cross-referencing this verse and related forms elsewhere. [3 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR(أبو جعفر,أبو عمرو)]

## 92:10/q07 How are al-ʿusrā's feminine form and definiteness explained?  [لِلْعُسْرَىٰ · grammar]
- 92:10/q07/p1 The feminine form can imply a way, deed group, condition, place, or extreme degree of hardship. — Al-Rāzī lists a group of deeds, khilla or faʿla, a prior act, or a feminine way. Al-Alūsī says the form mirrors al-yusrā. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a state or place. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrasts ʿusrā/yusrā with verbal nouns as intensified forms, comparing 79:25 and 92:14. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 92:10/q07/p2 The definite article can mark a class or a previously known referent. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the choice depends on interpretation. [2 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:10/q08 How can making hardship easy be expressed?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ لِلْعُسْرَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the apparent contradiction is paired idiom, naming by outcome, or deliberate intensification.
- 92:10/q08/p1 The matching clauses extend the language of ease to the opposite fate, as an ironic or emphatic warning. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, al-Thaʿlabī, and al-Qurṭubī compare 'give them glad tidings of painful punishment' at 3:21; al-Naḥḥās cites Sībawayh's poem calling a painful beating a greeting. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites similar ironic announcements at 9:34, 4:138, 9:3, 31:7, 45:8, 3:21, and 84:24. Corpus Coranicum notes near homophony veils the outcome. [12 notes: FARRA; TAB; NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI; QURTUBI(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:10/q08/p2 The paths receive their names from the ease or hardship of their eventual outcomes. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī report this; al-Rāzī attributes it to al-Qaffāl, Ibn ʿAṭiyya says deeds are named by their ends, and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise allows this reading. [9 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR(ابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)]
- 92:10/q08/p3 The choice of al-ʿusrā carries substantive warning, beyond rhyme. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects a rhyme-only explanation: the unique Qur'anic form and the related usages point to resurrection and maximal hardship. [4 notes: BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI+]

## 92:10/q09 Who is the person facilitated toward hardship in reports about the passage?  [فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Which occasion of revelation or named pair the reports identify; several sources also apply the wording generally.
- 92:10/q09/p1 The stingy owner of a date palm refused the Prophet's offer and another believer obtained it for a poor household. — ʿAṭāʾ names Abū al-Daḥdāḥ as buyer; Ibn ʿAbbās says the exchange involved forty palms. The owner let fallen dates reach poor children but took them back. Al-Baghawī calls the owner an Anṣārī. Ibn Kathīr calls Ibn Abī Ḥātim's report very unusual; versions differ over garden versus forty palms and the exact donation. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(عطاء); BAGHAWI(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI]
- 92:10/q09/p2 Umayya ibn Khalaf contrasts with Abū Bakr, who bought and freed Bilāl. — Al-Baghawī's versions give a cloak or garment and ten coins or ten awqiya. Ibn Masʿūd's version also names Ubayy among the opposed efforts, and takes the good as monotheism or testimony of faith. [4 notes: BAGHAWI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 92:10/q09/p3 Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥarb is the miser contrasted with Abū Bakr. — Al-Zamakhsharī reports the pair; Ibn ʿAbbās is transmitted through al-Kalbī and Abū Ṣāliḥ in al-Durr, repeated for several verses. [7 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح)]
- 92:10/q09/p4 The wording applies generally to people of the described conduct, including any named occasion. — Al-Alūsī says general wording governs while named people remain included; Kur'an Yolu likewise reports an Abū Bakr occasion for verses 5–19 but says the message and rulings are general. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirler)]

## 92:10/q10 What do the reports teach about written destiny and human action?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether creation for a destined course entails particular deeds, and how divine knowledge relates to the command to act.
- 92:10/q10/p1 Each destination and its deeds are decreed, yet people must act; each is enabled for the deeds appointed to them. — ʿAlī's funeral report locates the exchange at Baqīʿ; variants name a Bedouin, two youths, Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, Jābir, Surāqa, and Abū al-Dardāʾ. The youths answer that they will strive. Al-Tirmidhī grades a version ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ; Ibn Kathīr says Aḥmad alone has the Abū al-Dardāʾ route. Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān's question follows 54:49. Al-Rāzī says the answer fits God's unchangeable knowledge; al-Biqāʿī says the blessed and wretched were designated eternally. [48 notes: TAB(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بشير بن كعب,جابر بن عبد الله,علي); IBNHIBBAN(شعبة,علي بن أبي طالب); BAGHAWI(علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(علي); QURTUBI-FULL(الغلامان,علي عن النبي,غلامان شابان عن النبي); RAZI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن علي عليه السلام); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI(رسول الله); BIQAI-FULL(النبي); DURR(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب); DURR-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن السلمي,جابر بن عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,علي بن أبي طالب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI(علي بن أبي طالب); ALUSI-FULL(علي بن أبي طالب); IBNASHUR(علي); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet); RAZI+]
- 92:10/q10/p2 The hadith's creation purpose is worship, so it does not establish fixed evil deeds. — Al-Qaffāl cites 51:56, that jinn and humans were created only to worship. Al-Rāzī rejects this answer as weak against the Prophet's instruction to act according to God's unchangeable knowledge. [1 notes: RAZI(القفال); RAZI-]
- 92:10/q10/p3 Written destiny denotes God's exact prior knowledge; deeds through death reveal it and are the means to one's end. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes revealed commands from hidden knowledge. - 92:10/q10/p4 The verses are used by the Prophet when describing Paradise and urging work. — Al-Burhān notes the Prophet paired al-Layl with the call to work, 32:17 with unseen delight, and 56:30 with a tree's long shade. [1 notes: BURHAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 92:10/q11 What report connects giving and withholding with this passage?  [* · reports]
- 92:10/q11/p1 Angels pray for replacement for each giver and loss for each withholder. — Abū al-Dardāʾ transmits the Prophet's saying: two angels call at sunrise, or morning and evening in al-Qurṭubī, and the giving and withholding verses are recited. ## 92:10/q12 What ethical or legal point is drawn from this contrast?  [* · law]
- 92:10/q12/p1 Generosity is praiseworthy and miserliness blameworthy when giving is due. — Al-Qurṭubī cites 2:3 and 2:274. He defines generosity by giving where appropriate and miserliness by withholding what is due; praise, blame, reward, and punishment determine the judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says giving is singled out to urge Muslims to give, distinguishing them from polytheists. [5 notes: QURTUBI(العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 92:10/q12/p2 Blameworthy, unrewarded spending is waste and may justify legal restraint. — Al-Qurṭubī calls such spenders brothers of devils and subject to interdiction. [2 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:10/q12/p3 Prudent withholding can merit praise and qualify a person to manage others' property. — Al-Qurṭubī distinguishes restraint that brings no blame or punishment from miserliness. [2 notes: QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 92:10/q12/p4 Since worldly ease and next-world hardship both belong to God, people should follow His commands. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī notes that ease and hardship can occur in either life. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 92:10/q13 How does the verse relate to its surrounding verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection concerns the mirrored clauses, the later Fire image, the preceding sūra, or other verses.
- 92:10/q13/p1 It completes the second half of a structured contrast between conduct and recompense. — Al-Irshād says giving and withholding lead their clauses because they ground subsequent piety, belief, denial, or self-sufficiency. Ibn ʿĀshūr names combination with division and four antitheses. Corpus Coranicum notes mirrored conduct-to-result sequences, cross-rhyme, near-homophonous ease/hardship, and the first-person plural identifying God's promised recompense; its final stanza reverses the order. Neuwirth places verse 11 with verses 12–13. [14 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL(الإرشاد); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM(Neuwirth); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 92:10/q13/p2 The later Fire warning makes the earlier difficult outcome explicit. — Al-Farrāʾ compares 92:14's blazing Fire; Corpus Coranicum says verse 14 resolves the enigmatic threat of verse 10. [2 notes: FARRA; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 92:10/q13/p3 The sūra unfolds the preceding sūra's success and failure through the traits leading to Fire or safety. — Abū Ḥayyān cites 91:9–10 and the later warning. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 92:10/q13/p4 The ease/hardship pairing echoes other Qur'anic promises and contrasts. — Corpus Coranicum compares 94:5–6 and the messenger-directed promise in 87:8. Ibn ʿĀshūr also notes that the recited verse can explain the hadith's facilitation or support its command to act. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 92:10/q14 What does the contrasting easy course denote?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the easier counterpart means a beneficial condition generally or Paradise specifically.
- 92:10/q14/p1 It is a good and pleasing condition, broadly what benefits or suits a person without hardship. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya places the condition in this life and the next; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the feminine form may mark an afterlife condition or place and broadens the sense to what suits its possessor. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNASHUR]
- 92:10/q14/p2 It denotes Paradise. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this from Zayd ibn Aslam and Mujāhid. [1 notes: IBNASHUR(زيد بن أسلم ومجاهد)]

## 92:10/q15 Why does the contrast use divine plural speech for both paths?  [* · rhetoric]
- 92:10/q15/p1 The majestic plural marks the remoteness of either course. — Al-Biqāʿī says good is surrounded by hardship, while reason and original nature check evil. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 92:10/q16 What conduct characterizes the miser in this contrast?  [* · interpretation]
- 92:10/q16/p1 He withholds God's due, considers himself self-sufficient, and denies the promise. — Al-Durr reports this explanation from Qatāda, paired with the giver who pays God's due, avoids prohibitions, and believes the promise. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
