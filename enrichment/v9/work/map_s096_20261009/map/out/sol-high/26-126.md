# 26:126: 11 questions

## 26:126/q01 What does fearing God require here?  [فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes variously explain God-fearing through worship, obedience and moral reform, acceptance of the messenger, fear of punishment, the people's particular excesses, or an inward spiritual discipline.
- 26:126/q01/p1 Obey God and turn from disobedience toward praiseworthy conduct. — Al-Tabari specifies following God's commands and prohibitions. The Qur'an Yolu commentary renders it as avoiding disobedience. Sulami reports the view that it means leaving everything blameworthy and turning to everything praiseworthy. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; SULAMI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:126/q01/p2 Worship God. — Muqatil glosses the command to fear God as worshipping Him. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 26:126/q01/p3 Do not reject the trustworthy messenger. — Al-Nasafi applies God-fearing to the people's response to Hūd as a trustworthy messenger. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 26:126/q01/p4 Fear God's punishment. — Al-Bursevi specifies punishment as what the people are to fear. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 26:126/q01/p5 Abandon indulgence and arrogance. — Al-Tabataba'i relates the command to the people's excess in appetite and anger and their departure from servitude. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 26:126/q01/p6 Maintain limitless God-consciousness, guarding even against one's own piety. — Sulami reports al-Wasiti's view that this is both the first and last station and has no final limit. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 26:126/q02 Whom must the people obey, and how?  [وَأَطِيعُونِ · interpretation]
- 26:126/q02/p1 They must obey Hūd by heeding his counsel, warning, and commands. — Muqatil calls it obedience to Hūd's counsel. Al-Tabari says to heed his call and warning and cites 3:50. Al-Biqa'i extends it to everything Hūd commands concerning steadfast reverence; al-Bursevi says the truth he commands. Al-Nasafi frames refusal as rejection of a trustworthy messenger. Islahi says they evade his call and will not listen, and should follow their messenger instead of corrupt leaders. Al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, Elmalili, and the Qur'an Yolu commentary identify Hūd as the one to obey; al-Qurtubi says the command repeats a meaning explained earlier. Al-Tabataba'i relates the paired command to their departure from servitude. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:126/q03 How is the final yāʾ of أَطِيعُونِ treated?  [وَأَطِيعُونِ · readings]
turns on: The notes distinguish Yaʿqub's pronunciation of the extra yāʾ from its omission for the verse ending.
- 26:126/q03/p1 The final first-person yāʾ is pronounced in the full form. — Ibn al-Jazari reports that Yaʿqub affirms sixteen extra yāʾs in both connected and pause forms: eight quoted forms and أطيعون in eight places. Qiraat-ER says the full yāʾ lengthens the final syllable and makes the first person phonetically prominent without changing the meaning. [2 notes: NASHR(يعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]
- 26:126/q03/p2 The yāʾ is omitted for the verse ending. — Ibn ʿAshur says the omission serves the verse ending, as in the accounts of Noah and Abraham. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:126/q04 Why is this command pressed upon Hūd's people here?  [فَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُونِ · links]
turns on: The notes give different contextual functions for the appeal: consequence of Hūd's message, correction of excess, rebuke of evasion, and a final chance to repent.
- 26:126/q04/p1 It follows Hūd's appeal and states what his message requires. — Ibn ʿAshur compares its place after the appeal to Noah's story. Al-Biqa'i says Hūd concludes that his message calls for obedience in all he commands concerning steadfast reverence. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:126/q04/p2 It corrects their indulgence and arrogance. — Al-Tabataba'i points to their excess in appetite and anger and their departure from servitude. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 26:126/q04/p3 It rebukes people who evade Hūd's call and refuse even to listen. — Islahi takes the repeated exhortation as a rebuke of their evasion. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:126/q04/p4 It is an earnest and affectionate final warning while they still have time to change. — Islahi says they can abandon wrongdoing and follow their messenger instead of corrupt leaders. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:126/q05 How does Hūd's opening match the other prophetic accounts?  [* · links]
- 26:126/q05/p1 It repeats the prophetic opening pattern, especially Noah's appeal. — The Study Quran compares Hūd's first five verses with Noah's in 26:105–109 and later openings in 26:141–145, 160–164, and 176–180. Ibn ʿAshur says Hūd's command follows his appeal as in Noah's story. Al-Qurtubi says this command repeats an earlier explained meaning. Islahi says this sūrah details successive nations' dealings with their messengers and fates in historical order, with each account ending in a refrain. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:126/q06 What does the recurring call to piety, obedience, and no wage show?  [* · links]
- 26:126/q06/p1 The prophets share a mission of truth and saving conduct despite differences in detailed laws. — Al-Alusi says the five narratives repeat piety, obedience, and no wage to show that prophets had no worldly ambition and called people to truth and saving obedience despite differing subsidiary laws. Al-Tabataba'i reports some exegetes who say trustworthiness, no wage, piety, and obedience show a shared mission of true knowledge and conduct leading to reward and away from punishment across eras. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين)]

## 26:126/q07 Why does the formula vary in the Moses and Abraham narratives?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the altered formula is stylistic variation or reflects the greater importance of the matters in those narratives.
- 26:126/q07/p1 For stylistic variety; other signs there convey the same meaning. — Al-Alusi reports this explanation of the variation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:126/q07/p2 Their matters were more important. — Al-Alusi reports this as another view of the variation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:126/q08 How does this sūrah's treatment of these nations relate to the previous sūrah?  [* · links]
- 26:126/q08/p1 The previous sūrah detailed Moses and Noah but briefly mentioned intervening nations; this one details their dealings and fates in historical order. — Islahi says each account in this sūrah ends with a refrain. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:126/q09 What does the ending repeated after all seven stories show?  [* · links]
- 26:126/q09/p1 Most peoples turn away from God's signs; God requites deniers with might and saves believers with mercy. — Al-Tabataba'i connects the repeated ending to the sūrah's stated purpose. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:126/q10 Why are Hūd's people called deniers of the messengers?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:126/q10/p1 They outwardly rejected only one messenger, Hūd, while the story calls them deniers of the messengers. — Al-Tabataba'i identifies the story as that of Hūd and ʿAd and compares this wording with Noah's story. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 26:126/q11 Where were ʿAd's lands, and what became of them?  [* · reports]
- 26:126/q11/p1 Between Oman and Hadramawt; fertile and populous at first, then turned by God into wilderness and dunes. — Al-Alusi reports the land's location and its change. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
