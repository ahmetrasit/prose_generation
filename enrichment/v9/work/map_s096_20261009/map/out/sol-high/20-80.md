# 20:80: 32 questions

## 20:80/q01 Whom does the address to the Children of Israel address?  [يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the narrative continues addressing Moses's Israelites or turns to descendants; 20:83 is cited against the latter.
- 20:80/q01/p1 Israelites in Moses's time after the sea crossing. — Al-Zamakhshari prefers this. Ibn Atiyya notes intervening events and finds it clearer; Abu Hayyan calls it apparent. Al-Alusi cites the next question to Moses (20:83) against a later audience. Al-Nasafi frames the passage as recalling God's favor after rescue from the sea. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 20:80/q01/p2 Israelites in Muhammad's time, through their ancestors' favors. — Ibn Atiyya supplies 'your fathers' and says the later command to eat needs omitted reported speech; the address rebukes ancestral ingratitude. Abu Hayyan calls it an aside. Al-Alusi rejects it from 20:83. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:80/q02 How is the address introduced?  [يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · grammar]
- 20:80/q02/p1 An implicit 'We said' introduces the address. — Al-Zamakhshari says such omission is common; al-Alusi coordinates implied قلنا with أوحينا. Al-Tabatabai agrees. Al-Nasafi connects it with God's command to Moses to travel by night. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:80/q03 What does the example of this vocative illustrate?  [يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · grammar]
- 20:80/q03/p1 Arabic can omit yā in vocatives, especially possessive and divine address. — Al-Burhan gives 'you here,' 'Joseph' (12:90), and 'Lord' (19:4) as examples, citing 20:80 too. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 20:80/q04 What variant affects the spelling of Isrāʾīl?  [إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · readings]
- 20:80/q04/p1 Hamza's variant treats the hamza differently in writing. — Qiraat-ER states no semantic difference. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 20:80/q05 Who is Israel's enemy?  [عَدُوِّكُمْ · referent]

## 20:80/q06 What happened when Israel was saved?  [أَنجَيْنَٰكُم مِّنْ عَدُوِّكُمْ · reports]
- 20:80/q06/p1 God saved Israel through the sea and drowned Pharaoh's forces before their eyes. — Ibn Kathir says the whole army drowned in one morning and cites 2:50. Al-Qushayri says Pharaoh claimed the parted sea showed his divinity, entered with his army, and waves covered them all. Al-Bursevi says he refused Moses's counsel; al-Alusi says the subsequent address was not immediate. Al-Tabari accepts both forms as equal in sense. - 20:80/q08/p2 Hamza and al-Kisa'i, with other named readers, use singular 'I' in rescue, promise and provision. — Lists also name Khalaf, Talha, al-A'mash and Yahya ibn Waththab. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء الكوفة); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وعاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة، الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حمزة، الكسائي، طلحة); SAMIN-DURR(الأخوان); NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 20:80/q08/p3 Humayd reads najjaynākum with a doubled jīm. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin report the intensive form; al-Alusi specifies no hamza and a majestic plural. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حمزة، الكسائي، طلحة); SAMIN-DURR(حميد); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:80/q09 What was promised at the Mount?  [وَوَٰعَدْنَٰكُمْ جَانِبَ ٱلطُّورِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promised benefit is Torah and communion through Moses, Israel's hearing, or collective honor.
- 20:80/q09/p1 Moses would commune with God and receive the Torah, its duties and laws. — Muqatil includes seventy men. Al-Wahidi cites 2:51. Al-Wahidi al-Wajiz says Moses promised to go there and return. Al-Kalbi says Israel requested the book and Moses promised return after forty nights. Ibn Kathir adds Moses's request to see God and Israel's calf worship. Al-Tabatabai identifies the forty-night appointment; Study Quran points to 20:83-84. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 20:80/q09/p2 Israel was to hear God speak to Moses in their presence. — Al-Nahhas says Moses was ordered to bring them; al-Qurtubi repeats him. Al-Wahidi reports this alternative. Abu Hayyan says hearing by seventy reassured all Israel. [4 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:80/q09/p3 All Israel was promised appearance before God and honor at places of mercy. — Al-Biqa'i stresses collective honor and also mentions the Torah account in al-Baqara. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:80/q10 Who directly participated in the Mount appointment?  [وَوَٰعَدْنَٰكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural denotes direct attendants or the community represented by Moses and selected men.

Al-Nasafi calls them witnesses and Abu Hayyan says their hearing reassured all. [6 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:80/q10/p3 Moses and the Israelites collectively. — Ibn Atiyya says both would reach Sinai; Ibn Kathir describes their appointment and al-Biqa'i says all Israel was honored. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:80/q11 How is the promise verb read, and does it imply reciprocity?  [وَوَٰعَدْنَٰكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the longer form implies two parties or equals the simple one-agent promise.
- 20:80/q11/p1 The longer wāʿadnā may mean mutual appointment or dialectal simple promise. — Al-Akhfash says alif need not imply two agents, comparing istafʿala forms that do not mean requesting. Ibn Atiyya says Israel's readiness could provide reciprocal action. Ibn al-Jazari calls the longer form reciprocal; Nafi and Asim are named by al-Razi. Qiraat-ER calls it Form III. - 20:80/q11/p2 The shorter waʿadnā states a unilateral promise. — Al-Wahidi, Abu al-Hasan via al-Tabrisi and Abu Ubayd via al-Qurtubi prefer this clearer form without implied mutuality. Ibn al-Jazari names Abu Ja'far and Basrans; al-Tabrisi adds Ya'qub and Sahl. Abu Amr uses it throughout according to Ibn Mujahid and Ibn Atiyya. Qiraat-ER says Form I removes reciprocity. ## 20:80/q12 How does 'side of the Mount' function after 'promised'?  [جَانِبَ ٱلطُّورِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the bounded side can be a locative or must be construed as an object.
- 20:80/q12/p1 It is a second object with implicit arrival at or staying by the side. — Al-Qurtubi rules out a bare locative for a definite place; Makki and al-Samin supply arrival. Al-Burhan says wāʿada takes two objects, like 'give.' Abu al-Baqa through al-Alusi also supplies arrival. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(مكي); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:80/q12/p2 It is an adverbial place where the promised conversation or Torah occurred. — Al-Samin reports this; Ibn Ashur says the side's expanse makes it an indefinite locative and takes the omitted object as intimate conversation. Al-Alusi identifies the event if it is adverbial. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:80/q12/p3 The place itself is an expanded object of the promise. — Al-Samin compares traveling two farsakhs or postal stages; al-Alusi reports Abu al-Baqa's expanded object. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:80/q15 What does the Mount's 'right' side mean?  [ٱلْأَيْمَنَ · referent]
turns on: The directional reference point or a nonspatial meaning of 'blessed'.
- 20:80/q15/p1 The side to Moses's right as he approached. — Al-Wahidi and al-Qurtubi use Moses as reference; al-Qurtubi says a mountain has no inherent right. Ibn Atiyya allows a person's or other reference point's right. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 20:80/q15/p2 The right side along the route from Egypt toward Syria or Abraham's homeland. — Al-Razi, al-Bursevi and al-Alusi use this direction; al-Biqa'i adds a view toward the sea and Mecca and Yemen. Al-Alusi rejects an unspecified person merely facing the Mount. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:80/q15/p3 The right of someone facing the mountain. — Abu Hayyan and al-Samin give this; al-Alusi objects that the observer is unspecified. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:80/q15/p4 The blessed side or blessed mountain. — Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin and al-Alusi give blessed as a possible sense. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:80/q16 How is 'right' read and attached grammatically?  [ٱلْأَيْمَنَ · readings]
turns on: Whether genitive case comes by attraction or direct modification.
- 20:80/q16/p1 Accusative al-aymana modifies 'side.' — Al-Nasafi and al-Bursevi give this analysis. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 20:80/q16/p2 Genitive al-aymani is case attraction to the neighboring Mount. — Al-Zamakhshari compares جحر ضب خرب; al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi report it. Abu Hayyan and al-Samin find it rare or weak; al-Alusi says rarity does not preclude a noncanonical reading. - 20:80/q16/p3 Genitive al-aymani directly modifies the Mount as blessed or to the right. — Abu Hayyan favors this and al-Samin transmits it. Al-Alusi rejects an unspecified observer's right, but reports both senses. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:80/q17 What inward meaning is given to the right side of the Mount?  [جَانِبَ ٱلطُّورِ ٱلْأَيْمَنَ · inward]
- 20:80/q17/p1 It is the heart's mountain facing the Holy Spirit, the locus of revelation and inner inspiration. — Al-Qashani identifies the inner heart and inspiration. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:80/q18 What is manna?  [ٱلْمَنَّ · meaning]
turns on: Material food and an inward interpretation.
- 20:80/q18/p1 A sweet taranjabīn substance, dew-like and white like snow or honey. — Muqatil says it appeared on trees; al-Bursevi and al-Alusi name taranjabīn. Qatada calls it honey-like, Ibn Kathir sweet food from heaven, and Elmalılı kudret helvası. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 20:80/q18/p2 Inwardly, manna-like knowledge and certainty-based gnosis. — Al-Qashani also speaks of spiritual states and experiential ways. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:80/q19 When and in what amount did manna appear or get gathered?  [ٱلْمَنَّ · reports]
turns on: The reports give differing timings; the collection rule includes a Sabbath exception.
- 20:80/q19/p1 It appeared at night on trees or fell from dawn to sunrise, with one ṣāʿ per person in later accounts. — Muqatil describes the trees; Qatada through Yahya says honey-like manna fell at the camp dawn to sunrise; al-Bursevi and al-Alusi say snow-like manna and a ṣāʿ each. [4 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:80/q19/p2 They gathered one day's supply, except two on Friday for Saturday. — Muqatil says they did no work on Saturday; Ibn Kathir also says they took only enough for the next day. [2 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 20:80/q20 What is salwā?  [ٱلسَّلْوَىٰ · meaning]
- 20:80/q20/p1 A quail or quail-like bird, called samānī. — Yahya reports Qatada, al-Hasan and al-Dahhak; al-Itqan calls it quail-like. Al-Bursevi and al-Alusi use samānī; Elmalılı renders bıldırcın. [7 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة، الحسن، الضحاك بن مزاحم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 20:80/q21 How and why were quails provided?  [ٱلسَّلْوَىٰ · reports]
- 20:80/q21/p1 Israel requested meat through Moses; a cloud or south wind brought abundant birds. — Muqatil says red birds traveled by Egypt, covering a mile-wide area to spear height. Al-Bursevi and al-Alusi mention the south wind and each man's share; Ibn Kathir says birds fell and were gathered for the next day. [5 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

- 20:80/q22/p2 In the inward reading, descent is of spiritual states, knowledge, and gnosis. — Al-Qashani gives this explanation. [1 notes: QASHANI]

- 20:80/q23/p1 Israelite garments grew with children and men's clothes did not wear out, become shabby, or get dirty. — Muqatil gives this accompanying report. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 20:80/q24 How is the following command to eat introduced?  [وَنَزَّلْنَا عَلَيْكُمُ ٱلْمَنَّ وَٱلسَّلْوَىٰ · grammar]
- 20:80/q24/p1 An unspoken 'We said' or 'saying' introduces the command. — Al-Burhan and Ibn Ashur supply this; Ibn Ashur compares 2:57. Ibn Atiyya says the ellipsis also follows the later-audience interpretation. [3 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:80/q25 What do the later good provisions and ban on transgression mean?  [ٱلْمَنَّ ٱلسَّلْوَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The ensuing notes explain either required conduct with gifts or mercy during punishment.
- 20:80/q25/p1 Good provisions are lawful and delicious; transgression includes ingratitude, neglect of worship, sin and withheld dues. — Abu Hayyan lists denying gifts, distraction by enjoyment, sinful spending and withholding duties; Ibn Ashur emphasizes gratitude to and worship of the benefactor. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:80/q25/p2 Wilderness hardship disciplined Israel without ending God's care. — Al-Bursevi compares the forty years to a parent's discipline without withholding kindness. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:80/q26 What are the images in the subsequent warning about provision and anger?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The two adjacent metaphors explain different clauses.
- 20:80/q26/p1 Transgression is pictured as dwelling inside an abundant blessing. — Ibn Ashur explains the preposition as a vessel-like surrounding gift. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:80/q26/p2 The effects of divine anger are pictured as an army settling in a land. — Ibn Ashur explains the arrival of anger by this image. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:80/q27 How are the later forms of yaḥilla read?  [* · readings]
turns on: The vowel and derivation yield an obligation falling due or something settling.
- 20:80/q27/p1 Most read kasra, from a debt coming due. — Ibn Ashur reports this for both forms. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:80/q27/p2 Al-Kisa'i reads damma, from settling somewhere. — Ibn Ashur says al-Kashshaf accepts this. Ibn Malik and Bihraq omitted the distinction; al-Misbah's contrary account is unreliable, and al-Qamus suggests a locative sense without an example Ibn Ashur could find. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:80/q28 What does the order of blessings express?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes emphasize priority of benefits or the ancestral reminder's purpose.
- 20:80/q28/p1 Rescue removes harm, revelation gives the greater religious benefit, and manna and quails supply worldly needs. — Al-Razi follows these with the ban on transgression and hope of repentance. Abu Hayyan names humiliation, taxation and slaughter, saying removal of harm exceeds worldly gain. Al-Biqa'i sets guidance before bodily sustenance; al-Qushayri says the gifts summon obedience and gratitude. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:80/q28/p2 The reminder honors Israel through its ancestry and recalls favor amid ingratitude. — Al-Biqa'i mentions past and coming favors; Kur'an Yolu recalls Israel's special blessings and Sinai covenant. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:80/q29 What lesson is drawn from these favors and the preceding downfall?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources draw lessons from Israel's gifts and Pharaoh's prior fate.
- 20:80/q29/p1 God's gifts call for gratitude, obedience, moderation and repentance despite grave failure. — Al-Qushayri includes ancestors' trials; al-Qurtubi says deliverance should bring thanks. Kur'an Yolu applies enjoyment without excess and hope of mercy to everyone. Al-Biqa'i says later apostasy despite visible and audible signs shows God's power over hearts. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:80/q29/p2 Pharaoh's refusal warns one to heed counsel, follow a guide and persist in spiritual effort. — Al-Qushayri says Pharaoh's late confession after despair could not save him. Al-Bursevi cites Hafiz on the sincere adviser and Jami on the dawn after separation; he warns about youthful distractions, orders belief, acknowledgment and struggle, and invokes 9:41 for persistent worship. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(المولى الجامي)]

## 20:80/q30 How does this passage connect to neighboring and parallel texts?  [* · links]
turns on: Different parts point to the calf narrative, Sinai passages and earlier provision accounts.

## 20:80/q31 Why is 'We sent down' plural even alongside singular readings?  [وَنَزَّلْنَا · rhetoric]
