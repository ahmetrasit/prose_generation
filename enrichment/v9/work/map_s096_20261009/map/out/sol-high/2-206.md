# 2:206: 30 questions

## 2:206/q01 Who is described by this warning?  [لَهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse is tied to a specific occasion or applies to a class by its traits.
- 2:206/q01/p1 The hypocrite or unbeliever of the surrounding description. — Tabari and Tabrisi link him to corruption of crops and offspring; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi describe an arrogant unbeliever or hypocrite. Bursavi calls him a hypocritical corrupter, and Ibn Kathir a wicked man. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل التأويل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:206/q01/p2 Al-Akhnas ibn Shurayq in particular. — Al-Suddi names him; Tabrisi says he feigned affection for the Prophet and Islam; Elmalılı identifies him as a Thaqafi allied to Banu Zuhra who professed love, then burned a Muslim field and killed livestock. Nasafi says he was admonished for destruction. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); JISHUMI(السدي); TABRISI(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:206/q01/p3 The warning applies generally to anyone with the described traits, including sinners who are not hypocrites. — Tabari transmits 'any sinner and hypocrite'; Ibn Atiyya extends it to sinners sharing traits; Razi argues the five traits do not prove hypocrisy and pride on admonition alone does not prove it. Elmalılı, Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran retain a general application despite specific reports. [9 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(müfessirînin ekser-i muhakkikīni); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî); STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭ)]
- 2:206/q01/p4 An unnamed particular hypocritical pretender. — Ibn Abbas says the three verses concern someone whose exterior opposes his interior; a report from al-Sadiq identifies a particular person without naming him. [2 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس,الصادق)]
- 2:206/q01/p5 The man who defiantly killed Khubayb. — Tha'labi identifies the executioner who killed Khubayb after being told to fear God; Baghawi names the spear wielder Salaman, whose defiance increased. [2 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 2:206/q02 What occasion of revelation is reported for these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports locate the passage in different events or treat its class description as general.
- 2:206/q02/p1 Al-Akhnas's profession of Islam and subsequent destruction of crops and livestock. — Elmalılı gives his tribe, alliance, profession before the Prophet, and burning of a Muslim field and killing of animals. [4 notes: ELMALILI; JISHUMI(السدي); TABRISI(السدي); TAB-FULL(آخرون)]
- 2:206/q02/p2 The al-Raji expedition and the hypocrites' mockery of its martyrs. — Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak recount Quraysh's false conversion request, six Muslims lured, three killed, and Khubayb and Zayd captured; Ibn Abbas reports hypocrites calling the deaths pointless. Ibn Jawzi's report names al-Zubayr, al-Miqdad and Khubayb with the hypocrites. Tha'labi adds Khubayb's refusal of treachery and miraculous fruit in Mecca. Baghawi reports Umar marveling that God protected Asim's body from polytheists after death, in keeping with Asim's vow not to touch or be touched by one. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس والضحاك); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 2:206/q02/p3 Suhayb's escape after giving up his wealth to reach Medina. — Nasafi reports polytheists attempting to make him leave Islam, killing companions with him, then allowing him to travel after taking his wealth. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:206/q02/p4 One who enjoins right and forbids wrong until killed. — Nasafi also reports this possible occasion or application. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]
- 2:206/q02/p5 Hypocrites generally, with no exclusive individual occasion. — Ibn Abbas and al-Hasan reports identify hypocrites; Kur'an Yolu reports they appeared friendly before the Prophet while acting destructively in secret. Study Quran says most cited commentators prefer a universal teaching despite incident reports. [4 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(الحسن); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭ)]

## 2:206/q03 Who tells him to fear God?  [وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether an unnamed passive clause permits identifying a historical speaker.
- 2:206/q03/p1 The Prophet addressed the man. — Wahidi says the Messenger summoned al-Akhnas to respond inwardly and outwardly, and Razi reports this from him. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)]
- 2:206/q03/p2 Any person may admonish him; the speaker is unstated. — Biqa'i explicitly says anyone may warn him. Razi objects to Wahidi's identification: the verse neither names a speaker nor establishes that an actual exchange happened, though the Prophet summoned everyone to piety. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT-; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)-]

## 2:206/q04 What does the call to fear God ask him to do?  [ٱتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: How broadly the unstated object of 'fear' and the surrounding faults are supplied.
- 2:206/q04/p1 Revere God and guard against His punishment and disobedience. — Tabrisi derives ittiqa' from taking protection against danger; al-Asamm includes fearing punishment and avoiding disobedience. Baghawi gives the simple command, while Kur'an Yolu emphasizes reverence and respect for right rules. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; JISHUMI(الأصم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:206/q04/p2 Stop the corruption, hypocrisy and harmful acts he is practicing. — Tabari and Tabrisi specify destruction of crops and offspring; Wahidi-Wajiz calls it gentle warning to stop; Bursavi includes wrongdoing and hypocrisy. Razi extends it to worldly ambition, false oaths, obstinate defense of falsehood, corruption and destroying crops and progeny. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 2:206/q04/p3 Bring his inner state into accord with his outward profession. — Al-Asamm includes this formulation; Wahidi's reported summons demanded an inward and outward response. [2 notes: JISHUMI(الأصم); WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 2:206/q05 How does he respond to the admonition?  [أَخَذَتْهُ ٱلْعِزَّةُ بِٱلْإِثْمِ · interpretation]
turns on: The attachment and force of bi-l-ithm: compelled action, prior cause, accompaniment, or qualification of ʿizza.
- 2:206/q05/p1 Pride and anger make him reject correction and persist or advance in wrongdoing. — Qatada says he presses further into sin when told to hold back; Kashshaf lists harming others, stubbornness, and rejecting the preacher. Qushayri says he deems advice beneath him; Ibn Kathir says anger makes him refuse truth. Biqa'i contrasts those who stop when corrected; Ibn Ashur describes anger at counsel. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); KASHSHAF-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 2:206/q05/p2 The wording allows pride to compel him into the forbidden sin. — Muqatil and Tustari link sinful zeal to 38:2; Wahidi and others use the idiom of being compelled or bound, including jāhiliyya partisanship. Baghawi specifies injustice; Baydawi says the very sin forbidden. Razi identifies ignoring counsel itself as the sin. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:206/q05/p3 His pride arises from prior sin or unbelief in his heart and blocks acceptance. — Mawardi, al-Hasan and others connect the cause to sin already inside; Razi names disbelief, ignorance and neglect of proof and compares 38:2. This is an alternative to reading the sin as what pride drives him toward. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:206/q05/p4 Pride accompanies him while he is sinning, or pride itself is tainted with sin. — Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi give the accompaniment reading; Biqa'i describes arrogance during base, unlawful conduct. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:206/q05/p5 His 'dignity' is acquired through sin and is therefore counterfeit. — Tabataba'i attaches bi-l-ithm to al-ʿizza and sets outward status against a hypocritical heart. He rejects attachment to the verb as causal or causative because those construals would treat this as genuine dignity. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]

## 2:206/q06 What does أَخَذَتْهُ convey?  [أَخَذَتْهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the image stresses seizure and enclosure or the resulting compulsion.
- 2:206/q06/p1 Taking or grasping, extended to overwhelming and enclosing a person. — Jishumi and Tabrisi give the opposite of 'give'; Abu Hayyan says pride surrounds him like a grasping hand. Ibn Ashur traces literal hand-taking to control, overpowering, receiving and enveloping, citing 9:5, 6:42 and 3:81. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:206/q06/p2 Taking by compulsion, binding or captivity. — Wahidi's linguistic authorities treat taking by a thing as compelling it; Kashshaf and Baydawi explain force into sin, while Alusi imagines pride making him a captive shackled by sin. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:206/q07 What does ٱلْعِزَّةُ mean here?  [ٱلْعِزَّةُ · meaning]
turns on: The word's base sense of strength versus its contextual sense as zeal or claimed status.
- 2:206/q07/p1 Strength, might, protection or invulnerability, turned into haughtiness in context. — Jishumi and Tabrisi define strength warding off humiliation; Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur add force, tribal backing or confidence that no one may challenge him. Ibn Jawzi distinguishes scriptural uses: might at 26:44 and 38:82, power at 4:139, zeal at 38:2; Qurtubi cites 38:23 and Ibn Ashur cites a proverb, al-Samaw'al and 2:209. Biqa'i recalls 10:65 to say all true might belongs to God. [17 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو سليمان الدمشقي,بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:206/q07/p2 Partisan, jāhiliyya zeal and proud obstinacy. — Muqatil and Tustari compare 38:2; Ibn Abbas is reported glossing it as zealotry with a cited poem; Ibn Jawzi's third Quranic use cites 38:2. Qurtubi quotes a poet on ignorant pride and gives unbelief born of zeal; Qashani interprets egoistic zeal. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); QASHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:206/q07/p3 A felt dignity acquired from sinful worldly status, unlike God-given dignity. — Tabataba'i cites 3:26, 63:8 and 4:139 for genuine dignity, and interprets this man's apparent dignity as counterfeit. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 2:206/q08 Why add بِٱلْإِثْمِ to the pride?  [ٱلْعِزَّةُ بِٱلْإِثْمِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the addition primarily disambiguates a potentially good quality or defines a counterfeit source of dignity.
- 2:206/q08/p1 It marks this pride as blameworthy, since dignity or strength can be praiseworthy. — Abu Hayyan names the device tatmim and cites 5:54, 63:8 and 4:139; Samin gives 63:8 and 5:54. Ibn Ashur contrasts 63:8 and says the pride obstructs admonition; Tabataba'i contrasts true dignity from God. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:206/q08/p2 It exposes the claimant's sinful status as counterfeit dignity. — Tabataba'i says linking the preposition to ʿizza makes the contrast between a hypocritical heart and outwardly impressive words or status explicit. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 2:206/q09 How is the bāʾ in بِٱلْإِثْمِ construed?  [بِٱلْإِثْمِ · grammar]
turns on: Its syntactic attachment and whether it signals action, cause, accompaniment, or sinful quality.
- 2:206/q09/p1 It is causative or transitive: pride drives him into sin. — Wahidi relates the idiom 'made him do'; Abu Hayyan reports Zamakhshari but observes this bāʾ normally follows an intransitive verb and is rare after a transitive one, citing 2:20. The shaykh in Samin objects similarly, citing 2:17, 2:20 and an example of striking stone with stone. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; TABATABAI-]
- 2:206/q09/p2 It is causal or stands for lām: previous sin causes pride to seize him. — Tha'labi and Baghawi call it bāʾ for lām; Razi gives the reason/punishment analogy. Qurtubi cites Antara's cauldron verse, and Samin cites an unattributed poem about ignorant pride. Al-Hasan specifies disbelief in the heart. [10 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عنترة,قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI-]
- 2:206/q09/p3 It marks accompaniment, circumstantially describing the person or pride. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi state the alternative; Abu Hayyan and Samin permit either the man while sinning or pride itself tainted by sin. Ibn Ashur chooses accompaniment; Biqa'i describes him as proud while sinning. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:206/q09/p4 It attaches to ʿizza: he takes pride in, or derives his apparent dignity through, sin. — Wahidi gives pride in sin; Tabataba'i calls attachment to the noun most natural and rejects attachment to akhadhat as causal or causative. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABATABAI+; TABATABAI-]

## 2:206/q10 How does the conditional clause connect syntactically with what precedes?  [وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the conditional is independent or extends the earlier syntactic description.
- 2:206/q10/p1 It is a fresh sentence. — Abu Hayyan offers an independent sentence, recalling the comparable conditional passive at 2:11; Samin also gives a fresh statement. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:206/q10/p2 It is coordinated within the preceding relative description. — Abu Hayyan gives inclusion in the relative clause; Samin gives coordination with yuʿjibuka. Both point to a comparable passive construction at 2:11; Samin notes debate about its deputy subject. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:206/q11 What does the response to advice teach?  [ٱتَّقِ ٱللَّهَ أَخَذَتْهُ ٱلْعِزَّةُ بِٱلْإِثْمِ · interpretation]
turns on: The moral and practical implications drawn from accepting or rebuffing counsel.
- 2:206/q11/p1 Rejecting sincere correction from pride is a grave sin, and one should accept advice humbly. — Ibn Mas'ud repeatedly calls 'mind yourself' to an admonisher one of God's gravest sins, or an unforgiven sin; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi warn believers against such conduct. Jishumi calls arrogant refusal a major sin approaching unbelief; Kur'an Yolu says pride obstructs honest deliberation and taqwa. [16 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض العلماء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); TABRISI(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI(عبد الله بن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:206/q11/p2 Humility when admonished is exemplified by Umar and others. — Baghawi, Abu Hayyan and Bursavi report Umar putting his cheek on the ground; Durr adds Umar's statement that there is no good without candid admonition. Sufyan reports Malik ibn Mughawil likewise laying his cheek down; Harun al-Rashid dismounted, prostrated and granted a Jewish petitioner's request when the verse came to mind. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(هارون الرشيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمر,هارون الرشيد); DURR-FULL(الحسن,سفيان); BURSEVI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 2:206/q11/p3 A judge may discipline a litigant for 'be just', but not for 'fear God'. — Alusi distinguishes the two forms of rebuke in judicial speech. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:206/q12 How is ٱتَّقِ pronounced here?  [ٱتَّقِ · readings]
- 2:206/q12/p1 Its qāf has either pure kasra or a blend of ḍamma and kasra; both are canonical and mean the same. — Qiraat-ER says the blended vowel preserves an archaic pronunciation. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vhr)]

## 2:206/q13 What does فَحَسْبُهُۥ جَهَنَّمُ mean?  [فَحَسْبُهُۥ جَهَنَّمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'suffices' is read as requital, reversal of pride, exclusive fate, or an inward foretaste.
- 2:206/q13/p1 Hell is sufficient punishment or recompense for his wrongdoing. — Tabari, Tha'labi and many later authors give this reading; Wahidi and Razi compare a dirham sufficing someone, while Imru al-Qays's verse uses hasb for contentment and drink sufficing as wealth. Ibn Ashur says sufficiency usually means fitness, here fitting recompense, citing Abu al-Tayyib and 3:173; Study Quran observes positive and negative Quranic use. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون,امرؤ القيس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:206/q13/p2 Hell is the humiliation that answers his worldly pride. — Ibn al-Anbari's alternative and Abu Hayyan set his pride against ultimate abasement; Biqa'i relates the place to how he prepared himself for corruption. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:206/q13/p3 Its sufficiency implies no further punishment beyond Hellfire. — Jishumi infers this from the wording. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 2:206/q13/p4 It includes present inner alienation, darkness and constriction before the greater punishment. — Qushayri reads a present lesser torment and cites 32:21, 'nearer punishment before the greater'. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 2:206/q13/p5 His conduct leaves him no destination except Hell. — Kur'an Yolu draws this consequence from the clause. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:206/q14 What is the grammatical function of حَسْبُهُۥ and جَهَنَّمُ?  [فَحَسْبُهُۥ جَهَنَّمُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ḥasb is an ordinary inflected noun, participle-like predicate, or verb-like expression.
- 2:206/q14/p1 Ḥasbuhu is the subject and Jahannam its predicate. — Samin and Alusi call this primary; Abu Hayyan describes ḥasb as a noun for sufficiency. He and Samin say it takes annexation without becoming definite, has no number or gender inflection, may take an extra bāʾ when subject, and lacks it as predicate. Wahidi says the triliteral verb is obsolete, while aḥsabani al-shayʾ means that a thing sufficed me. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 2:206/q14/p2 Jahannam is the subject and ḥasb an active-participle-like predicate whose agent fills its predicate slot. — Abu al-Baqa' gives this analysis, supported by the linking fāʾ; Abu Hayyan and Alusi report related analyses. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:206/q14/p3 Ḥasb is an indeclinable noun or particle of action meaning 'sufficed' or 'suffice'. — Abu Hayyan and Samin reject this: ḥasb takes prepositions, is adjectival and inflects for case. Alusi also questions it. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 2:206/q15 What does the name جَهَنَّمُ refer to?  [جَهَنَّمُ · referent]
turns on: Whether Jahannam names the whole abode or one of its levels.
- 2:206/q15/p1 It is a proper name for the abode or fire of punishment. — Wahidi's grammarians call it the afterlife's fire; Baydawi says a proper name of the abode, originally a synonym for fire or a loanword; Ibn Ashur notes the Quran calls it fire and describes people and stones as fuel at 2:24. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(يونس وأكثر النحويين); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:206/q15/p2 It may name the lowest level of Hell. — Abu Hayyan and Alusi report this narrower referent as an alternative. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:206/q16 Is جَهَنَّمُ Arabic or borrowed, and how is it declined?  [جَهَنَّمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the deep-well evidence reflects a native root or comparison, and whether faʿnal is an Arabic pattern.
- 2:206/q16/p1 It is a foreign or Arabized proper name, indeclinable through foreign origin and definiteness. — Yunus and most grammarians say this; Ibn al-Anbari agrees. Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi report derivation from Persian Kehnam/Kahanam with kāf changed to jīm and alif lost; Ibn Ashur also reports a Hebrew possibility and says most grammarians prefer foreign origin. Ibn Jawzi cites Ru'ba and al-A'sha's nondeclension as Arabicized foreign usage; Elmalılı reports kehennam. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(يونس وأكثر النحويين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(يونس وأكثر النحويين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 2:206/q16/p2 It is Arabic, connected with depth or harshness; its nūn is extra and its pattern faʿnal, indeclinable as feminine proper name. — Others derive it from the deep bottom of the fire, supported by Ru'ba's deep-well word. Abu Hayyan supplies jahm, harshness, and extra nūn, defending faʿnal with ḍaghanṭ, safanj, hajanf and zūnak, including Hassan's verse. Samin prefers this and rejects denial of faʿnal; Alusi reports the derivation but rejects a proposed faʿnall pattern. Elmalılı gives a bottomless foul well. [17 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون,قطرب عن رؤبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL(آخرون,رؤبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 2:206/q16/p3 It is Arabic with an original nūn and a faʿlal pattern. — Samin reports this rival analysis, based on the claim faʿnal is absent; he and Abu Hayyan rebut that claim with attested patterns. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 2:206/q16/p4 The deep-well expression does not establish an Arabic etymology. — Ibn Ashur argues the well may simply be likened to Hell for depth and fire; he treats etymological pursuit as unnecessary under the prevailing foreign-name analysis. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:206/q17 Why use the name جَهَنَّمُ here?  [جَهَنَّمُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The significance assigned to the particular name in this context.
- 2:206/q17/p1 Its harsh reception fits the man's confrontational response to counsel. — Biqa'i says the name evokes grim face-to-face reception, and reports al-Harali's principle that the Fire's particular name suits each judgment. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 2:206/q17/p2 Its depth and darkness express the lowest spiritual state. — Qashani reads Jahannam as ultimate depth and darkness of a low inner rank, using the deep-pit name. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 2:206/q18 How is Hell depicted as fire?  [جَهَنَّمُ · interpretation]
- 2:206/q18/p1 A dark fiery oven fueled by people and stones. — Ibn Ashur cites a poem attributed uncertainly to Waraqa ibn Nawfal or Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt lamenting Zayd ibn Amr, alongside hadith and 2:24. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ورقة بن نوفل أو أمية بن أبي الصلت)]

## 2:206/q19 What does ٱلْمِهَادُ mean?  [ٱلْمِهَادُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the concrete bed image or the extended sense of settled abode is foregrounded.
- 2:206/q19/p1 A bed, bedding, cradle, or prepared place for sleeping or lying. — Majaz, Ibn Qutayba and many others give bed or resting place; Ibn Qutayba derives it from preparing a bed and a child's cradle. Wahidi links it to leveling; Ibn Atiyya to one's own mattress; Razi cites 51:48 and 30:44 for spreading and preparation; al-Harali adds a comfortable trodden surface. [22 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:206/q19/p2 A settled abode or evil settlement. — Al-Hasan emphasizes permanence beneath the occupant; Razi compares 14:29's evil settlement, and Tabari calls it what the hypocrite prepared for himself. [3 notes: TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; TAB-FULL]

## 2:206/q20 Is ٱلْمِهَادُ singular or plural?  [ٱلْمِهَادُ · grammar]
turns on: The morphological analysis of the noun.
- 2:206/q20/p1 It is the plural of mahd, a prepared bed or cradle. — Wahidi, Qurtubi and Samin explicitly give the plural analysis; Abu Hayyan reports it as one option. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:206/q20/p2 It is a singular noun for a prepared bed. — Samin reports the alternative; other glosses use it as a bed without specifying morphology. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]

## 2:206/q21 Why call Hell a wretched ٱلْمِهَادُ?  [وَلَبِئْسَ ٱلْمِهَادُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the bed image is prepared recompense, physical contact, ironic replacement, or settled permanence.
- 2:206/q21/p1 It is the bed or abode he has prepared for himself by his deeds. — Tabari attributes his bed to hypocrisy, immorality and rebellion; Mujahid and Ibn Abbas call it what its occupants prepared; Biqa'i connects the bed to his chosen corruption. Razi cites 30:44 on preparing for oneself. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 2:206/q21/p2 It is the bed on which he is laid or which contacts his sides and back. — Razi's scholars picture a sleeper laid upon fire; Ibn Ashur says those thrown in meet it with their sides and backs. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض العلماء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:206/q21/p3 It is ironic substitution: Hell replaces the comforting bed he expected. — Wahidi compares glad tidings of painful punishment at 84:24, Tabrisi at 31:7 and Qurtubi at 3:21; Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi cite a 'greeting' consisting of a painful blow. Samin calls it derisive irony; Alusi says it is used mockingly. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:206/q21/p4 It emphasizes Hell as his permanent settled place. — Al-Hasan sees a resting place as something enduring beneath its occupant; Qurtubi calls it the unbelievers' settled abode; al-Harali describes a long or eternal afterlife stay. [3 notes: TABRISI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:206/q22 How is وَلَبِئْسَ ٱلْمِهَادُ parsed?  [وَلَبِئْسَ ٱلْمِهَادُ · grammar]
turns on: How the omitted مخصوص, the blame verb, and the implied oath are syntactically related.
- 2:206/q22/p1 It answers an implied oath, with Jahannam understood as the omitted specific object of blame. — Baydawi and Bursavi say the lām carries an implied oath; Abu Hayyan and Samin supply Jahannam or hiya as the omitted مخصوص. Alusi also notes the implied oath and obvious ellipsis. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:206/q22/p2 Biʾsa is a frozen verb with al-mihād its subject and the specified object a topic, placed before or after. — Abu Hayyan follows Basran grammar and Sibawayh; he says treating the omitted specific object as one word preserves a single clause regardless of its position. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:206/q22/p3 The omitted specified object is a subject whose praise/blame clause is its predicate. — Samin reports this construal, while Abu Hayyan and Samin reject analyses deleting an entire unsupported sentence or leaving an unconnected clause. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:206/q22/p4 Hell is appositional to al-mihād, receiving the same name. — Jishumi reports this alternative construction. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 2:206/q23 Why omit the specific object after بئس?  [وَلَبِئْسَ ٱلْمِهَادُ · rhetoric]
- 2:206/q23/p1 It is already clear as Jahannam, and omission lets the verse end on al-mihād. — Abu Hayyan says Quranic verse endings often omit the object for cadence; Samin says the verse ending makes omission apt. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:206/q24 What other verse parallels the reaction to admonition?  [* · links]
- 2:206/q24/p1 The anger resembles disbelievers' reaction to clear revelation at 22:72. — Ibn Kathir draws this explicit comparison. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 2:206/q25 How does this verse relate to 2:204–207?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the sequence is read through reported combat, a moral character contrast, or its broader sura structure.
- 2:206/q25/p1 It is the arrogant corrupt figure contrasted with one who gives himself for God; the two are pictured as fighting. — Ali says 'the two fought' when reading into 2:207; Ibn Abbas depicts one man admonishing the other, then risking himself in combat, an explanation Umar praised. Tabari places the hypocrites' mockery of al-Raji martyrs against their reward; Tha'labi recounts Khubayb's faithful conduct. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,علي); IBNABIHATIM(علي بن أبي طالب); THALABI(ابن عباس والضحاك,ابن عباس وعلي)]
- 2:206/q25/p2 The admonition tests his contentious character and leads to the stated fate. — Biqa'i says refusal distinguishes him from those who stop under correction; Abu Hayyan says the preceding call to fear God is recalled by the punishment that follows. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 2:206/q25/p3 The surrounding five traits are a moral portrait rather than proof of formal hypocrisy. — Razi says they can characterize show-offs and other sinners, and the admonition response alone does not establish hypocrisy. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:206/q25/p4 The portrait contrasts hypocrisy with sincerity after verses contrasting worldly and otherworldly aims. — Tabataba'i links it to the Hajj sequence, including 2:200. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 2:206/q26 What broader lesson does the passage teach about outward appearances?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The practical lesson drawn from the verse's portrait.
- 2:206/q26/p1 Sweet speech can hide corruption; test conduct and inner sincerity before trusting appearances or entrusting responsibility. — Razi says the traits encourage sound conduct of heart and limbs and recall that God sees hidden affairs; Elmalılı urges evaluating responsibility by deeds. Ibn Ashur warns against pleasing words and looks concealing plots; Kur'an Yolu describes an attractive-speaking corrupter. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:206/q26/p2 Taqwa and freedom from egoistic pride protect against hypocrisy and enable honest response to truth. — Kur'an Yolu links reverence to avoiding hypocrisy, ostentation, discord and corruption, and to considering justified warnings. Qushayri says mercy lets a person recognize faults and accept counsel. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QUSHAYRI]

## 2:206/q27 What theological conclusions are drawn from the rebuke and threat?  [* · theology]
turns on: Different theological inferences drawn from the same censure.
- 2:206/q27/p1 Human agents are responsible for their own acts. — Jishumi argues that rebuke, describing sinful pride, condemnation and threatened punishment would be senseless if human deeds were not their own. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 2:206/q27/p2 There is no punishment beyond Hellfire. — Jishumi infers this from saying Hell is sufficient. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 2:206/q28 Why is punishment deferred to the hereafter?  [فَحَسْبُهُۥ جَهَنَّمُ · interpretation]
- 2:206/q28/p1 The community receives respite out of regard for its Prophet, while evildoers face a long or eternal afterlife stay. — Al-Harali says immediate worldly punishment of believers would leave worldly life to disbelievers while believers receive the next life. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:206/q29 Where does the form وَلَبِئْسَ occur?  [وَلَبِئْسَ · links]
- 2:206/q29/p1 Wa-la-biʾsa occurs twice in al-Baqara and once each in al-Hajj and al-Nur; fa-la-biʾsa occurs only in al-Nahl. — Burhan cites 16:29 for the latter form. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 2:206/q30 What does interpreting this Arabic idiom require?  [أَخَذَتْهُ ٱلْعِزَّةُ بِٱلْإِثْمِ · meaning]
- 2:206/q30/p1 One must understand Arabic vocabulary, grammar and idiomatic construction beyond a paraphrase. — Wahidi cites a poem where a woman's tears seize the speaker's heart and says its paraphrase does not explain its structure; sayings about a contradictory excuse, composure and seasoned leadership show idiom cannot always be reconstructed word by word. He cites 3:175 and 25:71 when insisting on Arabic foundations. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
