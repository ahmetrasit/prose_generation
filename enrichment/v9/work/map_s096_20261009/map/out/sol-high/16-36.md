# 16:36: 24 questions

## 16:36/q01 What does ummah mean here?  [أُمَّةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word marks a human group, a span of time, or both.
- 16:36/q01/p1 A human community or people, broadly understood. — Asad allows a civilization embracing both people and period; Kuranyolu defines a large group united by ancestry, traits, interests, ideals, religion, time or homeland, notes its plural umam and its narrower religious use for a prophet's followers, and prefers the broad sense here, citing 2:128, 2:134, 2:213, 11:118–119. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 16:36/q01/p2 A period of time, so the sending occurred at every period. — Asad offers this as an alternative rendering of ummah. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 16:36/q02 How universal was the sending of messengers?  [بَعَثْنَا فِى كُلِّ أُمَّةٍۢ رَّسُولًا · interpretation]
- 16:36/q02/p1 God sent a messenger to every community or generation, including the present community. — Muqatil, Tabari, Zajjaj, Jishumi, and others state the universal practice; Wahidi, Baghawi, Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and Bursevi compare Muhammad's mission to earlier missions. Ibn Kathir spans Noah, first messenger to earth's people, through Muhammad, sent to humans and jinn east and west; Biqai says none on earth was unreached and that the messenger need not be from the addressed people, citing Lot, Shuayb among al-Aykah, and Solomon. Tabatabai ties universality to human need and 16:35; Study Quran cites 10:47, 35:23, 67:9 and notes witnesses and books in 4:41, 16:84, 28:75, 45:28. Yahya reads the surrounding messengers as those to subsequently destroyed peoples. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 16:36/q03 What did every messenger call people to do?  [أَنِ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ وَٱجْتَنِبُوا۟ ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ · interpretation]
- 16:36/q03/p1 Worship God alone, affirm His oneness, and shun false worship. — Tabari adds exclusive obedience and sincere devotion; Jishumi says prophets begin with monotheism and justice, urge reflection on proofs, then teach laws because laws depend on those foundations. Ibn Kathir cites 21:25 and 43:45; Study Quran lists prophetic calls in 5:72, 5:117, 7:59, 23:23, 29:16, 7:65, 11:50, 7:73, 11:61, 27:45, 7:85, 11:84, 29:36, and 21:25. Asad sees the command as the ethical basis of every true religion; Kuranyolu says avoiding beliefs, ideas, and acts undermining oneness is essential. Tabatabai stresses that a human command invites, rather than compels. [30 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; SULAMI(محمد بن الفضل); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 16:36/q04 How does an introduce the command?  [أَنِ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an explains the speech implied by sending or forms a verbal noun governed by an understood preposition.
- 16:36/q04/p1 It is explanatory: sending a messenger implies a spoken message or command. — Tabrisi, Bursevi, Ibn Ashur, and Alusi derive the explanatory force from the verbal or command sense of sending; Samin records this parse. Jishumi also records an implied 'he said' or 'they said' before the accusative clause; Tabatabai rejects inserting a distinct omitted li-yaqula lahum. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 16:36/q04/p2 It is infinitival, with an omitted preposition bi: sent with the command to worship God. — Wahidi supplies bi before the clause; Jishumi, Samin, and Alusi report or allow the same construction. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:36/q05 What inward sense does worship or the call receive?  [ٱعْبُدُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · inward]
turns on: The distinct inward emphases are worship as the knower's servanthood and the prophetic call's disciplining of desire.
- 16:36/q05/p1 Worship adorns knowers, whose best state is service and surrender of personal claims for what is due to God. — Tustari states the reading; Sulami reports it from Sahl in nearly the same terms. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل)]
- 16:36/q05/p2 Prophets call to divine oneness and servanthood and away from natural impulses, desires, and passions. — Sulami transmits this from Muhammad ibn al-Fadl. [1 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن الفضل)]

## 16:36/q06 What is the taghut to be avoided?  [ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether taghut names all illicit objects of worship, misleading agents, Satan in particular, or idols in particular; several broad definitions include the narrower cases.
- 16:36/q06/p1 Anything worshipped besides God, including idols, people, or Satan. — Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Qurṭubi and others state the general sense; Ibn Atiyya includes a consenting human, stone or wood; Ibn Qutayba calls stone, image or Satan both jibt and taghut, and Ibn al-Jawzi reports al-Zajjaj's Arabic-speaker definition. Qurṭubi adds soothsayers and callers to error; Raghib, reported by Tabatabai, includes transgressors and notes singular or plural use with 2:256, 39:17, 2:257. Baydawi says false deity or tyrant. [9 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 16:36/q06/p2 Satan and whoever calls people to error, or every call toward error. — Wahidi's two commentaries, Tabrisi, Bursevi and Alusi extend the referent from Satan to other misleading callers; Bursevi and Alusi say anything that calls toward error. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 16:36/q06/p3 Satan specifically; avoiding him means refusing his call and obedience. — Yahya cites 4:117 and says Satan called people to idol worship; Tabari stresses fear of his diversion from God's path; Razi reports avoidance of obedience to Satan; Alusi reports al-Hasan's gloss. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 16:36/q06/p4 Idols or false gods specifically. — Yahya reports al-Suddi's alternative to Satan; Ibn al-Jawzi reports a Quranic use for idols; Ibn Ashur calls it a generic term for idols and notes the plural tawaghit with 4:51; Kuranyolu renders it false gods, citing 5:60. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 16:36/q07 What is the derivation and form of taghut?  [ٱلطَّٰغُوتَ · meaning]
turns on: These are complementary accounts of sense and morphological formation.
- 16:36/q07/p1 It derives from transgression or exceeding bounds and can denote a transgressing human or jinn as well as a false object of worship. — Ibn al-Jawzi derives it from tughyān and lists unbeliever, sorcerer, idol, Satan, rebellious jinn or human; Tabatabai reports Raghib's 'any transgressor or object worshipped besides God,' tracing it to unjust excess. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; TABATABAI(الراغب)]
- 16:36/q07/p2 It follows the fa'alut pattern, with metathesis and an added nonfeminine ta. — Bursevi compares jabarut and malakut, derives the source form as ṭugh-yut, moves the lam before the ayn, and says the extra ta is not feminine. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 16:36/q08 How is the contrasted division among the communities construed?  [فَمِنْهُم مَّنْ هَدَى ٱللَّهُ وَمِنْهُم مَّنْ حَقَّتْ عَلَيْهِ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةُ · grammar]
turns on: The notes offer a semantic division and two permitted grammatical parses of man, rather than rival accounts of who is divided.
- 16:36/q08/p1 After messengers delivered their charge, the nations divided into guided and erring groups. — Bursevi takes minhum back to the nations and the fa as implying that consequence. Tabatabai likewise says every community split; Muqatil, Tabari, Thalabi, Biqai, Ibn Ashur and others explain the resulting two groups, with Biqai adding reward and punishment. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 16:36/q08/p2 Man in each clause may be relative or an indefinite noun qualified by a clause; its antecedent is omitted either way. — Samin al-Durr reports both syntactic parses. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 16:36/q09 What does God's guiding some mean here?  [مَّنْ هَدَى ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether guidance is divine enabling, the uptake of universally offered guidance, a resulting reward, or bare presentation of proofs.
- 16:36/q09/p1 God actually directs or enables some people to believe and worship rightly. — Wahidi and Ibn al-Jawzi gloss it as divine direction; Baghawi and Qurṭubi specify His religion; Baydawi says He enabled belief while not granting that enabling to others; Bursevi says God creates guidance after their partial power and choice turns to worship; Alusi calls it granted success; Asharite theologians, as reported by Study Quran, infer that God guides some to belief after revelation. Razi and Alusi make divine creation or will ultimate. [11 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarite theologians)]
- 16:36/q09/p2 God's guidance is a favor fitted to those who respond, seek, or choose guidance. — Tabrisi says God gave a favor He knew would lead to belief; al-Kabi, reported by Razi, says seekers were adjudged guided; Nasafi and Asad stress taking up available guidance; Abu Hayyan reports al-Zamakhshari's fitness for divine favor and abandonment of those from whom no good will come; Ibn Atiyya says some reflected and gained insight, and Kuranyolu stresses good faith and open minds. Ibn Ashur says God showed the way to all, and those following His clarification were guided by Him, answering 16:35. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الكعبي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL-]
- 16:36/q09/p3 God guided them to Paradise or reward through their faith. — Tabrisi permits guidance to Paradise through faith; Razi reports al-Jubbai's reading of guidance as attainment of divine reward and Paradise, paired with punishment for deviation. [2 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي)]
- 16:36/q09/p4 Guidance here is merely the presentation of proofs to all. — Tabrisi rejects this because proofs are presented equally to believers and unbelievers; he cites 41:17 as a contrasting use of guidance as showing proofs. [0 notes: TABRISI-]

## 16:36/q10 What does haqqat mean?  [حَقَّتْ · meaning]
- 16:36/q10/p1 It became due, binding, established, or inevitable. — Jishumi compares a judgment becoming due and cites 5:107 for liability; Asad gives the literal senses 'error inevitably established' or a verdict of error made inevitable. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 16:36/q11 What became fixed for the other group, and why?  [وَمِنْهُم مَّنْ حَقَّتْ عَلَيْهِ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause states actual divinely decreed error, earned and persistent error, its punishment, its disclosure, or a consequent decree after wrongdoing.
- 16:36/q11/p1 Actual error or unbelief became fixed by God's prior knowledge or decree; they died unbelieving. — Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas on prior knowledge and invokes 7:30 and 10:96 for binding unbelief; Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi and Qurṭubi explicitly say prior decree, while Razi argues God's true report cannot fail and cites 7:30, 10:96, 36:7. Yahya compares the wretched and fortunate in 11:105. Tabari links denial and following taghut with destruction; Wahidi says the following passage confirms those fixed in error will not be guided. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 16:36/q11/p2 Their own turning away, refusal, and persistent choice made their error fixed, with divine forsaking or sealing following. — Tabrisi says they turned from the messenger and God forsook them; Ibn Atiyya says they turned away after others reflected; Nasafi makes choosing error decisive; Bursevi says defiance and persistence prevent God creating guidance or purifying the heart; Asad describes conscious refusal and sealing; Kuranyolu notes stubborn denial among some Meccan idolaters. Alusi's Irshad report similarly attributes continuing error to obstinacy; Study Quran reports Muʿtazilites that error is earned by choice. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Muʿtazilite commentators); RAZI-FULL-]
- 16:36/q11/p3 The phrase means punishment or destruction became due for their misguidance, rather than naming the act of error itself. — Tabrisi reports both an unnamed gloss and al-Hasan's 'punishment for error'; Razi reports al-Jubbai's reward and punishment pairing and his appeal to the subsequent travel command and destroyed peoples, noting 16:37. Ibn Atiyya allows Hell or worldly punishment as the consequence. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن,قيل); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الجبائي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 16:36/q11/p4 Their misguidance became manifest or evident. — Razi reports al-Kabi's analogy of saying an oppressor's injustice became manifest; Razi dismisses this as forced in light of rational proofs that guidance and misguidance come from God. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكعبي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 16:36/q11/p5 God decrees that wrongdoers be led astray once they themselves stray. — Razi reports al-Kabi's alternative with 14:27 and rejects the forced reading on the strength of rational proofs for God's sole control. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكعبي); RAZI-FULL-]

## 16:36/q12 How are divine will, command, and human choice related here?  [فَمِنْهُم مَّنْ هَدَى ٱللَّهُ وَمِنْهُم مَّنْ حَقَّتْ عَلَيْهِ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةُ · theology]
turns on: Whether God's universal command entails universal willing or enabling, and whether human rejection is wholly proximate or decisive in producing error.
- 16:36/q12/p1 God commands belief universally but wills or creates faith for some and unbelief or misguidance for others; His command and creative will differ. — Zajjaj and Wahidi say the polytheists' 16:35 claim would be true if held as belief; Razi says God creates both outcomes, argues rational proofs, and says a universal command can coexist with selective will, unlike the Muʿtazilite alignment of command and will. Baydawi says outcomes occur by appointed means. Ibn Kathir distinguishes revealed will forbidding shirk from creative will allowing it, without approving unbelief. Biqai says divine will always occurs but command can be disobeyed and that divine willing does not make disobedience morally right, since disobedient peoples were punished. Alusi says pairing implies misguidance by divine will and rejects the claim that willing evil makes God evil: evil characterizes its acquirer. Ibn Ashur reconciles human proximate causes—temperament, imitation, false preaching—with God's creation of their laws; Study Quran notes 14:27 and 16:37 on God leading wrongdoers astray. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(القاضي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ashʿarite theologians); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 16:36/q12/p2 The verse supports human choice under universal divine guidance or favor suited to recipients, and denies that God wills evil. — Abu Hayyan reports al-Zamakhshari's argument: every nation received commands to believe and avoid false gods, while God guides those fit for gracious help and forsakes the irredeemably unbelieving; the deniers' fate removes doubt that God creates or wills evil. Study Quran reports Muʿtazilites making deserved error the product of moral choice; Ibn Ashur says persistence is the proximate cause though God creates causal conditions. Alusi reports the Irshad explanation of willing in voluntary acts through choices and his own cautious suggestion that inward preparedness may matter, which he calls remote and disputable. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Muʿtazilite commentators); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 16:36/q12/p3 God guided and enabled everyone equally, including those who ultimately erred. — Qurṭubi attributes this claim to the Qadariyya and uses the verse's distinction to reject it. Tabrisi separately says proofs were equally available to believers and unbelievers, but rejects equating that presentation with guidance in this clause. [0 notes: QURTUBI-FULL-; TABRISI-]

## 16:36/q13 Why send messengers if God determines the outcomes?  [بَعَثْنَا فِى كُلِّ أُمَّةٍۢ رَّسُولًا فَمِنْهُم مَّنْ هَدَى ٱللَّهُ · theology]
- 16:36/q13/p1 Sending and clear proclamation are God's established practice and complete the proof against rejecters, despite His knowledge or will concerning outcomes. — Razi presents the objection that God's determining faith makes messengers futile, parallels 6:148 and the Muʿtazilite argument, and answers that God cannot be required to justify His acts; their error is inferring that prophets could not be sent. Alusi reports the Imam's same reply. Bursevi says the command removes excuses despite foreknown disobedience; Biqai says punishment of disobedient peoples proves messengers truthful and removes their defense; Jishumi says messengers must live to deliver their charge. Ibn Kathir says the revealed prohibition of shirk supplies decisive proof and wisdom; Tabatabai says a prophetic command invites rather than compels. [13 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); TABATABAI]

## 16:36/q14 Was the polytheists' divine-will excuse sincere or sarcastic?  [بَعَثْنَا فِى كُلِّ أُمَّةٍۢ رَّسُولًا · theology]
turns on: Whether the excuse in 16:35 was earnest belief or mockery, and whether its flaw lies in the statement or its inference.
- 16:36/q14/p1 They sincerely claimed God willed their shirk, but falsely used that claim to excuse it or disallow prophetic missions. — Jishumi likens their belief to the Mujbira, says they were sincere, and argues the messengers' contrary teaching refutes the excuse; Razi says the error is the inference that willing makes sending prophets impermissible, not the claim of divine will itself, and rejects the sarcasm answer. Jishumi also notes their acknowledgement of God coexisted with association. [5 notes: JISHUMI(المجبرة); RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI+; RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون والمفسرون)+]
- 16:36/q14/p2 They said the claim sarcastically, as Shuayb's people called him forbearing and upright. — Razi attributes this answer to earlier theologians and exegetes, comparing 11:87, but rejects it because sincere belief in the claim would make them believers. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL(المتكلمون والمفسرون)-]

## 16:36/q15 Why say that misguidance became due instead of directly saying God misled them?  [وَمِنْهُم مَّنْ حَقَّتْ عَلَيْهِ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةُ · rhetoric]
- 16:36/q15/p1 The asymmetric wording foregrounds their bad choice and persistence as the immediate cause of error. — Alusi contrasts the expected wa-minhum man adalla Allah and compares attribution of healing in 26:80; Ibn Ashur says their resolve fixed their earlier error, while other verses still ascribe misguidance to God, citing 6:125 and the majority reading of 16:37. His reconciliation traces proximate causes to minds, temperaments, imitation and false preaching, ultimately created by God. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 16:36/q16 Why is the verb feminine in haqqat, unlike comparable wording elsewhere?  [حَقَّتْ عَلَيْهِ ٱلضَّلَٰلَةُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether agreement is explained by grammatical distance and the feminine antecedent, or by a contrast between worldly deviation and afterlife punishment.
- 16:36/q16/p1 Feminine agreement fits the feminine subject and the feminine community implied by minhum; the comparison has more separation and a masculine equivalent. — Burhan compares 7:30, saying min refers to umma and greater verb-subject separation there better licenses omission. Itqan states the general rule for a truly feminine subject, adds that more intervening words favor omission, and contrasts feminine umma with masculine fariq, citing 3:113. Samarrai notes that masculine agreement can follow a masculine intended sense. [6 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 16:36/q16/p2 Feminine agreement signals worldly misguidance itself; masculine agreement elsewhere signals afterlife punishment. — Samarrai distinguishes worldly contexts, where misguidance remains real, from the afterlife, where realities become clear and the intended masculine sense is punishment; he cites 7:29. The two Lamasat passages repeat the semantic-agreement argument. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 16:36/q17 How is the nūn vowel in an iʿbudu read and explained?  [أَنِ ٱعْبُدُوا۟ · readings]
- 16:36/q17/p1 A kasra breaks the meeting of two consonants at rest; a damma may follow the preceding vowel or the vowel in ʿibāda. — The qiraat note gives both phonological explanations, without naming a reader in the supplied text. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vhr)]

## 16:36/q18 Who is addressed by the command to travel?  [فَسِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
- 16:36/q18/p1 The present polytheist deniers, especially Quraysh or Mecca's unbelievers; the warning also applies more widely. — Muqatil names Mecca's disbelievers; Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi name Quraysh or the polytheists. Study Quran particularly identifies Arabs who reportedly passed ruined sites in travel, while extending the command more broadly. Biqai addresses doubters of the messengers. [6 notes: MUQATIL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 16:36/q19 What should travel and looking at the deniers' fate teach?  [فَسِيرُوا۟ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ فَٱنظُرُوا۟ كَيْفَ كَانَ عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْمُكَذِّبِينَ · interpretation]
- 16:36/q19/p1 Inspect the ruins of earlier deniers, reflect, and take warning that the same punishment may overtake present deniers. — Tabari, Thalabi, Baghawi, Qurṭubi and Nasafi describe ruined homes and divine destruction; Wahidi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Razi, Tabrisi and Biqai emphasize reflection, proof and warning. Ibn Kathir says the earlier disobedient were punished after warning and cites 47:10 and 67:18. Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi name Ad and Thamud; Biqai adds later peoples and their dwellings, plus inherited stories from disbelieving ancestors. Study Quran cites travel parallels 3:137, 6:11, 27:69, 30:42 and the general value of journeying at 30:9. Ibn Atiyya urges investigation; Abu Hayyan says inspection confirms rejection's outcome. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 16:36/q20 What fate did the deniers meet?  [عَٰقِبَةُ ٱلْمُكَذِّبِينَ · interpretation]
- 16:36/q20/p1 Worldly ruin and destruction through punishment, with the Fire following for the deniers. — Yahya reports Qatada's sequence of destruction then Fire; Muqatil points to visible remains of this-world punishment; Thalabi, Baghawi, Qurṭubi, Nasafi, and others describe devastated lands or homes. Ibn Atiyya allows worldly punishment or Hell as the decisive end; Razi reports al-Jubbai using the destroyed nations to explain misguidance as punishment. ## 16:36/q21 What rhetorical force do the order, fa, and question about the fate carry?  [فَمِنْهُم فَسِيرُوا۟ فَٱنظُرُوا۟ كَيْفَ · rhetoric]
- 16:36/q21/p1 The sequence turns the division into an urgent, visible proof: guided people are mentioned first, then the audience is directed at once to ask about and inspect deniers' end. — Biqai says mentioning the guided first supports the following claim about those fixed in error, and that direct address follows a proof accessible to insight because sensory evidence remains; he says the fa indicates immediate action unlike slower thumma in 6:11, and kayfa prompts inquiry. Bursevi also takes fa as urging prompt reflection; Alusi says the travel command follows misguidance without spelling out punishment because the result is obvious, and its second fa urges saving reflection. Alusi also analyzes the fa of fa-minhum as explanatory or resumptive after an omitted consequence. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 16:36/q22 How does the verse connect to 16:35 and the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
- 16:36/q22/p1 It explains that messengers clearly deliver God's universal command, rebuts the polytheists' excuse, and sets past guided and ruined peoples before the present audience. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan connect it to 16:35's limit on messengers' duty; Ibn Ashur says it develops that rebuttal and the earlier example of predecessors. Biqai says an earlier plural for messengers implies distribution, made explicit here; it answers the expected question, rebuts objections, consoles the Prophet, and urges reflection. Tabatabai ties the universal practice to the preceding verse. Alusi questions two proposed interpretive hints and whether a division must be exclusive, especially if the following clause answers part of the first objection. Samarrai says the universal call to worship fits al-Nahl's worship context. Abu Hayyan reports an alternative connection in which li-yubayyina refers to sending a messenger to every nation to expose disagreement and former error. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 16:36/q23 What ancillary lexical and report material appears in the supplied notes?  [* · reports]
- 16:36/q23/p1 Ibn al-Jawzi's adjoining lexical notes treat food and prompting, and report the Prophet's description of Zamzam and a recitation-prompting instruction. — Ibn al-Jawzi says ta'am means food, ta'imtu 'I tasted,' istaṭʿama al-hadith asks for narration, ta'im can describe someone well fed, mit'am a frequent host or heavy eater, muṭ'am one provided for, and tu'ma food or livelihood. A bow is muṭ'ima because it brings game, illustrated by Dhu al-Rumma's verse. He reports the Prophet calling Zamzam satisfying food and a cure, and a report telling congregants to prompt an imam who requests it. These notes do not explain a word of 16:36. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 16:36/q24 Why are deniers accountable although their acts occur within God's will?  [* · theology]
- 16:36/q24/p1 Their chosen disobedience of the messengers violates God's command and establishes the proof against them by ordinary human standards. — Biqai distinguishes the occurrence of their choice within God's will from their own choice to disobey, for which they are answerable. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
