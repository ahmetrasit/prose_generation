# 107:5: 19 questions

## 107:5/q01 How does the relative clause identify the worshippers?  [ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it restricts the warning, and how the relative clauses attach.
- 107:5/q01/p1 It restricts the woe to prayer neglecters, with the next relative clause attaching to this group. — Al-Māwardī says it supplies the omitted qualification of 107:4; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it sharpens the taunt; al-Samīn allows nominative, accusative, or genitive adjective, apposition, or explanatory substitution. [8 notes: MAWARDI; SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(برهان القرآن)]
- 107:5/q01/p2 The plural relative describes a generic singular denier; an alternative construes the prayer warning as a separate digression. — Al-Zamakhsharī omits the answer to araʾayta and coordinates fa-dhālika with the denier; Abū Ḥayyān reports both a separate group and the same orphan-abuser; al-Ālūsī cites the objection that a named orphan-abuser does not fit later labels. [6 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI(صاحب الكشف); QASHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 107:5/q02 Who are those described as heedless of their prayer?  [ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the praying group is hypocrites, other deniers, sinful believers, or all accountable people.
- 107:5/q02/p1 Hypocrites who pray before people but abandon it in private. — Ibn ʿAbbās links public ostentation and private omission to 107:6 and 4:142; al-Jishumī and al-Qurṭubī prefer hypocrites because the next clause says they show off; al-Rāzī uses outward prayer to answer the objection that the verse calls them worshippers; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Mālik's Medinan interpretation. ## 107:5/q04 What does sāhūn mean here?  [سَاهُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the basic lapse is inadvertent or a figure for settled neglect.
- 107:5/q04/p1 It denotes heedlessness, negligence, or turning away from prayer. — Abū Ḥayyān defines sahā ʿan as neglecting or leaving through inattention; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes its other Qurʾānic use in 51:11–14; Corpus Coranicum compares ghafla and an observation attributed to Andrae about Ephrem. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; BIQAI]
- 107:5/q04/p2 Its literal inadvertent forgetfulness is used sarcastically for deliberate neglect. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 6:41, 7:136, and 10:7, where forgetting or heedlessness conveys turning away; the mockery calls people who scarcely pray worshippers. [6 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 107:5/q07 Are unintended mistakes within prayer subject to this warning?  [عَن صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ · law]
turns on: Whether a lapse is unavoidable and excused or habitual inattention shows contempt.
- 107:5/q07/p1 Ordinary involuntary distraction or forgetfulness is excused and can be repaired. — Saʿd says everyone has thoughts; al-Māwardī says unavoidable lapse is forgiven; al-Khaṭṭābī and al-Zamakhsharī contrast fī; al-Rāzī cites prostration or voluntary prayer; Ibn al-ʿArabī says no one avoids forgetfulness and the Prophet and Companions experienced it. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects making it sinful. [34 notes: MUJAHID(سعد بن أبي وقاص); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL(سعد بن أبي وقاص); KHATTABI(أبو العالية؛ الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عبد الله,أنس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعض العلماء); RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن العربي,الزمخشري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,الزمخشري); NASAFI(أنس والحسن); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 107:5/q07/p2 Persistent, self-produced distraction betrays disrespect and is blameworthy. — Ibn al-Jawzī reads Abū al-ʿĀliya's count error as habitual, not rare; al-Burṣawī distinguishes involuntary lapses from self-caused heedlessness such as drunken misconduct. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI]
- 107:5/q07/p3 The Prophet's apparent lapse was an enacted demonstration of the law, not real forgetfulness. — Al-Rāzī reports many scholars who say God let the Prophet perform a forgetful person's action, since practice teaches more forcefully than words. [3 notes: RAZI(كثير من العلماء); RAZI-FULL]

## 107:5/q08 Why can people called worshippers be condemned for neglecting prayer?  [صَلَاتِهِمْ سَاهُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'worshippers' denotes actual prayer, outward form, former practice, or sarcasm.
- 107:5/q08/p1 They count as worshippers by outward form, though their prayer lacks faith and sincerity. — Al-Jishumī invokes 9:69 for naming an outward form by extension; al-Rāzī answers that hypocrites show prayer but forget it inwardly; al-Nasafī says they move mechanically without seeking nearness or duty. [11 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN]
- 107:5/q08/p2 Calling them worshippers is sarcastic: deliberate omission makes the title hollow. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the mocking use to 74:43–44 and says the contrast with real forgetfulness reinforces the taunt. [10 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 107:5/q08/p3 Calling a total abandoner a worshipper is strained; occasional abandoners still qualify. — Al-Rāzī objects that abandonment alone is neither hypocrisy nor unbelief; al-Ālūsī suggests former identity for a total abandoner and continuing occasional worship for someone who sometimes omits prayer. [7 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الرازي); ALUSI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 107:5/q10 What legal or theological consequence is drawn from this threat?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the severity implies hypocritical unbelief and whether disbelievers bear subsidiary legal duties.
- 107:5/q10/p1 The threat is specifically to hypocrites, whose violations bring additional punishment for legal duties. — Al-Rāzī prefers this because mere neglect, ostentation, and withheld help do not alone make a Muslim an unbeliever; he says it supports al-Shāfiʿī's view that unbelievers are addressed by subsidiary rulings. [8 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI(قيل); RAZI-FULL+; RAZI+]
- 107:5/q10/p2 The warning marks a grave moral failure but does not automatically make every negligent believer a denier. — Al-Biqāʿī compares trait language in hadith about hypocrisy and fornication; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ treats ritual practice without care for the vulnerable as failed faith. [7 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); BINTSHATI; ELMALILI]

## 107:5/q11 How does this verse connect to the sūra's surrounding warning?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the warning develops the same denier's traits, shifts to another group, or contrasts duties to God and people.
- 107:5/q11/p1 Neglect of prayer and ostentation expose denial of judgment alongside neglect of the orphan and needy. — Al-Rāzī contrasts injury to creatures with failed service to the Creator and answers why this prayer does not restrain indecency; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī describe an escalation. Al-Biqāʿī calls denial of recompense the root; Corpus Coranicum sees crossing religious and social omissions. [34 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(ابن عباس ومجاهد والإمام مالك,بعضهم,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 107:5/q11/p2 The later warning may address a separate group of religious pretenders. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the apparent separate-group reading; al-Kashshāf offers the alternate syntax; Elmalılı says these pretenders are even more blameworthy than deniers who mistreat the poor. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); KASHSHAF; ELMALILI; ALUSI(صاحب الكشف)]
- 107:5/q11/p3 The wording expands a denier's traits to a generic class and makes woe cover all the preceding vices. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ links the warning, the plural generalizes a named denier, and placing wayl before the noun signals punishment for prior vices. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(مالك); QASHANI]

## 107:5/q12 What other passages or sūras are used to explain this warning?  [* · links]
- 107:5/q12/p1 Other Qurʾānic passages illustrate negligent or showy prayer and its consequences. — Commentators cite 4:142 for lazy ostentatious hypocrites, 19:59 for lost prayer, 2:238 for the middle prayer, and 8:35 for prayer at the House. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites 29:45, 2:177 and 90:11–17 for prayer's moral fruit. [21 notes: BAGHAWI; RAZI; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:5/q12/p2 Sūrat al-Kawthar answers al-Māʿūn's vices through giving, prayer, exclusive devotion, and sacrifice. — Al-Biqāʿī and al-Itqān give the four-part correspondence; Elmalılı says al-Kawthar condenses preceding sūras especially from al-Ḍuḥā. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ELMALILI]
- 107:5/q12/p3 Al-Anfāl 8:4 pairs true prayer with giving, reversing al-Māʿūn's neglect. — Al-Biqāʿī cites the true believers who establish prayer and spend from provision. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:5/q13 What inward reading is drawn from prayer heedlessness?  [* · inward]
- 107:5/q13/p1 The warning concerns lost heart presence and the unseen realities of prayer. — Al-Sulamī presents prayer as connection and communion with God; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says an unusually severe warning to those whose prayer lacks the heart implies still worse peril for abandonment. Al-Burṣawī says prayer is an ascent and warns against missing its subtle sciences. [8 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,بعضهم); BURSEVI; QASHANI; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 107:5/q14 What revelation setting or occasion is reported for these verses?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the passage arose in Mecca, Medina, or a named incident.
- 107:5/q14/p1 The sūra is Meccan. — Al-Jishumī reports Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrima and Qatāda; Asad dates it early, probably after sūra 102, and rejects a later Medinan split for lack of evidence. [4 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس، الحسن، عكرمة، قتادة); ASAD-NOTES(some commentators); STUDYQURAN]
- 107:5/q14/p2 The sūra is Medinan. — Al-Jishumī attributes this to al-Ḍaḥḥāk and al-Wāqidī; Qutb favors it because ostentatious hypocrisy suited Medina, while allowing thematic later addition. [3 notes: JISHUMI(الضحاك، الواقدي); QUTB-ZILAL; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:5/q14/p3 The opening is Meccan and the warning from 'woe to worshippers' onward is Medinan. — Muqātil is cited for the split; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ lists it as another possibility. [3 notes: JISHUMI(مقاتل); BURHAN(مقاتل بن سليمان); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(some commentators)-]
- 107:5/q14/p4 A report places revelation among coerced Meccan converts who sometimes prayed with Muslims and later harmed them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports their weak commitment and suffering under persecution. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA]
- 107:5/q14/p5 A report says the sūra concerned Abū Sufyān striking an orphan who sought meat. — Ibn Jurayj says Abū Sufyān slaughtered a camel weekly and struck the orphan with a stick. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA(ابن جريج)]
- 107:5/q14/p6 A report assigns verses 4–6 to ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Hibat Allāh ibn Salāma's al-Nāsikh wa-l-Mansūkh. [1 notes: IBNASHUR(هبة الله بن سلامة)]

## 107:5/q15 What special virtue or numerical reading of the sūra is reported?  [* · sciences]
- 107:5/q15/p1 A report promises forgiveness for one who recites the sūra and pays zakāt. — Al-Jishumī transmits it from Ubayy without grading. [1 notes: JISHUMI(النبي)]
- 107:5/q15/p2 Al-Biqāʿī draws historical dates from counting the sūra's words and the basmala. — Twenty-five words point, in his count, to year twelve AH and Abū Bakr's defeat of apostates and zakāt refusers; adding the basmala points to year sixteen and the departure of other religious groups from Arabia; he rejects pronoun and letter counts. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 107:5/q16 What rhetorical form sharpens the description of these worshippers?  [ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ سَاهُونَ · rhetoric]
- 107:5/q16/p1 A stable descriptive clause makes the neglect a settled trait rather than a passing event. — Al-Biqāʿī says the description identifies people obligated to pray and its inward absence is exposed by the next verse. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BIQAI]
- 107:5/q16/p2 Repeating the relative expression permits coordination of verbal clauses. — Burṣawī cites Burhān al-Qurʾān: a verb cannot simply be coordinated directly with a noun. [1 notes: BURSEVI(برهان القرآن)]

## 107:5/q17 What accompanying notes about adjacent wording or sources bear on this verse?  [* · links]
- 107:5/q17/p1 The warning extends through ostentation and withholding aid, which expose deficient prayer. — Al-Nasafī distinguishes public obligatory worship from showing off and explains the reciprocal verb for seeking praise; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives similar morphology. Ibn ʿAbbās identifies al-māʿūn as refused loans; Ibn ʿĀshūr lists money, zakāt, utensils, water, fire, and salt; Corpus Coranicum points to 108:2 for ṣalāh. [18 notes: TAB(ابن عباس); DURR(ابن عباس); NASAFI; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب,عائشة,مالك); CORPUSCORANICUM; BINTSHATI; RAZI-FULL]
- 107:5/q17/p2 The surrounding warning uses wayl for a grave offense. — Al-Ṭabarī glosses wayl as a valley of Hell's pus; al-Rāzī cites similar warnings at 83:1, 2:79, and 104:1 and a report on the damned wailing by sin. [5 notes: TAB; RAZI; STUDYQURAN]
- 107:5/q17/p3 The sūra is framed as a rebuke of denial and failures in worship and aid. — Al-Jishumī links Quraysh's security and provision to denial; Qutb describes integrated religious and social duties; Corpus Coranicum calls verses 4–6 religious omissions. [6 notes: JISHUMI; QUTB-ZILAL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 107:5/q17/p4 Two linguistic and one emancipation reports occur beside the commentary without applying the verse's prayer warning. — Al-Khaṭṭābī cites a Prophetic distinction between freeing a person and contributing to emancipation, then reports Sībawayh and Bedouin judges rejecting al-Kisāʾī's use of iyyā in a nominative context. [3 notes: KHATTABI(الكسائي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,سيبويه)]

## 107:5/q18 What further traits make the worshippers' heedlessness blameworthy?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether culpability lies in intention, outward identification, general distraction, or ostentation.
- 107:5/q18/p1 Their neglect is knowing and attributable to their own choice. — Asad specifies knowing neglect; al-Jishumī says rebuke makes sense only if the servant, rather than God, causes the heedlessness. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES; JISHUMI+]
- 107:5/q18/p2 They remain outwardly identified with prayer while inwardly careless of it. — Ibn Kathīr describes people committed to prayer yet heedless; al-Rāzī says prayer left unmade beyond its time and token hypocritical prayer can become mockery of religion. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR; RAZI]
- 107:5/q18/p3 They are distracted and inattentive to prayer. — Mujāhid and Qatāda give these concise glosses; Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Mujāhid's 'distracted' wording. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI(قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد)]
- 107:5/q18/p4 They pray for show and feel no regret when missing prayer. — Al-Ḥasan gives this combined description in the notes of Mujāhid and al-Thaʿlabī. [2 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); THALABI(الحسن)]

## 107:5/q19 What prayer requirement is cited with this warning?  [* · law]
- 107:5/q19/p1 An adult woman's prayer is not accepted without a head covering. — Al-Biqāʿī cites this as a Prophetic ruling and supplies no grading. [1 notes: BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
