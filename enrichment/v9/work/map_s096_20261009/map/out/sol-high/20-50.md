# 20:50: 15 questions

## 20:50/q01 Who answers Pharaoh, and why is the reply singular?  [قَالَ · referent]
- 20:50/q01/p1 Moses alone answers. — Al-Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī say Pharaoh addressed Moses specifically; al-Qurṭubī cites the singular verb. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds that Moses claims attributes Pharaoh cannot share. Al-Rāzī offers two explanations for singling him out after addressing both brothers: Moses is the principal prophet and Aaron his minister, or Pharaoh aimed at Moses' stutter and Aaron's eloquence. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:50/q02 Who is included in 'our Lord'?  [رَبُّنَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural names the two envoys or the full creation.
- 20:50/q02/p1 The pronoun includes Moses and Aaron. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as one scope of the plural. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:50/q02/p2 The pronoun includes all creatures, including Pharaoh. — Bursevī expressly includes Moses, Aaron, Pharaoh and others; al-Ālūsī attributes the universal scope to some investigators. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:50/q03 How does 'our Lord, the one who...' function grammatically?  [رَبُّنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an unstated pronoun precedes 'our Lord'.
- 20:50/q03/p1 'Our Lord' is subject and the relative clause is its predicate. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as the first parse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:50/q03/p2 'Our Lord' is the predicate of an omitted 'He is,' and the relative clause modifies it. — Al-Ālūsī gives this alternative parse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:50/q04 What does God give each thing as its 'creation'?  [أَعْطَىٰ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلْقَهُۥ · interpretation]

Al-Biqāʿī includes shape, size, color, temperament, benefits and effects; Asad includes inner nature and outer form. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes genera, species, classes and individuals. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite poetry using khalq for form. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr adds fitting mating and that creatures do not control their own creation, provision or mating. Al-Qurṭubī says the wording embraces all reported interpretations. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī (TABATABAI:6:122-127/r1) states that God gives each thing its form before directing it toward its created perfection. ## 20:50/q05 How are the two objects of 'gave' parsed?  [أَعْطَىٰ كُلَّ شَىْءٍ خَلْقَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Which noun receives the gift and which names the gift.
- 20:50/q05/p1 'Every thing' is the first object and 'its creation' the second. — Al-Ālūsī treats khalq as a passive participle with a pronoun returning to every thing; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes khalq as origination. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn also connect this order to fitted form, and al-Samīn allows a mate. Al-Ṭabarī construes an omitted 'like' in the mate explanation. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القشيري); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:50/q05/p2 'Its creation' is the first object and 'every thing' the second, fronted. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and others read the gift as everything creatures need or use; al-Bayḍāwī says the second object is advanced for emphasis. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the syntax allows it and cites 6:99. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Jubbāʾī's version but finds the usual parsing clearer. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:50/q05/p3 Khalq is a verbal noun: each thing receives the act of creation suited to its readiness. — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposed construction and judges it weak. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:50/q06 How is khalqahu read, and what follows from the reading?  [خَلْقَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Whether the lām is quiescent or open, and whether the resulting clause leaves an object implicit.
- 20:50/q06/p1 With a still lām, khalqahu is a noun meaning 'its creation.' — Al-Jishumī says the readers otherwise have sukūn; al-Ṭabrisī says the other readers pronounce it so and explains the verse as each animal's own form. [3 notes: JISHUMI(القراء); TABRISI(نصر عن الكسائي)]
- 20:50/q06/p2 With an open lām, khalaqahu is a past verb, 'he created.' — Nusayr/Naṣr transmits al-Kisāʾī; reports also name ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Aʿmash, Ibn al-Sumayfaʿ, ʿAbd Allāh, al-Ḥasan, Abū Nuhayk, Ibn Abī Isḥāq, Ibn Nūḥ, Salām and Companions. The clause modifies 'all' or 'thing'; the second object of 'gave' is omitted, whether a creature's completion, benefit, needs, or God's bounty generally. Al-Kashf favors the absolute omission, while al-Ālūsī hesitates and prefers another analysis. [22 notes: THALABI(نصير); JISHUMI(نصير عن الكسائي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(نصر عن الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عمر بن الخطاب وابن عباس والأعمش وابن السميفع ونصير عن الكسائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(نصير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,عبد الله، أناس من أصحاب رسول الله، أبو نهيك، ابن أبي إسحاق، الأعمش، الحسن، نصير، ابن نوح، وسلام); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله والحسن والأعمش وأبو نهيك وابن أبي إسحاق ونصير عن الكسائي وناس من أصحاب رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,عبد الله، وأناس من أصحاب رسول الله، وأبو نهيك، وابن أبي إسحاق، والأعمش، والحسن، ونصير، وابن نوح، وسلام)]

## 20:50/q07 Does 'every thing' cover individuals or kinds?  [كُلَّ شَىْءٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether incidental defects and the past tense limit a universal statement.
- 20:50/q07/p1 It covers every individual in its fitting rank. — Al-Ālūsī rejects restricting it because incidental defects do not undo each thing's completeness in its own rank; he cites 32:7 and 67:3 and says past tense does not establish a restriction. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows distributive generality: each receives its own creation as each rider mounts their own animal. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues unqualified wording extends guidance to everything. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī (TABATABAI:6:122-127/r1) expressly applies creational guidance to each kind and each individual, including its appointed course and lifespan. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 20:50/q07/p2 It denotes kinds, or conventional generality limited to suitable recipients. — Some in al-Ālūsī invoke defective individuals; al-Ālūsī counters that even if many individuals do not yet exist, already existing kinds do not settle the past-tense objection. Ibn ʿĀshūr permits conventional generality; the animal-mate explanation likewise takes 'all' as abundance. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، ابن جبير، السدي,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:50/q08 To what does God guide creatures?  [هَدَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance is general practical direction, mating instinct, the universal movement toward an end, religious guidance, destiny, or a later faculty.
- 20:50/q08/p1 He directs each creature toward its needs, welfare and livelihood. — Mujāhid, Qatāda and Yahyā speak of learned benefit and taking what is needed; al-Suddī and Muqātil specify pasture and food, with grain and meat differing by animal. Ibn Qutayba includes avoiding danger; Ibn ʿAbbās lists food, drink, dwelling, birth and mates. Al-Rāzī adds refining materials, preparing medicines and food, bees' hexagonal cells, and offspring finding mothers' breasts. Al-Ḥasan connects guidance to what makes a thing sound; Abū Isḥāq and al-Zajjāj include mating within the broader welfare sense. Al-Qurṭubī says general wording encompasses the explanations, and Ibn Qutayba says their senses relate. ## 20:50/q09 What does 'then' mark between giving and guiding?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the sequence is temporal, ranked, or both.
- 20:50/q09/p1 It marks real succession: formation precedes later guidance or powers. — Al-Rāzī argues bodies precede cognitive and motive faculties, citing Adam's form before spirit and human stages in 15:29 and 23:12–14; Bursevī similarly says creation precedes endowment with powers. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows both temporal and ranked sequence and compares the paths shown in 90:8–10. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:50/q09/p2 It marks rank or ontological order, without requiring elapsed time. — Al-Biqāʿī makes the imparting of spirit majestic beside other differences. Al-Ālūsī says guidance follows creation in order of being even without time separating them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says bodily beings' progression can also unfold in time; Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise allows both. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:50/q10 Why say God 'gave' creation?  [أَعْطَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:50/q10/p1 The verb presents creation as a bestowed blessing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'gave' reminds hearers that creation is a blessing and proof of lordship. Al-Burhān says the verb suits beings whose existence lasts only a moment. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]

## 20:50/q11 What does Moses' formulation establish about the Lord?  [قَالَ رَبُّنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَعْطَىٰ هَدَىٰ · theology]
turns on: How acts of creation and guidance establish divine lordship and human response.
- 20:50/q11/p1 Creation and guidance prove one self-sufficient, powerful, knowing Lord to whom beings depend. — Al-Qushayrī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Rāzī infer a creator and guide from the acts; al-Rāzī cites 87:1–3 and 26:77–78, biological design, bees' hexagonal cells, and the body's reversed elemental order. Death proves powers contingent; neither humans nor parents designed them. A bodily cause regresses, so an incorporeal necessary manager acts by choice, knows, and has unlimited power. Al-Biqāʿī rejects nature and stars as explanations for differentiated creatures. Al-Bayḍāwī stresses God's self-sufficiency; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says creating entails ownership and governance, including only dependent delegated possession (3:26). Ibn ʿĀshūr includes Pharaoh under 'everything'; he knows he did not create it. Al-Iṣlāḥī cites birdsong and plants, and 87:1–3; human capacities and sciences remain God's gifts. [34 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:50/q11/p2 The created world directs the self to God through proof and obliges following guidance. — Al-Qāshānī calls this guidance of the self by proof; al-Jishumī rejects imitation, necessity, inspiration and anthropomorphist accounts of knowing God, and says innovators lack salvation. Al-Ālūsī says Moses' method rebuts blind imitation and learning God solely from a messenger's assertion; one must hear objections to answer them, and even Pharaoh argued rather than abusing the claimant. Al-Iṣlāḥī cites 75:14–15 for inner testimony and warns that intellect and scientific achievement should not breed defiance. [8 notes: QASHANI; JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:50/q12 Why answer Pharaoh with these acts and words?  [قَالَ رَبُّنَا ٱلَّذِىٓ أَعْطَىٰ هَدَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the distinctive wording serves proof, the immediate call to guidance, or a timeless view of creation.
- 20:50/q12/p1 The answer is concise and comprehensive and supplies proof that Pharaoh cannot claim. — Al-Zamakhsharī and those citing him call it clear to an unbiased seeker; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Pharaoh cannot share the attributes. Al-Bayḍāwī says Pharaoh was confounded and changed subject; al-Iṣlāḥī calls the answer gentle yet cornering. Al-Ālūsī contrasts the mere title 'Lord of the worlds' with a description of origination, dependence and differing ranks; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī likewise says it supplies proof. Bursevī says the act points to its agent. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:50/q12/p2 Guidance is foregrounded because Pharaoh's exchange concerns a call to guidance. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says universal guidance particularly suits the context. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:50/q12/p3 The past tense expresses ongoing, timeless creation. — Asad reads the grammatical past as an unceasing present. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:50/q13 Did Pharaoh already know God?  [ٱلَّذِىٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether the relative description presupposes Pharaoh's recognition of a creator.
- 20:50/q13/p1 He knew of God but rebelled or questioned His essence. — Al-Rāzī and some in al-Ālūsī say a relative description assumes recognition. They cite Pharaoh's acknowledgment in 17:102, opponents' inner certainty in 27:14, 28:39 as denial of resurrection, his 'who' question and 'madman' charge in 26:23,27, and his rational awareness of his origin and limited realm. Al-Ālūsī says the question may concern unknowable essence after a proof of existence, but calls the arguments disputable; the Study Quran says God's essence is inaccessible, citing 26:23–24 and 3:13,15. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN]
- 20:50/q13/p2 He was ignorant of God in some manner. — Al-Ālūsī reports possibilities of denial of a maker, necessary-cause philosophy, star or idol worship, indwelling, or anthropomorphism. He says Pharaoh's claim to lordship over subjects demands their obedience, not necessarily that he created everything. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(من الناس)]

## 20:50/q14 How does this verse connect with other guidance passages and what follows?  [* · links]
turns on: Which other passages illuminate this general proof and its place in the exchange.
- 20:50/q14/p1 It parallels other creation-and-guidance verses and leads from general proof to particular signs. — Al-Rāzī cites 87:1–3 and 26:77–78, then says the next verse begins with the earth as a specific proof after the general one. Al-Damghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī connect inspired livelihood and mating to 87:2–3; Asad traces the idea to this early passage. Ibn Kathīr's reported deterministic gloss also cites 87:3. Al-Iṣlāḥī invokes 87:1–3 for Moses' answer. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī (TABATABAI:6:122-127/r1) links God's giving each thing its form and guiding it to 87:2–3. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض المفسرين); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 20:50/q14/p2 The exchange points to divine signs and the question of God's inaccessible essence. — The Study Quran links Pharaoh's later question to 26:23–24 and cites 3:13,15 for the limits of human description. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 20:50/q15 What inward reading follows from creation and guidance?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward point concerns universal living glorification or differing preparedness for destined ends.
- 20:50/q15/p1 Every being has life and its own capacity to know and glorify God. — Bursevī reports spiritual masters who distinguish formal life in humans, jinn and angels from meaningful life in inanimate things and plants; he cites 17:44 and ties knowledge to each thing's intellect, spirit and life. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]
- 20:50/q15/p2 Each being is prepared and eased toward its allotted purpose. — The Najmiyya reading says believers are receptive to faith and follow prophets, while disbelievers are disposed to rejection. Bursevī cites the Prophetic command to act because each is eased toward what they were created for; al-Maghribī's poem assigns obedience and disobedience, and Mālik and Riḍwān, to different people. [4 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,المغربي,النبي)]
