# 75:34: 11 questions

## 75:34/q01 What does awlā laka mean as a warning?  [أَوْلَىٰ لَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the idiom is heard as a curse, approaching harm, deserved punishment, an elliptical expression, or an ironic predication of his conduct; Iṣlāḥī explicitly disputes the 'befitting' rendering.
- 75:34/q01/p1 A menace or curse: woe to you, may the hated calamity befall you. — Ibn Qutayba calls it a fixed warning, comparing 47:20; his anonymous poet, al-Khansāʾ, a line addressed to Aws, and a poem cited by Qurtubī repeat awlā as a threat. Al-Zajjāj explains Arab usage as a curse; al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī call it a prayer for a hated outcome. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī report the idiomatic woe gloss; al-Burṣawī combines woe with approaching harm. Elmalılı likens it to a prayer of deserved calamity; Iṣlāḥī says wayl expresses anger, reproach, and hatred and cites al-Khansāʾ. The Study Quran records a fourfold woe rendering. Samarrāʾī explains the invocation in the context of hastening sins. [31 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أحمد بن يحيى بن تغلب,الخنساء); MAWARDI-FULL(الخنساء); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الخنساء); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs, Z); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 75:34/q01/p2 Harm or destruction has drawn near to the addressee. — Al-Naḥḥās says the formula addresses someone already ruined or nearly so, and elsewhere describes nearly perishing but escaping. Al-Aṣmaʿī cites poems for nearness, praised by Thaʿlab; al-Jawharī calls this the best explanation. Thaʿlab, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Jishumī, and al-Itqān connect it with walī and 9:123. Al-Ṭabrisī applies it to Abū Jahl; al-Bayḍāwī says God brings his hated fate close. Al-Ālūsī compares suḥqan and quotes poetry. Al-Burṣawī cites al-Qāmūs. Samarrāʾī says its comparative form signals proximity; Study Quran records both a hated outcome and punishment now nearer. Asad takes the passage to say the human end draws nearer hourly. [28 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(العرب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ITQAN(النحاس,ثعلب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الأصمعي); ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الأصمعي,الكشاف,روح المعاني); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Iṣ,JJ); ITQAN(الأصمعي)+; QURTUBI-FULL(ثعلب)+]
- 75:34/q01/p3 The addressee is more fittingly entitled to the punishment or destruction he deserves. — Some scholars in al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī stress merited punishment. Al-Jishumī gives both entitlement and nearness. Al-Qurṭubī reports suitability for punishment; al-Burṣawī's al-Qāmūs has destruction as the more fitting fate. Al-Ālūsī reports a favored reading with an implied Fire. Elmalılı takes woe and ruin as befitting him. Iṣlāḥī expressly disputes this rendering on Arabic usage and context. [9 notes: THALABI(بعض العلماء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(بعض العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(قيل); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قائلون)+; ELMALILI+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 75:34/q01/p4 Blame or condemnation is more fitting for him than leaving it unsaid. — Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Itqān, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe an omitted completion, lost through frequent idiomatic use until the phrase meant woe. [4 notes: TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 75:34/q01/p5 It rebukes him and urges him to stop his conduct. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it an Arabic rebuke and compares 47:20. Al-Biqāʿī says it can urge abandonment of the present behavior for something beneficial. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:34/q01/p6 It means separation and ruin in this life and the next, or distance from their good things. — Al-Qāḍī in al-Rāzī speaks of severed worldly and afterlife affairs; al-Jubbāʾī in al-Ṭabrisī speaks of exclusion from both worlds' goods. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); TABRISI(الجبائي)]
- 75:34/q01/p7 His present conduct is 'better for him' ironically: its burden and punishment now belong to him. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī supplies an omitted subject referring to the person's behavior and calls the predication metonymic; he says the reported comparative-Hell analysis comes close but does not fully fit. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]
- 75:34/q01/p8 A separate lexical gloss of al-ala denotes a condition or a funeral bier. — Al-Māwardī records both senses of al-ala; the note does not explain how either applies to this threat. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 75:34/q02 How is awlā formed and construed?  [أَوْلَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Its status as comparative, verb, verb-like noun, or proper name, and which subject or omitted completion its syntax requires; feminine awlāt is cited against a simple comparative, while al-Ālūsī challenges the metathesis and proper-name case.
- 75:34/q02/p1 It comes from the root for nearness, originally a comparative describing approaching ruin. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya derives it from walī; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and al-Itqān cite 9:123 for nearness. Al-Ālūsī calls it originally comparative, later used as a threat. Samarrāʾī and his ḥalaqāt account retain the comparative force. Al-Jishumī permits both entitlement and nearness. [11 notes: THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(العرب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي); ITQAN(ثعلب); ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:34/q02/p2 It is a comparative with an omitted element: Hell or punishment is more fitting for him. — Al-Ālūsī reports an implied Fire as subject; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports Hell as the omitted predicate or the addressee as deserving it. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this approaches but fails to match his own account of conduct as the omitted subject. [2 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قائلون)+; TABATABAI-]
- 75:34/q02/p3 It is a comparative with the addressee's conduct as its omitted subject. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes the conduct the subject and assigns the consequent burden and punishment to it by metonymy. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]
- 75:34/q02/p4 It is a fixed proper name for a threat or ruin, serving as subject with laka as predicate. — Al-Mahdawī denies that it is a comparative, citing Abū Zayd's feminine awlāt. Al-Qurṭubī calls it indeclinable like Aḥmad. Abū al-Baqāʾ gives faʿlā with an augmentative alif or afʿal, and Abū Zayd's feminine form retains no declension. Al-Itqān notes dispute over its pattern. Abū ʿAlī in al-Ālūsī derives the name for ruin from awīl by metathesis, patterned afʿal; al-Ālūsī objects that wayl is already indeclinable, the yawm aywam analogy is nonstandard, and neither inversion nor a generic proper name is grounded. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(المهدوي); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,أبو زيد); ITQAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:34/q02/p5 It is an indeclinable imperative or verb-like noun meaning successive evil is coming. — Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Itqān call it an ism fiʿl; laka then explains the address. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Samarrāʾī report the same analysis as woe or evil after evil. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ITQAN(قوم); TABATABAI(قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 75:34/q02/p6 It is a past verb or optative with destruction understood as its subject. — Al-Ālūsī reports al-Aṣmaʿī's verbal analysis with an implicit subject, destruction, and a possibly extra lām. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the optative 'may destruction approach you.' Al-Naḥḥās also supplies 'destruction' after laka in a nearly perished reading. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ITQAN(النحاس); TABATABAI-]
- 75:34/q02/p7 It is a past imprecative verb with God as subject, bringing near what he hates or destroying him. — Al-Bayḍāwī compares an extra lām with 27:72. Al-Burṣawī describes a shift from triliteral to ifʿāl to govern two objects. Al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report God's agency; the latter distinguishes extra lām ('God gives you what you hate') from original lām ('God brings you near it') and separately records a wish for uniquely near destruction. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 75:34/q02/p8 It is an inversion of awīl or wayl, yielding a word for woe. — Abū al-Haytham in al-Thaʿlabī cites another scholar and comparable Arabic inversions. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Itqān, and Elmalılı report the proposal, with al-Khansāʾ's line as evidence in al-Itqān. Abū ʿAlī applies the inversion to a proper-name analysis; al-Ālūsī disputes its grounds. [5 notes: THALABI(أبو الهيثم الجمي); BAYDAWI-FULL; ITQAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); ELMALILI(bazıları); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:34/q02/p9 A frequent ellipsis leaves 'blame is more fitting than silence' implicit. — Al-Ṭabrisī says the omitted completion can no longer be expressed in the fixed idiom; al-Qurṭubī, al-Itqān, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also report this analysis. [4 notes: TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ITQAN(قيل); TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]

## 75:34/q03 What is the force of the lām in laka?  [لَكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether awlā is a noun, verb-like noun, or verb, and whether the preposition contributes meaning, merely explains the addressee, or intensifies the curse.
- 75:34/q03/p1 It emphasizes the threat. — Al-Biqāʿī says the lām strengthens the warning. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 75:34/q03/p2 It is extra in a verbal construction. — Al-Bayḍāwī compares 27:72; al-Ālūsī says some take it as extra with destruction the verb's implicit subject; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says extra lām permits 'God gives you what you hate.' [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 75:34/q03/p3 It is the predicate of the subject awlā. — Al-Qurṭubī, Abū al-Baqāʾ, and al-Itqān use this with the indeclinable proper-name analysis. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ITQAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 75:34/q03/p4 It merely clarifies whom the verb-like noun addresses. — Abū al-Baqāʾ and al-Itqān call it explanatory with the ism fiʿl analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ITQAN(قوم)]
- 75:34/q03/p5 It is original to the verbal construction and denotes bringing the addressee near what he hates. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes this from the extra-lām reading. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]

## 75:34/q04 Is there a transmitted reading relevant to awlā here?  [أَوْلَىٰ · readings]
- 75:34/q04/p1 Abū ʿAmr opens the listed non-final words on routes that incline verse endings. — Al-Nashr adds that for Mūsā he uses his customary intermediate vowel where imāla is transmitted; this note does not specify a separate variant for this verse. [1 notes: NASHR(أبو عمرو)]

## 75:34/q05 Who receives this warning?  [أَوْلَىٰ لَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the reported address to Abū Jahl limits the verse or provides an example of the general description; Ibn ʿĀshūr uses the opening of the sūra and the flexibility of occasion language for the broader reading.
- 75:34/q05/p1 Abū Jahl ibn Hishām is its immediate addressee. — Muqātil and numerous occasion reports place the confrontation with him; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this the majority interpretation of the passage. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī name him as the threatened man; the report also calls him this community's Pharaoh. Al-Rāzī records al-Qaffāl's enemy as its target. [34 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); THALABI(أبو جهل,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); TABRISI(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(القفال,قتادة والكلبي ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة,محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(ابن جرير); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 75:34/q05/p2 It addresses every disbeliever who shares those traits, with Abū Jahl as an example. — Elmalılı extends the plainly applicable warning beyond its reported occasion; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the sūra's opening about resurrection and self-witness (75:3, 75:14) and cautions that early scholars can speak loosely of an occasion; the Study Quran records all swaggering rejecters. Ibn Kathīr calls the addressee a swaggering unbeliever, comparing the taunting threats of 44:49, 77:46, 39:15, and 41:40. Al-Qaffāl allows a new divine warning to unbelievers. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK); ELMALILI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 75:34/q06 Why is the threat repeated and joined with fa?  [أَوْلَىٰ فَأَوْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether repetition is emphatic, marks distinct faults, or distributes distinct harms; fa and thumma can mark immediate succession, elapsed stages, or relative rank. Al-Qurṭubī specifically rejects the life/death/resurrection/Hell assignment.
- 75:34/q06/p1 Repetition reinforces the same warning. — Ibn Qutayba compares emphatic repetitions in 102:3–4, 94:5–6, and 82:17–18; ʿAwf ibn al-Kharaʿ repeats awlā in a poem about Fazāra. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Burṣawī, al-Samīn, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, and Samarrāʾī explicitly call it intensification. Rūḥ al-Maʿānī says thumma can reinforce like a repeated oath. The ḥalaqāt account calls Qurʾānic repetition part of its inimitability. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(عوف بن الخرع); IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني,قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:34/q06/p2 Each recurrence is another threat or calamity after the previous one; fa signals close succession. — Muqātil links the threat after threat to Abū Jahl's own death threat. Qatāda, al-Māwardī, al-Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Damghānī describe successive warnings. Al-Bayḍāwī says they return again and again; al-Rāzī records another 'woe after that.' Ibn ʿĀshūr says fa brings the next imprecation immediately. Al-Biqāʿī says harms may be immediate or delayed and differ in severity, while the repetition marks the enormity and lasting desert of the offense. [12 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:34/q06/p3 The four warnings across 75:34–35 answer four faults: no faith, no prayer, denial, and turning away. — Al-Qurṭubī lists failure to believe the Messenger, failure to pray before God, denial, and turning away from prayer, noting strutting came before denial; another reported view distributes threats over the four deeds. Samarrāʾī says the later denial and turning away restate the two earlier faults, so each gets an initial and repeated warning. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:34/q06/p4 The repetitions distribute threatened sufferings over successive stages of death, grave, resurrection, and Fire. — Al-Thaʿlabī gives death, resurrection, and Hell; al-Ṭabrisī reports Badr, grave, Judgment, then Fire, with thumma marking sequence. Al-Qurṭubī reports life, death, resurrection, and Hell but calls that assignment weak. Kāshifī in al-Burṣawī specifies hard death, painful grave, resurrection's horrors, eternal Hell. Samarrāʾī mentions death, resurrection, and Hell; he also says fa joins closely spaced harms while thumma marks a longer interval involving grave and afterlife, comparing Pharaoh's people in 40:46. The Study Quran reports Badr, grave, resurrection, and Hell for Abū Jahl. [9 notes: THALABI; TABRISI(قتادة,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 75:34/q06/p5 The later warning is greater in rank; thumma need not express elapsed time. — Samarrāʾī compares 90:11–17, where faith stands beyond freeing and feeding in rank, to argue for a stronger later threat. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:34/q07 Why does the first warning include laka while the next omits it?  [لَكَ فَأَوْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the omission chiefly differentiates the faults' gravity or belongs to the repeated verbal pattern; Samarrāʾī states both.
- 75:34/q07/p1 The fuller first address gives the greater threat to unbelief, while the shorter one answers failure to pray. — Samarrāʾī says the different gravity of faith and prayer explains the omission, though the verse ending also supports it; his ḥalaqāt account compares the shift in 18:67 and 18:72. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:34/q07/p2 The formulation marks a patterned sequence of repeated warning. — Samarrāʾī observes that awlā repeats inside and across the verses while laka does not, with fa inside the verse and thumma between verses; he contrasts the single warning in 47:20. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:34/q08 Why does the passage turn to direct address here?  [أَوْلَىٰ لَكَ فَأَوْلَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 75:34/q08/p1 The shift from indirect speech conveys sorrow and hatred toward the person. — Iṣlāḥī identifies the change in address as the vehicle of these feelings. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:34/q09 What is reported about the warning's occasion and first speaker?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ on whether the Prophet spoke before revelation or used revealed words, who directed the warning, the encounter's place and gesture, and which later boast or death detail is included.
- 75:34/q09/p1 The Prophet first said the warning to Abū Jahl, and revelation then repeated his words. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās explicitly say the Prophet spoke first, with the latter specifying his own initiative. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Rāzī say the revelation confirmed or matched his warning; al-Qaffāl offers this as one possibility. Ibn ʿĀshūr cautions that reports of an occasion need not establish that the event first caused revelation. [9 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(القفال,قتادة والكلبي ومقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس)]
- 75:34/q09/p2 God first directed the Prophet to confront Abū Jahl with these words. — Al-Biqāʿī transmits al-Ṭabarī's report that Abū Jahl had mocked the nineteen keepers of Hell; Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Suyūṭī links this to the revelation of 74:30. Al-Māwardī says the encounter at Mecca's Baṭḥā followed Abū Jahl's swaggering. Al-Ṭabrisī says God revealed the words as foretold. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن جرير); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); TABRISI(رسول الله)]
- 75:34/q09/p3 The Prophet confronted him at al-Baṭḥā, seized his garment or lapels, and Abū Jahl boasted before his defeat at Badr. — Muqātil says Abū Jahl had threatened to kill the Prophet, who shoved his chest; al-Māwardī adds a poke and says verses followed. Qatāda's versions have a garment seizure and Abu Jahl's boast that neither Muhammad nor God could harm him; al-Thaʿlabī says the sons of ʿAfrāʾ and Ibn Masʿūd killed him, comparing 3:12. Al-Thaʿlabī also reports his claim that he would never follow a Quraysh youth even if Muhammad were God's messenger; Ibn ʿĀshūr places the gesture at a mosque door in one transmission. Ibn Kathīr's Qatāda version reverses the grip, making Abū Jahl seize the Prophet by his clothes. [11 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(أبو جهل,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 75:34/q09/p4 At the mosque entrance, the Prophet took Abū Jahl's hand, warned him, and he entered shaken. — Al-Jishumī locates this encounter outside the mosque; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports the mosque door and says the revelation answered Abū Jahl's boast. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 75:34/q09/p5 At the Banū Makhzūm gate, the Prophet took and shook Abū Jahl's hand before the words were revealed. — Al-Qurṭubī records this distinct location and hand shaking, followed by the boast of status and revelation in the spoken wording. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 75:34/q09/p6 The Prophet took Abū Jahl's hand, and Abū Jahl was later killed at Badr after another boast. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq's Qatāda report has Abū Jahl at Badr boast that God would no longer be worshipped. Al-Rāzī's report names Qatāda, al-Kalbī, and Muqātil and says revelation confirmed the warning. No location is specified in these notes. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); RAZI-FULL(قتادة والكلبي ومقاتل)]
- 75:34/q09/p7 The verse came about Abū Jahl's killing at Badr, and he was called this community's Pharaoh. — Al-Jishumī reports a terrible death; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, and al-Durr record the Prophet's saying that every community has a Pharaoh and Abū Jahl was this one's. [4 notes: THALABI(النبي); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 75:34/q09/p8 The verse is a fresh divine warning to unbelievers, rather than a repetition of the Prophet's earlier utterance. — This is al-Qaffāl's first possibility as reported by al-Rāzī. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال)]
- 75:34/q09/p9 God commands the Prophet to warn his enemy of an unbearable, near calamity. — This is al-Qaffāl's third possibility as reported by al-Rāzī. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال)]
- 75:34/q09/p10 The Prophet used an already revealed threat when addressing Abū Jahl. — Ibn ʿĀshūr makes this reading of the encounter, explaining that early occasion language can be loose. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 75:34/q10 How does the warning connect with the passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes relate the warning to the preceding faults, the sūra's haste motif, and the different circumstances of the similar formula in 47:20.
- 75:34/q10/p1 It answers the man's preceding denial, failure to pray, and arrogant departure. — Ibn Kathīr calls the taunting threat apt for a swaggering rejecter and compares 44:49, 77:46, 39:15, and 41:40. Al-Qurṭubī enumerates the faults in 75:31–33. Al-Biqāʿī sees the threat as a fitting substitute for his family's greeting. Elmalılı describes the dire fate, broken back, and dark distressed face rather than a laughing face. Iṣlāḥī says the deniers lack prayer and spending, their only useful provisions for an exacting journey. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:34/q10/p2 Its immediate warning matches the sūra's theme of hastening sin while delaying obedience. — Samarrāʾī says they chose immediate pleasures; ruin therefore comes quickly. He relates immediate fa to 75:5 and 75:20. The ḥalaqāt account cites preference for present life over the next at 75:20–21. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:34/q10/p3 Its repetition and strength contrast with the single awlā warning in 47:20 because the situations differ. — Ibn Qutayba and Ibn ʿAṭiyya cite 47:20 as a parallel threat or rebuke; al-Samīn refers its discussion to the end of Sūrat Muḥammad. Samarrāʾī says Muḥammad concerns living people hesitant before fighting, still able to repent, and names one fault, cowardice; Qiyāma concerns a denier after death with multiple faults and realized punishment, so its warning is repeated. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī also compares 47:20 to the consequence of the present state. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:34/q11 What spiritual consequence does the divine warning impose?  [* · theology]
- 75:34/q11/p1 It is God's word stamped on the person's heart, barring faith and piety and decreeing the Fire. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains the warning as a divine sealing with this effect. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
