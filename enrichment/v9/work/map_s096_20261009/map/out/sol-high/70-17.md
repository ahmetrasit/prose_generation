# 70:17: 12 questions

## 70:17/q01 How does the Fire call those named in this verse?  [تَدْعُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the Fire speaks by a God-created voice, the keepers speak, or calling is figurative; the Arabic idiom for destruction and poetic examples of figurative calling support other readings.
- 70:17/q01/p1 The Fire itself calls aloud, naming unbelievers and hypocrites, then seizes them. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj specify individual names; Ibn ʿAbbās says the clear or eloquent call is followed by snatching like a bird picking grain. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān add fathers' names. Al-Kashshāf, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursawī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr explain that God can create speech or voices in fire as in limbs, skin or a tree; Abū Ḥayyān says the tree comparison preserves al-Zamakhsharī's Muʿtazilite position. Some reports give its words as 'Come, unbeliever; come, hypocrite.' Ibn Kathīr locates the clear call and seizure at the gathering on Resurrection Day. [24 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:17/q01/p2 Calling figuratively describes the Fire drawing its allotted victims toward itself or their appearance and arrival at their destined place. — Ibn Qutayba says the destination seems to summon those bound for it; his she-camel, ʿAntara's horse, a wolf, Dhū al-Rumma's water sources, a valley and Abū al-Najm's buzzing flies illustrate speech inferred from condition or an attractive sign without words. Al-Māwardī likewise uses the mute fly. Al-Kashshāf cites Dhū al-Rumma and Abū al-Najm for people coming into view. Rāzī compares Hell's assigned places with the earth answering by reflection, without a roar. Al-Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī cite Dhū al-Rumma for drawing near; Ālūsī also cites his nights of pleasure enticing a follower. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites plants 'calling' a bull's nose. Al-Khalīl's version makes the Fire's readiness or power to punish, not an invitation, the point. [31 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبو النجم,ذو الرمة,عنترة); THALABI(الخليل); MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الخليل بن أحمد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(ذي الرمة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل وثعلب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ذو الرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL(ذو الرمة)]
- 70:17/q01/p3 Hell's keepers actually call, while the verse attributes their call to the Fire. — Al-Naḥḥās explicitly calls the attribution figurative. Rāzī and Ālūsī allow an omitted 'keepers'; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains attribution by place or cause. Al-Bayḍāwī's alternative says the Fire calls its keepers, rather than their calling the condemned. [10 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:17/q01/p4 Tadʿū means the Fire destroys or torments the people. — Taghlib, Thaʿlab, al-Mubarrad and al-Khalīl cite Arabic 'God called you' as a curse meaning destroyed, caused to die, made suffer, or punished. Al-Kashshāf, Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī cite the same idiom; Ālūsī also cites a verse asking God to call down a serpent and lexical usages for harm or the calamities of time. Ālūsī regards this possible but unusual and uncertain. Some sources use 'punish' or 'bring into torment' for al-Khalīl's explanation rather than simple destruction. [14 notes: THALABI(تغلب); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); DAMGHANI(المبرد,ثعلب); BAGHAWI-FULL(الخليل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ثعلب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ثعلب); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الخليل وثعلب); ALUSI-FULL(ثعلب والخليل)]
- 70:17/q01/p5 The Fire is personified as a host inviting guests to a banquet in sarcasm. — Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the verb here from the noun for invitation and says the description of the invited people cues or elaborates the sarcastic image. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 70:17/q02 How is tadʿū connected grammatically to the preceding description of the Fire?  [تَدْعُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The case reading of nazzāʿa and whether tadʿū supplies a predicate or a circumstantial description.
- 70:17/q02/p1 It is a predicate, either of inna, a second predicate, or of an omitted subject. — Al-Samīn lists the predicate of inna and of an omitted subject. Ālūsī lists the omitted subject or a second predicate on the nominative reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a second predicate on the nominative reading of nazzāʿatan. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:17/q02/p2 It is a circumstantial clause describing laẓā or nazzāʿa. — Al-Samīn allows this under either reading; Ālūsī allows nested or coordinate circumstantial clauses on the nominative reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr permits a circumstantial clause under either reading. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 70:17/q03 Who is the one whom the Fire calls?  [مَنْ أَدْبَرَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · referent]
- 70:17/q03/p1 The summoned are unbelievers, polytheists or hypocrites who rejected faith and truth. — Muqātil places the call on Judgment Day. Ibn Zayd says the Fire has power only over the disgraced unbeliever, not one believing in God and His Messenger. Ibn ʿAbbās enumerates polytheists, unbelievers, hypocrites, wrongdoers and sinners; Ibn ʿĀshūr relates the description to the polytheist criminals who seek ransom in 70:14. Al-Biqāʿī includes anyone deliberately rejecting the original sound nature and saving deeds. Some descriptions name sinners or all those destined for the Fire rather than only specific confessional labels. [26 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(IK,Ṭ)]

## 70:17/q04 What does adbara describe?  [أَدْبَرَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the first turning concerns belief and truth, obedience to God's command, or acceptance as distinct from practice.
- 70:17/q04/p1 Turning away from faith, Islam or the truth, including rejecting the Qur'an or the call to monotheism. — Mujāhid says 'truth'; Muqātil says faith abandoned for unbelief. Ibn ʿĀshūr makes this rejection from the outset, before listening, and cites 41:26; his description of rejecting monotheism is joined to hoarding. Al-Biqāʿī describes the original sound nature deliberately rejecting salvation. Al-Kashshāf's two verbs both concern truth, as Ibn ʿĀshūr reports. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد,مقاتل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(البيضاوي,الكشاف); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(IK,Ṭ)]
- 70:17/q04/p2 Turning away from God's obedience or command. — Qatāda gives obedience or command, while reserving God's Book and its due for tawallā; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī repeat obedience. Ibn ʿĀshūr says al-Fakhr and al-Nīsābūrī follow Qatāda's distribution. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:17/q04/p3 Turning away from accepting the message. — Al-Māwardī pairs failure to accept with tawallā as failure to act. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 70:17/q05 What does tawallā describe?  [وَتَوَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Which object the second verb has, and whether it marks a later refusal after hearing rather than another description of the same rejection.
- 70:17/q05/p1 Turning away from God's obedience, practice or saving deeds. — Al-Bayḍāwī assigns obedience to the second verb after the Messenger's call has been heard; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this. Ibn Kathīr makes it abandonment of deeds by the limbs alongside denial by the heart. Ālūsī glosses tawallā as obedience abandoned, and al-Biqāʿī includes deeds that could save. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(البيضاوي); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:17/q05/p2 Turning from the truth or faith. — Mujāhid assigns truth to this turning; al-Kashshāf assigns truth to both verbs. Muqātil describes the turn as toward unbelief after turning one's back on faith. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports both al-Kashshāf and al-Bayḍāwī's different assignments. [8 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد,مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL(البيضاوي,الكشاف); ASAD-NOTES]
- 70:17/q05/p3 Turning from God's Book and its due rights. — Qatāda gives this gloss in al-Ṭabarī, al-Māwardī and al-Durr; al-Naḥḥās repeats it. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes al-Fakhr and al-Nīsābūrī follow Qatāda's order of objects. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 70:17/q05/p4 After hearing the Qur'an, turning away from it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts this with adbara as rejection without listening and cites 8:31 for the later turning; he says both states characterize polytheists and merit punishment. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 70:17/q06 Why pair adbara with tawallā?  [أَدْبَرَ وَتَوَلَّىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the conjunction adds a temporal distinction, intensifies one idea, or coordinates rejection of different objects.
- 70:17/q06/p1 The verbs distinguish rejection from the outset and departure after hearing or engaging. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reads adbara as turning one's back initially and tawallā as leaving after engagement; 41:26 illustrates rejection without listening and 8:31 turning after hearing. He says the conjunction preserves the distinction and both states warrant punishment. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 70:17/q06/p2 The paired terms reinforce the act of turning away. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says this doubled wording is answered by the exception for constant worshippers in 70:22–23. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:17/q06/p3 The verbs can share one object, the truth. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Kashshāf's assignment of truth to both verbs. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الكشاف)]
- 70:17/q06/p4 The verbs can take separate objects: truth then obedience. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Bayḍāwī's distinction: rejection of faith, then withdrawal from obedience after hearing the Messenger. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(البيضاوي)]
- 70:17/q06/p5 The verbs can take separate objects: obedience then God's Book. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Qatāda's reverse assignment and says al-Fakhr and al-Nīsābūrī followed it. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 70:17/q07 What moral pattern does the verse and its continuation identify as the cause of punishment?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the two faults are framed as unbelief plus selfish wealth, or as inward denial plus outward inaction.
- 70:17/q07/p1 Rejection of God, faith or obedience together with greed, hoarding and withholding wealth causes the punishment. — Al-Māwardī and Qatāda identify relentless accumulation and withholding God's due. Rāzī says rejecting knowledge and obedience to God, greed in gathering and worldly attachment in hoarding are the roots of religious affliction and the two principal sources of corruption. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them traits and consequences of shirk, with hoarding driven by fear for wealth. Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes refusal to spend on the asker and deprived, linking the exception and prayer and spending in 70:22–25. Asad and Iṣlāḥī place this with the Fire seizing those who refuse truth and hoard. Kuranyolu says the two central duties are recognizing God's oneness and helping people; Muslim scholars phrase them as reverence for God's command and compassion for creatures, a recurring criticism especially in Meccan sūras. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İslâm âlimleri); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:17/q07/p2 Denial in the heart and abandonment of deeds in the limbs are the paired grounds. — Ibn Kathīr splits inward disbelief and outward failure to act between the two verbs. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 70:17/q08 How does the Fire's call connect this verse with its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which feature of the passage supplies the connection: its sequence, the sūra's opening, or the correspondence between hoarding and stripped skin.
- 70:17/q08/p1 The call names the condemned after the failed ransom and anticipates the wealth hoarder and the saved worshippers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the threat is made explicit here, the condemned are the criminals seeking ransom in 70:14, and the hoarding detail follows in 70:18. Iṣlāḥī describes cosmic upheaval and social bonds collapsing as each would ransom himself even with family and tribe. Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects the later exception and prayer and spending in 70:22–25. Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts those who turn away with constant worshippers and the share given to the asker and deprived in 70:22–25. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:17/q08/p2 The punishment scene suits al-Maʿārij's opening with punishment, as another sūra's flight scene suits its opening with turning away. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts al-Maʿārij 70:1–2 and 70:16–18 with the opening and flight at 80:1 and 80:34. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 70:17/q08/p3 The stripping of skin answers the hoarder's act of enclosing wealth. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says skin encloses and protects entrails like a container protects wealth; the hoarder's enclosing skin is torn off and its contents exposed. The saying 'save your skin' marks skin as dearer than wealth, making the punishment a fitting recompense. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 70:17/q09 What does shawā in the preceding Fire description denote?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Which body part shawā names, and whether human or horse usage is at issue.
- 70:17/q09/p1 Human limbs, including the hands, feet and head; one account includes scalp and limbs. — Abū ʿUbayda distinguishes human limbs from a horse's legs and reports a Medinan saying 'my limbs bristled.' He notes al-Aʿshā's shawātahu and Abū ʿAmr's correction to sarātahu. Al-Wāḥidī says most exegetes and linguists, including Mujāhid, take limbs, and cites al-Aʿshā. Muqātil describes the Fire stripping scalp and limbs, flesh and skin. [5 notes: MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأعشى,مجاهد,مقاتل)]
- 70:17/q09/p2 For a horse, shawā means the legs, excluding the head. — Abū ʿUbayda cites ʿablu al-shawā and says horses are praised for slender cheeks and a refined face. [1 notes: MAJAZ]
- 70:17/q09/p3 It denotes sinews and heels. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 70:17/q09/p4 It denotes flesh of the lower legs. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Abū Isḥāq's gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)]
- 70:17/q09/p5 It denotes the finest features of human faces. — Al-Wāḥidī reports Thābit al-Bunānī's gloss. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ثابت البناني)]
- 70:17/q09/p6 It can mean human skin, stripped to expose the entrails. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī reads the stripping in 70:16 against the Fire's call and the hoarding in 70:17–18. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 70:17/q10 How does the verse ending function in the passage's sound pattern?  [وَتَوَلَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 70:17/q10/p1 The ending is one of the passage's fawāṣil. — Al-Zarkashī cites the sequence ending with 'it calls whoever turned away and turned back' as an example of the verse endings. He notes that some rhetoricians call a different paired arrangement with corresponding meter, rhyme and order tarṣīʿ, but says that ornate type does not occur in the Qur'an. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 70:17/q11 What kind of pause may occur at the end of wa-tawallā?  [وَتَوَلَّىٰ · readings]
- 70:17/q11/p1 A permissible incomplete pause can have the same surface form as continuous recitation. — Al-Zarkashī cites the end of wa-tawallā in the Fire's description between 70:15 and 70:18. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 70:17/q12 What inward meaning is drawn from the Fire calling those who turn away?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward image stresses the lower world's attraction or the soul's failure to orient toward God and the afterlife.
- 70:17/q12/p1 Worldly life and the lower nature act as a fire that draws kindred hearts toward greed and darkness. — Al-Qushayrī calls worldly life a hell that hooks hearts through greed, accumulation and preference for wealth over family, with few escaping. Al-Qāshānī says escape is impossible because one's self merits punishment and is drawn by affinity: like inclines to like, and the lower fire seizes the heart of those turning from truth and holiness toward dark matter and transient things. Bursawī transmits that reading from al-Qāshānī. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]
- 70:17/q12/p2 The turning is failure to face God through obedience and to leave the lower world for the afterlife. — Bursawī transmits the Najmiyya interpretation: resisting the lower nature and obeying the law are the means of facing God; turning away abandons the approach to the afterlife and departure from worldly life. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
