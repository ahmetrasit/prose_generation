# 47:16: 20 questions

## 47:16/q01 To whom does 'among them' refer?  [وَمِنْهُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is fixed by the immediate preceding description, by the earlier Meccan reference, or by the conduct of the hypocrites.
- 47:16/q01/p1 The hypocrites, especially those present with the Prophet; some accounts name particular members. — Muqatil names Rifaʿa ibn Zayd, al-Harith ibn Amr, and a Banu Zuhra ally. Mawardi and Qurtubi give a different five-name list including Ibn Ubayy, Rifaʿa ibn al-Tabut, Zayd ibn al-Sulayt/Salt, al-Harith, and Malik ibn al-Dakhsham/Dukhshum. Tabarsi includes hypocrites among disbelievers. Asad extends the description to later people who feign reverence while refusing Quranic meaning. [34 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي، مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ, Z)]
- 47:16/q01/p2 They are the previously mentioned disbelievers or unbelievers. — Tabarsi says hypocrites are included as disbelievers. Tabatabai takes the pronoun as unbelievers hearing recitation and instruction. [2 notes: TABRISI; TABATABAI]
- 47:16/q01/p3 They may be people in general. — Razi compares Quran 2:8, where some people are mentioned after disbelievers. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 47:16/q01/p4 They may be the people of Mecca who expelled Muhammad. — Razi points to 47:13. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 47:16/q01/p5 They may be some of the people destined for Hell in the preceding verse. — Razi points to 47:15; Biqai likewise relates them to the people of Hell before the Garden parable. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 47:16/q02 What kind of listening is described?  [يَسْتَمِعُ إِلَيْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the occasion is a Friday sermon or general attendance, and whether the outward attention is absent, hostile, or earnest.
- 47:16/q02/p1 They attend the Prophet's Friday sermon, particularly when he censures hypocrites. — Muqatil, Farra, Zajjaj, al-Kalbi, and others connect it with the sermon and departure from the mosque; Muqatil supplies named listeners. Qushayri says exposure made hypocrites dislike revelation. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي، مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 47:16/q02/p2 They hear the Prophet's speech or recitation in his gathering, but fail to attend to or retain it. — Tabari compares 6:25; believers in Ibn Jurayj's account retain what hypocrites do not. Tabatabai includes recitation and explanation of principles and laws. Islahi distinguishes aversion from feigned attendance. [24 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 47:16/q02/p3 Some listen intently to find a flaw in the recitation and sow doubt. — Biqai says the effort is hostile; his grammar note treats singular listening as few and plural turning away as many. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 47:16/q02/p4 The listening and request may appear diligent and aimed at understanding. — Razi reads the sequence linked by hatta as intense listening followed by a request to repeat the speech. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 47:16/q03 How does singular 'listens' agree with plural 'leave' and 'follow'?  [مَّن يَسْتَمِعُ خَرَجُوا۟ وَٱتَّبَعُوٓا۟ · grammar]
- 47:16/q03/p1 Man takes singular agreement by its form and plural agreement by its meaning. — Majaz says min is plural in sense. Nahhas includes the later demonstrative, sealing, and following. Biqai adds that singular hints at few listeners and plural at many who turn away. [9 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 47:16/q04 What does 'until' contribute to the sequence?  [حَتَّىٰٓ · grammar]
- 47:16/q04/p1 It makes leaving and asking a limit or part of the listening sequence. — Razi says hatta, unlike wa, cannot connect unrelated clauses; this supports his reading of sustained listening and a request for repetition. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 47:16/q05 What does 'they left your presence' mean?  [خَرَجُوا۟ مِنْ عِندِكَ · interpretation]
- 47:16/q05/p1 They left or parted from the Prophet's presence or gathering. — Mawardi identifies 'your' as the Messenger; Baghawi and Qurtubi gloss the departure, sometimes specifically from the mosque after a sermon. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل)]

## 47:16/q06 Who are 'those given knowledge'?  [لِلَّذِينَ أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural designates learned Companions collectively or particular Companions named in reports.
- 47:16/q06/p1 The knowledgeable Companions or believers who understood and retained the Prophet's message. — Muqatil says guided by the Quran; Tabarsi says God gave knowledge and understanding; Alusi specifies attentive Companions. Tabatabai says companions who know God. Asad says believers who know the truth or Muhammad's message. Ali says he and others retained revelation. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(علي عليه السلام); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(القاسم بن عبد الرحمن، ابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ, Z)]
- 47:16/q06/p2 The person asked was Abdullah ibn Masud. — Muqatil calls him al-Hudhali; Ibn Burayda and transmissions through Ibn Abbas also identify him. Several sermon accounts say hypocrites asked him after leaving the mosque. [17 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS(عبد الله بن بريدة); THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن بريدة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس وابن بريدة ومقاتل); JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس، عبد الله بن بريدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(مقاتل); DURR-FULL(ابن بريدة,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 47:16/q06/p3 Ibn Abbas was among those asked. — He states this himself in several transmissions; Ikrima describes visitors asking him and receiving his answer. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود وابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 47:16/q06/p4 The learned person was Abu al-Darda. — Al-Qasim ibn Abd al-Rahman reports this; Bursevi includes him with Ibn Masud and Ibn Abbas. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(القاسم بن عبد الرحمن); QURTUBI-FULL(القاسم بن عبد الرحمن، ابن زيد); BURSEVI]
- 47:16/q06/p5 Ali and other retainers of revelation were among the knowledgeable. — Ali says the hypocrites asked after he and other retainers left the Prophet. [1 notes: TABRISI(علي عليه السلام)]

## 47:16/q07 Why is knowledge expressed as something 'given'?  [أُوتُوا۟ ٱلْعِلْمَ · rhetoric]
- 47:16/q07/p1 The passive wording magnifies beneficial knowledge as God's gift. — Biqai says God prepares its recipients with clear minds, freedom from ego, and responsiveness to primordial nature. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 47:16/q08 To whom does 'he' in their question refer?  [مَاذَا قَالَ · referent]
- 47:16/q08/p1 It refers to the Prophet Muhammad. — Tabatabai identifies the pronoun explicitly; the accounts all place the inquiry after his words. [2 notes: TABATABAI; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 47:16/q09 Why did they ask what he had said?  [مَاذَا قَالَ ءَانِفًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the question signals mockery and contempt, real failure of comprehension, a pretense of obedience, or earnest request.
- 47:16/q09/p1 They asked mockingly, feigning ignorance or inattention and belittling the Prophet. — Zajjaj says the pose showed that they had paid no attention; Razi compares a clever person's sarcastic request to a dull speaker and cites 2:14. Ibn Atiyya says they question meaning or benefit. Kuranyolu says the question disregards both speech and speaker. Alusi prefers this explanation; Tabatabai finds it plausible. [24 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+]
- 47:16/q09/p2 They genuinely asked because, despite hearing, they had not understood or retained the words. — Muqatil says they had heard; Wahidi prefers this from the context and their inattentive hearts, against Zajjaj's preference. Razi cites sealed hearts and 7:101, 49:14; Tabatabai cites 4:78 and attributes failure to pride and desire. Ibn Atiyya adds worldly distraction and forgetfulness; Baydawi adds disregard. [16 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ELMALILI; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 47:16/q09/p3 They implied that his speech had no useful or coherent meaning. — Tabarsi calls it belittling; Tabatabai says they insinuated falsehood and finds this reading supported. Islahi says they suggested revelation required profound deliberation while believers were simpletons, comparing 9:124–125. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 47:16/q09/p4 They feigned a sincere wish to hear and obey or memorize, concealing hypocrisy. — Tabarsi says they pretended to forget only this portion and wanted repetition; Islahi says they claimed readiness to obey while professing inability to understand. [2 notes: TABRISI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 47:16/q09/p5 They asked for the words to be repeated from the beginning so that they would miss nothing. — Razi treats the request as a diligent student's attempt to understand, supported by his reading of hatta and anifan. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 47:16/q10 What does 'ānifan' mean here?  [ءَانِفًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the root's 'beginning' denotes the near past, the present moment, or the start of the speech.
- 47:16/q10/p1 Just now, a short time before the present, from the sense of a beginning or foremost part. — Zajjaj cites newly begun pasture; Abu Zayd cites beginning speech. Wahidi cites first cold or rain and Imru al-Qays's horse run. Farisi and Wahidi cite the freshest first food in bowls. Qurtubi cites new pasture, unused cup, journey, and the nose as the first part. Bursevi and Alusi explain the nose metaphor. Qamus resolves the time as immediately before the present, not the present hour. [33 notes: ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو زيد,امرؤ القيس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمر غلام ثعلب,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(امرؤ القيس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI(الراغب,القاموس); ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج,غير واحد); TABATABAI]
- 47:16/q10/p2 It means from the beginning of the Prophet's speech. — Razi prefers this because asking for repetition from the start would ensure they miss nothing; Mawardi also reports beginning as a sense. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 47:16/q10/p3 It can mean at this very moment or hour. — Nasafi glosses it 'at this moment'; Alusi reports this alongside near past; Tabatabai allows both. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); TABATABAI]

## 47:16/q11 What derivation and form explain 'ānifan'?  [ءَانِفًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the attested derivation directly supports the form, or analogy and poetic forms are needed.
- 47:16/q11/p1 It is a participial form associated with beginning, although the simple verb is unattested. — Abu Ali says active participles can be formed where corresponding simple verbs are unused, comparing faqir and shadid; Ibn Atiyya applies this to both readings. Alusi calls it irregular or formed after dropping a derived prefix; Abu Hayyan derives the sense from iʾtanafa. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي الفارسي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(البزي)]
- 47:16/q11/p2 The root's other poetic senses include proud people and first, finest food; only the latter supports beginning here. — Sukkari and Abu Zayd cite al-anf for nobles refusing humiliation, and Farisi says that is not the verse's sense; a Sibawayh line on nimr supports a plural pattern. Farisi and Wahidi cite anf al-qisaʿ for freshest food. Tabarsi cites muʾtanafan for a newly begun month with 'month' omitted and al-anf al-nusur as plural evidence. [10 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو زيد,السكري); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(أبو علي,أبو علي عن أبي زيد); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 47:16/q11/p3 The short form can be understood by analogy with pairs such as hadhir/hadhir. — Farisi and Abu Ali propose the analogy, although Farisi prefers the long form because the short root meaning 'beginning' is unattested. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي)]

## 47:16/q12 How is the word 'ānifan' read?  [ءَانِفًا · readings]
turns on: Whether the short Ibn Kathir transmission is sound and how its form relates to the established long reading.
- 47:16/q12/p1 The majority lengthen the initial vowel, reading ānifan on the fāʿil pattern. — Farisi says all readers other than the reported Ibn Kathir variant use it; Tabarsi calls it famous. Farisi prefers it because the verse means newly begun from iʾtanafa; Wahidi upholds the common reading and calls al-Bazzi's short report a mistaken supposition. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(الباقون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(ابن كثير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(البزي); FARISI-HUJJA+; TABRISI(أبو علي)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 47:16/q12/p2 A transmission of Ibn Kathir reads the initial vowel short, anifan on the faʿl pattern. — Al-Bazzi transmits it while Qunbul also reports long; Ibn al-Jawzi adds Ikrima, Humayd, and Ibn Muhaysin. Zamakhshari says the form is accusative; Farisi and Abu Ali explain it by analogy to hadhir/hadhir, but Wahidi rejects this attribution. [11 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); JISHUMI(ابن كثير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); TABRISI(ابن كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو علي,ابن كثير وعكرمة وحميد وابن محيصن); BAYDAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(البزي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير); FARISI-HUJJA-; WAHIDI-BASIT-; TABRISI(أبو علي)-]

## 47:16/q13 What is the syntactic role of 'ānifan'?  [ءَانِفًا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the temporal gloss establishes grammatical adverb status or only conveys the sense of a circumstantial participle.
- 47:16/q13/p1 It is an adverbial accusative of time, meaning just now. — Zamakhshari gives this parse; Baydawi allows it, and Tabatabai adopts it. Alusi argues a participle may become a lexicalized adverb. Abu Hayyan, the Sheikh cited by Samin, and Biqai reject the parse because grammarians do not classify it as an adverb. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; BIQAI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]
- 47:16/q13/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative referring to the speaker, what he had just begun to say. — Abu al-Baqa supplies the expansion mādhā qāla muʾtanafan; Abu Hayyan applies it to the statement begun before they left. Baydawi allows this parse; Alusi cites Abu Hayyan approvingly. [9 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الشيخ); BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]

## 47:16/q14 What does God's sealing of their hearts signify?  [طَبَعَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the seal is a divine mark, figurative condition, abandonment to choice, or consequence of their own conduct.
- 47:16/q14/p1 Their hearts are marked or closed so they cannot believe or understand for benefit. — Muqatil specifies disbelief and failure to grasp faith. Biqai adds rigid indifference; Ibn Kathir adds lack of sound understanding and intention. Study Quran notes outward profession with inward blindness. Jishumi equates tabʿ and khatm as marking; Raghib says tabʿ shapes or imprints, broader than sealing but narrower than engraving. [11 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); STUDYQURAN]
- 47:16/q14/p2 The seal may be understood literally or metaphorically. — Ibn Atiyya refers back to his earlier discussion without choosing between them. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 47:16/q14/p3 The hearts may bear a stamp of disbelief or be left to the people's own choice. — Tabarsi reports both explanations. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 47:16/q14/p4 Sealing means loss of perfected knowledge, paired with following desires as loss of righteous action. — Tabatabai says the two clauses can also be read with the second explanatory of the first. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 47:16/q14/p5 The seal expresses an incapacity to receive revelation after persistent wrongdoing or desire. — Bursevi says they no longer turn toward good; Elmalili says desire prevents the Prophet's words entering comprehension; Islahi says misdeeds extinguish innate vision; Kuranyolu says the conduct conditions mind and heart. [7 notes: BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 47:16/q15 How are sealing and following desires related?  [طَبَعَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَٱتَّبَعُوٓا۟ أَهْوَآءَهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether desire causes the seal, the seal helps explain desire, or the two clauses describe distinct intellectual and practical losses.
- 47:16/q15/p1 Their following desires brings about or explains the sealed heart. — Asad explicitly makes sealing result from following desires, citing 2:7; Elmalili and Islahi likewise link desire or misdeeds to loss of receptivity. Tabatabai calls the second clause explanatory of the first and contrasts those pure in original nature. [5 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 47:16/q15/p2 Sealing leaves them without faith and understanding, and they follow desires instead of truth. — Muqatil says the seal is disbelief and desires are disbelief and hypocrisy. Ibn Kathir pairs defective understanding and intention. Baydawi says the two conditions explain mockery. Razi says they turned away because they could not understand or would not listen for benefit. [11 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 47:16/q15/p3 The pair describes loss of knowledge and loss of righteous action. — Tabatabai offers this reading while allowing the explanatory relation too. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 47:16/q16 What are 'desires' and what does following them entail?  [أَهْوَآءَهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression is confined to a reported occasion or extends by its general wording.
- 47:16/q16/p1 Ahywāʾ is the plural of hawā, the soul's inclination or desire; following it opposes proof and truth. — Jishumi distinguishes al-hawā, air, and istahwā, calling toward desire; Tabarsi also gives the plural and verb. Biqai says it abandons reason, restraint, and the great discourse for worldly accumulation. Alusi says they turn to what has no good; Bursevi says false desires explain fruitless conduct. [9 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 47:16/q16/p2 The desires are disbelief and hypocrisy. — Muqatil explicitly gives both; Baghawi says disbelief, and Wahidi transmits Muqatil. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 47:16/q16/p3 The warning applies generally to every unbeliever and innovator. — Jishumi says wording extends beyond the occasion and rejects confinement to Abu Jahl and associates. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]
- 47:16/q16/p4 It refers specifically to Abu Jahl and his polytheist associates. — Jishumi reports this occasion but objects that the wording is general. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); JISHUMI-]

## 47:16/q17 What broader lesson is drawn from the contrasting listeners?  [* · interpretation]
- 47:16/q17/p1 Hearing can lead to action, understanding, or heedless abandonment; benefit requires receptive attention. — Qatada's formulations vary: some divide three kinds as acting, understanding, and abandoning; others contrast one who benefits with one who neither understands nor benefits. The verse warns believers to listen with understanding. Ghazzali lists pronunciation alone, dogmatic school loyalty, sin or pride and worldly passion, and limiting meaning to early reports as obstacles. [7 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(al-Ghazzālī)]
- 47:16/q17/p2 People of the heart gain insight by removing blameworthy traits; people of the lower self adorn innovation and breaches of law. — Bursevi links the latter's desire in belief and action to estrangement from God and Paradise. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 47:16/q18 How does the verse connect to nearby passages and the surah?  [* · links]
- 47:16/q18/p1 It continues the treatment of diseased hearts, hypocrisy, and the relation of deeds to belief. — Tabatabai includes apostates after faith; Islahi says 16–19 turn to the hidden enemy and 16–38 expose weakness and collusion before a major campaign. - 47:16/q18/p2 The listeners resemble those in verse 14 and the people of Hell before the Garden parable. — Islahi says misdeeds in 47:14 became attractive; Biqai identifies the pursuers of worldly accumulation with the earlier people of Hell in 47:15. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 47:16/q19 What inward contrast does the verse suggest?  [* · inward]
- 47:16/q19/p1 The polished heart witnesses truth, whereas the lower self makes innovation and disobedience attractive and follows desire. — Bursevi says the first gains insight and the second strays from God and Paradise. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 47:16/q20 What does the cited lexical digression say 'sky' can mean?  [* · meaning]
- 47:16/q20/p1 Samāʾ names what is elevated and can denote the visible sky, clouds, rain, a roof, or the ceiling of Paradise or Hell. — Ibn al-Jawzi derives it from sumūw, cites samā for rising sight or a visible figure and samāwa for the moon's body, and illustrates the senses with 2:29, 64:3, 51:22, 51:47, 15:22, 71:11, 22:15, and 11:107–108. These are a source's digression, not words of 47:16. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
