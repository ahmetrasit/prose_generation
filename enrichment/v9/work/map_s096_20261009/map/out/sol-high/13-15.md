# 13:15: 21 questions

## 13:15/q01 What does the placement of 'to God' convey?  [وَلِلَّهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Exclusivity or thematic naming.
- 13:15/q01/p1 Prostration belongs exclusively to God. — Alusi says fronting لله rules out another independent or shared object of submission; Ibn Ashur ties this to God's true invocation in 13:14. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 13:15/q01/p2 The divine name replaces a pronoun to open a main theme. — Ibn Ashur compares the surah's opening style. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:15/q02 Who are those in the heavens and earth?  [مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: The scope of من and whether it is literal, predominant, or inward.
- 13:15/q02/p1 Angels above and morally responsible humans or jinn below. — Muqatil names angels and believers; Tabrisī includes accountable creatures; Bursevi adds higher spirits, saints, prophets, earthly angels, and believing jinn; Elmalili names humans and jinn. [9 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 13:15/q02/p2 Every created thing, using a rational pronoun by predominance or extension. — Biqai includes animate and inanimate, rational and irrational; Asad cites shadows and 16:48–49, 22:18; Tabatabai reports but rejects the predominance explanation. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES+; TABATABAI-]
- 13:15/q02/p3 Rational beings are central; their prostration implies that of other creatures. — Alusi centers angels, humans, and jinn; Tabatabai says this addresses polytheists; Samarrai says willingness suits rational من; Ibn Ashur calls the generality conventional, a great multitude. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:15/q02/p4 They may signify spiritual realities above and lower souls below. — Elmalili gives intellects, spirits, higher faculties and lower souls; Qashani takes heavenly and earthly beings as spiritual realities with shadow forms. [3 notes: ELMALILI; QASHANI]

## 13:15/q03 How does من function grammatically here?  [مَن · grammar]
- 13:15/q03/p1 It is a relative pronoun. — Burhan distinguishes relative, interrogative, conditional, and qualified indefinite من; Samarrai contrasts rational من and broader ما, and 22:18 repeats من; Ibn Ashur says its generality is conventional. [5 notes: BURHAN; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:15/q04 What does prostration mean here?  [يَسْجُدُ · interpretation]
turns on: Bodily posture, worship, or real cosmic dependence.
- 13:15/q04/p1 Universal humility and dependence under God's creative will. — Ibn Qutayba gives surrender; Jishumi derives lowering and cites bowed hills; Nahhas prefers health and illness. Razi derives every contingent being's dependence on the Necessary Existent and cites 2:116, 3:83. Biqai cites 11:119 and al-Nawawi on inanimate obedience. Asad reports natural law; Tabatabai insists cosmic prostration is real, citing 30:26, 17:44, 55:29, 16:49, 41:11; Study Quran cites 19:93. Alusi names creation and annihilation. [39 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(الشاعر); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي,الشيخ محيي الدين النووي); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari,most of the classical commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 13:15/q04/p2 Bodily worship, especially forehead to ground. — Razi describes physical prostration and the problem of non-prostrating unbelievers; he proposes obligation stated as accomplished or universal glorification and servitude, citing 31:25. Wahidi dislikes treating a report as command; Bursevi defines posture; Ibn Ashur notes idolaters rarely do it. [15 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض المفسرين); WAHIDI-BASIT-; TABATABAI-]
- 13:15/q04/p3 Worship and submission together, with bodily posture their utmost form. — Nasafi combines them; Kuranyolu contrasts rewarded free human worship and compelled universal submission; Biqai quotes al-Nawawi. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL(الشيخ محيي الدين النووي)]
- 13:15/q04/p4 It denotes prayer. — Damghani lists prayer, prophets, submission, bowing, and literal prostration among senses and illustrates prayer here, comparing 16:49. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 13:15/q04/p5 It includes the familiar self's prostration and the heart's spiritual presence. — Qushayri distinguishes these kinds. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 13:15/q05 Who prostrates willingly and who unwillingly?  [طَوْعًۭا وَكَرْهًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Coerced worship, difficulty, legal duty, need, or cosmic dependence.
- 13:15/q05/p1 Angels and believers willingly; unbelievers, hypocrites, or coerced converts reluctantly. — Farra includes the born Muslim and voluntary converts; Ibn Qutayba, Qatada, and Ibn Zayd specify the sword; Wahidi says the account fails to cover uncoerced unbelievers. Ibn Atiyya links it to bodily posture; Bursevi includes devils and captives and cites people led to Paradise in chains. Abu Jafar adds shadows where people do not prostrate. [36 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري عن بعض أهل العلم,الحسن وقتادة وابن زيد والفراء,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(الحسن وقتادة وابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما,الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر,الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT-; TABATABAI-; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 13:15/q05/p2 All submit to providence: believers knowingly and unbelievers unwillingly through need or affliction. — Nahhas names health and illness; Jubba'i and Ibn Atiyya cite pain; Wahidi names wealth, poverty, life, death and 2:116, 3:83; Alusi names creation and annihilation; Islahi says human choice remains within divine law. Biqai defines voluntary submission as answering a command the self is invited to obey. [24 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب المعاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(الجبائي); ASAD-NOTES(most of the classical commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; NAHHAS+]
- 13:15/q05/p3 Both groups can be believers: eager or habituated worshippers versus those struggling or new to the duty. — Ibn al-Anbari contrasts established and new converts; Wahidi offers self-compulsion and a reading excluding unbelievers. Ibn Atiyya finds sinful-believer application strained; Razi restricts the general words; Baydawi includes hardship and ease. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون من المفسرين,بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النحاس والزجاج)-]
- 13:15/q05/p4 All rational beings are obliged to prostrate, irrespective of performance. — Wahidi reports the legal reading but finds its report-as-command wording unlikely; Razi and Abu Hayyan allow emphatic obligation by indicative; Qushayri keeps the general liability of unbelievers. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 13:15/q05/p5 Devotion is willing; need, fear, reward-seeking, or distress makes it reluctant. — Qushayri says even a disbeliever may seek relief willingly but need compels him; Ibn Ashur prefers love, nearness and glorification versus distress (16:53) and rejects coercion; Maybudi contrasts worship without reward and seeking advantage; Alusi cites 29:65 but reports objection to crisis worship as proof of exclusivity. [5 notes: QUSHAYRI(أهل التفسير); STUDYQURAN(al-Maybudī); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(الإرشاد)-]
- 13:15/q05/p6 Easy commands are willingly accepted and burdensome ones reluctantly. — Zajjaj and reports in Abu Hayyan and Alusi give this contrast. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 13:15/q05/p7 The knower willingly yields; calamity humbles the heedless, who grow proud in hope. — Junayd and spiritual interpreters in Mawardi say so. [3 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); MAWARDI-FULL(بعض أصحاب الخواطر)]
- 13:15/q05/p8 An unbeliever unwillingly submits when faith no longer benefits him. — Qatada in Qurtubi. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة)]
- 13:15/q05/p9 Heavenly beings are willing; earthly beings are both willing and unwilling. — Al-Hasan in Durr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 13:15/q05/p10 All beings are intrinsically willing; material beings unwillingly yield when thwarted by death or decay. — Tabatabai cites 41:11 and contrasts life, persistence, perfection with opposing causes. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 13:15/q06 How do طوعا and كرها work grammatically?  [طَوْعًۭا وَكَرْهًۭا · grammar]
turns on: State, cause, or implied verbal action.
- 13:15/q06/p1 Circumstantial expressions equivalent to willing and unwilling states. — Tabrisi calls them verbal nouns in place of states; Alusi supplies طائعين وكارهين. [4 notes: TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 13:15/q06/p2 Causal accusatives. — Alusi treats كره as passive compulsion if agent matching is required, otherwise ordinary reluctance; an unwanted prompt or result can still be a cause. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 13:15/q06/p3 Emphatic or absolute verbal nouns after an implicit act. — Samin and Alusi posit implicit سجود طوع وكره. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 13:15/q07 What do their shadows refer to?  [وَظِلَٰلُهُم · referent]
turns on: Literal shade, bodies, or spiritual images.
- 13:15/q07/p1 Cast shade of bodies and other opaque physical objects. — Damghani includes trees and walls (28:24); Jishumi, Tabrisī, Biqai, Abu Hayyan and Ibn Ashur distinguish shade and returning fayʾ. Ibn Ashur says bodies intercept light; he and Alusi limit the pronoun to shadow-casting bodies, since heavenly beings lack shadows absent an inclusive reading. [11 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:15/q07/p2 The word means their bodies or persons. — Baghawi reports this; Tabrisī says the shadow accompanies the person; al-Hasan identifies unbelievers' obedient limbs except heart. Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, and Abu Hayyan reject the reading; Alusi says it requires broad metaphor, causative ellipsis, or combined literal and figurative senses. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ZAJJAJ(قيل)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 13:15/q07/p3 They are bodily forms and psychic images of spiritual beings. — Qashani uses a Prophetic prostration prayer: face, shadow, imagination signify essence, body, psychic image—being, identity, individual form. Elmalili calls dark bodies shadows of luminous spirits; Bursevi reports souls as shadows of spirits, though its excerpt ends. [5 notes: QASHANI; ELMALILI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 13:15/q08 How do the shadows prostrate?  [وَظِلَٰلُهُم · interpretation]
turns on: Visible motion, accompanying bodily worship, endowed minds, or real created dependence.
- 13:15/q08/p1 They incline, lengthen, shorten, or shift under God's rule. — Farra calls evening shade fayʾ and cites 16:48; Ibn Abbas says right or left; the semanticists relate motion to the sun. Arabic calls a heavy-fruited palm prostrating and Labid's poem illustrates bending. Dahhak gives west at sunrise and east after noon. Qushayri and Abu Hayyan object to shadow intellect since shadows are accidents; Alusi agrees and cites 41:37. Islahi and Kuranyolu describe their daily rising and falling. [41 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة,أهل المعاني,المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,الطبري); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(M)]
- 13:15/q08/p2 They prostrate with owners during bodily prostration. — Farra, Kalbi, Baghawi describe synchronized posture; Bursevi supplies an omitted يسجد and calls it derivative. [4 notes: FARRA; THALABI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 13:15/q08/p3 God endows shadows with minds so they really prostrate. — Ibn al-Anbari compares mountains receiving understanding to speak or glorify; Wahidi says most exegetes follow him. Qushayri, Abu Hayyan and Alusi object that shadows are accidents incapable of life. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 13:15/q08/p4 They are subdued to the purpose for which they were made. — Baghawi reports this. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 13:15/q08/p5 Their created existence and visible form both genuinely submit and signify God. — Tabatabai argues that even a privative shadow exists in its effects, citing 16:48; visible falling leads sensory understanding toward reason, not poetic fiction. Ibn Ashur says blocked light casts the shape; two opposite suns or transparent earth would erase it, so its design testifies to uniqueness and creaturely need. [10 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI+]

## 13:15/q09 Do believers' and unbelievers' shadows prostrate differently?  [وَظِلَٰلُهُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether a shadow shares its owner's willingness.
- 13:15/q09/p1 Both willingly prostrate, even when the unbeliever resists or worships an idol. — Mujahid contrasts willing believer with resisting unbeliever; Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq report simultaneous idol worship and the shadow's prostration to God. Wahidi Wajiz says the manner is unknown. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قيل,مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(رُوي); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 13:15/q09/p2 The believer's shadow willingly prostrates and the unbeliever's unwillingly. — Mawardi, Nasafi, Qurtubi's Mujahid report and one Durr version say so; Elmalili says the noncompliant owner's shadow obeys under compulsion. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI]
- 13:15/q09/p3 The believer willingly prostrates, but the unbeliever's shadow is the reluctant prostrator. — Durr reports distinct wording from Mujahid and Qatada. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد)]
- 13:15/q09/p4 All shadows prostrate willingly and continually. — Tabatabai treats their created obedience as constant, with morning and afternoon making it visible. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 13:15/q10 How is 'their shadows' joined to the sentence?  [وَظِلَٰلُهُم · grammar]
turns on: Coordination or ellipsis.
- 13:15/q10/p1 It is coordinated with من and is another subject of يسجد. — Nasafi, Samin and Ibn Ashur say this. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:15/q10/p2 It starts a clause with omitted predicate: their shadows prostrate. — Qurtubi reports this alternative. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 13:15/q11 What does الغدو mean and what form is it?  [بِٱلْغُدُوِّ · meaning]
turns on: Plural noun or verbal noun.
- 13:15/q11/p1 It denotes early mornings and is a plural of ghadāh or ghudwah. — Baydawi compares qanā from qanāh; Biqai means the day's first part; Ibn Ashur connects it to going out for needs; Qurtubi cites its pairing with plural āṣāl. [9 notes: AKHFASH; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 13:15/q11/p2 It is a verbal noun denoting morning or morning's time. — Akhfash says its form is verbal; Ibn Ashur allows an omitted 'time of'; Qurtubi offers this too. Akhfash compares ibkār from abkara and rejects deriving abkār from bakr because it is a verbal noun and faʿla does not normally pluralize as afʿāl. [4 notes: AKHFASH; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:15/q12 What does الآصال mean and how is it formed?  [وَٱلْءَاصَالِ · meaning]
- 13:15/q12/p1 Late afternoons or evenings, from ʿaṣr to sunset; plural of aṣīl directly or through aṣl. — Majaz cites Abu Dhu'ayb and al-Nabigha; Tabari, Thalabi and others give aṣl and poetic aṣāʾil; Jishumi calls it evening's beginning; Biqai notes occasional midday use and night arising from aṣīl; Ibn Ashur mentions yellow sun. Bahr al-Ulum starts at zenith, al-Kawashi at ʿaṣr. [21 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(الكواشي,بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 13:15/q13 What is the reading والإيصال?  [وَٱلْءَاصَالِ · readings]
- 13:15/q13/p1 Abu Mujliz reads al-īṣāl, a verbal noun for entering late afternoon. — Tabrisi calls it noncanonical; Ibn Jinni, quoted by Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi, compares the verb for entering morning; Alusi notes kasra on hamza. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو مجلز); TABRISI(أبو مجلز); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو مجلز,ابن جني); SAMIN-DURR(أبو مجلز,ابن جني); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مجلز)]

## 13:15/q14 What further word associations does Biqai draw around āṣāl?  [وَٱلْءَاصَالِ · meaning]
- 13:15/q14/p1 He explores joining and similar sounding roots, ending with aṣīl as an evening link or end. — Biqai derives ṣalā and prayer from connecting servant and Lord by supplication, mercy, praise, pardon, or ritual, and notes bodily hindquarters and the following racehorse. He traces ṣāl to pursuit or attack and waṣl to union, using joints, relatives, ewe offspring, garments, yarn, land and adjoining months. Roasting and heating join meat or hands to fire; ṣalāya is forehead or perfume pounder through repeated contact. A hadith supplies maṣālī for Satan's snares; Qazzaz explains the ṣilyān pasture plant and accusation forms. Similar forms name a snake, camel assault and continuous neigh. Aṣīl also means lineage; aṣl is foundation; al-aṣī is evening, night, day's end, even death. He refers to 11:87. [16 notes: BIQAI-FULL(القزاز,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 13:15/q15 Why mention morning and evening?  [بِٱلْغُدُوِّ وَٱلْءَاصَالِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Permanence or visible shadow behavior.
- 13:15/q15/p1 They signify continual submission. — Baydawi, Qashani, Bursevi, Alusi and Study Quran infer permanence; Ibn Ashur says dawn-going and evening span shadow times. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(N)]
- 13:15/q15/p2 Shadows are longest or most numerous then, and their motion most visible. — Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and Alusi give length and abundance; Tabatabai contrasts midday diminishment or absence (20:130); Asad and Kuranyolu describe daily changes. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 13:15/q16 What does the time phrase modify?  [بِٱلْغُدُوِّ وَٱلْءَاصَالِ · grammar]
turns on: The whole prostration or shadows specifically.
- 13:15/q16/p1 It modifies يسجد, with ب meaning في. — Samin and Bursevi treat the periods as prostration time; Alusi allows an implicit سجود. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 13:15/q16/p2 It may describe the shadows' especially visible movement. — Baydawi and Alusi allow this attachment. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 13:15/q17 What other Qur'anic passages explain this verse?  [* · links]
turns on: The passage used as interpretive parallel.
- 13:15/q17/p1 16:48–49 and related prostration passages explain shadows and creation's submission. — Tabari, Ibn Abbas through Thalabi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan, Tabatabai, Kuranyolu and Study Quran cite 16:48; Study Quran adds 16:49 and 22:18; Samarrai contrasts 22:18's repeated من. [12 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(M); SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 13:15/q17/p2 2:116 and 3:83 parallel universal obedience and willing or unwilling surrender. — Ibn Qutayba cites 3:83; Wahidi and Razi cite both; Study Quran invokes 2:116 and 3:83. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أصحاب المعاني); RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 13:15/q18 How does the verse serve its context?  [* · links]
turns on: Idolatry rebuke, call by decree, word order, or broader natural signs.
- 13:15/q18/p1 It rebukes idolatry by showing all beings, including alleged deities, subject to God. — Ibn Atiyya offers display of power or rebuke of Quraysh and says Tabari favors rebuke; Abu Hayyan and Alusi stress idols lack power to help or harm; Ibn Ashur links 13:14's true invocation; Islahi calls rival calls a mirage and sees every object witnessing oneness; Tabatabai says the rational wording and shadows confront polytheists. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإرشاد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد)+]
- 13:15/q18/p2 It follows the call by command with a call by decree. — Biqai connects the books and universal submission to 11:119; he describes the surah's aim as revelation's differing reception, like thunder, rain and lightning that benefit or harm depending on where they fall. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 13:15/q18/p3 The order willing then unwilling guides benefit before harm in 13:16. — Samarrai reports this and contrasts Saba 34:39. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(قالوا)]
- 13:15/q18/p4 The surah's natural wonders show dependence on and glorification of God. — Study Quran cites 13:12–13; Islahi calls the shadow image insinuative reasoning through human nature. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 13:15/q19 What did Rabiʿ ibn Khuthaym say or do on reciting this verse?  [* · reports]

## 13:15/q20 What further inward lesson is drawn from voluntary submission?  [* · inward]
- 13:15/q20/p1 The lover values service beyond Paradise and may be led there in golden chains. — Bursevi says devoted lovers refuse Paradise to continue serving; Khujandi's poem devalues the garden for a lover. A hadith describes people led to Paradise in chains. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الحديث,كمال الخجندي)]

## 13:15/q21 Was Surat al-Ra'd wholly revealed in Mecca?  [* · sciences]
- 13:15/q21/p1 It was wholly Makkan, with no Madinan verse. — Islahi rejects the codices' Madinan classification. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
