# 2:239: 29 questions

## 2:239/q01 What fear does the conditional describe?  [فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the implied threat is restricted to enemies, extends to other dangers, or concerns loss of prayer time.
- 2:239/q01/p1 Fear of an enemy or combatant. — Ibn Qutayba, Muqatil, Wahidi and Razi's report of Wahidi identify the omitted object as enemy; Wahidi says the context supplies it. Study Quran adds battle or persecution. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); STUDYQURAN]
- 2:239/q01/p2 Fear from an enemy or any other danger. — Tabari, Thalabi, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Abu Hayyan and others cite pursuit, predator, attacking camel, fire or flood; Razi argues from the verse's general wording and the harm-prevention rationale. Biqai also mentions a creditor one may lawfully flee. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)+]
- 2:239/q01/p3 Fear that the prayer time will end before fighting ceases. — Razi reports this third reading and says it stresses praying within the prescribed time. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(قول ثالث)]

## 2:239/q02 How severe must the danger be for the concession?  [خِفْتُمْ · law]
turns on: How certain and grave the threat must be.
- 2:239/q02/p1 A real, life-threatening inability to perform ordinary prayer is required. — Ibn Qutayba excludes suspicion; Tabari requires likelihood of death if ordinary prayer is completed. Thalabi, Qurtubi and Tabarisi describe danger obstructing full postures; Shafi'i requires visible armed enemy or a reliable warning of rapid approach. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 2:239/q02/p2 Any feared danger that prevents the normal prayer warrants performance as possible. — Abu Hayyan says any feared circumstance permits the verse's forms; Asad specifies that standing in one place increases peril; Muqatil and others focus on inability rather than a named threat. [8 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); TABATABAI]

## 2:239/q03 What does rijālan mean and what is its form?  [رِجَالًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term simply denotes pedestrians or specifies a moving battle group.
- 2:239/q03/p1 People on foot, walking or standing without mounts; a plural of rājil or rajul. — Mujahid and Malik gloss walking, Malik and Damghani cite 22:27. Ibn Qutayba, Zajjaj, Wahidi, Baghawi and others give analogical plural patterns; Tabari, Nahhas, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and Samin list dialect plurals and cite Banu Uqayl, Banu Ghudana and pilgrimage poetry. Ibn Atiyya contrasts 2:282's ordinary men; Qaffal allows a plural of a plural. [39 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(بعض بني عقيل,مالك); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن الأعرابي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشاعر); SAMIN-DURR(الشاعر,عكرمة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:239/q03/p2 Walkers advancing as a congregational group toward the enemy. — Abu Hayyan reports this separate application, paired with mounted worshippers gesturing. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 2:239/q04 How is rijālan parsed?  [رِجَالًا · grammar]
- 2:239/q04/p1 It is an accusative circumstantial expression under an omitted command to pray or maintain prayer. — Farra says the condition is temporary; Tabari supplies the answer 'pray', Nahhas calls it circumstantial, and Abu Hayyan and Samin prefer supplying 'maintain the prayers' from 2:238. Tabatabai describes the omitted safe-condition antecedent. [12 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 2:239/q05 What readings of farijālan are reported?  [فَرِجَالًا · readings]
turns on: The transmitted consonants and vowels, and Tabari's preference for the widespread reading.
- 2:239/q05/p1 The inherited common reading is farijālan. — Tabari rejects two uncommon variants because they depart from widespread transmitted recitation. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم)+]
- 2:239/q05/p2 Forms with rāʾ ḍamma and doubled or undoubled jīm are reported. — Ikrima and Abu Mujliz transmit a doubled-jim form; another Ikrima report leaves jim light. Abu Hayyan and Alusi record variants; Samin attributes forms also to Ibn Mukhallad. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عكرمة,عكرمة وأبو مجلز); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة، وأبو مجلز); SAMIN-DURR(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:239/q05/p3 A singular or alif-less farajulan or farujulan is reported. — Ibn Atiyya transmits Tabari's alif-less form; Abu Hayyan reports two vowellings, and Razi and Alusi mention singular rajulan. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:239/q06 What does rukbānan mean and what is its form?  [رُكْبَانًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether rākib is general or technically limited to camel riders.
- 2:239/q06/p1 Mounted riders; a plural of rākib. — Tabari lists other Arabic plurals; Wahidi and Jishumi compare fāris/fursān, and Tabarisi explains the root of mounting and rikāb. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(المفضل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بديل بن ميسرة); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI]
- 2:239/q06/p2 Properly a camel rider, with other mounts named separately. — Al-Mufaddal, al-Qaffal, al-Fadl and Samin distinguish horse rider fāris and names for donkey and mule riders; al-Fadl finds sahib plus animal more eloquent. [4 notes: THALABI(المفضل); RAZI-FULL(القفال); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفضل); SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:239/q07 How does aw connect the two modes, and is another reading reported?  [أَوْ رُكْبَانًۭا · readings]
turns on: The transmitted conjunction and the force of aw.
- 2:239/q07/p1 The majority read aw rukbānan, joining the alternatives. — Ibn Atiyya reports the majority; Samin says aw can divide cases, grant permission, or offer choice. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:239/q07/p2 Badīl ibn Maysara reads a second fāʾ before rukbānan. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report farijālan farukbānan. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بديل بن ميسرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بديل بن ميسرة)]

## 2:239/q08 What prayer form does the fear concession permit?  [فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether walking combat remains prayer and how far inability can justify delaying it.
- 2:239/q08/p1 Pray in the feasible posture, on foot or mounted, moving if needed, using head gestures when full bowing and prostration are unsafe. — Mujahid, Ibrahim, Zuhri, Hasan, Jabir and numerous authorities report combat or pursuit, any heading, and gestures, with prostration lower than bowing. Wahidi retains opening takbir and recitation and travel shortening; Razi retains intention and recitation, forbids needless cries; Muqatil forbids placing forehead on anything. The Prophet's fear-prayer report via Ibn Umar distinguishes this from guarded group prayer. Abdullah ibn Unays prayed gesturing while approaching Khalid ibn Sufyan; Ibn Kathir calls the chain good. [80 notes: MUJAHID(إبراهيم); MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(الزهري); AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن سيرين,ابن عمر,الحسن,الربيع,الزهري,السدي,سعيد,سعيد بن جبير,عطاء,قتادة,مجاهد,هرم بن حيان); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الحسن ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وعطاء وعطية والحكم وحماد وقتادة,الحسن ومجاهد ومكحول والحكم والسدي ومالك والأوزاعي والثوري وحسين بن صالح,جابر بن عبد الله); THALABI(المفسرون); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عمر ونافع,الشافعي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشافعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عمر,الحسن ومجاهد وسعيد بن جبير وعطاء وعطية والحكم وحماد وقتادة,الحسن ومجاهد ومكحول والسدي والحكم ومالك والأوزاعي والثوري والحسن بن صالح,جابر بن عبد الله,عبد الله بن أنيس الجهني,مالك ونافع وابن عمر); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر,البغوي,نافع); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس,ابن عمر,جابر بن عبد الله,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن أنيس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الشافعي); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(إمامنا,بعض الحنفية)-]
- 2:239/q08/p2 One may pray mounted but a walker in active fighting must stop if possible or delay until safety. — Abu Hanifa and companions hold walking and fighting invalidates prayer. They cite the Prophet's delayed prayer at the Trench; some Hanafis argue rājil may mean standing on foot and combat destroys prayer form. [9 notes: JISHUMI(أبو حنيفة وأصحابه); RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة); ALUSI-FULL(إمامنا,بعض الحنفية)]
- 2:239/q08/p3 If even gestures are impossible, delay prayer until safe and then perform it. — Al-Awza'i and Makhul allow deferral under siege only when gesturing cannot be done; Tustar's fighters prayed after sunrise. Bukhari, Makhul and Awza'i defend a rare deferral using the unchallenged Tustar practice. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس بن مالك,الأوزاعي ومكحول,البخاري,مكحول والأوزاعي والبخاري)]

## 2:239/q09 How many units does fear prayer require?  [فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether fear itself shortens the prescribed count; sources also vary on fallback when two are impossible.
- 2:239/q09/p1 Fear leaves the normal unit count intact, or at least requires the travel count; two units are commonly specified. — Zuhri and Ibrahim report two; Tabari prefers the full safe count but validates one. The majority, Malik, Shafi'i and Iraqi jurists reject reduction; Jishumi argues one unit is not prayer and dawn is never shortened. Ibn Abd al-Barr doubts Bukayr's isolated one-unit report and chooses two with certainty. Tabarisi prescribes two in travel and residence except three-unit Maghrib. [23 notes: MUJAHID(إبراهيم); ABDURRAZZAQ(الزهري); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,الربيع,الزهري,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(إبراهيم النخعي والزهري ومكحول والربيع بن أنس وسفيان الثوري والحسن بن صالح,الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL(أكثر أهل العلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك وجماعة من العلماء); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عبد البر,مالك والشافعي وجماعة من العلماء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); JISHUMI(أهل العراق)+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس)-]
- 2:239/q09/p2 One unit suffices in intense fear. — Qatada, Hasan, Ibn Abbas, Jabir and others report one; Ibn Abbas's formula is four at home, two in travel, one in fear. Ahmad allows it when armies engage; Ibn Kathir reports Ibn Jarir selecting one on Jabir's report. Wahidi says Ibn Abbas's school position is not acted upon. [29 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الحكم، حماد، قتادة,جابر بن عبد الله,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(عطية العوفي); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن بن أبي الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن بن أبي الحسن وقتادة وغيرهما); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل,ابن عباس,الحسن البصري وقتادة والضحاك,الحكم وحماد وقتادة والثوري); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة,مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(IK); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، وقتادة، وغيرهما); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+]
- 2:239/q09/p3 Two units if able, one if unable. — Tabari transmits Qatada and Hasan's graded rule; Mujahid gives the same sequence followed by one takbir. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI]
- 2:239/q09/p4 Two units in travel and residence except Maghrib, which remains three. — Tabarisi states this specific prescription. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 2:239/q10 What suffices when even a full unit cannot be performed?  [فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ · law]
turns on: How little outward action can still fulfill prayer under extreme inability.
- 2:239/q10/p1 Two takbirs or brief verbal remembrance may constitute the prayer. — Dahhak permits two takbirs if one unit is impossible; Said ibn Jubayr names tasbih, tahmid, tahlil and takbir during actual blows. Mujahid allows speaking as possible; Hurm ibn Hayyan called for one prostration or any gesture. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك,مجاهد,هرم بن حيان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك بن مزاحم); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك بن مزاحم); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك بن مزاحم)]
- 2:239/q10/p2 A single takbir suffices if that alone is possible. — Ishaq ibn Rahawayh and Mujahid give this fallback; Biqai says scholars reduce extreme-fear prayer to one takbir. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(إسحاق بن راهويه); QURTUBI-FULL(إسحاق بن راهويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(إسحاق); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 2:239/q10/p3 Remember God inwardly when no bodily gesture is possible. — Thalabi transmits Zuhri's final fallback; Harali says the heart's turn to God is prayer's essence even when outward acts cannot be done. [2 notes: THALABI(الزهري); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]

## 2:239/q11 Does this concession require facing the qibla?  [فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًۭا · law]
- 2:239/q11/p1 Face it when feasible; otherwise pray in any direction needed for safety. — Ibn Umar's report, attributed by Nafi to the Prophet, and many early authorities permit whichever direction. Wahidi explicitly says face it if possible. Razi argues continuous facing is impossible in severe danger; Qurtubi reports agreement that one may turn to save oneself. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,الحسن,السدي,عطاء); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن ومجاهد ومكحول والحكم والسدي ومالك والأوزاعي والثوري وحسين بن صالح); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عمر,المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عمر ونافع); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مالك ونافع وابن عمر); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN]

## 2:239/q12 Which causes of fear qualify, including property and prohibited fighting?  [خِفْتُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the concession follows any serious danger or only lawful causes of danger.
- 2:239/q12/p1 Life-threatening enemy and nonenemy dangers generally qualify. — Tabari includes combat, predator, attacking camel and flood because the verse excludes no type at the requisite severity; most jurists treat enemy and other dangers alike. Razi includes defending protected lives, rebels by 49:9, fire, flood, animals and a debtor legitimately fleeing detention. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أكثر فقهاء الأمصار); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:239/q12/p2 Defending property also qualifies. — Razi prefers this, citing the report that one killed defending property is a martyr, making the defense comparable to self-defense. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:239/q12/p3 Defending property does not qualify. — Razi reports the opposing argument that another protected interest has greater sanctity. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:239/q12/p4 Prohibited fighting does not qualify. — Razi says a concession would assist wrongdoing. He reports Qadi Abu al-Mahasin's nuance that self-defense is generally permitted, but obligatory against an unbeliever targeting the person. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي أبو المحاسن الطبري)]

## 2:239/q13 Must a completed fear prayer be repeated after safety?  [فَإِذَآ أَمِنتُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether the original act was valid, and whether enemy and nonenemy fear differ.
- 2:239/q13/p1 No repetition is required for an excused fear prayer. — Hijazi jurists say the excuse makes it valid; the Prophet's reported sword-fighting prayer need not be repeated. Another opinion waives repetition after a reliable but mistaken enemy warning. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أهل الحجاز); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:239/q13/p2 Repeat it after safety. — Iraqi jurists say walking is external to prayer. Abu Hanifa requires repetition when an alleged approaching enemy departs or an apparent enemy turns out to be a dark mass. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أهل العراق); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 2:239/q13/p3 For nonenemy danger, repeating within the time is recommended. — Malik distinguishes a beast or flood from enemy fighting; Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan note most jurists make no such distinction. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، وأكثر الفقهاء)]

## 2:239/q14 Can prayer continue across a change between fear and safety?  [فَإِذَآ أَمِنتُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether mounting and movement break continuity, and whether the direction of change matters.
- 2:239/q14/p1 Build on the prayer through either transition. — Malik, Muzani and one Shafi'i view permit completing from the first unit or after dismounting. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي والمزني,مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك، وأحد قولي الشافعي، والمزني)]
- 2:239/q14/p2 Restart when safety changes to fear, but build when fear changes to safety. — Abu Hanifa draws this directional distinction. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حنيفة)]
- 2:239/q14/p3 A pedestrian may build, but a rider may not. — Qurtubi transmits another Shafi'i ruling. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 2:239/q14/p4 Build in neither transition. — Abu Yusuf requires restarting in both cases. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو يوسف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو يوسف)]

## 2:239/q15 What role do purification and speech retain during fear prayer?  [فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًۭا · law]
turns on: Which acts remain integral and whether necessary combat or speech invalidates prayer.
- 2:239/q15/p1 Purification remains required, with tayammum allowed when using water is unsafe. — Razi says water or earth remains available; he prefers dust even when water exists if using it threatens life, analogizing the danger to fear of thirst. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]
- 2:239/q15/p2 Intention and recitation remain; unrelated speech and needless cries are barred. — Razi states these limits despite waived standing and replacement gestures. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 2:239/q15/p3 Necessary action in fear may remain permissible in prayer, with speech treated like action. — Shafi'i infers combat does not invalidate prayer; Biqai traces Ibn Masud's greeting reports and Dhū al-Yadayn reports, discounts weak Asim against Zayd, and prefers no speech-action distinction if chronology allows; Tatimma's author, Nawawi and Subki concur. [11 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حنيفة,الشافعي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن مسعود,الشافعي,صاحب التتمة,محيي الدين النواوي والسبكي); BIQAI-FULL+]

## 2:239/q16 What does becoming safe mean?  [فَإِذَآ أَمِنتُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether safety is the end of imminent danger, settled residence, or any secure state.
- 2:239/q16/p1 The danger or fear has ended. — Tabari says security from enemies or other lethal threats; Muqatil specifies enemy. Tabari rejects travel return: amintum and context contrast severe fear with safety, while travel shortening remains permissible. Ibn Atiyya calls travel reading strained. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:239/q16/p2 Return from travel to one's settled residence. — Mujahid's reading is reported by Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Atiyya, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Durr and Alusi. Abu Hayyan adds that travel tends to be fearful and home secure; Alusi treats it as illustrative. Tabari objects that the word for settling would be iqama. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); TAB-FULL-]
- 2:239/q16/p3 Whenever one is safe, whether before or after danger. — Ibn Atiyya reports this interpretation of the command; Abu Hayyan likewise reports it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 2:239/q17 What does the command to remember God require?  [فَٱذْكُرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether dhikr here names the restored ritual prayer, thanksgiving, or both.
- 2:239/q17/p1 Resume the complete prescribed prayer. — Muqatil, Ibn Zayd and many later commentators identify dhikr with prayer; Tabari notes the safe prayer again has standing, bowing and prostration, and Razi cites 62:9. Damghani, Ibn Jawzi and Itqan attest dhikr for five prayers, Damghani citing 24:37. Some specify all five with full rights. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مقاتل بن حيان); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(الحسن وابن زيد والأصم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 2:239/q17/p2 Praise and thank God for safety, divine teaching and the valid fear prayer. — Tabari includes praise and thanks for true guidance; Kashshaf and Ibn Atiyya cite safety and teaching of worship; Qadi objects that gratitude is also due in fear, while Razi reports that objection. Qurtubi says gratitude is for an instruction that saves a prayer from being missed. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(قول ثان); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL(القاضي)-]
- 2:239/q17/p3 Both prayer and gratitude. — Razi's third interpretation places gratitude upon safety alongside timely prayer; Abu Hayyan and Elmalili combine worship, rites and thanks. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(قول ثالث); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:239/q18 How does kamā express the relation between remembrance and God's teaching?  [كَمَا عَلَّمَكُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether the kāf is comparative or causal and whether mā is infinitival or relative.
- 2:239/q18/p1 Comparison: remember or pray in the manner or measure God taught. — Ibn Atiyya distinguishes comparing thanksgiving with the gift from comparing prayer with its taught form. Abu Hayyan makes the kāf a simile and mā a verbal noun; Baydawi, Bursevi, Tabatabai and Alusi relate it to fear and safety prayer or proportional thanks. Samin calls the kāf an adjective of an omitted verbal noun or a circumstantial expression. [15 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 2:239/q18/p2 Cause: remember and thank God because He taught you. — Abu Hayyan calls knowledge the greatest gift; Biqai says God created rescuing knowledge in people. Ibn Ashur says the causal sense follows from comparison; Burhan gives kāf among substitutes for the lām of purpose. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN]
- 2:239/q18/p3 Mā in kamā is a verbal noun. — Tabarisi, Abu Hayyan and Samin parse it as infinitival; Samin prefers it. Alusi reports the option. [4 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 2:239/q18/p4 Mā in kamā is relative, referring to the taught prayer. — Samin says this permits 'pray like the prayer He taught'; Abu Hayyan attributes it to Naqqash and others, and Alusi calls it strained. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النقاش وغيره); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:239/q19 How is mā lam takūnū taʿlamūn parsed and why that wording?  [مَّا لَمْ تَكُونُوا۟ تَعْلَمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical antecedent of mā and the rhetorical emphasis of former ignorance.
- 2:239/q19/p1 It is the second object of 'He taught you'. — Tabarisi, Qurtubi, Baydawi and Abu Hayyan identify the object. Abu Hayyan says it highlights knowledge unattainable unaided by reason. [5 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 2:239/q19/p2 It is an appositive of the mā in kamā. — Ibn Atiyya offers this on the prayer reading; Abu Hayyan reports it via Ibn Atiyya, and Samin reports its acceptance. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية)]
- 2:239/q19/p3 It substitutes for the omitted resumptive pronoun in allamakumūhu. — Abu Hayyan and the Shaykh prefer this; the grammarians license omitted relative pronouns, illustrated by 'the one I struck your brother' meaning 'I struck him, your brother'. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)+]
- 2:239/q19/p4 The phrase's breadth and auxiliary verb stress God's favor and prior ignorance. — Razi locates the ignorance before Muhammad's mission; Alusi contrasts shorter 96:5. Tabatabai says the broader wording emphasizes the scope of teaching. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]

## 2:239/q20 What did God teach that had previously been unknown?  [عَلَّمَكُم مَّا لَمْ تَكُونُوا۟ تَعْلَمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the teaching is specifically prayer or the broader divine instruction.
- 2:239/q20/p1 Prayer and its appointed times and fear and safety forms. — Ibn Abi Hatim reports Ibn Abbas on riding and walking; Wahidi specifies appointed prayer times; Kashshaf, Bursevi and others include prayer in safety and fear. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:239/q20/p2 The wider religion, laws and beneficial knowledge. — Tabari lists lawful and forbidden things, past histories, future events and afterlife knowledge; Tabarisi adds religion and other subjects. Biqai includes this sura's rulings and subtle sciences; Harali adds bodily prayer, humility and inner secrets; Ibn Kathir names faith and welfare in both lives. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); TABATABAI; ELMALILI]

## 2:239/q21 How does God impart this knowledge?  [عَلَّمَكُم · theology]
turns on: The theological account of divine teaching.
- 2:239/q21/p1 God creates knowledge in people. — Razi attributes this explanation to his Ashʿarite colleagues; Biqai says God graciously creates rescuing knowledge. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا والمعتزلة); BIQAI-FULL]
- 2:239/q21/p2 God furnishes proofs and acts of grace. — Razi attributes this to the Muʿtazila. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا والمعتزلة)]

## 2:239/q22 Why are the conditions and modes worded or ordered this way?  [فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًۭا فَإِذَآ أَمِنتُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The marked order of walkers and riders and the contrast between the two conditionals.
- 2:239/q22/p1 Walkers come before riders as compensation for the harder form. — Burhan says mounted prayer is easier, so the wording advances walkers. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 2:239/q22/p2 If presents fear as less frequent, while when presents expected security. — Alusi links the brief first answer and expanded second to their settings; Ibn Ashur reads the switch as a promise of victory and security. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:239/q23 How does this verse connect with prayer commands and other fear-prayer verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding command or broader sequence explains the insertion and scope of this concession.
- 2:239/q23/p1 It qualifies the preceding command to preserve prayer and stand devoutly, while 4:101–103 treats related fear-prayer forms. — Tabarisi and Razi say the preceding full posture applies in safety; Thalabi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir and Kuranyolu distinguish this intense individual danger from imam-led divided-group prayer in al-Nisa. Ibn Jawzi reports a view that this verse came after 4:102. [14 notes: TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قيل); RAZI-FULL; THALABI(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:239/q23/p2 It interrupts family law to recall the priority of worship amid worldly concerns. — Biqai cites attachments and conflict after women's rulings and moves from relations among people to their relationship with God. Elmalili sees an exhortation to virtue, prayer and jihad amid death and divorce before family rulings continue. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:239/q23/p3 The return to full prayer accords with 4:103's appointed-time command. — Ibn Kathir makes the explicit link. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 2:239/q24 What historical prayer reports bear on this verse and its chronology?  [* · reports]
turns on: The order of revelation and whether exceptional combat practice remained valid.
- 2:239/q24/p1 At the Trench the Prophet delayed prayers, then performed them after fighting. — Ibn Jawzi reports four prayers after twilight, Abu Said says they were before this verse, and Biqai and Durr say Bilal called each prayer after battle. Ibn Kathir also reports the Banu Qurayza march, where some prayed en route and others on arrival without censure. [7 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سعيد، أبو بكر الأثرم,ابن عباس عن النبي); BIQAI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الصحابة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
- 2:239/q24/p2 The Trench delay was superseded by the later severe-fear instruction. — Ibn Jawzi quotes al-Athram; Razi says the Trench danger had not reached this degree; Ibn Kathir reports the majority's claim that formal fear prayer came later, while Alusi reports a distinction between older 4:102 and this later verse. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سعيد، أبو بكر الأثرم); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الجمهور); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:239/q24/p3 The formal fear prayer existed before the Trench or exceptional deferral remained permissible. — Alusi cites Ibn Ishaq placing Dhat al-Riqa before the Trench. Bukhari's preference for occasional delay draws on Trench and Tustar; Makhul and Awza'i say later legislation does not exclude rare inability. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(البخاري,مكحول والأوزاعي والبخاري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن إسحاق)]
- 2:239/q24/p4 The Prophet or Ali prayed with gestures in severe combat. — Tabarisi reports the Prophet gesturing at the Trench; Ali prayed five prayers by gestures or, in another version, takbir on the night of al-Harir. Abdullah ibn Unays's mission supplies another concrete precedent. [4 notes: TABRISI(النبي,علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن أنيس الجهني); DURR-FULL(عبد الله بن أنيس)]
- 2:239/q24/p5 Reports about speaking in prayer have disputed chronology and strength. — Biqai cites Ibn Masud's Abyssinia return report and Dhū al-Yadayn, notes Aisha's dating of Baqara and Nisa to her cohabitation in year two, and weighs Asim's weak route against Zayd in the two Sahihs. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن مسعود,عائشة)]

## 2:239/q25 What lasting importance of prayer does the verse teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis falls on unceasing obligation, inward remembrance, prayer's benefits, or the isolated reclining comparison.
- 2:239/q25/p1 Prayer remains obligatory in every state, performed to the extent possible. — Ibn Jawzi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan, Tabatabai and Kuranyolu reason from the fear concession to illness, work and all other excuses; Qurtubi even permits eye gestures and says prayer cannot be delegated. Razi cites doing what one can; Ibn Kathir calls this a burden-easing concession. Ibn al-Arabi compares abandonment to repudiating a pillar akin to the testimonies; Qurtubi defers the legal discussion. [17 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أكثر العلماء); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:239/q25/p2 Its essential purpose is continuing remembrance and the heart's attention to God. — Harali makes remembrance the basis of safety and says complete inward turning preserves prayer; Biqai invokes God's name to reinforce presence. Kuranyolu says movements serve this inner relation and may be omitted when impossible; Elmalili says ongoing prayers preserve awareness of divine favor. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:239/q25/p3 The five prayers are a banquet of varied benefits. — Bursevi compares their pillars to dishes bringing pleasure and atonement; Ka'b al-Ahbar's address to Moses assigns distinct benefits to noon, afternoon, sunset and night prayers. [2 notes: BURSEVI(كعب الأحبار)]
- 2:239/q25/p4 Qur'anic reclining evokes complete comfort in Paradise. — Samarrai says reclining there normally accompanies food, drink and family, with one exception. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 2:239/q26 What does fear itself mean?  [خِفْتُمْ · meaning]
- 2:239/q26/p1 Fear is the opposite of security and denotes alarm or panic. — Jishumi defines it by its opposite; Qurtubi specifies fright or panic. [2 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 2:239/q27 What posture is normal before fear requires a concession?  [فَإِنْ خِفْتُمْ · interpretation]
- 2:239/q27/p1 Standing calmly and devoutly with still limbs is the normal prayer. — Ibn Atiyya defines qunūt as solemn tranquility and stillness. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 2:239/q28 Does the verse address prayer amid active combat?  [فَرِجَالًا أَوْ رُكْبَانًۭا · law]
turns on: Whether the wording covers combat generally or identifies a particular prayer.
- 2:239/q28/p1 Yes, including sword fighting when ordinary posture is impossible. — Tawus locates it in sword fighting; Wahidi and Tabari identify intense combat rather than the divided-group prayer. Hasan and Shafi'i permit gestures while fighting, and Baydawi says Shafi'i deems prayer obligatory even while moving. Tabari cites the Prophetic distinction between ordinary fear and intermingled combat. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(طاووس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 2:239/q28/p2 The prayer is specifically the dawn prayer in life-threatening sword combat. — Abu Hayyan reports this narrower interpretation, also distinguishing it from imam-led divided groups. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 2:239/q29 Which other word-order explanations appear in the surrounding rhetorical material?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The differing rhetorical rationales in the cited adjacent discussions.
- 2:239/q29/p1 Hearing precedes knowing because sound seems nearer and warns more directly. — Burhan adds that God's knowledge still encompasses hidden and manifest matters. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 2:239/q29/p2 Forgiveness precedes mercy because safety comes before gain. — Burhan contrasts Saba 34:2, where mercy precedes because it covers all creation while forgiveness is narrower. [1 notes: BURHAN]
