# 90:6: 18 questions

## 90:6/q01 How does yaqūlu function in its sentence?  [يَقُولُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the speech is attached to the earlier human or begins a new sentence.
- 90:6/q01/p1 It is circumstantial, describing the human and his hardship in the preceding verse. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it with 90:4. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 90:6/q01/p2 It may be an independent clause. — Al-Samīn offers this alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 90:6/q02 When is the boast spoken?  [يَقُولُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether yaqūlu describes worldly speech or anticipates judgment.
- 90:6/q02/p1 It is speech in earthly life. — Accounts connect it with real-world opposition, display, or expiations. [6 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF; QURTUBI(مقاتل); ELMALILI]
- 90:6/q02/p2 He will say it at Resurrection, when his spending proves useless. — Al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı report this; the loss explains 'destroyed.' [4 notes: ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(some exegetes); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]

## 90:6/q03 Who is the speaker?  [يَقُولُ · referent]
turns on: Whether broad wording or a particular reported occasion controls identification.
- 90:6/q03/p1 A human being generally, especially a proud, self-sufficient disbeliever. — Al-Ṭabarī calls him stubborn and strong; Ibn Kathīr's transmitters say Adam's son. Al-Jishumī prefers the general wording over a specific occasion. Al-Kashshāf calls him a Quraysh notable; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ compares 96:6–7. [11 notes: TAB; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNKATHIR(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة والسدي وغيرهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة والسدي); DURR-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; JISHUMI+]
- 90:6/q03/p2 Abū al-Ashadd or Abū al-Ashaddayn al-Jumaḥī. — Al-Māwardī reports the first form; Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Qurṭubī gives the second. [4 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 90:6/q03/p3 Al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith. — Al-Māwardī gives this alternative name. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 90:6/q03/p4 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Al-Jishumī reports his boast of hostile spending. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 90:6/q03/p5 Al-Ḥārith ibn ʿĀmir ibn Nawfal, who complained about costly expiations after entering Islam. — Muqātil says he asked the Prophet how to expiate sins; some accounts shorten the name to al-Ḥārith ibn Nawfal. Elmalılı calls it resentment of religion. [12 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); ELMALILI(Mukātil); TABATABAI(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭs)]

## 90:6/q04 What does ahlaktu mean here?  [أَهْلَكْتُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it simply denotes expenditure or carries the force of ruin.
- 90:6/q04/p1 I spent or expended wealth. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Baghawī gloss it this way; al-Qāshānī cites Arab usage for expenditure used to assert superiority. [5 notes: BAGHAWI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 90:6/q04/p2 I squandered, wasted, or destroyed wealth. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr retain the sense of loss; al-Ālūsī says the spender expected no return. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNASHUR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]

## 90:6/q05 Why call his spending destruction?  [أَهْلَكْتُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The proposed force is failed benefit, a boast of indifference, or a sura-wide echo.
- 90:6/q05/p1 It exposes vain spending that brings no true benefit. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the word suits vanity; Elmalılı contrasts feeding the poor (89:18–19). Bursevī and al-Tibyān call misdirected spending wasted; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Arabs treated lavish dissipation as virtue. [11 notes: BINTSHATI; ELMALILI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(التبيان,روح المعاني); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:6/q05/p2 It flaunts how little a large expense matters to one with much more wealth. — Al-Biqāʿī infers remaining wealth from this boast; al-Ālūsī hears indifference. [3 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 90:6/q05/p3 The word echoes the sura's hardship, death, danger, and ruin. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares 90:4–5, 11, 14–16, 19–20 and calls it the Qurʾān's sole use of 'destroyed' for money. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 90:6/q06 What attitude does the speech express?  [يَقُولُ أَهْلَكْتُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the claim is a boast, a lament, or an excuse.
- 90:6/q06/p1 He boasts arrogantly of the amount he spent. — Al-Māwardī calls it transgression; al-Qurṭubī says even a complaint about expiations could be self-important. Al-Biqāʿī sees a demand for gratitude; al-Qāshānī sees false virtue in public favors. [13 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF; RAZI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(روح المعاني)]
- 90:6/q06/p2 He laments or regrets the loss. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī list this alternative; one report connects regret to expensive expiations, another to opposing the Prophet. [8 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Bazı yorumlar)]
- 90:6/q06/p3 He treats previous spending as sufficient when called to give for good. — Quṭb places it at a call to give. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

- 90:6/q07/p4 On desires such as gambling and wine. — Ibn ʿĀshūr names both when asking why he did not free captives or feed the hungry; al-Tibyān contrasts desire-led waste with God-commanded spending. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(التبيان); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 90:6/q08 Is the claim of spending truthful?  [أَهْلَكْتُ مَالًۭا · interpretation]
- 90:6/q08/p1 His hostile-spending claim is false; God saw and recorded his acts. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī call it a lie; Ibn ʿAṭiyya invokes God's observation. Al-Sāmarrāʾī leaves a false claim among the possibilities. [9 notes: FARRA; TAB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 90:6/q09 What does the indefinite mālan convey?  [مَالًۭا · rhetoric]
- 90:6/q09/p1 The amount remains unrestricted and the speech broadly applicable. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ includes 'wealth,' 'abundant,' and 'anyone' among unrestricted indefinites; al-Sāmarrāʾī preserves multiple possible motives. [2 notes: BINTSHATI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 90:6/q10 What does lubadan mean?  [لُّبَدًا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word depicts a dense amount or exceptional age.
- 90:6/q10/p1 Great, dense quantities of wealth piled or gathered in layers. — Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, and Ibn Zayd say 'much.' Abū ʿUbayda and Ibn Qutayba derive piling or clinging; al-Māwardī compares felt. Al-Layth adds abundance without fear of exhaustion; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites thick mane and foliage; al-Zajjāj sees intensive abundance. Al-Rāzī connects 72:19. [62 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج,مجاهد); NAHHAS; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو عبيدة,الفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج,الفراء,الليث); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,الزجاج,الفراء,الليث); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة والسدي وغيرهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد والحسن وقتادة والسدي); BURHAN; DURR(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(القمي); BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:6/q10/p2 It may describe wealth old or held for a long time. — Al-Māwardī relates it to abad or proverbially ancient Labīd/Lubad; the reports vary the name's spelling. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]

## 90:6/q11 What morphological form is lubad?  [لُّبَدًا · grammar]
turns on: Plural clump, intensive singular or collective, and a further plural analysis differ.
- 90:6/q11/p1 It is a plural of lubda or libda, a felted clump. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī give the singular; al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr specify matted hair or wool. Al-Kashshāf allows ḍamma or kasra on lām. [18 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(الآخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA; RAZI(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI(الباقون); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(الكشاف); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:6/q11/p2 It is a singular or collective intensive adjective for piled abundance. — Al-Farrāʾ compares quṯam and ḥuṭam; Sībawayh classes لبد as an adjective alongside صرد، نغر، ربع. His surrounding examples chart noun and adjective triliteral patterns, including rare إبل; al-Burhān adds verbal-noun and shifted-form uses with 3:7 and 2:184. Ibn Jinnī allows a singular like زمل. [26 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA; ZAJJAJ; IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(أبو جعفر); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI(الآخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI(الزجاج,الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج,الفراء); BURHAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:6/q11/p3 One analysis makes it a plural of lubb. — Al-Naḥḥās records this beside the collective singular. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 90:6/q13 Why use lubad rather than merely 'much'?  [لُّبَدًا · rhetoric]
- 90:6/q13/p1 It depicts piled density and reinforces gathering imagery across the sura. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares hostile crowds at 72:19, parents and offspring, paired faculties and paths, ascent duties, mutual exhortation, and gathered disbelievers. Al-Biqāʿī says the doubled reading intensifies innumerable heaps. [7 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 90:6/q14 What does the boast reveal about the person?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether emphasis falls on power through wealth or false credit for favors.
- 90:6/q14/p1 It reveals pride, self-sufficiency, and a wish to command others through wealth. — Al-Biqāʿī says favors bind free people and imagines limitless riches; sayings about another valley of gold and a belly filled only by dust illustrate insatiability. Al-Qāshānī cites veiling and sickness of heart. Corpus Coranicum links possessiveness to 104:1–3, 80:5, 92:8, 96:7; Asad sees limitless worldly ambition; al-Iṣlāḥī recalls Makkah's history and Abraham's purpose. [15 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES; CORPUSCORANICUM; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; IBNASHUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 90:6/q14/p2 He claims credit for favors despite receiving better gifts from God. — Ibn Jurayj answers his claim with eyes and other blessings; another report says what people preferred was better. Al-Biqāʿī sees a demand for gratitude from all. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 90:6/q15 How does the next verse confront the boast?  [* · links]
turns on: The sight and accounting are agreed; the reading note concerns the adjoining question.
- 90:6/q15/p1 God sees and accounts for what he earned, spent, and intended. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk identifies the unseen observer as God; Abū Ḥayyān mentions recorders. Al-Ḥasan asks who will audit him: the maker of his limbs. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Bursevī cite questioning at Judgment; Abū Hurayra says showy charity earns punishment. Study Quran cites God's control of provision (13:26, 17:30, 28:82, 29:62, 30:37, 34:36, 34:39). Al-Tibyān says lam marks witnessed past expenditure. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA; RAZI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو هريرة,الحسن,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN; BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(التبيان); STUDYQURAN]
- 90:6/q15/p2 A report reads yaḥsabu with a rounded sīn at both occurrences. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits the Prophetic reading of the adjoining questions. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 90:6/q16 How does this boast relate to the sura's call to use wealth?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether attention is on the spending's destination or the inward condition of its benefit.
- 90:6/q16/p1 Fruitless display contrasts with freeing people, feeding the needy, and showing mercy. — Al-Kashshāf compares showy spending with a wind-ruined crop (3:117). Ibn ʿĀshūr asks why the money was not used for liberation or famine relief. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ requires hard charity and joining believers; al-Sāmarrāʾī names orphans, poor, debtors, and enslaved people. Corpus Coranicum says 90:13–16 reverses the heroic poetry ideal. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(التبيان); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 90:6/q16/p2 Even pre-Islamic charity brings no benefit without seeking forgiveness for Judgment Day. — ʿĀʾisha asks about ʿAbdallāh ibn Judʿān's charity and care for kin; the Prophet answers that he never sought such forgiveness. [2 notes: BURSEVI(رسول الله عليه السلام,عائشة رضي الله عنها)]

## 90:6/q17 How does its spending image compare with poetry and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The comparison is with heroic poetry, hoarding elsewhere, or this sura's design.
- 90:6/q17/p1 It reverses poetry's praise of lavish waste. — Corpus Coranicum cites Taʾabbaṭa Sharran's rebuke of waste and ʿAntara's boast of drinking away wealth. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites ʿAntara spending while retaining honor; a later biblical allusion overturns that worldview. [5 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; IBNASHUR(عنترة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 90:6/q17/p2 Al-Humazah's wealth hoarder contrasts with this squanderer. — Corpus Coranicum connects 104:2–3 with supposed security; al-Sāmarrāʾī says sealed Hell mirrors hoarding. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 90:6/q17/p3 The verse participates in a structured sura-wide argument. — Corpus Coranicum describes two rhymed triplets and repeated questions. Al-Sāmarrāʾī names toil, breadth, collective action, patience, and mercy; Elmalılı ties divine gifts to knowledge and reckoning. turns on: The notes supply distinct cross-references.
- 90:6/q18/p1 One lexical note defines aḥzāb as opinion-defined groups. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the plural of ḥizb without stating a closer relation to 90:6. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 90:6/q18/p2 Al-Qurṭubī points to his discussion of the reading and word in Sūrat al-Jinn. — This is a cross-reference to his treatment of the word elsewhere. [1 notes: QURTUBI]
