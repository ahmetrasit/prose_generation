# 20:47: 34 questions

## 20:47/q01 Why is the command to approach Pharaoh repeated here?  [فَأْتِيَاهُ · links]
- 20:47/q01/p1 The renewed instruction advances or specifies the earlier command as the dialogue and mission develop. — Rāzī traces four stages through 20:23–24, 20:42–43; Biqāʿī says God emphasizes speech before dictating it. Ālūsī relates arrival to the reassurance in 20:46. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they are already near Pharaoh in his city. Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces Moses' commission, Aaron's joining, and the renewed order after their fear is answered. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:47/q01/p2 Coming conveys ease of arrival or intention to arrive, so it adds something to the prior command to go. — Bursevī distinguishes coming as easy arrival from going as reaching, while allowing both to describe the intention to arrive. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:47/q02 To whom are the two to come?  [فَأْتِيَاهُ · referent]
- 20:47/q02/p1 They are to come to Pharaoh. — Nasafī identifies the pronoun as Pharaoh; Ṭabrisī and Muqātil give the same addressee in the mission account. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; MUQATIL]

## 20:47/q03 What does 'come to him' suggest about approaching Pharaoh?  [فَأْتِيَاهُ · rhetoric]
- 20:47/q03/p1 It suggests close, direct contact and diminishes Pharaoh's grandeur. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts coming with going: direct contact is striking given the difficulty of approaching Egypt's king, whom the Copts regarded as a god. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:47/q04 What action or speech does the narrative leave implicit?  [فَأْتِيَاهُ فَقُولَآ · rhetoric]
- 20:47/q04/p1 They went to Pharaoh and delivered the commanded message; the account omits the intervening action or speech. — Muqātil places the end of God's quoted instruction here and narrates Moses' demand on arrival. Zajjāj says context supplies 'they said to him,' confirmed by Pharaoh's question in 20:49. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī likewise supply their arrival and address. [4 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:47/q05 Why is 'to him' absent after 'say'?  [فَقُولَآ · rhetoric]
- 20:47/q05/p1 The omission keeps Pharaoh from receiving special verbal attention. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the unexpressed addressee diminishes Pharaoh. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:47/q06 Why do the messengers state their claim at the outset?  [فَقُولَآ إِنَّا رَسُولَا رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 20:47/q06/p1 It establishes their standing and lets Pharaoh understand and answer the message. — Bursevī says the tyrant can frame his response accordingly; Ālūsī notes both the initial establishment of rank and the gentleness of calling God 'their Lord.' Ibn ʿĀshūr says the request follows as established by a known mission or by its importance, since God's messenger must be obeyed. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:47/q07 What work does the emphatic particle إن do?  [إِنَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle is prompted chiefly by an implied inquiry or by expected resistance to the claim.
- 20:47/q07/p1 It answers an implicit question about their identity and purpose. — Jurjānī endorses Abū al-ʿAbbās's distinction: إن without lām answers a question, while إن with lām addresses denial. He cites 18:83–84, 18:13, 26:216, 6:56, 15:89, 26:16, 43:46, and 26:49–50. [2 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(أبو العباس)]
- 20:47/q07/p2 It emphasizes a claim Pharaoh might doubt or find implausible. — Jurjānī says إن suits an assertion contrary to what the hearer suspects or expects. Biqāʿī adds that the emphatic dual stresses mutual support. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 20:47/q08 How is the dual رسولَا explained?  [رَسُولَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the dual assigns messengership to each brother directly or applies Moses' title to the pair by predominance.
- 20:47/q08/p1 It agrees with Moses and Aaron as two messengers and suits this sūra's joint presentation of their mission. — Bursevī calls rasūl a rare faʿūl bearer of a message, human or angel, unlike nabī restricted to a human. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passive faʿūl can agree in number or remain singular, citing Arabic usage and 26:16. Samarrāʾī contrasts the dual focus in Ṭā Hā, including 20:42 and 20:63, with singular focus in al-Shuʿarāʾ and al-Zukhruf; rasūl can have singular or plural reference like بشر, طفل and ضيف (54:24, 5:18, 40:67). [5 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 20:47/q08/p2 Both are called messengers by predominance of Moses, who is messenger, over Aaron, who is prophet. — Iṣlāḥī calls this ʿalā sabīl al-taghlīb and connects the commission with 7:104–105. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:47/q09 Why call God Pharaoh's Lord?  [رَبِّكَ رَّبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 20:47/q09/p1 It asserts Pharaoh's created dependence and challenges his claim to lordship. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says calling him Pharaoh rather than endorsing his claim belittles him. Abū Ḥayyān and Biqāʿī describe him as God's owned servant; Biqāʿī says God created and nurtured him and repeated the address as reproach. Ibn ʿĀshūr argues God's lordship of the messengers implies lordship of all people. Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts Pharaoh's 'I am your highest lord.' Ālūsī recognizes both a gentle address and its humbling force, and says repeating رب strengthens the proof. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:47/q10 What does 'send the Children of Israel with us' ask Pharaoh to do?  [فَأَرْسِلْ مَعَنَا بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'send with us' specifies the destination of an exodus or simply release from Pharaoh's control.
- 20:47/q10/p1 Free the Israelites from captivity, service, and Pharaoh's oppressive control. — Damghānī cites 26:22 and 7:105 for arsala as release from punishment. Al-Irshād, preferred in Bursevī, says the wording need not command travel to Syria; the ban on torment supports freedom from control. Ālūsī likewise rejects making travel to Syria part of the request. Biqāʿī says release lets them worship God. Ibn ʿĀshūr links rescue to an independent nation with reforming law (17:104; 79:17). [21 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Kl); BURSEVI(الإرشاد)+]
- 20:47/q10/p2 Let them accompany Moses and Aaron to Palestine, their ancestral home. — Muqātil identifies 'with us' as the two brothers. Al-Baḥr locates Palestine between Syria and Egypt and names Ramla, Gaza, and Ascalon. Iṣlāḥī links the destination to his treatment of 7:104–105. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI(الإرشاد)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:47/q11 What sense of arsala is noted here?  [فَأَرْسِلْ · meaning]
turns on: The lexical range of arsala.
- 20:47/q11/p1 It can mean releasing people from punishment or restraint. — Damghānī compares 26:22 and 7:105. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]
- 20:47/q11/p2 The lexicon also lists 'send down' as another sense of arsala. — Damghānī records this as the seventh sense in his entry. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 20:47/q12 Why is Israel's release demanded at this point?  [فَأَرْسِلْ مَعَنَا بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the sequence marks the priority of rescue, a gradual appeal, an omitted faith invitation, or removing an obstacle to Israel.
- 20:47/q12/p1 Rescuing believers from unbelievers takes priority over inviting unbelievers to faith. — Bayḍāwī gives this as one explanation, and Bursevī invokes its place between messengership and sign. Ālūsī reports it but demands proof that Israel already believed in Moses or another prophet. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]
- 20:47/q12/p2 The order is a gradual invitation: obtain release before changing Pharaoh's belief. — Both Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī present this alternative. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:47/q12/p3 The call to faith was already given or is supplied elsewhere; this account foregrounds the release request. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the request supplements an earlier call to faith and Pharaoh's commission differs from Israel's. Abū Ḥayyān and Study Quran include faith and release together. Ālūsī says 79:18 states the faith request while this verse states release. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 20:47/q12/p4 Release removes an obstacle to Israel's following Moses. — Ālūsī records this view but argues that the context concerns summoning Pharaoh and resisting his tyranny. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:47/q13 What torment of the Israelites is meant?  [وَلَا تُعَذِّبْهُمْ · interpretation]
- 20:47/q13/p1 Pharaoh's oppression included arduous forced labor, humiliation, killing sons, and enslavement or exploitation of women. — The sources describe digging, construction, carrying stones, clay and brick work, and tasks beyond capacity. Qurṭubī says Pharaoh made them kill sons and spare women; Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī say sons were killed in alternating years. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Pharaoh treated resident Israelites as slaves and servants. Asad refers to 2:49, 7:141, 14:6, and Exodus 1:8–22 for fuller accounts. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Kl); ASAD-NOTES]
- 20:47/q13/p2 Their situation resembled protected tax communities in Yahyā's society. — Yahyā uses that contemporary comparison to characterize their status among Egyptians. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 20:47/q14 What meanings or origins are given for ʿadhāb?  [تُعَذِّبْهُمْ · meaning]
- 20:47/q14/p1 It denotes severe pain or affliction. — Bursevī gives three proposed derivations: hunger and sleeplessness, loss of life's sweetness, and repeated blows from a whip tip. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:47/q15 What is the role of the clause announcing a sign?  [قَدْ جِئْنَٰكَ بِـَٔايَةٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the statement presents proof immediately or offers proof if Pharaoh asks.
- 20:47/q15/p1 It supplies proof of their messengership and grounds Pharaoh's duty to obey. — Zamakhsharī, reported by Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn, says the claim requires a confirming proof. Jishumī says miracles make belief obligatory; another explanation has the brothers answer Pharaoh's request for evidence. Rāzī finds it better to state the demands before proving them. Bayḍāwī and Ibn ʿĀshūr treat the clause as an explanation of the claim; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its proof function or explanatory resumption permits separation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the indefinite form signals magnitude and clarity. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:47/q15/p2 It announces readiness to show Pharaoh a sign should he request one. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says belief without demanding a miracle would be more complete and identifies the initial sign as the staff becoming a serpent (7:106). [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:47/q16 What is the 'sign' brought to Pharaoh?  [بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular names an individual miracle, the pair or whole set, or proof in a generic sense.
- 20:47/q16/p1 It is the staff and the shining hand, jointly treated as proof. — Muqātil calls them tokens. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿAṭiyya say one messenger produced the signs but both shared the mission, so the dual speaker claims them. Study Quran calls the singular collective. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 20:47/q16/p2 It is a generic proof or miracle, with no fixed count of signs. — Al-Qaffāl says one or multiple proofs may be called a sign. Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī say the point is establishing a claim, not counting miracles; compare 7:105–106, 26:30, 26:154. Iṣlāḥī calls āyah an argument regardless of number and compares 7:105. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the indefinite suggests importance and clarity. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:47/q16/p3 It is specifically the staff. — Wāḥidī records one identification; Ibn al-Jawzī attributes it to Ibn ʿAbbās. Abū Ḥayyān reports it as an alternative; Ibn ʿĀshūr names the staff-serpent as the first sign (7:106). [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:47/q16/p4 It is specifically the hand. — Wāḥidī and Abū Ḥayyān record this alternative; each sign can attest the mission. [3 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:47/q16/p5 It can encompass all of Moses' signs. — Ibn al-Anbārī argues that singular āya may denote a collection, as one word may signify a collection of words. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)]
- 20:47/q16/p6 The hand and staff were displayed on separate occasions. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from Muqātil. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 20:47/q17 Why is 'a sign' singular?  [بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether singularity is generic proof or indefinite emphasis.
- 20:47/q17/p1 It names proof as a kind or collective argument, rather than enumerating miracles. — Al-Qaffāl allows one or several proofs; Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī emphasize substantiating the claim. Ibn al-Anbārī permits a singular collective; Study Quran includes staff and white hand. Parallels cited include 7:105–106 and 26:30, 26:154. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القفال); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Kl); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري)]
- 20:47/q17/p2 It leaves the number unspecified and underscores the sign's greatness and clarity. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the indefinite expression this way. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:47/q18 What did Moses show Pharaoh when asked for the sign?  [بِـَٔايَةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The chronology of displaying the two miracles.
- 20:47/q18/p1 He first showed a radiant hand; Pharaoh saw the staff later at the festival. — Qurṭubī's account has the hand shining like or brighter than the sun and astonishing Pharaoh. Nasafī also reports a hand shining like sunlight. Qurṭubī says the staff was not seen until festival day. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:47/q18/p2 The hand and staff appeared on different occasions, without a stated sequence here. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the account to Muqātil. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 20:47/q19 What force does قد have in the sign clause?  [قَدْ · rhetoric]
- 20:47/q19/p1 It affirms the certainty of their bringing the sign. — Ālūsī treats قد as emphatic certainty and calls an expectation reading strained. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:47/q20 How is 'peace upon whoever follows guidance' understood?  [وَٱلسَّلَٰمُ عَلَىٰ مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether salām is safety, an earthly salutation, a future angelic salutation, or an inward state.
- 20:47/q20/p1 It promises safety from God's displeasure and punishment, in this life and the next, to those who follow guidance. — Zajjāj rejects a greeting because this is not the start of an encounter; Bagawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Nasafī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr agree. Wāḥidī allows ʿAṭāʾ's blessing report to mean this or greeting. Rāzī infers safety for the knower of God who follows guidance. Ālūsī and al-Baḥr argue safety in both lives already gives encouragement and warning alongside 20:48. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds honor and says no addressee is named; his comparison is the Heraclius letter's 'accept Islam and be safe.' Study Quran adds peace from God, angels, and believers. [30 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات); ALUSI-FULL(البحر); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 20:47/q20/p2 It is a closing or farewell greeting intended to invite Pharaoh toward guidance. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's reported group separates it from the preceding speech and says it reproaches Pharaoh. Rāzī reports God's order to say it; Samīn gives both syntactic readings. Bursevī says the definite article gives general peace and the greeting implies safety in both abodes. Abū Ḥayyān treats it as a closing salutation, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī as a farewell-like summary. Iṣlāḥī connects its sincerity and subtle warning to 20:44. Ibn Kathīr explicitly addresses the conditional peace to Pharaoh. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(بعضهم); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:47/q20/p3 It is the angels' and Paradise keepers' greeting to the guided. — Zamakhsharī contrasts Paradise's greeting with the Fire keepers' rebuke, an implied promise of the Garden matching the exhortation and 20:48. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī record this alternative. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشرى); ALUSI-FULL(البحر)-]
- 20:47/q20/p4 Inwardly, peace is freedom from defects and attachments and influx of spiritual light. — Qāshānī reads the promise as a spiritual state. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:47/q21 Who is the peace directed to?  [عَلَىٰ مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the saying offers a present conditional invitation or describes a future greeting.
- 20:47/q21/p1 It applies generally to anyone following guidance, with Pharaoh included if he accepts. — Biqāʿī says the repeated address reproaches Pharaoh, then broadens the promise to everyone; refusal exposes him to destruction. Ibn Kathīr addresses the possibility directly to Pharaoh. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the indirect promise invites him. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says avoiding direct address undercuts Pharaoh's self-importance. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:47/q21/p2 It is especially the guided recipients of an angelic greeting in Paradise. — Zamakhsharī identifies angels and Paradise keepers as speakers; others report this reading. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشرى)]

## 20:47/q22 What does following guidance entail?  [ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the expression describes outward faith and obedience or its inward enabling condition.
- 20:47/q22/p1 It means believing in God, submitting to His message and affirming His signs in obedience. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī specify faith in God; Thaʿlabī says submission. Bursevī says affirming the signs brought by prophets, whether bodily following or conforming obedience, comparing 2:38. [7 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); BURSEVI(التأويلات); STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 20:47/q22/p2 Inwardly, it requires prior divine guidance and an illuminated heart that follows proof and divine light. — Al-Wāsiṭī says God first guides the follower in every state. Qushayrī says gnosis illumines the receptive heart while ignorance blocks hearing. Qāshānī describes adherence to proof and divine light. [3 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QUSHAYRI; QASHANI]

## 20:47/q23 How does على function with السلام?  [وَٱلسَّلَٰمُ عَلَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether على must be read as equivalent to لام.
- 20:47/q23/p1 It may carry the sense of لِـ, so peace or safety is for the guided. — Rāzī cites 13:25, 41:46, 17:7 for comparable usage; Qurṭubī reports Farrāʾ. Bursevī compares 40:52. Ālūsī says prepositions can exchange functions and notes stylistic correspondence with العذاب على من كذب in 20:48. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:47/q23/p2 Its ordinary force already suits established security, so replacing it with لِـ is unnecessary. — Samīn rejects the recourse to lām; Ibn ʿĀshūr says على conveys security settled upon the follower. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:47/q24 How does the peace statement relate to the next warning?  [وَٱلسَّلَٰمُ عَلَىٰ مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · links]
- 20:47/q24/p1 It pairs promise for guidance with threat for denial, encouraging Pharaoh to believe. — Jishumī contrasts safety in both worlds with punishment of those denying the messengers; Qushayrī says prophets promise reward and warn of punishment though deferred details are unknown. Zamakhsharī sees Paradise and Fire guardians; Ālūsī and al-Baḥr say safety versus punishment in both lives already explains the pair. Ibn ʿĀshūr links ʿalā with the next verse, and Iṣlāḥī sees a subtle warning. [13 notes: JISHUMI; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر,الزمخشرى); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:47/q25 How is the formula used when addressing non-Muslims?  [وَٱلسَّلَٰمُ عَلَىٰ مَنِ ٱتَّبَعَ ٱلْهُدَىٰٓ · law]
- 20:47/q25/p1 It serves as a conditional greeting in speech or writing to non-Muslims. — Yahyā reports its use in letters to polytheists. Ibn Kathīr recounts the Prophet's letter to Heraclius, followed by an invitation to Islam, safety and doubled reward; he also recounts Muhammad's answer to Musaylimah's claim of shared prophethood and land, citing 7:128. Ibn ʿAbbās transmits the Heraclius letter in Durr. Qatāda prescribes the formula on entering homes of People of the Book. Abū Ḥayyān concludes ordinary greetings to unbelievers should be avoided; Ālūsī cites both the letter and Qatāda. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة)]

## 20:47/q26 What spiritual mission is read in the release command?  [فَأَرْسِلْ مَعَنَا بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · inward]
- 20:47/q26/p1 The soul should cease enslaving its animal powers and release them toward divine presence and true knowledge. — Qāshānī contrasts holy light with sensory pleasure and worldly adornment. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:47/q27 What wider purpose did Moses' mission serve?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether political emancipation exhausts the prophetic mission.
- 20:47/q27/p1 It joined faith in God to ending oppression and rescuing Israel; Egypt's ruler was also called to monotheism. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Moses' primary mission was to establish Israel as an independent nation with reforming law, rather than legislate for Egyptians; he still summoned Pharaoh's people to monotheism to oppose wrong and secure release, citing 17:104 and 79:17. [5 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 20:47/q27/p2 It was merely a nationalist campaign for freedom. [0 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 20:47/q28 How does the message balance gentleness and firmness?  [* · rhetoric]
- 20:47/q28/p1 The message begins with gentle invitation yet speaks firmly against tyranny and Pharaoh's false power. — Rāzī lists four forceful elements: a king must submit, surrender the labor on which his power rests, end abuse, and accept the sign; he says gentleness comes first, then firmness when obstinacy appears. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says truthful directness avoids both harshness and flattery. Iṣlāḥī connects sincere sympathy to the gentle-speech instruction in 20:44. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:47/q29 What does God's gentleness toward Pharaoh imply?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:47/q29/p1 If God treats a claimant to divinity gently, His gentleness toward affirming, repentant seekers is greater. — Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh makes the comparison; Abū al-Qāsim al-Ḥusayn extends it from opponents, idolaters, sinners and the proud to lovers, confessors, repenters and seekers of forgiveness. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو القاسم الحسين,يحيى بن معاذ الرازي)]

## 20:47/q30 What does Israel's long suffering show about divine punishment?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:47/q30/p1 God may grant an oppressor time before painfully seizing him, then rescue those he oppressed. — Qushayrī draws this pattern from Israel's prolonged suffering under Pharaoh and eventual rescue. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:47/q31 What immediate punishments may fall on a person?  [* · inward]
- 20:47/q31/p1 Punishment varies by station and can take the form of a hardened heart, impeded obedience, or loss of wealth and life. — Qushayrī says its kind and degree correspond to what is due. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:47/q32 What happened when Moses and Aaron sought entry to Pharaoh?  [* · reports]
turns on: The differing accounts of their arrival, delay, and admission.
- 20:47/q32/p1 They waited at Pharaoh's door until an obstacle was lifted and he admitted them. — Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn ʿAbbās's ḥadīth of al-Futūn: they waited without permission for a time before a severe barrier was lifted. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:47/q32/p2 They waited at the palace for two years before a court entertainer told Pharaoh. — Ibn Isḥāq says nobody dared inform Pharaoh; Pharaoh summoned them, and Moses identified himself as a messenger Pharaoh recognized. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق بن يسار)]
- 20:47/q32/p3 They came by night after Moses reunited with his family; staff-knocking brought them before Pharaoh. — Al-Suddī says Moses met his mother and Aaron, arranged Aaron's help, knocked with the staff, was called mad by guards, and received Pharaoh's order to admit them. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]

## 20:47/q33 What earlier events are recalled in the report about Moses' preparation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The report recalls different stages of Moses' preparation.
- 20:47/q33/p1 Moses fulfilled ten years, though eight were obligatory. — Ibn ʿAbbās says Moses would not shorten the required eight years and God fulfilled the promised ten. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:47/q33/p2 Fear over the killing and a speech impediment led Moses to ask for Aaron as spokesman. — Durr reports God granting the request and ordering Aaron to meet Moses. [1 notes: DURR-FULL]

## 20:47/q34 How is Isrāʾīl pronounced in the reported variant?  [بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · readings]
- 20:47/q34/p1 The variant handles the hamza between rāʾ and yāʾ differently while keeping the full syllabic length. — Qirāʾāt ER attributes this variant to HzT. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(HzT)]
