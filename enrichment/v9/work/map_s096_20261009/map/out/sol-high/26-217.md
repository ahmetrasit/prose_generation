# 26:217: 12 questions

## 26:217/q01 How is the conjunction before 'trust' read?  [وَتَوَكَّلْ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted consonant in the regional codices and the readers associated with them: fāʾ or wāw.
- 26:217/q01/p1 It is read with initial fāʾ: 'so trust.' — Al-Farisi, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, Ibn ʿAtiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn al-Jazari, and al-Alusi associate it with the Medinan and Syrian codices. The named readers vary in the reports: Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir; some add Abū Jaʿfar and Shayba; Ibn al-Jazari says the two Medinans and Ibn ʿĀmir. Al-Farisi finds both transmitted readings sound. Al-Biqaʿi calls this fāʾ causal; Qiraat-Er renders it 'therefore.' [19 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع وابن عامر); THALABI(أهل المدينة والشام); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر وشيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وابن عامر); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); NASAFI-FULL(مدني وشامي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن عامر); NASHR(المدنيان، وابن عامر); BIQAI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام); ALUSI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر وشيبة); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]
- 26:217/q01/p2 It is read with initial wāw: 'and trust.' — Al-Farisi and the other reading reports assign this to the other readers and their codices; Abū Hayyān calls them the other seven canonical readers, and Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this the majority reading. Al-Farisi judges both readings sound. [13 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع وابن عامر); THALABI(أهل المدينة والشام); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر وشيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الباقون); NASHR(المدنيان، وابن عامر); BIQAI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER]

## 26:217/q02 How does the initial conjunction connect this command to what precedes?  [وَتَوَكَّلْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the fāʾ supplies the conditional consequence or coordinates with an earlier command, while the wāw reading supplies coordination; Ibn ʿĀshūr says both routes require prompt trust.
- 26:217/q02/p1 With fāʾ, trust follows as the consequence of the preceding condition or disavowal. — Jishumi says fāʾ stands in place of the answer to 'if they disobey'; al-Bayḍāwī makes it the conditional consequence. Al-Samīn says it makes the command a consequence. Ibn ʿĀshūr ties it to disavowal in 26:216 and immediate refuge from enemies. Al-Biqaʿi says it more clearly links trust to the previous command; Qiraat-Er calls it causal. Al-Alusi finds this conditional analysis more apparent than coordination. [7 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع); BAYDAWI-FULL(نافع وابن عامر); SAMIN-DURR(نافع وابن عامر); BIQAI-FULL(أهل المدينة والشام); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:217/q02/p2 With wāw, the command coordinates with an earlier command or its conditional answer. — Jishumi links it with 'lower your wing'; al-Samīn calls it coordination of sentences. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to the conditional answer in 26:216, which still requires prompt action; Qiraat-Er says it coordinates with what precedes. [4 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR(الباقون); IBNASHUR-FULL(الجمهور); QIRAAT-ER]
- 26:217/q02/p3 With fāʾ, the command coordinates specifically with 'say' or 'do not invoke.' — Al-Kashshaf gives فقل and فلا تدع as the two possible points of coordination; al-Nasafi repeats them. Al-Alusi reports this analysis from al-Kashshaf but finds the conditional analysis more apparent. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(مدني وشامي); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 26:217/q03 What does reliance on God mean here?  [وَتَوَكَّلْ · meaning]
turns on: The notes emphasize different degrees and acts of reliance: entrusting affairs, inner detachment, conduct toward people and means, surrender of planning, and the heart's fear and hope.
- 26:217/q03/p1 Entrust one's affairs to God, the capable manager who suffices and can bring benefit or harm. — Jishumi defines it as placing a matter with its manager; al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Nasafi, and al-Alusi specify the owner who can benefit or harm. Abū ʿUthmān in al-Sulamī grounds sufficiency in God's agency over dependent creation and His own eternal self-sufficiency. Ibn ʿĀshūr says He suffices needs and refers back to 3:159. Al-Wahidi's al-Kalbī, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, and Abū Hayyān speak of entrusting affairs; Ibn Kathir and Ibn ʿAtiyya say every affair. [14 notes: MUQATIL; SULAMI(أبو عثمان); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وابن عامر وأبو جعفر وشيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:217/q03/p2 Turn wholly to God, away from others and from attachment to created things. — Ibn Isḥāq calls it contentment with God instead of people. Al-Junayd, quoted by al-Sulamī and al-Nasafi, grounds this in need for God in both abodes; he also names severed desires and expectations as its sign. Abū Yazīd says one finds reliance when truly seeking it and describes life without attachment. Bursevi calls wholehearted turning a mark of the perfected and says to leave reliance on others. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن اسحاق); SULAMI(أبو يزيد,الجنيد); NASAFI-FULL(الجنيد); BURSEVI]
- 26:217/q03/p3 Do not burden, complain to, or blame people; see giving and withholding as God's acts. — Al-Nahrajūrī in al-Sulamī says the truly reliant person makes no demand of creation, does not complain of hardship, and does not blame a person who withholds. [1 notes: SULAMI(النهرجوري)]
- 26:217/q03/p4 Surrender one's own planning to God's planning and accept His direction. — Abū Yazīd gives this definition in al-Sulamī. [1 notes: SULAMI(أبو يزيد)]
- 26:217/q03/p5 A reliant person does not repel a crisis by disobeying God; permissible help from people remains possible. — Al-Kashshaf reports the definition and says seeking another person's help does not violate reliance if no sin is used; al-Nasafi and al-Alusi transmit the refusal of sinful self-help. Some cited by al-Alusi call this only a low, limited rank of reliance. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 26:217/q03/p6 Reliance has ascending ranks: using means, leaving means, then resting in God's prior apportionment without anxious calculation or expected compensation. — Some gnostics cited by al-Alusi distinguish these three ranks. Al-Ṭībī says the verse's wording subtly signals them. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(العلامة الطيبي,بعض العارفين)]
- 26:217/q03/p7 Place all fear and hope in God; reliance of the heart increases with faith. — Study Quran calls trust central to Islamic spirituality and cites 4:81, 5:23, 8:61, 25:28, and especially 14:11–12 among the many injunctions to rely on God. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 26:217/q04 What does this command ask the Prophet to entrust to God in its context?  [وَتَوَكَّلْ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources stress different objects of trust in the setting: protection from opponents, all affairs, or the response to prophetic warning.
- 26:217/q04/p1 Rely on God for safety, help, and victory against disobedient people and enemies. — Al-Thaʿlabi and al-Baghawi mention enemies' schemes; al-Kashshaf includes harm from the Prophet's people or others. Al-Ṭabrisī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi, Elmalılı, and Ibn ʿĀshūr link divine might and mercy to defeating opponents and protecting him. Ibn Kathir adds support, victory, and making his word prevail; Bursevi says God alone suffices against enemies. Ibn ʿĀshūr invokes God's sufficiency in 65:3 against hostile kin. Islahi sees hardship after acquittal and loss of family allies; Kur'an Yolu says leaders must disregard the hostility caused by disowning rebellious relatives. Al-Biqaʿi adds deliverance and turning those warned toward obedience. [19 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:217/q04/p2 Entrust every affair or state to God, whose care will suffice and support the Prophet. — Al-Wahidi's al-Kalbī says all affairs; Ibn ʿAtiyya, Ibn Kathir, and Bursevi say every matter or state. Ibn ʿAtiyya says the names reassure him and recall God's care for each prophet. Al-Qurtubi says God does not abandon His allies; Bursevi says He never humiliates those loyal to Him and grants victory to those who rely on Him. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 26:217/q04/p3 Entrust the outcome of the call to God; the Prophet is responsible for conveying it, not controlling obedience. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God will punish rebels and save faithful followers. Al-Biqaʿi includes hope that those warned will turn to obedience; Kur'an Yolu says verses 213–217 direct believers to convey religion first to their closest people. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:217/q05 Who is the command addressed to through the Prophet?  [وَتَوَكَّلْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the interpretation stays with the Prophet's immediate situation or extends its directive through him to all believers.
- 26:217/q05/p1 The Prophet is directly commanded to trust God. — The sources apply the command to Muḥammad amid opposition, hardship, and uncertainty after disavowal; Ibn ʿAtiyya says God orders Muḥammad to rely on Him in every matter. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:217/q05/p2 All believers are addressed through the Prophet and should begin conveying religion to those nearest them. — Kur'an Yolu extends verses 213–217 to each believer and says leaders should rely on God's power and mercy when estrangement from rebellious relatives brings hostility. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:217/q06 What does 'the Mighty' contribute to the command?  [ٱلْعَزِيزِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the name is explained by its effect on hostile people or by God's unconquerable power as the basis for trust.
- 26:217/q06/p1 God can overpower, punish, or take vengeance on the Prophet's enemies and thus protect him. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī's Muqātil, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzi stress punishment or retribution. Al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafi, al-Alusi, Elmalılı, and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress overpowering enemies. Al-Biqaʿi also says He can protect and avenge; Bursevi says He can overpower enemies. Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes punishment of rebels. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:217/q06/p2 God cannot be overcome; none can stop what He wills, so He is worthy of trust. — Al-Qurtubi describes Him as unconquerable; Abū Hayyān says the same. Islahi applies irresistible power to coming hardship and servants who leave relatives for Him. Al-Alusi says might is the ground validating reliance. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:217/q07 What does 'the Merciful' contribute to the command?  [ٱلرَّحِيمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whom mercy is directed toward here—faithful allies, disobedient people spared immediate punishment, or all people hoped to be reconciled—and how it motivates trust.
- 26:217/q07/p1 God aids and protects the Prophet and His faithful allies through mercy. — Al-Ṭabrisī says He cares for allies; al-Kashshaf, al-Razi, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafi, Abū Hayyān, al-Alusi, and Elmalılı say He helps the Prophet against enemies. Al-Qurtubi says He does not abandon allies; Bursevi says He helps friends. Ibn ʿĀshūr says mercy protects him. Islahi expects aid for those who forsake relatives for God; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says He saves faithful followers. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:217/q07/p2 God mercifully delays punishment of the disobedient or accepts their repentance. — Muqātil, repeated by al-Wāḥidī, says He does not hasten punishment for them; Ibn al-Jawzi likewise says He delays it. Al-Ṭabarī points to mercy toward those who repent and links the attribute to 1:1. [4 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 26:217/q07/p3 God's mercy gives hope that discord will end and all will be honored through obedience. — Al-Biqaʿi hopes God will end dissension and put everyone to work in what pleases Him. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:217/q07/p4 Mercy draws one to reliance on God. — Al-Alusi contrasts might as the ground validating trust with mercy as the motive attracting one to it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:217/q08 Why is might mentioned before mercy?  [ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the order responds chiefly to the Prophet's hardships or sets out power as warrant and mercy as attraction.
- 26:217/q08/p1 Might comes first because it consoles the Prophet amid coming hardships. — Al-Alusi reports this explanation from an unnamed source. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 26:217/q08/p2 Might first establishes the ground for relying on God; mercy then draws one toward reliance. — Al-Alusi gives this as another explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:217/q09 What situation surrounds the command?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes describe either an invitation to ancestral religion or the social aftermath of the Prophet's disavowal.
- 26:217/q09/p1 Meccan unbelievers invited the Prophet to follow his forefathers' religion. — Muqātil says the Meccan unbelievers spoke of this invitation and God knew it. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 26:217/q09/p2 Disavowing rebellious kin creates an unknown, hard phase and turns friends into foes. — Islahi describes the life after acquittal as uncertain and arduous; Kur'an Yolu says disowning rebellious relatives can cool or sour family relations. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:217/q10 How do the divine names connect this command to the earlier prophetic stories?  [ٱلْعَزِيزِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ · links]
- 26:217/q10/p1 They recall the stories' repeated ending: God punishes disobedient nations and cares for or saves His prophets and faithful followers. — Ibn ʿAtiyya says the names recall the endings of the nations' stories and God's care for each prophet; Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees a recurring pattern of punishment and rescue. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 26:217/q11 How do the descriptions following this command support reliance?  [* · links]
- 26:217/q11/p1 They affirm God's attentive knowledge and sufficiency, reassuring the Prophet that He will secure benefit and avert harm. — Al-Bayḍāwī says awareness of the Prophet's deserving state follows the promise of victory and support, affirming trust. Al-Biqaʿi says encompassing knowledge secures benefit and repels harm. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the names, the next relative clause, and the closing description show God attends to the Prophet's words and intent. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:217/q12 How does this concluding command fit the sūra's opening theme?  [* · links]
- 26:217/q12/p1 It returns to the truth of Qur'anic warnings: punishment signs can come, and prophetic histories warn the stubborn Quraysh. — Islahi connects the closing discourse to the sūra's opening and treats the earlier prophetic histories as signs. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
