# 86:12: 10 questions

## 86:12/q01 What is the earth invoked here?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
- 86:12/q01/p1 The familiar land in which people live and which they continually encounter. — Al-Biqāʿī stresses people's direct, constant experience of their earthly dwelling. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 86:12/q02 What is the earth's ṣadʿ?  [ٱلصَّدْعِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣadʿ names the growth that breaks the soil, a kind of surface cleft made by nature or people, an opening for springs, or graves opening at resurrection; Mujāhid's narrow pass is explicitly distinguished from valleys.
- 86:12/q02/p1 The earth's splitting open to bring forth vegetation. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Ibn Zayd and many exegetes give this explanation. Al-Layth calls the vegetation itself ṣadʿ because it splits the soil; al-Rāzī makes the same naming point. Al-Ṭabarī reports yearly emergence, and ʿAṭāʾ likewise says plants emerge each year. Al-Ṭabarī's Qatāda and al-Durr's Qatāda include fruits; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī add trees, fruits, and rivers. Ibn Zayd connects growth to plowing and 80:26–27. Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Qurṭubī cite 80:26; Elmalılı cites 80:26–28. Al-Wāḥidī says rain and water cause the opening; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says vegetation suits al-rajʿ as rain. Al-Bayḍāwī allows either emerging plants or cracks made by plants and springs. The prophetic report transmitted by al-Durr adds wealth to vegetation. Biqāʿī includes springs and says the growth happens initially and anew. Kur'an Yolu stresses delicate shoots breaking through hard soil. Al-Ālūsī prefers the rain-and-growth pairing because it foreshadows resurrection. [52 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة والفراء والزجاج وابن عباس والمفسرون,الليث); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI(قيل); JISHUMI(ابن عباس وقتادة والضحاك وابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون وأهل اللغة); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الليث); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، سعيد بن جبير، عكرمة، أبو مالك، الضحاك، الحسن، قتادة، السدي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عطاء,قتادة,معاذ بن أنس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(عطية وابن زيد); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI(القمي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 86:12/q02/p2 A split or fissure in the earth generally, including ravines, trenches and other cracks. — Al-Wāḥidī explains the verb ṣadaʿa as cleaving a thing and cites cracks in glass and walls; he relates 30:43 and the Arab expression for dividing sheep. Al-Rāzī also cites 30:43. Al-Mufradāt, as reported by Bursevī, describes a crack in hard bodies such as glass and iron. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ distinguishes cracking from shattering in 59:21, notes headache as a metaphor and figurative separation, and cites 56:19. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report Mujāhid's ravines, valleys, trenches, tilling breaks and cracks as signs for reflection; al-Ālūsī reports the broader list. Elmalılı includes cultivation, valleys, trenches, paths and footprints as varieties of one earth fissure. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 86:12/q02/p3 Roads and paths opened across the earth by people walking. — Al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit this as a report from Mujāhid. Elmalılı includes paths and footprints within his wider reading of earth fissures. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI]
- 86:12/q02/p4 Mountain passes, especially a narrow cleft between two mountains. — Mujāhid compares the cleft with al-Maʾzim in Minā and excludes valleys and steep banks; al-Thaʿlabī reports two barriers separated by an open road near ʿArafāt. Al-Rāzī cites 21:31 for passes and open roads. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this land-feature reading suits the alternate explanation of al-rajʿ. [7 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 86:12/q02/p5 The earth opening over the dead or graves for resurrection. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī report the dead being released, as do Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī. Elmalılı adds graves opening for burial and rising, describing the interval as like a mother's womb. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 86:12/q02/p6 Cultivated soil and its furrows, opened by plowing. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī give plowing as the cause. Ibn Zayd links the fissure to plowing and 80:26–27. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Elmalılı include tilling or cultivation within wider cleft descriptions. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); ELMALILI]
- 86:12/q02/p7 Valleys or their clefts splitting the earth. — Ibn Jurayj, as reported by al-Māwardī, gives valleys. Ibn ʿAbbās, as reported by al-Durr, names the clefts of valleys. Mujāhid's pass report explicitly distinguishes its feature from valleys. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 86:12/q02/p8 Openings through which springs emerge. — Al-Bayḍāwī includes cracks produced by springs alongside plants. Biqāʿī includes springs released with vegetation. Al-Ālūsī reports this view but rejects it because the paired oaths point instead to resurrection. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 86:12/q02/p9 The ground's opening to expose what lies beneath it or bring out provision. — Al-Ṭabarī reports al-Ḥasan's broad description of exposure and ʿIkrima's description of bringing forth provision. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,عكرمة)]

## 86:12/q03 What grammatical form does ṣadʿ have here?  [ٱلصَّدْعِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form names the fissure itself or conveys the earth's being split.
- 86:12/q03/p1 It is a noun for a crack or split, not a verbal noun. — Al-Wāḥidī gives ordinary Arabic usage for a crack in glass or a wall and says the verse uses the noun. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 86:12/q03/p2 It can be a passive verbal noun describing the earth as split by vegetation. — Al-Ālūsī offers this as an alternative to taking ṣadʿ as the thing produced by the earth's splitting. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 86:12/q04 How is ذات الصدع pronounced when stopping?  [ذَاتِ ٱلصَّدْعِ · readings]
- 86:12/q04/p1 Al-Kisāʾī is reported to stop with hāʾ on ذات الصدع, as on ذات الرجع and other ذات phrases. — Ibn al-Jazarī transmits this stopping practice for al-Kisāʾī. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 86:12/q05 What inward meaning is given to the earth's cleaving?  [ٱلْأَرْضِ ٱلصَّدْعِ · inward]
turns on: Whether the inner earth is the body in relation to spirit or a condition of the heart and conduct.
- 86:12/q05/p1 The earth is the body, split when spirit departs at death or when spirit and body join. — Al-Qāshānī gives the two bodily moments; Bursevī repeats them and pairs the body's earth with the spirit's sky and its return in the second creation. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]
- 86:12/q05/p2 The heart turns back in remorse after sin. — Al-Tustarī distinguishes this inward reading from the outward emergence of vegetation. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 86:12/q05/p3 The inward fissuring occurs through conformity in deeds and words. — Al-Tustarī gives this further inward account. [1 notes: TUSTARI]

## 86:12/q06 Why are the returning sky and splitting earth paired?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes emphasize either generative complementarity, the analogy between growth and resurrection, or the contrast between changing oath signs and enduring speech.
- 86:12/q06/p1 Heaven acts from above and earth receives its effects, together producing life. — Al-Rāzī shifts from animal to plant creation and likens sky and earth to father and mother. Biqāʿī likens upper and lower worlds to man and woman: heaven waters earth as a man waters a woman, and vegetation is like a child. Bursevī gives father, mother and child the same roles. Elmalılı says all three readings of earth's crack respond to effects from above; he reports the Sufi fathers-and-mothers analogy, relates rain and vegetation to reproductive fluid and seed with 86:6 and 36:36, and says this pair displays becoming and decay under God's creative, preserving power. Quṭb likens rain, growth, reproductive fluid and an embryo emerging from the womb. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 86:12/q06/p2 Rain returning and plants emerging together make return after death intelligible. — Al-Ālūsī prefers this pairing and says it explains why rain is called al-rajʿ; he weakens other readings of al-rajʿ. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the vegetation reading fits rain as al-rajʿ, while Mujāhid's land-feature reading suits an alternate reading of al-rajʿ. Biqāʿī says the return of plants after decay parallels human return. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the paired oaths fit emergence from graves. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 86:12/q06/p3 The changing sky and earth frame a sworn-to speech grounded in an unchanging reality. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the speech is fixed in the reality of its taʾwīl, which he promises to explain later. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 86:12/q07 What does the earth's splitting show about resurrection?  [* · interpretation]
- 86:12/q07/p1 Life emerging from split earth shows God's power to restore people after death. — Biqāʿī says new and renewed growth, including springs, points visibly to resurrection, and that restoring people is no different from restoring decayed plants. Bursevī calls plant emergence a sign as the earth opens. Al-Ālūsī prefers the rain-and-growth explanation because it foreshadows resurrection. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the paired images to return to God and opening graves. Asad sees creative and re-creative power; Study Quran relates seasonal growth to humans emerging from base fluid in 86:6–8. Elmalılı includes graves opening for burial and rising. Al-Iṣlāḥī says the physical phenomenon makes the threatened Judgment certain and calls for preparation. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 86:12/q08 What do these earthly and heavenly signs show about God?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the sign is described broadly as creative power and wisdom or through Quṭb's specific parallel with the star and exposed secrets.
- 86:12/q08/p1 They display God's creative power, wisdom and ordered making. — Asad says the returning sky and opening earth help one grasp creative and re-creative power. Elmalılı attributes life and its preservation to God and cites 36:36. Quṭb says the shared life and motion disclose a stable order and one maker unlike the things made. Kur'an Yolu sees divine wisdom in soft shoots piercing hard soil and treats the cosmic events as real manifestations of God's power. Bursevī says the repeated oaths display perfect knowledge and power. [8 notes: ASAD-NOTES; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI]
- 86:12/q08/p2 The same divine craft joins the earth's splitting to the piercing star and the exposure of secrets. — Quṭb links the fissure with the piercing star of 86:3 and uncovered secrets of 86:9, inferring one maker from the repeated penetrating imagery. [1 notes: QUTB-ZILAL]

## 86:12/q09 What is the rhetorical function of the oath by earth?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the note identifies the oath's structure, its assurance of Judgment and Qur'anic truth, or the abundance of benefits and divine attributes conveyed by repetition.
- 86:12/q09/p1 It is a second oath alongside the oath by heaven. — Al-Qurṭubī identifies heaven as one oath and earth as a second; al-Māwardī says the earth readings he discusses are oaths. Al-Ṭabrisī says this further oath reinforces resurrection. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 86:12/q09/p2 The cosmic oaths make the promised judgment and the Qur'an's truth emphatic. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says 86:11–14 use a physical phenomenon to establish that Judgment is certain, not jest. Quṭb says a categorical truth answers residual doubt, links the cosmic scene to the sūra's opening, and hears force and decisiveness in the expression's sound. Biqāʿī says the decisive signs and the Qur'an's eloquence make attack on it implausible. Kur'an Yolu says the signs are real and so is the Qur'an, which manifests God's speech. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 86:12/q09/p3 Repeated oaths call attention to the signs' abundant benefits and God's knowledge and power. — Bursevī contrasts the first oath by sky with the later oaths by sky and earth and their descriptions. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 86:12/q10 How does the mountain's splitting in 59:21 illuminate this image?  [* · links]
- 86:12/q10/p1 It portrays lifeless rock as fearing God and becoming humble, using human inward responses as a rhetorical image. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes that fear and humility properly belong to the heart; her lexical discussion distinguishes cracking from shattering and mentions figurative rupture. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
