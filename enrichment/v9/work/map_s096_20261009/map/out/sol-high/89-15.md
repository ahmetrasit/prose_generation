# 89:15: 24 questions

## 89:15/q01 How does the passage connect to its context?  [فَأَمَّا · links]
turns on: Its point of connection may be God's watch, earlier punished peoples, or a prior proof.
- 89:15/q01/p1 God watches for final recompense while people pursue worldly pleasure. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursevī and al-Ālūsī connect it to 89:14. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the fāʾ from universal divine watch and wisdom. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn object to al-Zamakhsharī's exclusive account of divine will. [21 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; KASHSHAF+; RAZI+; ABUHAYYAN-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 89:15/q01/p2 The earlier nations' luxury and punishment warn arrogant Meccans against trusting prosperity. — Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr link the prior nations to Quraysh's denial of warning and resurrection; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 23:55–56. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees the sura's contrasts preparing the opposed outcomes. [10 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:15/q01/p3 It contrasts proof of truth in verse 5 with ordinary fixation on immediate gain. — Asad reports this link from al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari, Razi, Baydawi)]
- 89:15/q01/p4 Human pride in wealth and despair in poverty explain why punishment follows. — Al-Ālūsī reports and dismisses this proposed connection. [0 notes: ALUSI(قيل)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 89:15/q02 What work does ammā do?  [أَمَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Paired contrast and conditional linkage are the two emphases.
- 89:15/q02/p1 Its conditional force calls for the fāʾ in the predicate. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī explain the fāʾ; al-Biqāʿī says the time phrase emphasizes the onset of trial. [6 notes: BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI]
- 89:15/q02/p2 It sets up a paired contrast of prosperity and restriction and strengthens each response's link to the discourse. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says ammā has a 'whatever may happen' force, normally pairs contrasts rather than unpacks a summary, and compares 2:26. [4 notes: IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:15/q04 How do the time and saying clauses parse?  [إِذَا مَا فَيَقُولُ · grammar]
turns on: Which clause predicates the subject and what governs the fronted time phrase.
- 89:15/q04/p1 Fa-yaqūlu predicates al-insān, idhā modifies it despite being fronted, and the second clause has an implicit subject. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī support this; al-Shihāb calls it correct. Abū Ḥayyān says the fāʾ does not bar the temporal attachment. [15 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(الزمخشري وأبو حيان والسمين والسفاقسي); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+; SAMIN-DURR+; ALUSI(الجلبي,الرضي ومن تبعه)-; ALUSI-FULL(الجلبي,الرضي)-]
- 89:15/q04/p2 Idhā is a condition, yaqūlu its answer, and the whole condition predicates al-insān. — Abū al-Baqāʾ is reported by al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī. Al-Samīn objects that ammā's required fāʾ is lost except after omitted speech or in necessity; he cites 3:106. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:15/q04/p3 The time phrase belongs to an implied 'state of the human'. — Al-Jalabī proposes this after claiming consensus against the ordinary parse; al-Ālūsī says its saying predicate becomes strained. [2 notes: ALUSI(الجلبي); ALUSI-FULL(الجلبي); ALUSI-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 89:15/q04/p4 A phrase can precede the fāʾ only if it is the sole separator after ammā. — Al-Raḍī, al-Damāmīnī and followers disallow an additional separator. [2 notes: ALUSI(الرضي ومن تبعه); ALUSI-FULL(الرضي)]

## 89:15/q06 What does ibtalāhu mean?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ · meaning]
- 89:15/q06/p1 God tested or tried him, including by a favorable gift. — Al-Zajjāj, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn ʿĀshūr use 'test'; al-Sāmarrāʾī says ابتلى is stronger than بلا and balāʾ need not be harmful. ## 89:15/q07 What is accomplished by testing through fortune?  [ٱبْتَلَىٰهُ رَبُّهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Moral trial, manifestation of known character, or wise governance.
- 89:15/q07/p1 Abundance tests gratitude and generosity, while restriction tests patience and contentment. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite 21:35; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds 27:40. Qāshānī invokes faith's halves of patience and gratitude. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ and Iṣlāḥī add others' rights and 89:28. [39 notes: TUSTARI; THALABI; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BAGHAWI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI; BINTSHATI+]
- 89:15/q07/p2 It brings out known good or evil and establishes proof and accountability. — Al-Jishumī lists competing calls of reason, law, guidance and Satan; al-Burhān says righteous and wicked are tested to establish proof; Elmalılı connects duties with reckoning. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN(أبو العباس); ELMALILI; BIQAI]
- 89:15/q07/p3 Allotments follow God's wisdom, welfare and varied causes, not an observable simple rule. — Al-Ṭabrisī stresses wisdom; Ibn ʿĀshūr describes ordinary and extraordinary causes, repeated tests (9:126), and the need for caution about hidden intentions. [11 notes: TABRISI; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:15/q08 Why is there a fāʾ before 'honored him'?  [فَأَكْرَمَهُۥ · rhetoric]
turns on: Explanatory identity versus causal sequence.
- 89:15/q08/p1 It explains what the prosperous test consists of; honor and ease are one combined gift. — Abū al-Suʿūd and al-Ālūsī take the fāʾ as explanatory; al-Ālūsī says the human compresses the two gifts into one statement. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebussuud); ALUSI+; ELMALILI-]
- 89:15/q08/p2 It is causal: honor and blessing flow through a test with duties. — Elmalılı rejects the explanatory analysis and distinguishes grant from trial. [1 notes: ELMALILI+; ELMALILI(Ebussuud)-]

## 89:15/q09 What does 'He honored him' mean here?  [أَكْرَمَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Worldly benefit versus proof of approved rank.
- 89:15/q09/p1 He received money, status, social respect, or another worldly good. — Al-Wāḥidī's specialists distinguish this from the opposite of humiliation; al-Biqāʿī mentions social esteem. Ibn ʿĀshūr uses al-Rāghib's distinction between giving an unblemished benefit and making noble. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ calls ikrām generosity. [14 notes: TAB; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI; QURTUBI; BAYDAWI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR(الراغب); BINTSHATI; JISHUMI; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 89:15/q09/p2 The gift is genuine generosity in a limited sense, without proving merit before God. — Qatāda allows the claim in this sense; al-Zamakhsharī distinguishes a gift from earned rank. Al-Rāzī notes that God calls the first state honor but never calls poverty humiliation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says informed theologians agree unbelievers receive worldly blessings. [12 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR(المحققون من المتكلمين); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 89:15/q11 Why pair comfort with restricted provision instead of pairing honor with humiliation?  [فَأَكْرَمَهُۥ وَنَعَّمَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 89:15/q11/p1 Withholding a gift is not an insult and poor righteous people are not disgraced. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares a patron who neither gives nor insults. Abū Ḥayyān cites Dāwūd al-Iṣfahānī. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this audience generally retained bodily health and cites al-Nābigha about death by killing or age. [7 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; IBNASHUR(النابغة); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 89:15/q11/p2 The first clause names an actual gift, while the second only names reduced provision. — Al-Rāzī and Qatāda observe the text affirms honor in the first case but not humiliation in the second. [5 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); RAZI; RAZI-FULL]

## 89:15/q12 How is the human's speech presented?  [فَيَقُولُ رَبِّىٓ أَكْرَمَنِ · rhetoric]
- 89:15/q12/p1 As a recurring, literal and confident boast, often without gratitude. — Al-Qurṭubī notes joy without thanks; al-Biqāʿī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ note pride. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the imperfect repeats, the speech is literal (compare 3:75), and fronted rabbī strengthens certainty. [9 notes: QURTUBI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 89:15/q12/p2 The fāʾ expresses the result of each test. — Al-Bursevī balances the two clauses and says the utterance follows receipt of the gift. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 89:15/q15 What does the human imply by 'my Lord honored me'?  [رَبِّىٓ أَكْرَمَنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Self-meriting approval versus acknowledgment of a limited gift.
- 89:15/q15/p1 He assumes wealth proves deserved, lasting approval, entitlement or superiority. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī invoke Qārūn (28:78). Al-Rāzī says he ignores prior gifts of health, reason and religion, like the garden owner in 18:35–37. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Ṭarafa's boast and the chiefs demanding expulsion of poor believers (6:52); al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says the person imagines license to act at will. [49 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل، ابن عباس); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR(طرفة); IBNASHUR-FULL(طرفة); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; THALABI(قتادة)]
- 89:15/q15/p2 He recognizes a real benefit, which alone does not reveal divine approval. — Qatāda allows the limited claim; al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguish receiving a gift from having approved rank. [13 notes: TAB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 89:15/q16 What is real honor or disgrace?  [أَكْرَمَهُۥ أَكْرَمَنِ · theology]
turns on: Obedience, afterlife result, or unknowable worldly purpose.
- 89:15/q16/p1 Honor follows obedience, piety, faith and good action; disgrace follows sin or abandonment. — Mujāhid, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Baghawī and others make the contrast. Al-Nasafī calls honor enablement to obey. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports al-Ṭabarī's preference; the Study Quran cites a ḥadīth qudsī and 49:13. [16 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; DURR(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(God in a ḥadīth qudsī); KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; BINTSHATI(الإمام الطبري)+]
- 89:15/q16/p2 Final standing is known in the Hereafter, not from present fortune. — Al-Rāzī argues pleasure ending in damnation is no honor and hardship ending in salvation no disgrace; wealth can impede worship and make death painful. He says this reasoning presupposes resurrection, yet even without it wealth may invite murder or robbery and lack may preserve safety. Al-Jishumī locates recompense at resurrection. [47 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(بعض المفسرين,بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:15/q16/p3 No automatic worldly sign is reliable because God's purposes and causes differ. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 6:44, 7:94–95 and 9:126 for testing and punishment, yet 16:97, 71:10–12 and 72:16 for temporal reward; extraordinary victories can signal special treatment. Al-Nābigha counsels awareness of changing outcomes and Ibn al-Rāwandī's couplet observes reversed fortunes. [26 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR(النابغة); IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة); BINTSHATI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN]

## 89:15/q17 What does the following 'No!' reject?  [رَبِّىٓ أَكْرَمَنِ · interpretation]
turns on: Both inferences, only the poverty inference, or the claim of earned favor.
- 89:15/q17/p1 It rejects treating either prosperity or poverty as proof of divine esteem or contempt. — Al-Ḥasan rejects both; the failure to honor orphans reveals moral fault. Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguish real gifts from claims of rank; Ibn ʿĀshūr directs people to revelation, law and sound reasoning. [69 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; DURR(الحسن); DURR-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR; BURHAN(أبو العباس); IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(ابن القيم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(God in a ḥadīth qudsī); BINTSHATI+]
- 89:15/q17/p2 It rejects the boast that wealth was earned by status, ancestry or merit. — Al-Zamakhsharī invokes 28:78, al-Nasafī compares Qārūn, and al-Rāzī notes ingratitude for health, reason and religion. [12 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ASAD-NOTES(Razi)]
- 89:15/q17/p3 It rejects only treating a withheld favor as humiliation. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī emphasize that absence of generosity is not insult; al-Rāzī notes the text affirms the first honor but no humiliation of the poor. [11 notes: KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 89:15/q17/p4 It rejects gratitude only in plenty and complaint during want. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports the view that both conditions call for thanks. [1 notes: BINTSHATI(بعضهم)]

## 89:15/q18 How should people respond to both conditions?  [* · interpretation]
- 89:15/q18/p1 Thank God and share in prosperity; remain patient and content under restriction. — Al-Farrāʾ says praise is due in either state; Qāshānī calls for orphans and the poor, citing the saying that faith divides between patience and gratitude. Iṣlāḥī and Kur'an Yolu stress rights of others and solidarity; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ relates these duties to the following verses. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI(الحسن); THALABI(الفراء); QURTUBI; QASHANI; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(بعضهم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR]
- 89:15/q18/p2 Remember nonmaterial blessings and worldly risks before judging one's lot. — Al-Rāzī names safety, reason, faith and protection from harm; wealth may distract, deepen attachment and worsen separation at death, while poverty may lead to worship. [24 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BINTSHATI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 89:15/q19 What occasions or incidents are reported?  [* · reports]
turns on: The specific names and circumstances vary.
- 89:15/q19/p1 The occasion names ʿUtba and Abū Ḥudhayfa, or ʿUtba alone. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited through ʿAṭāʾ for the pair; al-Suhaylī names ʿUtba alone. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(السهيلي); IBNASHUR(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI(ابن عباس)]

## 89:15/q20 Which readings of neighboring words are mentioned?  [* · readings]
- 89:15/q20/p1 Qadara is light for most readers, doubled for Abū Jaʿfar and Ibn ʿĀmir. — Al-Thaʿlabī accepts both forms; Abū ʿAmr distinguishes restriction from sufficiency, while al-Baghawī treats both as restriction. Al-Nasafī names Syrian readers and Yazīd. [7 notes: THALABI(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 89:15/q20/p2 Subsequent verbs vary between third person and direct address, with several taḥāḍḍūna forms. — Al-Thaʿlabī names Baṣrans for third person; Abū Jaʿfar and Kūfans add an alif, al-Kisāʾī has a rare ḍamma, and others lack alif. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو جعفر وأهل الكوفة,أهل البصرة)]

## 89:15/q21 How do later verses develop the lesson?  [* · links]
- 89:15/q21/p1 They expose neglect of orphans, the poor and inheritance rights. — Al-Ḥasan points to 89:17 as the true rebuke. Al-Thaʿlabī records consuming one's own and others' inheritance, withholding women's and children's shares (4:127), and Abū ʿUbayda's idiom for gathering and eating all. [15 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); THALABI(أبو عبيدة,ابن زيد,الحسن,بكر بن عبد الله); JISHUMI; DURR(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:15/q21/p2 They lead from present testing to Judgment and the tranquil soul. — Iṣlāḥī links a successful trial to 89:28; Ibn ʿĀshūr says wealth cannot help at Judgment. Corpus Coranicum pairs worldly speech with the response to Hell and God's final address. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:15/q22 What is reported about the sura and verse division?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Regional verse counts and the disputed subdivision of these long verses.
- 89:15/q22/p1 Al-Fajr is Meccan and tenth in revelation; regional verse totals vary. — Ibn ʿĀshūr notes al-Dānī's minority Medinan report, places it after al-Layl and before al-Ḍuḥā, and gives Medina and Mecca 32, Kūfa and Syria 30, Baṣra 29. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:15/q22/p2 Dividing verses 15 and 16 in two has been proposed but the rhyme evidence is inconclusive. — Corpus Coranicum reports Neuwirth's length and cross-rhyme argument, compares 101:6–9, and questions the 69:19/25 parallel for unusual vowel equivalence and distance. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:15/q23 How do sound and arrangement shape the passage?  [* · rhetoric]
- 89:15/q23/p1 The endings rhyme loosely, and the first clause uses fāʾ where the second uses wāw. — Corpus Coranicum compares the approximate rhyme with verses 23–24 and 29–30. Al-Rāzī says mercy and more frequent blessings precede pain, citing 14:34. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; RAZI; RAZI-FULL]
- 89:15/q23/p2 A proposed insertion pairs human speech in this life and at Hell with God's final address. — Corpus Coranicum calls 15–16 later-added, notes the ʾakrama–ʾahāna opposition and echo of orphan-honoring in 17, and compares speech in 23b–24 and 27–30. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:15/q23/p3 Natural contrasts prepare for the outcomes of tyrants and the tranquil soul. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees the sura's contrasts preparing trials of wealth and deprivation. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 89:15/q24 Which wider linguistic comparisons appear with the verse?  [* · grammar]
- 89:15/q24/p1 Sībawayh contrasts final yāʾ and alif in pause and liaison. — He cites قاضي, غلامي, vocatives, إني and Labīd's exceptional metrical alif loss. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 89:15/q24/p2 Ibn al-Jawzī catalogues conditional, mā, laqad and idh senses of in. — He cites 3:31, 4:43, 49:6 for the condition; 21:17 and others for mā; five examples for laqad, though he separates 87:9; and 2:278, 3:139, 9:13 for idh. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 89:15/q24/p3 Al-Burhān discusses oaths, genuine questions, repeated inquiries and commands after questions. — It rejects al-Zamakhsharī's oath reading of an appeal, cites Ibn Fāris on istikhbār and istifhām, requires uncertainty for a question, and applies a pending command to whoever the answer names. [5 notes: BURHAN(ابن فارس,الزمخشري)]
- 89:15/q24/p4 Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ sets aside other interpretations as having little value. — The note gives no detail about those interpretations. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
