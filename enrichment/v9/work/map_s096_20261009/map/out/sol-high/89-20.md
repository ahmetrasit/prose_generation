# 89:20: 14 questions

## 89:20/q01 How is تحبون read, and what does each person form do?  [وَتُحِبُّونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the preceding generic human remains under third-person description or the discourse turns to confront its addressees; transmitted reader attributions also vary.
- 89:20/q01/p1 Second person, “you love,” directly addresses the offenders. — Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are named by al-Fārisī; other accounts call this the reading of the majority or of all except the Basrans. Al-Fārisī construes it as 'tell them this'; al-Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr see a shift from generic 'the human' to direct confrontation. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 90:6–7. Al-Nashr excepts al-Zubayrī from Rawḥ from the Basran group. Al-Durr also reports ʿĀṣim's tāʾ in the surrounding clauses, with a lengthened unhamzated taḥāḍḍūn, tāʾ in turāth and geminated lamman. Ibn Khālawayh notes the adjacent Kūfan alif in تحاضون. [19 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAGHAWI(أهل البصرة); KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI(أبو عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(البصري); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; DURR(عاصم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); QIRAAT-ER]
- 89:20/q01/p2 Third person, “they love,” continues the description of humanity or exposes the offenders to the hearers. — Al-Fārisī says Abū ʿAmr keeps agreement with the earlier generic, collectively plural al-insān; he invokes 7:4 to show agreement may follow form or meaning. Ibn Khālawayh also cites continuity with context. Reports name Abū ʿAmr, Yaʿqūb, Sahl, al-Ḥasan, or the Basrans, with differing scope; al-Nashr excepts al-Zubayrī from Rawḥ. Abū Hurayra and Ibn ʿUmar report the Prophet reading the four clauses with yāʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this exposes offenders to the Prophet and believers. Qirāʾāt-ER says only the tu-/yu- prefix changes, preserving form and object; Study Quran accepts either reading. [21 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); BAGHAWI(أهل البصرة); KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI(أبو عمرو); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI(البصري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR; DURR(النبي); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عمر); ALUSI(أبو عمرو والحسن ومن معه); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); QIRAAT-ER]

## 89:20/q02 What does the verb تحبون itself mean?  [وَتُحِبُّونَ · meaning]
- 89:20/q02/p1 It means to love; ḥabbahu and aḥabbahu have equivalent senses. — Al-Nasafī explicitly equates the two Arabic verb forms, and his fuller account says 'love' and 'love greatly' are equivalent in sense. [2 notes: NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL]

## 89:20/q03 What wealth does المال include here?  [ٱلْمَالَ · referent]
turns on: Whether المال is material wealth in all its forms or a symbolic object of lower desire.
- 89:20/q03/p1 Wealth of every kind, including wealth acquired lawfully or unlawfully. — Al-Biqāʿī expressly includes every kind. Al-Qurṭubī says lawful or unlawful wealth, and Bint al-Shāṭiʾī approves this in light of indiscriminate consumption of inheritance in 89:19. Al-Ḥasan says excess love brings lawful and unlawful holdings together. [7 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; MAWARDI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(القرطبي)+]
- 89:20/q03/p2 In an inward reading, the wealth signifies many evil deeds and corrupt states driven by lower desire. — Al-Bursevī offers the symbolic possibility, and reports some masters who make this identification. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 89:20/q04 What does حبًّا جمًّا say about the love of wealth?  [حُبًّۭا جَمًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether جمّا chiefly measures the intensity of love or the amount accumulated, and whether the love manifests as withholding, indiscriminate acquisition, useless storage, or wasteful spending.
- 89:20/q04/p1 It is abundant, great, intense or excessive love. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Ibn Zayd variously say abundant, much, intense, or excessive; Ibn Kathīr reports some calling it immoderate and shameful. Abū ʿUbayda, al-Farrāʾ, al-Kisāʾī and al-Zajjāj gloss loving much and forcefully. Al-Ṭabarī also treats the love as intense. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains plentifulness applied to an abstract emotion as metaphorical intensity. Al-Qurṭubī allows lawful or unlawful wealth. Differences between 'much wealth' and 'much love' are preserved in the next position. [44 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة، الفراء، الكسائي، الزجاج); WAHIDI-QT; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); RAZI; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR(بعضهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الزمخشري,الطبري); ITQAN; IBNASHUR]
- 89:20/q04/p2 They love accumulating or possessing a great quantity of wealth. — Muqātil calls the gathering of abundant wealth a Mālik b. Kināna dialect expression. Al-Ṭabarī and ʿAṭāʾ stress collecting and acquiring; reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk specify plentiful wealth. Al-Baghawī combines eagerness to amass with passionate attachment. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(الضحاك); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الطبري)]
- 89:20/q04/p3 The love is greedy and rapacious and withholds wealth or other people's due rights. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī explicitly add greed and withholding rights; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Ālūsī and al-Biqāʿī emphasize avarice. Al-Ṭabrisī reports hoarding rather than good spending. Al-Bursevī says natural love becomes blameworthy when excessive and rights are withheld. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Wāḥidī stress failure to spend for good. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الزمخشري)]
- 89:20/q04/p4 They stockpile wealth and use it for neither religious nor worldly benefit. — Al-Māwardī calls this the worst state of a wealthy person; the fuller account repeats it as a further possibility. [2 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 89:20/q04/p5 Their excess makes them collect lawful and unlawful wealth together. — Al-Ḥasan gives this account; al-Qurṭubī also states that their love encompasses lawful and unlawful wealth. Bint al-Shāṭiʾī favors al-Qurṭubī's account because 89:19 describes indiscriminate inheritance consumption. [5 notes: MAWARDI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(القرطبي)+]
- 89:20/q04/p6 Greedy people collect wealth by any means and spend it badly without regard to consequences. — Al-Ṭabrisī transmits this as another view, distinct from hoarding and refusing to spend. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 89:20/q05 What is the lexical force and Arabic usage of جمّا?  [جَمًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the gloss states the basic quantitative sense or its application to the intensity of an emotion.
- 89:20/q05/p1 It means much or abundant, with a root sense of gathering into fullness. — Abū ʿAmr says jamm yajumm means to increase, including wealth; al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and others cite him. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Rāghib illustrate water gathering in a pool, well or basin; Qurtubī gives jumma for a collecting place and jumūm for a water-rich well or replenished water. Ibn ʿAṭiyya also notes a multitude of people. Ibn Fāris derives abundance from gathering; Ibn al-Athīr derives jamm ghafīr from gathering, with ghafīr conveying covering and comprehensiveness. Bint al-Shāṭiʾī notes a full measure, collected water, mass of hair and this root's sole Qurʾānic occurrence. Zuhayr's water verse, Abū Khirāsh's and an unnamed poet's plentiful forgiveness, and Umayya's abundant amount supply poetic attestations; attributions vary between Umayya ibn Khalaf and Umayya ibn Abī al-Ṣalt. [38 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB(زهير بن أبي سلمى,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI(أبو عمرو); QURTUBI(الشاعر); QURTUBI-FULL(الشاعر); ABUHAYYAN; IBNKATHIR; DURR(ابن عباس,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ITQAN; ALUSI(أمية,ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن الأثير,ابن عباس,ابن فارس,الراغب); TABATABAI]
- 89:20/q05/p2 In describing love, it conveys intensity or excess. — Ibn Zayd, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAbbās and other interpreters use 'intense' or 'severe'; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the transferred sense from quantitative plenty to emotional intensity. [16 notes: TAB(ابن زيد,قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,قتادة); FARRA; MAJAZ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة، الفراء، الكسائي، الزجاج); IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR(ابن عباس,الحسن,قتادة); DURR-FULL(الحسن,قتادة); IBNASHUR]

## 89:20/q06 How is حبًّا جمًّا constructed grammatically?  [حُبًّۭا جَمًّۭا · grammar]
- 89:20/q06/p1 The adjective is accusative with the verbal noun, intensifying the expression. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾī compares accusative qāṭibatan and ṭurran, and says the adjective occurs only with a qualifier; al-Biqāʿī says the verbal noun and adjective together intensify the love. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BIQAI]

## 89:20/q07 What effect does the wording of this clause create?  [وَتُحِبُّونَ حُبًّۭا جَمًّۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the notable force lies in intensification of love or explicit disclosure of its enduring motive.
- 89:20/q07/p1 The verbal noun, adjective, metaphor and sound make the love emphatically intense. — Al-Biqāʿī cites the verbal noun and its adjective; Ibn ʿĀshūr says plentifulness of an abstract emotion is metaphorical; Quṭb says the forceful rhythm and sound enact the denunciation of greed. [3 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR; QUTB-ZILAL]
- 89:20/q07/p2 The explicit statement of love shows the action springs from lasting attachment, not a reluctant necessity. — Al-Biqāʿī says the verse makes this motive explicit and extends it to every kind of wealth. [1 notes: BIQAI]

## 89:20/q08 Who is addressed or described by the rebuke?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the general human address is foregrounded, the earlier named tyrants are its referent, or the Meccan polytheist setting is emphasized.
- 89:20/q08/p1 Human beings generally, or the offenders among them. — Al-Fārisī connects the plural verbs with generic al-insān; al-Samīn sees generic humanity addressed. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the third-person reading exposes offenders to the Prophet and believers. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:20/q08/p2 The charges specify those earlier associated with ʿĀd, Thamūd and Pharaoh. — Corpus Coranicum treats verses 17–20 as specifying their earlier tyranny and corruption through treatment of vulnerable people and possessions. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:20/q08/p3 The immediate social pattern is pre-Islamic Meccan or polytheist greed. — Quṭb adduces usury and exploitation of orphaned girls in pre-Islamic Mecca; Kur'an Yolu describes the passage as criticizing polytheist arrogance and selfishness while teaching Muslims. [2 notes: QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:20/q09 How does this verse explain the neighboring rebuke about vulnerable people and inheritance?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the clause is another independent vice or names the motive for the injustices just listed.
- 89:20/q09/p1 Love of wealth is the motive behind neglect of orphans and the poor, unjust consumption, and failure to spend for good. — Al-Wāḥidī ties gathering without good spending to 89:17–18; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it explains failure to urge feeding in 89:18. Corpus Coranicum prefers motive behind 89:17–19 over a separate offense and compares 76:8. Quṭb says possessive love leaves no generosity; Bint al-Shāṭiʾī links greed to dulled conscience and plundered inheritance. Study Quran cites the laws and warnings on orphan property at 2:220, 4:2–10, 4:127, 6:152 and 107:1–3; it says false inferences from wealth lead to injustice. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Naḥḥās take kallā as a rebuke or prohibition of this conduct. Al-Jishumī says their own neglect, not restricted provision, is the fault. [15 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF; TABRISI(مقاتل); QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; CORPUSCORANICUM+]
- 89:20/q09/p2 The verse may also be heard as a separate condemnation of possessive love itself. — Corpus Coranicum says the wording seems to condemn possession-love as such, while preferring the contextual motive reading; it compares 100:8 and 76:8. Study Quran notes other Qurʾānic condemnations at 102:1–2 and 104:1–3. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; CORPUSCORANICUM-]

## 89:20/q10 What moral or spiritual harm does this attachment cause?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes stress different consequences: damaged moral perception, the proper response to life's tests, or the fate of hoarded property.
- 89:20/q10/p1 It narrows people to worldly desire, eclipsing the Hereafter, reason, compassion, justice and their duties. — Al-Rāzī says exclusive focus on this life turns them from the Hereafter; al-Jishumī says 'you love' censures a person confined to worldly things. Al-Biqāʿī says calculation displaces reason's restraint of whims and turning toward God. Ibn ʿĀshūr names seizure, embezzlement, theft and betrayal of trusts as fruits of excess. Bint al-Shāṭiʾī links greed-driven individualism with tyranny and corruption; Iṣlāḥī says higher values and compassion lose place in the heart. Quṭb cites Meccan usury and exploitation of orphaned girls; Kur'an Yolu describes selfish indifference. [9 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QUTB-ZILAL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:20/q10/p2 One should meet prosperity with gratitude and care, deprivation with patience or contentment, and prepare for the afterlife. — Elmalılı says forgetting God's watch and the afterlife explains the failures; people should honor orphans and feed the poor now. Iṣlāḥī says both favor and deprivation are tests, and the peaceful soul succeeds. Kur'an Yolu adds sharing, organized solidarity and reverence for God's command. [4 notes: ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:20/q10/p3 Hoarded wealth may pass to wasteful heirs, leaving the owner only sin. — Elmalılı says heirs may squander what the owner failed to spend for good, sharpening the warning against piling it up. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 89:20/q11 What report explains what truly counts as a person's wealth?  [ٱلْمَالَ · reports]
- 89:20/q11/p1 Only what a person consumes, wears out, or gives away is truly theirs; the rest belongs to the heir. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr transmit the Prophetic report, the latter through Sufyān; its opening says people value an heir's wealth more than their own. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR(النبي); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 89:20/q12 Do the acts named in this rebuke belong to human agency?  [وَتُحِبُّونَ · theology]
- 89:20/q12/p1 Yes: loving, eating, honoring and urging are human acts. — Al-Jishumī points to the praise and blame attached to the acts as evidence that humans perform them. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 89:20/q13 What wider literary and scriptural connections illuminate this rebuke?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes offer biblical literary parallels and Qurʾānic or pre-Islamic parallels, without presenting them as incompatible.
- 89:20/q13/p1 Its social accusations recur in biblical prophecy and may reflect a familiar literary genre. — Corpus Coranicum identifies neglect of the poor and vulnerable and excess possession-love as motifs in biblical prophetic books. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 89:20/q13/p2 Other Qurʾānic passages criticize possession-love and its obstruction of care for the needy. — Corpus Coranicum cites 100:8 and 76:8; Study Quran cites 102:1–2 and 104:1–3. Ibn ʿĀshūr's quoted lines of Ṭarafa voice the pre-Islamic boast that wealth, noble offspring and rank show divine favor, the mistaken premise rebuked in this passage. [3 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(طرفة)]

## 89:20/q14 Is there an inward reading of the loved wealth?  [ٱلْمَالَ · inward]
- 89:20/q14/p1 The loved wealth can symbolize ego-driven evil deeds and corrupt states of desire. — Al-Bursevī presents this possibility and reports some masters who spell out the symbolic referent. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]
