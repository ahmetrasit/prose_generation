# 23:100: 25 questions

## 23:100/q01 Who seeks to return and do good?  [لَعَلِّىٓ أَعْمَلُ صَٰلِحًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the deathbed warning about missed charity and pilgrimage includes believers here, or the context confines the plea to unbelievers.
- 23:100/q01/p1 The dying unbeliever or polytheist. — Al-Tabari describes regret for neglected obedience; al-Razi argues believers shown Paradise would not want more, and even the least rewarded would otherwise grieve over higher ranks. Kuran Yolu specifies deniers of resurrection whose worldly attachments fall away. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL+]
- 23:100/q01/p2 Every dying wrongdoer, including those who neglected obligations, can make the plea. — Ibn Abbas names unpaid charity or zakat and neglected pilgrimage and cites 63:10 against restricting the wish to unbelievers; al-Razi replies that 63:10 describes worldly life rather than the state of reward. Ibn Kathir includes unbelievers and neglecters of commands; al-Hasan says even a mute person without divine friendship asks unheard when the Fire is disclosed. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); RAZI-FULL-]

## 23:100/q02 When does the plea to return occur?  [لَعَلِّىٓ أَعْمَلُ صَٰلِحًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The explicit attachment to death's arrival versus parallel descriptions of requests after seeing Hell.
- 23:100/q02/p1 At the approach of death, before death is tasted. — Ibn Zayd places it when worldly life ends and the next life appears; al-Razi says recognition of God and disobedience is forced at death and prefers the verse's apparent timing, though the plea may recur later. Study Quran reports an address to the soul-taking angels before death; Kuran Yolu calls the Hell-sighting alternative weak. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 23:100/q02/p2 After death, when the condemned see the Fire. — Al-Razi cites other accounts of Hell's inhabitants requesting return; Study Quran gives the same alternative. Ibn Kathir says the request is denied at death, resurrection, judgment, and the showing of Fire. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Others (R)); IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî)-]

## 23:100/q03 What does the dying person see or say in reports about this plea?  [لَعَلِّىٓ أَعْمَلُ صَٰلِحًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different deathbed visions and consequences.
- 23:100/q03/p1 A believer offered return by angels declines worldly grief, while an unbeliever asks to return. — Reports are addressed to Aisha or attributed to the Prophet by al-Tabari, Jishumi, Kashshaf, Ibn Atiyya, Razi, Baydawi and Bursevi; Jishumi states no grading. The believer seeks to proceed to God. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]
- 23:100/q03/p2 Neglected obligations or obstructions to truth appear before the dying person and prompt the plea. — Yahya's unnamed Banu Haritha report varies between before him and between his eyes. Razi reports withheld rights; Alusi cites a Prophetic report through al-Daylami from Jabir and uses it to support a wealth reading. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله)]
- 23:100/q03/p3 The unbeliever sees few good deeds, many bad ones, and the Angel of Death; descendants cannot hear his wish. — Al-Suddi's account has God reject the return. Al-Hasan similarly says bystanders do not hear, even when the dying person had been mute. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,السدي)]
- 23:100/q03/p4 The unbeliever sees his place in Fire, is refused return, and suffers constriction and creatures in the grave. — Abu Hurayra says the allocated lifespan has ended before the grave torment begins. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]

## 23:100/q04 What does لَعَلِّىٓ express?  [لَعَلِّىٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle marks confidence, uncertain hope, or purpose.
- 23:100/q04/p1 Hope or resolve to do good, without doubt about the speaker's own intention. — Al-Razi likens it to certainty about repairing a known failure. Bursevi says faith is definite while righteous action is hoped for; Alusi allows hope over work alone or over both work and faith. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:100/q04/p2 Uncertainty about the future return or ability to act. — Al-Razi says future events are unknown and invokes 6:28, where returnees repeat wrongdoing; Qurtubi allows uncertainty about return or action if returned. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 23:100/q04/p3 Purpose: so that the speaker may act. — Alusi cites al-Waqidi, and a route through al-Suddi from Abu Malik, saying Qur'anic laʿalla marks purpose except 26:129. Ibn Ashur calls the hope clause the reason for the return request. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البغوي عن الواقدي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:100/q05 How is the final yāʾ of لَعَلِّىٓ read?  [لَعَلِّىٓ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vocalization of the yāʾ; the core meaning is unchanged.
- 23:100/q05/p1 With a still final yāʾ. — Ibn Atiyya assigns it to al-Hasan and the majority; Nasafi names al-Kafi, Sahl and Yaqub; Nashr names the Kufans and Yaqub for the single possessive yāʾ. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والجمهور); NASAFI-FULL; NASHR(الكوفيون ويعقوب)]
- 23:100/q05/p2 With fatḥa on the final yāʾ. — Ibn Atiyya names Talha ibn Musarrif; Nashr names Nafi, Ibn Kathir, Abu Amr, Ibn Amir and Abu Jafar across six instances in Yusuf, Taha, al-Muminun, al-Qasas and Ghafir. Qiraat ER says both are canonical and only prosody changes. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن والجمهور); NASHR(نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]

## 23:100/q06 What righteous work does the speaker hope to do?  [أَعْمَلُ صَٰلِحًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the promised good names faith, all obedience, or a particular neglected duty.
- 23:100/q06/p1 Believe or pronounce the testimony of God's oneness. — Ibn Abbas and al-Jalalayn specify the testimony; Muqatil says faith. Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan compare doing good on an omitted faith to building on a foundation that first has to be laid. Baydawi and Ibn Ashur mention rejected faith in God and His messenger. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الجلالين); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 23:100/q06/p2 Obey God and repair neglected good deeds and duties generally. — Qatada excludes family, clan, wealth and pleasure as motives. Razi prefers this scope because bodily and financial worship and rights all need repair. Tabarsi specifies neglected prayers, fasts and worship. Asad includes wrongful deeds committed; Samarrai says righteousness, rather than charity alone, grounds salvation in this passage. Al-Ala ibn Ziyad urges the living to imagine their reprieve was granted and obey now. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(العلاء بن زياد,قتادة); ASAD-NOTES; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; BURSEVI(الكبير)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 23:100/q06/p3 Perform the pilgrimage left undone. — Damghani gives pilgrimage as the righteous act; Ibn Abbas's broader deathbed account names pilgrimage alongside charity. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 23:100/q06/p4 Pay the obligatory alms or God's due from the estate. — Al-Sadiq specifies withheld zakat. Tabarsi also gives paying God's due; Ibn Abbas mentions unpaid charity, and Kashshaf and Razi mention using left wealth. [5 notes: TABRISI(الصادق); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل)]

## 23:100/q07 What is فِيمَا تَرَكْتُ—the thing left behind?  [فِيمَا تَرَكْتُ · referent]
turns on: Whether tark means physically leaving life or property, figuratively rejecting faith, or omitting duties.
- 23:100/q07/p1 The world or the past and remaining span of earthly life. — Ibn Abbas says the passed time or remaining life; Ibn Ashur takes tark literally as leaving the world. Baydawi and Tabarsi mention worldly life; Nasafi says the world left behind. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:100/q07/p2 Wealth or property left behind, to be spent rightly. — Kashshaf gives using abandoned wealth; Razi mentions paying God's due; Tabarsi names the estate; Alusi's Jabir report supports this; Abu Hayyan attributes it to Ibn Abbas. Qurtubi specifies charity. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله)]
- 23:100/q07/p3 Faith or truth formerly rejected or denied. — Al-Kalbi says what he denied; Ibn Ashur explains figurative rejection of faith in God and His messenger and admission of past error. Kashshaf and Abu Hayyan use the foundation analogy. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 23:100/q07/p4 Neglected duties or righteous acts left undone. — Another Wahidi interpreter says neglected matters; Razi prefers the inclusive sense of bodily and financial duties and rights. Ibn Jawzi reports Muqatil; Asad includes both omitted good and committed wrong. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; BURSEVI(الكبير)+; RAZI-FULL+]

## 23:100/q08 What does كَلَّآ do here?  [كَلَّآ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether kalla rejects the plea, confirms the following clause, or functions as an opening or transition.
- 23:100/q08/p1 It rebukes and refuses the request to return. — Zajjaj calls it rebuke and alert; Ibn Kathir says God neither grants nor accepts the request; Qurtubi invokes 6:28 that a returnee would break the promise. Razi prefers refusal. Burhan permits pausing before or beginning after kalla in this class, depending on rebuke or confirmation, and lists twelve examples. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن عبد الله مولى غفرة); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 23:100/q08/p2 It confirms that the plea is indeed what he will say. — Razi reports this second analysis. Al-Kisai makes kalla an accusative verbal noun, haqqan, with an omitted verb; Burhan says confirmation opens a statement to reinforce what follows, but its author objects here that this would require an unattested fatḥ on inna. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN(الكسائي); BURHAN-]
- 23:100/q08/p3 It opens or alerts to the following statement, or marks the boundary between negation and affirmation. — Jishumi offers negating what precedes and affirming what follows; Abu Hatim takes it as opening ala and cites revelation beginning at 96:6 after a pause at 96:5; Burhan's author takes it as illa here. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURHAN(أبو حاتم)]

## 23:100/q09 Who speaks كَلَّآ and the following response?  [كَلَّآ إِنَّهَا كَلِمَةٌ هُوَ قَآئِلُهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the rebuke is divine speech or the dying person's self-reproach.
- 23:100/q09/p1 God speaks the rejection and response. — Alusi expressly rejects putting kalla in the dying person's own mouth; Ibn Kathir's report has God answer the plea. Abu Hayyan allows God's rebuke. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:100/q09/p2 The dying person says kalla to himself in self-reproach. — Abu Hayyan reports this alternative with repeated, useless words; Alusi objects that kalla and what follows are God's speech. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 23:100/q10 Whose utterance is إِنَّهَا كَلِمَةٌ?  [إِنَّهَا كَلِمَةٌ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent of 'it': the human plea or God's prior promise.
- 23:100/q10/p1 It is the dying person's plea to return and act righteously. — Muqatil identifies 'Lord, send me back'; Tabari specifies the polytheist's plea; Kashshaf and Nasafi take the whole connected utterance; Ibn Kathir's Muhammad ibn Kab says God answers this plea. [5 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 23:100/q10/p2 It may be God's unfailing word or promise. — Qurtubi says God has decreed that no soul outlives its term and this disbeliever will not believe; he also reports the alternative that the dying person's own words avail nothing. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 23:100/q11 What does calling the plea a mere كَلِمَةٌ imply?  [إِنَّهَا كَلِمَةٌ هُوَ قَآئِلُهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause stresses inevitable utterance, lack of benefit, or the falsity of the promised reform.
- 23:100/q11/p1 It is futile speech with no answer or benefit. — Wahidi compares 9:30 and 33:4, speech made only with mouths; Bagthawi and Ibn Jawzi say nothing is obtained; Ibn Ashur calls the wording proverbial, with inna stressing that the word has no property beyond utterance. Alusi compares a retort consigning someone to pointless solitary speech; Islahi prefers unavailing regret although parallels support the alternative. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(آخرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 23:100/q11/p2 He inevitably says or repeats it under grief and regret. — Ibn Zayd and Ibn Kathir say every dying wrongdoer speaks it; Kashshaf and Razi allow relentless repetition; Baydawi, Nasafi, Alusi and Study Quran emphasize inevitability. Alusi says fronting the subject lends emphasis or exclusivity. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Some commentators (Ṭ))]
- 23:100/q11/p3 The promise is false: if returned, he would repeat his old conduct. — Tabarsi invokes 6:28 and transmits al-Rida's answer to al-Fath ibn Yazid that God knows this counterfactual; Ibn Kathir says speech lacks action and is lying, and Umar ibn Abd Allah takes kalla to call the speaker a liar. Biqai cites past deceit. Islahi reports this reading but prefers futile regret. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(العياشي); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن عبد الله مولى غفرة); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 23:100/q12 How does كَلِمَةٌ denote the whole plea?  [كَلِمَةٌ · rhetoric]
- 23:100/q12/p1 A single 'word' names a whole utterance. — Samin calls it part for whole and cites Labid's line that all things perish except God as the truest 'word' of a poet. Ibn Ashur cites that report, the testimony and Islam formulas, and 9:74. Alusi says grammarians call this figurative while lexicographers call it literal or a familiar metaphor. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:100/q13 What is the construction of هُوَ قَآئِلُهَا?  [هُوَ قَآئِلُهَا · grammar]
- 23:100/q13/p1 It is an adjective qualifying 'word'. — Samin gives the syntactic analysis; Alusi notes that fronting the subject adds emphasis or exclusivity, and Ibn Ashur says the otherwise obvious description limits the plea to utterance. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:100/q14 What does وَرَآئِهِم mean?  [وَرَآئِهِم · meaning]
turns on: Whether warāʾ here is interpreted as approaching ahead, an interval between, or its ordinary spatial 'behind'.
- 23:100/q14/p1 Before or ahead of them, as a future barrier approaching them. — Abu Ubayda, Abu Salih, Sufyan and others gloss 'ahead'; Damghani cites the king and Hell in 18:79, 14:16 and 45:10. Ibn Ashur makes it a pursuer-like metaphor and cites 85:20, 45:10, 14:17, 18:79 and Labid's verse. Tabatabai says the future approaches and overtakes the person. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); DAMGHANI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح وغيره); DURR-FULL(سفيان بن حسين); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(لبيد); TABATABAI]
- 23:100/q14/p2 Between them, or literally behind them. — Wahidi also allows 'between'; for literal behind he cites Hell behind a person in 14:16. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 23:100/q15 Who does the pronoun in وَرَآئِهِم refer to?  [وَرَآئِهِم · referent]
- 23:100/q15/p1 The dying person of the preceding context, taken collectively. — Wahidi links it to the preceding 'when one of them is reached by death'; Alusi says the earlier wording is singular but its meaning includes all of them. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:100/q16 What does بَرْزَخٌ mean in general Arabic usage?  [بَرْزَخٌ · meaning]
- 23:100/q16/p1 A barrier, interval, or partition between two things. — Wahidi gives distance, wall, days, hostility, and even the boundary between certainty and doubt; al-Farra says barrier and interval are close. A report about Ali uses it for space between two prayer positions. Mawardi and Razi cite the separation of waters in 55:20; Samin says a barrier between incompatible things or any partition, equivalent senses. Raghib derives it from Persian barza and describes separation from high afterlife stations. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI(مجاهد وابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,علي); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:100/q17 What is the بَرْزَخٌ ahead of the dead?  [بَرْزَخٌ · referent]
turns on: Whether the term names a blocking partition, the whole intermediate period, a specific place or span, or an active realm of interim recompense.
- 23:100/q17/p1 A barrier preventing return to worldly life until resurrection. — Mujahid repeatedly defines its blocking function; Tabari, Tha'labi, Mawardi, Wahidi, Kashshaf, Tabarsi and others say the dead cannot cross back. Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur stress that resurrection returns them to the afterlife, not the world. Tabatabai rejects a merely preventive reading because the time limit would be pointless if that were all the verse meant. Ibn Abbas also calls it a veil. [30 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد وابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك ومجاهد وابن زيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 23:100/q17/p2 The interval or state between death and resurrection, between this world and the next. — Mujahid, Ibn Zayd, Dahhak, Qatada and others specify death to resurrection or the interval between worlds; Sha'bi and Muhammad ibn Kab say the dead belong to neither worldly eating and drinking nor final recompense. Tabari calls barrier and interval near synonyms. Qurtubi says varied definitions are close. Kuran Yolu attributes the common Islamic meaning to Razi. [41 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سعيد,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الشعبي); THALABI(الضحاك وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن زيد,علي بن عيسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري,الشعبي,الضحاك وابن عباس والسدي وقتادة وابن عيسى والكلبي,الضحاك ومجاهد وابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد,محمد بن كعب); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,الشعبي,سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد,محمد بن كعب القرظي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن زيد); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)+]
- 23:100/q17/p3 The graves where the dead remain until resurrection. — Al-Hasan, Abu Umama, Abu Sakhr and al-Rabi identify the grave or cemeteries; Ali ibn Isa combines grave with respite. The grave is neither ordinary earthly life nor final recompense. [10 notes: MUJAHID(الحسن); TAB-FULL(أبو أمامة); THALABI(أبو أمامة); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صخر); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو صخر,الربيع); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:100/q17/p4 The remainder of worldly life while the dead await its end. — Qatada is repeatedly transmitted with this wording; Wahidi says they stay there until the world ends and they rise. Ibn Ashur lists it among reported views. [8 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك وابن عباس والسدي وقتادة وابن عيسى والكلبي); DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:100/q17/p5 An appointed term or respite until resurrection. — Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi, Muqatil, Ibn Isa and Ali ibn Isa use term or respite language; Qurtubi treats these variants as close to other interval definitions. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي ومقاتل); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك وابن عباس والسدي وقتادة وابن عيسى والكلبي)]
- 23:100/q17/p6 The interval between the two trumpet blasts, said to be forty years. — Al-Suddi gives the two blasts; al-Kalbi and Qurtubi add forty years. Kuran Yolu attributes this alternative to Tabari and Shawkani. [4 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك وابن عباس والسدي وقتادة وابن عيسى والكلبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî ve Şevkânî)]
- 23:100/q17/p7 An imaginal grave realm with awareness, reward and punishment before resurrection. — Tabatabai invokes context, other verses and many Shiite and Sunni reports, called numerous and mutawatir; al-Sadiq says the interim has reward and punishment. Ibn Ashur notes a Sufi-favored intermediate realm where souls see their future station; al-Sayyid defines an imaginal world between abstract meanings and material bodies. Al-Jubba'i says death reveals one's rewarded or punished station. [6 notes: TABRISI(الجبائي); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله); IBNASHUR-FULL(السيد)]
- 23:100/q17/p8 A barrier to complete requital, which arrives on Judgment Day. — Alusi reports this further interpretation; Muhammad ibn Kab's interim people have not yet received final recompense. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب); DURR-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]

## 23:100/q18 What do reports say happens to souls in the barzakh?  [بَرْزَخٌ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different conditions of punished and believing dead, including the form and location of believers' spirits.
- 23:100/q18/p1 The grave contains interim punishment, including constriction and snakes. — Abu Hurayra describes the disbeliever's constricted grave and tormenting creatures; Aisha describes black snakes biting sinners' heads and feet until meeting midbody; Ibn Kathir says punishment continues until resurrection and cites a hadith of punishment in the earth. Al-Sajjad says the grave may be a pit of Fire. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة); TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله,علي بن الحسين)]
- 23:100/q18/p2 Believers' spirits inhabit body-like forms, not green birds around the Throne. — Al-Sadiq rejects the green-bird claim as beneath believers' honor and describes bodies like their own. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله)]
- 23:100/q18/p3 Believers' spirits reside in a Paradise tree, eating, drinking, and awaiting the Hour. — One al-Sadiq report has them asking that later believers join earlier ones; another puts them in body-like forms, recognizing one another and inquiring about living or dead acquaintances. [2 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 23:100/q19 What does the barzakh warning convey?  [وَمِن وَرَآئِهِم بَرْزَخٌ · interpretation]
turns on: The warning is explained as interim punishment or the loss of any opportunity to repair deeds.
- 23:100/q19/p1 It threatens the dying wrongdoer with continuing barzakh punishment. — Ibn Kathir compares threats of severe punishment behind people in 45:10 and 14:17 and says the dead continue to be punished in earth until resurrection. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 23:100/q19/p2 It closes the time for action and makes regret and excuses ineffective. — Qushayri says neither kinship nor admission helps when one meets earned results; Sulami's Abu Uthman urges obedience now rather than spiritual claims and abandonment of the path. Islahi says the opportunity ended at death; al-Ala ibn Ziyad counsels acting as if a requested reprieve had been given. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(أبو عثمان); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(العلاء بن زياد)]

## 23:100/q20 How is يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ constructed?  [يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ · grammar]
- 23:100/q20/p1 The time noun 'day' is annexed to the verb clause 'they are raised'. — Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq, Jishumi, Nahhas and Qurtubi say time adverbs can annex to verbal clauses; Jishumi notes the absent nunation and paraphrases 'day of their resurrection'. Nahhas also relays Abu Ishaq's view that the construction is essentially a quotation; Qurtubi says it expresses the verbal noun. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 23:100/q21 What does the limit إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ imply?  [إِلَىٰ يَوْمِ يُبْعَثُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the time limit solely closes worldly return or also identifies a substantive interim state.
- 23:100/q21/p1 There is no worldly return before resurrection, and resurrection itself is a return to the afterlife. — Kashshaf, Razi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur reject reading the terminus as renewed earthly life; Razi says naming resurrection extinguishes hope. Alusi likens earthly return to a camel passing through a needle's eye in 7:40. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:100/q21/p2 The limit marks resurrection after a real intermediate existence. — Tabatabai argues that merely saying return is blocked until resurrection leaves the specified limit without point; he reads the span as the imaginal grave life. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]

## 23:100/q22 What does this verse imply about human agency and return after death?  [* · theology]
turns on: Different doctrinal inferences are drawn from the denied request.
- 23:100/q22/p1 The promise of good work presupposes human ability and choice. — Jishumi argues that if deeds and ability were not their own, their request would be meaningless; he uses it against compulsion. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 23:100/q22/p2 Worldly return after death, including reincarnation, is refused; resurrection remains certain. — Jishumi argues against the Imamis; Kuran Yolu argues against transmigration of souls until purification, tracing it to ancient Indian religions and Plato. If rebirth were possible, God would grant the sinners' corrective request. [2 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:100/q23 How does this plea connect to other verses and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw either cross-verse parallels or the immediate sura trajectory.
- 23:100/q23/p1 Other verses repeat a doomed wish to return for charity or righteous deeds. — Yahya cites 63:10; Ibn Kathir lists 63:10–11, 14:44, 7:53, 32:12, 6:27–28, 42:44, 40:11–12 and 35:37; Study Quran adds 2:167, 26:102 and 39:58. Razi disputes using 63:10 to include believers in this deathbed scene. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 23:100/q23/p2 The passage completes the sūra's account of opponents persisting until death. — Study Quran says 'till' continues the rejection at 23:83 and notes recurrent opposition by affluent and powerful people to prophets, comparing 7:59–62 and 7:136; the conclusion warns of disbelievers' too-late wish to do good instead of evil. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 23:100/q24 What surrounding textual observations are recorded with this verse?  [* · sciences]
turns on: These are separate surrounding observations preserved because the supplied notes include them.
- 23:100/q24/p1 The word kalla occurs thirty-three times in fifteen suras, all in the Qur'an's second half. — Burhan records the distribution while analyzing this occurrence and its pause. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 23:100/q24/p2 The following trumpet word has readings al-ṣūr and al-ṣuwar. — Ibn Atiyya reports the majority's 'trumpet' and Ibn Abbas's fatḥa on wāw, 'forms' or 'images', in the adjacent resurrection context. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 23:100/q24/p3 The cited wujūh work keeps traditional distinctions, chooses earlier material, and adds suitable transmitted exegesis. — Ibn al-Jawzi says some categories might be combined but that would erase most inherited categories; he says interchapter comparison and linguistic derivations sharpen understanding and prays for sincere benefit. His adjacent example gives yamīn as an oath or the right hand. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 23:100/q24/p4 Particles called extra should be reexamined for their rhetorical function. — Bint al-Shati says the bāʾ still conveys attachment and directly governs the predicate; she cites 'barzakh behind them' beside 'from behind walls' in 59:14 as a parallel. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 23:100/q25 Whom does the dying person's plea address?  [لَعَلِّىٓ أَعْمَلُ صَٰلِحًۭا · referent]
- 23:100/q25/p1 God and then the angels of death. — Bursevi reports a view that the speaker first appeals to God and then to the Angel of Death and his helpers, with the mention of Lord functioning as an oath. Study Quran reports that after addressing God the dying person asks the soul-taking angels to return him. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Some commentators)]
