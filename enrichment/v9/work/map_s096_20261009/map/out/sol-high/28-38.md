# 28:38: 38 questions

## 28:38/q01 When does Pharaoh make this address?  [وَقَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ · links]
turns on: Which preceding stage of the confrontation the address follows.
- 28:38/q01/p1 After Moses' proofs and warnings, in a separate address to the courtiers. — Al-Biqai says the signs and exhortations preceded it and Pharaoh repeatedly delayed belief when Moses supplied proof; Ibn Ashur explains the conjunction as joining this separate court scene to the exchange with Moses. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:38/q01/p2 After Pharaoh assembled magicians to challenge Moses. — Bursevi supplies this sequence. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 28:38/q02 Whom does al-mala' address?  [ٱلْمَلَأُ · referent]
- 28:38/q02/p1 Pharaoh's chiefs or conspicuous assembly. — Muqatil specifies the chiefs of his people; Elmalili calls it an impressive public gathering and portrays a tyrant summoning his community. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ELMALILI]

## 28:38/q03 Why does Pharaoh address the nobles directly?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْمَلَأُ · rhetoric]
- 28:38/q03/p1 To court their loyalty and keep them from Moses' teaching. — Al-Biqai says the honorific address wins their hearts; Ibn Ashur says Pharaoh seeks to secure their belief in him against Moses' Lord of forefathers, heaven and earth (26:24, 26:26). [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:38/q04 What claim of divinity is Pharaoh making?  [مَا عَلِمْتُ لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ilah means exclusive sovereign worship, universal creator, or a local ruler, and how Egyptian idol worship fits Pharaoh's claim.
- 28:38/q04/p1 He claims exclusive worship and obedience as his people's god. — Al-Tabari says he denies Moses' other lord and object of worship. Ibn Kathir calls it unbelieving tyranny accepted by the people (43:54), connects the highest-lord claim (79:23-26) and threat in 26:29; Muqatil calls it unbelief. Al-Razi says ilah means worshipped ruler, not creator of sky, sea or people; Elmalili says Pharaoh demanded obedience, love, fear and legislative authority while knowing he was created. Islahi says autocrats pass mockery off as proof; Kuranyolu says kings were worshipped as divine sons. The Study Quran compares 79:24. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:38/q04/p2 He denies another creator but leaves Egyptian cult gods outside this dispute. — Ibn Ashur says the denied god is Moses' universal creator; Egyptian gods were thought Pharaoh's offspring or essence. Tabataba'i calls it a counter-restriction of Moses' sole God, leaving their other objects of worship aside. Bursevi says Pharaoh denies a god on earth while Moses asserts heaven's creator (13:16). Asad notes the assertion cannot literally exclude Egypt's many gods: Pharaoh was adored as their divine incarnation and Lord All-Highest (79:24). Jishumi reports an alternative that he ordered idol worship while claiming universal divinity. [5 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 28:38/q04/p3 Each ruler is god within his own territory. — Al-Alusi reports this explanation of the exclusive claim. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 28:38/q05 Does 'I know no other god' deny existence or only knowledge?  [مَا عَلِمْتُ لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرِى · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether denial of knowing functions as metonymy for nonexistence or is the literal epistemic claim; the force of 10:18 and the ending matters.
- 28:38/q05/p1 It indirectly denies that another god exists. — Al-Kashshaf compares 7:59 and 10:18: knowledge follows its object. Al-Baydawi limits the inference to active, not passive, knowledge; al-Alusi calls nonexistence a customary cause of not knowing, hence metonymy rather than logical entailment, and reports al-Baydawi's distinction. Ibn Ashur says Pharaoh pretends omniscience, so any rival would be known. Tabataba'i rejects this reading because the verse's ending does not fit it. Al-Razi rebuts Pharaoh's no-proof premise by arguing originated heavens and stars require an originator, and distinguishes more radical deniers who infer nonexistence from failed inquiry. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(فرعون); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي البيضاوي); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-; RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:38/q05/p2 He literally says he knows of no other god, without proving nonexistence. — Al-Baydawi says certainty of nonexistence is unavailable and the tower supposedly investigates; al-Alusi says ignorance proves nothing. Al-Kashshaf says calling Moses false without knowing so even leaves open another god; al-Nasafi and Abu Hayyan present both readings, the latter noting the drowning confession (10:90). Al-Razi says Pharaoh withholds affirmation for supposed lack of rational or sensory evidence while suspecting Moses. Tabataba'i says the ending moves from professed ignorance to suspicion. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(فرعون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم)]

## 28:38/q06 Did Pharaoh believe his own denial?  [مَا عَلِمْتُ لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرِى · theology]
turns on: Whether his claim and search show genuine uncertainty or calculated public defiance despite inward recognition.
- 28:38/q06/p1 He knowingly lied and used the claim to deceive. — Al-Hasan calls it a deliberate lie; Yahya cites 27:14 and Qatada says denial follows knowledge. Jishumi says he knew he had a creator; Ibn Abbas says 43:87 confirms it. Al-Razi says he knew God but misled the gullible, referring to 20:49. Bursevi says his need and afflictions ruled out his own godhood; Elmalili says he knew he did not create his people. Al-Alusi cites Moses' 17:102 challenge, also noted by Tabataba'i; al-Biqai, Ibn Ashur and Tabataba'i describe calculated court deception. Pharaoh's drowning admission in 10:90 is adduced by Abu Hayyan. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,قتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); ELMALILI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; JISHUMI+]
- 28:38/q06/p2 He may sincerely have doubted or imagined a spatial god. — Al-Zajjaj says signs gave him suspicion Moses was a prophet and 17:102 confronts him with decisive proof. Al-Kashshaf says a known falsehood would not require a search, and he imagined God spatial like himself. Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi take the tower's investigation seriously; al-Alusi allows Pharaoh's ignorance as a fallback. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:38/q07 Why phrase the denial as lack of knowledge?  [مَا عَلِمْتُ · rhetoric]
- 28:38/q07/p1 To pose as fair and make the audience doubt Moses. — Al-Biqai says Pharaoh knows the truth but wants delay; al-Alusi calls it a satanic stratagem; Elmalili and Tabataba'i say he looks fair-minded, the latter comparing 40:29. Ibn Ashur adds his pose that his knowledge covers all truth; Tabataba'i says the miracles are presented as insufficient evidence. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:38/q08 How long apart were Pharaoh's two divinity claims?  [مَا عَلِمْتُ لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرِى · reports]
- 28:38/q08/p1 Forty years. — Ibn Abbas, also transmitted by al-Qurtubi and al-Durr, places this claim forty years before 'I am your highest lord' (79:24); al-Durr's Prophetic report says God punished both claims (79:26). Bursevi transmits the same interval. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(قيل)]

## 28:38/q09 What do reports say Gabriel and God did in response to Pharaoh's claims?  [مَا عَلِمْتُ لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرِى · reports]
turns on: These reports describe different divine and angelic responses at successive moments.
- 28:38/q09/p1 Gabriel requested his destruction, but God deferred it to an appointed time. — Ibn Abbas says Gabriel asked at the first divinity claim; God recalled the plea after the later highest-lord claim and ordered the brickmaking episode. Al-Durr reports the divine message to Gabriel that Pharaoh had rebelled and his appointed destruction had arrived (79:24). [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(الله تعالى)]
- 28:38/q09/p2 God invited even Pharaoh to repent. — Ibn Abbas says no one may make people despair of repentance and that God's enabling turn precedes the servant's repentance. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 28:38/q09/p3 Gabriel later feared Pharaoh would receive mercy while drowning. — Gabriel reports stuffing Pharaoh's mouth at drowning after his claims of divinity. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(جبريل)]

## 28:38/q10 Who is Haman?  [يَٰهَٰمَٰنُ · referent]
turns on: The notes supply his office and a separate report of origin.
- 28:38/q10/p1 Pharaoh's minister and administrator. — Ibn Kathir calls him minister, administrator and adviser; al-Biqai calls him minister, Ibn Ashur a minister's name or title, and Islahi prime minister or construction minister. Ibn Ashur says royal ministers implemented commands through officials. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:38/q10/p2 Haman was Nubian. — Al-Durr transmits an unnamed informant in Ibn Abd al-Hakam. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(محدث حدثه)]

## 28:38/q11 What does firing clay command?  [فَأَوْقِدْ لِى يَٰهَٰمَٰنُ عَلَى ٱلطِّينِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the object of firing is discrete bricks or a kiln or clay structure.
- 28:38/q11/p1 Fire molded clay into bricks for construction. — Mujahid, Ibn Jurayj, Qatada, Ibn Zayd, Sa'id b. Jubayr, Ibn Abbas and many commentators gloss the operation as kiln firing mud or clay into brick. Bursevi defines clay as earth and water. Al-Alusi and Elmalili stress the verse literally says fire clay, leaving bricks implicit. Ibn Ashur says the preparatory task stands for construction, with the minister ordering workers. Islahi says heated mud building was common in Egypt and Nineveh. [31 notes: MUJAHID(ابن جريج); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد,مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وقتادة وأبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:38/q11/p2 It can mean making tiles or bricks in molds, or firing a clay structure. — Ibn Atiyya supplies the tile or molded-brick option; Islahi proposes either a brick kiln or a clay building fired in place. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:38/q12 Was Pharaoh the first brickmaker?  [فَأَوْقِدْ لِى يَٰهَٰمَٰنُ عَلَى ٱلطِّينِ · reports]
- 28:38/q12/p1 He was first to fire bricks and build with them. — Reported from Ibn Jurayj and Qatada and repeated by Muqatil, Yahya, al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj and later writers; some qualify it as 'it is said' or 'I heard.' Al-Alusi quotes Umar in Syria saying he knew no brick builder besides Pharaoh. Ibn Abi Hatim also preserves an unnamed report. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(بلاغ غير مسمى,قتادة); THALABI(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل وقتادة وأبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج,عمر); ELMALILI]

## 28:38/q13 Why say 'fire clay' instead of 'make bricks'?  [فَأَوْقِدْ لِى يَٰهَٰمَٰنُ عَلَى ٱلطِّينِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the expression chiefly teaches technique or selects a more eloquent common word.
- 28:38/q13/p1 It teaches the new craft and names its first operation. — Al-Razi says Pharaoh was first brickmaker and knew the craft, and the expression suits Quranic eloquence and tyrant speech. Al-Nasafi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Elmalili and al-Alusi say the wording instructs Haman in unfamiliar brickmaking; al-Biqai says Pharaoh's personal attribution makes the task imperative. Ibn Ashur says the first task could begin immediately and brick allowed a fast temporary tower; durability was irrelevant. [10 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:38/q13/p2 Clay is chosen for elegant, lighter diction. — Ibn al-Athir says it avoids commonplace words for brick and tile; Ibn Ashur prefers the lighter and more eloquent wording. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن الأثير); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 28:38/q14 What does naming and commanding Haman convey?  [لِى يَٰهَٰمَٰنُ · rhetoric]
- 28:38/q14/p1 The inserted vocative and menial command display royal arrogance. — Al-Razi, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi point to the mid-sentence address and order to fire clay; al-Baydawi says the vocative interrupts the sentence. Ibn Ashur says attribution to Haman is figurative agency, as when a ruler is said to build a bridge or Baghdad, because ministers direct workers. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:38/q15 What is a sarh?  [صَرْحًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the generic elevated building is described as a palace or specifically a tower used for observation.
- 28:38/q15/p1 A lofty, exposed palace or elevated flat-roofed building. — Ibn Abbas and exegetes call it a high palace; al-Zajjaj says broad and high, al-Tabari raised and level and cites the poetic plural suruh. Ibn Atiyya specifies a roof or flat surface; al-Biqai cites al-Tabari's flat roof and al-Zajjaj's breadth. Al-Alusi and Tabataba'i derive exposure from being visible. Ibn Kathir compares 40:36-37; Ibn Ashur compares 27:44. Muqatil describes its glass-slag mortar and dangerous wind; Abu al-Layth through Bursevi adds 5,000 cubits height and 3,000 width. [24 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الزجاج,الطبري); BURSEVI(أبو الليث); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:38/q15/p2 A tower, minaret or observation post. — Al-Baghawi offers tower or lighthouse; Bursevi says like a pillar or minaret; Elmalili calls it a sky-surveying observatory. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 28:38/q16 Was the tower actually built?  [فَٱجْعَل لِّى صَرْحًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether reports of construction are accepted; an order alone establishes no completed structure, and al-Razi invokes physical implausibility.
- 28:38/q16/p1 A huge unprecedented tower was built. — Accounts say Haman gathered fifty thousand builders plus support crews, who made brick, plaster or lime, timber and nails. Al-Kashshaf and al-Razi supply materials; Ibn Kathir says it was the world's tallest. Al-Mawardi cites Qatada that it was the first lofty structure of this sort; Bursevi says Israelites including Moses and Aaron were tormented in building. Ibn Ashur reports completion followed by Pharaoh's ascent. Al-Alusi notes that the accounts vary about what followed. [16 notes: THALABI(أهل التفسير); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل التفسير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(التفسير); RAZI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(صاحب كشاف); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some traditions)]
- 28:38/q16/p2 No tower was built; the command was a bluff. — Al-Razi reports rational objections: mountains do not seem nearer sky than ground and no arrow could reach it. He rejects a literal tale that would invite attack on the Quran and prefers a pretense or mockery (40:36). Abu Hayyan says an order proves no completion; al-Alusi and Elmalili transmit the unbuilt view. Ibn Ashur reports construction never began, postpones another explanation to Ghafir; the Study Quran says completion is unestablished and cites al-Razi's objections. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL(ومن الناس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Bazıları); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:38/q16/p3 It was begun but collapsed before completion. — Ibn Ashur transmits a report of an unfinished tower killing builders and soldiers when it fell. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:38/q17 What does the arrow report say Pharaoh did atop the tower?  [فَٱجْعَل لِّى صَرْحًۭا أَطَّلِعُ إِلَىٰٓ إِلَٰهِ مُوسَىٰ · reports]
- 28:38/q17/p1 He shot heavenward, received a bloodied arrow, and claimed to have killed Moses' God. — Al-Suddi's report recurs through al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Mawardi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Durr and al-Alusi. Ibn Atiyya says God returned the arrow bloodstained to deepen blindness. Al-Kashshaf questions soundness and says that, if authentic, God mocked Pharaoh; al-Alusi doubts it and calls it mockery if true. Some tellings append Gabriel's demolition. The Study Quran preserves a variant in which the returning arrow strikes Pharaoh and draws his own blood, without grading. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI(أهل التفسير); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل التفسير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(التفسير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(السدي); STUDYQURAN(some traditions); ALUSI-FULL(السدي)-; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)-]

## 28:38/q18 How do reports say the tower ended?  [صَرْحًۭا · reports]
turns on: Whether the collapse was angelic destruction of a completed tower or an unfinished construction failure.
- 28:38/q18/p1 Gabriel shattered it into three pieces, with mass deaths. — Al-Razi specifies a sunset strike, one piece killing a million soldiers, one falling into the sea and one in the west, killing workers. Al-Baghawi, al-Kashshaf, Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Nasafi give variants of the same three-piece destruction. Al-Qurtubi mentions a wing strike but questions reliability. Al-Kashshaf's arrow variant places destruction after the boast. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل التفسير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(التفسير); RAZI-FULL(قوم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 28:38/q18/p2 It collapsed unfinished, killing many workers and soldiers. — Ibn Ashur records this separate report; Elmalili also reports a built tower's collapse without details. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 28:38/q19 What was Pharaoh trying to do through the tower request?  [لَعَلِّىٓ أَطَّلِعُ إِلَىٰٓ إِلَٰهِ مُوسَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the search is literal spatial ignorance, deliberate mockery, astronomical observation, or an argument against heavenly messengers.
- 28:38/q19/p1 Reach or see a god imagined as physically in the sky. — Al-Zajjaj says he thought the tower might reach the sky; al-Baghawi says he sought God's condition; al-Kashshaf, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi describe a spatial body like Pharaoh. Al-Biqai says a literal plan exposes ignorance. Al-Wahidi says Pharaoh sought to make listeners think God needs direction; al-Bursevi's Kashifi reports his own bodily imagining. Ibn Ashur calls both reachable heaven and located God false assumptions; Tabataba'i pictures an atmospheric or celestial body and says the intended result precedes its premise. Al-Wahidi's Wajiz and Bursevi say he would look down upon or stand above God. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:38/q19/p2 Stage an impossible search to ridicule Moses and manipulate the court. — Yahya says Pharaoh knew Moses was God's messenger and lied about seeking God. Ibn Atiyya calls the sky view a pretense; al-Razi prefers mockery because rational and sensory proof are supposedly unavailable (40:36). Al-Biqai says it delays belief; Ibn Kathir says the great structure made Moses look false. Abu Hayyan says he knew ascent impossible but fear enforced compliance. Al-Alusi prefers sarcasm and rejects a proposed private agreement with Haman because the fa and flow oppose it. Ibn Ashur says a staged failed search supplies false firsthand proof; Elmalili calls it scientific-sounding; Asad compares a pyramid-like tower mocking a transcendent God; Islahi notes followers could mistake mockery for evidence; the Study Quran allows irony. [23 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 28:38/q19/p3 Build an observatory and seek signs of Moses' mission in the stars. — Al-Baydawi says planetary positions might signal prophecy or regime change; Tabataba'i cites 40:36-37 for a stellar test. Al-Alusi says the interpretation must supply a noun and take ila as ala, inspecting divine judgment through the stars, and finds it strained; he rejects identifying Moses' god with stars or their judgment. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:38/q19/p4 Demonstrate that a messenger could not descend from an inaccessible heavenly God. — Al-Alusi reports that inability to climb to such a God is made to discredit a human messenger and doubles as mockery. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:38/q20 Does this verse locate God in heaven?  [إِلَىٰٓ إِلَٰهِ مُوسَىٰ · theology]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh's characterization of Moses' God is reliable evidence of Moses' own teaching.
- 28:38/q20/p1 No; Pharaoh's words or distortions cannot establish God's location. — Jishumi rejects the anthropomorphists and Karramiyya. Al-Razi replies to their citation that Moses called God Lord of heaven and earth (26:24), while Pharaoh distorted this. Al-Wahidi says Muqatil's and al-Kalbi's readings suggest anthropomorphism and direction; al-Kalbi says Pharaoh claimed nothing existed in heaven. Bursevi's Persian verses say the maker of place and time occupies neither. Al-Alusi rejects arguments that Moses had taught bodily containment from Pharaoh's words, even with 67:16. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); RAZI-FULL(المشبهة); BURSEVI; JISHUMI(المشبهة والكرامية)-; ALUSI-FULL(بعض من يقول إن الله تعالى في السماء)-]
- 28:38/q20/p2 Some cite Pharaoh's wording to say Moses taught that God is in heaven. — Al-Razi reports the anthropomorphists' proof; al-Alusi reports proponents invoking 67:16 and claiming Pharaoh merely misunderstood heavenly location as bodily containment. Jishumi condemns the Karramiyya version; al-Alusi calls the inference very weak. [0 notes: JISHUMI(المشبهة والكرامية)-; RAZI-FULL(المشبهة)-; ALUSI-FULL(بعض من يقول إن الله تعالى في السماء)-]

## 28:38/q21 What does attali'a mean here?  [أَطَّلِعُ · meaning]
- 28:38/q21/p1 To ascend or climb up and look out, with effort. — Ibn Abbas glosses 'climb up to him'; Ibn al-Jawzi adds looking out; al-Razi says tala'a and ittala'a both denote ascent, as up a mountain. Al-Nasafi and al-Alusi say the ifti'al form here has the bare verb's meaning. Al-Biqai and Ibn Ashur stress effort and difficulty. Asad offers ascending to Moses' god as an alternative translation. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 28:38/q22 How does the construction 'that I may look' work?  [لَعَلِّىٓ أَطَّلِعُ · grammar]
- 28:38/q22/p1 The intended result is stated before its premise; the wording implies climbing and peering. — Tabataba'i identifies result before premise; Islahi calls the implied climb and look taḍmīn and compares 43:47. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:38/q23 Whom does the pronoun in 'I think him' identify?  [أَظُنُّهُۥ · referent]
- 28:38/q23/p1 Moses. — Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Biqai identify Moses; al-Biqai says the plural description brands him a habitual liar. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:38/q24 What is Moses accused of lying about?  [مِنَ ٱلْكَٰذِبِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the accusation targets the existence of Moses' God, his divine commission, or both.
- 28:38/q24/p1 That another God exists besides Pharaoh, including the God said to be in heaven. — Muqatil and al-Wahidi specify heaven; al-Tabari cites 7:66 and Ibn al-Jawzi transmits him. Ibn Kathir adopts al-Tabari over a mission-only charge, adducing Pharaoh's questions and threat in 26:23 and 26:29. Al-Baghawi says Moses claimed earth and creation have another god; al-Bursevi joins this charge with the claim of mission. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+]
- 28:38/q24/p2 That God sent Moses as a messenger. — Ibn Atiyya reads the lie as about a sender; al-Nasafi says God who sent him; al-Baghawi and Bursevi include this alongside the other god. Al-Alusi's alternative supposes Pharaoh denies knowledge of God but disputes the mission, arguing no messenger could descend from an unreachable heavenly God. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)-]

## 28:38/q25 What force does zann have here?  [لَأَظُنُّهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether zann denotes genuine uncertainty or certainty, and whether the search is sincere or staged.
- 28:38/q25/p1 Doubt or suspicion, falling short of certainty. — Al-Zajjaj says zann can be doubt or certainty but here Pharaoh admits he lacks certainty Moses lies, although signs made him suspect prophethood; Abu Ishaq through al-Wahidi agrees. Al-Qurtubi says he disbelieved while doubting despite evident proofs. Al-Baydawi says he thinks Moses false. Al-Kashshaf's literal-knowledge alternative and al-Alusi's first alternative permit doubt. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 28:38/q25/p2 Certainty or settled resolve to deny. — Al-Zajjaj notes this possible lexical force. Al-Kashshaf permits certainty under the existence-denial explanation; Ibn Atiyya calls it settled disbelief; al-Alusi cites a line of Duraid b. al-Simma and says Pharaoh certainly takes Moses for a liar. Ibn Ashur says the search reinforces his certainty or persuades the audience. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(دريد بن الصمة); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:38/q26 How are Pharaoh's contradictory claims understood?  [مَا عَلِمْتُ لَكُم مِّنْ إِلَٰهٍ غَيْرِى لَعَلِّىٓ أَطَّلِعُ إِلَىٰٓ إِلَٰهِ مُوسَىٰ لَأَظُنُّهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clauses should be reconciled as sarcasm or literal limited knowledge, or allowed to display a flaw in Pharaoh's argument.
- 28:38/q26/p1 His denial and proposed search expose a contradiction. — Jishumi says denying other gods yet looking for Moses' God is typical of deviants; al-Nasafi adds that Haman's help and admission of uncertain accusation compound it. Al-Alusi reports al-Zamakhshari that the people missed it or were afraid, and permits ignorance or fear if no reconciliation works. [4 notes: JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 28:38/q26/p2 The clash is resolved by taking the proposed search as irony or the denial as merely epistemic. — Al-Alusi first prefers reconciliation, especially a sarcastic search. He rejects a separate public-claim/private-Haman-agreement hypothesis because the flow and fa do not support it. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 28:38/q27 Why did Pharaoh's audience accept the tower argument?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلْمَلَأُ لَعَلِّىٓ أَطَّلِعُ · interpretation]
- 28:38/q27/p1 Many were credulous; others complied from fear or self-interest. — Al-Kashshaf says the attempt shows Pharaoh's and his nobles' gross ignorance but allows deliberate deception of gullible people. Abu Hayyan says rational people may obey violent Pharaoh from fear. Al-Alusi says most were extraordinarily ignorant, while some recognized the bluff but feared him or wanted favors. Al-Zamakhshari through al-Alusi similarly suggests foolishness or fear. Ibn Ashur calls the courtiers' thinking weak; Islahi says followers who viewed Pharaoh as Sun God's avatar might take mockery literally. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:38/q28 What lesson does this episode draw about false belief?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:38/q28/p1 False imaginations and deceptive leaders lead people astray. — Ibn Ashur says deviant beliefs rest on false imagination and sophistry; al-Razi says Pharaoh habitually deployed sophisms against Moses' proofs; Jishumi calls the contradiction characteristic of innovators. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL; JISHUMI]

## 28:38/q29 What follows Pharaoh's refusal in the wider story?  [* · links]
- 28:38/q29/p1 Moses leads Israel across the opened Red Sea; Pharaoh drowns, and his despairing faith is not accepted. — Kuranyolu cites 7:103, 7:138 and 7:136 for the calamities, departure, crossing and drowning, and 10:90-91 for the rejected drowning confession. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:38/q30 What reading of the possessive ya' is noted?  [لَعَلِّىٓ لِّى · readings]
- 28:38/q30/p1 The final first-person ya' is pronounced with fatha without changing the word's root, sense or syntax. — Qiraat-ER states this; Ibn Mujahid says Nafi opens all but ma'iya rid'an among twelve disputed possessive ya's, while Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr open nine but leave inni urid, satajiduni in sha Allah, and ma'iya rid'an quiescent. [3 notes: QIRAAT-ER; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,نافع)]

## 28:38/q31 How is Moses' preceding speech read and linked to Pharaoh's reply?  [وَقَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ · readings]
turns on: Presence of the conjunction and whether the discourse is direct response or juxtaposition.
- 28:38/q31/p1 Ibn Kathir reads 'qala Musa' without waw, following Meccan codices. — Al-Kashshaf prefers it as a direct response to Pharaoh's charge that the signs were sorcery or fabrication. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن كثير); KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 28:38/q31/p2 The reading with waw juxtaposes opposed claims. — Al-Kashshaf says comparison lets observers see one false and one true: opposites clarify. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 28:38/q32 What is reported about al-Qasas's revelation?  [* · sciences]
- 28:38/q32/p1 The whole sura is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzi transmits this from al-Hasan, Ata and Ikrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:38/q33 How does hope language here fit al-Qasas beside al-Naml?  [لَعَلِّىٓ · links]
- 28:38/q33/p1 Hope characterizes al-Qasas, while al-Naml favors confidence and certainty. — Al-Samarrai counts ten hopeful expressions in al-Qasas, including 28:38, versus two in al-Naml. Moses' fear at 28:29 explains his uncertain speech against 27:7; al-Naml's honor for Moses suits its confident wording. The hoopoe and throne-bringers speak confidently (27:22, 27:39-40). The Halaqat version gives the same ten-versus-two contrast and cites 27:7, 27:46. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:38/q34 Why do repeated Moses and Pharaoh passages differ?  [* · links]
- 28:38/q34/p1 Each retelling contributes a distinct nuance of wording, sequence or detail. — Al-Burhan contrasts the distributed Moses and Pharaoh account across al-Qasas (28:3-43) with Joseph's story gathered in one sura, unlike earlier scripture's single accounts. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 28:38/q35 What devotional use is reported for verses evoking Pharaoh?  [* · reports]
- 28:38/q35/p1 Sufyan al-Thawri reportedly wrote a charm for a divorced woman. — The charm, worn over her heart, invoked verses about sky and earth opening and Pharaoh emerging. [1 notes: BURHAN(سفيان الثوري)]

## 28:38/q36 How do the cited sun comparisons express nearness and distance?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:38/q36/p1 They make the beloved celestial and thus reachable in light yet inaccessible in person. — Al-Jurjani says Ibn Abi Uyaynah's categorical 'she is the sun' gives the fact itself, not an explicit simile, and does not deny comparison. Bashshar explicitly compares a distant moon and al-Mutanabbi the sun whose rays approach but cannot be grasped. Beauty is a customary secondary implication; the direct point is simultaneous nearness and distance. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 28:38/q37 What does the failure to classify the Quran as poetry show?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:38/q37/p1 Its eloquent first opponents did not regard it as poetry. — Al-Baqillani argues that poetry was easy for those Arabs and they urgently wanted to discredit the Quran; their failure to exploit the charge makes later critics' alleged discovery implausible. [2 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 28:38/q38 Which later threat echoes Pharaoh's claim of exclusive divinity?  [* · links]
- 28:38/q38/p1 His threat to imprison Moses for taking another god. — Al-Alusi links this to Pharaoh's earlier declaration that his people have no god besides him, citing 26:29. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
