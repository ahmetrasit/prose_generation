# 28:78: 34 questions

## 28:78/q01 How does Qarun's reply answer his advisers?  [قَالَ إِنَّمَآ أُوتِيتُهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 28:78/q01/p1 He silences their advice by crediting himself and denying an unearned favor. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he evades their demand; al-Rāzī calls the prior counsel complete. Al-Biqāʿī says إنما restricts the cause and the passive obscures the giver. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the particle and its spelling; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says he omits God and claims freedom to pursue luxury and power. Ibn ʿAbbās says Qarun's self-crediting rejected God's gift; ʿAbd Allāh ibn al-Ḥārith contrasts Solomon's acknowledgment of grace at 27:40. [20 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل)]

## 28:78/q02 What is the thing Qarun says he was given?  [أُوتِيتُهُۥ · referent]
- 28:78/q02/p1 His wealth or treasures. — Ibn ʿĀshūr traces it to 'what God gave you' in 28:77; the passive leaves the giver implicit. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:78/q04 How is 'on knowledge' construed?  [عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ · grammar]
turns on: Attachment and the agent of knowledge differ.
- 28:78/q04/p1 It is circumstantial to the passive verb's subject. — Al-Ālūsī says most grammarians use it to express Qarun's claimed entitlement. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:78/q04/p2 It attaches causally to 'I was given.' — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says ʿalā can express superiority or cause. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI]
- 28:78/q04/p3 The verbal noun 'knowledge' can be Qarun's or God's. — Abū Ḥayyān gives both; Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions further undetailed analyses. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرون)]

## 28:78/q05 How did Qarun allegedly acquire alchemy?  [عِلْمٍ · reports]
turns on: The teachers and route of transmission vary.
- 28:78/q05/p1 Moses divided the art among Qarun, Joshua and Caleb; Qarun tricked the others into sharing. — Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib and al-Ḍaḥḥāk are cited; al-Rāzī specifies lead to silver and copper to gold. [8 notes: THALABI(سعيد بن المسيب); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); KASHSHAF-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب); RAZI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب والضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن المسيب); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 28:78/q05/p2 Moses taught his sister, who taught Qarun. — Al-Qurṭubī calls her Qarun's wife; Bursawī names her Kulthūm. [5 notes: THALABI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:78/q05/p3 Moses, Joshua and Aaron or his sons supplied separate shares. — Al-Naqqāsh's versions vary on teachers, shares and deception. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); TABRISI(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش)]
- 28:78/q05/p4 The attribution is doubtful. — Abū Ḥayyān doubts it and cites al-Zajjāj; al-Ālūsī says a miracle could not have belonged to Qarun. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:78/q06 What story explains Qarun's move from worship to wealth?  [أُوتِيتُهُۥ · reports]
- 28:78/q06/p1 Iblis gradually redirected forty years of worship into work and attachment to wealth. — Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī says Iblis outdid Qarun in worship, persuaded him to abandon communal and charitable duties, then alternate work with worship, after which wealth poured in. Bursawī retells the progression. [2 notes: THALABI(أبو سلمان الداراني); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 28:78/q07 Is alchemy a real and possible craft?  [عِلْمٍ · theology]
turns on: Whether metals share a species, whether craft prepares a God-created form, and whether a reliable method exists.
- 28:78/q07/p1 Human craft cannot genuinely transmute metal. — Al-Zajjāj calls alchemy unreal. Ibn Kathīr says only God changes created kinds, citing 22:73 and a hadith challenge. Ibn Sīnā treats metals as distinct species. Other objections cite unknown ratios, fire resistance, art versus nature, mines, gold's economic rarity, absence of a reproducible method and a faulty yeast analogy. [29 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن الله تعالى); BURSEVI(الزجاج,الكواشي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن تيمية,ابن خلدون,ابن سينا,الزجاج,قوم من الفلاسفة); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)+; BURSEVI-; ALUSI-FULL(جابر بن حيان)-]
- 28:78/q07/p2 It is a possible but very difficult natural craft. — Bursawī cites purification, saints and personal witness. Al-Fārābī and Ibn Bājjah treat metals as one species with alterable qualities. Al-Tughrāʾī says craft prepares matter for a God-created form; al-Rāzī argues from medicine, antidotes, heat, artificial fire and salts. Al-Ālūsī favors a rare hidden craft and answers Ibn Khaldūn's objections. [29 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن باجة,ارس,الإمام الرازي,الطغرائي,الطيبي,الفارابي,جابر بن حيان,جماعة أجلّة); ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]
- 28:78/q07/p3 A real transmutation would be a miracle, wonder or other supernatural effect. — Ibn Kathīr cites Ḥaywa ibn Shurayḥ's God-given wonder; Ibn Khaldūn likens it to walking on water. Al-Ṭībī suggests a miracle, which al-Ālūsī says cannot fit Qarun. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(حيوة بن شريح المصري); ALUSI-FULL(ابن خلدون,الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:78/q07/p4 Its ontological or practical possibility remains unresolved. — Ibn Sīnā admits coloring metals but doubts a changed species form; al-Rāzī says visible differences may be accidental. Al-Ālūsī notes scholarly vacillation. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن سينا,الإمام الرازي)]
- 28:78/q07/p5 Practitioners propose accelerated natural mineral growth or an elixir. — Ibn Khaldūn describes intensified natural powers over 1,080 great solar cycles, or a yeast-like elixir; this would require knowledge of element ratios, innate heat and every stage. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن خلدون)]

## 28:78/q08 What does the word 'alchemy' itself derive from?  [عِلْمٍ · meaning]
turns on: The proposed source language and derivation differ.
- 28:78/q08/p1 It may be Greek, meaning artifice or trick. — Al-Ālūsī reports this etymology. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:78/q08/p2 It may derive from Hebrew words meaning 'it is from God.' — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposed derivation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:78/q08/p3 It may derive from Persian words meaning 'when by a particular method.' — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposed derivation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:78/q09 What accounts explain alchemy's origin and concealment?  [عِلْمٍ · reports]
turns on: The alleged transmission and reliability of the accounts differ.
- 28:78/q09/p1 The art came by revelation and passed among prophets and sages. — Ars, Hermes, Marya, Dhu Simus, al-Ṭughrāʾī and al-Ṭarsūsī link it variously to Seth, Moses, Joseph, Solomon and Adam. An ungraded report mentions the Prophet and white and red sulfur; reports also name Jaʿfar and ʿAlī. Al-Ālūsī doubts hadith authenticity. [12 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ارس,الطرسوسي,الطغرائي,جعفر الصادق,ذو سيموس,رسول الله صلى الله تعالى عليه وسلم,علي,مارية,هرمس)]
- 28:78/q09/p2 Sages hid it in symbols and taught selected readers. — Jābir says secrecy protects work and the economy; al-Ālūsī says literal readers are misled and qualified teachers rare. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(جابر بن حيان)]
- 28:78/q09/p3 Modern claimants include swindlers and ignorant seekers. — Al-Ālūsī cites greed, false hopes and neglected work; al-Ṭughrāʾī faults Ibn Sīnā's missing mixtures, weights, stages and tools. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن سينا,الطغرائي)]
- 28:78/q09/p4 Claims of prophetic authority remain unverified. — Al-Ālūsī says accepted hadith would be widespread and notes Ibn Taymiyya's rejection and a qualified claim about Najm al-Dīn al-Baghdādī. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن تيمية)]

## 28:78/q10 What inward lesson is drawn from Qarun?  [إِنَّمَآ أُوتِيتُهُۥ عَلَىٰ عِلْمٍ عِندِىٓ · inward]
turns on: Self-crediting versus recognition of divine grace.
- 28:78/q10/p1 Self-admiration and claims to one's own states or deeds bring ruin; the fortunate see God's favor. — Sahl links outward sinking to inward loss of protection, ingratitude and inflated power. Al-Qushayrī calls imagined independent agency deadly poison; Bursawī cites Hafez and Saib on pride. [13 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL(سهل); BURSEVI(الحافظ,الصائب); STUDYQURAN(Sufis)]
- 28:78/q10/p2 Knowledge should first be attributed to God. — Al-Jishumī says God creates necessary knowledge and makes acquired knowledge possible. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 28:78/q11 What does 'with me' mean and how does it attach?  [عِندِىٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase locates knowledge or qualifies the claim as Qarun's judgment.
- 28:78/q11/p1 It means 'in my opinion' and attaches to the gift or stands as an independent clause. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zamakhsharī compare 39:49; al-Kashf prefers an omitted-subject sentence. [14 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(آخرون); NAHHAS(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد وغيره); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف)+]
- 28:78/q11/p2 It modifies 'knowledge': knowledge in Qarun's possession. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī permit it; Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this as fitting the rebuke. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 28:78/q12 How is the final yāʾ of 'with me' read?  [عِندِىٓ · readings]
turns on: Transmission determines opening or stilling the yāʾ.
- 28:78/q12/p1 Nāfiʿ, Abū Jaʿfar and Abū ʿAmr open it; Ibn Kathīr varies by route. — Ibn Mujāhid lists related yāʾs; Ibn al-Jazarī differentiates Maghribi, Egyptian and Iraqi routes. Qiraat-ER says the dialectal difference has no semantic effect. [6 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو,نافع); NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو,نافع وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو,نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]
- 28:78/q12/p2 Other readers leave it quiescent. — Ibn al-Jazarī gives the opposing reading. [4 notes: NASHR(المدنيان وأبو عمرو,نافع وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو,نافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:78/q13 What does 'Did he not know?' imply?  [أَوَلَمْ يَعْلَمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Known history ignored versus genuinely absent useful knowledge.
- 28:78/q13/p1 Qarun knew or could know earlier fates but ignored them. — The Torah, Moses and historians could teach him. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads astonished rebuke; al-Iṣlāḥī cites Pharaoh's drowning; Bursawī quotes a Persian warning against trusting wealth. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(المفسرون); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 28:78/q13/p2 The question denies he had useful knowledge despite his boast. — Al-Zamakhsharī contrasts claimed merit with ignorance of historical lessons; al-Ālūsī supplies elliptical and circumstantial parsings. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:78/q14 Who are the earlier destroyed generations?  [مِن قَبْلِهِۦ مِنَ ٱلْقُرُونِ · referent]
turns on: Different named examples are supplied for the collective.
- 28:78/q14/p1 Earlier unbelievers who denied messengers. — Al-Ṭabrisī names ʿĀd, Thamūd and Lot's people. [13 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:78/q14/p2 Nimrod ibn Canaan is one named example. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil name him; Bursawī notes his riches. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BURSEVI]
- 28:78/q14/p3 Pharaoh is one named example. — Al-Biqāʿī says he consulted Qarun yet perished; al-Iṣlāḥī cites his drowning. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

- 28:78/q15/p1 It restricts comparison to preceding generations. — Al-Biqāʿī says omission of min would widen the scope. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:78/q16 What does 'generations' mean?  [ٱلْقُرُونِ · meaning]
turns on: Temporal definition and etymological image.
- 28:78/q16/p1 A people living in one period. — Bursawī gives this gloss. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 28:78/q16/p2 The word evokes the hardness of horns. — Al-Biqāʿī gives this etymological image. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:78/q17 How are the phrases about the past generations parsed?  [مِن قَبْلِهِۦ مِنَ ٱلْقُرُونِ مَنْ هُوَ أَشَدُّ · grammar]
turns on: Attachment of the phrases.
- 28:78/q17/p1 'Who' is the object of 'destroyed,' relative or qualified indefinite. — Al-Samīn gives both analyses. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 28:78/q17/p2 'Before him' attaches to 'destroyed.' — Al-Samīn gives this attachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 28:78/q17/p3 'From the generations' attaches to 'destroyed' or qualifies 'who is stronger.' — Al-Samīn gives both. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 28:78/q18 What is the greater 'strength'?  [قُوَّةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: The scope of power.
- 28:78/q18/p1 Bodily, military or material power. — Al-Qurṭubī gives equipment; Bursawī numbers; al-Iṣlāḥī weapons and grandeur; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 8:60. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:78/q18/p2 Moral, intellectual or devotional strength. — Al-Biqāʿī adds knowledge; al-Ālūsī moral power; Bursawī worship, comparing Iblis. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:78/q19 What is a 'greater gathering'?  [جَمْعًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Collected property, people or, in one report, knowledge.
- 28:78/q19/p1 More followers, servants, helpers, family or tribe. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives a gathered group and transmits a count of 250 Israelite leaders among Qarun's followers; al-Iṣlāḥī specifies family or tribe. [13 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 28:78/q19/p2 More accumulated property. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others allow amassed wealth. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:78/q19/p3 More knowledge and worship. — Bursawī reports this with Iblis as comparison. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 28:78/q20 What is the lesson of their destruction?  [قَدْ أَهْلَكَ قُوَّةًۭ جَمْعًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The comparison tests wealth as a sign of merit and safety.
- 28:78/q20/p1 Strength, wealth and followers do not prove favor or prevent punishment. — Al-Ṭabarī and al-Qurṭubī reject wealth as proof of merit; al-Rāzī says multiplied resources cannot resist God. Ibn Kathīr names unbelief and ingratitude. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ says earlier peoples too had prosperity through skill; Study Quran cites 6:6, 19:74, 40:82, 47:13, 50:36. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:78/q20/p2 They were destroyed for denying messengers or wrongdoing. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī specify denial; others call them unbelievers. [5 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 28:78/q21 Whose sins does 'their sins' mean?  [ذُنُوبِهِمُ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent may be the criminals or the destroyed peoples.
- 28:78/q21/p1 The earlier generations' sins, about which later criminals are not asked. — Muqātil and Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb give this antecedent; al-Wāḥidī calls it weak and al-Ālūsī dismisses it. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TAB-FULL-; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 28:78/q21/p2 The criminals' own sins. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers this: no innocent person is asked for another's sin. Al-Ālūsī also uses this antecedent. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL+]

## 28:78/q22 In what sense are criminals not asked about sins?  [وَلَا يُسْـَٔلُ عَن ذُنُوبِهِمُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether denial concerns accounting, information, identification, excuse, or immediate worldly punishment.
- 28:78/q22/p1 Angels recognize criminals by their marks. — Mujāhid cites 55:41: blue eyes and blackened faces; al-Ṭabrisī adds seizure by forelocks and feet. Al-Wāḥidī objects that the verse concerns sins, not identification. [22 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(غيره); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة ومجاهد); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي)+; WAHIDI-BASIT-]
- 28:78/q22/p2 They enter punishment without reckoning or questioning. — Qatāda and al-Suddī say punishment is direct; al-Qurṭubī says sins are too many and obvious. Al-Wāḥidī prefers Qatāda. Kuranyolu disputes an inference that accountability vanishes. [21 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(بعضهم); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة)+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 28:78/q22/p3 God needs no inquiry because He already knows the facts. — Al-Biqāʿī contrasts human rulers who lack knowledge; records, limbs and manifest guilt furnish further evidence. Kuranyolu retains accountability. [18 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة,محمد بن كعب); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 28:78/q22/p4 The denied question seeks information; questioning elsewhere is rebuke. — Al-Ḥasan, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others distinguish inquiry from reproach, citing 15:92, 37:24 and 55:39. - 28:78/q22/p5 They cannot present excuses, amends or a plea for satisfaction. — Ibn Baḥr cites 30:57; Abū Muslim prefers denial of amends, citing 16:84 and 77:35–36. Al-Qurṭubī cites 41:24. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Q); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)+; TABATABAI+]
- 28:78/q22/p6 Later wrongdoers are not asked about earlier nations' sins or fates. — Muqātil says God recorded those deeds; Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb says later people failed to learn from them. Al-Wāḥidī calls Muqātil's reading weak. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(محمد بن كعب); IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:78/q22/p7 In this world God can seize them suddenly without further hearing. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the messengers' prior clarification, 6:44 and an ungraded prophetic report; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ prefers this context. [12 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة,محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI+; ALUSI-FULL-]

- 28:78/q22/p9 The warning states manifest guilt and certain ruin while retaining accountability. — Kuranyolu rejects the idea that no accounting occurs; al-Biqāʿī says the noun 'criminals' makes the cause explicit and derives جرم from wrongful severing and joining. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:78/q23 Does the final sentence concern this world or Judgment?  [وَلَا يُسْـَٔلُ عَن ذُنُوبِهِمُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Immediate worldly context versus Judgment scene.
- 28:78/q23/p1 It concerns worldly destruction or seizure. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb joins it to earlier destruction; Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ read sudden worldly punishment. Al-Ālūsī rejects this. [12 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة,محمد بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TABATABAI+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:78/q23/p2 It is an afterlife warning. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a Resurrection scene; al-Ālūsī prefers an extension to the harsher afterlife. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]

## 28:78/q24 How is denied questioning reconciled with promised questioning?  [وَلَا يُسْـَٔلُ · links]
turns on: Different kinds of question versus different times.
- 28:78/q24/p1 Other questioning rebukes or confirms; this denies information-seeking or amends. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this, comparing 37:24 and 55:39; al-Ḥasan cites 15:92. Abū Muslim distinguishes accounting, rebuke and opportunity to make amends. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; THALABI(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,الحسن,قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(R); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم)+]
- 28:78/q24/p2 The passages describe different stages, groups or times. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports stations and groups; al-Rāzī assigns two times; al-Ālūsī notes Judgment's length. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الناس); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 28:78/q25 How is the final clause read?  [وَلَا يُسْـَٔلُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · readings]
turns on: Person, voice and case differ.
- 28:78/q25/p1 Abū Jaʿfar, Ibn Sīrīn and Abū al-ʿĀliya read a second-person prohibition. — Abū Ḥayyān says it addresses the Prophet; Abū Jaʿfar has accusative al-mujrimīn. Al-Ālūsī is unsure of the other case endings. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر,ابن أبي إسحاق); SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر,ابن أبي إسحاق,ابن سيرين وأبو العالية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية وابن سيرين,أبو جعفر)]
- 28:78/q25/p2 A nominative noun under the prohibition is proposed but disputed. — Al-Lawāmiḥ offers ellipsis or substitution; Abū Ḥayyān finds the analogy unsupported and al-Samīn calls it strained. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق,صاحب اللوامح); SAMIN-DURR(صاحب اللوامح); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب اللوامح); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 28:78/q25/p3 The common reading is third-person passive with nominative al-mujrimūn. — Al-Samīn calls it the majority reading. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]

## 28:78/q26 How is 'the criminals' parsed under the variant reading?  [ذُنُوبِهِمُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether nominative is licensed or accusative required.
- 28:78/q26/p1 A nominative can be supplied through ellipsis. — Al-Lawāmiḥ proposes an implicit subject or predicate. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب اللوامح); SAMIN-DURR(صاحب اللوامح); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب اللوامح)]
- 28:78/q26/p2 A nominative can substitute for the underlying case of the pronoun in the verbal noun. — Al-Lawāmiḥ compares 2:26; al-Samīn calls it strained and al-Ālūsī notes disputed agreement. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب اللوامح); SAMIN-DURR(صاحب اللوامح); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب اللوامح); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 28:78/q26/p3 It takes accusative case as the active verb's object. — Ibn Abī Isḥāq requires this; al-Samīn agrees. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن أبي إسحاق); SAMIN-DURR(ابن أبي إسحاق); SAMIN-DURR+]

## 28:78/q27 Can the pronoun precede its antecedent here?  [ذُنُوبِهِمُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · grammar]
- 28:78/q27/p1 Yes, when the referent is already present in meaning or syntactic rank. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī distinguish word order from intended or grammatical order. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 28:78/q28 How does the closing sentence relate to the earlier destruction?  [وَلَا يُسْـَٔلُ عَن ذُنُوبِهِمُ ٱلْمُجْرِمُونَ · links]
turns on: Independent conclusion versus syntactic continuation.
- 28:78/q28/p1 It is an independent summary or further threat. — Al-Zamakhsharī calls it concluding; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it is uncoordinated. Al-Ālūsī extends the worldly threat to the afterlife and rejects circumstantial attachment. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:78/q28/p2 It attaches to the destroyed peoples: each is punished for his own sin without inquiry. — Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb and Abū Ḥayyān report this; al-Ālūsī rejects attachment. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(محمد بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة,محمد بن كعب); ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 28:78/q29 What wider warning does the episode convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 28:78/q29/p1 Prosperity is a divine favor; self-crediting blocks attention to warnings. — Al-Iṣlāḥī cites a wealthy Jewish capitalist; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ points to overlooked destiny and circumstances; Asad says sinners ignore their faults. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 28:78/q29/p2 God can remove knowledge, wealth and life. — Al-Biqāʿī says Qarun had no independent agency in receiving them. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:78/q30 How does the verse connect with surrounding discourse?  [* · links]
- 28:78/q30/p1 It follows counsel to Qarun and links worldly destruction to judgment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the question is parenthetical and the discourse turns to Muslims; al-Biqāʿī connects prior helplessness in the Hereafter to worldly destruction. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:78/q30/p2 Other verses repeat the warning against presumptuous wealth. — Ibn Kathīr cites 39:49 and 41:50; Study Quran cites 6:6, 19:74, 40:82, 47:13, 50:36; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites 39:52 and 40:82–83. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:78/q31 What do the cited notes say about Arabic poetic rhyme?  [* · meaning]
- 28:78/q31/p1 Iqwaʾ is a rhyme fault involving vowel or metrical variation. — Ibn Qutayba records Abu ʿUbayda's raised/lowered vowel definition, a raised/genitive variant, Ibn al-Riqāʿ's revision image, Dhu al-Rumma's avoidance of sanad and muḥāl, and a line fixed by مشروبة. He prefers a derivation from quwwa, a rope strand. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(أبو عبيدة,ابن الرقاع,الشاعر,بعضهم,ذو الرمة); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL+]

## 28:78/q32 What wider expressive powers of Arabic are discussed?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:78/q32/p1 Qur'anic expression uses figurative and allusive modes and carefully joined passages. — Ibn Qutayba lists metaphor, inversion, ellipsis, changes of number and address, and says translation cannot carry the full Arabic range. Al-Bāqillānī illustrates allusion and transitions through 17:1–3, from the Night Journey through Moses, Israel and Noah. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BAQILLANI]
- 28:78/q32/p2 Circumstantial waw and fronted pronouns carry specific emphasis. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir cites 7:196, 25:5, 27:17, 23:59, 36:7, 28:66 and 8:55 for subject-plus-verb and stronger negation. [5 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 28:78/q33 What is said about transmission requirements?  [* · reports]
- 28:78/q33/p1 Hadith transmission requires narrated receipt or authorization; no parallel Qur'an requirement is established. — Ibn Khayr claims hadith consensus; al-Suyūṭī prefers the distinction because the Qur'an is preserved and widely received. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن خير); ITQAN+]

## 28:78/q34 What is reported about the place of revelation of al-Qasas?  [* · sciences]
- 28:78/q34/p1 One report says the entire sūra was revealed in Mecca. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]
