# 20:54: 17 questions

## 20:54/q01 What do the commands to eat and pasture livestock mean?  [كُلُوا۟ وَٱرْعَوْا۟ أَنْعَٰمَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the commands describe material use of vegetation, a broader idiom of benefit, or an inward reading.
- 20:54/q01/p1 People may eat the plants and fruits God produced and let their livestock graze on the vegetation. — Yahya, Wahidi, and others connect both uses to the rain-grown plants just described. Kashshaf distinguishes human food from animal fodder; Ibn Ashur says the different plant kinds are distributed between them. Bursawi specifies fruits and grains; Tabari says the plants provide livestock provisions. Ibn Atiyya says the chosen actions are especially desirable and calming. Wahidi cites Ibn Abbas's comparison with 80:27–32. Ibn Jawzi describes turning animals loose in pasture. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:54/q01/p2 Eating extends to every beneficial use of what God produced. — Razi compares Qur'anic idioms of consuming wealth in 2:188 and 4:10. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:54/q01/p3 Inwardly, eating means receiving fitting spiritual states and provisions, while grazing livestock concerns the animal faculties and their proper morals and manners. — Qashani lists contentment, patience, knowledge, perceptions, intentions, stations, morals, and gifts among the nourishment. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:54/q02 How is the command to eat and pasture attached to the preceding sentence?  [كُلُوا۟ وَٱرْعَوْا۟ أَنْعَٰمَكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: The attachment of the omitted saying: to the act of bringing forth or to the plants.
- 20:54/q02/p1 An omitted saying introduces the commands as a circumstance of God's bringing forth the plants. — Razi attaches the circumstance to the pronoun of 'We brought forth'; Samīn specifies the subject of أخرجنا. Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, Bursawi, and Ibn Ashur explain the implied saying and permission. Samīn adds that the object of 'eat' is omitted, as in 2:60. Alusi prefers this attachment to the alternative. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:54/q02/p2 The implied saying is attached to the plants brought forth. — Alusi reports this alternative and judges the attachment to God's act more apt. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:54/q03 What is the force of the commands to eat and pasture?  [كُلُوا۟ وَٱرْعَوْا۟ أَنْعَٰمَكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 20:54/q03/p1 They grant permission to use the plants and remind people of God's blessing. — Wahidi explicitly permits grazing in rain-grown pasture; Tabrisi says the order is permissive. Razi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi, and Ibn Ashur likewise treat it as permission. Ibn Jawzi stresses remembrance of blessing; Ibn Atiyya says the imperatives choose comforting and desirable actions. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:54/q04 What does 'pasture your livestock' mean in Arabic usage?  [وَٱرْعَوْا۟ أَنْعَٰمَكُمْ · meaning]
- 20:54/q04/p1 The livestock graze; a keeper pastures them by letting them out and tending them, using the verb transitively here. — Thalabi, Wahidi, Baghawi, Jishumi, Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan reporting Zajjaj, Alusi reporting Zajjaj, Samīn, and Ibn Ashur attest both intransitive animal grazing and transitive human pasturing. Wahidi specifies letting out and bringing back. Ibn Ashur gives الرعي and الرعاية as their respective infinitives and cites al-Nabigha for the transitive use. Bursawi says the root concerns protecting an animal's life through feeding or warding off harm; he names camels, cattle, sheep, and goats here. [11 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الزجاج); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:54/q05 What does providing food and pasture show about the care of creatures?  [كُلُوا۟ وَٱرْعَوْا۟ أَنْعَٰمَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the provision is described as a practical blessing or as the purpose of the created order.
- 20:54/q05/p1 God provides people with cultivated food and livestock with otherwise unusable plants, allowing people direct and indirect benefit. — Abu Hayyan reports the kindness of supplying fodder from surplus people cannot eat. Biqai contrasts cultivated human food with the surplus animals consume. Bursawi notes both direct and indirect livestock benefits; Study Quran points to cattle's benefits in 6:142 and 16:5–7. Alusi and Ibn Ashur distinguish the two uses of vegetation. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:54/q05/p2 All creatures and provisions serve human livelihood and the needs of creatures on their journey to God. — Bursawi reports the Najmiyya claim that sky, water, plants, and livestock were made for human livelihood and would not otherwise have been created. He quotes al-Maghribi's poem that the world exists for its addressee. Biqai says provisions fit every creature across changing conditions until presentation before God. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,المغربي)]

## 20:54/q06 What does 'in that' point to as signs?  [فِى ذَٰلِكَ لَءَايَٰتٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'that' gathers the created provisions broadly, their visible plant diversity specifically, or an earlier punitive act.
- 20:54/q06/p1 The signs lie in God's acts of arranging earth, paths, rain, plants, and provisions. — Abu Hayyan lists the earth as resting place, its paths, rain, and plants. Alusi calls these God's acts and affairs. Ibn Ashur says the closing sentence gathers the signs in creation; Bursawi calls the divine acts numerous and clear. Nasafi glosses signs as indications. Razi points to the just described blessings. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:54/q06/p2 The signs are specifically in the plants' differing colors and tastes. — Farra and Ibn Jawzi identify the plants' diversity; Ibn Ashur additionally describes differing forms and kinds suitable for people or animals. [3 notes: FARRA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:54/q06/p3 The signs include earlier divine punishment. — Tabari says the transmitted exegetes agree with his account. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]

## 20:54/q07 What do the signs establish?  [لَءَايَٰتٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Which divine claims the created provisions are taken to demonstrate.
- 20:54/q07/p1 They show God's oneness, lordship, power, wisdom, and provision, inviting reflection and admonition. — Muqatil calls the food and pasture a lesson in oneness. Tabari says provisions display power and sovereignty and points to 10:67; Wahidi also sees oneness in rain and plants. Ibn Kathir calls the earth's signs proofs that God alone is deity and Lord. Bursawi adds immense power and wisdom; Alusi calls the many clear signs proofs of God's attributes and essence. Ibn Ashur includes the Creator's existence and blessing. Islahi says the created order shows providence, wisdom, power, and mercy, and that only its arranger deserves worship and obedience. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:54/q07/p2 The complete fitness of provisions for changing needs proves that God does not err or forget. — Biqai contrasts divine provision with a human maker who later discovers product defects and repeatedly repairs them. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:54/q07/p3 The created order also supports judgment. — Islahi links providence and mercy in these first two verses with monotheism and judgment. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:54/q08 Why does 'that' use a distant demonstrative?  [ذَٰلِكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the distance is rhetorical elevation or ordinary absence.
- 20:54/q08/p1 Its distance signals the lofty rank and perfection of God's acts. — Alusi gives this as one explanation of the demonstrative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:54/q08/p2 It is distant because what it refers to is absent. — Alusi records this alternative. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:54/q09 Why are 'signs' indefinite?  [لَءَايَٰتٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 20:54/q09/p1 The indefiniteness magnifies their number and quality. — Alusi says they are many, great, and clear proofs of divine attributes and essence. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:54/q10 Why is the assertion of signs emphasized?  [إِنَّ لَءَايَٰتٍۢ · rhetoric]
- 20:54/q10/p1 The audience acts as if it denies the signs despite claiming wisdom. — Ibn Ashur says reflection and reminders would uncover the many indications; inna therefore reinforces the assertion. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:54/q11 Who are 'possessors of al-nuhā'?  [لِّأُو۟لِى ٱلنُّهَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the phrase names the faculty of reason, the piety it entails, or the conduct of avoiding prohibitions.
- 20:54/q11/p1 They are people of intellect, understanding, insight, or sound judgment. — Al-Hasan, Farra, Ibn Qutayba, Tabari, Zajjaj, Suddi, Wahidi, Jishumi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, and others give this sense. Ibn Qutayba cites Dhu al-Rumma's dhī nihya for understanding. Wahidi's Ibn Abbas report describes insightful people who restrain disobedience; Durr also reports Ibn Abbas on intelligence and sound judgment. Mawrdi lists judgment among the senses. [28 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,اللحياني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:54/q11/p2 They are people of piety and scrupulousness. — Yahya and Tabari report Qatada; Tabari, Thalabi, Tabrisi, Durr, Wahidi, and Alusi report Ibn Abbas in various transmissions. Thalabi and Baghawi report Qatada's scrupulousness; Mawrdi gives this as another sense. Alusi treats the piety report as interpretation by implication. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); THALABI(علي بن أبي طلحة عن ابن عباس,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(ابن عباس,قتادة); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:54/q11/p3 They are those who refrain from what God has forbidden. — Thalabi, Baghawi, and Tabrisi transmit this from al-Dahhak; Durr reports Sufyan similarly. Wahidi's Ibn Abbas report says they restrain themselves from disobedience and notes a similar explanation from al-Dahhak. [5 notes: THALABI(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(الضحاك); DURR-FULL(سفيان)]

## 20:54/q12 What grammatical form is al-nuhā?  [ٱلنُّهَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the form is a plural of nuhya or a verbal noun or collective usable for multiple possessors.
- 20:54/q12/p1 It is the plural of nuhya or nahya, an intellect. — Tabari, Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya, Tabrisi, Qurtubi, Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, and Tabatabai state the singular. Wahidi says addition to 'possessors' supports plurality; Alusi says most commentators take the plural. Samīn calls the weak-final verbal noun an unusual fifth example while also reporting the plural view. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); TABATABAI]
- 20:54/q12/p2 It can be a singular verbal noun or collective expression for reason. — Abu Ali al-Farisi, reported by Wahidi, Razi, Abu Hayyan, Samīn, and Alusi, compares hudā; Wahidi also compares ẓulum for the plural and says a singular verbal noun can stand for a plural. Samīn adds surā as a parallel. Ibn Ashur calls it a collective noun. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); RAZI-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:54/q13 Why is reason called al-nuhā?  [ٱلنُّهَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name derives from restraining oneself or others, or from reaching and following authoritative judgment.
- 20:54/q13/p1 Reason restrains its possessor from shameful, forbidden, false, or destructive conduct and directs good action. — Tabari adds intellect, comprehension, and religion as restraints on harm. Zajjaj and Ibn Jawzi say reason also leads to good. Lihyani, reported by Wahidi, says the mind stops at what it is commanded and does not overstep. Abu Ali derives it from confinement, like a place holding water; Wahidi says the etymologies converge. Bursawi and Ibn Ashur compare ʿaql and ḥijr; Study Quran notes the root's sense of restraint or prohibition. Tabatabai specifies following desire as what it restrains. [24 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي,اللحياني); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:54/q13/p2 They follow their reached judgment, or others defer to their opinions as the endpoint of judgment. — Zajjaj approves the linguists' explanation; Abu Ishaq, reported by Wahidi, considers following reason's judgment better than the restraint derivation. Mawrdi, Jishumi, Qurtubi, Nasafi, and Tabrisi also give the idea that judgment returns to these people. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ(بعض أهل اللغة); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق)+]
- 20:54/q13/p3 They restrain other people from indecencies. — Tabrisi's formulation makes others, rather than the possessor, the object of restraint. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 20:54/q14 Why are the signs assigned to people of al-nuhā?  [لِّأُو۟لِى ٱلنُّهَىٰ · interpretation]
- 20:54/q14/p1 People of discerning reason reflect on the signs, learn from them, and take admonition, so they benefit fully. — Tabari stresses reflection and learning. Bursawi says the signs address everyone but those with intellect benefit. Alusi says only rational people recognize their full force and explains why people, rather than livestock, receive the address. Biqai says blindness to the signs makes unused reason effectively absent, and the point is stressed against deniers. Wahidi's Ibn Abbas report calls the signs a lesson for people who restrain disobedience. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:54/q15 Why is al-nuhā used here among words for understanding?  [لِّأُو۟لِى ٱلنُّهَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 20:54/q15/p1 Synonymous expressions are placed in distinct Qur'anic locations. — Itqan contrasts Taha's لأولي النهى with وليذكر and أولو الألباب elsewhere. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 20:54/q16 Who speaks the verse in the surrounding Moses and Pharaoh passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the first-person 'We brought forth' marks a shift from Moses' address to direct divine speech.
- 20:54/q16/p1 God speaks directly, interrupting the past account to address the present audience. — Razi argues that making the prior phrase Moses' speech conflicts with the shift to 'We brought forth.' Abu Hayyan says God continues after Moses' proof. Islahi calls four verses a divine insertion addressing Quraysh, identifying their true Lord and rightful object of worship and obedience; the first-person wording confirms God's speech. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 20:54/q16/p2 Moses says this to Pharaoh. — Muqatil places the lesson in divine oneness within Moses' address to Pharaoh. Razi rejects parses that keep this speech with Moses because of the subsequent first-person shift; Islahi also reads the verses as inserted divine speech. [1 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 20:54/q17 Who is credited with the cited poem challenging Pharaoh?  [* · reports]
turns on: The differing attributions reported from Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham.
- 20:54/q17/p1 Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl. — Biqai reports Ibn Kathir citing Ibn Ishaq's Sira attribution. The poem challenges Pharaoh to claim he made unsupported earth, raised an unpillared sky, gave it light, brings the sun, and grows crops and grain. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن إسحاق)]
- 20:54/q17/p2 Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt. — Biqai reports Ibn Hisham's alternative attribution of that same poem, whose challenge names earth, sky, light, sun, crops, and grain. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL(ابن هشام)]
