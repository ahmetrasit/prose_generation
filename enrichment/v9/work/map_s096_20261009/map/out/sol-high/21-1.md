# 21:1: 34 questions

## 21:1/q01 What does iqtaraba mean and add to qaruba?  [ٱقْتَرَبَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the augmented form conveys emphasis beyond the shared root meaning.
- 21:1/q01/p1 It means to draw near; the augmented form can emphasize nearness. — Al-Mubarrad, al-Baḥr, al-Biqāʿī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī find emphasis in its expanded form; al-Biqāʿī connects it to this being the final community. Jishumī says qurb is opposite distance, literal spatially but also temporal or circumstantial. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 21:1/q01/p2 It has the same basic sense as qaruba, without requiring additional force. — Jishumī treats qurb and iqtirāb as equivalent; Abū Ḥayyān and others also give the basic equivalence while reporting possible emphasis. [5 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد)]

## 21:1/q02 What is drawing near in this verse?  [ٱقْتَرَبَ حِسَابُهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ḥisāb directly names final accounting, its time, a worldly requital, or death.
- 21:1/q02/p1 The resurrection and God's actual accounting of deeds draw near. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly rejects supplying 'time' as an omitted noun: the account itself approaches. The accounting discloses deeds and leads to recompense; compare 38:16 and 40:28. Al-Rāzī says spatial approach is impossible. [18 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+; TABATABAI-]
- 21:1/q02/p2 The time of the Hour or of people's reckoning draws near. — Al-Zajjāj supplies 'time'; al-Rāzī says the proximity must be temporal; 54:1 and 21:97 provide parallels. Al-Ālūsī says saying 'account' evokes the Hour's fearful effects; Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects that the context would then say Day or time. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(R); TABATABAI-]
- 21:1/q02/p3 A near worldly punishment, especially Mecca's defeat at Badr, is meant. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says Meccans mocked delayed punishment and were killed at Badr. Al-Biqāʿī includes Badr and the Conquest; al-Būrsawī prefers Badr because death immediately connects to resurrection. Iṣlāḥī connects near accountability to a messenger's conclusive warning and a nation's destruction. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI+]
- 21:1/q02/p4 It concerns each person's near death or minor resurrection. — Al-Qāshānī says understanding resurrection would allow witnessing one's account now. Al-Qurṭubī says each person's death is their Hour; Study Quran reports death as sufficient reason to call reckoning near. [3 notes: QASHANI; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 21:1/q02/p5 It concerns what people have been promised, including worldly affairs. — Ibn Jurayj calls it what was promised; the report of Abū Hurayra in al-Durr connects the approaching account with worldly affairs. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن جريج)]

## 21:1/q03 Why is the account described as near despite the delay?  [ٱقْتَرَبَ حِسَابُهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: What measure of nearness the verse uses: elapsed world time, certainty, divine measure, a progressing deadline, prophetic signs, or personal death.
- 21:1/q03/p1 More of the world's or the period of obligation has passed than remains. — Al-Māwardī, People of Meanings, and later commentators give this comparison. Al-Zamakhsharī cites the final Prophet, his 'breath of the Hour' saying, and a sermon comparing the remainder to dregs. Al-Rāzī cites his sealing of prophecy, two fingers; Study Quran notes that iqtaraba may imply nearer destination than origin. Al-Ālūsī records an objection that elapsed duration is unnecessary and a reply tying approach to the Prophet's mission. [25 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL(بعض المتقدمين,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود,الزمخشري,بعض الأفاضل); STUDYQURAN(R,some theologians (Q, Z))]
- 21:1/q03/p2 Every event still to come is near; what has passed is distant. — Al-Māwardī and People of Meanings give this maxim. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī contrast awaited events with the irretrievable past; a poet supplies the same contrast. Al-Ālūsī reports the objection that certainty alone may not explain the eventive past tense. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل,قيل)]
- 21:1/q03/p3 It is near by God's measure, knowledge, or decree. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī cite 22:47, and al-Bayḍāwī cites 70:6–7. Al-Ālūsī explains knowledge or decree without spatial proximity, cites 18:12, and reports an objection that divine proximity does not renew; Study Quran says God knows all moments together. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل,بعضهم,قيل); STUDYQURAN(M); ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)-]
- 21:1/q03/p4 Each passing hour brings the reckoning closer after it was distant. — Al-Būrsawī and Abū al-Suʿūd take approach as a renewed relation. Al-Ālūsī says actual temporal approach suits the threat better than a proximity existing unchanged since creation. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود)]
- 21:1/q03/p5 The Prophet's mission and signs show how near the Hour is. — The Prophet compares his coming with the Hour to adjacent index and middle fingers; another report has the Trumpet already at the angel's mouth; the afternoon-to-sunset analogy makes this community late in history. Al-Ālūsī's learned critic traces approach to the mission. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 21:1/q03/p6 Certain death makes accountability near for each person. — Al-Qurṭubī says a person's death is their own Hour; Study Quran cites the same consideration independently of the world's end. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 21:1/q04 Why use a past-tense form for the approaching account?  [ٱقْتَرَبَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the tense marks certainty or a temporally occurring approach.
- 21:1/q04/p1 It expresses certainty that the Day of Judgment will come. — Kur'an Yolu gives the certainty of its occurrence as the reason for the past tense. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q04/p2 It presents approach as already begun, with real temporal progress. — Al-Ālūsī reports that an unchanging nearness from creation would call for an ongoing form; a critic answers that most of the world's span had elapsed by the Prophet's mission. He also reports that mere certainty of occurrence may fail to explain the verb's eventive force. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل)]

## 21:1/q05 How does the lām in lil-nās work?  [لِلنَّاسِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition attaches to 'approached', stands for min or ilā, or reinforces the possessive construction.
- 21:1/q05/p1 It is governed by iqtaraba as its complement. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī give this as the straightforward analysis; al-Nasafī calls it an added connective. Abū Ḥayyān objects to possessive emphasis as unattested, ungoverned, and out of order; al-Ālūsī prefers verbal attachment. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)]
- 21:1/q05/p2 It stands for min, expressing approach from or relative to the people. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Māwardī's successors and al-ʿUyūn give min. Al-Ālūsī says min can mark an endpoint, cites al-Shumnī and Ibn Mālik's 'approached from him' for 'approached him', and rejects deriving it from a starting point; al-Raḍī explains preposition use by analogy with an opposite verb. [12 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI(العيون); ALUSI-FULL(الرضي,الشمني وابن مالك,صاحب الكشف); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 21:1/q05/p3 It means toward the people, like ilā. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says min would measure from the object and ilā toward it; the verse depicts accounting coming at the people. Al-Ālūsī accepts ilā as the more characteristic endpoint marker but rejects restricting the phrase to it. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; TABATABAI+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 21:1/q05/p4 It reinforces the possessive relation in 'their account'. — Al-Zamakhsharī compares Arabic expressions such as 'their departure drew near for the people'; al-Bayḍāwī says it develops 'people's account' to 'their account'. Al-Samīn defends analogy with lā abā laka; Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh object that examples differ, the governor is missing, and the lām is wrongly ordered. Al-Ālūsī finds it strained. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)-]
- 21:1/q05/p5 It cannot be parsed by replacing lil-nās with a later group or by displacing a hidden element. — Al-Naḥḥās rejects placing an implicit element before the explicit element that it is meant to follow. Al-Zarkashī rejects strained substitution of 'those who wronged' for 'the people' and compares another legally driven parsing. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BURHAN]

## 21:1/q06 Why put 'for the people' before 'their account'?  [لِلنَّاسِ حِسَابُهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fronting primarily hastens alarm, evokes pursuit, or strengthens the possessive relation.
- 21:1/q06/p1 It delivers the threat immediately and makes the delayed subject frightening. — Al-ʿUyūn says fronting hastens dread; al-Biqāʿī and others say delaying 'their account' leaves listeners imagining what terrible thing approaches. Al-Ālūsī says this can be the intended effect even if heedless hearers do not fear. [8 notes: BURSEVI(العيون); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام,قيل)]
- 21:1/q06/p2 It pictures the people as pursued by an approaching danger. — A rhetorician cited by al-Ālūsī says the verb and fronted phrase depict an advancing thing chasing a fleeing target, conveying danger. Abū al-Suʿūd adds that attributing approach to the account magnifies it as a pursuer that inevitably catches people; al-Ālūsī approves. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود,بعضهم)]
- 21:1/q06/p3 It reinforces the account's attribution and restriction to those people. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the fronted expression, lām and definite article together strengthen the possessive sense and restriction; al-Ālūsī reports objections to the underlying parsing. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)-]

## 21:1/q07 Who are 'the people'?  [ٱلنَّاسِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the general noun keeps its full range or is restricted by the following descriptions and historical threat.
- 21:1/q07/p1 They are the Meccan polytheists or deniers of resurrection. — Al-Kalbī, Ibn ʿAbbās and others cite the ensuing heedlessness, playful hearing and accusation of magic in 21:2–3. Al-Ālūsī says extending those traits to monotheist sinners is strained. Iṣlāḥī says using the general term for them conveys aversion and regret. [17 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس والقرطبي والزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Ibn ‘Abbās)]
- 21:1/q07/p2 They are all morally responsible people, though Quraysh was immediately addressed. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī preserve the general warning. Al-Rāzī limits it to those subject to reckoning; al-Biqāʿī includes addressees and others. Al-Ālūsī reports a generic noun allowing majority traits or later pronouns narrowing to Meccan polytheists. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة,بعضهم,من الناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 21:1/q07/p3 They are all deniers of resurrection, beyond Meccan polytheists. — Abū Ḥayyān explicitly gives all resurrection deniers as an alternative to the Meccan polytheists. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 21:1/q07/p4 They are Quraysh or all Arabs facing worldly reprisal. — Al-Biqāʿī ties this scope to interpreting the account as Badr or the Conquest. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 21:1/q08 What is the sense of nās here?  [ٱلنَّاسِ · meaning]
- 21:1/q08/p1 Nās is linked to naws, a restless instability in human beings. — Al-Biqāʿī says restlessness prevents settled security and steadiness, a deficiency from which few are saved. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 21:1/q09 What does ḥisāb mean?  [حِسَابُهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ḥisāb names the accounting process, the resulting punishment, a counted quantity, or a promised event.
- 21:1/q09/p1 It is God's accounting of people's deeds and blessings, with recompense for good or evil. — Al-Ṭabarī specifies inquiry into deeds, blessings and obedience; al-Būrsawī says it discloses what is for and against someone so recompense follows. Al-Biqāʿī includes enumerating and requital. Al-Jishumī equates ḥisāb and muḥāsaba as verbal nouns, with an Arabic cognate example. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q09/p2 It can mean punishment or recompense. — ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās argues that whoever has an account examined is punished; al-Ḍaḥḥāk identifies Mecca's Badr punishment. Al-Biqāʿī allows worldly requital at Badr and the Conquest. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 21:1/q09/p3 It can mean reckoning an amount or the amount reckoned. — Abū Ḥayyān gives these two lexical senses while explaining the approach of the Hour. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 21:1/q09/p4 It means what has been promised. — Ibn Jurayj's report gives this gloss without further detail. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن جريج)]

## 21:1/q10 Why name the Resurrection by the account?  [حِسَابُهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which feature of the Day the wording aims to foreground.
- 21:1/q10/p1 Accounting exposes people's state and is the most consequential, fear inducing part of the Day. — Al-Rāzī says the name makes the warning more frightening; al-Būrsawī calls it the event weighing most on hearts. Al-Ālūsī says naming the account suffices to evoke the Hour's consequences and that even a precursor should deter denial. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:1/q10/p2 It suits the ensuing criticism of heedlessness toward reminders. — Abū al-Suʿūd says the discourse immediately turns to heedlessness and turning away; al-Ālūsī questions whether this fully explains the wording. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود)]
- 21:1/q10/p3 It makes accountability for deeds, rather than a spectacle of terror, the warning's focus. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the context calls people to heed the divine reminder seriously; if terrors were intended, 'Day of Reckoning' or its time would be more fitting. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 21:1/q11 What does 'their' in 'their account' emphasize?  [حِسَابُهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is read as general human responsibility or a targeted Meccan threat.
- 21:1/q11/p1 The addressees themselves must answer for deeds and divine blessings. — Al-Ṭabarī says they will answer for blessings and obedience; Ibn al-Zubayr connects 20:131 and 102:8 with the previous warning against coveting worldly goods; Kur'an Yolu frames life, death, and blessings as a test calling for gratitude and obedience. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q11/p2 The account of sinful Meccan polytheists is at issue. — Al-Būrsawī links their evil deeds to resurrection and al-Biqāʿī to possible Badr reprisal. [2 notes: BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 21:1/q12 What is ghafla?  [غَفْلَةٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ghafla means bare inattention, ignorance of the outcome, or deliberate neglect.
- 21:1/q12/p1 It is inattention or lack of vigilance and alertness. — Al-Jishumī calls it loss of a meaning from the mind, opposite to alertness; al-Būrsawī traces it to insufficient vigilance. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI]
- 21:1/q12/p2 It is ignorance of the reckoning and what God will do. — Al-Kalbī glosses it as ignorance; exegetes say people do not know what awaits them. Al-Ālūsī reports either ignorance confined to the account or ignorance extending to God, revelation, messengers, resurrection and recompense. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:1/q12/p3 It can mean neglect or excessive immersion. — Al-Ālūsī cites Arabic usage and 17:67 for the intense sense, and 23:17 for neglect, which avoids a conflict with deliberate turning away. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل)]

## 21:1/q13 What kind of heedlessness is condemned here?  [غَفْلَةٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the description is denial, worldly absorption, sinful failure to prepare, or inward absorption in God.
- 21:1/q13/p1 They fail to contemplate or prepare for the final account. — Al-Ṭabarī says worldly life keeps them from reflecting on its severe trials. Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī add that reason points to requital of good and evil. Al-Būrsawī stresses outright denial, beyond simple carelessness; al-Ālūsī warns that heedlessness about requital produces every error. Al-Biqāʿī says resurrection or deadly events expose their heedlessness and humble survivors, who then discern truth from falsehood. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ, Q); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 21:1/q13/p2 They are engrossed in worldly life and desire, ignoring the Hereafter. — Reports attributed to the Prophet identify worldly life; al-Qushayrī says this alienation ends only at death. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī pair worldly absorption with turning from the afterlife; Study Quran reports the same. [10 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Aj,Ṭ, Q); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 21:1/q13/p3 They are heedless through erroneous beliefs and turn from guidance. — Al-Māwardī states this alternative; al-Būrsawī says deniers shut out faith, signs and warnings. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 21:1/q13/p4 Believers can be heedless when they believe in the afterlife but fail to act. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes sinful believers in the warning; Kur'an Yolu names neglected repentance, worship, good works and avoidance of sin. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q13/p5 Heedlessness can be absorption in God, beyond concern for world or even Paradise and Hell. — Al-Qushayrī contrasts this union with alienated worldly heedlessness: those effaced in divine existence do not return from ecstatic absence. Al-Nasafī says such people awaken only at the vision of the Lord; Study Quran transmits the Sufi distinction. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]

## 21:1/q14 Who is described as heedless and turning away?  [وَهُمْ غَفْلَةٍ مُّعْرِضُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the ensuing traits restrict the general noun to polytheists or can also characterize believers.
- 21:1/q14/p1 The description singles out unbelievers, especially Meccan polytheists. — The following verses describe disbelievers' play and accusations; Ibn al-Jawzī says the account's approach is universal while this description is specific. Al-Ālūsī calls extending the description to monotheist sinners strained. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس والقرطبي والزمخشري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q14/p2 It may apply to sinful believers as well as the disbelievers it first addresses. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives sinful believers a share; al-Ālūsī reports an argument that the traits need not restrict a generic 'people', but judges it far from the apparent sense. Kur'an Yolu distinguishes a believer's failure to prepare from a disbeliever's denial. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة,بعضهم,من الناس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q14/p3 It describes humanity generally while the later pronouns can narrow to Meccan polytheists. — Al-Ālūsī reports the grammatical possibility of generic nās with subsequent unannounced reference to Meccans. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(من الناس)]

## 21:1/q15 What are they turning away from?  [مُّعْرِضُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Which warning or object their aversion concerns.
- 21:1/q15/p1 They turn from preparing for and thinking about their reckoning. — Al-Thaʿlabī and other commentators pair heedlessness with lack of preparation; al-Zamakhsharī says they shut their ears to warnings once roused. [8 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 21:1/q15/p2 They turn from faith, the Qur'an, the Prophet's message, signs and warnings. — Yaḥyā says they turn from the Qur'an; ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās says from what Muḥammad brought. Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī specify faith and signs that should awaken them; Kur'an Yolu cites prophets and revelation. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q15/p3 They turn away from the Hereafter or from guidance. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī contrast their worldly involvement with aversion to the afterlife; another explanation pairs error with rejection of guidance. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 21:1/q15/p4 They are obstinate in heedlessness. — Asad renders the phrase literally this way, emphasizing persistence. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 21:1/q16 What does muʿriḍūn imply lexically?  [مُّعْرِضُونَ · meaning]
- 21:1/q16/p1 Aʿraḍa is to turn aside while exposing one's side. — Al-Būrsawī gives the physical source of the figurative aversion. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 21:1/q17 How can they be both heedless and turning away?  [غَفْلَةٍ مُّعْرِضُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the phrases describe successive responses, a state beside an action, or a broader sense of neglect.
- 21:1/q17/p1 They initially fail to think of the outcome, then deliberately ignore warnings that awaken them. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī explain the sequence; al-Kashf distinguishes persistent neglect of rational proofs from rejection when scriptural proofs are presented. Al-Biqāʿī reverses the causal direction: turning from warnings produces heedlessness. Al-Ālūsī says even a remote chance of the warning's truth can unsettle them. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,الكشف)]
- 21:1/q17/p2 Heedlessness is a settled state while turning away is a renewed act. — Al-Ālūsī contrasts the nominal prepositional clause with the participle, and considers treating them as one state weaker. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:1/q17/p3 Ghafla can mean neglect, so deliberate turning away is compatible with it. — Al-Ālūsī cites 23:17 and a usage of excessive heedlessness; these senses remove the apparent contradiction. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأفاضل)]

## 21:1/q18 How is the closing clause parsed?  [وَهُمْ فِى غَفْلَةٍۢ مُّعْرِضُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the inner prepositional phrase is a predicate or a circumstance, and how the whole clause attaches.
- 21:1/q18/p1 The wāw introduces a circumstantial clause describing the people. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Jishumī identify the wāw; Sībawayh equates it to idh and cites 8:11. Al-Samīn says the whole nominal clause is a circumstance from lil-nās. Al-Qurṭubī calls it an independent report and allows an accusative circumstantial form outside the Qur'anic reading. [6 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 21:1/q18/p2 Both 'in heedlessness' and 'turning away' are predicates of hum. — Al-Bayḍāwī offers this, Abū al-Baqāʾ treats the phrase as a second predicate, and al-Samīn notes it is actually first in order. Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī likewise call turning away the second predicate. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:1/q18/p3 The prepositional phrase is circumstantial from the subject of muʿriḍūn. — Al-Bayḍāwī offers it as an alternative, and al-Samīn supplies an omitted governing expression. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 21:1/q18/p4 The phrase fī ghafla depends on an omitted predicate. — Al-Ālūsī gives this syntactic analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:1/q19 What does the verse teach about rational and revealed proof of requital?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether reason independently establishes the moral premise of reward and punishment.
- 21:1/q19/p1 Requital for good and evil is required by reason and divine wisdom. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī say reason requires it; al-Būrsawī argues treating obedience and sin alike would violate wisdom and justice. Al-Biqāʿī says reason already requires the requital their later confession confirms. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 21:1/q19/p2 That rational claim depends on whether moral good and evil are knowable by reason. — Al-Ālūsī says Ashʿarites strongly deny the premise needed to make that argument. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 21:1/q20 Is the reckoning itself an affirmed article of belief?  [حِسَابُهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether the reported sectarian denials concern reckoning itself or other eschatological features.
- 21:1/q20/p1 The verse establishes real reckoning and recompense; only polytheists deny it in this sense. — Al-Ālūsī cites 2:284 and distinguishes denial of the balance or bridge from denial of the account. His gloss of believers includes bodily resurrection and recompense among necessarily known teachings. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 21:1/q20/p2 Muʿtazilites and Khārijites have been said to deny reckoning or interpret it as justice. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports the charge; al-Nasafī includes the balance, bridge, pool and intercession in it. Al-Ālūsī counters that standard kalām accounts report other denials without denial of reckoning. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(البيضاوي); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 21:1/q21 What spiritual response should the near account prompt?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which conduct follows from taking the warning seriously.
- 21:1/q21/p1 People should repent, prepare, reckon themselves, and shorten worldly hopes. — Sulamī's authorities urge waking, vigilance and self-accounting; al-Rāzī says nearness and concealed timing promote repentance. Al-Qurṭubī calls each death a person's Hour; al-Nasafī urges remembrance and avoidance of the heedless; Kur'an Yolu calls for gratitude, obedience, repentance, worship and good works. [11 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم,عبد العزيز المكي,يحيى بن معاذ); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 21:1/q21/p2 The warning can make hearers abandon worldly projects and interests. — A Companion stopped building a wall on hearing the newly revealed verse; ʿĀmir ibn Rabīʿa refused a valley allotment after the revelation displaced worldly concern. Abū al-ʿAtāhiya's death couplet was said to be inspired by the verse. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(رجل من أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العتاهية,عامر بن ربيعة); DURR-FULL(عامر بن ربيعة)]
- 21:1/q21/p3 The heedless delay awakening until regret or death. — Sulamī reports that persistent sin blinds hearts after most of life is spent; al-Qushayrī says worldly engrossment ends at death, whereas the spiritually absorbed have a different waking. [4 notes: SULAMI(عبد العزيز المكي); QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL]

## 21:1/q22 Why leave the exact time of the account unstated?  [ٱقْتَرَبَ حِسَابُهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 21:1/q22/p1 Concealing it encourages preparation, like concealing the time of death. — Al-Rāzī says the unspecified time benefits those obligated to repent; al-Būrsawī likewise calls concealment more fitting. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 21:1/q23 How was this warning reported to have come to its audience?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported setting emphasizes original addressees, their mockery, or listeners' immediate reactions.
- 21:1/q23/p1 It was revealed concerning Meccan unbelievers who denied resurrection. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī name Mecca's unbelievers; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī specify resurrection denial. [4 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 21:1/q23/p2 It answered mockery that earlier warnings of the Hour had not yet happened. — Abū Ḥayyān says Quraysh called resurrection and judgment false or distant after the preceding warning. Al-Ālūsī reports their taunt at the delay, temporary cessation of activity on hearing the warning, renewed fear, then renewed mockery; he cites 17:51. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المفسرين)]
- 21:1/q23/p3 A Companion stopped building when he heard the newly descended sūra. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī report he vowed never to build again after hearing that judgment was near. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(رجل من أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 21:1/q23/p4 ʿĀmir ibn Rabīʿa declined land because the newly revealed sūra displaced worldly interests. — Ibn Kathīr and al-Durr relate his refusal of part of a valley granted by the Prophet. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عامر بن ربيعة); DURR-FULL(عامر بن ربيعة)]

## 21:1/q24 What is reported about the sūra's revelation and count?  [* · sciences]
turns on: For the count, whether the Kūfan 112 or other 111 is followed.
- 21:1/q24/p1 Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ is Meccan. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Aṣamm and Ibn al-Zubayr report Meccan revelation; Abū Ḥayyān and Ṭabāṭabāʾī call it undisputed, the latter citing the flow. Asad, citing al-Itqān, places it in the last Meccan group. [8 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس وقتادة والأصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن الزبير,ابن عباس); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 21:1/q24/p2 It has 112 verses in the Kūfan count. — Jishumī prefers that count and attributes it to the Commander of the Faithful; al-Durr and al-Būrsawī also state 112. [3 notes: DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; JISHUMI+]
- 21:1/q24/p3 Other verse counts give 111. — Jishumī records them while saying the Kūfan 112 is soundest. [0 notes: JISHUMI-]
- 21:1/q24/p4 Ibn Masʿūd counted it among his earliest acquired sūras. — He groups it with al-Kahf, Maryam and Ṭā Hā; al-Durr also includes al-Isrāʾīl and calls them inherited old recitation. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 21:1/q25 What merit is reported for reciting this sūra?  [* · sciences]
- 21:1/q25/p1 A report promises easy reckoning and greetings from every prophet named in it. — Jishumī transmits the report from Ubayy, attributed to the Prophet, without an evaluation in the note. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبي بن كعب)]

## 21:1/q26 How does this opening connect with the end of Ṭā Hā?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the preceding close chiefly sets up final judgment, a demanded worldly sign, accountability for blessings, or consolation.
- 21:1/q26/p1 It makes the preceding warning of final judgment imminent. — Jishumī and al-Biqāʿī connect 20:135's promise that people will know who follows the right way with the unveiled, witnessed Day of Reckoning. Iṣlāḥī says the two passages dovetail and the opening needs no introduction. [5 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:1/q26/p2 It announces the approaching worldly punishment sign demanded by Quraysh. — Iṣlāḥī says Ṭā Hā told Quraysh to wait for the punishment sign they demanded; the Messenger's advent makes their accountability imminent. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:1/q26/p3 It follows the warning against coveting worldly blessings because people will answer for them. — Ibn al-Zubayr, reported by al-Biqāʿī, cites 20:131 and 102:8: abundance is a trial and every blessing will be questioned. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]
- 21:1/q26/p4 It continues consolation through prophetic struggles and the prospect of reward. — Ibn al-Zubayr says earlier warnings about contentious people and ruined nations called for fuller explanation. Ṭā Hā consoles through Moses and Adam; al-Anbiyāʾ follows with judgment, reward, admonitions, signs and destruction of rejecters. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو جعفر بن الزبير)]

## 21:1/q27 How does the sūra's prophetic material advance its purpose?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the prophetic narratives chiefly prove the Hour, defend the current messenger, or establish the unity of revelation.
- 21:1/q27/p1 Prophetic stories collectively prove the Hour's certainty, divine power, and judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī says no one story proves all aspects; together they show the sole Creator cannot be impeded, never breaks His promise, and judges great and lowly. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 21:1/q27/p2 Earlier prophets answer charges against Muḥammad and show revelation's shared message. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces accusations of magic, dreams, fabrication and poetry into accounts of earlier prophets, monotheism, resurrection, judgment and the pious inheriting earth. Iṣlāḥī says the revelation matches earlier messages and rejecters repeat ruined nations' fate. Iṣlāḥī also says Quraysh imagined the world as amusement for its Creator; the prophetic histories correct that view. [9 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:1/q27/p3 Their stories carry a continuous call to divine oneness and one believing community. — Asad stresses unity of revelation and religious experience, linking one God to one community and brotherhood across outward labels, citing 21:92 and 21:108. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 21:1/q28 How are al-Anbiyāʾ's contents arranged and related to earlier sūras?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the organizing relation is its prophetic selection, warning sequence, or opening form.
- 21:1/q28/p1 It details prophets omitted or only briefly mentioned in Maryam and Ṭā Hā. — Al-Ālūsī says Ṭā Hā expands Moses from Maryam, while al-Anbiyāʾ details other prophets and briefly recalls several. Iṣlāḥī says Ṭā Hā recounts Moses and this sūra cites other prophets. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:1/q28/p2 It moves from warning Quraysh through prophetic histories back to their coming fate. — Iṣlāḥī divides it into three sections: warning, historical proofs of Qur'anic truth, then return to warning, including predicted conquest of Mecca and reassurance to Muslims. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 21:1/q28/p3 Its opening is a declarative announcement. — Al-Zarkashī classifies al-Anbiyāʾ among twenty-three sūras with declarative openings, alongside al-Qamar and al-Raḥmān. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 21:1/q29 What overarching theme does this opening establish for the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: Which of the sūra's connected themes is placed at its center.
- 21:1/q29/p1 The Hour, resurrection and universal judgment are certain and near. — Al-Biqāʿī includes death as a route to the Hour and judgment of eminent and humble; the Creator has no partner to impede it. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the sūra treats prophethood on monotheism and resurrection, emphasizing threat; Study Quran cites 54:1. Ibn Kathīr connects the warning with 16:1 and 54:1–2, where God's command and the Hour are near but disbelievers reject signs. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 21:1/q29/p2 The core theme is God's oneness, transcendence and unified prophetic revelation. — Asad says the prophets repeatedly teach this truth, which people forget; the title reflects their recurrence and the flow supports one community. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 21:1/q29/p3 The warning anticipates truth's triumph and Quraysh's defeat. — Iṣlāḥī says the sūra predicts the conquest of Mecca while giving embattled Muslims glad tidings and warning Quraysh. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 21:1/q30 What divine attributes or spiritual states does the opening evoke?  [* · inward]
turns on: How the announced account is read as a present spiritual warning and encounter.
- 21:1/q30/p1 The divine names express justice, mercy, sovereignty, respite and decisive judgment. — Al-Biqāʿī describes God as wise and just, Allah as unrivaled King, the All-Merciful as granting existence, and the Merciful as saving whom He wills afterward. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 21:1/q30/p2 God's favor shelters the obedient and gives neglecters respite, while repentance is met with mercy and arrogance crushed. — Al-Qushayrī connects confession to remembrance, concealment of sin to divine favor, and return to mercy. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 21:1/q30/p3 God enables outward striving and grants inner realization and witnessing. — Al-Qushayrī says striving brings future recompense while abiding witnessing grants immediate nearness; obedient and disobedient people receive reward and punishment respectively. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 21:1/q30/p4 The minor resurrection can make accounting spiritually present now. — Al-Qāshānī says people who understood resurrection would already witness their own accounting. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 21:1/q31 How do theologians classify closeness and forgetfulness?  [ٱقْتَرَبَ غَفْلَةٍ · theology]
turns on: Whether forgetfulness is knowledge loss, a distinct meaning, or a condition of the heart.
- 21:1/q31/p1 Closeness is a created state, and forgetfulness is loss of needed knowledge. — Jishumī reports theologians classing proximity with combination, separation, motion and rest. Al-Qāḍī defines forgetfulness as loss of necessary ordinary knowledge. [2 notes: JISHUMI(القاضي,المتكلمون)]
- 21:1/q31/p2 Being or occurrence can be treated as a separate genus of state. — Jishumī reports this alternative classification among theologians. [1 notes: JISHUMI(بعضهم)]
- 21:1/q31/p3 Forgetfulness can be an independent entity or meaning. — Jishumī reports this view without further argument. [1 notes: JISHUMI(بعضهم)]
- 21:1/q31/p4 Forgetfulness can be corruption in the heart. — Jishumī records this as another account. [1 notes: JISHUMI(بعضهم)]

## 21:1/q32 What did the opening inspire in reported poetry or preaching?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported response gives poetic or homiletic expression to the warning.
- 21:1/q32/p1 It inspired a couplet on people heedless while death grinds on. — Ibn Kathīr relays Ibn ʿAsākir's report that Abū Nuwās praised Abū al-ʿAtāhiya's verse as drawn from this opening. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العتاهية)]
- 21:1/q32/p2 It accompanies preaching that the world is nearly spent. — Al-Zamakhsharī quotes a predecessor likening the remaining world to a little liquid in a vessel. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(بعض المتقدمين)]

## 21:1/q33 What is the status of Qur'anic speech discussed with this passage?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether 'speech' means recited conventional signs or a wider disclosure, and what can bear temporal predicates.
- 21:1/q33/p1 Recited speech is a conventional sign of meaning; its sound tokens arise in time, while the convention itself has no temporal status. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says each reciter's sounds are distinct, and speech counts as one by shared intended meaning. Ordered eternal letters are impossible; conventional speech is neither a real act nor an essential attribute, although external sounds are acts. His analysis treats meaningful verses as neither eternal nor originated in themselves. [10 notes: TABATABAI]
- 21:1/q33/p2 Speech can be understood more broadly as whatever reveals hidden reality. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says effects disclose latent perfection and essential attributes disclose essence; philosophers call God's knowledge, power, life and world His speech. Divine knowledge is eternal, created signs originated, and revelation originated as imparted but eternal as known. He rejects multiplying divine attributes from such reductions. [6 notes: TABATABAI(الفلاسفة)]
- 21:1/q33/p3 The dispute over an eternal or created recited Qur'an is unproductive. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says eternal sounds are impossible and divine knowledge needs no additional Qur'an attribute; multiplying every reduced attribute would produce limitless attributes. Scripture's plain use concerns recited speech. [4 notes: TABATABAI]

## 21:1/q34 What variant writing or recitation issue is reported nearby?  [* · readings]
- 21:1/q34/p1 A wāw may be dropped in connected recitation and then omitted in writing. — Al-Lawāmiḥ says avoiding two sukūns accounts for the spelling, comparing 42:24; al-Ālūsī cites a poetic line where ḍamma betrays the missing wāw, although some grammarians consider the omission poetic necessity. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(اللوامح)]
