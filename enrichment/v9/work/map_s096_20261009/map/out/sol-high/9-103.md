# 9:103: 41 questions

## 9:103/q01 What occasion prompted the command to take charity?  [خُذْ صَدَقَةًۭ · reports]
turns on: Whether the command addresses the repentant stay-behinds, Abū Lubāba alone, or a different alms episode.
- 9:103/q01/p1 Repentant men who stayed behind offered their wealth; the Prophet awaited revelation, then accepted charity. — Muqātil says three men brought the wealth that held them back. Al-Farrāʾ, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Zayd, and later exegetes connect the offer to release from mosque pillars and 9:102; some report he took a third. Ibn ʿĀshūr relates their gift to repairing Tabūk losses. Al-Biqāʿī calls it confirmation of accepted repentance. Qatāda confirms the Tabūk group. Elmalılı allows that the Prophet's initial refusal concerned earlier refusal of alms from people such as Ḥāṭib ibn Saʿlaba. [31 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,زيد بن أسلم,سعيد بن جبير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 9:103/q01/p2 The command arose from Abū Lubāba's repentance over warning Banū Qurayẓa by pointing to his throat. — Mujāhid and al-Bāqir identify him alone; reports describe his binding himself to a pillar and fasting until forgiven. Al-Zuhrī instead connects his binding and seven days of abstention to missing Tabūk. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الزهري,قوم,مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري عن مجاهد,مجاهد); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر الباقر عليه السلام)]
- 9:103/q01/p3 A report connects the verse to Thaʿlaba's refusal to pay alms, contrasted with a man of Sulaym who willingly gave good camels. — Al-Ṭabarī reports Thaʿlaba called the collectors' demand a tax. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ثعلبة بن حاطب الأنصاري)]

## 9:103/q02 Who is to collect or distribute the charity?  [خُذْ · law]
turns on: Whether the imperative is a continuing office, a Prophetic privilege, or permits direct distribution.
- 9:103/q02/p1 The imam or later authority collects and administers it. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, Elmalılı, and Asad derive an official role from the imperative. Ibn al-ʿArabī explains that address to the Prophet can be shared in practice with the community, citing prayer and divorce commands. Elmalılı connects the collectors to 9:60 and says the Prophet collected as imam, not for himself. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 9:103/q02/p2 Only the Prophet could receive these alms; the obligation ended with him. — Post-Prophetic refusers argued that only his prayer could purify them; al-Qurṭubī preserves their poem questioning Abū Bakr. Abū Bakr vowed to fight those separating prayer from zakat; Ibn al-ʿArabī and al-Rāzī reject the claim, citing the poor's independent right in 9:60 and 51:19. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(مانعو الزكاة في زمان أبي بكر); QURTUBI-FULL(شاعر مانعي الزكاة,مانعو الزكاة على أبي بكر); STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر,ابن العربي)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض مانعي الزكاة من أحياء العرب)-; STUDYQURAN-]
- 9:103/q02/p3 Eligible people may sometimes pay zakat directly to the poor. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī ties direct payment to individual authority, though a ruler may forbid it for public interest. Elmalılı says ʿUthmān ended the imam's collection right for hidden assets and permitted direct payment; collectors could not inspect protected homes and shops, but collected visible trade assets. [7 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI(عثمان); ELMALILI-]

## 9:103/q03 May officials compel payment, and does payment to an unjust collector discharge it?  [خُذْ · law]
turns on: Whether collection power includes force, and whether intention suffices under unjust collection.
- 9:103/q03/p1 The imam can forcibly collect from a refuser. — Al-Ikhtiyār cites the verse for collection and placement where due; Abū Bakr fought withholders, swearing even an ʿiqāl or ʿanāq formerly paid must be given. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الاختيار); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر)]
- 9:103/q03/p2 Officials should not seize zakat by force; the owner must pay personally. — Al-Ashbāh reports this as the relied-upon school position. Al-Muḥīṭ says forceful seizure without consent does not discharge the obligation and imprisonment may compel payment. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الأشباه,المحيط)]
- 9:103/q03/p3 Payment to an unjust official can discharge the duty if made with charitable intent. — Al-Mabsūṭ applies its sounder view to charity, tithes, jizya, kharāj, levies, and confiscations, while another view advises repeating payment if the official knew its nature. [1 notes: BURSEVI(المبسوط)]

## 9:103/q04 What does 'take' mean here?  [خُذْ · meaning]
- 9:103/q04/p1 It means accept the offered charity. — Al-Dāmghānī lists covenant, judgment, alms and pardon as senses of acceptance, while a cited passage denies acceptance of ransom. The Prophet's delay until revelation shows the issue was authorization to accept. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 9:103/q05 How much of their wealth was taken or required?  [مِنْ أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · law]
turns on: Whether 'from' and the occasion establish a third, or external zakat specifications govern the portion.
- 9:103/q05/p1 The Prophet took one third and left two thirds. — Al-Thaʿlabī and later reporters infer this from 'from their wealth,' not all wealth. Al-Zuhrī says the Prophet told Abū Lubāba one third sufficed for his vow; Mālik likewise says one third fulfills a vow to give all one's property. Al-Rāzī accepts the report while still preferring a zakat reading. [14 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الزهري,قالوا جميعا); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 9:103/q05/p2 The command concerns a known, previously specified due portion rather than an arbitrary amount. — Al-Rāzī says a single grain cannot be the intended minimum. Al-Qurṭubī says Sunnah and consensus specify thresholds and rates. Al-Shāfiʿī requires payment in the specified kinds and quantities rather than monetary value. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:103/q05/p3 The wording alone leaves the amount open, and even a small voluntary amount can meet it. — Abū Ḥayyān says this on the legal reading of the broad wording. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 9:103/q06 How does 'from their wealth' work grammatically?  [مِنْ أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: Its attachment to the verb or to the following indefinite charity.
- 9:103/q06/p1 Min is partitive and the phrase attaches to 'take'. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls this strongest; al-Ṭabrisī says all wealth need not be given. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow attachment to the verb. Reported one-third collections illustrate partitivity. [6 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 9:103/q06/p2 The phrase modifies 'charity' as an advanced circumstantial expression. — Al-Samīn says it was originally an adjective of ṣadaqa and advanced; al-Ālūsī allows an omitted circumstantial modifier. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:103/q07 How many penitents were involved, and who were they?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports count different groups and distinguish the pardoned penitents from the deferred three.
- 9:103/q07/p1 Seven penitents were involved, four identified as Jadd ibn Qays, Abū Lubāba, Ḥarām, and Aws. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Qatāda give seven; Qatāda's four had promised striving, spending, and charity. Saʿīd's separate report describes illness and blindness among three. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك وقتادة); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,قتادة); TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 9:103/q07/p2 The penitents were distinct from the three whose case was deferred. — Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda say so; all count traditions cited by al-Baghawī include Abū Lubāba. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة,قالوا جميعا); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وقتادة)]
- 9:103/q07/p3 Ten men delayed, seven tied themselves to pillars and three were deferred. — Ibn ʿAbbās's report in al-Durr links the seven's pardon to 9:102 and the three's later case to 9:117; another Ibn ʿAbbās report in al-Baghawī counts ten including Abū Lubāba. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 9:103/q07/p4 Eight penitents were involved. — Al-Baghawī attributes the count to Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and Zayd ibn Aslam. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير وزيد بن أسلم)]
- 9:103/q07/p5 Five penitents were involved, including Abū Lubāba. — Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭiyya gives this count. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 9:103/q08 Whom does 'their' refer to, and how general is the command?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the historical occasion limits the pronoun or the wording yields a general collection rule.
- 9:103/q08/p1 It first refers to the penitents who confessed, but the command or ruling extends to Muslims who owe charity. — Al-Ṭabarī identifies those confessing sin. Al-Wāḥidī distinguishes occasion from general law; Ibn Kathīr rejects confinement to them. Al-Ālūsī distinguishes the expiation and zakat readings. Asad applies it to later authorities accepting repentant Muslims' zakat. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل العلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 9:103/q08/p2 It refers generally to owners of zakatable wealth. — Jurists and the majority read a new zakat command without a previously named group; al-Ālūsī says the pronoun can have this reference on that reading. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة من الفقهاء); RAZI-FULL(أكثر الفقهاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fakihlerin çoğunluğu)]
- 9:103/q08/p3 It concerns the specific repentant confessors and their property. — Some commentators limit the pronoun to 9:102; al-Ṭabarī's account is specific. Ibn ʿĀshūr says another group of confessors; the report of charity specific to their wealth is judged weak by Ṭabāṭabāʾī. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم)-; TABATABAI-]

## 9:103/q09 What does 'wealth' mean here?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish ordinary inclusive ownership from narrower regional or technical usages.
- 9:103/q09/p1 It includes owned property generally, in multiple kinds. — Al-Jishumī gives māl's derivation from acquisition and increase and contrasts singular uniform wealth with plural varied assets. Abū ʿUmar appeals to ordinary Arabic and a Prophetic saying; Abū Qatāda called a garden acquired for armor his first wealth. Some of Daws called clothing and goods al-amwāl, as Abū Hurayra's Khaybar report illustrates. [6 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر,أبو قتادة,أبو هريرة,بعض العرب وهم دوس)]
- 9:103/q09/p2 It denotes coined gold and silver. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this usage from others. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(غيرهم)]
- 9:103/q09/p3 It means camels specifically. — Al-Qurṭubī cites the saying that wealth is camels. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 9:103/q09/p4 It denotes livestock generally. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this usage. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 9:103/q09/p5 Gold or silver below the zakat threshold is not called wealth. — Thaʿlab cites a poem about never attaining the threshold. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أحمد بن يحيى ثعلب)]

## 9:103/q10 What kinds of property may the command cover?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · law]
turns on: Whether wealth is taken in its broad owned-property sense or specified by zakat law and intention.
- 9:103/q10/p1 The plural covers multiple categories, subject to legal specification. — ʿIkrima explains 'from' by zakat being due only on some kinds. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Ālūsī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī list livestock, crops, gold and silver, and other qualifying assets; al-Qurṭubī notes Sunnah and consensus specify quantities and disputed categories, with related discussions elsewhere. ʿIkrima also names camels and sheep. [9 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:103/q10/p2 Zakat concerns zakatable property, excluding houses, clothing, slaves, and like assets. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān report this legal construction; Abū Ḥayyān says no amount is fixed in the wording. Al-Ṣādiq's report names gold, silver, livestock, wheat, barley, dates, and raisins, exempting other property. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة من الفقهاء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 9:103/q10/p3 A vow to give all one's wealth includes every kind of property unless a kind was intended. — Al-Qurṭubī also reports a narrower view limiting the vow to zakatable property. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 9:103/q11 What property liability and payment form follows from this command?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ صَدَقَةًۭ · law]
turns on: Whether zakat attaches to the person or asset, and whether sin capacity is a condition.
- 9:103/q11/p1 Zakat is a debt on the owner's liability, not shared ownership of the threshold property. — Al-Rāzī reasons from calling the wealth 'theirs'; al-Shāfiʿī therefore says liability remains if delayed zakat property perishes. The verse's generality also covers debtors' wealth and guaranteed wealth. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 9:103/q11/p2 Prescribed zakat must be given in its specified kinds and quantities, not as monetary value. — Al-Shāfiʿī cites the camel schedules as an instance. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 9:103/q11/p3 Children's property is exempt because purification from sin presupposes capacity to sin. — Abū Ḥanīfa argues children cannot sin. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)-]
- 9:103/q11/p4 Children's and mentally incapacitated persons' property is zakatable. — Al-Shāfiʿī says loss of one purifying cause does not negate the rule. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); RAZI-FULL(أبو حنيفة)-]

## 9:103/q12 What are the monetary zakat thresholds and rates?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · law]
turns on: The disputed increments above silver nisab and the competing gold nisabs.
- 9:103/q12/p1 Silver reaches nisab at 200 dirhams after a year, with one fortieth due; every excess amount is taxed. — Al-Qurṭubī records agreement that an uqiyya is forty dirhams and five dirhams are due at 200. Mālik, al-Layth, al-Shāfiʿī and many others charge every excess, relying on the year requirement reported by al-Tirmidhī. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(العلماء,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مالك والليث والشافعي وأكثر أصحاب أبي حنيفة وابن أبي ليلى والثوري والأوزاعي وأحمد بن حنبل وأبو ثور وإسحاق وأبو عبيد)]
- 9:103/q12/p2 Silver above 200 dirhams incurs no further zakat until forty more dirhams accrue. — Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ, Ṭāwūs, al-Shaʿbī, al-Zuhrī, Makḥūl, ʿAmr ibn Dīnār, and Abū Ḥanīfa require another dirham at 240. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب والحسن وعطاء وطاوس والشعبي والزهري ومكحول وعمرو بن دينار وأبو حنيفة)]
- 9:103/q12/p3 Gold becomes zakatable at twenty dinars; half a dinar is due, then one at forty. — The majority cites ʿAlī's report. Al-Tirmidhī authenticates two chains; al-Bājī calls the chain weak but the accepted practice corroborative. Al-Qurṭubī cites ʿAlī, Ibn ʿUmar, and ʿĀʾisha against the forty-dinar view. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد,الباجي,الترمذي,الجمهور من العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة أهل العلم)+]
- 9:103/q12/p4 Gold is not zakatable until forty dinars. — Al-Ḥasan, al-Thawrī, and some followers of Dāwūd ibn ʿAlī dissent. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن والثوري وبعض أصحاب داود بن علي); QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة أهل العلم)-]

## 9:103/q13 What livestock zakat schedules are cited?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · law]
turns on: The schedules differ at 121 camels, 301 sheep, and below thirty cattle.
- 9:103/q13/p1 At five camels one sheep is due; at 121 camels the collector may choose three bint labūn or two ḥiqqas. — Mālik's 121-camel option is shared by ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz ibn Abī Salama, Ibn Abī Ḥāzim, and Ibn Dīnār. Al-Qurṭubī reports consensus below five and cites Abū Bakr's letter to Anas for schedules. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر,الأمة,العلماء,عبد العزيز بن أبي سلمة وعبد العزيز بن أبي حازم وابن دينار,مالك)]
- 9:103/q13/p2 At 121 camels three bint labūn are due until 130, then one ḥiqqa and two bint labūn. — Ibn Shihāb gives this schedule; Ibn al-Qāsim prefers it. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شهاب); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن القاسم)+]
- 9:103/q13/p3 For sheep, three are due at 201 through 399, four at 400, then another per hundred. — Al-Qurṭubī cites the majority, with agreement on the subsequent hundred increment. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 9:103/q13/p4 For sheep, four are due at 301, five at 401, then another per hundred. — Al-Ḥasan ibn Ṣāliḥ ibn Ḥayy and Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī are reported to hold this. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(إبراهيم النخعي,الحسن بن صالح بن حي)]
- 9:103/q13/p5 For cattle, one yearling is due at thirty and one mature cow at forty. — Muʿādh's Yemen report includes this schedule and a dinar or Maʿāfir cloth from each adult as poll tax; al-Tirmidhī authenticates it. Abū ʿUmar says the scholars agree. He notes some versions are mursal, stopped, or interrupted; Baqiyya's connected version is disputed and al-Ḥasan ibn ʿUmāra is weak. Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr says Ibn al-Mundhir confused attributions. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمر,ابن عبد البر,الترمذي,الحسن بن عمارة,بقية,معاذ بن جبل)]
- 9:103/q13/p6 One sheep is due for every five cattle up to thirty. — Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib, Abū Qilāba, al-Zuhrī, and Qatāda dissent from the yearling-at-thirty rule. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن المسيب وأبو قلابة والزهري وقتادة)]

## 9:103/q14 How are trade goods and hidden assets collected?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · law]
turns on: Whether property is visible to collectors, and how initial ownership and public shares are conceived.
- 9:103/q14/p1 Visible livestock, crops, fruits, and circulating trade goods are collected by officials at their locations. — Elmalılı reports the Prophet's collectors visited livestock at watering places; the Thaqīf condition meant no assembly of herds. ʿUmar extended collection to open trade goods and no Companion objected; ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz followed this. For Muslims, a year, nisab, and ownership are required; ʿUmar's rates were quarter-tithe for Muslims, half-tithe for dhimmīs, full tithe for enemies. ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz reports half a dinar per twenty from Muslim traders and one from dhimmīs. [8 notes: ELMALILI(عمر بن الخطاب,عمر بن عبد العزيز)]
- 9:103/q14/p2 Owners presented hidden-asset zakat voluntarily; later direct payment to the poor was allowed. — Elmalılı says the Prophet and first caliphs received it, because homes and shops could not be searched. ʿUthmān ordered debts paid first and allowed owners to choose recipients. [4 notes: ELMALILI(عثمان)]
- 9:103/q14/p3 Zakat and similar shares arise when new wealth appears, while the remainder belongs to the worker or owner. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues from 2:29 and 4:5 that wealth might initially be society's, with an individual's share assigned; after ownership settles, further charges are usurpation except emergency needs. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 9:103/q15 How did Ibn Zayd identify the people addressed?  [أَمْوَٰلِهِمْ · referent]
- 9:103/q15/p1 They were hypocrites who missed Tabūk, confessed hypocrisy and doubt, and renewed repentance by offering charity. — Ibn Zayd links the passage to 9:84's prohibition on prayer over dead hypocrites; a separate report says they had promised to fight and give charity. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم)]

## 9:103/q16 What kind of charity is meant?  [صَدَقَةًۭ · law]
turns on: Whether the occasion fixes the kind of alms or the general wording and purifying function indicate prescribed zakat.
- 9:103/q16/p1 It is the obligatory zakat. — ʿIkrima, al-Jubbāʾī, and many jurists hold this. Al-Rāzī argues purification from sin fits an unpaid duty and willing payment tests repentance for attachment to wealth; al-Ṭabrisī says the apparent wording is general and gives nisāb categories. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Jubbāʾī's concern that rejecting their payment would liken them to hypocrites. Elmalılı allows extra gifts. Asad says state acceptance recognizes Muslim standing. [27 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI(عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(عكرمة); BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة من الفقهاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); RAZI-FULL(أكثر الفقهاء,القائلون بالقول الثاني); QURTUBI-FULL(جويبر عن ابن عباس وعكرمة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الجبائي); ELMALILI(ekser-i fukahâ); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fakihlerin çoğunluğu,bazı âlimler); STUDYQURAN(commentators); ELMALILI+; RAZI-FULL+; TABRISI(الجبائي وأكثر أهل التفسير)+]
- 9:103/q16/p2 It is a voluntary expiatory gift from the repentant men. — Ibn Zayd, Ibn Kaysān, al-Ḥasan and others place it after accepted repentance; al-Rāzī's advocates say a new zakat ruling would break the surrounding passage's coherence. ## 9:103/q17 When and how did fixed zakat become obligatory?  [صَدَقَةًۭ · sciences]
turns on: Whether 9:103 begins zakat's obligation or applies a prior institution.
- 9:103/q17/p1 This passage establishes a new command to collect obligatory zakat. — Most jurists in al-Rāzī, al-Bursevī, and Kur'an Yolu infer a general ruling. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(أكثر الفقهاء); BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fakihlerin çoğunluğu)]
- 9:103/q17/p2 Fixed-threshold zakat was probably obligated in year two after Ramadan, with regular state collection perhaps beginning in year nine. — Kur'an Yolu distinguishes eight Meccan uses of zakat as a flexible duty from twenty-two definite Medinan uses and twelve of ṣadaqa; it acknowledges uncertainty in the dating. An al-Ṣādiq report says the Prophet announced it in Ramadan, then after a year dispatched collectors. [7 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
- 9:103/q17/p3 Zakat was already obligatory when this verse addressed the repentants. — Al-Rāzī's second view says they sincerely professed Islam and offered due zakat; al-Ālūsī uses prior obligation to favor voluntary expiation instead. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:103/q18 What social purpose and distribution policy does zakat serve?  [صَدَقَةًۭ · law]
- 9:103/q18/p1 It supplies society's needs while improving individuals and reducing class differences. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares zakat and khums to earlier communal provisions, describes zakat's eight and khums's six recipient classes, and argues public shares should aid people rather than prestige works. Kur'an Yolu calls zakat moral and economic worship benefiting giver and society, paired often with prayer. The Study Quran calls charity a prominent sūra theme. [6 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 9:103/q18/p2 An authority's acceptance recognizes the repentant giver as a Muslim. — Asad notes the Prophet refused zakat from some exposed hypocrites; the later state can accept from those whose deeds show repentance. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 9:103/q19 How is 'a charity' construed, and why is it called that?  [صَدَقَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: The accusative construction and the moral etymology supplied by the sources.
- 9:103/q19/p1 Ṣadaqa is the object taken from their wealth. — The commentators treat it as the alms accepted on command; its unspecified form invites legal specification. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 9:103/q19/p2 The name ṣadaqa derives from truthfulness of faith. — Al-Qurṭubī says it shows agreement of inner and outer belief, against those who disparage voluntary donors in 9:79; al-Bursevī calls it truthful servitude. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 9:103/q19/p3 Ṣadaqa is a circumstantial accusative: take from their wealth while giving charity. — Al-Bursevī supplies this parsing. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 9:103/q20 What does purifying them through charity mean?  [تُطَهِّرُهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the object of cleansing is sin, conduct, property, or inward attachment.
- 9:103/q20/p1 The charity removes sins, including the stain of staying behind and attachment to wealth. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk and many successors identify cleansing of sin; al-Bayḍāwī adds attachment to property as the source of recurrence. Abū ʿAlī compares removing people's moral refuse to cleansing and distinguishes legal impurity from bodily dirt, citing 9:28. Al-Qurṭubī's lexicon defines ṭahūr as pure and purifying. The Study Quran cites prophetic purification with teaching in 2:129, 2:151, 3:164 and 62:2. [28 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ثعلب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق)]
- 9:103/q20/p2 Purification is obedience and sincerity. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it thus in al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Abī Ḥātim; the action exhibits repentance. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس)]
- 9:103/q20/p3 In an inward reading it removes attachment to created things and egoic darkness. — Al-Sulamī reports purification from the stain of created things; Ruwaym describes cleansing inner secrets and refining souls. Al-Qāshānī says wealth feeds desires and charity detaches the soul from them. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم,رويم); QASHANI(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام)]
- 9:103/q20/p4 The alms reform the people. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this interpretation. [1 notes: THALABI]
- 9:103/q20/p5 The purification pertains to their wealth itself. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this alternative referent. [1 notes: THALABI]

## 9:103/q21 How are the two purification verbs parsed?  [تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the feminine tāʾ refers to ṣadaqa or addresses the Prophet, and whether the verbs are raised or jussive.
- 9:103/q21/p1 Both verbs address the Prophet, who purifies and develops them by taking charity. — Al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq, Abū ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ālūsī, and later commentators prefer connected second-person address; both can be circumstantial, adjectival with omitted 'by it', or resumed. The presence of bihā strongly favors address in al-Zamakhsharī's argument. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī say the second verb must be address. Al-Samīn lists syntactic combinations. [26 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج)+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أبو علي)+]
- 9:103/q21/p2 The first verb describes charity; the second addresses the Prophet or starts a new clause. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the adjective for the first because the canonical readers raise it, and a new clause for the raised second. Basran grammarians and al-Akhfash allow repetition for emphasis; others make the second circumstantial. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn object to Makkī's mixed parse under coordination, and Abū Ḥayyān says it needs a rare implied subject. [24 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الأخفش); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي); THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس ومكي); SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL; TAB-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 9:103/q21/p3 The verbs can be jussive as the result of the imperative. — Kufan grammarians favor jussive for a Prophet-addressed verb lacking a pronoun returning to charity; al-Zajjāj gives 'if you take, you purify' and al-Naḥḥās quotes a poetic parallel. Others note the actual raised reading is circumstantial or adjectival. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL; THALABI-]
- 9:103/q21/p4 Both verbs can describe charity itself, with bihā emphasizing the charitable cause. — Al-Akhfash, Basran grammarians, al-Naḥḥās, and al-Qurṭubī allow feminine charity as subject; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Nasafī constrain the second verb to direct address. [5 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض نحويي البصرة); NAHHAS(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI-; NASAFI-FULL-]

- 9:103/q23/p1 It elevates them from the rank of hypocrites to sincere believers and increases good deeds. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī and others give the rank change; al-Ālūsī warns that it implies actual hypocrisy, contrary to his sounder view. ## 9:103/q26 Is prayer for a charity donor required of the collector or recipient?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · law]
turns on: The imperative's force and whether donor request, type of gift, or recipient's office changes it.
- 9:103/q26/p1 The prayer is recommended, not required. — Al-Māwardī argues God rewards charity, not the collector; al-Ālūsī prefers recommendation in all cases. - 9:103/q26/p5 It is required for voluntary charity but recommended for obligatory zakat. — Al-Māwardī records this distinction. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 9:103/q26/p6 It is recommended for a donor to the ruler but required of a poor recipient. — Al-Māwardī says the poor recipient has not been individually designated. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 9:103/q26/p7 It is required when the donor asks, recommended otherwise. — Al-Māwardī gives this sixth view; Abū Awfā requested prayer in one report. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الله بن أبي أوفى)]

## 9:103/q27 Was the command to pray for them abrogated?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · sciences]
turns on: Whether 9:84 addresses the same persons and practice as 9:103.
- 9:103/q27/p1 It was abrogated by the prohibition on prayer over dead hypocrites in 9:84. — Al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and al-Qurṭubī transmit this claim; some link it to the Prophet's special status and 24:63. - 9:103/q27/p2 It was not abrogated; prayer for repentant donors remains. — Scholars cited by al-Naḥḥās point to the Prophet's repeated donor prayers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says 9:84 concerns unbelieving hypocrites whereas these were repentant believers. Al-Qurṭubī says Muslims follow the Prophet's practice. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; NAHHAS(العلماء)+; QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 9:103/q28 May the blessing formula be used for people besides the Prophet?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · law]
turns on: Whether Prophetic donor prayers establish a general formula or his standing makes it exclusive.
- 9:103/q28/p1 Prayer and blessing for donors or other Muslims are permitted. — Al-Qurṭubī says the Prophet is an exemplar, citing donor and Jābir reports. ## 9:103/q29 How is the greeting of peace analyzed in the discussion of blessings?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical structure, word order, and purpose of the greeting.
- 9:103/q29/p1 'Peace be upon you' can have an indefinite subject with 'upon you' as predicate. — Al-Rāzī says the indefinite is qualified by complete peace, citing 2:221 and 2:96; alternatively 'upon you' qualifies the subject and the predicate is omitted for grandeur. He says indefinite salām has fuller qualities than definite al-salām, with Qurʾanic comparisons and Yaḥyā as an exception. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(الشافعي)]
- 9:103/q29/p2 The reversed reply 'and upon you, peace' foregrounds what concerns the respondent. — Sībawayh says speakers put the most important element first; al-Rāzī adds that it promises more and reads 4:86 as support. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(سيبويه)]
- 9:103/q29/p3 Greeting with peace reassures a person amid pervasive potential harm and creates a spiritual bond. — Al-Rāzī argues restraint of harm preserves safety more generally than adding benefit; unknown creatures naturally elicit flight. The plural can include guardian angels and different spirit classes. Mutual spiritual reflection explains a student's greeting and prayer for a teacher. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 9:103/q30 What hermeneutic principle governs a legal and an ordinary sense of a word here?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · interpretation]
- 9:103/q30/p1 When ordinary linguistic and legal senses are equally plausible, the legal sense takes priority absent contextual evidence. — Al-Burhān orders legal over customary over linguistic use. Al-Itqān says ijtihād clarifies general wording, ambiguous points, and legal rulings; scholars must use evidence rather than mere opinion, and this verse's prayer serves as an example of the legal-versus-linguistic choice. [4 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 9:103/q31 What does the prayer command imply for funeral prayer?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · law]
turns on: Whether 9:103's prayer includes funeral practice and how that practice is historically grounded.
- 9:103/q31/p1 Funeral prayer is prescribed as a communal obligation, alongside washing and burial duties. — Al-Bursevī cites al-Kāfī's use of the verse and a report to pray over righteous and sinful dead; he says it became obligatory in year one after the Prophet prayed four takbīrs at Barāʾ ibn Maʿrūr's grave. Al-Qunya treats denial of its obligation as unbelief. Washing is communal duty; intention is not required for validity, though it discharges duty. He says death makes the body impure and washing honors it, even if found in water. [5 notes: BURSEVI(القنية,الكافي,النبي عليه السلام)]
- 9:103/q31/p2 Adam's funeral establishes ancient precedent for washing, shrouding, prayer, burial and laḥd. — One report has angels wash and pray; another has Seth lead thirty takbīrs after Gabriel declined. Al-Bursevī says pre-Islamic Arabs may have washed and shrouded bodies and recited virtues, reconciling reports that funeral prayer is unique to this community. [5 notes: BURSEVI(جبريل)]

## 9:103/q32 How is the reply in al-Aʿshā's cited prayer verse parsed?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · grammar]
turns on: The case assigned to the reply in the poetic citation.
- 9:103/q32/p1 The reply can be nominative like a greeting returned with 'peace be upon you'. — Abū ʿUbayda's citation treats the answer as a nominal expression. [1 notes: MAJAZ]
- 9:103/q32/p2 The reply can be accusative as an imperative to repeat the same words and pray. — Al-Majāz reports another reading of the poetic line. [1 notes: MAJAZ]

## 9:103/q33 What does God's ṣalāt mean when attributed to Him?  [وَصَلِّ عَلَيْهِمْ · theology]
turns on: Whether God's ṣalāt is described as an idiom of desert or as mercy.
- 9:103/q33/p1 It describes persons deserving divine blessing, not God supplicating for them. — Sībawayh's idiom compares 'woe to the deniers' in 52:11 and 37:12 and 'woe to the defrauders' in 83:1; al-Ṭabrisī applies this account to God's prayer on His Messenger. [2 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(سيبويه); TABRISI(سيبويه)]
- 9:103/q33/p2 God's prayer signifies mercy, while the Prophet's prayer is supplication. — Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the subjects and compares 9:99. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 9:103/q34 Why does the verse state that his prayer is a comfort after commanding it?  [إِنَّ صَلَوٰتَكَ سَكَنٌۭ لَّهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The causal and emphatic functions of inna.
- 9:103/q34/p1 The inna-clause gives the reason for the command and answers an implied 'why?'. — Al-Burhān says explicit causes strengthen and help transmit rules, and causal fāʾ could replace inna while losing the question-answer effect; al-Itqān cites Ibn Jinnī's 'because' sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the clause explanation of the imperative. [8 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن جني); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:103/q34/p2 The emphatic clause confirms the significance of the preceding commands. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī compares other inna confirmations, including Luqmān's counsel and passages in 22:1, 31:17, 11:37 and 12:53. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 9:103/q35 How does the pronoun of the affair interact with emphatic inna?  [إِنَّ · grammar]
- 9:103/q35/p1 In conditional clauses, a pronoun of the affair is scarcely used without inna. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir cites 12:90, 9:63, 6:54, 23:117 and 22:46; even if Abū al-Ḥasan's forward reference to al-abṣār in 22:46 is allowed, he rejects a version lacking inna. He notes 112:1's exceptional ungoverned pronoun and possible non-affair parse. [4 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(أبو الحسن)]

## 9:103/q36 How is 'your prayer' read?  [صَلَوٰتَكَ · readings]

- 9:103/q36/p1 The plural ṣalawātuka is read, indicating repeated supplications or multiple recipients. — Medinan readers and most named canonical readers including Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir, Abū Bakr from ʿĀṣim, plus Abū Jaʿfar and Yaʿqūb, read the plural, with kasra on tāʾ. Abū Ḥātim prefers it and denies feminine plural implies few, citing 18:109 and 31:27; al-Jishumī favors its transmission by most readers and the two sanctuaries. Al-Wāḥidī also cites muṣḥaf wāw here and in Hūd and al-Muʾminūn. Al-Fārisī says distinct kinds can pluralize a verbal noun. - 9:103/q36/p2 The singular ṣalātuka is read, expressing prayer generically or collectively. — Iraqi and some Meccan readers, notably Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, Ḥafṣ and Khalaf, use singular with fatḥa; Ḥafṣ differs in al-Muʾminūn. Al-Ṭabarī prefers singular because the point is reassurance, not a count of prayers; Abū ʿUbayd or Abū ʿUbayda prefers its frequent collective use and argues plural denotes few. Al-Fārisī says a verbal noun can be singular with collective reference. Al-Ṭabrisī rejects the number argument. ## 9:103/q37 What does 'sakan for them' signify?  [سَكَنٌۭ لَّهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether sakan names emotional assurance, mercy, dignity, nearness, purification, or inner stillness.
- 9:103/q37/p1 The Prophet's prayer gives calm, reassurance, security, and confidence that God accepted repentance or charity. — Muqātil, al-Farrāʾ, al-Ṭabarī, al-Kalbī and many later readers connect repose to accepted repentance or charity; some call it safety or strengthening of hearts. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines sakan as what one rests in, figurative mental calm, comparing night, home and spouse in 6:96 and 16:80; he also says it inclines the soul to righteous action, comparing 9:45 and 13:28. Al-Ālūsī prefers making prayer itself tranquility by hyperbole over likening resort to it to a home; al-Rāzī's soul-transmission account is rejected as philosophical by Ibn al-Naqīb. [52 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(أبو عبيدة,الكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكفراء,الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو عبيدة,قتادة والكلبي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس,أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي,أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس,قتادة); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(commentators); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 9:103/q37/p2 It is mercy for them. — Ibn ʿAbbās repeatedly gives this gloss; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī and others report it. Some connect it to God's response to the prayer. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 9:103/q37/p3 It gives dignity, composure, gravitas, or honor. — Qatāda consistently gives this gloss; al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others preserve it. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 9:103/q37/p4 It is nearness to God. — Ibn ʿAbbās through al-Ḍaḥḥāk gives this in al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī; Abū Ḥayyān includes nearness. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 9:103/q37/p5 It purifies them. — Muʿādh is cited in al-Thaʿlabī, repeated by Ibn al-Jawzī. [2 notes: THALABI(معاذ); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي)]
- 9:103/q37/p6 In an inward reading it is spiritual tranquility from Prophetic attention. — Al-Qāshānī describes a settled light strengthening certainty and freeing the heart from egoic and satanic agitation; al-Sulamī says it cuts worldly attachment and turns one to the afterlife. [4 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم,قيل); QASHANI]
- 9:103/q37/p7 The Prophet's radiant soul affects and elevates the repentants' souls through prayer. — Al-Rāzī gives this philosophical account; Ibn al-Naqīb rejects it as unsuitable to Qurʾanic exegesis. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن النقيب)-]

## 9:103/q38 How is the word 'sakan' read or construed?  [سَكَنٌۭ · grammar]
turns on: The noun's active or passive force and a consonantal reading variant.
- 9:103/q38/p1 It is an active sense: something that calms them. — Al-Samīn favors this because the verse says 'for them'; a passive noun would normally require 'to it'. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the wording metonymy for the cause of tranquility. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)+]
- 9:103/q38/p2 It is a passive-form noun, like a thing spent or broken. — Abū al-Baqāʾ explains its masculine form this way; al-Bursevī repeats a passive-form analogy. [1 notes: BURSEVI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]
- 9:103/q38/p3 A reading gives the kāf of sakan sukūn. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the variant. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قُرِئ)]

## 9:103/q39 What does the final statement say God hears and knows?  [وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the closing attributes refer chiefly to the penitents' speech or the Prophet's supplication.
- 9:103/q39/p1 He hears the penitents' request, confession, repentance and speech, and knows their remorse, intentions, grief and sincerity. — Muqātil emphasizes their request to have wealth accepted; al-Ṭabarī includes all creatures' speech, and al-Wāḥidī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Bursevī and others specify remorse. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this reassures all hearers charged with duties. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 9:103/q39/p2 He hears the Prophet's prayer and knows its object, worthiness, reforming intent and wise response. — Al-Ṭabarī includes what the Prophet seeks for them, Ibn Kathīr says God knows who deserves it, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says hearing hints at answering prayer and knowing at its good effect. Al-Biqāʿī stresses the Prophet's sincerity; al-Ālūsī adds the divine wisdom required. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:103/q39/p3 He hears their confession and knows how they handle charity. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives this paired explanation. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 9:103/q40 How does the command connect to the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection is the penitents' practical repair, visible obedience, or a wider closing summary.
- 9:103/q40/p1 It follows the confession and repentance in 9:102 and repairs the missed Tabūk contribution. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says repentance repairs what can be repaired: they missed fighting and spending, and Tabūk depleted communal funds. Al-Biqāʿī sees the partial taking as compassionate confirmation of acceptance. Al-Rāzī's expiation advocates argue a new general zakat order would interrupt this link; zakat advocates answer that willing payment tests repentance. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القائلون بالقول الأول,القائلون بالقول الثاني)]
- 9:103/q40/p2 It relates to the visible duties by which belief can be known, unlike concealed hypocrisy. — Al-Karmānī and Durra al-Tanzīl contrast inward hypocrisy known to God and outward obedience seen by God, the Messenger and believers. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الكرماني,درة التنزيل)]
- 9:103/q40/p3 The final names confirm the prayer's rationale and may conclude the preceding two verses. — Al-Ālūsī offers both scopes for the closure. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:103/q41 What does the verse suggest about repentance and inward reform?  [* · inward]
- 9:103/q41/p1 The Prophet's presence and aspiration convey tranquility and purify egoic attachment. — Al-Qāshānī says wealth fuels desires and spiritual training first weakens them; his attention and companionship bring a settled light to the heart. Al-Sulamī reports turning hearts from the world toward the afterlife. [9 notes: QASHANI(النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام); SULAMI(بعضهم,رويم,قيل)]
- 9:103/q41/p2 True repentance requires admission, humility, tears, forgiveness-seeking, and leaving deliberate sin. — Al-Bursevī treats confession as Adam's legacy, reports his two hundred years of weeping, cites Ḥāfiẓ's verse on Adam's sin, urges blessings upon the Prophet and prayer for believers, defines divine forgiveness as concealment, and says mistakes and forgetfulness are pardoned. [6 notes: BURSEVI(الحافظ)]
