# 5:27: 41 questions

## 5:27/q01 What does “recite” command?  [وَٱتْلُ · meaning]
- 5:27/q01/p1 Muhammad is commanded to read aloud and recount the account. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say it also means making listeners hear. Jishumī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī derive tilāwa from following in sequence; al-Naḥḥās calls the verb imperative with its wāw dropped. [5 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 5:27/q02 Who are “them” to whom the account is recited?  [عَلَيْهِمْ · referent]
turns on: The prior addressee and the story's intended warning.
- 5:27/q02/p1 The Children of Israel or People of the Book. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Asad connect the pronoun to the preceding scriptural audience; their concealed knowledge supplies proof of revelation. Al-Qurṭubī says Jews plotted violence; al-Bursawī names the People of Scripture. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:27/q02/p2 The Meccans, idolaters, or Muhammad's people generally. — Muqātil names Mecca; al-Rāzī reports a general audience; al-Ālūsī reports Muhammad's community; the Study Quran allows Jews and/or idolaters. [4 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 5:27/q03 What does nabāʾ mean here?  [نَبَأَ · meaning]
- 5:27/q03/p1 A report or consequential account. — Ibn Qutayba, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān gloss it as news; al-Biqāʿī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress its weight and benefit. Al-Akhfash retains its hamza from anbaʾa and shortens it in pause because it is annexed. [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; AKHFASH; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 5:27/q04 Who are “the two sons of Adam”?  [ٱبْنَىْ ءَادَمَ · referent]
turns on: Whether “Adam” names humanity's first father or a generic human/Israelite, and the crow's burial lesson and 5:32.
- 5:27/q04/p1 They are Adam's biological sons, Hābīl and Qābīl (Abel and Cain). — This is the majority view in al-Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī. Name spellings vary (Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī). Al-Ṭabarī says a remote Israelite story would add little; al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Kuranyolu cite 5:31's first burial; Ibn al-Jawzī and Abū Ḥayyān also cite the first-murderer report. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says an alternate Adam needs a clue; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes generic singular “son of Adam” from this dual. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī questions Torah names due to textual transmission. - 5:27/q04/p2 They were two Israelites, called Adam's sons as human descendants, or sons of another Israelite Adam. — Al-Ḥasan and al-Ḍaḥḥāk are cited; sacrifices and the law in 5:32 are offered as evidence, as is the Israelite request for a fire-consumed offering in 3:183. - 5:27/q04/p3 Their names are less important than the real moral event. — Elmalılı says the general wording avoids legend, and the story's use remains even if treated hypothetically; he also reports some call it mythical, while Ömer Faruk Harman argues ancient parallels can preserve a historical event. [3 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(efsanvî olduğunu söyleyenler,Ömer Faruk Harman)]
- 5:27/q04/p4 In an inward reading, they are the intellect and estimative faculty in Adam's heart. — Al-Qāshānī identifies Hābīl with intellect and Qābīl with estimation, each with a twin faculty; imagination serves estimation, practical reason serves intellect. [3 notes: QASHANI]

## 5:27/q05 What birth and sibling background do the reports give for the two sons?  [ٱبْنَىْ ءَادَمَ · reports]
turns on: Reports vary on birth order, where conception occurred, and whether sibling marriage was lawful.
- 5:27/q05/p1 Eve bore boy-girl twins; Cain and his sister were born before Abel and his sister. — Names vary: Iqlīmā/Aqlima for Cain's twin and Labūdā/Luwaiza/Liuda for Abel's. Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī report twenty twin births and forty children except Seth, born alone. Ibn ʿAbbās says Adam saw forty thousand descendants. Some place Cain's birth one hundred years after descent; Ibn Isḥāq says Cain was conceived in Paradise before sin, Abel on earth after descent. Al-Kalbī says births were two years apart. [20 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; THALABI(أهل العلم بالقرآن,ابن عباس,الكلبي,بعض أهل الكتاب,بعضهم); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل العلم,ابن عباس,محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(قالوا); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(المترجم); BURSEVI(العلماء)]
- 5:27/q05/p2 Adam's descendants became many and corrupt in his lifetime. — Ibn ʿAbbās says he saw forty thousand descendants and witnessed adultery, drinking and corruption; al-Qurṭubī rejects an uncorroborated report of his daughter's fornication, questioning with whom it occurred. [4 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 5:27/q05/p3 A report gives Eve a daughter before Cain and Abel, then a jinn and a heavenly spouse for the brothers. — Al-Ṣādiq calls the first daughter Luwaiza; al-Qurṭubī's version calls the first transgressing daughter ʿAnāq, killed before the brothers were born. This account denies that Adam married a daughter to a son. [5 notes: THALABI(الصادق,معاوية بن عمار); QURTUBI-FULL(جعفر الصادق)]
- 5:27/q05/p4 Cain and his sister were born after Abel and his sister. — Ibn al-Jawzī records this alternate birth order without further evidence. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 5:27/q06 How were the brothers to marry, and why did they dispute it?  [ٱبْنَىْ ءَادَمَ · reports]
turns on: Whether exceptional cross-sibling marriage was allowed or the brothers received nonhuman spouses.
- 5:27/q06/p1 Adam directed each brother to marry the other's twin; Cain wanted his own more beautiful twin. — The only available women were sisters, so cross-birth marriages were temporarily allowed, not one's own twin; Ibn Jurayj says this lasted four generations. Cain variously argued that he was older, more entitled, or born in Paradise, and feared his descendants' inferiority. Al-Qurṭubī cites 4:1 for common parentage and says the arrangement was later abrogated; Ibn Kathīr calls it necessity. Al-Ālūsī says Adam knew Cain's offering would fail and did not intend an impermissible match. [43 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق عن بعض أهل العلم بالكتاب الأول,السدي); THALABI(الكلبي,قابيل); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج,قابيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وجماعة من المفسرين); TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر وغيره من المفسرين,قالوا); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من المفسرين منهم ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المفسرون,بعض المفسرين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,بعض أهل العلم بالكتاب الأول,غير واحد من السلف والخلف); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ناس من الصحابة); BURSEVI(العلماء); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة); QASHANI; STUDYQURAN(commentators); QURTUBI-FULL+; THALABI(الصادق,معاوية بن عمار)-; QURTUBI-FULL(جعفر الصادق)-]
- 5:27/q06/p2 Sibling marriage was forbidden; Cain and Abel were paired with a jinn woman and a heavenly maiden. — Al-Ṣādiq's report calls them Jihāna and Nuzla; al-Qurṭubī's version says Jamāla and Bazla and adds a created womb. Al-Ṣādiq reasons Adam's religion matched Muhammad's, which forbade sibling marriage. Al-Qurṭubī doubts this account and prefers the cross-birth report. [7 notes: THALABI(آدم,الصادق,معاوية بن عمار); QURTUBI-FULL(جعفر الصادق); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 5:27/q07 What does “in truth” specify?  [بِٱلْحَقِّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether truth qualifies the account, the manner of recitation, or its purpose.
- 5:27/q07/p1 The report is true, sound, accurate, and free from fabrication. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī say it agrees with earlier scriptures; Ibn Kathīr compares 3:62, 18:13 and 19:34. Al-Biqāʿī says it agrees with reality and earlier books. Elmalılı and Ibn ʿĀshūr warn against legendary embellishment. [16 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:27/q07/p2 The reciter must tell it truthfully and sincerely. — Al-Zamakhsharī gives this as a manner of telling; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Ālūsī parse it as a circumstantial phrase from the reciter. Al-Samīn says the bāʾ denotes accompaniment. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:27/q07/p3 It is a truthful recitation, with the phrase modifying an omitted verbal noun. — Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī supply the omitted noun; al-Samīn says al-Zamakhsharī may prefer this parse. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 5:27/q07/p4 It marks the proper serious purpose of condemning envy and teaching a lesson. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī apply it to envy against Muhammad; al-Rāzī cites 12:111 against idle entertainment. Ibn ʿĀshūr says recite it seriously, not for amusement. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 5:27/q08 How is the mīm of Ādam read before bi-l-ḥaqq?  [ءَادَمَ بِٱلْحَقِّ · readings]
- 5:27/q08/p1 Abū ʿAmr makes the mīm still before bāʾ. — Al-Samīn says this applies to any vowel-preceded mīm before bāʾ. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو)]

## 5:27/q09 How does “when they offered” connect grammatically to the account?  [إِذْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether idh is governed by nabāʾ, stands for an omitted “time,” or is circumstantial.
- 5:27/q09/p1 Idh is governed by nabāʾ: the account concerns that time. — Al-Ālūsī allows the verbal force of the noun nabāʾ to govern time. Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr agree. Sajawandī requires a stop after “in truth” to keep the future imperative “recite” from governing past idh. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري وأبو البقاء); NASHR(السجاوندي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:27/q09/p2 Idh is an apposition to nabāʾ with an omitted “account of the time.” — Al-Zamakhsharī proposes this; Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh object that idh takes a time noun whereas nabāʾ is not time. Al-Ālūsī reports the reply that the construction remains sound. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(البحر)-]
- 5:27/q09/p3 Idh is circumstantial to nabāʾ through an omitted governor. — Abū al-Baqāʾ proposes this; al-Samīn finds it unclear and objects to attaching a past time to the future recitation. Al-Ālūsī says parses must be distinguished. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 5:27/q10 Why did the brothers make offerings?  [إِذْ قَرَّبَا قُرْبَانًۭا · reports]
turns on: The text does not say whether God/Adam ordered the offerings, a marriage contest prompted them, or they were voluntary.
- 5:27/q10/p1 Adam used acceptance to settle the contested marriage. — The brothers' offerings would decide who could marry Cain's beautiful twin; some say Adam ordered them, while others say they agreed. Al-Ālūsī says the test did not itself license a forbidden marriage. [27 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق عن بعض أهل العلم بالكتاب الأول,السدي); THALABI(قابيل); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وجماعة من المفسرين); TABRISI(أبو جعفر الباقر وغيره من المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من المفسرين منهم ابن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المفسرين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,بعض أهل العلم بالكتاب الأول,غير واحد من السلف والخلف); DURR-FULL(ناس من الصحابة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود وناس من الصحابة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Kesîr); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 5:27/q10/p2 The brothers initiated offerings themselves as voluntary devotion or ritual worship. — Al-Ṭabarī says offerings sought nearness to God regardless of command. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī transmit spontaneous offering; Ibn Kathīr prefers the Qur'an's apparent account without a woman as cause and cites Ibn ʿAbbās that the rite was not charity to a poor recipient. The Study Quran says al-Ṭabarī's emphasis on nearness casts doubt on the marriage reports. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 5:27/q10/p3 God commanded the two brothers to offer. — Al-Ṭabarī reports this but says the text does not settle the issue. A report from Adam in Ibn Kathīr says he expected sacrifice in his descendants and asked his sons to offer; the Study Quran distinguishes this from the marriage account. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن علي بن الحسين); STUDYQURAN(commentators)]
- 5:27/q10/p4 The immediate occasion was unspecified. — Al-Māwardī gives a no-particular-reason report; al-Ṭabarī explicitly leaves God's command undecided. The Qur'an's sparse account makes the type of offering irrelevant to al-Biqāʿī. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 5:27/q10/p5 Adam ordered the brothers to offer, without a stated marriage motive. — Ibn al-Jawzī records this separately from the report that the offerings occurred during Adam's absence. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 5:27/q11 What does qarrabā mean here?  [قَرَّبَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether it simply means drawing near or the established act of offering worship.
- 5:27/q11/p1 Each brought a devotional offering to seek God's nearness. — Al-Wāḥidī excludes literal movement in place; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls qarrabā a lexicalized sacrificial verb derived from qurbān. The Study Quran notes it can describe charity. Al-Aṣmaʿī cites transitive taqarraba bi- to mean qarraba. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL(الأصمعي); SAMIN-DURR(الأصمعي); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:27/q11/p2 A proposed reflexive derivation means they offered it and thereby drew near. — Al-Zamakhsharī's analysis treats taqarraba bi- as the response to qarraba; Abū Ḥayyān objects that both have the same agent and are not passive counterparts. Al-Samīn finds the derivation remote but challenges the Shaykh's rule. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الأصمعي); KASHSHAF-FULL(الأصمعي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,الشيخ)-]

## 5:27/q12 What is a qurbān?  [قُرْبَانًۭا · meaning]
- 5:27/q12/p1 Anything offered to draw near to God, including sacrifice, charity, prayer, or another gift. — Ibn Qutayba and later lexicographers derive it from nearness; al-Māwardī calls it a pious act seeking mercy. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī compare royal courtiers. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes prayer. Kuranyolu distinguishes the broad lexical sense from the technical timed slaughter. The Study Quran stresses nearness as sacrifice's aim. [26 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]

## 5:27/q13 Why is qurbān singular though two brothers offered?  [قَرَّبَا قُرْبَانًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the singular is a distributive, a collective noun, or an uninflected verbal noun.
- 5:27/q13/p1 The noun began as a verbal noun or collective, so it need not be dual. — Al-Ṭabarī compares furqān and ʿudwān; al-Wāḥidī says verbal nouns are not dualized. Al-Samīn says it later named the offering itself and that singular form supports this history; al-Ṭabrisī allows generic or verbal-noun analysis. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 5:27/q13/p2 Each brother offered one offering; the singular is distributive. — Al-Wāḥidī says the dual verb entails two offerings; Abū ʿAlī al-Fārisī compares 24:4's distributive eighty lashes; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the noun is generic. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي الفارسي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 5:27/q14 What legal status do later Islamic animal sacrifices have?  [قُرْبَانًۭا · law]
turns on: The dispute concerns the Eid sacrifice's obligation versus confirmed Sunnah.
- 5:27/q14/p1 The Eid sacrifice is obligatory for those meeting the conditions, in Abū Ḥanīfa's view. — Kuranyolu says this view invokes hadith and 108:2; its legal indication is probabilistic but sufficient for obligation. The rite was instituted in 2 AH by Qur'an, Sunnah and consensus; 37:107 supports legitimacy. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Hanîfe,Ebû Hanîfe ve aynı görüşü paylaşanlar)]
- 5:27/q14/p2 The Eid sacrifice is a confirmed Sunnah in the majority view. — Kuranyolu says the hadith's “if you wish” clause allows choice; the Prophet's constant practice and reports of merit establish the Sunnah. Vowed sacrifices are obligatory, while others can be voluntary. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Ebû Hanîfe,Kurban kesmenin sünnet olduğu kanaatini taşıyan bilginler,Çoğunluğa)]

## 5:27/q15 What wider history of sacrifice is drawn from qurbān?  [قُرْبَانًۭا · interpretation]
- 5:27/q15/p1 Offerings across religions vary; revealed religions include animal sacrifice. — Kuranyolu lists plants, grain, money, animals and even human sacrifice across religions, cites 22:34–37 for animal sacrifice in revealed laws, contrasts Christian claims about Jesus with the Qur'an's rejection of human sacrifice and the ram given after Abraham's test. The Study Quran notes Hajj and Eid sacrifices and charitable distribution. [6 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 5:27/q16 What corresponds to the brothers' offering for this community?  [قُرْبَانًۭا ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether prayer alone or the wider field of righteous action is intended.
- 5:27/q16/p1 Prayer is the God-fearing person's offering. — ʿAdī b. Thābit and others give this identification; al-Qurṭubī limits it to one form of worship. Abū al-Dardāʾ and Ibn ʿUmar say certainty of one accepted prayer or prostration would outweigh the world. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم,عدي بن ثابت); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عدي بن ثابت وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL(عدي بن ثابت وغيره); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عدي بن ثابت); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء); DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,ابن عمر)]
- 5:27/q16/p2 Prayer, charity, fasting, and other righteous deeds perform the role of an offering. — Al-Ṭabarī compares earlier sacrifice to charity and zakat, but says Muslims cannot immediately know acceptance. The Study Quran cites prayer, fasting and obligatory alms; its 22:36–37 comparison says reverence matters more than flesh. Al-Sulamī privileges divinely promised accepted deeds and prostration, citing 96:19. Al-Qurṭubī cites the hadith on obligatory and voluntary deeds drawing a servant near. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; SULAMI(السلامي); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī)]

## 5:27/q17 What did each brother offer, and whose was accepted?  [قَرَّبَا قُرْبَانًۭا فَتُقُبِّلَ مِنْ أَحَدِهِمَا وَلَمْ يُتَقَبَّلْ مِنَ ٱلْءَاخَرِ · reports]

## 5:27/q23 How can a negative construction make a broad claim?  [وَلَمْ يُتَقَبَّلْ · grammar]

## 5:27/q28 How is the threat's emphatic form read and parsed?  [لَأَقْتُلَنَّكَ · readings]
turns on: The final nūn is doubled in the majority reading and light in named variants.
- 5:27/q28/p1 The majority read a doubled emphatic nūn; the lām and nūn answer an omitted oath. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn identify an unspoken oath; Sībawayh's separate discussion explains how oath particles, substitution by hā or an alif, coordination by wāw or thumma, and genitive oath nouns work. His examples include 92:1–3 and Zuhayr's line. Sībawayh says substituted hā or an alif cannot combine with wāw, whereas naʿam and ī can; thumma may coordinate oath nouns, a completed clause may make a later oath independent, and delayed nouns remain genitive only while syntactically linked. - 5:27/q28/p2 Zayd b. ʿAlī, and Yaʿqūb via Zayd, read a light nūn with sukūn. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes the reading to Yaʿqūb via Zayd; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn to Zayd; al-Ālūsī records both forms. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يعقوب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن علي); SAMIN-DURR(زيد); ALUSI-FULL]

Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn preserve the ellipsis; al-Samīn thinks a long reconstruction unnecessary. Al-Zamakhsharī treats this as interpretive sense, not iʿrāb, according to al-Samīn. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية,الزمخشري,غيره); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 5:27/q32/p2 No extended omitted speech is needed: the concise reply itself explains that piety, not Cain's brother, caused the verdict. — Al-Samīn prefers the direct reading; al-Ālūsī calls it a wise redirection of blame and tells the envier to acquire piety rather than remove another's favor. Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's Muʿtazilī elaboration as unnecessary. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 5:27/q32/p3 Abel means he will not evade God's threatened judgment or kill Cain in preemption. — Al-Ālūsī records both alternatives and calls them remote. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 5:27/q33 Who are the muttaqīn from whom God accepts?  [ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether piety here means avoidance of shirk, avoidance of all sins, or sincerity in this act.
- 5:27/q33/p1 Those who fear God, discharge duties, and avoid sins. — Al-Ṭabarī gives the broad duty-and-prohibition definition; al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī say the omitted feared object is sin. Al-Biqāʿī says habitual piety excludes persistence in idolatry or other sin. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this ordinary sense may reflect the brothers' law. Musa b. Aʿyan includes avoiding some permissible things to avoid forbidden ones. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(موسى بن أعين); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:27/q33/p2 Those who avoid shirk and worship God alone. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk and others give this sense; Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes it to Sunni agreement and says the monotheist's sincere acts are accepted, while avoiding all sins brings the highest acceptance. Al-Ālūsī says this first level alone is insufficient for Cain's case. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك,جماعة من أهل التأويل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أهل السنة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(معاذ بن جبل); DURR-FULL(الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 5:27/q33/p3 Those sincere and pure-hearted in what they say, do, and offer. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ stresses sincerity, Ibn ʿAbbās pure hearts; Sahl calls piety and sincerity the heart's receptacles. Al-Rāzī and Bursawī cite the Prophet pointing to his heart; Ibn ʿĀshūr finds a pure “sincerity in the act” gloss a stretch of customary taqwā, but also considers intending reverence through an offering. [10 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,سهل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(النبي); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:27/q33/p4 Inwardly, they protect themselves through God or against dark bodily and imaginative tendencies. — Al-Qāshānī supplies the two inward senses of protection within his intellect-versus-estimation reading. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 5:27/q34 What does “God accepts only from the God-fearing” imply for deeds and sinners?  [إِنَّمَا يَتَقَبَّلُ ٱللَّهُ مِنَ ٱلْمُتَّقِينَ · theology]
turns on: How exclusive the condition is, what acceptance means, and whether a sinner's good act can be accepted.
- 5:27/q34/p1 Acceptance of obedience requires belief and piety; the verse warns against outward deeds without inward reverence. — Al-Kashshāf calls it a warning to many who labor; al-Rāzī cites 22:37, where piety rather than flesh or blood reaches God. A report says God does not accept until pleased with the servant. Al-Ṭabrisī defines acceptance as obligating reward; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says assurance of mercy is learned through revelation, not rational necessity. ʿĀmir wept at death over this verse; Abū al-Dardāʾ and Ibn ʿUmar valued certainty of one accepted prayer, prostration or dirham above the world, Faḍāla said the same of a mustard seed of deeds, ʿAlī denied that a pious deed can be small, and Muṭarrif prayed over an accepted fast. [33 notes: TAB-FULL(عامر بن عبد الله العنبري); KASHSHAF-FULL(عامر بن عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أهل السنة); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(عامر بن عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,عامر بن عبد الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,معاذ بن جبل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء,ابن عمر,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عامر بن عبد قيس,علي بن أبي طالب,عمر بن عبد العزيز,فضالة بن عبيد,مطرف,همام بن يحيى); ALUSI-FULL(عامر بن عبد الله); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 5:27/q34/p2 A sinner can still have a sincerely obedient deed accepted. — Al-Ṭabrisī rejects the inference that every act of a sinner must fail: reward is due for an obedient act done as obedience. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes basic monotheistic acceptance from highest acceptance through avoiding sins. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Islam accepts a believer's good acts despite other lapses. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أهل السنة); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI+]
- 5:27/q34/p3 The restriction may concern complete acceptance, sacrifices alone, or correction of Cain's mistaken expectation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares complete guidance and afterlife in 2:2 and 43:35 and offers a sacrifice-only scope. Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes exclusion of impious persons from reversing Cain's expected preference. Al-Samīn says the object may be their offerings/deeds or intentionally left unspecified, comparing 92:5. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMIN-DURR]

## 5:27/q35 What broad moral does the brothers' story teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources develop different aspects of the lesson.
- 5:27/q35/p1 Envy and oppression lead to fratricide, ruin, and accountability; readers should follow Abel. — Muqātil and the Prophetic report present the better son as a model. Ibn Kathīr says the victim gained Paradise while Cain lost this world and the next. Elmalılı stresses the first bloodshed; Ṭabāṭabāʾī sees just cosmic recompense; the Study Quran sees fallen humanity's propensity for violence. Reports of the Prophet describe envy and hatred as a disease that cuts religion; ʿUmar's report links sudden wealth to discord. [16 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر); DURR-FULL(ناس من الصحابة); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī)]
- 5:27/q35/p2 Piety and justice govern acceptance and reward, regardless of ancestry or outward offering. — Al-Biqāʿī applies this to claims of inherited divine favor; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the brothers knew one Lord, afterlife, prophethood, law, and the duty to avoid injustice. Al-Sulamī says greed deprives, pride humiliates and envy forsakes. Kuranyolu draws a universal human-rights principle from 5:32. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the crow's burial lesson as gradual human learning through senses and reason. [14 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,السلامي,سهل,ممشاد الدينوري); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 5:27/q36 Why does this account follow the preceding discussion of Israel and its covenants?  [* · links]
turns on: Sources offer several complementary links through envy, violence, ancestry, revealed knowledge and contrasting conduct.
- 5:27/q36/p1 It rebukes Jewish envy, violence and broken covenants while consoling and vindicating Muhammad. — Al-Ṭabarī says the Jews plotted against the Prophet; Jishumī's al-Aṣamm and ʿAlī b. ʿĪsā warn that injustice returns on perpetrators. Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān connect 5:11, 5:15, 5:18 and the story's attempted violence and disclosed scriptural truth; the Study Quran cites 5:11's hand-stretching idiom and other envy passages. Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects rejection of Muhammad to Cain's fruitless envy. Jishumī also says obedience and disobedience retain the same consequences from Adam's sons onward; al-Ṭabrisī compares Cain's punishment with covenant-breaking Jews. [31 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(الأصم,علي بن عيسى,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī); TABRISI]
- 5:27/q36/p2 Revealing a hidden account from earlier scripture proves Muhammad's prophethood. — Al-Naḥḥās says he had not read books; Abū Muslim in Jishumī says disclose hidden reports to the People of the Book. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān likewise say revelation alone gave access to these obscurities; al-Samīn makes conformity to scripture proof. [9 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:27/q36/p3 The story contrasts Israel's cowardice before the giants with Cain's audacity in murder. — Abū Ḥayyān ties it to 5:24; al-Ālūsī notes both acts are sins. Al-Biqāʿī contrasts Moses and Aaron's compassion with Cain's deed, compares rejected spoils, Cain's exile with Israel's wandering, and proposes a stronger link to the Holy Land promise in 5:26. Al-Rāzī also sees Israel's regret after refusing the giants mirrored in the brothers' story. Bursawī's connected Joshua account says Moses lived 120 years and described death as flaying; after forty years Joshua besieged Jericho six months, its walls fell at horn blasts, and a Friday sun was held for victory over thirty-one kings. The offering of spoils burned only after a thief produced a jeweled golden bull's head; Joshua died at 126 after twenty-seven years' leadership. A Persian couplet urges attachment to the Creator rather than the transient world. [17 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL+]
- 5:27/q36/p4 The story introduces Israel's law of retaliation and the moral in 5:32. — Mujāhid connects the killing with 5:32 and 4:93; al-Rāzī connects concealed Torah law to 5:15; Asad says Bible followers concealed the moral that 5:32 states; Kuranyolu calls it a universal principle. [5 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 5:27/q36/p5 Adam's son shows that descent from prophets does not guarantee God's favor. — Abū Ḥāmid in Jishumī, al-Rāzī and al-Biqāʿī answer the claim in 5:18 that Jews are God's children and beloved; even a son reportedly born in Paradise could lose favor. The brothers shared a law, and obedience decided their fates. [6 notes: JISHUMI(أبو حامد); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(قيل)]
- 5:27/q36/p6 The story warns later Muslims against envy after wealth and victory. — Al-Biqāʿī imagines Israel after entering the land and Muslims after the Prophet's death; prosperity can renew hatred. He quotes Prophetic reports with graded chains and ʿUmar's concern over war spoils. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عمر)]

## 5:27/q37 How does an inward reading map the story onto the faculties of the soul?  [* · inward]
- 5:27/q37/p1 Abel is intellect, Cain estimation; their twins are practical reason and imagination. — Al-Qāshānī says the heart pairs faculties to discipline estimation and use imagination for knowledge. Intellect's offering is a valid universal conclusion corresponding to reality; estimation offers a fallacy or partial image, so envy arises and it tries to stop intellect's activity and spiritual guidance. [12 notes: QASHANI]

## 5:27/q38 What other recitation techniques are cited near this verse?  [* · readings]
turns on: The notes address distinct phonetic rules and report their transmission.
- 5:27/q38/p1 A slight pause (sakt) may occur on a vowelless consonant before hamza, including the article lām. — Ibn al-Jazarī distinguishes same-word and cross-word settings and long vowels from other consonants; reports routes through Ḥamza, Ibn Dhakwān, Ḥafṣ, Ruways and Idrīs. A route through Khalaf and Khallād pauses on article lām and shayʾ; al-Dānī instead gives madd on shayʾ and sakt on lām only. [5 notes: NASHR(الداني,جماعة من أهل الأداء,حمزة، وابن ذكوان، وحفص، ورويس، وإدريس)]
- 5:27/q38/p2 Ikhfāʾ of nūn or tanwīn occurs before fifteen specified letters, with degrees of nasal concealment. — Ibn al-Jazarī lists tāʾ through kāf, while al-Dānī defines it between clear articulation and assimilation; proximity strengthens it. Abū Jaʿfar extends nasal articulation before ghayn and khāʾ, whereas other readers treat them as throat letters. [4 notes: NASHR(أبو جعفر,الداني)]
- 5:27/q38/p3 An anomalous report of showing feminine tāʾ before dāl is rejected. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects al-Mubhij's attribution to routes of Qālūn and Ibn Buwayyān; showing it before ṭāʾ is also very weak. [1 notes: NASHR(صاحب المبهج)]

## 5:27/q39 How do reason, piety and revelation guide judgment in this story?  [* · theology]
- 5:27/q39/p1 Innate reason recognizes broad truth and morality, while detailed religious law comes through revelation and must be approached with piety. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 30:30 for innate cognition, argues that objections to reason and appeals to experiment depend on rational premises, and says analogies cannot establish revealed laws. He cites 17:36, 2:168, 2:213, 10:32 and 16:37 against speculation and unjust means; 2:232, 2:193 and 29:45 pair legal teaching with moral cultivation. [7 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:27/q40 What accounts for the expressive force of Qur'anic wording?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether excellence is mere sound or composed meaning.
- 5:27/q40/p1 Eloquence comes from fitting expression, crafted images and precise word choice. — Al-Jurjānī, explaining al-Jāḥiẓ, says ordinary meanings gain new form through arrangement, like fashioned jewelry; he rejects reducing excellence to sound alone. Al-Sāmarrāʾī calls the Qur'an's fit between expression and context its inimitability and contrasts a verbal noun for giving with ʿaṭāʾ, which also names a good gift. [5 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(الجاحظ); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 5:27/q41 What collateral report is preserved with these verse notes?  [* · reports]
- 5:27/q41/p1 A report of the Prophet's final illness identifies ʿAlī as one of the two men supporting him. — ʿĀʾisha recalls that he stayed with her by his wives' permission and entered supported by al-ʿAbbās and another man whom Ibn ʿAbbās identifies as ʿAlī; the note supplies no link to the brothers' verse.
