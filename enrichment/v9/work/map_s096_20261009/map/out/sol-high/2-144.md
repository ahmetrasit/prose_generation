## 2:144/q01 What does qad nara convey?  [قَدْ نَرَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether qad itself conveys frequency, rarity, confirmation, or past reference.
- 2:144/q01/p1 Qad conveys frequent divine seeing. — KASHSHAF-FULL: Qad conveys frequent divine seeing. ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 (الزمخشري): Al-Zamakhsharī glosses قد نرى as perhaps seeing, then says the construction conveys frequent seeing, comparing it with قد أترك. Against: ABUHAYYAN-FULL, d. 745 (author): Abū Ḥayyān rejects that explanation: perhaps usually conveys rarity, so it conflicts with the claim of frequency. SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (الزمخشري): Al-Zamakhsharī glosses قد نرى as perhaps we see and takes it to suggest abundant seeing, citing قد أترك القرن. Against: SAMIN-DURR, d. 756 (الشيخ): The shaykh says al-Zamakhsharī contradicts himself: ربما suggests rarity on the relevant account, not abundance; قد itself does not encode abundance, which comes from repeated turning being seen. BURHAN, d. 794 (جماعة من المتأخرين): Some later scholars assign قد an unusual sense of abundance; al-Zamakhsharī applies that reading to قد نرى تقلب وجهك. BURHAN, d. 794 (الزمخشري): Al-Zamakhsharī takes قد in قد نرى to indicate frequent or abundant occurrence. ITQAN, d. 911 (الزمخشري): Sibawayh and others recognize an intensive sense of قد; al-Zamakhshari applies it to 2:144 as repeated seeing, glossing it as perhaps or often seeing the Prophet's face turn heavenward. BURSEVI, d. 1127 (author): Although قد with a present verb usually suggests rarity, here it is used by antithetical metaphor to suggest abundance. ALUSI-FULL, d. 1270 (سيبويه): The frequency may be a lexical force of “قد” or an inference from the actual context; the latter account is attributed to Sibawayh. SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT (author): Here قد can indicate frequency: God has often seen the Prophet looking toward the sky for the direction of prayer. ## 2:144/q52 When may a mosque's mihrab be followed?  [وَحَيْثُ مَا كُنتُمْ · law]
