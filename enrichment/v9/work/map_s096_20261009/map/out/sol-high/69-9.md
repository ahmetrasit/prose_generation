# 69:9: 17 questions

## 69:9/q01 Who is Pharaoh here, and what marks him out?  [فِرْعَوْنُ · referent]
- 69:9/q01/p1 The Pharaoh of Moses, the arrogant Egyptian ruler. — Bursevî says he is singled out for extreme arrogance and self-exaltation. Biqāʿī says he claimed divinity, forgot God’s power, denied the Creator, and adopted naturalism. Kuran Yolu cites 7:103. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 69:9/q02 How is وَمَن قَبْلَهُ read, and whom does it include?  [وَمَن قَبْلَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: The qāf and bāʾ vowels distinguish temporal precedence from spatial company, though some notes also allow ‘with him’ under the fatḥa wording; the context determines whether a general expression includes believers.
- 69:9/q02/p1 With fatḥa on qāf and still bāʾ, it means earlier unbelieving peoples. — Ṭabarī lists most Medinan, Kufan and Meccan readers except Kisāʾī; other sources list Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim in some transmissions, Ibn ʿĀmir, Ḥamza and others. Ibn ʿAṭiyya points to the ensuing Noah account; Abū Ḥayyān names Noah’s people. Wāḥidī, Rāzī and Fārisī say general man is contextually restricted to disbelievers (Fārisī cites 69:10 and 22:42). Bursevî and Ālūsī call this a general mention after ʿĀd and Thamūd. Ṭabarī accepts both readings. Naḥḥās answers Abū ʿUbayd’s objection that Pharaoh’s people included believers: the context specifies wrongdoers. Sources vary in describing which transmitters use each vocalization. [35 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء المدينة والكوفة ومكة خلا الكسائي); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو جعفر وشيبة ونافع وابن كثير والأعمش وحمزة); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم في رواية أبان وأبو عمرو والكسائي); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ(من قرأ); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الآخرون); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة وأبو جعفر وشيبة وأبو عبد الرحمن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الباقون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور وأبو جعفر وشيبة والسلمي); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قراءة الفتح); NASHR(البصريان، والكسائي); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عاصم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; QIRAAT-ER; STUDYQURAN; NAHHAS+; TAB-FULL+; NAHHAS(أبو عبيد)-]
- 69:9/q02/p2 With kasra on qāf and fatḥa on bāʾ, it means those with or around Pharaoh: his followers, army, Copts or contemporaries. — Ṭabarī, Baghawī, Nashr and others associate it with the Basrans and Kisāʾī; further lists include Abū ʿAmr, Yaʿqūb, al-Ḥasan, Abū Rajāʾ, al-Jaḥdarī, Ṭalḥa and some ʿĀṣim transmissions. Ubayy’s and Ibn Masʿūd’s ‘those with him,’ Abū Mūsā’s ‘alongside him,’ Ṭalḥa’s ‘around him,’ and a reported ‘those following him’ support the spatial sense. Sībawayh, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Rāzī trace qabl from what is in front to adjacent or attached persons; Ibn Jawzī also records qibal as ‘with me’ or ‘within my capacity.’ Fārisī says even fatḥa qabl can denote adjacency, supported by Ubayy. Ḥasan combines those before and those with him; Ibn Jurayj interprets the fatḥa wording as company. Abū ʿUbayd and Abū Ḥātim prefer the companion reading; Ṭabarī validates both. ## 69:9/q03 Do the earlier peoples include ʿĀd and Thamūd?  [وَمَن قَبْلَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the general expression repeats the two named peoples or adds other earlier peoples.
- 69:9/q03/p1 They are included among earlier generations, which extend back to Noah’s people. — Elmalılı expressly includes ʿĀd and Thamūd and calls the phrase a generalization after particular examples. Kuran Yolu likewise includes both. [3 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:9/q03/p2 They are excluded here because the preceding verses already named them. — Bursevî says the prior unbelievers here are other than ʿĀd and Thamūd, a general statement after their particular mention. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 69:9/q04 How is ٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ read?  [وَٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the noun is transmitted in singular or plural form.
- 69:9/q04/p1 The common reading is plural, ‘the overturned ones/towns.’ — Jishumī and Qurṭubī contrast the common plural with a singular variant. [2 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن والجحدري)]
- 69:9/q04/p2 Al-Ḥasan reads the singular al-muʾtafikah; al-Jaḥdarī is also named. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ālūsī name al-Ḥasan; Qurṭubī also names al-Jaḥdarī. [4 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن والجحدري); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 69:9/q05 Whom or what does ٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ designate?  [وَٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the term identifies Lot’s places and people, a wider class of overturned sinners, or Qārūn’s sunken people.
- 69:9/q05/p1 Lot’s overturned towns and their inhabitants. — Sources variously say towns, people, lands, crops, or communities; Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı explain the towns as a name for inhabitants, ellipsis, or collective usage. Ālūsī notes coordination with persons who can ‘come.’ Muqātil calls them deniers. Asad, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran identify Sodom and Gomorrah; Asad cites 11:69 and 11:83, Kuran Yolu cites 7:80–84 and 11:77–83, and Study Quran cites these Lot passages. Elmalılı calls their mention specification after generalization. [34 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الأكثرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ, Z)]
- 69:9/q05/p2 More broadly, peoples overturned or destroyed because of their wrongdoing. — Farrāʾ and Abū Isḥāq say they were overturned for wrong; Zajjāj and Ibn Jawzī emphasize denial or lying. Baghawī gives the generic view, while Ṭabrisī extends the term from Lot’s towns to overturned nations. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 69:9/q05/p3 Qārūn and his people, who were sunk into the earth. — Māwardī offers this alternative; Ibn Jawzī attributes it to him. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي)]

## 69:9/q06 Why are they called ٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ?  [وَٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name points to their physical inversion or their falsehood, though some notes combine the two.
- 69:9/q06/p1 The name evokes physical overturning: their places were turned upside down. — Ibn Qutayba, Qatāda, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Nasafī and Bursevî explain the name by inversion. Damghānī groups this use of ifk with 53:53 and 9:70. Māwardī also allows sinking into the earth. [9 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 69:9/q06/p2 It can evoke ifk, falsehood: the overturning of truth into lies. — Māwardī offers derivation from afika, a lie. Zajjāj associates the name with their denial of messengers and inversion of truth; Ibn Jawzī reports this explanation. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج)]

## 69:9/q07 How many of Lot’s towns are named, and what are their names?  [وَٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different counts and town-name lists; some name two cities without claiming these are the full list.
- 69:9/q07/p1 Four: Sodom, Amorah, Sabura and Damura. — Muqātil gives this list; Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise counts four villages without naming them. [2 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 69:9/q07/p2 Five: Sabʿa, Saʿra, ʿAmra, Duma and Sodom. — Qurṭubī reports Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb al-Quraẓī’s list and calls Sodom the largest. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن كعب القرظي)]
- 69:9/q07/p3 Five: Ṣaʿbah, Ṣaʿdah, ʿUmrah, Dūmā and Sodom. — Bursevî gives this differently worded five-town list. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 69:9/q07/p4 The two named towns are Sodom and Gomorrah. — Asad, Kuran Yolu and Study Quran use this identification; their notes do not give a total count. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ, Z)]

## 69:9/q08 What happened to Lot’s settlements?  [وَٱلْمُؤْتَفِكَٰتُ · reports]
turns on: The reports agree on ruin but differ in the agents, sequence, and accompanying stones, sinking, flood, or wind.
- 69:9/q08/p1 Their land or towns were overturned upon the people. — Qatāda reports the land overturned with them; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says tops became bottoms. This is the shared core of several accounts. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL]
- 69:9/q08/p2 Gabriel uprooted and raised the city, overturned it, and then stones followed. — Ibn Zayd cites 53:53 and the stone description in Hūd 11:83; he glosses musawwamah there as prepared for punishment. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 69:9/q08/p3 They were overturned, pelted with sulfur stones, sunk, and inundated. — Biqāʿī supplies this sequence of punishments. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 69:9/q08/p4 They were ruined by an earthquake and covered by a stone-bearing wind with sand and pebbles. — Iṣlāḥī gives this account and says Quraysh could often see the remains. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 69:9/q09 How is the hamza in بِٱلْخَاطِئَةِ pronounced?  [بِٱلْخَاطِئَةِ · readings]
- 69:9/q09/p1 It may be softened to yāʾ without changing the meaning. — Qiraat ER calls this a phonological substitution. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 69:9/q10 What is بِٱلْخَاطِئَةِ referring to?  [بِٱلْخَاطِئَةِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the form is an abstract noun, a description of deed(s), or a person; sources also specify different sins within the broader error.
- 69:9/q10/p1 Sin, error, or sins generally. — Ibn Qutayba, Mujāhid and ʿAṭāʾ gloss it as sins committed. Zajjāj and Jurjānī stress a grave error; Elmalılı says error is the opposite of correctness rather than intention. Rāzī, Ṭabrisī, Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr analyze it as a verbal noun; Ibn ʿĀshūr says its feminine ending no longer confines it to a single instance. Bursevî also gives heedlessness. Ibn Kathīr reports al-Rabīʿ’s disobedience. [21 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الجرجاني,مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الربيع,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 69:9/q10/p2 The particular error is disbelief, denial of revelation and messengers, or disobedience. — Zajjāj and Wāḥidī cite 69:10, ‘they disobeyed their Lord’s messenger’; Muqātil, Ibn Kathīr and Elmalılı specify disbelief or denial. Kuran Yolu says each people denied and disobeyed its own prophet. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls this missing the path of servitude. [15 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:9/q10/p3 The particular sin is associating partners with God. — Al-Kalbī gives shirk; Baghawī and Ṭabrisī specify it alongside disobedience and disbelief. Biqāʿī includes it among Lot’s people’s offenses. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 69:9/q10/p4 It means the wrongful deed or deeds characterized by grave error. — Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and others offer sinful action(s) rather than error as an abstract noun. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Samīn posit an omitted ‘deed’; Ālūsī says error is figuratively attributed to acts, reflecting the perpetrators’ gravity. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an active participle with feminine ending and says the definite form is generic, each group bearing its punishable sin. Biqāʿī includes Lot’s people’s sexual wrongdoing, shirk and other disobedience. [18 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قول آخر); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 69:9/q10/p5 It can mean the erring person responsible for the wrongful acts. — Ṭabrisī reports this alternative alongside wrongful deeds. [1 notes: TABRISI(قول آخر)]

## 69:9/q11 What does the bāʾ in بِٱلْخَاطِئَةِ express?  [بِٱلْخَاطِئَةِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the bāʾ accompanies their coming with the sin or gives its cause.
- 69:9/q11/p1 Accompaniment: they came with the sin. — Bursevî allows this analysis. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 69:9/q11/p2 Causation: they came because of the sin. — Bursevî regards this as the more apparent analysis. [1 notes: BURSEVI+]

## 69:9/q12 How does this verse frame the surrounding account?  [* · links]
- 69:9/q12/p1 It widens the examples of punished peoples and leads to the next verse’s explanation of disobeying messengers. — Study Quran sees a shift from pre-Islamic Arabian deniers to opponents of prophets in the Judeo-Christian tradition. Bursevî and Ālūsī see generalization after ʿĀd and Thamūd; Elmalılı and Kuran Yolu include those two in the broader set. Iṣlāḥī says Lot’s people shared their crime and fate. Kuran Yolu explains the next verse’s singular ‘messenger’ distributively: each people had its own prophet. Ṭabrisī refers back to an earlier explanation of ‘Pharaoh and those before him.’ [8 notes: STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI]

## 69:9/q13 What significance do the named peoples’ sins and punishments have?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources draw either the historical moral of shared wrongdoing and punishment or Bursevî’s exhortation about lasting life.
- 69:9/q13/p1 Their shared denial and disobedience bring severe punishment, and Lot’s unprecedented sins brought an unparalleled punishment. — Kuran Yolu says each people denied its own prophet; Iṣlāḥī aligns Lot’s fate with ʿĀd and Thamūd. Bursevî says Lot’s indecency prompted specific mention. Biqāʿī uses the unprecedented sins and extraordinary punishment to refute natural philosophers. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 69:9/q13/p2 Their obliteration urges the wise to seek enduring truthful remembrance and a second life through divine unveiling. — Bursevî’s Persian verse depicts wrathful blasts reducing people to traceless dust; he invokes Abraham’s prayer for a good name in later generations (26:84) and says true lasting life is through divine unveiling and universal effusion. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 69:9/q14 What does the verse imply about falsehood, sin and disobedience?  [* · theology]
- 69:9/q14/p1 They are human acts. — Jishumī states this explicitly. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 69:9/q15 How are the people in this verse characterized?  [* · interpretation]
- 69:9/q15/p1 They are deniers. — Ṭabarī transmits this gloss from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 69:9/q16 What does the verse’s imagery of reversal and undulation evoke?  [* · rhetoric]
- 69:9/q16/p1 It evokes the Ḥāqqah and the scenes of rebellion that caused it. — Elmalılı connects the expression’s reversal and undulation to the sūra’s event and those scenes. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 69:9/q17 What inward realities do Pharaoh, his company, the overturned cities and their error signify?  [* · inward]
- 69:9/q17/p1 Pharaoh is the commanding soul, his company its faculties and helpers, and the overturned cities spiritual faculties diverted toward sensory appearances. — Qāshānī says the error is turning from inward realities to outward appearances. [2 notes: QASHANI]
