# 28:64: 18 questions

## 28:64/q01 How is قِيلَ read?  [وَقِيلَ · readings]
- 28:64/q01/p1 The passive reading admits ishmām of the qāf. — Qirāʾāt-ER says ishmām blends a trace of the active ḍamma into the passive kasra, signaling the underlying active form while preserving passive morphology. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:64/q02 Who is addressed by the command?  [وَقِيلَ ٱدْعُوا۟ · referent]
- 28:64/q02/p1 The idolaters or unbelieving worshippers are addressed. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Durr call them the unbelievers or children of Adam; Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies those summoned in 28:62, after their leaders begin excusing them. Al-Ṭabarī calls them idolaters; al-Ṭabrisī calls them followers. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; DURR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:64/q03 Who speaks, and why is the speaker unstated?  [وَقِيلَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the passive command is voiced by God or by Hell's keepers.
- 28:64/q03/p1 God speaks; the passive leaves Him unnamed because context identifies Him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the context makes God clear despite the passive wording. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:64/q03/p2 The keepers of Hell issue the call as a threat and rebuke. — Bursevī identifies the keepers and says they command worshippers of other than God to summon idols and similar claimed partners. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 28:64/q04 What is the force of the command to call the partners?  [ٱدْعُوا۟ شُرَكَآءَكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the imperative with its following fa marks actual obedience, or depicts a confused call; the notes also give rebuke and a fleeting hope as effects.
- 28:64/q04/p1 It is a real command that publicly rebukes and humiliates the worshippers by exposing the partners' inability to help. — Al-Rāzī calls it reproach and a warning to people now; al-Biqāʿī says followers obey any order. Al-Ālūsī says the fa after the imperative favors a genuine command, and that public exposure heightens humiliation. Bursevī calls it threat and rebuke; Kuranyolu says it is mocking. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:64/q04/p2 The command briefly raises hope before the unanswered call brings despair. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the false hope is immediately reversed; he and Ibn Kathīr link the call to their former hope that idols would intercede or rescue them. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 28:64/q04/p3 There is no genuine request; the worshippers call in bewilderment. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative and says a real command is suggested by the fa. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī describe extreme bewilderment; al-Biqāʿī describes desperate confusion. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 28:64/q05 Who are the 'partners'?  [شُرَكَآءَكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the claimed partners are imagined idols or actual beings who were worshipped.
- 28:64/q05/p1 They are the idols, gods, or other objects of worship falsely treated as God's partners. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī and others identify idols or beings worshipped besides God. Asad calls them beings or powers; Study Quran cites 7:195, 17:36 and 34:22. Islahi names Lāt, Manāt, ʿUzzā and Nāʾilah and says these imagined Arab deities never existed. [16 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:64/q05/p2 Actual beings who were worshipped are a separate case from Arabia's imagined idols. — Islahi distinguishes these cases. He reports Jesus disowning his worshippers, denying knowledge of their worship and affirming that he taught worship of the One God. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Jesus (sws))]

## 28:64/q06 Why are the partners called 'yours'?  [شُرَكَآءَكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 28:64/q06/p1 The possessive attributes the partnership to the worshippers' own false claim. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Bursevī and Ibn ʿĀshūr stress that God did not confer partnership; Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 6:94. Al-Biqāʿī says the claim arose through the worshippers' dedication of wealth, speech, time and conduct to idols. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:64/q07 Why do they call the partners?  [فَدَعَوْهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the appeal is driven by hope of rescue or made without expectation of a reply.
- 28:64/q07/p1 They ask for rescue or intercession against punishment, repeating their worldly reliance on those beings. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Kuranyolu say they seek rescue. Ibn Kathīr says this matches their worldly hope; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies belief in intercession. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī add bewilderment, while al-Biqāʿī calls it desperate clinging. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:64/q07/p2 They call without expecting an answer; the futile act itself is chastisement. — Study Quran reports this view from some commentators. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 28:64/q08 What does the partners' failure to answer mean, and why do they fail?  [فَلَمْ يَسْتَجِيبُوا۟ لَهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations differ between simple powerlessness, lack of a useful argument, temporary inability to speak, and the nonexistence of imagined gods.
- 28:64/q08/p1 They give no effective answer or help, since powerless idols cannot respond or rescue anyone. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī cite the parallel in 18:52. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls idols inert; al-Biqāʿī says they cannot perceive or act and are doomed; Bursevī says they lack power. Ibn Kathīr says the worshippers then realize they are bound for Hell. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others stress failure of aid. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:64/q08/p2 They provide no good or persuasive reply. — Al-Zajjāj says they fail to answer with an argument; Qatāda, as reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, says they give no good answer or reply. Al-Wāḥidī says no reply could benefit them. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 28:64/q08/p3 They cannot reply because they are occupied or their mouths have been sealed. — Al-Ālūsī reports these alternatives without choosing between them. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:64/q08/p4 The imagined deities cannot answer because they never existed. — Islahi specifically names the imagined Arab deities and distinguishes them from actual beings who were worshipped. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:64/q09 Who sees the punishment?  [وَرَأَوُا۟ ٱلْعَذَابَ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun's antecedent and whether nonliving idols can be described as seeing or being guided.
- 28:64/q09/p1 The idolaters who called their partners see it. — Al-Ālūsī prefers the nearest, apparent referent; Abū Ḥayyān says the worshippers are the natural subject and cites 2:166 against al-Rāzī's idol reading. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe the callers' vision. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 28:64/q09/p2 Both the worshippers and the worshipped see it. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this from al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Muqātil; al-Rāzī connects it with their escape from punishment had they been guided. Al-Biqāʿī says everyone sees it; al-Ālūsī also reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك ومقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:64/q09/p3 The pronoun can refer to the idols alone. — Al-Rāzī argues that the rational plural follows the worshippers' belief and the idols would see punishment if alive and guided; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī reject or find this remote. Study Quran reports the same reading. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); RAZI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 28:64/q10 What punishment do they see?  [ٱلْعَذَابَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the sight is of immediate afterlife punishment or worldly traces of former peoples' punishment.
- 28:64/q10/p1 They see the punishment of the Hereafter, now present and unavoidable. — Al-Ṭabarī says they see it with their own eyes and connects 2:166; al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī say it engulfs them. Al-Biqāʿī says all know it will reach them without prevention; Ibn Kathīr identifies Hell. Ibn ʿĀshūr's first two readings place the scene in the afterlife, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the sight explains the failed rescue plea. [14 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:64/q10/p2 They see traces of punishments inflicted on earlier nations in this world. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's fifth reading cites 14:45: they had inhabited wrongdoers' dwellings and seen what God did; learning from this would have led to faith and escape from greater punishment. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:64/q11 In what sense do they 'see' the punishment?  [وَرَأَوُا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether رأوا denotes visual perception or cognition, and whether a second object may be omitted.
- 28:64/q11/p1 They see it visually. — Al-Ṭabarī explicitly says with their own eyes. Ibn ʿĀshūr's first two readings describe afterlife sight; al-Ālūsī allows seeing the beginnings of punishment or seeing it as though already present. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:64/q11/p2 Seeing means inward recognition or knowledge that punishment is real or has overtaken them. — Al-Rāzī uses inward sight in his third direct reading. Ibn ʿĀshūr's third reading leaves the second object of 'know' unstated and allows either conjunction or circumstance; al-Ālūsī reports grammarians who dispute that omitted object and an alternative circumstantial parse. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:64/q12 How is 'and they saw' connected to the preceding clause?  [وَرَأَوُا۟ ٱلْعَذَابَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the wāw coordinates events in sequence or marks the callers' existing circumstance.
- 28:64/q12/p1 The conjunction coordinates sight with the partners' failure to answer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's first reading treats the subsequent لو clause as independent; his cognitive third reading also permits coordination. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:64/q12/p2 The conjunction is circumstantial: punishment is already in view when they are told to call. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's second reading says this explains their bewilderment over escape and treats لو independently; his cognitive third reading permits the same construction. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:64/q13 How does the لو clause complete the scene, and what does guidance mean here?  [لَوْ أَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا۟ يَهْتَدُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether لو is conditional with an omitted consequence, optative, or a complete conditional; the proposals also differ on the referent and time of the punishment and the sense of guidance.
- 28:64/q13/p1 If they had followed guidance in worldly life, they would have escaped or not seen afterlife punishment. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, al-Baghawī, al-Nasafī and others supply an omitted answer. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this completion expresses grace; al-Ālūsī defends its contextual ellipsis. Study Quran calls it salvation from the Fire. Bursevī allows guidance as a means to avert torment. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون وأهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وغيره من المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 28:64/q13/p2 Had they been guided, they would not have followed or called upon idols. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Burhān and al-Biqāʿī give this completion; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it expresses regret over idolatry. The implied answer is omitted. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون وأهل المعاني); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج وغيره من المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:64/q13/p3 Had they been guided, they would have recognized the punishment or truth as real. — Al-Ṭabrisī prefers this omitted completion because context signals it; al-Rāzī reports it, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran describe recognizing punishment or truth. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr glosses يهتدون as 'know/recognize.' [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); RAZI-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); TABATABAI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); TABRISI+]
- 28:64/q13/p4 The clause expresses their regretful wish that they had followed guidance in worldly life. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr connect the wish with seeing punishment; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports linking the conditional to the preceding scene. Al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Islahi and Kuranyolu take لو as optative or explain the wish. Al-Ālūsī reports objections to third-person optative wording and replies using reported speech, bewildered longing or God's compassionate metaphor in 2:103; al-Ṭībī calls it effect for cause. Islahi says it conveys sorrow and the torment's severity. [15 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); RAZI-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التقريب); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:64/q13/p5 Had they known a stratagem or path of escape, they would have repelled punishment. — Al-Rāzī reports this completion; al-Samīn al-Ḥalabī gives 'when they saw' or 'to repel it' as omitted responses. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī mention a way to avert it; al-Ālūsī reports this conditional answer. Ibn ʿĀshūr supplies an omitted object of 'be guided,' namely escape. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:64/q13/p6 Seeing punishment makes it time for them to seek guidance, though belief then is too late. — Al-Rāzī reports this completion and cites 26:201, where belief comes only after painful punishment is seen. Yaḥyā reports that afterlife recognition cannot save one who did not follow worldly guidance. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم)]
- 28:64/q13/p7 With no omitted answer, terror from the command prevents the worshippers from seeing anything. — Al-Rāzī prefers direct syntax to an ellipsis that breaks the verse's arrangement; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report it but reject al-Rāzī's proposals as strained. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الرازي)+; RAZI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:64/q13/p8 With no omitted answer, the idols would see punishment if they were alive and capable of guidance. — Al-Rāzī treats the rational plural as reflecting the worshippers' view; Study Quran reports this reading. Abū Ḥayyān objects that 'the guided' cannot naturally mean living beings; al-Ālūsī finds the proposals obscure. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ALUSI-FULL(الرازي)+; RAZI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:64/q13/p9 With no omitted answer, guided people would inwardly know the punishment to be true. — Al-Rāzī's third direct reading takes seeing as knowledge in worldly life; Study Quran says guided idolaters would have known to fear punishment in the Hereafter. Al-Ālūsī reports it but finds the direct proposals unconvincing. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الرازي); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); ALUSI-FULL(الرازي)+; RAZI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 28:64/q13/p10 If they had learned from worldly traces of earlier nations' punishment, they would have abandoned shirk and escaped greater punishment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr's fifth reading invokes 14:45 and says they should have believed the Prophet. He says all five readings contribute intended meanings and the verse is concise yet rich. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:64/q14 What do كانوا and the imperfect يهتدون contribute?  [كَانُوا۟ يَهْتَدُونَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical force changes with conditional versus optative لو.
- 28:64/q14/p1 In conditional readings, كانوا reinforces the proposition and the imperfect marks guidance that is now cut off. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this account for his first three readings, where an omitted object of guidance supplies escape. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:64/q14/p2 In the wish reading, كانوا has its ordinary past sense and the imperfect evokes sustained guidance regretted after death. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says useful guidance would have needed to continue until life's end. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:64/q15 How does this verse fit the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes focus either on the immediate sequence of questions and replies or on the broader argument against intercession.
- 28:64/q15/p1 It continues the rebuke after the previous challenge and failed excuses, then leads to the question about the messengers. — Abū Ḥayyān notes this is a second order to invoke partners following their failed reply; Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the addressees to 28:62 and the leaders' attempted excuses. Al-Biqāʿī says the scene moves from failed rescue without guidance to inquiry about messengers. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:64/q15/p2 It exposes the false promise of Quraysh's idols and intercessors and their inability to help in the Hereafter. — Islahi says Quraysh saw idols as sources of success and feared abandoning them; the coming passage refutes their hope for help after death. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:64/q16 What other Quranic scenes illuminate this verse?  [* · links]
- 28:64/q16/p1 Other verses depict the same futile invocation of partners and the sight of punishment. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī and al-Wāḥidī cite 18:52; Ibn Kathīr adds 18:53 and Hell. Al-Ṭabarī and Study Quran cite 7:195; al-Ṭabarī links visual punishment with 2:166. Study Quran also mentions 17:36 and 34:22 concerning the supposed partners. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:64/q17 What does the whole scene teach about alleged partners?  [* · theology]
- 28:64/q17/p1 Reliance on alleged partners or intercessors collapses at judgment and offers no rescue. — Al-Rāzī says their unanswered invocation makes punishment certain and warns people now; Islahi says the scene reveals the baselessness of Quraysh's hopes. Ibn Kathīr says the worshippers realize they are bound for Hell. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 28:64/q18 What is reported about the revelation setting of al-Qasas?  [* · sciences]
- 28:64/q18/p1 All of al-Qasas is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]
