# 26:219: 14 questions

## 26:219/q01 What does taqallub mean as a word?  [تَقَلُّبَكَ · meaning]
- 26:219/q01/p1 Turning or moving about, including coming and going. — Jishumī derives it from turning something over by hand or turning oneself; Bursevī applies the movement to rounds of inspection, and Iṣlāḥī calls it coming and going. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:219/q02 How does the accusative taqallubaka connect to the preceding verb?  [تَقَلُّبَكَ · grammar]
- 26:219/q02/p1 It is a verbal noun object coordinated with the object of 'He sees': He sees you and your turning. — Jishumī supplies an understood yarā, while al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān describe coordination with the object of yarāka; Abū Ḥayyān identifies this as the majority reading's syntax. [3 notes: JISHUMI; SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 26:219/q03 What variant verbal reading is reported here?  [وَتَقَلُّبَكَ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted verbal form and person, in contrast with the common nominal reading.
- 26:219/q03/p1 Junāḥ ibn Ḥubaysh reads wa-yuqallibuka, 'and He turns you.' — Al-Samīn specifies the doubled lām and coordination with yarāka; Abū Ḥayyān reports it as a present verb coordinated with yarāka. Ālūsī says it may coordinate with yarāka or taqūm and takes the turning to suggest complete, concentrated prayer. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(جناح بن حبيش); ALUSI-FULL(جناح بن حبيش)]
- 26:219/q03/p2 A first-person plural reading has wa-nuqallibuka, 'and We turn you.' — Rāzī reports this form. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 26:219/q04 What does the Prophet's turning among the prostrators describe?  [وَتَقَلُّبَكَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether turning denotes prayer postures, rounds of worshippers, rearward sight, activity among believers, ancestral or prophetic passage, or an inward movement; several authors weigh the apparent wording and surrounding time frame.
- 26:219/q04/p1 His prayer movements among worshippers, especially while leading the congregation. — Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, al-Farrāʾ and others enumerate standing, bowing, prostration and sometimes sitting; Qatāda, Muqātil and others distinguish God seeing him alone and with the congregation. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this as the apparent wording; Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls prayer apparent, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers congregational prayer. Ālūsī connects full movement with concentrated humility. - 26:219/q04/p2 His visits to inspect companions engaged in night worship. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī and others relate this to the Prophet's own night prayer, the worshippers' work for the afterlife, and his unobtrusive inspection. Iṣlāḥī places visits in the mosque and relates them to his care for followers. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 26:219/q04/p3 His gaze moving over worshippers behind him, whom he could see during prayer. — Mujāhid's explanation is supported by reports of the Prophet seeing those behind as clearly as those ahead; Study Quran compares turning the face in 2:144. Ibn ʿAṭiyya rejects the connection to this passage; al-Qurṭubī accepts the vision report but calls this interpretation remote; Ālūsī also deems it far-fetched. [21 notes: MUJAHID(قيل); YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس، مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(another interpretation); IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-; TAB-FULL(من قال)-]
- 26:219/q04/p4 His movements and changing states among believing companions generally. — Al-Ḥasan explains movements and travels among believers; Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda can describe believers as prostrators. Asad contrasts the believers with disobedient people in 26:216. Al-Ṭabarī rejects movement merely among people as distant from the ordinary sense of prostration, though all things and their shadows prostrate. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); TAB-FULL-]
- 26:219/q04/p5 His activity among believers while managing religion and advancing God's word. — Al-Māwardī relates it to affairs and rulings among Muslims, paired with rising to fight polytheists; al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān stress that no state is hidden from God. Ālūsī describes proclamation, and Elmalılı service among believers. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); ELMALILI]
- 26:219/q04/p6 His passage through the loins of prophetic or monotheist forebears until his birth. — Reports name Adam, Noah and Abraham and sometimes say lawful marriage without fornication; some speak of his light or spirit. Bursevī allows intervening nonprophetic ancestors. Elmalılı objects that ancestral transfer is past while the context concerns present and future; Ālūsī says this lineage interpretation cannot establish the parents' faith. - 26:219/q04/p7 His conduct in the states and mission of earlier prophets. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr compares the Prophet's activity to earlier prophets; Elmalılı calls it service in the prophetic office. Ālūsī dismisses this reading as far-fetched. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد); IBNABIHATIM(سعيد); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير); ELMALILI]
- 26:219/q04/p8 His remembrance and description passing over the tongues of earlier prophets. — Al-Māwardī transmits this further interpretation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 26:219/q04/p9 His being alone in private devotion. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الحسن البصري)]
- 26:219/q04/p10 An inward turning of the Prophet's attributes through saints and prophets. — Al-Sulamī transmits an unnamed inward reading. [1 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:219/q04/p11 The inward secret's continual turning in divine nearness. — Al-Sulamī's unnamed source associates prostration with intimacy and nearness, citing 96:19; al-Sulamī describes continual turning in the sanctified place of witnessed presence. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]
- 26:219/q04/p12 His progress through annihilation in God's acts, attributes and essence, through self, heart and spirit. — Al-Qāshānī locates this turning among those already annihilated in God. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 26:219/q04/p13 His place among prostrating spirits created from his spirit. — Bursevī transmits a mystical saying that God knew his mastery of Adam's children and worthiness of honor. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ويقال)]

## 26:219/q05 Who are the prostrators?  [ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether prostration identifies those now praying, believers broadly, earlier prophets or ancestors, or spirits.
- 26:219/q05/p1 People performing prayer, especially the Prophet's congregation. — Qatāda, Ibn Zayd, al-Wāḥidī and others gloss them as worshippers. Al-Ṭabarī says ordinary usage points to familiar prostration; Ālūsī explains their designation by prostration's special nearness to God and its rank as the best pillar according to many imams. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(عكرمة وعطية عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وعكرمة وغيرهما); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وجماعة من المفسرين); TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 26:219/q05/p2 Believers generally, among whom the Prophet moves. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda call believers prostrators; Asad contrasts believers with those disobeying him at 26:216. Al-Ṭabarī argues broad reference to people departs from the phrase's ordinary sense. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وقتادة); ASAD-NOTES; TAB-FULL-]
- 26:219/q05/p3 The earlier prophets or monotheist forebears. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr understands prophetic conduct; lineage reports refer to prophetic or believing loins. Ālūsī dismisses the prophetic-conduct reading as far-fetched; Elmalılı rejects the ancestral reading because its past time jars with the context. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد); THALABI(سعيد بن جبير); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(ابن عباس وعكرمة وأبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جبير); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); ELMALILI-]
- 26:219/q05/p4 Worshippers' spirits in the world of spirits. — Bursevī transmits that each spirit was created from the Prophet's spirit. [1 notes: BURSEVI(ويقال)]

## 26:219/q06 What did the Prophet say or do concerning worshippers behind him?  [وَتَقَلُّبَكَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · reports]
- 26:219/q06/p1 He said he saw worshippers behind his back as clearly as those before him and instructed them to straighten their rows or complete their bowing and prostration. — Anas, Abū Hurayra, Jābir from al-Bāqir and other reports vary in the instruction: align or close rows, complete bowing and prostration, or do not precede him in rising and bowing. Al-Qurṭubī calls the vision report authentic, Ibn Kathīr sound, Ālūsī locates Anas in al-Bukhārī and Abū Hurayra in Abū Dāwūd; Rāzī and Study Quran give no grading. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explains the rising as lifting the forehead from prostration. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أنس بن مالك); THALABI(أنس); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); KASHSHAF-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة,أنس); TABATABAI(جابر); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]

## 26:219/q07 What is reported about his visits after the night vigil duty ended?  [وَتَقَلُّبَكَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · reports]
- 26:219/q07/p1 He visited companions' homes to inspect their continuing worship and heard recitation and remembrance buzzing like wasps or bees. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Study Quran relate the same account; Rāzī gives no grading. Bursevī says the five prayers on the Night Journey lifted the obligation. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 26:219/q08 What obligation of night vigil preceded these visits?  [وَتَقَلُّبَكَ · sciences]
- 26:219/q08/p1 The Prophet and companions had been obliged to keep night vigil before the duty was lifted. — Bursevī calls this the sounder view and relates the lifting to the five prayers on the Night Journey; he also reports Ibn ʿAbbās saying earlier prophets had the same obligation. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ابن عباس); BURSEVI+]

## 26:219/q09 Does this verse attest congregational prayer in the Qur'an?  [وَتَقَلُّبَكَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · law]
- 26:219/q09/p1 Yes; the Prophet's turning among prostrators was cited as its Qur'anic mention. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī report Muqātil reciting this verse when Abū Ḥanīfa could not recall a proof; Abū Ḥayyān instead reports Abū Ḥanīfa citing the verse after Muqātil's question. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 26:219/q10 What does the lineage interpretation imply about the faith of the Prophet's forebears?  [وَتَقَلُّبَكَ ٱلسَّٰجِدِينَ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'prostrators' proves every ancestor's belief, whether 'father' Āzar can mean another relative, and the force of the pure-lineage report.
- 26:219/q10/p1 It supports the belief that his ancestors were monotheists or believers, his lineage passing through pure, lawful unions. — The Rāfiḍa infer believing ancestors from descent among prostrators, cite the report of pure loins and wombs and 9:28 on unbelievers' impurity, and explain Āzar in 6:74 as Abraham's uncle or maternal forefather: Ishmael is called Jacob's sons' father in 2:133, the Prophet called al-ʿAbbās father, and Jesus belongs to Abraham's descendants in 6:84–85. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report lawful marriage and no fornication. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(الرافضة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الرافضة); TABRISI(ابن عباس وعكرمة وأبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله); TABATABAI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL-]
- 26:219/q10/p2 The verse cannot establish universal ancestral belief against the apparent designation of Āzar as Abraham's father. — Rāzī rejects moving 'father' from its apparent meaning in 6:74, rejects assigning every sense to an ambiguous expression at once, and says a solitary pure-lineage report cannot outweigh the Qur'an. Ālūsī likewise withholds this verse as proof of the parents' faith, although he warns against confidently denying their belief; Bursevī notes that lineage through prophets allows nonprophetic ancestors. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]

## 26:219/q11 How does God's seeing here relate to what can exist or be known?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether sight is distinct from knowledge and whether its object must exist externally.
- 26:219/q11/p1 Divine vision is an unveiling distinct from knowledge and can relate to things not yet externally existent. — Ālūsī reports the knowers' comparison with seeing the Resurrection in a dream, while saying the divine mode befits God's majesty. Al-Sulamī's al-Wāsiṭī reads pre-eternal vision of the cosmos, and al-Sulamī says the Prophet's lineage was seen in absence and existence. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(العارفون); SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 26:219/q11/p2 Divine vision does not relate to nonexistent things. — Ālūsī attributes this to many theologians without settling it here. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(كثير من المتكلمين)]
- 26:219/q11/p3 Divine vision reduces to God's knowledge. — Ālūsī reports this view from some theologians and says the issue is discussed elsewhere. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(كثير من المتكلمين)]

## 26:219/q12 What reassurance follows from God seeing the Prophet here?  [* · interpretation]
- 26:219/q12/p1 God's awareness of his worship and state confirms support, sufficiency and protection, strengthening his reliance. — Al-Bayḍāwī connects it to power over enemies and help for allies; al-Biqāʿī says encompassing knowledge secures benefit and repels harm; Ālūsī says his state merits guardianship. Bursevī's inward source says awareness of the Beloved's sight makes worship's hardship light, even like carrying mountains. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 26:219/q13 How does this phrase connect with its surrounding discourse?  [* · links]
turns on: The scope of context considered: neighboring trust and care commands or the whole sūrah's closing discourse.
- 26:219/q13/p1 It follows divine mercy and the call to trust God by showing care for the Prophet and the believers. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī connect God's mercy and the Prophet's night worship; al-Biqāʿī ties the description to divine sufficiency after the trust command. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the compact pairing gives Muslims care alongside their Messenger. Iṣlāḥī links the Prophet's spiritual vigilance to the command of affection in 26:215 and compares Jesus urging disciples to stay awake in the Gospels. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:219/q13/p2 It belongs to the sūrah's return to true Qur'anic warnings and the prophetic histories as signs for stubborn Quraysh. — Iṣlāḥī says the concluding discourse revisits the opening theme: punishment signs may come and earlier prophetic stories already furnish signs. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:219/q14 How do the following divine names bear on God's seeing the Prophet?  [* · theology]
- 26:219/q14/p1 The All-Hearing covers what he says and the All-Knowing what he intends and does; hearing is a distinct attribute. — Rāzī argues that if hearing were merely knowledge of audible things, 'All-Knowing' would add nothing to 'All-Hearing.' [2 notes: RAZI-FULL]
