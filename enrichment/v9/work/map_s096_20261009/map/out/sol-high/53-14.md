# 53:14: 24 questions

## 53:14/q01 What does ʿinda indicate here?  [عِندَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adverb locates bodies, a moment, or rank.
- 53:14/q01/p1 Literal presence or nearness at the tree. — Damghānī glosses ʿinda as near or beside and says the tree and Garden are near one another; Burhān cites this sighting and Joseph's master at the door (12:25) for literal nearness. Suyūṭī allows physical presence or proximity; Rāzī calls this the spatial reading. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 53:14/q01/p2 A time: when the wise reach extreme bewilderment. — Rāzī says the word may denote the moment when wise minds are most bewildered, while Muhammad was not bewildered. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:14/q01/p3 Figurative nearness in honor and rank. — Suyūṭī distinguishes figurative proximity as elevated rank and cites 27:40, 38:47, 54:55, 3:169 and 66:11. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 53:14/q02 What does 'at the Sidra' modify, and whose location does it give?  [عِندَ سِدْرَةِ ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier action or person receives the locative phrase, and whether a divine vision can entail divine place.
- 53:14/q02/p1 It modifies 'he saw him': the sighting occurred with Muhammad at the tree. — Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Qurṭubī, Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr attach ʿinda to raʾāhu. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it modifies seeing rather than descent. Rāzī says Muhammad's presence there is clearer even if Gabriel is the seen one; if the seen one is God, place belongs to Muhammad, not God. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:14/q02/p2 It is a circumstantial phrase describing Gabriel at the tree. — Bursevī permits this grammatical attachment because Gabriel, a creature, can be seen at a particular place; he also permits attachment to the verb. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 53:14/q02/p3 It locates one of the descents at the tree. — Qurṭubī explains the phrase as Muhammad's location during one of those descents; Bursevī's alternative attachment to the verb of seeing remains possible. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 53:14/q02/p4 It cannot locate God physically at the tree. — Rāzī rejects taking the phrase as God's spatial location if the pronoun refers to God, while allowing Muhammad to be there during the vision. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-]

## 53:14/q03 What is sidra here?  [سِدْرَةِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the reported tree is literal, figurative, or an inward cosmic reality.
- 53:14/q03/p1 A real lote or nabq tree. — Ṭabarī appeals to reports from the Messenger and scholars. Wāḥidī calls sidr a kind of lote; Qurṭubī says its fruit is nabq, noting Yaʿqūb's Egyptian vowel variant but favoring the eloquent Prophetic form. Elmalılı cites the Kāmūs on garden and wild varieties, sweet or tart fruit and dense shade. Ālūsī cites Miʿrāj reports and God's ability to grow vegetation anywhere, comparing zaqqūm at Hell's root (37:64). Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the tāʾ makes one tree. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(يعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:14/q03/p2 A tree-like image of a celestial station where angels gather. — Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī report the gathering-under-shade analogy; Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests likeness to branching lote growth or boundary trees and compares the palm as an image for a believer. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:14/q03/p3 A figure for supreme astonishment or bewilderment. — Rāzī reports this gloss and says Muhammad was not bewildered or absent; Elmalılı notes seder's sense of dazzlement and reports Rāzī's alternative derivation, while recording Rāzī's preference for the physical tree. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî,müfessirîn); ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî)-]
- 53:14/q03/p4 The greatest spirit and return from annihilation to subsistence. — Qāshānī's inward reading says no rank lies beyond this spirit except pure ipseity. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 53:14/q03/p5 A symbol of Paradise's shade, peace, and fulfillment. — Asad transmits Rāghib's reading of the leafy tree as the shade of Paradise. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Raghib)]
- 53:14/q03/p6 The ultimate formation of the created world. — Elmalılı treats it as the boundary of formed bodies and dimensions, where the realm of command begins. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 53:14/q04 Why is a lote tree singled out for this station?  [سِدْرَةِ · rhetoric]
- 53:14/q04/p1 Its qualities suit the honored station and convey spiritual qualities. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the place is singled out for honor, signs and ascent's end; he names deep shade, good fruit and fragrance. Māwardī, reported by Qurṭubī, likens shade to deeds of faith, taste to hidden intention and scent to open speech. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الماوردي)]
- 53:14/q04/p2 Its shade pictures a gathering place. — Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī say angels gather there as people gather under a tree. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 53:14/q04/p3 The Qur'an leaves the tree's precise nature deliberately obscure. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes that God does not explain it and the next phrase only says something covered it. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 53:14/q05 Where is the Sidra located?  [سِدْرَةِ ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · referent]
turns on: The reports place it in different heavens or in relation to Paradise and the Throne; a symbolic reading defines a cosmic boundary.
- 53:14/q05/p1 In the seventh heaven, often described as right of the Throne. — Prophetic reports through Anas and Mālik ibn Ṣaʿṣaʿa put it at the seventh heaven after Abraham; Ibn al-Jawzī cites both Ṣaḥīḥ collections. Nasafī calls this the majority view; Rāzī calls it famous. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite sound or transmitted Miʿrāj reports. Some add the Throne's right side. [22 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس عن النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مالك بن صعصعة ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL(المشهور); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك); BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); STUDYQURAN(most commentators); NASAFI-FULL(الجمهور)+]
- 53:14/q05/p2 Above the seventh heaven, to the right of the Throne. — Muqātil gives the right-of-Throne location and jewel branches; Kalbī and Muqātil are cited by Wāḥidī and Ṭabrisī. Ibn al-Jawzī reports a view above the seventh, Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a celestial place above it, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī records similar reports. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); TABRISI(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مالك بن صعصعة ومقاتل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الروايات)]
- 53:14/q05/p3 In the sixth heaven. — Ibn Masʿūd's report puts the Night Journey's endpoint there; Ibn al-Jawzī says Muslim alone reports this location and attributes it also to Ḍaḥḥāk. Qurṭubī cites Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim. Later sources record both sixth and seventh. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود والضحاك); RAZI-FULL(المشهور); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); ELMALILI]
- 53:14/q05/p4 Between the sixth and seventh heavens. — Study Quran attributes this to Ṭū, with the Garden of Refuge identified as the seventh heaven. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Ṭū)]
- 53:14/q05/p5 At the base of the Throne, above its bearers. — Kaʿb's paired reports put it at the Throne's root or above the bearers; Ibn ʿAbbās asks Kaʿb. Baghawī and Durr transmit the same setting. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(كعب الأحبار); BAGHAWI-FULL(كعب); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب,كعب); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas)]
- 53:14/q05/p6 In the middle of Paradise. — Ibn Masʿūd's description places it at Paradise's center and speaks of silk and brocade there. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 53:14/q05/p7 At the far end of Paradise. — Nasafī allows al-muntahā to name Paradise's far end. Kashshāf, cited by Bursevī and Ālūsī, and Ibn Masʿūd as quoted by Elmalılı place it at Paradise's extremity. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL(الجمهور); BURSEVI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); ELMALILI(İbn Mes‘ûd)]
- 53:14/q05/p8 At the created world's boundary, where the realm of command begins. — Elmalılı locates the symbolic limit of formed bodies and dimensions here. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 53:14/q06 What does al-muntahā mean and what form can it have?  [ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the noun denotes a place, an act of ending, or, in a disputed reading, God.
- 53:14/q06/p1 The endpoint or place where things stop. — Ṭabarī, Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Ṭabrisī and Sāmarrāʾī gloss it as arrival's terminal point, beyond which one does not pass; Nasafī also calls it the far end of Paradise. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL(الجمهور); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:14/q06/p2 A place noun, possibly also a mīmī verbal noun meaning ending. — Zamakhsharī as quoted by Bursevī gives both forms; Ālūsī favors place noun but allows verbal noun. Elmalılı likewise gives both forms and glosses final limit or ultimate boundary. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 53:14/q06/p3 God, to whom is the ultimate end. — Rāzī makes divine possession a possible genitive reading by 53:42; Abū Suʿūd, quoted by Elmalılı, suggests the same. Ālūsī rejects it as far-fetched. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebussuud); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 53:14/q07 How does the genitive 'Sidrat al-Muntahā' work?  [سِدْرَةِ ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Which term qualifies or possesses the other, and whether 53:42 licenses a divine possessive.
- 53:14/q07/p1 The tree is related to the place or boundary where it stands. — Rāzī describes annexing a tree to its place, beyond which no angel or spirit passes. Ālūsī allows joining a thing to its location; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the endpoint defines the tree. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 53:14/q07/p2 The endpoint is related to the tree in which or at which it occurs. — Rāzī says a locus can be annexed to what it contains; the tree is at knowledge's endpoint. Ālūsī permits this reverse relation. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 53:14/q07/p3 It is an explanatory genitive. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this as an alternative to definition by the endpoint. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:14/q07/p4 It means God's tree through an understood divine possessive. — Rāzī connects this to 53:42 and honor by association; Abū Suʿūd offers the same ownership analogy, while Ālūsī calls it far-fetched. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Ebussuud); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 53:14/q08 Why is it called the Tree of the Limit?  [سِدْرَةِ ٱلْمُنتَهَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Which kind of thing reaches its limit there; Ṭabarī and Naḥḥās say the reports do not decide exclusively.
- 53:14/q08/p1 Created knowledge ends there; only God knows beyond. — Muqātil, Kaʿb, Ibn ʿAbbās and many exegetes state this, variously specifying all scholars, angels, prophets or all creatures. Wāḥidī reports an exception: the Messenger passed when the rifraf descended. Asad draws the epistemic lesson even for Paradise. Bursevī adds that ordinary deeds reach it through angels while elect deeds may go beyond. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(كعب الأحبار); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل,عطاء عن ابن عباس,عكرمة عن ابن عباس وكعب); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(كعب); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الكلبي ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وكعب,كعب); BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); ELMALILI(İbn Abbas); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 53:14/q08/p2 Ascending things and descending commands end and are received there. — Ibn Masʿūd says what ascends from earth and descends from above terminates there; Ḍaḥḥāk emphasizes God's command. Versions mention souls, angels, rulings or effects. Ṭabarī and Naḥḥās say no decisive report singles out one explanation. [25 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك,عبد الله); NAHHAS(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله,عكرمة عن ابن عباس وكعب); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن مسعود والضحاك,غير مسمى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الله); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب,ابن مسعود,الضحاك); BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Dahhâk); TABATABAI]
- 53:14/q08/p3 Prophetic followers or believing souls reach their endpoint there. — Rabīʿ and an uncertain Abu Hurayra report say those following Muhammad's Sunnah reach it; Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies them as true believers in every generation. Another Rabīʿ report speaks of believers' souls, while Qushayrī and Abū Ḥayyān also record souls of all creatures. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة أو غيره,أبو هريرة، أو غيره,الربيع); NAHHAS(الربيع بن أنس); MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 53:14/q08/p4 Martyrs' souls reach it. — Qushayrī, Ṭabrisī, Nasafī, Qāshānī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī report this narrower soul account. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(غير مسمى); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); QASHANI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل)]
- 53:14/q08/p5 Angels, prophets or all creatures stop at its boundary. — Kaʿb speaks of angels and prophets; Wāḥidī and Ṭabrisī specify angels, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī's Abū Jaʿfar report has Gabriel stop while Muhammad continues. Ibn ʿĀshūr says no creature can endure beyond it; Nasafī reports none pass. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(كعب); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام); STUDYQURAN(most commentators)]
- 53:14/q08/p6 Deeds are collected or presented there. — Māwardī gives Ḍaḥḥāk's account; Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī include deeds among what ends there. Bursevī distinguishes ordinary works carried by angels from the elect's sincere works going further. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 53:14/q08/p7 It marks the furthest end of Paradise. — Nasafī and the Kashshāf tradition give this sense; Qāshānī calls it the Garden's furthest station. [5 notes: NASAFI-FULL(الجمهور); QASHANI(قيل); BURSEVI(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); ELMALILI(İbn Mes‘ûd)]
- 53:14/q08/p8 It is the passage from this world to the Hereafter. — Mujāhid calls the Sidra the first day of the Hereafter and last day of this world. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 53:14/q08/p9 Several limits may apply together; no decisive report selects only one. — Ṭabarī explicitly allows knowledge, commands, followers or all of them; Naḥḥās says all explanations are plausible because no decisive report settles the matter. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS]

## 53:14/q09 What did the tree look like?  [سِدْرَةِ · reports]
turns on: Descriptions vary in the fruit image and in measurements of shade, leaves and branches.
- 53:14/q09/p1 Its fruit resembled large Hajar jars or pitchers, and its leaves elephant ears. — Anas's Prophetic reports repeatedly give both comparisons; Ibn al-Jawzī explicitly calls one sound. Ṭabarī also transmits later accounts of its extraordinary beauty after divine covering. [20 notes: MUJAHID(أنس بن مالك); ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس عن النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); RAZI-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس,أنس بن مالك); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Resûlullah); TABATABAI(أنس)]
- 53:14/q09/p2 Its fruit resembled locusts, with leaves like elephant ears. — Durr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmit this variant through Anas, rather than the jar comparison; both say divine covering changed its appearance. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أنس); TABATABAI(أنس)]
- 53:14/q09/p3 Its branches bore pearls, rubies, emeralds, or ornaments. — Muqātil describes gem branches; Mujāhid gives pearls, rubies and emeralds; Baghawī transmits Muqātil's ornaments and multicolored garments and fruit. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 53:14/q09/p4 It bore silk and brocade. — Ibn Masʿūd places abundant or extra silk and brocade on it; Elmalılı says they hung at Paradise's extremities. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); ELMALILI(İbn Mes‘ûd)]
- 53:14/q09/p5 Its leaves, shade or circumference were immeasurably vast. — Muqātil says a rider circling its trunk would die before return and one leaf shades a nation; Prophetic and other reports say one leaf covers the ummah, a branch shelters a community or one hundred thousand riders, or its shade takes seventy or one hundred years to cross. The counts and formulations vary. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة، أو غيره,الربيع بن أنس,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); ALUSI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Resûlullah); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 53:14/q09/p6 It would illuminate earth and yield multicolored robes and fruit. — Muqātil says a single leaf placed on earth would light it and produce colored robes and fruit; Baghawī transmits the variant description. [2 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 53:14/q09/p7 Golden moths were upon it. — Asmāʾ's Prophetic report includes golden moths, which al-Tirmidhī grades ḥasan; Ibn Masʿūd also says they covered the tree. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر,ابن مسعود)]

## 53:14/q10 Which rivers flow from the tree?  [سِدْرَةِ · reports]
turns on: The reports variously name earthly rivers, Paradise springs, or the four liquids of 47:15.
- 53:14/q10/p1 Four rivers: two hidden in Paradise and two visible, the Nile and Euphrates. — Anas's Prophetic reports say four flow from the trunk and Gabriel explains the visible and hidden pair; Muqātil's separate account names the four Paradise streams by their liquids. [6 notes: MUJAHID(أنس بن مالك); ABDURRAZZAQ(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); MAWARDI-FULL(النبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس عن النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس بن مالك)]
- 53:14/q10/p2 The hidden rivers are Salsabil and Kawthar, and Kawthar branches into four Paradise rivers. — Muqātil names the two springs from the trunk and links the four of water, milk, honey and wine to 47:15; Wāḥidī reports his identification of the hidden pair. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 53:14/q10/p3 Rivers of water, milk, wine and honey flow from it. — The uncertain Abu Hurayra report lists four liquids; Wāḥidī points to 47:15. Elmalılı also relates its rivers to those promised to the pious. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو هريرة، أو غيره); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو هريرة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 53:14/q11 What covered or transformed the Sidra?  [سِدْرَةِ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on what the covering consisted of and whether its changed appearance could be described.
- 53:14/q11/p1 God's command covered it and made its beauty indescribable or gemlike. — Anas's reports say it changed to ruby, emerald or unknown colors, beyond the Prophet's description. Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the Qur'an leaves the covering unspecified. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي); QURTUBI-FULL(أنس); DURR-FULL(أنس); TABATABAI(أنس)]
- 53:14/q11/p2 Golden moths covered it. — Ibn Masʿūd reports this in descriptions linked to the ascent; Asmāʾ's report says it contained golden moths. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر,ابن مسعود)]
- 53:14/q11/p3 Angels like ravens, divine light and unidentifiable colors covered it. — Ibn Kathīr summarizes earlier Night Journey reports and transmits an uncertain Abu Hurayra attribution in which light and ravenlike angels covered it before God spoke. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة أو غيره (شك أبو جعفر الرازي)); MUJAHID(أنس بن مالك)]

## 53:14/q12 Was the Sidra identified with another named tree?  [سِدْرَةِ · links]
turns on: Whether 13:29 names this same tree or a different designation.
- 53:14/q12/p1 It is Ṭūbā, the blessed tree in 13:29. — Muqātil identifies them; Baghawī reports his colorful description and the link; Ṭabrisī calls it an alternative. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); TABRISI(مقاتل)]
- 53:14/q12/p2 It is called the tree of prophecy. — Ṭabrisī records this other title alongside the Ṭūbā identification. [1 notes: TABRISI(مقاتل)]

## 53:14/q13 What vision occurred at the Sidra?  [* · referent]
turns on: The pronoun and vision reports are read as Gabriel, God by heart or eye, or God's signs.
- 53:14/q13/p1 Muhammad saw Gabriel in his true created form for the second time. — Zajjāj and Ibn Kathīr take the seen figure as Gabriel; Ibn Kathīr places this on the Night Journey and cites Ibn Masʿūd's six hundred wings and green, pearl-adorned appearance, grading the chains good. A further report makes one sighting at the horizon and the second on ascent; Ibn Kathīr calls it strange. ʿĀʾisha denies the God vision. [11 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عائشة); ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود,ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد)); TABATABAI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 53:14/q13/p2 Muhammad saw his Lord, with the heart. — Kaʿb contrasts Moses' speech with Muhammad's heart-vision; Mujāhid says he saw the tree and his Lord with his heart. Ibn Kathīr and Durr transmit Mujāhid. Study Quran reports heart-only as a God-vision view. [4 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); STUDYQURAN(many others)]
- 53:14/q13/p3 Muhammad saw his Lord twice. — Ibn ʿAbbās attributes this to Banū Hāshim; al-Ḥasan swore by it. Maʿmar challenges preferring ʿĀʾisha over Ibn ʿAbbās. Study Quran's proponents put special Self-Disclosure above the Throne and at the Sidra, allowing eye-and-heart vision. [5 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,الحسن,معمر); STUDYQURAN(many others,those who maintain the vision of God)]
- 53:14/q13/p4 Muhammad saw divine signs or light without a direct bodily sight of God. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī interprets Abū Jaʿfar's report as vision through God's signs; Gabriel stops, Muhammad proceeds, and divine light appears behind a veil of majesty. The Prophet's answer to Companions mentions nearness of two bow lengths. [4 notes: TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم)]
- 53:14/q13/p5 He saw Gabriel, not God. — ʿĀʾisha explicitly rejects the Lord-vision and says he saw Gabriel in his own form; she also cites 31:34 against knowledge of the five unseen matters. Maʿmar objects to ranking her above Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(عائشة)+; ABDURRAZZAQ(ابن عباس,الحسن,معمر)-]

## 53:14/q14 Why does the Sidra setting matter for identifying the seen figure?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Gabriel's stopping point and the location of the descent fit the referent.
- 53:14/q14/p1 The upper station excludes a human or jinn impersonation. — Rāzī says the earlier earthly sight allowed a remote possibility of jinn despite Muhammad's certain knowledge that it was an angel; above the sixth heaven no human or jinn could be present. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:14/q14/p2 Gabriel cannot be the figure descending from beyond the Sidra. — Elmalılı argues Gabriel does not pass that boundary; the described descent from above therefore does not fit him. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 53:14/q14/p3 Gabriel could be seen there because he is a creature located in space. — Bursevī allows the circumstantial phrase describing Gabriel, while Rāzī says a Gabriel reading admits either grammatical account. [2 notes: BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL]

## 53:14/q15 What occurred at the Sidra during the ascent?  [* · reports]
turns on: The ascent accounts emphasize arrival, direct communication, gifts, or the seen figure.
- 53:14/q15/p1 The Prophet arrived there in the Night Journey and reached exceptional nearness. — Anas reports arrival by Buraq or an angel; the Prophet saw the immense light, leaves, fruit and rivers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports his nearness to the Lord as two bow lengths or closer; Abū Jaʿfar says Gabriel stopped and told him to proceed where no creature had gone. [8 notes: MUJAHID(أنس بن مالك); TAB-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر عليه السلام,النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); ASAD-NOTES]
- 53:14/q15/p2 God spoke to him and he received revelation directly. — An uncertain Abu Hurayra report says light and angels covered the tree, then God told Muhammad to ask; Ibn ʿĀshūr says direct revelation without an angel honored him. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة أو غيره (شك أبو جعفر الرازي)); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:14/q15/p3 He received five prayers, al-Baqara's closing verses and forgiveness for grave sins without shirk. — Ibn Masʿūd's report is transmitted by Ibn Kathīr, Durr and Suyūṭī; Ibn Kathīr says Muslim alone transmits it. Suyūṭī treats it as sound support for the exceptional revelation of 2:285–286. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); ITQAN]
- 53:14/q15/p4 Prayer was set at fifty then reduced to five with the reward of fifty. — Biqāʿī gives the lighter obligation, mentions congregational prayer's twenty-sevenfold reward, and treats prayer as the Night Journey's culmination in monotheism; Ibn ʿĀshūr records the fifty-prayer prescription and the scratching pens. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 53:14/q15/p5 The Prophet saw Gabriel's immense created form there. — Ibn Masʿūd describes six hundred wings, with companions saying one spanned east to west, and greenery adorned with pearls; Ibn Kathīr calls both chains good. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود,ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد))]

## 53:14/q16 Did the ascent occur bodily, spiritually, awake, or in a dream?  [* · reports]
turns on: How the ascent is understood and whether modern astronomical recasting fits its reports.
- 53:14/q16/p1 Reports disagree between dream and waking ascent, and between body with soul and soul alone. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī refers readers to his discussion at the opening of Sūrat al-Isrāʾ (17:1), where these alternatives were reviewed. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 53:14/q16/p2 The imagery can describe divine acts and attributes figuratively without specifying how. — Kur'an Yolu says if this is the Miʿrāj, appearance, horizon, Sidra, descent and nearness are figurative descriptions understood without measuring their manner. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:14/q16/p3 An astronomical journey to planets or galaxies is untenable. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says that recasting the ascent as travel to star systems contradicts detailed reports and fails to fit the verses. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 53:14/q17 What conduct or spiritual state is reported at the Sidra?  [* · inward]
- 53:14/q17/p1 Muhammad did not prostrate there because of the station's intimacy; he will prostrate for intercession at Resurrection. — Wāsiṭī contrasts the reverent nearness there with future supplication where need demands it. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 53:14/q17/p2 He remained wholly attentive to his Lord, without self-regard or wandering sight. — Wāsiṭī says his unveiling reaches that boundary and his sight neither swerved nor overstepped (53:17); Sahl says he did not turn to witness himself; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ describes balanced faculties and freedom from doubt. [3 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي,سهل)]

## 53:14/q18 What legal warning is connected with a lote tree?  [* · law]
- 53:14/q18/p1 Unjustly cutting a tree that shelters travelers and animals incurs a threat of Fire. — Qurṭubī cites Abū Dāwūd's transmission from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ḥubshī of the Prophet's warning about cutting a lote tree; Abū Dāwūd restricts it to unjust cutting of a sheltering tree in open land. No grading is given. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو داود,عبد الله بن حبشي)]

## 53:14/q19 When did the ascent associated with this scene occur?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The chronological reports differ over how long before the Hijra the ascent took place.
- 53:14/q19/p1 Late in the Meccan period, shortly before the Hijra. — Biqāʿī places the second descent in the thirteenth prophetic year; Kirmānī says the Night Journey began at Umm Hānī's house in Rabīʿ I before the Hijra. Kur'an Yolu says 13–18 are generally tied to that late Meccan event; Study Quran says most date it to about 619. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL(حمزة الكرماني); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 53:14/q19/p2 About 614, seven years before the Hijra. — Study Quran reports this minority date. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(a minority)]

## 53:14/q20 How do the ascent's encounters foreshadow Muhammad's later mission?  [* · links]
- 53:14/q20/p1 Idris foreshadows writing and royal correspondence. — Biqāʿī links Idris in the fourth heaven as first recipient of the pen to Muhammad's letters to kings. He cites Abū Sufyān before Heraclius and the varying responses of the Negus, Oman, Heraclius, Muqawqis and resisters. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو سفيان)]
- 53:14/q20/p2 Aaron foreshadows reconciliation and love among former opponents. — Biqāʿī links Aaron in the fifth heaven with Muhammad's eventual acceptance among Quraysh and the Arabs; the Prophet's pardon at Mecca echoes Joseph's words to his brothers (12:92). [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:14/q20/p3 Moses foreshadows the Syria campaign and return to Mecca. — Biqāʿī connects Moses in the sixth heaven with Tabūk, the ruler of Dūma's tribute and the conquest of Mecca. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:14/q20/p4 Abraham foreshadows the Prophet's final pilgrimage. — Biqāʿī places Abraham beside al-Bayt al-Maʿmūr in the seventh heaven; its angelic visitors mirror the Kaʿba and Abraham's call to pilgrimage, culminating in Muhammad's Hajj with about seventy thousand Muslims. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:14/q20/p5 Prayer seals the ascent's monotheistic purpose. — Biqāʿī sees prayer as the barrier between Islam and polytheism and the source of religious blessings; the two endpoint prayers have special merit because both angelic shifts witness and record them. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 53:14/q21 How does this scene connect with the passage's argument and other scriptural scenes?  [* · links]
- 53:14/q21/p1 It confirms trustworthy revelation against accusations of invention or illusion. — Iṣlāḥī says 53:1–18 refutes soothsaying and fortune-telling by naming God's dependable angel and treats the verses as true, not illusory. Study Quran connects the opening to 52:33's charge of invention. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees a rise from disputed earthly sight to greater upper-realm sight. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:14/q21/p2 The ascent mirrors and surpasses Moses' encounter with God. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts Moses' sacred valley and Sinai with Muhammad's higher Sidra, which removes the disbelievers' objection to his revelation. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:14/q21/p3 The star's descent and the Prophet's ascent mirror worship's nearness. — Sāmarrāʾī says a falling star can evoke prostration toward earth, corresponding to Muhammad's ascent toward the nearest journey to God; he also contrasts ʿalā here with fī in the previous verse (53:12; 42:18). [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:14/q21/p4 The scene warns against materializing divinity and straining reason beyond its limit. — Kur'an Yolu reads its vivid mystery as an appeal to faith beyond the senses and as a rejection of pagan intermediary deities; the Sidra intensifies awe at created knowledge's limit. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:14/q21/p5 The passage's opening is rhetorically powerful and disputed. — Study Quran observes the opening's rhetorical force and the interpretive divide over whether Gabriel or God was seen. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(many commentators,many others)]

## 53:14/q22 Which revelations were said to descend outside the usual earthly setting?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports and Suyūṭī's assessment differ on which unusual revelation settings are supported.
- 53:14/q22/p1 The last two verses of al-Baqara were received at the Sidra. — Suyūṭī cites Muslim's Ibn Masʿūd report, along with five prayers and forgiveness, as support for 2:285–286; Hudhalī instead places the verses at two bow lengths. [4 notes: ITQAN(الهذلي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 53:14/q22/p2 Six verses, including three in al-Ṣāffāt, one in al-Zukhruf and al-Baqara's last two, descended neither in heaven nor on earth. — Suyūṭī reports Hibat Allāh's unusual-location claim (37:164, 43:45, 2:285–286), but finds no support except for al-Baqara's ending. [1 notes: ITQAN(هبة الله المفسر); ITQAN-]
- 53:14/q22/p3 Revelation can be celestial, terrestrial, between heaven and earth, or beneath the earth. — Ibn al-ʿArabī gives this classification and interprets the unusual verses as between heaven and earth; he cites a sound Ibn Masʿūd report for Sūrat al-Mursalāt in a cave (77:1). [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن العربي)]

## 53:14/q23 How was the knowledge-limit explanation reported?  [* · reports]
- 53:14/q23/p1 Ibn ʿAbbās asked Kaʿb, who said scholars' knowledge ends there and only God knows beyond. — Naḥḥās reports Ibn ʿAbbās's question to Kaʿb and Kaʿb's answer. [1 notes: NAHHAS(ابن عباس)]

## 53:14/q24 What report evidence explicitly mentions the celestial Sidra?  [* · reports]
- 53:14/q24/p1 Sound Miʿrāj reports from several Companions explicitly name it. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the group of Companion reports when describing the station above the seventh heaven. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(جمع من الصحابة)]
