# 69:25: 20 questions

## 69:25/q01 Whom does the left-hand recipient represent or concern?  [وَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِىَ كِتَٰبَهُۥ بِشِمَالِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: Whether the wording is general, tied to one named person, or applied to a later group; the reports give different names, while Jishumi explicitly prefers general scope.
- 69:25/q01/p1 The passage applies generally to the wretched, disobedient recipients of a corrupt record. — Jishumi includes Abu Salama within the general wording rather than restricting it to him. Tustari specifies evil deeds and disbelief; Tabatabai calls them wretched criminals. Asad and Kuranyolu describe an unrighteous earthly life; Study Quran says their deeds lead to Hell. [7 notes: TUSTARI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL; JISHUMI+]
- 69:25/q01/p2 It was revealed about al-Aswad al-Makhzumi, killed by Hamza at Badr. — Muqatil names him al-Aswad al-Makhzumi and locates the killing at the watering place. Wahidi reports Ata and Muqatil naming al-Aswad ibn al-Asad and calling him the brother of the right-hand recipient at 69:19; Ibn al-Jawzi reports Muqatil naming al-Aswad ibn Abd al-Aswad and calling him Abu Salama's brother. The forms of the name vary. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]
- 69:25/q01/p3 It concerns Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad. — Jishumi transmits this from Ibn Abbas, then says the wording is general and Abu Salama is included within it. [2 notes: JISHUMI(ابن عباس)]
- 69:25/q01/p4 It was revealed about Abu Jahl. — Ibn al-Jawzi records this as another account. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 69:25/q01/p5 It can be applied to Ali's enemies as an instance of the verse. — Tabatabai says reports applying the right-hand record to Ali or Ali and his Shi'a, and the left-hand record to his enemies, are applications (jary), not exegesis. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 69:25/q02 Who gives the recipient his book?  [أُوتِىَ كِتَٰبَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: The notes identify the giver either by the angel's recording role or by a hostile role in the judgment scene.
- 69:25/q02/p1 The angel who recorded his worldly deeds hands it to him. — Muqatil states this directly; Wahidi attributes the same explanation to Muqatil. [2 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 69:25/q02/p2 A hated opponent reviews the disbeliever's account and hands it over against him. — Elmalili contrasts this with the believer's account, which a friend reviews and hands over favorably. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 69:25/q03 Why is the giving of the book expressed passively?  [أُوتِىَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes explain the passive through the recipient's lack of control or through God's governance of judgment; Elmalili also preserves human responsibility.
- 69:25/q03/p1 The passive underscores his humiliation and inability to refuse the unwanted result. — Biqai connects the wording to the recipient's humiliation when the outcome is forced upon him. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 69:25/q03/p2 It points to God's enabling, decree, judgment, and execution of the outcome. — Elmalili contrasts being given a book with self-writing it, while also holding that a person's voluntary choices effectively dictate the record to the angels; he cites 17:13. [2 notes: ELMALILI]

## 69:25/q04 What is in his book, and how is its record made?  [كِتَٰبَهُۥ · interpretation]
- 69:25/q04/p1 It records his evil, voluntarily chosen deeds faithfully through the angels. — Tustari adds disbelief. Bursevi says the book is his because it gathers all his evil deeds. Elmalili says heedlessness and failure to audit oneself leave one dictating a harmful record (17:14); distorted right and wrong fill it with liabilities, while angels record facts faithfully. Choosing deeds effectively dictates it to the angels like a scribe (17:13). Wahidi notes the shameful deeds he sees. [6 notes: TUSTARI; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]

## 69:25/q05 When do sinful believers receive or read their records?  [أُوتِىَ كِتَٰبَهُۥ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether receipt or reading precedes punishment, follows release from Hell, or awaits divine permission; the invitation to read is cited in favor of delayed receipt.
- 69:25/q05/p1 They receive their books with everyone else, and this comforts them during punishment. — Ibn Atiyya reports this as one view; Abu Hayyan says a monotheist destined for Hell may receive the record in the right hand on the display day and find it consoling during punishment. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 69:25/q05/p2 They receive their books only after leaving Hell; faith comforts them during punishment. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan report this view. Abu Hayyan reports the argument that it fits better because someone being led to Hell would not joyfully invite others to read his book. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(آخرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 69:25/q05/p3 They do not read their books until God gives permission, then invite others to read them. — Ibn Atiyya attributes this account to al-Hasan. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن)]

## 69:25/q06 How is the left-hand book delivered behind the recipient's back?  [بِشِمَالِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: The accounts describe twisting, pulling, or tying the hand; the behind-the-back detail is used to relate this verse to 84:10–12.
- 69:25/q06/p1 His left hand is twisted behind his back to receive the book. — Thalabi attributes this to Ibn al-Thabit; Baghawi attributes a version specifying movement from the chest to Ibn al-Sa'ib; Bursevi adds that the delivery exposes his ugly deeds. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن الثابت); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن السائب); BURSEVI]
- 69:25/q06/p2 His left hand is pulled from his chest behind his back before the book is handed over. — Thalabi and Baghawi each record this as another account. [2 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 69:25/q06/p3 His left hand is tied behind his back. — Study Quran uses this to reconcile receiving the book in the left hand here with receiving it from behind the back at 84:10–12. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 69:25/q07 What does receiving the book in the left hand signify?  [بِشِمَالِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources read the left hand as public dishonor and a bad record, or as an inward symbol of the animal soul.
- 69:25/q07/p1 It marks rejection, humiliation, and an unrighteous earthly record and fate. — Mawardi says Arabs use the right for acceptance and honor, the left for rejection and humiliation. Elmalili gives right-hand connotations of strength, honesty, propriety, purity, good fortune, good and gain, while left-hand conduct reverses them; good-deed angels stand on the right, bad-deed angels on the left, with angel/right and Satan/left associations. Bursevi stresses humiliation; Asad contrasts 69:19; Kuranyolu specifies disobedience, dishonesty and vice; Study Quran says deeds lead to Hell. Elmalili also contrasts a friend's favorable review with a hated opponent's adverse review. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ELMALILI; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; TUSTARI]
- 69:25/q07/p2 In an inward reading, it signifies the weaker, animal-soul side. — Qashani interprets the left hand as the animal-soul side. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 69:25/q08 What conduct leads a person to the adverse, left-hand record?  [بِشِمَالِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 69:25/q08/p1 Disbelief, disobedience, neglected self-accounting, distorted values, and deeds done for people's approval produce the harmful record. — Tustari names evil deeds and disbelief. Elmalili says the person who never audits himself heedlessly dictates the record (17:14); distorting right and wrong fills it with liabilities; ostentatious good works done for people lose their worldly rewards like a bankrupt's assets, leaving pain and deprivation. Such people have written their books backward and left-handed. Kuranyolu specifies dishonest, unvirtuous living; Asad calls it an unrighteous earthly life. [7 notes: TUSTARI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 69:25/q09 When does the recipient speak this lament?  [فَيَقُولُ · interpretation]
- 69:25/q09/p1 He speaks at the gathering after seeing his record and the certainty of his fate. — Ibn Kathir places receiving the book at the gathering place. Biqai says the veil has been lifted and he sees his committed evil deeds and certain fate. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 69:25/q10 What explains any apparent hesitation in his speech?  [فَيَقُولُ · interpretation]
- 69:25/q10/p1 It is stubborn, false argument and resistance like his behavior in worldly life. — Biqai characterizes the apparent hesitation this way. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 69:25/q11 Why does he wish he had not received his book?  [يَٰلَيْتَنِى لَمْ أُوتَ كِتَٰبِيَهْ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish anguish at disclosure and reckoning, a desire to escape punishment itself, a wish for death or nonresurrection, and a possible hopeful utterance.
- 69:25/q11/p1 The record exposes ugly deeds, his reckoning and painful fate, causing shame, grief and regret. — Muqatil and Qurtubi add a wish not to know the reckoning; Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Nasafi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Alusi and others emphasize shameful deeds. Ibn Ashur says the book reveals punishment and the grief of knowing it. Biqai says he impossibly wishes for ignorance of deeds and recompense as in worldly life. Tabatabai notes painful punishment and a link to al-Isra; Kuranyolu says he does not want to see what is written. Mawardi also offers exposure after concealment. Qashani adds regret for life, wealth, power and status that harmed rather than helped him. [21 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 69:25/q11/p2 He wishes to avoid the punishment itself altogether. — Study Quran reports this as one possible sense of wishing not to receive the book. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 69:25/q11/p3 He wishes he had never been resurrected, or that death had ended everything. — Tustari specifies no resurrection. Muqatil and Qurtubi add that death had ended life; Qurtubi says he longs for death. Qashani says he wishes for death; Kuranyolu says he wishes death had ended everything. [5 notes: TUSTARI; MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL; QASHANI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 69:25/q11/p4 He may voice the wish in hope. — Mawardi gives this as one possibility, alongside the account that he was concealed and has now been exposed. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 69:25/q12 How does the pain of seeing the record compare with bodily punishment?  [يَٰلَيْتَنِى لَمْ أُوتَ كِتَٰبِيَهْ · interpretation]
- 69:25/q12/p1 The shame and spiritual torment can be worse than fire or bodily pain. — Razi says humiliation from the record is worse than fire, so the sinner would prefer to be burned without seeing it. Bursevi calls his grief, regret and fear more painful than bodily pain. Study Quran reports that shame may be worse than the Fire and make entry without judgment preferable. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 69:25/q13 What form of ليتني is also used by Arabs?  [يَٰلَيْتَنِى · grammar]
- 69:25/q13/p1 Some Arabs drop its nūn, as they do after إن. — Nahhas cites Arab usage of this omission. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 69:25/q14 What does the passive لَمْ أُوتَ express?  [لَمْ أُوتَ · grammar]
- 69:25/q14/p1 It expresses his wish that he had not been given the record. — Bursevi explicitly explains the passive form in this negative clause as not being given the book. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 69:25/q15 What is the final hāʾ in كِتَٰبِيَهْ, and may it remain when speech continues?  [كِتَٰبِيَهْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the pausal grammatical rule can count against an established connected recitation; Abu Hayyan appeals to mass transmission, while Nahhas appeals to grammar and pausing at the rasm.
- 69:25/q15/p1 It is hāʾ al-sakt, a still pausal letter that clarifies the preceding vowel. — Nahhas says it clarifies the vowel at pause. Elmalili says the underlying form ends in -iya and the final hāʾ after fatḥa is always still, also in hisābiyah, māliyah and sulṭāniyah. Burhan and Itqan say the speaker's lament is worded for a pause before continuation into 69:26; Burhan contrasts a complete stop ending nār ḥāmiyah and mentions 69:29. [5 notes: NAHHAS; ELMALILI; BURHAN(حمزة); ITQAN]
- 69:25/q15/p2 Retaining the hāʾ in connected recitation is valid because its transmitted reading is authoritative. — Abu Hayyan rejects al-Zuhrawi's grammatical objection because the reading is mass-transmitted and must be accepted. Burhan says retention in kitābiyah and ḥisābiyah is on pausal intent; most readers retain it in both connection and pause. [3 notes: BURHAN(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 69:25/q15/p3 Retaining the hāʾ in connected speech is grammatically faulty. — Nahhas reports grammarians known to him who judge it faulty and recommends following the written rasm with a pause. Abu Hayyan reports al-Zuhrawi making the same objection, then rejects it on the authority of mass transmission. [1 notes: NAHHAS+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]

## 69:25/q16 How is the final hāʾ of كِتَٰبِيَهْ and related forms read?  [كِتَٰبِيَهْ · readings]
turns on: The readers differ about connected pronunciation and which words take omission; all reports here retain the hāʾ at pause where specified, and Jishumi gives conflicting attributions for related forms.
- 69:25/q16/p1 Most readers retain the hāʾ in both connected speech and pause. — Abu Hayyan includes book, reckoning, wealth, power and mā hiyah at 101:10. Nashr contrasts the other readers with Yaqub; Jishumi likewise reports retention by others. Burhan explains connected retention as pausal intent; Qiraat-ER says canonical ten variants allow forms with and without full hāʾ al-sakt and a trailing-vowel difference. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); NASHR(يعقوب); JISHUMI(يعقوب); BURHAN(حمزة); QIRAAT-ER]
- 69:25/q16/p2 Yaqub omits the hāʾ in connection but retains it at pause. — Nashr specifies kitābiyah and ḥisābiyah. Jishumi reports Yaqub as the sole omitter for the discussed related forms in one note, but pairs him with Hamza for māliyah, sulṭāniyah and mā hiyah in another; all retain h at pause. Qiraat-ER notes canonical variation in full hāʾ and trailing vowel. [4 notes: NASHR(يعقوب); JISHUMI(حمزة ويعقوب,يعقوب); QIRAAT-ER]
- 69:25/q16/p3 Ibn Muhaysin deletes the hāʾ and silences the yāʾ. — Abu Hayyan reports this particular form. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 69:25/q16/p4 Ibn Abi Ishaq and al-A'mash omit the hāʾ only in connection. — Abu Hayyan distinguishes their practice from Ibn Muhaysin's deletion. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 69:25/q16/p5 Hamza omits the hāʾ in connection in selected related forms, retaining it at pause. — Jishumi names māliyah, sulṭāniyah and mā hiyah at 101:10, though another Jishumi note attributes omission there to Yaqub alone. Burhan also says Hamza deletes it in three other words, while retaining it in kitābiyah and ḥisābiyah; Abu Hayyan says his omission is limited to selected forms. [3 notes: JISHUMI(حمزة ويعقوب); BURHAN(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 69:25/q17 How does this left-hand passage relate to the right-hand passage?  [* · links]
- 69:25/q17/p1 It counterposes the accepted right-hand recipient to warn through the rejected person's fate. — Biqai says the accepted person comes first to encourage hope, then the rejected person warns against his deeds. Ibn Ashur says 69:25–29 counterposes 69:19 and lets the reader infer the left-hand fate by contrast. Asad contrasts righteous and unrighteous earthly lives. Wahidi calls al-Aswad the brother of the person associated with 69:19; Elmalili contrasts a friendly favorable review with a hostile adverse one. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء ومقاتل); ELMALILI]

## 69:25/q18 How does al-Haqqah relate to the preceding al-Waqi'ah?  [* · links]
- 69:25/q18/p1 Both affirm the Resurrection, depict its terrors, and describe outcomes for the right- and left-hand companions. — Islahi states both shared themes of the two surahs. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 69:25/q19 What did the mockers ask to have hastened in the related record discussion?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The differing interpretations identify the requested thing as records, afterlife homes, or punishment; the root and poetry evidence concerns the record sense.
- 69:25/q19/p1 They asked for their records to be hastened, like those given in right or left hands. — Bint al-Shati reports this exegetical link. She relates the relevant expression to a root meaning cutting, with a third consonant distinguishing what is cut; the word maqṭūṭ in al-A'sha's poem is agreed to mean award documents or letters of grants. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 69:25/q19/p2 They asked to see their afterlife homes sooner. — Bint al-Shati reports this as another exegetical interpretation. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 69:25/q19/p3 They asked that their punishment be hastened. — Bint al-Shati reports this as another exegetical interpretation. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 69:25/q20 What recitation-merit reports are cited alongside these notes?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes transmit three different reports, with different stated gradings.
- 69:25/q20/p1 Al-Baqara's recitation brings blessing, its abandonment regret, and the devoted Qur'an reader and parents receive reward. — Baghawi reports Burayda's narration from the Prophet: magicians cannot withstand al-Baqara; it and Al Imran shade their companion; the Qur'an raises its reader through Paradise. Baghawi grades the report gharib. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي)]
- 69:25/q20/p2 Listening to a verse earns multiplied good, and reciting one gives light on Judgment Day. — Baghawi reports this Prophetic saying without stating a grading. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي)]
- 69:25/q20/p3 Three verses recited in prayer surpass three large pregnant camels returned to their owner. — Baghawi reports this Prophetic saying and grades it sahih. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي)]
