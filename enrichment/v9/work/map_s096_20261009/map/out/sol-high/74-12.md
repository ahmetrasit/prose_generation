# 74:12: 10 questions

## 74:12/q01 Who is the man to whom God gave the wealth?  [لَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the named occasion exhausts the passage's scope.
- 74:12/q01/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra is the person addressed. — Al-Zajjāj, Jishumī, Biqāʿī, and Tustarī identify him; Jishumī calls him al-Makhzūmī and reports that his people called him al-Wāḥid. Kuranyolu calls him a wealthy Quraysh notable with many sons; Study Quran adds seven or ten sons, learning, and eloquence. Ibn ʿĀshūr says 68:10 may also concern him but leaves that identification uncertain. [7 notes: TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:12/q01/p2 The description extends to wealthy, high-status opponents generally, while still applying to al-Walīd. — Iṣlāḥī says the passage portrays a general mentality of affluent leaders in Mecca and Ṭāʾif. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:12/q02 How does لَهُۥ function grammatically?  [لَهُۥ · grammar]
- 74:12/q02/p1 لَهُۥ is the second object. — Al-Naḥḥās states this grammatical analysis. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 74:12/q03 What is meant by مَالًۭا مَّمْدُودًۭا, and what wealth did he possess?  [مَالًۭا مَّمْدُودًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether madd denotes quantity, spatial reach, renewal or duration, and whether transmitted sums identify the wealth or merely illustrate its scale.
- 74:12/q03/p1 It denotes abundant, expansive wealth without fixing a particular asset or sum. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers the scriptural breadth: abundance in number or spatial extent; he notes the divergent identifications. Thaʿlabī, Qushayrī, Ibn Kathīr, Asad, and others likewise say abundance. Kashshāf and Ālūsī compare expansion to a river enlarged by a tributary. Rāzī says its enumeration could extend at length. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports a derivation from madd, 'lengthen,' likening abundance to bodily expanse. Al-Biqāʿī says its abundance and availability throughout life came from God, not the man's own power. Ālūsī says transmitted figures, if genuine, illustrate greatness relative to their audience and do not set the intended amount. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن أبي صالح); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; TAB-FULL+]
- 74:12/q03/p2 His wealth was one thousand dinars. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq and al-Ṭabarī transmit Mujāhid; al-Ṭabarī also transmits Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. Other transmitters name both, and some add Ibn ʿAbbās. Al-Farrāʾ, quoted by Ibn al-Jawzī, says a thousand is the boundary at which counting recommences. Al-Zajjāj also reports the figure; Kashshāf gives a wording in mithqāls. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وسعيد بن جبير); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد، ابن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، مجاهد، ابن جبير); RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وابن جبير); RAZI-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 74:12/q03/p3 His wealth was a recurring income received month after month. — ʿAṭāʾ's route is sometimes attributed to ʿUmar and sometimes omits him (al-Ṭabarī); Ibn Jurayj transmits the report from ʿAṭāʾ in Thaʿlabī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes a productive estate and says 'extended' refers to time rather than amount. Wāḥidī's people of meanings say mamdūd suggests successive support, one addition after another. Qushayrī as quoted by Qurṭubī prefers recurring income from crops, livestock, and trade. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(عطاء); IBNABIHATIM(عمر بن الخطاب); THALABI(عمر); MAWARDI-FULL(عمر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني,عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن جريج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عمر بن الخطاب); RAZI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(عمر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); ALUSI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)+]
- 74:12/q03/p4 He owned a Ṭāʾif orchard that bore fruit through winter and summer. — Muqātil's explanation is repeatedly transmitted. Muqātil, Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, and Rāzī relate its uninterrupted benefit to the 'extended shade' of 56:30. Thaʿlabī's note names Muqātil as speaker but its claim calls him Mujāhid; later reports likewise vary in attribution. Ṭabrisī adds Mujāhid's report of yearly yield, ten sons and one hundred thousand dinars. Nasafī describes land at Ṭāʾif with unceasing produce; Elmalılı gives an all-season garden. [12 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قاتل); RAZI-FULL(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 74:12/q03/p5 It denotes wealth continually increasing through crops, livestock, produce, and trade. — Thaʿlabī and Wāḥidī describe replenishing assets; Wāḥidī prefers this explanation. Māwardī offers combined crops, commerce and livestock, each supporting the next, and separately self-renewing palms and trees. Kashshāf and Ālūsī liken increase to a river fed by a tributary. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives madd from adding a thing's like: earnings augment holdings. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bagha wī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Elmalılı, and Wāḥidī's Wajīz allow growth along with abundance. [19 notes: THALABI(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري)+; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 74:12/q03/p6 It describes diverse holdings spread between Mecca and Ṭāʾif. — Ibn ʿAbbās, through varying routes, lists camels, branded or fine horses, sheep and other livestock, gardens, trees, rivers, cash, caravans, servants, enslaved men and women, and concubines. Kashshāf, Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Bursawī and Elmalılı transmit shorter variants. Iṣlāḥī interprets the spread as diversified ventures in different places, comparing modern international accounts and investments. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:12/q03/p7 It means land or cultivated land spread out in space. — Al-Nuʿmān ibn Sālim is named by Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Qurṭubī and Durr; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī attribute a land explanation to al-Nuʿmān ibn Bashīr. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān explain extension as spatial spread. Ibn Kathīr calls it cultivable land yielding produce. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(النعمان بن سالم); THALABI(النعمان بن سالم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النعمان بن بشير); QURTUBI-FULL(النعمان بن سالم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النعمان بن بشير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(النعمان بن سالم); ALUSI-FULL(النعمان بن بشير)]

Kashshāf gives a million mithqāls, Rāzī includes a million among estimates, and Elmalılı says roughly a million coins. ## 74:12/q04 What inward meaning is given to the wealth and its recipient?  [لَهُۥ مَالًۭا مَّمْدُودًۭا · inward]
turns on: Whether the extension concerns inward appetite or a symbolic account of the rebellious self.
- 74:12/q04/p1 God extended al-Walīd's greed and long expectation. — Tustarī gives this inward explanation while naming al-Walīd. [1 notes: TUSTARI]
- 74:12/q04/p2 The solitary evil soul and its wealth represent bad traits, followers, prestige, sins, and dark natural powers. — The Najmī allusion reported by Bursawī makes al-Walīd this soul; Abū Bakr as the hidden, ʿUmar as spirit, ʿUthmān as secret, and ʿAlī as heart use spiritual light to erase darkness, defeat its acts and followers, and end its rule. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 74:12/q05 How is this gift of wealth placed in the surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: Which surrounding frame the note emphasizes: the sequence of gifts, the warning, or a possible parallel in another sūra.
- 74:12/q05/p1 The specific gift of wealth follows the general gift of creating him and begins a rehearsal of blessings before reproach. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the blessings prepare a warning of worldly harm and punishment in the afterlife. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:12/q05/p2 Verses 11–17 warn disbelievers that Judgment Day will be severe despite their expectation of more wealth and rank. — Iṣlāḥī says God gave wealth and status, after which the man became conceited and vain; wealthy leaders of Mecca and Ṭāʾif took riches as evidence that God favored them. He compares al-Muddaththir with the preceding al-Muzzammil: similar names and central themes. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:12/q05/p3 The blessings here contrast with the vices described in 68:10. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 68:10 may refer to al-Walīd, but treats this as possible because that context warns against his harm and treachery. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:12/q06 What does the divine grant of wealth teach about blessings and gratitude?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the lesson is framed as gratitude owed or the mistaken inference people draw from prosperity.
- 74:12/q06/p1 Abundant blessings make disobedience to their giver more serious and call for gratitude. — Jishumī says the abundance heightens culpability. Kuranyolu says gratitude follows from reason and justice; al-Walīd's ingratitude toward God and the Prophet and efforts to suppress Islam illustrate the fault. Failure to thank God for life and material and spiritual gifts is especially grave when it advances denial, polytheism, and injustice. Iṣlāḥī says conceit replaced gratitude. [5 notes: JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:12/q06/p2 Worldly wealth and status can prompt a mistaken claim to divine favor and an expectation of still more. — Iṣlāḥī describes affluent leaders of Mecca and Ṭāʾif, says Muhammad should leave their fate to God, and takes the passage to apply to al-Walīd and the broader wealthy elite. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:12/q07 Can God grant worldly blessings to unbelievers?  [* · theology]
- 74:12/q07/p1 Yes; worldly goods and conditions are blessings from God even when granted to unbelievers. — Jishumī explicitly disputes the Mujbira on this point and calls worldly conditions and goods divine blessings. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 74:12/q08 What did al-Walīd and other Meccan opponents say about Muhammad and the Qurʾān?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported accusation and its occasion is being described.
- 74:12/q08/p1 Al-Walīd urged Quraysh to call Muhammad a sorcerer or magician because his message divided families. — Ibn Hishām says Quraysh sought one story for the pilgrimage season; al-Walīd first rejected soothsayer, madman, poet and sorcerer as accurate labels before choosing 'sorcerer' for its persuasive appeal. Jishumī likewise reports his rejection of soothsayer, poet and madman and his final choice of 'magician.' [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(الوليد بن المغيرة); JISHUMI]
- 74:12/q08/p2 Meccan opponents also alleged human fabrication, ancient lore, or copied and dictated earlier writings. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the Qurʾān records these charges and argues against them, citing 68:10–15, 68:44–47, 25:4–10, 17:87–96, 6:7–9 and 6:25–26, and 34:43–45. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 74:12/q09 What was reported to happen to al-Walīd's wealth after revelation?  [* · reports]
- 74:12/q09/p1 His wealth and children steadily declined until his death. — Ṭabrisī gives an unnamed report; Abū Ḥayyān attributes it to Muqātil. [2 notes: TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل)]

## 74:12/q10 What unrelated noun-form analogies are recorded among these notes?  [* · grammar]
turns on: Different noun shapes, broken plurals, gender and declension govern the cited forms; these notes do not connect those paradigms to a word of this verse.
- 74:12/q10/p1 Ordinary dual nouns take alif and nūn in the nominative, and yāʾ and nūn in the accusative and genitive. — Sībawayh says ordinary non-defective, non-hamzated nouns open the final consonant in forming the dual. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 74:12/q10/p2 Triliteral nouns with the two feminine alifs retain their consonant and both alifs in the diminutive. — Sībawayh gives ḥumayrāʾ and ṭurayfāʾ as examples. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 74:12/q10/p3 Faʿlān nouns with feminine faʿlā counterparts retain nūn in diminutives. — Sībawayh says nūn follows an alif and substitutes for the feminine alif like the hamza of ḥamrāʾ; he extends this to five-letter faʿlān-like nouns without a mafāʿīl plural. Where no broken plural is known, use this analogy until one is known. Yūnus and Abū ʿAmr give sarḥān → surayḥīn, also for proper names. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(يونس وأبو عمرو)]
- 74:12/q10/p4 A faʿlān noun with a mafāʿīl broken plural follows the sirbāl pattern in its diminutive. — Sībawayh gives sarḥān, ḍabʿān, ḥawmān, sulṭān and farzān; speakers with farāzina as the plural of farzān still form furayziyyīn, like jaḥājiḥa and zanādiqa. Sībawayh attributes material in this and the next chapter to Yūnus. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH(من قال فرازنة,يونس)]
- 74:12/q10/p5 Ẓarbān forms ẓuraybān, whereas warshān forms warayshiyyīn. — Sībawayh grounds the first in ẓarābī dropping nūn, unlike sirbāl's retained root lām, and treats its plural as ẓarbāʾ; warshān's plural is warāshīn. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 74:12/q10/p6 Some proper names retain regular diminutive forms despite indeclinability. — Sībawayh gives ʿalqā → ʿulayqī, maʿzā → muʿayzī, and a woman named sirbāl → suraybāl. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 74:12/q10/p7 A declinable triliteral noun with two added letters and a long hamza follows a same-length form with replacement root yāʾ. — Sībawayh cites ʿalbay and ḥurayb; where the root yāʾ remains visible, as daraḥiya → durayḥiya, the diminutive retains it because the additions are not feminine markers. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 74:12/q10/p8 The diminutive of ghawghāʾ or qūbāʾ varies with masculine/declinable versus feminine/indeclinable treatment. — Sībawayh reports masculine ghawghāʾ → ghwayghī, feminine ghawghāʾ → ghwayghāʾ; masculine qūbāʾ → quwaybī and feminine qūbāʾ → quwaybāʾ, by analogy to ʿalbay or ḥumayrāʾ. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(من قال غوغاء,من قال قوباء,من قال هذه قوباء,من لم يصرف وأنث)]
