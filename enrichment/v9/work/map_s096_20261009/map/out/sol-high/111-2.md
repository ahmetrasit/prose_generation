# 111:2: 20 questions

## 111:2/q01 How is the first mā construed?  [مَآ أَغْنَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is a negative particle or a fronted interrogative with denial; both deny that wealth helped.
- 111:2/q01/p1 It is a negation: his wealth did not avail him. — Al-Ṭabarī's sense is a direct denial; Ibn ʿAṭiyya applies it after punishment was decreed. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī call this the apparent or usual reading. Bergsträßer prefers it from parallels 69:28 and 92:11; Elmalılı says it better explains the ruin of both hands. [24 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN+; ALUSI+; CORPUSCORANICUM(Bergsträßer)+; ELMALILI+; NASAFI+]
- 111:2/q01/p2 It is a rhetorical question denying any benefit: what did his wealth avail him? — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān place interrogative mā in the accusative as the object; al-Ālūsī also allows a cognate object, 'to what extent?' Al-Samīn says it is fronted as an interrogative. Al-Rāzī invokes Qārūn and Sulaymān, whose possessions could not repel death. Biqāʿī calls the question a rebuke. [27 notes: TAB; THALABI; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 111:2/q02 What does aghnā ʿanhu say his wealth failed to do?  [أَغْنَىٰ عَنْهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Which threatened outcome the clause is applied to: opposition to the Prophet, afterlife punishment, or his worldly death; some sources encompass more than one.
- 111:2/q02/p1 It did not benefit, suffice for, or protect him from ruin and divine punishment. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk glosses the verb as benefiting or protecting from harm; Biqāʿī adds sufficing and meeting a need. Al-Ṭabarī mentions God's displeasure. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Baghawī specify God's punishment; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says torment after death had been decreed. [49 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TUSTARI; TAB; THALABI; MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI+]
- 111:2/q02/p2 It did not aid his hostility toward the Messenger. — Al-Māwardī and al-Rāzī give this as one scope of the denial; al-Biqāʿī recalls his use of clan standing to discredit the Prophet. [6 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 111:2/q02/p3 It could not shield him from the Fire on Judgment Day. — Al-Rāzī points to the next verse, 111:3; Muqātil and Tustarī locate the failure in the afterlife. Corpus Coranicum says verse 3 clarifies the afterlife setting. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TUSTARI; MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 111:2/q02/p4 It also failed to avert a disgraced worldly end. — Kuran Yolu says wealth, children, status, and reputation did not save him from a bad death; al-Biqāʿī cites the disease and ignominious burial as the visible failure. [4 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 111:2/q03 What is the māl that failed him?  [مَالُهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether māl denotes his general property, livestock in particular, inherited assets, or trade capital in contrast to the following earnings.
- 111:2/q03/p1 His accumulated property or assets generally. — Al-Baghawī says he gathered property and owned herds. Al-Qurṭubī speaks of accumulated wealth. Al-Biqāʿī stresses its abundance and its usual capacity to rescue a person. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR]
- 111:2/q03/p2 His herds or grazing livestock. — Abū al-ʿĀliya says he owned herds. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that māl often means camels among Arabs, adducing ʿUmar and a line of Zuhayr; in Medina, Khaybar, and Bahrain it often denotes date palms. [8 notes: THALABI(أبو العالية); MAWARDI(أبو العالية); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 111:2/q03/p3 Inherited or longstanding property, contrasted with later acquisitions. — Al-Māwardī names inherited talīd and acquired ṭārif; al-Nasafī offers inherited versus self-earned and old versus new; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies inheritance from ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. [13 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 111:2/q03/p4 His capital, paired with profits made from it. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī distinguish principal from its returns; Elmalılı includes this among compatible readings. [5 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ELMALILI]

## 111:2/q04 How is mā kasaba construed grammatically?  [وَمَا كَسَبَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical function of the second mā: relative pronoun, verbal noun particle, interrogative, or direct negator.
- 111:2/q04/p1 Mā is a nominative relative pronoun, 'what he earned,' with an optional omitted object pronoun. — Al-Zajjāj calls it nominative; al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn ʿĀshūr mention the omitted resumptive. Abū Ḥayyān calls this apparent, and al-Baḥr prefers it for profits, advantages, status, and followers. Al-Ālūsī rejects ʿIṣām al-Dīn's parsing that would make wealth the subject of kasaba. [22 notes: ZAJJAJ(المفسرون); KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN+; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI(أبو حيان)+; IBNASHUR+]
- 111:2/q04/p2 Mā and kasaba form a nominative verbal noun: 'his earning' or 'what he earned by effort.' — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī allow the maṣdar reading. [14 notes: KASHSHAF; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 111:2/q04/p3 The second mā can itself be interrogative: what did he earn? Nothing. — Abū Ḥayyān makes this depend on interrogative first mā. Al-Samīn says the question amounts rhetorically to a negation; al-Ālūsī transmits it. Shawkānī is cited for asking both what wealth helped and what he earned. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 111:2/q04/p4 The second mā can be directly negative: he earned nothing of benefit. — Al-Samīn allows the sense but finds this direct parse unpersuasive. ʿIṣām al-Dīn gives a negative analysis; al-Ālūsī objects that it makes wealth the subject of 'earned.' Elmalılı allows negation. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI(عصام الدين); ALUSI-FULL(عصام الدين); ELMALILI; ALUSI-]

## 111:2/q05 What does 'what he earned' refer to?  [وَمَا كَسَبَ · referent]
turns on: How broad kasb is here: offspring, material acquisitions, deeds, or all gains; arguments cite Prophetic reports and anecdotes, grammar, Qurʾānic usage, and context.
- 111:2/q05/p1 His children or sons. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿĀʾisha and many commentators give this gloss; Muqātil names ʿUtba, ʿUtayba and Muʿattib. Reports from the Prophet say a child is among a father's earnings and one's best food is from one's earnings. Ibn ʿAbbās reportedly called the fighting sons 'evil earnings'; some versions instead attribute the remark to Abū Lahab. Islāhī calls this particularization far-fetched, and Corpus Coranicum cautions against anecdotal over-specification. - 111:2/q05/p2 His personal acquisitions and material gains, including property, trade profits or the yield of livestock. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya mentions real estate and stresses wealth gained through work rather than inheritance or gift. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān distinguish capital/profits, livestock/offspring/produce, inheritance/personal earnings, and old/new assets. Ibn ʿĀshūr lists money, weapons, estates, goods, and food. Corpus Coranicum notes a prima facie parallel with 7:48 but objects that kasaba is predominantly figurative, denoting deeds, including in pre-Islamic poetry and 74:38. [28 notes: NAHHAS(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI(أبو حيان)+; CORPUSCORANICUM-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some others)-]
- 111:2/q05/p3 His deeds, especially wicked acts and plotting against the Messenger. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk identifies evil work and plotting; Qatāda says deeds he wrongly expected to profit him, compared with works made scattered dust at 25:23. Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes deeds or their effects; Corpus Coranicum points to dominant Qurʾānic figurative usage and pre-Islamic poetry. Rubin specifies service to al-ʿUzzā and former protection of Muhammad, later renounced; Corpus Coranicum rejects that cult reading because the sura does not accuse idolatry and verse 2 connects wrongdoing to wealth. Islāhī prefers outwardly good welfare works for the poor and pilgrims as temple treasurer, rendered worthless by evil character and polytheism. [26 notes: MAWARDI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(الضحاك); KASHSHAF(الضحاك,قتادة); RAZI(الضحاك,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN(الضحاك,قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); ALUSI(الضحاك,قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة); ELMALILI(Dahhâk,Katâde); TABATABAI; CORPUSCORANICUM(Rubin); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 111:2/q05/p4 Every gain or advantage he counted as his, including children, standing, followers, deeds and social influence. — Al-Biqāʿī says kasb exceeds money: wealth brings status and effort brings gains, but only what God commands benefits; he used clan standing against Muhammad despite once calling him truthful. Elmalılı says the particular interpretations do not conflict. Kuran Yolu lists children, wealth, status and reputation. [15 notes: QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI+; CORPUSCORANICUM-]
- 111:2/q05/p5 His son ʿUtba in particular. — Al-Bayḍāwī identifies him by the report of a lion mauling him on the Syria road. Other accounts name the attacked son ʿUtayba; the notes preserve both names. [2 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 111:2/q05/p6 His illicit earnings specifically. — Islāhī reports this explanation and rejects it as linguistically unsuitable and redundant with māluhu. [0 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some others)-]

- 111:2/q06/p1 Some read wa-mā iktasaba, with the added tāʾ of iftiʿāl. — Al-Aʿmash transmits the form from Ibn Masʿūd; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also names Ubayy ibn Kaʿb. ## 111:2/q07 Why use the past tense aghnā for a future fate?  [أَغْنَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the perfect marks certainty about the future or expresses a resultative state with no clear contrast to the parallel imperfect.
- 111:2/q07/p1 The past makes the failure certain, even though its outcome lies ahead. — Al-Rāzī contrasts 92:11 and cites 69:28 and 16:1 as comparable past expressions; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it establishes certainty. Ibn al-Jawzī explains the sense as future benefit denied. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR]
- 111:2/q07/p2 The perfect has a resultative, present reference without a clear semantic contrast with the imperfect at 92:11. — Paret's analysis is followed by Corpus Coranicum; its author notes that 92:11 uses the imperfect in a like afterlife statement with no evident difference in meaning. [2 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; CORPUSCORANICUM(Paret)+]

## 111:2/q09 What occasion is reported for the sūra's revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported triggering incident: Abū Lahab's response to the public warning or his wife's conduct amid opposition.
- 111:2/q09/p1 After the Prophet warned Quraysh or his nearest kin, Abū Lahab cursed or insulted him and the sūra followed. — Ibn ʿAbbās places the warning on Ṣafā and reports the Prophet asking whether they would credit a cavalry warning; another version names Banū Hāshim and Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib. Sufyān names the uncle ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā. The command to warn kin is 26:214. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,سفيان); STUDYQURAN(many)]
- 111:2/q09/p2 The passage was revealed amid Quraysh's mockery because Umm Jamīl scattered thorns on the Prophet's path. — Ibn Isḥāq says the Prophet's uncle and clan had protected him while Quraysh opposed him. [1 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق)]

## 111:2/q10 What became of Abū Lahab's sons in the reports connected to his 'earnings'?  [وَمَا كَسَبَ · reports]
turns on: The reports vary over the attacked son's name and over who initiated Ruqayya's divorce.
- 111:2/q10/p1 A son called ʿUtba in some reports and ʿUtayba in others insulted the Prophet and was killed by a lion on the Syria route despite protective precautions. — Al-Māwardī and al-Bayḍāwī name ʿUtba; al-Ālūsī names ʿUtayba. Some reports tie the insult to 53:1 and 53:8, spitting toward the Prophet, a divorce from Umm Kulthūm, and the Prophet's prayer that a beast attack. Al-Biqāʿī says Abū Lahab arranged companions for protection; al-Ālūsī adds camels, a monk's warning, and Ḥassān's verse about the lion. [14 notes: MAWARDI; MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI(حسان); ALUSI-FULL(حسان)]
- 111:2/q10/p2 ʿUtba and Muʿattib later became Muslims, while ʿUtayba did not. — Al-Ālūsī says the first two converted at the conquest, received the Prophet's prayer, and attended Ḥunayn and al-Ṭāʾif; verses from Kitāb al-Albāʾ praise their Islam and warn against insulting a Muslim. [3 notes: ALUSI(صاحب كتاب الألباء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 111:2/q10/p3 His sons divorced the Prophet's daughters after the sūra. — One Qatāda report says Ruqayya, wife of ʿUtba, divorced at the Prophet's request and later married ʿUthmān. Another says Abū Lahab forced both ʿUtba and ʿUtayba to divorce; al-Ālūsī names Umm Kulthūm as ʿUtayba's wife and says their mother Hind urged the separation because the daughters converted. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 111:2/q11 What happened to Abū Lahab after Badr?  [* · reports]
turns on: The sources differ about the immediate cause of death and burial details, while the Badr altercation supplies a narrated sequence.
- 111:2/q11/p1 He contracted al-ʿadasa, died within days, and his feared, decomposing body was buried with difficulty. — Al-Biqāʿī, citing Abū Rāfiʿ, says six or seven nights; al-Ālūsī says seven. Al-Qazzāz calls ʿadasa a usually fatal plague-like pustule and gives the verbs ʿadasa and maʿdūs. Accounts say his body lay three days, then hired Sudanese/Black men buried him, or relatives pushed it into a pit or against a wall from a distance with poles and stones. Al-Bayḍāwī-FULL calls the illness smallpox, whereas al-Bayḍāwī calls it ʿadasa. [12 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI(أبو رافع,القزاز); BIQAI-FULL(القزاز); ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 111:2/q11/p2 He missed Badr through smallpox, financed a substitute, then died of grief seven days after the defeat. — Kuran Yolu gives this sequence in the second year of the Hijra. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 111:2/q11/p3 The defeat at Badr and an altercation with Abū Rāfiʿ preceded his death. — Abū Sufyān ibn al-Ḥārith described Muslim victory and white riders on piebald horses; Abū Rāfiʿ called them angels, whereupon Abū Lahab beat him. Umm al-Faḍl struck Abū Lahab with a tent pole, and he left humiliated. [4 notes: BIQAI(أبو رافع,أبو سفيان بن الحارث); BIQAI-FULL(أبو رافع)]

## 111:2/q12 How is the verse's prediction said to have been fulfilled?  [* · interpretation]
- 111:2/q12/p1 His actual ruin, death and burial fulfilled the unseen warning and signaled prophecy. — Al-Bayḍāwī cites his death after Badr. Al-Biqāʿī says the disgrace showed that none of his supposed protections worked and notes continued stoning of the grave. Ibn ʿĀshūr says its revelation in his lifetime foretold death in unbelief. Al-Ālūsī says events unfolded as foretold. [9 notes: BAYDAWI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 111:2/q13 How does this verse connect to its sūra and other passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources connect the verse either to its immediate curse and Fire, nearby sūras, or a wider scriptural critique of wealth.
- 111:2/q13/p1 It explains the opening curse or ruin and leads into the Fire announced in verse 3. — Al-Biqāʿī sees an answer to those trusting wealth, children and strength after verse 1; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a new statement shifting from rebuke to hopeless ruin. Corpus Coranicum says verse 3 specifies the initially unspecified threat and that the pronouns through verse 4 return to Abū Lahab. [4 notes: BIQAI; IBNASHUR; CORPUSCORANICUM]
- 111:2/q13/p2 It belongs with Qurʾānic warnings that wealth cannot save its owner. — Corpus Coranicum compares 69:28, 92:11 and 104:1–3 and the wider criticism at 100:8, 102:1–2 and 107:1; it also notes Psalm 39, 1 Timothy 6, and Luke 18/Mark 10. Islāhī connects the false promise of immortality to 104:2–3. [6 notes: RAZI; RAZI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 111:2/q13/p3 The sūra contrasts with al-Kāfirūn and al-Naṣr: worship and victory versus Abū Lahab's worldly and afterlife loss. — Al-Rāzī starts from the purpose of creation, worship at 51:56; he compares rejection of partners in 109:1 and victory in 110:1 with the threat of al-Masad. - 111:2/q13/p4 Following mass conversion in the previous sūra, it portrays the one Meccan who remained outside that faith. — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts Abū Lahab's loss with the preceding sūra's conversion scene. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 111:2/q14 What general lesson is drawn from the failure of wealth and earnings?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the emphasis is on wealth's inability to protect or on deeds judged worthless.
- 111:2/q14/p1 Worldly possessions, kin and standing cannot rescue a person from God's judgment. — Biqāʿī says disbelievers presumed wealth, children and strength could secure them, but only what God commands benefits. Corpus Coranicum situates the warning among early Meccan critiques of wealth; Islāhī says wealth gave Abū Lahab and associates a false sense of immortality. [10 notes: BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI]
- 111:2/q14/p2 Good works performed amid evil disposition and polytheism have no worth before God. — Islāhī describes Abū Lahab's welfare work for poor people and pilgrims as treasurer of the House as a cover for misdeeds; Qatāda's comparison is 25:23, deeds scattered as dust. ## 111:2/q15 What inward meaning is given to his wealth and earnings?  [مَالُهُۥ وَمَا كَسَبَ · inward]
- 111:2/q15/p1 Original wealth symbolizes innate preparatory knowledge, and earnings symbolize false belief; both fail him and deepen torment. — Al-Qāshānī says the beliefs do not match reality. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 111:2/q16 What background or virtue of the sūra is reported?  [* · sciences]
turns on: These reports address different background matters: classification and naming, or a narrated virtue.
- 111:2/q16/p1 It is an early Makkan sūra of five verses, called al-Masad from its final word and also al-Lahab. — Study Quran says the final term is associated with a Hell punishment and the sūra depicts punishment of Abū Lahab and his wife. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(many)]
- 111:2/q16/p2 The Prophet hoped that a reader of Tabbat would not share Abū Lahab's abode. — Al-Bayḍāwī transmits this report as a virtue. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 111:2/q17 Who is the man referred to by the verse's pronouns?  [عَنْهُ مَالُهُۥ · referent]
- 111:2/q17/p1 Abū Lahab, the 'Father of Flame,' whose nickname is associated with a bright red complexion. — Biqāʿī calls him the outcast deprived of mercy. Corpus Coranicum says all the sūra's pronouns except verse 5 refer to the man cursed at the start. Study Quran explains the nickname. [3 notes: BIQAI; CORPUSCORANICUM; STUDYQURAN]

## 111:2/q18 How should the reports and specific identifications be used in interpreting the verse?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse's broad expression welcomes multiple concrete applications or should be protected from narrowing by later anecdotes.
- 111:2/q18/p1 The different concrete identifications of kasb can all fit its broad sense. — Elmalılı says capital, profits, inherited and self-earned assets, children, social rank, customary good works and plots are compatible, while none saved him. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 111:2/q18/p2 Anecdotes from reception should not force a narrow referent into the verse. — Corpus Coranicum cites parallel over-specifications in interpretation of 93:3, 93:6–8 and 108:3 and resists the anecdotal child reading. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 111:2/q19 What difficulty is raised by pairing wealth and offspring?  [مَالُهُۥ وَمَا كَسَبَ · interpretation]
- 111:2/q19/p1 Ibn Qutayba objects that a prior description of the person as having wealth and offspring is hard to reconcile with this verse. — The note records his objection to the earlier point and cites the verse's wealth-and-earnings wording; it does not specify a resolution. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 111:2/q20 What does al-Ṭabarī report about the ruin announced before this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 111:2/q20/p1 The opening curses his hands and deeds, then reports his certain ruin. — Al-Ṭabarī distinguishes the divine curse from the report, citing ʿAbd Allāh's reading with 'already'; Qatāda calls the outcome loss and ruin. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة)]
- 111:2/q20/p2 One report links his ruin to rejecting a religion that would treat him equally after conversion. — Ibn Zayd reports Abū Lahab asking whether he would receive equal treatment; he curses such a religion, and his deeds bring ruin. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
