# 32:21: 17 questions

## 32:21/q01 What does the oath prefix add to God's promise?  [وَلَنُذِيقَنَّهُم · grammar]
- 32:21/q01/p1 The lām marks an oath. — Al-Naḥḥās identifies the lām as the oath particle. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 32:21/q02 Why is the punishment described as something they will taste?  [وَلَنُذِيقَنَّهُم · rhetoric]
- 32:21/q02/p1 Tasting means undergoing punishment, vividly presenting pain as a sensory taste. — Al-Rummānī says tasting suggests active sensory seeking and unexpectedly applies an experience associated with pleasure to pain. Al-Dāmghānī cites the town made to taste hunger and fear in 16:112 as evidence that 'taste' can mean punish. [2 notes: RUMMANI; DAMGHANI]

## 32:21/q03 Who are 'them' in the promise?  [هُم · referent]
- 32:21/q03/p1 The unbelievers of Quraysh, especially the people of Mecca. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly identifies Quraysh's unbelievers; Bursawī identifies the direct addressees as Meccans. Al-Biqāʿī says the Meccan revelation was followed by Quraysh's drought, division, killing and captivity. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 32:21/q04 What does al-adnā mean here?  [ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ · meaning]
- 32:21/q04/p1 Nearer in time or position, with a lesser degree of punishment also implied. — Yaḥyā b. Sallām and al-Dāmghānī cite 53:9 for the sense 'nearer'; al-Naḥḥās and Asad give the same literal sense. Bursawī says its original sense is a lower place. Al-Ālūsī allows 'nearer' or 'lesser' and says worldly punishment is both. Ibn al-Jawzī records 'nearer' alongside an alternative referent, return; that alternative is mapped under the punishment question. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 32:21/q05 What is the nearer punishment?  [ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the clause names a general earthly warning or a particular affliction, whether death and grave punishment allow the stated return, and whether the account is outward or inward.
- 32:21/q05/p1 Worldly afflictions broadly, including illness, hardship, fear, loss, drought, killing and captivity. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ubayy, Abū al-ʿĀliya, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan, Ibrāhīm and others give worldly trials, sometimes of body and property, until repentance. Al-Farrāʾ is unsure whether his report is from Ibrāhīm or Mujāhid. Al-Zajjāj cites 2:155 for fear, hunger and loss and adds a report of captivity and killing. Al-Ṭabarī explicitly prefers an inclusive reading because all these forms occurred and fulfilled the promise; al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz prefers calamities together with famine because they can prompt return. Al-Rāzī argues that earthly pain cannot be both maximally severe and prolonged, unlike the afterlife's pain. Al-Bayḍāwī includes seven years' drought, killing and captivity; Bursawī adds starvation and smoke-like vision after the Prophet's prayer against Mecca. Al-Biqāʿī includes direct and mediated chastisement, even grave punishment, and recounts repeated drought, division, killing and captivity. Ubayy's lists add the smoke, violent seizure or assault, al-lizām/al-luzūm and, in some transmissions, the Romans; the reports vary over whether seizure or smoke is named, and Ibn Kathīr says one version makes the assault and al-lizām still future while calamities and smoke had passed. Muslim preserves Shuʿba's uncertainty between assault and smoke. The Prophet's reported list gives misfortunes, illnesses and burdens/anṣāb: chastening for the profligate, purification or expiation for believers. Ibn Kathīr reports al-Suddī's detail that every Meccan household lost or ransomed a relative. The Study Quran adds defeat, humiliation and natural disaster. Kur'an Yolu prefers material and spiritual distress that permits self-examination, supported by 32:22. The two reports ascribed to Ibn Masʿūd differ: one concerns famine, another Badr; they need not narrow this broad category. ## 32:21/q06 Why call one punishment nearer and the other greater?  [ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ ٱلْأَكْبَرِ · rhetoric]
- 32:21/q06/p1 Nearness stresses the immediate warning; greatness stresses the afterlife's severe and enduring threat. — Al-Rāzī says people can be deterred more by a mild immediate penalty than a severe delayed one and may prefer immediate small gain over later great gain. Calling the earthly pain 'small' would soften it; calling afterlife pain 'distant' would weaken its warning. Al-Nīsābūrī summarizes the Imām similarly. Abū Ḥayyān says the unstated opposites are entailed: earthly pain ends at death and the greater pain is farther away. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that adnā better warns than aṣghar or abʿad. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان,النيسابوري); TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 32:21/q07 What does dūna express here?  [دُونَ · meaning]
- 32:21/q07/p1 Before the greater punishment in time. — Ibn al-Jawzī and Bursawī explicitly gloss dūna as 'before'; Ibn ʿAṭiyya describes earthly punishment short of the afterlife. Bursawī says its basic sense is a slightly lower position, extended to wealth and rank. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 32:21/q08 What is the greater punishment?  [ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْأَكْبَرِ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'greater' points to the afterlife, a harsher historical or future event, or inward separation from God.
- 32:21/q08/p1 Punishment on Resurrection Day, especially Hell or the eternal Fire. — This is repeatedly described as the agreed or majority interpretation by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Taḥrīr and others; al-Ālūsī prefers it as the relied-upon view. Al-Rāzī contrasts the afterlife's combined severity and endurance with finite earthly pain. Yaḥyā b. Sallām, al-Dāmghānī, al-Ṭabrisī and Kashf al-asrār specify Fire. The reports differ in saying Judgment Day, hereafter, Hell or eternal Fire, but agree on a postmortem greater torment. - 32:21/q08/p2 Killing at Badr. — Muqātil regards it as more severe than the seven-year famine; ʿAṭāʾ reports the attribution to Ibn ʿAbbās, despite al-Wāḥidī's statement that the afterlife identification is otherwise agreed. [3 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]
- 32:21/q08/p3 Killing, captivity and enslavement or imprisonment. — Abū Ḥayyān reports killing, captivity and enslavement; al-Ālūsī reports killing, captivity and imprisonment, requiring the nearer punishment to be milder, such as famine or illness. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 32:21/q08/p4 The Mahdī's emergence with the sword. — Attributed to Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad by al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī; al-Naqqāsh's reported version pairs it with inflation as the lesser torment. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(جعفر بن محمد); BURSEVI(تفسير نقاش); ALUSI-FULL(جعفر بن محمد)]
- 32:21/q08/p5 The Beast and the Antichrist. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this greater-punishment identification to some members of the Prophet's family, saying it requires a lesser worldly counterpart; other reports place the same pair under the nearer punishment. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض أهل البيت)]
- 32:21/q08/p6 Abandonment by God. — One report in al-Sulamī pairs humiliation as the lesser with abandonment as the greater; al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya treats abandonment, estrangement and hardness of heart as the seeker's greater spiritual torment. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 32:21/q08/p7 Veiling from divine attributes and essence or witnessing the Known. — Al-Qāshānī describes darkness veiling the divine attributes and essence. Ḥaqāʾiq al-Buqlī calls it veiling from witnessing the Known; Bursawī's Persian couplet says separation from union with God is worse than every burning fire. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(حقائق البقلي)]
- 32:21/q08/p8 The chastisement resulting from worldly attachment. — Al-Warrāq pairs it with attachment to worldly life as the lesser torment. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو الحسن الوراق)]
- 32:21/q08/p9 Distance from God's nearness. — Bursawī reports a pairing of falling into sin with distance from God. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 32:21/q08/p10 Misery in the Hereafter. — Bursawī reports this paired with worldly humiliation, distinct in wording from the separate eternal-Fire report. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 32:21/q09 What does laʿalla convey in this clause?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the particle expresses the chastening's purpose or hope as perceived by the addressees.
- 32:21/q09/p1 Purpose: so that punishment may lead them toward repentance. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses laʿalla as kay; al-Ālūsī says it expresses exposure to repentance. Bursawī allows the same purposive sense, repentance from disbelief and sins. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); BURSEVI]
- 32:21/q09/p2 Hope from the perspective of those addressed. — Al-Ālūsī transmits Sībawayh's account that the hope is situated in the addressees rather than uncertainty in divine knowledge. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه)]

## 32:21/q10 Is yarjiʿūn read as an active or a passive return, and what follows?  [يَرْجِعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the return is performed by people themselves or is caused for them, and whether its destination is faith or worldly life.
- 32:21/q10/p1 The active reading allows their own repentance or request to return. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān discuss the ordinary sense of returning or wishing to return; al-Ālūsī prefers the transmitted repentance interpretation. A reading attributed to Mujāhid and Abū ʿUbayda links the clause to tasting and takes return as return to this world. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد وأبو عبيدة); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 32:21/q10/p2 A passive reading favors being returned to worldly life, though it can also be caused return to faith. — Al-Qurṭubī says the passive supports the grave-punishment interpretation and the wish for restored life. Al-Ālūsī says it does not naturally mean self-initiated repentance, then allows that chastisement may turn them from disbelief to faith even under the passive. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 32:21/q11 What return is hoped for?  [يَرْجِعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether return means repentance now, restoration to earthly life after death, later people's moral avoidance, or an inward renewal.
- 32:21/q11/p1 Repentance: turning from unbelief and sin to faith, truth and righteousness. — ʿAbd Allāh, Abū al-ʿĀliya and Qatāda gloss it as repentance; Muqātil and others specify disbelief to faith, al-Ṭabrisī truth, Asad righteousness. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses this purpose to identify the nearer punishment as worldly warning. The Study Quran calls affliction a possible mercy, prompting reflection and return to humanity's natural state, citing 7:172 and 30:30. Bursawī includes sins as well as disbelief. Al-Ālūsī prefers this transmitted explanation, allowing the passive reading to mean that punishment turns them toward faith. Al-Jishumī also reads the clause as a threat and warning meant to induce return, or as killing followed by Islam's ascendancy despite resistance. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,عبد الله,قتادة); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبي بن كعب، إبراهيم، الحسن ومجاهد عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(عبد الله، وأبو العالية، وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية,مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو العالية,مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 32:21/q11/p2 A wish or plea to be returned to worldly life. — Applied especially when the nearer punishment is grave torment: the dead ask to return, as in 32:12 or 23:99. Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān explain that an intended act may be named as if performed, as 'rising' in 5:6 names the intention to rise for prayer; a passive reading also supports return by another's act. Al-Zamakhsharī reports the same explanation through al-Ālūsī. [6 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري,مجاهد وأبو عبيدة)]
- 32:21/q11/p3 Later people refrain from the earlier sinners' acts. — Al-Ṭabrisī reports this as an alternative to the sinners' own repentance. [1 notes: TABRISI(قيل)]
- 32:21/q11/p4 An inward return to sincere seeking and love after heedlessness. — Al-Qāshānī says hardship cleanses primordial nature before corruption and veiling. Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says afflictions rouse the stalled seeker; Bursawī connects renewed sincerity and love to the turning of hearts in 6:110. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 32:21/q12 Who can return if the punishment kills some of them?  [هُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ · referent]
- 32:21/q12/p1 Those who survive or remain may repent; the pronouns can refer to different subsets. — Yaḥyā b. Sallām applies the phrase to those left alive. Ibn ʿAṭiyya makes the differing pronoun references explicit on the sword interpretation. Al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, Ibn Masʿūd and Bursawī agree; al-Biqāʿī says some first turned from fear of the sword and later loved Islam after seeing its merits. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(إبن مسعود)]

## 32:21/q13 How can God express hope for their return while knowing the outcome?  [لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْجِعُونَ · theology]
turns on: How divine knowledge, divine will, servants' choice and the conventional use of hope language relate.
- 32:21/q13/p1 Hope language describes an act whose outcome is not entailed by that act, even if independently known. — Al-Rāzī compares a trader hoping for profit despite independent certainty with cutting a throat 'hoping' for death, which is impossible because death follows from the act itself. Punishment does not itself guarantee repentance. He also offers that God chastens gradually like one who hopes, or that observers might say 'perhaps they return'; 26:82 and 29:36 show hope language beside certainty, while 32:14 is noted in the discussion. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 32:21/q13/p2 God wills servants' free return even if He knows they will refuse; this does not imply inability. — Al-Jishumī says the hoped-for return proves God wills everyone's return, against the Mujbira. Al-Zamakhsharī distinguishes God's own acts, which necessarily happen when He wills them, from His willing servants to choose obedience, and compares a person wishing a servant would obey. Al-Ālūsī reports and dismisses al-Zamakhsharī's interpretation of divine hope as willing; Abū Ḥayyān rejects the account as Muʿtazilī doctrine, saying Sunnis refuted it. [3 notes: JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري)-]

## 32:21/q14 What does the transmitted report about Ubayy's statement establish?  [ٱلْعَذَابِ ٱلْأَدْنَىٰ · reports]
- 32:21/q14/p1 Ubayy is reported to include named worldly events, though the wording and chain vary. — The Musnad report says calamities and smoke had passed and names al-baṭsha and al-lizām. Al-Ṭabarī notes variants over seizure versus smoke. Ibn Kathīr says Muslim has a similar stopped report and al-Bukhārī a report from Ibn Masʿūd; another wording separates past smoke from future assault and lizām. Muslim preserves Shuʿba's uncertainty between assault and smoke. Abū Mūsā notes that Yaḥyā b. Saʿīd's first transmission omits Yaḥyā b. al-Jazzār, one link in Ubayy's chain. [5 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبي بن كعب); TAB-FULL(أبو موسى,أبي بن كعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبي بن كعب); MUSLIM(أُبَىِّ بْنِ كَعْبٍ)]

## 32:21/q16 How does the verse fit the sūra's progression?  [* · links]
- 32:21/q16/p1 It sets the opponents' worldly warning and final fate beside the believers' worship and hidden reward. — Iṣlāḥī says believers bow to the Qur'anic signs, pray at night and spend for God, with a concealed reward; the succeeding material contrasts their fate with opponents' worldly and next-world fate. The Study Quran describes the sūra's second half as contrasting prostration before signs with turning away, then recalling Israel's guidance through those signs, citing 32:15–22. Al-Biqāʿī says the promise answers believers' haste for opponents' worldly humiliation and counters opponents' denial of punishment after death and confidence in their numbers and strength. Kur'an Yolu says 32:22 confirms that stubbornness after opportunities to return deserves punishment. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 32:21/q17 What is the revelation setting of the sūra containing this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 32:21/q17/p1 Sūrat al-Sajdah is Makkan. — The Study Quran identifies the sūra as Makkan; al-Biqāʿī places Quraysh's later drought, division and losses after its Meccan revelation. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]
