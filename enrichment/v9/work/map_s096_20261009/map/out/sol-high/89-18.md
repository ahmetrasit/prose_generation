# 89:18: 17 questions

## 89:18/q01 How is the exhortation verb read?  [تَحَٰٓضُّونَ · readings]
turns on: The readings vary in person, alif, first vowel, and whether the reciprocal tāʾ is retained; sources also differ on whether the verb is simple or reciprocal.
- 89:18/q01/p1 Second person taḥāḍḍūna, with an alif and open tāʾ or ḥāʾ. — Kufan readers, Abū Jaʿfar and named transmitters use this form; it is explained as reciprocal exhortation. NASHR notes lengthening before the following sukūn. QURTUBI transmits Abū ʿUbayd's preference for it. Al-Ṭabarī accepts the three principal regional readings as sound in meaning (TAB 89:17/r11, TAB 89:18/r9, TAB-FULL r5); al-Farrāʾ also calls the readings sound (FARRA r11). IBNASHUR-FULL adds that one tāʾ of the original tataḥāḍḍūna is dropped. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it group exhortation. ## 89:18/q02 What action does the exhortation verb describe?  [تَحَٰٓضُّونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the reciprocal form means mutual positive exhortation, a simpler transitive urging, protective care, or mutual discouragement.
- 89:18/q02/p1 People fail to urge one another to feed the poor. — Al-Farrāʾ calls the form reciprocal. NAHHAS compares the deletion of a tāʾ with 3:103. TABATABAI connects the failure with love of wealth (89:20). QUTB treats mutual prompting toward good as a norm of communal solidarity. Ibn ʿAṭiyya distinguishes self-encouragement possible under the simpler reading. [27 notes: FARRA; TAB(أبو جعفر وعامة قراء الكوفة,قراء المدينة وأهل الكوفة); TAB-FULL(عامة قراء الكوفة); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(أبو جعفر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي); KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI; QURTUBI(الكوفيون); QURTUBI-FULL(الكوفيون); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 89:18/q02/p2 People fail to urge others to feed the poor; reciprocal morphology can carry this simpler force. — FARISI-HUJJA cites the poetic taḥāsantu meaning ḥassantuhu, plus istqarra/qarra and istaʿlāhu/ʿalāhu (17:43). Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites Abū ʿAlī's taḥāsana and takhāzara. Abū Ḥayyān says tafāʿala and fāʿala can mean faʿala. FARISI-HUJJA and RAZI invoke 69:34. [24 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعض قراء مكة وعامة قراء المدينة); NAHHAS; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; IBNATIYYA(أبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن المبارك); TABRISI; RAZI(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وعلقمة وزيد بن علي وابن المبارك والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; BAGHAWI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 89:18/q02/p3 They fail to look after or preserve one another or the poor. — Al-Farrāʾ allows a protective sense; al-Ṭabarī's less common report explains the ḍamma reading as mutual care, while TAB-FULL explains it as care for the poor. [4 notes: FARRA; TAB(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 89:18/q02/p4 They mutually encourage one another to refrain from urging others to feed. — IBNASHUR-FULL reads the reciprocal form as complicity in discouragement. ELMALILI also says they may discourage aid and exploit the poor. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 89:18/q03 Whom does the unexpressed object of urging include?  [تَحَٰٓضُّونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the transitive verb has an understood object or is used without one.
- 89:18/q03/p1 It can include oneself, one's family, or other people. — NAHHAS says listeners understand the omitted object as others. SAMIN-DURR says it may be oneself or others, or omitted altogether. ALUSI lists self, household and anyone; al-Qurṭubī, al-Zamakhsharī and al-Nasafī explicitly mention households. Ibn ʿAṭiyya permits self-encouragement. [18 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF; IBNATIYYA(ابن كثير وابن عامر,عبد الله بن المبارك); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن المبارك); QURTUBI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI; NASAFI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله وعلقمة وزيد بن علي وابن المبارك والكسائي); SAMIN-DURR(الكسائي، زيد بن علي، علقمة); BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 89:18/q03/p2 The verb is intransitive here, leaving the reproach wholly general. — ALUSI explicitly prefers this analysis over supplying self, family or anyone as object. SAMIN-DURR also permits no object. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI+]

## 89:18/q04 What does ṭaʿām mean here?  [طَعَامِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṭaʿām denotes feeding as an act, food supplied through an omitted expression, or provision in a broader sense.
- 89:18/q04/p1 It is the act of feeding. — IBNATIYYA, ALUSI and IBNASHUR allow ṭaʿām as a verbal noun; ALUSI compares ʿaṭāʾ for giving. IBNASHUR makes the poor person the object of the feeding. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:18/q04/p2 It is actual food, with an understood act of giving or provision. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports an omitted construction; Abū Ḥayyān prefers supplying “giving of” over treating ṭaʿām as a verbal noun, a preference ALUSI reports. IBNASHUR says the annexation marks the poor person's entitlement; SAMIN-DURR also allows ordinary food without further ellipsis. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA(قوم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN+; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+; ALUSI(أبو حيان)+]
- 89:18/q04/p3 Food here extends to meeting the needy person's needs broadly. — ISLAHI-TADABBUR links the broad sense to active communal care for orphans and needy people. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 89:18/q05 Who is the miskīn?  [ٱلْمِسْكِينِ · meaning]
- 89:18/q05/p1 A poor or needy person, broadly including the poor. — ALUSI explicitly includes both needy and poor. IBNASHUR refers back to his treatment in Sūrat al-Tawbah; IBNATIYYA-FULL says he discussed miskīn and faqīr at 9:60. QUTB adds that even a needy person who does not ask is included. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; QUTB-ZILAL]

## 89:18/q06 What does failure to urge imply about one's own giving?  [وَلَا تَحَٰٓضُّونَ طَعَامِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether exhortation is a sign of one's own giving, an act valued beyond individual giving, or the inward motive itself.
- 89:18/q06/p1 It implies that they fail to feed the poor themselves. — BURSEVI says failure to urge makes their own failure more blameworthy. IBNASHUR reasons that people unwilling even to advocate will not give, and that exhorting an act can stand for doing it when able; he compares mutual counsel to truth and patience in 103:3. BIQAI says urging is greater than giving because exhortation entails giving, while giving need not spur others. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-QT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI(مقاتل); BURSEVI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 89:18/q06/p2 The lack of urging also means there is no inward impulse to feed. — ASAD-NOTES compares 107:3 and construes the phrase as absence of the urge to feed the needy. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 89:18/q07 How does this rebuke answer the claims about wealth and poverty?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the correction chiefly identifies obedience and neglect as true honor and disgrace, or calls for constant praise in either condition.
- 89:18/q07/p1 Wealth and poverty test people; real disgrace lies in neglect of the vulnerable and disobedience. — TAB reports Qatāda: honor comes through obedience, disgrace through disobedience; al-Ṭabarī prefers this because 89:17–19 name the conduct. JISHUMI and TABRISI locate fault in neglect, not limited provision. BAYDAWI says neglect is stronger evidence of greed than their claims. IBNASHUR-FULL says “rather” exposes hoarding against a boast of generosity. ISLAHI-TADABBUR contrasts gratitude and service in prosperity with contentment in hardship. STUDYQURAN cites 2:220, 4:2–10, 4:127, 6:152, 107:1–3 on injustice to orphans. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI; NASAFI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); TAB+]
- 89:18/q07/p2 The proper response is to praise God in both prosperity and poverty. — TAB reports this as another interpretation: people should not thank God only when rich and complain when poor; al-Ṭabarī chooses the conduct-based explanation instead. [1 notes: TAB(آخرون); TAB-]

## 89:18/q08 Why do people fail to urge care for the needy?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes give both neglected accountability in the hereafter and greed as explanations.
- 89:18/q08/p1 They do not expect the afterlife or heed God's watch. — MUQATIL ties the rebuke to Umayya after a break in discourse; IBNJAWZI-ZAD ties neglect to lack of hope for reward; ELMALILI urges using present opportunity to prepare for the hereafter. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ELMALILI]
- 89:18/q08/p2 Greed and attachment to wealth suppress care. — BIQAI says people may hoard when recipients are wealthier, fear claims from equals, or deflect help to another; TABATABAI points to 89:20. BINTSHATI says plunder and wealth love dull conscience and insight. KURANYOLU-TEFSIR describes selfish indifference and arrogance. [6 notes: KASHSHAF; ABUHAYYAN(الزمخشري); BIQAI; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:18/q09 What social or legal duty does this verse support?  [* · law]
- 89:18/q09/p1 The poor have a claim to support, and people should urge others to fulfill it. — JISHUMI expressly calls protection of poor people's rights obligatory. BIQAI says the poor share in wealthy property to the extent of zakat. TABRISI says wealth was given for this care. QUTB emphasizes communal solidarity; ELMALILI and KURANYOLU-TEFSIR urge active and organized mutual encouragement. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; QUTB-ZILAL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 89:18/q10 Why do the verbs shift between addressing people and describing them?  [تَحَٰٓضُّونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the reading turns toward the audience for rebuke or continues third-person description of the people already mentioned.
- 89:18/q10/p1 Direct address confronts humanity collectively and intensifies reproach. — SAMIN-DURR calls the shift from generic humanity a change of person; BIQAI says singular “human” is generalized by plural verbs. IBNASHUR says the majority reading confronts offenders more forcefully. TABATABAI connects the shift and “rather” to intensified rebuke. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR(الجمهور); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 89:18/q10/p2 Third person describes those people to the Prophet and believers. — IBNASHUR compares 90:6–7; STUDYQURAN says this reading refers back to people described in 89:15–16. IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA says it continues the preceding context; SAMIN-DURR says the singular “human” is understood collectively. [5 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); IBNASHUR(أبو عمرو ويعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 89:18/q11 What links does this rebuke have to nearby and parallel passages?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources connect the passage to different contexts: the sūra's tests, Qurʾānic parallels, or a proposed wider literary genre.
- 89:18/q11/p1 It develops the sūra's account of failed tests of wealth and care for vulnerable people. — CORPUSCORANICUM takes 89:17–20 as specifying earlier charges against ʿĀd, Thamūd and Pharaoh; the orphan and poor stand for the vulnerable. ISLAHI-TADABBUR reads prosperity and hardship as tests leading toward the peaceful soul. KURANYOLU-TEFSIR relates the rebuke to Meccan ethics. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 89:18/q11/p2 The orphan and poor motif recurs in other Qurʾānic passages. — CORPUSCORANICUM cites 107:2–3 and 90:11–16; ASAD-NOTES cites 107:3. FARISI-HUJJA, WAHIDI-BASIT, and RAZI cite 69:34 for urging food. STUDYQURAN points to 69:34 and passages on the orphan. [7 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; ASAD-NOTES; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); RAZI(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]
- 89:18/q11/p3 The motif resembles biblical prophetic rebukes within a literary genre familiar to hearers. — This is CORPUSCORANICUM's proposed literary connection. [1 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM]

## 89:18/q12 What occasion of revelation is reported?  [* · reports]
- 89:18/q12/p1 Qudāma ibn Maẓʿūn, an orphan under Umayya ibn Khalaf, was denied his rights. — BURSEVI attributes this report to Muqātil. [1 notes: BURSEVI(مقاتل)]

## 89:18/q13 What report about honoring an orphan is cited?  [* · reports]
- 89:18/q13/p1 A home in which an orphan is honored is the most beloved home to God. — ABUHAYYAN reports this as a hadith and supplies no grading. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN(الحديث)]

## 89:18/q14 What rule about an orphan's labor is mentioned alongside the verse?  [* · law]
- 89:18/q14/p1 Using an orphan's labor without pay is forbidden, with stated exceptions. — BURSEVI includes brothers and teachers in the prohibition; citing al-Ashbāh and al-Qunya, he excepts the mother and a case of sending the child to fetch his partner. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 89:18/q15 Who performs the acts praised and blamed in this passage?  [* · theology]
- 89:18/q15/p1 Humans perform the acts of honoring, urging, eating and loving. — JISHUMI argues that attaching praise and blame to these acts shows they are human acts. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 89:18/q16 How are nearby words explained in the accompanying notes?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The accompanying notes gloss three distinct words in neighboring verses.
- 89:18/q16/p1 Al-turāth derives from inheritance, with tāʾ replacing wāw. — SAMIN-DURR compares tawalluj, tawrāh and tukhmah. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 89:18/q16/p2 Lammā means collected or gathered together. — SAMIN-DURR cites al-Ḥuṭayʾa; al-Nābigha's talummuhu describes gathering a companion in disarray. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(النابغة)]
- 89:18/q16/p3 Jamm means abundant. — SAMIN-DURR cites Zuhayr on abundant water, abundant hair and an idiom for a great crowd. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 89:18/q17 How is ʿalā ṭaʿām attached syntactically?  [تَحَٰٓضُّونَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامِ · grammar]
- 89:18/q17/p1 The prepositional phrase ʿalā ṭaʿām is governed by taḥāḍḍūn. — SAMIN-DURR states this syntactic attachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
