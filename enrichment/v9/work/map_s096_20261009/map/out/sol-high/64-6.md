# 64:6: 30 questions

## 64:6/q01 What does dhālika refer to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
- 64:6/q01/p1 The worldly calamity already mentioned, often including the afterlife punishment. — Baydawi, Biqa'i and Ibn Ashur identify the preceding calamity; Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi and Tabataba'i explicitly include both punishments. Tabataba'i says this independent sentence answers an implied question about their cause. [8 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 64:6/q02 Why recall the earlier peoples here?  [ذَٰلِكَ · links]
- 64:6/q02/p1 Their fate warns present rejecters that the same rejection may bring the same punishment. — Tabari addresses Quraysh by recalling Noah, Ad, Thamud, Abraham and Lot; Elmalili adds Pharaoh. Ibn Atiyya compares Quraysh's refusal of prophethood and resurrection and envy of the chosen messenger. Ibn Ashur calls the warning an indirect allusion. Biqa'i says the passage identifies the cause of destruction. Study Quran cites 25:7 for the same objection to Muhammad. Bursevi says worldly pain did not expiate these unbelievers though sound reports say believers' misfortunes expiate sins. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 64:6/q03 What is the hāʾ in bi-annahu?  [بِأَنَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the pronoun has an antecedent or introduces the affair.
- 64:6/q03/p1 A pronoun of the matter, explained by the clause about the messengers and their hearers. — Nahhas calls the clause the predicate of anna; Tabrisī, Samin and Ibn Ashur call the pronoun one of affair. Ibn Ashur says it magnifies the explanation. Biqa'i says the compact form suits a sura concerned with the Hour. [6 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 64:6/q03/p2 A pronoun referring to firmness. — Jishumi records this alternative. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 64:6/q04 Why were these peoples punished?  [بِأَنَّهُۥ كَانَت تَّأْتِيهِمْ رُسُلُهُم بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ فَكَفَرُوا۟ وَتَوَلَّوا۟ · interpretation]
- 64:6/q04/p1 Messengers brought clear proof, but their peoples rejected human guidance and disbelieved. — Muqatil, Tabari, Wahidi, Jishumi, Ibn al-Jawzi, Bursevi, Elmalili and Islahi trace this causal sequence. Qurtubi singles out disbelief of the messengers. Ibn Ashur says rejecting the Messenger and miraculous Quran adds another form of unbelief. Tabataba'i says successive rejected missions left nations as tales, citing 23:44. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 64:6/q05 How often did their messengers come, and to whom?  [كَانَت تَّأْتِيهِمْ رُسُلُهُم · rhetoric]
- 64:6/q05/p1 God's chosen messengers came repeatedly to their own peoples with messages intended for them. — Tabataba'i says kana with the imperfect depicts repeated arrivals against one decisive refusal; Asad also says time and again. Biqa'i says God chose each people's messengers and sent them habitually with clear proofs. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 64:6/q06 What were al-bayyināt?  [بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ · meaning]
- 64:6/q06/p1 Clear explanations and proofs, including arguments, miracles and scriptures, establishing the messengers' truth. — Muqatil calls them explanations; Tabari, Nahhas, Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir call them proofs; Jishumi and Nasafi specify miracles; Biqa'i includes scriptures. Ibn Ashur treats the Quran as a miraculous proof. Islahi calls the arguments sound. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 64:6/q07 How does the bāʾ in bil-bayyināt work?  [بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ · grammar]
turns on: Accompaniment versus transitivity.
- 64:6/q07/p1 It marks accompaniment: they came with proofs. — Bursevi records the possibility. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 64:6/q07/p2 It makes the verb transitive to the proofs brought. — Bursevi records this other possibility. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 64:6/q08 How is fa-qālū joined?  [فَقَالُوٓا۟ · grammar]
- 64:6/q08/p1 It is coordinated with kānat. — Bursevi states this analysis. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 64:6/q09 What does bashar denote here?  [بَشَرٌۭ · meaning]
- 64:6/q09/p1 Human beings as a kind; the word can denote one human or a group. — Mawardi equates bashar with insan, derives bashar from visible skin, and gives insan alternative derivations from sociability or forgetfulness. Baydawi and Ibn Ashur say it can apply to one or many; Ibn Ashur cites 18:110, 12:31, 19:17. Qurtubi cites 12:31. Nahhas reports al-Mazini's contrast with nafar and raht and Muhammad ibn Yazid's objection that bashar itself is singular or plural. Tabataba'i says it is plural here by the following verb. [11 notes: NAHHAS(المازني,محمد بن يزيد); MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI]

## 64:6/q10 What did the objection to human guides express?  [أَبَشَرٌۭ يَهْدُونَنَا · interpretation]
- 64:6/q10/p1 They scornfully denied that humans like themselves could be divinely appointed guides. — Tabari and Mawardi stress contempt; Wahidi, Ibn Kathir and Qurtubi stress incredulity. Nasafi and Razi note that they accepted stone worship while denying human messengers. Abu Hayyan says they assumed all humans equal. Biqa'i says envy and misplaced pride led them to think divine greatness required angels, though ordinary humans could not bear seeing angels and receive humans better. Jishumi calls the objection evasive in face of miracle. Ibn Ashur cites 25:7 and 17:95 for human capacity and divine choice. Tabataba'i reads indefinite bashar as belittling. Asad associates distrust with moral estrangement. Study Quran lists parallels including 17:94 and 54:24; Kuranyolu cites 14:10-11, 18:110, 23:24, 23:33 and 25:7; Bursevi and Alusi compare Thamud's 54:24. [30 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İnkârcılar); STUDYQURAN]

## 64:6/q11 How is abashar parsed?  [أَبَشَرٌۭ يَهْدُونَنَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the question is a nominal clause or has an implied verb.
- 64:6/q11/p1 Nominative bashar is the subject, with yahdūnanā its predicate. — Ibn Atiyya and Tabrisī allow an indefinite subject in a nonassertive question; Abu Hayyan and Alusi report al-Hawfi and Ibn Atiyya's analysis. Qurtubi and Samin also give it. Ibn Ashur says fronting intensifies denial. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي وابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي وابن عطية); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 64:6/q11/p2 Nominative bashar is the agent of an omitted verb explained by yahdūnanā, through ishtighāl. — Tabrisī, Abu Hayyan, Samin, Biqa'i, Bursevi and Alusi cite the interrogative hamza's preference for a verb. Burhan contrasts accusative abasharan in 54:24, where the resumed object is accusative, with nominative here, where it is subject. [8 notes: TABRISI(قول آخر); QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; SAMIN-DURR+]

## 64:6/q12 Why is bashar indefinite and fronted?  [أَبَشَرٌۭ yَهْدُونَنَا · rhetoric]
- 64:6/q12/p1 It targets human nature, belittles the messengers and strengthens the denial. — Ibn Ashur says the indefinite names the kind, and fronting the subject intensifies denial; Tabataba'i says the indefinite expresses contempt. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 64:6/q13 Why is yahdūnanā plural?  [بَشَرٌۭ يَهْدُونَنَا · grammar]
- 64:6/q13/p1 The verb agrees with the collective sense of bashar although its grammatical form is singular. — Akhfash, Tabari, Nahhas, Wahidi, Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn al-Jawzi, Qurtubi, Samin, Biqa'i, Bursevi and Alusi explain the agreement. Nahhas contrasts singular yahdīnā; Qurtubi cites 12:31. Biqa'i says the plural sustains the force of combined rejection; Alusi compares 23:51. [13 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 64:6/q14 What guidance did they reject?  [يَهْدُونَنَا · meaning]
- 64:6/q14/p1 Guidance in religion toward God or nearness to Him. — Bursevi gives these possible scopes; Asad notes the literal reply guide us. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 64:6/q15 What did disbelief and turning away entail?  [فَكَفَرُوا۟ وَتَوَلَّوا۟ · interpretation]
- 64:6/q15/p1 They denied messengers and truth and refused faith, proofs, reflection, admonition or action. — Nahhas names prophets and signs; Mawardi the messengers and proof; Tabrisī, Baydawi and Alusi reflecting on signs; Ibn Kathir acting on truth. Qurtubi reports refusal of admonition. Ibn Ashur says tawallī literally means leaving a place and metaphorically refusing the summons, comparing 2:64. Islahi says they were left to face the consequences. [18 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 64:6/q16 What does fa-kafarū wa-tawallaw emphasize?  [فَكَفَرُوا۟ وَتَوَلَّوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 64:6/q16/p1 Their disbelief followed immediately without reflection, and turning away adds complete refusal. — Abu Hayyan and Biqa'i read the fa as immediate; Razi says the second verb is not redundant but describes total rejection. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 64:6/q17 What does wa-staghnā Allāh mean?  [وَّٱسْتَغْنَى ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause names timeless independence or how it becomes manifest.
- 64:6/q17/p1 God had no need of their faith, worship, obedience, deeds, charity or any creature. — Muqatil, Tabari, Wahidi, Razi and others stress timeless independence. Mawardi and Qurtubi report Muqatil grounding it in sovereignty; Mawardi reports al-Bara's mention of charity. Jishumi, Tabrisī, Biqa'i and Islahi say commands and messengers benefit people. Qashani grounds it in divine essence. Tabataba'i reports but rejects this as the specific explanation here. [29 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(البراء بن عازب,مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî ve Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN(R, Z); TABATABAI-]
- 64:6/q17/p2 God displayed His existing independence by destroying them and ending their line. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan say destruction made it manifest. Bursevi and Alusi argue He would not have destroyed them if He needed their obedience. Elmalili agrees. Tabataba'i's narrower explanation says destruction refutes their confidence in knowledge, strength and survival, citing 18:35 and 41:50. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 64:6/q17/p3 He did not compel belief although He had power to do so. — Razi quotes Kashshaf; Abu Hayyan calls Zamakhshari's analysis a Mu'tazili insinuation. Kuranyolu transmits Zamakhshari and Shawkani; Study Quran reports R and Z. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكشاف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Zemahşerî ve Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN(R, Z)]
- 64:6/q17/p4 Clear proof made any renewed summons unnecessary. — Mawardi, Tabrisī and Qurtubi give this alternative; Ibn Ashur says proof was enough absent obstinacy and punishment was hastened. Tabataba'i reports then rejects it. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قول آخر); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); TABATABAI-]
- 64:6/q17/p5 Worldly and final consequences show God owes people no happiness or favor. — Tabataba'i prefers this broader explanation to the destruction-only one, citing 41:50 and 18:36; both show majesty and power. [2 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 64:6/q18 What does the form istaghnā contribute?  [وَّٱسْتَغْنَى · grammar]
turns on: How the added letters relate to God's eternal sufficiency.
- 64:6/q18/p1 Form X intensifies or manifests already complete sufficiency, without seeking or acquiring it. — Ibn Atiyya notes Form X commonly seeks, but says manifestation fits; Abu Hayyan denies seeking, Samin quotes Zamakhshari that sin is no request marker, Ibn Ashur says sin and ta intensify and cites 39:7, and Tabataba'i says God cannot acquire wealth. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 64:6/q18/p2 It can be understood from the triliteral as full independence because seeking entails need. — Bursevi reports Sa'di al-Mufti's analysis. [1 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي)]

## 64:6/q19 How is wa-staghnā connected syntactically?  [وَّٱسْتَغْنَى ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: Coordination versus circumstance.
- 64:6/q19/p1 The waw coordinates this with the preceding sequence. — Alusi prefers coordination. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 64:6/q19/p2 The waw introduces a circumstantial clause about God's preexisting independence. — Ibn Ashur cites eternity; Bursevi reports Sa'di al-Mufti's understood qad. Alusi reports the alternative but prefers coordination. [3 notes: BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 64:6/q20 What does ghanī affirm?  [غَنِىٌّ · theology]
- 64:6/q20/p1 God is intrinsically independent of all creation and everything requested of them. — Tabari, Baydawi, Nasafi, Biqa'i and others generalize independence. Elmalili says God could replace the universe; Qashani says His perfection needs no human knowledge; Tabataba'i calls intrinsic richness the ground of the judgment. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 64:6/q21 What does hamīd affirm?  [حَمِيدٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Intrinsic worthiness, actual praise received, or God's relation to praise.
- 64:6/q21/p1 He is intrinsically worthy of praise for perfection, blessings and just or beneficent acts, even if no one praises Him. — Mawardi gives this alternative to actual praise; Jishumi, Razi, Tabrisī, Baydawi, Biqa'i, Elmalili and Tabataba'i stress deserved praise. Bursevi defines praise as naming perfect attributes and links it to remembrance. Study Quran cites the pairing with Self-Sufficiency at 4:131, 14:8, 22:64, 31:12, 31:26, 35:15 and 60:6. Sibawayh distinguishes ḥamidtu, rewarding, from aḥmadtu, finding worthy of praise. [17 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Āl); SIBAWAYH]
- 64:6/q21/p2 He is praised by creatures for gifts and by each creature's state. — Muqatil speaks of praise throughout His dominion; Tabari, Nahhas and Mawardi reporting Ali mention favors. Qashani, Bursevi and Alusi include each creature's particular perfection or eloquent state; Ibn Ashur mentions the obedient and grateful. [11 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL(علي); JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 64:6/q21/p3 He praises, or loves His servants to praise Him. — Razi lists the one who praises; Mawardi reports Ibn Abbas on God's love of their praise. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 64:6/q22 What verbal-form analogies accompany the explanation of praise?  [حَمِيدٌۭ · grammar]
- 64:6/q22/p1 Faʿala, afʿala and related forms may differ in causation, acquired state, readiness, exposure, evaluation and dialect, or share meanings. — Sibawayh gives dakhala/adkhala, fatanahu/aftanahu, ṭaradtu/aṭradtu, saqaytu/asqaytu, qabartu/aqbartu, alāma/lāma, arāba/rāba and crop readiness; Khalil and some Arabs report variants. Dhū al-Rumma supports asqaytu, al-Farazdaq supports aghlaqa, and Nuṣayb's line varies sawadtu/suddat. Abū Amr distinguishes nazaltu/anzaltu. Sibawayh also treats unused underlying verbs of majnūn and janantuhu. These form analogies accompany his ḥamidtu/aḥmadtu distinction. [30 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو عمرو,الخليل,العرب,بعض العرب,بعضهم)]

## 64:6/q23 What grammatical analogies concern predicates and circumstantial states?  [وَٱللَّهُ غَنِىٌّ حَمِيدٌۭ · grammar]
- 64:6/q23/p1 A locative may precede a nominative subject or predicate without governing it; nominative and accusative alternatives can mark adjective versus circumstance or verbal noun. — Sibawayh gives fīhā ʿAbd Allāh, inna constructions, huwa laka khāliṣ and rajul ṣidq maʿlūman. At 7:32 khāliṣa may be nominative or, more commonly, accusative like khulūṣan; Khalil analyzes ḥasana ummuhu karīman abūhā as circumstantial, with a nominative adjective also possible. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]

## 64:6/q24 What is the lighter reading of rusul?  [رُسُلُهُم · readings]
- 64:6/q24/p1 Sukūn on the sīn lightens the broken plural by removing its medial vowel. — Qiraat-ER gives this phonological reason. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 64:6/q25 What does the verse teach about responsibility and guidance?  [* · theology]
- 64:6/q25/p1 Punishment follows people's own acts, while divine commands and messengers serve their benefit. — Jishumi says the causal wording proves punishment deserved for human sins. Tabrisī and Biqa'i say obligations and messengers serve creatures, not God's need. Islahi says God leaves those who disregard guidance to consequences without forcing them. Tabataba'i says just punishment returns their deeds to them. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 64:6/q26 How do inward readings explain rejection?  [* · inward]
- 64:6/q26/p1 Ego and lack of innate light kept them from recognizing the messengers' perfection behind their humanity. — Qushayri says focus on created humanity obscures God's decree and leaves false multiplicity. Qashani says recognition requires a corresponding meaning within and only the perfect know perfection. Bursevi transmits and develops this, saying none knows God but God and the people felt no need of guidance. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 64:6/q27 Why are saints' ranks hidden?  [* · inward]
- 64:6/q27/p1 Their ordinary bodily needs conceal perfection, mercifully preventing knowingly hostile treatment. — Bursevi reports gnostics who say a perfected saint is harder to know than God because of visible human weakness; if unveiled, people might worship him as Jesus and his radiance eclipse sun and moon. Bursevi says concealment gives ignorant offenders an excuse and prevents conscious war against God. [3 notes: BURSEVI(بعض العارفين)]

## 64:6/q28 How does this episode fit the sura's argument?  [* · links]
- 64:6/q28/p1 Past rejection leads to denial of resurrection and then the call to faith and final judgment. — Tabataba'i says divine attributes entail resurrection and law conveyed by messengers; he compares sealed hearts after refusal in 7:101 and 10:74. Biqa'i says establishing the Hour, rather than classifying groups, is this sura's aim, unlike Ghafir. [3 notes: TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 64:6/q29 What devotional use of al-Hamīd is reported?  [حَمِيدٌۭ · reports]
- 64:6/q29/p1 Regular recitation of an invocation beginning yā Hamīd al-fiʿāl is said to bring uncountable wealth. — Bursevi transmits al-Suhrawardi's devotional report invoking God's favor and gentleness toward all creation. [1 notes: BURSEVI(السهروردي)]

## 64:6/q30 How does human praise compare with the Prophet's perfection?  [حَمِيدٌۭ · inward]
- 64:6/q30/p1 Human praiseworthy qualities retain faults beside Muhammad, Ahmad and Mahmoud, who is perfect in every respect. — Bursevi makes the comparison while expounding praise. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
