# 79:19: 18 questions

## 79:19/q01 What guidance does Moses offer Pharaoh?  [وَأَهْدِيَكَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance here names knowledge of God, the practices of worship and obedience, the path to divine approval, or an inward realization; Tabrisi expressly gives knowledge and the path to approval as alternatives.
- 79:19/q01/p1 He offers to bring Pharaoh to knowledge of God, His oneness, attributes, greatness, and the truth of His religion. — Muqatil specifies God's greatness. Qushayri says Moses would establish his message with signs and teach the truth of religion. Tabrisi says knowledge of Pharaoh's Creator and Sustainer is one explanation. Nasafi specifies recounting God's attributes. Bursevi understands an omitted complement, 'knowledge of your Lord.' Bint al-Shati connects direction to the straight path with the root's raised rock in water and daylight, both making a route clear. Ibn Ashur defines guidance as pointing out a path to a desired goal, conditional on the addressee's acceptance. Jishumi calls it an indication and path to knowledge, rather than faith itself. Razi allows instruction and indication to help disclose truth. Biqai calls the Lord creator, benefactor, and nurturer, who teaches pleasing deeds and blameworthy traits. [14 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; RAZI-FULL]
- 79:19/q01/p2 He calls Pharaoh to worship and obey God and affirm His oneness. — Wahidi reports Ibn Abbas and Muqatil on worship. Baghawi and Ibn al-Jawzi add affirming God's oneness and fearing His punishment. Qurtubi emphasizes obedience and heeding God. Abu Hayyan identifies the promised purification with guidance to oneness and knowledge. Biqai says the Lord teaches pleasing actions and blameworthy traits. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:19/q01/p3 He directs Pharaoh toward God's approval and reward. — Tabrisi offers this as an alternative to teaching him to know his Creator and Sustainer; Ibn Ashur describes guidance as pointing along a path to a desired goal if the person accepts it. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:19/q01/p4 Inwardly, guidance is knowledge of the unity of the divine Essence, softening and dissolving selfhood. — Qashani reads the preceding purification as annihilation of selfhood and this guidance to fear as knowledge of the Essence's unity. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:19/q02 Is a human guide indispensable for knowing God?  [وَأَهْدِيَكَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ · theology]
turns on: Whether Moses' offer makes a teacher a necessary condition for knowledge or shows the benefit of instruction and indication.
- 79:19/q02/p1 Yes: knowledge of God must come through a guide, and this guidance is the mission's chief aim. — Razi reports proponents citing Moses' explicit offer of guidance as proof. They argue that purification already includes all obligatory duties, so the added guidance must be central, and that ending the address with guidance marks its noblest objective. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بأن معرفة الله لا تستفاد إلا من الهادي); RAZI-FULL-]
- 79:19/q02/p2 No: instruction can help disclose truth, but knowing God is not impossible without a teacher. — Razi expressly accepts the usefulness of instruction and indication while rejecting their necessity. Jishumi describes guidance as an indication and path to knowledge rather than faith itself. Ibn Ashur says pointing out the route leads to the goal only if the addressee accepts it. [3 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 79:19/q03 Why does Moses call God 'your Lord'?  [رَبِّكَ · rhetoric]
- 79:19/q03/p1 The familiar title gently presses God's lordship over Pharaoh and opens a path to monotheism and fear. — Ibn Ashur says it could win a hearing without provoking Pharaoh, so faith may enter gradually after Moses' proof. Bint al-Shati says it establishes the claim against Pharaoh's denial of Moses' Lord and prepares for fear, which requires faith in his Lord. Biqai explains Lord as creator, benefactor, and nurturer. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:19/q04 What does 'then you will fear' mean?  [فَتَخْشَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the fear is explained by religious conduct and punishment or by Qashani's inward account of divine unity and loss of selfhood.
- 79:19/q04/p1 Pharaoh would fear God and His punishment, with reverence expressed in duties and avoidance of disobedience. — Jishumi equates khashya with khawf, fear. Nahhas specifies fear of punishment through avoiding disobedience. Wahidi's gloss adds salvation through fear and affirmation of oneness; the scholars of meaning specify performing required duties from fear of punishment. Tabrisi adds leaving prohibited acts; Baydawi and Biqai add both duties and prohibitions. Elmalili specifies reverent fear of wrath and punishment. Ibn Ashur says religious usage means fear of God and the verb is intransitive here because God is its understood object. Tabatabai's reported repentance reading makes this faith-driven fear motivate obedience and restrain sin. [14 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 79:19/q04/p2 Inwardly, fear is knowledge of divine unity that softens and dissolves selfhood. — Qashani links it with the preceding purification understood as annihilation of selfhood. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:19/q05 Why does fear follow guidance?  [وَأَهْدِيَكَ فَتَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the explanation emphasizes knowledge as fear's cause, Pharaoh's acceptance of guidance, or an inward transformation.
- 79:19/q05/p1 Knowledge of God gained through guidance produces reverent fear, which then grounds obedience and good action. — Sulami reports Muhammad ibn Ali al-Tirmidhi calling fear the fruit of sound guidance. Ibn Atiyya places knowledge after guidance and fear after knowledge, citing 35:28. Razi says guidance precedes fear and knowledge precedes obedience, citing 16:2 and 20:14; he also cites 35:28. Baydawi, Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Alusi, Ibn Ashur, and Tabatabai also invoke 35:28, that the learned fear God. Nasafi reports a sage saying one who knows God cannot disobey Him even for an instant. Elmalili says lack of knowledge leaves one without reverence or moral guard and leads to wrongdoing. Bint al-Shati says fear requires faith in one's Lord. Bursevi frames knowledge as adornment following purification as cleansing. Ibn Ashur reports al-Wasiti's successive stages: the beginning of knowledge is fear, followed by reverence, glorification, awe, and finally annihilation. [16 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن علي الترمذي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(بعض الحكماء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الواسطي); TABATABAI; BINTSHATI]
- 79:19/q05/p2 Fear follows if Pharaoh accepts the offered guidance and its path to the goal. — Ibn Ashur conditions the sequence on Pharaoh's readiness for purification and says guidance points to the goal only if the guided person accepts it; the order of clauses follows the order of events. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:19/q05/p3 Inward knowledge of the divine Essence's unity yields fear by softening and dissolving selfhood. — Qashani connects the guidance to fear with annihilation of selfhood in purification. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:19/q06 What conduct or outcome does fear produce?  [فَتَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
- 79:19/q06/p1 It motivates duties and every good while restraining disobedience and evil; feeling secure emboldens wrongdoing. — Nahhas and Tabrisi specify avoiding disobedience and prohibitions. Wahidi's scholars of meaning, Baydawi, and Biqai specify carrying out duties; Baydawi and Biqai also specify leaving prohibitions. Wahidi's gloss adds salvation and affirming oneness. Razi, Nasafi, Biqai, Bursevi, and Alusi contrast fear as a source or foundation of good with false security as an invitation to evil. Nasafi reports a sage's claim that one who knows God cannot disobey for an instant. Elmalili says the ignorant lack reverence and moral restraint. Tabatabai's reported reading has faith-driven fear promote obedience and check sin. [13 notes: NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(بعض الحكماء); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 79:19/q07 Why is fear singled out as the result of guidance?  [فَتَخْشَىٰ · rhetoric]
- 79:19/q07/p1 Fear is the foundation of good conduct, so naming it concisely conveys the other goods it brings. — Abu Hayyan calls it the core of the matter. Razi, Nasafi, Biqai, Bursevi, and Alusi call it the source or foundation of good and contrast it with feeling secure, which invites evil. Ibn Ashur explicitly calls mention of fear alone concise and eloquent. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:19/q08 How is 'then you will fear' joined to the preceding offer?  [وَأَهْدِيَكَ فَتَخْشَىٰ · grammar]
- 79:19/q08/p1 It is joined to what precedes and presents fear as the outcome after guidance. — Nahhas says فتخشى is coordinated with what precedes. Ibn Ashur says the clauses follow the order of events; waw can express sequence contextually, and readiness for purification leads to guidance and fear. [2 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:19/q09 What Prophetic saying illustrates the benefit of fear?  [فَتَخْشَىٰ · reports]
- 79:19/q09/p1 Whoever fears sets out at night, and whoever sets out reaches the destination. — Razi reports the Prophet's comparison of the fearful traveler reaching his goal with God-fear attaining good. Nasafi and Bursevi quote the saying. Alusi reports it from Abu Hurayra through al-Tirmidhi. Ibn Ashur attributes it to Abu Hurayra in al-Tirmidhi's Jami, phrases the departure as early from fear, and gives no grading. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL(الترمذي); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو هريرة عن النبي)]

## 79:19/q10 What broader Quranic uses of fear and humility illuminate this phrase?  [فَتَخْشَىٰ · links]
turns on: The cited usages concern different bearers and expressions of fear or humility, rather than a stated dispute.
- 79:19/q10/p1 The Quran attributes fear of God to messengers, followers of the Reminder, believers, scholars, and people God approves. — Bint al-Shati cites 36:11, 50:33, 21:49, 35:18, 67:13, 13:21, 23:57, 98:8, 33:37, 5:3, 5:44, 9:13, 2:150, 4:77, 9:18, and 3:173. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 79:19/q10/p2 Attributing fear to mountains and stones portrays lifeless matter as able to perceive, respond, fear God, and humble itself. — Bint al-Shati cites 59:21 and 2:74. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 79:19/q10/p3 Khusūʿ is inward humility toward God in worldly life, expressed outwardly through voice, face, and gaze. — Bint al-Shati says the heart is humble and outward expressions reflect it; she cites 17:107–109 for Quranic khusūʿ directed to God and describing believers in this life. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 79:19/q11 What does the preceding purification mean?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether purification names removal of unbelief, growth in good, repentance from tyranny, moral obedience, or inward loss of selfhood.
- 79:19/q11/p1 It is purification from unbelief and association of partners with God through oneness and knowledge. — Tabrisi explains tazakkā as purifying oneself from unbelief. Abu Hayyan identifies promised purification with guidance to God's oneness and knowledge. Biqai says guidance follows purification from associating partners. Wahidi's gloss connects salvation with fear and affirming God's oneness. [4 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 79:19/q11/p2 It is becoming or seeking to become good and pure. — Jishumi says zakāh is growth in good, a zākī grows in good, and tazakkī seeks to become zākī. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 79:19/q11/p3 It is repentance from tyranny and a return to God. — Tabatabai reports this reading, on which the ensuing fear is faith-driven and motivates obedience while restraining sin. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 79:19/q11/p4 It is obedience and avoidance of sin. — Tabatabai reports this as a premise under which guidance and fear explain purification by apposition. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 79:19/q11/p5 Inwardly, it is annihilation of selfhood. — Qashani connects this to knowledge of the unity of the divine Essence, which softens and dissolves selfhood. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 79:19/q12 How is the preceding tazakkā read and construed?  [* · readings]
turns on: Assimilation of the initial ta into z versus deletion of that ta.
- 79:19/q12/p1 Hijazi readers, Abbas, and Yaqub read it with a doubled z. — Tabrisi explains the doubling as assimilation of an initial ta into z because the sounds are close. [2 notes: TABRISI(أهل الحجاز وعباس ويعقوب)]
- 79:19/q12/p2 Other readers use the lighter form. — Tabrisi derives it from tatazakkā with its initial ta deleted. He also understands an omitted noun in the purification invitation, like asking whether someone has a wish or need for it. [3 notes: TABRISI(أهل الحجاز وعباس ويعقوب)]

## 79:19/q13 How does guidance and fear relate to the preceding purification?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether guidance and fear are later stages that complete purification, an explanation of purification, or an added statement of the mission's chief aim.
- 79:19/q13/p1 Purification first cleanses; guidance to knowledge and fear then adorns or completes it. — Bursevi and Alusi use the order of cleansing before adornment. Biqai places guidance after removal of shirk and says fear completes purification through duties and avoidance of prohibitions. Ibn Ashur says the order of clauses follows events when Pharaoh is ready for purification. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:19/q13/p2 Guidance to the Lord and ensuing fear explain what purification consists of. — Tabatabai reports that if purification means obedience and avoidance of sin, the next clause is apposition explaining it. Abu Hayyan identifies promised purification with guidance to God's oneness and knowledge. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 79:19/q13/p3 Guidance is repeated after purification because guidance to God is the mission's foremost and noblest aim. — Razi reports proponents of an indispensable guide arguing that purification already covers every necessary duty, so the additional guidance must be the chief aim; its placement at the end of Moses' address marks it as noblest. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بأن معرفة الله لا تستفاد إلا من الهادي)]
- 79:19/q13/p4 Repentance from tyranny precedes faith-driven fear that motivates obedience and restrains sin. — Tabatabai reports this interpretation of purification and its consequence. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 79:19/q14 How does Moses' offer fulfill the instruction to speak gently?  [* · links]
- 79:19/q14/p1 The offer is a tactful invitation that draws Pharaoh toward faith, elaborating the command to speak gently in 20:44. — Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Elmalili, Ibn Ashur, and Bint al-Shati connect the offer with 20:44; Bint al-Shati also cites 20:42–43 and the hope Pharaoh might remember or fear. Nasafi compares the interrogative opening to a host inviting a guest and says gentle speech coaxes Pharaoh from arrogance. Bursevi notes the wording avoids blunt accusations of polytheism, ignorance, and unbelief while detailing purification and guidance. Alusi says the question draws him down from arrogance. Ibn Ashur says it offers and encourages acceptance. Study Quran says Moses was instructed to invite Pharaoh peacefully to Revelation first, as the Prophet invited Quraysh. Ibn Ashur and Bint al-Shati explain the gentle force of 'your Lord.' [12 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; STUDYQURAN]

## 79:19/q15 What events are left unstated between the offer and the sign?  [* · links]
- 79:19/q15/p1 Moses went to Pharaoh, delivered the invitation, and then showed a miracle confirming his truthfulness. — Tabrisi says the narrative omits Moses' going to Pharaoh and calling him. Abu Hayyan says it omits his delivery of the commanded message before he performed the confirming miracle. [2 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 79:19/q16 What was Moses told when he wondered why to approach a Pharaoh who would refuse?  [* · reports]
- 79:19/q16/p1 God ordered him to go and cited twelve thousand angels who seek knowledge of the divine decree without attaining it. — Qurtubi reports this from Sakhr ibn Juwayriyya. Bursevi reports that God's indication Pharaoh would not purify himself prompted Moses' question; both versions give the same command and number of angels. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(صخر بن جويرية); BURSEVI]

## 79:19/q17 How does this account serve the surah and its audience?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes emphasize distinct purposes or parallels of the account; they do not record a dispute.
- 79:19/q17/p1 It supports the surah's case for resurrection and gathering and displays humanity's final fate. — Biqai says this is the surah's purpose after earlier surahs' fuller exposition and that its oaths mark a decisive climax. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:19/q17/p2 It warns resurrection-denying polytheists, consoles the Prophet amid rejection, and threatens his opponents. — Tabatabai says the opponents use denial of resurrection against religious law. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 79:19/q17/p3 Pharaoh's destruction vindicates Moses' divine mission and God's lordship over people. — Tabatabai argues that the destruction of Pharaoh and his army proves Moses' authorization, contradicting the polytheist claim that lesser lords govern humanity. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 79:19/q17/p4 Moses' peaceful invitation parallels the Prophet's invitation to Quraysh. — Study Quran links both invitations to the instruction to speak gently in 20:44. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 79:19/q18 What does sending guidance before punishment teach about divine justice?  [* · theology]
- 79:19/q18/p1 People must receive the guidance needed for moral discernment before God punishes their wrongdoing. — Asad says full awareness of God is necessary to distinguish right from wrong, and God's justice withholds punishment until moral discernment, linking this to 6:31. Study Quran says a community is not punished before guidance through a messenger, citing 28:59, 26:208, 6:131, 10:13, and 15:4. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
