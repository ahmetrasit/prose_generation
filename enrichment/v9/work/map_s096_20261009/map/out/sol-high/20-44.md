# 20:44: 20 questions

## 20:44/q01 How does this command continue the joint mission?  [فَقُولَا · links]
- 20:44/q01/p1 God renews the address to Moses and Aaron together as partners in delivering the message. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the renewed dual command to the preceding joint address and warning against slackening. Sāmarrāʾī traces the shared mission, fear, reassurance, delivery, and Pharaoh's joint address across 20:29, 20:42, 20:63, 20:90, and 20:92. [2 notes: TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 20:44/q02 What does gentle speech mean?  [قَوْلًۭا لَّيِّنًۭا · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish the plain manner of address from its figurative description.
- 20:44/q02/p1 Soft, courteous, accessible speech that invites rather than affronts the hearer. — Ibn ʿĀshūr extends physical pliancy to fine meanings in speech, citing ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm's تلينا. Jishumī calls the description figurative and eloquent. Ibn ʿAṭiyya insists the full message be conveyed courteously. Ibn Kathīr says accessible words reach hearts. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds that the listener is treated as able to judge truth. [16 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو معاذ); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 20:44/q02/p2 Calling speech gentle is a figurative transfer of physical softness to verbal meaning. — Jishumī calls it Qurʾānic metaphor; Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the source sense as moist, pliant touch and quotes ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm. [2 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:44/q03 How is لَّيِّنًۭا read?  [لَّيِّنًۭا · readings]
- 20:44/q03/p1 A lightened form līnan is reported instead of layyinan. — Al-Zamakhsharī records līnan. Ibn al-Jawzī names Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī and ʿĀṣim al-Jaḥdarī and describes a quiescent yāʾ. Al-Samīn attributes a lightened form to Abū Muʿādh, likening it to the two forms of ميت. Al-Ālūsī reports a light nūn from Abū Muʿādh; the descriptions vary. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمران الجوني وعاصم الجحدري); SAMIN-DURR(أبو معاذ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو معاذ)]

## 20:44/q04 What particular words or manner make the address gentle?  [قَوْلًۭا لَّيِّنًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The accounts differ over the actual wording and whether an honorific, an invitation, or promised benefits satisfy the command.
- 20:44/q04/p1 Use a courteous, complete invitation, exemplified by Moses' offer of purification and guidance in 79:18–19 and the message in 20:47. — Muqātil, Ibn Masʿūd, and others cite 79:18–19; Rāzī and al-Ālūsī add 20:47. Al-Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān stress question, offer, and counsel. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers conveying the full meaning gently. Abū Ḥayyān reports that some identify the whole message through the greeting to followers of guidance. Ibn al-Jawzī also transmits this invitation through Abū Ṣāliḥ from Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil. [18 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ, Z); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو صالح عن ابن عباس ومقاتل); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 20:44/q04/p2 Address Pharaoh respectfully by his kunya. — Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, al-Suddī, and others are named. Qurṭubī cites the Prophet's honoring a people's notable and use of Abū Wahb for Ṣafwān and Abū Ḥubāb for ʿAbd Allāh ibn Ubayy. Rāzī's critics allow this as part of the command but say a kunya alone is too slight to exhaust it. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(بعض الكوفيين); TUSTARI(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي وعكرمة); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); TABRISI(السدي وعكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة عن ابن عباس والسدي); RAZI-FULL(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(الكلبي وعكرمة وابن عباس ومجاهد والسدي,النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(علي عن الضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وسفيان الثوري); RAZI-FULL(معترضون)-]
- 20:44/q04/p3 Offer faith in exchange for continuing youth, rule, worldly pleasures, and, in many reports, Paradise. — Reports vary in details: Wāḥidī mentions health and wealth, ʿAmr ibn Dīnār gives Pharaoh's 409-year lifespan, and Qurṭubī has four hundred extra years. Some accounts include Hāmān's intervention. Rāzī's critics object that such benefits could compel faith and undermine responsibility. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL(عمرو بن دينار,غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL(معترضون)-]
- 20:44/q04/p4 Say the testimony that there is no god but God. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports Muʿādh and Ibn ʿAbbās; Ibn Kathīr names ʿIkrima. Abū Ḥayyān says its gentleness is ease on the tongue. Al-Ālūsī calls this the most remote view and favors Ibn ʿAbbās's general courteous invitation. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(خالد بن معدان عن معاذ والضحاك عن ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة); STUDYQURAN(IK); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)-]
- 20:44/q04/p5 Tell Pharaoh he has a Lord and a return, with Paradise for faith and Fire for unbelief. — Al-Ḥasan's formulation is transmitted by Ibn al-Jawzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Ālūsī; Ibn Kathīr calls it giving Pharaoh an excuse before destruction. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمرو بن عبيد عن الحسن البصري); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 20:44/q04/p6 Lead with a promise before a threat. — Al-Māwardī says enticement softens Pharaoh first. Abū Ḥayyān cites a poet's instruction; al-Ālūsī cites a maxim that promises soften ignorant tribes. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:44/q04/p7 Tell Pharaoh that God is nearer to pardon and forgiveness than to wrath. — Wahb ibn Munabbih gives this wording as reported by Ibn Kathīr. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 20:44/q06 Why was gentle speech commanded?  [فَقُولَا قَوْلًۭا لَّيِّنًۭا · rhetoric]
turns on: The sources give distinct purposes: persuasion, a caregiver's claim, proof, Moses' formation, and the order of admonition.
- 20:44/q06/p1 It makes admonition more persuasive and less likely to provoke a tyrant into obstinacy or violence. — Rāzī and al-Burṣawī say harshness increases tyrants' defiance; al-Burṣawī adds the danger of Pharaoh striking or killing Moses. Abū Ḥayyān calls the question and offer delicate. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says gentle wording better reaches the aim. Al-Tustarī cites the Prophet's saying that hearts love benefactors and hate mistreatment. [12 notes: TUSTARI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 20:44/q06/p2 Pharaoh had raised Moses, so gentleness acknowledges the caregiver's claim. — Al-Zamakhsharī likens it to a father's claim; Rāzī and al-Burṣawī extend it to respect owed parents. Al-Bayḍāwī offers this as another reason. Al-Ālūsī says the linking ف points chiefly to Pharaoh's transgression, not merely parental care. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:44/q06/p3 It establishes clear proof and removes Pharaoh's excuse before punishment. — Al-Tustarī adds that the magicians and others known to be guided could hear. Al-Zajjāj says proof requires messengers, since God's private knowledge is no human proof. Al-Zamakhsharī invokes 20:134, Abū Ḥayyān 17:15, and al-Burṣawī says delivery before punishment is God's custom. [9 notes: TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 20:44/q06/p4 It trains Moses in patient restraint and gracious speech. — Al-Tustarī says the aim was to stir hearts, not predict Pharaoh's faith. Al-Burṣawī describes Moses as sharp tempered and says the injunction softens him; another account contrasts Muḥammad's merciful temperament with Moses' forcefulness and appeals to the maxim 'Adopt God's qualities' and 9:73. [4 notes: TUSTARI; BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 20:44/q06/p5 It begins the encounter softly, leaving room to consider, with firmness permitted after obstinacy. — Rāzī says gentleness comes first. Al-Burṣawī cites al-Asʾila al-Muqḥima and 16:125, a Persian couplet joining gentleness with firmness, and contextual judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 29:46 and Moses' later warning in 20:48. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:44/q07 What conduct does this command teach callers and family members?  [قَوْلًۭا لَّيِّنًۭا · law]
turns on: The practical applications differ by audience and stage of admonition.
- 20:44/q07/p1 Invite and correct people gently, even the wicked or powerful, with wisdom and good counsel. — Qurṭubī connects 2:83 and says protection matters when admonishing rulers; Ibn Kathīr cites 16:125. Ṭabrisī says gentleness encourages acceptance. Asad applies the lesson to every effort at conversion; Iṣlāḥī describes a teacher's sympathy, rejects arrogant prophetic self assertion and compares worldly propaganda. Kuranyolu stresses method and tone. [14 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 20:44/q07/p2 A child may admonish a parent calmly but must avoid harsh rebuke or striking, and should fall silent when the parent becomes angry. — Al-Burṣawī cites al-Ḥasan via al-Iḥyāʾ and distinguishes a student confronting a scholar who fails to practice knowledge. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن)]
- 20:44/q07/p3 It is permissible to use an unbeliever's respectful kunya. — Qurṭubī says the hope of conversion is useful but not a necessary condition. Al-Ālūsī draws the same permissibility from the transmitted kunya account. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(علي وابن عباس وسفيان الثوري)]
- 20:44/q07/p4 Firm speech can follow if gentle invitation fails. — Rāzī, al-Burṣawī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr present the shift as conditioned by stubborn rejection; al-Burṣawī says either manner may be praised or blamed according to circumstances. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:44/q08 What inward meaning is drawn from the gentle address?  [قَوْلًۭا لَّيِّنًۭا · inward]
turns on: The sayings draw different spiritual analogies from the same gentle command.
- 20:44/q08/p1 God's gentleness to a defiant claimant of divinity suggests still greater gentleness toward servants and worshippers. — Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh is repeatedly quoted, often weeping. Al-Nahrajūrī contrasts God's enemy with a worshipper. Other sayings contrast the one who harms with one harmed for God, or the enemy with God's allies. Al-Faḍl and Yazīd al-Raqqāshī say God endears Himself even to opponents. [15 notes: SULAMI(النهرجوري,قيل,يحيى بن معاذ); MAWARDI-FULL(بعض المتصوفة); JISHUMI(يحيى بن معاذ); BAGHAWI-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ); TABRISI(يحيى بن معاذ); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يحيى بن معاذ); QURTUBI-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ); NASAFI-FULL(يحيى بن معاذ); IBNKATHIR-FULL(يزيد الرقاشي); DURR-FULL(الفضل بن عيسى الرقاشي); BURSEVI(يحيى بن معاذ); ALUSI-FULL(الفضل بن عيسى الرقاشى,يحيى بن معاذ)]
- 20:44/q08/p2 The heart and intellect should gently invite the soul to obey divine command and law. — Al-Qāshānī reads Moses and Aaron as heart and intellect, Pharaoh as soul, and the hoped for softening as obedience. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:44/q08/p3 Gentleness repays Pharaoh's kindness in raising Moses. — Al-Nahrajūrī reports God telling Moses that Pharaoh's early kindness had not otherwise been repaid; al-Māwardī repeats the explanation. [2 notes: SULAMI(النهرجوري); MAWARDI-FULL]

## 20:44/q09 What does لَعَلَّهُۥ mean here?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ordinary hope is assigned to the messengers, the particle is causal or interrogative, or divine speech assures an outcome.
- 20:44/q09/p1 It conveys hope or expectation, voiced from the human messengers' standpoint. — Al-Zajjāj and Sībawayh paraphrase 'go with your hope'; God knows the outcome but the messengers do not. Al-Baḥr and al-Ālūsī prefer the ordinary sense over causal and interrogative alternatives. Al-Burhān notes Arabic hope can even be voiced about an impossible bygone age in Ibn al-Dahhān's poem, and apparent doubt can emphasize a certain regret. [47 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج,سيبويه); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,سيبويه وغيره من كبراء النحويين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(ابن الدهان,المحققون,سيبويه); BIQAI-FULL(سيبويه); ITQAN(البرهان,سيبويه); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); STUDYQURAN(JJ, R); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BURHAN(الخليل وسيبويه)+]
- 20:44/q09/p2 It means 'so that,' expressing the purpose of their address. — Al-Akhfash compares لنتغدى, حتى نتغدى, and لعلك تأخذ أجرك. Al-Farrāʾ, al-Kisāʾī, al-Wāqidī, and Abū Mālik are named for a causal use; the last two except 26:129, which they render 'as if you would live forever.' Al-Burhān objects that causation departs from ordinary function. [13 notes: AKHFASH; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); BURHAN(الفراء); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مالك,الفراء,الواقدي,قتادة); TAB-FULL(آخرون)+; BURHAN(الخليل وسيبويه)-]
- 20:44/q09/p3 It introduces a question: will Pharaoh reflect or fear? — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Ṭabarī, al-Suyūṭī, and al-Ālūsī. Al-Ṭabarī finds this sound and also accepts purpose. Al-Samīn rejects divine questioning as no less problematic than divine hope; al-Ālūsī calls it unlikely. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس)+; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:44/q09/p4 Divine 'perhaps' is certain to be fulfilled. — Al-Warrāq and others say Qurʾānic لعل and عسى point to what occurred, locating Pharaoh's response at drowning, 10:90. Al-Samīn attributes a general certainty rule to Sībawayh; the Burhān report in al-Itqān distinguishes divine certainty from human hope. Al-Ālūsī rejects the inference that this secures Pharaoh's salvation. [5 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر محمد بن عمر الوراق); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق وغيره); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(سيبويه); ITQAN(البرهان); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المتبعين لمن قال بنجاة فرعون)-]
- 20:44/q09/p5 It figuratively depicts God's invitation as hopeful or elicits human hope. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this alongside a direct instruction to hope; al-Ālūsī mentions theologians' figurative sense of a general request. Al-Itqān compares expressions of divine amazement redirected to their human audience, citing 74:27. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN]

## 20:44/q10 Whose words or hope does the tentative clause express?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ · referent]
turns on: The usual reading locates hope in the two messengers; al-Ḥasan assigns the words to Aaron and Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a figurative divine voice.
- 20:44/q10/p1 It gives Moses and Aaron's hope as they act; divine knowledge of Pharaoh's future is unaffected. — Sībawayh's paraphrase is 'go with your hope and expectation.' Prophets lack knowledge of the unseen. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Wāḥidī say hopeful effort improves preaching; al-Bayḍāwī contrasts earnest effort with feigned work. Al-Burhān says possible events are certain to God but uncertain to creatures. [38 notes: ZAJJAJ(سيبويه); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سيبويه); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج,سيبويه وغيره من كبراء النحويين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURHAN(المحققون,سيبويه); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(سيبويه); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); STUDYQURAN(JJ, R); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BURHAN(الخليل وسيبويه)+]
- 20:44/q10/p2 It quotes Aaron's words to Moses. — Al-Ḥasan gives this as the speaker of 'perhaps Pharaoh will remember or fear.' [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 20:44/q10/p3 It figuratively voices God's invitation or request in human terms. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this alternative; al-Ālūsī mentions figurative general request while choosing ordinary hope. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:44/q11 What does the hope clause attach to grammatically?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: The question is whether the clause modifies the command to speak or the earlier command to go.
- 20:44/q11/p1 It is circumstantial to the implied dual subject of 'speak.' — Al-Ālūsī prefers attachment to قولا and says أو keeps both outcomes possible. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:44/q11/p2 It is circumstantial to the dual subject of the earlier 'go.' — Al-Bayḍāwī allows attachment to ذهبا or قولا; al-Ālūsī reports the former but prefers the latter. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 20:44/q12 What does يَتَذَكَّرُ mean?  [يَتَذَكَّرُ · interpretation]
turns on: The glosses vary between intellectual recognition, recollection of lordship, and practical repentance.
- 20:44/q12/p1 Reflect on the reminder and acknowledge the truth, yielding to it. — Al-Zamakhsharī, Abū Ḥayyān, and al-Ālūsī describe reflection leading to fairness. Al-Biqāʿī specifies God's care through Pharaoh's changing life stages. Ibn ʿĀshūr says recognition corrects his false confidence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes it acceptance of the reminder, proof, and faith. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:44/q12/p2 Recall God's lordship and his own servitude. — Al-Ṭabrisī defines the mission's desired recollection this way. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 20:44/q12/p3 Turn back from forbidden conduct or misguidance. — Ibn Kathīr contrasts this with fear as attaining obedience and cites 87:10; the Study Quran reports heeding the warning or turning from misguidance. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(JJ, IK)]

## 20:44/q13 What does يَخْشَىٰ mean?  [يَخْشَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes differ over fear from a possibly true warning, fear of God leading to obedience, and fear of divine power.
- 20:44/q13/p1 Fear that the warning is true and denial may bring punishment or ruin. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ālūsī stress fear despite incomplete certainty. Al-Biqāʿī says it might lead Pharaoh to release Israel. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this cautious compliance. Al-Ālūsī prefers it to a generic account of divine punishment. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:44/q13/p2 Fear God, submit, and become obedient to Him. — Al-Ṭabrisī connects fear with the threatened punishment; al-Baghawī says fear and submission to Islam. Ibn Kathīr defines fear as attaining obedience, citing 87:10. The Study Quran says fear may return Pharaoh to God. Al-Burṣawī links reverent fear especially with the learned, citing 35:28. [6 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(JJ, IK); BURSEVI]
- 20:44/q13/p3 Become wary of God's power and punishment. — Al-Ālūsī reports this explanation but favors fear that the messengers' warning may be true. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 20:44/q14 Why is remembering mentioned before fearing?  [يَتَذَكَّرُ يَخْشَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The disagreement concerns whether fear is the lesser response after recognition is offered, or a stage leading toward it.
- 20:44/q14/p1 Recognition and wholehearted acceptance come first; fear of a possibly true warning is a lesser fallback. — Al-Bayḍāwī says remembrance follows recognized truth, fear suspected truth. Rāzī ranks persistent truth, suspension, and persistent falsehood and says either improvement beats Pharaoh's fixed rejection. Al-Ālūsī calls remembrance the response of the grounded. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:44/q14/p2 Fear can precede full acceptance, so either may bring Pharaoh toward faith. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says fear comes before acceptance of the reminder, making the pair 'believe or approach belief and answer them.' [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:44/q15 What does أَوْ do between the two outcomes?  [أَوْ · grammar]
turns on: Some read the conjunction as additive; others retain two possible outcomes, while the lexical notes also discuss ordinary uncertainty.
- 20:44/q15/p1 It is additive, equivalent to 'and.' — Al-Suddī, exegetes cited by Ibn Qutayba and Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Dāmghānī, al-Burhān, and al-Itqān take it this way. Examples include 77:6, 80:3, 20:113, a Jarīr verse and a Tawba verse. Ibn al-Jawzī also reports al-Farrāʾ's poetic 'rather' and ordinary choice, but those are general functions of أو, not asserted for this clause. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(المفسرون); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,الفراء,توبة,جرير); BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 20:44/q15/p2 It permits either remembrance or fear as a sought outcome, without excluding both. — Al-Burhān says either outcome may be sought. Al-Irshād, quoted by al-Burṣawī, says أو excludes neither. Al-Ālūsī likewise says the particle prevents excluding either. [3 notes: BURHAN; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:44/q15/p3 It can express uncertainty between alternatives. — Ibn Qutayba illustrates with seeing ʿAbd Allāh or Muḥammad; Ibn al-Jawzī gives Zayd or ʿAmr and also an ordinary choice between a coin and silver value. Al-Dāmghānī's excerpt begins a separate awwal entry on first unbelief and belief but gives no local application. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; DAMGHANI]

## 20:44/q16 Who might remember or fear?  [لَعَلَّهُۥ · referent]
turns on: The pronoun's immediate target is Pharaoh, while reported readings transfer the hoped for response to witnesses.
- 20:44/q16/p1 Pharaoh himself. — This is the surface referent throughout the address; Muqātil and others explain the hope as his chance to heed. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 20:44/q16/p2 A witness or others who hear and see God's gentleness. — Al-Ḥusayn ibn al-Faḍl says a witness might learn from kindness to the claimant of divinity; al-Nasafī reports 'a reminder may remember or a fearful person may fear,' adding that many did. Al-Tustarī names magicians and others whom God knows He will guide. [3 notes: TUSTARI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسين بن الفضل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل)]

## 20:44/q17 Did Pharaoh remember or fear, and when?  [يَتَذَكَّرُ يَخْشَىٰ · reports]
turns on: Reports place a response at drowning, before Hāmān's counsel, or in the Nile episode; another report emphasizes nonbelief.
- 20:44/q17/p1 He remembered, feared, and professed faith only while drowning, too late to benefit him. — Al-Warrāq, Muʿādh, Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, and al-Burṣawī connect the response to 10:90. Al-Ālūsī rejects applying this late response to the clause, and rejects a salvation argument built from it. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر محمد بن عمر الوراق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(خالد بن معدان عن معاذ); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر الوراق وغيره); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المتبعين لمن قال بنجاة فرعون)-]
- 20:44/q17/p2 He remembered and feared earlier, but Hāmān diverted him from following Moses. — Al-Nasafī says Pharaoh wanted to follow Moses but made no decision without Hāmān. Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabrisī say he liked the offered youth, rule, pleasures, and Paradise until Hāmān shamed him as a deity becoming a subject. Qurṭubī adds that his believing wife urged faith, Hāmān offered to restore his youth and dyed his beard black, reportedly first to do so; Abū Ḥayyān names Āsiyah. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي); TABRISI(قيل,هامان); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 20:44/q17/p3 He prostrated when the Nile receded and might have recalled God's forbearance when it followed his horse. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī transmit this Nile episode; Abū Ḥayyān frames the hoped for remembrance and fear around divine generosity and punishment. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:44/q17/p4 He was divinely fated not to believe. — Kaʿb's Torah report says God commanded gentle speech while announcing He would harden Pharaoh's heart. The theologians and commentators also discuss God's advance knowledge of nonbelief, without making it a narrative of prior remembrance. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(كعب); RAZI-FULL(كعب)]

## 20:44/q18 How many years were included in the reported promise to Pharaoh?  [قَوْلًۭا لَّيِّنًۭا · reports]
turns on: The two reports give different numbers and differ over whether years are a matching lifespan or an addition.
- 20:44/q18/p1 A lifespan of 409 years, matching Pharaoh's reported age. — ʿAmr ibn Dīnār says Pharaoh lived 409 years and Moses promised him that lifespan and Paradise if he obeyed. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(عمرو بن دينار)]
- 20:44/q18/p2 Four hundred extra years. — Qurṭubī reports this with youth, kingdom until death, and Paradise. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 20:44/q19 Why send messengers when God knew Pharaoh would not believe?  [* · theology]
turns on: The accounts turn on proof, decree and command, divine knowledge, and whether people originate their acts.
- 20:44/q19/p1 Their mission establishes proof, removes excuse, and manifests the result before punishment. — Al-Zajjāj says divine knowledge alone cannot establish proof for humans. Al-Zamakhsharī cites 20:134; Abū Ḥayyān cites 17:15. Al-Burṣawī says message precedes punishment, while al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says the mission establishes the messengers and makes Pharaoh's rejection manifest, citing 8:42. [10 notes: TUSTARI; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
- 20:44/q19/p2 The reason is a mystery before which reason should submit. — Rāzī asks why exhorting one known not to believe increases his liability, comparing it to handing a doomed person a knife. Al-Ālūsī endorses the Imam's submission. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام)]
- 20:44/q19/p3 A prescriptive command can differ from God's decree; fixed knowledge reveals capacities and outcomes. — Al-Burṣawī reports a distinction between command and decree, says a sinner can bear a duty without readiness to perform it, and treats the command as displaying felicity and misery. He quotes Ḥāfiẓ on surrender to a divinely directed course. [4 notes: BURSEVI(الحافظ,بعض أرباب الحقيقة)]
- 20:44/q19/p4 Divine foreknowledge of Pharaoh's freely chosen disbelief does not make belief impossible. — Al-Baḥr al-ʿUlūm says knowledge follows the known act as it is; al-Burṣawī argues the verse shows human capacity affects acts, against al-Ashʿarī's denial of such effect. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); BURSEVI(الأشعري)-]
- 20:44/q19/p5 God intended Pharaoh and his people to remember, and human acts originate in the servant. — Jishumī uses لعل against determinists and argues that if God created each act, gentleness and other deeds would be equivalent; the real issue would become God's creating faith. [2 notes: JISHUMI]

## 20:44/q20 What surrounding reports or scriptural links illuminate the mission?  [* · reports]
turns on: These are distinct reports about the mission rather than rival explanations of a single event.
- 20:44/q20/p1 Aaron met Moses on the way to Egypt after receiving inspiration. — Al-Baghawī says Aaron was in Egypt, God ordered Moses toward him, inspired Aaron to meet him, and they met after a day's journey. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 20:44/q20/p2 The Torah reportedly commands gentle speech even though God will harden Pharaoh's heart. — Kaʿb swears to this Torah wording as cited by Ibn al-Jawzī and Rāzī. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(كعب); RAZI-FULL(كعب)]
- 20:44/q20/p3 Poetry depicts Moses and Aaron questioning Pharaoh about the Creator of heaven and earthly life. — Ibn Kathīr cites lines ascribed to Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl, alternatively to Umayya ibn Abī al-Ṣalt by Ibn Isḥāq. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(زيد بن عمرو بن نفيل، ويروى لأمية بن أبي الصلت)]
- 20:44/q20/p4 Moses and Aaron should first give Pharaoh warning and a chance to respond. — Al-Ḥasan says they should not demand his destruction before giving him an excuse and warning. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري)]
