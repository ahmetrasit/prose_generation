# 35:15: 25 questions

## 35:15/q01 Who is addressed as 'people'?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'people' names all humanity, the polytheists addressed in context, or creatures included through rhetorical extension.
- 35:15/q01/p1 Humanity generally is addressed. — Islahi says the address in verses 15–17 is to everyone. Al-Alusi allows that the address may challenge people displaying strength and stubbornness; al-Tabataba'i says the judgment reaches all creatures through the common cause of dependence, even if the immediate audience is narrower. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 35:15/q01/p2 The immediate addressees are Meccan unbelievers or polytheists in the preceding discourse. — Muqatil identifies Meccan unbelievers, and Ibn Abbas, reported by al-Wahidi, names the people of Mecca. Ibn Ashur identifies polytheists and points to the preceding passage, including 35:13. Al-Tibi, reported approvingly by al-Alusi, takes 'people' as those addressed in 35:13, while reading poverty generically. [4 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(العلامة الطيبي)+]
- 35:15/q01/p3 The address includes human beings and other creatures by rhetorical inclusion. — The author of al-Fara'id, as reported by al-Alusi, argues that the present audience can stand for the absent and the superior for other beings; al-Alusi calls this very remote. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الفرائد)-]

## 35:15/q02 Can this vocative introduce a statement without a following command?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ · grammar]
- 35:15/q02/p1 Yes; a vocative can introduce a declarative statement. — Al-Suyuti's Itqan cites this declaration of human need alongside 43:68, 66:1, and 40:41 as vocatives without following commands. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 35:15/q03 What does faqir mean and how is it formed?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ · meaning]
- 35:15/q03/p1 A faqir is one in need, especially in severe need. — Al-Baghawi defines it by need. Bursevi calls the form intensive and derives it from breaking a vertebra; Taj al-Masadir gives the daraba pattern, while al-Qamus gives the karuma pattern. Al-Raghib, reported by Bursevi, records iftaqara, muftaqir, and faqir, with faqara rare despite analogy. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب)]

## 35:15/q04 How does the definite 'the poor' shape the description of people?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether definiteness chiefly conveys intensified class identity, recalls shared knowledge, or effects a restriction relative to God.
- 35:15/q04/p1 It portrays people as the very class of the needy and intensifies their weakness and need. — Al-Zamakhshari says an indefinite predicate would merely make them some needy people; he cites 4:28 and 30:54 on human weakness. Al-Qurtubi transmits him. Al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi, Biqa'i, Alusi, and Elmalili stress the encompassing or unusually intense need; Bursevi and Alusi make other creatures' need negligible by comparison. Alusi adds that jinn need less food and clothing and cites 4:28; Biqa'i adds that thought, knowledge, and intellect multiply human needs. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 35:15/q04/p2 Its definiteness recalls an already known fact, so the statement reminds rather than first informs. — Al-Razi says a predicate is ordinarily indefinite, but a definite one suits a proposition whose two terms are known; everyone already knows human need. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 35:15/q04/p3 Together with the definite subject, it restricts poverty to the addressees relative to God. — Ibn Ashur calls it a restrictive reversal that humbles the audience, rejecting a literal absolute restriction or an asserted hyperbole as needless; he cites 39:7. Elmalili says the restriction indicates that religion and worship answer human need. Al-Tabataba'i says the deniers' reliance on idols is refuted: poverty belongs to people and richness to God; possession and lack cannot both be absent, so the wording is exclusive. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 35:15/q05 How may the hamzas at 'the poor, to' be pronounced?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ إِلَى · readings]
turns on: Which of the two adjacent hamzas is eased or elided, or whether both are fully pronounced; the vowel-class note classifies the same junction.
- 35:15/q05/p1 Ease the second hamza; al-Khalil considers this best. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi report al-Khalil's preference at the meeting of the two hamzas. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل)+]
- 35:15/q05/p2 Ease the first hamza only. — Listed as permitted by al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل)]
- 35:15/q05/p3 Drop the first hamza. — Al-Nahhas lists this among the permitted ways; al-Qurtubi's summary does not mention it. [1 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل)]
- 35:15/q05/p4 Ease both hamzas. — Listed as permitted by al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل)]
- 35:15/q05/p5 Pronounce both hamzas fully. — Listed as permitted by al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الخليل); QURTUBI-FULL(الخليل)]
- 35:15/q05/p6 This is an agreed instance of the fifth vowel class, damma followed by kasra. — Ibn al-Jazari's Nashr counts 22 agreed occurrences in this class, including this phrase and 2:282; six others are disputed, including the Maryam address to Zakariyya and five addresses to the Prophet attributed to Nafi. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 35:15/q06 In what ways are people poor toward God?  [أَنتُمُ ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the verse speaks of creaturely dependence in every condition or of a distinctive human need for God's essence and attributes.
- 35:15/q06/p1 Their existence, preservation, every condition, and all worldly and otherworldly needs depend on Him. — Yahya and al-Tabari state the general dependence; al-Tabari adds that He meets needs. Ibn Atiyya says not even an instant or a small matter is independent of Him. Al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, Abu Hayyan, Nasafi's Dhu al-Nun, Sulami's Dhu al-Nun and Junayd, Qushayri, and Study Quran stress existence, every breath or movement, and sustenance. Al-Wahidi names provision and forgiveness; Bursevi adds protection and forgiveness in the hereafter. Al-Baydawi and Alusi include people's selves and all that befalls them; Elmalili cites weakness and the trust. Al-Kashifi reads contingent essences' need for an agent; al-Tabataba'i grounds universal dependence in God's creating and governing. Al-Razi says God meets worldly and believers' future needs; Islahi likewise includes both worlds. [29 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; TUSTARI; SULAMI(الجنيد,ذو النون); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(ذو النون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 35:15/q06/p2 Human beings uniquely need God's own essence and attributes, beyond need for His acts. — Some Sufi authorities reported by Bursevi distinguish other creatures, which need the divine treasury or acts, from human lovers, who need the ruler's own person and attributes; the analogy is a ruler's subjects versus lovers. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 35:15/q07 What kinds of poverty does the verse allow one to distinguish?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · inward]
turns on: Whether poverty is classified by created nature and spiritual detachment, by the object of need, or by awareness of one's need.
- 35:15/q07/p1 There is universal poverty of created nature and spiritual poverty through detachment. — Al-Qushayri distinguishes dependence on the Creator for origination, sustenance, and preservation from a spiritual quality: ordinary people detach from wealth and the elect from impediments. Biqa'i transmits this and adds detachment of the heart from effects as praiseworthy poverty. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري)]
- 35:15/q07/p2 Need of God is intrinsic, while need for other things is accidental or derivative. — Study Quran says every felt need ultimately points to God, while needed things both veil and reveal Him. Al-Qushayri contrasts need directed to God with need for something from Him: dependence on the thing resembles sufficiency through it, while need of God becomes richness through God. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; STUDYQURAN]
- 35:15/q07/p3 Poverty is blameworthy when one's need of God is hidden and praiseworthy when it is made visible. — Al-Qushayri reports this distinction according to whether God veils or discloses the places of need. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 35:15/q08 How is genuine inward poverty toward God realized?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · inward]
turns on: Whether realization is described by exclusive inward dependence and detachment or by recognizing divine favor as the entrance to protected poverty.
- 35:15/q08/p1 Depend inwardly on God alone, relinquish other attachments and self-management, and live in humble servanthood. — Tustari and Sahl as transmitted by Sulami, Nasafi, and Study Quran call for inward need and severance from all else; servanthood is abasement, humility, and submission. Tustari and Sulami's Sahl require primordial and present need and the death of self-management, otherwise the claim is false. Qushayri describes detachment from causes, an exclusively Godward heart, spiritual completeness without self-claim, and ease without resistance. Sulami's Junayd contrasts divine knowledge, power, dominion, and will with the servant's incapacity and need. Study Quran says recognition of divine fullness rests on recognizing one's complete poverty. [12 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(الجنيد,سهل); QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL(سهل); STUDYQURAN(al-Tustarī)]
- 35:15/q08/p2 Recognizing favor opens the protected abode of poverty and need. — Al-Juzjani, reported by Sulami, calls poverty and need an abode of protection and recognizing divine favor its gate. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجوزجاني)]

## 35:15/q09 What outward conduct marks the truthful poor person?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ · inward]
turns on: Which conduct is used as the sign: dealings with goods and requests, freedom from possession, or response to affliction.
- 35:15/q09/p1 One neither asks nor withholds, and does not refuse or store up goods. — Tustari says neither asks, refuses, nor withholds; Sa'd, reported by Sulami, has neither asks, stores, nor withholds. Sahl, reported by Nasafi, names asking no one as a sign of pure servitude; the middle act varies between reports. [3 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سعد); NASAFI-FULL(سهل)]
- 35:15/q09/p2 One takes little, prefers less to more, and does not possess or act for oneself. — Abu Sa'id al-Kharraz defines true poverty by choosing less even when in need. Ruwaym describes possessing nothing and entering things for another's sake. Al-Qushayri says no thing possesses the sincere poor person, even while such a person may in another sense own everything. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو سعيد الخراز,رويم); QUSHAYRI]
- 35:15/q09/p3 One conceals affliction, avoids complaint, and shows gratitude while broken. — Al-Khawwas names leaving complaint and hiding affliction; al-Qushayri reports gratitude at the height of brokenness and gratitude without complaint in affliction. [3 notes: SULAMI(الخواص); QUSHAYRI]

## 35:15/q10 What spiritual value can poverty and its affliction have?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ · inward]
turns on: Whether the benefit is humility and honor, reward beneath affliction, or a hidden divine trust.
- 35:15/q10/p1 Poverty is honorable and brings humility, while wealth can foster pride. — Yahya ibn Mu'adh, reported by Sulami and Nasafi, prefers returning humbly to God over arriving with many deeds. Qushayri says humility ennobles poverty and imagined self-sufficiency abases a servant. Al-Shibli, reported by both Sulami and Nasafi, calls every affliction of poverty an honor. Bishr ibn al-Harith calls it a hidden treasure for believers, comparable to martyrdom and bestowed on those God loves. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(الشبلي,بشر بن الحارث); NASAFI-FULL(الشبلى); NASAFI-FULL(يحيى)+; SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ)+]
- 35:15/q10/p2 Poverty's outward affliction conceals inward blessing and promised reward. — Amr al-Makki says to manifest the inner blessing and conceal the visible trial because reward is promised. Ibrahim ibn Adham says patience with poverty led him into it. [2 notes: SULAMI(إبراهيم بن أدهم,عمرو المكي)]
- 35:15/q10/p3 Poverty is a divine secret entrusted only to those who conceal it. — Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, reported by Sulami, explains that God suffices and enriches His friends and does not entrust this secret to one who discloses it. [1 notes: SULAMI(محمد بن عبد الوهاب)]

## 35:15/q11 How does a servant's poverty relate to richness through God?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · inward]
turns on: Whether the relationship is described as need leading to God-given richness or as two mutually completing states.
- 35:15/q11/p1 Acknowledged need connects one to God's wealth; claiming self-sufficiency veils one from Him. — Tustari and Sahl as reported by Sulami, Nasafi, and Study Quran state the veil and connection. Al-Wasiti, reported by Sulami and Nasafi, says sufficiency or honor through God leaves one neither needy nor abased. Al-Husayn says greater dependence brings greater God-given richness. Al-Wasiti also contrasts concealing the Real's due, which makes one poorest, with laying it bare, which makes one truly rich. Qushayri says absence of a self-sufficiency claim brings God's enriching gift beyond sufficiency; Study Quran says full realization of divine richness requires awareness of one's poverty. [12 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(الحسين,الواسطي,سهل); QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL(الحسين,الواسطى,سهل); STUDYQURAN(al-Tustarī)]
- 35:15/q11/p2 Sound need and richness through God complete each other; both belong to servanthood. — Al-Junayd, reported by Sulami, says neither state is complete without the other and both remain qualities of servanthood. [2 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]

## 35:15/q12 What marks God's friends in their dependence on Him?  [ٱلْفُقَرَآءُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · inward]
- 35:15/q12/p1 They trust God, need Him, and return to Him in everything. — Tustari reports this from Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz. Nasafi reports the same triad anonymously as three marks of the saints. [2 notes: TUSTARI(عمر بن عبد العزيز); NASAFI-FULL(قيل)]

## 35:15/q13 Why does the verse explicitly say 'to God'?  [إِلَى ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 35:15/q13/p1 Dependence belongs to God alone, so worship and reliance must be directed to Him alone. — Al-Razi says the added phrase excludes worship of anything on which people do not depend. Elmalili says only God can satisfy human need and no other deity merits worship; he points to 35:13, where other supposed owners possess not even a qitmir. Ibn Atiyya says one can dispense with everyone else through God. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 35:15/q14 What is the syntactic role of 'He'?  [هُوَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether huwa is syntactically extra or begins a new nominal clause.
- 35:15/q14/p1 It is an extra or separating pronoun without a syntactic position. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi give this analysis as a possibility. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 35:15/q14/p2 It is the nominative subject of a nominal sentence. — Al-Nahhas and al-Qurtubi give this as the other possible analysis. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 35:15/q15 What does God's being 'the Rich' affirm?  [ٱلْغَنِىُّ · theology]
turns on: Whether independence is stated absolutely or restricted to the immediate addressees and their worship.
- 35:15/q15/p1 God alone is absolutely self-sufficient, needing no creature, helper, worship, or service. — Muqatil, al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, Biqa'i, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalili, Islahi, Kuran Yolu, and Study Quran variously stress freedom from all beings and their worship. Al-Tabataba'i grounds it in creation and governance and says God can dispense with or replace people. Bursevi contrasts rulers needing servants and merchants needing carriers; al-As'ila al-Muqhima says creating, sustaining, or charging creatures changes no divine need. Islahi says God's relation to them is mercy and favor, and cites a Persian couplet that creation displays generosity rather than divine benefit. Al-Alusi prefers this broader sense over al-Tibi's restricted formulation. [30 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(الجنيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj); ALUSI-FULL(العلامة الطيبي)+]
- 35:15/q15/p2 He is independent specifically of the addressees and their worship, since other servants praise Him. — Al-Tibi's narrower formulation is reported by al-Alusi, who explicitly prefers the broader sense of complete independence. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(العلامة الطيبي)-]

## 35:15/q16 Does divine wisdom require God to charge people to know and thank Him?  [ٱلْغَنِىُّ · theology]
turns on: Whether obligation or creation supplies any benefit or protection to God, which Bursevi denies.
- 35:15/q16/p1 No; creation and command serve no divine benefit or protection from harm. — Bursevi says making creation serve God's benefit or avert harm contradicts His independence; he compares a protective rationale to the Magian claim that angels shield God from Satan. Al-As'ila al-Muqhima says creating or not creating and commanding or not commanding are alike with respect to divine independence. [3 notes: BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة)]
- 35:15/q16/p2 The Mu'tazili claim makes divine wisdom require charging people to know and thank God. — Bursevi reports and rejects this claim because it would make creation serve a benefit or avert harm. [0 notes: BURSEVI-]
- 35:15/q16/p3 The Magian claim says angels were created to protect God from Satan. — Bursevi cites and rejects it as an analogy for the notion that God created beings to protect Himself. [0 notes: BURSEVI-]

## 35:15/q17 What does 'the Praiseworthy' mean?  [ٱلْحَمِيدُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether praise is explained through received favors, all divine acts and their wisdom, or an intrinsic quality independent of actual praisers.
- 35:15/q17/p1 God is praised and deserves thanks for His favors and beneficence. — Yahya says creatures praise God and are charged to do so. Al-Tabari says all blessings are His and praise is due in every circumstance, citing 14:1. Al-Baghawi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Baydawi, Nasafi, Bursevi, Alusi, Elmalili, and Kuran Yolu connect praise to beneficence; Nasafi says every tongue praises Him. Alusi says hamid originally means 'praised' and cites Ka'b al-Ghanawi's line about a forbearing man respected by an enemy. Bursevi's al-Kashifi calls manifestation of divine names through beings a favor meriting praise. Study Quran's al-Maybudi says only those fully aware of their poverty truly praise Him. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Maybudī)]
- 35:15/q17/p2 God is worthy of praise in every act, word, decree, and law, including giving and withholding. — Al-Tabrisi says all divine acts merit praise. Ibn Kathir extends it to everything God says, decrees, and legislates. Biqa'i says all creation is made fittingly and at its appointed measure; blame comes from isolating parts. His report from al-Harali defines praise as the whole's perfection when all parts reach their ends, and al-Ghazali's parallel describes a human praiseworthy in all habits, character, and deeds. Biqa'i adds that both giving and withholding follow subtle wisdom without self-interest. [6 notes: TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي,الغزالي)]
- 35:15/q17/p3 God's praiseworthiness is intrinsic and remains whether or not anyone praises Him. — Al-Wahidi says He is praiseworthy with or without creatures. Ibn Atiyya calls Him absolutely praiseworthy. Study Quran makes praise intrinsic to the Divine Nature; Islahi calls Him praiseworthy in His attributes. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 35:15/q18 Why pair 'the Rich' with 'the Praiseworthy'?  [ٱلْغَنِىُّ ٱلْحَمِيدُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the added epithet chiefly explains the benefit of divine wealth or makes explicit the duty of grateful worship despite divine independence.
- 35:15/q18/p1 Divine wealth benefits the needy because God gives generously, so recipients praise Him. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Qurtubi say wealth alone benefits no one without generosity. Abu Hayyan, Nasafi, Alusi, and Ibn Ashur apply the same pairing to God. Al-Qushayri says He gives until praised and that His declaration of human poverty signals generosity, since boasting or rivalry are unworthy divine motives. Biqa'i contrasts created wealth's limits with God's unrestricted gifts, cites 17:20 for gifts not barred, and recalls visible and hidden blessings. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الزمخشري); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 35:15/q18/p2 Praiseworthy adds that independence does not excuse failure to worship: His favors call for gratitude and worship. — Al-Razi says the added epithet makes favors a ground for thanks and worship. Ibn Ashur says worshippers praise and answer God's call, citing 39:7; he also notes the balance between people's need 'to God' and God's praise. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 35:15/q19 Whose need does the call to worship serve?  [* · interpretation]
- 35:15/q19/p1 Worship and accepting the call benefit people; God has no need of their response. — Al-Tabari says people should worship and seek God's pleasure because He meets their needs. Al-Razi answers disbelievers who inferred divine need from repeated commands: the command is human care, while needy people fail to answer God's call. Elmalili says religion and worship meet human need; Kuran Yolu calls worship an innate, irrepressible human inclination. Islahi says acceptance brings success in both worlds and refusal ruin, and that the Messenger's persistent efforts do not show divine need. Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, and others deny need of human worship. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 35:15/q20 What occasion is reported for this verse?  [* · reports]
- 35:15/q20/p1 After the Prophet repeatedly prayed while unbelievers persisted in rejection, they asked whether God needed their worship; the verse answered them. — Al-Alusi reports this occasion but says it does not restrict the verse to that circumstance. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 35:15/q21 Why remind people of poverty before declaring divine independence?  [* · rhetoric]
- 35:15/q21/p1 It humbles an arrogant audience and draws out an implication of dependence they know but neglect. — Ibn Ashur says proofs and reminders have failed and may feed arrogance; placing human need first is more humbling than merely asserting God's independence and may awaken sound minds veiled by misguidance. Some persistent unbelievers instead meet life with bewilderment and bitterness, unlike the praise of ancestors and idols formerly heard. Nasafi says naming poverty directs them toward dependence rather than demeaning them. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 35:15/q22 How is the eloquence of this verse assessed?  [* · rhetoric]
- 35:15/q22/p1 Its eloquence, like that of the Qur'an as a whole, cannot be fully expressed. — Ibn Atiyya says some passages' eloquence appears more clearly to readers because of their own limitations. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 35:15/q23 How does the verse fit the surrounding passage and surah?  [* · links]
turns on: These are different links: the immediate warning, an earlier national example, and a gloss on the next verse.
- 35:15/q23/p1 It intensifies the prior warning: only God creates and governs, idols own nothing, and He can replace those who reject Him. — Al-Tabataba'i connects the verse to proofs of God's creation and governance, false gods' lack of ownership, and the following threat of replacement as recompense. Ibn Ashur points to the preceding polytheists and says Islam will find other adherents. Islahi says verses 15–17 intensify the earlier admonition. Study Quran describes the surah's focus on the sole Creator and Sustainer against idol claims. Elmalili invokes 35:13 and its qitmir image. [7 notes: TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ELMALILI]
- 35:15/q23/p2 Sheba earlier exemplifies a nation that answered favor with arrogance and lost its blessings. — Islahi says Sheba fulfilled Satan's prediction, suffered exemplary punishment and deprivation, and became a tale of the ancients. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 35:15/q23/p3 In the following verse, 'bi-aziz' means beyond God's power or impossible for Him. — Al-Zamakhshari glosses the following verse's wording this way. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 35:15/q24 How does receiving charity bear on the poor person's role?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The report ranks giver and receiver; the author's explanation identifies the receiver's necessary role in giving.
- 35:15/q24/p1 The giving hand is better than the receiving hand. — Burhan reports the Prophet defining the upper hand as the giver and the lower as the receiver, with the loan verse 2:245 offered as support. [1 notes: BURHAN(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 35:15/q24/p2 The receiving hand enables the giver's charity and purification. — Burhan says charity depends on a recipient accepting the due owed to God; without receiving, a wealthy person could not give. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 35:15/q25 How does Qur'anic poverty differ from bu's and ba'sa?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether al-Raghib's proposed lexical associations fit the Qur'anic distribution of ba'sa, ba's, and faqr.
- 35:15/q25/p1 Faqr is expressly contrasted with divine wealth; Qur'anic ba'sa denotes adversity, and ba's denotes war or force. — Bint al-Shati cites 3:181, 24:32, 35:15, and 47:38 for faqr versus wealth, 2:177, 2:214, and 3:140 for ba'sa with adversity, and 6:65 and 4:84 for ba's in fighting, force, and dominance. [3 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)]
- 35:15/q25/p2 Al-Raghib associates bu's especially with poverty and war, and ba's or ba'sa with attack. — Bint al-Shati reports al-Raghib's lexical distinction and objects that the cited Qur'anic usages pair ba'sa with adversity and explicitly oppose faqr to wealth. [0 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب)-]
