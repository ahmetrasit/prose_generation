# 26:107: 6 questions

## 26:107/q01 Why does Noah say إِنِّى emphatically?  [إِنِّى · rhetoric]
- 26:107/q01/p1 The emphasis anticipates a challenge to Noah's trustworthiness. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Noah's earlier record of honesty supports his message and indirectly rebukes Quraysh for denying Muhammad after calling him trustworthy. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:107/q02 What does Noah's being a messenger to them imply about his mission and relationship to them?  [لَكُمْ رَسُولٌ · interpretation]
- 26:107/q02/p1 God sent Noah to his people with a message he was obliged to convey. — Al-Ṭabarī calls him God's messenger entrusted with revelation for his people and cites 7:68. Al-Wāḥidī reports from Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil that God entrusted Noah with the message and sent him to them. Al-Biqāʿī says a messenger from their Creator has no option but to convey his command. Al-Bursawī says his apostleship is from God. Ibn Kathīr says Noah calls himself God's messenger to them. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he conveys only what God commands. Elmalılı says Noah tells them he was sent to them. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 26:107/q02/p2 Noah is a messenger sent for his people's benefit. — Al-Ālūsī explicitly describes his mission as for their benefit. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:107/q02/p3 Noah shares his people's pain and joy and wants them to avoid God's anger through monotheism. — Al-Biqāʿī grounds Noah's concern for their protection in his solidarity with them. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:107/q03 In what sense is Noah أَمِينٌۭ?  [أَمِينٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the trust concerns faithful delivery, a reputation already known to the people, or God's entrusting the community to Noah.
- 26:107/q03/p1 He is trustworthy in conveying God's revelation and message faithfully. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī's report from Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil describe trust between the people and their Lord; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī specify delivery or revelation. Al-Ṭabarī cites 7:68. Al-Jishumī glosses amīn as trustworthy, the opposite of khāʾin, and applies it to conveying a messenger's message. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report this as one of two senses. Ibn Kathīr says Noah neither adds nor omits anything. Al-Biqāʿī says his long familiarity with his people rules out deceit or treachery, so he must deliver the entire message. Al-Bursawī reasons that someone trustworthy in worldly matters is trustworthy in revelation. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he conveys only God's commands. Iṣlāḥī says he neither fabricates nor falsely claims prophethood. Elmalılı states his trustworthiness without narrowing its field; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls him their trustworthy messenger while explaining his exhortation. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 26:107/q03/p2 His people already knew him as honest and trustworthy before his mission. — Al-Wāḥidī reports this from al-Kalbī. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī's alternate view, al-Nasafī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and the Study Quran compare Noah's reputation with Muhammad's among Quraysh; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes Muhammad was called al-Amīn. Al-Rāzī says that prior knowledge should stop accusations after Noah's call. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī report prior reliability as one of two senses. Al-Biqāʿī appeals to long familiarity as evidence against deceit and treachery; al-Bursawī infers reliability in revelation from worldly reliability. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites a poetic line by al-Nābigha presenting Noah as one who does not betray trust. The Study Quran says his lifelong truthfulness gave his people no reason to mistrust him. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة); STUDYQURAN(Q, R); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 26:107/q03/p3 God entrusted Noah with the community to which he was sent. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as a possible sense of amīn. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:107/q04 How does Noah's claim to be a trustworthy messenger support his appeal?  [رَسُولٌ أَمِينٌۭ · interpretation]
- 26:107/q04/p1 His established honesty warrants believing his message and heeding his rebuke or exhortation. — Al-Rāzī says the people knew Noah's honesty before his call and should not accuse him now, just as Quraysh knew Muhammad. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they persisted in polytheism despite Noah's prohibition and that his trusted status explains the rebuke or exhortation; his earlier honesty supports the truth of his message and indirectly rebukes Quraysh. Iṣlāḥī says the description excludes fabrication and a false claim to be God's messenger. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:107/q04/p2 Because he conveys only God's command, they should fear God and obey Noah; obeying him is obeying God. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly draws this consequence from Noah's faithful conveyance. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 26:107/q04/p3 His shared experience with his people grounds his concern that they embrace monotheism and escape God's anger. — Al-Biqāʿī links Noah's sharing their pain and joy to his protective appeal. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:107/q05 What happened to Noah as he preached to his people?  [* · reports]
- 26:107/q05/p1 People strangled him until he fainted; he recovered and prayed for their forgiveness, then complained to God after generations of worsening defiance. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from ʿUbayd ibn ʿUmayr al-Laythī. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عبيد بن عمير الليثي)]

## 26:107/q06 How does Noah's account connect with the other prophet narratives in the sūra?  [* · links]
- 26:107/q06/p1 After Moses and Abraham, the account turns to Noah, the first of the foremost prophets; most of his people disbelieved, so God drowned them and saved Noah and his believers. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives this sequence and outcome. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 26:107/q06/p2 Noah, Moses, and Abraham share the same essential call, and their peoples respond to true religion in comparable ways. — Kur'an Yolu says the three narrative groups deliver the same message and mentions 11:25 and 11:49. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
