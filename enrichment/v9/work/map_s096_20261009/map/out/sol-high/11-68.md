# 11:68: 20 questions

## 11:68/q01 What does 'as though they had not lived there' mean?  [كَأَن لَّمْ يَغْنَوْا۟ فِيهَآ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ghaniya describes residence itself or the ease and prosperity once enjoyed there.
- 11:68/q01/p1 They seemed never to have dwelt, lived, or existed there; destruction effaced their former presence. — Muqatil extends the sense to never living in this world. Al-Tabari and Ibn Abbas compare 7:92; Ibn Abbas adduces Labid's غنيت, also cited by al-Suyuti. Jishumi derives dwellings (maghani) from sufficiency; Ibn Atiyya adds living in ease. Al-Tabrisi cites al-Nabigha and notes senses of wealth and song. Al-Razi and Bursevi give maghna as residence; Tabatabai takes the pronoun as their dwellings. Al-Tabrisi says their traces vanished except disgraced bodies; Biqai links the image to the sudden stillness of death. Bint al-Shati notes the same expression for Madyan at 7:92 and 11:95 and a parallel at 10:24; Study Quran relates the morning after punishment to 7:77-78 and 11:94-95. Al-Wahidi refers to his discussion at 7:92. [31 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(أبو مالك,ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,لبيد بن ربيعة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; STUDYQURAN]
- 11:68/q01/p2 They seemed never to have enjoyed the place's prosperity or the good life there. — Qatada says they had not enjoyed or prospered there; al-Mawardi records this as an alternative. Biqai notes that ghana can suggest prosperity and song, and Ibn Atiyya describes dwelling in ease. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 11:68/q02 How does the clause 'as though they had not lived there' function grammatically?  [كَأَن لَّمْ يَغْنَوْا۟ فِيهَآ · grammar]
- 11:68/q02/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing the stricken people after they became motionless corpses. — Bursevi relates it to their crouched state and says the comparison suggests they had never existed or stayed anywhere; al-Alusi reports the same parsing after they became prostrate corpses. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:68/q03 What force does the first 'alā' have?  [أَلَآ · rhetoric]
- 11:68/q03/p1 It calls attention to an important declaration and makes the affirmation emphatic. — Biqai says it alerts a heedless listener. Al-Rummani analyzes interrogative alif plus lā as affirmation through negation; Biqai prefers an analysis in which interrogative denial negates lā's negation, emphatically affirming what follows. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الرماني); BIQAI-FULL+]

## 11:68/q04 How is the first 'Thamūd' read and construed?  [ثَمُودَا۟ · readings]
turns on: Whether the name denotes a masculine people or ancestor or a feminine tribe, and which transmitted reader and location are in view; one attribution conflicts with the other reader lists.
- 11:68/q04/p1 It is declined with tanwīn, as the name of a people, their ancestor, or an Arabic masculine name. — Al-Farra connects accusative declension with the written alif; al-Akhfash prefers it for a masculine people or ancestor. Ibn Mujahid, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Atiyya, Jishumi, Baghawi, Abu Hayyan, al-Nashr, and al-Alusi give the majority reader patterns, with al-Kisai declining all occurrences. Reports from Abu Bakr differ on al-Najm: Yahya ibn Adam gives three declined instances; another route gives four; al-Nashr records one route offering both faces there. Al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi say tribe and people are both sound and recitation follows transmission; examples include Saba, Yahud, Majus, poetry, hadith, and a Taghlib verse. Ibn Ashur says an ancestor-name analysis is established Arab usage; Qiraat-ER takes tanwin as Arabic triptote rather than foreign proper name. Biqai reads contempt and ease of accusative pronunciation into it. Sibawayh uses Qur'anic accusatives including Thamud as evidence for verb-governed accusative coordination, extends the pattern to kana and laysa, allows nominative for a new clause, and says lakin and bal do not govern; these are parallel case examples. [40 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA(الكسائي); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر,الكسائي,يحيى بن آدم عن أبي بكر عن عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الحديث,الشاعر); JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة وحفص ويعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(باقي القراء,حمزة); TABRISI(سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو ونافع وابن عامر,الكسائي,حسين الجعفي عن أبي بكر عن عاصم,يحيى بن آدم عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL(حمزة وحفص عن عاصم والباقون); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر والكسائي وابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حمزة وحفص); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(أبو علي العطار); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أكثر السبعة,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(CE); AKHFASH+; SIBAWAYH+]
- 11:68/q04/p2 It is left without tanwīn, treating Thamūd as a feminine tribal name or other proper name. — Al-Aswad and then Hamza read it thus throughout; Hafs and Yaqub are also reported to omit tanwin in the listed occurrences. Ibn Mujahid, Ibn al-Jawzi, Ibn Atiyya, Jishumi, Baghawi, al-Nashr, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, and al-Alusi give details of the reader distributions, including Abu Bakr's omission at al-Najm. Sibawayh, al-Akhfash, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, al-Razi, and Ibn Ashur allow a tribal understanding; poetry and hadith use Yahud and Majus similarly. Qiraat-ER contrasts the diptote foreign or tribal proper name with a triptote Arabic name. [29 notes: SIBAWAYH; FARRA(الأسود بن يزيد); AKHFASH; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة,يحيى بن آدم عن أبي بكر عن عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الحديث,الشاعر); JISHUMI(حمزة، وحفص عن عاصم); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة وحفص ويعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة); TABRISI(سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حفص عن عاصم,حمزة); RAZI-FULL(حمزة وحفص عن عاصم والباقون); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر والكسائي وابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(حمزة وحفص); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR; ALUSI-FULL(حفص وحمزة); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(CE)]
- 11:68/q04/p3 One report attributes the declined reading specifically to Hamza and Hafs. — Al-Nasafi reports this attribution while also assigning people or ancestor to declension and a feminine tribe to indeclinability; the other recorded reader lists assign Hamza and Hafs to the form without tanwin. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 11:68/q05 What does 'Thamūd disbelieved their Lord' say they denied?  [ثَمُودَا۟ كَفَرُوا۟ رَبَّهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of their rejection or ingratitude the direct wording specifies.
- 11:68/q05/p1 They denied God's oneness. — Muqatil gives the oneness of their Lord; Bursevi says the warning also cautions later peoples. [2 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI]
- 11:68/q05/p2 They denied God's signs. — Al-Tabari explains the disbelief by their denial of His signs. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 11:68/q05/p3 They rejected their Lord's threat. — Al-Mawardi records this gloss. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 11:68/q05/p4 They disbelieved God's command. — Al-Mawardi records this as another gloss. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 11:68/q05/p5 They were ungrateful for their Lord's blessing and obscured their benefactor and His attributes and acts. — Al-Farra quotes the gloss كفروا نعمة ربهم and cites كفرتك and كفرت بك alongside constructions of شكر; al-Kisai heard شكرت بالله by analogy with كفرت بالله. Biqai names creation, provision, and sending as the Lord's benefits. [3 notes: FARRA(الكسائي,جاء في التفسير); BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:68/q06 Why does 'kafarū' directly govern 'their Lord'?  [كَفَرُوا۟ رَبَّهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 11:68/q06/p1 The direct object marks the coarseness of their unbelief. — Biqai contrasts this construction with many parallels where kufr has a preposition; al-Farra notes Arabic usage also permits كفرتك beside كفرت بك. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; FARRA(جاء في التفسير)]

## 11:68/q07 Why state their disbelief explicitly after the punishment?  [أَلَآ إِنَّ ثَمُودَا۟ كَفَرُوا۟ رَبَّهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 11:68/q07/p1 It censures Thamūd, explains the ensuing curse, and warns later peoples. — Al-Alusi says the disbelief was already evident yet is named to justify the prayer for ruin. Bursevi says the statement warns those after them; Tabatabai treats the clause as a summary of the outcome of Salih's mission. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]

## 11:68/q08 Why replace an expected pronoun with the name 'Thamūd'?  [ثَمُودَا۟ · rhetoric]
- 11:68/q08/p1 The explicit name identifies the people under condemnation. — Al-Alusi notes the replacement of a pronoun by the name. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:68/q09 Why repeat 'alā' before the curse?  [أَلَآ بُعْدًۭا لِّثَمُودَ · rhetoric]
- 11:68/q09/p1 The repetition confirms the condemnation and announces enduring destruction. — Biqai takes the second alerting formula as confirmation of the first. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:68/q10 What does 'buʿdan li-Thamūd' mean and how is it constructed?  [بُعْدًۭا لِّثَمُودَ · interpretation]
- 11:68/q10/p1 It invokes distance, ruin, and destruction upon Thamūd. — Muqatil refers the prayer to their destruction; al-Tabari says it asks God to put them far away by the punishment that fell. Al-Nasafi invokes distance and ruin. Bursevi calls buʿdan a verbal noun in place of its verb and the lām the marker of those cursed; the posthumous curse says their disbelief, denial, and hamstringing of the she-camel merited annihilation. Al-Tabrisi says the formula was treated in the account of ʿĀd; Ibn Ashur points to 11:44, and Ibn al-Jawzi to earlier discussions at 7:73 and 9:70. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:68/q11 How is the second 'Thamūd' after lām read and construed?  [لِّثَمُودَ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted reading and whether the repeated name is treated as a declinable people or ancestor or an indeclinable tribe; al-Kisai also invokes proximity to the first occurrence.
- 11:68/q11/p1 Most readers give it fatḥa without tanwīn, often construing it as the feminine tribe. — Al-Farra says al-Kisai generally avoids declension outside the accusative; Ibn Mujahid and Ibn al-Jawzi give the four-readers' pattern of tanwin elsewhere but not here. Jishumi and Baghawi give the fatḥa form; al-Samin adduces two poetic lines for the alternatives. Biqai reads lasting banishment in the indeclinable form. Al-Wahidi and al-Tabrisi accept the tribe analysis using Yahud and Majus in poetry and hadith; Ibn Atiyya calls both forms eloquent. [29 notes: FARRA(الأسود بن يزيد,الكسائي); IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر,حمزة,يحيى بن آدم عن أبي بكر عن عاصم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي,الحديث,الشاعر); JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم,باقي القراء,حفص عن عاصم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو ونافع وابن عامر,حفص عن عاصم,حمزة,يحيى بن آدم عن عاصم); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر والكسائي وابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(الكسائي والباقون); BIQAI-FULL(غير الكسائي); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي)]
- 11:68/q11/p2 Al-Kisāʾī reads kasra with tanwīn, declining the name as a people or masculine ancestor. — Al-Farra says al-Kisai declined the repeated name because its proximity to the preceding occurrence made differing cases awkward. Jishumi repeats this and Baghawi assigns the declined form to a masculine name; al-Samin calls it the name of a people, citing poetic forms. Qiraat-ER says genitive tanwin favors an Arabic noun over a foreign proper name. [12 notes: FARRA(الكسائي); IBNMUJAHID(الكسائي); JISHUMI(الكسائي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر والكسائي وابن كثير ونافع وابن عامر وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(الكسائي والباقون); ALUSI-FULL(الكسائي); QIRAAT-ER(CE)]

## 11:68/q12 How is a tanwīn or non-tanwīn reading of 'Thamūd' stopped upon?  [ثَمُودَا۟ لِّثَمُودَ · readings]
- 11:68/q12/p1 Readers with tanwīn stop with alif, while those without tanwīn normally stop without it. — Al-Nashr defers this stopping issue from its earlier discussion to Hūd; it records one exceptional Hafs route stopping with alif despite omission of tanwin. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 11:68/q13 Who escaped Thamūd's destruction while in the sanctuary?  [* · reports]
turns on: The surviving man's reported name; both reports place the exception in the sanctuary.
- 11:68/q13/p1 Abū Ramḥān was spared because he was in God's sanctuary. — Al-Mawardi says the destruction otherwise annihilated Thamūd. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 11:68/q13/p2 Abū Righāl was spared while sheltered in the sanctuary. — Jabir's report of the Prophet's Tabuk sermon says the camel drank on its day and gave milk on theirs; after rebellion and a three-day warning they were destroyed, with this exception. [1 notes: BURSEVI(جابر)]

## 11:68/q14 What destroyed Thamūd, and what became of their bodies?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether cry, thunderous blast, and earthquake are treated as distinct descriptions or a single noisy calamity, and which narrative stage a report emphasizes.
- 11:68/q14/p1 A cry or blast struck the deniers and left them motionless in their courtyards. — Al-Tabari links the death to hamstringing God's she-camel and disbelief, referring to 11:67. Islahi calls sayhah a roar of thunder and lightning and says the masculine verb follows the intended sense. Kuranyolu says Salih likely left with the believers during the three-day respite and the fourth-day blast destroyed Thamud. Bursevi cites Bahr al-Ulum: juthum is silent motionlessness and majthumah a bound animal; the Mathnawi pictures kneeling beneath divine punishment. Biqai calls juthum a euphemism for death and the morning scene an intensification of sudden terror and regret. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(المثنوي,بحر العلوم); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:68/q14/p2 The destructive calamity was a noisy earthquake. — Kuranyolu compares rajfah at 7:78 with sayhah here, taking the different terms as descriptions of one event. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:68/q14/p3 God countered the clans' murder plot with the blast, quake, and cry, while saving believers. — Tabatabai says the clans planned to kill Salih and his family and deny responsibility to their heir; after the destruction Salih rebuked their rejection of his message. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:68/q15 How are the two closing clauses related to the story and its parallels?  [* · links]
- 11:68/q15/p1 They summarize Salih's mission and Thamūd's fate, then state God's recompense, paralleling Hūd's ending. — Tabatabai compares 11:60. Al-Tabrisi and al-Qurtubi direct the closing declaration to earlier commentary; Ibn Kathir says the story was fully covered in al-Araf, and Ibn Ashur notes analogous material in al-Araf's account of Hud and prior discussion of Shuayb. Al-Tabrisi also supplies Ibn al-Anbari's ellipsis: God rescued them from punishment and that day's disgrace, and glosses khizy as public, shameful disgrace. [7 notes: TABATABAI; TABRISI(ابن الأنباري); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:68/q16 What inward meaning is given to this destruction?  [* · inward]
- 11:68/q16/p1 The destruction figures the soul and its traits struck by separation; refuge in Sacred Law leads to nearness and Paradise. — The Ta'wilat Najmiyya, quoted by Bursevi, connects the sanctuary image to 89:29-30. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 11:68/q17 Who were Thamūd and what was their society like?  [* · reports]
- 11:68/q17/p1 They were a later Arab people in the Medina-to-Syria region, with settled agriculture, strong dwellings, and a tribal order. — Tabatabai locates them in Wadi al-Qura, says surviving historical traces are sparse, and cites the Qur'anic picture of palaces, mountain homes, springs, gardens, palms, and crops. He describes notables and elders and nine corrupt idol-worshipping men. Ibn Hajar and others, reported by al-Suyuti, place them after Ad as Ad followed Noah. Nawf says they settled after Ad's destruction. [5 notes: TABATABAI; ITQAN(ابن حجر,نوف الشامي)]

## 11:68/q18 What is reported about Ṣāliḥ's life and mission among Thamūd?  [* · reports]
- 11:68/q18/p1 Ṣāliḥ was an Arab prophet who called Thamūd to monotheism and justice; few believed, and the elite rejected him. — Nawf says he was sent young and preached until old age, and that only Hud and Salih were prophets between Noah and Abraham. Al-Tha'labi via al-Nawawi supplies a genealogy, places his people between Hijaz and Syria, gives twenty years of preaching and death in Mecca at fifty-eight. Tabatabai says he was respected, intelligent, and competent, urged rejection of idols and arrogance, and was called foolish and a sorcerer by a hostile majority that humiliated weak believers. He notes Salih is absent from the present Torah and is the third prophet in the Qur'anic sequence after Noah and Hud, praised and chosen by God. [5 notes: ITQAN(الثعلبي عن النووي,نوف الشامي); TABATABAI]

## 11:68/q19 What lineage does the historical excursus give for Mūsā?  [* · reports]
- 11:68/q19/p1 Mūsā is son of ʿImrān in the line of Yaʿqūb. — Al-Suyuti reports no disagreement over this lineage and calls the name Syriac. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 11:68/q20 How does the historical excursus explain Mūsā's name?  [* · meaning]
- 11:68/q20/p1 It is explained as Coptic mū, water, plus sā, tree, because he was cast between water and a tree. — Al-Suyuti cites a report from Ibn Abbas; another note in the excursus calls the name Syriac. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن عباس)]
