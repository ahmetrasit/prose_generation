# 23:13: 20 questions

## 23:13/q01 What does thumma mark in this creation sequence?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle is explained by the stage's rank and marvel or by the interval between stages.
- 23:13/q01/p1 It marks the remarkable transition from clay to reproductive fluid or the higher rank of placing that fluid in the womb. — Biqāʿī calls water humanity's second, purer origin and the source of life, and calls the transition striking. Ibn ʿĀshūr says placement in the firm lodging is more remarkable and thus ranked above the preceding stage. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:13/q01/p2 It marks a complete break between stages, unlike fa for the closer succession of the later embryonic stages. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this account for the change from thumma to fa at the clot, morsel, bones, and flesh stages. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 23:13/q02 How does jaʿalnāhu function grammatically and semantically here?  [جَعَلْنَٰهُ نُطْفَةًۭ فِى قَرَارٍۢ مَّكِينٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The sense assigned to jaʿala and the syntactic role of nuṭfa and the prepositional phrase.
- 23:13/q02/p1 Jaʿala means 'make' with nuṭfa as its second object. — Al-Ālūsī calls this the majority analysis. Biqāʿī says jaʿala conveys producing a result through a cause. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 23:13/q02/p2 Jaʿala means 'place'; nuṭfa is circumstantial and the phrase 'in a firm lodging' is its second object. — Ibn ʿĀshūr argues that fī expresses placement of reproductive material in the womb, hence jaʿala rather than khalq; nuṭfa is accusative as a circumstantial expression. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:13/q02/p3 Jaʿala means 'create,' and nuṭfa is accusative after an omitted preposition. — Al-Ālūsī reports that some investigators favor this analysis. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:13/q03 Who or what does the pronoun in jaʿalnāhu refer to?  [جَعَلْنَٰهُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the antecedent is Adam with an understood descendant, the inclusive human kind, or the preceding extracted substance; Ālūsī weighs the need for an omitted antecedent.
- 23:13/q03/p1 It refers to Adam's offspring or a descendant, supplied by context even though Adam or 'human' was named previously. — Muqātil, Zajjāj, Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, Qurṭubī, Burhān, and Itqān say Adam himself was not a drop. Wāḥidī's Ṣāḥib al-Naẓm licenses the shift by context; Wāḥidī compares 5:101. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī compare 38:32. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Samīn, and Ālūsī report supplying an omitted 'offspring' or possessive; Nasafī cites 32:7–8. Qatāda's clay Adam and fluid descendants are reported by Durr. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the shift from Adam to his descendants an ancestral reference. Ālūsī finds an unstated son of Adam or omitted possessive strained; Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects limiting 'human' to descendants, arguing that this would call for 'created him as a drop.' [22 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ITQAN; BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI-]
- 23:13/q03/p2 It refers to humankind as a species, with Adam's origin and the descendants' reproductive origin treated together. — Zamakhsharī, as reported by Samīn, lets each pronoun select the relevant origin or descendants. Ibn Kathīr cites 32:7–8; Bursevī speaks of individuals distinct from Adam. Ālūsī prefers this because it avoids strained omitted antecedents. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid reading al-insān generically. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers the inclusive kind and cites 32:7–8, rejecting restriction to Adam alone or descendants alone. Elmalılı allows humankind or its progeny. [9 notes: SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 23:13/q03/p3 It refers to the extracted clay substance, made into a drop. — Zajjāj reports Adam's semen as the clay-derived extract; Bayḍāwī interprets the masculine pronoun as a substance, extracted thing, or water. Biqāʿī allows the clay itself; Ālūsī and Elmalılı report the extract reading. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 23:13/q03/p4 It refers to the human formed from the extract, whose reproductive substance God places in the womb. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the pronoun from the human derived from the preceding extract and reads the clause as placement in a lodging. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:13/q04 What does nuṭfa mean in Arabic and in this verse?  [نُطْفَةًۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether nuṭfa is read by its general water sense, male seminal fluid, the particular seed or zygote, or combined reproductive material.
- 23:13/q04/p1 It is the small drop of reproductive fluid, commonly a man's semen. — Māwardī specifies male fluid from which a child forms by adhering. Jishumī derives the root from dripping and cites nuṭūf for a rainy night. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Jishumī, Nasafī, Study Quran, and Ibn Kathīr identify seminal or weak fluid; Ibn Kathīr cites 77:20, and Study Quran lists 16:4, 18:37, 22:5, 35:11, 36:77, 40:67, 53:46, 75:37, 76:2, 80:19. Biqāʿī notes the flowing drop has no visible clay. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 23:13/q04/p2 It can mean water generally, little or much, or clear pure water. — Māwardī cites a Hudhayl poet's thirsty drinkers for ordinary water. Jishumī mentions an account of a rider between the eastern and western seas as evidence of wide water usage. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says little or great quantity; Bursevī calls it clear, pure water; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says a little water and sometimes water generally. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(بعض شعراء هذيل); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 23:13/q04/p3 In this verse it specifically denotes the seed within semen or the fertilized zygote. — Elmalılı argues that what actually settles in the womb is the seed, not semen as a whole. Kur'an Yolu describes sperm fertilizing the ovum, with the resultant zygote called nuṭfa as it reaches the womb. [2 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 23:13/q04/p4 The drop stage involves the union of male and female reproductive extracts. — Ibn ʿĀshūr traces the extracts through earth-derived nourishment, blood, male semen and female ova; he says the male fluid is named for dripping through its channel into the womb. Ibn Kathīr reports a Prophetic answer that male thick fluid makes bones and sinews while female thin fluid makes flesh and blood, confirmed by a Jewish questioner. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 23:13/q05 How is fī qarārin makīn attached syntactically?  [فِى قَرَارٍۢ مَّكِينٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase completes the verb or qualifies the drop.
- 23:13/q05/p1 The phrase attaches to jaʿalnāhu. — Samīn gives this as one permissible attachment; Ibn ʿĀshūr analyzes it as the second object when jaʿala means place. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:13/q05/p2 It is an omitted adjective of nuṭfa. — Samīn gives this as the alternative grammatical attachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 23:13/q06 What does qarār mean?  [قَرَارٍۢ · meaning]
- 23:13/q06/p1 It is a place of settling or residence, expressed by a verbal noun used as a place name. — Wāḥidī, Rāzī, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī note the verbal noun; Bursevī, Ālūsī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī see emphatic force. Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Samīn state the place sense. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 23:13/q07 What is the qarār makīn, the drop's secure lodging?  [قَرَارٍۢ مَّكِينٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the lodging names only the womb or includes earlier sites through which the generative material passes.
- 23:13/q07/p1 It is the womb, prepared to receive, retain, and protect the embryo until its appointed term. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Thaʿlabī, Māwardī, Wāḥidī citing Ibn ʿAbbās and commentators, Wāḥidī's Wajīz, Jishumī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Ibn Kathīr, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī, Kur'an Yolu, and Study Quran identify the womb. Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn Kathīr and Jishumī stress its preparation and fixed term; Ibn Kathīr cites 77:21–23. Ibn al-Jawzī points to explanations of the stages at 22:5. [23 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 23:13/q07/p2 The secure receptacle includes the fathers' loins and ribs before the womb. — Biqāʿī extends the lodging across the route of reproductive fluid, emphasizing protection from corrupting influences. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 23:13/q08 What does makīn describe and why is the lodging secure?  [مَّكِينٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective names the womb's own stability or transfers the secure position of what it contains.
- 23:13/q08/p1 It describes the womb's own fixed, fortified, protective position and ability to retain its contents. — Wāḥidī reports al-Mufaḍḍal's 'firmly settled, not unstable.' Jishumī says protected; Nasafī calls it fortified. Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī allow the womb's own stability; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls attribution to the place a rational metaphor emphasizing security. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it is firmly situated. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفضل); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 23:13/q08/p2 It transfers the security or settledness of the drop or occupant to its lodging. — Rāzī allows transfer from what settles there; Bayḍāwī says the term is applied emphatically to the location; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn compare calling a road 'travelling'; Bursevī describes the womb by its contents' quality; Ālūsī calls the attribution to the drop metaphorical; Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows the drop's secure position. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 23:13/q09 What happens to the drop in the womb according to the reported accounts?  [نُطْفَةًۭ فِى قَرَارٍۢ مَّكِينٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The reports vary in sequence, timing, and which acts are assigned to the angel; one is expressly graded weak.
- 23:13/q09/p1 It remains a drop for forty days, then becomes a clot, then a morsel for forty days each, followed by an angel's breathing and decree. — Ibn Kathīr reports Ibn Masʿūd from the Prophet: the angel writes provision, lifespan, deeds, and blessed or wretched destiny; apparent deeds can differ from final destiny. Ibn Kathīr says both Ṣaḥīḥ collections transmit it through al-Aʿmash. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 23:13/q09/p2 The fluid first spreads through every hair and nail, stays forty days, then settles or descends and becomes a clot. — Ibn Kathīr transmits Ibn Masʿūd through Khaythama, and Durr reports Ibn Masʿūd. Their wording varies over whether descent or settling follows the forty days. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود عن خيثمة); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 23:13/q09/p3 After forty nights an angel enters the womb and records and seals the person's traits and destiny. — Ibn Kathīr reports Ḥudhayfa b. Usayd from the Prophet: the angel writes sex, blessedness or wretchedness, deeds, effects, misfortunes, and provision, with no later addition or subtraction. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(حذيفة بن أسيد عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 23:13/q09/p4 An angel assigned to the womb reports successive drop, clot, and morsel stages and, if creation is willed, writes the person's traits and allotment. — Ibn Kathīr reports Anas from the Prophet: the angel asks and writes sex, blessedness or wretchedness, provision, and lifespan in the mother's womb. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 23:13/q09/p5 When generative fluid leaves and falls into the womb, limbs and veins tremble. — Durr cites a report attributed to the Prophet through Ibn ʿAbbās and explicitly calls its chain weak. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 23:13/q10 What legal analogy is drawn from the reproductive fluid?  [نُطْفَةًۭ · law]
- 23:13/q10/p1 Coitus interruptus is concealed or hidden infanticide. — Durr reports this judgment from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib and Ibn Masʿūd; wording varies between concealed and hidden. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,علي بن أبي طالب)]

## 23:13/q11 What does the creation sequence show about human development?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize different aspects of one sequence: bodily differentiation, transmission from earth through reproductive material, or the change from first creation to reproduction.
- 23:13/q11/p1 A uniform drop is differentiated into a complex body through successive measured stages. — Qushayrī lists flesh, bone, hair, nails, nerves, skin, brain, and veins with each member's form. Rāzī counts nine stages. Elmalılı distinguishes the first extraordinary origin from subsequent customary natural formation. Kur'an Yolu describes soil-derived food and plants through gestation, with sperm, ovum, and zygote; İslāḥī says science has uncovered aspects only hinted at, and Sāmarrāʾī says embryonic descriptions accord with modern anatomy and medicine. Biqāʿī reports al-Ḥarālī's account of measured components appearing as a form after mixture and applies it to clay. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 23:13/q11/p2 The clay origin passes through fathers' semen into the mother's womb. — Rāzī describes clay becoming semen in fathers' loins and being conveyed by intercourse. Nasafī reports another view in which 'human' means Adam's children, sulāla means semen extracted ultimately from clay through Adam; Ibn ʿĀshūr traces earth-derived nourishment into the combined reproductive extracts. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:13/q11/p3 Creation changes mode from the first clay-formed human to reproduction through a lowly drop. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes the change of mode; Elmalılı cites 32:8 for the first human followed by offspring from fluid. Wāḥidī's Wajīz calls the drop the start of an individual's formation. Elmalılı elsewhere calls fluid the nearest and clearest starting point for reflection, though technically the second stage. [4 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 23:13/q12 Does the creation account support a claim about what a human being is?  [* · theology]
turns on: These are distinct claims about bodily identity and the later emergence of human capacities.
- 23:13/q12/p1 Its description of bodily formation opposes theories that a human is only spirit or a nonbodily indivisible entity. — Ṭabrisī names al-Naẓẓām's spirit view and Maʿmar's indivisible nonbodily view as contradicted by the passage. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 23:13/q12/p2 The later human is transformed from lifeless, ignorant, powerless matter into a living, knowing, capable being. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the pronoun in the following 'We brought him into being' refers to the human whose bones were clothed with flesh. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 23:13/q13 What does the sequence teach about the cause of natural development?  [* · theology]
- 23:13/q13/p1 God's creative power governs nature and causes its changes, including development from clay and drop. — Elmalılı says nature is internal to a thing and cannot externally cause its own transformation; stable natures require an outside influence to change. Natural sciences seek such causes. Nature may undergo selection and development, but only the Creator rules over natures; otherwise clay and nutfa could not develop into later forms. [5 notes: ELMALILI]

## 23:13/q14 How does this account argue for resurrection?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:13/q14/p1 The God who formed people through these stages can recreate them after death; the ordered process displays power and wisdom. — İslāḥī says deniers mock afterlife promises, yet creation from a drop and familiar development into learned people such as Socrates, Hippocrates, Aristotle, and Galen cannot plausibly be accidental or meaningless. He cites 22:5 and its 'so that We make evident for you,' invites reflection on one's own body, and says this passage elaborates the same proof. Rāzī treats development as one of several proofs. Biqāʿī describes the sūra's turn from Paradise to first creation, cosmos, earlier peoples, gratitude, resurrection, and oneness. Study Quran's introduction recalls the stages and 22:5. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:13/q15 How does the creation passage fit the sūra's argument and adjacent verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes link the account either to the sūra's ongoing exhortation or to parallel origin and resurrection passages.
- 23:13/q15/p1 After believers and their promised reward, creation and provision show God's sole lordship and invite gratitude and worship. — Jishumī connects prior afterlife blessings to blessings at human origins. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the passage opens a new proof against polytheists after 23:1, contrasting grateful believers with ungrateful idolaters. Ṭabāṭabāʾī similarly says God alone deserves worship. Kur'an Yolu calls for gratitude; İslāḥī notes the earlier glad tidings and their rejection. Biqāʿī traces the sūra's larger movement through creation, cosmos, earlier peoples, and resurrection; Noah begins the narrative because he was the first prophet and the ark rescue recalls humanity's descent from him, while the next sūra continues the theme of guarding sexual integrity. Study Quran notes the recurrent opposition of powerful and affluent people to prophets. [8 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 23:13/q15/p2 The passage recalls other Qur'anic descriptions of origin from earth and a drop, especially 22:5 and 32:7–8. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 18:37 and 22:5. Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, Elmalılı, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite 32:7–8 or 32:8 for clay then offspring from fluid. Qurṭubī and Ibn al-Jawzī refer discussion of the drop, clot, morsel, and legal rulings back to commentary on Sūrat al-Ḥajj. İslāḥī says the present passage develops the argument of 22:5 further. [9 notes: NASAFI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 23:13/q16 Why are the creation verbs expressed as past events?  [جَعَلْنَٰهُ · rhetoric]
- 23:13/q16/p1 The past form can emphasize that the statement is certain, while the process continues in each person. — Kur'an Yolu renders the Arabic past verbs in the present to convey ongoing creation and says Qur'anic past forms can mark certainty. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:13/q17 Why is creation more emphatically stated than resurrection in this passage?  [* · rhetoric]
- 23:13/q17/p1 Creation is foregrounded and death recurs more often, so creation receives two emphases and resurrection one; resurrection is easier than first origination, though both are easy for God. — Sāmarrāʾī makes the comparison with 39:30 and ties the distribution of emphasis to this sūra's context. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 23:13/q18 Why does the passage state what the polytheists already know?  [* · rhetoric]
- 23:13/q18/p1 Its emphatic opening addresses them as though they denied creation, because they fail to act on that knowledge. — Ibn ʿĀshūr points to the oath lām and emphatic particle in the passage's opening. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 23:13/q19 What is the clay extract that precedes the drop?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the preceding extract is Adam's selected clay or the reproductive substance of his descendants traced back to clay.
- 23:13/q19/p1 It is specially selected clay from which Adam was formed, before his descendants' fluid origin. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Qatāda's reading of al-insān as Adam and sulāla as selected clay; he explains the later pronoun by Adam's descendants and cites 32:7–8. Ibn ʿĀshūr says sulāla is what is pulled from another substance, like a sword from its sheath, and its form can suggest a small quantity, like qalamāma and ṣubāba. Biqāʿī reports al-Ḥarālī's account of components mixed into an unexpected human form. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 23:13/q19/p2 It can designate semen ultimately derived from clay through Adam. — Nasafī reports this account for Adam's children, noting Arab usage of sulāla for semen. Zajjāj reports an alternative identifying the clay-derived sulāla as Adam's semen. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ZAJJAJ(قيل)]

## 23:13/q20 What does 'creation' mean in this argument?  [* · meaning]
- 23:13/q20/p1 It means bringing into being or making. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 3:47, where God creates what He wills. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
