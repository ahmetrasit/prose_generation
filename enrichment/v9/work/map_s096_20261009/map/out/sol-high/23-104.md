# 23:104: 15 questions

## 23:104/q01 What does talfaḥ mean, and how forceful is it?  [تَلْفَحُ · meaning]
- 23:104/q01/p1 The fire strikes the faces with a burning, scorching blast; lafḥ is more forceful than nafḥ. — Muqātil glosses it as blowing; Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Ṭabarī says the fire blows upon them, while Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Rāzī says it hits and consumes facial flesh and skin. Al-Zajjāj, cited by al-Wāḥidī, al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Rāzī, says lafḥ and nafḥ share a basic sense but lafḥ has the stronger effect. Al-Ṭabrisī calls nafḥ a light touch and lafḥ a severe scorching touch; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī transmits the comparison of a scorching poisonous blast with a breeze. Al-Qurṭubī compares tanfaḥu in 21:46, adds that fire or a hot wind burns and that a sword's lafḥa is a light blow. Al-Wāḥidī cites usage of burning by fire or the hot sirocco; Bursawī cites al-Qāmūs for burning and al-Irshād for the stronger force. Abū Ḥayyān calls it the scorching impact of heat and reports al-Zajjāj's comparison with laqīḥ. Al-Samīn calls it striking, scorching and burning; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds the sense of a severe impact. Ibn ʿAbbās is also transmitted with the gloss tanqaḥ. Al-Ṭabrisī says the flames strike the faces; the Study Quran notes the range smite, burn, or scorch. ## 23:104/q02 How is the clause talfaḥu wujūhahumu al-nār construed?  [تَلْفَحُ وُجُوهَهُمُ ٱلنَّارُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the clause depends circumstantially on the preceding description, starts a new sentence, or supplies another predicate.
- 23:104/q02/p1 It is a circumstantial clause describing those who lost themselves, governed by khālidūn. — Al-Ṭabrisī specifies accusative circumstantial status and khālidūn as governor; Ibn ʿĀshūr relates it to those who lost themselves. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī also allow this construction. [4 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 23:104/q02/p2 It begins a new independent sentence. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī both present this as an alternative syntactic analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 23:104/q02/p3 It is another predicate of ulāʾika. — Al-Samīn lists this third construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 23:104/q03 Why are their faces singled out and placed before the fire?  [وُجُوهَهُمُ ٱلنَّارُ · rhetoric]
- 23:104/q03/p1 Faces are the most honored and protected parts; scorching them implies the lesser parts suffer too and makes the punishment a stronger deterrent. — Abū Ḥayyān says this leads into the description of the grimacing lips. Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say the face's nobility explains its punishment and its precedence over the agent in the wording. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 23:104/q04 What does kāliḥūn describe?  [كَٰلِحُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is explained by the exposed teeth and retracted lips, a more general frown or grimace, or open mouths and darkened faces.
- 23:104/q04/p1 Their lips contract away from their teeth, leaving the teeth exposed in a painful grimace. — Ibn Masʿūd repeatedly compares the face to a singed or roasted head, sometimes a sheep's head; Ibn Zayd compares faces to sheep touched by fire. Al-Zajjāj, al-Ḥasan, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Rāzī, and Bursawī compare it to roasted animals' heads. Al-Aʿshā's cited verse uses kalih or kalaḥ for a cheek or mouth drawn back from a tooth, as reported by al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī. Al-Wāḥidī describes bared teeth while scowling; al-Qurṭubī also records kalaha, kulūḥan and kalāḥan, kalḥa for mouth and surroundings, and a grim or severe day. Abū Ḥayyān compares a dog's or lion's grimace; al-Samīn likewise cites a lion's bared teeth and a harsh day or cold. Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes the contraction to facial nerves tightening with severe pain; al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and others attribute it to the heat. Ibn ʿAbbās adds in one report that pus flows. The Study Quran attributes the explanation to commentators including al-Qurṭubī and al-Ṭabarī. [40 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مسعود); ABDURRAZZAQ(عبد الله ابن مسعود); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الأعشى,عبد الله); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن مسعود,الأعشى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسعود,الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,الأعشى); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(مجمع البيان); STUDYQURAN(some commentators (Q, Ṭ))]
- 23:104/q04/p2 They are frowning, scowling, or grimacing, their faces contorted. — Ibn ʿAbbās is repeatedly cited for the frowning gloss; al-Ālūsī transmits his description of an ugly, contorted face. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Itqān give this expression gloss. Al-Ṭabrisī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ṭabarī says they frown. Some of these sources also specify contracted lips in the same or companion notes. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 23:104/q04/p3 Their mouths are open and their faces darkened. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports this explanation from al-Qummī, attributed there to Abū ʿAbd Allāh, alongside the fire blazing over and burning their faces. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 23:104/q05 What reading of the verb talfaḥu is reported?  [تَلْفَحُ · readings]
turns on: Whether the consonant in the verb is light or doubled.
- 23:104/q05/p1 The verb is read with a light consonant. — Al-Bayḍāwī mentions this alongside a reading with a doubled consonant. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 23:104/q05/p2 The verb is read with a doubled consonant. — Al-Bayḍāwī mentions this alongside the light form. [1 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 23:104/q06 How is kāliḥūn read?  [كَٰلِحُونَ · readings]
turns on: The presence or absence of the alif in the word.
- 23:104/q06/p1 A reading has kāliḥūn with the alif. — Al-Rāzī reports this form as a reading. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 23:104/q06/p2 A reading omits the alif, giving kalihun or kulihun. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes the form to Abū Ḥaywa; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī name Abū Ḥaywah, Abū Baḥriyya and Ibn Abī ʿAbla. Al-Ālūsī calls it the plural of kalih. Al-Zamakhsharī also reports the alifless form. The notes vary in vocalization or transliteration of the form. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة وأبو بحرية وابن أبي عبلة)]

## 23:104/q07 What precise disfigurement of the lips is reported?  [كَٰلِحُونَ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different extents, directions and appearances of the distorted lips.
- 23:104/q07/p1 The fire pulls the upper lip to the middle of the head and lets the lower lip hang to the navel. — The report is attributed to the Prophet, often through Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī. Ibn al-Jawzī notes al-Ḥākim's inclusion in his Ṣaḥīḥ; al-Qurṭubī cites al-Tirmidhī's ṣaḥīḥ gharīb grading; Abū Ḥayyān cites ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ; Ibn Kathīr cites ḥasan gharīb. Other sources transmit it without a grading. Al-Samīn uses it to explain kulūḥ. [14 notes: THALABI(أبو سعيد الخدري); WAHIDI-BASIT(الخدري عن رسول الله); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو سعيد الخدري عن رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); ITQAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
- 23:104/q07/p2 The upper lip curls over the face while the lower lip hangs to the chest. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām transmits this from Abū Hurayra. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو هريرة)]
- 23:104/q07/p3 The upper lip rises and exposes the teeth, and the lower lip strikes the belly. — Muqātil gives this as his description of their grimace. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 23:104/q07/p4 The lips reach the navel. — Al-Baghawī transmits this detail from Abū Hurayra in a report about the disbeliever's huge body. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 23:104/q07/p5 The lips are swollen and dark blue. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām transmits this from Abū Hurayra in a report on the disbeliever's size; that report also says Hell asks whether it is full until God places His foot in it. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو هريرة)]

## 23:104/q08 What further bodily effects or sizes of the damned are reported?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different measurements and different ways the body is affected; versions of the flesh-blast report also vary in where the flesh falls.
- 23:104/q08/p1 Their teeth protrude forty cubits by the stature of the first creation, and each canine is like Mount Uḥud. — Muqātil gives both the measurement and the mountain comparison. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 23:104/q08/p2 A disbeliever is as tall as a seven-night journey and has molars like Mount Uḥud. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and al-Baghawī transmit versions from Abū Hurayra. Yaḥyā adds swollen dark-blue lips and says Hell asks whether it is full until God places His foot in it; al-Baghawī adds that the lips reach the navel. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو هريرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو هريرة)]
- 23:104/q08/p3 Hell meets those driven to it with a blast that strips their flesh from the bones, leaving or dropping it at the ankles or heels. — Ibn Kathīr attributes a version to the Prophet through Abū Hurayra and says flesh falls away except at the ankles. Al-Durr transmits Abū Hurayra's account of Hell meeting them with its neck, and Ibn Masʿūd's account of flesh being cast to the heels, also reported by Abū al-Hudhayl. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن مسعود)]
- 23:104/q08/p4 A blast of fire makes their flesh flow down onto the nerves or to the heels. — Al-Durr transmits the nerves wording from Abū al-Dardāʾ; al-Ālūsī transmits from him the heels wording. The transmitted destinations vary. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو الدرداء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 23:104/q08/p5 A cup of snake venom makes a man's flesh and bone separate as it nears his mouth. — Al-Durr transmits this description from Mughīth ibn Sumayy. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مغيث بن سمي)]

## 23:104/q09 What happened when ʿUtba saw a head taken from an oven?  [* · reports]
- 23:104/q09/p1 He repented and remained unconscious for three days after seeing it. — Al-Zamakhsharī and Bursawī transmit the account from Mālik ibn Dīnār; Bursawī says three days and nights and calls ʿUtba a young ascetic. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(مالك بن دينار); BURSEVI(مالك بن دينار)]

## 23:104/q10 What unusual death-place report is cited?  [* · reports]
- 23:104/q10/p1 The Prophet reportedly said a man would die at al-Rabwa, but he died at al-Ramla. — Al-Itqān records Ibn Kathīr's judgment that the report is very unusual. [1 notes: ITQAN(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 23:104/q11 How does the ensuing address to the condemned function?  [* · links]
- 23:104/q11/p1 It reproaches and rebukes them, reminding them why they deserved the punishment they now suffer. — Bursawī explains the address in relation to their punishment. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 23:104/q12 Which other Quranic passages describe fire or punishment on faces?  [تَلْفَحُ وُجُوهَهُمُ ٱلنَّارُ · links]
- 23:104/q12/p1 Several passages likewise depict fire or punishment reaching faces. — Ibn Kathīr cites 14:50 and 21:39. The Study Quran lists 8:50, 10:27, 14:50, 17:97, 18:29, 25:34, 27:90, 33:66, 54:48 and 67:27. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 23:104/q13 What determines loss or salvation in the surrounding judgment scene?  [* · interpretation]
- 23:104/q13/p1 The outcome depends on deeds and their weight: faith and good works yield success, while light scales lead to enduring loss and Hell; relationships do not help. — Iṣlāḥī stresses that only deeds matter on Judgment Day and relationships avail no one. Kur'an Yolu ties salvation to the reward earned through faith and good works and contrasts heavy and light scales. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 23:104/q14 What opposition recurs through this sūra?  [* · links]
- 23:104/q14/p1 Affluent and powerful people repeatedly oppose prophets. — The Study Quran says this theme also appears elsewhere in the Quran, citing 7:59–62 and 7:136. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 23:104/q15 What warning frames the sūra's conclusion?  [* · links]
- 23:104/q15/p1 Disbelievers who face perdition wish to return and do good, but the chance has passed. — The Study Quran identifies this as the concluding warning. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
