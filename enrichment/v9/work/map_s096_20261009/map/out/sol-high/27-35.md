# 27:35: 30 questions

## 27:35/q01 Why did the queen decide to send a gift?  [مُرْسِلَةٌ بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the gift is chiefly appeasement, a test of kingship and prophecy, or a means to obtain information; these purposes often coexist.
- 27:35/q01/p1 She sought to conciliate Solomon, avert war or loss of her kingdom, and test whether he was a worldly king or a prophet. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī say she hoped wealth would appease him over her kingdom. Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Qurṭubī, and others explain that a king would accept and withdraw or could be fought; a prophet would refuse and demand religious submission. Ibn Kathīr adds her hope for annual tribute. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the gift accompanied a letter answering Solomon's letter. Al-Iṣlāḥī says she expected little success because of his principles. [39 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,وهب بن منبه); IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح,ابن عباس,السدي,زهير بن محمد,قتادة,يزيد بن رومان); THALABI(بلقيس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وزهير); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,مقاتل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قالوا); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس وغير واحد); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بلقيس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAGHAWI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 27:35/q01/p2 She sent a conciliatory gift to soften Solomon's stance or induce him to yield. — Al-Māwardī preserves this as one stated aim; al-Biqāʿī calls it a courteous overture to king and soldiers. Bayḍāwī says she hoped to deflect him from taking the kingdom. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل)]
- 27:35/q01/p3 She sent a gift to learn Solomon's aims and decide her next course. — Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān infer from her waiting that she anticipated either acceptance or refusal; the queen's wording betrays uncertainty. Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī and Bursawī describe a deliberate decision after the envoys' report. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:35/q02 What does her decision reveal about the queen?  [وَإِنِّى مُرْسِلَةٌ فَنَاظِرَةٌۢ · interpretation]
- 27:35/q02/p1 She was discerning and politically experienced, seeking a peaceful outcome. — Qatāda says she understood gifts influence believers and unbelievers. Wahb's account mentions Solomon's unusual letter, her consultation of Yemen's chiefs, and her assessment of a prophet against a rival king. Al-Iṣlāḥī calls her peace-loving and farsighted; Kur'an Yolu relates her fear of a destructive invasion. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 27:35/q02/p2 Her nominal wording indicates firm resolve to carry out her judgment. — Al-Ālūsī says the confirmed nominal sentence signals determination despite anything that could turn her aside. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:35/q03 How does this decision connect with the preceding speech and the story?  [* · links]
- 27:35/q03/p1 Her warning about kings invading cities prepares her conciliatory proposal to send a gift and await the reply. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly links her words about kings' entry to this proposal; Kur'an Yolu connects her consultation with the officials' military confidence and her preference for peace. [2 notes: TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 27:35/q03/p2 The Solomon and Queen of Sheba episode closes with her embrace of faith in the One God. — Study Quran places the gift episode within the account of Solomon's miraculous host and his encounter with her. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 27:35/q04 To whom does 'to them' refer, and why is it plural?  [إِلَيْهِم · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural denotes the ruler's company or represents the ruler through his people.
- 27:35/q04/p1 It refers to Solomon together with his people or entourage. — Abū Ḥayyān and Bursawī read envoys going to Solomon and his party. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says naming a ruler through helpers, troops and subjects portrays royal power. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 27:35/q04/p2 It refers to Solomon himself, expressed through a royal or idiomatic plural. — Al-Farrāʾ identifies the referent as Solomon. Ṭabarī compares the idiom for Pharaoh and chiefs in 10:83; al-Naḥḥās calls it the convention of naming rulers through their people. [3 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS]

## 27:35/q05 What is a hadiyya here?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the contextual transfer is an ordinary courtesy or can amount to bribery.
- 27:35/q05/p1 A courteous gift meant to create closeness, honor or affection. — Baghawī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Biqāʿī and Bursawī define it as an offered object; al-Mufradāt restricts it to a kindly exchange. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it draws affection and gives hadāyā as standard plural and hadāwā in the lower Madi dialect. [11 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI(المفردات); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:35/q05/p2 A gift or donation, possibly a bribe in this context. — Al-Dāmghānī lists gift, donation and bribe among the senses and uses the Queen of Sheba's gift as his example. [1 notes: DAMGHANI]

## 27:35/q06 How is hadiyya formed and pluralized?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · grammar]
- 27:35/q06/p1 It names the gifted object, perhaps historically a passive-participle noun; the regular form-IV verbal noun is ihdāʾ. — Samīn rejects treating hadiyya as a regular maṣdar of ahdā. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives plural hadāyā from hadāʾi by easing the raised yāʾ and replacing the hamza between two alifs with yāʾ; the lower Madi dialect has hadāwā. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:35/q07 What was the gift said to contain?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The conflicting narrative reports give different objects, while several authors stress that the verse gives no inventory and question the reports' reliability.
- 27:35/q07/p1 Disguised male and female attendants, sometimes alongside treasure and objects used as tests. — Ibn ʿAbbās describes identical clothes; Mujāhid and others have boys dressed as girls and girls as boys. Reports add jewels, mounts, crown, perfumes, a pearl, bent-hole bead, cup and staff. Al-Suddī adds gold bricks and a pierced jewel. Ibn ʿAbbās' variant gives twelve of each, musk and amber trays and a royal staff. Ibn al-Jawzī's Ibn ʿAbbās version gives three hundred-ratl gold bricks and a ruby. Numbers differ and are mapped separately. - 27:35/q07/p2 Gold in the form of a brick, ingot, plates or vessels, at times wrapped in silk or brocade. — Qatāda describes a gold brick in silk and brocade; Thābit gives plates in brocade containers; Abū Ṣāliḥ and Ibn ʿAbbās give a brick or ingot; one report has four bricks; the Mathnawī describes a whole load of gold bricks. Zajjāj cites Solomon's question about wealth as evidence that the gift was money. Ibn Kathīr prefers golden vessels. [23 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(ثابت البناني); TAB-FULL(أبو صالح,ثابت البناني); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(أبو صالح,ثابت البناني,قتادة); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ثابت البناني,قول غير مسمى); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,ثابت البناني); WAHIDI-BASIT(ثابت البناني); BAGHAWI-FULL(ثابت البناني,قتادة وسعيد بن جبير,قيل); TABRISI(ثابت اليماني); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI(المثنوي); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 27:35/q07/p3 Jewelry, gemstones, pearls and other valuables. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr says jewelry; Ibn Kathīr transmits a general report of gold, jewels and pearls; other accounts combine gems with attendants and treasure. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(غير واحد من المفسرين، من السلف وغيرهم); MUQATIL; THALABI(وهب وغيره من أهل الكتب)]
- 27:35/q07/p4 Its specific contents cannot be established from the verse and the accounts are unreliable or immaterial. — Rāzī notes the Qur'an leaves the gift unspecified; Ibn ʿAṭiyya omits proliferating unreliable details; Abū Ḥayyān calls the reports contradictory; Ālūsī says they cannot be verified and some look fabricated; Study Quran says their sources are unclear and reports Rāzī's dismissal. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī,commentators); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]

## 27:35/q08 How many attendants were sent?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted counts and whether totals are per sex or combined differ.
- 27:35/q08/p1 Five hundred boys and five hundred girls. — Wahb and other scriptural authorities transmit the large retinue, commonly with cross-dressing and lavish mounts. [9 notes: THALABI(وهب وغيره,وهب وغيره من أهل الكتب); BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب وغيره); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(روي); ALUSI-FULL(وهب وغيره)]
- 27:35/q08/p2 Two hundred boys and two hundred girls. — Ibn Jurayj and Mujāhid are cited; some reports add exchanged clothing. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد ومقاتل); TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]

## 27:35/q09 Who carried the gift?  [مُرْسِلَةٌ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported carrier was singular, a named leader, or a group.
- 27:35/q09/p1 Al-Mundhir ibn ʿAmr led an embassy of advisers or attendants. — Muqātil and many later reports name the noble leader; the reports vary in how much detail they give about his companions and clothing. [8 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(روي)]
- 27:35/q09/p2 A single envoy carried it, accompanied by attendants and servants. — Al-Qurṭubī records this account; al-Farrāʾ also mentions a report of one woman as envoy. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); FARRA]
- 27:35/q09/p3 A group of messengers carried it, with a leader addressed later. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī infers a group from the plural and a leader from the later singular command; al-Burhān and Itqān note royal custom of sending several. [4 notes: TABATABAI; BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن فارس)]

## 27:35/q10 How are the disguised attendants said to have been prepared?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether disguise used identical dress or swapped male and female dress.

## 27:35/q11 What further challenges did the queen send?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in the number and form of the tests.
- 27:35/q11/p1 She tested Solomon's ability to identify the sexes and hidden contents, pierce an unpierced pearl and thread a crooked bead. — Accounts describe a box containing a pearl and bent-hole bead; the letter prohibited human or jinn help. Some also required water neither from earth nor sky and identification of a royal staff's top and bottom. Ibn al-Jawzī's variant calls the object a pierced ruby to be threaded and sealed. [16 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; THALABI(وهب وغيره من أهل الكتب); TABRISI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,بلقيس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(روي); ALUSI-FULL(وهب وغيره); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 27:35/q11/p2 Her envoy was to judge Solomon by his demeanor: anger marked a king, a gentle reception a prophet. — Thaʿlabī adds instructions for the youths' speech; al-Qurṭubī says the envoy should understand and answer the prophet's words. [6 notes: THALABI(بلقيس); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(بلقيس); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(روي); ALUSI-FULL(وهب وغيره)]

## 27:35/q12 How did Solomon identify the boys and girls?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: The varying reports describe different distinguishing signs and methods.
- 27:35/q12/p1 He distinguished them by their manner of washing for ablution. — Muqātil says girls poured water over the inner forearm and boys over its back. Al-Suddī has boys scoop water and girls pour it onto their hands. Qatāda says boys wash the outer forearm before the inner, girls the reverse. Ibn Jubayr's direction of washing varies across accounts: elbow to palm for boys in al-Māwardī, elbow to palm for girls in al-Qurṭubī. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Biqāʿī add differing face and hand motions. Ibn Kathīr says the descriptions need not conflict. [22 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جبير,السدي,قتادة); TABRISI(وهب وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي,قيل,يعلى بن مسلم عن سعيد بن جبير); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(وهب وغيره)]
- 27:35/q12/p2 He distinguished them by changing the boys' and girls' clothing. — Ibn al-Jawzī transmits this as Mujāhid's version; it also says Solomon refused the gift. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد)]

## 27:35/q13 How did Solomon answer the object and water tests?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Different narratives specify different objects, helpers and methods.
- 27:35/q13/p1 Gabriel disclosed the hidden contents; a termite and worm dealt with the pearl and crooked bead, and horse sweat supplied the requested water. — Muqātil says a worm in soft silver threaded the crooked gem and a termite pierced the other, without human or jinn aid. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī report Gabriel's disclosure. Al-Qurṭubī adds the termite's and worm's requests for tree and fruit food. Accounts vary over which creature carries hair or thread and whether the pearl was pierced or unpierced. Solomon ran horses and collected sweat in crystal cups in Muqātil's version. [18 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(وهب وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(التفسير); QURTUBI-FULL(يعلى بن مسلم عن سعيد بن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل,وهب وغيره)]
- 27:35/q13/p2 An ant threaded a pierced ruby and Solomon sealed it. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives this within his Ibn ʿAbbās version, differing from worm and termite reports. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس)]
- 27:35/q13/p3 He found the inherited staff's base by suspending or dropping it, and used horse sweat for the cup. — Al-Ṭabrisī says he suspended the staff; al-Qurṭubī and Ālūsī say the first end to hit the ground revealed its base. [4 notes: TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(يعلى بن مسلم عن سعيد بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:35/q14 How did Solomon receive the envoys and make the gift seem small?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
- 27:35/q14/p1 He displayed abundant gold or a grand court so that the envoys saw their gift as insignificant. — Qatāda places a gold brick under animals' feet, soiling it with urine and dung; Thābit describes gold-plated bricks along the road. Ibn ʿAbbās mentions gold walls or gilded palaces. Other accounts describe miles or farsakhs of gold and silver paving, columns, ranks of jinn, humans, birds and animals, sea creatures and thousands of seats. The envoys dropped the gifts; one variant says Solomon left an unpaved strip for them. The reported lengths vary: eight miles each way, six farsakhs, and nine farsakhs. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(ثابت البناني); TAB-FULL(ثابت البناني); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي,ثابت البناني); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ثابت البناني); TABRISI(ثابت اليماني,وهب وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض الروايات,سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس,قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(روي); ALUSI-FULL(وهب وغيره)]
- 27:35/q14/p2 He answered the tests, returned the gift, and the queen inferred his prophetic status. — Muqātil includes the box disclosure and horse sweat; Mujāhid says he sorted the attendants. In Saʿīd's report the queen calls it a matter from heaven. [8 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); QURTUBI-FULL(يعلى بن مسلم عن سعيد بن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد)]

## 27:35/q15 Who warned Solomon about the arriving embassy?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Whether the warning came by the hoopoe or directly from God.
- 27:35/q15/p1 The hoopoe informed him of the embassy and its plans. — Thaʿlabī says the hoopoe hurried ahead; al-Qurṭubī says it disclosed clothing and speech; Bursawī's report also begins with the hoopoe's warning. [3 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI(روي)]
- 27:35/q15/p2 God informed Solomon directly. — Al-Qurṭubī preserves this as an alternative to the hoopoe report. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:35/q16 What does 'I shall wait' mean?  [فَنَاظِرَةٌۢ · meaning]
turns on: The notes distinguish the word's primary visual sense from its contextual sense of awaiting.
- 27:35/q16/p1 Nāẓira means waiting or expecting the envoys' return. — Jishumī cites Arab usage of 'looking for' someone as awaiting him. Ibn al-Jawzī gives waiting as a Qur'anic sense beside 2:104, 4:46, 36:49, 57:13 and 38:15; he also notes related 'delay' forms. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the Commander of the Faithful's use of this verse for the sense. [10 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,شيخنا علي بن عبيد الله); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي,كشف الأسرار); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(أمير المؤمنين)]
- 27:35/q16/p2 The root's basic sense is sight, with other contextual uses. — Ibn al-Jawzī says visual perception is primary and figurative usage requires a contextual signal. His teacher lists sight, waiting, mercy, facing another thing, and reflection that produces knowledge; naẓīr is a comparable equal. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(شيخنا علي بن عبيد الله)]

## 27:35/q17 What exactly was she waiting to learn?  [فَنَاظِرَةٌۢ بِمَ يَرْجِعُ ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the awaited return is specified as acceptance or rejection, or described more broadly as intelligence for her decision.
- 27:35/q17/p1 Whether the envoys returned with Solomon's acceptance or rejection of the gift. — The answer would distinguish a king from a prophet; al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān stress that the conditional wait shows she anticipated refusal. [13 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:35/q17/p2 What report, words, or appearance they brought back, so she could choose her next step. — Bayḍāwī emphasizes Solomon's disposition; al-Biqāʿī explicitly says words or appearance and avoidance of premature confrontation; Bursawī says she would act as circumstances required. [6 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 27:35/q18 How is 'fa-nāẓiratun bi-ma yarjiʿu' parsed?  [فَنَاظِرَةٌۢ بِمَ يَرْجِعُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the preposition is governed by 'awaiting' or the following verb; the interrogative's obligatory initial position is the decisive grammatical argument.
- 27:35/q18/p1 Fa-nāẓiratun coordinates with mursilatun; bi-ma belongs to yarjiʿu, and the interrogative clause supplies what she awaits. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn say the question suspends nāẓira; Ālūsī says its clause is the complement of waiting. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows either an explanation of her waiting or a new sentence; interrogative mā has clause-initial precedence, creating the marked order and uncertainty. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:35/q18/p2 Bi-ma attaches directly to nāẓira. — Al-Ḥawfī's analysis is preserved only in rebuttal; Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Suyūṭī and Ālūsī reject it because the interrogative must begin its clause. [0 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي)-; SAMIN-DURR(الهوفي)-; ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي)-; ITQAN-]

## 27:35/q19 Why is the alif absent from interrogative 'bi-ma'?  [بِمَ · grammar]
turns on: The usual contracted Qur'anic spelling is agreed; sources differ on how acceptable poetic alif retention is.
- 27:35/q19/p1 Interrogative mā loses its alif after a preposition, distinguishing it from declarative or relative mā. — Al-Farrāʾ says the interrogative means 'what thing'; Ṭabarī and others cite 78:1 and 4:97. Al-Zajjāj treats the preposition and mā as one unit. Al-Naḥḥās contrasts declarative fīmā ʿindaka with interrogative fīm naẓarta. Poetry may retain the alif in ʿalā mā or fīmā; al-Farrāʾ calls this permissible while al-Naḥḥās calls it irregular against Qur'anic usage. Bursawī glosses the question 'with what/by what means?'. [18 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج,الفراء,المفضل); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; FARRA+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 27:35/q19/p2 The full-alif form is also permitted in interrogation. — Al-Farrāʾ cites poems transmitted by al-Mufaḍḍal with fīmā and ʿalā mā; Ṭabarī cites a wāfir line and al-Qurṭubī a written ʿalā mā. Al-Naḥḥās objects that this is irregular, despite its poetic attestation. [8 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(المفضل); QURTUBI-FULL; FARRA+; QURTUBI-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء)+; NAHHAS(الفراء)-]

## 27:35/q20 What pausal or recitational form is noted at interrogative mā?  [بِمَ · readings]
- 27:35/q20/p1 A pausal hāʾ may be added after interrogative mā. — The recitation note says the hāʾ only marks the pause; it leaves meaning unchanged and confirms that mā is interrogative rather than relative. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 27:35/q21 Why is 'messengers' plural despite a later singular address?  [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the singular command in 27:37 proves only one envoy or addresses the leader of several.
- 27:35/q21/p1 The plural can denote a single envoy, as the next verse's singular 'return' suggests. — Al-Farrāʾ says one woman was reportedly sent; Ibn Qutayba and Ibn Fāris use 27:37's singular command as support. [5 notes: FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; BURHAN(ابن فارس); ITQAN(ابن فارس)]
- 27:35/q21/p2 The embassy was a group, and the singular later addresses its leader or uses a generic messenger. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān object that kings ordinarily send groups and address their chief. Ṭabāṭabāʾī infers a leader. Abū Ḥayyān says an omitted 'messenger' before 'when he came' can be generic with variable number and gender, and al-Ṭabrisī supplies the omitted rasūlan. [6 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(ابن فارس); TABATABAI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABRISI]

## 27:35/q22 Is there a reported plural reading in the following response?  [ٱلْمُرْسَلُونَ · readings]
- 27:35/q22/p1 ʿAbd Allāh reads 'they came' and 'return [plural]', agreeing with the plural envoys. — Abū Ḥayyān gives jāʾū and irjiʿū as the matching forms in the next verses. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 27:35/q23 What moral or legal status does this offered gift have?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · law]
turns on: How the recipient's religious duty and the donor's purpose affect acceptance, and whether conflicting reports require abrogation.
- 27:35/q23/p1 An ordinary gift offered freely to build affection is lawful, but a gift intended to buy away truth is forbidden bribery. — Al-Qurṭubī says Solomon demanded submission, so Bilqīs could not ransom that demand with a gift. He notes the Prophet accepted and honored gifts, not charity, and says earlier prophets did likewise; he qualifies the rule for polytheist givers. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 27:35/q23/p2 A gift from someone seeking victory over Muslims, their land, or entry into Islam by means of it should be refused. — Al-Qurṭubī prefers reconciling apparently conflicting reports on polytheist gifts by the donor's purpose; Solomon's case exemplifies refusal. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 27:35/q23/p3 Reports prohibiting and allowing polytheists' gifts were held by some scholars to abrogate one another. — Al-Qurṭubī cites a hadith prohibiting polytheist aid and gifts against an attributed Mālik report of the Prophet accepting a gift. He records another view denying abrogation. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(جماعة من العلماء)]
- 27:35/q23/p4 The reports about polytheists' gifts can be reconciled without abrogation. — Other scholars deny abrogation; al-Qurṭubī prefers distinguishing hostile or manipulative aims from ordinary gifts. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(آخرون); QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 27:35/q24 What effect does exchanging gifts have?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · law]
- 27:35/q24/p1 Gifts cultivate affection, remove rancor and make meetings gracious. — Al-Qurṭubī gives reports from ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī, Muʿāwiya ibn al-Ḥakam and Ibn Shihāb; he says one Muʿāwiya route is singular and unreliable, as is a route through ʿUthmān al-Waqqāṣī. Yūnus glosses sukhīma as rancor. Two poets praise the closeness and high value of gifts. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن شهاب,شاعر,عثمان الوقاصي,مالك عن عطاء بن عبد الله الخراساني,معاوية بن الحكم,يونس)]

## 27:35/q25 Must those sitting with a gift recipient share in the gift?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether 'sharing' in the cited hadith means property, specific food, courtesy, or shared joy.
- 27:35/q25/p1 They literally share the gift. — Al-Qurṭubī cites a hadith that those sitting with a person share in his gift; one interpretation takes that share as an actual portion. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 27:35/q25/p2 They share only fruit and similar foods. — Abū Yūsuf limits the companions' entitlement in that way. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(أبو يوسف)]
- 27:35/q25/p3 Sharing expresses generosity and manners, with no right to compel the recipient. — Al-Qurṭubī says the report applies to communal groups such as the People of the Bench and hospice residents; a scholar personally given a gift need not share it. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 27:35/q25/p4 They share in the happiness occasioned by the gift. — Some interpreters take the hadith as shared joy rather than ownership of the object. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 27:35/q26 Does receiving a letter call for a reply?  [مُرْسِلَةٌ · law]
- 27:35/q26/p1 A letter containing a greeting should receive a written return greeting. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the old custom of replying to letters and al-Qurṭubī's analogy with face-to-face greetings. Ibn ʿAbbās reportedly considered a reply obligatory. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers the written reply by analogy, while noting that face-to-face silence causes hostility more readily; he found no formal school ruling and no prophetic example except the reply to Musaylima ending with peace for one who follows guidance. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,القرطبي); IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 27:35/q27 What inward meaning is assigned to the queen's gift?  [بِهَدِيَّةٍۢ · inward]
- 27:35/q27/p1 It represents sensory perceptions, appetites, imagined pleasures and material supports adorned by thoughts and motives. — Al-Qāshānī makes Solomon's acceptance an inclination toward the self and his rejection a firmer inclination toward the Real. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:35/q28 How is the poem cited in discussion of the passage evaluated?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The disputed relation of the opening exclamation to the slaughter and the literary merit of the simile.
- 27:35/q28/p1 The verse can recall the day of slaughtering a mount or connect back to Dārat Jaljal; the first hemistich is foolish. — Al-Bāqillānī allows both constructions. He rejects a critic's claim that 'O wonder' refers to slaughtering for women as unsupported by the wording. [1 notes: BAQILLANI; BAQILLANI(بعض الأدباء)-]
- 27:35/q28/p2 'O wonder' refers to the young speaker's slaughtering his mount for the women. — An unnamed adab critic proposes this; al-Bāqillānī rejects it as far-fetched. [0 notes: BAQILLANI(بعض الأدباء)-]
- 27:35/q28/p3 The praised simile and food boast have serious defects. — Al-Bāqillānī says definite 'meat' and indefinite 'fat' obscure whether the same animal is described and leave one comparison unfinished; silk for fat is commonplace, and 'twisted' mostly fills the rhyme rather than creating badīʿ. He questions boasting of food for loved ones, while noting Arab pride in feeding guests, Persian disapproval, and a possible joking exception. [4 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 27:35/q29 How many ranks of connected madd are reported in the recitational discussion?  [* · readings]
turns on: Authorities differ in grouping the durations and in whether a pause before hamza counts as an additional rank.
- 27:35/q29/p1 Four ranks, from fullest to omitted incidental lengthening. — Ibn al-Jazarī cites Ṭāhir ibn Ghalbūn, al-Dānī, Ibn Balīma, Ibn al-Bādhish and others. He rejects a fifth rank supposedly implied by al-Taysīr. Al-Dānī assigns Sūsī and Ibn Kathīr short separate madd but medium connected madd, and Dūrī and Qālūn medium connected madd. [4 notes: NASHR(الداني,طاهر بن غلبون والداني وابن بليمة وابن الباذش); NASHR+]
- 27:35/q29/p2 A fifth, longer rank is transmitted for specified routes pausing before hamza. — Al-Dānī's Jāmiʿ adds it; Ibn al-Jazarī interprets it as the length of a pause with fully prolonged madd. [1 notes: NASHR(الداني)]
- 27:35/q29/p3 Three ranks are counted by another group of authorities. — Ibn Mihrān, Ibn al-Faḥḥām, al-Ahwāzī, al-ʿIrāqī and others give three; the excerpt begins with middle and higher ranks. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن مهران وابن الفحام والأهوازي والعراقي وابنه وابن الفخر الجاجاني)]

## 27:35/q30 What evidence is cited for prolongation in recitation?  [* · readings]
- 27:35/q30/p1 A report from Ibn Masʿūd is treated as evidence for the rule of madd. — Ibn Masʿūd objected that a recitation was not how the Prophet taught him and cited 'innamā al-ṣadaqātu lil-fuqarāʾi wa-l-masākīn' as the form to prolong. Ibn al-Jazarī calls the transmitters trustworthy and attributes the report to al-Ṭabarānī's Muʿjam al-kabīr. [2 notes: NASHR(ابن مسعود)]
