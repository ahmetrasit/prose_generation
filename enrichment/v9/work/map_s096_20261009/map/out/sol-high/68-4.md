# 68:4: 12 questions

## 68:4/q01 What do the emphatic particles add to the assertion?  [وَإِنَّكَ لَعَلَىٰ · grammar]
- 68:4/q01/p1 The inna and lām strongly confirm the Prophet's character; the lām moves into the predicate after inna. — Al-Suyūṭī explains that the lām of initiation cannot remain sentence-initial alongside inna and cites its positions with subjects and predicates. Elmalılı adds the oath response and magnifying tanwīn; Ibn ʿĀshūr counts three emphases answering the deniers. [4 notes: ITQAN; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:4/q02 What does ʿalā convey in the phrase?  [لَعَلَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether ʿalā is read as possession and establishment or as mastery.
- 68:4/q02/p1 It presents great character as something firmly possessed and inhabited by the Prophet. — Al-Dāmghānī takes ʿalā as possession or inherence and compares 39:22. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it metaphorical elevation and firm establishment, citing 2:5, 27:79, 43:43, and 22:67; the establishment covers person and mission. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:4/q02/p2 It pictures the Prophet as master of the noble traits. — Rāzī says the preposition depicts command over character like a master over a servant. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 68:4/q03 What does khuluq mean and how is it distinguished from related words?  [خُلُقٍ · meaning]
turns on: The extent of the word's semantic field and how its cognates differ.
- 68:4/q03/p1 It is a settled inward disposition or cultivated manner from which conduct flows readily. — Al-Māwardī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī and Ibn al-Jawzī contrast cultivated khuluq with innate khaym; al-Aʿshā's poem illustrates khaym. Rāzī stresses effortless action versus the act itself. Al-Rāghib and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ contrast inward khuluq with visible khalq; Asad includes habitual conduct that becomes second nature. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾ note unqualified khuluq usually means good character. Elmalılı gives its plurals huluk and akhlāq and Turkish usage. [19 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأعشى); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الماوردي); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعشى); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); ELMALILI(Râgıb); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 68:4/q03/p2 Its cognate khalāq denotes an allotted share, particularly otherworldly recompense. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ records al-Farrāʾ on 2:102, Abū ʿUbayda on 3:77, al-Rāghib's narrower earned-virtue sense, and al-Ṭabarī's transmitted share glosses including Ibn ʿAbbās's 'what sustains him.' She prefers share, citing a Prophetic saying, and compares 40:47, 42:20 and 4:7. She also distinguishes ikhtilāq, invention or fabricated speech. [7 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو عبيدة,الراغب,الفراء,مجاهد والسدي وسفيان); BINTSHATI-IJAZ+]
- 68:4/q03/p3 Khuluq can include a settled readiness for true knowledge as well as practical conduct. — Rāzī replies to his own theoretical-versus-practical distinction: habit can concern cognition or action, and a soul can be effortlessly receptive to true knowledge and unreceptive to false belief. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 68:4/q04 How is the greatness of the character expressed?  [خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the adjective heightens human character or alludes to divine attributes.
- 68:4/q04/p1 ʿAẓīm marks exceptional excellence and rank; the indefinite khuluq magnifies a quality beyond full human comprehension. — Al-Dāmghānī glosses ʿaẓīm as excellent or good. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains metaphorical rank from physical size. Elmalılı points to magnifying tanwīn and says no other description fully captures the quality; al-Ṭabrisī calls this the highest praise. [5 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABRISI]
- 68:4/q04/p2 The adjective hints that the Prophet bears divine attributes. — Al-Kashf sees an implicit allusion in ʿaẓīm; al-Murṣafī says ʿĀʾisha reverently left this sense of her answer unstated. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,المرصفي)]

## 68:4/q05 What is the Prophet's great character?  [خُلُقٍ عَظِيمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources variously identify character with Islam, Qur'anic conduct, human virtues, social conduct, or inward states; objections concern lexical usage, chronology, context and the scope of the praise.
- 68:4/q05/p1 It is the Qur'an embodied as manners, obedience, and avoidance of its prohibitions. — ʿĀʾisha repeatedly calls his character the Qur'an, sometimes reciting 23:1–10; ʿAlī, ʿAṭiyya and al-Ḥasan call it Qur'anic discipline. Qatāda specifies commands and prohibitions; 7:199 and 16:90 supply examples. Al-Qurṭubī prefers ʿĀʾisha's Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim report; al-Zajjāj prefers divinely commanded Qur'anic conduct. Ibn Kathīr adds God-given virtues; al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı explain that he embodied all virtues and rejected defects, with 11:112. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes the early Meccan chronology objection. Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī and Asad list transmission routes. - 68:4/q05/p2 It is the great religion of Islam, or the divinely entrusted way of life. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and others give this gloss; some call Islam God's dearest and most pleasing religion. Al-Ḍaḥḥāk includes the entire entrusted command. Al-Naḥḥās expands it to doctrine, way and obedience; Asad prefers 'way of life' from dīn. Rāzī objects that religion chiefly perfects knowledge whereas character perfects action; Ṭabāṭabāʾ accepts the reports only insofar as Islam includes contextual social virtue; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the Qur'an nowhere uses khuluq for religion. [33 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الضحاك,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس ومجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وأبو مالك); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس ومجاهد والحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس,الله تعالى); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وأبو مالك والسدي والربيع بن أنس والضحاك وابن زيد); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك,ابن أبزى وسعيد بن جبير,ابن عباس); ELMALILI(Mücâhid,ابن عباس,ضحاك); TABATABAI(أبو جعفر); STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ); ASAD-NOTES(Asad)+; RAZI-FULL-; TABATABAI-; BINTSHATI(بعضهم)-]
- 68:4/q05/p3 It is the highest noble human disposition, gathering every virtue. — Al-Māwardī prefers the apparent sense. Al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī cite his mission to perfect noble traits. Al-Jubbāʾī lists patience, generosity, judgment, endurance, pardon and freedom from envy. Ibn ʿĀshūr enumerates piety, knowledge, justice, humility, chastity, courage and more; Rāzī distinguishes practical perfection from knowledge at 4:113 and says 6:90 gathered the earlier prophets' diverse virtues in him. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ grounds the praise in virtues known since youth. [23 notes: NAHHAS; QUSHAYRI; THALABI(قائل غير مسمى); SULAMI(الجنيد); TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عطية); BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; MAWARDI-FULL+]
- 68:4/q05/p4 It is exceptional patience, gentleness, forgiveness and generosity in dealing with people. — Al-Zamakhsharī and al-Bayḍāwī stress bearing abuse; al-Ālūsī says he bore more than other resolute prophets. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ lists steadfastness, tact and humility as the context's social virtues; al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī mention honoring the community. Rāzī details gracious speech and lenience, and Ibn ʿAṭiyya names justice, pardon and maintaining ties. [9 notes: TUSTARI; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]
- 68:4/q05/p5 He outwardly lives with people while his inward attention remains fixed on God. — Al-Thaʿlabī repeats this and a sage counsels sincerity with God alongside truth with people; al-Junayd and al-Ṭabrisī give the same outward/inward distinction. [6 notes: THALABI(بعض الحكماء,قائل غير مسمى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الحكماء,جنيد); TABRISI(قيل)]
- 68:4/q05/p6 His only aspiration is God, so created things and people's harshness do not capture his heart. — Al-Junayd names sole aspiration; al-Wāsiṭī says he neither disputes nor is drawn into dispute after Night Journey knowledge. Al-Ḥusayn says beholding the Real makes harshness ineffective and creation small; al-Qāsim says the cosmos leaves no effect. Abū Saʿīd says no concern remains apart from God. [9 notes: SULAMI(أبو سعيد الخراز,الحسين,القاسم,الواسطي); THALABI(جنيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جنيد); QURTUBI-FULL(الجنيد); STUDYQURAN]
- 68:4/q05/p7 He relinquished both this world and the next for the Real. — Al-Wāsiṭī and Ibn ʿAṭāʾ give the exchange; al-Nasafī says he was generous with both worlds and relied upon their Creator; al-Qushayrī reports the refusal of earthly keys and the Night Journey vision without looking away, citing 53:17. [5 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,الواسطي); THALABI(الواسطي); NASAFI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
- 68:4/q05/p8 He embodies divine attributes and is sustained by God rather than his own strength. — Sahl traces mercy to abandoning reliance on personal power and cites God's patient self-description to David; al-Wāsiṭī places Muhammad's manifestation of divine qualities beside Jesus, Āṣif and Moses' staff, while insisting they belong to God. Abū Saʿīd names generosity, forbearance and forgiveness and cites the report about more than one hundred traits. Al-Qāshānī cites 16:127; al-Murṣafī reads ʿĀʾisha this way. [11 notes: TUSTARI(الله); SULAMI(أبو سعيد,أبو سعيد القرشي,الواسطي); BIQAI-FULL(الواسطي); QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,المرصفي)]
- 68:4/q05/p9 His self-will and independent choice disappear in submission to divine decree. — Muḥammad ibn ʿAlī al-Tirmidhī calls abandonment of will his greatest character; Ibn ʿAṭāʾ adds surrender to decree and forgiveness and annihilation of self and attachments. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء,محمد بن علي الترمذي)]
- 68:4/q05/p10 He sees through God's witnessing and enjoys divine disclosure in his inner secret. — Al-Ḥusayn contrasts divine witnessing with ruinous self-seeing; al-Wāsiṭī speaks of the sweetness of beholding and lights adorning the secret, with a third direct communion favoring him above creation. [4 notes: SULAMI(الحسين,الواسطي)]
- 68:4/q05/p11 His character marks movement beyond moral attributes into annihilation and abiding in the divine Essence. — Al-Ḥusayn says he reached the Essence's reality and that passing beyond annihilation is followed by abiding. [1 notes: SULAMI(الحسين)]
- 68:4/q05/p12 He devoted himself wholly, then was veiled after presence. — Fāris gives this sequence as the state of a person of great character. [1 notes: SULAMI(فارس)]
- 68:4/q05/p13 Its exaltation contains treasures of provision. — Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh gives this inward explanation. [1 notes: SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ)]
- 68:4/q05/p14 It is the essence of faith and the reality of divine oneness. — Jaʿfar gives this inward gloss. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 68:4/q05/p15 It was refined through estrangement, hardship and withdrawal from pleasures. — Al-Wāsiṭī says God placed him in the Hijaz and exposed him to hardship until moral blemishes were removed, then called his character great. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 68:4/q05/p16 It is a soul naturally drawn to the unseen and away from bodily and worldly pleasures. — Rāzī draws this reading from ʿĀʾisha's Qur'an description. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 68:4/q05/p17 It is exceptionally high because God granted him favors beyond those of other prophets. — Al-Wāsiṭī places the fashioning of this character after surpassing grants of divine favor. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 68:4/q06 What examples show the Prophet's character in action?  [خُلُقٍ · reports]
turns on: The reports supply different incidents and descriptions, rather than competing accounts of one event.
- 68:4/q06/p1 He answered calls graciously, served others and bore ordinary frustrations without rebuke. — ʿĀʾisha says he answered family and companions 'at your service'; Anas says ten years, or eleven in another report, passed without reproach. Al-Barāʾ praises appearance and manners. The broken platter episode ends in calm replacement; another report says he pardoned, avoided obscenity and chose the easier lawful course. Maymūna's locked-door report and Anas's decree explanation add particular incidents; Ibn Kathīr names al-Shamāʾil as a collection. [14 notes: RAZI-FULL(أنس,عائشة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس بن مالك,البراء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أنس,البراء,عائشة); DURR-FULL(أنس,عائشة,ميمونة); QURTUBI-FULL(عائشة)]
- 68:4/q06/p2 He endured injury, forgave his people and put his community before himself. — Al-Junayd recalls his prayer to forgive those who did not know; al-Qushayrī contrasts his 'my community' with others' 'myself,' and Gabriel explains 7:199 as reconnecting, giving and pardoning those who do the opposite. Biqāʿī recounts kind service to a man who pulled his cloak. [6 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 68:4/q06/p3 His noble character was taught by God and was central to his prophetic mission. — Reports say God educated him excellently and sent him to complete noble character and good works. The versions are transmitted by al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Ṭabāṭabāʾ, Kur'an Yolu and Study Quran; al-Baghawī states no grade and Ibn Kathīr notes Aḥmad's unique route. [10 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن جابر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL(رسول الله); TABATABAI(رسول الله); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 68:4/q06/p4 His character was known even before revelation through trustworthiness, truth and service. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ cites Abū Ṭālib's marriage speech, Khadīja's list of kinship, truth, trust, hospitality and help, and Quraysh's title 'the trustworthy.' [4 notes: BINTSHATI(أبو طالب,خديجة)]

## 68:4/q07 What occasion is reported for the verse's revelation?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported occasions identify different episodes and scopes of the passage.
- 68:4/q07/p1 His gracious response to anyone who summoned him occasioned the verse. — ʿĀʾisha says no one had better character: to family and companions alike he answered 'at your service.' Al-Wāḥidī, al-Suyūṭī and al-Durr transmit the occasion. [3 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB(عائشة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 68:4/q07/p2 A report locates the surrounding polemic in insults to ʿAlī after the Prophet honored him. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says Quraysh's slander of ʿAlī explains the passage's contrast of those astray and guided. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الضحاك)]

## 68:4/q08 What does good character comprise and require?  [خُلُقٍ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize practical social virtues or an inward spiritual transformation.
- 68:4/q08/p1 It joins devotion to God with just, generous and gentle treatment of people. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names justice, beneficence, pardon and ties; Ibn al-Mubārak names a pleasant face, doing good and avoiding harm. Biqāʿī divides duties toward God, rulings and people, and links Islam especially to modesty; a report calls modesty its defining character. The Prophet's instruction to Abū Dharr joins reverence, repairing bad deeds and good treatment; Rāzī details generosity, speech, restraint of anger and lenient dealings. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن المبارك); RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 68:4/q08/p2 The greatest spiritual gift is changing a blameworthy trait into a praiseworthy one. — Sahl says other marvels soon pass, while character transformation endures; al-Muntakhab likewise describes divine favor removing blame and supplying praise. [2 notes: TUSTARI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 68:4/q08/p3 It is the greatest spiritual station because it connects one to divine attributes. — Sahl and al-Wāsiṭī distinguish this connection from lower stations tied to ordinary people, citing God's patience and His command to David to take on His qualities. [2 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(الواسطي)]

## 68:4/q09 What rewards or standing do reports attach to good character?  [خُلُقٍ · reports]
turns on: The reports name distinct rewards and rankings; some combine them in a single narration.
- 68:4/q09/p1 It brings Paradise, while bad character or misuse of the mouth and sexual organ brings the Fire. — ʿAlī's report and the report through al-Riḍā contrast Paradise and Fire. In Abū Hurayra's report, God-consciousness and good character chiefly admit to Paradise, while mouth and sexual organ lead to Hell; al-Tirmidhī calls the latter ṣaḥīḥ gharīb. Study Quran repeats the Paradise point. [4 notes: THALABI(علي بن أبي طالب); TABRISI(الرضا علي بن موسى ع); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 68:4/q09/p2 It weighs especially heavily in the judgment balance. — Abū al-Dardāʾ's report is repeated by al-Thaʿlabī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī. Al-Qurṭubī gives one route graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ, adding that God hates obscene speech, and another called gharīb. [6 notes: THALABI(أبو الدرداء); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 68:4/q09/p3 It raises its possessor to the rank of one who prays by night and fasts by day. — ʿĀʾisha's and later Prophetic reports give the comparison; al-Qurṭubī's combined balance-and-rank route is called gharīb by al-Tirmidhī. [5 notes: THALABI(عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 68:4/q09/p4 Those of finest character are especially loved and near to God or the Prophet; gossips and arrogant speakers are far. — Abū Hurayra's reports oppose easy, loving people to divisive tale-bearers. Jābir's report places the best-mannered nearest the Prophet on Judgment Day and affected arrogant speakers farthest; al-Tirmidhī calls it ḥasan gharīb, and explains mutafayhiqūn as arrogant with an Abū Hurayra parallel. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Study Quran transmit best-character sayings. [6 notes: THALABI(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]

## 68:4/q10 How does this praise relate to the surrounding denial of madness and the sūra?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes connect the praise to refutation, consolation, the opening Pen, or the wider moral contrast.
- 68:4/q10/p1 The character praise refutes the charge of madness and strengthens the Prophet under deniers' abuse. — Abū Ḥayyān says the opening answers charges of poetry, magic and madness and follows a prior contrast of blessed and wretched; the reward promise and this praise encourage patience. Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds keen understanding and good temperament. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees renewed refutation with matching emphases; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ sees early reassurance and a closing echo at 68:51. Iṣlāḥī contrasts the Prophet's morals with poets and soothsayers; Kur'an Yolu calls him a trustworthy model. Some, reported by al-Ālūsī, argue good morals and madness cannot coexist. [17 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 68:4/q10/p2 The ordering and wording protect and honor the Prophet before quoting his accusers. — Biqāʿī says denial precedes the accusation and character praise comes before its quotation at 68:51 to soften the impact; he also contrasts the Prophet's reason with his opponents' lack of it after 68:3. Al-Wāḥidī sees praise joined to a reward promise. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 68:4/q10/p3 The verse is a supreme sign in the Pen's written testimony to revelation and character. — Elmalılı links it to 68:1, prefers the primordial or Highest Pen because Muhammad did not read or write before revelation (29:48; 42:52), and reads reward and moral contrast as clarifying the Pen's significance. [2 notes: ELMALILI]
- 68:4/q10/p4 The sūra's account of social vice contrasts with this character. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says al-Qalam's hammaz and list of bad traits with a command not to obey fit its social-conduct focus; unlike al-Humaza, it gives little detail about the final outcome. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 68:4/q11 Why are the Qur'anic words fitting to this praise?  [* · rhetoric]
- 68:4/q11/p1 Divine knowledge supplies precisely fitting words and meanings, making the Qur'an maximally eloquent. — Al-Bārizī says humans cannot reliably summon each meaning with its best expression, whereas God knows both; his general principle explains the Qur'an's eloquence. [1 notes: ITQAN(البارزي)]
- 68:4/q11/p2 The verse stacks emphatic devices as assurance and as reinforcement of the preceding oath response. — Elmalılı counts inna, lām, magnifying tanwīn and ʿaẓīm, tied to 68:1–2. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 68:4/q12 What further testimony illustrates the praise of his character?  [* · reports]
turns on: The transmitted illustrations invoke testimony, prayer, obedience, or perception.
- 68:4/q12/p1 ʿĀʾisha testified that he was the best of people in character and that his character was the Qur'an. — Al-Durr transmits both parts of her testimony. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 68:4/q12/p2 The Prophet prayed for beautiful character corresponding to the beautiful form God gave him. — Study Quran transmits the prayer as an analogy between inward character and outward form. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Prophet)]
- 68:4/q12/p3 His character fulfilled God's command to take the forgiving course. — Al-Thaʿlabī cites 7:199 as the divine instruction he carried out. [1 notes: THALABI(قائل غير مسمى)]
- 68:4/q12/p4 God made created colors small in his sight so he could know their Creator. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports this as an explanation of how God magnified his character. [1 notes: THALABI(قائل غير مسمى)]
