# 26:115: 10 questions

## 26:115/q01 What does the restriction إِنْ ... إِلَّا convey?  [إِنْ أَنَا۠ إِلَّا · rhetoric]
- 26:115/q01/p1 It confines Noah to the role of a clear warner, excluding compliance with the chiefs' wishes as part of his commission. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this an additional restriction that limits the described subject to one quality. Alūsī reports a view that placing the pronoun after negation makes this and the preceding clause together answer the demand to expel believers; he notes a difficulty with that view. Sāmarrāʾī points to his earlier comparison of negation particles and cites parallel formulations of being only a clear warner, without stating a further conclusion here. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:115/q02 How is أَنَا۠ read?  [أَنَا۠ · readings]
- 26:115/q02/p1 The final alif can be shortened, producing ana rather than anā. — QIRAAT-ER names Qālūn and Ibn Kathīr as examples of the shortened reading. It says the difference changes cadence but not meaning; anā is the contrasted unshortened form. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Qālūn, Ibn Kathīr)]

## 26:115/q03 What role does Noah claim by calling himself نَذِيرٌۭ?  [نَذِيرٌۭ · interpretation]
- 26:115/q03/p1 Noah is a messenger whose assigned task is to warn people plainly and call them to God; their response and standing are not his to control. — Muqātil and the Muqātil report in Wāḥidī say 'messenger'; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and Elmalılı simply say 'clear warner.' Ṭabarī says Noah warns from his Lord, with no counsel concealed. Ṭabrisī mentions a call to obedience. Rāzī says the mission addresses rejecters and makes accepting followers near to Noah. Qurṭubī says Noah conveys God's message without favoring the rich. Bayḍāwī, Būrsawī, and Alūsī say the warning addresses accountable people regardless of rank; Bayḍāwī also offers the duty of warning with proof as an alternative account. Nasafī says sound proof leaves hearers responsible for their course; Abū Ḥayyān also mentions sound proof. Ibn Kathīr says believing followers belong with Noah regardless of status. Biqāʿī excludes probing inner states or imposing demands on followers. Alūsī's alternative says clear proof has already been delivered. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the role explains refusal of the chiefs' demand. Iṣlāḥī says Noah has discharged his duty and rejection is the rejecter's responsibility. [18 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:115/q04 What does Noah warn people about?  [نَذِيرٌۭ · interpretation]
- 26:115/q04/p1 He warns of the Fire and divine punishment for unbelief and sins, urging people away from what displeases God. — The Ibn ʿAbbās report in Ibn Abī Ḥātim specifies the Fire. The Ibn ʿAbbās report in Wāḥidī adds that Noah clarifies what brings people near to God. Ṭabarī specifies punishment for disbelief. Bayḍāwī names unbelief and sins; Alūsī describes deterrence from what God dislikes. Ṭabrisī says Noah calls people to obedience. Būrsawī stresses that this warning addresses both elite and poor accountable people. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:115/q05 What does مُّبِينٌۭ add to the warning?  [مُّبِينٌۭ · meaning]
- 26:115/q05/p1 The warning is made clear through explanation and proof, without obscurity or concealed counsel. — Ibn ʿĀshūr derives mubīn from transitive abāna, 'to make clear or explain.' Ṭabarī says Noah made his warning plain and concealed no counsel, mentioning 2:168. Wāḥidī's Ibn ʿAbbās report says he clarifies what brings people near God. Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān say sound proof distinguishes truth from falsehood; Abū Ḥayyān calls him a manifest warner. Bayḍāwī and Alūsī give clear proof as the alternative account of his duty. Biqāʿī says he explains without obscurity. Ṭabrisī calls him a clear warner inviting obedience. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:115/q06 Where else does the clear-warner phrase occur?  [نَذِيرٌۭ مُّبِينٌۭ · links]
- 26:115/q06/p1 It recurs for Noah and is also used often for Muhammad. — The Study Quran lists Noah parallels at 7:184, 11:25, and 71:2, and Muhammad parallels at 15:89, 22:49, 29:50, 38:65, 38:70, 46:9, 51:50, and 67:26. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 26:115/q07 What other role is نَذِيرٌۭ often paired with?  [نَذِيرٌۭ · links]
- 26:115/q07/p1 A bearer of glad tidings, bashīr. — The Study Quran cites 4:165 for this frequent pairing. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 26:115/q08 How does Noah's declared role answer the demand to expel believers?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes offer different grounds for refusing expulsion: warning for all ranks, a duty already fulfilled by clear proof, God's authority over believers' hidden states, or the restrictive form of the reply. Bayḍāwī and Alūsī explicitly present the first two grounds as alternatives.
- 26:115/q08/p1 His warning addresses people regardless of status, and believers belong with him; he cannot exclude the poor to please the wealthy. — Ṭabarī says he refuses to expel those who believe and follow his message. Ṭabrisī rejects barring people from faith for being called low-status. Rāzī says acceptance brings a person near Noah and rejection makes one distant, so his mission supplies no grounds to expel believers. Qurṭubī says God did not send Noah to favor the rich and obedience makes even a poor person fortunate before God. Bayḍāwī's first explanation, Būrsawī, and Alūsī's first explanation say warning addresses all accountable people regardless of rank; expelling the poor to attract the wealthy contradicts the mission. Ibn Kathīr says obedient believers belong with Noah whatever their rank. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the role explains refusal of the chiefs' demand. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:115/q08/p2 He is charged with delivering clear warning and proof, not satisfying listeners by expelling others; rejection remains each listener's responsibility. — Bayḍāwī gives this as an alternative to the equal-address explanation. Nasafī says sound proof distinguishes truth from falsehood and leaves people responsible for their course. Alūsī gives the same alternative and says the proof has already been delivered. Iṣlāḥī says Noah will not abandon believers for acceptance, has discharged his duty as an open warner, and rejecters alone bear responsibility. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:115/q08/p3 He is not charged with investigating believers' inward states or past deeds; their accounting belongs to God. — Biqāʿī says Noah is not tasked with probing inward states or imposing demands on followers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this statement explains why Noah will neither expel believers nor investigate their past to judge them, since God handles accounting. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:115/q08/p4 The pronoun's place after negation restricts Noah to warning, so both preceding clauses reply by denying that he will follow the chiefs' wishes. — Alūsī reports this as one view of the two clauses, while noting a difficulty with it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 26:115/q09 Is Noah's similar exchange in Hūd the same occasion?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the accounts record one exchange with complementary details or two occasions; Ibn ʿĀshūr does not decide.
- 26:115/q09/p1 It may be the same exchange retold with complementary details. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Hūd 11:29 and 11:31 and leaves this possibility open. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:115/q09/p2 It may be a separate occasion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Hūd 11:29 and 11:31 and also leaves this possibility open. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:115/q10 How does Noah's call fit the other prophet narratives in this sūra?  [* · links]
- 26:115/q10/p1 Noah, Moses, and Abraham call to the same essential principles; their peoples' beliefs and responses are alike, so the three narrative groups convey the same message. — Kur'an Yolu compares the narratives and mentions 11:25 and 11:49. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
