# 68:9: 11 questions

## 68:9/q01 What does waddū say about the opponents' wish?  [وَدُّوا۟ · interpretation]
- 68:9/q01/p1 They loved or wished for an accommodation that they actually proposed. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says a merely private wish would not explain the command against obeying them; their offer was conveyed to the Prophet. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 68:9/q02 How does law tudhinu function after waddū?  [لَوْ تُدْهِنُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether law voices an unattained wish, retains a conditional apodosis, or acts as a verbal noun particle.
- 68:9/q02/p1 Law expresses a wish, 'if only you would compromise.' — Al-Burhān compares 11:80, 2:167, and 39:58. Bursevī calls it optative; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says it conveys a remote, frustrated wish and reports the comparison with layta. [4 notes: BURHAN; BURSEVI; BINTSHATI]
- 68:9/q02/p2 Law remains conditional, with an unspoken object of waddū and an unspoken answer to law. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this to most grammarians; al-Samīn prefers 'they wished for your accommodation; had you done so, they would have rejoiced.' Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr also supply an omitted consequence. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(جمهور النحاة); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 68:9/q02/p3 Law can serve as a verbal noun particle like an: they wished for his accommodation. — Abū Ḥayyān attributes this to Basrans; Ibn ʿĀshūr names al-Farrāʾ, al-Fārisī, al-Tabrīzī and Ibn Mālik. Al-Itqān reports this construal for the reading without nūn. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الفراء والفارسي والتبريزي وابن مالك); ITQAN(بعضهم)]

## 68:9/q03 What does tudhinu or idhān mean?  [تُدْهِنُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the oil metaphor denotes ordinary softness or specifically deceptive religious laxity.
- 68:9/q03/p1 It means softening, lenience, accommodation or ingratiating speech and conduct. — Al-Layth and Abū al-Haytham stress gentle speech; al-Farrāʾ and al-Mubarrad's discussants specify softness toward someone undeserving. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Jushumī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ derive the figurative softness from oil. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī cite poetry about accommodating an enemy; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes literal oil in 23:20 and 55:37 and figurative usage in 56:81; al-Wāḥidī also points to 56:81. [25 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو الهيثم,الليث); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(الليث); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI]
- 68:9/q03/p2 It can mean dissembling: concealing hostility, betraying religion, or abandoning sincere counsel. — Al-Mufaḍḍal gives hypocrisy and lost counsel; al-Mubarrad says one outwardly shows the opposite of one's inward stance. Al-Jawharī and al-Jushumī distinguish dāhana, concealment, from adhana, cheating. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ gives evasive appeasement and laxity as related senses; Ibn Barrajān speaks of knowing neglect of truth. [13 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(المفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL(المبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(الجوهري,المبرد,المفضل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان); BINTSHATI]
- 68:9/q03/p3 The related verb can mean demeaning, denying, or taking something lightly. — Study Quran gives these additional senses without supporting argument. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 68:9/q04 What did the opponents want the Prophet to do?  [تُدْهِنُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'softening' names a reciprocal concession generally or a specific act: idolatry, silence, disbelief, deception, abandonment, lying or weakness.
- 68:9/q04/p1 Make mutual concessions or soften his stance in religion. — Ibn Qutayba, al-Kalbī, al-Ḥasan and many others describe a bargain; al-Rāzī specifies abandoning some commitments. Al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda. Asad and Kur'an Yolu extend it to ethical and spiritual principles. Al-Qurṭubī accepts the listed glosses as compatible applications of softness, against Ibn al-ʿArabī's narrowing. [46 notes: FARRA(بعضهم); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن,الكلبي); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس,الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب,ابن عباس,ابن كيسان,الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,الفراء والكلبي,القتبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبان بن ثعلب,ابن عباس,الحسن,الكلبي والفراء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BINTSHATI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bg)]
- 68:9/q04/p2 Disbelieve, allowing or confirming their own disbelief. — Reports from Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿAṭiyya, al-Suddī and others repeat this. Al-Wāḥidī treats it as a form of concession; Ibn al-ʿArabī calls disbelief or lying strongest, while al-Qurṭubī rejects narrowing the verse to these. [15 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(بعضهم); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,سفيان); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي والضحاك); WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء عن ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); TABRISI(الضحاك وعطاء وابن عباس في رواية أخرى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطية، الضحاك، مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس، عطية، الضحاك، السدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس والضحاك وعطية والسدي); STUDYQURAN(Bg); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 68:9/q04/p3 Incline toward their idols and leave the truth. — Mujāhid is repeatedly cited. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this and cites 17:75; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ reports his preference and cites 17:74. Al-Wāḥidī calls it the majority view and links al-Wālibī's report from Ibn ʿAbbās. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); BINTSHATI(قيل); BINTSHATI(الطبري)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 68:9/q04/p4 Take turns worshipping their idols and God. — Ibn Qutayba's proposed exchange is repeated by al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī and Kur'an Yolu. Al-Biqāʿī relates the year-for-year proposal to the occasion of Sūrat al-Kāfirūn (109:1). [8 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(القتبي); BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN(Bg)]
- 68:9/q04/p5 Stop denouncing idolatry, unbelief, sins or their forebears, letting each side practice its religion. — Al-Naḥḥās adds that such softness corrupts religion; al-Bayḍāwī mentions ceasing to forbid shirk or sometimes agreeing to it. Al-Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr specify idols and ancestors. The latter links their offer to accusations in 68:5–7 and 68:51; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ reports 'leave him and his Lord alone.' [9 notes: NAHHAS; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الكلبي وزيد بن أسلم والحسن); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 68:9/q04/p6 Dissemble or display piety outwardly while concealing the opposite. — Zayd ibn Aslam gives reciprocal hypocrisy; al-Ālūsī clarifies that the opponents wanted only outward softness from the Prophet, since they would dislike his inward dissent. Bursevī warns that outward compliance without inward conviction is fleeting hypocrisy. [7 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(زيد بن أسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(زيد بن أسلم); ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI(بعضهم)]
- 68:9/q04/p7 Abandon the religious mission altogether. — Al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān transmit this from Qatāda. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة)]
- 68:9/q04/p8 Lie, so that they could lie too. — Attributed to al-Rabīʿ ibn Anas; Ibn al-ʿArabī considers lying, alongside disbelief, among the strongest glosses. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الربيع بن أنس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+; QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 68:9/q04/p9 Weaken or relent, allowing them to do likewise. — Attributed to Abū Jaʿfar by al-Māwardī, al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبو جعفر); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو جعفر)]

## 68:9/q05 What would the opponents do in return?  [فَيُدْهِنُونَ · interpretation]

## 68:9/q06 How is fa-yudhinūna joined to the wish?  [فَيُدْهِنُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fāʾ coordinates within the wish, joins waddū, supplies an omitted subject's predicate, or marks result or their current inducement.
- 68:9/q06/p1 It is indicative and coordinated with tudhinu within the wish. — Al-Naḥḥās, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī cite the retained nūn: a subjunctive result would lose it. Al-Qurṭubī says the construction depicts imitation rather than recompense. Al-Bayḍāwī says they delay their concession until his; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ prefers coordination as revelation's grammar while retaining a causal nuance. [9 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI+]
- 68:9/q06/p2 It is coordinated with waddū: they wished, then themselves softened. — Al-Biqāʿī says their apparent softness was deceptive and they delayed it to induce Muhammad to begin. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 68:9/q06/p3 An omitted nominal subject precedes the indicative predicate: 'then they would soften.' — Al-Zamakhsharī's analysis, repeated by al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ, permits either future reciprocity or current flattery. Al-Rāzī compares 72:13. Ibn ʿĀshūr says subject fronting confines this accommodation to the opponents; Islāhī also supplies the suppressed inchoative. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 68:9/q06/p4 The fāʾ marks a consequence of the Prophet's concession. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī give this as an alternative to coordination; al-Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ also explain the causal/result relation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it does not make the verb subjunctive. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 68:9/q06/p5 It can describe softness they already show, hoping to induce his. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī give the present-time account. Ibn ʿĀshūr says bad-intentioned people want others to resemble them; Study Quran notes this second reading. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 68:9/q07 What readings and inflections are reported for fa-yudhinūn?  [فَيُدْهِنُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the nūn is retained in the common indicative or omitted in the reported subjunctive codex form.
- 68:9/q07/p1 The common codices retain nūn, giving indicative fa-yudhinūna. — Abū Ḥayyān and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ explicitly say most codices have the nūn; al-Samīn describes the common raised reading. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BINTSHATI(أبو حيان); BINTSHATI+]
- 68:9/q07/p2 Some codices have fa-yudhinū without nūn, read as a subjunctive wish-result or by analogy with an. — Sībawayh transmits Hārūn's report. Al-Samīn adds an analysis by attraction to an imagined subjunctive. Al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, al-Itqān and Bint al-Shāṭiʾ give its result or verbal noun construals. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(هارون); KASHSHAF-FULL(سيبويه); RAZI-FULL(هارون); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(هارون); ITQAN(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(هارون); BINTSHATI]

## 68:9/q08 What offers or events are reported behind this wish?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which reported proposal or related episode supplies the setting.
- 68:9/q08/p1 Quraysh polytheists invited the Prophet to their forefathers' religion or offered mutual noninterference. — Al-Jushumī reports the revelation setting; Ibn ʿĀshūr says sīra books place an offer through Abū Ṭālib and ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ reports the proposal that he stop mentioning their idols and they leave him and his Lord alone; al-Kāshifī also mentions their ancestral religion. [6 notes: JISHUMI(جماعة); IBNASHUR-FULL(الكلبي وزيد بن أسلم والحسن,كتب السيرة); TABATABAI(قيل); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 68:9/q08/p2 They proposed alternating years of worship; the proposal is also reported as an occasion for al-Kāfirūn. — Al-Biqāʿī specifies a year of the Prophet worshipping their gods and a year of their worshipping his God, citing 109:1. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 68:9/q08/p3 After the Prophet prostrated at the end of al-Najm, some emigrants returned from Abyssinia mistakenly believing Quraysh had converted. — Al-Biqāʿī reports that disbelievers, believers, jinn and humans followed the prostration, then the returnees discovered their inference was false; he cites 53:62. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 68:9/q09 What conduct does the verse require, and how does it distinguish courtesy from compromise?  [* · law]
turns on: The motive and substance of the apparent softness: protection and reform, or surrender of religious truth for gain.
- 68:9/q09/p1 The Prophet must remain firm against religious or moral concessions to deniers. — Bursevī says he must persist despite their numbers and that obedience to a sinner or yielding to a tyrant is wrong; Elmalılı calls refusal a foundation of noble character; Kur'an Yolu says truth cannot be mixed with falsehood. Al-Naḥḥās says silence about sin would corrupt religion. [5 notes: NAHHAS; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 68:9/q09/p2 Courteous mudārā is allowed when it protects religion or reforms someone; selling religion for worldly advantage is blameworthy mudāhana. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya contrasts unlawful lenience with lawful mudārā; al-Mufaḍḍal calls hypocrisy and abandoned counsel blameworthy, and al-Qurṭubī says the Prophet did none of it. Bursevī cites a Prophetic command to be courteous, al-Ghazālī on motive, and Abū al-Dardāʾ on smiling while inwardly condemning feared harm. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(المفضل); QURTUBI-FULL(المفضل); BURSEVI(أبو الدرداء,الإمام الغزالي,النبي,بعضهم)]
- 68:9/q09/p3 Words and outward action should agree with conviction. — Bursevī cites a Persian admonitory verse: words without deeds are false, a sleeper cannot awaken another, and counsel must be heard attentively; he warns that outward compliance without inner assent is hypocrisy. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 68:9/q10 How does this wish connect with the surrounding command and opening of the sūra?  [* · links]
- 68:9/q10/p1 It explains the command not to obey the deniers and answers their attacks on the Prophet. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wish clause stands apart because it explains forbidden obedience; he connects their offer with accusations around 68:5–7 and 68:51. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the opening affirms his mission against those calling the Qur'an ancient tales; Bursevī says he must keep confronting them. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 68:9/q11 What motive or inward pattern does the wish reveal?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether their desire reflects a wish to spread their own condition or self-interested bargaining.
- 68:9/q11/p1 Those who compromise want others to share their own condition. — Al-Qushayrī likens this to a sick person wishing others sick and says those marked by separation wish it on opponents; al-Biqāʿī repeats the analogy. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the fāʾ can explain their wish because people of bad intent seek others' likeness. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 68:9/q11/p2 Their bargaining comes from desire rather than sincere religious conviction. — Islāhī says their opposition is bargaining against desire; Bursevī portrays them as steeped in vice and shifting desires, already flattering in hope of reciprocity. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]
