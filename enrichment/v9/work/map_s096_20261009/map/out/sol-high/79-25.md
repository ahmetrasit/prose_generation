# 79:25: 18 questions

## 79:25/q01 Who is the one God seized?  [فَأَخَذَهُ · referent]
- 79:25/q01/p1 Pharaoh. — Bint al-Shati identifies Moses' Pharaoh. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 79:25/q02 What does God’s seizing him convey?  [فَأَخَذَهُ · interpretation]
- 79:25/q02/p1 God overpowered and punished Pharaoh. — Ibn Ashur says أخذ literally means taking by hand but figuratively conveys power and triumph; 54:42 and 69:10 show this usage. Tabataba'i likewise calls the taking punishment. Al-Biqa'i emphasizes God's sole sovereignty and irresistible force; Ibn Kathir cites 11:99 and 28:41 for vengeance and deterrence. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 79:25/q03 What does نَكَالَ mean?  [نَكَالَ · meaning]
turns on: The sense emphasized: exemplary deterrence, fettered humiliation, or incapacity.
- 79:25/q03/p1 A severe exemplary punishment that deters others. — Jishumi derives it from prevention, comparing shackles and refraining. Al-Layth in Razi cites refusing an oath or a restraining fetter; Razi describes a disgraceful spectacle. Qurtubi, Bursawi, Alusi, Elmalili and Ibn Ashur stress warning witnesses or hearers. Ibn Ashur traces the root to retreat and cites 2:66. Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Bursawi equate نكال with تنكيل as سلام with تسليم. Bint al-Shati lists root forms at 73:12, 4:84, 2:66 and 5:38; Qurtubi recalls al-Muzzammil. [15 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(الليث); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:25/q03/p2 An animal restraint or bridle, extended to humiliation of the fettered. — Bint al-Shati gives the primary concrete meaning and the extension through degradation. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]
- 79:25/q03/p3 It also connotes weakness and inability. — Study Quran says God rendered Pharaoh powerless to oppose Moses. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 79:25/q04 How is نَكَالَ parsed?  [فَأَخَذَهُ نَكَالَ · grammar]
turns on: Which governor or omitted element licenses the accusative; Samin objects to the circumstantial reading.
- 79:25/q04/p1 A cognate accusative governed by أخذ in its sense of punishing. — Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq and others say 'taking' here means punishing; Wahidi's al-Mubarrad cites 11:102. Ibn Atiyya attributes government by أخذ to Sibawayh; Razi's Zajjaj compares 'a severe leaving'. Ibn Ashur says the accusative specifies the kind of taking. Samin allows figurative usage in verb or noun; Alusi and Elmalili list the parse. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق، المبرد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:25/q04/p2 An implied verb نكّل governs it, or it confirms the clause. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan attribute the implied verb to al-Mubarrad. Baydawi also supplies one. Zamakhshari calls it a confirming verbal noun, comparing 4:122 and 2:138 in Samin. Abu Hayyan objects that this needs a verb inferred from the clause. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 79:25/q04/p3 An understood noun 'seizure' governs it: a deterrent seizure. — Farra gives this paraphrase in Razi and Qurtubi; Nahhas and Wahidi report punitive seizure. Alusi lists object of the verb. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); NAHHAS(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:25/q04/p4 An object of purpose: God seized him to punish as a warning. — Samin, Alusi and Elmalili offer this parse. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 79:25/q04/p5 Accusative after an omitted preposition: 'with exemplary punishment'. — Qurtubi reports it; Alusi includes it. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:25/q04/p6 A circumstantial accusative in a participial sense. — Alusi lists it. Samin calls it weak: نكال is definite and requires an unmeasured interpretation such as جهدك or طاقتك. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 79:25/q05 What do ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ and ٱلْأُولَىٰٓ designate?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pair names realms, specific boasts or other offenses, periods of life or punishment, or generations; some reports reverse the boasts.
- 79:25/q05/p1 The Hereafter and this world, with Fire there and drowning here. — Qatada, Hasan, Ibn Zayd and many authors specify drowning and Fire. Al-Dahhak adds casting ashore and later chains above Fire. Study Quran adds the plagues and cites 20:78–79 and 7:135–137; Asad cites 7:137. Al-Biqa'i cites 28:40–42. Ibn Kathir calls this correct; Tabataba'i and Kur'an Yolu prefer it; Bint al-Shati says context gives no signal for two boasts. Ibn Ashur distinguishes worldly literal nakal from figurative Hereafter chastisement, since no one is deterred on Judgment Day; Bursawi says أخذ spans literal worldly and figurative future taking. Tabataba'i says both punishments deter. [39 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن، قتادة,الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(الحسن وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن، قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وقتادة); RAZI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن وابن زيد); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الضحاك); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BINTSHATI(مفسرون); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; BINTSHATI+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABATABAI+]
- 79:25/q05/p2 His earlier claim to know no god but himself and later claim to be the highest lord. — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrima, Sha'bi and others link 28:38 and 79:24. Qurtubi says punishment was delayed after the first claim but fell for both. Samin says descriptions such as 'statements' are omitted. Tabataba'i calls the view obscure; Bint al-Shati objects that context gives no sign of it. [34 notes: MUJAHID(ابن عباس,مجاهد); MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(بعضهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد، الشعبي، سعيد بن جبير، مقاتل، عطاء والكلبي عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس، ومجاهد والشعبي، وقتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وجماعة من المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي نجيح عن مجاهد,ابن عباس وعكرمة والشعبي ومقاتل والفراء,الفراء); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد والشعبي وسعيد بن جبير ومقاتل، وعطاء والكلبي عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد وعكرمة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الشعبي,عكرمة والضحاك); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI(İbn Cerîr); TABATABAI(قيل); BINTSHATI(مفسرون); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Kurtubî); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ, Ṭs); TABATABAI-; BINTSHATI-]
- 79:25/q05/p3 The later offense was the highest-lord claim, the earlier one denial of Moses, disbelief or disobedience. — Abu Razin and Abu Zayd say prior unbelief and rebellion. Qatada and Study Quran specify denial of Moses. Al-Qaffal identifies rejection after the sign and the boast, preferred by Razi because 79:20–24 names both in order. Tabataba'i finds the story-order reading obscure. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو رزين); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو رزين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو زرين); ALUSI-FULL(أبو رزين); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Q); ABDURRAZZAQ(بعضهم); TABATABAI(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL(القفال)+; TABATABAI-]
- 79:25/q05/p4 The first and last of Pharaoh's deeds or sins, embracing all his offenses. — Mujahid speaks of first and last deeds, or disbelief and disobedience; Baydawi speaks of deeds in general. Tabataba'i finds this obscure. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(منصور عن مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NAHHAS(الفراء); TABATABAI-]
- 79:25/q05/p5 Affliction at the beginning and end of Pharaoh's life. — Mawardi and Qurtubi report Mujahid; Abu Hayyan repeats it. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 79:25/q05/p6 The reverse assignment of the two boasts: highest-lord first, sole-divinity claim last. — Abu Hayyan attributes this to Ibn Abbas; Alusi reports a reversal and an interval of forty years. Ibn Atiyya notes disagreement about their order. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 79:25/q05/p7 Respite first and Hell last. — Mawardi reports al-Rabi and cites 40:46. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الربيع)]
- 79:25/q05/p8 Drowning at the beginning of a day and Fire at its end. — Ibn Jawzi reports al-Rabi ibn Anas. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الربيع بن أنس)]
- 79:25/q05/p9 His deed for the afterlife and his deed in the earlier life. — Tabari reports this gloss from Mujahid. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 79:25/q05/p10 A lesson to former and latter generations. — Razi describes the turn from Pharaoh's deeds and words to his fate as a lesson to generations. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 79:25/q06 What does the genitive relation to the paired terms mean?  [نَكَالَ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the construction locates punishment or identifies its causes.
- 79:25/q06/p1 Punishment 'in' the two abodes. — Ibn Ashur explicitly gives this sense; Alusi allows it, and Bursawi says both abodes are named because punishment occurs in each. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 79:25/q06/p2 Punishment 'for' the two offenses. — Alusi calls this annexation of effect to cause and also notes a 'for' sense. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:25/q06/p3 An understood noun such as 'statements' accompanies the adjectives. — Samin gives this on the two-boasts reading. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 79:25/q07 How much time separated Pharaoh's claims?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · reports]
turns on: The number and whether it measures between claims or after the final claim.
- 79:25/q07/p1 Forty years. — Muqatil, Ibn Abbas, al-Sha'bi, Abdullah ibn Amr, Khaythama and others give forty between claims. Razi says he was spared forty years after the first. Ibn Atiyya records another report measuring forty years after the highest-lord claim. [17 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والسدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد، الشعبي، سعيد بن جبير، مقاتل، عطاء والكلبي عن ابن عباس); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد وجماعة من المفسرين); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وعكرمة والشعبي ومقاتل والفراء); RAZI-FULL(مجاهد والشعبي وسعيد بن جبير ومقاتل، وعطاء والكلبي عن ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(الشعبي,خيثمة,عبد الله بن عمرو); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 79:25/q07/p2 Thirty years. — Mawardi attributes the interval to Mujahid and al-Suddi; Ibn Jawzi reports from al-Suddi thirty years after the later claim. Nasafi lists thirty. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد والسدي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); NASAFI-FULL]
- 79:25/q07/p3 Twenty years. — Nasafi lists twenty among reported numbers. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 79:25/q08 Why does the Hereafter come before the first life?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
turns on: Semantic emphasis versus matching the verse endings.
- 79:25/q08/p1 Its punishment is greater, more severe and lasting. — Al-Biqa'i says the order emphasizes severity and explicitly rejects rhyme as explanation; Ibn Ashur and Bint al-Shati agree. Bint al-Shati Ijaz calls the order semantically necessary to the threat, comparing 93:4–5, and argues that verse endings join sound to meaning, never forcing word order for ornament. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 79:25/q08/p2 The order matches the verse endings. — Burhan compares 53:25. Al-Biqa'i and Bint al-Shati contest a rhyme-only cause. [1 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL-; BINTSHATI-IJAZ-]

## 79:25/q09 How can a past-tense seizure include future punishment?  [فَأَخَذَهُ ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ · rhetoric]
- 79:25/q09/p1 The future is treated as already begun after death. — Ibn Ashur says Pharaoh begins tasting punishment and sees his final destination after death, as a hadith relates. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 79:25/q10 What does punishment in both abodes teach?  [* · theology]
- 79:25/q10/p1 Punishment in one abode does not exclude punishment in the other. — Jishumi states the principle. Alusi notes that those accepting Pharaoh's faith instead confine the punishment to worldly drowning. [2 notes: JISHUMI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 79:25/q11 What reports explain Pharaoh's claim and delayed punishment?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports address different aspects of the claim and delay.
- 79:25/q11/p1 Pharaoh asked protection from a snake despite claiming divinity. — Tabrisi's anonymous account says his fear and plea for help disprove divinity. [1 notes: TABRISI]
- 79:25/q11/p2 He was spared four hundred years because God repaid his courtesy and hospitality. — Tabrisi reports Ibn Abbas's account of Moses asking why a blasphemer was spared so long. [1 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 79:25/q11/p3 God said only one afraid of missing out makes Pharaoh's claim. — Tabrisi transmits through Abu Ja'far a Prophetic report about Gabriel's question. [1 notes: TABRISI(رسول الله)]

## 79:25/q12 How does Pharaoh's fate fit the sura and other passages?  [* · links]
- 79:25/q12/p1 It consoles Muhammad, urges patience, warns deniers and supports resurrection. — Tabrisi connects the Moses account to deniers of prophets and resurrection. Al-Biqa'i says the sura establishes resurrection and gathering after prior suras, with oaths marking a decisive climax. Study Quran considers this likely the earliest revealed Moses account, assumes Arab familiarity and sees warning to Makkah's unjust rulers; it lists other Moses accounts at 7:103–155, 10:75–93, 17:101–104, 20:9–97, 26:10–66, 27:7–14, 28:3–46, 40:23–45, 43:46–56, 44:17–31. [3 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 79:25/q12/p2 It links to his drowning and the curse on followers. — Al-Biqa'i cites 28:40–42 and says nobody else is condemned or called wretched so forcefully. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 79:25/q13 What inward lesson follows from Pharaoh's claim?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the lesson is the soul's humility or a distinction between two apparently similar utterances.
- 79:25/q13/p1 The soul must humble itself and renounce claims to lordship. — Qashani says the fearful soul should soften and break. Sulami reports Bishr that God made Pharaoh's tongue utter unreal lordship, and Sari that a servant in a master's guise becomes a punished example, refuted even by himself. [3 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(بشر,سري)]
- 79:25/q13/p2 Pharaoh's self-exalting claim differs from al-Hallaj's self-effacing utterance. — Al-Sumnani's spiritual encounter says Pharaoh saw only himself and diminished God, while al-Hallaj saw only God. Rumi's verses compare stone to precious stone, enemy to lover of light, curse to mercy; he disclaims union and incarnation. Study Quran repeats the contrast. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ركن الدين علاء الدولة السمناني,مولانا); STUDYQURAN]

## 79:25/q14 How are later claims of divine identity judged?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether al-Hallaj repeated Pharaoh's self-exalting claim or spoke from effacement.
- 79:25/q14/p1 Those endorsing Pharaoh's claim share his condemnation and Fire. — Al-Biqa'i names al-Hallaj, Ibn Arabi, Ibn al-Farid and followers, charging them with applying the Greatest Name to themselves and everything, contrary to the Qur'an. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 79:25/q14/p2 Al-Hallaj's utterance differs in meaning and fate. — Al-Sumnani and Rumi distinguish self-effacement from Pharaoh's self-glorification; Rumi says one brings mercy and the other curse. [3 notes: BURSEVI(ركن الدين علاء الدولة السمناني,مولانا); STUDYQURAN]

## 79:25/q15 Why was Iblis singled out for a curse compared with Pharaoh?  [* · theology]
turns on: Intention, precedence, direct defiance, or responsibility for followers.
- 79:25/q15/p1 His intention was worse. — Bursawi transmits this answer from As'ilat al-Hikam. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أسئلة الحكم)]
- 79:25/q15/p2 He first initiated disobedience and discord. — The source says later lordship claims and oppression followed his whispering in word, deed and intention. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أسئلة الحكم)]
- 79:25/q15/p3 He defied God directly and never confessed or humbled himself. — Pharaoh and others confronted prophets and intermediaries, sometimes confessing or supplicating to creatures. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أسئلة الحكم)]
- 79:25/q15/p4 He originated disbelief and bears followers' misguidance. — The reported burden continues until Judgment Day. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أسئلة الحكم)]

## 79:25/q16 What human capacity made Pharaoh's claim possible?  [* · inward]
- 79:25/q16/p1 Humans combine majesty and beauty and can claim the greatest perfection. — Bursawi says humans were created with two hands, unlike Iblis, who has only majesty and never claimed a station he lacked, and angels, who have pure beauty. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 79:25/q17 Is an alternate wording of the paired punishment reported?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · readings]
- 79:25/q17/p1 A reported reading says 'of the last and the first'. — Study Quran attributes it to Qurtubi, Tabari and Tabrisi; explanations link it to either the two boasts or denial of Moses followed by the highest-lord boast. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q,Q, Ṭ, Ṭs)]

## 79:25/q18 What disobedience does al-Kalbī connect to the paired punishment?  [ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ وَٱلْأُولَىٰٓ · interpretation]
- 79:25/q18/p1 The later punishment is for Pharaoh’s disobedience, alongside an earlier act. — Al-Kalbī, as reported by Abd al-Razzaq, calls the Hereafter punishment retribution for disobedience; Tabari’s report speaks of the later act’s punishment for disobedience and the earlier act. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(الكلبي); TAB-FULL(الكلبي)]
