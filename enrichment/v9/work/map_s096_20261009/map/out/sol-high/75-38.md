# 75:38: 16 questions

## 75:38/q01 What does thumma convey in the transition to the clot?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
- 75:38/q01/p1 It marks both elapsed time and the astonishing distance between the stages. — Biqāʿī calls it a particle of distance: the transformation is astonishingly great and also takes time. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 75:38/q02 What is the ʿalaqah that follows the semen drop?  [عَلَقَةًۭ · meaning]
- 75:38/q02/p1 It is a clinging, congealed clot of blood formed from the drop. — Jishumī calls it congealed blood; Ibn ʿAṭiyyah derives the designation from blood being called ʿalaq. Biqāʿī describes fresh, intensely bright-red thick blood, whereas Bursawī describes a thick, dark-red clot after forty days, contrasting it with the earlier white fluid and citing 23:14. Nasafī also gives forty days and calls the clot solid. Qurṭubī stresses the change from the lowly nuṭfah. Thalabī, Māwardī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn Kathīr, Ālūsī, and Iṣlāḥī describe the drop becoming a clot; Ālūsī cites 23:14, and Ibn Kathīr adds the subsequent morsel. [15 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:38/q03 What is the subject of 'became a clot'?  [كَانَ عَلَقَةًۭ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the implicit subject is the person or the preceding semen drop.
- 75:38/q03/p1 The human being became a clot. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly gives the human as one possible subject; Māwardī and Rāzī likewise describe the human as becoming the clot. [3 notes: TABATABAI; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 75:38/q03/p2 The semen became a clot. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī explicitly gives semen as the other possible subject; Jishumī, Nasafī, and Bursawī describe the semen or drop as becoming the clot. [4 notes: TABATABAI; JISHUMI; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 75:38/q04 How is the preceding verb for the semen's emission read?  [* · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb agrees with masculine al-manī or feminine al-nuṭfah.
- 75:38/q04/p1 Yumnā, with yāʾ, agrees with al-manī. — Jishumī reports this from al-Ḥasan, Abū ʿAmr, Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim, and Yaʿqūb, and says Abū ʿUbayd prefers it. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن، وأبو عمرو، وحفص عن عاصم، ويعقوب)]
- 75:38/q04/p2 Tumnā, with tāʾ, agrees with al-nuṭfah. — Jishumī reports this for the remaining readers and says Abū Ḥātim prefers it. [1 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن، وأبو عمرو، وحفص عن عاصم، ويعقوب)]

## 75:38/q05 What do 'created and fashioned' describe in human development?  [فَخَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: How khalaqa and sawwā divide divine determination, embryonic shaping, spirit, functional fit, and possible maturation after birth.
- 75:38/q05/p1 God creates the embryo and proportionately forms it into a complete human being. — Muqātil, Wāḥidī, Bayḍāwī, and Ālūsī state the basic creation and completion; Ālūsī links the stages to 23:14. Naḥḥās specifies a speaking, hearing, seeing human; Thalabī includes the eventual male and female forms. Jishumī and Ṭabrisī specify internal and external organs in the womb; Jishumī also reports proportioning the clot into a human as people observe. Ibn ʿAṭiyyah describes a composite of elements becoming an independent person. Nasafī describes upright formation; Biqāʿī an adjusted independent new creation. Bursawī traces a further forty days to differentiable flesh, bones clothed with flesh, preparation for powers and spirit, and perfected proportion; he also reports the Mufradāt's explanation of perfection as formation conforming to divine wisdom. Iṣlāḥī describes provident shaping and perfection in the womb's darkness. Ibn ʿĀshūr defines taswiyah as straightening and making creation precise, with parallels in 91:7, 75:38, and 87:2; Asad directs readers to his notes on 87:2 and 91:7 for his rendering of sawwā, though the cited sentence breaks off. [18 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL+; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BIQAI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+; JISHUMI+; MUQATIL+; NAHHAS+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABRISI+; THALABI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 75:38/q05/p2 God determines or measures the person's constitution, then forms and proportionately completes it. — Rāzī reports an interpretation that God creates, apportions, and proportions. Qurṭubī glosses khalaqa as determined and sawwā as fashioned and proportioned, including placement of the spirit. Biqāʿī specifies determination and formation of flesh, bones, nerves, other substances, and attributes. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God measured and formed with adjustment and completion. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 75:38/q05/p3 God breathes the spirit into the embryo, then completes its bodily formation. — Rāzī reports Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil taking khalaqa as breathing the spirit and sawwā as completing the limbs. Baghawī also places the soul before proportional formation; Ibn al-Jawzī says God breathed the spirit and fashioned the creation. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL+; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 75:38/q05/p4 God shapes the body and limbs, then breathes the spirit into it. — Bursawī reports Kāshifī's gloss of forming the person's shape and limbs and breathing the spirit. Ibn Kathīr says the clot becomes a morsel, is shaped and receives the spirit, becoming a sound, complete human by God's permission and decree. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكاشفي); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 75:38/q05/p5 God creates the human in the womb, then brings bodily strength and movement to maturity after birth. — Māwardī gives this as one possibility; Ṭabrisī reports a similar view, saying the person is made human after birth and strength is completed. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل)]
- 75:38/q05/p6 God creates bodies and fits each limb for its particular action. — Māwardī and Ṭabrisī both give this explanation, assigning each limb its own function. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل)]
- 75:38/q05/p7 Fashioning matches each paired bodily organ with its counterpart. — Bursawī reports this as another explanation of proportioning. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 75:38/q06 What variant form of 'created' is reported?  [فَخَلَقَ · readings]
- 75:38/q06/p1 Ibn Masʿūd's codex has yakhluq, the imperfect form. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah reports the codex reading يخلق, in contrast with the verse's perfect fa-khalaqa. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(مصحف ابن مسعود)]

## 75:38/q07 Why is the agent left unspoken in the creation verbs?  [فَخَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 75:38/q07/p1 The omission follows the nearby passive wording and suits the sūrah's urgent tone. — Sāmarrāʾī notes that the agent is omitted from 'left aimless,' 'created,' 'fashioned,' and 'made,' matching the preceding passive. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:38/q08 How does the singular wording here relate to the sūrah's plural divine forms?  [فَخَلَقَ فَسَوَّىٰ · rhetoric]
- 75:38/q08/p1 The sūrah uses singular 'He created' and 'He proportioned' alongside plural forms for divine ability, gathering, and shaping. — Sāmarrāʾī lists 'We are able,' 'We gather,' and 'We shape' alongside the singular creation and proportioning verbs. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:38/q09 How does this fashioning relate to the earlier mention of fingertips?  [فَسَوَّىٰ · links]
- 75:38/q09/p1 The earlier image singles out exact fingertip shaping; this verse describes human shaping generally. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts restricted, partial fingertip shaping with unrestricted, general shaping here. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the fine restoration of fingertips a metonym for creating them, because well-proportioned recreation entails their creation; he cites 91:7, 75:38, and 87:2. Asad points to his discussions of 87:2 and 91:7 for sawwā. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 75:38/q10 What governs the order of the creation stages named here?  [* · rhetoric]
- 75:38/q10/p1 The order tracks successive stages, while wording and placement also answer to local context and overall coherence. — Sāmarrāʾī identifies the semen drop and clot as the two named stages. In another discussion he says Quranic word placement is precise and depends on general ordering patterns and context; he also says chronological priority may govern word order, alongside context and overall coherence. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:38/q11 Whose creation does this passage describe?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage's target is identified as Abū Jahl or left as the human denier generally.
- 75:38/q11/p1 It addresses the human being, especially the denier of resurrection, through a common human origin. — Ṭabarī identifies the denier's origin in a small amount of semen in a man and cites 16:4; Muqātil describes the human being's formation. The other embryonic descriptions likewise speak generically of the human. [2 notes: MUQATIL+; TAB-FULL+]
- 75:38/q11/p2 The whole passage concerns Abū Jahl ibn Hishām. — Ibn ʿAṭiyyah attributes this view to most interpreters. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين)]

## 75:38/q12 How does this creation sequence prove resurrection?  [* · theology]
- 75:38/q12/p1 The power that first makes and perfects a human from semen can recreate the dead. — Ṭabarī says the denier's humble origin demonstrates recreation after death, citing 16:4. Zajjāj connects the formation of the sexes to the question in 75:40. Iṣlāḥī says the stages and granted abilities display divine power, wisdom, and creativity sufficient for resurrection. Kuran Yolu says the final question warns that the creating power will resurrect. Sāmarrāʾī places development from semen through clot, formation, and male and female within this proof. Study Quran lists parallel creation and resurrection passages: 18:37; 22:5–6; 23:13–16; 35:11; 40:67; 76:2; and 80:18–22. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 75:38/q13 What does the process show about who directs human development?  [* · interpretation]
- 75:38/q13/p1 Divine providence alone guides the stages to a complete person with wisdom; the human does not plan or control the process. — Iṣlāḥī says the passive account of the fluid's emission conveys lack of human attention or elaborate arrangement, while providence guides development in darkness and no one besides the Creator forms the resulting person. Biqāʿī says God never abandons the drop, clot, morsel, or bones, carrying each through to a complete creation according to manifest wisdom. Qurṭubī notes the ordered ascent from a lowly origin. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 75:38/q14 How does this ending connect to the sūrah's opening?  [* · links]
- 75:38/q14/p1 It returns to the opening doubt about resurrection and closes with a question about accountability and God's power to raise the dead. — Iṣlāḥī says that after arrogance and Judgement the sūrah returns to 75:3–4 and asks whether a person will be left unaccountable. Kuran Yolu describes the closing warning question as moving from human creation to resurrection. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 75:38/q15 Why can people not be left unaccountable?  [* · theology]
- 75:38/q15/p1 Leaving them unaccountable would violate divine justice and wisdom. — Iṣlāḥī states this while connecting the closing passage to the sūrah's opening doubts in 75:3–4. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 75:38/q16 How does this example fit the Quran's varied proofs for resurrection?  [* · links]
- 75:38/q16/p1 It is one varied presentation of the same proof, alongside the revival of dead earth. — Sāmarrāʾī explains taṣrīf as change or variation, including changes of word forms in Arabic morphology, and cites 2:164. He says the Quran varies examples of one issue until the evidence becomes clear: the revival of dead earth in 41:39 prepares the argument, and development from semen to clot, human form, and male and female supplies another proof. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
