# 80:5: 13 questions

## 80:5/q01 What does ammā contribute to the sentence?  [أَمَّا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle is explained chiefly through its contrast or through the conditional force and its rhetorical effect.
- 80:5/q01/p1 It marks a contrast with the other kind of person. — Bursevi identifies the particle as the marker of the contrast between the self-sufficient person and the seeker addressed next. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 80:5/q01/p2 It makes the case conditional and recurrent: whenever such a person appears, the Prophet attends to him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses ammā as 'whatever may occur'; attaching the response to the condition exaggerates the persistence of that attention. The two cited entries repeat this explanation. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 80:5/q02 What does istaghnā mean of this person?  [ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the implied object of self-sufficiency is wealth, faith and guidance, God, or an unspecified sense of one's own sufficiency; several notes make wealth the cause of spiritual refusal rather than the whole sense.
- 80:5/q02/p1 He is wealthy or has acquired wealth and standing. — Al-Qushayrī reports wealth through property; al-Wāḥidī also gives the wealth gloss, and al-Wajīz says he was made wealthy. Jishumī specifies money and standing; al-Ṭabrisī describes prominence among his people; al-Nasafī specifies money. Al-Biqāʿī says he sought and acquired wealth, irrespective of whether he feared God or sought guidance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links wealth and public grandeur to arrogance toward truth, citing 96:7–8. Kuran Yolu applies this to wealthy Meccan notables and chiefs. Ibn Kathīr calls him the rich man. Rāzī rejects wealth alone as the sense here: the Prophet did not court anyone for riches. Al-Ālūsī objects that poverty is not the stated contrast, while pointing to a later answer. Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise rejects a simple rich-versus-poor contrast. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says wealth leads the person to consider himself independent. Abū Ḥayyān calls wealth the apparent sense, but objects that material wealth does not suit the station of prophethood: if it were intended, the contrasting person would be described as poor and lowly. [15 notes: QUSHAYRI(ويقال); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي، مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL(بعضهم)-; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-; IBNASHUR-FULL-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 80:5/q02/p2 He thinks he needs no faith, Qur’anic knowledge, or prophetic guidance. — ʿAṭāʾ specifies independence from faith. Rāzī allows that riches made him think he needed neither faith nor Qur’an, and refers to 80:8–9; this qualifies, rather than equates the word with, material wealth. Bursevī and al-Ālūsī name faith and Qur’anic knowledge or guidance; al-Ālūsī also reports that unbelief produces this sense of no need. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he declines the Prophet’s guidance, apparently seeing no objection to it but no need for it. Asad especially applies this to arrogant pagan chiefs. Kuran Yolu says leaders' pride in wealth and followers sustained disbelief and their claimed independence from divine and prophetic guidance. Abū Ḥayyān also reports a view that the person is independent of faith in God. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عطاء); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(عطاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 80:5/q02/p3 He considers himself independent of God. — Muqātil, al-Kalbī, and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās give this object of independence; the Ibn ʿAbbās reports make wealth its cause and also include faith. Al-Qushayrī says feeling self-sufficient in oneself entails feeling self-sufficient from God. Study Quran says the literal self-sufficiency has this connotation. Kuran Yolu says the chiefs acted as though they needed no guidance from God or His Messenger. Abū Ḥayyān transmits al-Kalbī's gloss that the person considers himself independent of God. [9 notes: MUQATIL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي، مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 80:5/q02/p4 He counts himself self-sufficient, an attitude that can signal pride and trust in his own power. — Al-Bayḍāwī leaves the object unstated. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the matter of self-sufficiency is inferred from speech, acts, or knowledge; the added letters express supposition, and the term often signals pride and confidence in one's power. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites al-Rāghib's equation of ghinā, istighnāʾ, taghannī, and taghānī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the person considers himself independent because of his wealth. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 80:5/q03 Who is identified as the self-sufficient person?  [مَنِ ٱسْتَغْنَىٰ · referent]
turns on: The transmitted identifications differ on the name and, in Mujāhid's ʿUtba report, whether Shayba is included.
- 80:5/q03/p1 Umayya ibn Khalaf. — Mujāhid and Muqātil name him; al-Itqān reports this identification alongside a different report naming ʿUtba. [3 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; ITQAN]
- 80:5/q03/p2 ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa and Shayba ibn Rabīʿa. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn al-Jawzī transmit Mujāhid's paired identification. Jishumī also transmits Mujāhid's identification of ʿUtba and Shayba ibn Rabīʿa. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); JISHUMI(مجاهد)]
- 80:5/q03/p3 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this is the most widely transmitted identification from early exegetes; the duplicate entry specifies al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra al-Makhzūmī. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(سلف المفسرين)]
- 80:5/q03/p4 ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa alone. — Al-Itqān gives this as an alternative to Umayya ibn Khalaf; unlike Mujāhid's paired report, this report does not name Shayba. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 80:5/q03/p5 An unnamed Umayyad man present with the Prophet. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites a report from al-Ṣādiq in al-Majmaʿ: this man recoiled, frowned, and turned away on Ibn Umm Maktūm's arrival. [1 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق)]
- 80:5/q03/p6 The Quraysh leaders being addressed, including Abū Jahl and ʿUtba. — ʿĀʾisha reports that the Prophet was addressing Quraysh leaders including Abū Jahl and ʿUtba when Ibn Umm Maktūm came; the Prophet turned away, and the passage was revealed. Al-Ṭabrisī's report names ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa, Abū Jahl, al-ʿAbbās, Ubayy, and Umayya ibn Khalaf among the Quraysh leaders the Prophet was addressing; Jishumī reports the setting more generally as an effort to convert Meccan leaders. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(عائشة); JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل)]
- 80:5/q03/p7 Al-ʿAbbās. — Jishumī transmits Sufyān's report that the phrase was revealed about al-ʿAbbās. [1 notes: JISHUMI(سفيان)]

## 80:5/q04 Why was attention given to the Quraysh leaders?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes give different motives and phases: hoped-for conversion, concern over the leaders' view of poor followers, and continued pursuit after their refusal became plain.
- 80:5/q04/p1 The Prophet hoped to guide them, and their conversion could open Arabia to Islam. — Ibn Kathīr says the Prophet approached the rich man in hope of guidance. Iṣlāḥī says he initially concentrated on Quraysh in keeping with the command to warn his near kin (26:214), since their conversion could open Arabia; his devotion to persuasion made him neglect his own comfort and risk leaving little time for poor believers. Al-Ḥasan and others, as reported by Jishumī, describe the Prophet's effort to convert Meccan leaders; al-Ṭabrisī's reports say he was urging Quraysh leaders toward Islam when Ibn Umm Maktūm came. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل)]
- 80:5/q04/p2 The Prophet feared that leaders would reject a following of poor, blind, and enslaved people. — Al-Thaʿlabī says Ibn Umm Maktūm repeatedly asked for instruction while the Prophet addressed Quraysh leaders, and the Prophet turned from him under this concern. Al-Ṭabrisī's report names the leaders present as ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa, Abū Jahl, al-ʿAbbās, Ubayy, and Umayya ibn Khalaf, and says Ibn Umm Maktūm repeatedly asked to be taught. [2 notes: THALABI; TABRISI(قيل)]
- 80:5/q04/p3 The Prophet persisted with the haughty leaders even after their arrogance made reform unlikely. — Iṣlāḥī says they ignored the message and wanted the poor companions excluded to secure an audience. Pursuing them then took time from teaching poor believers; God used the incident to redirect the strategy toward companions eager for reform. The Prophet had assumed more responsibility than God required. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:5/q05 Who is the object of the passage's rebuke?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the report's unnamed frowning subject is the Prophet or another man, and whether the grammatical addressee or the leaders are the intended target. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ argues from the Prophet's praised character, early commands, and reason.
- 80:5/q05/p1 The Prophet is addressed for attending to the wealthy while neglecting the poor. — Al-Zajjāj says the addressee attends to the self-sufficient person. Al-Qushayrī calls the passage a reproach on behalf of the poor; Jishumī describes the same imbalance. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ's account of the rebuked pattern says the Prophet attends to wealthy scorners of truth, unconcerned whether they purify themselves, while neglecting an earnest God-fearing seeker. Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet is addressed and the incident teaches him to shift his attention. In ʿĀʾisha's account, the Prophet turned away when Ibn Umm Maktūm came while he was addressing Quraysh leaders including Abū Jahl and ʿUtba; the passage was then revealed. Al-Ḥasan and others, as reported by Jishumī, say God rebuked the Prophet after he turned back to the Meccan leaders when Ibn Umm Maktūm interrupted; al-Ṭabrisī reports that Ibn Umm Maktūm repeatedly asked for teaching before the Prophet turned away. [9 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; DURR-FULL(عائشة); TABRISI(قيل); TABATABAI(المرتضى)-; TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)-]
- 80:5/q05/p2 The frowning and turning away were an unnamed Umayyad man's conduct, which God condemned. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites al-Ṣādiq's report and argues that the verses do not name the Prophet as the one who frowned. Al-Murtaḍā, as cited by al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ, says neglecting the poor for the wealthy does not resemble the Prophet's noble character. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites the earlier praise of his character (68:4), early commands to warn kin and lower his wing to believers (26:214–15), and commands against worldly longing and toward gentleness to believers and turning from polytheists (15:88, 15:94). Reason also finds the conduct ugly without a new prohibition; he rejects the proposal that it became sinful only after the present prohibition. Al-Murtaḍā, as reported by al-Ṭabrisī, adds that the passage does not name the Prophet as the frowning subject and that frowning at a believing seeker conflicts with his noble character. [9 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق,المرتضى); TABRISI(المرتضى علم الهدى)]
- 80:5/q05/p3 The haughty leaders are the rebuke's intended target, though it addresses the Prophet. — Iṣlāḥī distinguishes the grammatical addressee from the leaders who ignored the message and sought exclusion of the Prophet's poor companions. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:5/q06 What does the shift from third-person narration to direct address do?  [* · rhetoric]
- 80:5/q06/p1 It intensifies the direct rebuke without conciliatory intimacy. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ rejects a proposed honorific reading of the shift because the ensuing direct address is stronger reproach and contains no conciliatory intimacy. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 80:5/q07 How should the Prophet allocate attention between the self-sufficient and those seeking guidance?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes frame the practical direction as priority for receptive seekers, honor of the poor, equal warning to all groups, or the limit of responsibility for those who refuse.
- 80:5/q07/p1 Attend to eager, God-fearing seekers rather than pamper indifferent leaders. — Iṣlāḥī says the Prophet's duty is to teach devoted companions who come eagerly, not to pursue absent leaders; only seekers of reform who fear God merit this attention. The incident redirected time from unlikely-to-reform Quraysh to poor believers. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ describes the opposed pattern of attending to rich scorners without regard to their purification while neglecting the earnest seeker. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 80:5/q07/p2 Keep company with and honor the poor, and avoid love of the wealthy. — Abū ʿUthmān, as reported by al-Sulamī, separately states the commands to accompany and honor the poor and the prohibition of loving the wealthy. Al-Qushayrī says the reproach is on behalf of the poor; Jishumī calls it a rebuke for attending to the wealthy while neglecting the poor. Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī report that the Prophet subsequently honored Ibn Umm Maktūm, welcomed him as the person over whom his Lord had reproached him, and asked after his needs; al-Ṭabrisī adds that he appointed him deputy in Medina twice. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 80:5/q07/p3 Warn everyone equally, without privileging status or class. — Ibn Kathīr explicitly lists the eminent and weak, rich and poor, masters and slaves, men and women, adults and children; God guides whom He wills. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 80:5/q07/p4 The Prophet is not accountable for the self-sufficient person's refusal to purify himself. — Ibn Kathīr says failure to purify is not the Prophet's responsibility. Iṣlāḥī likewise says he need not pamper those who refuse to come and had assumed more than God required in pursuing Quraysh. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:5/q08 How does this self-sufficiency contrast with fear and receptivity in the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
- 80:5/q08/p1 Self-sufficiency tends toward rejection, while fear, trial, and desire for reform make a person receptive. — Corpus Coranicum calls istaghnā the antonym of ittaqā, relates 80:5 to the piety of 80:9, and notes 92:5 and 96:7. Al-Biqāʿī says luxury invites disbelief and wrongdoing, while affliction can humble and purify; the deeply tried can be more receptive to fear. Iṣlāḥī contrasts those absorbed in worldly pleasures and heedless of the afterlife with those who fear meeting God. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ links the grandeur of wealth to arrogance toward truth, citing 96:7–8. [4 notes: CORPUSCORANICUM; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 80:5/q09 How is this passage related to al-Nāziʿāt and the sūra's larger argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Al-Biqāʿī stresses the warning and resurrection argument; Iṣlāḥī stresses shared discourse with a different order of topics.
- 80:5/q09/p1 It develops the call to warn those who fear through signs of resurrection and purification. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the sūra to 79:45. Human creation and the cycle of food demonstrate God's power, making rejection astonishing; warning reaches hearts receptive to fear. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 80:5/q09/p2 ʿAbasa pairs with al-Nāziʿāt in theme, style, and reasoning, but rearranges their topics. — Iṣlāḥī cites 79:45 and says the changed topic sequence gives this sūra its distinct majesty. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 80:5/q10 What incident is reported as the occasion for this passage?  [* · reports]
- 80:5/q10/p1 Ibn Umm Maktūm came seeking instruction while the Prophet was urging Meccan leaders toward Islam; the Prophet turned away, and the passage was revealed. — Al-Ḥasan and others, reported by Jishumī, say Ibn Umm Maktūm interrupted the Prophet's effort to convert Meccan leaders, and God rebuked the Prophet after he turned back to them. Al-Ṭabrisī reports that Ibn Umm Maktūm, also named ʿAbd Allāh ibn Shurayḥ ibn Mālik ibn Rabīʿa al-Fihrī, came while the Prophet was urging Quraysh leaders toward Islam. Another report in al-Ṭabrisī says he repeatedly asked to be taught while the Prophet spoke with ʿUtba ibn Rabīʿa, Abū Jahl, al-ʿAbbās, Ubayy, and Umayya ibn Khalaf. [3 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(قيل)]

## 80:5/q11 How did the Prophet treat Ibn Umm Maktūm afterward?  [* · reports]
- 80:5/q11/p1 He honored him, greeted him as the man over whom his Lord had reproached him, and asked about his needs; he also appointed him deputy in Medina twice. — Al-Ḥasan and others, as reported by Jishumī, give the honoring, greeting, and inquiry about his needs. Al-Ṭabrisī reports these acts and adds the two appointments as deputy in Medina. [2 notes: JISHUMI(الحسن وجماعة); TABRISI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 80:5/q12 What is reported of Ibn Umm Maktūm at al-Qādisiyya?  [* · reports]
- 80:5/q12/p1 Anas saw him there in armor, carrying a black banner. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī transmit Anas ibn Mālik's eyewitness report. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أنس); TABRISI(أنس بن مالك)]

## 80:5/q13 What is said about the Prophet's disclosure of this passage?  [* · reports]
- 80:5/q13/p1 If the Prophet had concealed any revelation, he would have concealed this passage. — Jishumī transmits this saying through Ibn Zayd. [1 notes: JISHUMI(ابن زيد)]
