# 27:28: 25 questions

## 27:28/q01 To whom does Solomon give these commands?  [ٱذْهَب · referent]
- 27:28/q01/p1 He addresses the hoopoe, making it his letter bearer and observer of the response. — Muqātil says it is sent to Sheba; Elmalılı distinguishes its observation duty from simple pigeon delivery; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī names the queen and council as its destination. [4 notes: MUQATIL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]

## 27:28/q02 What is the letter called 'my book, this'?  [بِّكِتَٰبِى هَٰذَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the letter is identified as the physical document in the story or read symbolically.
- 27:28/q02/p1 It is Solomon's present, prepared letter, written and sealed before being entrusted to the hoopoe. — Abū Ḥayyān says hādhā points to the letter just written, and the narrative leaves its preparation implicit; al-Ālūsī likewise takes it as present. Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī report writing and sealing; al-Baghawī adds perfume and Solomon's ring; Abū Ṣāliḥ specifically reports the seal. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers it was loaded on the bird. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Solomon wrote the letter after postponing judgment on the hoopoe. [10 notes: MUQATIL(أبو صالح); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 27:28/q02/p2 In an inward reading, Solomon's book signifies practical wisdom and divine law. — Al-Qāshānī gives this symbolic identification. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:28/q03 What did the letter say and what kind of message was it?  [بِّكِتَٰبِى · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize either the stated contents or the force those contents acquire in the continuation.
- 27:28/q03/p1 It was a concise summons to submission, opening with Solomon's name and the basmala and forbidding the recipients to exalt themselves over him. — Muqātil says these were its only contents; Mujāhid also mentions the basmala and summons to submit, linking the account to 27:30. Al-Biqāʿī stresses its brevity and the urgency of correcting sun worship. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL]
- 27:28/q03/p2 It was an ultimatum, as the following verses show. — Iṣlāḥī cites the later verses for its force. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 27:28/q04 Why did Solomon choose the hoopoe to carry the letter?  [ٱذْهَب بِّكِتَٰبِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the choice is explained by the bird's moral and intellectual fitness or its capacity to carry a message.
- 27:28/q04/p1 Its demonstrated knowledge, wisdom, truthfulness and counsel qualified it despite stronger available servants. — Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say Solomon could have used powerful jinn but saw signs of intelligence in the bird, leaving the queen without excuse. Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya, quoted by Bursevī, calls the commission a reward for its truthful report and sincere counsel despite its weak outward form. [3 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:28/q04/p2 The bird was usable as a domesticated letter carrier. — Ibn ʿĀshūr classifies the hoopoe with pigeons and infers Solomon fastened the prepared letter to it; Elmalılı adds that this messenger also had to watch the response. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 27:28/q05 Why did Solomon send this letter to Sheba?  [ٱذْهَب بِّكِتَٰبِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the immediate purpose was primarily verification of the bird's claim or a contact Solomon had already contemplated.
- 27:28/q05/p1 The queen's answer would test the hoopoe's report; silence would expose it as false. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects the letter with the preceding promised verification in 27:27; Study Quran cites the hoopoe's categorical claim in 27:22; Kur'an Yolu says Solomon sent the letter to verify the report and obtain her answer. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says Solomon withheld judgment until he could distinguish the hoopoe's truth from falsehood, then wrote the letter. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]
- 27:28/q05/p2 Solomon already intended contact with Sheba, and the letter sought submission and religious reform. — Iṣlāḥī finds a mere truth test improbable and suggests the lapwing flew there because Solomon already had Sheba in mind. Ibn ʿĀshūr says divine inspiration directed contact, extending influence over the kingdom and its resources and trade; he adds that nearby kings feared and favored Solomon despite his smaller realm and locates that realm around Jordan, Egypt's frontier and the Mediterranean. Al-Biqāʿī stresses prompt removal of sun worship if the report proved true. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 27:28/q06 Who is meant by 'to them'?  [إِلَيْهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural names the whole people or specifically the queen's council.
- 27:28/q06/p1 Bilqis and her people, the sun-worshipping group mentioned in the hoopoe's report. — Al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī appeal to 27:24 and the earlier mention of queen and people; al-Biqāʿī says their shared worship makes religion the reason for the plural; al-Irshād, quoted by Bursevī, says the noble letter calls everyone to Islam; al-Ālūsī says it is meant for the whole people. Al-Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī explicitly name the people of Sheba. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الإرشاد); ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:28/q06/p2 The queen and her ruling council or the country's decision-making notables. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī names the Queen of Sheba and her council; Elmalılı interprets the collective address as the country's notables. [2 notes: ELMALILI; TABATABAI]

## 27:28/q07 What does the fā before 'cast it' signal?  [فَأَلْقِهْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle chiefly marks haste or an understood narrative transition.
- 27:28/q07/p1 It conveys urgency in sending the bird to correct sun worship. — Al-Biqāʿī imagines the bird flying like lightning with God's added help, in honor of His prophet. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 27:28/q07/p2 It is an explicative fā, making omitted intervening acts understood. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares unspoken steps after commands to go and strike the stone, and to the hoopoe's carrying and delivery of the letter. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH]

## 27:28/q08 Why is the verb 'cast it' used, and how literally may it be taken?  [فَأَلْقِهْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb describes the bird's physical dropping of the document or extends to handover from its leg.
- 27:28/q08/p1 It means physically dropping the letter. — Al-Ālūsī says delivery required casting; Ibn ʿĀshūr says ilqāʾ literally means casting to the ground and allows dropping it from the beak, comparing 12:10. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:28/q08/p2 It can figuratively describe people taking the letter from the bird's leg. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares people casting words to each other in 16:86. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:28/q09 How did the hoopoe carry the letter?  [بِّكِتَٰبِى فَأَلْقِهْ · reports]
turns on: The reports place the document in the beak, at the foot, on the wing, or around the neck.
- 27:28/q09/p1 It carried the letter in its beak. — Muqātil, Mujāhid and a report in Ibn Kathīr say so; Mujāhid adds that it circled the queen's chamber, and al-Māwardī says she remarked on the bird; al-Qurṭubī has it flutter above her troops; Abū Ḥayyān also records this report. [6 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل)]
- 27:28/q09/p2 It carried the letter in or on its foot. — Yazīd ibn Rūmān reports the foot; Ibn ʿĀshūr suggests a letter fastened to the leg could be taken off by people. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(يزيد بن رومان); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 27:28/q09/p3 It bore the letter on its wing. — Ibn Kathīr records this as one report. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل)]
- 27:28/q09/p4 It hung the letter around its neck. — Abū Ḥayyān records this account, followed by its landing over Bilqis among her troops. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:28/q10 Where and in what circumstances did the hoopoe deliver the letter?  [فَأَلْقِهْ إِلَيْهِمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on her location and state, and on whether the letter fell on throne, lap or chest.
- 27:28/q10/p1 It dropped the letter to the queen on or at her throne. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk says it flew level with her throne; Ibn ʿAbbās says it reached her throne, while al-Suyūṭī's Ibn ʿAbbās report specifies its middle and says she read the message. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(الضحاك); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,الضحاك); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 27:28/q10/p2 It dropped the letter into her lap while she sat among her troops. — Muqātil, al-Qurṭubī's Muqātil report, al-Nasafī's alternative and Abū Ḥayyān's neck-carrying account place the queen with soldiers; some say it fluttered overhead. Yazīd ibn Rūmān instead has it drop through her window onto her lap after blocking the sunlight. [5 notes: MUQATIL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(يزيد بن رومان)]
- 27:28/q10/p3 It entered the locked sleeping queen's chamber through an opening and dropped the letter on her chest or belly. — Qatāda says keys lay under her head and gives the landing as belly or between breasts; Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Māwardī say chest; Wahb says she awoke startled and summoned her court. Al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr and Bursevī report variants; Bursevī names Maʾrib. Some say the bird then hid in the opening. [13 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; WAHIDI-BASIT; FARRA(يقال)]
- 27:28/q10/p4 It approached through or at the sun-facing window, blocked the sun and then delivered the letter. — Ibn Zayd says it blocked her window before casting the letter and flying away; Yazīd ibn Rūmān says Bilqis worshipped the sun there; Wahb calls it the opening for sunrise; Abū Ḥayyān says blocking the light made her rise to receive the letter. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(يزيد بن رومان); IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:28/q11 What do the reports say about the queen's identity and background?  [إِلَيْهِمْ · reports]
turns on: The reports supply different ancestry, personal capacities and descriptions of her people rather than a single agreed biography.
- 27:28/q11/p1 Bilqis was a Yemeni queen of a royal family, named as the daughter of Sharāḥbīl, who ruled after her people died. — Qatāda is reported by Yaḥyā ibn Sallām and Ibn Abī Ḥātim; al-Māwardī's Yazīd ibn Rūmān instead describes earlier rulers in her family and her training in government. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(يزيد بن رومان)]
- 27:28/q11/p2 She was a literate Arab descendant of Tubbaʿ al-Ḥimyarī. — Muqātil calls her and her people Arabs of Tubbaʿ ibn Abī Sharāḥīl; Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī add her literacy, and Bursevī says she read and wrote Arabic. Names and lineage wording vary. [3 notes: MUQATIL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI]
- 27:28/q11/p3 She was an astute, educated ruler from a royal house whose people practiced Zoroastrianism. — Yazīd ibn Rūmān gives this description in Ibn Abī Ḥātim. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(يزيد بن رومان)]

## 27:28/q12 What effect did the bird and seal have on the queen?  [بِّكِتَٰبِى فَأَلْقِهْ · reports]
- 27:28/q12/p1 The seal frightened her and her troops, and a bird serving as envoy convinced her that Solomon's kingship surpassed hers. — Muqātil says Solomon's authority resided in his seal; Bursevī says its imposing appearance made her tremble and submit, while the bird's obedience showed the greater king. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BURSEVI]

## 27:28/q13 How is the hāʾ of 'fa-alqih' read?  [فَأَلْقِهْ · readings]
turns on: The suffix's vowel, whether its vowel is lengthened, and whether a pause form can be used in connected speech.
- 27:28/q13/p1 With kasra extended by a following yāʾ. — Al-Fārisī names Ibn Kathīr, Ibn ʿĀmir, al-Kisāʾī and some Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr routes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds Warsh, and Ibn al-Jawzī repeats the three readers. Al-Fārisī prefers this regular form and calls suppression of yāʾ mainly poetic; Qirāʾāt-ER says the long ī extends the suffix vowel for recitational rhythm. Al-Fārisī cites Abū al-Ḥasan's dialect claim but says Sībawayh omitted it and treats the poetic case as necessity. Al-Zajjāj lists five forms and calls retention of yāʾ the commonest and strongest; he derives the form from فألقيه, explaining that the yāʾ deleted by the jussive may disappear before the pronoun hāʾ. [14 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(أبو الحسن,أبو عمرو,ابن كثير وابن عامر والكسائي,نافع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير، ابن عامر، الكسائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وابن عامر والكسائي); QIRAAT-ER; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(السبعة); NASHR; ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 27:28/q13/p2 With kasra on the hāʾ but no connected yāʾ, including reduced or light kasra. — Nāfiʿ and several Qālūn routes are reported; al-Baghawī names Abū Jaʿfar, Yaʿqūb and Qālūn for light kasra and others for full kasra. Ibn al-Jawzī names Qālūn; al-Nasafī names Yazīd, Qālūn and Yaʿqūb. Hishām has both this and the connected form according to al-Samīn. Al-Naḥḥās lists deletion retaining kasra; Qirāʾāt-ER notes kasra instead of stillness. Al-Zajjāj includes the form with yāʾ dropped and kasra retained; he relates it to the preference for dropping yāʾ after hāʾ in ألقيه. [12 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة وأبو جعفر ويعقوب وقالون والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قالون عن نافع); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(السبعة); QIRAAT-ER; ZAJJAJ]
- 27:28/q13/p3 With the hāʾ still, without a following vowel letter. — Reports name Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, Abū Bakr and particular routes, with source variation over Abū Jaʿfar. Al-Nashr says Ḥafṣ makes it still, hence all of ʿĀṣim, and notes route-specific Abū Jaʿfar forms. Al-Naḥḥās reports grammarians treating stillness as pause but ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān rejects this allowance in connected speech; Bursevī instead allows ease or a pause form. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī claims vowelless hāʾ in every recitation, whether connected or paused, as hāʾ of pause. Al-Zajjāj includes a still hāʾ among the five reported forms. [14 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو,عباس وعاصم وحمزة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وعاصم وحمزة وأبو جعفر ويعقوب وقالون والآخرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو وعاصم وأبو جعفر وحمزة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASHR(حفص وعاصم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السبعة); TABATABAI; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(علي بن سليمان)-; ZAJJAJ(الشاعر)-]
- 27:28/q13/p4 With ḍamma on the hāʾ and a following wāw. — Al-Naḥḥās lists the wāw form, Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports some readers, and Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī name Muslim ibn Jundub. Al-Samīn calls ḍamma the original vowel. Al-Zajjāj says the wāw form restores the original connected pronoun ending, as in ألقيتهو; at a pause or in writing it is represented by hāʾ. [7 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قوم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مسلم بن جندب); SAMIN-DURR(مسلم بن جندب); ALUSI-FULL(مسلم بن جندب); ZAJJAJ]
- 27:28/q13/p5 With ḍamma retained but the wāw omitted. — Abū Isḥāq's fivefold list, reported by al-Naḥḥās, includes this form. Al-Zajjāj likens dropping wāw while retaining ḍamma to dropping yāʾ while retaining kasra. [3 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); ZAJJAJ]

## 27:28/q14 What grammatical forms do 'kitābī hādhā' and 'alqih' have?  [بِّكِتَٰبِى هَٰذَا فَأَلْقِهْ · grammar]
turns on: The analyses address separate grammatical features of the phrase.
- 27:28/q14/p1 Hādhā can qualify kitābī, replace it appositionally, or explain it in apposition. — Al-Samīn lists the three analyses. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 27:28/q14/p2 Alqih is a jussive command with an object suffix referring to the letter. — Bursevī identifies the suffix and explains silent hāʾ as ease or pause pronunciation. Al-Zajjāj distinguishes the verb's jussive deletion of yāʾ from its object pronoun: the pronoun itself is not jussive, so he calls a still hāʾ reading mistaken. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ZAJJAJ]

## 27:28/q15 What does 'then turn away from them' mean?  [ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ · meaning]
turns on: How far the bird moves, and whether tawallā describes temporary concealment or final departure.
- 27:28/q15/p1 Move aside or hide nearby, out of sight but close enough to watch and hear their reply. — Wahb and Ibn ʿAbbās give the nearby reading; al-Ṭabarī prefers it because the queen's consultation happened after receipt and had to be witnessed. Ibn Abī Ḥātim's Zuhayr specifies some distance; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Rāzī, Bursevī and others describe concealment and listening. Muqātil says withdraw and watch, al-Biqāʿī adds a safe position beyond reach, and Ibn Kathīr says the bird left her private chamber courteously. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Wahb's account that the hoopoe stayed close enough to observe the rulers' reply. [26 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA(يقال); TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,زهير بن محمد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); TAB-FULL+]
- 27:28/q15/p2 Leave the recipients and return to Solomon after seeing their answer. — Ibn Zayd gives this sense in al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām says leave after delivery. This requires taking observation as preceding final departure or as occurring meanwhile. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ibn Zayd's reading of tawallā as returning to Solomon after delivering the letter. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 27:28/q15/p3 Tawallā denotes turning aside, departing or showing disregard. — Muqātil's gloss in al-Wāḥidī is withdrawal; another al-Wāḥidī gloss adds outward disregard. Al-Dāmghānī illustrates departing with 9:92; Ibn al-Jawzī contrasts the sense with other Qurʾānic uses and cites Moses' movement toward shade; one lexical sense of the same verb is assuming authority, such as a governor, though it is not applied to this command. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 27:28/q16 In what order should delivery, withdrawal and observation be understood?  [فَأَلْقِهْ ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ فَٱنظُرْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether withdrawal is a nearby concealed position compatible with watching, or a final departure that must follow observation.
- 27:28/q16/p1 As written: deliver, withdraw nearby, then observe from concealment. — Al-Ṭabarī says the bird must witness the later consultation; al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn prefer the order without rearrangement. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says this leaves the recipients to the letter's contents without escort or pressure. Al-Biqāʿī says thumma allows time to verify the delivery. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls inversion untenable; al-Ālūsī finds it unsuitable because full departure prevents observation. Wahb, as reported by Ibn ʿAṭiyya, has the hoopoe withdraw yet remain near enough to observe the reply. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(وهب بن منبه وغيره); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; TAB-FULL+; TABRISI+; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 27:28/q16/p2 The intended order is deliver, observe the reply, then withdraw or return. — Ibn Zayd supplies the delayed order; al-Zajjāj approves because fronting and delaying are common. Al-Farrāʾ calls it a semantic ordering that urges haste. Abū ʿAlī proposes the same inversion; al-Burhān argues one cannot observe after actually withdrawing, and al-Itqān affirms the inversion. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ibn Zayd's explicit ordering: observation is logically before withdrawal. [15 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); THALABI(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن زيد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو علي,ابن زيد); SAMIN-DURR(أبو علي); ITQAN; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); BURHAN+; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج)+; TABRISI-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR-; BURHAN-; ALUSI-FULL-; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]

## 27:28/q17 Why should the hoopoe withdraw after delivery?  [ثُمَّ تَوَلَّ عَنْهُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: The explanations emphasize royal etiquette, freedom of deliberation, or safe surveillance; Iṣlāḥī disputes the deliberation claim.
- 27:28/q17/p1 Standing back is proper etiquette before a monarch while the messenger remains close enough to hear. — Wahb is cited by al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī; Ibn Kathīr calls the bird's withdrawal courteous. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Wahb's description of the withdrawal as courtly etiquette while the bird stays within hearing. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب); ALUSI-FULL(وهب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]
- 27:28/q17/p2 The recipients should face the letter's substance without the envoy escorting or pressing them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives this explanation. Iṣlāḥī rejects a related claim that the courtiers needed privacy to talk freely before a foreign envoy, because the narrative never says they believed birds could understand speech. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(some scholars)-]
- 27:28/q17/p3 The bird should get to a safe listening place after its bearer's identity has become clear. — Al-Biqāʿī says it first fluttered above them until recognized, then withdrew beyond their reach but still able to hear. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 27:28/q18 What does 'look' mean here?  [فَٱنظُرْ · meaning]
turns on: Whether naẓara is a verb of cognition and reflection or means waiting for the reply.
- 27:28/q18/p1 Observe or reflect on their response and find out what they say. — Abū Ḥayyān allows mental consideration; Bursevī says reflection and discovery; al-Ālūsī prefers reflection and learning their speech. Al-Qurṭubī reports a gloss of knowing, with 78:40 cited. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the following question suspends the verb from its object. [7 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:28/q18/p2 Wait for the response. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn explain that this is not a cognition verb and does not suspend the following clause; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite 57:13. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:28/q19 What are they to 'return'?  [مَاذَا يَرْجِعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the response is viewed as a formal answer, their internal consultation, or the acceptance or rejection disclosed by it.
- 27:28/q19/p1 An answer or reply to Solomon's letter, which the hoopoe is to learn. — Al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī gloss it as their answer; al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz says the queen's answer; Ibn Zayd says the bird must see it before returning to Solomon; Elmalılı notes that obtaining a response distinguishes this mission from simple carriage. [7 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 27:28/q19/p2 What they say back and forth among themselves as they consult about the letter. — Al-Rāzī compares reciprocal answering in 34:30; al-Qurṭubī reports the internal-speech gloss; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī describe hearing their talk. Al-Ṭabarī's reasoning rests on the queen's consultation following delivery; al-Ālūsī prefers this evident sense and says God enabled the hoopoe to understand what it heard. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 27:28/q19/p3 Their reaction will show whether they accept obedience and submission or refuse. — Al-Qāshānī gives this inward account of the response the bird is to inspect. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 27:28/q20 How is 'mādhā yarjiʿūn' parsed?  [فَٱنظُرْ مَاذَا يَرْجِعُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether unẓur means cognition or waiting, and whether mādhā is one interrogative, a split interrogative plus relative, or a relative whole.
- 27:28/q20/p1 Mādhā is one interrogative object of yarjiʿūn, and the question suspends unẓur if it means consider. — Abū Ḥayyān states the single-interrogative option but calls yarjiʿūn its predicate; al-Samīn rejects that combination because the verb cannot govern the interrogative and also predicate it, citing 26:227. Al-Samīn retains the object analysis with the whole interrogative clause under suspended unẓur. Bursevī reports Ibn al-Shaykh's object parse; al-Ālūsī calls yarjiʿūn transitive; Ibn ʿĀshūr agrees the interrogation suspends unẓur and points to 16:24. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMIN-DURR-]
- 27:28/q20/p2 Mā is interrogative and dhā is relative, with yarjiʿūn its relative clause and an understood resumptive object. — Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh give the relative alternative; al-Samīn accepts it under suspended unẓur. Ibn al-Shaykh, cited by Bursevī, calls mā the subject and dhā meaning 'the one which' its predicate; al-Ālūsī explicitly notes the omitted resumptive. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BURSEVI(ابن الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL]
- 27:28/q20/p3 If unẓur means wait, mādhā is a wholly relative object directly governed by it, without suspension. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī make this conditional parse; the latter two cite 57:13 for waiting. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 27:28/q21 What does the verse establish about sending written calls to rulers or polytheists?  [* · law]
- 27:28/q21/p1 A ruler may send letters inviting polytheists to Islam. — Al-Qurṭubī cites the Prophet's letters to Kisra and Caesar and 3:64; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī repeat the precedent, naming Khosrow, Caesar and Arab rulers. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls letter exchange customary among states and preachers and notes the importance of official scribes in Islamic governments. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 27:28/q22 What actions does the narrative leave unstated?  [* · rhetoric]
- 27:28/q22/p1 It omits preparing the letter and the hoopoe's taking, carrying and delivering it; the queen's response supplies the missing sequence. — Abū Ḥayyān identifies the omitted order to write and send and the later carrying; al-Burhān says the queen's reply establishes delivery; al-Sāmarrāʾī adds the bird's waiting and says the meaning remains clear between 27:28 and 27:29. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 27:28/q23 What broader lessons or links are drawn from this passage?  [* · links]
turns on: These are separate links and lessons drawn from the narrative.
- 27:28/q23/p1 The preceding challenge to the hoopoe is explained by testing its strong knowledge claim. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the instruction to 27:27, and Study Quran points to the hoopoe's assertion in 27:22. Ibn ʿAṭiyya links the letter to Solomon's postponed judgment until the hoopoe's truth or falsehood could be determined. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 27:28/q23/p2 A leader should admit legitimate excuses from subordinates. — Study Quran cites al-Qurṭubī's hadith that God loves excuses most and therefore revealed Books and sent messengers. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q,al-Qurṭubī)]
- 27:28/q23/p3 The Solomon and Sheba account concludes with the queen's faith in the One God. — Study Quran's sūra introduction situates this meeting within Solomon's miraculous host. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 27:28/q23/p4 The command implies that God gave the hoopoe the capacity to understand the recipients' speech. — Al-Ālūsī infers this from the task of listening for their answer. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 27:28/q24 What ancillary reports are cited alongside discussion of this passage?  [* · reports]
turns on: The ancillary reports address distinct illustrative matters.
- 27:28/q24/p1 A prophetic report says a people ruled by a woman will not prosper. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām transmits it from Abū Bakra alongside Qatāda's account of Bilqis becoming ruler. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو بكرة)]
- 27:28/q24/p2 Poetry can be used in serious discourse even when its literal utterance is playful or false. — Al-Jurjānī notes al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's sermonic use of a verse about changing lovers and invokes an example attributed to Abū Tammām. He also preserves the story of ʿUmāra ibn al-Walīd, the woman's request that he stop drinking, his later hosting of drinkers and slaughtering his camel, and his verse answering her. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(الحسن البصري)]
- 27:28/q24/p3 A report illustrates ʿUmar's fairness in distributing Yemeni garments. — Al-Jurjānī transmits al-Marzubānī's report from ʿAbd al-Malik ibn ʿUmayr: ʿUmar rejected Zayd's favored choice, hid the garments and assigned them by drawing lots; the narrator praises his fairness. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 27:28/q25 What is said about the readings' place in broader pronoun rules?  [فَأَلْقِهْ · readings]
- 27:28/q25/p1 The forms of fa-alqih are treated among exceptional pronominal hāʾ readings. — Al-Nashr says twelve letters across twenty places depart from ordinary rules, naming fa-alqihi ilayhim and an lam yarahu; its later discussion refers this word back to the hāʾ rules. Al-Zajjāj refers readers to his fuller explanation of the same pronoun construction at 4:75. [3 notes: NASHR; ZAJJAJ]
