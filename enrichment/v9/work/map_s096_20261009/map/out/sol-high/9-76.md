# 9:76: 16 questions

## 9:76/q01 What does lamma indicate here?  [فَلَمَّآ · grammar]
- 9:76/q01/p1 It introduces a past occurrence and makes their stinginess follow causally upon God's gift. — Al-Tabrisi compares lamma with idha, but distinguishes past-oriented lamma and lawla from future-oriented in and idha; the second event occurred because the first did. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 9:76/q02 What did God give them from His bounty?  [ءَاتَىٰهُم مِّن فَضْلِهِۦ · interpretation]
- 9:76/q02/p1 God gave them the wealth they had requested and hoped to receive. — Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Nasafi identify the gift as the requested wealth; al-Qurtubi expressly makes God the giver. Muqatil, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, al-Bursawi and al-Alusi connect the gift with their subsequent withholding. Al-Tabatabai describes poor hypocrites enriched after their promise. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:76/q03 Why is from His bounty repeated?  [مِّن فَضْلِهِۦ · rhetoric]
- 9:76/q03/p1 It echoes the pledger's own words and stresses that the gift was entirely God's favor. — Al-Biqa'i says repetition confirms the earlier pledge's wording and underscores that God owes no one a gift and no creature controls His gifts. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 9:76/q04 What object is omitted after gave them?  [ءَاتَىٰهُم مِّن فَضْلِهِۦ · grammar]
- 9:76/q04/p1 The omitted second object is what they had requested from His bounty. — Al-Tabrisi supplies the object from the preceding request. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 9:76/q05 What is the stinginess described here?  [بَخِلُوا۟ بِهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether bukhl names a withheld obligatory right, a wider refusal to give, a constricted view of ownership, or an inward withholding; al-Rummani challenges equating generosity with easy discharge of debt.
- 9:76/q05/p1 They withheld the due of the gift: promised charity or zakat, other obligatory rights, and good spending. — Al-Tabari names charity, kin maintenance and spending for God; Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Nasafi, al-Bayḍawi, al-Alusi, al-Biqa'i, al-Tabrisi and al-Bursawi emphasize God's due or their pledge. Al-Qurtubi also names spending on good. Al-Razi calls it withholding a right. Al-Jishumi and al-Qushayri distinguish religious or outward obligations from broader senses; Abu Hayyan argues a threat after stinginess denotes an obligatory right. Al-Tabrisi connects religious usage to zakat but says the original sense involves refusing a petitioner because giving feels difficult. Al-Rummani objects that defining generosity by easy payment of an obligation would absurdly make debt repayment generosity. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI(الرماني)-]
- 9:76/q05/p2 Stinginess is failing to give when one has the capacity or when generosity is needed. — Al-Jishumi defines bukhl generally as withholding what one could give. Abu Hafs says it is abandoning generosity at the moment of need. Al-Tabrisi gives the older usage of refusal to a petitioner because giving is difficult. Zuhayr's poem, cited by al-Tabrisi, says the miser remains blameworthy and the generous person praiseworthy wherever they are. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص); JISHUMI; TABRISI(زهير)]
- 9:76/q05/p3 It is withholding a gift that wisdom calls for when giving would cause no harm and keeping it brings no benefit. — Al-Rummani's definition makes the refusal a sign of constricted spirit even without material harm. He challenges a definition tied simply to difficult discharge of an obligation with the example of paying a debt. [2 notes: TABRISI(الرماني); TABRISI(الرماني)+]
- 9:76/q05/p4 The possessive belief that wealth is one's own is itself stinginess. — Hamdun al-Qassar says this attitude keeps other people's hands away from the wealth. [1 notes: SULAMI(حمدون القصار)]
- 9:76/q05/p5 At the inward level, stinginess includes preferring anything to God's pleasure and withholding oneself from obedience or God. — Al-Qushayri says each person's stinginess takes a form suited to their state: wealth withheld loses blessing, the self withheld from obedience loses health, and the spirit withheld from God is forsaken and made miserable. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 9:76/q06 From what did they turn away?  [وَتَوَلَّوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: The explanations specify the broken covenant, obedience and divine commands, religion itself, or gratitude as the object of their turning.
- 9:76/q06/p1 They turned away from the covenant and promise they made to God. — Al-Tabari, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Ashur explicitly identify the covenant; al-Razi makes this distinct from disregard of God's duties. Al-Biqa'i stresses the emphatic pledge and their knowledge that breaking it was ugly. Al-Qushayri describes an unfulfilled covenant followed by entrenched hypocrisy. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:76/q06/p2 They turned away from obedience to God and His commands. — Al-Tabrisi describes turning from His command; al-Qurtubi, al-Bayḍawi, al-Nasafi and al-Alusi specify obedience. Al-Razi separately mentions disregarding divine duties and commands. Al-Jishumi connects turning from repentance and obedience to punishment. [10 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:76/q06/p3 They openly turned away from Islam or God's religion. — Al-Qurtubi calls the turning openly away from Islam; al-Tabrisi describes aversion to God's religion. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 9:76/q06/p4 They turned away from gratitude for their Lord's blessing. — Ibn Ashur couples ingratitude with turning from their covenant. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 9:76/q07 What does the closing description add?  [وَّهُم مُّعْرِضُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause primarily marks a persistent disposition or distinguishes inward aversion from outward turning; the two descriptions can coexist.
- 9:76/q07/p1 Their aversion was a settled, continuing habit, already present beneath their pledge. — Al-Bayḍawi, al-Nasafi and al-Bursawi describe persistent aversion to obedience. Al-Biqa'i says it was established when they pledged, and al-Alusi says that established habit explains their conduct under either syntax. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:76/q07/p2 Their outward turning was matched by inward aversion in their hearts. — Al-Biqa'i speaks of both hearts and bodies; al-Alusi reports the distinction between bodily turning and inward aversion. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 9:76/q08 How is the closing clause construed?  [وَّهُم مُّعْرِضُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Al-Alusi permits either syntactic attachment without changing the continuing character of their aversion.
- 9:76/q08/p1 It is an independent clause describing their established aversion. — Al-Alusi allows an independent construction and says continued habitual aversion fits it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:76/q08/p2 It is a circumstantial clause describing them while they turned away. — Al-Alusi also allows a circumstantial construction and says habitual aversion fits this syntax too. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:76/q09 Who are the people denoted by the plural pronouns?  [ءَاتَىٰهُم بَخِلُوا۟ وَتَوَلَّوا۟ وَّهُم · referent]
turns on: Competing attributions identify one Tha'laba, two named men, one Mu'attib whose clan takes the plural, or a general class; Kur'an Yolu disputes the common occasion reports on historical grounds.
- 9:76/q09/p1 The reported occasion centers on the Anṣārī Tha'laba ibn Hatib. — Ibn Abbas and other commentators identify Tha'laba. The numerous flock accounts narrate his vow and refusal. Ibn Kathir cites Ibn Abbas, Hasan and many commentators; al-Tabatabai acknowledges the reports, while Study Quran reports the linkage but reads the verses generally. Kur'an Yolu questions the historical evidence behind the common occasion reports. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,ثعلبة بن حاطب الأنصاري); IBNABIHATIM(ثعلبة بن حاطب); WAHIDI-ASBAB; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة); TABRISI(أبو أمامة الباهلي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن البصري); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو أمامة,ابن عباس); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 9:76/q09/p2 They are Tha'laba and Mu'attib ibn Qushayr together. — Other authorities and al-Hasan name the two men; al-Hasan says both belonged to Banu Amr ibn Awf. Al-Hasan and Mujahid in al-Baghawi say their vow was public. Ibn Ashur allows that Mu'attib and others followed Tha'laba and became similarly stingy, though no separate story about them is known. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,الحسن); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:76/q09/p3 The plural denotes one man, Mu'attib ibn Qushayr, with his deed ascribed to his clan. — Ibn Ashur reports this attribution and explains the plural as Arab usage attaching an individual's deed to his group. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:76/q09/p4 They are a class or group of hypocrites rather than an identified individual. — Ibn Zayd calls them a class of hypocrites whose hypocrisy lasted to death without repentance, forgiveness or pardon, comparing Iblis. Ibn Kathir and al-Tabatabai describe a group that pledged charity. Study Quran reads such descriptions in the sura generally; Kur'an Yolu treats the passage as an example of conduct that faith failed to discipline. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]

## 9:76/q10 How can a singular person's act be expressed with plural forms here?  [ءَاتَىٰهُم بَخِلُوا۟ وَتَوَلَّوا۟ · grammar]
- 9:76/q10/p1 An individual's deed can be attached to his clan, producing a plural reference. — Ibn Ashur uses Arab usage to explain the attribution to Mu'attib alone. Al-Zarkashi's al-Burhan cites this verse among examples of singular-form clauses followed by plural meaning, treating it as an exception alongside 6:36, 9:49 and 9:75. [2 notes: BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 9:76/q11 What occasion or sequence of events is reported for this verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ over a multiplying flock, inherited wealth, an unnamed Anṣārī, two assembly participants, or a general group; al-Suyuti flags a weak report and Kur'an Yolu disputes common historical attributions.
- 9:76/q11/p1 Tha'laba sought wealth, gained a multiplying flock after the Prophet's prayer, neglected worship, refused zakat, and his later offer was rejected. — Al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, al-Tabrisi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Bursawi, Elmalılı, Ibn Ashur and Study Quran relay stages of the account. The Prophet warned that thankable little wealth is better than unmanageable plenty (al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, Elmalılı); Tha'laba pledged every person's due, moved out of Medina as sheep multiplied and abandoned congregation then Friday prayer (al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Kathir, Elmalılı). Collectors brought a written order after zakat was imposed (Ibn Atiyya); he called zakat a tax like jizya and delayed (Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tha'labi, Ibn Atiyya, Ibn Kathir, Elmalılı). A Sulami man freely gave choice camels, and the Prophet blessed him (Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tha'labi, al-Wahidi, Ibn Kathir). The Prophet lamented Tha'laba, in some versions before hearing the collectors (al-Baghawi, al-Bursawi, Elmalılı). A relative or Umar warned Tha'laba of revelation (al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Bursawi). The Prophet said God forbade accepting the later offer; Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman also refused it, and Tha'laba died under Uthman (al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Bursawi, Elmalılı, Ibn Ashur). Al-Bursawi attributes his distress to public shame rather than repentance and cites al-Haddadi for Uthman's refusal. Al-Suyuti labels Abu Umama's report weak and also gives an Ibn Abbas version. Ibn Kathir links 9:103 to the case. Kur'an Yolu objects that common occasion reports conflict with historical evidence. [44 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,ثعلبة بن حاطب الأنصاري,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNABIHATIM(السلمي,ثعلبة بن حاطب); THALABI(أبو بكر وعمر وعثمان,النبي); WAHIDI-ASBAB; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(أبو أمامة الباهلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ثعلبة بن حاطب الأنصاري); SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو أمامة,ابن عباس); BURSEVI(الحدادي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النبي عليه السلام); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 9:76/q11/p2 Tha'laba inherited wealth from a cousin after promising charity, kin care and every due, but failed to keep that promise. — Al-Baghawi attributes the inheritance account to Ibn Abbas, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Qatada; al-Hasan in al-Durr also says the Anṣārī inherited his cousin's wealth and hypocrisy followed the broken promise. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وسعيد بن جبير وقتادة); DURR-FULL(الحسن)]
- 9:76/q11/p3 An unnamed Anṣārī at an assembly promised to distribute rightful dues if enriched but acted otherwise. — Qatada in al-Tabari gives the assembly and vow without naming the man. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة)]
- 9:76/q11/p4 Two men left an assembly after vowing charity if enriched, but withheld it when wealth came. — Mujahid in al-Tabari gives two unnamed men; al-Hasan and Mujahid in al-Baghawi identify the pair as Tha'laba and Mu'attib and call their vow public. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد)]
- 9:76/q11/p5 A group of hypocrites promised charity and righteousness if enriched but broke the promise. — Al-Qushayri says they remained trapped in hypocrisy; Ibn Kathir and al-Tabatabai describe a group of hypocrites. Ibn Zayd compares their unrepentant persistence until meeting God to Iblis. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 9:76/q12 Was their pledge spoken aloud?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the preceding covenant was a public vow or an unspoken intention; Sa'id invokes 9:78 for the latter.
- 9:76/q12/p1 The pledge was publicly spoken. — Ibn Abbas in al-Tabari says Tha'laba pledged publicly; Qatada places the vow at an assembly, and al-Hasan and Mujahid describe the two men's public vow. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن ومجاهد)]
- 9:76/q12/p2 It was an inward intention, never voiced. — Some authorities and Sa'id ibn Thabit say this. Sa'id appeals to 9:78 on God's knowledge of secrets and private conversations. Mu'tamir recounts an analogous unspoken sea-storm vow; his father Sulayman told him to voice or fulfill it. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن ثابت,قوم,معتمر بن سليمان التيمي)]

## 9:76/q13 What does this passage establish about signs of hypocrisy?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the familiar three-trait hadith gives a universal diagnostic or describes particular hypocrites; Ata's example of Joseph's brothers and the reported Prophetic clarification support restriction.
- 9:76/q13/p1 Lying, breaking promises and betraying trusts or covenants are three signs of hypocrisy. — Al-Tabari cites Companions, Successors and Prophetic reports; Abdullah, Abdullah ibn Amr, Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Umar and Muhammad ibn Ka'b recite this passage, with Muhammad ibn Ka'b also invoking 33:72 and 63:1. Prophetic versions in al-Tabari, al-Tha'labi and al-Durr say the traits mark a hypocrite even if he prays, fasts and claims Islam. Abdullah ibn Amr or his father honored a marriage promise on his deathbed for fear of one-third hypocrisy; al-Hasan's question about an insolvent debtor shows his concern about a blanket application. In an account in al-Tha'labi, Muqatil ibn Hayyan feared for himself and all people, left his judgeship, asked scholars in Bukhara, Merv and Nishapur, was directed by Shahr to Sa'id in Ray, and Sa'id sent him to Hasan. Al-Hasan initially answers that the hadith is as transmitted. Al-Tabari connects broken promises and lying in 9:75-77 to hypocrisy. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله,عبد الله بن عمرو,عبد الله بن عمرو بن وائل,محمد بن كعب القرظي); THALABI(الحسن,النبي,سعيد بن جبير,عبد الله بن عمرو,عبد الله بن مسعود,مقاتل بن حيان); DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمر,محمد بن كعب القرظي); TAB-FULL(الحسن,جابر بن عبد الله,عطاء بن أبي رباح)-; THALABI(الحسن البصري,جابر بن عبد الله,عطاء بن أبي رباح)-]
- 9:76/q13/p2 Four marks constitute complete hypocrisy: lying, breaking promises, betraying covenants and indecency in dispute. — Al-Tha'labi transmits an Ibn Umar report through Masruq that any one remains a mark until abandoned. [1 notes: THALABI(النبي)]
- 9:76/q13/p3 The hadith's three traits refer specifically to the hypocrites who lied to the Prophet, betrayed his trust and broke their campaign pledge. — Ata cites Joseph's brothers, who committed the three acts without being hypocrites, against a universal reading. Jabir says the intended men lied to the Prophet, disclosed his confidential report of Abu Sufyan's location and failed to campaign, connecting 8:27 with 9:75-77. Al-Hasan accepts Ata's account. Al-Tha'labi warns that the hadith's apparent wording can mislead; in his account Hasan says Shahr heard half the report, Fatima conveyed the Companions' alarm, and the Prophet publicly clarified the restriction. The Prophet relates their false faith claim to 63:1, their prayer and religious trust to 4:142 and 107:4-5, and their broken promise to Tha'laba's charity pledge. Their clarification relieved the Companions, who gave generously. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(جابر بن عبد الله,عطاء بن أبي رباح); THALABI(الحسن البصري,النبي,جابر بن عبد الله,عطاء بن أبي رباح); TAB-FULL(الحسن)+; THALABI+]

## 9:76/q14 What moral consequence or guidance do the sources draw from their conduct?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw related consequences of punishment, loss of trustworthy conduct, and abiding hypocrisy, without opposing one another.
- 9:76/q14/p1 Turning away from repentance and obedience incurs punishment, so the warning urges obedience and deters sin. — Al-Jishumi draws this warning from their turn away. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 9:76/q14/p2 Self-interested promises followed by stinginess show faith failing to govern conduct and destroy responsibility and trust. — Kur'an Yolu contrasts this with Prophetic reports that deeds depend on intentions and that one may be rewarded for intending good even if unable to do it; its point concerns people who can act but choose not to. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Hz. Peygamber)]
- 9:76/q14/p3 The unrepentant withholding leaves hypocrisy in their hearts until they meet God. — Ibn Zayd says there was no repentance, forgiveness or pardon, comparing Iblis; al-Qushayri says the broken covenant trapped them in hypocrisy; al-Hasan in al-Durr says the broken promise brought hypocrisy until death. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); QUSHAYRI; DURR-FULL(الحسن)]

## 9:76/q15 What response to withheld zakat is reported?  [بَخِلُوا۟ بِهِۦ · law]
turns on: These are distinct reported responses to withheld zakat; the notes do not claim they address the same case.
- 9:76/q15/p1 The Prophet refused Tha'laba's belated offering by God's order, and the first three caliphs likewise refused it. — Al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir, al-Bursawi, Elmalılı and Ibn Ashur transmit the refusal; the Prophet also called it a consequence of his prior disobedience. Reports differ in detail over which caliphs are individually named, while the fuller accounts include Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(ثعلبة بن حاطب الأنصاري); IBNABIHATIM(ثعلبة بن حاطب); THALABI(أبو بكر وعمر وعثمان,النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ثعلبة بن حاطب الأنصاري); BURSEVI(الحدادي,النبي عليه السلام); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:76/q15/p2 Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz told an official to leave a zakat withholder to the contempt his act would incur. — Abu Hayyan reports this response to an official's notice of nonpayment. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز)]

## 9:76/q16 How does this verse connect with its surrounding passage and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources draw distinct links to the adjacent covenant and requital, the sura's wider portrait, 9:103, and illustrative commands and promises.
- 9:76/q16/p1 The passage escalates from ingratitude without a pledge to the graver ingratitude of betraying an explicit covenant. — Al-Biqa'i says the next verse's grave requital, 9:77, underscores the seriousness of that betrayal. Ibn Ashur says the charity pledge explains the earlier covenant in 9:75. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 9:76/q16/p2 The verse belongs to a broader portrayal of hypocrites, including criticism of believers' donations. — Al-Tabatabai juxtaposes the enriched pledge-breakers with hypocrites who criticized both affluent and poor donors, all condemned as hypocrites beyond forgiveness. Al-Jishumi says they could fault nothing except that God enriched them from His bounty. Abu Qilaba likens sectarian followers of desire to hypocrites, citing 9:58 and 9:61 alongside this passage. Study Quran notes the sura's other general descriptions of hypocrites. [4 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(أبو قلابة); STUDYQURAN]
- 9:76/q16/p3 The account associates the charity command in 9:103 with Tha'laba's case. — Ibn Kathir reports the connection, and al-Suyuti's weak Abu Umama account and Ibn Abbas version also mention 9:103. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; SUYUTI-LUBAB(أبو أمامة,ابن عباس)]
- 9:76/q16/p4 The passage exemplifies the warning against neglected divine commands and broken pledges. — Al-Tabari transmits the Prophet's account of Israelites asking Moses for three manageable commands instead of the Torah, then neglecting even those. Another Prophetic promise offers Paradise to those who avoid lying, promise-breaking and betrayal and guard eyes, hands and private parts from betrayal, theft and adultery. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
