## 2:40/q02 What does the name Israel mean?  [إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · meaning]
turns on: The reported Hebrew and narrative derivations differ.
- 2:40/q02/p1 Servant of God, equivalent to Abd Allah. — Ibn Abbas parses Isra as servant and El as God; Abdullah ibn al-Harith confirms El; angelic names are compared. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الحارث); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الحرث البصري); ITQAN(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:40/q02/p2 God's chosen one. — Tha'labi, Kashshaf and others report the Hebrew derivation; Abu Hayyan attributes it to Ibn Abbas and others. [13 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:40/q02/p3 One whom God strengthened or firmly formed. — Al-Mahdawi derives Isra from strength; Kur'an Yolu finds this probable; Abu Hayyan calls popular etymologies weak. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(المهدوي); JISHUMI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 2:40/q02/p4 Man of God. — Al-Qaffal says Hebrew Isra means human. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:40/q02/p5 Night migrant toward God. — Al-Suhayli and other accounts connect the name to Jacob's night migration; Abu Hayyan questions popular etymologies. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(السهيلي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ITQAN; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:40/q02/p6 Other proposed meanings include God's sword and one striving in God's path. — Kur'an Yolu reports these. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 2:40/q05 How is Banī Isrāʾīl formed grammatically?  [بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · grammar]
turns on: The disputed lost radical and range of collective descent.
- 2:40/q05/p1 Banī is an accusative construct vocative; Israel is genitive in position but diptote. — Zajjaj supplies implicit 'I call'; Nahhas notes deleted nūn and accusative yā; Ibn Atiyya cites al-Khalil and Abu Ali; the plural can take feminine agreement. [25 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو علي الفارسي,الخليل); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النابغة,يسدد); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:40/q05/p2 Ibn derives from yā connected to building. — Zajjaj, Abu Ishaq and Jawhari prefer this; the father is the foundation and the son a branch. Fatūwa answers the banūwa objection. [12 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والأخفش); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(ابن درستويه,الجوهري)]
- 2:40/q05/p3 Ibn derives from wāw. — Al-Akhfash cites banūwa and more frequent deletion of wāw; Nahhas, Samin and Alusi dispute those grounds. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والأخفش); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); ALUSI-FULL(الأخفش); NAHHAS-; ALUSI-FULL(الجوهري)-]
- 2:40/q05/p4 The plural title includes women as well as men. — Alusi says ibn literally denotes male sons but the construct commonly covers all descendants; al-Salikuti calls this figurative. Alusi notes prohibited metaphorical divine sonship. [5 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(الساليكوتي)]

## 2:40/q07 What accounts explain Jacob's name and history?  [إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · reports]
turns on: The narratives and counts differ.
- 2:40/q07/p1 Jacob wrestled an angel and was named Israel. — Abu Majliz says Jacob seized the angel and demanded a name; Durr adds a blow to his thighs and calls it an angelic name. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو مجلز); ITQAN(أبو مجلز)]
- 2:40/q07/p2 Jacob captured a jinn called Il that extinguished Temple lamps. — Ka'b describes Jacob's Temple service; Samin mentions a mixed-language name. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(كعب); SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم,قيل)]
- 2:40/q07/p3 Jacob and Esau's rivalry, Isaac's blessings and Jacob's flight are recounted. — Tha'labi reports the twins' birth, Rebecca's disguise, blessings and Esau's threat; Abu Hayyan mentions night migration to their uncle. [6 notes: THALABI(أم يعقوب,إسحاق,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غير مسمى)]
- 2:40/q07/p4 Reports differ over prophets with two names. — Ibn al-Jawzi singles out Jacob besides Muhammad; al-Khalil lists five pairs; an Ibn Abbas report singles out Israel and Jesus; Jawhari calls Messiah underived. ## 2:40/q08 What does 'remember' ask them to do?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether recollection alone or gratitude is intended.
- 2:40/q08/p1 Preserve the favor in heart and memory, without forgetting. — Mawardi and Ibn Atiyya oppose recollection to forgetting; Qurtubi regards inward retention as a good explanation. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); BURSEVI; ELMALILI]
- 2:40/q08/p2 Give thanks, praise and obey the Giver. — Baghawi transmits Hasan; Kashshaf requires recognition, magnification and obedience; Ibn Jawzi says memory without gratitude is incomplete; Wahidi identifies belief in Muhammad as required gratitude. [14 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن الأنباري); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:40/q08/p3 Bring something to attention by heart or tongue. — Jishumi and Tabrisi list honor, scripture, prayer and speech among dhikr's uses; Harali defines recalling what was forgotten. [13 notes: FARRA; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:40/q09 How do the forms and senses of dhikr differ?  [ٱذْكُرُوا۟ · meaning]
- 2:40/q09/p1 Heart remembrance and spoken mention differ by vowel or are synonymous. — Al-Kisai assigns damma to heart and kasra to speech; others treat both alike; Alusi weighs homonymy against a shared general sense. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكسائي); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:40/q09/p2 The imperative derives from the remembrance verb. — Nahhas explains deletion of nūn and connecting alif and initial damma; Farra equates a form with tadhakkaru; Baydawi reports udhkuru and idhtakiru. [4 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:40/q10 What does niʿmah mean?  [نِعْمَتِىَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether a benefit's ultimate outcome affects its name.
- 2:40/q10/p1 A bestowed benefit intended kindly. — Damghani and Ibn Jawzi gloss favor; Jishumi requires good intent; Razi excludes pure harm, self-serving help and poisoned gifts; Harali includes bodily and spiritual suitability. [9 notes: DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي)]
- 2:40/q10/p2 All benefits ultimately come from God. — Razi cites 16:53 and 31:14: God creates intermediaries, capacity, motive and guidance; people may still be thanked, as a hadith says. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 2:40/q10/p3 Worldly benefits bless even disbelievers. — Razi prefers Baqillani, citing creation, provision, rescue, security and prosperity; the dispute is partly terminological. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(أهل السنة); RAZI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)+; RAZI-FULL(بعض أهل السنة)-]
- 2:40/q10/p4 Worldly benefits that end in eternal harm are not blessings to disbelievers. — Some Sunnis compare them to poisoned sweets and cite 3:178. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(بعض أهل السنة); RAZI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)-]
- 2:40/q10/p5 All accountable people receive equal religious aid; some also require the best worldly aid. — Razi attributes the latter to Baghdadi Mu'tazilites, not Basrans; Sunnis say God creates both benefits and harms. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة)]

## 2:40/q13 How do favors to ancestors count for descendants?  [أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Ancestral benefit and the motive for direct address.
- 2:40/q13/p1 Descendants inherit ancestral benefit and honor. — Zajjaj cites Arabic attribution of forebears' deeds to descendants; Razi adds existence itself; Alusi invokes shared religious inheritance. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن البصري); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وجمهور العلماء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قتادة)]

- 2:40/q13/p3 Recalling past gifts raises hope for future favor. — Razi says a repeated benefactor is expected to continue kindness, restraining opposition. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:40/q14 How is the yāʾ of niʿmatī read?  [نِعْمَتِىَ · readings]
turns on: Resolution of yāʾ before connecting alif.
- 2:40/q14/p1 Vowel it with fatḥa before the article. — Farra, Akhfash, Zajjaj, Tabrisi and Ibn Atiyya prefer preserving the letter and avoiding two silent letters; Abu Hayyan and Alusi say the seven readers agree. [9 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; AKHFASH+; FARRA+; TABRISI+; ZAJJAJ+]
- 2:40/q14/p2 Leave it still, dropping it where two sukūns meet. — Farra permits this before alif-lam and discusses hamza contexts and 39:53/17; Baydawi notes a pause/connection practice. [7 notes: FARRA(الكسائي); NAHHAS; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 2:40/q15 How is the yāʾ of biʿahdī read?  [بِعَهْدِىٓ · readings]
- 2:40/q15/p1 It remains quiescent before the following alif. — Ibn Mujahid records unmoved forms, Nafi's general vocalization rule and exceptions, Ibn Jammaz and Ismail ibn Ja'far's still yāʾ; Nashr says readers agree here because of the many letters. [5 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن جماز وإسماعيل بن جعفر,نافع); NASHR(القراء)]

## 2:40/q16 How is the relative phrase constructed?  [ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ · grammar]
- 2:40/q16/p1 Allatī modifies niʿmah; the relative clause omits a resumptive object. — Nahhas, Abu Hayyan, Samin and Alusi supply 'I bestowed it'; Samin cites a 9:69 reading for deletion after preposition loss. [6 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:40/q17 What does ʿahd mean?  [بِعَهْدِىٓ بِعَهْدِكُمْ · meaning]
- 2:40/q17/p1 A pledge, testament or undertaking to preserve and fulfill. — Mawardi cites scripture and oath; Jishumi says command/testament; Tabatabai derives preservation; Biqai's Harali, Isfahani and Khalil describe prior private undertaking and continual tending; Kur'an Yolu lists compact, promise and oath. - 2:40/q34/p1 Seeking refuge is a prayer for protection under reciprocal servitude and lordship. — Razi says the declarative form asks protection, and discusses the present/future imperfect, first-person self-narration and bāʾ attachment. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 2:40/q35 What wider conduct is drawn from the command passage?  [* · interpretation]
- 2:40/q35/p1 Keeping commands requires abstaining from hidden and public wrongs and excess. — Biqai transmits Harali on greed, usury, spying, mockery, sexual misconduct and pride; worship without abstinence is medicine without diet. The Qur'an can guide or testify against; lawful breadth permits gratitude and restrictions cultivate patience. [17 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي,عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 2:40/q35/p2 An unattributed dialogue depicts temptations opposed to obedience. — Durr lists corrupt book, recitation, food, drink, house, snares, caller and market. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(غير مسمى)]
