# 11:53: 24 questions

## 11:53/q01 How is this reply introduced after Hūd's appeal?  [قَالُوا۟ · links]
- 11:53/q01/p1 It is the people's direct answer to Hūd's call and counsel. — Bursevī says قالوا starts a new answer to an implied question about their response after his commands and prohibitions. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the absence of a conjunction marks dialogue responding to an invitation. Biqāʿī says Hūd's exceptionally clear counsel and signs drew only rejection. [3 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:53/q02 What does their address to Hūd convey?  [يَٰهُودُ · rhetoric]
- 11:53/q02/p1 It marks harsh distance, reproach, and the importance of their coming statement. — Biqāʿī finds rudeness in using his bare name without an honorific. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the opening address gives weight to what follows, treats Hūd as distant through heedlessness, and may express blame figuratively or through literal and figurative address together. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:53/q03 What does bayyina mean here?  [بِبَيِّنَةٍۢ · meaning]
- 11:53/q03/p1 A clear proof or sign establishing the truth of Hūd's claim and distinguishing truth from falsehood. — Al-Rāzī derives the name from its making truth distinct from falsehood. Biqāʿī connects it to bayān, which separates and clarifies a meaning in the mind. Abū Ḥayyān calls it an argument for the truth of Hūd's claim; Bḍ, reported by Study Quran, says it verifies his being God's prophet. The other glosses call it clear explanation, proof, argument, or miracle. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]

## 11:53/q04 How do interpreters understand the claim that Hūd brought no proof?  [مَا جِئْتَنَا بِبَيِّنَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'no proof' states what the people demanded or accurately describes the evidence Hūd had actually shown.
- 11:53/q04/p1 The people assert that Hūd supplied no evidence adequate to establish his divine mission. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī and others explain the claim as refusing to concede his truthful call to worship God alone and his prophethood. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they treat the call as unsupported and thus unworthy of heed. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they deny that his miracles count as signs; his analogy is Quraysh's dismissal of the Quran as magic or poetry. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 11:53/q04/p2 They demanded a palpable miracle or signs matching their own demands. — Iṣlāḥī says they required a palpable miracle before accepting Hūd or abandoning their deities. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Hūd's actual signs failed to meet their demands; their claim is presented as a reason to keep the gods, though this is no logical inference from seeing no sign. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:53/q04/p3 Their denial was false: Hūd had brought real signs and miracles. — Al-Wāḥidī calls it slander and transgression. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī and Biqāʿī compare Quraysh's sign demands despite the Prophet's signs; Biqāʿī cites 13:7. Al-Rāzī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī affirm Hūd's miracles. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuranyolu cite 11:59 while saying no particular miracle is identified; Ibn ʿĀshūr tentatively suggests a prediction of exceptional abundance and continuous fertility, and also mentions 41:15. Al-Kuranyolu adds rational proofs. Bḍ, as reported by Study Quran, attributes the failure to see proofs to obstinacy. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ); BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 11:53/q05 Why did they deny or fail to recognize Hūd's evidence?  [مَا جِئْتَنَا بِبَيِّنَةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes attribute the denial variously to willful resistance, poor attention, learned assumptions, or inward veiling.
- 11:53/q05/p1 Deliberate obstinacy, slander, and refusal made them deny clear signs. — Al-Wāḥidī calls the claim slander and refusal to accept evidence; al-Bayḍāwī calls it extreme obstinacy. Biqāʿī says they resisted clear reason and transmitted proof and that their emphatic defiance belies sincere uncertainty. Al-Bursawī and Bḍ, reported in Study Quran, likewise stress obstinate rejection; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the denial slander. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 11:53/q05/p2 Blindness to truth and failure to examine the signs led them to misjudge them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls their failure blindness. Abū Ḥayyān reports blindness and neglect of consideration as an explanation. Al-Ālūsī offers blindness and inattention as an alternative to stubborn resistance. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:53/q05/p3 Imitation, doubt, false premises, and hostility toward the messenger obstructed reflection. — Al-Jishumī names doubts, imitation of leaders, and false premises. Al-Ṭabrisī adds imitation of ancestors, rejection of the bearer without examining evidence, and suspicion about validity. Biqāʿī adds suppression of doubt, accusing the source, and envy; he says doubt underlies these causes. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 11:53/q05/p4 Natural veils blinded their inner sight to the demonstration. — Al-Qāshānī gives an inward account of why they could not grasp the proof. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 11:53/q06 What prophetic report bears on whether Hūd had signs?  [بِبَيِّنَةٍۢ · reports]
- 11:53/q06/p1 Every prophet received signs by which people could believe, so Hūd too had signs even though this passage does not specify them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite this prophetic report. Abū Ḥayyān gives no grading; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the hadith authentic. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(النبي); IBNASHUR-FULL(محمد)]

## 11:53/q07 What are the gods they refuse to leave?  [ءَالِهَتِنَا · referent]
- 11:53/q07/p1 They are the idols or partners the people worshipped. — Ibn al-Jawzī expressly identifies them as idols. Muqātil, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī describe their worship or the partners they refuse to abandon. [9 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; MUQATIL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:53/q08 Why did they worship idols?  [ءَالِهَتِنَا · interpretation]
- 11:53/q08/p1 They sought nearness to God, expected worldly favor, or fashioned divine likenesses under anthropomorphic ideas. — Al-Ṭabrisī gives these alternatives: presumed intercession, Satan's promise of worldly benefit, and images shaped by a conceived divine form. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 11:53/q09 What do they mean by refusing to leave their gods, and how sound is their reason?  [وَمَا نَحْنُ بِتَارِكِىٓ ءَالِهَتِنَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether lack of a newly accepted sign justifies continued idol worship when reason already distinguishes God from powerless idols.
- 11:53/q09/p1 They will keep worshipping the idols and will not abandon them merely on Hūd's word. — Muqātil and al-Jishumī say they reject his order to leave worship they regard as permissible. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they consider his statement unsupported by a sign; al-Ālūsī calls it a bare assertion in their view. Ibn ʿĀshūr says they present unmet sign demands as a reason, though it does not logically follow. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the refusal answers one part of Hūd's appeal. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL-]
- 11:53/q09/p2 Reason itself requires leaving powerless idols, regardless of Hūd's personal assertion. — Al-Rāzī argues that the people already admit only God brings benefit and harm while idols bring neither, making their stated reasoning poor. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 11:53/q10 Why is the nūn absent from tārikī?  [بِتَارِكِىٓ ءَالِهَتِنَا · grammar]
- 11:53/q10/p1 The active participle is annexed to ālihatinā, so its final nūn is dropped. — Al-Bursawī explains the omission by the iḍāfa construction. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 11:53/q11 What does ʿan qawlik mean and how is it constructed?  [عَن قَوْلِكَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ʿan is circumstantial or causal, and whether a departure expression is omitted or understood through semantic inclusion.
- 11:53/q11/p1 It is a circumstantial phrase tied to the pronoun in tārikī: they would leave their gods while departing from Hūd's saying. — Al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, supplies the circumstantial analysis; al-Nasafī, al-Samīn and al-Bursawī follow it. Al-Bayḍāwī describes their state in refusing. Al-Samīn explicitly prefers it over causal attachment; al-Bursawī says this construction makes Hūd's word the effective cause more strongly than bi or li. Al-Ālūsī reports a variant in which 'departing' means turning away from the word, restricting the negation and answering Hūd's 11:52 injunction not to turn away. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 11:53/q11/p2 ʿAn marks cause and attaches to 'leaving': Hūd's word will not induce them to abandon their gods. — Al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī say ʿan and bāʾ can interchange; al-Wāḥidī cites 7:187 and 25:59, and Ibn al-Jawzī's Nuzha cites 53:3. Al-Ṭabrisī reports the same replacement. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn, makes it causal; 9:114 is cited. Al-Burhān and al-Itqān use Abraham's prayer after a promise as a parallel, with al-Burhān also comparing Lot's people's reported words. Al-Bursawī reports al-Saʿdī al-Muftī's بسبب analysis and 9:114. Al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr take causal ʿan; Ibn ʿĀshūr connects its basic departure sense to causal usage and cites 18:82. [12 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(ابن عطية); BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI(السعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 11:53/q11/p3 An omitted circumstantial expression of departing and acting on Hūd's word mediates the phrase. — Al-Ālūsī reports careful grammarians attaching the phrase to an omitted state rather than directly to 'leaving.' He explains that departure from and return to water became metaphors for managing affairs, cites a verse about sound judgment, and says Arabic may omit 'returning' because departure implies it. On this reading negation covers both actions: they neither leave their gods nor act on Hūd's word. Abū Zakariyyā, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, says ʿan fundamentally signifies passing beyond, illustrated by speech reaching a listener and turning from Zayd; he also says it is a preposition except after min, where it becomes a noun because a preposition cannot govern another. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو زكريا)]
- 11:53/q11/p4 The construction may use semantic inclusion rather than an omitted complement. — Al-Ālūsī reports this analysis from some interpreters and says al-Zamakhsharī's wording appears to support it, as al-Kashf explains. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 11:53/q12 Why does the second refusal use waw rather than causal fa?  [وَمَا نَحْنُ بِتَارِكِىٓ · rhetoric]
- 11:53/q12/p1 Waw coordinates the refusal without making the earlier no-proof claim its formal cause. — Biqāʿī notes that fa would make the first statement the cause of the second. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 11:53/q13 What does their refusal to believe Hūd cover?  [وَمَا نَحْنُ لَكَ بِمُؤْمِنِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the rejected object is his mission, truth generally, or all of his words and acts.
- 11:53/q13/p1 They will not affirm Hūd's prophethood, message, or call to worship God alone. — Muqātil and al-Ṭabarī specify his messengerhood. Al-Ṭabrisī says they do not affirm his message; al-Bursawī says the call includes monotheism and abandonment of idols. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they refuse both belief and obedience, the second part of his appeal. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī explain that they see someone of Hūd's standing as unfit to be believed by people like themselves. [15 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 11:53/q13/p2 Believing means accepting the truth. — Saʿīd ibn Jubayr gives this gloss, as reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 11:53/q13/p3 They disbelieve either Hūd's message specifically or everything he says and does. — Al-Ālūsī states the two scopes as alternatives; the broader includes the message. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:53/q14 What force does the emphatic refusal to believe have?  [وَمَا نَحْنُ لَكَ بِمُؤْمِنِينَ · rhetoric]
- 11:53/q14/p1 It declares settled defiance and extinguishes Hūd's hope of their believing. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Nasafī and al-Bursawī say they aim to end his hope. Al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī see entrenched imitation or unbelief. Biqāʿī points to emphatic negation and the nominal sentence; al-Ālūsī adds the repeated refusal, added bā, and fronted subject as markers of no remaining hope. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:53/q15 What pronunciation rule concerns mīm before bā?  [بِتَارِكِىٓ · readings]
turns on: The preceding letter's vowel or quiescence, the transmission route, and the correct name for the sound.
- 11:53/q15/p1 After a vowel the mīm is made quiescent and concealed with nasalization; after a quiescent letter it is generally clear. — Ibn al-Jazarī gives this distinction to avoid successive vowels and says the precise term for the concealment is ikhfāʾ. [2 notes: NASHR; NASHR+]
- 11:53/q15/p2 Concealment is also reported after a madd or līn letter. — Al-Qaṣbānī from Shujāʿ from Abū ʿAmr reports it, but Ibn al-Jazarī says this route is outside the book's transmissions. [1 notes: NASHR(القصباني عن شجاع عن أبي عمرو)]
- 11:53/q15/p3 Some earlier scholars call this concealment idghām. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports this terminology but rejects it in favor of ikhfāʾ and postpones fuller discussion. [0 notes: NASHR-]

## 11:53/q16 How is the nūn of naḥnu pronounced before lām?  [نَحْنُ لَكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the usual block from a quiescent preceding letter applies to the frequently used word naḥnu.
- 11:53/q16/p1 Nūn generally assimilates into lām or rāʾ after a vowel but remains clear after a quiescent letter. — Ibn al-Jazarī states the general rule. Al-Suyūṭī says naḥnu is an exception because of frequent occurrence, doubled nūn, continuous vocalization, and its heaviness. [2 notes: NASHR; ITQAN]
- 11:53/q16/p2 A transmitted practice assimilates the final nūn of naḥnu into lām in ten occurrences. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports this from companions of al-Yazīdī from Abū ʿAmr, specifying that other words after a quiescent preceding letter do not assimilate. Al-Suyūṭī gives the exceptional phonetic reasons for naḥnu. [2 notes: NASHR(أصحاب اليزيدي عن أبي عمرو); ITQAN]

## 11:53/q17 How does a command expression such as ruwayd govern its complement?  [لَكَ · grammar]
- 11:53/q17/p1 Bare ruwayd acts as a verb meaning 'go gently' and can govern an object; it can also modify or imply a verbal noun. — Sībawayh cites ruwayd Zaydan for the verbal function, and says ruwayd may modify an explicit verbal noun adverbially or stand with that noun omitted; an expressed noun can permit more than one analysis. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 11:53/q18 What grammatical role does an addressed kāf have in comparable commands?  [لَكَ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical environment: directly attached kāf in ruwaydaka versus kāf governed by lām in halumma laka.
- 11:53/q18/p1 In ruwaydaka, kāf specifies or emphasizes the addressee without being a subject or noun. — Sībawayh says the form itself does not mark person or number, so kāf singles out a listener. He rejects taking it as the subject or a noun: the implied command subject is a doer and the noun analysis produces impossible constructions. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH; SIBAWAYH-]
- 11:53/q18/p2 In halumma laka, kāf is a genitive noun after lām while still specifying or emphasizing the addressee. — Sībawayh distinguishes this construction from ruwaydaka. A visible noun cannot coordinate directly with this genitive kāf; coordination can instead target the understood nominative addressee, made explicit by anta. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 11:53/q19 How can a hidden addressee be emphasized or coordinated with an overt noun?  [لَكَ · grammar]
- 11:53/q19/p1 Making the nominative addressee explicit smooths emphasis and coordination. — Sībawayh gives ruwaydakum antum wa ʿAbd Allāh and mentions 5:24 and 2:35; in halumma laka, an overt noun may coordinate with the understood nominative addressee after anta rather than directly with genitive kāf. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 11:53/q20 How can bi-bayyina attach syntactically?  [بِبَيِّنَةٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the prepositional phrase depends directly on jiʾtanā or on an understood circumstantial expression.
- 11:53/q20/p1 It can attach to jiʾtanā, stating that no proof was shown to them. — Al-Samīn gives the direct attachment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 11:53/q20/p2 It can modify an omitted circumstantial state such as 'settled' or 'accompanied by proof.' — Al-Samīn supplies the omitted-state analysis as an alternative. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 11:53/q21 How did the opponents characterize Hūd's call?  [* · interpretation]
- 11:53/q21/p1 They portrayed his call to worship God alone as madness caused by their gods' anger. — Abū Ḥayyān says they attributed it to his insulting the gods and urging their worshippers to abandon them. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 11:53/q22 Who were ʿĀd, and what background and fate frame their reply?  [* · reports]
- 11:53/q22/p1 They were an ancient, powerful Arab people who turned to idolatry, rejected their kinsman Hūd, and were destroyed by wind while Hūd and believers were saved. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their history survives in unreliable tales and the extant Torah omits them; the Quran places them after Noah in al-Aḥqāf, perhaps as 'first ʿĀd' implying a second. They were tall and strong, with cities, fertile gardens, palms and crops (89:6–11). After divine bounty they worshipped idols, built monuments and grand structures, and obeyed tyrants. Hūd, their kinsman and the second prophet whose anti-idolatry struggle the Quran recounts, preached truth, justice, mercy and God's worship. Few believed; most called him foolish or mad and demanded punishment. A barren wind lasted seven nights and eight days, leaving them like hollow palm trunks. They mistook its approaching cloud for rain; God destroyed them and saved Hūd and the believers by mercy. [9 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:53/q23 How does Hūd's next answer address this objection?  [* · links]
- 11:53/q23/p1 His disavowal and challenge directly answer their demand for proof. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the answer does not leave their objection unanswered. Al-Burhān says Hūd's shift from calling God as witness to commanding the idolaters to witness affirms monotheism through God's testimony and shows disregard for their religion by addressing them this way. [2 notes: TABATABAI; BURHAN]

## 11:53/q24 What kinds of concise wording and interpretive breadth can occur in Qur'anic expression?  [* · rhetoric]
- 11:53/q24/p1 Concision may come through grammatical compression or through wording that supports several interpretations. — Al-Suyūṭī lists restriction, conjunction, passive voice, pronouns, verbs governing complement clauses, omitted coordinated terms or objects, interrogative and conditional particles, generic terms, and dual or plural forms. He reports Ibn Abī al-Iṣbaʿ's further category of interpretive breadth, exemplified by sūra openings. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن أبي الإصبع)]
