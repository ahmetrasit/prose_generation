# 39:23: 41 questions

## 39:23/q01 What does 'God sent down' establish about the Quran?  [ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ · interpretation]
turns on: The form of the verb and the theological implication of naming God.
- 39:23/q01/p1 It is revelation and divine speech delivered through Gabriel and the Prophet. — KURANYOLU says Gabriel and the Prophet transmit God's speech; Ibn Kathir calls it the great Book revealed to His Messenger. Bāqillānī, Bursevī, and Biqāʿī ground its rank in its divine source. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL; BAQILLANI; BURSEVI]
- 39:23/q01/p2 Nazzala stresses progressive, continuing revelation. — Asad bases his present tense on the verb form; Biqāʿī says stages train listeners and answer objections. Burhān catalogs both nazzala and anzala across many suras, including this one. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN]

## 39:23/q02 Why is God's name made the opening subject?  [ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ · rhetoric]
- 39:23/q02/p1 The fronted divine name magnifies the revelation and its unmatched divine source. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ālūsī make exaltation the point; Abū Ḥayyān compares topicalizing a king and cites 22:75; Ālūsī derives perfection of speech from the perfect Speaker and reports Zamakhsharī's restrictive reading. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ITQAN(الزمخشري)]

## 39:23/q03 What is the 'best discourse'?  [أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · referent]
turns on: Whether ḥadīth here denotes the revealed Book broadly or its narratives specifically.
- 39:23/q03/p1 The Quran. — Mujāhid, Muqātil, Ṭabarī and successors identify it; Qurṭubī links it to the preceding command to follow the best saying; Burhān lists it among Quranic names. [15 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:23/q03/p2 The best narratives or stories. — Ḍamghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī give the narrative sense; the companions' request for stories is relevant. [2 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]

## 39:23/q04 Why is this discourse called the best?  [أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · interpretation]
turns on: The grounds given for 'best': form, content, divine status, and effect.
- 39:23/q04/p1 Its expression is uniquely eloquent and inimitable. — Māwardī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bursevī, and others cite eloquence, composition, genre, pleasing style and strong diction. Bāqillānī contrasts poetry, sajʿ and ordinary prose, cites 4:82, and notes human critics' disagreement; Jurjānī's eight poets illustrate human literary craft and claims. Samarrāʾī stresses the artistic unity of varied diction. [27 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; BAQILLANI(أبو عمرو بن العلاء,الأصمعي); JURJANI-DALAIL(أبو تمام,أبو شريح العمير,ابن ميادة,الفرزدق,بشار,عدي بن الرقاع,عقال بن هاشم القيني,كعب بن زهير); SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الفخر الرازي); STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:23/q04/p2 Its truth, wisdom, comprehensiveness, and beneficial guidance make it best. — Māwardī calls it the most complete book; Ṭabrisī lists proof, law, exhortation, stories, promise and warning; Rāzī surveys divine essence and attributes, cosmology, nature, human origins, prophecy, law, ethics, angels, scriptures and resurrection with numerous cited verses, concluding no book equals its range. KURANYOLU names truth, justice and salvation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says falsehood cannot touch it. [31 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:23/q04/p3 It is God's uncreated or eternal speech. — Qushayrī explicitly grounds the superlative in uncreatedness; Bursevī contrasts God's eternal speech with originated human speech. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]
- 39:23/q04/p4 Its palpable power over hearts proves its excellence and inimitability. — Khaṭṭābī says sweetness and majesty converted would-be killers; Bāqillānī describes awe, joy and conviction; ʿIyāḍ and Burhān compare 59:21 and cite moved believers and even opponents. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Jubayr at 52:35–37 and ʿUtba at 41:13. Study Quran calls bodily reaction an attribute of beauty. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the Quran's production of both fear and desire in the same believers a wonder that fits their conduct to God's majesty, forbearance, and mercy. [16 notes: KHATTABI; BAQILLANI; BURHAN(الخطابي); ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(جبير بن مطعم,عياض,محمد بن كعب القرظي); STUDYQURAN(Aj); QURTUBI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 39:23/q05 How should ellipses be chosen when the Quran is the best discourse?  [أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · rhetoric]
- 39:23/q05/p1 Choose the shortest, most fitting and specific implied wording. — Suyūṭī minimizes ellipsis at 65:4; ʿIzz al-Dīn chooses the Kaaba's sanctity over construction at 2:125, which Suyūṭī prefers, and specific field compensation over general affair at 21:78. The ground is the Quran's excellence. [5 notes: ITQAN(عز الدين); ITQAN(عز الدين)+]

## 39:23/q06 Why is the Quran called ḥadīth?  [ٱلْحَدِيثِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the title reflects communication, relative lateness, or rhetorical matching.
- 39:23/q06/p1 It is speech or a communicated report. — Māwardī compares prophetic speech; Qurṭubī says the Prophet related it and cites 7:185, 53:59, 18:6, 4:87, 68:44; Raghib includes what one hears or receives in revelation or dream. Ibn al-Jawzī links speech and new information and gives other senses of the root. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:23/q06/p2 It is later than earlier revealed books. — Māwardī and Ṭabrisī give relative lateness of revelation. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 39:23/q06/p3 It is a matching expression (mushākala). — Ālūsī allows this alternative usage. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:23/q07 Does calling the Quran ḥadīth imply that God's speech is created?  [ٱلْحَدِيثِ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'new' describes the divine speech itself or its temporal recitation and verbal expression.
- 39:23/q07/p1 The verbal Quran of successive letters and sounds is originated. — Rāzī reports arguments from ḥadīth in 52:34 and 56:81, descent into a locus, the comparison 'best,' and kitāb as a gathered object; he accepts their force for letters, sounds and utterances. Ālūsī reports the Ashʿarite distinction for verbal speech. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(القائلون بحدوث القرآن); ALUSI-FULL(الأشاعرة)]
- 39:23/q07/p2 The term does not establish createdness of the eternal speech. — Qushayrī distinguishes newly occurring recitation from what is recited, comparing remembrance and divine names at 21:2; Bursevī and Ālūsī say ḥadīth means discourse spoken or told, without temporal origination of the divine speech. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القائلون بحدوث القرآن)-]

## 39:23/q08 What occasion is reported for this verse?  [ٱللَّهُ نَزَّلَ أَحْسَنَ ٱلْحَدِيثِ · reports]
- 39:23/q08/p1 Companions asked the Prophet for stories or a fresh narration, and this verse was revealed. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, Saʿd and others vary the request: a story, good accounts of past times, or relief from weariness. Related reports place 12:3 and 57:16 at subsequent requests; Ibn al-Jawzī says these requests directed listeners back to the Quran. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the report an application rather than a strict occasion. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عمرو بن قيس); THALABI(ابن مسعود وابن عباس); WAHIDI-ASBAB(سعد); JISHUMI(الصحابة); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سعد بن أبي وقاص,عون بن عبد الله); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود,سعد بن أبي وقاص); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(أصحاب رسول الله); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]

## 39:23/q09 How is kitāban related syntactically to 'best discourse'?  [كِتَٰبًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether kitāban renames the discourse, describes its state, or specifies it.
- 39:23/q09/p1 It is an appositive or substitute for 'best discourse'. — Zajjāj, Naḥḥās, Zamakhsharī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī and others adopt it; al-Samīn calls it clearest. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 39:23/q09/p2 It is a circumstantial accusative describing 'best discourse'. — Zamakhsharī allows it; Abū Ḥayyān questions definiteness by annexation, while al-Samīn and Ālūsī defend it, adducing qualification by mutashābih or the force of 'written'. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]
- 39:23/q09/p3 It is an accusative of specification distinguishing the Quran from other speech. — Jishumī states this analysis. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 39:23/q10 Why is the revelation described as a written Book?  [كِتَٰبًۭا · interpretation]
- 39:23/q10/p1 Its written form preserves a lasting guide for reading until Resurrection. — KURANYOLU explains the documentary form and enduring public benefit; Biqāʿī says the Book gathers all good. Rāzī points to his discussion of the Book at 2:2. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 39:23/q11 What does mutashābihan say the Quran resembles?  [مُّتَشَٰبِهًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether comparison is among Quranic passages or with earlier scriptures; internal resemblance includes confirmation and literary equality.
- 39:23/q11/p1 Its own parts resemble, confirm, and explain one another without contradiction. — Ṭabarī cites Qatāda on verses and letters, Saʿīd on mutual confirmation, and 2:25; most later commentators cite uniformly true, wise, clear, beautiful and inimitable wording and meaning. Rāzī notes long human works vary while repeated Moses accounts remain equally eloquent. Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 4:82; Islahi notes unity of teaching despite different styles and themes. Samarrāʾī cites studies of imagery, sound, diction, coherence, and Rāzī's image of the Quran as a single sura or verse. [64 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,يحيى بن سلام); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير عن ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); BURHAN(الحسين بن محمد بن حبيب النيسابوري,بعضهم); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); ITQAN(ابن حبيب النيسابوري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ,IK, Ṭ,Q,R); SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الفخر الرازي)]
- 39:23/q11/p2 It resembles earlier revealed scriptures while surpassing them. — Māwardī, Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī name shared commands, prohibitions, promises and warnings, with the Quran more comprehensive and inimitable; Burhān gives a second exposition of earlier accounts. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 39:23/q11/p3 It is internally alike in eloquence and literary quality. — Qushayrī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī and Study Quran emphasize uniformly excellent composition and diction. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(قيل); RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ,Q,R)]

## 39:23/q12 How does mutashābih here relate to other uses of the term?  [مُّتَشَٰبِهًۭا · links]
- 39:23/q12/p1 Here it describes the whole Quran as mutually consistent, distinct from ambiguous verses in 3:7. — Ibn Kathīr, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran distinguish the uses. Burhān contrasts obscurity with mutual confirmation and discusses knowability and scholarly inquiry. Itqān reports whole-muḥkam, whole-mutashābih and partitioned views, preferring the partition in 3:7 while defining this whole-book quality as truth and inimitability. Biqāʿī says this wording avoids a form that might suggest obscurity. [13 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض العلماء عن سفيان بن عيينة); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(IK); BURHAN(ابن عباس,بعضهم,عامة المتكلمين); ITQAN(ابن حبيب النيسابوري); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN+]

## 39:23/q13 What does mathānī mean here?  [مَّثَانِىَ · meaning]
turns on: The root's reference may be thematic recurrence, pairs, repeated recitation, mutual explanation, praise, or a named portion of the Quran.
- 39:23/q13/p1 Its accounts, judgments, commands, warnings and other teachings recur in varied forms. — Muqātil lists past peoples, prophets, Adam and Iblīs, Paradise and Hell, resurrection, plants, rain and Pharaoh; Ṭabarī cites repeated law and accounts; Zamakhsharī adds commands and prohibitions; Ibn Kathīr reports Ḍaḥḥāk's pedagogic purpose. Asad prefers repeated statements of truth in manifold forms. Islahi rejects this derivation on linguistic and parallel-construction grounds. An unattributed gloss spans repeated discourse from God's praise to His servant. [52 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قيل); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس,الحسن,السدي,سعيد بن جبير,قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI(المفسرون); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن وعكرمة,سفيان,عبد الله بن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم,عكرمة، والحسن,مجاهد); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الحسن,عكرمة,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari)+; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(People who think that the word means “something which is repeated often”)-]
- 39:23/q13/p2 It pairs contrasting teachings or subjects. — Rāzī lists command/prohibition, general/particular, concise/expanded, heaven/earth, Paradise/Hell, light/dark; he infers that all created things have opposites whereas God alone is unique. Sufyān b. ʿUyayna contrasts this with recurring single subjects and 3:7. Biqāʿī says each explicit side implies its opposite; Study Quran relates the fear and hope of this very verse. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض العلماء عن سفيان بن عيينة); BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK); ELMALILI]
- 39:23/q13/p3 The Quran is recited repeatedly without becoming wearisome. — Māwardī, Zamakhsharī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī and Bursevī cite enduring pleasure; Bursevī adduces the Burda and a report about ʿUthmān's worn codices. Al-Khujandī contrasts receptive hearts hearing the Quran continually with false people who tire of it. [16 notes: THALABI(المفسرون); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الخجندي,القصيدة البردية,عثمان); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:23/q13/p4 Its parts turn back to and interpret one another. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr speak of reciprocal explanation; Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives the plural from a thing turned back, citing 4:82. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABATABAI; BURHAN]
- 39:23/q13/p5 Its suras occur in complementary pairs. — Islahi values this reading for teaching, pointing to 15:87 and the differing but connected themes of suras; he rejects mere repetition. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(People who think that the word means “something which is repeated often”); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 39:23/q13/p6 It derives from praise, since the Quran praises God or is itself praised. — Bursevī appeals to 56:77 and 85:21 and the admired eloquence; Ālūsī reports the derivation but finds it less fitting beside mutashābih unless it praises its Speaker. Al-Kashf permits a verbal noun functioning adjectivally. [5 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,قيل)]
- 39:23/q13/p7 It denotes Quranic verse endings or subdivisions. — Māwardī reports Ibn Baḥr: the Quran is whole, sura section, verse division, and mathānī verse endings. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن بحر)]
- 39:23/q13/p8 It means the whole Quran as a category. — Ṭabarī and al-Durr report this designation, as does Ibn Kathīr of Mujāhid; Ibn Qutayba relates it to recurring accounts. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(جماعة يكثر تعدادهم); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BURHAN]
- 39:23/q13/p9 It denotes al-Fātiḥa, the seven oft-repeated verses. — Ṭabarī cites repetition in prayer and rajaz poetry; Māwardī cites al-Ḥasan and a poem; Burhān cites 15:87 and Fātiḥa's many names. [12 notes: MAJAZ(سليمان); TAB-FULL(أبو النجم العجلي,الراجز الآخر,جماعة أخرى); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن البصري); BURHAN]
- 39:23/q13/p10 It is a class of suras following the miʾūn. — Ibn Qutayba and Ṭabarī explain that the hundred-verse group comes first and mathānī second; poetry lists the categories. Māwardī's report gives suras of roughly two hundred verses as another delimitation. [12 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قائل الأبيات); MAWARDI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 39:23/q13/p11 It means the succession of verses and suras. — Māwardī reports al-Kalbī's sequential account of mathānī. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي)]
- 39:23/q13/p12 Its repeated descent marks stages before and after inward annihilation. — Qāshānī reads the true Book as descending to the heart while it alternates between the Real and creation. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 39:23/q14 Why does the Quran repeat material?  [مَّثَانِىَ · interpretation]
- 39:23/q14/p1 Repetition makes admonition take root and reaches audiences who receive different passages. — Zamakhsharī and Biqāʿī say resistant souls need repeated counsel; the Prophet repeated admonition three or seven times. Ibn al-Jawzī says scattered Arab delegations heard the stories in different suras; KURANYOLU says repeated forms enlighten readers. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:23/q14/p2 Repetition renews delight and benefit without exhausting the Quran. — Māwardī and Bursevī say recurrent recitation stays pleasing; Bursevī quotes Burda, a report of ʿUthmān's worn codices, and inexhaustible wonders. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى); BURSEVI(القصيدة البردية,عثمان); BIQAI-FULL]

## 39:23/q15 What is the grammatical role of mathānī?  [مَّثَانِىَ · grammar]
turns on: How a plural form qualifies a singular Book and whether it is adjective, specification, or circumstantial.
- 39:23/q15/p1 It qualifies kitāban despite its plural form, because the Book contains many parts. — Zajjāj and Naḥḥās call it an adjective and a diptote irregular plural; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes lack of tanwīn. Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, Bursevī and Ālūsī compare a human made of bones and veins, or supply 'sections'. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,قيل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:23/q15/p2 It is an accusative specification of mutashābihan: its parts or qualities resemble one another. — Zamakhsharī compares 'a man beautiful in qualities'; Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī and Ālūsī report the transformed underlying clause. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 39:23/q15/p3 It is a descriptive plural like collective expressions for a pot or cloth. — Zamakhsharī compares برمة أعشار and ثوب أخلاق; Abū Ḥayyān reports this. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)]
- 39:23/q15/p4 It can be a circumstantial qualifier of the Book. — Ālūsī and al-Samīn permit the analysis. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]

## 39:23/q16 How is mathānī read?  [مَّثَانِىَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the yāʾ bears fatḥa or sukūn, and how the silent-yāʾ form is parsed.
- 39:23/q16/p1 The majority pronounce the final yāʾ with fatḥa. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn report the common reading; al-Samīn allows adjectival, circumstantial, or specification syntax. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 39:23/q16/p2 Hishām from Ibn ʿĀmir and Abū Bishr pronounce a still yāʾ. — Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn and Ālūsī attribute the reading; heaviness may prompt sukūn, with either an accusative or omitted-subject predicate analysis. Al-Samīn adduces a comparable reading and poem, but objects that a nominative predicate should lose yāʾ and take tanwīn. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(هشام، وابن عامر، وأبو بشر); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ,هشام عن ابن عامر وأبو بشر); ALUSI-FULL(هشام وأبو بشر)]

## 39:23/q17 What is the form and root of mathānī?  [مَّثَانِىَ · grammar]
turns on: The proposed derivation from doubling or return versus praise.
- 39:23/q17/p1 It is a plural connected with mathnā or muthannā, from doubling or turning back. — Jishumī derives repetition by adding a like; Zamakhsharī compares karratayn at 67:4 and paired forms; Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Suyūṭī, Bursevī and KURANYOLU give related forms; Ṭabāṭabāʾī gives mathniya, a turned-back thing. [9 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:23/q17/p2 It may derive from praise. — Bursevī relates it to muthnā ʿalayh; Ālūsī and al-Kashf examine the adjectival form. [5 notes: BURSEVI(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(الكشف,قيل)]

## 39:23/q18 What are the linked traditional Quranic divisions called mathānī?  [مَّثَانِىَ · sciences]
turns on: Different units and starting points are reported for the traditional sura divisions.
- 39:23/q18/p1 They are a sura group after the seven long suras and miʾūn, before the mufassal. — Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabarī and Burhān define miʾūn as near one hundred verses and mathānī as succeeding them. A poem lists ṭawāsīn, ḥawāmīm and mufassal; basmala divisions explain mufassal. Wāthila's report compares the four groups with Torah, Gospel, Psalms and a special gift; Burhān calls it gharīb with weak Saʿīd b. Bashīr. [27 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(العجاج,قائل الأبيات); MAWARDI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURHAN(أبو حيان التوحيدي)]
- 39:23/q18/p2 The seven long suras end at Yūnus. — Saʿīd b. Jubayr and Ibn ʿAbbās list Baqara through Aʿrāf then Yūnus; Māwardī calls it correct; Ṭabarī says ʿUthmān's Anfāl/Barāʾa report does not establish those two in the seven. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BURHAN(سعيد بن جبير); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)+]
- 39:23/q18/p3 The seven long suras treat al-Anfāl and Barāʾa as one ending unit. — A poem and Burhān give this count; ʿUthmān said similar stories and absence of clarification led him to pair them without basmala. Al-Ālūsī discusses whether this reflects independent judgment or a prescribed order. [6 notes: MAJAZ(سليمان); TAB-FULL(عثمان بن عفان); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:23/q18/p4 The mufassal begins at Sūrat Muḥammad. — Māwardī attributes it to most scholars; Burhān lists it among twelve reported starting points. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الأكثرون); BURHAN]
- 39:23/q18/p5 The mufassal begins at Qāf. — ʿĪsā b. ʿUmar reports this from many Companions; a report from the Thaqīf delegation says a Companion hizb began there; Burhān also lists it. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); BURHAN(الخطابي)]
- 39:23/q18/p6 The mufassal begins at al-Ḍuḥā. — Ibn ʿAbbās connects this beginning with Meccan takbīr between suras. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 39:23/q18/p7 Other reported mufassal starts include al-Jāthiya and al-Ḥujurāt. — Burhān counts twelve views and lists these alongside Muḥammad and Qāf. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 39:23/q18/p8 Al-Fātiḥa bears the name seven mathānī. — Poetic evidence and its repeated prayer recitation support this title; Burhān lists it among more than twenty names. [9 notes: MAJAZ(سليمان); TAB-FULL(أبو النجم العجلي,الراجز الآخر,جماعة أخرى); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن البصري); BURHAN]

## 39:23/q19 How does taqshaʿir describe the skin?  [تَقْشَعِرُّ جُلُودُ · meaning]
- 39:23/q19/p1 It means intense contraction, trembling, and raised hair from fear. — Baghawī, Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, Qurṭubī, Bursevī and others give bodily definitions; Zamakhsharī derives qashʿ from dry hide with extra rāʾ intensifying the form, followed by Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī. Biqāʿī instead mentions dry, aged fat; Imruʾ al-Qays's poem uses the form for a fearful heart. Qurṭubī lists verbal noun and participial forms. ## 39:23/q20 What do the 'skins' denote?  [جُلُودُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the noun is literal bodily skin or a transferred name for the inner organ.
- 39:23/q20/p1 The body's actual outer covering and sometimes its hair. — Biqāʿī names the body's outer surfaces; Islahi notes Arabic can use part for whole and whole for part, including hair. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:23/q20/p2 The hearts of the God-fearing. — Baghawī reports this alternative transferred sense. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]

## 39:23/q21 How is the clause 'their skins shiver' attached syntactically?  [تَقْشَعِرُّ مِنْهُ جُلُودُ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the listeners' response remains a modifier of the Book or begins a new statement.
- 39:23/q21/p1 It qualifies the Book as an adjectival clause. — Naḥḥās, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and al-Samīn permit this; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says no pause at mathānī on this parse. [5 notes: NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 39:23/q21/p2 It is a circumstantial clause for the Book. — Al-Samīn and Ālūsī report this analysis. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 39:23/q21/p3 It begins an independent sentence about the Book's effects. — Ālūsī prefers the transition after the Book's attributes; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says one may pause at mathānī on this parse. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 39:23/q22 Is skin shivering literal or a figure for fear?  [تَقْشَعِرُّ مِنْهُ جُلُودُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the bodily sign is an observed reaction or figurative depiction; several sources allow both.
- 39:23/q22/p1 It is an actual bodily response to revelation and warning. — Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ālūsī prefer a palpable effect; Ṭabarī and others describe skin reacting to heard recitation. The Companions' weeping and shivering corroborate its actuality. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; TAB-FULL; THALABI(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); QURTUBI-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر الصديق); IBNASHUR-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL+; BURSEVI+]
- 39:23/q22/p2 It is a vivid portrayal of overwhelming fear. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī describe fear through its visible sign; Ālūsī prefers figurative exemplification to a state-to-state parable but still allows actual bodily change. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:23/q23 What brings the first shiver and subsequent softening?  [تَقْشَعِرُّ ثُمَّ تَلِينُ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources vary between a warning/mercy sequence and deeper stages of contemplation.
- 39:23/q23/p1 Warnings and punishment cause fear; mercy, promise and reward bring hope and softness. — Muqātil, Farrāʾ, Ibn Qutayba, Ṭabarī and most commentators make this temporal contrast. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes awe may attract believers but burden deniers; Burhān reconciles trembling with 13:28 and 8:2 as fear of deviation within settled conviction. Ibn Jurayj explicitly pairs warning with Paradise and hoped-for mercy. Biqāʿī notes bodily outer surfaces, intense reverent fear, and remembrance of divine majesty. Qurṭubī compares the response with the mountain's splitting and humility at 59:21. Ibn ʿĀshūr says warnings move believers to avoid prohibitions, while promises make them rejoice, recognize promised good in their deeds, and expect it hopefully; the paired feelings fit their conduct to God's majesty, forbearance, and mercy. [54 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عيسى,السدي); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي,الماوردي); RAZI-FULL(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); BURHAN; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 39:23/q23/p2 Awe arises from contemplating divine majesty, then serenity from beauty and oneness. — Rāzī says initial reward-and-punishment response is only the first level: divine transcendence beyond place and pre-eternity make the mind shiver, while reasoning about unity and necessary existence brings calm; Qushayrī and Bursevī allow fear/hope, contraction/expansion, disclosure/concealment; al-Nahrjurī contrasts beginners and advanced knowers. Māwardī also describes awe followed by softness on recitation. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Fakhr quoting perfected seekers: divine majesty stuns them, then a trace of beauty revives them. [15 notes: QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(النهرجوري); ALUSI-FULL; QASHANI; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(الفخر)]

## 39:23/q24 Whose skins shiver in this verse?  [ٱلَّذِينَ يَخْشَوْنَ رَبَّهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase describes all God-fearing hearers, the Companions as exemplars, or a spiritual rank.
- 39:23/q24/p1 God-fearing believers, God's friends, and receptive hearers. — Ṭabarī, Qatāda and Ibn Kathīr describe friends who weep, shiver and settle. Ibn Kathīr contrasts attentive believers with listeners of song and cites 8:2–4 and 25:73; Ibn ʿĀshūr says believers are singled out because they subsequently soften, though deniers too may feel awe and turn away. [17 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); RAZI-FULL(قتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL(عياض); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:23/q24/p2 Specifically the Prophet's Companions in the cited reports. — Asma describes their eyes watering and skin shivering during recitation; Study Quran reports some commentators' identification. [7 notes: THALABI(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); QURTUBI-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر الصديق); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن عروة بن الزبير); IBNASHUR-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 39:23/q24/p3 In an inward reading, God-knowing scholars and seekers affected by luminous disclosure. — Qāshānī says light descending on hearts reaches their bodies; Nahrjurī distinguishes seekers' outward state from knowers' calm. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(النهرجوري)]

## 39:23/q25 How should hearers behave when moved by recitation?  [تَقْشَعِرُّ ثُمَّ تَلِينُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether shivering and composure are the normative limits of sincere response, or swooning can be involuntary.
- 39:23/q25/p1 Weep, shiver, become calm, listen attentively and act; avoid contrived loss of control. — Qatāda says God's allies do not lose reason; Asma and Ibn ʿUmar contrast the Companions with collapsing hearers and blame Satan. Ibn Sīrīn proposes the rooftop or wall test; an anecdote of Moses rejects torn clothes without an opened heart. Ibn Kathīr values stillness and deliberate prostration; al-Zubayr's family and Ibrahim likewise challenge fainting and visionary claims. Rāzī reports Ghazālī's question why some swoon at love poems more than Quran; Rāzī contrasts Quranic majesty with poems of creaturely union and separation. [50 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); THALABI(أبو عمران الجوني,أسماء بنت أبي بكر,ابن سيرين,ابن عمر); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر,ابن سيرين,ابن عمر); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أسماء بنت أبي بكر,إبراهيم النخعي,ابن عمر,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(أبو حامد الغزالي,قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر الصديق,ابن سيرين,ابن عمر,الله); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر,ابن سيرين,ابن عمر); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عمر); DURR-FULL(إبراهيم,عامر بن عبد الله بن الزبير,عبد الله بن عروة بن الزبير,قتادة,قيس بن جبير); ALUSI-FULL(ابن جبير,ابن سيرين,ابن عمر,عبد الله بن الزبير,عبد الله بن عروة بن الزبير,قتادة)]
- 39:23/q25/p2 Involuntary swooning may reflect weak capacity, and the verse need not exclude other states. — Ālūsī reports Sufi shaykhs' defense: weak hearts cannot bear influx, and the relative clause may concern firm saints rather than all believers. He calls swooning a deficiency in contrast to completed wayfarers, yet says involuntary reactions are not blameworthy. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مشايخهم); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة)-; THALABI(أسماء بنت أبي بكر,ابن عمر)-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 39:23/q26 What benefit is reported for a shiver or tenderness from fear of God?  [تَقْشَعِرُّ تَلِينُ · reports]
turns on: The reports promise remission, protection from Fire, or a propitious time for supplication.
- 39:23/q26/p1 Fear-induced shivering sheds sins like leaves from a dry tree. — Reports through al-ʿAbbās and others repeat the image; Ibn al-Jawzī, Biqāʿī and Māwardī state no grading here. - 39:23/q26/p3 Tenderness or gooseflesh is an occasion to pray and expect a response. — The report of Ubayy's recitation before the Prophet says to seize tenderness for prayer; Umm al-Dardāʾ and an unnamed worshipper describe shivering, fear and tears as signs of answered supplication. ## 39:23/q27 What does talīnu mean here?  [تَلِينُ جُلُودُهُمْ وَقُلُوبُهُمْ · meaning]
- 39:23/q27/p1 Their bodies and hearts grow tender, calm, hopeful and ready to obey. — Ṭabarī and Thaʿlabī include acting on and affirming the Book; Qurṭubī adds turning to Islam; Qāshānī makes limbs compliant; Islahi excludes bodily arrogance and notes humble gait; Bursevī distinguishes literal soft skin and figurative inner character. ## 39:23/q28 Why does talīnu take the preposition ilā?  [تَلِينُ إِلَىٰ · grammar]

- 39:23/q28/p1 It incorporates settling, tranquility or inclining toward remembrance. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Islahi explain the verb's extended sense. ## 39:23/q29 What is the remembrance toward which they soften?  [ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · referent]

- 39:23/q29/p1 Remembrance of God Himself. — Study Quran's rendering cites 13:28; Rāzī says the highest lover seeks God for His own sake and reads the phrase as approaching His unsensed presence. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN; RAZI-FULL]
- 39:23/q29/p2 His mercy, Paradise, reward and promise, understood by ellipsis. — Muqātil and Ibn al-Jawzī give Paradise and reward; Ṭabrisī says the object is omitted. - 39:23/q29/p4 God's remembrance of the person. — Study Quran offers this possibility with 29:45. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 39:23/q30 Why are hearts named with softening but only skins with shivering?  [جُلُودُ جُلُودُهُمْ وَقُلُوبُهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 39:23/q30/p1 Fear in the heart is already implicit, while its outward mark is on skin; both soften in hope. — Zamakhsharī, Nasafī and Bursevī say fear's heart location is understood; Bayḍāwī and Abū Ḥayyān link inward fear and hope. Biqāʿī avoids implying hardened hearts; Ālūsī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī note hearts or souls do not physically shiver; Rāzī says hopeful disclosure fills heart and spirit more fully. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:23/q31 Why does the verse say God's remembrance instead of naming mercy?  [ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Mercy evoked by the name versus devotion to God Himself.
- 39:23/q31/p1 The divine name evokes mercy before wrath and dignifies the passage. — Zamakhsharī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Bursevī and Ālūsī say mercy is primary or first recalled; Ālūsī cites 'My mercy preceded My anger.' Biqāʿī says explicit God avoids a misleading pronoun that could favor unionism and elevates the designation over discourse or book. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:23/q31/p2 The highest love is directed to God, not a distinct benefit. — Rāzī cites 6:125, 13:28, 2:40 and 2:152 for God-centered remembrance. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 39:23/q32 What does dhālika refer to?  [ذَٰلِكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the demonstrative resumes the Book or the bodily and inward effects it brings.
- 39:23/q32/p1 The Quran, the Book just described. — Ṭabarī, Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Zamakhsharī, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Nasafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Islahi and Study Quran take the pointer to the Book. - 39:23/q32/p2 The fear, shivering, hope and softening God grants the hearer. — Ṭabarī, Zajjāj, Naḥḥās, Zamakhsharī, Ibn al-Anbārī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and others read the demonstrative as the described effect; Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the Book reading, while Ālūsī dismisses this one. ## 39:23/q33 How does God guide by it, and whom does He guide?  [هُدَى ٱللَّهِ يَهْدِى بِهِۦ مَن يَشَآءُ · theology]
turns on: The agency of God's will, the hearer's readiness, and whether guidance denotes the Book or its effects.
- 39:23/q33/p1 God guides receptive servants through the Quran and its proofs. — Muqātil and Ṭabarī say the Quran guides to religion and belief; Jubbāʾī specifies recipients of its proofs; Ālūsī says reflection on divine origin enables guidance. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the revelation supplies the means fully and God prepares receptivity; Islahi says worthiness follows God's established practice. Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects receptivity with freedom from obstruction by sin. Asad accepts the syntactically possible rendering 'He guides therewith whomever He wills.' Bāqillānī says only divine guidance enables recognition of Quranic excellence and cites 2:26 for guidance and misguidance through it. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي,قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES; BAQILLANI]
- 39:23/q33/p2 God grants fear and hope themselves as guidance and enabling. — Ṭabarī and Zajjāj call the listener's shivering and softening divine enabling; Zamakhsharī calls it God's luṭf, which may even draw observers to imitate the believers. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 39:23/q33/p3 The person who wills guidance is guided. — Ālūsī considers referring the pronoun in 'wills' to the person, but calls it weak. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:23/q33/p4 Guidance can come directly from God or through guides He has guided. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 2:120, 92:12, 21:73 and 42:52 and denies that this verse proves guidance belongs to God's act exclusively. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 39:23/q34 What does God's leading someone astray mean here?  [وَمَن يُضْلِلِ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether misguidance is created in the person, grace withheld, loss of Paradise, or a result of rejecting the Quran.
- 39:23/q34/p1 God creates or decrees misguidance in one who turns away, leaving no rescuer. — Nasafī, Bursevī and Ālūsī explicitly speak of created misguidance; Biqāʿī says none can overturn divine judgment; Ṭabarī relates it to failure to affirm the Quran. Qurṭubī and Thaʿlabī say the clause refutes the Qadariyya; Rāzī refers to the debate at 6:125. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 39:23/q34/p2 God withholds luṭf or leaves a transgressor to his own hard heart. — Zamakhsharī gives withholding grace from wrongdoers and alternatively grace's ineffectiveness on hardened hearts; al-Najmiyya says leaving a person to his intellect and depriving him of faith in prophets makes philosophical proof unable to save him. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 39:23/q34/p3 It means turning someone away from Paradise and divine mercy. — Jubbāʾī explains loss of the path to Paradise; Abū Muslim says straying from God and mercy; another view makes it deprivation of increase in grace. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو مسلم,الجبائي,قيل)]
- 39:23/q34/p4 It describes the different human responses to a revelation that guides and heals. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Quran supplies guidance, yet some turn away; Biqāʿī says hardened hearts remain unmoved by the same revelation, which then misleads. Bursevī reports the image of believer faces lit and hypocrite faces burned by the Quran. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أحد الصحابة); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:23/q35 Why can no one guide the person God misleads?  [فَمَا لَهُۥ مِنْ هَادٍ · interpretation]
- 39:23/q35/p1 No guide can reverse God's judgment or overcome the hardened person's refusal. — Ṭabarī says no one can enable acceptance of the Quran; Biqāʿī appeals to sovereign decree; Islahi to unworthiness; Study Quran cites parallels at 4:88, 7:186, 13:33, 39:36 and elsewhere, interpreting the unsoftened heart as unguided. Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes God and His authorized guides; Bursevī's al-Najmiyya says unaided philosophical proof is insufficient. Al-Jāmī counsels seekers of realization not to travel without a guide. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,المولى الجامي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES]

## 39:23/q36 How is hād pronounced in pause or connection?  [هَادٍ · readings]
turns on: Retention or omission of the final yāʾ in pausal performance.
- 39:23/q36/p1 Some readers preserve the final yāʾ in pause, and Yaʿqūb-style performance preserves a hādī-like glide. — Qurṭubī names Ibn Kathīr and Ibn Muḥayṣin at this and 39:36; QIRAAT-ER describes the Yaʿqūb-style continuation and pause texture. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير وابن محيصن); QIRAAT-ER(Yaʿqūb)]
- 39:23/q36/p2 Other readers omit the final yāʾ at pause. — Qurṭubī contrasts the remaining readers with Ibn Kathīr and Ibn Muḥayṣin. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير وابن محيصن)]

## 39:23/q37 How does 39:23 connect to its surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The links made are to the earlier 'best' command, the rain parable, parallel response verses, and the passage's close.
- 39:23/q37/p1 It identifies the 'best' saying to be followed in 39:17–18 as the Quran. — Abū Muslim, Qurṭubī and Ṭabrisī connect the earlier command to this naming of the Quran. Jishumī makes following truth over falsehood and calling others to truth consequences of hearing the best. [5 notes: JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو مسلم)]
- 39:23/q37/p2 It resumes the sura's Quranic guidance theme and draws the lesson of the rain parable. — Ibn ʿĀshūr connects 39:21–23 to the guidance theme begun at 39:2, says growth parallels the receptive listener, and sees hardened hearts as their own rust, comparable with 2:6–7. Biqāʿī says this verse answers the preceding puzzle that remembrance can harden some hearts. Bursevī warns that complacency, even in permissible pleasure, hardens hearts while hardship recalls God. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts believers' softening here with the hardness condemned in 39:22. [10 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURSEVI(الحسين النوري,بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 39:23/q37/p3 It reconciles trembling with hearts resting in remembrance elsewhere. — Burhān and Itqān distinguish settled certainty from fear of deviation, citing 13:28 and 8:2; Baghawī cites 13:28 for the ensuing tranquility. [6 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; BAGHAWI-FULL]
- 39:23/q37/p4 The final clause concludes the explanation of Quranic guidance. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a conclusion; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the statement recurs elsewhere; Biqāʿī notes ellipsis by correspondence between guidance and misguidance. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:23/q37/p5 It distinguishes awe at God's mention in 8:2 from the two reactions to Quran recitation described here. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 8:2 with this verse and says the former concerns mention of God, while the latter concerns Quran recitation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 39:23/q38 What aspects of the Quran's status are illustrated by this verse?  [* · interpretation]
- 39:23/q38/p1 Its rank makes recitation, preservation, and devotion especially valuable. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya collects reports: the Quran as supreme gift, greatest intercessor, best worship, nobility of bearers, rewards for hundred/two hundred/three hundred verses, double reward for difficult recitation, and peril of neglect. ʿUmar comments on 35:32. Bāqillānī notes preservation and recitation in prayer, opened by seeking refuge at 16:98; a sermon urges ongoing attention to God's speech and remembrance. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاصي,عليه السلام,عمر بن الخطاب); BAQILLANI(خطيب غير مسمى)]
- 39:23/q38/p2 The Quran is an integrated discourse whose whole and details reinforce each other. — Samarrāʾī quotes Rāzī comparing it to one sura or even one verse; his study covers imagery, rhythm, diction and openings/endings. Burhān lists 'best discourse' among Quranic titles. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الفخر الرازي); BURHAN]

## 39:23/q39 What related Quranic sciences claims arise from calling it a Book?  [كِتَٰبًۭا · sciences]
turns on: The passage's side discussions concern fixed order, the Anfāl/Barāʾa exception, and names of suras.
- 39:23/q39/p1 The canonical order of suras is prescribed, matching the Preserved Tablet. — Ālūsī prefers this on prophetic indication and Companion consensus; al-Anbārī says Gabriel placed every verse and sura, and al-Kirmānī and Ṭībī cite annual reviews. Ālūsī treats isolated contrary reports as inadequate and explains ʿUthmān's action as compatible with a textual sign. [9 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو بكر الأنباري,الجمهور,الطيبي,الكرماني); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 39:23/q39/p2 Al-Anfāl and Barāʾa may be an exception settled by ʿUthmān's judgment. — Ālūsī records Ibn ʿAbbās's question to ʿUthmān and al-Bayhaqī's qualified position, which al-Suyūṭī welcomed; reports about the last Barāʾa verses figure in the compilation discussion. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبي,ابن عباس,الإمام السيوطي,البيهقي,عبدالله بن الزبير عن أبيه)]
- 39:23/q39/p3 Sura names are likely received, not freely inferred from themes. — Burhān says a clever reader could otherwise multiply names endlessly; it surveys names of Barāʾa, Fātiḥa and others. [5 notes: BURHAN(ابن عباس,حذيفة); BURHAN+]

## 39:23/q40 Is 39:23 reported as a Medinan exception?  [* · sciences]
- 39:23/q40/p1 Some add this verse to reported Medinan exceptions. — Itqān cites Ibn al-Jazarī; Study Quran says other commentators regard verses 10 and 23 as Medinan. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن الجزري); STUDYQURAN(Other commentators)]

## 39:23/q41 Why are both fear and hopeful softening foregrounded here?  [تَقْشَعِرُّ ثُمَّ تَلِينُ · rhetoric]
- 39:23/q41/p1 The two responses display the Quran's wondrous effect, shaping believers' conduct toward God's majesty, forbearance, and mercy. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the combination of fear and desire in the same believers a wonder of the Quran. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 39:23/q41/p2 The paired responses suit the Book's description as repeated discourse. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two responses are foregrounded because the verse calls the Quran mathānī, 'repeated'. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
