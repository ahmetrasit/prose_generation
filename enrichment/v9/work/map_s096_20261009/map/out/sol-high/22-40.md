# 22:40: 52 questions

## 22:40/q01 Who were the people expelled from their homes?  [ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the verse describes the emigrants generally, Muhammad with his companions, or extends to his family.
- 22:40/q01/p1 Believers expelled by Quraysh from Mecca, especially the Emigrants. — Al-Tabari and Baydawi name Mecca and Quraysh; Razi uses 22:41 to distinguish the emigrants from the Helpers. Al-Tabataba'i says the whole community is described by some members' ordeal. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:40/q01/p2 Muhammad and his companions specifically. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr relates the alarm at the Prophet's departure; Ibn Abbas names the Prophet and companions and their move to Medina. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(العوفي عن ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس)]
- 22:40/q01/p3 The emigrants, with the Prophet's family also included by application. — Abu Ja'far says the family of Muhammad too were expelled and intimidated. [1 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر)]

## 22:40/q02 How were the believers driven out, and where did they go?  [أُخْرِجُوا۟ مِن دِيَٰرِهِم · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in destination and in whether expulsion means direct removal or persecution that forced departure.
- 22:40/q02/p1 Quraysh tortured, threatened, and harassed believers until they left Mecca for Medina. — Muqatil and Tabari describe torture and threats; Ibn al-Arabi says some were tempted from faith. Tabataba'i says compelled departure, not physical deportation. [6 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:40/q02/p2 Some fled to Abyssinia, others to Medina, and some endured in Mecca. — Tabrisi treats Medina as implying a Medinan revelation and Abyssinia as implying a Meccan one; Biqa'i also names the mountain valley. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); TABRISI; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:40/q02/p3 Specific Meccan emigrants were prevented from migrating to Medina. — Mujahid says fighting was permitted against the unbelievers who blocked their migration. [1 notes: THALABI(مجاهد)]

## 22:40/q03 How does 'those expelled' attach grammatically to the previous verse?  [ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The relative may continue an earlier relative in the genitive or stand in the nominative or accusative by ellipsis.
- 22:40/q03/p1 It is genitive, qualifying or replacing the preceding relative expression. — Tabari says the second relative resumes those permitted to fight; Ibn Ashur ties it to 22:39. Kashshaf calls the genitive most apparent. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL+]
- 22:40/q03/p2 It is accusative with an implied 'I mean' or as praise. — Tabrisi and Samin give an omitted verb of specification or praise. [5 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:40/q03/p3 It is nominative with an implied subject such as 'they'. — The construction begins a nominal clause with an understood subject. [5 notes: TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:40/q04 How is the expulsion attributed to Quraysh if believers left under pressure?  [أُخْرِجُوا۟ · rhetoric]
- 22:40/q04/p1 A forced departure is attributed to the coercer, assigning blame to Quraysh. — Qurtubi compares 9:40, 'when the unbelievers expelled him'; Tabataba'i details persecution forcing departure. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 22:40/q05 What spiritual failings can betray the way of those defended by God?  [ٱلَّذِينَ أُخْرِجُوا۟ · inward]
turns on: Qushayri distinguishes motives by spiritual station.
- 22:40/q05/p1 Ascetics, worshippers, gnostics, lovers and unifiers each risk betrayal at their own station. — Qushayri names seeking afterlife compensation, falling back on concessions, seeking rank, craving relief from longing, and renewed self-choice after effacement. Biqa'i says sincere devotion attracts plots and harm. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 22:40/q05/p2 God protects the common through the elect as a lasting divine custom. — Qushayri says He pardons lesser people for the honor of the great. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 22:40/q06 What does diyār denote and how is dār derived?  [دِيَٰرِهِم · meaning]
- 22:40/q06/p1 It is their dwellings, homes, or country; dār denotes an enclosed circuit. — Bursevi says the plural can denote countries because inhabitants move within them; al-Raghib derives dār from a wall's circuit. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الراغب)]

## 22:40/q07 May houses in Mecca be sold or leased?  [دِيَٰرِهِم · law]
turns on: Whether residents own their houses despite pilgrimage access.
- 22:40/q07/p1 Sale and lease are permissible. — Baghawi prefers this: the verse calls the homes theirs; the Prophet called Abu Sufyan's house his, and Umar bought a Meccan house for a prison for four thousand dirhams. Tawus, Amr ibn Dinar, and al-Shafi'i allow sale. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(طاووس وعمرو بن دينار والشافعي); BAGHAWI-FULL+]
- 22:40/q07/p2 Sale and lease are impermissible because pilgrims have common access. — Abd al-Rahman ibn Sabit says pilgrims had equal entitlement to houses; Umar forbade closing doors during pilgrimage. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن سابط,عمر بن الخطاب); BAGHAWI-FULL-]

## 22:40/q08 What makes the expulsion 'without right'?  [بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources distinguish the absence of a lawful offense from the inherent right to remain in one's homeland.
- 22:40/q08/p1 They committed no wrong; their sole alleged offense was affirming God's oneness. — Al-Hasan says they shed no blood, stole no property, and severed no kinship, citing 85:8; Ibn Kathir cites 60:1 and 85:8; Razi cites 5:59. Ibn Ashur says a declaration of faith harms no one. Al-Jubba'i says no action merited exile; Ibn Ashur notes the religious cause. [32 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن,قتادة); FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); TABRISI(الجبائي)]
- 22:40/q08/p2 People have a natural right to live in their homeland unless a valid law or custom warrants exile. — Ibn Ashur traces the right to settlement or conquest; legitimate banishment punishes wrongdoing, and Zuhayr's verse illustrates old customary grounds. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(زهير)]

## 22:40/q09 How does 'without right' work grammatically?  [بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ · grammar]
turns on: Circumstantial qualification versus an omitted verbal noun.
- 22:40/q09/p1 It is a circumstantial phrase describing the exiles' unjust state. — Ibn Ashur says no valid ground existed for imposing removal. [3 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 22:40/q09/p2 It qualifies an omitted cognate verbal noun: an unjust expulsion. — Tabrisi supplies an understood 'expulsion'. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 22:40/q10 How is the exception 'except that they say' parsed?  [إِلَّآ أَن يَقُولُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the construction is disconnected, connected to ḥaqq, or uses an adjectival 'other than'; Abu Hayyan disputes the proposed apposition.
- 22:40/q10/p1 It is connected to the supposed 'right' or cause, grammatically genitive through bāʾ or appositive to ḥaqq. — Al-Farra and Zajjaj allow understood bāʾ; Wahidi cites 92:20, al-Mubarrad's connected pretext, and al-Nabigha's ironic sword nicks. Alusi rejects objections to the apposition. [23 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء وأبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,المبرد); JISHUMI; THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(النابغة); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 22:40/q10/p2 It is a disconnected accusative exception, with illā meaning 'but' or 'because'. — Sibawayh gives Qur'anic parallels 11:43, 10:98, 11:116 and poets; Abu Hayyan says a positive governor cannot reach the exception and Arabs agree on accusative here. Tabataba'i prefers the contrast. Nahhas transmits Sibawayh's non-connected accusative and Abu Hayyan's objection. Qurtubi transmits Farra and Zajjaj's genitive cause. [23 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو الخطاب,الفرزدق); FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(سيبويه); JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء والزجاج,سيبويه); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو إسحاق والزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABRISI; NAHHAS(سيبويه); TABATABAI+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 22:40/q10/p3 Illā is an adjective meaning 'other than' (siwā) of ḥaqq. — Alusi offers this alternative; Samin distinguishes it from the rejected apposition. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 22:40/q10/p4 The exception is elliptic: 'except that they say the truth'. — Abu Ubayda supplies the omitted truth. [1 notes: MAJAZ]
- 22:40/q10/p5 It is appositive to ghayr, implying they were only maintained in their homes by profession. — Alusi reports this parse but calls it weak. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 22:40/q11 Why present their confession as an exception to wrongdoing?  [إِلَّآ أَن يَقُولُوا۟ رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
turns on: The paradox treats a virtue as a supposed fault.
- 22:40/q11/p1 The ironic exception intensifies innocence and praises them by feigning blame. — Wahidi, Baydawi, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur invoke al-Nabigha's 'only fault' of battle-nicked swords; Kashshaf, Nasafi and Razi compare 5:59. Islahi says Quraysh treated the greatest truth as the greatest sin. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المبرد); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(النابغة); ITQAN; IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 22:40/q11/p2 It states the sole actual cause of persecution: affirmation of one God. — Qatada says monotheism distressed Satan and his troops; Tabataba'i says polytheists perversely treated truth as grounds for eviction. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 22:40/q12 What did Quraysh understand by the confession?  [رَبُّنَا ٱللَّهُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether they viewed it as a harmless private belief or understood its social force.
- 22:40/q12/p1 They recognized its far-reaching implications and opposed it. — Islahi says their opposition to the greatest truth was deliberate. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 22:40/q12/p2 They harassed Muslims for an innocuous belief. — Some interpreters see profession of one God as no offense. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Some people); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 22:40/q13 How does 'were it not for' work here?  [وَلَوْلَا · grammar]
- 22:40/q13/p1 Lawlā introduces a counterfactual: destruction does not occur because God's defense does occur. — Ibn Ashur explains the consequent is withheld by the existence of the condition. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:40/q14 How is 'God's repelling' read and formed?  [دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verbal noun is Form I dafʿ or difāʿ with alif and how it relates to the corresponding verb.
- 22:40/q14/p1 Difāʿ with alif is read with yudāfiʿ and suggests mutual or sustained defense. — Nafi, Abu Ja'far and Ya'qub are reported among its readers. Abu Ali compares qitāl from qātala; Jishumi calls difāʿ reciprocal. [11 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع,يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع والحسن وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); TABRISI(أهل المدينة ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(مدني ويعقوب); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أهل المدينة ويعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL(نافع); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 22:40/q14/p2 Dafʿ without alif is read, including with yadfaʿ without alif. — Ibn Kathir and Abu Amr use the simple form; Asim is reported with dafʿ. Qiraat-ER says Form I may indicate one act. Jishumi calls dafʿ the verbal noun of dafaʿa; he explains al-madfaʿ as a poor man pushed away. [9 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وأبو عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع والحسن وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); DURR-FULL(عاصم); NASHR(ابن كثير، والبصريان); QIRAAT-ER(Lvw); TAB-FULL(ابن زيد)]
- 22:40/q14/p3 Yudāfiʿ with dafʿ combines the derived verb with the simple verbal noun. — Abu Ali says fāʿala may have the meaning of faʿala, as in ʿāqaba and ṭābaqa. [4 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI(عاصم وحمزة والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع والحسن وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي); NASHR(الباقون)]
- 22:40/q14/p4 Yadfaʿ with difāʿ is also possible; difāʿ may be a Form I verbal noun. — Abu Ali compares kitāb from kataba, qiyām and ghiyāth. [3 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; JISHUMI(يعقوب); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو علي)]

## 22:40/q15 What is the grammatical structure of God's repelling people through one another?  [دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍۢ · grammar]
- 22:40/q15/p1 An understood 'that' follows lawlā; baʿḍahum is a partitive substitute for al-nās, and the second baʿḍ is the instrument. — Zajjaj supplies lawlā an dafaʿa Allāh; Akhfash, Tabrisi and Ibn Ashur analyze the substitute. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; AKHFASH; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 22:40/q17 Who are 'the people' in the repelling clause?  [ٱلنَّاسَ · referent]
turns on: A general human principle versus the immediate combatants.
- 22:40/q17/p1 The people are generic across times and communities. — Tabari refuses a restriction; Ibn Ashur permits the historical sense and recalls David, Goliath, Solomon and Nebuchadnezzar. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 22:40/q17/p2 The people in context are Muslims and Meccan polytheists. — Ibn Ashur ties this to expulsion and Muslim self-defense; Kashshaf speaks of believers overcoming unbelievers. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 22:40/q18 What legal duty follows from God's protecting worship?  [وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍۢ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse directly establishes defensive fighting, religious liberty, and wider moral duties.
- 22:40/q18/p1 Believers may fight defensively to protect worship and freedom of religion. — Qurtubi says earlier communities also used jihad; Asad calls defense of religious freedom a foremost reason for arms, citing 2:193 and 2:251; Kuran Yolu cites passages on defensive preparations. [22 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي); RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]
- 22:40/q18/p2 The verse also indicates a duty to command right and forbid wrong. — Jishumi infers the obligation; Uthman's report links 22:40–41 to later command of right. [2 notes: JISHUMI; DURR-FULL(عثمان بن عفان)]

## 22:40/q19 What inward meaning is given to the repelling and the worship places?  [وَلَوْلَا دَفْعُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلنَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍۢ · inward]
turns on: The inward readings assign the conflict to faculties, the ego and spiritual aspiration.
- 22:40/q19/p1 Divine help lets hearts resist the ego so sacred law, spiritual manners, reality and remembrance endure. — Qushayri says promptings corrupt hearts and God defeats them by turning and unveiling; Bursevi describes mosques of the heart. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI]
- 22:40/q19/p2 God counters psychic forces through spirit and intellect; the four sites mark secret, heart, breast, and spirit. — Qashani sets desire against anger, spirit against psyche and intellect against fancy; remembrance culminates in divine character and annihilation. [4 notes: QASHANI]
- 22:40/q19/p3 The saints may seem humiliated, yet God avenges or later empowers them. — Qushayri cites 27:52 and says truth outlasts falsehood. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 22:40/q19/p4 God overcomes self-exalting claims of independent existence with His light. — Qashani applies divine victory inwardly. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 22:40/q20 How is 'would have been demolished' read?  [لَّهُدِّمَتْ · readings]

- 22:40/q31/p3 Supply a second verb: buildings would be demolished and prayers abandoned. — Akhfash and Basran grammarians say prayer itself cannot be torn down. [4 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(بعض أهل العربية من أهل البصرة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); BURHAN]
- 22:40/q31/p4 Coordinate an undemolishable activity with demolished buildings without a supplied noun. — Razi compares carrying a sword and a spear in a single expression. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 22:40/q32 What are masājid here?  [وَمَسَٰجِدُ · referent]

- 22:40/q32/p1 Muslim mosques, places of prostration in Muhammad's community. — Ibn Ashur places Quba and Medina mosque building at the Hijra; Alusi says the name honors closeness to God through prostration. [26 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(رفيع، قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وغيره); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(خصيف); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الأكثرون); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 22:40/q32/p2 Masājid can generically include the preceding Jewish and Christian worship sites. — Study Quran cites 72:18; Islahi notes broader Qur'anic usage; al-Hasan calls all four mosques. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(another opinion); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 22:40/q33 Why is masājid a diptote?  [وَمَسَٰجِدُ · grammar]
- 22:40/q33/p1 It follows the terminal plural pattern mafāʿil, so it has no nunation or ordinary declension. — Sibawayh contrasts terminal mafāʿil/mafāʿīl with singular-like or further-pluralizable forms; suffixes or diminutives can change declension. His examples include maqātil, ṣayāqila, madāʾinī, and the Qur'anic 16:66. Nahhas applies the maximal-plural rule to both ṣawāmiʿ and masājid. [18 notes: SIBAWAYH; NAHHAS]

## 22:40/q34 Which sanctuaries would God's defense preserve, and in which era?  [صَوَٰمِعُ وَبِيَعٌۭ وَصَلَوَٰتٌۭ وَمَسَٰجِدُ · interpretation]

- 22:40/q34/p1 Each prophetic community's lawful worship places were protected in its own era. — Wahidi prefers this because sanctuaries were protected before dhimma and because earlier laws were later superseded. Tabrisi varies which era gets churches; Razi says remembered places differ from idol worship; Islahi says earlier houses originally stood like mosques. [27 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(الزجاج); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الأزهري); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(many commentators and jurists); TABRISI(الزجاج); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 22:40/q34/p2 Muslim victory now protects both Muslim and protected non-Muslim sanctuaries. — Kashshaf says polytheists would overpower Muslims and People of the Book; Wahidi reports al-Hasan's dhimma reading but prefers historical succession. ## 22:40/q35 Why are Magian and polytheist sanctuaries absent?  [صَوَٰمِعُ وَبِيَعٌۭ وَصَلَوَٰتٌۭ وَمَسَٰجِدُ · interpretation]
- 22:40/q35/p1 They lack the protected revealed worship and divine remembrance relevant to this list. — Ibn Atiyya and Qurtubi exclude their institutions on those grounds; Alusi's objection to Sabian identification follows a similar premise. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 22:40/q35/p2 The verse is about preserving visible signs of religion against erasure, not only buildings. — Tabataba'i says buildings remind people of religion, while Alusi sees threatened ruin as a figure for wider corruption. [2 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 22:40/q36 Why are the worship places named in this order?  [صَوَٰمِعُ وَبِيَعٌۭ وَصَلَوَٰتٌۭ وَمَسَٰجِدُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Chronology, risk and word proximity, ascending dignity, or response to critics.
- 22:40/q36/p1 The order follows historical age: older institutions before later Muslim mosques. — Wahidi says pre-Qur'anic sites existed first; Bursevi warns earlier mention does not prove higher merit, citing 64:2. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:40/q36/p2 Mosques come last to stand farthest from demolition and nearest remembrance. — Tha'labi and Qurtubi compare 35:32, where the foremost is delayed. [5 notes: THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 22:40/q36/p3 The list rises toward the noblest and most populated place of worship. — Ibn Kathir says mosques have more frequent worship and sound intent; Alusi contrasts monk-only cells with congregational churches. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض العلماء); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 22:40/q36/p4 Mosques appear last because context warrants that order despite their merit. — Samarrai compares the varying order of Moses and Aaron. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BURSEVI(الأسئلة المقحمة)]
- 22:40/q36/p5 Monasteries appear first because they were familiar Arabian beacons and shelters. — Ibn Ashur says churches then follow by analogy, prayer houses next, and general mosques last. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 22:40/q36/p6 Christian sites appear first because Christians were strong critics of Muslim jihad. — Islahi says monasticism contributed to their opposition and churches and monasteries were equally central for them. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 22:40/q37 What protection do non-Muslim worship places receive?  [صَوَٰمِعُ وَبِيَعٌۭ وَصَلَوَٰتٌۭ · law]
turns on: Whether the verse itself protects existing treaty sanctuaries including fire temples, and what conditions apply.
- 22:40/q37/p1 Existing treaty communities' churches and synagogues may not be destroyed; their covenant protects them. — Ibn Khuyz Mindad compares worship sites to protected homes and property; Study Quran extends treaty protection within and beyond Muslim lands. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن خويز منداد); STUDYQURAN]
- 22:40/q37/p2 The verse itself does not protect fire temples, though the dhimma covenant does. — Ibn Ashur says God is not remembered there in the relevant sense. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد)-]
- 22:40/q37/p3 Protected communities may not add new sanctuaries or expand old ones; unauthorized additions may be removed. — Ibn Khuyz Mindad distinguishes protected existing buildings, new additions, and temples in enemy territory; he also discourages Muslims entering or praying there. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد)]
- 22:40/q37/p4 A mosque may be demolished in order to rebuild it. — Ibn Khuyz Mindad cites Uthman's rebuilding of the Prophet's mosque. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن خويز منداد)]

turns on: The nearest-noun rule favors mosques; coordinated-description syntax and historical remembrance support all four.

## 22:40/q39 What does abundant remembrance mean?  [كَثِيرًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Frequency of repetition or continuity through time.
- 22:40/q39/p1 It means repeated or continuously sustained mention of God. — Ibn Ashur favors remembrance across all times; Kuran Yolu calls it the central aim of worship in all revealed religions. [3 notes: BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 22:40/q39/p2 It marks Islam's primary worship aim and may contrast other institutions' later practice. — Elmalili draws the contrast; Kuran Yolu reports and disputes the implied abandonment. [2 notes: ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Elmalılı Muhammed Hamdi); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]

## 22:40/q41 What does it mean to help God?  [مَن يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · interpretation]

## 22:40/q42 What does naṣr mean?  [يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · meaning]
turns on: Help versus revenge, rain, or coming to a place in broader Arabic usage.
- 22:40/q42/p1 Here it means giving help to God's cause. — Ibn al-Jawzi cites 59:12 and 47:7. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 22:40/q42/p2 In other uses naṣr may mean rain or coming to a place, while intiṣār can mean revenge. — Ibn al-Jawzi quotes a poem using unṣurī for visiting a land. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(الشاعر)]

## 22:40/q43 How does God fulfill the promised help?  [وَلَيَنصُرَنَّ ٱللَّهُ مَن يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · theology]
turns on: Worldly victory, argument and knowledge, later historical fulfillment, or help in the Hereafter.
- 22:40/q43/p1 He grants victory over enemies, including the early Muslim conquests. — Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi cite Arab chiefs, Persian kings and Byzantine emperors; Biqa'i includes ordinary causes and miracles; Ibn Kathir cites 37:171–173 and 58:21. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KASHSHAF-FULL(عثمان)]
- 22:40/q43/p2 He supports believers in argument, knowledge, obedience, and steadiness. — Razi names proof and learning; Ibn Kathir links 47:7–8. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 22:40/q43/p3 God needs no aid; human striving lets believers receive benefits and His promised support. — Bahr al-Ulum says God could destroy enemies unaided; Elmalili links the servant's will to religious acts; Islahi says effort opens the way to help. - 22:40/q43/p4 His aid is victory here and a proof in the Hereafter. — Yahya ibn Sallam makes the twofold distinction. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 22:40/q43/p5 He alternates hardship and victory as tests without negating the promise. — Bursevi says forced faith would cancel moral responsibility; trials expiate believers' sins, and a dream report depicts reward for an oppressed victim. [3 notes: BURSEVI(عامر)]

## 22:40/q44 Why is the promise so emphatic?  [وَلَيَنصُرَنَّ ٱللَّهُ · rhetoric]
- 22:40/q44/p1 The oath lām and emphatic nūn guarantee divine support and urge endurance in battle. — Kashshaf compares rulers' authoritative promises and 22:38; Ibn Ashur notes both markers. ## 22:40/q45 What does the explicit name of God in the aid promise accomplish?  [وَلَيَنصُرَنَّ ٱللَّهُ مَن يَنصُرُهُۥٓ · rhetoric]
- 22:40/q45/p1 It universalizes the rule to anyone who supports God and ties aid to that quality. — Biqa'i contrasts the name with an available pronoun. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 22:40/q46 What do 'strong' and 'mighty' affirm of God?  [لَقَوِىٌّ عَزِيزٌ · theology]

- 22:40/q46/p1 He has unrestricted power to aid believers and cannot be overcome or resisted. — Tabari contrasts idols' inability to create or protect a fly (22:73); Ibn Kathir invokes creation and decree; Ibn Ashur distinguishes strength as power and might as invulnerability. ## 22:40/q47 Why does strength come before might?  [لَقَوِىٌّ عَزِيزٌ · rhetoric]
- 22:40/q47/p1 Strength causes victory and thus leads to might. — Samarrai's discussions phrase this as power enabling conquest and domination. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 22:40/q48 How can a following noun be raised after inna?  [إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ · grammar]

- 22:40/q49/p3 It permitted defense if Quraysh barred pilgrimage to the House, including fighting in the Haram or sacred months. — Islahi says Quraysh used sanctuary rules as a shield while occupying Abraham's House and accusing Muslims of impiety. [13 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

turns on: Destination of exile and the disputed dating of the sūra.

- 22:40/q50/p5 The sura mixes Makkan and Madinan verses. — Study Quran says most authorities hold this; a tradition describes revelation by day and night, travel and home, war and peace. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]

## 22:40/q52 What unusual reports accompany the permission and later praise?  [* · reports]
- 22:40/q52/p1 Uthman said the verse and its sequel describe him and companions later empowered for prayer, alms, and right action. — The report calls the praise advance praise before the deeds. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(عثمان بن عفان); KASHSHAF-FULL(عثمان)]
