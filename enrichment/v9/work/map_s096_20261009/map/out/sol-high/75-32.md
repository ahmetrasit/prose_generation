# 75:32: 10 questions

## 75:32/q01 How is ولكن construed?  [وَلَٰكِن · grammar]
- 75:32/q01/p1 It begins a coordinated corrective clause; the lightened لكن is an inceptive conjunction and does not govern case like إن. — Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates ولكن كذب with فلا صدق and observes that the lightened conjunction commonly follows wāw. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 75:32/q02 Why add ولكن كذب after the preceding failure to affirm?  [وَلَٰكِن كَذَّبَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the preceding ṣaddaqa means affirmation or almsgiving, and whether lack of affirmation already entails explicit denial.
- 75:32/q02/p1 It adds active denial to mere failure to affirm, correcting an inference of silence, doubt, or lesser neglect. — Samīn says many Muslims fail to give or pray without denying, so the clause identifies rejection and aversion as the cause; he calls a reading of the first denial as rejection of the Messenger weak because it would repeat كذب with لكن between equivalents. Biqāʿī says lack of affirmation is not denial and describes persistent rejection overcoming original sound nature. Bursevī puts doubt between belief and denial. Ālūsī rules out silence or hesitation. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says open denial is harsher than nonaffirmation, and turning away broader than omission of prayer. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 75:32/q02/p2 It reinforces or clarifies the preceding negation by stating its contrary. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives two functions of لكن: reinforcement by the contrary, citing 33:5, and clarification of a general negation, citing 33:40. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 75:32/q02/p3 It contrasts denial with an earlier failure to give alms and pray. — Elmalılı prefers taking ṣaddaqa as almsgiving, supported by 74:43–44; on that reading this verse adds denial instead of merely reinforcing an earlier denial. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 75:32/q02/p4 It restates and intensifies the preceding lack of belief and prayer. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the two failures are restated as active denial and aversion: public denial is worse than nonaffirmation, while aversion exceeds simply not praying. His Ḥalaqāt account calls the pair reinforced restatements and connects each to a separate warning in 75:34–35. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 75:32/q03 What did he deny?  [كَذَّبَ · interpretation]
turns on: The object understood for the unexpressed object of كذب; several sources name more than one object.
- 75:32/q03/p1 He denied the Qur'an, God's Book, or the truth conveyed by it. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, and Qatāda in al-Ṭabarī, al-Ṭabrisī, and al-Durr specify the Qur'an or God's Book. Al-Māwardī reports this as one reading. Al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Bayḍāwī, and Asad say truth; al-Biqāʿī says God's message. Bursevī includes what was conveyed about the Qur'an. Al-Ṭabrisī's Qatāda includes the Messenger too. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the Qur'an's true call and its beliefs; Study Quran says he failed to affirm its revelation. Ibn ʿĀshūr includes the Qur'an. Al-Kur'an Yolu includes revelation and truth, and calls the refusal deliberate lying; al-Iṣlāḥī contrasts denial with the affirmation due to the Messenger and Hereafter. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI(قتادة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:32/q03/p2 He rejected the Messenger, his message, or his invitation. — Al-Thaʿlabī and al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz say message; al-Māwardī reports a reading that he called the Messenger a liar. Al-Kashshāf says he disbelieved in the Messenger. Al-Ṭabrisī's Qatāda names Book and Messenger together; al-Qurṭubī counts failure to believe the Messenger among four faults. Abū Ḥayyān says he rejected what the Messenger brought, and Bursevī includes what was conveyed about him. Ibn ʿĀshūr names Muhammad's message; Elmalılı names God, Messenger, Qur'an, and afterlife. Al-Iṣlāḥī says they should have affirmed the Messenger and Hereafter. Kur'an Yolu names the Prophet and says he lies about truth and its messenger; Study Quran says Muhammad's prophethood went unaffirmed. Al-Sāmarrāʾī says denial strikes at the Messenger and all pillars of faith. [15 notes: THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 75:32/q03/p3 He denied resurrection or the Hereafter. — Ibn ʿĀshūr names resurrection alongside the Qur'an and Muhammad's message. Elmalılı includes the afterlife among the objects of denial. Al-Iṣlāḥī says he denied the Hereafter despite the required affirmation. Al-Sāmarrāʾī connects this denial to his question about the Hour in 75:6 and the gathering of bones, calling belief in the Last Day central. Kur'an Yolu puts resurrection among the proofs he resists. [6 notes: ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 75:32/q03/p4 He denied God. — Elmalılı names God together with the Messenger, Qur'an, and afterlife. [1 notes: ELMALILI]

## 75:32/q04 From what did he turn away?  [وَتَوَلَّىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: What object is supplied to the verb and whether the stress is on faith, obedience, the Messenger, or attention to revelation.
- 75:32/q04/p1 He turned away from faith or belief. — Muqātil, al-Wāḥidī in both works, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, and al-Ṭabrisī's Qatāda say faith. Al-Kashshāf describes disbelief in the Messenger before the man's departure. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says he denied the call; al-Iṣlāḥī contrasts denial and aversion with the faith due from him. [9 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 75:32/q04/p2 He turned away from obedience, worship, or prayer. — Al-Ṭabarī, his Qatāda report, and al-Māwardī's Qur'an reading name obedience; Qatāda in al-Durr says the same. Al-Qurṭubī specifies prayer as the fourth fault. Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, and Elmalılı name obedience, with al-Biqāʿī adding prayer. Rāzī says abandonment of prayer merits blame and punishment. Al-Kur'an Yolu says he avoids worship; al-Sāmarrāʾī links aversion to neglected prayer and outward action. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:32/q04/p3 He turned away from the Messenger or the One who sent him. — Al-Māwardī reports the latter alongside calling the Messenger a liar. Abū Ḥayyān and Bursevī name the Messenger. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 75:32/q04/p4 He refused the invitation to attend to the Qur'an or truth. — Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies the invitation to examine and reflect on the Qur'an. Asad says he turned from the truth. Kur'an Yolu describes blind bigotry: he refuses fair listening, shuts his ears, and locks his heart. Elmalılı calls it turning from truth and obedience. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 75:32/q04/p5 Tawallī simply means turning away, with no object specified. — Al-Jishumī gives the lexical gloss without naming what is left behind. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 75:32/q05 Who is the person described?  [* · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage identifies a historical opponent or describes the earlier human being more generally.
- 75:32/q05/p1 Abū Jahl ibn Hishām. — Al-Thaʿlabī names him; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this as the majority interpretation of the whole passage. Al-Durr transmits Qatāda's identification and his account of denial, failure to pray, and swaggering home. Study Quran reports Abū Jahl as a possibility, referring to 75:3. [4 notes: THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المتأولين); DURR-FULL(قتادة); STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 75:32/q05/p2 ʿAdī ibn Rabīʿah. — Study Quran reports this as an alternative, referring to 75:3. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Q)]
- 75:32/q05/p3 The human being mentioned earlier in the sūra, described here as an unbeliever or sinner. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the pronouns to the human of 75:3. Rāzī speaks of the unbeliever; Elmalılı of the sinner previously absorbed in worldly pleasure; Kur'an Yolu treats the passage as criticism of disbelievers. [4 notes: TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 75:32/q06 What moral contrast does the pair of actions express?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Which aspect of the paired conduct the source draws out: inward and outward failure, active wrongdoing plus neglected duties, or wilful arrogance.
- 75:32/q06/p1 Inward rejection is joined to outward neglect of righteous action. — Ibn Kathīr calls the denial inward and the aversion outward, leaving neither inward nor outward good. Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes inward assent from belief and denial expressed by heart, speech, and limbs: he lacked inward faith, openly denied, and abandoned action (75:14). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī pairs rejection of the Qur'an's beliefs with failure to perform ṣalāt, the central pillar of worship. Al-Qurṭubī lists four faults: failure to believe the Messenger, failure to pray, denial, and aversion from prayer. Rāzī similarly joins denial of faith's fundamentals to prayer's abandonment. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; TABATABAI; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 75:32/q06/p2 The two acts are positive wrongs alongside the neglected goods of faith, prayer, and giving. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī counts denial and aversion as two sins; wrongful action and neglected good both provoke reproach, recalling 75:2. Al-Iṣlāḥī says prayer and spending in God's way are the two deeds that would provision the demanding journey; both rest on firm belief in the Hereafter and are the sharīʿah's primary deeds. Elmalılı says he neither affirmed the Hereafter nor worked for God, gave no alms or prayer, then denied and turned away. Rāzī says omitting prayer merits blame and punishment just as unbelief does. [6 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL]
- 75:32/q06/p3 He wilfully and arrogantly refuses the truth despite its proofs. — Kur'an Yolu invokes the proofs of God's unity, resurrection, and punishment, then describes deliberate lying, refusal to listen fairly, blind bigotry, stopped ears, and a locked heart. Al-Iṣlāḥī says reminders of duty meet an arrogant, conceited departure. Al-Kashshāf says he returns to his people strutting after disbelief and aversion. [5 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 75:32/q07 How does this conduct connect with the sūra's earlier account?  [* · links]
turns on: Which earlier passage explains or anticipates the two acts and their outcome.
- 75:32/q07/p1 It follows his preference for sin and immediate life and his doubt about resurrection. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī links his desires and continued sinning to 75:5, his lack of resurrection belief to his question about the Hour in 75:6, and his denial to the earlier question about gathering bones. He also says love of immediate life over the afterlife in 75:20–21 explains denial and aversion. Elmalılı says the fā joins these clauses to the sinner's worldly pleasure, resurrection question, and eventual return to God. [5 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; ELMALILI]
- 75:32/q07/p2 His deeds are what he sent ahead and what he left undone, and he will confront them. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī links 75:13 to disbelief and aversion sent ahead and delayed faith and prayer left behind. He links 75:14 to the discrepancy between absent inward assent, open denial, and action abandoned; he links 75:15 to later excuses when questioned about failure to pray and turning away. [3 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:32/q07/p3 Belief and prayer would have placed him among radiant faces; denial and aversion place him among grim faces. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the two groups in 75:22–25. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]

## 75:32/q08 How does his return to his family develop this description?  [* · links]
- 75:32/q08/p1 He goes back to his family or people after denial and aversion, swaggering or conceited. — Al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ notes the return to family. Al-Kashshāf says he returns to his people strutting. Qatāda in al-Durr says Abū Jahl goes home swaggering. Al-Iṣlāḥī describes an arrogant walk away after being reminded of duties. Al-Qurṭubī adds the chronological nuance that his strutting predated his denial. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 75:32/q09 How do the following warnings match the faults described here?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the warning's repetition is counted by the two faults, read as greater severity or temporal sequence, or tied to the person's lifetime.
- 75:32/q09/p1 Each of the two faults receives a warning when first stated and again when restated, making four warnings. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī counts one warning per fault in each formulation. His Ḥalaqāt version identifies the restatement and links it to 75:34–35. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 75:32/q09/p2 The later warning is more forceful, with ثم allowing distance in rank as well as time. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites 90:11–17, where freeing and feeding are distinguished from faith, as an example of rank rather than mere sequence. He also reads ثم as marking an interval between two punishments and their difference in severity. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:32/q09/p3 The threats encompass punishment in the grave and a more severe punishment after resurrection. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says the condemned meet fire morning and evening in the grave, then a stronger punishment after resurrection; ثم marks the interval and difference in severity. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
- 75:32/q09/p4 The repeated words of woe apply to the person during life. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī reports this from al-Fatḥ al-Qadīr; the excerpt ends before the explanation is complete. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT(فتح القدير)]

## 75:32/q10 Why is this person's threat stronger than the comparison in 47:20?  [* · links]
- 75:32/q10/p1 He openly denies, turns away, and struts, whereas the people in 47:20 may still profess faith and pray outwardly. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the outward conduct of the two figures to explain the stronger threat here. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT]
