# 20:70: 21 questions

## 20:70/q01 What happened between Moses' command and the magicians' fall?  [فَأُلْقِىَ · links]
- 20:70/q01/p1 Moses cast his staff; it became a serpent and swallowed the magicians' work, an event the account leaves implicit. — Muqatil says God then caused their fall. Wahidi says the omitted details occur in suras 7 and 26; Ibn Atiyya allows an equivalent event. Qurtubi says the swallowed ropes and sticks amounted to three hundred camel loads and the staff returned to normal, with only God knowing where the objects went. Bursevi adds the crowd fled and Moses caught the serpent. Biqai says the omission focuses this sura on God's power to soften hearts. Ibn Ashur compares the omitted result of the sea-striking command in 26:63 and cites 20:69. Asad points to 7:117–119 for the defeat. [12 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:70/q02 What does the opening fa connect to?  [فَأُلْقِىَ · grammar]
- 20:70/q02/p1 It follows omitted events: Moses cast the staff and it swallowed their devices. — Ibn Ashur calls it coordination with an omitted clause and compares 26:63; Alusi says fear ended before Moses cast the staff. Bursevi calls the fa explanatory, following the staff's swallowing and ensuing events. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:70/q03 Why are the magicians described as being cast down?  [فَأُلْقِىَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the passive chiefly marks their own action or the overwhelming force and speed of their response.
- 20:70/q03/p1 The passive depicts a sudden, powerful fall into prostration under the force of what they witnessed. — Al-Akhfash, reported by Nasafi, compares their earlier throwing of ropes in denial with throwing their heads in thankful worship. Kashshaf, Biqai, and Jar Allah as reported by Bursevi make the same contrast. Abu Hayyan and Alusi stress the miracle's irresistible effect; Alusi adds divine guidance from obstinate unbelief and stylistic correspondence with their casting of implements. Biqai stresses extraordinary speed and ease. Tabatabai emphasizes God's overwhelming power, leaving them no choice. Bursevi likens the wording to someone hurling them. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الأخفش); BIQAI-FULL(الأصبهاني); BURSEVI(جار الله); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:70/q03/p2 The passive means that the magicians cast themselves down; nobody else threw them. — Tabrisi takes it as their own act. Ibn Ashur adds that their public prostration acknowledged Moses as God's messenger. [2 notes: TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:70/q04 To whom was the magicians' prostration directed?  [سُجَّدًۭا · referent]
turns on: Whether the physical prostration was worship, an acknowledgment of Moses, or a precaution before their spoken confession.
- 20:70/q04/p1 They prostrated in worship of God. — Mawardi calls it obedience to God and affirmation of Moses; Wahidi Wajiz says it accompanied faith in the brothers' Lord. Baydawi and Alusi add repentance and reverence. Ibn Ashur offers this as one possible sense. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:70/q04/p2 They prostrated to honor Moses after his victory. — Ibn Ashur gives this as another possible sense; Islahi notes that prostration could signal acknowledgment of someone's mastery, while the magicians also publicly professed faith in God. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:70/q04/p3 They may have prostrated to Pharaoh as a precaution against his violence before declaring faith. — Ibn Ashur presents this as a possible sequence. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:70/q05 What does sujjadan express?  [سُجَّدًۭا · grammar]
- 20:70/q05/p1 It is the plural of sajid and describes the magicians' state as they fell, with prostration intended at the moment of falling. — Ibn Ashur gives the plural form. Zajjaj parses it as an accusative circumstantial expression whose state is prospective: they fell intending to prostrate before actually doing so. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:70/q06 Why did the magicians recognize Moses' sign and believe?  [ٱلسَّحَرَةُ ءَامَنَّا · interpretation]
- 20:70/q06/p1 Their expertise showed that the staff's transformation and swallowing of their devices was a divine miracle rather than sorcery, so they accepted Moses' mission. — Qushayri says they saw the sign, prostrated in faith, and left Pharaoh and his people defeated. Ibn Atiyya says the staff remained unaffected by sorcery, transformed, consumed the props, then returned to its former state. Baydawi calls the devices tricks. Ibn Kathir says experienced sorcerers recognized a feat possible only for the Creator who says 'Be,' citing 26:47–48. Bursevi's Persian narrative adds the fleeing crowd and Moses catching the restored staff. Bursevi and Alusi transmit the chief's argument: in their magic, tools remained after victory; their disappearance implied a powerful, knowing Creator. Alusi also infers the Creator from bodily transformation and allows total annihilation. Qashani stresses fair-minded recognition of a decisive proof. Ibn Ashur and Islahi stress their expertise; Islahi says they already suspected Moses' sign was not magic and were pressured to face him (20:73). Kuranyolu contrasts their belief with Pharaoh's continued unbelief. [12 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(رُوي); QASHANI; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(رئيسهم); ALUSI-FULL(رئيسهم); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:70/q07 How many magicians were there?  [ٱلسَّحَرَةُ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted estimates give different counts; the notes offer no criterion for choosing among them.
- 20:70/q07/p1 There were seventy-three magicians. — Muqatil also names their chief Sham'un. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 20:70/q07/p2 There were a little over thirty thousand. — Ibn Kathir transmits this estimate from al-Suddi. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]
- 20:70/q07/p3 There were nineteen thousand. — Ibn Kathir transmits al-Thawri through Abd al-Aziz ibn Rafi from Abu Thumama. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(الثوري عن عبد العزيز بن رفيع عن أبي ثمامة)]
- 20:70/q07/p4 There were fifteen thousand. — Ibn Kathir transmits Muhammad ibn Ishaq's estimate. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق)]
- 20:70/q07/p5 There were twelve thousand. — Ibn Kathir transmits Ka'b al-Ahbar's estimate. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(كعب الأحبار)]
- 20:70/q07/p6 There were seventy men. — Ibn Kathir transmits Ibn Abbas through Ibn Abi Hatim; the seventy began as sorcerers and ended the day as martyrs. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:70/q07/p7 There were eighty thousand. — Ibn Kathir transmits Muhammad ibn Ka'b's estimate. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن كعب)]
- 20:70/q07/p8 There were seventy thousand. — Ibn Kathir transmits al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza's estimate. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(القاسم بن أبي بزة)]

## 20:70/q08 Who was the magicians' chief?  [ٱلسَّحَرَةُ · reports]
- 20:70/q08/p1 Their chief was Sham'un. — Muqatil gives the name along with a count of seventy-three magicians; other notes quote an unnamed chief's argument about the disappearance of their tools. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 20:70/q09 What did the magicians see while prostrating?  [سُجَّدًۭا · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on whether they saw Paradise alone, both final realms, or their homes in construction.
- 20:70/q09/p1 They saw Paradise or their allotted places there. — Mawardi says they did not lift their heads until seeing Paradise and its people's reward. Ikrima, in Kashshaf and Alusi, says God showed their places; Baydawi and Nasafi report their homes and Nasafi says they then raised their heads. Ibn Kathir transmits al-Awza'i that Paradise was raised before them. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(عكرمة); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الأوزاعي); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 20:70/q09/p2 They saw both Paradise and Hell, including the reward and punishment there. — Kashshaf says they stayed prostrate until seeing both realms and the inhabitants' rewards. Alusi likewise reports the Garden and Fire, reward and punishment. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:70/q09/p3 They saw their future abodes being built. — Ibn Kathir transmits this from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, and also from Ikrima and al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,عكرمة والقاسم بن أبي بزة)]

## 20:70/q10 Would seeing their Paradise stations have compelled their faith?  [سُجَّدًۭا ءَامَنَّا · theology]
turns on: Whether the vision preceded the choice to believe, and whether the promised stations depended on their plea for forgiveness.
- 20:70/q10/p1 Yes; such a vision would compel belief and undermine moral responsibility. — Alusi reports al-Qadi's objection to Ikrima's account of God showing their stations. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة)-]
- 20:70/q10/p2 No; their faith came before the unveiling, so the vision did not compel it. — Alusi answers al-Qadi in this way. Al-Irshad, reported by Alusi, says seeing their stations accords with their later plea for forgiveness at 20:73: those stations were theirs conditional on that declaration. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إرشاد العقل السليم); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:70/q11 How does qalu relate syntactically to the prostration?  [قَالُوٓا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the speech starts a new explanatory sentence or is syntactically tied to the prostration.
- 20:70/q11/p1 It is an explanatory resumption stating what they said while prostrating. — Bursevi gives the content-of-speech explanation; Alusi calls it a resumption like earlier passages. Tabatabai says its lack of coordination answers an implied question about what they said. Ibn Ashur includes an independent opening of dialogue among possible analyses. [4 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:70/q11/p2 It can be a circumstantial clause or an appositional specification of the prostration. — Ibn Ashur presents both syntactic alternatives as ways to explain what the prostration contained. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:70/q12 What does 'we believe' mean here?  [ءَامَنَّا · meaning]
- 20:70/q12/p1 They affirm the truth of God as Aaron and Moses' Lord. — Muqatil glosses belief as affirmation. Biqai says their declaration was true belief and affirmation; Yahya, Wahidi Wajiz, and Baghawi state the public profession of faith. Qushayri contrasts their earlier oath by Pharaoh's might with their new oath by God. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]

## 20:70/q13 Whom do they identify as the Lord of Aaron and Moses?  [بِرَبِّ هَٰرُونَ وَمُوسَىٰ · referent]
- 20:70/q13/p1 They mean God, whom both messengers called people to worship, not Pharaoh or either brother. — Mawardi says He is Lord of the brothers and all creation. Tabrisi says both called people to Him and were His messengers; Bursevi adds He performed miracles through them. Razi says God's favor gave them miracles and high ranks. Abu Hayyan says the explicit identification counters Pharaoh's claim to lordship. Biqai says God raised the brothers above the magicians and Pharaoh, who could not help his followers. Ibn Ashur says the brothers' names identified the God familiar to them amid other gods and Pharaoh worship. Tabatabai says the formula testifies to divine lordship and both men's messengership. Qashani says divine attributes manifested in the brothers according to their capacity, through them rather than independently. [9 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:70/q14 Why is Aaron named before Moses here?  [هَٰرُونَ وَمُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes weigh cadence, age, Pharaoh's claim, rank, contextual meaning, and whether the two accounts quote one speech, two groups, or a narrator's retelling.
- 20:70/q14/p1 Aaron comes first to suit this passage's verse endings. — Ibn Atiyya compares the delayed shatta in 20:53. Nasafi says waw does not require temporal order, unlike the wording at 26:48. Abu Hayyan adds that Moses is the staff miracle's agent; Burhan and al-Sakkaki note Moses otherwise has stronger precedence or merit and cite 7:122 and 26:48. Burhan's author gives the default order of subject before object, topic before predicate, and possessor before circumstantial phrase unless a reason favors change. Baydawi, Bursevi, Alusi, and Ibn Ashur also offer cadence; Ibn Ashur says a narrator could select this order without reproducing the original speech. Samarrai says the order also fits the scene's meaning: Ta-Ha ends in Musa's alif, its opening letters end in ha, Harun's first letter, while Ta-Sin-Mim ends in Musa's mim; neither opening has the other's initial. [15 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(السكاكي,مخالفوهم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; BURHAN+; BURHAN(القاضي)-; BIQAI-FULL(الرازي)-]
- 20:70/q14/p2 Aaron comes first because he was the elder brother. — Baydawi and Abu Hayyan report this. Alusi says it may reflect the actual spoken order and rejects al-Sayyid's assertion that Moses was older. Ibn Ashur says this seniority could explain one of two formulas the magicians uttered. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:70/q14/p3 Naming Aaron first prevents 'Moses' Lord' from being mistaken for Pharaoh, who raised Moses. — Razi relates Pharaoh's boast about raising Moses to 26:18 and his lordship claims to 79:24 and 28:38. Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, and Bursevi report the same concern. Alusi says the al-Araf order may be the narrator's arrangement for this reason and cites 7:122. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:70/q14/p4 Aaron's precedence serves this sura's meaning, guiding proud people to humble support of the Prophet. — Biqai says the order foreshadows the Qur'an's guiding of supporters despite their former pride. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:70/q14/p5 The order traces a meaningful progression through the minister and translator to the one who sent him. — Biqai argues this is the order's substantive point, rejecting an explanation merely by rhyme. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]
- 20:70/q14/p6 Moses is placed second as the more honored figure and primary bearer of the mission. — Alusi says the miracle came through Moses and the order also preserves rhyme. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:70/q14/p7 Different groups of magicians voiced the brothers' names in opposite orders, and both statements can be attributed to all. — Abu Hayyan proposes this to reconcile 7:122 with this verse; Alusi explicitly prefers it for this sura. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 20:70/q14/p8 The magicians themselves may have uttered both orders for two different merits. — Ibn Ashur says Aaron could lead for age, Moses for his greater prophetic rank and divine speech, citing 7:121–122 and 26:47–48. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:70/q14/p9 A narrator may select different wording and order when recounting the same declaration. — Ibn Ashur says rhyme may govern the narrator's choice rather than the magicians' exact words; Burhan reports al-Qadi's view that varied retelling preserves meaning and displays inimitable eloquence. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURHAN(القاضي)]

## 20:70/q15 Does this order imply that Aaron outranks Moses?  [هَٰرُونَ وَمُوسَىٰ · grammar]
- 20:70/q15/p1 No; the coordinating waw includes both without establishing sequence or rank. — Nasafi contrasts the order at 26:48; Abu Hayyan says coordinated names do not require sequence. Ibn Ashur contrasts 7:121–122 and says neither ordering implies relative rank. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:70/q16 Does the name order exemplify Qur'anic saj or rhyme driven wording?  [هَٰرُونَ وَمُوسَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether cadence governs the wording or merely accompanies an independently intended meaning; the sources dispute whether the Qur'anic pattern should be called saj.
- 20:70/q16/p1 Yes; the reversal shows deliberate cadence and the Qur'an's frequent saj is a rhetorical excellence. — Burhan reports opponents citing the reversed names and defining saj as successive speech on one meter; unlike poetry's necessarily intended meter, Qur'anic saj is too frequent to be accidental. Al-Sakkaki and Burhan's author also describe this order as fitting the verse endings. [4 notes: BURHAN(السكاكي,مخالفوهم); BURHAN+; BURHAN(القاضي)-; BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري,الرازي,القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)-]
- 20:70/q16/p2 No; Qur'anic expression follows intended meaning, while saj would make meaning follow rhyme. — Burhan reports al-Qadi that varying story wording and order without changing meaning is difficult eloquence proving imitators' inability. Biqai explains that completed meaning may incidentally resemble saj, with rhyme only decorating it, and refers to his discussion at 9:129. He cites the Prophet's criticism of contrived soothsayer-like rhyme; Razi as quoted by Abu Hayyan says no Qur'anic order is changed for rhyme because meaning and wording together are inimitable. Biqai reports al-Baqillani's school and al-Ash'ari opposing the label. Biqai's own alternative reads a progression through minister and translator to sender. [8 notes: BURHAN(القاضي); BIQAI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(أبو الحسن الأشعري,الرازي,القاضي أبو بكر الباقلاني)+]

## 20:70/q17 How does this account relate to the other Qur'anic tellings?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes compare different emphases and wording across the retellings; they do not present them as incompatible.
- 20:70/q17/p1 Other passages supply the defeat, confession, and varying order of the brothers' names. — Wahidi Basit points to al-Araf for the episode; Qurtubi for the staff's swallowing. Abu Hayyan notes the name order differs from 7:122. Burhan notes the reversal between Ta-Ha and al-Shu'ara without resolving it in one note. Asad points to 7:117–119 for defeat and 7:120 for their response. Study Quran points to 7:119–122. Kuranyolu suggests comparing the Qur'anic account at 7:103–126 with the Biblical episode. Samarrai Asrar Muh and Samarrai Lamasat raise the order and verse-ending question without answering it in those segments. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:70/q17/p2 The al-Araf account adds 'Lord of all worlds'; this account need give only wording suited to its context. — Ibn Ashur cites 7:121 and says a report need not reproduce every word spoken. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:70/q18 What change did the magicians undergo that day?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:70/q18/p1 They moved rapidly from sorcery and defiance to faith, righteous witness, and martyrdom. — Qushayri contrasts oaths by Pharaoh's might with oaths by God. Ibn Kathir transmits Ibn Abbas and Ubayd ibn Umayr that they began as sorcerers and ended righteous martyrs; an Ibn Abbas report specifies seventy men. Biqai says they ended as righteous witnesses. Islahi says the declaration went beyond the customary acknowledgment of superior skill. Kuranyolu says after tasting faith they valued the afterlife above worldly reward or even being spared and spoke openly. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عباس وعبيد بن عمير); BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:70/q19 How did Pharaoh respond to their public faith?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:70/q19/p1 He feared others would follow them and answered their public profession with a verbal attack. — Razi says Pharaoh feared imitation after witnessing their prostration and confession. Islahi says his attack aimed to influence the crowd. Kuranyolu describes political anxiety over the Israelites in Egypt and Moses' plan to lead them home as a setting for psychological warfare; he contrasts Pharaoh's persistent unbelief with the magicians' recognition. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:70/q20 What inward change accompanied their prostration?  [* · inward]
- 20:70/q20/p1 Divine illumination and devotion drew them from ego and imitation to inward sight, sincere faith, and prostration of the heart. — Qushayri describes inward suns of gnosis and lights of divine care, through which they saw God inwardly. Bursevi transmits a Sufi authority that devotion removes ego's claims and brings sincerity, certainty, and divine lights; the magicians believed by proof, while Pharaoh saw no proof of his claimed lordship. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]

## 20:70/q21 What does their defeat teach about miracles, saintly gifts, and sorcery?  [* · theology]
- 20:70/q21/p1 A prophet's miracle or saint's gift draws on higher divine orientation, whereas sorcery weakens through attachment to nature. — Qashani says extraordinary acts can arise through material combinations, prepared images attracting celestial forces, or soul-power; a prophetic miracle comes with challenge and opposition, unlike a non-prophet saint's gift. He says the sorcerer may first exert strong influence but then weakens by turning from the higher source, while prophet or saint strengthens; a defeated sorcerer often accepts the prophet unless natural inclination has erased his capacity. [3 notes: QASHANI]
