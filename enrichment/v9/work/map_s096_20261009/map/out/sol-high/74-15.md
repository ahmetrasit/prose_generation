# 74:15: 20 questions

## 74:15/q01 What does thumma convey here?  [ثُمَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether thumma marks the moral distance of the expectation or a rise in rhetorical rank.
- 74:15/q01/p1 It expresses astonishment and reproach at expecting more after such favors. — The author of al-Naẓm, al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī compare a fed and sheltered guest who then insults his host; al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī and al-Qurṭubī compare 6:1. Unnamed grammarians in al-Burhān allow thumma to mark astonishment. Al-Biqāʿī stresses the distance between God's favors and denial; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī describe moral remoteness as temporal distance. Al-Ālūsī rejects explaining it as a rise in rank. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(صاحب النظم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURHAN(غير مسمى); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:15/q01/p2 It marks an ascent to a still more astonishing degree. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the expectation is a higher degree of astonishment after the earlier gifts, and the following kallā makes its improbability plain. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 74:15/q02 What does yatmaʿ mean?  [يَطْمَعُ · meaning]
- 74:15/q02/p1 It is seeking something great. — Ibn ʿĀshūr defines ṭamaʿ this way. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:15/q03 Who is the man whose hope is described?  [يَطْمَعُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular example is exclusive, shared with another named opponent, or representative of a broader human tendency.
- 74:15/q03/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra. — Ibn Hishām reports al-Walīd's role among Quraysh; al-Jishumī reports Ibn ʿĀmir's identification of the rich father of sons called al-Wāḥid; Study Quran cites his wealth, seven or ten sons, learning and eloquence. The other notes name al-Walīd in their explanation of the hope. [10 notes: IBNHISHAM(الوليد بن المغيرة); JISHUMI; MUQATIL; THALABI; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(أبو مالك); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:15/q03/p2 The description includes al-Walīd and ʿUtba. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names both as desiring further worldly increase. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 74:15/q03/p3 It depicts affluent people generally, while still applying to al-Walīd. — Iṣlāḥī says the passage describes a wealthy, high-status mentality rather than al-Walīd alone; he also describes Meccan and Ṭāʾif leaders who took wealth as proof of divine favor. Study Quran cites the general tendency to want more, 102:1, and the prophetic saying about wanting two valleys of gold after receiving one. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Prophet); ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]

## 74:15/q04 What further increase did he hope for?  [أَزِيدَ · interpretation]
turns on: What kind of additional favor he imagined: worldly increase, Paradise, divine support, or continued family provision.
- 74:15/q04/p1 More worldly wealth, children, comfort, or blessings. — Muqātil says God had already expanded his wealth; Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Kalbī and Muqātil specify wealth and children. Al-Baghawī adds comfort; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds arrangements. Al-Nasafī, al-Kashshāf and others stress greed after abundance. Iṣlāḥī says he treats possessions as an inherited entitlement and leaders mistake wealth for proof of divine favoritism. Al-Biqāʿī allows worldly or afterlife favor. [26 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:15/q04/p2 Admission to Paradise or a reward there. — Al-Ḥasan says he hoped to enter Paradise. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı report his claim that, if Muḥammad were truthful, Paradise was created for him. Al-Nasafī adds wealth and children there and cites his boast in 19:77; al-Rāzī also cites 19:77. Iṣlāḥī says he claimed still more in the Hereafter; al-Biqāʿī allows afterlife favor. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); NASAFI-FULL(الحسن); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:15/q04/p3 God's support despite his disbelief. — Al-Māwardī offers this as a further possibility; al-Qurṭubī reports it as another interpretation. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 74:15/q04/p4 Continued provision among his descendants. — Al-Qurṭubī reports that al-Walīd thought Muḥammad's memory would end at his death and hoped provision would continue in his descendants. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 74:15/q05 Why is the hoped-for increase attributed directly to God?  [أَنْ أَزِيدَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the divine first person recalls a forgotten giver or reflects how idolaters spoke of provision.
- 74:15/q05/p1 The wording recalls that God gave the blessing he wants enlarged, although he forgets its giver. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'that I increase' rather than 'he hopes for an increase' or 'he hopes to be increased' makes the reminder explicit. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:15/q05/p2 It reflects idolaters' failure to attribute provision to idols. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as another explanation for attributing the increase to God. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:15/q06 Why is his expectation censured as unreasonable?  [ثُمَّ يَطْمَعُ أَزِيدَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes cite prior abundance, ingratitude and disbelief, or active hostility as the reason the expectation fails.
- 74:15/q06/p1 He has already received an extraordinary abundance, leaving no reasonable prospect of more. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī say he has reached what is customarily the maximum; al-Zamakhsharī, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān, says no more can be added. Al-Ālūsī calls his existing wealth ample; al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī emphasize his greed. Muqātil says God had fully expanded his wealth. [7 notes: MUQATIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:15/q06/p2 He is ungrateful and disbelieves while asking the Benefactor for more. — Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī and Muqātil's 'despite disbelief'; al-Ṭabrisī says he failed to thank God; al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say his ingratitude makes him undeserving. Al-Biqāʿī says he denies God's messenger; Iṣlāḥī says the favors should have made him obedient. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL(الكلبي ومقاتل); TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:15/q06/p3 His knowing, obstinate opposition to God's signs makes continued favor especially incongruous. — Muqātil connects the hope with 74:16's obstinacy; Ibn Kathīr says his hostility was knowing disbelief in response to favors. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the next verse explains the rebuke because ingratitude became active opposition; Iṣlāḥī connects opposition to the Qur'an with severe penalty. [4 notes: MUQATIL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:15/q07 What does the following kallā do to his hope?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether kallā negates the preceding hope or opens an affirmative clause.
- 74:15/q07/p1 It rebukes him, commands him to desist, and cuts off any expected increase. — Al-Thaʿlabī says it connects to the preceding hope. Al-Jishumī compares ṣah and mah as commands to stop. Al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān reporting al-Zamakhsharī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr say it cancels or restrains his hope. Al-Ṭabrisī also reports the command-to-stop nuance; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a rebuke. [8 notes: THALABI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 74:15/q07/p2 It means 'indeed' and begins an affirmative statement. — Al-Thaʿlabī reports that it can be an oath meaning 'truly'; al-Jishumī reports 'indeed' as an alternative. Al-Itqān reports that affirmative uses of kallā cannot be paused at. [3 notes: THALABI(قيل); JISHUMI; ITQAN]

## 74:15/q08 How does the sense of kallā affect pausing and beginning?  [* · readings]
turns on: The pause depends on whether kallā rebukes what precedes or affirms what follows, and on the classified occurrence.
- 74:15/q08/p1 Where rebuke and affirmation are both possible, pausing as rebuke and beginning as 'indeed' are both allowed. — Al-Burhān places examples in a first class permitting both, listing twelve including 70:11, 70:39, 74:52, 75:10, 80:11, 83:13, 89:16 and 104:3. Al-Itqān reports the same principle and Makkī's eleven cases, where pausing as rebuke is preferred though beginning as 'indeed' is allowed. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN; ITQAN(مكي)+]
- 74:15/q08/p2 In the remaining class, one begins with kallā but does not pause before it. — Al-Burhān counts eighteen instances in fifteen late Qur'anic suras, including 74:32, 74:54, 75:20, 75:26, 78:4, 80:23, 82:9, 83:7, 83:15 and 89:21; al-Itqān also reports eighteen. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(مكي)]

## 74:15/q09 What happened to the increase he expected?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reports describe no new gain, a gradual loss, or eventual destitution.
- 74:15/q09/p1 He was denied further increase. — Al-Baghawī says God would give no more; Abū Mālik, in al-Durr, says no later child was born and his wealth did not increase. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the warning announces that provision will be cut. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو مالك); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:15/q09/p2 His wealth and children steadily declined until he died. — Mujāhid is reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr; Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Māwardī says they kept diminishing. Al-Baghawī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī report that decline began after revelation. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); TABATABAI(قيل)]
- 74:15/q09/p3 He became impoverished, begged, and died poor. — Muqātil says God cut off his provision and destroyed him among those marked for destruction. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 74:15/q10 What does the warning about this hope teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw lessons about gratitude, false confidence in status, and the public meaning of an opponent's prosperity.
- 74:15/q10/p1 Blessings call for gratitude and obedience; ingratitude makes their loss likely. — Al-Jishumī says plentiful gifts make disobedience graver and an increase may corrupt. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 14:7 and Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh's saying that gratitude binds blessings while ingratitude exposes them to loss. Kur'an Yolu grounds gratitude in reason and justice, calling denial, polytheism and injustice grave failures to thank God for life and material and spiritual gifts. [6 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL(الشيخ ابن عطاء الله); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 74:15/q10/p2 Wealth and rank cannot secure further divine favor or avert Judgment. — Iṣlāḥī says verses 11–17 warn of a severe Judgment Day for those presuming wealth and rank bring more; the affluent man faces severe penalty if he opposes the Qur'an, and Muḥammad should leave such leaders' fate to God. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:15/q10/p3 The Prophet is reassured that an opponent's prosperity does not prove defiance harmless. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the threat of lost provision prevents observers from taking prosperity as evidence against the afterlife; he compares Moses' prayer that Pharaoh's wealth be erased because it led people astray, 10:88. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:15/q11 Can an unbeliever receive God's favor?  [* · theology]
turns on: How 'God's favor' is used of worldly gifts to unbelievers.
- 74:15/q11/p1 Yes, an unbeliever may receive divine favor. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes this view to al-Māturīdī and the Muʿtazilites. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الماتريدي والمعتزلة)]
- 74:15/q11/p2 No, an unbeliever does not receive divine favor in this sense. — Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes the denial to al-Ashʿarī. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الأشعري)]
- 74:15/q11/p3 The disagreement is verbal. — Ibn ʿĀshūr follows careful scholars who treat the apparent disagreement as one of terminology. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 74:15/q12 What did al-Walīd say about Muḥammad when Quraysh sought a charge against him?  [* · reports]
- 74:15/q12/p1 He rejected several charges before recommending 'sorcerer' because the message divided families. — Ibn Hishām reports the pilgrimage-season consultation and his rejection of soothsayer, madman, poet and sorcerer as accurate descriptions before recommending the last as a public line. Al-Jishumī reports the same sequence, with 'magician' and the Qur'an's separation of families. [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(الوليد بن المغيرة); JISHUMI]

## 74:15/q13 How does the opponents' response bear on the Qur'an's inimitability?  [* · links]
- 74:15/q13/p1 Their resort to allegations and fighting instead of answering the challenge displays their inability to match it. — Al-Jishumī says vehement enemies failed the Qur'anic challenge and chose insults and combat. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ lists allegations of fabrication, ancient lore, and borrowed dictated writings, citing 68:10–15, 68:44–47, 25:4–10, 17:87–96, 6:7–9, 6:25–26 and 34:43–45, and says the Qur'an records and refutes them. Al-Bāqillānī contrasts attainable poetry with Qur'anic composition beyond imitators' ambition. [3 notes: JISHUMI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; BAQILLANI]

## 74:15/q14 What does the surrounding discussion say about human acts of reflection and expression?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether the notes address the moral quality of reflection or the agent of mental and facial acts.
- 74:15/q14/p1 Reflection can be morally bad when used to invalidate truth. — Al-Jishumī says this supports Abū Hāshim against Abū ʿAlī. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 74:15/q14/p2 Thinking and grimacing are acts of the human servant. — Al-Jishumī attributes both acts to the servant. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 74:15/q15 How does this passage relate to its sūra and the preceding sūra?  [* · links]
- 74:15/q15/p1 Al-Muddaththir corresponds to al-Muzzammil in theme and even in the sense of its name. — Iṣlāḥī calls the two sūras counterparts with essentially the same central themes. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:15/q16 What broader pattern is noted for Qur'anic kallā?  [* · sciences]
- 74:15/q16/p1 It occurs in the latter half of the Qur'an and did not descend in Yathrib. — Al-Dayrīnī's mnemonic, reported in al-Burhān, states both claims. Al-Burhān explains the concentration by many Meccan revelations addressing numerous tyrants with threats and reproaches, contrasting the weak and humiliated Jews associated with the first half. [2 notes: BURHAN(عبد العزيز الديريني)]

## 74:15/q17 What judicial conduct does ʿUmar's reported instruction require?  [* · law]
turns on: The reported instruction enumerates distinct duties of judges, not competing accounts of a single duty.
- 74:15/q17/p1 A judge must treat litigants equally in appearance, justice, and seating. — ʿUmar says this prevents the powerful expecting favoritism and the weak despairing of justice. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 74:15/q17/p2 Proof rests with the claimant, the denier swears, and settlements must preserve lawful limits. — ʿUmar allows settlement except where it makes forbidden things lawful or lawful things forbidden. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 74:15/q17/p3 A judge should revise an erroneous prior ruling after reconsideration. — ʿUmar says truth is old and returning to it is better than persisting in error. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 74:15/q17/p4 When text and Sunna do not settle a case, compare analogous cases. — ʿUmar says to identify which analogy is closest to truth. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 74:15/q17/p5 Give a claimant with absent proof a deadline and decide if proof does not arrive. — ʿUmar says this enforces rights when proof appears and removes doubt if it does not. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 74:15/q17/p6 Presume Muslims upright except for specified disqualifications and judge by outward evidence. — ʿUmar excepts a punished ḥadd offender, a proven false witness, and one suspect in clientage or lineage; God knows the hidden. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 74:15/q17/p7 Avoid anger, impatience, annoyance, and hostility when judging. — ʿUmar connects just decisions with divine reward and enduring merit. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]
- 74:15/q17/p8 Judge with sincere intent and self-discipline. — ʿUmar says God supports sincerity and disgraces a person who feigns qualities despite God's reward and mercy. [1 notes: BAQILLANI(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 74:15/q18 How are poetic descriptions evaluated in the cited literary discussion?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Different critical standards and examples underlie the evaluations.
- 74:15/q18/p1 Judge poets against peers of comparable rank, era, and standing. — Al-Bāqillānī says genre and quality are difficult for others but readily assessed by trained critics. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]
- 74:15/q18/p2 Al-Buḥturī's poetic surface excels those of Ibn al-Rūmī and contemporaries. — Al-Bāqillānī prefers its fine, smooth, sweet expression and relative lack of complexity. [1 notes: BAQILLANI+]
- 74:15/q18/p3 Abū al-Hawl al-Ḥimyarī's sword poem is the best among extended sword descriptions. — Al-Bāqillānī reports a literary authority's evaluation and observes that poets elaborate horse and sword descriptions. [1 notes: BAQILLANI]

## 74:15/q19 What makes composite similes more or less striking?  [* · rhetoric]
- 74:15/q19/p1 The less possible an image is to see, the stronger its novelty and aesthetic wonder. — Al-Jurjānī contrasts Dhū al-Rumma's relatively possible silver touched by gold with rarer pearls on blue cloth; impossible ruby banners on emerald spears are further from sight still. He divides images into impossible and rare possible, explaining different degrees of strangeness, delight and thrill. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 74:15/q20 How should the Qur'an's outward and inward meanings be approached?  [* · inward]
turns on: The notes differ on how many levels they enumerate and how they define their boundary and access point.
- 74:15/q20/p1 Master outward interpretation first, then seek inward understandings that preserve the linguistic sense. — Al-Itqān says surface interpretation alone cannot attain all Qur'anic knowledge, but transmitted outward tafsīr must precede inference; claiming secrets first is like reaching a house's center without its door. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh says inward readings do not displace language and cites the outward/inward report. Al-Ālūsī repeats the doorway analogy. [6 notes: ITQAN(تاج الدين ابن عطاء الله); ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:15/q20/p2 Qur'anic meanings are layered and inexhaustible beyond outward exegesis alone. — Ibn Masʿūd, in al-Itqān and al-Ālūsī, urges deep reading to gain earlier and later knowledge; Ibn ʿAbbās describes many subjects, inexhaustible wonders, outward recitation and inward interpretation, warning to approach gently. Al-Itqān and al-Ālūsī transmit the claim of sixty thousand understandings per verse. [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس,إبن مسعود)]
- 74:15/q20/p3 Each verse has an outward, inward, limit, and access point. — Al-Ālūsī cites al-Ḥasan's Prophetic report phrased for each verse and letter and another transmitted wording applying all four to each verse. Ibn al-Naqīb calls outward meanings apparent to exoteric scholars and inward meanings secrets disclosed to spiritual people; a letter's limit ends its intended meaning and its access point helps reach an obscure meaning or ruling. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(إبن النقيب,الحسن)]
- 74:15/q20/p4 The inward is divine speech perceived through sanctified spiritual perception. — Al-Ālūsī treats the outward as wording, conventional and signaled meanings from which five legal rulings are derived; the inward is the words' spirit beyond ordinary discursive instruments. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 74:15/q20/p5 The access point is an ascent from inward speech toward the divine Speaker's Name. — Al-Ālūsī relates al-Ṣādiq's saying that God manifests in His Book though servants do not see; one mystical account places the limit either between outward and inward or between inward and access, where a servant's attribute is effaced under theophany. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الصادق)]
- 74:15/q20/p6 The access point ascends from human understanding to witnessing God. — Another view reported by al-Ālūsī names outward tafsīr, inward taʾwīl, limit the reach of human understanding, and access the ascent beyond it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
