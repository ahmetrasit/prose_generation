# 79:23: 10 questions

## 79:23/q01 What does ḥashr mean here?  [فَحَشَرَ · meaning]
- 79:23/q01/p1 To muster or assemble a crowd. — Jishumi says it is gathering from every direction, calls the gatherer al-ḥāshir, and notes the Day of Gathering. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Arabic chiefly uses it for mustering a crowd amid pressure or hardship; she notes more than thirty Qurʾanic references to the Last Day and worldly instances involving Solomon's troops (27:17), the expelled People of the Book (59:2), David's birds (38:19), and Pharaoh and Moses (20:59, 7:111, 26:36, 26:53). [4 notes: JISHUMI; BINTSHATI]

## 79:23/q02 Whom did Pharaoh gather?  [فَحَشَرَ · referent]
turns on: Which episode this brief gathering recalls: a public proclamation, the contest with Moses, protection from the serpent, or pursuit of the Israelites; the use of 26:53 and 20:60 to identify this gathering is specifically disputed.
- 79:23/q02/p1 His people or subjects, sometimes specified as Copts, inhabitants of his kingdom, or people together with soldiers. — Muqātil specifies Copts; al-Ṭabarī says people and followers and reports Ibn Zayd's account of their assembling before the proclamation. Al-Naḥḥās cites Ibn Zayd for the understood object. Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī say they were gathered to attend. Al-Wāḥidī's Basīṭ, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, and Ibn al-Jawzī specify people and soldiers or troops; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says people of his kingdom. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies city inhabitants of every class. Al-Ālūsī also reports subjects as an alternative to troops. Study Quran attributes assembly of people for the sovereignty declaration to most commentators. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(most)]
- 79:23/q02/p2 The magicians gathered for their contest or plot. — Al-Rāzī links the gathering to al-Shuʿarāʾ (26:53). Bursawī says Pharaoh assembled the sorcerers and their devices and links this to 20:60. Al-Ālūsī calls this the most supported explanation, citing the sending of summoners (26:53) and assembly of plotters (20:60). Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports the same citations but argues that those passages do not establish that this gathering is the same event. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 79:23/q02/p3 The magicians together with soldiers, officials, or an army. — Al-Māwardī says the magicians were assembled for a contest and the army summoned to fight. Al-Qurṭubī likewise assigns the soldiers to battle and sorcerers to Moses' challenge. Al-Nasafī names magicians and troops; al-Biqāʿī says Pharaoh gathered magicians by choice or force and added soldiers; Abū Ḥayyān names magicians and officials. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 79:23/q02/p4 The magicians together with his people. — Al-Wāḥidī's Wajīz explicitly names both groups. [1 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 79:23/q02/p5 His troops or army and elite. — Al-Ālūsī reports the troops as one alternative. Elmalılı places the collection of the army and elite after the magicians' defeat, with collectors sent through the cities, and cites al-Shuʿarāʾ (26:53). [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 79:23/q02/p6 His companions, for protection against the serpent. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this motive; it corresponds to the account of Pharaoh fleeing the serpent in fear. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 79:23/q02/p7 His people summoned to pursue the Israelites. — Study Quran reports this as an alternative connected to 26:53. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 79:23/q03 Why are the verbs' objects left unstated?  [فَحَشَرَ فَنَادَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether context supplies the omitted people, only the actions matter, or the sense of ḥashara itself supplies a crowd.
- 79:23/q03/p1 The objects are understood from context: he gathered his people and called to them. — Al-Naḥḥās cites Ibn Zayd for the omitted object of ḥashara. Al-Samīn says both verbs omit their objects but can be supplied as people and the call to them. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the obvious object of ḥashara is the city's inhabitants from every class. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 79:23/q03/p2 The objects are omitted because the actions themselves are the point. — Al-Samīn gives this as the other explanation of both omissions. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 79:23/q03/p3 Ḥashara itself clearly implies a crowd, so no object needs stating. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ bases this on the word's explicit sense of assembling a crowd. [1 notes: BINTSHATI]

## 79:23/q04 In what order did the gathering and call occur?  [فَحَشَرَ فَنَادَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the two verbs give the event's actual chronology or a sequence whose real order Ibn ʿAbbās reverses.
- 79:23/q04/p1 He gathered the people and then called out to them. — Al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn Zayd's sequence: the crowd assembled, Pharaoh declared himself their highest lord, then God punished him in both lives (79:24–25). Al-Māwardī says the people attended before he addressed them. Ibn al-Jawzī explicitly puts the call after their assembly; al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān locate the call at the assembly. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 79:23/q04/p2 He called first, then gathered the people, despite the verse's wording order. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this explanation from Ibn ʿAbbās. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 79:23/q05 How and through whom did Pharaoh call out?  [فَنَادَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh personally voiced the proclamation or a herald voiced it for him; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ disputes textual support for the herald.
- 79:23/q05/p1 He addressed the assembled people himself. — Al-Rāzī, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī allow a personal address alongside a herald. Al-Nasafī places the call where those assembled met; Abū Ḥayyān says he addressed that assembly. Al-Qurṭubī adds that the call was loud. Ibn Kathīr locates the call among his people. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:23/q05/p2 A herald announced Pharaoh's words on his behalf. — Al-Rāzī, Bursawī, and al-Ālūsī give this as an alternative; al-Biqāʿī says the herald's words represented Pharaoh's own. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ rejects al-Zamakhsharī's suggestion that Pharaoh ordered a caller to announce his message because she says the text does not support it. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI(الزمخشري)-]
- 79:23/q05/p3 He called loudly. — Al-Qurṭubī explicitly explains the call as a loud voice. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 79:23/q06 What did Pharaoh proclaim?  [فَنَادَىٰ · interpretation]
- 79:23/q06/p1 He claimed to be his people's highest lord. — Al-Ṭabarī calls the claim a foolish lie and reports Ibn Zayd's account that God then seized Pharaoh in punishment for both lives (79:24–25). Ibn ʿAṭiyya also states the claim. Al-Rāzī places it at the gathering. Al-Samīn says the following fa-qāla explains the content of the call. Study Quran describes the announcement as a declaration of sovereignty. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; STUDYQURAN(most)]

## 79:23/q07 How does his prior turning away and striving lead into this gathering?  [* · links]
turns on: The preceding adbara and yasʿā may describe flight, scheming, the start of action, figurative rejection, literal angry departure, or an evasive pretext; al-Ālūsī tests the flight account against thumma and the timing of the magicians' gathering.
- 79:23/q07/p1 He fled the serpent in fright, then sought protection or acted rashly. — Al-Rāzī reports fear after seeing the serpent and al-Ḥasan's description of Pharaoh as reckless and quick-moving; Bursawī transmits al-Kashshāf's terrified flight and al-Ḥasan's rash, impulsive Pharaoh. Al-Qurṭubī reports gathering companions for protection. Al-Ālūsī objects that flight fits poorly after the customary gathering of magicians and, if earlier, leaves the delay indicated by thumma unclear; he also allows that the wording might emphasize the shock of the flight. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحسن,الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL-]
- 79:23/q07/p2 He turned from Moses and exerted himself to plot against him, including mustering magicians. — Al-Rāzī reports this reading. Al-Biqāʿī says the turning led him to gather magicians voluntarily or by force and add soldiers. Ibn ʿĀshūr reads the striving figuratively as strenuous efforts to prevent people hearing Moses and to gather magicians against what Pharaoh mistook for sorcery, citing 20:60. Bursawī says the effort came from obstinacy, not confidence that the sign could really be matched. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 79:23/q07/p3 Adbara means he began to act or proceed, with a hint of ruin. — Al-Rāzī compares adbara to aqbala yafʿalu and says adbara avoids describing Pharaoh as approaching. Al-Ālūsī allows an ironic use of 'began' that signals his ruin. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 79:23/q07/p4 Turning away figuratively means rejecting the invitation to faith. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Musaylima's rejection and quotes the Prophet's warning that God would have Musaylima killed if he turned away. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 79:23/q07/p5 He literally left in anger and moved quickly. — Ibn ʿĀshūr allows this reading of both preceding verbs as an angry, rejecting departure. Bursawī cites al-Rāghib's gloss of adbara as presenting one's back and explains 'striving' grammatically as a circumstantial qualifier of the subject, meaning he hurried and exerted himself. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(الراغب)]
- 79:23/q07/p6 His effort was a pretext to avoid the gathering or court. — Bursawī calls it a false pretext. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 79:23/q08 What does this action show about human agency?  [* · theology]
- 79:23/q08/p1 Human acts originate with the servants themselves. — Jishumi cites Pharaoh's transgression, denial, turning away, and calling out as evidence. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 79:23/q09 How does gathering here relate to the sūra's theme?  [* · links]
- 79:23/q09/p1 It echoes the final gathering and the sūra's establishment of resurrection and humanity's fate. — Al-Biqāʿī says the sūra establishes resurrection and the gathering, displays humanity's final fate after the preceding sūras' exposition, and uses oaths to mark the decisive climax. Jishumi connects ḥashr with the Day of Gathering; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the Qurʾan uses the word most often for the Last Day, in more than thirty places. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI; BINTSHATI]

## 79:23/q10 What inward reading is given to Pharaoh's conduct?  [* · inward]
- 79:23/q10/p1 He pursued satanic schemes and psychic tricks against Moses and became more veiled from the holy presence. — Al-Qāshānī says Pharaoh was expelled from the holy presence and his veil increased. [1 notes: QASHANI]
