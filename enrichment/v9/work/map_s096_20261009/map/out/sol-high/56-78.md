# 56:78: 9 questions

## 56:78/q01 What does kitāb mean, and why is the Qur'an called a book?  [كِتَٰبٍۢ · meaning]
- 56:78/q01/p1 Kitāb derives from writing or gathering; the Qur'an is a book because it gathers its contents in an ordered form. — Al-Zarkashī derives writing from gathering and says the Qur'an gathers stories, verses, rulings, and reports; he calls 'book' for a written object metaphorical and defines writing as ordered, meaningful marks on a medium, allowing that a writer may make meaningless marks. Al-Suyūṭī says it gathers sciences, stories, and reports with supreme eloquence. [3 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]

## 56:78/q02 What is the hidden book?  [كِتَٰبٍۢ مَّكْنُونٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Whether the book is a heavenly archetype, a human manuscript, earlier scripture, an inward locus, or a metaphor for the divine source; some reports allow more than one locus.
- 56:78/q02/p1 It is the heavenly book, specifically the Preserved Tablet. — Muqātil places it at God's right hand beside the Throne, hidden from creatures. Al-Ṭabarī, Mujāhid, Jābir ibn Zayd, Abū Nuhayk, Ibn ʿAbbās, Saʿīd ibn Jubayr, and Salmān give its heavenly location; Salmān says only angels touch it. Al-Zajjāj and the many named exegetes identify the Tablet; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Baghawī say it is guarded from devils. Al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite 85:21–22; al-Rāzī answers the objection that a tablet cannot be called a book by comparing names of receptacles. Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī restrict access to purified or near angels. Study Quran reports that it contains every revealed book; Kuran Yolu calls this a spiritual description of the Qur'an's exalted status, and al-Sāmarrāʾī invokes the angelic referent. Al-Ālūsī particularly favors this identification. [36 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(جابر بن زيد وأبي نهيك,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وجابر بن زيد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,جابر بن زيد وابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,سلمان الفارسي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس وغيره); TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN(Aj, Q, R, Sh, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin birçoğu)+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 56:78/q02/p2 It is the written Qur'an in people's hands, the Muslim codices. — Mujāhid and Qatāda are reported for this view. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say that, because codices did not yet exist, this would report the unseen; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says their protection lasts to Resurrection and touching may mean touching manuscripts or figuratively the angels. Al-Ālūsī likewise calls it foreknowledge and says the codices are guarded from alteration. Al-Biqāʿī includes purified, honored codices among several loci. Kashshāf derives a rule about handling the written copy from this reading. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); QUSHAYRI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 56:78/q02/p3 It is the Torah and Gospel, or the earlier revealed scriptures as a class. — ʿIkrima says the Torah and Gospel mention the Qur'an and its messenger; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān say this makes the Qur'an's honor attested by earlier revelation, with Ibn ʿAṭiyya citing 9:36. Al-Rāzī includes other earlier scriptures. Al-Ālūsī reports ʿIkrima through ʿAbd ibn Ḥumayd and Ibn Jarīr, and says 'book' must then be generic; his quotation of al-Baḥr makes the Qur'an's honor and nobility the content mentioned there. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس ومجاهد); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(البحر,عكرمة)]
- 56:78/q02/p4 It is the Psalms. — Al-Māwardī reports this as another view; al-Qurṭubī attributes it to al-Suddī. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(السدي)]
- 56:78/q02/p5 It is the Prophet's heart, where Qur'anic intellect is lodged beyond the senses. — Al-Qāshānī describes the Qur'an as exalted, ancient, honorable comprehensive knowledge; only purified angels reach what is deposited in the Prophet's heart. He quotes Jesus that knowledge is placed in hearts and manifests through spiritual manners before God, rather than coming down from heaven, rising from earth, or crossing seas. [2 notes: QASHANI(عيسى عليه السلام)]
- 56:78/q02/p6 It is the first Spirit, locus of decree and abode of the Muhammadan Spirit. — Al-Qāshānī offers this as one inward alternative. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 56:78/q02/p7 It is the Muhammadan Spirit itself. — Al-Qāshānī offers this as the other inward alternative. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 56:78/q02/p8 It is the heart of the believer. — Al-Ālūsī reports this anonymous suggestion and dismisses it. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 56:78/q02/p9 It figuratively names the Qur'an's correspondence with God's concealed knowledge, will, and command. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the wording and meanings correspond to what God knew and willed and to the command conveyed to the angel. He also calls 'book' a metaphor for fixed, unalterable reality, and cites a poetic fragment for this use. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 56:78/q02/p10 It resides together in the Preserved Tablet, honored hearts, and purified codices. — Al-Biqāʿī describes these as concurrent locations of the precious, fixed, protected Qur'an, safe from alteration. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 56:78/q03 What does maknūn say about the book?  [مَّكْنُونٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the guarding is against damage or falsehood, concealment from sight, concealment of meanings, figurative exaltation, or protection specifically from alteration.
- 56:78/q03/p1 It is guarded or preserved from harm, falsehood, alteration, and corrupting access. — Al-Ṭabarī and Mujāhid specify dust and earthly contact; al-Thaʿlabī mentions devils and stains. Al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid's 'preserved' and Yaʿqūb from Mujāhid's protection from falsehood; al-Qurṭubī gives both protection with God and protection against falsehood. Al-Rāzī calls the protection complete, explaining that precious things are hidden, and distinguishes the written Qur'an's protection from alteration and corrupting hands. Al-Biqāʿī places it in the Tablet, hearts and codices; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 85:21–22 against alteration or replacement. Al-Ālūsī reports a narrower alteration-only reading citing 15:9, but favors earlier explanations. [20 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد,يعقوب عن مجاهد); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 56:78/q03/p2 It is hidden from creatures' sight and access, apart from selected or purified angels. — Muqātil says hidden from creation; Ibn al-Jawzī transmits him. Al-Rāzī says the Tablet is literally concealed except to chosen angels and uses 56:79 against the ancient-lore charge. Kashshāf, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī mention near or purified angels. Al-Qāshānī locates the inward knowledge beyond the senses. Study Quran reports that concealment excludes any influence except God's. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الربيع بن أنس وغيره); STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 56:78/q03/p3 Its meanings are concealed within it. — Al-Māwardī reports this further possibility. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 56:78/q03/p4 Concealment figuratively expresses its lofty rank. — Al-Ālūsī says this is possible if the Torah and Gospel are meant, because what is exalted is concealed. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 56:78/q03/p5 It means only that the book is protected from alteration. — Al-Ālūsī reports this explanation with 15:9 but prefers the earlier explanations, especially the Preserved Tablet. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL-]

## 56:78/q04 How does 'in a hidden book' relate to the preceding description of the Qur'an?  [فِى كِتَٰبٍۢ مَّكْنُونٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The attachment of the prepositional phrase and whether 'in' expresses literal location or figurative correspondence.
- 56:78/q04/p1 The Qur'an itself is described as being in a book. — Al-Rāzī identifies that book as the Preserved Tablet and prefers this reading as most strongly honoring the heavenly recited text, while allowing all readings. Al-Ālūsī calls the adjectival construal more eloquent. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL+]
- 56:78/q04/p2 The phrase qualifies 'noble': the Qur'an is noble in the book even if disbelievers deny its nobility. — Al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī give this syntactic reading; al-Ālūsī favors the adjectival construal instead. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 56:78/q04/p3 The whole description 'noble Qur'an' is the content written in the book. — Al-Rāzī compares the statement about ʿIlliyyūn in 83:19. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 56:78/q04/p4 The locative 'in' figuratively expresses correspondence with the divine original. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the containment honorific metaphor: the heard Qur'an's wording and meanings match God's knowledge, will, power and divinely made eloquent discourse. He prefers this to rational-metonymic attribution of the counterpart and cites 6:59, 35:11, and 87:18–19. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 56:78/q04/p5 The Qur'an's description is rational-metonymically attributed to its counterpart. — Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions this analysis only to prefer the figurative locative instead. [0 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL-]

## 56:78/q05 What status does the hidden book give the Qur'an?  [فِى كِتَٰبٍۢ مَّكْنُونٍۢ · interpretation]
- 56:78/q05/p1 It attests the Qur'an's exalted honor, divine source, and secure preservation. — Kashshāf calls it excellent in kind, beneficial, or honored by God. Ibn Kathīr says both Qur'an and book are exalted, guarded and honored. Al-Biqāʿī says preciousness explains its fixation and protection across Tablet, hearts and codices. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the guarded tablet, exalted wise source and protected book to enduring realities behind the meanings. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Prophet's recitation matches the miraculous discourse God willed, confirming divine speech rather than human composition. Iṣlāḥī treats 75–96 and the oath as proclaiming exalted revelation shielded from Satan, jinn and devils, accessible to pure near angels; Quraysh risk doom by ignoring it. Kuran Yolu calls the Tablet description spiritual exaltation. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Müfessirlerin birçoğu)]

## 56:78/q06 Which charges against the Qur'an does this wording answer?  [فِى كِتَٰبٍۢ مَّكْنُونٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Which reported accusation the book's concealment and protection is invoked to rebut.
- 56:78/q06/p1 It rebuts the charge that jinn or devils supplied the revelation. — Al-Rāzī says the angel received it from a book before delivering it, so it is not even angelic speech, much less jinn speech. Iṣlāḥī says only pure near angels access the protected book. Study Quran says its concealment excludes influence by anyone but God. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 56:78/q06/p2 It rebuts the charge that the Qur'an is ancient lore taken from open books. — Al-Rāzī contrasts those books with the concealed book touched only by the purified, citing 56:79. Study Quran reports the ancient-fables accusations and cites 6:25, 8:31, 16:24, 23:83, 25:5, 27:68, 46:17, 68:15 and 83:13. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]

## 56:78/q07 Does this description imply the Qur'an is originated or ancient?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether descriptions of a revealed discourse in a guarded book imply origination, or describe an ancient inward knowledge.
- 56:78/q07/p1 It is originated rather than eternal. — Al-Jishumī says the descriptions discourse, revelation, guarded book and untouched fit neither literal nor figurative eternity. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 56:78/q07/p2 It is ancient, honorable comprehensive knowledge. — Al-Qāshānī describes that knowledge as lodged in the Prophet's heart beyond sense perception, except to purified angels. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 56:78/q08 What rule about touching the Qur'an is drawn if the book is the written copy?  [* · law]
- 56:78/q08/p1 Only someone ritually pure should touch the written Qur'an; some extend this to recitation. — Kashshāf draws the rule conditionally from taking the protected book as the written Qur'an. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes that touch can refer to manuscripts or figuratively to angels. In Salmān's report the heavenly book is touched only by angels, after which he recited to his visitors whatever Qur'an they requested. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(سلمان الفارسي)]

## 56:78/q09 What claims about abrogation accompany citations of this guarded book?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The direct disagreement concerns whether naskh can mean copying; the other reported principles give the surrounding abrogation discussion.
- 56:78/q09/p1 The preserved record of revelation supports naskh in the sense of copying or transcription. — Ibn Barakāt al-Saʿdī and al-Saʿīdī support al-Naḥḥās with records of deeds, the Mother Book, and this guarded book; their cited verses include 43:4, 45:29 and 56:78. Al-Suyūṭī also lists transcription among the lexical senses of naskh. Makkī rejects this Qur'anic sense because an abrogating passage uses different words. [3 notes: BURHAN(أبو عبد الله محمد بن بركات السعدي); ITQAN(السعيدي); ITQAN(مكي)-]
- 56:78/q09/p2 Naskh may mean transfer, as with inheritance moving to another person. — Al-Zarkashī gives inheritance; al-Suyūṭī additionally lists removal, replacement, transformation and transcription, citing 22:52 and 16:101. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 56:78/q09/p3 Copying is not a Qur'anic sense of naskh. — Makkī argues that an abrogating passage does not copy the abrogated wording but has different wording. [1 notes: ITQAN(مكي)+]
- 56:78/q09/p4 An interpreter must know abrogating and abrogated passages. — Al-Zarkashī and al-Suyūṭī relay the imams' requirement. Their reports of ʿAlī vary between a preacher and a judge: when he lacked knowledge of abrogation, ʿAlī said he was doomed and would doom others. [4 notes: BURHAN(علي بن أبي طالب); ITQAN(الأئمة,علي)]
- 56:78/q09/p5 Many scholars wrote dedicated works on abrogation. — Al-Zarkashī names Qatāda, Abū ʿUbayd, Abū Dāwūd, al-Naḥḥās and Ibn al-Jawzī among others; al-Suyūṭī also names Ibn al-Anbārī, Makkī and Ibn al-ʿArabī. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 56:78/q09/p6 Abrogation is a facilitative dispensation for this community, specifying a ruling's duration. — Al-Suyūṭī says Muslims accept it; he rejects the Jewish equation with changed divine knowledge and compares changing conditions of life, health and wealth, citing 2:106. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 56:78/q09/p7 Only the Qur'an can abrogate the Qur'an. — Some scholars cite 2:106's better or equal replacement and argue that only the Qur'an can match or surpass it. [1 notes: ITQAN(قوم من العلماء)]
