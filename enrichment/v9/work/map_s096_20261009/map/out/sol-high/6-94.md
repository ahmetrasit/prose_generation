# 6:94: 37 questions

## 6:94/q01 When and to whom is this address spoken?  [وَلَقَدْ جِئْتُمُونَا · referent]
turns on: Whether it is spoken at death or resurrection, and whether God or angels voice the rebuke.
- 6:94/q01/p1 God addresses the resurrected people at judgment. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya allows judgment day; al-Ṭabrisī reports this view. Bayḍāwī and Nasafī specify reckoning and recompense; Qurṭubī calls it resurrection. Al-Ālūsī answers that divine speech to unbelievers can be angry rather than beneficial, despite 2:174. [18 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); IBNASHUR-FULL(الراجز); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 6:94/q01/p2 Angels speak the rebuke on God's behalf. — Rāzī prefers continuation of the angels' words in 6:93 because of the conjunction; their duties are soul taking or punishment. Ṭabrisī says angels convey God's words at death. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows coordination with the angel scene while God remains the referent of nā. Ālūsī finds the angel reading far-fetched. [7 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الإمام); IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 6:94/q01/p3 The words may be said when souls leave bodies, before resurrection. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ṭabrisī allow death; Ibn ʿĀshūr says past form then anticipates the near future as in the prayer call. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:94/q02 Why is 'you have come' in the past tense?  [جِئْتُمُونَا · rhetoric]
turns on: A completed event in the future scene versus a certain future cast as past.
- 6:94/q02/p1 The certain future is portrayed as already past. — Māwardī, Wāḥidī, Samīn and Bursawī cite the certitude of the event; Bursawī compares 16:1. Ibn ʿĀshūr says qad reinforces it, with the death scene making it near. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:94/q02/p2 It quotes what they will be told after arrival, so their coming is already past. — Māwardī and Wāḥidī give the afterlife perspective; Ibn ʿĀshūr says qad confirms completion if God voices the scene. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:94/q03 What does coming to God mean here?  [جِئْتُمُونَا · interpretation]
- 6:94/q03/p1 They stand before God for reckoning and recompense. — Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Bursawī spell out the judgment; Biqāʿī sees death displaying God's power and knowledge. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 6:94/q04 What does furādā mean for their arrival?  [فُرَٰدَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether solitude is simple separation, loss of worldly company, loss of alleged helpers, or an inward stripping.
- 6:94/q04/p1 They arrive individually, cut off from company, possessions and worldly support. — Mujāhid/Ḥasan gloss one by one; Ibn ʿAbbās lists family, wealth, children and even deeds sent ahead. Zajjāj, Ibn Kaysān and others specify former partners in error, allies and worshipped beings. The prophetic report adds that each is occupied with his own affair. Study Quran compares 19:95, 22:2, 80:32–37. [59 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI(ابن كيسان,الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس,ابن كيسان,الحسن); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي,الحسن,الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة,ابن عباس,ابن كيسان,الحسن,الزجاج,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن كيسان,الحسن,الزجاج,قيل,مقاتل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(عكرمة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:94/q04/p2 In an inward sense, the servant arrives stripped of works, states, attachments or individual traits. — Sulamī reports highest station as insolvency and Abū Ḥafṣ's offering of poverty to the Rich. Qāshānī says absorption in unity removes traits and personal existence; Bursawī describes tajrīd and tafrīd as detachment from one or both worlds. [6 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص,بعضهم); QASHANI; BURSEVI(القشيري)]

## 6:94/q05 What is the form and derivation of furādā?  [فُرَٰدَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Which singular or collective analysis accounts for the plural.
- 6:94/q05/p1 It is a plural of fard, meaning a single person. — Farrāʾ lists fard and farīd; Ibn Qutayba compares kaslā/sukarā, Ṭabarī cites al-Nābigha, an anonymous paired line and Yūnus's tuʾam/tuʾām. Samīn also records the feminine alif and a collective-noun objection; Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses this differs from distributive furād. [36 notes: FARRA(بعضهم); MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(النابغة الذبياني,شاعر,يونس الجرمي); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء,الليث); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مقبل); TABRISI(النابغة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(الفراء); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 6:94/q05/p2 It is the broken plural of fardān. — Ibn Qutayba compares sukkārā from sukkārān and kusālā from kaslān; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this the stronger derivation. Other authors list it among possible singulars. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن قتيبة,الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن قتيبة,الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:94/q05/p3 It is plural of farīd. — An anonymous grammarian compares radīf/ridāfā; Rāghib and others compare asīr/asārā. Ṭabarī records related forms. [10 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(يونس الجرمي); WAHIDI-BASIT(غيره); RAZI-FULL(الفراء,غيره); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(الراغب,الفراء)]
- 6:94/q05/p4 It can be treated as a collective expression meaning one by one. — Samīn notes that some deny a regular plural from fard. Farrāʾ compares the indeclinable distributive forms thulāth and rubāʿ; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes the verse's form from that distributive form. [4 notes: FARRA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:94/q06 How is furādā read in variant forms?  [فُرَٰدَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Tanwīn, indeclinable furād/firād, and shortened fardā.
- 6:94/q06/p1 Furādā is the usual indeclinable form. — Naḥḥās ties its nondeclension to the feminine alif; Farrāʾ compares thulāth and rubāʿ. Samīn calls it circumstantial accusative. [7 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:94/q06/p2 Fardā/firdī without the long medial alif is read. — Al-Aʿraj, and in one report Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr, use a feminine collective form like sukrā; Samīn compares 22:2. [7 notes: THALABI(الأعرج); BAGHAWI-FULL(الأعرج); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعرج); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(خارجة عن نافع وأبي عمرو); ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:94/q06/p3 Furād or firād is read without tanwīn. — Aḥmad ibn Yaḥyā compares thulāth/rubāʿ; Samīn calls it an indeclinable shifted adjective, while Ālūsī notes the pattern need not be numeral-only. [6 notes: NAHHAS(أبو حيوة); QURTUBI-FULL(أحمد بن يحيى); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:94/q06/p4 Furādan is read with tanwīn. — Abū Ḥaywa and ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar transmit the Tamīm form; Abū al-Baqāʾ calls the declined plural rare, like tuʾām or rikhāl. Qurṭubī says Tamīm does not use it in the nominative. [6 notes: NAHHAS(أبو حيوة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو حيوة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو حيوة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,عيسى بن عمر وأبو حيوة)]

## 6:94/q07 What does 'as We created you the first time' compare?  [كَمَا خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it evokes naked birth, initial helpless solitude, resurrection itself, or primordial spiritual creation.
- 6:94/q07/p1 They return as at birth: naked, barefoot, uncircumcised, and without possessions. — Zajjāj's reported gloss defines ghurl; Saʿīd ibn Jubayr and scholars say removed parts are restored. The Prophet's reports describe the same state, and ʿĀʾisha asks whether the sexes will see one another. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares the resurrection to witnessed first creation in 50:15. [27 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(جاء في التفسير); NAHHAS; THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي عليه السلام); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(العلماء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,سعيد بن جبير); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 6:94/q07/p2 They return in the solitude and helplessness of first creation. — Jubbāʾī specifies the womb, without defender; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Biqāʿī stress loss of ties and status. Iṣlāḥī notes that God first develops humans from lack of ability and later tests gratitude. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]
- 6:94/q07/p3 The comparison affirms that God recreates people as He first created them. — Zajjāj calls this linguistically possible; Ibn al-Jawzī says they return alive, and Ibn Kathīr cites 18:48 against resurrection-denial. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:94/q07/p4 In an inward reading, the first creation is primordial identity or the spirit before embodiment. — Qāshānī connects it to the primordial covenant; Bursawī links the spirit's prebody state to 23:14 and 7:11. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 6:94/q08 How does kamā khalaqnākum attach grammatically?  [كَمَا خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Circumstance, substitute/apposition, adjective to an omitted verbal noun, or absolute object.
- 6:94/q08/p1 It is a circumstance describing the arriving people or their solitary state. — Samīn prefers direct circumstance because the verb indicates its verbal noun most strongly; Abū al-Baqāʾ makes it a circumstance to the implicit subject of furādā, which Samīn qualifies as resemblance to their state. [9 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:94/q08/p2 It is an appositive or substitute for furādā. — Grammarians who disallow multiple circumstances use this; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī also list it. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 6:94/q08/p3 It describes an omitted verbal noun, a coming or singleness like the first state. — Makkī supplies a singleness comparison; Samīn reports and ranks it below the direct circumstance. Ibn ʿĀshūr instead makes the kāf an absolute object and mā verbal-noun-forming. [6 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:94/q09 What does awwala marratin do grammatically?  [أَوَّلَ مَرَّةٍۢ · grammar]
- 6:94/q09/p1 It is a time adverb governed by khalaqnākum. — Samīn says marra was a verbal noun then broadened to 'time'; Abū al-Baqāʾ notes its verb-like behavior. Abū Ḥayyān and the Shaykh reject 'first creation' as it would suggest a second creation rather than return or recreation. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء,الشيخ)]

## 6:94/q10 What does the report about ʿĀʾisha and the resurrected say?  [كَمَا خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ · reports]
- 6:94/q10/p1 People are gathered naked, but each is too occupied with personal concerns to look at the other sex. — Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Thaʿlabī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, Durr, Bursawī and Ālūsī transmit the question and answer. Qurṭubī calls it sound and says Muslim relates its meaning; Ālūsī says al-Ḥākim authenticated it. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عائشة); THALABI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله,عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة); BURSEVI(عائشة رضي الله عنها والرسول صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(عائشة)]

## 6:94/q11 What creation report is associated with this context?  [كَمَا خَلَقْنَٰكُمْ · reports]
- 6:94/q11/p1 Al-Ṣādiq reports Adam's clay formed from two handfuls, with the right destined for prophets and believers and the left for tyrants, polytheists, hypocrites and the wretched. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ transmits the report without a stated grade. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله)]

## 6:94/q12 What are the things God granted that were left behind?  [مَّا خَوَّلْنَٰكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Worldly goods alone or also inward attainments.
- 6:94/q12/p1 They are worldly wealth and blessings, including property, servants, children, estates and livestock. — Ṭabarī and Suddī cite wealth and servants; Ibn ʿAbbās adds comforts, estates and livestock; Thaʿlabī and Baghawī add children. Bayḍāwī says not even a tiny amount is carried away. Biqāʿī says the gifts were meant for seeking God's pleasure. [42 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 6:94/q12/p2 An inward reading includes worldly means, knowledge and virtues among what is left. — Qāshānī views all acquired means as attachments at absorption in divine unity; Bursawī similarly treats gifts as worldly attachments on the seeker's journey. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 6:94/q13 What does khawwalnākum mean in Arabic?  [خَوَّلْنَٰكُمْ · meaning]
turns on: Basic grant/ownership versus proposed original sense of dependents or gratuitous gift.
- 6:94/q13/p1 God gave, granted ownership, or empowered people with goods. — Ṭabarī, Māwardī, Wāḥidī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Samīn cite Abū al-Najm; Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya also cite Abū ʿAmr's recitation of Zuhayr. Jishumī notes a property steward; Rāghib offers caretaking responsibility. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the word broadened to all useful gifts. [26 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(أبو النجم,أبو عمرو بن العلاء); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو النجم); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو بن العلاء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو النجم,الراغب); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 6:94/q13/p2 Its root sense is making someone owner of dependents or retainers. — Ṭabrisī compares tamwīl for owning wealth; Jishumī gives khawl as followers and a gift without recompense; Bursawī lists ownership of retainers or a gratuitous grant. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports servants as the proposed original sense. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الراغب); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(قيل)]
- 6:94/q13/p3 It can denote regular attention, as in the Prophet's admonition of people. — Jishumī reports the hadith yatakhawwal and Abū ʿAmr's correction to yataḥawwal, meaning choosing receptive times. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 6:94/q14 How is 'you left what We granted behind your backs' constructed?  [وَتَرَكْتُم مَّا خَوَّلْنَٰكُمْ وَرَآءَ ظُهُورِكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Coordination versus circumstance, and one-object leave versus two-object make.
- 6:94/q14/p1 Wa-taraktum is coordinated with jiʾtumūnā, and taraka means leave with one object. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the clause further explains furādā; Samīn says mā is the object and khawwala itself takes two objects. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 6:94/q14/p2 Wa-taraktum is a circumstantial clause, with implicit qad. — Samīn and Ālūsī also allow an independent clause; Abū al-Baqāʾ makes abandonment the point of censure. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 6:94/q14/p3 Taraktum can mean 'made,' taking the gift and locative as two objects. — Samīn gives this alternative, yielding that they made the gift something behind their backs; in the leave reading warāʾa attaches to taraktum. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:94/q15 What does 'behind your backs' convey?  [وَرَآءَ ظُهُورِكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 6:94/q15/p1 It pictures possessions remaining in worldly life as the person departs. — Suddī glosses the location as this world. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts what is left behind one's back with what one has prepared ahead; Ibn Kathīr quotes an authentic report that only consumed food, worn clothing and charity sent ahead are one's own. [11 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 6:94/q16 What lesson does the abandoned wealth carry?  [وَتَرَكْتُم مَّا خَوَّلْنَٰكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether wealth was wasted, sent ahead through charity, or inherently a distracting false support.
- 6:94/q16/p1 Wealth and status distracted people from God and the afterlife and now cause regret. — Ṭabarī calls the verse a rebuke of pride; Ibn al-Jawzī says life's acquisitions vanished. Rāzī links the body's proper work to knowledge and virtue and describes regret, shame and despair. Biqāʿī calls gifts a test; Iṣlāḥī says humans were given abilities to test gratitude or arrogance. [20 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(السعدي,القشيري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:94/q16/p2 Unused wealth is left to others, while charity and good action are sent ahead. — Rāzī cites 2:110; Ibn Kathīr cites the authentic hadith on consumption, clothing and charity. Ḥasan's young-goat report asks what a person amassed and sent ahead, finding nothing. Ṭabrisī urges obedience that benefits after death. [14 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحسن البصري,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 6:94/q17 Who are the absent supposed intercessors?  [شُفَعَآءَكُمُ · referent]
turns on: Idols, angels, or inward causes and attachments.
- 6:94/q17/p1 They are idols or deities worshipped as advocates before God. — Ṭabarī, Suddī, Thaʿlabī, Ṭabrisī and later authors give this. Muqātil links the claim to 10:18; Ibn ʿĀshūr adds 39:3. Study Quran notes that their power of intercession is merely claimed, with 2:48 and 2:255. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,بعضهم); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); NAHHAS; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 6:94/q17/p2 They are angels whose intercession the idolaters expected. — Muqātil identifies angels; Mawardī and Ibn al-Jawzī report him. Abū Ḥayyān cites the claim that they worshipped them only for nearness to God, 39:3. [5 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 6:94/q17/p3 In an inward reading, they are causes, beloved objects, deeds and states mistakenly credited with influence. — Qāshānī lists means, causes and idols; Bursawī treats deeds and states as supports left behind at the journey's end. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 6:94/q18 How is 'we do not see your intercessors with you' parsed?  [وَمَا نَرَىٰ مَعَكُمْ شُفَعَآءَكُمُ · grammar]
turns on: Visual perception with one object versus knowledge with two; status of maʿakum.
- 6:94/q18/p1 Narā means visual seeing and takes one object; maʿakum attaches to the verb. — Samīn prefers this apparent sense, and Abū al-Baqāʾ calls it a portrayal of the present scene. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 6:94/q18/p2 Narā may mean know, taking a second locative object. — Samīn reports the analysis; Abū al-Baqāʾ finds 'we do not know them with you' unsuitable. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]
- 6:94/q18/p3 Maʿakum can be circumstantial to the intercessors. — Abū al-Baqāʾ rejects it as implying present but unseen figures; Samīn replies that negating a qualified thing can deny the thing itself or just the qualification and points to 2:273. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)]

## 6:94/q19 Why say 'we do not see your intercessors'?  [وَمَا نَرَىٰ مَعَكُمْ شُفَعَآءَكُمُ · rhetoric]
- 6:94/q19/p1 The question taunts and rebukes idolaters for absent supposed helpers. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the present tense pretends the intercessors might yet appear, recalling 16:27; the later clause removes that hope. Biqāʿī depicts terror and humiliation; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says it exposes idolatry's error. [9 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 6:94/q20 What did they claim about the intercessors being partners 'among you'?  [ٱلَّذِينَ زَعَمْتُمْ أَنَّهُمْ فِيكُمْ شُرَكَآؤُا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Creation, lordship/worship, intercession, or sharing burdens and punishment.
- 6:94/q20/p1 The objects were alleged partners in God's lordship over people and entitlement to worship. — Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, Ālūsī and others give both aspects; Ibn Kathīr stresses their supposed share in worship. Ibn ʿĀshūr says fronting fīkum spotlights the strange claim about humans God created. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(السدي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; BURSEVI+]
- 6:94/q20/p2 They were alleged partners with God in creating the people. — Ibn Qutayba gives this reading; Samīn says the claim covers both creation and worship; Ṭabāṭabāʾ says they were treated as creative partners. [6 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والمفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن قتيبة); SAMIN-DURR; TABATABAI]
- 6:94/q20/p3 They were partners as intercessors on the people's behalf. — Kalbī and others connect 'partners' directly with their hoped intercession; Abū Ḥayyān says fīkum can mark their service over the people or their rescue from punishment. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 6:94/q20/p4 They were expected to share or bear the people's burdens or punishment. — Māwardī compares partners carrying one another's loads; Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn mention sharing punishment or calamities. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:94/q21 How does fīkum function in the claim?  [فِيكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Attachment to shurakāʾ, or its sense as 'with/among/in your estimation'.
- 6:94/q21/p1 Fīkum attaches to shurakāʾ, locating the alleged partnership concerning the people. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn make the syntactic attachment explicit; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes its fronting as rhetorical emphasis. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:94/q21/p2 It can mean 'among you' or 'in your estimation'; a proposal takes fī as 'with'. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the first gloss. Samīn regards the 'with' proposal as unnecessary. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR]

## 6:94/q22 What does zaʿamtum imply?  [زَعَمْتُمْ · meaning]
turns on: A generally neutral report of a claim versus a false claim here.
- 6:94/q22/p1 It marks their false assertion in this context, whether mistaken or deliberately deceitful. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares false uses at 4:60 and 6:22; Ālūsī says the broader verb can still be true in other contexts. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:94/q22/p2 In Arabic, zaʿm can report something true or false. — Ṭabrisī gives a poetic example of a true claim; Ālūsī repeats the contextual distinction. [2 notes: TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 6:94/q23 How is the hamza in shurakāʾ written and explained?  [شُرَكَآؤُا۟ · readings]
turns on: Which alif survives the orthographic deletion.
- 6:94/q23/p1 The final hamza is written on wāw, with one account retaining the second alif. — Ibn al-Jazarī lists eight uncontested words, including this shurakāʾ, with hamza on wāw. The first alif is extra, both are silent, and a sound change already affected the second. His replies distinguish added letters used for expansion, ordinary silent-letter changes, and spelling from sound change. [3 notes: NASHR]
- 6:94/q23/p2 An opposing account retains the first alif and deletes the second. — The first conveys meaning and is word-final; the second drops in connected speech and creates a disliked identical sequence. Ibn al-Jazarī reports replies against these grounds. [2 notes: NASHR(غيره)]

## 6:94/q24 What hamza and pause variants are counted for the related form?  [شُرَكَآؤُا۟ · readings]
- 6:94/q24/p1 Final hamza with kasra or ḍamma admits five forms, or nine for non-Hijazi readers, with further combinations by initial hamza, pause, transfer and rounded wāw. — Ibn al-Jazarī counts eighteen and twenty-seven combinations in his examples, and twelve in a ḍamma case. [2 notes: NASHR]

## 6:94/q30 How is the final mā kuntum tazʿumūn constructed?  [مَّا كُنتُمْ تَزْعُمُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Relative/descriptive mā with omitted objects versus verbal-noun mā.
- 6:94/q30/p1 Mā is relative or descriptive, with omitted objects of tazʿumūn recovered as idols and their intercession. — Samīn says both objects are elided for concision, citing a poem on omitted object of taḥsab; Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr recover them from context. Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers mā referring to useless idols. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 6:94/q30/p2 Mā is verbal-noun-forming, referring to their claim itself. — Samīn says the second object still needs supplying; Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya and others on this reading but prefers the idols' loss of way. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية وغيره)]

## 6:94/q31 What remains with a person at death and in the grave?  [فُرَٰدَىٰ وَتَرَكْتُم مَّا خَوَّلْنَٰكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Deeds and knowledge can accompany the believer, while worldly companions fail.
- 6:94/q31/p1 Knowledge of God, righteous deeds and taqwā accompany the believer, unlike worldly possessions. — Rāzī says sound knowledge and virtue remain in grave and resurrection. Bursawī names testimony, prayer, fasting and remembrance as four grave companions, and reports good deeds protecting and brightening the grave while evil deeds torment. Saʿdī's verses say grief and joy pass but deeds and good name remain. A Yemeni grave vision presents a black dog as the person's deeds and Yā Sīn with companion sūras as protection. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(السعدي,اليافعي); STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 6:94/q32 How do inward readers describe the end of the journey?  [لَقَد تَّقَطَّعَ بَيْنَكُمْ وَضَلَّ عَنكُم · inward]
turns on: Whether perceived independent entities vanish or the seeker passes beyond all supports.
- 6:94/q32/p1 At unity, independent entities and causal supports are seen to have no separate influence. — Qāshānī says supposed entities vanish when everything is witnessed in God. Bursawī traces tajrīd, tafrīd and tawḥīd to divine majesty; Gabriel stopped at the Lote Tree, and primordial care alone lets the seeker pass the known station of 37:164. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI]

## 6:94/q33 What does the return to God reveal about power and dependence?  [* · theology]
- 6:94/q33/p1 God alone governs people; worldly causes have no independent efficacy, and death exposes this. — Ṭabāṭabāʾ says apparent causes act only by God's measure and that bodily separation removes material supports. The next verses explain the causes as God's managed creations. Qāshānī's inward reading likewise sees claimed separate entities vanish. Iṣlāḥī says abilities were grants testing gratitude. [10 notes: TABATABAI; QASHANI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:94/q34 What conduct does the verse urge?  [* · interpretation]
- 6:94/q34/p1 Prepare for judgment through gratitude, obedience and righteous action rather than relying on wealth or imagined helpers. — Ibrāhīm ibn Adham lists disobedience despite knowing God, neglect of Sunna and Qur'an, ingratitude, failure to prepare for death, and fixation on others' faults as reasons prayer goes unanswered. Elmalılı urges imagining the reckoning and avoiding injustice. Ṭabrisī urges obedience that saves. [8 notes: JISHUMI(إبراهيم بن أدهم); TABRISI; ELMALILI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:94/q35 What occasion or audience is reported for the verse?  [وَلَقَدْ جِئْتُمُونَا شُفَعَآءَكُمُ · reports]
turns on: Whether al-Naḍr's saying is a specific occasion or an example of a general challenge.
- 6:94/q35/p1 Al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith claimed al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā would intercede for him, prompting the verse. — ʿIkrima's report recurs in Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Durr, Suyūṭī, and Ālūsī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes no grade. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows mockery or ignorance but denies a discrete occasion. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(عكرمة); IBNABIHATIM(النضر); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI(عكرمة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النضر بن الحارث); DURR-FULL(عكرمة); SUYUTI-LUBAB(عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNASHUR-FULL(النضر بن الحارث)]
- 6:94/q35/p2 The rebuke concerns idol worshippers generally. — Ṭabarī reports all idol worshippers' claim; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says most Arabs sought nearness through idols, excluding those recognizing no other deity. Bursawī names Quraysh and their boast of wealth and children; Iṣlāḥī notes the shift from People of the Book to Meccan idolaters. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL(النضر بن الحارث)]

## 6:94/q36 How does 6:94 connect with its surrounding passage and sūra?  [* · links]
- 6:94/q36/p1 Other verses echo solitary return, broken bonds and idols' failure. — Qurṭubī links 7:29–30 to 6:94; Baghawī and Abū Ḥayyān cite 2:166; Ibn Kathīr lists 2:166–167, 23:101, 29:25, 28:64, 6:22–24 and 26:92–93. Study Quran compares 19:95 and scenes of false powers disowning followers. [8 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 6:94/q36/p2 It makes judgment vivid after the death scene, closing a monotheism discussion before the sūra turns to God's created signs. — Biqāʿī says the previous passage answers deniers of the dead's awareness and shows angels seizing them; he links the opening angels to final judgment. Ibn ʿĀshūr coordinates this with 6:93 and calls it a rebuke of resurrection denial. Iṣlāḥī sees the next section arguing from the world for monotheism, prophethood and hereafter, within the sūra's recurring themes. [8 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 6:94/q37 What does the related discussion say about Qur'anic bal?  [* · rhetoric]
- 6:94/q37/p1 Bal may retract and correct, as well as mark a transition. — Burhān rejects al-Basīṭ's and Ibn Mālik's claim that Qur'anic bal only marks transition. [1 notes: BURHAN]
