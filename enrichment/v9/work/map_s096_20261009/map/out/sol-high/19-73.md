# 19:73: 18 questions

## 19:73/q01 What does the recitation of God's signs convey?  [تُتْلَىٰ ءَايَٰتُنَا · meaning]
- 19:73/q01/p1 The Qur'an is recited to the hearers, conveying God's warnings, promises, and proofs. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Wajīz identify the signs with the Qur'an and what God explains in it. Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses tilāwa as recitation and says the Prophet recited warnings and promises; he compares 2:102 and 8:2. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:73/q02 How is تُتْلَىٰ read, and why?  [تُتْلَىٰ · readings]
turns on: Whether the plural āyāt calls for feminine agreement or permits a masculine verb because its femininity is figurative and the verb is separated from it.
- 19:73/q02/p1 The majority read feminine tutlā, agreeing with āyāt. — Abū Ḥayyān says believers recited the Qur'an to unbelievers and praised the Prophet's signs; al-Samīn contrasts this reading with the masculine one. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة والأعرج وابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيوة والأعرج وابن محيصن)]
- 19:73/q02/p2 Abū Ḥaywa, al-Aʿraj, and Ibn Muḥayṣin read masculine yutlā. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya records the lower yāʾ; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn name the readers. Al-Ālūsī explains that āyāt is metaphorically feminine and separated from the verb. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأعرج وابن محيصن وأبو حيوة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو حيوة والأعرج وابن محيصن); SAMIN-DURR(أبو حيوة والأعرج وابن محيصن); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيوة، الأعرج، ابن محيصن)]

## 19:73/q03 To whom does عَلَيْهِمْ refer?  [عَلَيْهِمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun includes all hearers before the disbelievers are singled out, or is restricted by the immediate reply.
- 19:73/q03/p1 It refers broadly to people, both believers and disbelievers. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the following explicit mention of those who disbelieve replaces or specifies the broad pronoun. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]
- 19:73/q03/p2 The recitation reaches the disbelievers who answer it with a boast. — Al-Ālūsī identifies the signs heard as including the preceding verses, which exposed their condition and outcome; al-Qurṭubī links the disbelievers with those in 19:66 and 19:72. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 19:73/q04 In what sense are the signs بَيِّنَٰتٍ, and what does the adjective do?  [بَيِّنَٰتٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether clarity is located chiefly in expression, explanatory context, miraculousness, or proof, and whether the adjective adds information or emphasizes an inherent quality.
- 19:73/q04/p1 Their wording, compact meanings, and purposes are clear; difficult signs are explained by decisive signs or by the Prophet. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī give these verbal and explanatory senses; al-Bayḍāwī includes both wording and meaning. Al-Ālūsī includes the preceding verses. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 19:73/q04/p2 The signs are manifest miracles that opponents could not match. — Al-Rāzī cites the Qur'anic challenge and opponents' failure; al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Ālūsī and Bursawī include miraculousness. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 19:73/q04/p3 They are plain, compelling proofs that leave no real objection. — Al-Rāzī cites the resurrection proof in 19:67; al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr speak of evident arguments and persuasive evidence. Al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī and al-Biqāʿī also include proofs. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:73/q04/p4 The adjective emphatically states the inherent clarity of God's signs. — Al-Kashshāf favors an emphatic circumstantial description; al-Qurṭubī favors it and compares 2:91. Al-Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān accept this force; al-Biqāʿī allows an emphatic or explanatory status. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Kashshāf's preference despite the clause lacking two nominal parts. [6 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشاف); KASHSHAF-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 19:73/q05 Who are the disbelievers and believers in this scene?  [ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the historical speakers and hearers are foregrounded or the description extends to opposing kinds of people.
- 19:73/q05/p1 The disbelievers are Quraysh polytheists, especially al-Naḍr ibn al-Ḥārith and his associates; the believers are the Prophet's poor Companions. — Mujāhid names Quraysh and the Companions; Muqātil gives al-Naḍr's fuller name, ibn al-Ḥārith ibn ʿAlqama. Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī identify al-Naḍr; al-Jishumī and Abū Ḥayyān report revelation about him. The accounts vary between naming him and naming Quraysh generally. Qatāda and others emphasize the Companions' rough living. [24 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(قتادة,مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 19:73/q05/p2 The wording identifies rejecting people generally, especially hardened leaders, and believing people generally. — Al-Qurṭubī links the disbelievers with those mentioned in 19:66 and 19:72. Al-Ālūsī says the relative phrase marks their rejection, while allowing the specific leaders. Asad reads the parties as two kinds of society distinguished by faith and morality. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 19:73/q06 What does faith in ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ entail?  [ءَامَنُوٓا۟ · meaning]
- 19:73/q06/p1 Linguistically faith is assent; religiously it includes verbal profession, inward belief and calm assurance, with bodily works following these. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the linguistic and religious distinctions. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 19:73/q07 What does the lām in لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ mean?  [لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the lām governs the audience of their speech or its cause and subject; al-Ālūsī appeals to the two-party comparison.
- 19:73/q07/p1 It marks the addressees: the unbelievers speak directly to believers. — Al-Kashshāf, al-Samīn, Bursawī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allow this. Al-Ālūsī prefers it because the question compares the two parties. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 19:73/q07/p2 It expresses cause or concern: they speak about believers or for the purpose of misleading them, possibly among themselves. — Al-Kashshāf compares 46:11; Ibn ʿĀshūr says this may be a private remark among polytheists. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says it may express the purpose of misleading believers; Bursawī and al-Samīn permit the causal sense. Al-Ālūsī regards it as less apt because both parties are compared. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 19:73/q08 Which two parties does ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ designate?  [ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the comparison is confined to the immediate historical parties or read as a general social contrast.
- 19:73/q08/p1 The two parties are the disbelievers themselves and the believers. — Yaḥyā, al-Nasafī, al-Ālūsī and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī make the implicit us-versus-you comparison explicit; ʿAbd al-Razzāq transmits Qatāda's comparison of the groups' places and gatherings. Al-Burhān notes the paired comparatives khayr and aḥsan. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BURHAN; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 19:73/q08/p2 The two parties also exemplify societies with contrasting approaches to faith and morality. — Asad understands them as kinds of human society, one governed by expediency and one by moral obligations. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 19:73/q09 How does the question أَىُّ ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ function?  [أَىُّ ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ خَيْرٌۭ أَحْسَنُ · rhetoric]
- 19:73/q09/p1 It is a confirmatory, boastful question expecting the answer that the disbelievers are superior. — Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it confirmatory; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says they feign a question while asserting superior dwellings and gatherings. Al-Burhān treats khayr and aḥsan as comparatives, with parallels at 3:118, 16:95, 18:46 and 19:75. Al-Itqān explains ayy as asking which of two parties sharing a general feature excels in the named quality. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; BURHAN; ITQAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 19:73/q10 How is مَّقَامًۭا read?  [مَّقَامًۭا · readings]
turns on: Vowel on the first mīm, with parallels in 44:51 and 33:13; sources differ over how much the vowels change the sense.
- 19:73/q10/p1 Most readers give the mīm fatḥa, maqāman, a form that can indicate standing, its place, or a home. — Ibn Mujāhid names Abū ʿAmr, Abū Bakr, Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī with fatḥa throughout comparable passages, while Nāfiʿ and Ibn ʿĀmir use ḍamma only at 44:51 and Ḥafṣ differs at 33:13; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and others give variant lists. The cited comparisons are 44:51 and 33:13. Al-Rāzī takes the open form as place of standing; al-Qurṭubī as a home or place for important affairs, hence prestige and supporters. Al-Fārisī says both vowels here denote a dwelling place, supported by 19:74. [20 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر عن عاصم,أبي,حفص عن عاصم,نافع وابن عامر); TABRISI(ابن كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وأبو بكر وحفص عن عاصم); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); NASHR(ابن كثير); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير، ابن محيصن، حميد، الجعفي، أبو حاتم عن أبي عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 19:73/q10/p2 Ibn Kathīr and some others give the mīm ḍamma, muqāman, generally read as residence or its place. — Ibn Mujāhid, al-Nashr and others name Ibn Kathīr; al-Thaʿlabī names Meccan readers. Al-Qurṭubī adds Ibn Muḥayṣin, Ḥumayd and Shibl ibn ʿAbbād; al-Samīn reports Abū ʿAmr; al-Ālūsī adds al-Juʿfī and Abū Ḥātim from Abū ʿAmr. Al-Biqāʿī instead describes this as a place of standing or assembly. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes it as better worldly life. Both vowels can yield a place or verbal noun according to al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān. [21 notes: IBNMUJAHID; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI(أهل مكة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن كثير); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); TABRISI(ابن كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير); RAZI-FULL(ابن كثير); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن كثير وابن محيصن وحميد وشبل بن عباد); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(ابن كثير); NASHR(ابن كثير); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كثير، ابن محيصن، حميد، الجعفي، أبو حاتم عن أبي عمرو); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن كثير)]

## 19:73/q11 What does مَّقَامًۭا mean in the boast?  [مَّقَامًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the noun points to a home and its comfort, a standing place or social rank, or an eschatological or disputational setting; some distinguish its vowels.
- 19:73/q11/p1 It is a dwelling, residence, or home, with the comfort and prosperity attached to it. — Muqātil compares a noble residence and 44:26; Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Kathīr mention home, blessing and pleasure, with Pharaoh's prosperity as parallel. Al-Fārisī argues both readings here denote residence because 19:74 compares furnishings and outward appearance; Ḥassān's ruins attach to a place. A Kufa poem and 35:35 illustrate lasting residence. Al-Fārisī distinguishes a secure dwelling at 44:51 and possible residence at 33:13; al-Bayḍāwī permits standing or residing. Ibn ʿĀshūr interprets the ḍamma reading as good worldly life. [31 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN]
- 19:73/q11/p2 It is a standing place, seat, assembly scene, or social station and prestige. — Al-Farrāʾ gives assembly; al-Akhfash offers a seat or scene, comparing 27:39 and a poem about where a person stands. Al-Fārisī distinguishes Abraham's station, 27:39, an assembly, a rajaz line on Ribāḥ's feet, and a poem where maqāmāt means people of gathering places by omitted ahl; al-Ṭabrisī reports the latter. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the open form metaphorically as rank and prominence, comparing 55:46. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says grandeur and status enrich the home sense. [15 notes: FARRA; FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); TABRISI(أبو علي); MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 19:73/q11/p3 It can point to one's abode in Paradise or Hell, to the place of judgment, or to whose case prevails through obedience or fails through sin. — Al-Māwardī reports these distinct possible settings and the disputational interpretation; he also reports Thaʿlab's distinction between ḍamma for residence and fatḥa for assembly. The afterlife-abode, judgment-place, and disputed-case alternatives are separately preserved here. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 19:73/q11/p4 The form's standing or residence sense varies with vowel and context; here the two readings can converge on place. — Al-Fārisī says qāma and aqāma can produce action or place nouns; al-Sukkarī transmits al-Aṣmaʿī's distinction between maqāma as assembly and maqām as dwelling, also reported by al-Ṭabrisī. Al-Rāghib's examples at 17:79, 5:107, 37:164, 2:125 and 27:39 show standing or its circumstance, not merely residence. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(السكري); TABRISI(السكري); BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الراغب); SAMIN-DURR]

## 19:73/q12 How is مَّقَامًۭا formed and parsed here?  [مَّقَامًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether vowel variation permits the same action or place functions or encodes a more exact distinction between standing and established residence.
- 19:73/q12/p1 Either vowel may produce a place noun or verbal noun from qāma or aqāma; in this context the referent is a place or position. — Al-Fārisī adduces muj rāhā and mursāhā at 11:41 as verbal noun analogies and says maqām can name the action or place of residence. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn allow both noun functions; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says context chooses place. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Baḥr's verbal-noun allowance, while he himself concludes a specification of which party's position is better. [10 notes: FARISI-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير,نافع وابن عامر); TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(البحر)]
- 19:73/q12/p2 A sharper distinction makes open maqām any standing place and rounded muqām an externally established or lasting residence; quadriliteral verbal nouns require ḍamma. — Al-Ālūsī reports Abū al-Suʿūd's distinction between original self-standing and a necessary station for a volitional being, and holds that a specifically externally established or enduring quadriliteral place takes ḍamma. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود)]

## 19:73/q13 What does نَدِيًّۭا denote?  [نَدِيًّۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the noun names the gathering itself, its visible prosperity and company, or an afterlife gathering.
- 19:73/q13/p1 It is a council, assembly, or meeting place where people gather, sometimes specifically for consultation. — Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss assembly or council; Qatāda compares 96:17, and others compare 29:29, including Lot's assembly. Al-Ṭabarī explains nadawtu as gathering people; al-Wāḥidī says a place bears the name only with people present and cites Kathīr's unādīka. Ḥātim's and Salāma ibn Jandal's poems attest nadī and plural andiyah; Ibn Qutayba, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Jishumī, al-Qurṭubī and al-Rāzī explain Dār al-Nadwa as Quraysh's consultation house. Nadī and nādī are dialectal or equivalent forms according to al-Farrāʾ and others. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes nadī occurs once in the Qur'an, nādī twice at 96:17–18, and cites al-Bukhārī, al-Jawharī and al-Rāghib; reports of Ibn ʿAbbās' wording vary, adding meeting place or a place to lean. Al-Samīn derives nadī from root-final wāw; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes the calling and gathering root. Al-Baḥr specifies discussion or consultation; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows this narrower sense. [55 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; FARRA; MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,قتادة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(كثير); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(السكري); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(حاتم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(ابن عباس,البخاري,قتادة); ITQAN]
- 19:73/q13/p2 It can emphasize outward appearance, social display, broad comfortable life, prosperity, or pleasure in the gathering. — Ibn ʿAbbās is reported by al-Qurṭubī to add outward appearance and by Ibn Kathīr to add prosperity and pleasure. Al-Māwardī offers spacious life; Bint al-Shāṭiʾ stresses grandeur, status and abundance as objects of boasting. Al-Samīn and al-Jishumī mention a possible association with generous company, illustrated by Ḥātim's poem. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR(حاتم); JISHUMI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 19:73/q13/p3 The social setting could be imagined in Paradise or Hell, alongside the corresponding residence. — Al-Māwardī reports this alternative to a comparison of the current assemblies. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 19:73/q14 What is the grammatical role of مَّقَامًۭا and نَدِيًّۭا?  [مَّقَامًۭا نَدِيًّۭا · grammar]
- 19:73/q14/p1 Both are accusatives of specification after the comparatives. — Al-Naḥḥās, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān identify tamyīz; al-Jishumī explicitly applies it to nadiyyan. Al-Ālūsī concludes maqāman is tamyīz, excluding an adverbial or verbal-noun parsing; al-Ṭabrisī also notes that a nearby kam has an omitted counted generation supplied by context. [6 notes: NAHHAS; JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 19:73/q15 What happened when the disbelievers made this comparison?  [قَالَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ · reports]
turns on: Different reported actions in the social confrontation: a display and taunt, or a demand to exclude poor believers.
- 19:73/q15/p1 Well-dressed, groomed and perfumed Quraysh leaders, sometimes named as al-Naḍr and his companions, taunted poorly dressed and hard-living Muslims. — Muqātil notes fine clothes and oiled hair; Qatāda reports the Companions' hardship, rough dress and the boast, citing 96:17. Al-Baghawī adds silk; al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī add perfume. Al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī repeat the contrast. Some reports name al-Naḍr, others Quraysh generally. Ibn Kathīr says Mujāhid and al-Ḍaḥḥāk agree with Qatāda. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI; JISHUMI(غير مسمى); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 19:73/q15/p2 Some wealthy polytheists demanded the Prophet expel poor followers before they would listen. — Kuryolu connects this demand with 6:52–53; Ibn ʿĀshūr mentions the demand when explaining class-based rejection. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 19:73/q16 What claim did the disbelievers make from their worldly advantages?  [أَىُّ ٱلْفَرِيقَيْنِ خَيْرٌۭ مَّقَامًۭا وَأَحْسَنُ نَدِيًّۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the boast is used to prove present religious merit alone or also predict an advantaged final outcome.
- 19:73/q16/p1 Their better homes, wealth, dress, status and assemblies supposedly prove that God favors them and that their religion is true. — Al-Ṭabarī describes comfort and larger gatherings; al-Rāzī reports the claim that a wise God would not impoverish loyal believers while favoring deniers, comparing 46:11. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Qurṭubī, al-Jishumī, al-Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Ibn ʿĀshūr describe the inference. Ibn Kathīr compares 46:11, 26:111 and 6:53 and mentions believers' hidden meeting places; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 6:52–53. Kuryolu says Quraysh thought a good message should reach them first; Asad describes an expediency-based social argument that moral restraints hinder power. ## 19:73/q17 How is that claim about wealth and divine favor answered?  [* · theology]
- 19:73/q17/p1 Present wealth, leadership and outward success do not establish truth, divine approval, or safety at the Resurrection. — Al-Jishumī cites destroyed nations and says worldly leadership cannot help at resurrection. Al-Qurṭubī notes poor unbelievers and rich Muslims, and says God protects His friends from worldly delusion. Al-Biqāʿī points to God's power to remove prosperity; al-Ālūsī calls the inference foolish and says the next verse refutes it. Al-Bayḍāwī describes the answer and threat; al-Qushayrī says rejection rests on conjecture and that delayed judgment can make disbelievers mistake well-being for safety before the decree arrives. Asad says moral restraint limits selfish power yet avoids destructive social tensions and yields lasting well-being. Kuryolu notes that prophets' earliest followers are often poor and weak while wealthy opponents oppress them. ## 19:73/q18 How does this verse connect with its context and other cited passages?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the connection being drawn is immediate context and reversal, parallel class or council passages, or the lexical contrast in the next verse.
- 19:73/q18/p1 It follows clear proof and warnings with a worldly boast, then the following verses settle or reverse the comparison. — Al-Qurṭubī links the speakers to 19:66 and 19:72; Abū Ḥayyān links the taunt to the preceding resurrection proof. Al-Jishumī reports al-Aṣamm: recitation increases believers in guidance. Al-Ālūsī says the preceding verses exposed the disbelievers' state and outcome; al-Bayḍāwī says the sequel answers and threatens. Al-Ṭabarī says the contest over dwelling and council will be settled, and Ibn Kathīr says the later gathering recalls this boast. Al-Qushayrī links them with earlier deniers and their deserved end. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI(الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 19:73/q18/p2 The boast echoes other class-based objections to believers and other examples of councils or worldly prosperity. — Al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr compare 46:11; Ibn ʿĀshūr and Kuryolu compare 6:52–53. Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr relate dwelling and assembly to Pharaoh's former prosperity at 44:25–26 and Lot's people's meeting at 29:29; Qatāda and others compare 96:17. Bursawī also cites 2:247; al-Burhān compares uses of the paired comparative at 3:118, 16:95, 18:46 and 19:75. - 19:73/q18/p3 The next verse's contrast includes furnishings and visible appearance, whose transmitted readings and derivations explain the worldly boast. — Al-Fārisī uses the following comparison of furnishings and appearance to support the residential sense of maqām.
