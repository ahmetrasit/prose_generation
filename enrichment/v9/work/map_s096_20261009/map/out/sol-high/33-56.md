# 33:56: 33 questions

## 33:56/q01 What force do the opening and present tense have?  [إِنَّ يُصَلُّونَ · rhetoric]
- 33:56/q01/p1 They mark importance, continuity and repeated action. — Al-Alusi says inna answers an implicit question about extraordinary honor; Ibn ʿAshur and Bursevi infer renewed angelic prayer and invite repeated imitation. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 33:56/q02 What does God's ṣalāt mean?  [يُصَلُّونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether divine prayer is praise, mercy, forgiveness, a blessing, or a distinct honor.
- 33:56/q02/p1 Mercy, bounty and blessing. — Ibn Qutayba adduces verses pairing divine prayer with mercy. Al-Mawardi calls mercy most apparent; Ibn ʿAshur describes God's speech decreeing good. Kuran Yolu says the reality is unknowable but its effect opens eyes and hearts to truth. [25 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(ابن عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(سفيان الثوري وغير واحد من أهل العلم); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ); ALUSI-FULL(الماوردي)+]
- 33:56/q02/p2 Praise and exaltation before the angels. — Abu al-ʿAliya specifies heavenly praise; al-Alusi describes raised mention, visible religion and preserved law in this world, then intercession, reward, the Praised Station and precedence in the next. [9 notes: MUJAHID(أبو العالية); AKHFASH; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو العالية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية)]
- 33:56/q02/p3 Forgiveness. — Muqatil, al-Suddi and Saʿid ibn Jubayr distinguish God's forgiveness from angelic requests for forgiveness; Ibn Abbas says God does not pray as creatures do. [6 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 33:56/q02/p4 Blessing the Prophet. — Ibn Abbas gives a compact gloss of the shared verb. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 33:56/q02/p5 Special purification and added honor. — ʿAtaʾ mentions divine holiness and mercy preceding wrath; Abu ʿAbdillah specifies purification in the highest heavens; others distinguish the Prophet's special honor from general mercy, citing 2:157. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عطاء بن أبي رباح); TABRISI(أبو بصير); QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عطاء بن أبي رباح); BURSEVI(بعضهم)]

## 33:56/q03 What is the lexical range or root of ṣalāt?  [يُصَلُّونَ صَلُّوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Competing etymologies and the relation of supplication to ritual prayer.
- 33:56/q03/p1 Human ṣalāt chiefly means supplication, and in law formal prayer. — Jishumi distinguishes the lexical and ritual senses; Ibn ʿAshur adds good speech; Kuran Yolu describes formal prayer as presenting oneself to God. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(القاموس); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 33:56/q03/p2 It relates to fire or roasting, with a distinct tasliya form. — Kuran Yolu explains turning toward God and away toward fire; Bursevi and Ibn ʿAshur distinguish the verbal noun from the burning sense. [2 notes: BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:56/q03/p3 Its root suggests connection, inclination or favor. — Biqaʿi proposes connection; Tabatabai and authorities cited by al-Alusi propose turning or favor, disputing literal divine inclination. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 33:56/q04 What does the angels' ṣalāt mean?  [وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتَهُۥ يُصَلُّونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the act is characterized by its prayerful form or its elevating effect.
- 33:56/q04/p1 Supplication, especially for mercy and forgiveness. — Abu al-ʿAliya distinguishes this from God's praise; other authorities specify forgiveness or mercy. Ibn ʿAshur says angels and people supplicate while God decrees good. [24 notes: MUJAHID(أبو العالية); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العالية,الحسن,سعيد بن جبير); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو العالية,ابن عباس,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية,سفيان الثوري وغير واحد من أهل العلم); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, Ṭ)]
- 33:56/q04/p2 Blessing, praise, elevation and help. — Ibn ʿAtaʾ calls it elevation; Ibn Abbas calls it blessing; Biqaʿi describes their connection through revelation; Kuran Yolu adds congratulations. [12 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 33:56/q05 What common sense can the verb carry for God and angels?  [ٱللَّهَ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتَهُۥ يُصَلُّونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether semantic overlap, metaphor or ellipsis permits one plural verb.
- 33:56/q05/p1 Its senses vary with the agent. — Razi says literal supplication cannot apply to God. Jishumi lists God's mercy and praise, angelic supplication, prophetic intercession and communal prayer. Bursevi warns that one homonym cannot literally bear two senses at once. [6 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(القهستاني); ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:56/q05/p2 It shares a core of care, honor, favor or connection. — Al-Zarkashi prefers care and glorification; Biqaʿi proposes connection; Tabatabai and al-Alusi report inclination or favor. Critics object to literal inclination of God; defenders distinguish divine reality from bodily experience. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة,جمع من المحققين); TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; BURHAN+]
- 33:56/q05/p3 Ellipsis or distributed agents permit different senses. — Al-Alusi records an omitted divine or angelic verb, or a plural behaving as distinct predicates. Sadr al-Shariʿa proposes one literal 'supplication for good,' but al-Alusi doubts it. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(صدر الشريعة)]

## 33:56/q06 Who are 'His angels'?  [وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase initially points to one angelic class or the entire host.
- 33:56/q06/p1 All angels, initially including the subtle-bodied class. — Bursevi distinguishes embodied angels who prostrated to Adam from absorbed disembodied angels, but extends this verse to all. Biqaʿi describes their purity and nearness; al-Alusi says the annexation makes the reference comprehensive. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 33:56/q07 How is 'His angels' read and parsed?  [وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: The case ending and syntactic relation to inna.
- 33:56/q07/p1 Nominative, by syntactic coordination or an omitted predicate. — Attributed to Ibn Abbas and Abu Amr. Kufans except al-Farraʾ coordinate with the prior position; Basrans and al-Farraʾ supply a predicate. Analogies include 5:69, 2:62 and poetry. Ibn ʿAtiyya questions the reading. [15 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(البصريون); THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(ابن عباس); BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وعبدالوارث عن أبي عمرو,البصريون والفراء,الفراء,الكوفيون غير الفراء)]
- 33:56/q07/p2 Accusative, coordinated with the noun governed by inna. — The common or majority reading. [5 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(قراءة العامة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]

## 33:56/q08 Does yuṣallūna jointly refer to God and angels?  [يُصَلُّونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether reverence or different meanings require an omitted predicate.
- 33:56/q08/p1 A predicate is elided and the plural belongs to angels. — Abu Ali separates them out of reverence; others separate because their prayers have different senses. The rebuke to a preacher supplies an analogy. [8 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي,النبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(رسول الله)]
- 33:56/q08/p2 Yes, the plural covers both. — Ibn ʿAtiyya and al-Alusi say this may honor angels without diminishing God; the preacher's dual-pronoun rebuke is explained by his pause or by use across clauses. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,فرقة); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL+]

## 33:56/q09 Why are God and angels named in this order and joined?  [ٱللَّهَ وَمَلَٰٓئِكَتَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 33:56/q09/p1 God's precedence and the joint predication magnify the Prophet. — Razi contrasts 33:43 and makes God's prayer foundational; Biqaʿi says a joint statement sounds grander. Sibawayh and Ibn al-Saʾigh place the more important first for glorification. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن الصائغ,سيبويه)]

## 33:56/q10 Why is Muhammad called 'the Prophet' here?  [ٱلنَّبِىِّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the choice chiefly marks dignity or invokes a rank contrast.
- 33:56/q10/p1 The title and definite article mark exceptional dignity. — Al-Alusi says God avoids his personal name and makes him the preeminent bearer of the title. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:56/q10/p2 The title links to the prior 'Messenger of God' and highlights entitlement even at a lower rank. — Some scholars reason from the prevalent ranking of messengership over prophethood; al-ʿIzz ibn ʿAbd al-Salam disagrees. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة)]

## 33:56/q11 Who is addressed as a believer?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the Prophet is included in a general address concerning himself.
- 33:56/q11/p1 All believers, potentially including the Prophet. — Some usul authorities include him in a general address; reports show him blessing himself. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 33:56/q11/p2 Believers other than the Prophet in this context. — Al-Alusi says contextual indicators exclude him here, although he normally favors inclusion absent such an indicator. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 33:56/q12 What does believers' ṣalāt upon him mean?  [صَلُّوا۟ عَلَيْهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command primarily prescribes spoken supplication or also practical devotion.
- 33:56/q12/p1 Ask God to bless, honor, forgive or show mercy to him. — The reports vary in the requested gift. Al-Alusi says asking God acknowledges believers' inability to honor the Prophet adequately; Razi says the act expresses reverence. [28 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(أبو العباس ثعلب); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 33:56/q12/p2 Love, follow, accept and embody his guidance. — Ibn ʿAtaʾ and Qashani give inward readings; Biqaʿi includes following, praise and obedience; Bursevi says repetition manifests love. [7 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); QASHANI; BURSEVI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Su)]

## 33:56/q13 What wording did the Prophet teach for ṣalāt?  [صَلُّوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Whether the taught form is exclusive, ideal, or illustrative.
- 33:56/q13/p1 The Abrahamic formula asks God to bless Muhammad and his family as Abraham and his family. — Reports vary by including wives, descendants, household, servant and messenger, and 'us with them.' Ibn Kathir calls the corpus numerous and mass-transmitted, while grading individual routes differently. [67 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(كعب بن عجرة); TAB-FULL(إبراهيم,ابن عباس,الحسن,طلحة,عبد الرحمن بن بشر بن مسعود الأنصاري,قتادة,كعب بن عجرة); IBNABIHATIM(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,عبد الرحمن بن أبي ليلى); THALABI(أبو حميد الساعدي,أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو مسعود الأنصاري,كعب بن عجرة); MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن أبي ليلى); WAHIDI-BASIT(كعب بن عجرة); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو حميد الساعي,كعب بن عجرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(كعب بن عجرة); RAZI-FULL(النبي عليه السلام); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو حميد الساعدي,أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو مسعود الأنصاري,أبو مسعود البدري,ابن عباس,بريدة,كعب بن عجرة); BIQAI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري وكعب بن عجزة); DURR-FULL(أبو حميد الساعدي,أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو مسعود الأنصاري,أبو مسعود عقبة بن عمرو,أبو هريرة,إبراهيم,ابن عباس,رجل من أصحاب النبي,طلحة بن عبيد الله,عبد الرحمن بن أبي كثير بن أبي مسعود الأنصاري,علي,غير مذكور,كعب بن عجرة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حميد الساعدي,أبو سعيد الخدري,أبو هريرة,ابن بريدة,كعب بن عجرة); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو حميد الساعدي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Resûlullah); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 33:56/q13/p2 A longer invocation asks for mercy, blessings and the Praised Station. — Ibn Masʿud urges beauty because a prayer may be presented to the Prophet. Ali's extended version has an unknown transmitter who did not meet Ali, according to al-Mizzi. [6 notes: THALABI(عبد الله); TABRISI(عبد الله بن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الحافظ أبو الحجاج المزي,عبد الله بن عمرو أو عمر,عبد الله بن مسعود,علي)]
- 33:56/q13/p3 The minimal explicit request 'O God, bless Muhammad' suffices. — Al-Alusi says every taught formula contains this; Ibn Hajar accepts an intended supplication, while al-Nishaburi requires addressing God and rejects a first-person assertion. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر,النيسابوري)]
- 33:56/q13/p4 Other eloquent formulas clearly expressing his honor may suffice. — A group of Companions and later scholars allow variations; al-Alusi says several transmitted forms can be combined and a request is better than a bare declaration. [8 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض أئمة الحنابلة); BURSEVI(السخاوي,حديث); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 33:56/q14 What did the Companions seek when they asked how to pray?  [صَلُّوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Whether the question concerned manner alone or also meaning.
- 33:56/q14/p1 They asked for its manner and suitable form. — Al-Baji, al-Qurtubi and al-Alusi favor this: they knew greeting but needed to know how believers should honor one already honored by God and angels. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الباجي); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 33:56/q14/p2 They sought meaning as well as a fixed textual formula. — Al-Alusi records an alternative in which they wanted transmitted instruction rather than analogy. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الباجي)]

## 33:56/q15 Why should believers pray for one already honored by God?  [صَلُّوا۟ · interpretation]
- 33:56/q15/p1 It rewards and purifies believers who cannot repay the Prophet's due. — Razi says he does not need human prayer and God rewards believers out of mercy; he also prays for the community in 9:103. Al-Wasiti and others say the Prophet's due exceeds repayment. [11 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي,عبد الواحد السياري); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(الواسطي); ALUSI-FULL(أبو بكر,بعض الأجلة); STUDYQURAN(K,al-Ālūsī)]
- 33:56/q15/p2 It increases his rank and thereby the community's. — Bursevi cites Rumi: the follower's rank depends on the rank of the one followed. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 33:56/q16 How often is ṣalāt legally required?  [صَلُّوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether the unrestricted imperative repeats and how strongly warning reports bind.
- 33:56/q16/p1 Once in a lifetime, with more recommended. — Abu Hanifa, Malik and Qadi Iyad say an unrestricted command does not require repetition. Ibn Kathir calls the lifetime-only view strange given many occasions. [10 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الطحاوي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(القاضي عياض); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حنيفة ومالك); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 33:56/q16/p2 At every mention of the Prophet. — Al-Karkhi, al-Tahawi, Ibn Kathir and Elmalili cite warning reports. Al-Alusi gives objections from alleged earlier consensus, burden and observed practice, then answers several of them. [32 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي,بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قيل); NASAFI-FULL(الكرخي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن فارس,الطحاوي,صاحب المجتبى); ELMALILI(Prophet); ELMALILI+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 33:56/q16/p3 Once per gathering despite repeated mentions. — Some analogize to recitation prostration or responding to a sneeze; a gathering without remembrance and prayer incurs regret or punishment. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(بعضهم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو سعيد,أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب المجتبى); ELMALILI]
- 33:56/q16/p4 Frequent invocation is emphatic sunnah or recommendation. — Ibn ʿAtiyya urges it at all times; Ibn ʿAshur finds no early every-mention recitation or writing practice. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهور العلماء); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Âşûr); STUDYQURAN(IK, Q, R)]
- 33:56/q16/p5 At supplication's beginning and end, or often without a fixed number. — Some jurists require the paired supplication setting; Ibn Bukayr requires frequent invocation but sets no count. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(القاضي أبو بكر بن بكير); ELMALILI]

## 33:56/q17 Is ṣalāt a condition of ritual prayer?  [صَلُّوا۟ · law]
turns on: How the verse and graded hadiths bear on prayer validity.
- 33:56/q17/p1 Yes, especially in the final tashahhud. — Al-Shafiʿi, Ahmad's later view, Ishaq and Ibn al-Mawwaz are cited. Ibn Kathir and al-Alusi reject a supposed consensus against it; authorities differ on deliberate versus forgetful omission. [26 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الشافعي); KASHSHAF-FULL(الشافعي); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد بن حنبل وإسحاق بن راهويه وابن المواز,البندنيجي وسليم الرازي ونصر بن إبراهيم المقدسي,الشافعي,بعض أئمة الحنابلة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو مسعود البدري وابن عمر,أحمد,إسحاق بن راهويه,ابن حجر,ابن مسعود,الشافعي,الشعبي,محمد الباقر); ELMALILI(İmâm Şâfi‘î); STUDYQURAN(Imam al-Shāfiʿī and many followers of his legal tradition); IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 33:56/q17/p2 No; it is recommended or the tashahhud greeting suffices. — Abu Hanifa and Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi are cited. Al-Khattabi alleges lack of early precedent; Ibn Kathir notes the 'no prayer without blessing' report has an abandoned transmitter. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو حنيفة وأصحابه,إبراهيم النخعي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(İbrâhim Neha‘î); IBNASHUR-FULL(أهل الحديث,الخطابي,جمهور العلماء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعض المتأخرين من المالكية وغيرهم)-]
- 33:56/q17/p3 The exact taught form or prayer for his family is also obligatory. — Some Hanbalis require exact wording; some Shafiʿis require mention of family, though Ibn Kathir says most disagree. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(البندنيجي وسليم الرازي ونصر بن إبراهيم المقدسي,بعض أئمة الحنابلة)]

## 33:56/q18 Which formula fulfills a vow to bless him in the best manner?  [صَلُّوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether the taught formula alone counts as best.
- 33:56/q18/p1 The Abrahamic form taught to the Companions. — Al-Nawawi says only it fulfills such a vow; al-Subki says its performance and reward are certain. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السبكي,النووي)]
- 33:56/q18/p2 A superlative or expansive alternative form. — Al-Marwazi, Qadi Husayn, al-Barizi, Kamal ibn al-Humam and Ibn Hajar offer differing forms or selections. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيثمي,البارزي,القاضي حسين,الكمال بن الهمام,المروزي)]

## 33:56/q19 Should ṣalāt be said at slaughter?  [صَلُّوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether 94:4 extends to slaughter despite no specific Prophetic instruction.
- 33:56/q19/p1 Yes, it is recommended alongside God's name. — Some infer it from 94:4, read to mean that God is not mentioned without the Prophet. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 33:56/q19/p2 No, the majority reserves slaughter for mention of God alone. — Ibn Kathir compares eating, entering and intercourse, where no Prophetic instruction to bless the Prophet exists. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL+]

## 33:56/q20 Which occasions especially invite ṣalāt?  [صَلُّوا۟ · law]
turns on: The transmitted practice and strength of reports for each setting.
- 33:56/q20/p1 Friday and its eve. — Angels witness the day and prayers are presented. Ibn Kathir authenticates one report, calls Abu al-Darda's route interrupted and another Friday route weak. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو الدرداء,الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو أمامة وأبو مسعود,أبو الدرداء,أوس بن أوس الثقفي,الحسن البصري,الشافعي); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,عائشة); BURSEVI(الصديق الأكبر,بعض الكبار,بعضهم,سهل بن عبد الله التستري); IBNASHUR-FULL]

[34 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو رافع,أبو هريرة,أحمد بن حنبل,ابن عباس,الحسن بن الحسن,الذهبي,القاسم بن محمد بن أبي بكر الصديق,علي بن أبي طالب,علي بن الحسين,عمر بن الخطاب,فاطمة بنت رسول الله); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(أسماء بنت أبي بكر,عمر); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 33:56/q20/p3 Supplication, call to prayer, Witr, funeral and Eid prayer. — Fadala's sound report puts blessing between praise and petition; Umar says a supplication is suspended without it. Other reports concern al-wasīla and ordered prayer rites. [18 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو أمامة بن سهل,الحسن بن علي,الشافعي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,النسائي,جابر,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,عبد الله بن مسعود,عمر بن الخطاب,فضالة بن عبيد); DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن مسعود,الحسن); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet,ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb)]

## 33:56/q21 What rewards or threats do reports attach to ṣalāt?  [صَلُّوا۟ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in number, benefit, threat and chain strength.
- 33:56/q21/p1 Angels pray for practitioners and curse or withhold prayer from neglecters. — Warning reports call neglect miserliness, disgrace or a cause of loss. Ibn Kathir finds a two-angel grave route severely weak; other individual warnings receive varied gradings. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(عبد الله بن مسعود); THALABI(النبي); KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(محمد ﷺ); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو ذر,أبو سعيد,أبو هريرة,أم أنيس بنت الحسن بن علي,ابن عباس,الحسن البصري,الحسين بن علي); DURR-FULL(الحسن بن علي,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ELMALILI(Prophet); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 33:56/q21/p2 One invocation commonly earns ten divine prayers, with additions of ten deeds, removed sins or degrees. — Abu Talha, Abu Hurayra, Umar and Anas transmit tenfold versions; some add ten good deeds, erased sins or raised degrees, and Ibn Kathir states several chain gradings. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو طلحة); WAHIDI-ASBAB(أبو هريرة); TABRISI(أبو طلحة); RAZI-FULL(النبي عليه السلام); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو طلحة,أبو طلحة الأنصاري,أبو هريرة,أنس,عامر بن ربيعة,عبد الرحمن بن عوف,عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(أبو طلحة,أبو هريرة,يعقوب بن زيد التيمي); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 33:56/q21/p3 Frequent prayer brings nearness, intercession, sufficiency or love. — Ibn Masʿud's nearness report is called hasan gharīb; Ubayy's graded report promises relief when he devotes his prayer to the Prophet. Other reports mention al-wasīla and the Praised Station. [16 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,أبي بن كعب,ابن عباس,رويفع بن ثابت الأنصاري,عبد الله بن عمرو,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص,عبد الله بن مسعود,يعقوب بن زيد بن طلحة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن مسعود); STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
- 33:56/q21/p4 One invocation earns seventy divine and angelic prayers. — Abd Allah ibn Amr reports this larger number in Ibn Kathir. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو)]
- 33:56/q21/p5 Ten morning invocations earn a daily reward equal to the deeds of earth's inhabitants. — A report through Abu Bakr gives this exceptional reward for a specific repeated formula. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو بكر الصديق)]

## 33:56/q22 How do blessings and greetings reach the Prophet after death?  [صَلُّوا۟ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ · reports]
- 33:56/q22/p1 They reach him wherever offered, by presentation or angels. — Friday reports say prophets' bodies are not consumed; other reports describe traveling angels, named presentation, hearing near the grave, and his returned greeting. Ibn Kathir questions several chains, especially an interrupted Abu al-Darda route and a report through an abandoned transmitter. [22 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو الدرداء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو أمامة وأبو مسعود,أبو الدرداء,أبو هريرة,أم أنيس بنت الحسن بن علي,الحسن البصري,الحسن بن الحسن,عبد الله بن مسعود,علي بن الحسين,كعب); DURR-FULL(أبو طلحة,مجاهد); BURSEVI(حديث)]

## 33:56/q23 What does taslīm command?  [وَسَلِّمُوا۟ تَسْلِيمًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the imperative prescribes greeting, obedience or both.
- 33:56/q23/p1 Greet the Prophet with peace or pray for his safety. — Most cite the Islamic greeting; al-Alusi favors safety from harms and defects, while Ibn ʿAshur says believers address him directly because they already knew the greeting. [34 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البيضاوي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عبد السلام,بعض المعاصرين); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عمر,الجمهور,النابغة,النبي); STUDYQURAN(Āl); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 33:56/q23/p2 Submit to his commands, judgment and law. — Al-Mawardi, Abu Abdillah and others explain obedience; Biqaʿi's ellipsis permits both greeting and submission. Al-Alusi acknowledges this but favors the greeting on report evidence. [12 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(أبو بصير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Āl); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 33:56/q23/p3 Inwardly recognize his perfection and refine oneself. — Qashani makes greeting a transformation of the believer through the Prophet. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 33:56/q24 What does salām itself mean?  [وَسَلِّمُوا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether salām is a verbal noun, a divine name or a request for obedience.
- 33:56/q24/p1 Safety, peace and protection from harm. — Al-Qazzaz includes assurance against harm, divine safety and mercy; Ibn ʿAshur traces greeting from mutual security to courtesy and cites al-Nabigha. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو عبد الله القزاز,البيضاوي,القاموس); IBNASHUR-FULL(النابغة); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 33:56/q24/p2 The divine name al-Salām and God's guardianship. — Ibn Furak defines the name as God's freedom from defect; other interpretations supply blessing, protection, or an omitted word for preservation. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن فورك)]
- 33:56/q24/p3 Peaceable non-opposition that brings submission to his law. — Al-Alusi records this reading but prefers safety and greeting. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 33:56/q25 Why is taslīman added?  [تَسْلِيمًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether emphasis is grammatical or especially contextual.
- 33:56/q25/p1 It is an emphatic cognate accusative urging full greeting. — Razi, al-Zarkashi, al-Suyuti and Ibn ʿAshur give the grammatical account. Ibn al-Qayyim says prayer already has emphasis from inna and the angels; Ibn ʿAshur says taṣliya was avoided due to its burning sense. [13 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN(ابن الدهان,التنوخي,قالوا,قيل); BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(ابن القيم); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 33:56/q25/p2 It emphasizes believers' special need to avoid harming him. — Al-Khafaji relates it to the preceding harm passage; another explanation fears that without explicit emphasis greeting might seem superseded. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الشهاب الخفاجي)]

## 33:56/q26 How are prayer and greeting ordered and related?  [صَلُّوا۟ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Why the second command follows prayer.
- 33:56/q26/p1 Prayer comes first as superior; both display honor. — Al-Zarkashi asserts superiority. Biqaʿi and al-Alusi analyze ellipsis across the clauses. [4 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL(البيضاوي)]
- 33:56/q26/p2 Greeting is separately commanded because believers must explicitly submit and avoid harm. — Al-Alusi prefers this explanation, though he records a difficulty with saying divine and angelic prayer already entails greeting. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 33:56/q27 Must ṣalāt and salām be paired?  [صَلُّوا۟ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether paired commands require a paired utterance.
- 33:56/q27/p1 Pairing is preferred; separation is disliked or less preferable. — Al-Nawawi infers pairing from the verse; Ibn Hajar al-Haytami limits the dislike to contrary-to-better. Al-Alusi values early usage but denies the verse mandates simultaneity. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيثمي,النووي); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النووي)+]
- 33:56/q27/p2 Each may be said separately. — Al-Hamawi reports the Hanafi view; some distinguish speech from writing. Al-Alusi says morning prayer and evening greeting fulfill the two absolute commands. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحموي)]

## 33:56/q28 May ṣalāt or salām be given independently to others?  [صَلُّوا۟ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether examples authorize independent formulas or custom reserves them.
- 33:56/q28/p1 Others may be included with the Prophet, but isolated use is disliked. — Custom reserves the formula as a prophetic honorific. Al-Nawawi prefers mild dislike; Ibn ʿAshur and others connect it to social distinctions and sectarian usage. [35 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو محمد الجويني,أحمد بن حنبل,ابن عباس,البندنيجي وسليم الرازي ونصر بن إبراهيم المقدسي,الجمهور من العلماء,عمر بن عبد العزيز); ALUSI-FULL(أبو اليمن بن عساكر,أبو حنيفة,ابن عباس,البيري,التقي السبكي,الجويني,الحليمي,الدر المنضود,الشافعية,القاضي عياض,القرطبي); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عمر,الجمهور); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النووي)+]
- 33:56/q28/p2 Yes, believers may receive it independently. — Al-Kashshaf invokes 33:43 and 9:103; Qadi Iyad and others cite prophetic prayer for almsgivers, families and a woman with her husband. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,قائلون); ALUSI-FULL(البيهقي,القاضي عياض,مسلم)]
- 33:56/q28/p3 Independent use is prohibited, though dependent use is allowed. — Ibn Abbas and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz reserve it for prophets. Al-Bayhaqi distinguishes honorific usage from ordinary supplication; some Hanbalis distinguish general prayer from exclusive slogans. [17 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(آخرون,ابن عباس,عمر بن عبد العزيز); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(أبو اليمن بن عساكر,أبو حنيفة,ابن عباس,البيري,البيهقي,الشافعية,القاضي عياض,القرطبي,بعض الحنابلة,عمر بن عبد العزيز); IBNKATHIR-FULL(الجمهور من العلماء)+]
- 33:56/q28/p4 Other prophets and possibly angels may receive ṣalāt. — A prophetic report about other messengers has weak routes according to Ibn Kathir; al-Alusi accepts acting on them here. Angels are included by inference if they count as messengers. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة,المجد اللغوي)]

## 33:56/q29 May mercy or forgiveness be requested for the Prophet?  [صَلُّوا۟ · law]
turns on: Whether prophetic honorific convention restricts ordinary supplication.
- 33:56/q29/p1 Mercy may be requested even by itself. — Ibn Kathir cites the Bedouin's prayer and the majority; Ibn Hajar al-Haytami says the Prophet's approval overrides a general implication. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيثمي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن حجر الهيثمي)+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+]
- 33:56/q29/p2 Mercy should accompany blessing or greeting, or be avoided alone. — Qadi Iyad reports most Malikis' restriction; al-Qurtubi, al-Suyuti and al-Ghazali distinguish prophetic honorific prayer from general mercy, citing 24:63. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(القاضي عياض); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عبد البر,الغزالي,القرطبي)]
- 33:56/q29/p3 Standalone forgiveness or pardon is best avoided. — Al-Tahtawi and al-Alusi worry it suggests deficiency, while admitting it can ask for higher rank rather than imply sin. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطحطاوي)]

## 33:56/q30 What variant wording is reported for the verse?  [صَلُّوا۟ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ تَسْلِيمًا · readings]
turns on: Whether the additions are readings, explanatory expansions or transmission claims.
- 33:56/q30/p1 Al-Hasan reads faṣallū with an added fāʾ. — Ibn Jinni prefers the common bare imperative as stronger; Ibn ʿAtiyya says the fāʾ makes the conditional link clearer. [4 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود والحسن)]
- 33:56/q30/p2 Ibn Masʿud adds 'as God blessed him' before the greeting. — Al-Alusi says it makes imitation clearer. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود والحسن)]
- 33:56/q30/p3 ʿAʾisha's codex reportedly adds 'and those who pray in the first rows.' — Hamida reports this before ʿUthman's codices; al-Alusi gives no grading there and denies loss of mass-transmitted Quranic text, explaining omission reports otherwise. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(حميدة); ITQAN(حميدة بنت أبي يونس); ALUSI-FULL(حميدة بنت يونس)]
- 33:56/q30/p4 The common reading has the bare imperative ṣallū without fāʾ. — Ibn Jinnī prefers it as stronger because it supplies no stated cause that could invite objection. [1 notes: IBNJINNI-MUHTASAB+]
- 33:56/q30/p5 No loss of mass-transmitted Quranic text follows from a codex-addition report. — Al-Murtada and al-Tabrisi argue preservation from intense memorization, ordering and tracking of differences; al-Alusi rejects attributing textual-loss doctrine to Sunnis and treats such reports as noncanonical readings, abrogated recitation or material absent at the final review. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي,المرتضى)]

## 33:56/q31 What did reports say happened when the verse came?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ in occasion, speakers, ensuing verse and dating.
- 33:56/q31/p1 Believers asked what honor they would receive; a verse of blessing on them followed. — Versions differ on whether the speaker was the Muslims, Abu Bakr or Ubayy and whether the ensuing verse was 33:43 or 9:112. [5 notes: MUQATIL(المسلمون); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو بكر); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 33:56/q31/p2 Israelites asked Moses whether God prays; the verse followed God's answer. — Ibn Abbas versions say God told Moses that He and His angels pray for prophets and messengers. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 33:56/q31/p3 It came in the second year after Hijra. — Abu Dharr al-Harawi reports this. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر الهروي)]
- 33:56/q31/p4 It came on the Night Journey. — Al-Alusi records and rejects this date as inconsistent with the verse's Medinan setting. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو ذر الهروي)-]

## 33:56/q32 How does the verse connect to its sūra and other verses?  [* · links]
- 33:56/q32/p1 It follows rules against harming the Prophet and completes his honor. — Ibn ʿAtiyya says it clears suspicion about wives; Razi contrasts private household and public honor; Ibn ʿAshur sees a warning against hypocritical harm after the verse. [13 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 33:56/q32/p2 It parallels blessing of believers in 33:41–43 and the patient in 2:155–157. — Ibn Kathir cites both; Elmalili and Kuran Yolu relate divine grace and the move from darkness to light. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 33:56/q33 What ancillary material appears with these verse notes?  [* · interpretation]
- 33:56/q33/p1 Other reports describe angels and creation honoring the Prophet. — Study Quran reports rocks and trees greeting him; Ibn Kathir cites angels blessing the right sides of prayer rows and an exhortation to obey the final prophet. - 33:56/q33/p2 Ibn al-Jawzi gives lexical senses of rukūʿ. — He cites Labid, anonymous poetry and verses for bending, abasement, prayer, bowing and prostration. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,لبيد)]
- 33:56/q33/p3 Razi discusses whether devils can enter human bodies. — He cites reports about bloodlike movement, objections from physical passage, harm, fire and unseen effects, then replies involving subtle bodies, divine prevention, harmless fire and choice. - 33:56/q33/p4 Bursevi distinguishes angelic classes and human rank. — He describes embodied and absorbed angels, says humans unite Beauty and Majesty and reads Adam's trial as divine education. [5 notes: BURSEVI]
