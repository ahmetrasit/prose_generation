# 11:56: 25 questions

## 11:56/q01 What does Hūd's declaration of trust mean in his challenge?  [إِنِّى تَوَكَّلْتُ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 11:56/q01/p1 He commits himself to God's judgment, help, and protection, confident that his people and their idols cannot harm him outside God's will. — Muqātil relates it to the threat from their idols; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Biqāʿī stress Hūd's isolation against their numbers. Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī define tawakkul as accepting judgment and trusting help. Bayḍāwī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī say any harm depends on God's will. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls reliance the ground of the challenge; Study Quran (Kl) also reads his confidence this way. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]

## 11:56/q02 Why does Hūd call God 'my Lord and your Lord'?  [رَبِّى وَرَبِّكُم · rhetoric]
- 11:56/q02/p1 God owns and governs both sides, so Hūd can rely on Him against his opponents. — Ṭabarī calls God the owner and governor of both; Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī draw the protective inference. Qāshānī says universal lordship makes another guardian needless. Biqāʿī adds that God created and managed both before their creation, knowing their afterlife acts. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:56/q03 Why is tawakkaltu in the past tense?  [تَوَكَّلْتُ · rhetoric]
- 11:56/q03/p1 The past tense conveys Hūd's performative commitment at this moment. — Ālūsī explicitly gives this rhetorical reason. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 11:56/q04 What does dābba include here?  [دَآبَّةٍ · meaning]
- 11:56/q04/p1 It covers every living creature that moves, especially animals on earth; the wording admits all kinds. — Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī say every living thing can be called dābb or dābba and the feminine ending intensifies it. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says animals are named as the class under discussion. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says every earth moving animal. Muqātil phrases the scope as any creature or thing. [6 notes: MUQATIL; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:56/q05 How is the clause about every creature constructed?  [مَّا مِن دَآبَّةٍ إِلَّا هُوَ ءَاخِذٌۢ بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · grammar]
- 11:56/q05/p1 Mā min dābba occupies the subject position, and illā huwa ākhidh bi-nāṣiyatihā supplies its predicate. — Naḥḥās gives the subject and predicate analysis; Qurṭubī specifies that the first expression has nominative subject position. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 11:56/q06 How does the creature clause relate syntactically to God's name?  [مَّا مِن دَآبَّةٍ إِلَّا هُوَ ءَاخِذٌۢ بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · grammar]
- 11:56/q06/p1 It may be an adjective of God's name or a circumstantial clause; both reinforce the lordship description. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports both analyses and says they serve the same purpose. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:56/q07 What is a nāṣiya?  [بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · meaning]
- 11:56/q07/p1 It is the front of the head, its hairline, or the hair growing there. — Wāḥidī transmits al-Layth, al-Farrāʾ, al-Azharī, and a line of ʿAmr ibn Maʿdī Karib about grasping it. Jishumī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī citing al-Azharī, Qurṭubī, Samīn, Biqāʿī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the same anatomical range. Samīn and al-Azharī say the hair is named after its location. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري,الفراء,الليث,عمرو بن معدي كرب); JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الأزهري); QURTUBI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:56/q08 What derivation and usage are given for nāṣiya?  [بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · meaning]
- 11:56/q08/p1 Its root is connected with joining or drawing together; related verbs also mean seizing or stretching a forelock. — Jishumī, Ṭabrisī, and Biqāʿī compare adjacent desert tracts; Ṭabrisī cites Abū al-Najm's verse fragments. Samīn gives naṣawtu al-rajul, establishing wāw as the last radical, and reports nāṣāh with yāʾ changed to alif. Qurṭubī gives naṣawtu for stretching it; Wāḥidī cites al-Azharī's tugging verbs and ʿAmr's poem. [8 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(أبو النجم); BIQAI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; QURTUBI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري,عمرو بن معدي كرب)]

## 11:56/q09 What does God taking every creature by its forelock convey?  [هُوَ ءَاخِذٌۢ بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the image chiefly signifies sovereign control, life and death, prior decreed actions, or spiritual direction; Qurṭubī challenges the forelock localization of deeds.
- 11:56/q09/p1 Every creature belongs to God and is subdued, governed, and directed by His complete power. — Majāz speaks of grasp, ownership, and power; Ibn Qutayba and Ṭabarī of sovereign subjection. Zajjāj and Wāḥidī say God can act upon creatures as He wills. Ibn ʿĀshūr notes many creatures lack literal forelocks, confirming the figure. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, Ālūsī, Elmalılı, and Asad likewise take it figuratively. Rāzī stresses submission to decree; Qurṭubī and Naḥḥās infer that opponents cannot harm Hūd. Asad links 11:6. [42 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن جرير); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(الفراء,القُتيبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء,القتبي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(N)]
- 11:56/q09/p2 God gives each creature life and death. — Muqātil specifically says the grip causes death; al-Ḍaḥḥāk, reported by Baghawī and Qurṭubī, says it gives life and then death. Qurṭubī calls these meanings close to the control interpretation. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(الضحاك); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك)]
- 11:56/q09/p3 God's prior decree and knowledge drive creatures toward their assigned actions. — Al-Tirmidhī al-Ḥakīm says the light of prior knowledge was placed in forelocks, where hidden deeds become manifest, and motions were placed between the eyes. Qurṭubī also cites ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr's report that all measures were decreed fifty thousand years before creation. Qurṭubī objects that 96:16 calls Abū Jahl's forelock lying and sinful. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الترمذي الحكيم,عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 11:56/q09/p4 God draws each creature toward good or evil while all remain humbled in His power. — Bursevī quotes al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya for this inward account of creatures' movement. [1 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 11:56/q10 Why is the forelock singled out for control?  [بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · rhetoric]
- 11:56/q10/p1 Grasping or shaving a captive's forelock was an Arab sign of mastery and humiliation, so the part represents control of the whole. — Ṭabarī and Ibn Jarīr as cited by Wāḥidī mention shaved captives. Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Rāzī, Qurṭubī citing Ibn Jurayj, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn, Bursevī, and Ālūsī repeat the idiom or practice; Abū Ḥayyān likens it to leading a captive or horse. Ibn al-Jawzī says control of the forelock humbles the whole body; Ibn ʿĀshūr pictures the seized head preventing escape. Asad notes the same idiom in 96:15–16, its earliest chronological occurrence. [18 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن جرير); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جريج); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ASAD-NOTES; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 11:56/q11 What does 'my Lord is on a straight path' mean?  [إِنَّ رَبِّى عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'path' describes God's just conduct or governance, an unavoidable reach, the revealed religion and guidance, or the return for judgment; some readings posit an omitted noun or verb.
- 11:56/q11/p1 God stands for truth and justice: His acts and judgments are upright, and He rewards good, punishes evil, protects the trusting, and wrongs no one. — Mujāhid's truth gloss appears in Mujāhid, Ṭabarī, Māwardī, Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and Durr. Ṭabarī adds just recompense and acceptance of Islam and faith; Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq, Ibn al-Anbārī, Baghawī, and Naḥḥās stress that omnipotence never leads God to injustice. Wāḥidī cites Arab usage of a good path for just conduct. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains ʿalā as moral establishment (2:5) and the path as justice and wisdom (19:43). Qāshānī adds deserved discipline, purification, and even martyrdom; Asad extends recompense to both worlds. Study Quran (Z) relates it to 1:6. [37 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن الأنباري,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Z)]
- 11:56/q11/p2 God's governance and creation follow a sound, faultless, well ordered way. — ʿAlī ibn ʿĪsā in Māwardī names sound governance; Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī's anonymous explanation says His government has no defect and creation no disparity. Wāḥidī reports an omitted genitive, 'God's command and governance.' Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes ordered acts, truthful speech, and true promise, also with an implied noun. Biqāʿī describes a manifest, broad road without defect or disorder. [6 notes: NAHHAS(قيل); MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]
- 11:56/q11/p3 No fugitive or hidden person can escape God's reach; His path is unavoidable. — Wāḥidī and Rāzī cite 89:14. Ibn al-Anbārī, reported by Ibn al-Jawzī, connects this with every creature being in God's grasp. Bayḍāwī's assertion that no wrongdoer escapes and Ālūsī's guardian image overlap this sense. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 11:56/q11/p4 The path is the upright religion sent through Hūd, namely Islam. — Wāḥidī transmits Ibn ʿAbbās through ʿAṭāʾ and al-Kalbī; the latter calls Islam enduring and says God guides whom He wills. Wāḥidī's Wajīz and Baghawī also report the religious sense. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,عطاء عن ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 11:56/q11/p5 God guides or urges people onto the straight path. — Wāḥidī reports an ellipsis, God urges or leads them to call to Islam; Baghawī gives a similar omitted action. Ibn al-Jawzī supplies 'guides'; Rāzī speaks of guiding or urging through the call; Nasafī gives this as the alternative to God's being upon truth. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض أهل المعاني); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 11:56/q11/p6 The path is the afterlife route on which people return to God for recompense and judgment. — Māwardī gives this as another possible sense. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 11:56/q12 Why does the closing say 'my Lord' rather than repeat 'your Lord'?  [إِنَّ رَبِّى · rhetoric]
- 11:56/q12/p1 The singular wording points to Hūd's special care and personal bond of servitude as he seeks protection. — Ālūsī sees special care while universal lordship remains affirmed; Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes the earlier universal rule from this personal appeal for protection. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:56/q13 How does the closing clause support Hūd's trust and the surrounding challenge?  [إِنَّ رَبِّى عَلَىٰ صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · links]
- 11:56/q13/p1 God's just, watchful, dependable way assures Hūd protection and answers the people's charge that he has no proof. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says God supports messengers and will not hand a trusting servant to wrongdoers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says His constant practice vindicates truth. Biqāʿī makes inna causal, links the ending to the no proof charge in 11:53, and says it completes the argument from reliance and control. Ālūsī invokes 89:14 for watchfulness; al-Kashf describes progression from trust through shared lordship to universal control. Biqāʿī says clarity firms adherents and seizes those turning away. [9 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الكشف); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 11:56/q14 How does Ibn Abī Ḥātim handle the straight path here?  [صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · interpretation]
- 11:56/q14/p1 He refers back to his earlier explanations of the expression. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim says he has already explained the straight path several times. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM]

## 11:56/q15 How is ṣirāṭ read here?  [صِرَٰطٍۢ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted pronunciation of the initial consonant.
- 11:56/q15/p1 Qunbul and Ruways read its ṣād as sīn. — Qirāʾāt ER says both forms derive from the same Semitic root for road. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Qunbul/Ruways)]
- 11:56/q15/p2 Khalaf from Ḥamza uses ishmām, coloring ṣād with zāy. — Qirāʾāt ER describes an intermediate sound between ṣ and s. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Khalaf ʿan Ḥamza)]

## 11:56/q16 What is reported about reciting this verse for protection?  [* · reports]
- 11:56/q16/p1 Recitation against a robber, predator, or rebellious devil turns it away by God's action. — Durr reports this from Yaḥyā ibn Saʿīd. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(يحيى بن سعيد)]

## 11:56/q17 How does God treat believers and disbelievers under His control?  [هُوَ ءَاخِذٌۢ بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · reports]
- 11:56/q17/p1 He treats a believer more gently than a parent treats a child, while confronting the unbeliever with the question in 82:6. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports Yafʿ ibn ʿAbd al-Kalāʿī; Ibn Kathīr transmits the same report as from Aifa. Their spellings of the name vary. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(يفع بن عبد الكلاعي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أيفع بن عبد الكلاعي)]

## 11:56/q18 What does direct reliance on God imply about idols and mediators?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether access to God requires the deities as intermediaries.
- 11:56/q18/p1 God alone deserves reliance and worship; creatures and idols have no independent power to benefit or harm, and a believer can turn directly to Him. — Ibn Kathīr calls idols lifeless, unable to hear, see, benefit, harm, or take sides. Qāshānī denies creatures' independent efficacy, Biqāʿī says God is Lord of idols and worshippers, and Islahi says no intercessor is needed. Bursevī cites the divine essence as source of all actions and effects, drawing creatures according to capacity. [9 notes: QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(در نقد النصوص); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 11:56/q18/p2 The idolaters believed their deities mediated access to God. — Islahi reports this belief and rejects it as baseless, connecting the Qurʾanic charge that they obstruct and distort God's path with 39:3 and 7:45. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 11:56/q19 What theological principles do God's grasp and straight path establish together?  [* · theology]
- 11:56/q19/p1 They establish divine unity and divine justice as the two completing principles of religion. — Rāzī reports the Muʿtazila: grasping every creature proves unity, and the straight path proves justice. Qāshānī likewise places justice within the interplay of unity and multiplicity. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(المعتزلة); QASHANI]

## 11:56/q20 What inward lesson follows from every forelock being in God's grasp?  [هُوَ ءَاخِذٌۢ بِنَاصِيَتِهَآ · inward]
- 11:56/q20/p1 Claims of independent selfhood or possession are false and contest divine power. — Sulamī reports one reflection that dependent existence cannot claim independent standing and saying 'I' contests God's grip; another says every self claim wages war on divine power. [2 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم)]

## 11:56/q21 What path should a spiritual seeker follow?  [صِرَٰطٍۢ مُّسْتَقِيمٍۢ · inward]
- 11:56/q21/p1 The seeker follows the straight road of Sacred Law and spiritual discipline toward the reality whose end is God alone. — Bursevī cites 53:42 for the final destination and urges practice of the Sacred Law to reach reality. [2 notes: BURSEVI]

## 11:56/q22 How does Hūd's pronoun use compare with the related passage in al-Aʿrāf?  [رَبِّى · rhetoric]
- 11:56/q22/p1 Hūd repeatedly uses the first person yāʾ here, whereas al-Aʿrāf uses it once in 'my protector is Allah.' — Sāmarrāʾī compares 11:54–57 with 7:196 and says the repetition suits Hūd's speech. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 11:56/q23 Who were ʿĀd in the background to this passage?  [* · reports]
- 11:56/q23/p1 They were an ancient, powerful Arab people with prosperous towns who became idolatrous and arrogant. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says their history outside the Qurʾan survives only in unreliable tales, the Torah does not mention them, and the Qurʾan places them after Noah in al-Aḥqāf. 'First ʿĀd' may imply a second. He cites 89:6–11 for their stature, towns, gardens, palms, crops, monuments, and tyranny. [4 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:56/q24 What happened in Hūd's mission to ʿĀd?  [* · reports]
- 11:56/q24/p1 Hūd called his people to worship God and act justly; most rejected him, then a devastating wind destroyed them while Hūd and the believers were saved. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Hūd was their kinsman and the second prophet whose struggle against idolatry the Qurʾan narrates. Few believed; others called him foolish or mad and demanded punishment, whose timing he left to God. They mistook the approaching cloud for rain. The barren wind lasted seven nights and eight days, leaving hollow palm trunk like bodies and dwellings. [5 notes: TABATABAI]

## 11:56/q25 What does this verse imply about material causation?  [* · theology]
- 11:56/q25/p1 Material events, ordinary or miraculous, follow a stable created order and arise through preceding causes. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī grounds this in the Qurʾan's universal creation, ordered creation, and general causality, citing 40:62 and 11:56. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
