# 18:1: 34 questions

## 18:1/q01 Why does the verse open with praise of God?  [ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause performs divine self-praise or instructs human praise; the accounts of revelation's benefit largely agree.
- 18:1/q01/p1 God is inherently worthy of praise and praises Himself for revelation, the supreme gift benefiting Muhammad especially and humanity generally. — Yaḥyā says God deserves praise; al-Māwardī and al-Baghawī distinguish the Prophet's special benefit from creation's general benefit. Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī call Islam and the Book the means of salvation; al-Rāzī details revealed knowledge for Muhammad and guidance, laws, reward and warning for all. Ibn Kathīr and Study Quran describe passage from darkness to light; Bayḍāwī adds worldly and next-life welfare. Al-Biqāʿī says praise belongs to God intrinsically and for His acts; al-Ālūsī says the relative clause explains and magnifies His claim to praise. İslâhî says Arabs owe gratitude yet invent excuses. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 18:1/q01/p2 The verse itself is God's praise of Himself, rather than an instruction to people to utter praise. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the instruction reading on the verse's form; Qāshānī describes God as both praiser and praised in unity and differentiated disclosure, with the Prophet's later praise dependent on God's prior praise. [3 notes: TABATABAI; QASHANI]
- 18:1/q01/p3 God teaches His servants to say this praise. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī connect the instruction with Islam and scripture; al-Biqāʿī says its wording suits the occasion. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI-]

## 18:1/q02 How broad is the praise expressed here?  [ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ · interpretation]
- 18:1/q02/p1 It belongs to God in every state, in this world and the next, for creation, preservation, and final destiny. — Ibn Kathīr compares al-Fātiḥa, 6:1, 39:75, 28:70, 34:1, a prayer filling heaven and earth with praise, and Paradise's naturally inspired praise at 10:9–10. Al-Biqāʿī distinguishes praise for God's essence, attributes and acts. [7 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:1/q03 How does this opening's praise relate to the preceding sūra's glorification and praise?  [ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ · links]
- 18:1/q03/p1 The two openings form a sequence of glorification then praise, matching their paired use and the preceding sūra's ending. — Al-Rāzī says glorification affirms God's own perfection, then praise His perfecting of others: the Night Journey elevates the Prophet, while revelation brings higher teaching down. Al-Zarkashī cites the customary paired formula. Al-Biqāʿī, al-Ālūsī, Elmalılı and Sāmarrāʾī link 17:111 to this opening; Sāmarrāʾī says the command to praise at al-Isrāʾ's end receives its answer here. Al-Biqāʿī calls the book a wise, unrivaled religion; al-Ālūsī also reports essential praise there and deserved praise here. [12 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURHAN; ITQAN(الإمام); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 18:1/q04 Who is called His servant?  [عَبْدِهِۦ · referent]
- 18:1/q04/p1 Muhammad, the recipient of the Qur'an. — Yaḥyā, Ibn Isḥāq, Ibn al-Jawzī, Kuryolu and Study Quran identify him; Ibn Isḥāq says this confirms his prophethood against doubters. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK)]

## 18:1/q05 Why is Muhammad called God's servant here?  [عَبْدِهِۦ · rhetoric]
- 18:1/q05/p1 The possessive title honors him, authenticates his mission, and marks his dependence on God. — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts the preceding direct address with 'His servant'; al-Biqāʿī says the title anticipates the Night Journey and answers Quraysh without Jewish consultation. Al-Ālūsī and Bursevî say the messenger is God's servant and counter Christian claims about Jesus; Bursevî adds his concern for his community on Judgment Day. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 18:1/q05/p2 His servanthood marks the highest station of learning and conveying revealed knowledge. — Al-Rāzī quotes a report that one who learns and teaches is called great in the heavens and connects it to the servant's highest rank. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 18:1/q06 What inward meaning is given to the servant and the descent of the Book?  [عَبْدِهِۦ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · inward]
- 18:1/q06/p1 The servant owns nothing and attends wholly to the master, while revelation manifests inner realities in him. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says all belongs to God and the sincere servant owns nothing; definitions include seeing none beside the master, disputing nothing, settling in nothing, ready obedience, selfless nonpossession, and adopting the master's traits. He also says the Book manifests inner secrets. Qāshānī says Muhammad was eternally prepared to receive unified realities, which descend into human manifestation and return as praise. Bursevî and al-Ālūsī call him the perfect servant, free of all besides God and both worlds. [11 notes: SULAMI(أبو حفص,أبو عثمان,ابن عطاء,الجريري); QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:1/q07 What is the Book sent down to him?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · referent]
- 18:1/q07/p1 The Qur'an, either in its completed entirety or as revealed by that point. — Yaḥyā, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, and Kuryolu identify it. Al-Ālūsī notes the complete self-sufficient Qur'an uniquely merits 'the Book', while the phrase may also designate the revelation already sent. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNATIYYA-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:1/q08 What does kitāb mean and why can the Qur'an bear that name?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · meaning]
- 18:1/q08/p1 It denotes writing or a joined body of letters and can name the written result. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it a verbal noun like qiyām and ḥisāb, signifying joined or separate letters; the Qur'an is written. An anonymous divorce poem names a written divorce document kitāb. Bursevî derives the name from joining letters. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 18:1/q09 What does the Book's revelation provide or demonstrate?  [أَنزَلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · interpretation]
- 18:1/q09/p1 It provides clear guidance, warning, mercy, and a lasting proof of Muhammad's mission. — Ibn Qutayba calls it spirit, mercy, healing, light, and free of falsehood, citing 41:42. Al-Māwardī says it is a protected miracle proving revelation and the Messenger; al-Kashshāf says its ordered, staged Arabic discourse confirms former books and brings religious and worldly benefits. Al-Thaʿlabī notes punishment and Paradise; Ibn Kathīr calls it warning and glad news. Al-Biqāʿī says it gathers Torah's justice and Psalms' renunciation and benevolence. Study Quran says it remains beneficial and leads to the straight path. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 18:1/q09/p2 Its unmatched composition and concise, endlessly fruitful speech establish its inimitability. — Ibn Qutayba says opponents cannot match it, recitation never wearies, benefits and wonders never end, and compact wording resembles the Prophet's comprehensive speech. Al-Kashshāf says eloquent Arabs could not rival even its shortest sūra despite opposition and resorted to swords. Sāmarrāʾī says comparisons of wording, order, omission, letters and vowels convinced him it cannot be human speech, although earlier explanations seemed forced. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; KASHSHAF-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SUYUTI-TANASUQ]

## 18:1/q10 Is the Qur'an created or uncreated according to this wording?  [أَنزَلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ عِوَجَا · theology]
turns on: Whether descent and being made upright describe an originated Book, or the absence of crookedness points to uncreatedness.
- 18:1/q10/p1 It is uncreated, as a reported gloss of having no crookedness. — Reports ascribed to Ibn ʿAbbās apply the phrase and 39:28 to uncreatedness; Baghawī presents it as another view. Al-Zarkashī also attributes uncreatedness to Ibn ʿAbbās while cataloguing Qur'anic names. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURHAN(ابن عباس)]
- 18:1/q10/p2 It is created: descent, compilation, bestowed benefit and being made upright imply a produced, changeable thing. — Al-Qāḍī reasons from 'sent down', letters and words, praise for a benefit, and attributes unavailable to the eternal; Study Quran identifies the view as Muʿtazilite over against Ashʿarite uncreatedness. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي); STUDYQURAN(al-Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār)]

## 18:1/q11 What theological inference is drawn from praising and calling the Book upright?  [ٱلْحَمْدُ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ عِوَجَا · theology]
- 18:1/q11/p1 Its guidance presupposes human freedom and does not intend to cause unbelief. — Al-Qāḍī argues benefit from commands requires independent choice to act or refrain; he says a Book intended to cause some disbelief could hardly be called upright. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 18:1/q11/p2 Descent from above is taken to imply God's location above. — Al-Rāzī reports anthropomorphists' appeal to both revelation and the Night Journey, referring his answer to the throne discussion at 7:54. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(المشبهة)]

## 18:1/q12 What does ʿiwaj mean when the Book lacks it?  [عِوَجَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is taken narrowly as contradiction or broadly as every defect and deviation.
- 18:1/q12/p1 Inconsistency, variation, or internal contradiction. — Muqātil says 'different'; al-Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, Asad, and Study Quran cite 4:82 as a parallel. Ibn Isḥāq describes an upright, moderate Book. Al-Wāḥidī, al-Nasafī, Bayḍāwī and others mention no contradictory meanings. [17 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs, Z); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:1/q12/p2 Any defect, deviation from truth or wisdom, obscurity, falsehood, or misguidance. — ʿAlī b. ʿĪsā says deviation toward corruption; al-Wāḥidī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's 'confusion'. Al-Kashshāf extends it beyond contradiction; al-Rāzī also describes truth of creed, prophethood and law and guidance away from bodily concerns. Al-Ālūsī includes grammar, eloquence and false worship; Ṭabāṭabāʾī uses the indefinite negation for universal freedom from errors of language, proof, guidance, judgment and narrative, citing 41:42. Āsād lists tortuousness, distortion and deviousness. Qāshānī reads no turning toward another, paralleling the Prophet's undiverted sight at 53:17. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL(علي بن عيسى); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن الوالبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭs); ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:1/q12/p3 Crookedness or convolutedness in the basic lexical sense. — Al-Suyūṭī gives this gloss. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 18:1/q13 How do ʿiwaj and ʿawaj differ?  [عِوَجَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the kasra and fatḥa forms encode an abstract versus physical distinction; 20:107 challenges a strict division.
- 18:1/q13/p1 Kasra generally concerns immaterial or subtle crookedness, while fatḥa concerns visible physical crookedness. — Al-Zajjāj cites religion and a stick; Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes roads, al-Qurṭubī thought and affairs, and al-Ālūsī reports the distinction. Al-Biqāʿī applies it to the Book's meanings. Al-Ālūsī tests 20:107's physical earth and reports a reply about subtle unevenness, though its 'you do not see' suggests sight; Ibn al-Sikkīt makes kasra broader. [8 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:1/q13/p2 They have no difference in meaning. — Al-Ālūsī reports al-Marzūqī's choice in his commentary on al-Faṣīḥ. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(المرزوقي)]

## 18:1/q14 What does 'for it' refer to, and what does the preposition suggest?  [لَّهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Which earlier masculine noun supplies the pronoun's antecedent.
- 18:1/q14/p1 It refers to the Book; 'for' emphatically denies any possible crookedness in it. — Abū Ḥayyān says this is the apparent antecedent. Al-Biqāʿī says 'for' denies even possibility of distortion because revelation comes from the Knower of the unseen; Ṭabāṭabāʾī also takes the pronoun to the Book. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:1/q14/p2 It may refer to the Prophet. — Abū Ḥayyān records this less apparent proposal while discussing whether qayyiman modifies that pronoun. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 18:1/q15 How are the no-crookedness clause and qayyiman constructed?  [وَلَمْ يَجْعَل لَّهُۥ عِوَجَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the apparent order is transposed, circumstantial, coordinated inside the relative clause, or completed by an omitted verb; whether an intervening clause can separate qayyiman from its owner.
- 18:1/q15/p1 The wording is transposed in sense: God sent down an upright Book and made no crookedness in it; qayyiman is circumstantial. — Ibn Qutayba, grammarians quoted by al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Mujāhid and Ibn ʿAbbās report this. Al-ʿAskarī says eloquence places the important first. Al-Rāghib counts it as ambiguity of word order. Al-Zarkashī says Rāzī's intrinsic-versus-extrinsic objection confuses syntactic order with order of truth; al-Wāḥidī calls this the linguists' and exegetes' view. [15 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; NAHHAS(الأخفش سعيد، الكسائي، الفراء، أبو عبيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة والتفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(العلماء باللغة والتفسير); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش والكسائي والفراء وأبو عبيد وجمهور المتأولين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,العسكري); BURHAN(ابن عباس,الواحدي); ITQAN(الراغب,مجاهد); RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)-; BURHAN(فخر الدين الرازي)-]
- 18:1/q15/p2 The clause follows 'sent down' in the relative clause; an implicit verb governs qayyiman, as though 'but made it upright'. — Al-Zamakhsharī avoids separating a circumstantial from its owner by relative-clause material. Abū Ḥayyān reports this; al-Zarkashī finds coordination within the relative clause most apparent and notes an answer that coordination avoids interruption. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this against context. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURHAN(غير ابن المنير); BURHAN(الزمخشري)+; KASHSHAF-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 18:1/q15/p3 The no-crookedness clause and qayyiman are circumstantial descriptions of the Book in the stated order. — Al-Kirmānī and al-Aṣbahānī give sequential circumstantials; Ibn al-Munayyir says both clauses describe the same Book, an analysis earlier scholars also gave. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers it from context. Al-Zarkashī records a clause governed by 'sent down' and another as independent coordination, which he prefers because denial is absolute rather than limited to revelation. Qatāda supplies 'but made it upright'. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأصبهاني,الكرماني); BURHAN(ابن المنير,جماعة); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN+; TABATABAI+]
- 18:1/q15/p4 Qayyiman modifies the pronoun in lahu, as an emphatic stable or temporary circumstance. — Abū Ḥayyān proposes this; al-Zarkashī reports a circumstantial of the pronoun. Ṭabāṭabāʾī records and rejects it on contextual grounds. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN; TABATABAI-]
- 18:1/q15/p5 Qayyiman substitutes for the negated clause or for ʿiwajan. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Ḥall al-ʿIqd's substitute for the clause, noting grammatical dispute. Al-Zarkashī finds substitution for ʿiwajan unclear, and says opposites cannot qualify one another. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب حل العقد); BURHAN(بعضهم)-]
- 18:1/q15/p6 The stated order is meaningful: denying intrinsic defect precedes describing the Book's benefit to others. — Al-Rāzī rejects transposition as rationally impossible; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Zarkashī report it, and al-Zarkashī objects that this conflates syntax with temporal or logical priority. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبد الله الرازي); BURHAN(فخر الدين الرازي)+; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي)+; BURHAN-]

## 18:1/q16 How is the hāʾ pronoun read in ʿabdihi and similar forms?  [عَبْدِهِۦ · readings]
- 18:1/q16/p1 Before a silent consonant after kasra or silent yāʾ, it has kasra with no linking vowel, as in ʿabdihi. — Ibn al-Jazarī says all readers follow this rule; before a vowel, prior fatḥa or ḍamma yields wāw-linking and prior kasra yāʾ-linking; before a sākin after other sounds it has ḍamma without linking. He notes Ibn Kathīr's linking after a sākin before a vowel and Ḥafṣ's exceptions at 18:63, 48:10 and 25:69. [5 notes: NASHR(ابن كثير,حفص)]

## 18:1/q17 Why and how is a pause made after ʿiwajan?  [عِوَجَا · readings]
- 18:1/q17/p1 Ḥafṣ pauses briefly without taking breath, separating qayyiman from ʿiwajan so uprightness cannot be mistaken as a description of crookedness. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya also notes his pause at marqadnā, 36:52; Abū Ḥayyān calls a Companion codex's 'but made it upright' explanatory, not Qur'anic. Qatāda, Ibn al-Munayyir, Bursevî and al-Zarkashī explain the semantic and syntactic risk; al-Zarkashī cites other indefinite noun plus adjective examples, 1:6 and 12:2. Qiraat-ER says pause affects whether the two verses read as one sentence or two. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN(ابن المنير,حفص عن عاصم,قتادة); ITQAN; NASHR(حفص); BURSEVI; QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:1/q17/p2 A sound decision about pausing requires grammar, readings, exegesis, reports, and knowledge of revelation-era Arabic. — Ibn Mujāhid states this general criterion in the discussion of the pause. [1 notes: BURHAN(أبو بكر بن مجاهد)]

## 18:1/q18 Why is 'Book' placed after 'upon His servant'?  [عَلَىٰ عَبْدِهِ ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · rhetoric]
- 18:1/q18/p1 Delaying the direct object lets the following no-crookedness clause attach immediately to 'Book'. — Al-Ālūsī gives this word-order explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:1/q19 What questioners or event prompted the opening?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes report both the Jewish objection and a delayed reply to Quraysh; they emphasize different aspects of the opening's occasion.
- 18:1/q19/p1 Jewish questioners objected that Muhammad's Book came differently from the Torah's separately revealed sections. — Muqātil reports their objection. [1 notes: MUQATIL(اليهود)]
- 18:1/q19/p2 After revelation paused, the opening answered Quraysh's questions about the Cave youths and rebuked their wish for the Prophet's failure. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya emphasizes their seeing blessing descend on him. Ibn al-Zamalkānī says the delayed answer reassured him that God had not withdrawn favor. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it consoles him and then turns from worldly adornment to the youths. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ITQAN(ابن الزملكاني); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 18:1/q20 What does qayyiman mean of the Book?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ عِوَجَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether qayyiman primarily confirms intrinsic straightness, describes authority over scriptures, or denotes guidance of human affairs.
- 18:1/q20/p1 Upright, just, flawless, moderate, or balanced, confirming the denial of crookedness. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as just; al-Ḍaḥḥāk adds wisdom with no error, corruption or contradiction, which al-Qurṭubī commends. Al-Kashshāf says the double assertion rules out subtle defects, and Abū Ḥayyān attributes its confirmatory force to Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Ḍaḥḥāk. Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports balance without excess or deficiency; Ibn Isḥāq says moderate. [10 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); THALABI(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك)+]
- 18:1/q20/p2 It upholds, verifies, witnesses to, or abrogates earlier scriptures. — Al-Farrāʾ says it verifies other books; al-Thaʿlabī reports its authority and abrogation of their laws; al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī record variants including preserving and confirming them. [6 notes: FARRA; THALABI(الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI]
- 18:1/q20/p3 It orders people's interests through right belief, good action, laws, and welfare in both lives. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this in context and cites 46:30 and 30:43; al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān report people's laws and interests. Al-Qurṭubī also reports the Book as enduring proof. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI+]

## 18:1/q21 What larger purpose does the sūra's opening announce?  [* · interpretation]
- 18:1/q21/p1 It confirms revelation and the Prophet's mission while warning rejecters and promising believers reward. — Ibn Isḥāq says it answers deniers of prophethood; al-Wāḥidī says the ensuing verses rebuke Quraysh's claim that angels are God's daughters; Ibn ʿĀshūr says it confronts polytheists and their scriptural informants and denial of divine offspring. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says true belief and good action frame the sūra, with special rejection of divine child claims. İslâhî says warning and glad tidings are the Prophet's task and unbelief is no failure of Book or preaching. [5 notes: IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 18:1/q21/p2 It tests attachment to worldly beauty and uses stories to teach piety, faith, and accountability. — Asad points to the rich and poor men's parable. Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the Cave youths, Moses and companion, and Dhū al-Qarnayn as unusual narratives against association. İslâhî warns Quraysh against worldly success, calls the Prophet and companions to patience amid late Makkan hostility and possible migration, and says transient ornament explains people's blindness. Al-Biqāʿī says the Moses-Khiḍr story corrects Israelites' pride in answering Quraysh; the spirit question links al-Isrāʾ's unseen theme and Qur'an as greater spirit. [9 notes: ASAD-NOTES; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 18:1/q21/p3 Its connected narratives repeatedly warn of judgment, punishment, and resurrection. — Study Quran says the close emphasizes God's inexhaustible Word and the human, inspired Prophet at 18:109–110. Al-Biqāʿī sees earlier peoples' destruction as warning through responsible life, then a barzakh and resurrection sequence; the group from al-Kahf to Saba emphasizes resurrection. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:1/q22 How is al-Kahf's place among the sūras explained?  [* · links]
- 18:1/q22/p1 Its praise opening mirrors key divisions of the Qur'an and the sequence of praise-opened sūras. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it opens the Qur'an's second half with al-ḥamdu li-llāh, echoing al-Fātiḥa and the approximate fourth-quarter opening in 35:1. Al-Biqāʿī reports al-Taftāzānī's four blessings: first creation and preservation, second creation and preservation; al-Fātiḥa spans all and al-Anʿām first creation, while al-Kahf first preservation through prophets and scripture. He maps childhood preparation, accountable life, intermediate state, and final creation across the praise-opened sūras. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(سعد الدين التفتازاني)]
- 18:1/q22/p2 It continues and redirects the prior sūra's themes. — İslâhî says its beginning takes up the previous sūra's conclusion and shifts from exposing Jews to exposing Christians; Sāmarrāʾī sees al-Isrāʾ framed by glorification and praise. Al-Biqāʿī connects the spirit question to the hidden realm and the Qur'an. [4 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:1/q23 Where and when was al-Kahf revealed?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reported exception lists differ about which parts of this generally Meccan sūra are Medinan.
- 18:1/q23/p1 The whole sūra is Meccan, or late Meccan. — Al-Ālūsī attributes the majority view to Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr and notes al-Dānī's preference; Anas reports a single descent with seventy thousand angels. İslâhî locates it in late Makkan conflict; Study Quran likewise calls it late Makkan. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 18:1/q23/p2 It is Meccan apart from verse 38 and verses 83–101, which are Medinan. — Al-Kashshāf gives this exception list and says it followed al-Ghāshiya in revelation. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 18:1/q23/p3 Its opening through verse 8 and its closing passage from verse 107 are exceptions to Meccan revelation. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this to Muqātil; Study Quran mentions a minority considering the first eight verses Medinan. Al-Itqān lists the opening through 'barren ground', 'keep patient company', and the last passage as exceptions. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(مقاتل); STUDYQURAN; ITQAN]

## 18:1/q24 How many verses does al-Kahf contain?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Regional verse-counting conventions.
- 18:1/q24/p1 110 in the Kufan count. — Al-Kashshāf gives 110; al-Ālūsī specifies the Kufan tradition. [2 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:1/q24/p2 111 in the Basran count. — Al-Ālūsī reports the regional count. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:1/q24/p3 106 in the Syrian count. — Al-Ālūsī reports the regional count. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:1/q24/p4 105 in the Hijazi count. — Al-Ālūsī reports the regional count. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:1/q25 What names are reported for this sūra?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports give distinct names, and the barrier report is graded objectionable.
- 18:1/q25/p1 The Companions of the Cave. — Al-Ālūsī cites a report collected by Ibn Mardawayh. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:1/q25/p2 The barrier, as named in the Torah. — Al-Ālūsī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's statement that it protects the reciter from fire, and al-Bayhaqī grades the report munkar. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 18:1/q26 What recitations or memorization of al-Kahf are commended?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports specify different times, verse counts, benefits, and one explicitly weak chain.
- 18:1/q26/p1 Friday recitation brings light and forgiveness. — Ibn ʿUmar's report promises light from feet to sky until resurrection and forgiveness between Fridays; Abū Saʿīd's reports promise light between reader and Sacred House. Al-Ālūsī says imams recommend Friday and its eve and repetition. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري,ابن عمر)]
- 18:1/q26/p2 Reciting the sūra nightly protects the home from Satan. — Al-Ālūsī reports ʿAbd Allāh b. Mughaffal's claim and separately al-Ḥasan b. ʿAlī's nightly practice. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الحسن بن علي,عبد الله بن مغفل)]
- 18:1/q26/p3 Memorizing the first ten verses protects against al-Dajjāl. — Al-Ālūsī reports Abū al-Dardāʾ's version; Study Quran also records first and last ten as protection from grave trials or the Dajjāl. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء); STUDYQURAN(Reports in the source)]
- 18:1/q26/p4 Reciting the final ten verses protects against al-Dajjāl. — Al-Ālūsī gives the alternative Abū al-Dardāʾ version; Study Quran mentions first and last ten. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء); STUDYQURAN(Reports in the source)]
- 18:1/q26/p5 Reciting three opening verses protects against al-Dajjāl. — Al-Ālūsī says al-Tirmidhī authenticated this further report. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو الدرداء)]
- 18:1/q26/p6 Reciting the final five before sleep brings a divinely chosen night awakening. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this to ʿĀʾisha and says he tested it repeatedly. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عائشة)]
- 18:1/q26/p7 Praise including 18:1 is recited after completing the Qur'an. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn's Prophetic supplication; al-Bayhaqī calls its chain disconnected and weak. [2 notes: NASHR(البيهقي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 18:1/q27 What broader names are given to the Qur'an in connection with 'Book'?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · sciences]
turns on: A core four-name scheme versus an expanded catalogue of descriptive designations.
- 18:1/q27/p1 Qur'an, Furqān, Kitāb and Dhikr are four scriptural names. — Al-Ṭabarī says each has distinct Arabic usage, citing 12:3, 27:76, 25:1 and 15:9. Al-Māwardī lists the same four with 18:1 among citations. [2 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:1/q27/p2 A wider Qur'anic catalogue includes many descriptive names and titles. — Al-Zarkashī lists admonition, reminder, noble, wisdom, guardian, blessed, God's rope, straight path, qayyim, decisive word, great news, best discourse, revelation, spirit, inspiration, seven oft-repeated verses, Arabic, connected speech, insight, knowledge, truth, guidance, wonder, firmest bond, similitudes and truthfulness, with supporting verses. [3 notes: BURHAN]

## 18:1/q28 How do clear and obscure Qur'anic passages relate to the Book's accessibility?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · sciences]
- 18:1/q28/p1 The clear is accessible to everyone; scholars interpret the obscure. — Al-Māwardī says this lets all share the miracle while retaining degrees of distinction; he focuses his commentary on obscure material, using transmission and reasoning, marking conjectures, and omitting apparent meanings. He adds foundational chapters on hard interpretations and hidden evidence. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 18:1/q28/p2 Ambiguity can arise in word, whole wording, meaning, or both. — Al-Rāghib distinguishes unequivocally clear, unequivocally ambiguous and mixed passages; rare or polysemous words, concision, expansion and word order, divine attributes and resurrection, and circumstances, quantity or legal force all supply cases. Al-Suyūṭī warns against chasing difficulties before foundations, comparing the attitude to pagans demanding other signs. [8 notes: ITQAN(الراغب)]

## 18:1/q29 Why can Qur'anic narratives and expressions repeat without compromising the Book?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · rhetoric]
- 18:1/q29/p1 Repetition spreads prophetic warnings, aids retention, emphasis and understanding, and varies discourse. — Ibn Qutayba says scattered tribes taught different sūras thereby hear every prophet's story; practical duties were instead conveyed by letters detailing prayer, alms, fasting and pilgrimage. The need eased as Islam spread and the Qur'an was collected. Arabic repeats for force, exemplified by al-Kāfirūn and al-Raḥmān; he also discusses emphatic oaths and concise implied negation. [5 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 18:1/q30 What related reports concern early possession of the Qur'an?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · reports]
turns on: The notes transmit two different claims about completion and memorization.
- 18:1/q30/p1 Only ʿUthmān among the caliphs completed it. — Ibn Qutayba reports this claim. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]
- 18:1/q30/p2 ʿAlī died without having memorized it. — Ibn Qutayba reports al-Shaʿbī's sworn assertion. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشعبي)]

## 18:1/q31 What related Arabic examples are brought into discussion of Qur'anic wording?  [* · meaning]
turns on: The two notes give different lexical illustrations.
- 18:1/q31/p1 A poem uses nāʾ for a man leaning over a bow. — Ibn Qutayba reports al-Farrāʾ's citation. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الفراء)]
- 18:1/q31/p2 The expression mā sāʾaka wa-nāʾaka is explained through sound assimilation. — Ibn Qutayba derives nāʾaka from anāʾaka by dropping initial alif after sāʾaka, comparing hanaʾanī wa-maraʾanī; in isolation, it would be amraʾanī. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL]

## 18:1/q32 How are revelation locations and Makkan or Medinan status determined?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Definitions depend on physical place or addressees; the report's Syrian location has two identifications.
- 18:1/q32/p1 By place of descent, including nearby sites under Mecca or Medina. — Al-Suyūṭī says travel revelations may fit neither label; Minā, ʿArafāt and al-Ḥudaybiyya count with Mecca, while Badr, Uḥud and Silāʿ count with Medina. Qāḍī Abū Bakr grounds knowledge in Companions and Successors; Ibn Masʿūd claims to know every verse's place and occasion, and ʿIkrima points to Silāʿ. A report from Ibn ʿAbbās counts twenty-seven Medinan sūras. [6 notes: ITQAN(أبو بكر,ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,عكرمة)]
- 18:1/q32/p2 By the people addressed, Meccans or Medinans. — Al-Suyūṭī says this is how he understands a report from Ibn Masʿūd. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 18:1/q32/p3 Revelation occurred in Mecca, Medina and Syria, interpreted as Jerusalem or Tabuk. — A report from Abū Umāma supplies three places; al-Walīd says Syria means Jerusalem, while Ibn Kathīr prefers Tabuk. [2 notes: ITQAN(أبو أمامة); ITQAN(ابن كثير)+]

## 18:1/q33 What wider disciplines or structures are invoked to study the Book's arrangement?  [ٱلْكِتَٰبَ · sciences]
- 18:1/q33/p1 Its composition, verse order, sūra links and rhetorical devices form an intentionally wise system. — Al-Suyūṭī describes divine arrangement, eloquence and proofs; his Asrār al-Tanzīl studies how each sūra elaborates its predecessor, opening-to-purpose fit, beginnings and endings, interrelated verses, address shifts, metaphor, metonymy, wordplay, antithesis, concision and expansion. Al-Zarkashī reports Ibn Mujāhid's requirement of grammar, readings, exegesis, narrative and Arabic for pauses. [12 notes: SUYUTI-TANASUQ; BURHAN(أبو بكر بن مجاهد)]
- 18:1/q33/p2 The Qur'an is classified by thirty interpretive modes, including place, abrogation, clarity, order, ellipsis, legal force, reports and rhetoric. — Al-Suyūṭī quotes an early authority's catalogue, illustrating Makkan with 73:10 and Medinan with 2:190; he separately describes fronting and postponement as a class of apparently difficult verses. [4 notes: ITQAN(بعض الأقدمين)]
- 18:1/q33/p3 The presence of 'Book' here challenges an account of disjointed letters as mere attention signals. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says most letter-opened sūras mention the Qur'an, with three exceptions, and sees a clue to inimitability; she reports al-Rāzī's explanation that initials alert listeners to weighty revelation but rejects its generality because al-Kahf opens with Book without initials. [4 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفخر الرازي)]

## 18:1/q34 What rank is given to Muhammad and those who carry the Qur'an?  [* · interpretation]
- 18:1/q34/p1 Muhammad is God's beloved, protected, unlettered Messenger, named in earlier scriptures. — Al-Kashshāf invokes blessings on him, his family, successors, Emigrants and Helpers, and describes him as supported by wisdom and named in Torah and Gospel. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 18:1/q34/p2 The Qur'an's recipient is the best prophet and its bearers, reciters and teachers are especially honored. — Ibn al-Jazarī argues from its status as the greatest revealed Book to the excellence of its prophet and community; he cites Ibn ʿAbbās's report that the Prophet called its bearers the community's noblest. [2 notes: NASHR(ابن عباس عن النبي)]
