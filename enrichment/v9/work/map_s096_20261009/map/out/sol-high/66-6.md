# 66:6: 43 questions

## 66:6/q01 What should a reader make of the address to believers?  [يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether faith here identifies sincere believers or includes merely verbal profession.
- 66:6/q01/p1 It calls believers to heed a command or prohibition and reform themselves and their households. — Ibn Masʿūd expects a command or prohibition after this address; al-Burhān urges pause, reflection, acceptance, and repentance. Biqāʿī says faith here is acknowledged faith and takes the young as first addressed. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 66:6/q01/p2 The address includes the Prophet in the believers' duty. — Al-Zuhrī says the Prophet is among believers whenever they are commanded to act. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(الزهري)]
- 66:6/q01/p3 The address can include merely verbal believers, namely hypocrites. — Al-Kashshāf cites the ensuing address to unbelievers at 66:7. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 66:6/q01/p4 Quranic addresses to believers correspond to addresses to the poor in the Torah. — Khaythama makes this comparison. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(خيثمة)]

## 66:6/q02 What does قُوا۟ mean?  [قُوٓا۟ · meaning]
turns on: Whether 'protecting' names direct safeguarding or admonition figuratively.
- 66:6/q02/p1 Guard or preserve yourselves from harm and the Fire. — Jishumī defines wiqāya as guarding and preserving; al-Māwardī adduces a rajaz line for protection; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says to interpose a safeguard; Ṭabāṭabāʾī defines protection against injury; Bursevī calls it keeping safe and distant. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 66:6/q02/p2 The command can be understood as admonition and warning that save from punishment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats admonition either as the cause of protection or as a forceful metaphor equating the two. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 66:6/q03 How is the form قُوا۟ derived?  [قُوٓا۟ · grammar]
turns on: The proposed phonological environment versus a transitivity rule, and counterexamples.
- 66:6/q03/p1 It comes from وقى يقي; the initial wāw drops between yāʾ and kasra. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the underlying وقى يوقي. The Basrans cite the vowel environment and contrast يؤجل with following fatḥa; al-Samīn traces later deletions by analogy with the jussive. Al-Naḥḥās rebuts the rival rule with intransitive ورم يرم and يثق; Basrans also cite وسع يسع and ولغ يلغ, accounting for the guttural-induced vowel in وسع. [5 notes: NAHHAS(البصريون); SAMIN-DURR; NAHHAS+]
- 66:6/q03/p2 The initial wāw drops in transitive verbs to distinguish them from intransitives. — The Kufans compare وعد يعد with وجل يوجل. Makkī objects with intransitive يرم; al-Samīn replies that يوجل lacks a wāw between yāʾ and kasra and distinguishes يضع and يسع. [2 notes: NAHHAS(الكوفيون); SAMIN-DURR(الكوفيون); NAHHAS-; SAMIN-DURR(مكي)-]
- 66:6/q03/p3 The command forms are singular قِ, dual قِيا, and plural قُوا. — Jishumī compares the formation with الوفاء. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 66:6/q04 Why does the verse say أَنفُسَكُمْ instead of using a reflexive object pronoun?  [أَنفُسَكُمْ · grammar]
- 66:6/q04/p1 Arabic expresses the reflexive object separately, as in أكرم نفسك. — Sībawayh gives the usage; al-Mubarrad says the same thing cannot be agent and patient at once. Al-Naḥḥās says Kufans inconsistently reject the construction while citing عدمتني, whereas Basrans reject such forms. [3 notes: NAHHAS(الكوفيون,سيبويه,محمد بن يزيد)]

## 66:6/q05 What does أَنفُسَكُمْ refer to?  [أَنفُسَكُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether 'self' is individual, includes offspring, or covers a coordinated subject under the variant.
- 66:6/q05/p1 It denotes the person's own self. — Bursevī says نفس is the human person rather than the commanding lower self. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 66:6/q05/p2 Children may be counted within the parent's self. — Some scholars cite 24:61 and the child's being part of the parent; Abū Ḥayyān, Ālūsī and Elmalılı report the same while still requiring instruction in lawful and unlawful matters. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI]
- 66:6/q05/p3 It includes both addressed believers and their families under the ahlūkum reading. — Al-Bayḍāwī says second-person address prevails for the combined group; al-Zamakhsharī also keeps subject and object together. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]

## 66:6/q06 How must believers protect themselves from the Fire?  [قُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the protective act is outward obedience broadly or specifically guarding against the lower soul.
- 66:6/q06/p1 Obey God, perform duties, and avoid sin; cultivate reverence and prophetic conduct. — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid and Qatāda give obedience and fear; Ibn Qutayba adds obeying the Messenger; Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq frame it as nearness to God and distance from sin. Biqāʿī specifies manners toward Creator then creation and gentleness; Rāzī includes refraining from prohibitions. Bursevī includes counsel, discipline, and teaching. - 66:6/q06/p2 Guard against the evil promptings of one's lower soul. — Rāzī reports the view, grounded in the soul's command to evil. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(قيل)]

## 66:6/q07 Who belongs to أَهْلِيكُمْ?  [أَهْلِيكُمْ · referent]
turns on: The ordinary household and dependants versus spiritual attachment.
- 66:6/q07/p1 One's family and dependants, including spouses, children, kin, and servants or enslaved people. — Muqātil, Muqātil b. Ḥayyān, and later commentators include children and male and female slaves; Bursevī defines dependants supported by a man, wife, kin, children, servant or companions; Asad broadens ahl to people sharing race, religion, or occupation. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل بن حيان); QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء,مقاتل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الضحاك ومقاتل); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 66:6/q07/p2 True family consists of those joined by spiritual attachment and love, whether kin or not. — Al-Qāshānī says loved companions and inner powers accompany a person in this world and the next; Bursevī transmits the same. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 66:6/q08 What does protection of the family require?  [وَأَهْلِيكُمْ · law]

## 66:6/q09 Why is the household included and given priority?  [أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Household responsibility and proximity versus the inward bond of love.
- 66:6/q09/p1 Householders are accountable for those in their care, and nearest kin receive instruction first. — Al-Qurṭubī and Bursevī invoke the shepherd hadith; Abū Ḥayyān calls the householder caretaker. Jishumī says prophets' concern for offspring centers on religion. Al-Qushayrī and Biqāʿī put enjoining right among nearest kin first; Bursevī cites 9:123 and 26:214 and notes greater authority at home. Kuran Yolu says the responsibility also strengthens family bonds. [15 notes: JISHUMI; QUSHAYRI; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,بعض العلماء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(الحديث); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 66:6/q09/p2 Those spiritually attached to one another influence each other's fate. — Al-Qāshānī says love ties companions in this life and the next; attachment to unpurified souls or lower faculties draws one into their abyss, while love of saints gathers one with them. Bursevī repeats this account. [7 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 66:6/q10 How is أَهْلِيكُمْ formed and related syntactically to أَنفُسَكُمْ?  [أَهْلِيكُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Coordination of two objects versus a comitative construction.
- 66:6/q10/p1 It is accusative, coordinated with أَنفُسَكُمْ. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the parse; al-Farrāʾ says yāʾ marks the case as in رأيت الزيدين and the nūn falls in annexation. Bursevī derives it from أهلين and notes irregular plural أالي; al-Farrāʾ reports feminine-referent أهله. [4 notes: NAHHAS(الفراء); BURSEVI]
- 66:6/q10/p2 It is a comitative object: protect yourselves together with your families. — Al-Kirmānī gives this grammatical parse. [1 notes: ITQAN(الكرماني)]

## 66:6/q11 What is the variant reading of وَأَهْلِيكُمْ and how is it construed?  [وَأَهْلِيكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether 'families' is an object or a coordinated subject and whether the latter parse is syntactically valid.
- 66:6/q11/p1 وأهلوكم makes 'your families' nominative and coordinates it with the imperative's subject. — Al-Kashshāf, Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn cite the reading; intervening object material licenses the coordination. Al-Bayḍāwī says 'yourselves' covers both groups by dominance of second person; al-Zamakhsharī reads 'you and your families guard yourselves'. Abū Ḥayyān objects that an explicit noun cannot be raised by the addressee's imperative, pointing to al-Zamakhsharī's own earlier separate-verb construction. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 66:6/q11/p2 The standard wording coordinates 'your families' as an accusative object with 'yourselves'. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the accusative parse; Ibn al-ʿArabī says the conjunction makes both objects share the command; al-Qurṭubī adduces Arabic examples of coordinated objects governed by one verb. [3 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)+]

## 66:6/q12 What is the grammatical role of نَارًا?  [نَارًۭا · grammar]
- 66:6/q12/p1 It is the second object of قُوا۟. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Samīn identify it so. [2 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 66:6/q13 Which Fire is meant by نَارًا?  [نَارًۭا · referent]
turns on: Literal eschatological Fire versus an inward spiritual interpretation.
- 66:6/q13/p1 It is the Fire of Hell and the afterlife. — Muqātil says afterlife Fire; al-Dāmghānī cites a parallel in Āl ʿImrān; Ibn Kathīr calls the fuel human bodies; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 40:72; Kuran Yolu points to 2:24. [5 notes: MUQATIL; DAMGHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 66:6/q13/p2 It is an inward fire of divine overpowering and estrangement. — Al-Qāshānī says attachment to low desires and bodily density supplies its fuel; al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya describes selves forgetting the primordial covenant and hardened hearts with animal, predatory and satanic traits. [2 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 66:6/q14 Why is the Fire indefinite and described with this fuel?  [نَارًۭا وَقُودُهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the fuel description chiefly emphasizes terror or defines a distinctive kind of Fire.
- 66:6/q14/p1 The indefiniteness magnifies the Fire, and naming people and stones intensifies the warning. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says stones replacing firewood horrifies and evokes polytheists' fate, citing 21:98; Biqāʿī pairs the softest and hardest things so anything between is included. Al-Māwardī and Bursevī reason that burning stones shows heat intense enough to burn people; Bursevī cites a report that earthly fire is one seventieth of Hellfire. Al-Burhān contrasts the indefinite Meccan warning with definite Medinan Baqara after prior introduction. Al-Qushayrī reports a wordplay linking people and clods through earthly origin, and a prayer to become a stone spared from the Fire. [11 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(رسول الله); IBNASHUR-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
- 66:6/q14/p2 The wording marks a distinctive Fire fueled by people and stones rather than ordinary wood. — Al-Kashshāf, Rāzī and al-Nasafī say this kind of fire uses only these two fuels; al-Bayḍāwī compares its blazing with wood-fed fire. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 66:6/q15 How does the verse relate to earlier descriptions of Hellfire?  [نَارًۭا وَقُودُهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ · links]
turns on: Whether the address is to sinful believers, potential apostates, or hypocrites.
- 66:6/q15/p1 It reprises the human-and-stone fuel warning in 2:24 and the fate of idols in 21:98. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Muslims already knew both warnings; several commentators refer back to their Baqara discussions. Ibn Kathīr, al-Māwardī and others use 21:98 for the idol possibility. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 66:6/q15/p2 The warning to believers is striking because Hell was prepared for disbelievers, yet sinful believers can share its abode. — Al-Kashshāf and Rāzī say sinful believers occupy higher levels in the same abode; Bursevī accepts this and calls apostasy unlikely. Al-Kashshāf and Rāzī also allow an apostasy warning. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI+]
- 66:6/q15/p3 The warning can specifically address apostasy or merely verbal believers. — Al-Kashshāf reports both alternatives, citing 66:7 for hypocrites; Rāzī allows the apostasy warning; Bursevī rejects its likelihood. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI-]

## 66:6/q16 What does وَقُودُهَا mean?  [وَقُودُهَا · meaning]
turns on: The vowel of wāw and whether it changes noun of material into verbal noun.
- 66:6/q16/p1 With fatḥa on the wāw it names the combustible material or firewood. — Al-Zajjāj contrasts fatḥa with ḍamma; al-Qushayrī, Bursevī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give 'firewood'. Ibn Kathīr calls human bodies the fuel. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 66:6/q16/p2 With ḍamma it is the act of burning or ignition, or an intensive expression for fuel. — Al-Zajjāj and Bursevī call it a verbal noun; Bursevī also reports supplying 'cause of its fuel' or treating the usage intensively. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports one view making the two readings synonymous. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); BURSEVI; KASHSHAF-FULL]

## 66:6/q17 How is وَقُودُهَا read?  [وَقُودُهَا · readings]
turns on: The choice of fatḥa or ḍamma on wāw and whether their senses differ.
- 66:6/q17/p1 The majority read with fatḥa on wāw. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya identifies the majority; the resulting sense is the material used to light a fire, according to al-Zajjāj and others. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور); ZAJJAJ; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 66:6/q17/p2 Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, Ṭalḥa, ʿĪsā, al-Fayyāḍ b. Ghazwān and Abū Ḥaywa read with ḍamma. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names the readers and reports either synonymy or verbal-noun sense; al-Kashshāf, Rāzī and Bursevī also record this variant. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn refer to earlier Baqara discussions. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم,مجاهد والحسن وطلحة وعيسى والفياض بن غزوان وأبو حيوة); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 66:6/q18 How is وَقُودُهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ parsed?  [وَقُودُهَا ٱلنَّاسُ وَٱلْحِجَارَةُ · grammar]
- 66:6/q18/p1 وَقُودُهَا is subject, ٱلنَّاسُ predicate, and ٱلْحِجَارَةُ coordinated with ٱلنَّاسُ; the clause describes the Fire. — Al-Naḥḥās gives the subject, predicate, accusative-position adjective clause and coordination. Al-Samīn likewise treats this clause and عليها ملائكة as Fire descriptions, while allowing circumstantial clauses. [3 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 66:6/q19 Who are ٱلنَّاسُ as fuel?  [ٱلنَّاسُ · referent]
turns on: Whether the collective fuel term extends expressly to unbelieving jinn.
- 66:6/q19/p1 The condemned human beings themselves are cast into the Fire and burn as fuel. — Muqātil calls them its inhabitants; Ibn Kathīr says their bodies are firewood; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 40:72. Bursevī says people become fuel upon arriving while stones are there earlier. [6 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 66:6/q19/p2 The word includes unbelieving humans and jinn. — Bursevī says jinn are not named separately because the warning addresses humans and jinn follow human unbelievers in denial. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 66:6/q20 What are the stones that fuel the Fire?  [ٱلْحِجَارَةُ · referent]
turns on: Literal sulfur or stones generally, idols under 21:98, or hardened people metaphorically.
- 66:6/q20/p1 Sulfur stones. — Al-Zajjāj reports exegetes' view; Ibn Masʿūd, Mujāhid, Ibn ʿAbbās and others are cited across sources. Their rapid ignition and intense heat heighten punishment; Bursevī adds foul smell, smoke and adhesion to bodies; Mujāhid says worse than carrion. Muqātil describes stones hung on necks and burning faces. Al-Dāmghānī calls them an admonition as God willed. [12 notes: MUQATIL; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن مسعود ومجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); DAMGHANI(ابن مسعود); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود ومجاهد وأبو جعفر الباقر والسدي,مجاهد); BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]
- 66:6/q20/p2 Stone idols worshipped besides God. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn Kathīr cite 21:98 so worshippers see what led them to Hell; Study Quran says idols were made of stone. Bursevī adduces al-Kāshifī's Persian verses about idols and trusted objects of gold or silver. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 66:6/q20/p3 Stones of every kind, or ordinary stones themselves. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports all kinds of stone; Study Quran gives stones themselves as an alternative to idols. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 66:6/q20/p4 People whose hearts are like stone or harder. — Bursevī cites 2:74 for the metaphorical interpretation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 66:6/q21 What narratives illustrate the Fire's stones?  [ٱلْحِجَارَةُ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ in speaker, event, and size or effect of the stone.
- 66:6/q21/p1 One Hellstone is larger than all mountains on earth; an elder faints and later professes faith. — Al-Māwardī and Ibn Kathīr report the Prophet's statement, the elder's revival and promise of Paradise, with 14:14 cited. Ibn Kathīr says Ibn Abī Ḥātim called the report mursal and gharīb. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(رسول الله); IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 66:6/q21/p2 Jesus encounters a stone fearing it will become Hell's fuel. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports a moaning, weeping stone and suggests the story may come from Torah or Gospel, with the stone's speech rendered in Arabic. Al-Durr gives a different report: a suspended mountain asks Jesus to pray, is secured by God, and settles on earth. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى ابن مريم); DURR-FULL(عيسى عليه السلام)]
- 66:6/q21/p3 A young man collapses when the Prophet recites the verse; a Hellstone would melt the world's mountains. — Al-Durr says the Prophet comforted him and added that every inhabitant has a stone or devil. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 66:6/q22 What is the form of ٱلْحِجَارَةُ?  [ٱلْحِجَارَةُ · grammar]
- 66:6/q22/p1 It is an irregular plural of حجر. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the expected plural is أحجار; حجار follows فعال with a feminine ending, like بكارة and مهارة. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 66:6/q23 Who are the angels stationed over the Fire?  [عَلَيْهَا مَلَٰٓئِكَةٌ · referent]
turns on: Literal keepers of Hell versus an inward cosmological interpretation.
- 66:6/q23/p1 They are Hell's keepers or Zabāniya, commonly numbered nineteen with assistants. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Kashshāf, Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān and others give nineteen, often with assistants; Ibn ʿAbbās is cited in al-Wāḥidī and Study Quran cites 74:30. Al-Nasafī says they administer and torment; al-Qurṭubī says they were created from wrath and delight in punishment. Asad refers to his allegorical treatment of 74:27ff. [21 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 66:6/q23/p2 They symbolize celestial and spiritual powers acting in earthly affairs. — Al-Qāshānī identifies powers of seven planets and twelve zodiacal signs, with Mālik as corporeal nature; Bursevī transmits planetary, zodiacal and other bodily powers. Their action dominates souls entangled in matter. [4 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني)]

## 66:6/q24 What does عَلَيْهَا convey?  [عَلَيْهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Interpretive force of فوق/authority and grammatical attachment.
- 66:6/q24/p1 The angels have charge, authority and control over Hell and its inhabitants. — Biqāʿī says their control comes through God's discipline; Bursevī denies that it means physical position atop the Fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls على metaphorical and cites 2:5; al-Nasafī says it denotes administration and torment. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 66:6/q24/p2 It may modify the Fire as a descriptive phrase, or participate in a circumstantial clause. — Al-Samīn says عليها can be a modifier with ملائكة its subject, and allows the descriptive clauses to be circumstantial. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 66:6/q25 What do غِلَاظٌ and شِدَادٌ mean of the angels?  [غِلَاظٌۭ شِدَادٌۭ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjectives describe bodily constitution, harsh conduct, or nearly synonymous severity.
- 66:6/q25/p1 Harsh in heart, speech or treatment, and strong in body or action. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Baghawī emphasize harshness toward sinners; Ibn ʿAṭiyya compares 3:159. Al-Qurṭubī reports harsh speech and forceful action, severe seizure, or immense bodies. Al-Bayḍāwī gives harsh speech/strong action or nature/power. Abū Ḥayyān gives massive bodies or harsh conduct. Ibn ʿĀshūr derives the plurals from غليظ and شديد, describing roughness metaphorically and strength in harmful action. Ibn Kathīr says mercy was removed from their hearts and appearance is frightening. Bursevī says pleas cannot soften them. Al-Qāshānī reads their merciless strength as their innate constitution for overpowering, from which they take pleasure. [35 notes: NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; QASHANI]
- 66:6/q25/p2 They describe the angels' moral qualities and conduct rather than their spiritual substance. — Rāzī prefers this because angels are spirits; he allows severity toward God's enemies with mercy toward allies, citing 48:29. Ibn ʿĀshūr treats roughness metaphorically as treatment; Biqāʿī says severity is measured to sinners as God commands. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 66:6/q25/p3 Both words can describe severity toward unbelievers, nearly synonymous. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this view; it contrasts with differentiating hardness from physical strength. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعضهم)]

## 66:6/q26 What reports describe the keepers' size, number, and force?  [مَلَٰٓئِكَةٌ غِلَاظٌۭ شِدَادٌۭ · reports]
turns on: The reports give different spans, numbers and force.
- 66:6/q26/p1 A keeper's shoulders span a year's journey; a blow or shove drives seventy thousand into Hell. — Muqātil adds a mace blow scatters bones and a forty-year fall; Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by al-Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī. Bursevī gives a mouth a year's journey wide, while Kaʿb gives year-wide shoulders and a two-pronged club but says one push splits seven hundred thousand. Al-Baghawī and Biqāʿī give a shove or throw of seventy thousand. [8 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; DURR-FULL(كعب)]
- 66:6/q26/p2 A keeper's shoulders span east to west. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits a Prophetic report through Ibn Wahb and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
- 66:6/q26/p3 Nineteen keepers each have a two-hundred-year shoulder span. — Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī says they lack mercy, were created for punishment and crush with one blow. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني)]
- 66:6/q26/p4 Hell's first gate has four hundred thousand dark-faced keepers, followed by nineteen larger keepers and similar hosts at later gates. — ʿIkrima says gates are five hundred years of descent apart. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 66:6/q27 Why describe the angels as harsh and powerful?  [غِلَاظٌۭ شِدَادٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: The threat's force and the rhetoric of masculine agreement.
- 66:6/q27/p1 Their harshness makes punishment inexorable and follows God's order. — Muqātil says this counters Abū al-Ashdayn's boast of defeating seventeen guardians. Rāzī says pity cannot impede punishment; Ibn ʿAṭiyya connects it to disbelievers' inability to excuse themselves. Ibn ʿĀshūr says obedience after harshness balances aversion and shows the severity is commanded; Biqāʿī says it is measured to guilt. [6 notes: MUQATIL; RAZI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 66:6/q27/p2 Strength and severity shape masculine grammatical agreement in descriptions of angels. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī says masculine forms suit the harsher of two punishments, comparing 8:50, 47:27, 25:25, 41:30 and 97:4. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 66:6/q28 What does لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ say of the angels?  [لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: Personal obedience to commands versus an inward account of cosmological necessity.
- 66:6/q28/p1 They never refuse, exceed, diminish, or otherwise violate God's commands. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī specify no addition or omission; Ibn al-Jawzī reports not exceeding; Biqāʿī says always and contrasts fallible humans who forget times, pillars or conditions. Bursevī says they cannot be bribed, unlike royal agents. Elmalılı extends obedience to all angels; Asad understands subjection to divine cause and effect. Biqāʿī quotes a Prophetic exhortation, transmitted by Ibn Mājah and al-Ṭayālisī without grading, that humans should be upright even though they cannot attain perfection. [18 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; BIQAI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو وثوبات); BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 66:6/q28/p2 Their obedience is innate submission of lower powers to the Divine Presence. — Al-Qāshānī says the powers overpower bodies while being overpowered by God; this enables their effects in the world. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 66:6/q29 What does وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ add?  [وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Timing and completeness of the action, with grammatical reinforcement.
- 66:6/q29/p1 They carry out each command promptly, completely, at its appointed time, without delay or reluctance. — Al-Māwardī and al-Qurṭubī specify neither early nor late; Ibn Kathīr adds complete ability and no moment's delay; Bursevī says without omission or addition. Biqāʿī contrasts human lapses; al-Qāshānī reads continuous influence and power. [14 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 66:6/q29/p2 The imperfect verb presents renewed, ongoing execution; the passive leaves God understood as commander. — Biqāʿī explicitly draws both rhetorical implications. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 66:6/q30 How do the two clauses about angelic obedience relate?  [لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the difference is inward/outward, rhetorical emphasis, time, or object of duty.
- 66:6/q30/p1 The first concerns inward acceptance without refusal; the second outward, prompt performance. — Al-Kashshāf's distinction is repeated by Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and Ālūsī. Ālūsī compares 21:19–20: no rebellion, no weariness, full competence. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الكشاف); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL]
- 66:6/q30/p2 The second repeats or reinforces the first for emphatic praise. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it emphasis; Abū Ḥayyān reports that view; al-Suyūṭī notes affirmative reinforcement after negation, distinct from ellipsis. Ālūsī calls it antimetabole; Ibn ʿĀshūr says reiteration expands praise and makes obedience vivid while coordination treats senses as distinct. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ITQAN(الطيبي); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 66:6/q30/p3 The first concerns past obedience, the second future or continuing execution. — Al-Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī cite this temporal distinction; Bursevī says disobedience follows a command while punishment recurs in the future. [4 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI(القاضي,بعضهم); ALUSI-FULL(المحصول)]
- 66:6/q30/p4 The first concerns their own duties, the second what they are assigned to do to sinners. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this possible distinction. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 66:6/q31 How is مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ parsed?  [مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ · grammar]
turns on: Relative pronoun with an omitted link versus verbal noun or apposition.
- 66:6/q31/p1 ما is relative, with an omitted preposition or returning pronoun: what He commanded them concerning. — Al-Naḥḥās says a relative ما loses its return pronoun; al-Samīn reconstructs ما أمرهم به, with an accusative gap after preposition loss. Abū Ḥayyān also gives an omitted preposition. Al-Kashshāf gives 'in what He commands', comparing 20:93. [6 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 66:6/q31/p2 ما is a verbal noun or apposition expressing God's command. — Al-Naḥḥās says no return pronoun is needed; al-Samīn takes an inclusive apposition to God's name, لا يعصون أمره. Al-Kashshāf and Abū Ḥayyān compare an accusative appositive and 20:93; Bursevī calls it a supplement. [6 notes: NAHHAS; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]
- 66:6/q31/p3 The clause is an adjective in nominative position. — Al-Nasafī says لا يعصون الله is a nominative adjective clause and ما أمرهم an accusative substitute. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 66:6/q32 What does the angels' obedience imply about their moral status and duties?  [لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ · theology]
turns on: Moral assignment and grace, delight, or innate cosmological subjection.
- 66:6/q32/p1 Angels are charged with divine commands and protected from disobedience. — Rāzī says the verse shows angels are morally tasked in the afterlife and defines disobedience as violating command or prohibition; al-Riḍā says God's grace protects angels from disbelief and disgraceful acts. Bursevī reports a view that heavenly angels are intellects without competing appetite or desire; it distinguishes angels who never descend and those who never ascend. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI(الرضا); BURSEVI(بعض الكبار); ELMALILI]
- 66:6/q32/p2 Obeying God's command is itself the angels' delight. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes this to some Muʿtazilites, comparing it with the delight of Paradise's inhabitants. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المعتزلة)]
- 66:6/q32/p3 Angels as celestial powers obey innately because they are subject to divine overpowering. — Al-Qāshānī says their obedience enables their influence in terrestrial affairs; Asad relates angelic obedience to the spiritual and material law of cause and effect. [3 notes: QASHANI; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]

## 66:6/q33 Can God impose duties in the future, including on angels?  [مَآ أَمَرَهُمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ · theology]
turns on: The theological possibility of future divine obligation.
- 66:6/q33/p1 God can impose duties today or tomorrow and charge angels as He wills. — Al-Qurṭubī calls this the view of the people of truth and expressly rejects the opposing Muʿtazilite doctrine. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 66:6/q33/p2 Imposing a duty tomorrow is impossible. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this as the Muʿtazilite claim and rejects it. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعض المعتزلة); QURTUBI-FULL-]

## 66:6/q34 Do heavenly angels earn reward for avoiding prohibitions?  [لَّا يَعْصُونَ ٱللَّهَ · theology]
turns on: Whether refraining is available as a duty and whether it needs intention for reward.
- 66:6/q34/p1 These heavenly angels have no prohibitions to avoid and thus lack reward for refraining. — Bursevī transmits a distinction from humans and earthly angels, who can earn both by obeying commands and avoiding prohibitions. [1 notes: BURSEVI(بعض الكبار)]
- 66:6/q34/p2 Abstention earns reward only if intended as obedience, though no intention is needed merely to avoid liability. — Al-Kirmānī distinguishes intended refraining from merely not committing a sin, illustrated by leaving fornication. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الكرماني)]

## 66:6/q35 How are masculine forms used with angels?  [مَلَٰٓئِكَةٌ غِلَاظٌۭ شِدَادٌۭ لَّا يَعْصُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Grammatical freedom of the broken plural and the rhetorical weight assigned to gender forms.
- 66:6/q35/p1 Nominal descriptions, commands to angels, and verbs of their worship are masculine. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī surveys descriptors such as المقرّبون, باسطو أيديهم, مسوّمين, مردفين and منزلين; commands اسجدوا, أنبئوني, فقعوا; and verbs of entering, witnessing and glorifying. He says masculine worship is fuller and recalls male messengers. He contrasts angelic glad tidings, whose forms are feminine, and notes a broken plural may take either gender. [9 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR-MUH; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 66:6/q35/p2 A broken plural permits both masculine and feminine agreement. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares masculine angel agreement in Ṣād with feminine agreement in Āl ʿImrān, citing 38:73 and 3:39. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 66:6/q35/p3 Masculine forms suit greater breadth of worship and stronger punishment. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī contrasts masculine oath response and lam with a narrower feminine verb and qad, and compares harsh punishment forms in 8:50, 47:27, 25:25, 41:30 and 97:4. [2 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 66:6/q36 What inward lesson is drawn from protecting oneself and one's family?  [قُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ · inward]
turns on: Whether family is a spiritual bond, a field of ethical training, or an image of inner powers.
- 66:6/q36/p1 Purify worldly attachment and choose pure companions, since love binds souls together. — Al-Qāshānī says attachment to impure people or natural faculties drags one into an abyss, while loving saints gathers one with them under the report that people are resurrected with those they love. Bursevī transmits this and another allusion: a leader's lapse becomes followers' lapse, so purification can help household members follow. [8 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(القاشاني,بعض أهل الإشارة)]
- 66:6/q36/p2 Accept admonition, model worship, follow the Sunna, and cultivate character within the household. — Al-Sulamī transmits Ibn ʿAṭāʾ on accepting counsel, Abū ʿUthmān on acting on admonition, and Abū al-Ḥasan al-Warrāq on duties and Prophetic practices; al-Qushayrī adds example, Sunna, community and good character. [7 notes: SULAMI(أبو الحسن الوراق,أبو عثمان,ابن عطاء); QUSHAYRI]
- 66:6/q36/p3 The spiritual Fire is distance from God, fueled by lower selves and hardened hearts. — Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya says intellectual believers must guard against expulsion after neglecting the primordial covenant; al-Qāshānī speaks of divine overpowering of desire-entangled souls. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); QASHANI]

## 66:6/q37 What is reported about the severity of the Fire itself?  [نَارًۭا · reports]
turns on: Terrifying punishment and promised reward in the associated reports.
- 66:6/q37/p1 Its torment permits no rest, sleep, comfort, or excuse. — Al-Sulamī attributes this warning to Yaḥyā, adding that even iron could not withstand the Fire. [1 notes: SULAMI(يحيى)]
- 66:6/q37/p2 A family that fulfills its duties may be gathered together in Paradise. — Al-Zajjāj reports this hadith; similar reports in al-Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Bursevī, Ālūsī and Elmalılı urge prayer, fasting, alms and care for poor, orphans and neighbors. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Keşşâf)]

## 66:6/q38 How does this verse connect with the surah's surrounding passage?  [* · links]
turns on: The direction of the passage's shift and the verses used to explain family responsibility.
- 66:6/q38/p1 The Prophet's household episode broadens into a warning to all believers to reform themselves and their families. — Abū Ḥayyān, Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu trace the move from the Prophet and wives to all Muslims. Ibn ʿĀshūr says believers are accompanied by the Prophet in admonishing even when advice hurts; Biqāʿī says the wives' earlier warning preserves respect and their virtues reflect good upbringing. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 66:6/q38/p2 The passage alternates warnings and commands to believers, unbelievers and the Prophet. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces protection, the unbelievers' inability to excuse themselves at 66:7, repentance at 66:8 and struggle at 66:9; Elmalılı notes the turn to unbelievers. Study Quran describes the passage from wives (1–5) through family duty (6), unbelievers (7) and opponents (9). [3 notes: TABATABAI; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 66:6/q38/p3 The surah repeatedly vindicates the Prophet before disclosing the dispute involving his wives. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says shifts among the Prophet, believers and wives reveal the episode only after divine support is secured. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 66:6/q38/p4 Its command to family parallels 20:132 and 26:214, and more generally 5:2. — Al-Qurṭubī and al-Kiyā connect family prayer and warning nearest kin to 20:132 and 26:214; al-Qurṭubī also invokes cooperation in righteousness in 5:2. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الكيا)]

## 66:6/q39 What is distinctive about believers being told to protect themselves?  [قُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ · links]
- 66:6/q39/p1 Here believers are directly told to shield themselves; elsewhere they ask God to protect them. — Study Quran compares prayers in 3:16, 3:191 and 40:7. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 66:6/q40 What date and classification are given for the surah?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether all twelve verses or only the first nine are Medinan.
- 66:6/q40/p1 It is Medinan, probably around 7 AH. — Asad places it in the second half of the Medinan period; Study Quran says likely 7/629. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Asad); STUDYQURAN]
- 66:6/q40/p2 A minority counts verses 1–9 as Medinan and the last three as Makkan. — Study Quran reports this alternative. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 66:6/q41 What alternate title is reported for the surah?  [* · sciences]
- 66:6/q41/p1 Sūrat al-Nabī, 'the Sūrah of the Prophet'. — Asad cites al-Zamakhsharī; the title reflects the first half's concern with the Prophet's personal and family life. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES(Zamakhshari)]

## 66:6/q42 How is the metaphor of protection understood in discussions attached to this verse?  [قُوٓا۟ · rhetoric]
- 66:6/q42/p1 It transfers a word from its literal sense to clarify something that literal wording cannot express as vividly. — Al-Rummānī distinguishes metaphor from overt simile, analyzes borrowed expression, intended referent and source, and says good metaphor requires a literal basis plus new clarification. He illustrates with Imruʾ al-Qays's قيد الأوابد and with reasoning as a perceptible scale. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls admonition 'protection' either through causation or forceful equation. [6 notes: RUMMANI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 66:6/q43 What account of the written Quran and its transmission appears in the cited material?  [* · reports]
- 66:6/q43/p1 The companions collected the Quran from writing and memory and transmitted the revelation entrusted to them. — Ibn Kathīr defines ʿasab as immature palm-frond material through al-Jawharī and likhāf as flat stone writing pieces. Zayd gathered palm stems, stone pieces and memorized passages. Ibn Kathīr says the Prophet's full delivery is attested at ʿArafāt and by orders to convey even one verse; writing non-Quranic material was restrained to prevent confusion, not to stop Sunna transmission. He says no revealed Quran was lost; Abū Bakr's sheets passed through ʿUmar and Ḥafṣa to ʿUthmān. [9 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو النصر إسماعيل بن حماد الجوهري,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]
