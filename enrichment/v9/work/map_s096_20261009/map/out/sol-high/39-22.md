# 39:22: 26 questions

## 39:22/q01 How does the opening rhetorical question work?  [أَفَمَن فَوَيْلٌۭ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the question's hamza precedes the conjunction or an elided clause.
- 39:22/q01/p1 Its missing predicate compares the opened, guided person with a hardened or sealed, unguided person; the ensuing woe supplies the answer. — The warning supplies the omitted counterpart; Zamakhshari and Razi compare 39:9, Samin cites 39:19 and 39:24, Biqai calls the mutual omissions hitbak, Ibn Kathir cites 6:122, and Ibn Ashur also allows the condemned person of 39:20. [40 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 39:22/q01/p2 The interrogative hamza precedes the conjunction, and the fa links the result to what precedes. — Zarkashi prefers this and argues an omitted verb fails in 43:18, 13:19 and 13:33. Zajjaj and Abu Ishaq call fa consequential; Bursevi allows man as relative or conditional. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); BURSEVI; BURHAN+]
- 39:22/q01/p3 The hamza governs an omitted clause or verb before the fa. — Zarkashi reports this alternative from Zamakhshari and rejects it in comparable examples; Alusi reconstructs 'are all people equal?' as the denied premise. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN-]

## 39:22/q02 Why is chest expansion contrasted with hard hearts?  [صَدْرَهُۥ لِّلْقَٰسِيَةِ قُلُوبُهُم · rhetoric]
- 39:22/q02/p1 The contrast depicts full acceptance spreading through the believer's chest, whereas the heart harder than stone admits no remembrance. — Alusi prefers hardness over tightness since tightness allows some entry, names the heart because it governs the body, and contrasts the singular believer with plural rejecters to suggest believers' unity. Bursevi recalls light versus darkness and knowledge versus ignorance; Elmalılı renders the contrast. [6 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 39:22/q03 What does sharḥ literally mean?  [شَرَحَ صَدْرَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the opening is bodily or spiritual.
- 39:22/q03/p1 It means opening, spreading, or making spacious, originally of flesh. — Jishumi also notes explaining and cutting open; Bursevi and Alusi mention spreading meat; Qurtubi glosses spaciousness. [4 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:22/q03/p2 Here chest opening is figurative for spiritual faith and ease. — Ibn Atiyya gives sound reflection and belief. Ibn Ashur compares the relief of constricted breathing (26:13; 6:125). Bint al-Shati cites Moses' mission and five Meccan passages (20:25, 16:106, 6:125) against physical surgery; al-Raghib calls it light, calm, and spirit. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BINTSHATI(الراغب); ALUSI-FULL(بعض الأجلة); BINTSHATI+]

## 39:22/q04 What is God's expansion of the chest for Islam, and when does it occur?  [شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُۥ لِلْإِسْلَٰمِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether expansion precedes or follows the acceptance of Islam.
- 39:22/q04/p1 God gives a person readiness to receive Islam, followed by acceptance and steadfastness. — Muqatil specifies monotheism; Tabari adds knowledge of lordship, obedience and certainty. Ibn Abbas and Suddi describe establishment of Islam. Zamakhshari calls it لطف. Razi likens innate readiness to sulfur ignited by a small flame. Baydawi, Alusi and Bursevi describe original disposition and the chest as the heart's locus; Tabatabai adds discerning submission. [33 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(أبو السعود); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 39:22/q04/p2 Islam brings joy and tranquility, so chest expansion follows its acceptance. — Mawardi and Qurtubi contrast this timing with prior readiness and attribute it to Suddi. Ibn Ashur names confidence, resilience, freedom from doubt, wisdom, noble conduct, brotherhood, contentment, patience, hope and gratitude; the lam means for Islam's acceptance. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(السدي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 39:22/q04/p3 The verse describes prior readiness and the Prophetic report describes later expansion when light enters. — Alusi resolves the reversal in sequence by distinguishing earlier and later guidance, calling the answer wise redirection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:22/q05 By what means does God open and guide the heart?  [شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُۥ · theology]
turns on: How divine action, proofs and human receptivity relate.
- 39:22/q05/p1 Divine proofs, graces and insight enable a receptive heart to accept and sustain faith. — Tabrisi gives strong proofs especially for scholars, continually renewed graces, and proofs resolving doubts and casting insights; he cites 47:17. Jishumi links prior proofs of unity and justice; Biqai describes responsiveness to evidence. Razi says readiness is needed for proofs and refers the predestination debate to 6:125. [14 notes: TABRISI; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 39:22/q05/p2 God grants the fullest opening, while bad choices corrupt original receptivity. — Alusi attributes opening to divine wisdom and power and the opposite to changed disposition; Bursevi and Baydawi contrast God's gracious act with hardness attributed to the person. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 39:22/q05/p3 God creates faith in the heart, and the description applies generally. — Qurtubi and Bursevi generalize to believers; Tabrisi includes all morally responsible people despite the address to the Prophet. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABRISI]

## 39:22/q06 Who is the person whose breast is opened?  [مَن شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Named occasions differ, and other commentators take the description generally.
- 39:22/q06/p1 The opened people are Ali and Hamza. — Wahidi, Ibn Atiyya, Ata, Baydawi, Bursevi and Alusi pair them with Abu Lahab and his son or sons. [8 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL(المفسرون); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:22/q06/p2 The opened person is the Prophet Muhammad. — Muqatil places Muhammad on guidance and Abu Jahl opposite; Kalbi and Muqatil are also cited for a revelation about him. [6 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI(مقاتل); MAWARDI-FULL(الكلبي); JISHUMI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل والكلبي)]
- 39:22/q06/p3 The opened person is Ammar ibn Yasir. — Thumali and reports from Muqatil assign the revelation to Ammar. [4 notes: THALABI(الثمالي); MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); JISHUMI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 39:22/q06/p4 The description applies to every believer whose heart receives Islam. — Qurtubi and Bursevi generalize; Tabrisi says the Prophet's address includes all responsible people. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABRISI]
- 39:22/q06/p5 The opened person is Umar ibn al-Khattab. — Naqqash reports the identification. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش); QURTUBI-FULL(النقاش)]
- 39:22/q06/p6 The contrast concerns Abu Bakr al-Siddiq and Ubayy ibn Khalaf. — Al-Dahhak transmits it from Ibn Abbas. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الضحاك عن ابن عباس)]
- 39:22/q06/p7 The opened person is Ali alone. — Al-Qummi's tafsir gives this report, but Tabatabai objects that a single revelation of the whole sura conflicts with it. [1 notes: TABATABAI(القمي)]

## 39:22/q07 What is the light from the Lord?  [نُورٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّهِۦ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether light names guidance, Scripture, grace, decree or a distinct divine reality.
- 39:22/q07/p1 It is God's guidance, knowledge, clarity, insight, or certainty. — Suddi, Muqatil and many commentators gloss guidance; Ibn Atiyya and Ibn Ashur compare light revealing truth to physical light. Ibn al-Saib and Ibn Ata say clarification, Ibn Abbas certainty, Tabatabai discernment, and Study Quran maʿrifah. [20 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(السدي); SULAMI(ابن عطاء); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن السائب,ابن عباس,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Bḍ)]
- 39:22/q07/p2 It is God's Book, followed and returned to. — Qatada names the Quran; Biqai describes the great clarification used in every matter; Jishumi links it to the prior verse. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI]
- 39:22/q07/p3 It is divine لطف, favor and enabling grace. — Zamakhshari identifies لطف; Bursevi and Alusi describe illumination of signs in creation and revelation; Biqai stresses the benefactor Lord. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:22/q07/p4 It is faith in divine decree. — Al-Qasim gives this inward gloss. [1 notes: SULAMI(القاسم)]
- 39:22/q07/p5 It may be a divine reality distinct from grace or enabling guidance. — Alusi offers this further sense by which truth is apprehended. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 39:22/q08 What does the Prophetic report say about light entering the heart?  [شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُۥ نُورٍۢ · reports]
turns on: How the report joins opening, light and conduct.
- 39:22/q08/p1 Light entering the heart expands it; signs are turning to the eternal home, leaving worldly delusion, and preparing for death. — Ibn Masud, Qatada and Ibn Umar transmit versions. Ibn Umar calls the one most mindful and ready for death wisest. Tabatabai names transmissions through Ibn Mardawayh, Hakim Tirmidhi and Ibn Jarir; Ibn al-Jawzi points to 6:125. Qurtubi explains good works, contentment, taqwa and care as outward signs, with Paradise as reward (32:17). [30 notes: THALABI(عبد الله بن مسعود); MAWARDI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); QUSHAYRI(الرسول - صلى الله عيله وسلم -); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); KASHSHAF-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(محمد ﷺ); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحديث); BIQAI-FULL(النبي ﷺ); DURR-FULL(أبو جعفر عبد الله بن المسور,ابن عمر,عبد الله بن مسعود,قتادة); BURSEVI(النبي); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود); TABATABAI(النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم); STUDYQURAN(The Prophet)]
- 39:22/q08/p2 The opening is divine light, calm and spirit. — Al-Raghib connects Moses' prayer at 20:25 with this verse; Bursevi speaks of light, tranquility and spirit. [2 notes: BINTSHATI(الراغب); BURSEVI]

## 39:22/q09 Why is the believer 'upon' light 'from his Lord'?  [عَلَىٰ نُورٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّهِۦ · rhetoric]
- 39:22/q09/p1 'Upon' suggests settled, continuous guidance; 'from his Lord' identifies the full light's divine origin. — Alusi contrasts this with merely having light; Ibn Ashur compares 2:5, takes min as origin, and cites 24:35; Biqai stresses the benefactor Lord. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 39:22/q09/p2 The believer is depicted as riding light and thereby discerning truth. — Tabatabai says the unguided lack both spacious breast and light. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 39:22/q10 What warning accompanies those favored with divine light?  [نُورٍۢ مِّن رَّبِّهِۦ · reports]
- 39:22/q10/p1 People blessed with divine favors have enemies and should beware of them. — Bursevi quotes the Prophetic warning. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 39:22/q11 What is the grammatical role of 'for Islam' and 'he'?  [لِلْإِسْلَٰمِ فَهُوَ · grammar]
- 39:22/q11/p1 The lam marks purpose, namely acceptance of Islam; 'he' refers back to the person whose breast was opened. — Ibn Ashur supplies both analyses. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 39:22/q12 What does 'woe' mean here?  [فَوَيْلٌۭ · meaning]
- 39:22/q12/p1 It denotes severe punishment, misery and wretchedness. — Bursevi gives punishment; Ibn Ashur cites 2:79. Malik ibn Dinar calls a hard heart the worst punishment and links it to mercy being removed. [9 notes: BURSEVI(مالك بن دينار); IBNASHUR-FULL; THALABI(مالك بن دينار); BAGHAWI-FULL(مالك بن دينار); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك بن دينار); QURTUBI-FULL(مالك بن دينار); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك بن دينار)]

## 39:22/q13 Who are the hard-hearted people?  [لِّلْقَٰسِيَةِ قُلُوبُهُم · referent]
turns on: Named opponents differ, while exposition also generalizes the condition.
- 39:22/q13/p1 They are any whose heart rejects admonition or faith. — Tabrisi describes habituated disbelief. Study Quran links wrongdoing and lost divine favor to sealing with many cited verses, including 2:7, 5:13 and 57:16. Qushayri includes the outwardly faithful who are inwardly veiled. [16 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:22/q13/p2 They are Abu Lahab and his son or sons. — Reports pair them with Ali and Hamza; Qurtubi includes Abu Lahab's children, and Abu Hayyan treats them as examples. [8 notes: WAHIDI-ASBAB; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عطاء); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:22/q13/p3 They are Abu Jahl and other unbelievers of Quraysh. — Muqatil names Abu Jahl; Ibn Masud and Ibn Abbas extend to similar unbelievers; Thalabi and Mawardi give the wider group. [5 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(ابن مسعود، وابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل)]

## 39:22/q14 What is hardness of heart?  [ٱلْقَٰسِيَةِ قُلُوبُهُم · meaning]
- 39:22/q14/p1 Qasā means becoming hard, thick and unable to soften like stone, hence resisting admonition. — Mubarrad compares ʿatā and ʿasā. Jishumi mentions qasāwa as a form and links hardness to error. Ibn Atiyya, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur trace the stone image; Ibn Ashur cites 2:74 and 39:23. [7 notes: NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(المبرد); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]
- 39:22/q14/p2 Inward hardness is denial or veiling from recognition, often through attachment to bodily pleasure. — Qushayri describes unstruck hearts in ignorance; Qashani describes refusal of sacred perfection; Study Quran says desire distances people from guidance. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; QASHANI; STUDYQURAN]

## 39:22/q15 What makes hearts hard, and what are its effects?  [ٱلْقَٰسِيَةِ قُلُوبُهُم · interpretation]
- 39:22/q15/p1 Aversion to truth, repeated wrongdoing, pride, and worldly desire harden hearts and block guidance. — Muqatil connects it to abandoning oneness; Tabari to Quran rejection; Ibn Ashur to hatred entrenched by repeated avoidance, comparing 2:6–7; Islahi compares barren land. Qashani and Study Quran stress desire and wrongdoing, and Yahya ibn Muadh names following desire. [24 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; SULAMI(يحيى بن معاذ); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QASHANI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 39:22/q15/p2 A hard heart is an especially grave punishment and a sign of lost mercy. — Malik ibn Dinar calls it the worst punishment. Abu Said's divine report places mercy among the generous and anger among hard-hearted people; Abu al-Darda says God loves humble, patient, merciful teachers but hates heedless hard hearts. [9 notes: THALABI(أبو الدرداء,أبو سعيد الخدري,مالك بن دينار); BAGHAWI-FULL(مالك بن دينار); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مالك بن دينار); QURTUBI-FULL(الله تعالى,مالك بن دينار); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مالك بن دينار); BURSEVI(مالك بن دينار)]
- 39:22/q15/p3 Heedless speech, eating, sleeping, indulgence, and long worldly hopes harden hearts. — Ibn Umar and Jesus connect speech without remembrance to distance from God; Ali and Aisha warn of food and sleep. Bursevi cites divine counsel to Moses, a Prophetic counsel on hunger, Saʿdi's moderate-eating poem, Aisha on satiety, Dhu'l-Nun on sin and Shibli on hunger's wisdom. Al-Husayn contrasts hardness amid blessings and hardship and says pursuing pleasure wastes life. [14 notes: SULAMI(الحسين); DURR-FULL(أبو الجلد,ابن عمر,عائشة,علي); BURSEVI(الله,النبي,عائشة الصديقة)]

## 39:22/q16 What does min in 'from God's remembrance' mean?  [مِّن ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether min gives cause, estrangement or a starting point.
- 39:22/q16/p1 It marks cause: remembrance itself makes resistant hearts harder. — Zajjaj and Zamakhshari compare increased sickness or 9:125. Razi compares sun hardening salt but softening wax, cites 13:28 and the different responses to 23:12–14. Baydawi and Alusi call this stronger than mere estrangement. Ibn Ashur explains opposite effects by differing receptivity and rejects the need to substitute ʿan. [27 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 39:22/q16/p2 It means ʿan: hearts are hard away from, or against accepting, remembrance. — Tabari and Farra cite Arab alternation of min and ʿan in food expressions. Zajjaj distinguishes the senses. Qurtubi prefers this; Ibn Ashur rejects the grammatical need and reads cause, also differentiating 50:22. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء,مقاتل); NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(مغني اللبيب); KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الطبري)+; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 39:22/q16/p3 Min marks a starting point, or hardness remains despite reminders. — Zarkashi reports the starting-point analysis; Islahi says reminders failed to soften them. [2 notes: BURHAN; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:22/q17 Is there a variant reading of 'from God's remembrance'?  [مِّن ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · readings]
turns on: The two prepositions yield cause versus distance.
- 39:22/q17/p1 Some read ʿan dhikr Allāh in place of min dhikr Allāh. — Ibn al-Jawzi names Ubayy ibn Kaʿb, Ibn Abi ʿAbla and Abu ʿImran; Zamakhshari and Bursevi report it. Alusi favors min because causation signals stronger refusal. [3 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبي بن كعب وابن أبي عبلة وأبو عمران); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 39:22/q17/p2 The transmitted wording is min dhikr Allāh. — Alusi prefers its stronger causal force; Baydawi agrees that it exceeds hardness from another cause. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 39:22/q18 How should phonetic analogies be used in reciting dhikr Allāh?  [ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · readings]
- 39:22/q18/p1 Recitation requires transmission; analogy does not license untransmitted sounds or a light divine-name lam by analogy with the light ra in dhikr Allāh. — Ibn al-Jazari rejects extending analogy from strong or weak readings, including showing converted mim and lightening still ra before kasra or ya. Makki divides readings into documented, oral but unwritten, and the smallest inferred by analogy. [4 notes: NASHR(مكي بن أبي طالب)]

## 39:22/q19 What is God's remembrance here?  [ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase means Quranic recitation specifically or divine mention broadly.
- 39:22/q19/p1 It is mention of God or His signs more broadly. — Zajjaj and Zamakhshari mention God or recited signs; Razi compares one speech's opposite effects. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:22/q19/p2 It is the Quran, God's word and reminder, whose attribution to Him honors it. — Tabari says they turn from the sent reminder; Ibn Ashur explicitly identifies the Quran. Al-Sakhawi says all of it is remembrance because it praises God's mercy, power, creation, care, guidance, laws and the fate of opponents. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASHR(السخاوي)]

## 39:22/q20 What do the cited reports teach about practicing remembrance?  [ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: These reports treat continuous practice, comparative merit and a recitation sequence.
- 39:22/q20/p1 Remembrance is among the highest deeds, surpassing wealth-giving and combat and especially saving from punishment. — Muadh and Abu al-Darda report this through Ahmad, Malik, Ibn Majah and Tirmidhi. Tirmidhi notes connected and mursal transmissions but gives no explicit grade. Al-Sakhawi harmonizes the Quran as best remembrance; Ibn al-Jazari says 'best' can mean one among the highest rank, while sound reports also praise faith, jihad, pilgrimage, prayer, parents, patience, generosity and fasting. [13 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(رسول الله,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNMAJAH(النبي,معاذ بن جبل); MALIK(أبو الدرداء,معاذ بن جبل); TIRMIDHI(أبو الدرداء,معاذ بن جبل); NASHR(السخاوي)]
- 39:22/q20/p2 Keep the tongue continually moist with remembrance as a practice to hold fast to. — Ibn Busr's Bedouin or overwhelmed question appears in Ahmad, Ibn Hibban, Ibn Majah and Tirmidhi; Tirmidhi grades it hasan gharib. Ibn Hibban also reports Muadh being told the deed God loves most is to die with a moist tongue. [6 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(عبد الله بن بسر); IBNHIBBAN(عبد الله بن بسر,معاذ بن جبل); IBNMAJAH(رسول الله); TIRMIDHI(عبد الله بن بسر)]
- 39:22/q20/p3 After completing the Quran, supplication follows Surat al-Nas without extra al-Fatiha and five verses of al-Baqara. — Ibn al-Jazari reads Ahmad's 'no' as disallowing the added recitation; al-Amidi limits the restriction to before supplication. Another report from Ahmad recommends it. [2 notes: NASHR(الآمدي)]

## 39:22/q21 What does 'those are in manifest error' mean?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ فِى ضَلَٰلٍۢ مُّبِينٍ · interpretation]
- 39:22/q21/p1 'Those' are the hard-hearted; their error is unmistakable and firmly rooted. — Zajjaj and Nahhas identify the demonstrative; Nahhas calls it nominative subject with the following predicate. Tabrisi calls their turning from truth obvious; Baydawi, Abu Hayyan, Biqai, Bursevi and Ibn Ashur stress visibility. Ibn Ashur says error both causes and follows hardness, with the demonstrative indicating deserved consequence. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:22/q21/p2 Manifest error is guidance lost through refusal to heed God's signs. — Tabatabai sets out expanded breast and light as guidance's signs and hard heart as misguidance's; Islahi relates barren hearts to the fate of earlier rejecters. [5 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 39:22/q22 How does the verse connect with its neighboring passages?  [* · links]
- 39:22/q22/p1 The question contrasts those promised reward with hardened unbelievers and explains why reminders benefit people of understanding. — Abu Hayyan contrasts 39:20's promise with punishment. Biqai refers to discerning minds; Alusi and Tabatabai explain receptivity; Razi says proofs need divine opening to be effective. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL]
- 39:22/q22/p2 The earlier rain and growth image depicts how Quranic guidance affects hearts according to receptivity. — Ibn Ashur says the sura's Quran-and-Islam theme resumes after the excursus beginning 39:2; 39:21's growth prepares 39:22–23. Islahi contrasts flourishing and barren land and says the next verse completes the image. Kuranyolu relates power over plants to spiritual development. [7 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 39:22/q22/p3 The next passage shows the Quran softening the God-fearing and exposing rust in hard hearts. — Ibn Ashur links 39:23 to 2:2 and 2:6–7, denying any deficiency in the Quran's guidance. Razi calls the Quran light, healing, guidance and reassurance. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 39:22/q23 What inward path do opening and light describe?  [شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُۥ نُورٍۢ · inward]
- 39:22/q23/p1 Opening and illumination begin successive stages of faith, knowledge, certainty, unveiling, witnessing and divine unity. — Sulami relates stages to 49:3, 49:7, 58:22 and 5:41; al-Sayyari derives Islam from faith, knowledge, guidance and aid. Qushayri describes ascending lights; Biqai compares moonlit stars absorbed by dawn; Bursevi cites a path beyond nearness and separation (28:88). [9 notes: SULAMI(الترمذي,السيارى,القاسم,بعضهم); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,جامي)]
- 39:22/q23/p2 The opening's light is extraordinary love and longing sustained by divine care. — Wasiti says care protects bodies and spirits; Bursevi cites him. Bursevi gives testimony, service and heart longing as three lights leading to Paradise, Firdaws and beholding God (5:85; 18:107; 75:22–23). Najmiyya names erasing desire, good deeds and flight from worldly trials (13:39). [6 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,الواسطي); ALUSI-FULL]
- 39:22/q23/p3 The breast can contain divine reality and creation after annihilation, with the Lord seen in light. — Qashani calls Islam annihilation and surrender, then sees unity in multiplicity. Wasiti speaks of bearing Essence after attributes (53:11). Sulami describes witnessing that eclipses other objects and seeing God while turning from all else. [6 notes: QASHANI; SULAMI(الواسطي,بعضهم,عمرو المكي)]
- 39:22/q23/p4 The inner person is a lamp or layered dwelling of faith, witnessing and love. — Ali ibn Abd al-Hamid maps chest to oil, heart to wick, inner breast to flame. Al-Kashf al-Asrar gives breast, heart, fu'ad and shighaf as covenant, faith, witnessing and love (2:22; 53:11; 12:30), moving from purification through wisdom to annihilation. Najmiyya makes faith lamp and Islam ray. [4 notes: SULAMI(علي بن عبد الحميد); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية,كشف الأسرار)]
- 39:22/q23/p5 Hardness can signify deprivation of God's presence even for someone outwardly faithful. — Qushayri distinguishes Fire from being veiled today and says promises of Paradise, forgiveness and presence hold if seekers do not pause. Al-Husayn names rust, hardness and blindness as heart punishments. [3 notes: QUSHAYRI; SULAMI(الحسين)]
- 39:22/q23/p6 Faith's light has degrees among believers. — Bursevi compares common believers to candles, the truthful to moon and stars, prophets to the sun. A report on release from Hell from half or quarter of a grain to an atom illustrates differing faith and light on the Bridge. [3 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 39:22/q24 What other cited passages associate recitation with light or healing?  [نُورٍۢ ذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ · links]
- 39:22/q24/p1 Reciting al-Kahf yields light, and memorizing its first ten verses protects from Dajjal. — Suyuti cites Hakim on Friday-to-Friday light, Muslim on ten verses, Ahmad on beginning and ending or whole sura, and Bazzar on 18:110 yielding light from Aden to Mecca with angels. [4 notes: ITQAN(أبو الدرداء,أبو سعيد,عمر,معاذ بن أنس)]
- 39:22/q24/p2 Quran recitation and divine names heal by God's permission. — Ibn al-Tin calls protective recitation spiritual medicine; a cited report says firm conviction could move a mountain. Qurtubi and Shafii allow ruqyah with divine words and known remembrance; Ibn Battal explains prayers against magic, envy and Satan, and Ibn al-Qayyim finds al-Fatiha apt to heal through monotheism, guidance, worship, repentance and heart reform. [6 notes: ITQAN(ابن التين,ابن القيم,ابن بطال,الشافعي,القرطبي,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 39:22/q25 What do the cited poetic analogies show about compressed figurative language?  [شَرَحَ ٱللَّهُ صَدْرَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 39:22/q25/p1 A figure can suppress its comparison and present an imagined cause as fact. — Jurjani's linen poem claims the moon wears the garment through its buttons; an explicit simile would weaken the proof. Another linen poem is less forceful. Al-Abbas ibn al-Ahnaf treats the beloved as sun and her dwelling as sky. [4 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]

## 39:22/q26 What grammatical parallels are adduced for conditional ellipsis?  [أَفَمَن · grammar]
- 39:22/q26/p1 A condition pairs protasis and apodosis, the latter nominal, verbal, adverbial or conditional. — Zarkashi cites 2:38, 4:124, 6:125, 7:106, 7:143 and 10:46. He discusses omitted objects of sha'a and irada inferred from the answer; Ibn al-Nahwiyya requires a preceding condition, while Zamakhshari, Ibn al-Zamalkani and Tanukhi permit regular omission. [5 notes: BURHAN(ابن النحوية)]
