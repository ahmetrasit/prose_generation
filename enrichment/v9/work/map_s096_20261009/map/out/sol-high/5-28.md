# 5:28: 23 questions

## 5:28/q01 What does extending a hand mean here?  [بَسَطتَ يَدَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether this use describes the physical reach itself or the violent course it signifies.
- 5:28/q01/p1 It is the literal extension of a hand toward someone, including reaching to seize or grasp him. — Abū ʿUbayda glosses basatta ilayya yadaka as extending toward me; al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī contrast extension with contraction; al-Baghawī and al-Nasafī give the ordinary gloss. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sons of Adam passage uses the physical sense. [6 notes: MAJAZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:28/q01/p2 It denotes using force or taking the steps toward murder. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites the threat to extend hands and tongues in 60:2 for the figurative force sense; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the wording a euphemism for the means of killing. Ibn ʿĀshūr also records wider figurative uses for authority and generosity (5:64), and contrasts withholding a hand from evil (48:20). [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 5:28/q02 How do the oath and conditional in la-in work?  [لَئِنۢ مَآ أَنَا۠ بِبَاسِطٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical answer of the oath and the semantic consequence of the condition can be described differently.
- 5:28/q02/p1 The lām introduces an implied oath; the negative nominal clause formally answers that oath and supplies the condition's consequence. — Al-Ṭabrisī says mā cannot directly answer the condition. Al-Samīn and the Shaykh invoke the rule that when oath precedes condition its answer takes priority; a direct negative conditional answer would require fa. Al-Samīn defends al-Zamakhsharī's calling it a consequence as semantic terminology and cites his parallel discussion of 2:145. [5 notes: TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)]
- 5:28/q02/p2 The oath conditions Cain's threatened killing and emphasizes the response. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the lām an oath lām framing the threat. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:28/q03 Why cast the warning as an emphatic conditional oath?  [لَئِنۢ · rhetoric]
- 5:28/q03/p1 The assertion is grave and hard to believe, so Abel emphasizes it. — Al-Biqāʿī says the emphatic conditional oath suits a serious statement difficult for Cain to credit; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī includes the oath among the devices stressing Abel's distance from intended murder. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 5:28/q04 Why are the hand, direction, and killing phrases ordered this way?  [إِلَىَّ يَدَكَ يَدِىَ إِلَيْكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes variously explain the order by pronunciation, clause symmetry, moral contrast, or admonition.
- 5:28/q04/p1 The first order avoids a difficult succession of sounds; the second resumes normal object order. — Al-Burhān says a direct object ordinarily precedes the prepositional complement, comparing 48:24, but moving ilayya before yadaka separates the nearby articulation points of basatta and li-taqtulanī; with a participle in the reply the obstacle disappears. [3 notes: BURHAN]
- 5:28/q04/p2 The order and change of form contrast Cain's active aggression with Abel's denial of aggression. — Al-Burhān connects Cain's finite verb to eagerness and Abel's participle to refusal; the participle also makes the paired clauses comparable. [2 notes: BURHAN]
- 5:28/q04/p3 Fronting 'toward me' warns that the harm and consequences return to the attacker. — Al-Ālūsī reports this implication of the fronting. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:28/q04/p4 Fronting 'toward me' recalls the intended victim and their brotherhood to deter the murder. — Al-Ālūsī offers this as another explanation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:28/q04/p5 The fronted object extends the scope of Abel's refusal. — Al-Biqāʿī joins fronting to emphatic negation and says the refusal is generalized. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 5:28/q05 How is yadi read?  [يَدِىَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the possessive yāʾ is pronounced open or shortened; the notes report no semantic difference.
- 5:28/q05/p1 The final possessive yāʾ is opened: yadiya. — Al-Nasafī names the Medinan reader, Abū ʿAmr, and Ḥafṣ; al-Nashr specifies both Medinans, Abū ʿAmr, and Ḥafṣ. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL(مدنى وأبو عمرو وحفص); NASHR(المدنيان، وأبو عمرو، وحفص)]
- 5:28/q05/p2 The final possessive vowel is shortened: yadī. — Qirāʾāt ER says the phonetic difference from yadiya does not change the sense. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Y-id)]

## 5:28/q06 What does Abel mean by refusing to stretch his hand to kill?  [مَآ أَنَا۠ بِبَاسِطٍۢ يَدِىَ إِلَيْكَ لِأَقْتُلَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the words exclude initiating murder, intentional killing during defense, or defense itself.
- 5:28/q06/p1 He will not initiate wrongful killing or return Cain's aggression with an unjust attack. — Al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī and others distinguish beginning murder from lawful defense. Al-Ṭabarī says both brothers were prohibited from unjust killing. Al-Ṭabrisī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's reading; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the text leaves defense, flight, or surprise possible. Mevdûdî includes Cain's preparations. Al-Murtaḍā stresses that repelling danger without intent to kill remains possible. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس وحذيفة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TAB-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وجماعة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Mevdûdî); STUDYQURAN(commentators); TAB-FULL+; TABRISI(المرتضى)+; TABATABAI-]
- 5:28/q06/p2 He will neither retaliate nor fight his brother, even if Cain attacks. — Al-Ṭabarī reports Ibn ʿAbbās's refusal to retaliate; al-Zajjāj lists fighting and killing. Al-Qurṭubī calls it submission, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a tradition in which Abel lets Cain kill him. Ibn Kathīr presents patient endurance for reward. This reading underlies reports of his greater strength and scruple. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; TABATABAI(روايات); IBNKATHIR-FULL; MUQATIL; ELMALILI; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 5:28/q06/p3 He refuses to intend to kill Cain; force aimed only at repelling him is allowed. — Al-Wāḥidī and the scholars reported by al-Rāzī require the least necessary force and an intent to repel. Al-Murtaḍā says the purpose lām concerns intended killing, not escape. The Study Quran allows the words to reject killing in defense without rejecting every defense. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(أهل العلم); ALUSI-FULL(السيد المرتضى); STUDYQURAN(commentators); TABRISI(المرتضى)+]
- 5:28/q06/p4 He will commit no injustice because he fears God's judgment. — Al-Qurṭubī gives this alternative; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Abel will not choose injustice and Hell. Al-Ṭabarī specifies fear of punishment; Ibn Kathīr says he avoids becoming equal in sin. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI; TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 5:28/q07 Why would Abel refrain from defending himself?  [مَآ أَنَا۠ بِبَاسِطٍۢ يَدِىَ إِلَيْكَ · interpretation]
turns on: The evidence for a law against defense, a lawful personal choice, or an attack by surprise.
- 5:28/q07/p1 His era's law forbade resisting a would-be killer, requiring patient submission. — Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan, al-Jubbāʾī and Ibn Jurayj report this law, with Ibn Jurayj locating it among Israelites. Al-Ṭabrisī says God would exact justice. Al-Zajjāj reports a broader ban on swords, likened to Jesus's time and Muhammad's Meccan period. Ibn ʿĀshūr infers a bar on deadly defense from Abel's fear and says later law abrogated it. Al-Nasafī adds the risk of sharing the killer's sin. [24 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الحسن ومجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن ومجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد والحسن); RAZI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); TABATABAI(روايات); STUDYQURAN(commentators); ZAJJAJ(يقال); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI(الحسن، مجاهد، الجبائي)+; TAB-FULL-; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير)-; TABATABAI-]
- 5:28/q07/p2 He could defend himself but chose restraint out of scruple and hope of reward. — Reports say he was stronger than Cain. Al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī say defense was lawful; al-Baghawī says submission could seek reward. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this, reasoning that Cain was a sinful monotheist, since scrupling against an unbeliever would make less sense; he compares ʿUthmān. Al-Bayḍāwī gives the better course as an alternative, al-Ālūsī the greater reward, and Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses life's sanctity even for an unjust person. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); THALABI(عبد الله بن عمر); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL(عبد الله بن عمر); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو وجمهور الناس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عمر وابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية,عبدالله بن عمرو وجمهور الناس); BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو وابن عباس والجمهور); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī,commentators); IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 5:28/q07/p3 The words warned Cain before the attack; they do not establish a conscious refusal of defense when attacked. — Al-Ṭabarī says no proof shows Abel knew in time and reports he was killed asleep. Al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān say he spoke to deter Cain before a probable threat and was then struck with a stone in sleep. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers stealth and rejects reports of enabled murder as weak, inconsistent with Qurʾān and sound reflection; he raises complicity in sin. The Study Quran says sleep left no chance to defend. [15 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس وجماعة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن جرير,بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI(بعض المتقشفين من أهل العبادة والورع); STUDYQURAN(commentators); TAB-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 5:28/q07/p4 His refusal exposed the enormity of Cain's murderous intent. — The Study Quran frames the commentator dispute over refusal despite an explicit threat and reports this explanation; al-Rāzī says Abel's pre-attack words admonished Cain, and Ibn ʿĀshūr says they warn of murder's gravity. [4 notes: STUDYQURAN(commentators); RAZI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:28/q07/p5 An inherited charge from Adam may have forbidden taking any life, even in defense. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this as an alternative to Abel's own inference from slaughter and hunting; his own apparent reading is Abel's judgment that all life was sacred. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 5:28/q08 Was Abel stronger than Cain?  [يَدِىَ إِلَيْكَ · reports]
- 5:28/q08/p1 Yes; strength made his restraint a matter of scruple rather than incapacity. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr/ʿUmar is named in variant attributions. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Qurṭubī use the strength to infer Cain was a sinful monotheist; al-Biqāʿī likewise says resistance risked excess. Ibn Kathīr's longer report adds the rival sacrifices. ## 5:28/q09 Why use a nominal participle and emphatic bāʾ in the refusal?  [مَآ أَنَا۠ بِبَاسِطٍۢ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the form chiefly marks vile identity, the first steps of murder, or restraint in claiming future certainty.
- 5:28/q09/p1 They emphatically deny that Abel would acquire the shameful character of a killer. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Samīn contrast the verbal condition with its nominal response; the bāʾ strengthens negation. Abū Ḥayyān transmits al-Zamakhsharī; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī adds the oath. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); TABATABAI]
- 5:28/q09/p2 It disavows even the acts leading toward murder. — Al-Ālūsī says rejecting the stretch of the hand is stronger than merely denying the completed killing. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 5:28/q09/p3 It emphasizes his present refusal while reverently avoiding a certain claim about future conduct. — Al-Biqāʿī connects the nominal expression and emphatic negation with reverence toward God. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 5:28/q10 How is innī akhāfu read?  [إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ · readings]
- 5:28/q10/p1 The possessive yāʾ is opened, giving an added fatḥa in innī. — Al-Nasafī names the Hijazis and Abū ʿAmr; al-Nashr names the two Medinans, Ibn Kathīr, and Abū ʿAmr, also for lī an aqūla. Qirāʾāt ER says the longer pronunciation changes no meaning. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL(حجازى وأبو عمرو); NASHR(المدنيان، وابن كثير، وأبو عمرو); QIRAAT-ER(Y-id)]

## 5:28/q11 Why does Abel say he fears God?  [إِنِّىٓ أَخَافُ ٱللَّهَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish fear as restraint, a sign of knowledge, and the causal explanation of the refusal.
- 5:28/q11/p1 Fear of God's punishment restrains him from murder and urges Cain to fear God too. — Al-Ṭabarī names punishment for killing; al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, al-Muqātil and Elmalılı connect fear with refusal. Al-Ālūsī says the reason guides Cain and implies a murderer lacks fear. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects fear with divine recompense and piety, and says God affirms Abel's piety by repeating his words without correction. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 5:28/q11/p2 His fear reveals knowledge of God. — Al-Qāshānī cites 35:28, that the learned truly fear God. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 35:28 and 18:65; God's uncorrected quotation affirms the speaker's knowledge. [3 notes: QASHANI; TABATABAI]
- 5:28/q11/p3 The clause gives the cause of the preceding refusal. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the separated clause beginning with inna signals this causal explanation. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 5:28/q11/p4 Fear recalls God's perfection and care for what He created. — Al-Biqāʿī links divine perfection and the title Lord of the worlds to refusing to destroy God's work. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 5:28/q12 What does 'Lord of the worlds' add?  [رَبَّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the title chiefly evokes sovereign punishment or care for creation.
- 5:28/q12/p1 God owns all creatures and can punish the killer. — Al-Ṭabarī explains the title as owner of every creature, with power to punish wrongful killing. [1 notes: TAB-FULL]
- 5:28/q12/p2 It recalls God's creating and nurturing all, making murder a destruction of His work. — Al-Biqāʿī connects lordship with beneficence and nurturing. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 5:28/q13 How was Abel killed, and what preceded the murder?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether murder occurred by surprise or through Abel's conscious surrender; the accounts also differ in their surrounding details.
- 5:28/q13/p1 Cain killed him treacherously while he slept, striking his head with a large stone. — Al-Ṭabarī and later sources use the sleeping report to challenge an inference of chosen nonresistance. Al-Ṭabrisī says Cain dropped a rock; al-Rāzī and Abū Ḥayyān say a large stone struck his head. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers the stealth account; the Study Quran notes sleep prevented defense. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وجماعة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض المفسرين); TABATABAI(روايات); STUDYQURAN(commentators); TABATABAI+]
- 5:28/q13/p2 A report says Satan taught Cain to smash Abel's head with stones; a raven then taught burial. — The report from ʿAlī ibn al-Ḥusayn also has Abel offer a prized ram and Cain a sheaf, Adam mourn, and Hibat Allah (Shīth) be given. [1 notes: TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين)]
- 5:28/q13/p3 The quarrel followed rejected and accepted offerings, and Cain threatened Abel. — Ibn ʿAmr's report contrasts the shepherd's willing gift of his best fat sheep with the farmer's spoiled grain; Ibn ʿAbbās says fire accepted the sheep when no poor recipients existed and the public rejection prompted the threat. Another report links the threat to marriage. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عبد الله بن عمرو); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ناس من الصحابة)]
- 5:28/q13/p4 A report says Abel allowed Cain to kill him. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records this account but rejects this group of reports as mostly or entirely weak, inconsistent with the Qurʾān and sound reflection, and possibly corrupted. [1 notes: TABATABAI(روايات); TABATABAI-]

## 5:28/q14 What does the verse imply for defense against an attacker under Islamic law?  [مَآ أَنَا۠ بِبَاسِطٍۢ يَدِىَ إِلَيْكَ · law]
turns on: Whether permission to defend carries a duty, and whether nonresistance reports concern general assault or civil strife.
- 5:28/q14/p1 Defense is permitted, and the sounder view makes preventing aggression obligatory. — Al-Qurṭubī reports consensus on permission and prefers obligation; al-Jaṣṣāṣ extends the duty to defending others. Al-Ālūsī cites 49:9 and other texts. The Turkish commentary likewise calls disabling an attacker a right and generally a duty. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī require the least needed force and intent to repel, allowing a killing only if necessary. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(أهل العلم); QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ALUSI-FULL(الجصاص)+; QURTUBI-FULL(علمائنا)+; QURTUBI-FULL(الحشوية)-]
- 5:28/q14/p2 Defense is allowed but not required; one may submit to unjust death for reward. — Al-Wāḥidī cites ʿUthmān and the advice to Muḥammad ibn Maslama. Al-Rāzī, al-Baghawī and al-Ālūsī record the option; al-Bayḍāwī quotes the counsel to be God's slain servant. Abū Ḥayyān narrates ʿUthmān's dream of breaking his fast with the Prophet; Ibn Kathīr reports Ayyūb's statement that he first enacted the verse in this community. [14 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله,عثمان); RAZI-FULL(النبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أيوب السختياني); BURSEVI(البغوي); ALUSI-FULL(بعض المحققين); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī); ALUSI-FULL+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 5:28/q14/p3 One may not defend oneself against an attacker. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes this to the Ḥashwiyya, who cite a report from Abū Dharr; he counters that scholars apply it to uncertain civil strife. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحشوية); QURTUBI-FULL(العلماء,علمائنا)-]
- 5:28/q14/p4 The old rule of nonresistance may have been abrogated, though its timing is disputed. — Al-Ālūsī states this conditionally on Mujāhid's and Ibn Jurayj's reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr says later laws allow necessary defense. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 5:28/q15 How should the reports urging imitation of Abel during civil strife be understood?  [* · reports]
turns on: The fitna setting, the parties' intentions, and the reliability of surrender reports.
- 5:28/q15/p1 They counsel abstention from escalating fighting, even when threatened. — Al-Qurṭubī reports the Prophet's instruction to Saʿd to be like Adam's better son. Ibn Kathīr records Saʿd routes, al-Tirmidhī's ḥasan grading, an Abū Dāwūd version and Ibn ʿAsākir's transmitter identification; al-Durr adds al-Ḥākim's authentication. Reports advise Muḥammad ibn Maslama to cover his face, Abū Dharr to stay home and avoid weapons, and a funeral report to retreat home; Ibn Kathīr notes Abū Dharr transmission through Muslim and Sunan compilers except al-Nasāʾī and an unusual al-Mushaʿath route. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī records ʿUmar, Abū Mūsā and al-Ḥasan reports. Al-Ālūsī cites Khabbāb. ʿUthmān and early pious people reportedly modeled restraint. [19 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(رسول الله); RAZI-FULL(النبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو داود,أيوب السختياني,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DURR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(خباب بن الأرت); TABATABAI(النبي); BAYDAWI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī)]
- 5:28/q15/p2 The two-swords warning concerns mutual intent to kill, especially political conflict, not ordinary necessary defense. — Ibn Kathīr transmits the Ṣaḥīḥayn report that both armed Muslims are in Hell because the slain intended murder. Al-Ālūsī makes mutual murderous intention decisive; al-Murtaḍā distinguishes defense aimed only at escape. Ibn ʿĀshūr limits the setting to struggles for dominance where yielding would stop the attack. Al-Qurṭubī's scholars similarly confine Abū Dharr's advice to uncertain civil strife. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL(السيد المرتضى); IBNASHUR-FULL(رسول الله); QURTUBI-FULL(العلماء)]
- 5:28/q15/p3 Using fitna reports to require Abel's surrender is unsound. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Qurʾān and sound reports support defense of self and truth, citing 49:9. He distrusts narrators who sat out ʿAlī's wars and would reinterpret or discard their reports; he calls many surrender reports weak or corrupt. [4 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 5:28/q16 How does Abel's statement relate to Cain bearing both sins?  [* · links]
- 5:28/q16/p1 Cain bears the sin of killing Abel and his own earlier sin, not an unrelated person's liability. — ʿAbd al-Razzāq reports Qatāda's gloss; Mujāhid, Qatāda and al-Ḍaḥḥāk give the same explanation. The marriage-threat account frames the statement as Abel's response. The Turkish commentary cites 17:15 against ordinary transfer of another's sin. Abū ʿUbayda explains tabūʾa as bearing or admitting sin and cites ʿAmr b. Ḥunā for blood-for-blood use; he also notes retaliatory killing and arriving at lodging as other senses. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Abel did not seek death to damn Cain. [6 notes: MAJAZ; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); DURR-FULL(مجاهد,ناس من الصحابة); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI]

## 5:28/q17 What inward meaning is given to Abel's refusal and fear?  [* · inward]
- 5:28/q17/p1 Intellect does not destroy estimation, whose sensory and practical work God created for a wise purpose. — Al-Qāshānī says estimates of partial interests, sensory judgments, livelihood arrangements, hope and aspiration sustain human life. Intellect therefore leaves estimation to its proper function; Abel's God-fearing knowledge is supported with 35:28. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 5:28/q18 How does this passage connect with the plot against Muhammad in 5:11?  [* · links]
- 5:28/q18/p1 The repeated hand-stretching idiom links the plots and invites leniency toward those who threatened Muhammad and his followers. — The Study Quran notes the shared idiom and reports al-Ṭabarī's application of Abel's restraint toward the plotting Jews; it also cites a report to follow the better son. [3 notes: STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī)]

## 5:28/q19 What religious knowledge does Abel's dialogue convey?  [* · theology]
- 5:28/q19/p1 It shows innate knowledge of monotheism, prophecy, resurrection, piety, injustice, divine law, and just recompense. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God's uncorrected quotation of Abel's words about acceptance and fear affirms his piety and knowledge; he compares the learned who fear God in 35:28 and Moses' learned companion in 18:65. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:28/q20 Why can the Adam story be told in different lengths and forms?  [* · rhetoric]
- 5:28/q20/p1 Selective retelling fits its audience and rhetorical purpose, sometimes correcting a mistaken prior account. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares Moses' call expanded in Ṭā Hā and al-Shuʿarāʾ (20:24, 26:10) with its brief telling in al-Furqān (25:35–36); he says earlier scholars may have left this insight implicit. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 5:28/q21 How should the Qur'anic account of the brothers be assessed beside the Torah?  [* · links]
- 5:28/q21/p1 Compare the two accounts and judge their differences. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī urges the comparison without giving a further difference in this note. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 5:28/q22 What makes a report a cause of revelation rather than a case covered by a verse?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether 'revealed about' records a precipitating event or merely an included example, and how that claim is authenticated.
- 5:28/q22/p1 A cause is an event occurring at revelation; 'revealed about' may instead identify an included case. — Al-Suyūṭī defines an occasion this way and cites Ibn Taymiyya and al-Zarkashī on the broad phrase. He says a clearly precipitating event is unanimously treated as musnad. Al-Bukhārī and al-Ḥākim treat a Companion's 'revealed about' as musnad, the latter requiring witnessed revelation; other scholars and most musnads call it tafsīr. Al-Wāḥidī requires witness reports, while others allow tentative Companion inference. Al-Suyūṭī points to al-Itqān for examples. [10 notes: SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن تيمية,البخاري,الحاكم,الزركشي,الواحدي,غيره)]
- 5:28/q22/p2 Knowing the occasion is a strong aid, and sometimes essential, to interpretation. — Al-Suyūṭī rejects dismissing occasions as useless history; al-Wāḥidī says story and cause are necessary, Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd calls them a strong path, Ibn Taymiyya says they resolve ambiguity, and ʿUbayda warns that witnesses to revelation have passed. [5 notes: SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن تيميه,ابن دقيق العيد,الواحدي,عبيده)]

## 5:28/q23 What kinds of statements can be abrogated, and by what authority?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether Abel's words convey a binding earlier rule, and which later text superseded it.
- 5:28/q23/p1 Only prescriptions can be abrogated, including commands phrased as reports. — Al-Suyūṭī excludes ordinary reports, promises, and threats. Ibn Ḥabīb permits Sunna to abrogate when revealed by God's command, but not independent reasoning. Al-Shāfiʿī requires a supporting Qurʾānic text for Sunna abrogating Qurʾān and a supporting Sunna for the reverse, showing agreement. [3 notes: ITQAN(ابن حبيب النيسابوري,الشافعي)]
- 5:28/q23/p2 A prior nonresistance rule might be abrogated; whether this occurred before Islam is disputed. — Al-Ālūsī conditions this on the reading attributed to Mujāhid and Ibn Jurayj; Ibn ʿĀshūr says later law permits necessary defense. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
