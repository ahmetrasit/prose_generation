# 38:6: 15 questions

## 38:6/q01 What does انطلق mean here?  [وَٱنطَلَقَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the departure is physical or figurative speech.
- 38:6/q01/p1 It means to depart or go away. — Sībawayh compares ذهب and انطلق. Māwardī, Ṭabrisī and Ibn al-Jawzī relate the ease of departure to an open or cheerful face; Qurṭubī adds quickness. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls physical setting out the literal sense. [7 notes: SIBAWAYH; MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 38:6/q01/p2 It can mean launching into speech or beginning an undertaking. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports an idiom for spreading speech. Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Burhān compare a tongue setting off; Ālūsī also calls it metaphorical attribution. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains the verb of beginning from setting out toward an undertaking, citing al-Nabhānī's line with dhahaba plus a verb and the youths' qāma in 18:14. Itqān says the tongues set out. [10 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(النبهاني); ITQAN]

## 38:6/q02 Where did the chiefs set out from, or toward?  [وَٱنطَلَقَ ٱلْمَلَأُ · referent]
turns on: Whether departure is from or toward the council, or a general public action.
- 38:6/q02/p1 They left Abū Ṭālib's meeting after the Prophet's uncompromising answer. — Wāḥidī and others place departure after the council. Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī say the Prophet silenced them. Bursawī and Ālūsī stress lost hope of a compromise; Ṭabāṭabāʾī infers failed negotiation. Elmalılı depicts a hurried exit. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جماعة من المفسرين); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية); TABATABAI; ELMALILI]
- 38:6/q02/p2 They went toward Abū Ṭālib to complain about Muhammad. — Muqātil reports their approach and request for arbitration. Thaʿlabī and Muqātil in Wāḥidī construe the movement toward Abū Ṭālib; Qurṭubī notes their visit during his illness. [5 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 38:6/q02/p3 They spread their statements or repeatedly walked away from affected hearers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya cites common speech about setting out with a public appeal. Iṣlāḥī describes leaders leaving when listeners were affected by monotheism and urging others to avoid hearing it. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 38:6/q03 Who are the الملأ?  [ٱلْمَلَأُ مِنْهُمْ · referent]
turns on: The reported count and roster vary.
- 38:6/q03/p1 They are Quraysh's notables and leaders. — Muqātil counts twenty-seven and names al-Walīd, Abū Jahl, Umayya and Ubayy. Ṭabarī lists Abū Jahl, al-ʿĀṣ and al-Aswad ibn ʿAbd Yaghūth; al-Suddī adds al-Aswad ibn al-Muṭṭalib. Ibn Isḥāq in Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī adds the Rabīʿa brothers, Umayya and Abū Muʿayṭ. Itqān and Ālūsī give differing rosters. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says these leaders represent others in opinion; Rāzī and Bursawī derive the title from filling eyes and hearts with awe; Biqāʿī distinguishes them from followers. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(السدي); NAHHAS(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(غير ابن عطية)]
- 38:6/q03/p2 One account counts twenty-five chiefs. — Bursawī's count for the gathering. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 38:6/q03/p3 One account counts twenty-seven chiefs. — Wāḥidī's commentators give this count after ʿUmar's conversion; Muqātil concurs. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون)]

## 38:6/q04 Who voiced the command?  [ٱلْمَلَأُ أَنِ ٱمْشُوا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the counsel was collective or traced to an individual.
- 38:6/q04/p1 The chiefs spoke among themselves or to their followers. — Abū ʿUbayda expands compressed speech as mutual counsel, call or urging. Ibn Qutayba and others say one another; Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī say nobles addressed commoners, Abū Ḥayyān followers and assistants, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows either. [20 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 38:6/q04/p2 ʿUqba ibn Abī Muʿayṭ spoke. — Mujāhid via Ṭabarī, Māwardī and Durr names him; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī and al-Mahdawī do likewise. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(عقبة بن أبي معيط); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI(المهدوي); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 38:6/q04/p3 Abū Jahl was the leader. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies him; Māwardī says he left Abū Ṭālib despairing of stopping Muhammad. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 38:6/q04/p4 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra urged the others. — Muqātil says he urged a visit to Abū Ṭālib and fidelity to their gods, comparing 25:42 and God's response at 41:24. [1 notes: MUQATIL(الوليد بن المغيرة)]

## 38:6/q05 What does منهم identify?  [مِنْهُمْ · referent]
- 38:6/q05/p1 The chiefs belong to Quraysh, distinguished from lesser followers. — Rāzī explicitly says Quraysh; Biqāʿī distinguishes their elite from followers and outsiders. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 38:6/q06 How is أن امشوا constructed?  [أَنِ ٱمْشُوا۟ · grammar]
turns on: Whether أن explains implied speech or is governed as a source expression.
- 38:6/q06/p1 أن is explanatory, like أي, introducing implied speech without case. — Al-Khalīl via Sībawayh says they went out saying Go, comparing 5:117. Abū ʿUbayda, Zajjāj, Wāḥidī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī supply a saying verb. Ibn ʿAṭiyya denies case position. Zamakhsharī via Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn, with Bayḍāwī and Nasafī, reasons that departure from debate implies speech. Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn also supply a circumstantial conversation; others make departure itself speech. Ibn ʿĀshūr supports both with 38:4–5. Al-Ṣaffār in Burhān prefers explanation because a bare noun cannot be a speech complement unless the utterance itself. [31 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل); MAJAZ; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,أهل المعاني); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري,قيل); BURHAN(الخليل); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ITQAN; TABATABAI; BURHAN(الصفار)+]
- 38:6/q06/p2 أن is source-forming or accusative, with an implied bi. — Ṭabarī attaches accusative أن to انطلقوا. Naḥḥās, Zajjāj, al-Farrāʾ and Abū Isḥāq through Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī and Qurṭubī understand بأن; Wāḥidī says departure must be away from Abū Ṭālib. Samīn also permits a verbal noun governed by an omitted circumstantial clause. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء وأبو إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); SAMIN-DURR(قيل); BURHAN; BURHAN(الصفار)-]
- 38:6/q06/p3 An omitted verb of saying precedes the command. — Abū ʿUbayda expands it as mutual advice or call; Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Abū Isḥāq supply 'saying'. [4 notes: MAJAZ; TABATABAI; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); ZAJJAJ]

## 38:6/q07 What variant wording or pronunciation is reported?  [وَٱنطَلَقَ أَنِ ٱمْشُوا۟ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted wording and pause pronunciation.
- 38:6/q07/p1 The common reading has أن امشوا; restart امشوا with kasra after a pause. — Samīn and Biqāʿī derive the kasra from the original third-letter vowel; Biqāʿī contrasts the feminine ḍamma. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL]
- 38:6/q07/p2 Ibn Abī ʿAbla omits أن. — Rāzī names him; Bayḍāwī reports the form. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن أبي عبلة); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 38:6/q07/p3 Ibn Masʿūd reads that the chiefs left walking, then said to persevere. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya gives وانطلق الملأ منهم يمشون أن اصبروا. Ālūsī says يمشون is circumstantial or independent. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(عبدالله بن مسعود)]
- 38:6/q07/p4 Ibn ʿAbbās is credited with وانطبق الملأ منهم يمشون. — Rāzī reports this wording. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 38:6/q08 What does امشوا command?  [ٱمْشُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it signals literal movement, a continuing way, multiplication, or a prayer; Khaṭṭābī disputes its stylistic force.
- 38:6/q08/p1 Walk away from the gathering or leave the Prophet's presence. — Ibn ʿAbbās glosses it as go. Abū Isḥāq has them disperse from Abū Ṭālib's meeting. Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī take literal steps out; Samīn calls ordinary walking primary. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn ʿĀshūr allow a literal order. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 38:6/q08/p2 Continue in inherited religion and opposition to Muhammad. — Māwardī cites the idiom 'walk on a matter' for adherence. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers 'continue on your path'; Rāzī says they saw no way to stop Muhammad. Khaṭṭābī says calm 'walk on' suits familiar steady practice, while 'go forth' would imply agitation. Iṣlāḥī adds opposition until death. [16 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; KHATTABI+]
- 38:6/q08/p3 Go toward Abū Ṭālib to complain. — Thaʿlabī and Muqātil in Wāḥidī place the walk toward Abū Ṭālib. [3 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(بعضهم)]
- 38:6/q08/p4 Gather and increase in number to defend the gods. — Khaṭṭābī reports multiplication and assembly. Zamakhsharī, followed by others, compares a prolific woman and the auspicious livestock word. [5 notes: KHATTABI(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 38:6/q08/p5 It is a prayer for abundant cattle. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it grammatically weak and contextually unsuitable. Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī say livestock أمشى requires hamzat al-qaṭʿ; Ālūsī calls the sense remote. [0 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(بعض الناس)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)-; SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]
- 38:6/q08/p6 The word is weaker than 'proceed' or 'go forth'. — Khaṭṭābī reports this objection to its expressive force. [1 notes: KHATTABI; KHATTABI-]

## 38:6/q09 What does 'be patient over your gods' mean?  [وَٱصْبِرُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ ءَالِهَتِكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: How ṣabr and ʿalā express persistence.
- 38:6/q09/p1 Persist in worship and loyalty to their gods despite criticism. — Thaʿlabī, Wāḥidī, Damghānī and Ibn ʿĀshūr compare 25:42. Ibn ʿĀshūr traces ṣabr to tying an animal, then composure in hardship; ʿalā figuratively marks established adherence with worship implied, contrasting 73:10 and 68:48 and comparing 2:5. Bursawī and Ālūsī specify enduring disparagement; Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses not abandoning worship. Iṣlāḥī describes fidelity to ancestral religion until death. [29 notes: MUQATIL(الوليد بن المغيرة); IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 38:6/q10 What lesson is drawn from the chiefs' patience?  [وَٱصْبِرُوا۟ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the example is used to condemn idolatry or exhort believers.
- 38:6/q10/p1 Their persistence in idolatry is censured. — Sahl distinguishes patience in obedience, pain and suffering from persistence in disobedience. Ibn Fāris in Burhān and Itqān says patience is normally praised but these passages are exceptions. Burhān says continued idolatry ends in the Fire, citing 25:75. [5 notes: TUSTARI; BURHAN(ابن فارس); ITQAN(ابن فارس)]
- 38:6/q10/p2 Believers have greater reason to persevere in worship. — ʿUmar reproaches believers who abandon patience. Al-Qushayrī and Taʾwīlāt Najmiyya compare believers' resolve with idolaters'; the latter extends it to lovers seeking the Beloved. [3 notes: SULAMI(عمر); BIQAI-FULL(القشيري); BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 38:6/q11 What does 'this is a thing intended' mean?  [إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَشَىْءٌۭ يُرَادُ · interpretation]
turns on: The referent of هذا and the force of يراد: the Prophet's mission, a threat, fate, religion, or patience.
- 38:6/q11/p1 Muhammad's call is a sustained plan to establish monotheism and abolish their gods. — Abū al-Suʿūd and Ālūsī cite his resolve and failed mediation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says هذا points to the one-God confession heard in council; the chiefs frame it as contrived to suggest falsehood. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects a version saying merely that firmness shows a quest for something great, as inconsistent with context. [5 notes: BURSEVI(أبو السعود); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 38:6/q11/p2 It is a plot against Quraysh or their religion. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Muqātil via Wāḥidī link it to ʿUmar's conversion and more Muslim followers; Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurṭubī agree. Jalālayn calls it a scheme. Bursawī says they feared spreading danger, while noting that the chiefs themselves repeatedly plotted harm. [11 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس ومقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BURSEVI(الجلالين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 38:6/q11/p3 Muhammad seeks rank, sovereignty, and control through monotheism. — Māwardī says they accuse him of seeking leadership. Ibn Jarīr via Ibn Kathīr adds followers. Al-Qaffāl via Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī says power over property and children rather than religion. Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares the charge against Noah's messenger in 23:24. [10 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الفقال); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القفال); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير); ALUSI-FULL(القفال,قيل); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 38:6/q11/p4 The affair is God's decree and cannot be repelled. — Rāzī says the otherwise inexplicable growth of Muhammad's religion points to God's will; Nasafī says unavoidable decree requires patience. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 38:6/q11/p5 An unavoidable calamity of time is destined for them. — Rāzī, Nasafī and Ālūsī describe a fate with no escape and bitter patience. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects it as contextually weak; Kuran Yolu reports it. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 38:6/q11/p6 Their religion is sought for seizure and abolition. — Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī identify their ancestral religion as the threatened object. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 38:6/q11/p7 Their old religion or their patience is desirable and worth holding. — Biqāʿī and Saʿdī al-Muftī make continued old practice desirable; Ālūsī makes patience itself sought for its praised result. Asad calls it a sensible course. Kuran Yolu prefers this as a request to stand firm and defeat Muhammad. Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the patience-as-virtue account as out of context. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(سعدي المفتي); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABATABAI(قيل)-]
- 38:6/q11/p8 Abandoning idols would strip blessings and bring drought or distress. — Naḥḥās fears removed blessings and calamity; Ṭabrisī and Study Quran specify the belief that idols cause drought and distress; Qurṭubī extends harm to earth's people. [4 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭs)]
- 38:6/q11/p9 The intended event is corruption or destruction for the people of earth. — Ṭabrisī says it soon brings destruction and frees them of it; Baghawī and Qurṭubī extend the matter to earth's people. [3 notes: TABRISI; BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 38:6/q11/p10 Muhammad's prophetic rise and eminence are intended for them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they must submit to his rise; Abū Ḥayyān identifies it as the referent of هذا. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 38:6/q11/p11 Monotheism or leadership is what everyone wants. — Bayḍāwī offers the call or political leadership as universally desired. Ālūsī says not every wish is fulfilled. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 38:6/q11/p12 They should cling to the old religion until Muhammad dies. — Bursawī says patience is their strongest defense, expecting death to overtake him. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 38:6/q11/p13 The Prophet seeks something they do not understand. — Biqāʿī offers this alternative to desirable persistence. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 38:6/q12 What function does the final clause serve in their speech?  [إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَشَىْءٌۭ يُرَادُ · rhetoric]
- 38:6/q12/p1 It explains the order to persevere or the duty to obey it. — Bursawī and Ālūsī relate it to the chiefs' experience of Muhammad's resolve. Ibn ʿĀshūr says it strengthens suspicion that his call is false. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 38:6/q13 What episode prompted the chiefs' words?  [* · reports]
turns on: The notes give a council occasion, a chronology after ʿUmar's conversion, a later application at Badr, and a sequel.
- 38:6/q13/p1 Quraysh's delegation sought Abū Ṭālib's intervention, rejected the one-word confession, and left. — Ibn ʿAbbās in Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Wāḥidī and Durr makes the visit the occasion. Muqātil gives their arbitration request, Abū Ṭālib's summons, proposed mutual noninterference, Muhammad's single word promising Arab obedience and non-Arab submission, Abū Jahl's offer of ten, and their recoil from one God. Al-Suddī says they feared Arab reproach if they attacked after Abū Ṭālib died. Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes versions differ: one adds an offer of earth's gold, another the sun and moon. Kuran Yolu says prior ridicule failed; Durr includes a threat to Muhammad and his Lord and mentions 38:7. [29 notes: MUQATIL(أبو طالب,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,قريش); TAB-FULL(السدي); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(محمد بن إسحاق); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(IK, Q, R, Ṭ)]
- 38:6/q13/p2 The affair followed ʿUmar's conversion and growth of the Muslims. — Muqātil via Māwardī and Qurṭubī links the remark to ʿUmar. Wāḥidī's commentators say Quraysh then asked Muhammad to abandon his God and their idols; Baghawī stresses Muslim growth. [5 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-ASBAB(المفسرون); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل)]
- 38:6/q13/p3 At Badr the Prophet applied the verse to enemy chiefs. — Abū Mijlaz reports that a man called them women; the Prophet corrected him, called them chiefs and recited the verse. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو مجلز); DURR-FULL(أبو مجلز); ALUSI-FULL(أبو مجلز)]
- 38:6/q13/p4 Afterward Muhammad urged Abū Ṭālib to confess God's oneness, but he refused. — Al-Suddī says Abū Ṭālib feared Arabs would attribute conversion to fear of death. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says he feared descendants' blame and died on ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib's religion. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم وأبو طالب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]

## 38:6/q14 How does the verse connect to the sūra and other passages?  [* · links]
- 38:6/q14/p1 The chiefs' rejection continues the sūra's account of arrogant resistance to monotheism. — Iṣlāḥī says Ṣād shares its group's foundation in divine oneness: 1–11 presents the reminder rejected from arrogance like earlier nations, with a messenger from among Quraysh called sorcerer and liar. Kuran Yolu says monotheism threatened their traditions, status and interests. Study Quran points to 34:34 for notables' rejection. [5 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 38:6/q14/p2 The Abū Ṭālib episode also relates to 28:56. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn Kathīr tie Abū Ṭālib's refusal to profess faith to 28:56. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي)]

## 38:6/q15 What ancillary linguistic observations accompany citations of this verse?  [* · grammar]
turns on: These are grammatical and linguistic comparisons preserved in the notes.
- 38:6/q15/p1 Arabic has distinct forms with distinct meanings, synonyms, and polysemous forms. — Sībawayh contrasts جلس and ذهب; ذهب and انطلق share a sense; وجد عليه means resentment whereas وجد can mean finding a lost thing. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 38:6/q15/p2 Derived انطلق illustrates initial kasra in related imperfect patterns. — Sībawayh traces prefix kasra by analogy with forms beginning in hamzat al-waṣl, whose alif was omitted for ease. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 38:6/q15/p3 A definite and indefinite noun, or nouns under incompatible governors, cannot share one adjective; a new clause or accusative praise resolves it. — Sībawayh permits قائمين for a man and the pronoun in معه, implicit with مع امرأة; he bars shared adjectives for mismatched case, definiteness, nested annexations such as أخوى ابنين and أخوى ابنيك, or a known and unknown referent, while matching subject relations allow one. الرجلان الصالحان can start a sentence or be accusative praise, like al-Kharnāq's النازلين and الطيبون; the accusative is not ḥāl because an article-bearing adjective cannot be ḥāl. It can mark insult. Al-Khalīl rejects ناقة وفصيلها الراتعان and mismatched governors, and reads أنفسهما as fresh nominative identification or accusative 'I mean them,' not praise. [17 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 38:6/q15/p4 Independent إن after حتى differs from subordinate أن, and lightened or heavy أن may retain an implied pronoun. — Sībawayh makes إن after حتى an independent clause, not a subordinate noun; he compares فإذا إنه and an alternative فإذا أنه for a state of servitude. Al-Khalīl permits حتى أنك أحمق as حتى حمقك but rejects كما أنك on the pattern كما أنت. At 10:10 and 24:9 Sībawayh makes heavy أن with an omitted pronoun; at 37:104–105 he allows أنك قد صدقت while al-Khalīl also permits explanatory أي. Poetic lightened كأن وريديه and أن هالك retain governing force; nominative كأن وريداه or an إنما/لكن analysis is also possible. He supplies a pronoun in أن بسم الله and argues from the awkwardness of أن يقول ذاك without a negative or future marker. [14 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 38:6/q15/p5 Elsewhere أن with an imperative can be subjunctive, while explanatory أن resembles this verse. — Sībawayh analyzes كتبت إليه أن افعل and أمرته أن قم with subjunctive أن linked to an imperative, supported by أوعزت إليه بأن افعل where a preposition attaches as to a noun; he also offers explanatory أي in the comparison. [2 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 38:6/q15/p6 The Qurʾān's clear Arabic permits plain eloquence, and a cited discussion proposes several ellipses. — Abū ʿUbayda cites 14:4 for revelation in the audience's language and says early Arabs needed no explanation. Khaṭṭābī values grandeur, sweetness and ease over harsh obscurity. Nashr gives omitted replies such as 'you have surely been brought' or 'it is surely a miracle.' Itqān says its cited work details unnamed peoples, animals, places and times. [4 notes: MAJAZ; KHATTABI; NASHR(قيل); ITQAN]
