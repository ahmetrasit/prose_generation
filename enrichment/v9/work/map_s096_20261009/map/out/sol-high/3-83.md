# 3:83: 27 questions

## 3:83/q01 How does this rebuke connect to the preceding covenant and sūra?  [أَفَغَيْرَ دِينِ ٱللَّهِ يَبْغُونَ · links]
turns on: Whether the link is the covenant, preceding signs, universal submission, or prophetic authority.
- 3:83/q01/p1 It follows the covenant to affirm Muhammad and exposes rejecting scripturalists as seeking another religion despite the evidence. — Al-Rāzī cites 3:81 and says envy and hostility explain refusal, as with Iblīs; al-Bursawī says the covenant was in their scriptures. Al-Biqāʿī links 3:18–19, al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī the one religion and later messengers, and al-Sāmarrāʾī 3:85. [16 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; ELMALILI; ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL+]
- 3:83/q01/p2 The sequence follows the prior signs and turns their force into a fresh question. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, and al-Ṭabrisī say fāʾ adds sequence that wāw lacks; Abū Ḥayyān sees the link to 3:82. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 3:83/q01/p3 Universal submission supplies the argument that people should seek Islam, the religion suited to human nature. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says this clause gives the cause in place of its conclusion. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 3:83/q01/p4 Resistance to God's messengers is untenable because no one can evade His grasp. — Al-Biqāʿī notes that miracles establish messengership, so defiance of the messenger defies his sender. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 3:83/q02 How are the opening hamza and fāʾ construed?  [أَفَغَيْرَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether fāʾ connects directly, follows an omitted question, or the opening is rendered assertively.
- 3:83/q02/p1 The hamza introduces rebuke while fāʾ coordinates the question with the preceding statement or result. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, and al-Nasafī connect it to prior transgressors; al-Rāzī says the verb is questioned. Al-Samīn notes the hamza's precedence. Al-Ālūsī permits coordination with the combined condition and result or result alone and prefers direct linkage where ellipsis is unnatural. [15 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن هشام); BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 3:83/q02/p2 An omitted question, 'Do they turn away?', precedes the fāʾ and the seeking question. — Sibawayh and most grammarians supply it; Ibn Hishām calls it primary. Al-Shams ibn al-Ṣāʾigh defends comparable ellipsis in 13:33; al-Ālūsī accepts it when natural. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الشمس بن الصائغ,سيبويه والجمهور); BIQAI-FULL]
- 3:83/q02/p3 It can also be translated as the assertion that they seek another religion. — Kur'an Yolu reports al-Rāzī's rendering, but says either form marks the futility of turning away. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]

## 3:83/q03 Why is 'other than God's religion' placed before 'seek'?  [أَفَغَيْرَ دِينِ ٱللَّهِ يَبْغُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fronting focuses the false object or simply uses an allowable order while rebuking the act.
- 3:83/q03/p1 The object is fronted to focus denial and rebuke on seeking a false religion. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, al-Rāzī, and al-Ālūsī focus on the object. Al-Biqāʿī and al-Itqān say this does not entail exclusive seeking; interpreting it as 'only another religion' misplaces the rebuke. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ITQAN(تقي الدين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)-]
- 3:83/q03/p2 Denial concerns the act of seeking; fronting is syntactic latitude and resembles the verse's cadence. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects al-Zamakhsharī's rationale; al-Samīn questions the Shaykh's account, and al-Ālūsī dismisses Abū Ḥayyān's explanation. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); SAMIN-DURR-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]

## 3:83/q04 What is the grammatical role of 'other than'?  [أَفَغَيْرَ · grammar]
- 3:83/q04/p1 Ghayra is the accusative object of 'seek'. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī give this parse. [2 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 3:83/q05 What does 'God's religion' designate here?  [دِينِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether religion is described as general obedience, Muhammad's Islam, or universal dependence.
- 3:83/q05/p1 It is obedience and surrender to God, the true religion He legislated. — Al-Ṭabarī calls it obedience; Abū Ḥayyān says God legislated it; Ibn Kathīr describes worship of God alone; al-Biqāʿī notes its perfection. Al-Bursawī calls for sincere service. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 3:83/q05/p2 It is Muhammad's religion, Islam, which alone is accepted. — Ibn al-Jawzī names Muhammad's religion. Al-Zajjāj invokes affirmation of every messenger and 3:85; al-Ṭabrisī calls other religions false. Al-Qāshānī says only truth is prescribed; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links Islam with human nature. [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; QASHANI; TABATABAI]
- 3:83/q05/p3 All beings follow God's religion at the level of divine command, and no other religion is sought in reality. — Al-Qāshānī links final return to universal recognition. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 3:83/q06 What does 'seek' mean, and why do these people seek elsewhere?  [يَبْغُونَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the focus is the word's sense, sustained practice, or the motives and figurative scope.
- 3:83/q06/p1 Yabghūn means seek or pursue; here it describes following or practicing another religion. — Abū Ḥayyān says 'seeking' implies sustained practice; al-Jishumī traces baghy to seeking and unjust striving for superiority. Asad gives the literal object. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad)]
- 3:83/q06/p2 They knowingly turn away despite clear proof, from defiance, envy, or failure to seek truth properly. — Al-Zajjāj likens them to Iblīs. Al-Māturīdī says neglect of genuine inquiry is figurative seeking, while resistance after proof is obstinacy. [5 notes: ZAJJAJ; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الماتريدي)]

## 3:83/q07 Who are the seekers in the third-person reading?  [يَبْغُونَ · referent]
- 3:83/q07/p1 They are Jews, Christians, and others who disbelieved after the covenant. — Al-Wāḥidī calls the reading God's condemnatory report of their conduct. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]

## 3:83/q08 How are the seeking and returning verbs read?  [يَبْغُونَ يُرْجَعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Person and voice of each verb, and which reader uses each form.
- 3:83/q08/p1 Both are read in the second person, directly addressing the audience. — Al-Ṭabarī prefers continuity with the prior address; al-Fārisī cites 3:84. Al-Rāzī says the address can include believers and unbelievers. [27 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء الحجاز من مكة والمدينة وقراء الكوفة); IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الباقون); THALABI(أبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الباقون); BAGHAWI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو; عاصم; الباقون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو، الباقون، حفص عن عاصم، يعقوب); RAZI-FULL(الباقون); QURTUBI-FULL(الباقون); NASAFI-FULL(حفص وأبو عمرو وسائر القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو وحفص وعياش ويعقوب وسهل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الباقون); QIRAAT-ER(Imp); TAB-FULL+]
- 3:83/q08/p2 Both are read in the third person, reporting rejecters or transgressors. — Ḥafṣ is repeatedly named, with Yaʿqūb, Sahl, and others in varying assignments; 3:82 supports the reference. Al-Khalawayh says God expresses wonder to the Prophet; Ibn al-Jawzī unusually calls it the majority reading. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل الحجاز); IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو,حفص عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(حفص عن عاصم); THALABI(الحسن وحميد ويعقوب وسلام وسهل وصفوان); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر عن عاصم ويعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو جعفر,حفص عن عاصم); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو; عاصم; الباقون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو، الباقون، حفص عن عاصم، يعقوب); RAZI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(حفص وغيره); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو عمرو وعاصم ويعقوب); NASAFI-FULL(حفص وأبو عمرو وسائر القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو وحفص وعياش ويعقوب وسهل); SAMIN-DURR(حفص عن عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(عاصم)]
- 3:83/q08/p3 The first is third person, the return second person, moving from specific rejecters to everyone's return. — Abū ʿAmr is usually assigned this mixed form. Al-Khalawayh prefers its breadth; al-Ṭabarī permits the person shift. Sources specify vowels differently. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(بعض أهل البصرة); IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو); FARISI-HUJJA(أبو عمرو); THALABI(أبو عمرو); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عمرو); BAGHAWI-FULL(حفص عن عاصم); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عمرو; عاصم; الباقون); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عمرو، الباقون، حفص عن عاصم، يعقوب); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عمرو); NASAFI-FULL(حفص وأبو عمرو وسائر القراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عمرو وحفص وعياش ويعقوب وسهل); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(الباقون); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA+]
- 3:83/q08/p4 The first is second person, the return third person, shifting from addressed rejecters to all accountable beings. — Al-Jishumī and al-Rāzī attribute this to Abū ʿAmr, contrary to many other assignments. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو عمرو); RAZI-FULL(أبو عمرو)]
- 3:83/q08/p5 The return may be passive second person, 'you are returned,' or active third person, 'they return'. — Qirāʾāt ER contrasts imposed return with subjects portrayed as returning; Asad gives literal passive force. Al-Bayḍāwī notes the yāʾ form may refer back to 'whoever'. [4 notes: QIRAAT-ER(Imp,Pss); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); BAYDAWI-FULL]
- 3:83/q08/p6 Some notes register yāʾ and tāʾ variation without complete or consistent reader assignments. — Ibn Mujāhid's excerpt names no readers; al-Nashr lists third-person readers at a related locus. [2 notes: IBNMUJAHID; NASHR(ابن كثير وحمزة والكسائي وخلف ورويس)]

## 3:83/q09 What does aslama mean here?  [أَسْلَمَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether it denotes obedience, recognition, irresistible created dependence, or another Qur'anic sense of Islam.
- 3:83/q09/p1 It means submitted, obeyed, yielded, or humbled oneself. — Ibn Qutayba likens entering submission to entering a season and cites 4:94; Ibn al-Jawzī equates istislām. Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, al-Qurṭubī, and others gloss compliance and abasement; other Qur'anic examples include 10:90, 27:44, and 37:103. [13 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير,ابن قتيبة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(عامر الشعبي، الزجاج); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN; DAMGHANI]
- 3:83/q09/p2 It means recognition of God's lordship, creatorhood, and human servanthood, even without sincere worship. — Ibn ʿAbbās and Abū Sinān gloss knowledge; Mujāhid invokes 29:61, 31:25, 39:38, and 43:87. Al-Ḍāmghānī compares outward profession in 49:14 and 9:74. Abū al-ʿĀliya contrasts sincerity with associating partners. [21 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,أبو سنان,ابن عباس); THALABI(أبو العالية,عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية رفيع,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية، مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد وأبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,أبو سنان,ابن عباس,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية); DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 3:83/q09/p3 It means subjection to God's creative governance and decree, which no creature can evade. — Al-Zajjāj and Ibn Kaysān invoke created constitution; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses the past tense and willing/unwilling contrast, distinguishing creational from devotional Islam. Al-Ṭabarī also speaks of humbling and exclusive lordship. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(ابن كيسان); MAWARDI-FULL(عامر الشعبي، الزجاج); IBNABIHATIM(عامر); TAB-FULL(عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كيسان والزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج، الشعبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن كيسان والزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; DURR-FULL(الشعبي)]
- 3:83/q09/p4 Islam can elsewhere mean professed religion, monotheism, sincerity, or outward acknowledgment. — Ibn al-Jawzī cites 3:19, 22:78, 5:44, 2:131, 3:20, 31:22, 9:74, and 49:14. The Ḥadīth of Gabriel and Ibn Abī Shayba distinguish outward Islam from inward faith; al-Burhān relates al-Salām and al-Muʾmin; al-Ḍāmghānī also lists greeting among salām's senses. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); BURHAN(ابن أبي شيبة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); DAMGHANI]

## 3:83/q10 How may the adjacent mīms in 'aslama man' be pronounced?  [أَسْلَمَ مَن · readings]
- 3:83/q10/p1 The first mīm may optionally assimilate into the following mīm. — Al-Naḥḥās notes the optional assimilation. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 3:83/q11 Who are those in the heavens and the earth?  [مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase is exhaustive or particularly refers to named groups.
- 3:83/q11/p1 The phrase includes all created inhabitants: angels in heaven and humans and jinn on earth. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says man includes angels, humans, and jinn; al-Nasafī and Study Quran identify the inhabitants. Al-Biqāʿī includes those with bodily and intellectual defensive power. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); NASAFI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, R); BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; TAB-FULL]
- 3:83/q11/p2 Heavenly submitters are angels; earthly willing groups include the Emigrants, Helpers, ʿAbd al-Qays, Banū Sulaym, or Bahrainis. — The lists differ: the Prophetic report names Helpers and ʿAbd al-Qays; al-Ḥasan and Maṭar add or omit Emigrants, Banū Sulaym, or Bahrainis. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مطر الوراق); IBNABIHATIM(الحسن); THALABI(أنس بن مالك,الحسن والمفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أنس بن مالك,ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مطر الوراق); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(الحسن,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مطر الوراق)]
- 3:83/q11/p3 Universal wording may have particular earthly referents. — Al-Ḥasan says general wording is intended particularly; Ibn ʿAṭiyya notes not everyone converts by the sword, and Abū Ḥayyān prefers heavenly universality but earthly particularity. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 3:83/q12 How does 'to Him all submit' relate to the rebuking question?  [وَلَهُۥٓ أَسْلَمَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it functions as one circumstance, emphasizes exclusive direction, or splits heaven from earth.
- 3:83/q12/p1 It is a circumstantial clause: seeking another religion is absurd while all submit to God. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī supply the rhetorical force; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the clause proof of the implied conclusion. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:83/q12/p2 Placing 'to Him' first stresses exclusive submission to God. — Al-Biqāʿī notes the exclusive force; al-Rāzī relates it to one necessary existent and sole creative power. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 3:83/q12/p3 The heaven clause can end before a new clause begins about the earth. — Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, and ʿIkrima segment the wording this way. [3 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]

## 3:83/q13 What do 'willingly' and 'unwillingly' mean?  [طَوْعًۭا وَكَرْهًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the contrast concerns inner obedience or liked and disliked divine decrees.
- 3:83/q13/p1 They contrast easy willing obedience with submission amid difficulty, reluctance, or inner resistance. — Al-Thaʿlabī, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Qurṭubī distinguish ease and strain. Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī derive ṭawʿ from yielding and cite Ibn al-Sikkīt's equivalent verbs; al-Samīn distinguishes obeying a command from agreeing. [11 notes: THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت); RAZI-FULL(ابن السكيت,الواحدي); SAMIN-DURR; JISHUMI]
- 3:83/q13/p2 The willing accept welcome decrees; the unwilling endure illness, poverty, or death. — Al-Naḥḥās contrasts healthy and sick; al-Rāzī applies both states even to the righteous; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses like and dislike. [4 notes: NAHHAS; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:83/q13/p3 Two vowelings of karh are dialectal; one account reserves ḍamma for hardship and fatḥa for unwilling action. — Al-Jishumī accepts both. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 3:83/q14 What is the syntax of ṭawʿan wa-karhan?  [طَوْعًۭا وَكَرْهًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether they are parsed as circumstantial verbal nouns or broadly called adverbs.
- 3:83/q14/p1 Both accusative verbal nouns function circumstantially, meaning 'willingly and unwillingly'. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Ṭabrisī compare 'he came running'; al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī explain why speech cannot be a mode of coming. Al-Samīn reports the submission/obedience analysis but objects that it fits ṭawʿan and not karhan. [13 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 3:83/q14/p2 They are adverbial descriptions of submission in either state. — Al-Nasafī uses the broad adverbial description. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 3:83/q15 How is karhan read?  [كَرْهًۭا · readings]
- 3:83/q15/p1 Al-Aʿmash reads it with ḍamma on the kāf; fatḥa is also attested. — Al-Jishumī calls the forms dialectal; al-Samīn notes related readings elsewhere from al-Akhawayn, Kufans, and Ibn Dhakwān. [3 notes: JISHUMI(الأعمش); SAMIN-DURR(الأعمش); ALUSI-FULL(الأعمش)]

## 3:83/q16 What forms of willing and unwilling submission are described?  [طَوْعًۭا وَكَرْهًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the contrast points to acknowledged lordship, prostration, covenant, death, coerced conversion, distinct populations, decree, distress, worship, proof, or inward resistance.
- 3:83/q16/p1 Believers or sincere worshippers willingly obey; unbelievers acknowledge God as Creator or Lord while worshipping others. — Mujāhid, Abū al-ʿĀliya, and ʿĀmir cite admission of creatorhood in 29:61, 31:25, 39:38, and 43:87, and of servanthood; ʿIkrima invokes compelling proof. Al-Ālūsī says this does not erase accountability. [27 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(أبو العالية,عامر,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية,أبو سنان,ابن عباس); THALABI(أبو العالية,عكرمة); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو العالية رفيع,الشعبي,مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو العالية، مجاهد); TABRISI(مجاهد وأبو العالية); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عكرمة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(أبو العالية,أبو سنان,مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو العالية,بعضهم)]
- 3:83/q16/p2 The believer prostrates willingly; the unbeliever or his shadow prostrates unwillingly. — Mujāhid's reports vary: sometimes a believer's face or shadow is willing, sometimes a disbeliever's shadow alone is reluctant, and one report calls both face and shadow willing. Sources cite 13:15, 16:48, and 4:19. [13 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(مجاهد); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس، مجاهد); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 3:83/q16/p3 All submit at the primordial covenant, some reluctantly. — Ibn ʿAbbās and others cite the pledge in 7:172; al-Ṭabrisī says created states themselves point to God. Al-Bursawī urges fulfilling the covenant. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI(الضحاك); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس عن مجاهد، الأعمش عن مجاهد، السدي); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BURSEVI]
- 3:83/q16/p4 Believers submit willingly; disbelievers submit only when death, danger, or punishment makes reality unavoidable, too late to benefit them. — Qatāda repeatedly cites 40:85; other accounts mention 40:84 or imminent death. Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects that the proposed scene occurs only in some cases, not every disbeliever. ## 3:83/q17 What benefit does reluctant hypocritical submission bring?  [كَرْهًۭا · interpretation]
- 3:83/q17/p1 A reluctant hypocrite gains no benefit from his deeds. — Al-Ḥasan states this consequence. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 3:83/q18 What does the return to God mean and imply?  [وَإِلَيْهِ يُرْجَعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether return stresses judgment, creative power, or final recognition.
- 3:83/q18/p1 All return after death or at resurrection for recompense; this warns seekers of another religion. — Al-Ṭabarī includes Jews, Christians, and others. Al-Rāzī says no one else can harm or benefit; al-Biqāʿī says there is no escape; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls return a reason to seek Islam; Ibn Kathīr says deeds are repaid. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:83/q18/p2 Return confirms that the One who created people by His will will resurrect them by His will. — Al-Zajjāj and al-Wāḥidī relate return to created dependence. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن كيسان والزجاج)]
- 3:83/q18/p3 At final return every being recognizes God's religion; no other religion remains. — Al-Qāshānī gives this concluding acknowledgment. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 3:83/q19 How does the return clause relate syntactically to the prior clause?  [وَإِلَيْهِ يُرْجَعُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the return is independent or circumstantial, and what the pronouns denote.
- 3:83/q19/p1 It stands independently as a threat. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī list this possibility. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:83/q19/p2 It coordinates with 'to Him all submit' as another circumstantial clause. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī describe the return as part of the circumstantial frame. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:83/q19/p3 The third-person pronoun may refer to submitters or seekers; the second person refers to those addressed, with shifts in mixed readings. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī describe the alternatives. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(حفص عن عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(الباقون,عاصم)]

## 3:83/q20 Which other passages clarify universal submission?  [وَلَهُۥٓ أَسْلَمَ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ طَوْعًۭا وَكَرْهًۭا · links]
turns on: Which cross-reference is used: creation's prostration, disbelievers' admissions, or covenant context.
- 3:83/q20/p1 Verses on all creation's prostration and glorification illuminate this universal submission. — Ibn ʿAbbās cites 22:18; Mujāhid and Ibn Kathīr cite 13:15 and 16:48–50, including shadows, animals, and angels; al-Rāzī cites 17:44 and 41:11, where heaven and earth answer willingly. ## 3:83/q21 What theological claim does universal submission support?  [وَلَهُۥٓ أَسْلَمَ مَن فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضِ · theology]
turns on: Whether the universal wording supports metaphysical dependence, accountable agency, or obedience.
- 3:83/q21/p1 Everything besides God depends on Him to exist and cease; He alone is necessarily existent and creates every kind of being. — Al-Rāzī calls this strongest and cites 13:15 and 17:44; Kur'an Yolu repeats the contingency argument. [2 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 3:83/q21/p2 God's decree over unbelievers does not erase responsibility for disbelief. — Al-Ālūsī rejects the determinist inference. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 3:83/q21/p3 No one can resist divine authority, so defying God's messengers and commands is untenable. — Al-Biqāʿī notes even powerful people with allies are helpless; Ibn Kathīr speaks of irresistible dominion. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 3:83/q22 How is this verse treated in verse-end counting?  [وَإِلَيْهِ يُرْجَعُونَ · sciences]
turns on: Whether this ending is counted and how such endings are named.
- 3:83/q22/p1 Verse counters omit this ending though analogous endings count for harmony with surrounding endings. — Al-Burhān and al-Itqān compare 3:190, 18:15, 20:80, and 5:50. Al-Itqān defines fāṣila as a beautifying pause separating statements. [4 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN]
- 3:83/q22/p2 Qur'anic endings are fawāṣil, not poetic rhymes; whether sajʿ may name them is disputed. — Al-Itqān reports consensus against qāfiya and a majority against sajʿ because of its origin and lack of authorization. Al-Rummānī's Ashʿarī distinction says fawāṣil follow meaning, whereas sajʿ forces meaning to fit. [3 notes: ITQAN(الجمهور,الرماني)]

- 3:83/q23/p1 The People of the Book disputed before the Prophet over which followed Abraham, rejected his judgment that neither did, and the verse was revealed. — Ibn ʿAbbās is named by al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, and others. ## 3:83/q24 What inward meanings are drawn from this verse?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether surrender is explained by unveiled vision, effacement, enabling grace, or prophetic following.
- 3:83/q24/p1 Willing surrender is unobstructed vision; reluctant surrender occurs amid ego, veils, doubt, and attachment to intermediaries. — Al-Ḥusayn distinguishes beholding the Essence from beholding awe. Sufi authorities cited by al-Ālūsī contrast angels and elect people with hesitant seekers and relate unbelievers' creator-acknowledgment to self-darkness; Kur'an Yolu repeats this. [6 notes: SULAMI(الحسين); ALUSI-FULL(بعض ساداتنا الصوفية,مجاهد); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Âlûsî)]
- 3:83/q24/p2 The seeker must hold fast to God alone and be effaced under His decree; independent reliance leads to error. — Al-Wāsiṭī treats attachment beyond the One as misplaced worship. Al-Qushayrī likens independent worth to a mirage, false calculation to a barren valley, and warns against seeking God through other than Him. [6 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); QUSHAYRI]
- 3:83/q24/p3 Faith, knowledge, and obedience occur through God's disclosure and enabling rather than autonomous strength. — Al-Qushayrī says people know God through His teaching and affirm revelation through His enabling. Al-Shādhilī calls obedience with effaced self-awareness a blessing. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI; BURSEVI(الشيخ الشاذلي)]
- 3:83/q24/p4 Deeds are accepted through following the Prophet. — Al-Qushayrī reports that no deed is accepted outside his banner. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 3:83/q25 What recitation is recommended for a difficult being?  [* · reports]
turns on: Whether the reported use is limited to mounts or extends to people and children.
- 3:83/q25/p1 Recite this verse in a stubborn mount's ear to make it tractable by God's permission. — Mujāhid transmits Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Durr records Yūnus ibn ʿUbayd; al-Itqān records al-Bayhaqī's Ibn ʿAbbās report. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد عن ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(يونس بن عبيد); ITQAN(ابن عباس)]

## 3:83/q26 What cautions govern combining readings during recitation?  [* · readings]
- 3:83/q26/p1 Combine variants by letter while avoiding pauses and starts that distort meaning. — Ibn al-Jazarī prefers this because verse-by-verse repetition can force bad pauses. Al-Qayjāṭī attributes it to senior teachers and al-Dānī; seven conditions protect divine oneness, the Prophet's mission, and paired statements. [7 notes: NASHR(الأستاذ أبو الحسن علي بن عمرو الأندلسي القيجاطي); NASHR+]

## 3:83/q27 What rhetorical observations are recorded around the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the observation addresses the verse's antithesis or the merit of quoted poetry.
- 3:83/q27/p1 The willing/unwilling contrast is an antithesis among several patterns in the passage. — Abū Ḥayyān also notes repeated guidance and disbelief phrases and cognate variation. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 3:83/q27/p2 The cited poetic comparisons and antitheses are conventional or strained rather than especially eloquent. — Al-Bāqillānī faults al-Buḥturī's inflated antithesis, familiar moon/branch/dune images, needless qualifiers and filler, and awkward 'in where'; he prefers Kashājim's safer alternative. [7 notes: BAQILLANI; BAQILLANI+]
