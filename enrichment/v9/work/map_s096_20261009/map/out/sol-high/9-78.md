# 9:78: 20 questions

## 9:78/q01 Whom does the opening 'they' address?  [يَعْلَمُوٓا۟ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun continues the account of the covenant makers alone or broadens the warning to all hypocrites.
- 9:78/q01/p1 The hypocrites generally, including those who made and broke the covenant. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the warning applies to all hypocrites. Abū Ḥayyān calls this the apparent scope and explicitly includes the covenant breakers. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī allow the hypocrites as the implied subject. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 9:78/q01/p2 The group who made the preceding covenant. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this restriction from the interpreter who regards the pact as an inward intention. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī allow covenant makers as the subject; Bursevī treats their knowledge as acknowledged. [5 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(قائل المقالة المذكورة); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:78/q02 What is the force of 'Did they not know?'  [أَلَمْ يَعْلَمُوٓا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the interrogative chiefly censures the culprits, confirms what they know, or alerts listeners.
- 9:78/q02/p1 It rebukes and warns the hypocrites for acting despite God's knowledge. — Ṭabrisī says they ought to know what God knows; Abū Ḥayyān calls it censure. Rāzī says they conspire despite knowing God sees and punishes hidden acts like public ones. Qurṭubī makes requital the point; Ālūsī says it threatens grave acts and the explicit divine name magnifies awe and reckoning. Elmalılı and Ṭabāṭabāʾī call it reproach. Qushayrī compares warnings by God's knowledge here with warnings by His acts elsewhere. [8 notes: QUSHAYRI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI]
- 9:78/q02/p2 It confirms that the covenant makers already knew of God's knowledge, prompting acknowledgment of requital. — Bursevī identifies a confirmatory question to the covenant makers. Ālūsī allows a prompt to acknowledge that God will punish and requite them. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:78/q02/p3 It alerts listeners to an important matter. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites this verse alongside 9:63 as a parallel interrogative serving that purpose. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 9:78/q03 How is 'know' read, and whom does the alternate reading address?  [يَعْلَمُوٓا۟ · readings]
turns on: The yāʾ or tāʾ reading and whether a second-person address turns to believers or remains directed at the prior group.
- 9:78/q03/p1 The majority read yaʿlamū, in the third person, concerning the hypocrites or covenant makers. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the majority reading; al-Samīn calls it that of most readers. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī give the two possible third-person subjects. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:78/q03/p2 Taʿlamū reads with second-person tāʾ and addresses believers, assuring or admonishing them. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya names Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān and al-Ḥasan; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, and Biqāʿī also name ʿAlī. Abū Ḥayyān says God gradually exposes the hypocrites to believers; Biqāʿī says this knowledge benefits those prepared for faith. Bayḍāwī mentions the shift; Ālūsī reports ʿAlī's reading. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبد الرحمن والحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(علي وأبو عبد الرحمن والحسن); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL(علي وأبو عبد الرحمن والحسن); ALUSI-FULL(علي)]
- 9:78/q03/p3 The second-person reading addresses the first group by a shift in person. — Ālūsī reports this alternative but calls it strained because the continuation speaks of God knowing 'their' secrets and private conversation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(علي); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 9:78/q04 How does this verse bear on the preceding covenant?  [أَلَمْ يَعْلَمُوٓا۟ سِرَّهُمْ · links]
turns on: What can be inferred from this verse's mention of secrets and from a restricted use of language in the preceding context.
- 9:78/q04/p1 Mention of secrets supports taking the preceding vow as including an unspoken intention. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya links the opening to the interpretation that the pact was made inwardly. Rāzī reports proponents who include both a spoken promise and a silent intention in a vow and cite this verse's secrets and private counsel. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(أصحاب هذا القول)]
- 9:78/q04/p2 A restriction proved for one usage does not restrict every usage of the earlier expression 'meeting'. — Rāzī rejects extending evidence that excludes vision in a particular use of meeting to all other uses; this is his objection to that broader inference in the surrounding discussion. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 9:78/q05 What is meant by 'their secret'?  [سِرَّهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'secret' denotes inward states, a specific concealed belief or intention, the Aqaba plot, or quiet speech.
- 9:78/q05/p1 What they conceal inwardly, in their hearts or breasts. — Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Rāzī, Ṭabrisī, Biqāʿī, and Elmalılı describe inward concealment. Qushayrī and Sulamī say only God, the Knower of secrets, sees it; Sulamī contrasts private talk accessible to recording angels. Ibn Kathīr includes inner motives even behind professed charity. Study Quran says sirr can name either the secret itself or a thing's hidden or mysterious aspect. [10 notes: SULAMI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN]
- 9:78/q05/p2 Their concealed disbelief or hypocrisy, despite professing faith. — Ṭabarī and al-Ḥasan with Qatāda in Jishumī contrast hidden denial of God, His Messenger, or Muhammad with public profession to believers. Bayḍāwī offers inward hypocrisy; Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī and another view; Nasafī and Ālūsī apply it to hypocrites. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(الحسن وقتادة وجماعة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل); ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:78/q05/p3 Their concealed intent to break their covenant. — Bayḍāwī offers it as an alternative to inward hypocrisy. Nasafī joins it with hypocrisy. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī; Bursevī and Ālūsī explain it for the covenant makers. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:78/q05/p4 The plot of the Aqaba people to kill the Prophet. — Muqātil identifies the secret and private conversation with the plot they agreed upon. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 9:78/q05/p5 What some say quietly to one another, contrasted with their more open discussion together. — Abū Ḥayyān reports this further explanation and says it is close to the other interpretations. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 9:78/q06 What does 'their private conversation' mean or contain?  [نَجْوَىٰهُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether najwā is defined broadly as confidential speech or identified with a particular hostile discussion, plot, inward discourse, or relatively open group talk.
- 9:78/q06/p1 Confidential speech among themselves. — Qushayrī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Biqāʿī, and Elmalılı describe speech or discussion among confidants. Ṭabrisī lists nājā, tanājā, intajā, and plural anjiya, deriving the root from remoteness or a raised place safe from flood; Rāzī likewise derives it from an isolated elevation where speakers exclude others and cites 19:52, 12:80, 58:9, 58:12. Bursevī gives najāhu/nājāhu and the sense of confidential speech; Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Study Quran define it similarly. Sulamī says recording angels can see it, unlike their secret. Ibn Kathīr includes private talk and inner motives. [13 notes: SULAMI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 9:78/q06/p2 Their speech attacks Islam, the Prophet, believers, or the religion. — Ṭabarī says they speak improperly of Islam and adherents; Ibn ʿAṭiyya adds plotting against the Prophet, community, and law. Bayḍāwī says attacks among themselves. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī and another view specifying denigration of the Prophet and believers. Nasafī and Ālūsī mention religious attacks or mutual slander. Study Quran cites related private-talk passages: 4:114, 17:47, 20:62, 21:3, 43:80, 58:7-8, 58:10, 58:12-13. [8 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري,قيل); ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 9:78/q06/p3 They call zakāt a poll tax and discuss withholding alms. — Bayḍāwī mentions calling zakāt jizya; Nasafī and Abū Ḥayyān's report from al-Zamakhsharī add a plan to withhold it. Bursevī includes other speech devoid of good, secret or public; Ālūsī applies this account to covenant makers. [5 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:78/q06/p4 The agreed Aqaba plot to kill the Prophet. — Muqātil applies both their secret and private conversation to this plot. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 9:78/q06/p5 Their inner thoughts as surveyed by their own souls. — Qushayrī says najwā may extend from confidential speech to this inward discourse. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 9:78/q06/p6 What they discuss openly together, contrasted with quiet speech called their secret. — Abū Ḥayyān reports the distinction and regards the explanations as close in meaning. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل)]

## 9:78/q07 Why is 'secret' mentioned before 'private conversation'?  [سِرَّهُمْ نَجْوَىٰهُمْ · rhetoric]
- 9:78/q07/p1 Knowing the deeper secret inspires greater awe, so mentioning it first hastens awe or reassurance. — Ālūsī says secret knowledge is more awe-inspiring in ordinary perception; its effect can be fear or reassurance depending on the reading. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:78/q08 How does God's knowledge relate to what they hide and disclose?  [يَعْلَمُ سِرَّهُمْ وَنَجْوَىٰهُمْ · theology]
turns on: The breadth of hidden knowledge, including outward contradictions and future acts.
- 9:78/q08/p1 God knows both their private affairs and what they make public; outward appearances cannot deceive Him. — Abū ʿAlī in Jishumī says they already acknowledge the God who knows both. Al-Ḥasan and Qatāda specify concealed rejection and professed faith; Abū ʿAlī and Abū Muslim state the scope generally. Another Jishumī explanation says their deceptions cannot fool such a knower. Jishumī defines iʿlān as manifestation perceptible to others, opposite isrār; Abū Muslim grounds their duty to know in God's established signs and proofs. Ṭabarī applies it to outward faith and inward disbelief and says they should cease deceiving God's allies. Ibn Kathīr says even motives behind charity are known, along with hidden and manifest things. Biqāʿī says apparent enrichment might fool an ignorant observer, while God's judgment of hearts proves His knowledge exceeds each person's self-knowledge. [11 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI(unspecified,أبو علي,أبو علي وأبو مسلم,أبو مسلم,الحسن وقتادة وجماعة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 9:78/q08/p2 God knows even deeds not yet done but destined to be done. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, says this is more hidden than a secret. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الضحاك)]

## 9:78/q09 What is meant by 'the unseen things'?  [ٱلْغُيُوبِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the account explicitly includes nonexistent knowables as well as real things beyond current perception.
- 9:78/q09/p1 Things beyond creatures' sight, hearing, senses, or present perception, including concealed hearts. — Ṭabarī lists created beings' hearing, sight, senses, and hearts. Rāzī says creatures cannot perceive the unseen. Study Quran calls it real things absent from present sense perception or belonging to another time or state, and cites 2:3. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 9:78/q09/p2 Every thing absent from bodily or human perception, whether existent or nonexistent, insofar as it can be known. — Ṭabrisī gives this broad account of the plural and says the form is intensive. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 9:78/q10 How is 'al-ghuyūb' read?  [ٱلْغُيُوبِ · readings]
- 9:78/q10/p1 Al-ghiyūb with kasra and al-ghuyūb with ḍamma are canonical plurals of ghayb with the same meaning. — Qiraat-ER says the kasra may reflect dialect and that the vocalizations make no semantic difference. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER(vw)]

## 9:78/q11 What does calling God 'ʿAllām of the unseen things' assert?  [عَلَّٰمُ ٱلْغُيُوبِ · theology]
- 9:78/q11/p1 God knows every unseen thing, so no secret can be hidden from Him. — Baghawī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, and Ālūsī state universal knowledge; Bayḍāwī explicitly concludes none of this is hidden. Rāzī argues God's essence entails knowledge of everything including inner thoughts and compares the title with Jesus' words in 5:116. Ṭabrisī says the plural and form intensify the claim. Ibn Kathīr includes all hidden and manifest, unseen and witnessed things. Ṭabarī specifies things beyond creatures' senses and concealed in hearts. [10 notes: TAB-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:78/q12 What do the verb and the form 'ʿAllām' contribute to the wording?  [يَعْلَمُ عَلَّٰمُ · rhetoric]
- 9:78/q12/p1 The verb depicts continually renewed knowledge of successive secrets; the intensive noun conveys abiding, abundant knowledge of countless unseen things. — Ālūsī explicitly contrasts the verbal and nominal forms and says their combination gives the sentence force and eloquence. Rāzī says ʿallām intensifies ʿālim; Ṭabrisī likewise calls the form intensive. [3 notes: TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 9:78/q13 Can God be called 'ʿallāma'?  [عَلَّٰمُ · theology]
- 9:78/q13/p1 No; ʿallāma suggests knowledge gained through effort, which is impossible for God. — Rāzī distinguishes this form from the divine title ʿallām. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 9:78/q14 What practical warning follows from God's knowledge here?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stated consequence is a call to reform under threat or proof established by God's prior generosity.
- 9:78/q14/p1 The hypocrites should fear punishment, stop their deception, repent, and fulfill their covenant. — Ṭabarī urges fear of punishment, desistance, and repentance. Rāzī says hidden conspiracy is seen and punished like public action. Qurṭubī says God will requite them; Ālūsī similarly connects knowing to punishment. Biqāʿī says God knows their dealings and outcome, cannot be deceived, and they should fulfill their covenant. Bursevī cites a Persian couplet warning against contemplating disobedience while knowing God sees. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 9:78/q14/p2 God's provision to people whose betrayal He knew establishes the proof against them. — Biqāʿī connects God's prior knowledge, His giving, and their responsibility. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 9:78/q15 What does disclosure of their secrets show in the passage?  [* · interpretation]
- 9:78/q15/p1 It exposes hypocrites who recognized the truth but obstinately abandoned it, and is a sign of the Prophet's miracle. — Jishumī grounds the warning in their obstinate rejection and treats the disclosure itself as miraculous. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 9:78/q16 What report illustrates the traits of hypocrisy and private fidelity?  [* · reports]
- 9:78/q16/p1 A hypocrite lies, breaks promises, and betrays trusts; believers guard the religious trust even privately. — Ṭabrisī transmits the Prophet's three signs and calls the report sound. Qurṭubī reports Ibn ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās that the Prophet explained and reassured them, connecting lying, broken promises, and trust to 63:1, 9:75, and 33:72. [2 notes: TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عمرو وابن عباس)]

## 9:78/q17 How broadly do the reported hypocrisy traits apply?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the traits by themselves confer the designation, require predominance, or must be distinguished from disbelief and from hypocrisy of belief.
- 9:78/q17/p1 The traits apply broadly; one characterized by them remains a hypocrite until the Resurrection. — Qurṭubī says many Successors and imams adopted a broad reading; he reports this as the apparent view of al-Bukhārī and other scholars. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(البخاري وغيره من أهل العلم,كثير من التابعين والأئمة)]
- 9:78/q17/p2 The traits designate a hypocrite only when they predominate in the person's character. — Qurṭubī reports this from a group. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(طائفة)]
- 9:78/q17/p3 Such sins alone do not make a person an unbeliever or necessarily a hypocrite; belief and conduct must be distinguished. — Ibn al-ʿArabī says even predominant sins do not amount to unbelief unless belief is affected. Qurṭubī's scholars cite Joseph's brothers breaking a pledge, lying, and betraying Joseph without being hypocrites; ʿAṭāʾ says they were prophets. Al-Ḥasan distinguishes hypocrisy of lying in the Prophet's time from hypocrisy of conduct continuing to the Resurrection. Al-Bukhārī reports Ḥudhayfa that hypocrisy was in the Prophet's time, whereas later it is unbelief after faith. [5 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي,الحسن بن أبي الحسن البصري,حذيفة,عطاء بن أبي رباح,علماؤنا)]

## 9:78/q18 How does the surrounding passage portray conduct and hypocrisy?  [* · links]
- 9:78/q18/p1 It shows people whose professed faith does not govern conduct, exposing hypocrisy in its context and timing. — Kur'an Yolu draws this lesson from the passage. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 9:78/q19 What is the 'day they meet God' in the neighboring passage?  [* · links]
- 9:78/q19/p1 The Day of Resurrection. — Kur'an Yolu explains the earlier phrase this way. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 9:78/q20 How do virtuous people's private dealings contrast with the hypocrites'?  [* · inward]
- 9:78/q20/p1 They accept God's provision, worship, remember Him unseen, and make their private discourse worship rather than mockery. — Study Quran reports this contrast from Aj. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Aj)]
