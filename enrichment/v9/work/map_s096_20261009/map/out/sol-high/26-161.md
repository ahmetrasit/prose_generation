# 26:161: 9 questions

## 26:161/q01 Who were the people addressed as ‘them’?  [لَهُمْ · referent]
- 26:161/q01/p1 Lot addressed the people of Sodom and its surrounding area, including Gomorrah in one account. — Ibn Kathīr says God sent him to a great people during Abraham’s lifetime; they lived in Sodom and its environs, which later became a foul lake in al-Ghawr near Jerusalem, between al-Karak and al-Shawbak. Bursevī says Lot migrated with Abraham to Syria, was settled in Jordan, and was sent to Sodom. Kuranyolu says he left Iraq for Palestine with Abraham and was sent to reform the unbelieving people of Sodom and Gomorrah, citing 7:80; it notes their homosexual practices. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:161/q02 In what sense is Lot ‘their brother’?  [أَخُوهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether ‘brother’ denotes blood lineage, shared residence and family ties, or compassionate association despite outsider status.
- 26:161/q02/p1 He was their brother by lineage, not religion. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām explicitly distinguishes lineage from religion. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 26:161/q02/p2 He was their brother through residence and shared communal life, not lineage. — Al-Biqāʿī calls him Abraham’s nephew from Babylon; he says Lot’s long residence, marriage into the community, children with its women, and shared rural life explain the expression. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:161/q02/p3 It expresses compassionate kinship, though Lot was an outsider and no blood relative. — Bursevī reports Kāshifī’s explanation that the brotherhood is compassionate kinship rather than blood relation. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:161/q03 What reading omits ‘their brother’?  [أَخُوهُمْ · readings]
- 26:161/q03/p1 The codices of Ibn Masʿūd, Ubayy, and Ḥafṣa reportedly read ‘when Lot said to them,’ omitting ‘their brother.’ — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports this from al-Naqqāsh as the wording إذ قال لهم لوط in those three codices. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النقاش)]

## 26:161/q04 How does calling Lot ‘their brother’ compare with the wording about Shuʿayb?  [أَخُوهُمْ · links]
- 26:161/q04/p1 Prophets are called brothers when addressing their own people; Shuʿayb is called Midian’s brother but not the Aykah people’s. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī distinguishes Midian from Aykah: Shuʿayb belonged to Midian, and the other prophets in Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ address their own peoples; he cites 7:85, 11:84, and 23:36. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:161/q05 What is the origin of the name ‘Lot’?  [لُوطٌ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name is foreign or has an Arabic derivation from adherence.
- 26:161/q05/p1 Lūṭ may be a foreign name. — Al-Jishumī reports this as one possibility. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 26:161/q05/p2 Lūṭ may derive from the idea of sticking or adhering. — Al-Jishumī reports this possibility, citing the report about Abū Bakr that a child is more attached to the heart and the usage lāṭ for anything adhering to something. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 26:161/q06 What was Lot’s lineage and relation to Abraham?  [لُوطٌ · referent]
turns on: The notes give different paternal names and different formulations of Hārān’s place in Abraham’s family.
- 26:161/q06/p1 Lot was Hārān’s son and Abraham’s nephew; Ibn Kathīr identifies his grandfather as Āzar. — Ibn Kathīr gives the line Lūṭ ibn Hārān ibn Āzar. Kuranyolu likewise calls him Hārān’s son and Abraham’s nephew and says he left Iraq with Abraham for Palestine. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:161/q06/p2 Lot was Lūṭ ibn Ḥarāz ibn Āzar, and Sarah was his sister. — Muqātil gives this genealogy and identifies Sarah as Lot’s sister. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 26:161/q06/p3 Lot was Hārān’s son; Abraham was his paternal uncle, and Hārān was Tārīkh’s brother. — Bursevī reports this family relationship, adding that Lot migrated with Abraham to Syria and was settled in Jordan. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 26:161/q07 What does Lot’s appeal ‘Will you not fear God?’ ask of his people?  [أَلَا تَتَّقُونَ · interpretation]
- 26:161/q07/p1 Lot calls them to fear God, worship Him alone, obey His messenger, and leave disobedience and transgression. — Muqātil glosses the appeal as fearing God; Yaḥyā ibn Sallām, al-Ṭabarī, and Elmalılı likewise state that Lot called them to fear Him. Al-Biqāʿī says fear should protect them from God’s displeasure. Bursevī specifies fear of punishment for idolatry and sins. Ibn Kathīr explains the call as worship of God alone, obedience to His messenger, and avoidance of disobedience; he adds that Lot forbade their unprecedented practice of approaching males instead of the women God created for them. [8 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI]

## 26:161/q08 How does this opening connect with the other messenger accounts?  [* · links]
- 26:161/q08/p1 It follows the recurring prophetic account: a people deny messengers and their prophet calls them to fear God and obey him. — Al-Ṭabarī connects Lot’s call to the statement that his people denied the messengers in 26:160. Iṣlāḥī says the opening appeal and command to fear God and obey Lot recur in earlier anecdotes, where they were explained. Study Quran compares this opening with 26:105–109, 26:123–127, 26:141–145, and 26:176–180. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:161/q09 What supports Lot’s preceding argument in the parallel account?  [* · links]
- 26:161/q09/p1 His preceding argument rests on a demonstrative proof also reflected in the parallel wording of 29:29. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the discussion points to that proof and cites the parallel wording in Sūrat al-ʿAnkabūt, 29:29. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
