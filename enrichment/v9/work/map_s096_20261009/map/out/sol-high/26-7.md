# 26:7: 14 questions

## 26:7/q01 Who is called to look?  [يَرَوْا۟ · referent]
- 26:7/q01/p1 The people denying the message or resurrection are addressed. — Al-Wāḥidī identifies deniers of the message; Ibn al-Jawzī identifies deniers of resurrection and says they are to see vegetation brought forth where none was. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 26:7/q02 What force does the opening question carry?  [أَوَلَمْ يَرَوْا۟ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the interrogative is read chiefly as rebuke or as an affirmative question.
- 26:7/q02/p1 It rebukes their failure to consider visible signs and urges them to look. — Al-Burṣawī says the hamza rebukes Quraysh for denial and mockery and that the earth's marvels should deter them; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls it a rebuke of persistent aversion; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the growth clause elaborates seeing and carries the rebuke. Al-Ālūsī supplies an omitted reminder of their persistence; Ibn Kamāl supplies 'did they not reflect on God's wonders of power and look?' Al-Bayḍāwī describes a call to see earth's marvels. [7 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كمال); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:7/q02/p2 The question affirms that they have seen how many noble kinds God grew. — Yaḥyā ibn Sallām expressly calls the question an affirmation. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 26:7/q03 How are seeing and looking toward the earth construed?  [يَرَوْا۟ إِلَى ٱلْأَرْضِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether earth is read directly as the source of varied growth or as shorthand for its wonders, and how the growth clause attaches.
- 26:7/q03/p1 The wording directs sight to the earth and its wonders. — Al-Biqāʿī says ilā marks direction or limit, draws attention to broad extent, varied regions and soils, and hints at failure to reflect. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 'see' includes 'look,' explaining ilā. Ibn ʿĀshūr attaches ilā l-arḍ to yaraw and treats kam anbatnā as an appositive elaboration of it. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:7/q03/p2 An omitted genitive or metonymy makes 'earth' stand for its wonders; the planting clause then explains those signs. — Al-Ālūsī gives either an omitted 'wonders of' before earth or earth used metonymically for its wonders, and takes the planting clause as a new explanatory sentence that deters disbelief and invites faith. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:7/q03/p3 The one earth itself is the source of plants of different natures, with the planting clause as semantic apposition. — Al-Ālūsī reports this analysis as needing neither an omitted noun nor metonymy and calls the growth clause a semantic badal ishtimāl. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 26:7/q04 What does God grow in the earth?  [أَنۢبَتْنَا فِيهَا زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · referent]
turns on: Whether growth and 'noble pair' are confined to vegetation, extended to animals and humans, or read of particular people.
- 26:7/q04/p1 Many beautiful or useful plant kinds, including human and animal food. — Mujāhid specifies plants eaten by people and livestock, as transmitted by Yaḥyā, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Durr, and others. Muqātil names many colors and kinds; Ibn ʿAbbās names fruits and other fine pleasant things; al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarī and al-Biqāʿī describe growth after barren, dead earth. Al-Burṣawī lists fragrant flowers, colorful blossoms, leaves and food; Elmalılı adds varying forms, fruits and properties in one environment and renewed growth after drying. Ibn al-Jawzī addresses resurrection deniers with vegetation where none stood; al-Qurṭubī says nabata and anbata both denote growth, with God causing it. Al-Ālūsī calls plants the apparent scope; Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers a beautiful plant kind, with plant kinds created in pairs. Iṣlāḥī calls them beneficial things sprouted from earth; Study Quran transmits al-Ṭabarī's edible-vegetation gloss. [24 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وقيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ); TABRISI(مجاهد); TABATABAI+]
- 26:7/q04/p2 The growth also encompasses animals and humans because they arise from the earth or possess growth. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes sustaining foods and animals because they arise from earthly growth, citing 71:17 on people. Abū Ḥayyān includes food, plants and animals born of two parents. Elmalılı says all have a power of growth though considering plants suffices. Al-Ālūsī reports this wider view while treating plants as apparent; Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports animals, especially humans, with 71:17. Kur'an Yolu mentions varied plants and living things. [6 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:7/q04/p3 Humans are earthly growth; those entering Paradise are noble and those entering Hell are base. — Al-Shaʿbī's saying is transmitted by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Durr, al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī; al-Ṭabrisī and Abū Ḥayyān invoke 71:17. Study Quran attributes a similar saying to Sufyān al-Thawrī, calling the latter wretched. [10 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الشعبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الشعبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشعبي); TABRISI(الشعبي); QURTUBI-FULL(الشعبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي); DURR-FULL(الشعبي); BURSEVI(الشعبي); ALUSI-FULL(الشعبي); STUDYQURAN(Sufyān al-Thawrī)]
- 26:7/q04/p4 Adam and Eve are the noblest earthly pair. — Abū Bakr ibn Ṭāhir says they occasioned the appearance of messengers, prophets, saints and knowers; al-Sulamī and al-Burṣawī transmit him. Study Quran reports a reading of 'pair' as Adam and Eve, ancestors of prophets and saints. [3 notes: SULAMI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); BURSEVI(أبو بكر بن طاهر); STUDYQURAN(Su)]

## 26:7/q05 How does كم function here?  [كَمْ أَنۢبَتْنَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether grammarians name كم khabariyya or an exclamatory/interrogative quantity, although they agree on abundance and object function.
- 26:7/q05/p1 It is a declarative expression of abundance, accusative as the object of 'We grew.' — Al-Nasafī assigns the accusative object case; al-Ālūsī calls it khabariyya and says it signals abundance. Al-Zamakhsharī, transmitted by Abū Ḥayyān, and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise stress immense number, the latter describing an exclamatory or interrogative quantity expression used here as a noun of abundance and direct object. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:7/q05/p2 It is an exclamatory or interrogative quantifier of abundance, accusative as the verb's object. — Al-Samīn calls it multiplicative and makes min kulli zawjin its specification; al-Burṣawī reports the exegetes' accusative exclamatory parse. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the quantity expression functions nominally as the direct object while conveying many kinds. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(أهل التفسير); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:7/q06 What does كل add to the quantity phrase?  [كَمْ كُلِّ · rhetoric]
- 26:7/q06/p1 It covers every kind comprehensively, while كم indicates their immense number. — Al-Zamakhsharī makes kull detailed coverage and kam abundance, together evidencing complete power; Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn transmit him. Al-Nasafī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Burṣawī likewise distinguish comprehensiveness from number. Al-Ālūsī adds that the particles are not redundant; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes many places of growth from coverage of varieties and sees fine design. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI(أهل التفسير); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 26:7/q07 What is the grammatical role of من كل زوج?  [مِن كُلِّ زَوْجٍۢ · grammar]
turns on: Whether من specifies كم, marks a part of each kind, or explains the kinds; al-Samīn disputes a circumstantial parse.
- 26:7/q07/p1 It specifies the many things counted by كم. — Al-Samīn calls the phrase the tamyīz or specification of kam, and al-Burṣawī reports the same exegetical parsing. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI(أهل التفسير)]
- 26:7/q07/p2 من is partitive: numerous growths come from portions of every plant kind. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says kam covers many locations while kull covers varieties; this partitive construction dispenses with an explicit classifier for kam. Al-Ālūsī also allows partitive min. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:7/q07/p3 من may be explanatory rather than partitive. — Al-Ālūsī gives explanatory min as the alternative to partitive min and says the particles' functions do not repeat one another. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:7/q07/p4 The phrase may be circumstantial. — Abū al-Baqāʾ allows this parse; al-Samīn objects that it has no point. [0 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-]

## 26:7/q08 What does زوج mean here?  [زَوْجٍۢ · meaning]
turns on: Whether زوج is used as the name of a species, a literal counterpart pair, or a color variety.
- 26:7/q08/p1 A kind, class, genus or species, especially of plants. — Ibn Qutayba compares plant kinds with eight kinds of sheep; al-Dāmghānī cites 36:36, 6:143 and 11:40. Al-Biqāʿī describes members resembling one another; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the term also commonly names kinds outside animals, comparing 13:3 and 20:53. Al-Rāghib, as relayed by al-Ālūsī, connects all worldly things to opposites, likeness or composition. Abū Ḥayyān reports varieties distinguished by color and taste; Iṣlāḥī calls them varied beneficial things. [17 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; ZAJJAJ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:7/q08/p2 A thing together with its counterpart, literally a pair. — Al-Ṭabrisī says a kind is a type together with its counterpart; Abū Ḥayyān reports this possible sense. Study Quran states that zawj can also mean pair, allowing the Adam and Eve reading; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says plant kinds were created in pairs. [4 notes: TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; STUDYQURAN; TABATABAI]
- 26:7/q08/p3 A color or color variety. — Al-Farrāʾ's gloss is transmitted by al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān, with al-Qurṭubī also reporting 'kind.' Abū Ḥayyān elsewhere includes differences in color and taste among the kinds. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الفراء)]

## 26:7/q09 What does كريم mean of a kind?  [كَرِيمٍ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the adjective chiefly marks beauty and excellence or praiseworthy usefulness; several commentators combine them.
- 26:7/q09/p1 Beautiful, good, pleasant or excellent in appearance and quality. — Qatāda and Saʿīd ibn Jubayr are reported with 'good' or 'beautiful'; Mujāhid and Qatāda are also reported with 'beautiful and well formed.' Al-Kalbī says beautiful in appearance. Ibn Qutayba derives the good or beautiful usage in plants and gracious speech from nobility; Ibn al-Jawzī's Nuzha compares a good place and courteous parental speech. Al-Ṭabarī and al-Baghawī exemplify it with a fruitful palm and a milk rich she camel; al-Qurṭubī relates the root to honor and merit, including excellent palms and virtuous forgiving people. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it precious or excellent within its kind, comparing 8:4 and 25:72. [21 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(سعيد بن جبير,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وقتادة); DURR-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 26:7/q09/p2 Praiseworthy and valued for usefulness or beneficial results. — Al-Zajjāj says the useful species are praiseworthy and only the Lord can create them. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Burṣawī and al-Bayḍāwī compare a pleasing face and useful book; the Kashshāf's poetic karami and al-Ālūsī's cited line praise bravery. Al-Ṭabrisī reports that users prize the kind. Al-Nasafī compares a generous person who benefits many by feeding people and livestock; al-Biqāʿī adds good outcome, freedom from impurity and plants watered with one water. Iṣlāḥī connects the term to grapes named by Arabs for their benefits; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it excellent in its kind. [21 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وقيل); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:7/q10 Does 'every noble kind' cover only useful plants or all plants?  [كُلِّ زَوْجٍۢ كَرِيمٍ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether كريم selects plants with evident use or asserts a benefit and purpose even in apparently harmful plants.
- 26:7/q10/p1 The wording singles out useful, desirable plants and leaves harmful kinds unmentioned. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Rāzī report this answer; al-Bayḍāwī says this restricts the kinds that display power. Al-Burṣawī says useful plants display both power and blessing, while even harmful plants carry wisdom and benefit. Al-Ālūsī says emphasis on high benefit adds a deterrent blessing. Ibn ʿĀshūr says people seek and admire desirable species, so these lead them from benefit to proof. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:7/q10/p2 All plants are noble because even apparently harmful kinds have some benefit or wise purpose. — Al-Kashshāf says wise creation has a sound purpose; al-Rāzī says unnoticed benefit exists; al-Bayḍāwī allows benefit alone or in combination; al-Ālūsī invokes 2:29; Study Quran transmits al-Rāzī's view of benefit not apparent to people. Al-Burṣawī also concedes wisdom and benefit in harmful plants. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]

## 26:7/q11 What does the earth's growth demonstrate or call people to do?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw several compatible lessons from the same signs; the resurrection application is singled out as an alternative context by al-Ālūsī.
- 26:7/q11/p1 It displays God's power, unity, wisdom and sole right to worship. — Al-Jishumī takes the growth as proof of unity and blessings; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān connect it to correcting idol worship and neglected reflection. Al-Qurṭubī says insight into growth discloses divine greatness and sole worship. Al-Biqāʿī points to life after barrenness and diverse plants watered by one water. Elmalılı describes ordered variety and continual renewal and rules out blind nature evolving by itself. Kur'an Yolu points to varied fruit from the same soil, water, air and climate as proof of will, knowledge, wisdom and power, as well as the life-supporting earth. Al-Zajjāj says only the Lord can grow these beneficial kinds; al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī say kam with kull signals complete power. [14 notes: ZAJJAJ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 26:7/q11/p2 It reminds people of divine favors and calls for gratitude alongside the proof. — Al-Jishumī names blessings; Ibn ʿĀshūr says noble plants display craftsmanship and benefits that invite gratitude. Iṣlāḥī says the abundant blessings were made for human use and reminders; al-Burṣawī says useful plants show blessing as well as power. [4 notes: JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI]
- 26:7/q11/p3 Growth after a dead earth is a sign addressed to those who deny resurrection. — Ibn al-Jawzī identifies resurrection deniers as the audience and points to vegetation appearing where none had been. Al-Ālūsī reports an interpretation that connects the verse with denial of resurrection after denial of the Creator, but prefers his first, more general explanation of the created sign. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:7/q11/p4 Despite these visible signs, most people still do not believe. — Kur'an Yolu states this explicitly after adducing the earth and its varied products. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 26:7/q12 How is the verse connected to its context and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: How closely the verse is tied to 26:5, and which surrounding or parallel signs the commentators invoke.
- 26:7/q12/p1 It turns from rejection of revealed signs to the created signs of the earth, in a fresh appeal to reflection. — Al-Ālūsī describes this transition and reports Ibn Kamāl's apparently new discourse; he prefers this first explanation over a resurrection-centered one. Al-Burṣawī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī supply an omitted thought about denial, mockery and persistent turning away. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the following inna treats the audience as if denying that growth establishes divine oneness, which led to rejecting the Messenger. [6 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن كمال); TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 26:7/q12/p2 An omitted clause connects the question specifically to their rejection and mockery of the earlier sign in 26:5. — Abū al-Suʿūd, as reported by al-Ālūsī, connects the omitted clause to 26:5; al-Burṣawī likewise supplies Quraysh's rejection and mockery. Al-Ālūsī says the link fits wording but objects that earth's wonders do not clearly disprove denial of the Qur'an's divine origin. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 26:7/q12/p3 The earth's signs continue a call to consider the heavens and their signs. — Al-Biqāʿī relates the earth to the preceding heavens and constellations and cites 25:61. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:7/q12/p4 The growth wording parallels descriptions in 22:5 and 50:7. — Al-Wāḥidī cites the matching expression in those verses. [1 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT]
- 26:7/q12/p5 Earth's blessings answer those waiting for a punitive sign from heaven. — Iṣlāḥī says these blessings were created for benefit and reminders even as the audience awaits a heavenly punishment sign. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:7/q12/p6 The following declaration calls the described earth, growth, beauty and benefits a sign despite disbelief. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says 'that' embraces earth, plant kinds and their beauty and benefits; āya is singular generically or distributively, since each feature is a sign. He says inna treats the audience as denying that this proves oneness. Kur'an Yolu says most do not believe despite the signs. Al-Ṭabrisī explains the following divine names: al-ʿAzīz is unconquerable power and al-Raḥīm grants many favors. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABRISI]

## 26:7/q13 What inward growth is drawn from the image?  [أَنۢبَتْنَا فِيهَا · inward]
- 26:7/q13/p1 As earth grows noble plants, God grows faith and virtues in the hearts of knowers. — Al-Burṣawī names faith, trust, certainty, sincerity and fine character; he transmits a Prophetic report comparing the growth of faith in a heart to growing vegetation. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام)]

## 26:7/q14 What does the incidental grammatical aside say about حوله?  [* · grammar]
turns on: The status of حوله as a circumstantial adverb or a relative clause, and whether the layered parse adds anything.
- 26:7/q14/p1 It is an adverb used circumstantially with its governor omitted. — Abū Ḥayyān reports Ibn ʿAṭiyya's parse, illustrated by the circumstantial phrase in 'I passed Hind laughing.' This aside concerns wording outside 26:7. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 26:7/q14/p2 It is a relative clause qualifying الملأ, understood as 'those who were around him.' — Abū Ḥayyān reports this Kufan analysis rather than a separately governed adverb; the aside concerns wording outside 26:7. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الكوفيون)]
- 26:7/q14/p3 It is overtly accusative under an implied 'settled' and locally accusative as a circumstantial phrase. — Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Zamakhsharī's layered parse but dismisses it as needless elaboration; this aside concerns wording outside 26:7. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
