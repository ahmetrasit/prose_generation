# 20:71: 28 questions

## 20:71/q01 Who speaks in this verse, and to whom?  [قَالَ · referent]
- 20:71/q01/p1 Pharaoh speaks to the believing magicians. — Yahyā identifies Pharaoh as speaker; Muqātil identifies the magicians as those addressed; Ṭabarī's report from Wahb places the outburst after their belief and Pharaoh's defeat. [3 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه)]

## 20:71/q02 How is ءَامَنتُمْ read?  [ءَامَنتُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether an interrogative hamza is supplied, and how the second hamza is transmitted or eased; both sentence forms can express Pharaoh's rebuke.
- 20:71/q02/p1 As a declarative, without an interrogative hamza. — Ibn Mujāhid calls the unlengthened form established for Ḥafṣ and also records Ibn Kathīr and Warsh. Fārisī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya give the same three; Bayḍāwī names Qunbul and Ḥafṣ. Ibn al-Jazarī distinguishes Ruways and al-Aṣbahānī from Warsh, a solitary report through al-Khuzāʿī against other Azraq routes, and divergent reports for Qunbul. Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the later route distribution. Fārisī, Bursawī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ālūsī say the declarative still rebukes. [19 notes: IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير وحفص عن عاصم وورش عن نافع,حفص عن عاصم); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير وحفص عن عاصم وورش عن نافع); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وحفص عن عاصم وورش عن نافع); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير وحفص عن عاصم وورش عن نافع); BAYDAWI-FULL(قنبل وحفص); NASAFI-FULL; NASHR(الخزاعي,حفص ورويس والأصبهاني عن ورش,قنبل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 20:71/q02/p2 As an interrogative with one hamza followed by a lengthened vowel. — Ibn Mujāhid, Fārisī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ibn al-Jawzī name Nāfiʿ, Abū ʿAmr and Ibn ʿĀmir, while Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies Qālūn, Warsh via al-Azraq, Abū Jaʿfar and Rawḥ too. Fārisī says Abū ʿAmr avoids his usual inserted alif between hamzas because of the crowded interrogative hamza, pattern hamza and root alif. The interrogative censures the magicians as effectively as the statement. [17 notes: IBNMUJAHID(أبو عمرو ونافع وابن عامر); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر); BAYDAWI-FULL(قنبل وحفص); NASAFI-FULL; NASHR(قنبل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الأكثر); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER(Ḥamza and others)]

Fārisī notes that Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī keep their regular form. Ibn al-Jazarī gives further routes for Warsh and Hishām and distinguishes Hishām's fully realized from intermediate hamza; Qirāʾāt ER notes easing the second hamza by tashīl without losing the question. ## 20:71/q03 Which letter is extra in the written forms of ءَامَنتُمْ?  [ءَامَنتُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the waw itself is superfluous or represents a hamza whose eased pronunciation the extra alif signals.
- 20:71/q03/p1 The alif is extra, and the waw represents the hamza to signal easing in connected recitation. — Ibn al-Jazarī argues against al-Dānī's analysis of several spellings. [1 notes: NASHR+]
- 20:71/q03/p2 The waw is extra. — This is al-Dānī's view as reported and rejected by Ibn al-Jazarī. [0 notes: NASHR-]

## 20:71/q04 Who does لَهُۥ refer to?  [لَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun follows the immediately mentioned Moses or is understood of his Lord by the parallel wording and the substance of the magicians' faith.
- 20:71/q04/p1 Moses. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Naḥḥās, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad identify Moses; Ālūsī calls him the apparent referent. Ibn ʿĀshūr says both pronouns in the statement refer to Moses. The al-Aʿrāf parallel has bihi, whose referent Abū Ḥayyān says could be Moses or God. [11 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; NAHHAS; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; JISHUMI+]
- 20:71/q04/p2 God, believed in through Moses or because of his signs. — Al-Baḥr al-ʿUlūm takes the pronoun as their Lord and compares 7:123. Biqāʿī describes belief in God through following or affirming Moses. Ālūsī reports both a direct divine referent and a causal lām analysis, but calls the direct referent discontinuous. [4 notes: BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم,قيل)]

## 20:71/q05 What does belief with لَـ mean here?  [ءَامَنتُمْ لَهُۥ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the lām conveys simple assent, allegiance or following, a typical human object of belief, or cause through Moses.
- 20:71/q05/p1 It means believing or affirming Moses; Arabic uses both āmana lahu and āmana bihi. — Wāḥidī and Abū Ḥayyān compare 10:83, 17:90 or 2:55, 12:17, 29:26 and the parallel 7:123; Qurtubī cites Lot at 29:26. Ṭabrisī distinguishes belief as the opposite of disbelief with bāʾ from assent to a person with lām. Jishumī says īmān lexically means assent, while religious usage names specified acts. [11 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); JISHUMI+]
- 20:71/q05/p2 The lām adds following, submission or allegiance to the person believed. — Jishumī contrasts following with mere belief using bāʾ. Bayḍāwī and Bursawī say the verb incorporates following; Ālūsī derives the force from submission, inqāda lahu, but rejects a derivation from ittabaʿa lahu because ittabaʿa takes a direct object. Ibn ʿĀshūr says lām implies faith arising for his sake, while bāʾ gives cause. [6 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 20:71/q05/p3 The lām commonly marks belief in a human, while bāʾ commonly marks belief in God. — Wāḥidī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī's report from al-Baḥr cite Quranic examples; Kashshāf cites 9:61. Abū Ḥayyān notes that the parallel 7:123 uses bihi and its pronoun may denote Moses or God; Wāḥidī says the parallel has the same meaning. Abū Ḥayyān adduces 9:61, which distinguishes belief in God from believing the believers, as Qur'anic evidence for lām with a person other than God. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(البحر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 20:71/q05/p4 The lām is causal: they believed in God because of Moses and the signs. — Ālūsī reports this analysis; Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes faith arising for someone's sake from faith arising because of someone. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:71/q06 What is Pharaoh's charge in قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ?  [قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ · interpretation]
- 20:71/q06/p1 They believed and acted before Pharaoh authorized them. — Muqātil and Ibn Kathīr say they affirmed Moses before any order or leave; Yaḥyā stresses that they had already done it. Qurtubī calls it a rebuke for transgression. Ṭabrisī says Pharaoh foolishly imagined belief required his authorization. Ibn ʿĀshūr says he made unauthorized belief into a punishable insolence after losing the debate; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says his claim of sovereignty made him demand that recognition of truth await his leave. [15 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; JISHUMI]

## 20:71/q07 Does 'before I permit you' imply he might permit belief later?  [قَبْلَ أَنْ ءَاذَنَ لَكُمْ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether 'before' carries a tactical promise of later leave or only marks that no leave had been granted.
- 20:71/q07/p1 Pharaoh insinuates that later permission was possible, using hope as well as fear to restrain the crowd. — Biqāʿī says stressing 'before' keeps onlookers from following; Ibn ʿĀshūr says Pharaoh claimed he would have allowed belief to make the audience doubt the apparent miracle. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:71/q07/p2 No later permission is implied or expected; the point is simply that they acted without it. — Bursawī and Ālūsī deny this inference, with 18:109 cited in the discussion. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:71/q08 What is an idhn, and how does it differ from an order?  [ءَاذَنَ · meaning]
- 20:71/q08/p1 Permission is notification or release to act, without necessarily willing or obliging the action; an order entails the commander's will. — Jishumī gives the original sense of notification; Ṭabrisī contrasts the permission 'hunt' at 5:2 with the order to establish prayer at 6:72; Ālūsī reports Ṭabrisī's distinction. Bursawī calls permission informing another that an act is approved; Ibn ʿĀshūr glosses adhina lahu as authorizing it. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:71/q09 What does كَبِيرُكُمُ mean in Pharaoh's accusation?  [لَكَبِيرُكُمُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether kabīr describes the instructional relation or preeminence and leadership in the craft; al-Suddī and Wāḥidī exclude mere seniority in age.
- 20:71/q09/p1 Their teacher or master in magic. — Abū ʿUbaydah reports a Meccan boy calling his employer kabīrī; Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Kisāʾī report Meccan or Hijazi usage for a teacher. Kashshāf, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Nasafī cite the same usage. Ṭabrisī says pupils might not match their teacher. Qurtubī says Pharaoh knew the charge false because the magicians learned before Moses arrived or was born. - 20:71/q09/p2 Their chief or leader, the greatest or most knowledgeable sorcerer, rather than their eldest. — Al-Suddī specifies knowledge rather than age; Muḥammad ibn Isḥāq says greatest magician. Wāḥidī says Arabic kabīr denotes leadership and cites the most knowledgeable leader in 12:80. Kashshāf describes rank and skill; Ṭabrisī says followers could oppose their chief but refrained out of respect. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Bursawī and Ālūsī combine mastery with magical expertise. [16 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(محمد بن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الكسائي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الكاشفي); ALUSI-FULL; JISHUMI+; THALABI+]

## 20:71/q10 Why did Pharaoh say Moses taught the magicians magic?  [إِنَّهُۥ لَكَبِيرُكُمُ ٱلَّذِى عَلَّمَكُمُ ٱلسِّحْرَ · interpretation]
- 20:71/q10/p1 He fabricated prior training and collusion to explain away their defeat and conversion and keep the public from following them. — Wāḥidī's People of Meanings and Ṭabrisī say the chief story turns the crowd away; Rāzī calls the pupils' feigned defeat a second insinuation after his claim that instantaneous belief lacked reflection. Ibn Kathīr and Bursawī say Pharaoh knew it false; Qurtubī notes they learned magic before Moses was born or arrived. Biqāʿī, Ālūsī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress a staged public plot; Ṭabāṭabāʾī relates the city-expulsion charge at 7:123. Iṣlāḥī calls it an excuse from a defeated ruler whose own assembled forces had converted; Ibn ʿĀshūr likens it to a losing party alleging corruption or treason. Jishumī says Pharaoh knowingly deceived the crowd with the collusion charge, and that his failure to answer with proof drove him to sophistry. [19 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI]

## 20:71/q11 How are the threats to cut and crucify read?  [فَلَأُقَطِّعَنَّ وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · readings]
turns on: Whether the transmitted verb forms have simple or intensive consonants, and whether intensity stresses repetition, manner, or force.
- 20:71/q11/p1 With lighter forms of both verbs, from simple cutting and crucifying. — Kashshāf and Rāzī report an unnamed reading; Qurtubī assigns it to Ibn Muḥayṣin here and in al-Aʿrāf, with open hamza and lighter doubling; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī also record it. Abū Ḥayyān refers readers to the earlier al-Aʿrāf discussion. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن محيصن); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قرئ)]
- 20:71/q11/p2 With doubled, intensive forms of both verbs. — Bursawī says the first implies an oath and repeated cutting, with the same intensification in crucifying; Ālūsī says doubling intensifies action; Ibn ʿĀshūr takes the forms as manner and forceful nailing, while Asad emphasizes multiple mutilations and crucifixions; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls crucifixion intensified or multiplied. [6 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:71/q12 What cutting is threatened by مِّنْ خِلَٰفٍ?  [أَيْدِيَكُمْ وَأَرْجُلَكُم مِّنْ خِلَٰفٍ · interpretation]
- 20:71/q12/p1 Opposite limbs, ordinarily the right hand and left foot, or the reverse. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Wāḥidī, Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī give right hand and left foot. Baghawī allows the reverse; Biqāʿī describes one limb from each side. Ālūsī says this particular pairing follows external explanation, since the verse itself allows other opposite pairings. Abū Ḥayyān calls the act mutilation; Iṣlāḥī views it as punishment for insurgents, displayed publicly. [22 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(عامة المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; THALABI; JISHUMI]

## 20:71/q13 How does مِّنْ خِلَٰفٍ work grammatically?  [مِّنْ خِلَٰفٍ · grammar]
turns on: Whether min retains its originative or partitive force, or stands in for another preposition.
- 20:71/q13/p1 Min marks origin or the starting point of cutting, and the phrase is circumstantial: the limbs differ in side. — Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Rāzī, Samīn and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the circumstantial analysis; Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 7:124 and 5:33. Bursawī distinguishes mere difference from opposition. Ālūsī takes min as partitive or originative, and reports an alternative attachment to an omitted verbal noun, cutting begun from the opposite side. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 20:71/q13/p2 Min may substitute for ʿan or ʿalā, expressing cutting from or on opposite sides. — Ṭabrisī offers both prepositions and treats the phrase as an accusative circumstantial expression. Al-Farrāʾ allows ʿan, ʿalā and bāʾ in such phrases. Ālūsī explicitly rejects Ṭabrisī's substitution analysis. [2 notes: FARRA; TABRISI; ALUSI-FULL(الطبرسي)-]

## 20:71/q14 Why specify crosswise amputation?  [مِّنْ خِلَٰفٍ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the specified pattern signals exceptional terror or a known penal practice that avoids disabling or killing the victim outright.
- 20:71/q14/p1 It makes the threat more dreadful and concrete. — Bursawī says this method heightens terror; Ālūsī reports that some call it the more dreadful method and says the specification gives an assured, definite threat. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:71/q14/p2 It follows a customary penal pattern that preserves the use of remaining limbs. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says this was apparently the customary mark of criminals rather than a uniquely painful method; he compares the ḥirābah rule in 5:33, which cuts one limb per offense to preserve remaining use. Ālūsī says same-side cutting could kill victims and remove their usefulness. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:71/q15 What does crucifying فِى جُذُوعِ ٱلنَّخْلِ describe?  [وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ فِى جُذُوعِ ٱلنَّخْلِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether fī is Arabic usage for on, a metaphor of firm enclosure, or a literal account of hollowed trunks.
- 20:71/q15/p1 Fastening them on palm trunks; fī can mean ʿalā or portray the body as firmly held or enclosed by its support. — Muqātil, Abū ʿUbaydah, Ibn Qutayba, al-Farrāʾ, Ṭabarī, Zajjāj, Wāḥidī, Jishumī, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr and others cite Arab usage or poems about al-ʿAbdī, Abū Dhuʾayb, ʿAntara, al-Asadī or Suwayd. Muqātil, Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Farrāʾ compare 52:38; al-Farrāʾ contrasts the body aligned along a trunk with elevation upon it and compares 4:54. Jishumī retains containment as fī's core, and Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī and others take a container metaphor; Rāzī calls a bare replacement gloss weak. Wāḥidī al-Wajīz specifies the tops; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says they are tied on trunks rather than mounted like a rider. Bursawī locates the palms along the Nile and defines crucifixion as fastening the back to wood; Ibn ʿĀshūr allows binding or nailing. Burhān says fī is more exact for the middle, whereas ʿalā suggests the top. Jishumī reports both an on-trunks explanation and an explanation in which the trunks surround the victims like containers. [50 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); FARRA; MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-MUSHKIL(الشاعر,عنترة); TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(أبو ذؤيب); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأسدي,المبرد، أبو عبيدة، الفراء، الزجاج); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; THALABI+]
- 20:71/q15/p2 The trunks were hollowed out and the victims placed literally inside, to die of hunger and thirst. — Abū Ḥayyān and Samīn report the literal account. Ālūsī also reports it but calls it scarcely credible. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:71/q16 Why specify palm trunks and say 'in' them?  [فِى جُذُوعِ ٱلنَّخْلِ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the choice depicts the position and duration of suffering or suggests ease compared with upward movement.
- 20:71/q16/p1 The wording stresses height, public visibility, firm confinement and the duration or horror of the punishment. — Nasafī says palms are named for height; Iṣlāḥī says public crucifixion makes an example of them. Biqāʿī says enclosure intensifies horror and deters others; Bursawī says the containment image suggests remaining there a long time. Burhān calls the trunk's middle more exact than its top and reports an image of the trunk as a grave. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies judhūʿ as plural of judhʿ and compares 19:25. [7 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:71/q16/p2 Fī suggests the ease of crucifixion, whereas ʿalā would suggest moving upward. — Burhān twice reports this explanation. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 20:71/q17 Did Pharaoh carry out the threatened punishment?  [فَلَأُقَطِّعَنَّ وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · reports]
turns on: Whether narrative reports can establish an execution that this verse itself only threatens.
- 20:71/q17/p1 Yes: the magicians were cut, crucified and killed, becoming martyrs. — Al-Suddī reports killing and cutting; Naḥḥās and Qurtubī say the threat was carried out until death. Ibn ʿAbbās says they began the day magicians and ended martyrs after asking for patience and a Muslim death at 7:126. Ālūsī finds the apparent account unobjectionable and says the victory in 28:35 could be victory by proof rather than escape from execution. Rāzī says the Qur'an states only the threat, while reports establish that Pharaoh carried it out. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي); NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); RAZI-FULL]
- 20:71/q17/p2 The Qur'an states the threat but does not settle whether Pharaoh carried it out; the reports are not conclusive. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī distinguishes the Quranic account from later reports; Ālūsī reports Rāzī's objection that reports do not establish the event. [1 notes: TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-; RAZI-FULL-]

## 20:71/q18 Was Pharaoh the first to use this punishment?  [فَلَأُقَطِّعَنَّ وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · reports]
turns on: How much historical weight to give the reports that assign Pharaoh first use.
- 20:71/q18/p1 Yes, he was the first to cut crosswise or crucify, or both. — Saʿīd says both crosswise amputation and palm-trunk crucifixion; Ṭabarī says Pharaoh first cut in that fashion. Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr's Ibn ʿAbbās report, Bursawī and Ālūsī report priority in crucifixion or this punishment. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي,سعيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:71/q18/p2 Priority is not established by the reports. — Rāzī's reservation is quoted by Ālūsī, who nevertheless accepts the apparent account. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 20:71/q19 Who are the two parties in أَيُّنَا?  [أَيُّنَآ · referent]
turns on: Whether the earlier pronoun lahu points the comparison to Moses or Pharaoh instead answers the divine punishment invoked by the magicians.
- 20:71/q19/p1 Pharaoh and the Lord of Moses, or God whom the magicians believed. — Muqātil, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Qurtubī, Nasafī, Biqāʿī, Ibn ʿĀshūr and Asad take the divine comparison. Wāḥidī contrasts Pharaoh's punishment for belief with the Lord's for unbelief; Biqāʿī connects Moses' warning in 20:48, Ibn ʿĀshūr his warning in 20:61 and their avowal in 26:47. Ibn ʿAṭiyya prefers this as consistent with Pharaoh's fabrication; Bayḍāwī reports it as an alternative. Al-Baḥr al-ʿUlūm's divine referent for lahu supports it. Jishumī frames the comparison as punishment for disobedience to Pharaoh or to the Lord of Moses. [17 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(بحر العلوم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; JISHUMI+]
- 20:71/q19/p2 Pharaoh and Moses. — Yaḥyā, Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī and Bursawī take Moses as the other party; Kashshāf and Bayḍāwī point to the earlier lahu. Rāzī and Kashshāf say Pharaoh vaunts his habitual cruelty and mocks a man who never tortured; Bursawī suggests he thought the magicians feared Moses after the staff swallowed their tools. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports Ṭabarī's view but prefers the divine comparison; Ālūsī calls Ṭabarī and others' view preferable on the apparent pronoun. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Ṭabarī's account of the threat as belittling mockery of Moses. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(الطبري وجماعة)+]

## 20:71/q20 How is وَلَتَعْلَمُنَّ أَيُّنَآ أَشَدُّ parsed?  [وَلَتَعْلَمُنَّ أَيُّنَآ أَشَدُّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether ayy is an independent interrogative that suspends the verb or a built relative object under the recognition sense of ʿalima.
- 20:71/q20/p1 Ayyunā is an interrogative subject, ashaddu its predicate; the question suspends the preceding verb's syntactic government. — Zajjāj and Abū Isḥāq say the preceding report cannot govern an interrogative, hence nominative ayyunā. Ṭabrisī says the verb still acts on it semantically. Samīn and Ālūsī say the clause fills two object places if ʿalima means know and one if it means recognize. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the two-person interrogative suspends taʿlamunna. Abū Ḥayyān says the suspended interrogative clause fills two object slots, or one if the verb is construed as 'know'. [7 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 20:71/q20/p2 If ʿalima means recognize, ayyunā may be a built relative object, with an omitted subject before ashaddu in its relative clause. — Samīn compares one analysis of 19:69; Ālūsī attributes the built relative analysis to Sībawayh. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(سيبويه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه)]
- 20:71/q20/p3 Ayyunā is parsed as an object, with its predicate omitted. — Abū Ḥayyān records this as another parse of the clause after 'you will know'. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 20:71/q21 What does عَذَابًۭا modify?  [عَذَابًۭا أَشَدُّ أَبْقَىٰ · grammar]
- 20:71/q21/p1 It is a tamyīz clarifying 'more severe,' and it is understood with 'more lasting' too. — Ālūsī gives both the expressed and implied scope of the accusative noun. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:71/q22 What does أَبْقَىٰ mean?  [أَبْقَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether abqā comes from the ordinary root for permanence or a remote allusion to Pharaoh's gifts.
- 20:71/q22/p1 More lasting or enduring, especially of punishment. — Muqātil, al-Farrāʾ, Baghawī, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī give the direct gloss; Ālūsī derives it from baqāʾ, permanence. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys Qur'anic baqāʾ as opposed to perishing and as enduring good with God, including 20:131, 28:60, 42:36, 55:27, 87:17, 16:96, 18:46, 19:76, 21:86, 37:77, 43:28 and 2:248. Her cited Qatāda, Abū ʿUbayda and Rāghib discuss ghābir as remaining: in Lot's punishment, into old age, or like dust left behind; she prefers derivation from remainder or residue. Ṭabrisī's adjacent lexical glosses of īthār, tazkī and zakāt concern choosing and seeking growth or purification, not a competing gloss of abqā. Jishumī reports Muḥammad ibn Kaʿb and Ibn Isḥāq explaining the enduring consequence as punishment for disobedience and reward for obedience. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; BAGHAWI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ(أبو عبيدة,الراغب,قتادة); TABRISI; JISHUMI(محمد بن كعب، وابن إسحاق); JISHUMI+]
- 20:71/q22/p2 It could allude to gifts that Pharaoh gives favored people. — Ālūsī reports this remote alternative, comparing Nimrod's boast, and calls it far-fetched. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 20:71/q23 What is Pharaoh trying to achieve with the threats?  [فَلَأُقَطِّعَنَّ وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ وَلَتَعْلَمُنَّ · interpretation]
- 20:71/q23/p1 He tries to intimidate the magicians and deter the crowd from their faith, publicly displaying his power. — Rāzī says the threat follows his attempt to discredit them and aims to stop imitators; Qushayrī lists killing, crosswise cutting, crucifixion and severe punishment. Ibn Kathīr says the public execution would make an example; Iṣlāḥī calls it an insurgent's punishment and Kur'an Yolu reports the severe torture threat. Qatāda says the magicians recognized God's sign and prostrated before the threat; Iṣlāḥī says their faith challenged Pharaoh's sovereignty, not merely his religious claim. Iṣlāḥī also says Pharaoh invokes fear that a magician's disobedient pupil might suffer his mentor's calamity, then claims his own punishment is worse. Ibn Kathīr says Pharaoh brazenly falsified what his eyes and the public had seen. Abū Ḥayyān reports al-Ṭabarī's view that the threat displays Pharaoh's domination and his accustomed torture of people, while mocking Moses. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); QUSHAYRI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري)]

## 20:71/q24 How do sources explain Pharaoh's bravado after the miracle?  [وَلَتَعْلَمُنَّ أَيُّنَآ أَشَدُّ عَذَابًۭا · interpretation]
- 20:71/q24/p1 He may have been terrified privately but feigned confidence to preserve his rule. — Rāzī recalls the staff's frightening serpent and Pharaoh's need for Moses' help; he calls the lie about magical instruction and denial of divine punishment calculated public bravado. Bursawī repeats the fearful ruler's pretense; Ālūsī says the serpent even pursued his pavilion. Al-Taʾwīlāt al-Najmiyya, reported by Bursawī, says Pharaoh knew worldly pain but was blind to afterlife punishment. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية); ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:71/q24/p2 He could not answer their case with proof, so he resorted to sophistry, threats, and deception. — Jishumī infers this from Pharaoh's charge that Moses taught the magicians. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 20:71/q25 What picture of Pharaoh's authority does the scene convey?  [* · interpretation]
- 20:71/q25/p1 He treats faith as subject to his sovereignty and bodily punishment as decisive, while the magicians recognize another authority and an afterlife. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Pharaoh sees himself as Egypt's sole lord and imagines truth must await his leave; his threat reveals a bodily, material idea of human life and happiness. Iṣlāḥī says belief in Moses rejects Pharaoh's divinity and rule. Qushayrī says the magicians changed from oaths by Pharaoh's might to oaths by God in one day; Kur'an Yolu says they valued afterlife happiness over worldly reward or survival. Ibn Kathīr sets their claim of guidance against Pharaoh's threatened punishment; Kur'an Yolu places the confrontation amid Egypt's political anxiety over Israel and Moses' effort to lead them home. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 20:71/q26 Which parallel accounts illuminate this verse?  [* · links]
- 20:71/q26/p1 The confrontation recurs in al-Aʿrāf and al-Shuʿarāʾ, with useful differences in wording and narrative emphasis. — Study Quran points to 7:123–26 and 26:49; Ibn Khālawayh, Wāḥidī and Abū Ḥayyān refer to the al-Aʿrāf reading discussion. Abū Ḥayyān highlights bihi there versus lahu here; Ibn Kathīr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī link the conspiracy charge at 7:123. Kur'an Yolu invites comparison of the Qur'anic and Biblical accounts of the episode. Zarkashī catalogs further differences between the parallel passages in connective particles and forms such as after/from, to/for, and then/fa, without explaining each variation here. [10 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BURHAN]

## 20:71/q27 What demeanor does Pharaoh's remark show?  [قَالَ · interpretation]
- 20:71/q27/p1 A degree of softening and submission. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reads Pharaoh's remark this way. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL]

## 20:71/q28 What force does the nūn in Pharaoh's threats have?  [فَلَأُقَطِّعَنَّ وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ · rhetoric]
