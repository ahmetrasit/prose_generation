# 28:19: 28 questions

## 28:19/q01 How does the clause beginning with lammā an work?  [فَلَمَّآ أَنْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether an is treated as syntactically extra, emphatic, or a cue to the pace of Moses' action; Iṣlāḥī also posits an omitted verb.
- 28:19/q01/p1 An after lammā is extra and emphatic; the later an introduces the object clause of arāda. — Naḥḥās calls the first particle emphatic; Ṭabrisī distinguishes it from the second an; al-Samīn says its addition after lammā is regular and cites analogous addition before law or wāw after an oath, with two poetic lines. Burhān notes the construction here and in Joseph's story of the arriving bearer of good news. [6 notes: NAHHAS; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN]
- 28:19/q01/p2 An is retained to reinforce Moses' intention to act. — Biqāʿī argues that Moses' motion verifies intention; Burhān likewise says repetition adds emphasis. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN]
- 28:19/q01/p3 An marks a pause and deliberation before this attempted strike. — Sāmarrāʾī contrasts the spacing here with successive fāʾ clauses at 28:15; he says Moses rebuked his companion first and was not rushing. His general principle is that additions and omissions follow contextual meaning. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-ASRAR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 28:19/q01/p4 A verb such as 'when this happened' is suppressed before an. — Iṣlāḥī says Moses tried reconciliation before reaching for the Copt. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:19/q01/p5 Lammā here is temporal, meaning 'when'. — Sāmarrāʾī acknowledges the grammatical dispute whether lammā is particle or adverb and treats it as temporal. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 28:19/q02 What does yabṭisha mean here?  [يَبْطِشَ · meaning]
- 28:19/q02/p1 It means to seize forcibly, here to strike or repel. — Bursevī defines baṭsh as forceful taking; Ālūsī adds dominance; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies striking here. Ṭabrisī says Moses meant to repel the Copt, and Qirāʾāt ER says both vowel forms mean violent seizure. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABRISI; QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:19/q03 How is yabṭisha read?  [يَبْطِشَ · readings]
turns on: Two dialectal imperfect forms are attested; some grammarians favor ḍamma by analogy with an intransitive verb.
- 28:19/q03/p1 The ṭāʾ has kasra in the common reading. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it the majority reading; al-Samīn calls it the common reading; Nashr says the remaining readers use kasra in all three forms of the root. Maʿāz al-Qurʾān describes kasra and ḍamma as dialectal forms; Naḥḥās says kasra is more familiar. [7 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS(يزيد بن القعقاع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(الباقون)]
- 28:19/q03/p2 The ṭāʾ has ḍamma in the reading of Abū Jaʿfar and also al-Ḥasan. — Naḥḥās names Yazīd ibn al-Qaʿqāʿ, Abū Jaʿfar, and calls ḍamma more analogical for an intransitive verb; Qurṭubī prefers that analogy. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Ālūsī add al-Ḥasan; Nashr says Abū Jaʿfar uses ḍamma in all three root forms; Qirāʾāt ER calls this canonical and notes that some hear added emphasis. [9 notes: MAJAZ; NAHHAS(يزيد بن القعقاع); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن وأبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو جعفر); NASHR(أبو جعفر); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن وأبو جعفر); QIRAAT-ER; QURTUBI-FULL+]

## 28:19/q04 Who was about to strike or be struck?  [أَرَادَ أَن يَبْطِشَ · referent]
turns on: The subject and object of the intended blow and the antecedent of the preceding rebuke are disputed; Moses' remorse is cited against a second attack on a Copt.
- 28:19/q04/p1 Moses was about to strike the Egyptian Copt. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, Ibn ʿAbbās, Thaʿlabī, Baghdawī and others describe his move against the Copt; compassion or aid for the Israelite motivates it. Al-Samīn prefers both clause pronouns referring to Moses. Biqāʿī specifies a forceful seizure to free the Israelite; Iṣlāḥī says Moses first tried reconciliation. Ibn ʿAṭiyya objects that Moses' remorse and repentance make another assault inconceivable. [22 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عبد الله,السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMIN-DURR+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-]
- 28:19/q04/p2 The Israelite was about to strike the Egyptian; Moses stopped him. — Ibn Jurayj or Ibn Abī Najīḥ is tentatively credited by Ṭabarī through Ibn ʿAṭiyya; Ṭabarī's Ibn Abī Najīḥ report says Moses regretted the earlier killing and wished he had not struck either man. Al-Samīn's alternative makes the pronouns refer to the Israelite and Egyptian. Qurṭubī reports the same sequence. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن أبي نجيح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج أو ابن أبي نجيح); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى)]
- 28:19/q04/p3 Moses intended to chastise or strike the Israelite for fighting again. — Al-Riḍā's report says Moses intended chastisement. Mawardī's first view makes Moses' rebuke directed at the Israelite for leading him toward another killing; Abū Ḥayyān calls that the apparent antecedent of 'to him'. [3 notes: TABATABAI(الرضا عليه السلام); MAWARDI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 28:19/q05 Why is the target called an enemy to both?  [ٱلَّذِى هُوَ عَدُوٌّۭ لَّهُمَا · interpretation]
- 28:19/q05/p1 The Copt opposed Moses and the Israelite through Egyptian hostility to Israel or religious difference. — Bayḍāwī and Bursevī give both grounds; Nasafī and Ālūsī likewise. Iṣlāḥī adds ethnic and reformist conflict. Ālūsī adds oppression of the Israelite and disobedience to God as grounds; Ibn ʿĀshūr says oppression offended Moses' purified nature. Asad says kinship again swayed Moses. Biqāʿī calls the Copt the Israelite's oppressor. [12 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 28:19/q06 Why is ʿaduww indefinite?  [عَدُوٌّۭ · rhetoric]
- 28:19/q06/p1 Its indefiniteness intensifies the formidable enmity. — Ālūsī observes that the word is not in a possessive construction. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:19/q07 Who says 'O Moses' and the accusation?  [قَالَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether only the Israelite knew Moses' earlier killing or the Copt already knew or inferred it; the next clause's speaker and context are argued both ways.
- 28:19/q07/p1 The Israelite whom Moses had helped speaks. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ṭabarī, Ibn al-Jawzī and many others identify him. Ibn al-Jawzī says he knows no disagreement; Biqāʿī says the Israelite alone knew the secret. Ṭabāṭabāʾī prefers this on context and rejects the Copt; Iṣlāḥī rejects the Copt because the killing remained secret and Copts would not call Moses a reformer. The Study Quran treats it as a minority view. [25 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس وأكثر المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جبير); BAYDAWI-FULL(الإسرائيلي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وأكثر المفسرين); TABATABAI(جل المفسرين); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(some); TABATABAI+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:19/q07/p2 The Egyptian Copt speaks. — Al-Ḥasan says the earlier killing was already known, with a belief that an Israelite did it; Bayḍāwī says the Copt supposed Moses had killed for the Israelite. Rāzī prefers the Copt because the tyranny accusation suits an unbeliever and speech follows mention of the common enemy. Qushayrī says the man inferred Moses' role on seeing him intervene, though he had not known it. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes his words as a warning. Study Quran calls this usual; Ibn al-Jawzī denies known disagreement. Iṣlāḥī and Ṭabāṭabāʾī reject it. [11 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; QUSHAYRI(قيل); TABRISI(الحسن); BAYDAWI-FULL(القبطي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن); IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN(commentators); RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI-; ISLAHI-TADABBUR-]

## 28:19/q08 Who was the Israelite companion?  [لَّهُمَا · reports]
turns on: The notes give an identity report and a continuity report, without treating them as incompatible.
- 28:19/q08/p1 He was the Samaritan. — Yaḥyā says he heard this, but his following clause is cut in the supplied segment; Mawardī transmits Yaḥyā's report. [2 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(يحيى); MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام)]
- 28:19/q08/p2 He was the same man who had sought Moses' help the day before. — Ibn ʿAbbās and ʿIkrima both report this; Ṭabrisī says he sought aid against another Copt. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة); TABRISI]

## 28:19/q09 Why did the speaker fear Moses meant to kill him?  [أَتُرِيدُ أَن تَقْتُلَنِى · interpretation]
turns on: Whether Moses approached the Copt, restrained the Israelite, or threatened a Copt whose death could trigger retaliation.
- 28:19/q09/p1 The Israelite mistook Moses' anger, rebuke, and movement toward the Copt as an attack on himself. — Ṭabarī reports Qatāda and al-Suddī on the rebuke at 28:18; Abū Bakr ibn ʿAbd Allāh adds Moses' regret. Ibn ʿAbbās says anger at yesterday's conduct was mistaken for lethal anger. Biqāʿī says he saw Moses approach angrily. Sāmarrāʾī says the intervening rebuke supports a pause. Ibn Kathīr calls him fearful and weak. Wahidi-Basit note states he thought Moses meant to strike the Egyptian because of the rebuke, while the related explanations say he mistakenly feared himself. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(أبو بكر بن عبد الله,السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جبير); TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وأكثر المفسرين); TABATABAI(جل المفسرين); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]
- 28:19/q09/p2 He feared Moses would kill the Copt and the Israelite would face retaliation. — Mawardī attributes this explanation to Yaḥyā ibn Sallām; the Israelite thus tried to stop Moses. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(يحيى بن سلام)]
- 28:19/q09/p3 He feared Moses after Moses restrained the Israelite from attacking the Copt. — Ṭabarī's Ibn Abī Najīḥ report and Qurṭubī's alternative give the Israelite as initial aggressor; Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports tentative attribution to Ibn Jurayj or Ibn Abī Najīḥ. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن أبي نجيح); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن جريج أو ابن أبي نجيح); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]

## 28:19/q10 What did Moses say to the Israelite before this moment?  [أَرَادَ أَن يَبْطِشَ · links]
turns on: The antecedent and speaker of the preceding rebuke at 28:18 are disputed.
- 28:19/q10/p1 He rebuked the Israelite as plainly misguided or a persistent troublemaker. — Ibn Qutayba permits the Israelite to have uttered the preceding rebuke about Moses' prior killing instead; al-Ḥasan via Qurṭubī applies it to the Egyptian exploiting the Israelite. Qurṭubī lists 'ruined or unsuccessful' and 'misguided' senses of ghawī, and analyzes it as an intensive from aghwā or one astray. Ibn Kathīr calls the Israelite persistently troublesome. Mawardī says he had misled Moses into yesterday's killing. Ṭabrisī glosses seeking help as appeal against an enemy to deter him; Durr transmits Qatāda equating seeking victory and seeking help. [6 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; TABRISI; DURR-FULL(قتادة); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 28:19/q10/p2 The Israelite had called Moses plainly misguided for yesterday's killing. — Ibn Qutayba presents this as a possible speaker of the prior words. [1 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB]
- 28:19/q10/p3 The Egyptian was called plainly misguided for exploiting and threatening the Israelite. — Ibn Qutayba alternatively assigns the words to their enemy; al-Ḥasan via Qurṭubī says the Egyptian's exploitation prompts the label; Qurṭubī notes his behavior had led to a death and threatened another. [3 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 28:19/q11 What does al-ams denote and how is it inflected?  [بِٱلْأَمْسِ · grammar]
- 28:19/q11/p1 It denotes the previous day, relative to the event; bare ams is usually built on kasra, while prefixed or possessed ams is declinable. — Qurṭubī explains the kasra through two meeting vowelless letters and notes poetic or some Arab indeclinable forms even with the article, including nominative and poetic oblique use. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the article replaces a possessive complement and means yesterday relative to the killing, not revelation. Sībawayh says ams answers matā as a specific time, unlike kam for count or duration, and contrasts temporal and spatial adverbs. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH; QURTUBI-FULL(سيبويه وغيره); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:19/q12 How are comparable time adverbs used?  [بِٱلْأَمْسِ · grammar]
- 28:19/q12/p1 Time expressions may identify a time or count an entire duration, according to use. — Sībawayh says night, day, age and eternity as duration adverbs cover the whole span; named months such as Ramadan answer matā, while month as a count can answer kam. Ibn al-Riqāʿ's winter verse illustrates a season as a time answer or counted duration. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH(ابن الرقاع)]

## 28:19/q13 How does the question form convey the accusation?  [أَتُرِيدُ أَن تَقْتُلَنِى · rhetoric]
- 28:19/q13/p1 It is a rebuke or denial, not a request for unknown information. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the question's rhetorical force; Biqāʿī says naming Moses removes ambiguity in the denunciation. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:19/q14 How does interrogative alif combine with coordinating particles?  [أَتُرِيدُ · grammar]
- 28:19/q14/p1 Interrogative alif precedes a coordinating wāw or fāʾ; repeated alternatives are governed by their intended relation. — Sībawayh cites 7:97–99, 37:16 and 2:100, says the construction functions like fāʾ, and distinguishes a renewed question after aw from an uncertain choice requiring am; 'are you our brother or companion?' can ask about a shared set, while repeated negative clauses can offer alternatives. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 28:19/q15 What event did the Israelite's mention of yesterday disclose?  [كَمَا قَتَلْتَ نَفْسًۢا بِٱلْأَمْسِ · reports]
turns on: What was public before the outcry: the killing alone, the killer's identity, or merely clues from which the Copt inferred it.
- 28:19/q15/p1 It identified Moses as the previously unknown killer; a Copt overheard and informed Pharaoh, leading to an order to kill Moses. — Muqātil says Egyptians knew of the killing but not its actor; Ibn al-Jawzī says Pharaoh demanded a witness. Reports vary whether the Egyptian heard on the spot or a nearby Copt did. Thaʿlabī says the two men parted first; Mwardī says the Israelite released the Copt; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the Israelite voiced it in his language and fled. Ibn Kathīr says only Moses and the Israelite knew; Biqāʿī says Egyptians suspected Israelites and the remark made Moses' role certain. Ibn ʿAbbās through Biqāʿī says even a partial king requires proof. Pharaoh's threat brings a loyal man, described by Bursevī and Ālūsī as his believing cousin, to warn Moses in 28:20; Baghdawī reports Pharaoh sent executioners and Moses took the main road. [32 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ; TAB-FULL(ابن أبي نجيح,سعيد بن جبير,معمر); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الإسرائيلي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 28:19/q15/p2 The previous killing was already widely known or inferred by the Copt before the accusation. — Nasafī says the killing was known but the killer hidden, making the Israelite's remark identify Moses. Al-Ḥasan says the Copt already knew of the death and supposed an Israelite responsible; Qushayrī says he inferred Moses' guilt from intervention. Rāzī's Copt view presumes the Israelite had informed him. [6 notes: QUSHAYRI(قيل); TABRISI(الحسن); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]

## 28:19/q16 What does in turīdu illā mean?  [إِن تُرِيدُ إِلَّآ · grammar]
- 28:19/q16/p1 It restricts the intention: 'you want nothing except' or 'you only want'. — Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī and Biqāʿī explicitly equate in with mā; Bursevī gives the resulting exclusive accusation. ʿAbd al-Qāhir explains that negation plus illā can deny another actor or identify the sole actor, and that order shifts focus; his poetic horseman example does not assert no one shared the deed. He says ghayr has parallel interpretations and cannot append 'nor ʿAmr' after either exclusive construction. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 28:19/q17 What is meant by jabbār in this charge?  [جَبَّارًۭا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the label focuses on any unjust killing, a second killing, arbitrary force, or defiant arrogance.
- 28:19/q17/p1 A killer who kills unjustly or without lawful cause. — Al-Suddī glosses the word as killer; Ṭabarī says a tyrant kills without just cause, and Qatāda, Ibn Jurayj and Abū ʿImrān describe killing someone who killed no one or unlawful killing. Damghānī cites 26:130 and 40:35. Ibn ʿAṭiyya likens this to unjust rulers; Nasafī adds acting in anger; Wāḥidī says fighting people oppressively. [21 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو عمران الجوني,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني,السدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي); BIQAI-FULL(أبو حيان); DURR-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:19/q17/p2 A person counts as a jabbār after killing two people unjustly. — Al-Shaʿbī and ʿIkrima give the two-killing threshold, connected to the Israelite's fear of a second killing. Wording varies between 'is a tyrant after two' and 'not until two'. Zajjāj and Ṭabarī report this as a saying or social convention. [15 notes: TAB-FULL(الشعبي); ZAJJAJ(قيل); IBNABIHATIM(عكرمة والشعبي); NAHHAS(عكرمة); THALABI(عكرمة والشعبي); MAWARDI-FULL(عكرمة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الشعبي); TABRISI(عكرمة والشعبي); QURTUBI-FULL(عكرمة والشعبي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشعبي); DURR-FULL(الشعبي,عكرمة); ALUSI-FULL(الشعبي وعكرمة)]
- 28:19/q17/p3 A person who dominates, beats, or kills as he wishes without regard for consequences or the better course. — Rāzī and Qurṭubī specify arbitrary violent action; Bayḍāwī and Bursevī emphasize unconcern for outcome; Biqāʿī says dominance and rule with no superior; Ṭabrisī says dominance through oppression and killing. Ibn ʿĀshūr contrasts harsh injury with gentleness, citing 14:15 and 19:32. Study Quran attributes the formulation to Rāzī. Iṣlāḥī says dictatorial or out of control. [11 notes: RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(al-Rāzī)]
- 28:19/q17/p4 An arrogant person who refuses to submit to God's command or anyone's authority. — Abū Isḥāq gives refusal to submit to God; Zajjāj says an unjust killer also qualifies. Rāzī reports refusal of anyone's command. Ālūsī compares the word's original sense to a tall palm. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:19/q18 What land is meant by 'on earth'?  [فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ · referent]
- 28:19/q18/p1 Egypt. — Nasafī explicitly identifies the land. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 28:19/q19 Who are the reformers and what does the denial charge?  [ٱلْمُصْلِحِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether reform here centers on peacemaking, obedience that benefits others, or control of anger.
- 28:19/q19/p1 Those who reconcile people and settle disputes through the better course. — Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Bursevī and Ālūsī give reconciliation; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Israelite charges Moses with vengeance by force. Biqāʿī says a peacemaker would not kill this way and reads denial of a natural disposition to make peace. Iṣlāḥī infers Moses' people looked to him for reform. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(ابن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:19/q19/p2 Those who benefit the earth's people through obedience to God. — Ṭabarī gives this gloss; Muqātil calls reformers obedient to God. [2 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL]
- 28:19/q19/p3 Those who restrain their anger. — Nasafī reads the Israelite's accusation this way. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL]

## 28:19/q20 What lesson is drawn from Moses' renewed encounter?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Rāzī disputes whether the second event demonstrates punishment for an omitted exception.
- 28:19/q20/p1 He was tested after omitting 'if God wills' from his promise and facing another similar fight. — Ibn ʿAbbās via Rāzī gives the omitted exception; Wāḥidī says the repetition exposed the previous killing and forced flight. Rāzī rejects the account as weak because Moses refrained from helping and only the enemy feared tyranny. [2 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:19/q20/p2 The Israelite's conduct shows ingratitude to a helper once restrained. — Iṣlāḥī says a mean person praises aid then attacks the helper when controlled. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:19/q20/p3 One must not assist an oppressor, even by writing for or accompanying him. — Naḥḥās places ʿAṭāʾ's warning beside ʿIkrima's interpretation; ʿAṭāʾ told a petitioner to discard his pen and seek God's provision, referring to 28:17. [2 notes: NAHHAS(عطاء,عكرمة)]

## 28:19/q21 How does this scene connect to the larger Moses story?  [* · links]
turns on: These are complementary links at different levels of the narrative.
- 28:19/q21/p1 The accusation and resulting threat lead into the warning in 28:20 and Moses' flight. — Ibn al-Jawzī says a man loyal to Moses brought the warning; Ālūsī and Bursevī call him a believing cousin. Ṭabāṭabāʾī's Qummī report adds Moses' court upbringing, hiding after defending an Israelite, and flight after the repeated accusation. Ibn ʿĀshūr says an exchange preceded the visitor from the city's far end. [5 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:19/q21/p2 This wording shifts attention from the call to consider the afterlife to worldly consequences of violence. — Biqāʿī reads the sequence of the discourse this way. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:19/q21/p3 Retellings of Moses and Pharaoh vary in detail, order and wording to convey distinct nuances. — Burhān compares repeated Qurʾānic versions with the collected Joseph story and earlier scripture's separate tellings. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 28:19/q22 How is the Qur'anic scene compared with the present Torah?  [* · links]
- 28:19/q22/p1 The Torah account differs in additions and omissions and cannot overturn the Qur'an or sound Sunna. — Ālūsī says contradictory Torah material is disregarded, while otherwise Israelite reports are neither affirmed nor denied and may corroborate details; he notes omission of the believing adviser and lack of support for the tyranny accusation. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the common enemy being a Copt implicitly contradicts the present Torah's claim that both fighters were Israelites. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 28:19/q23 What inward reading is given to the confrontation?  [* · inward]
- 28:19/q23/p1 The heart's attempted attack on fancy is obstructed when fancy accuses it of tyranny instead of reform. — Qāshānī says the heart called its companion deviant because intellect was captivated by fancy and needed aid. Fancy recalls yesterday's killing, hopes to mislead the heart until the Great Resurrection, and cannot be restrained by intellectual or practical perfection before the heart reaches spirit and annihilation in sainthood with divine attributes. [5 notes: QASHANI]

## 28:19/q24 What broader grammatical examples accompany discussion of this verse?  [* · grammar]
turns on: These are distinct general constructions appearing in the supplied grammatical notes.
- 28:19/q24/p1 An emphatic or separating pronoun has limits in predicate constructions. — Sībawayh analyzes raʾaytuhu iyyāhu nafsahu, taking iyyāhu as apposition, nafsahu as emphasis and qāʾiman as a circumstantial accusative, citing 15:30 and 38:73. He rejects combining a separating pronoun and emphatic iyyāhu in one predicate. He finds separation stronger with verbs than inna; al-Khalīl accepts innaka iyyāka khayrun minhu, though Sībawayh considers it remote without an intervening phrase. [4 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل)]
- 28:19/q24/p2 Judgment verbs such as ẓanna and ḥasiba normally require two objects, with restricted one-object uses. — Sībawayh says the first object is judged and the second states its condition; dhāka can supply the notion of thought for a lone object, but khiltu Zaydan and arā Zaydan are unacceptable in this use. The bāʾ in ẓanantu bihi marks what is assigned to supposition and is not redundant. [3 notes: SIBAWAYH]
- 28:19/q24/p3 A quotation after qāla conveys a complete sentence unless the word form itself is being cited. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir says a bare word cannot stand for a quoted meaning after 'I said'. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 28:19/q25 What broader principles of expression and meaning accompany this verse?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: The supplied excerpts address different general rhetorical examples rather than rival explanations of this verse.
- 28:19/q25/p1 Artful wording succeeds when meaning naturally calls for it; preselected puns and rhyme are affected. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir uses 'awdaʿānī', Abū Tammām's Najd and ḥamām lines for natural wordplay, but criticizes Abū Tammām's forced place-name puns and a later dāʾ conceit. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-ASRAR-]
- 28:19/q25/p2 A forced pun chosen because of a place-name is contrived and invites criticism. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir cites Abū Tammām's treatment of every place-name as a demand for a pun and a later poet's dāʾ conceit; his positive examples let meaning lead wording. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR; JURJANI-ASRAR-]
- 28:19/q25/p3 Comparison depends on the imagined relation and movement, not merely isolated colors or objects. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir distinguishes wholly imaginary ruby standards from possible comparisons such as stars like pearls on blue carpet; dawn shifts a garment to expose much of the body rather than shedding it entirely; lightning resembles flashes of white when a piebald horse leaps, with Ibn Bābak and Ibn al-Muʿtazz as parallels. [5 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR]
- 28:19/q25/p4 True richness is freedom from need, while true bankruptcy concerns lost good deeds after wrongdoing. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir contrasts abundant money with greed and a miser unable to use it; he cites al-Buḥturī on generosity and judgment, says God alone is absolutely rich, and reports the Prophet's account of a worshipper whose victims take his good deeds and whose sins are placed on him. [6 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(رسول الله)]
- 28:19/q25/p5 Meaning arises from relations among words and from context rather than isolated dictionary forms. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir says subject, object, time, manner and purpose form one communicated meaning through the verb's relation to its agent. He says Qur'anic eloquence requires thought about arrangement or figurative agency, citing 2:16, not ordinary inflection, dialect preference or memorized rarities. Rare words often owe their apparent rarity to metaphor, with only a few true rare cases, citing 2:93, 12:80, 15:94, 38:16, 54:13 and 19:24. He rejects unexamined claims that wording exceeds meaning or that sabk and ṭābiʿ are mere sound properties. [10 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 28:19/q25/p6 Indirect expression names an accompanying effect, while metaphor transfers a comparison or gives an impossible attribute. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir illustrates kināya with a long sword strap and abundant cooking ash, describes istiʿāra and tamthīl, and distinguishes an image replacing explicit simile from attributing a hand to the north wind. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 28:19/q25/p7 Innamā marks a contextually evident disclosure even where the information is new. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir says it can introduce what a listener expects, as a predicate after kāna Zayd or subject after kāna ḥalīman; when apparently new, contextual evidence lets the listener infer it immediately. [3 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 28:19/q25/p8 Jesus' exclusive statement denies omission or contradiction of the command to worship God. — ʿAbd al-Qāhir cites 5:117 to distinguish this from merely adding nothing beyond the command. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
- 28:19/q25/p9 Fulk may evoke falak and cosmic order, while mashḥūn suggests a vessel filled to pressured capacity. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾī presents the first link tentatively and finds crowding beyond the simple gloss 'filled' in the second. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 28:19/q25/p10 Qur'anic address varies by addressee and conveys God's sovereignty through counsel, proof, promise and warning. — Itqān reports one scholar's threefold division: Prophet only, others only, or both; Ibn al-Qayyim lists guidance, praise, ends of friends and enemies, Paradise, Hell, dependence, justice and mercy. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن القيم,بعضهم)]

## 28:19/q26 What doctrine about prophets is reported alongside this narrative?  [* · theology]
- 28:19/q26/p1 Prophets are infallible. — In the reported meeting of al-Maʾmūn and al-Riḍā, al-Maʾmūn asks and al-Riḍā affirms it. [1 notes: TABATABAI(علي بن محمد بن الجهم)]

## 28:19/q27 Where was al-Qaṣaṣ revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 28:19/q27/p1 All of al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī transmits this from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:19/q28 On Zajjāj's grammatical reading, why did Moses rebuke the Israelite?  [أَرَادَ أَن يَبْطِشَ · interpretation]
- 28:19/q28/p1 The Israelite sought Moses' help to strike their common enemy, but Moses did not strike and called him plainly misguided. — Zajjāj connects the attempted enlistment with Moses' preceding rebuke. [1 notes: ZAJJAJ]
