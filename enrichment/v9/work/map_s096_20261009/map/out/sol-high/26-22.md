# 26:22: 16 questions

## 26:22/q01 What does Moses mean by Pharaoh's claimed favor, and does he accept it?  [وَتِلْكَ نِعْمَةٌۭ تَمُنُّهَا عَلَىَّ أَنْ عَبَّدتَّ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether a personal benefit remains a favor despite Pharaoh's prior persecution and collective oppression, and whether the clause asserts, ironically identifies, or questions the alleged favor.
- 26:22/q01/p1 Moses rejects the claimed favor because Pharaoh's persecution caused him to be raised in Pharaoh's house. — Al-Zajjaj, al-Azhari, Ibn Ishaq, al-Razi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Bursevi, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur, and Kuranyolu explain that enslavement and killing Israelite boys forced his mother to cast him into the river; his own family otherwise would have raised him. Al-Wahidi likens this to claiming credit after killing someone's parents. Ibn Ashur mentions Pharaoh's wife finding him (28:9). Al-Biqa'i adds Joseph's earlier benefit to Egypt. Tabataba'i reports this view, without endorsing it. Al-Wahidi prefers denial overall. [26 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأزهري,الزجاج,المبرد,محمد بن إسحاق بن يسار); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(الأزهري,الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المبرد والزجاج والأزهري); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج,الزمخشري,الضحاك); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 26:22/q01/p2 Moses acknowledges being raised and spared as a personal favor, while contrasting Pharaoh's enslavement of the Israelites. — Al-Farra, al-Tabari, al-Suddi and others supply an implied 'and left me unenslaved'; al-Farra compares a spared servant whose fellow is beaten. Some say Moses was not killed as other Israelite boys were. Ibn Atiyya says candid acknowledgment strengthens his argument and does not invalidate his mission. Al-Alusi finds this unpersuasive; Tabataba'i objects that the proposed ellipsis lacks support. The demonstrative can point back to Pharaoh's claim that he raised Moses (Abu Hayyan, al-Alusi). [35 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(السدي,نحويي الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الفراء); THALABI(الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(السدي,الفراء,الفراء، وأبو العباس,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL(بعضهم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); QURTUBI-FULL(السدي والطبري والفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(السدي، والطبري); BURHAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي، الطبري); TABATABAI(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI-]
- 26:22/q01/p3 Moses denies a personal favor because enslaving and humiliating his people cancels any kindness shown to him. — Maqatil says Pharaoh forgot his crime against the Israelites, exploited for the Copts. Al-Hasan and others stress that a people's humiliation disgraces its member; Ibn Kathir says raising one Israelite cannot compensate for abuse of all. Qatada says they were free people and Pharaoh had no right to kill or enslave them; al-Dahhak says Pharaoh had no right to claim this favor. Al-Razi describes the earlier oppression as greater. Ibn Ashur says kindness creates no debt amid oppression. Tabataba'i objects that communal wrong cannot erase the fact of a real kindness to an infant. [40 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن إسحاق,قتادة); NAHHAS(الضحاك); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(الفراء,علي بن عيسى); QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,قتادة,مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة); TABRISI(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الثعلبي,مقاتل); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قتادة وغيره,قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الضحاك,قتادة,وقيل); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; WAHIDI-BASIT+; TABATABAI-]
- 26:22/q01/p4 Moses denies a favor because Pharaoh spent the Israelites' seized wealth on his upbringing. — Yahya ibn Sallam says Pharaoh enslaved a formerly free people, seized their property, and so Moses had a stronger claim to what was spent. Al-Hasan says the money was earned or owned by Israelites rather than Pharaoh. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; THALABI(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(الحسن); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن); RAZI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 26:22/q01/p5 Moses denies a favor because his mother or fellow Israelites actually raised him under Pharaoh's order. — Al-Mawardi says enslaved Israelites raised Moses at Pharaoh's order; al-Jubba'i and al-Razi specify his mother and fellow Israelites, while Pharaoh merely refrained from killing him. Tabataba'i finds this less supported by the wording. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:22/q01/p6 Moses turns the claim into a rhetorical question: is enslaving Israel itself the favor Pharaoh boasts of? — Al-Akhfash, Qatada, Mujahid and others treat 'favor' as ironic or a rebuke; Tabataba'i calls it an inversion of cause and effect. Al-Biqa'i says Moses recasts the favor as the injustice that cancels it. The Study Quran reports Moses asking whether persecution is a blessing. Ibn Atiyya cites al-Dahhak's expanded 'you have no right to claim it' reading; al-Nahhas compares 4:79. [22 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(ابن إسحاق); THALABI(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,الزجاج,صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك، قتادة، الأخفش، الفراء); TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Moses); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الضحاك); NAHHAS]
- 26:22/q01/p7 Moses acknowledges the upbringing but denies that merely refraining from enslaving him is a favor. — Tabataba'i reports this narrower distinction with an implied interrogative hamza, then rejects it because the wording does not support the ellipsis. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعضهم); TABATABAI-]
- 26:22/q01/p8 Feeding a slave does not earn thanks from the slave merely because the master claims ownership. — Al-Razi reports this final explanation: Pharaoh claimed Israel as his slaves, and supplying a slave is no independent benefaction. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 26:22/q01/p9 Raising Moses gives Pharaoh no entitlement to enslave Israel or silence Moses's protest. — Islahi reads the reply against Pharaoh's reminder at 26:18–19. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:22/q02 What does تمنها mean and what does reminding someone of a favor imply?  [تَمُنُّهَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether mann denotes the act of giving or the speech that recounts it.
- 26:22/q02/p1 It means to count, mention, or boast of a favor to someone. — Al-Alusi derives it from enumerating a favor and says the present tense evokes Pharaoh's scene. Study Quran lists counting and boasting of charity and cites 2:264. Bursevi says verbal reminders are usually blameworthy, can expose ingratitude, and sayings warn they destroy good deeds. Al-Tirmidhi says recalling favors violates spiritual chivalry; Bursevi reports Pharaoh's reproach as vulgar. [5 notes: ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN; BURSEVI(بعضهم,محمد بن علي الترمذي)]
- 26:22/q02/p2 It can mean actually doing or bestowing a favor. — Bursevi says only God truly grants favor in this sense and cites 3:164; Study Quran also lists giving among the meanings. [2 notes: BURSEVI; STUDYQURAN]

## 26:22/q03 How does تمنها علي relate grammatically to نعمة?  [نِعْمَةٌۭ تَمُنُّهَا عَلَىَّ · grammar]
- 26:22/q03/p1 The verbal clause is an adjective qualifying نعمة. — Al-Samin states the adjectival relation; Bursevi parses تلك as subject and نعمة as predicate. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI]

## 26:22/q04 Why does تمنها take علي here?  [تَمُنُّهَا عَلَىَّ · grammar]
- 26:22/q04/p1 An expected preposition bi is omitted, or تمن carries the sense of mentioning. — Al-Samin gives both grammatical explanations. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 26:22/q05 Is the opening of Moses's reply an unstated question or a declarative rebuke?  [وَتِلْكَ نِعْمَةٌۭ تَمُنُّهَا عَلَىَّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether Arabic permits omitting a meaning-bearing interrogative particle here, and whether rebuke requires a question.
- 26:22/q05/p1 It is an incredulous question with an omitted interrogative hamza or understood aw. — Al-Akhfash and al-Nazm support omission with Arabic and poetic examples; Baghawi cites 21:34 and poems, including Umar ibn Abi Rabi'a. Al-Farra permits omission in doubt expressions such as ترى زيدا منطلقا; Ali ibn Sulayman calls this colloquial. Al-Qurtubi finds a poem proving omission without am, against al-Nahhas. Burhan and Itqan compare 6:76 and Ibn Muhaysin's 2:6 reading; al-Samin also connects 4:79. Al-Biqa'i says an unmarked question is concise and courteous. [26 notes: AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(نحويي البصرة); NAHHAS(الأخفش,الفراء); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,صاحب النظم); BAGHAWI-FULL(الشاعر,بعضهم,عمر بن عبد الله بن أبي ربيعة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL(الأخفش والفراء,الثعلبي,الغزنوي,الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش، والفراء); SAMIN-DURR(الأخفش); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن محيصن); ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك، قتادة، الأخفش، الفراء); IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS-; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش الأصغر,النحاس)-]
- 26:22/q05/p2 The words are a statement whose wording itself rebukes Pharaoh; no hamza need be posited. — Al-Zajjaj reads a declarative rebuke. Ibn Atiyya calls the omitted hamza forced and says Qatada's declarative reading works. Al-Dahhak says rebuke is possible with or without a question. Al-Nahhas, Abu al-Abbas, and al-Akhfash al-Asghar object that dropping a meaning-bearing hamza changes sense; some allow it only in poetry when am follows. Tabari reports most grammarians treating one clause as statement and the next as a question, with am serving as hamza. The disputed cited poetic 'تروح من الحي أم تبتكر' features am. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(أكثرهم,بعض أهل العربية); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو العباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(الضحاك,النحاس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش الأصغر,النحاس); QURTUBI-FULL-]
- 26:22/q05/p3 It is a straightforward acknowledgment of a favor, with an understood contrast that Moses was spared. — Al-Farra and al-Tabari supply 'and left me'; Ibn Atiyya says this declarative reading is candid and powerful. Al-Alusi and Tabataba'i dispute the implied wording. [4 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(السدي); TABATABAI-]

## 26:22/q06 How is أن عبدت بني إسرائيل attached and parsed?  [أَنْ عَبَّدتَّ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · grammar]
turns on: Which preceding element أن عبدت explains and whether an implied preposition or subject is supplied.
- 26:22/q06/p1 It is a nominative apposition or explanatory clause identifying the favor or the demonstrative's disgraceful referent. — Al-Farra, al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Thalabi and later grammarians give apposition to نعمة or تلك. Al-Razi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, and Ibn Ashur cite 15:66 as a parallel. Al-Razi, al-Baydawi, and al-Nasafi describe the demonstrative as an unspecified heinous trait clarified by the clause. Al-Samin also allows substitution for the hāʾ pronoun in تمنها; Abu al-Baqa calls it بدل. Al-Mubarrad makes an the subject, 'favor you claim' its predicate, and enslaving the explanation. [26 notes: FARRA; AKHFASH; TAB-FULL(نحويي البصرة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش,المبرد); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 26:22/q06/p2 It is accusative or genitive after an omitted preposition, giving the cause: because Pharaoh enslaved Israel. — Al-Farra and al-Tabari allow accusative with the favor Pharaoh claims; al-Zajjaj explains the causative irony: persecution placed Moses in Pharaoh's care. Al-Thalabi, al-Wahidi, al-Jishumi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Samin, al-Alusi and Ibn Ashur give an implicit bi or another preposition. Al-Samin separately allows genitive under bi. Al-Hawfi and al-Samin call it a purpose accusative. [18 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي,الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:22/q06/p3 It is the predicate of an omitted subject, 'it is your enslaving of Israel.' — Al-Samin and Bursevi supply the implicit subject; al-Alusi lists this among grammatical possibilities. Al-Nahhas reports a construction with omitted 'saying'; al-Samin also permits accusative after an implicit 'I mean.' [5 notes: NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:22/q06/p4 It is circumstantial, a conjunction, or a predicate with another attachment. — Al-Alusi lists circumstantial and explanatory analyses and conjunction alongside predicate and appositional analyses. Al-Baydawi permits a predicate analysis. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL]

## 26:22/q07 What does عبدت mean here?  [عَبَّدتَّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the term is understood through ordinary Arabic enslavement and humiliation or a reported Nabataean gloss.
- 26:22/q07/p1 Pharaoh made the Israelites slaves, subdued or humiliated them, and put them to forced work. — Mujahid and Ibn Jurayj add conquest and employment; Yahya transmits torture, exploitation and subjugation. Ibn Ishaq mentions seizing children, killing or enslaving at will. Al-Farra, al-Zajjaj, Ibn al-Sikkit, al-Jishumi, Ibn al-Jawzi, al-Qurtubi and al-Alusi say ʿabada and aʿbada can both mean making a slave; Ibn al-Sikkit adds taʿabbada, comparing taʾammat for taking a bondwoman. Poetry cited by al-Farra, al-Tabari, al-Zajjaj, al-Wahidi, Abu Hayyan, al-Qurtubi and al-Alusi confirms the usage, including Ru'ba's verse. Bursevi supplies a Persian gloss. Ibn Ashur stresses humiliation and a causative form. [31 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد,مجاهد); FARRA; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(الشاعر); IBNABIHATIM(ابن إسحاق,مجاهد); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن السكيت,رؤبة,مجاهد); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الشاعر); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 26:22/q07/p2 Taʿbīd means confinement and humiliation. — Aban ibn Taghlib gives this gloss. Al-Mawardi relates enslavement to humiliation through the image of a trodden road, supported by a line of Tarafa using maʿbad. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(أبان بن تغلب,طرفة بن العبد)]
- 26:22/q07/p3 In Nabataean, ʿabbadta means 'you killed.' — Al-Itqan reports Abu al-Qasim's foreign-language gloss. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو القاسم)]

## 26:22/q08 Who is addressed by the singular forms here compared with earlier plurals?  [تَمُنُّهَا عَلَىَّ عَبَّدتَّ · referent]
- 26:22/q08/p1 The singular refers to Pharaoh alone; the earlier plural fear and flight included Pharaoh and his courtiers. — Al-Kashshaf, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi distinguish Pharaoh's individual claim of favor and enslavement from the chiefs who plotted with him. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]

## 26:22/q09 How long did Pharaoh oppress the Israelites?  [عَبَّدتَّ بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · reports]
turns on: Two differing duration reports are transmitted without grounds for choosing between them.
- 26:22/q09/p1 The oppression lasted 430 years. — Muqatil locates the Israelites in Egypt since Jacob and his household came to Joseph. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 26:22/q09/p2 The oppression lasted forty years more, or 470 years. — Muqatil reports this as an alternative to 430 years. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 26:22/q10 Does the verse require thanks for a kindness done by an unbeliever?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether thanking a kindness entails reverence inconsistent with blame for disbelief.
- 26:22/q10/p1 Disbelief does not erase a real favor or the gratitude owed for it. — Al-Razi says disbelief affects reward and praise for faith, not recognition of kindness, and sees the verse as support. Al-Alusi distinguishes grounds for praise and blame. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]
- 26:22/q10/p2 An unbeliever deserves humiliation rather than thanks, since thanks entails reverence. — Al-Razi reports scholars who say reverence would conflict with the humiliation owed for disbelief; he prefers the opposing distinction. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]

## 26:22/q11 Why does Moses answer Pharaoh's reproach before the claim of favor?  [* · rhetoric]
- 26:22/q11/p1 The reproach was Pharaoh's later and more immediate point and attacked Moses's prophethood, so Moses answered it first. — Al-Biqa'i says Pharaoh combined reproach and boasting; Bursevi identifies the reproach as an attack on Moses's prophethood. Al-Samarrai traces Moses's answer through God's grant of judgment and messengership before this rebuttal. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; SAMARRAI-ASRAR]

## 26:22/q12 Why does Pharaoh turn away from Moses's rebuke?  [* · links]
- 26:22/q12/p1 Moses exposed God's complete power and knowledge in his life; Pharaoh shifted to the mission to mislead his people. — Al-Biqa'i links Pharaoh's change of subject to the force of Moses's response. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 26:22/q13 How does the earlier إذا describe Moses's reply to Pharaoh?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether إذا links Moses's deed to Pharaoh's alleged favor as conditional repayment or serves as a temporal or answer particle.
- 26:22/q13/p1 It is a conditional response to Pharaoh's accusation that Moses repaid a favor with killing. — Al-Zamakhshari reads Moses as accepting the deed as repayment. Al-Kashf prefers a hypothetical: if the deed were ingratitude, Moses did it, but denies the premise; 'I was astray' refers to acting before divine permission. Al-Alusi says bold action without concern for consequences best sustains a recompense reading. [4 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(صاحب الكشف)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 26:22/q13/p2 It is a temporal adverb or simple answer, without a conditional recompense sense. — Al-Alusi prefers the temporal adverb detached from annexation; if refused, he takes it as an answer. He says most interpreters favor the simple answer and conditional reward is strained. Ibn Atiyya calls it a filler and glosses 'then'; al-Alusi accepts the gloss but rejects the filler analysis. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطية); ALUSI-FULL+]

## 26:22/q14 What inward meaning is drawn from Moses and Pharaoh here?  [* · inward]
turns on: Different symbolic mappings of Pharaoh's oppression and Moses's experience.
- 26:22/q14/p1 The bodily, veiled self oppresses the spiritual powers and resists revelation. — Qashani maps Pharaoh's oppression to bodily nature placing Moses in a body-coffin of prime matter; spiritual powers would otherwise have nurtured him. The veiled self cannot know the Real, prophetic wisdom or law and seeks to overpower revelation. [2 notes: QASHANI]
- 26:22/q14/p2 Moses manifests divine gentleness and Pharaoh overpowering wrath; hardship fulfills their contrasting dispositions. — Bursevi reports the Najmiyya reading as a pattern repeated every age; he adds that humans can manifest gentleness in a way angels cannot, connecting this to angelic prostration. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]

## 26:22/q15 What reading detail concerns the written name بني إسرائيل?  [بَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ · readings]
- 26:22/q15/p1 A variant treats the hamza-alif sequence differently in writing without changing the meaning. — Qiraat-ER attributes the orthographic difference to canonical handling of hamza in a foreign-origin name. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 26:22/q16 Why is the n of نعمةٌ not assimilated into the following t?  [نِعْمَةٌۭ تَمُنُّهَا · readings]
- 26:22/q16/p1 Tanwin at a word's end blocks the large assimilation here. — Al-Nashr lists tanwin among three agreed blockers, with a pronominal first ta and a first letter already doubled. He also lists disputed blockers: jazm, shortness, successive weak changes, madd conversion, and consonant context. With no blocker, identical letters assimilate by silencing the first and unlike letters by first changing it to the second, without pause or vowel. Relative sonority also limits direction. [3 notes: NASHR]
