# 20:132: 37 questions

## 20:132/q01 Who are the Prophet's ahl whom he must command?  [أَهْلَكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether ahl is taken as the original Meccan household, blood kin, a wider kin group, Quraysh, or all who belong to the Prophet through faith.
- 20:132/q01/p1 His community of faith, including followers beyond blood relatives. — Muqātil compares Ismāʿīl's ahl in 19:55; Wāḥidī, Baghawī, and Rāzī likewise invoke it. Rāzī prefers this over kin. Islahi cites Arabic usage, Abū Bakr's broad use of ahl, and Ismāʿīl's example; he explicitly includes believers of every lineage, status, and sex and says the term honors poor Muslims. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects this as the intended referent. Saʿīd ibn Jubayr likewise glosses it as 'your people'; Wāḥidī points back to 19:55. Ibn al-Jawzī-zād reports the exegetes' explicit inclusion of Muhammad's own family within his people and coreligionists. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Abu Bakr (rta)); STUDYQURAN(others); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); RAZI-FULL+; TABATABAI(آخرين)-]
- 20:132/q01/p2 His close household or relatives under his care. — Māwardī gives kin by lineage; Ibn al-Jawzī says blood kin is the ordinary lexical sense, extendable by context. Wāḥidī reports the household alternative; Rāzī allows household residents because he could remind them at prayer times. Al-Ālūsī favors the household among reported views; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls them his exemplary dependents. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī accepts wives, daughters, and ʿAlī as a continuing application, though not the original Meccan household. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(Some); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:132/q01/p3 At the Meccan revelation, Khadīja and ʿAlī, possibly some daughters living with them. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues this verse is Meccan, when Khadīja was the Prophet's only wife there; he treats later household identifications as applications, not the original occasion. [2 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:132/q01/p4 Quraysh. — Wāḥidī's Wajīz states this directly. [1 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ]
- 20:132/q01/p5 His wives, daughters, ʿAlī, and believing Banū Hāshim and Banū al-Muṭṭalib. — Al-Ālūsī reports this expanded kin group. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports and rejects it as the intended referent. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(آخرين)-]

## 20:132/q02 To whom does the command to the Prophet extend?  [وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether a singular prophetic address is confined to him or includes Muslims by the shared reason for the command.
- 20:132/q02/p1 It addresses the Prophet first and applies to believers generally. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Study Quran extend it to the community. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reason for the command applies to all Muslims. Al-Ālūsī rejects restricting the ruling to the Prophet. [7 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+]
- 20:132/q02/p2 The command and ruling are particular to the Prophet. — Al-Ālūsī reports and rejects this claim, including the inference that a general ruling would permit everyone to leave earning. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 20:132/q03 Why is the family singled out for prayer?  [وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: The sources variously emphasize example and instruction, the family's special rank, and shared reliance on God.
- 20:132/q03/p1 The Prophet should guide the people closest to him and exemplify prayer with them. — Biqāʿī compares Ismāʿīl and says leading the family to good and teaching others purifies sincerity; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls family one's exemplary dependents. Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Qurṭubī say he models prayer with them; Rāzī says action teaches more fully than speech. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL]
- 20:132/q03/p2 Their special command makes the Prophet's family's standing with God known. — Abū Jaʿfar says they were first included in a general command, then expressly singled out; the household purification verse 33:33 accompanies the door reports. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو جعفر,أبو سعيد الخدري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
- 20:132/q03/p3 The family can cooperate in reliance on God when livelihood is scarce. — Al-Ālūsī connects the instruction with resisting luxury and fixation on earning. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:132/q04 What prayer is meant in the command?  [بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wording chiefly specifies obligatory prayer or embraces voluntary prayer as well.
- 20:132/q04/p1 The prescribed prayers, performed and enjoined at their appointed times. — Ṭabarī requires prescribed limits; Rāzī says household members can be reminded at set times. Al-Ālūsī calls obligatory prayer most likely and says perseverance does not require prayer all day and night. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:132/q04/p2 Prayer broadly, including voluntary or night prayer. — Al-Ālūsī allows this in light of reports of ʿUmar's night prayer and the Prophet calling his household amid hardship. Ibn ʿĀshūr takes iṣṭibār figuratively as abundant voluntary prayer and cites 73:1–2 and 17:79. [7 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أسلم); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); QURTUBI-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب); DURR-FULL(عمر بن الخطاب)]

## 20:132/q05 What does commanding one's family to pray accomplish?  [وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
turns on: Which effect of the prayer command the commentator draws out: moral restraint, aid in hardship, worship, or inward discipline.
- 20:132/q05/p1 It restrains wrongdoing and protects the family from punishment. — Wāḥidī and Baghawī say prayer restrains indecency and wrongdoing; Biqāʿī cites 29:45. Ibn Kathīr connects saving one's family through prayer to 66:6. [4 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:132/q05/p2 It gives help through hardship and turns attention from wealth toward worship. — Bayḍāwī says prayer helps with hardship and prevents preoccupation with livelihood or wealthy habits. Bursevī directs the poor to prayer rather than gazing at the wealthy; al-Wāsiṭī says the prior warning consoles the poor. Ibn Kathīr and Qushayrī connect prayer with relief and provision; Islahi says vigilant prayer builds perseverance. Al-Zamakhsharī directs the Prophet and his family to prayer for endurance in hardship and focus on the afterlife; he reports Bakr ibn ʿAbd Allāh al-Muzanī urging his poor family to rise and pray. [10 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI; SULAMI(الواسطي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QUSHAYRI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(بكر بن عبد الله المزني)]
- 20:132/q05/p3 It fulfills the worship God asks for, for the worshipper's benefit. — Muqātil and Abū Muslim cite 51:56–57; Ṭabarī contrasts bodily work with wealth and promises reward. Jishumī, Rāzī, Asad, and Ibn ʿĀshūr say God gains no benefit from the worship; Asad opposes a portrayal of worship as tribute to a jealous God. Ibn al-Jawzī-zād juxtaposes God's command to worship with His undertaking to provide. [10 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); ASAD-NOTES(Razi); IBNASHUR-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 20:132/q05/p4 In an inward reading, it calls the faculties to divine presence and obedience. — Qāshānī takes the family as spiritual and psychic faculties, and prayer as presence, vigilance, obedience, and response. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:132/q06 What training of children follows from the family prayer command?  [وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · law]
- 20:132/q06/p1 Children should be taught prayer before it is obligatory. — Al-Ālūsī says this establishes the habit; Abū Dāwūd's sound-chain raised report orders prayer at seven, discipline for it at ten, and separation of their beds. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو داود)]

## 20:132/q07 What did the Prophet do at ʿAlī and Fāṭima's door after this command?  [وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · reports]
turns on: The reports differ on duration and time of visit; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī treats a duration as the witness's period of observation and notes the setting problem.
- 20:132/q07/p1 He came for an unspecified number of months and called them to prayer. — Wāḥidī says each morning for several months without grading; Rāzī, Qurṭubī, and Bursevī report the morning visits. Ṭabrisī mentions multiple chains through Ibn ʿUqda, including Abū Barza and Abū Rāfiʿ. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABRISI(ابن عقدة)]
- 20:132/q07/p2 He came for nine months, at every prayer, and invoked 33:33. — Ṭabrisī attributes this to Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports a nine-month version and cautions that the duration may reflect a witness's observation. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو سعيد الخدري); TABATABAI(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
- 20:132/q07/p3 He came for eight months at dawn, urging prayer and invoking 33:33. — Abū Saʿīd's eight-month version appears in Durr, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī; al-Ālūsī says Imāmī reports likewise. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī notes the apparent Medinan setting is difficult for a Meccan verse. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو سعيد الخدري); TABATABAI(أبو سعيد الخدري)]
- 20:132/q07/p4 He came for forty mornings, greeted the Household, and urged prayer. — Abū al-Ḥamrāʾ says he witnessed these visits. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو الحميراء)]

## 20:132/q08 What did the Prophet do when hardship struck his household?  [وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · reports]
turns on: The reports tell either the household-hardship incident, a general resort to prayer in distress, or the distinct servant request.
- 20:132/q08/p1 He ordered his family to pray and recited this verse. — ʿAbd Allāh ibn Salām versions appear in Wāḥidī, Rāzī, Biqāʿī, Durr, al-Ālūsī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī; Durr grades a chain sound, Biqāʿī quotes al-Ṭabarānī that its chain is singular and Maʿmar alone transmitted it, and al-Ālūsī calls a chain sound. Other versions are attributed to the Prophet, a Qurashī narrator, Mālik ibn Dīnār, and Thābit; Wāḥidī states no grading. The detail varies between hardship, distress, and tight household provision. [16 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(عبد الله بن سلام); BAGHAWI-FULL(النبي); RAZI-FULL(عبد الله بن سلام); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); NASAFI-FULL(النبي عليه السلام); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(ثابت,عبد الله بن سلام); DURR-FULL(ثابت,رجل من قريش,عبد الله بن سلام); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ثابت,عبد الله بن سلام); TABATABAI(عبد الله بن سلام)]
- 20:132/q08/p2 The Prophet and earlier prophets resorted to prayer when trouble came. — Thābit generalizes the practice of prophets; Ḥudhayfa says the Prophet resorted to prayer when something troubled him, with Aḥmad transmitting it. Wahb says earlier believers sought needs through prayer and often found relief. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ثابت); BIQAI-FULL(ثابت,حذيفة); DURR-FULL(ثابت); ALUSI-FULL(ثابت); BURSEVI(وهب بن منبه)]
- 20:132/q08/p3 He instructed Fāṭima and ʿAlī in remembrance after she sought a servant. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Bukhārī: Fāṭima's grinding exhausted her, and the Prophet said thirty-three repetitions each of tasbīḥ, taḥmīd, and takbīr after prayer were better than a captive servant. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(النبي)]

## 20:132/q12 What does iṣṭabir ʿalayhā ask of the Prophet?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stress is ordinary persistence, the intensified form's effort, or plentiful voluntary observance.
- 20:132/q12/p1 Persist patiently in performing prayer, enjoining it, and observing its demands. — Muqātil, Ṭabarī, Māwardī, Jishumī, Baghawī, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ṭabrisī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, al-Ālūsī, and Islahi describe continued performance. Ṭabarī specifies prayer's limits; Rāzī says the Prophet should embody what he enjoins; Islahi likens persistence to a farmer tending a field until it yields. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 20:132/q12/p2 It calls for strenuous effort beyond simple patience. — Burhān says the augmented form is stronger than aṣbir; Biqāʿī says iftiʿāl signals effort even when prayer consumes livelihood time; al-Ālūsī infers that fully observing worship is difficult for the soul. Al-Sāmarrāʾī explains the intensified form by prayer's daily recurrence and the substantial patience needed to complete it properly at appointed times. [4 notes: BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT+]
- 20:132/q12/p3 It is abundant voluntary prayer, with the Prophet asked to persist even more than his family. — Ibn ʿĀshūr takes iṣṭibār as restraint and its figurative sense as plentiful voluntary prayer, citing 73:1–2 and 17:79. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:132/q13 How do inward readers distinguish iṣṭibār from ordinary patience?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا · inward]
turns on: The inward accounts differ on whether the added quality is stillness, struggle, or being comforted beyond pain.
- 20:132/q13/p1 It is inner stillness under trial, in the heart and secret as well as the lower self. — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ calls this the greatest or hardest patience, contrasting it with ordinary patience of the self; Sulamī and Bursevī transmit the formulation. [2 notes: SULAMI(ابن عطاء); BURSEVI(ابن عطاء)]
- 20:132/q13/p2 It is a station of struggle, whereas patience is a station of witnessing. — Bursevī reports the distinction from ʿArāʾis al-Baqlī. [1 notes: BURSEVI(عرائس البقلي)]
- 20:132/q13/p3 It surpasses patience because divine support carries the person without felt pain. — Qushayrī describes the extra quality of istiṣbār in these terms. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 20:132/q14 What inward meaning is given to the family's prayer?  [وَأْمُرْ أَهْلَكَ بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ وَٱصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا · inward]
turns on: Whether inward prayer is a maintained bond, graded forms of presence, or discipline of inner faculties.
- 20:132/q14/p1 It means joining God and steadfastly maintaining that connection. — Sulamī reports Junayd, who says only the divinely supported can bear this connection. [1 notes: SULAMI(الجنيد)]
- 20:132/q14/p2 It means presence of heart and a graded prayer of body, soul, heart, secret, and spirit. — Sulamī reports learning prayer as presence. Yaḥyā ibn Muʿādh likens prayer and fasting to the warp and weft of worshippers' robes. Bursevī expands this into bodily obligatory and voluntary prayer, the soul's ascent, the heart's vigilance, the secret's exclusive witnessing, and the spirit's annihilation and abiding through God, citing 89:29–30, 23:2, and 4:80. [5 notes: SULAMI(بعضهم,يحيى بن معاذ); BURSEVI(يحيى بن معاذ)]
- 20:132/q14/p3 It directs inner faculties toward presence, obedience, struggle, and unveiling. — Qāshānī treats ahl as psychic and spiritual faculties rather than human relatives. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:132/q15 What is the force of the command to persist compared with 18:28?  [وَٱصْطَبِرْ عَلَيْهَا · links]
- 20:132/q15/p1 The excerpt raises the contrast but gives no answer. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī asks how keeping oneself steadfast in 18:28 differs from persisting in prayer with one's family here. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:132/q16 What does 'We ask you no provision' deny?  [لَا نَسْـَٔلُكَ رِزْقًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: The object implicitly supplied for rizq: God's own provision or payment, the Prophet's and family's livelihood, other creatures' livelihood, or compensation for prophethood.
- 20:132/q16/p1 God asks no sustenance or payment from the Prophet for Himself; He asks worship, which benefits people. — Muqātil and Abū Muslim compare 51:56–57. Farrāʾ says no payment is asked; Ṭabarī contrasts bodily work with wealth. Jishumī compares worldly kings' extraction; Rāzī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī stress divine independence. Ibn ʿĀshūr compares taxes and tribute and cites 51:56–58; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects the alternative that the object is provision for creatures or oneself. [14 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); TABRISI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); TABATABAI+]
- 20:132/q16/p2 He is not required to secure livelihood for himself or his family. — Yaḥyā prefers this by extending al-Ḥasan's gloss of 51:57; Ibn Qutayba, Rāzī, Qurṭubī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī link it to freedom for worship and the Hereafter. Al-Thawrī says God does not burden him with seeking provision. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī rejects reading the request this way. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(الحسن); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الثوري); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(سفيان الثوري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; YAHYA-SALLAM+; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:132/q16/p3 He is not charged with providing for God's other creatures. — Ibn Qutayba, Ibn Muslim, Jishumī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, and Biqāʿī explicitly include others' sustenance; Māwardī extends God's provision to every creature. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī objects to taking the request as provision for creatures. [10 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن مسلم); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+; TABATABAI(بعضهم)-]
- 20:132/q16/p4 He owes no provision in return for the gift of prophethood. — Yaḥyā reports this explanation from some interpreters. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(بعضهم)]

## 20:132/q17 What does the wording of the provision statement do here?  [لَا نَسْـَٔلُكَ رِزْقًۭا نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The commentators locate the rhetorical point in reassurance, rejection of tribute, an answer to anxiety, or independence from wealthy opponents.
- 20:132/q17/p1 It reassures the family that prayer will not cost them their livelihood. — Al-Ālūsī says they should not abandon prayer for earning; Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Islahi tie the assurance to freedom for the Hereafter and the Prophet's task. Qushayrī says the promise consoles the poor when provision is delayed. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KASHSHAF-FULL]
- 20:132/q17/p2 It reverses the tribute expected by earthly rulers and displays divine favor and independence. — Māwardī says God benefits every creature without receiving benefit. Jishumī contrasts worldly kings, Ibn ʿĀshūr taxes and tribute, and Asad a jealous deity; Rāzī says prayer benefits people alone. Study Quran compares the assurance with 51:57–58, where God asks no provision from people. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Razi); IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 20:132/q17/p3 It answers anxiety about where provision will come from. — Biqāʿī says the answer invokes God's boundless dominion and unexpected means; his next note says no provision concern rivals prayer. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:132/q17/p4 It explains that preaching needs no patronage from wealthy opponents. — Islahi says the Prophet and his companions depend on God's support rather than the opponents' wealth. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 20:132/q18 How is 'We ask you no provision' placed in the sentence?  [لَا نَسْـَٔلُكَ رِزْقًۭا · grammar]
- 20:132/q18/p1 It is parenthetical between the prayer command and God's assurance of provision. — Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies this sentence-level placement. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:132/q19 Whom does 'We provide for you' include?  [نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular pronoun only states the Prophet's direct address or also carries family, community, and universal provision.
- 20:132/q19/p1 The Prophet, with his family or all believers included in the promise. — Māwardī and Ṭabrisī extend divine provision to all creatures. Jishumī states provision to the Prophet. Biqāʿī includes the family; Islahi explains the singular pronoun by the Prophet's headship of the Muslim family, encompassing all Muslims, and compares a ruler's duty to provide for the community. He cites Abū Bakr and ʿUmar's concern for their people, including ʿUmar's fear of responsibility for a distant hungry dog. Ibn al-Jawzī-zād pairs the Prophet's and other creatures' freedom from the burden of providing with God's undertaking of provision. [14 notes: MUQATIL; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 20:132/q19/p2 The Prophet is specifically addressed as the recipient of the earlier promised provision. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links the singular promise to 20:131, where the Lord's provision is better and lasting. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:132/q20 How is the fronted 'We' understood?  [نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether fronting conveys exclusivity or emphasis.
- 20:132/q20/p1 It restricts the provision to God. — Al-Ālūsī reports this as one force of fronting; Qushayrī says creatures have no causal power over provision. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; QUSHAYRI]
- 20:132/q20/p2 It adds emphasis to the divine promise. — Al-Ālūsī gives this as the other possibility. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:132/q21 How is narzuquka read?  [نَرْزُقُكَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the qāf is voiced with ḍamma, left still, or merged into kāf, and whether the word boundary permits assimilation.
- 20:132/q21/p1 The qāf has ḍamma and remains distinct. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya and Abū Ḥayyān give this as the majority reading; al-Lawāmiʿ explains Abū ʿAmr's avoidance of qāf-to-kāf assimilation at a stopping point. [3 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,صاحب اللوامح)]
- 20:132/q21/p2 The qāf is still. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes this to a group. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة)]
- 20:132/q21/p3 The qāf is assimilated into the following kāf. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī name Ibn Waththāb, Yaʿqūb, and a group; al-Lawāmiʿ says assimilating readers treat the ending as medial or avoid stopping. Samīn notes Ibn Waththāb usually assimilates only with a following plural mīm, citing 2:21. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(صاحب اللوامح,فرقة وابن وثاب ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR(ابن وثاب); ALUSI-FULL(ابن وثاب ويعقوب)]

## 20:132/q22 Does the assurance of provision license leaving work?  [لَا نَسْـَٔلُكَ رِزْقًۭا نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكَ · law]
turns on: Whether divine provision replaces ordinary livelihood or only protects the priority of prayer.
- 20:132/q22/p1 No; lawful earning continues, and only work that obstructs required prayer must yield. — Rāzī cites 24:37, whose pious traders are not distracted from remembrance; al-Ālūsī says the general address permits leaving earning only when it prevents prayer and clarifies that persistence does not mean praying all day and night. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)+]
- 20:132/q22/p2 Some infer that the command must be Prophet-specific to avoid authorizing everyone to abandon earning. — Al-Ālūsī reports and rejects this inference; his reply limits the effect of the general command to conflicts with prayer. [0 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 20:132/q23 What effects of worship and prayer on provision are described?  [بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ نَّحْنُ نَرْزُقُكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether provision is described as material relief, fixed allotment, witnessing the Provider, or spiritual knowledge.
- 20:132/q23/p1 Prayer and devotion open provision or relieve want, sometimes unexpectedly. — Qushayrī calls prayer a door to provision. Ibn Kathīr cites 65:2–3 and 51:56–58; a divine report via Abū Hurayra promises inner richness and relief from poverty for devotion, with the opposite for neglect. Biqāʿī records al-Tirmidhī's ḥasan gharīb grading of that report. Reports from Ibn Masʿūd and Zayd ibn Thābit contrast focus on the Hereafter, order and inner wealth with scattered worldly cares. Wahb says earlier believers often found relief through prayer. Islahi says only experience conveys prayer's guarantee and cites devout lives. Nasafī adds the maxim that whoever occupies himself with God's work receives God's care. [10 notes: QUSHAYRI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(الله تعالى,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI(وهب بن منبه); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; NASAFI-FULL]
- 20:132/q23/p2 Provision is allotted by God, unaffected by greater or lesser pursuit. — Biqāʿī says neither diligence nor neglect changes each person's eternally allotted share; trusting God yields contentment, while reliance on one's own effort yields toil. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:132/q23/p3 The elect are nourished by witnessing the Provider, beyond material provision. — Qushayrī distinguishes provisions that strengthen souls from witnessing the Provider that strengthens hearts; ordinary people live by provisions, the elect by witnessing God. Realizing the divine 'We' erases distinctions between kinds of provision; prayer heals and nourishes the heart especially when bodily sustenance is delayed. [4 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 20:132/q23/p4 God's provision includes higher knowledge and freedom from dependence on people. — Qāshānī contrasts lower-world perfections and sensory knowledge with spiritual knowledge and holy realities. Bursevī says inward forms of prayer free their practitioners from dependence on people, cites 93:8, and quotes the Prophet's saying that his Lord feeds and gives him drink at night. [3 notes: QASHANI; BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 20:132/q24 What does al-ʿāqibah mean here?  [وَٱلْعَٰقِبَةُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word itself denotes only a good end or can denote any consequence, with the context fixing its value.
- 20:132/q24/p1 The praiseworthy final outcome. — Jishumī, Baghawī, Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān, and Bursevī give the favorable sense; Bursevī says an unqualified ʿāqibah denotes the good reward, Paradise. Qurṭubī says others have outcomes too, but a blameworthy one is rhetorically treated as nonexistent. [7 notes: JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 20:132/q24/p2 Whatever follows an affair, good or bad, though common usage favors good. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives the broad lexical sense, then says piety's result is good. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:132/q25 What is the good outcome promised to piety?  [وَٱلْعَٰقِبَةُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the stated end is specifically Paradise, the wider favorable consequence of piety, an inward transformation, or an image of piety's sole value.
- 20:132/q25/p1 Paradise, the final reward of the God-fearing. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī in Wāḥidī, Wāḥidī's Wajīz, Qurṭubī, Bursevī, Durr, and Study Quran identify Paradise. Yaḥyā cites 43:35; Qurṭubī says a blameworthy outcome counts as no true outcome. Al-Ālūsī reports al-Suddī's narrower identification while allowing a broader good end. [10 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس، السدي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(السدي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدى); STUDYQURAN(JJ)]
- 20:132/q25/p2 A praiseworthy end or ultimate success for the God-fearing, in this life or the next. — Ṭabarī speaks of the final outcome of deeds, not the heedless; Ibn ʿAṭiyya names victory here and mercy hereafter; Ibn Kathīr includes both worlds, ending in Paradise. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Hereafter claim is absolute and worldly good possible. Islahi treats the verse as glad tidings of ultimate success after worldly sustenance; al-Ālūsī says the end is broader than Paradise. Ibn Kathīr cites an authentic dream report: dates from Ibn Ṭāb foretold believers' good worldly outcome, elevation, and sound religion. [24 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; QUSHAYRI; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدى); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 20:132/q25/p3 The true end is inward detachment or disciplined self and limbs. — Sulamī reports Abū ʿUthmān: restraint from all that knowledge does not permit; Bursevī repeats this. Qāshānī reads the worthy end as shedding bodily garments and psychic dispositions. Al-Ālūsī makes the spiritual end two-sided: people of spiritual strength shed psychic vices and acquire spiritual virtues. [4 notes: SULAMI(أبو عثمان); QASHANI; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:132/q25/p4 It is like a market where only piety has value. — Jishumī reports this image as another explanation of why the outcome belongs to piety. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل)]

## 20:132/q26 Who are the people of piety in the ending?  [لِلتَّقْوَىٰ · referent]
turns on: Whether the expression is read through its people or emphasizes piety as the named possessor of the outcome.
- 20:132/q26/p1 Those who fear God and obey, including believers who affirm and follow the Prophet. — Ibn ʿAbbās in Wāḥidī, Baghawī, and Ṭabrisī specifies affirmation, following, and fear of God. Wāḥidī's Wajīz and Bursevī name the Prophet and believers. Ṭabarī contrasts them with those who neither fear punishment nor hope for reward; Asad glosses taqwā as God-consciousness. [11 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD+]
- 20:132/q26/p2 The abstract piety stands for the pious person or people. — Al-Akhfash gives 'people of piety'; Qushayrī notes naming a person by the verbal noun, and Samīn and al-Ālūsī compare 7:128. Nasafī says two annexed nouns are elided; al-Ālūsī adds that omission highlights piety itself. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:132/q26/p3 People of spiritual strength who shed psychic vices and acquire spiritual virtues. — Al-Ālūsī identifies the recipients of the worthy final outcome by this inward transformation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:132/q27 How does the grammar of li-l-taqwā assign the outcome?  [لِلتَّقْوَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the construction is explained through an omitted possessor or the lām's assertion of possession.
- 20:132/q27/p1 An understood 'people of' makes the outcome belong to the pious. — Al-Akhfash, Qushayrī, Samīn, Nasafī, and al-Ālūsī explain the abstract noun for its bearers; Nasafī posits two omitted annexed nouns, while al-Ālūsī says omitting the noun foregrounds piety. Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite 7:128. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش); QUSHAYRI; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:132/q27/p2 The lām marks possession of the good outcome. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says it confirms the intended favorable consequence; the claim is absolute for the Hereafter and possible for worldly good. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 20:132/q28 How does the verse connect with the previous warning about worldly adornment?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the link is practical redirection from wealth, honor in the ordered commands, or evidence for revelation timing.
- 20:132/q28/p1 Prayer and divine provision redirect the Prophet and family from others' wealth. — Al-Wāsiṭī says the warning consoles the poor; Abū Ḥayyān places the family command after timed praise and the warning against unbelievers' wealth. Biqāʿī says zakāt is omitted because the previous verse already discourages attachment. Ibn ʿĀshūr sees ahl answering the preceding mention of spouses, so family finds enjoyment in prayer; Islahi says no patronage from opponents is needed. [6 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 20:132/q28/p2 The prior praise command and this prayer command mark the Prophet's elevated rank. — Al-Ālūsī reads the sequence from glorification to prayer to his direct perseverance as an honor. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:132/q28/p3 The sequence dates the verse early in the Meccan period. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī combines earlier daily prayer and patience commands with the warning against unbelievers' goods to infer early Meccan revelation. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:132/q29 How does the ending connect to the prayer command and other verses?  [وَٱلْعَٰقِبَةُ لِلتَّقْوَىٰ · links]
turns on: Which Qurʾānic context is used to explain the final statement.
- 20:132/q29/p1 It completes the prayer counsel by stating that piety yields the good end. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the sentence is coordinated with God's not asking provision and explains persistence in prayer; it forms a universal proverb. Study Quran points to 7:128, 11:49, and 28:83; Yaḥyā cites 43:35. [4 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN; YAHYA-SALLAM]
- 20:132/q29/p2 It echoes 22:37: what reaches God is piety, not sacrificial meat or blood. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes this connection while stressing God's independence from provision. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:132/q30 How does this passage continue the earlier call to patience?  [* · links]
turns on: The connection can be the continuing patience theme or the earlier command's prayer practice.
- 20:132/q30/p1 The following objection of the opponents continues the call to endure their speech. — Rāzī links the next account to 20:130, after the counsel on patience and prayer. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 20:132/q30/p2 The family command extends the earlier daily prayer and glorification instruction. — Islahi identifies the prayer of 20:130 with the vigilant prayer here. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says hardship reports support a broad understanding of that glorification as prayer. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; TABATABAI]

## 20:132/q31 What event is reported around the revelation of these verses?  [* · reports]
- 20:132/q31/p1 A Jewish lender demanded collateral when the Prophet sought a loan; the Prophet offered armor and these verses were revealed. — Abū Rāfiʿ's report in Ṭabarī includes a guest and links 15:87 with 20:131–132; Wāḥidī's Wajīz says the lender's demand saddened the Prophet. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو رافع); WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 20:132/q32 What is said about the revelation time of this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 20:132/q32/p1 It is Meccan like the rest of the sūra, probably early Meccan. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī finds no authority excluding it as Medinan; he infers an early date from daily prayers, patience, and the warning against unbelievers' wealth. Ibn Masʿūd calls Sūrat Ṭā Hā one of the earliest old sūras. He notes the tension between this timing and the apparent Medinan setting of door-visit reports. [4 notes: TABATABAI(ابن مسعود)]

## 20:132/q33 What lexical information is supplied about ahl beyond the referent?  [أَهْلَكَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the lexical note gives the ordinary and extended sense of ahl or only associated root vocabulary.
- 20:132/q33/p1 Ahl ordinarily denotes blood relatives, with broader figurative uses shown by context. — Ibn al-Jawzī defines the ordinary kinship bond and says context permits extension; Islahi argues for the wider Arabic use and cites Abū Bakr's expression and 19:55. [4 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Abu Bakr (rta))]
- 20:132/q33/p2 The lexical excursus also notes ihāla for melted fat and istaʾhala for eating it. — Ibn al-Jawzī records these cognate words without making them the verse's referent. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 20:132/q34 What prayer-related remedy is drawn from Jonah's case or an epidemic?  [بِٱلصَّلَوٰةِ · interpretation]
- 20:132/q34/p1 Prayer or glorification is invoked as a remedy in distress. — Bursevī quotes Ibn ʿAbbās on Jonah's prayer and 37:143: without it he would have remained in the fish. He reports al-Shāfiʿī's inference that glorification is especially beneficial against an epidemic. [2 notes: BURSEVI(الشافعي)]

## 20:132/q35 What is the rhetorical point of the comparison with night in the ancillary notes?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the comparison takes only universal reach or also a connotation of pursuit.
- 20:132/q35/p1 Night illustrates universal reach without importing its darkness into the comparison. — The Prophet's report says Islam will reach wherever night reaches; Jurjānī explains that an incidental unfavorable feature need not be counted. He compares the sun as apt for a welcome blessing and says even seemingly harsh praise can be hyperbolic rather than insulting. [3 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR(النبي)]
- 20:132/q35/p2 The choice of night may also suggest someone evading a resentful pursuer. — Jurjānī says day and night both reach everywhere, but offers this possible defense of night as the chosen image. [1 notes: JURJANI-ASRAR+]

## 20:132/q36 What general treatments of hamza are described in the ancillary notes?  [* · readings]
turns on: The notes distinguish phonetic practice and exceptions from disputed reports about early pronunciation.
- 20:132/q36/p1 Hamza may be softened by transfer of its vowel or substitution as a long vowel, with stated exceptions. — Itqān says hamza is hard to articulate and Quraysh and the Hijaz commonly softened it, often in Ibn Kathīr, Warsh from Nāfiʿ, and Abū ʿAmr. Warsh's naql transfers the vowel to a preceding still final consonant, as in qad aflaḥa; Yaʿqūb's transmitters from Warsh retain hamza in kitābiyah innī ẓanantu. Abū ʿAmr's ibdāl changes still hamza to a long vowel. The chapter selects four broad kinds but the excerpt details two. [5 notes: ITQAN(أبو عمرو,أصحاب يعقوب عن ورش,نافع)]
- 20:132/q36/p2 Hamza is retained where stillness, difficulty, or ambiguity requires it. — Itqān says jussive and construct-state stillness, or a harder and ambiguous result from dropping hamza, preserve it; moving hamza is always verified. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 20:132/q36/p3 Reports claim the Prophet and early caliphs omitted hamza, or distinguish nabī from nabīʾ. — The Ibn ʿUmar report via Nāfiʿ claims nonpronunciation until later; Abū Shāma rejects it because Mūsā ibn ʿUbayda is weak. An Abū Dharr report says the Prophet corrected nabīʾ; al-Dhahabī calls it anomalous and Ḥumrān untrustworthy. [2 notes: ITQAN(أبو ذر,ابن عمر); ITQAN(أبو شامة,الذهبي)-]

## 20:132/q37 What reading is reported for innaka lā in the ancillary note?  [* · readings]
- 20:132/q37/p1 Nāfiʿ and Abū Bakr read the hamza with kasra; the others use fatḥa. — Nashr gives the two reader groups for this cited phrase. [1 notes: NASHR(نافع وأبو بكر)]
