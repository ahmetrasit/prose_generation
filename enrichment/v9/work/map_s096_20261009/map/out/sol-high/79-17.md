# 79:17: 15 questions

## 79:17/q01 How does the command 'Go' relate to the preceding divine call?  [ٱذْهَبْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the call's content follows directly, requires an implied saying, or is construed through an understood an; the reading with explicit an supports the last analysis, while Ṭabāṭabāʾī considers the supplements unnecessary.
- 79:17/q01/p1 The command directly explains the content of the call. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya says the words explain what Moses heard; al-Qurṭubī says a call is speech, so the text proceeds to its content. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī allow this analysis. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī adopt it; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says further supplied words are unnecessary. [6 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 79:17/q01/p2 An implied 'he said to him' introduces the quoted command. — Al-Nasafī and al-Bursawī supply the divine utterance; al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī list an elided qawl. Al-Bayḍāwī treats the command as quoted speech and understands it to introduce words Moses is to say. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL+; BURSEVI+; NASAFI-FULL+; TABATABAI-]
- 79:17/q01/p3 An explanatory or nominal 'an' is understood before the command. — Al-Samīn says omitted an is supported by ʿAbd Allāh's reading with explicit an; explicit or omitted an may be explanatory or form a verbal noun, meaning that Moses was called to this message. Al-Ālūsī also lists the explanatory analysis. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله); TABATABAI-]

## 79:17/q02 What reading adds a particle before 'Go'?  [ٱذْهَبْ · readings]
- 79:17/q02/p1 A reading attributed to ʿAbd Allāh has an before the command. — Al-Bayḍāwī reports an adhhab as reflecting the speech implied by the address. Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī cite ʿAbd Allāh's explicit an as evidence for the grammatical analysis that supplies the particle. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله)]

## 79:17/q03 How is the transition from Ṭuwā to 'Go' recited?  [ٱذْهَبْ · readings]
turns on: Whether Ṭuwā takes tanwīn, with a masculine valley-name analysis, or lacks it, with a feminine place-name or other diptote analysis; the hamzat al-waṣl drops in either connected reading.
- 79:17/q03/p1 Ṭuwā is read with tanwīn before the command. — Ibn Khālawayh says the resulting kasra resolves adjacent sukūns and refers to his explanation in Ṭā Hā 20:12. Ibn ʿĀshūr names Ibn ʿĀmir, ʿĀṣim, Ḥamza, al-Kisāʾī, and Khalaf and explains tanwīn by taking Ṭuwā as a masculine valley name. Qirāʾāt-ER says connected recitation drops the hamzat al-waṣl of idhhab after Ṭuwā. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); QIRAAT-ER(canonical)]
- 79:17/q03/p2 Ṭuwā is read without tanwīn before the command. — Ibn Khālawayh reports sukūn on its yāʾ. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls this the majority reading and explains it as a feminine place name or by other proposed reasons for diptote treatment. Qirāʾāt-ER says the hamzat al-waṣl of idhhab drops in connected recitation. [3 notes: IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); QIRAAT-ER(canonical)]

## 79:17/q04 What purpose is given for sending Moses to Pharaoh?  [ٱذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes emphasize invitation and cleansing, rescuing believers from among enemies, or confronting a transgressor liable to recompense.
- 79:17/q04/p1 Moses is to invite Pharaoh to faith and purification. — Al-Qushayrī says Moses should invite Pharaoh to believe and cleanse himself of sins. Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls the wording a gracious invitation that a reasonable person would accept. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ recalls Ṭā Hā 20:42–44, where Moses and Aaron are to speak gently in hope that Pharaoh remembers or fears. [3 notes: BINTSHATI+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+; QUSHAYRI+]
- 79:17/q04/p2 Prophets are sent among enemies to bring God's believing friends out from among them. — Ibn al-Faraḥī, reported by al-Sulamī, reads the address as pointing to Pharaoh, who was himself sent to the magicians; such a mission does not honor the enemies. [1 notes: SULAMI(ابن الفرحى)]
- 79:17/q04/p3 Moses must confront a ruler whose transgression merits stern recompense. — Al-Biqāʿī says Pharaoh's transgression made him liable to stern recompense, and the command reassures Moses that Pharaoh has no power over him. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]

## 79:17/q05 Where do the reports place Pharaoh's origin?  [فِرْعَوْنَ · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted reports name Istakhr, Hamadan, or Isfahan; the notes do not decide between them.
- 79:17/q05/p1 Pharaoh came from Istakhr. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this from Mujāhid. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 79:17/q05/p2 Pharaoh was a non-Arab from Hamadan. — Al-Qurṭubī reports this from al-Ḥasan. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]
- 79:17/q05/p3 Pharaoh was from Isfahan. — A further report from al-Ḥasan in al-Qurṭubī calls him Dhū Ẓufr and says he stood four handspans tall. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الحسن)]

## 79:17/q06 What does 'Pharaoh' designate here, and what was this ruler's situation?  [فِرْعَوْنَ · referent]
- 79:17/q06/p1 It is the title used for Moses' Egyptian king. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Pharaoh was the title of Egypt's ancient Coptic kings, borrowed from Hebrew; the Qur'an uses it for Moses' ruler, whereas Joseph's ruler is called king, with 7:103 mentioned. Al-Biqāʿī calls him Egypt's king and says he enslaved Israel and feared an Israelite child; God raised Moses in Pharaoh's house as the means of his ruin, and Moses later fled after killing a man. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 79:17/q07 What is the linguistic sense of 'ṭaghā' and related words?  [طَغَىٰ · meaning]
- 79:17/q07/p1 Ṭughyān is exceeding a limit. — Al-Jishumī describes exceeding limits in corruption, compares baghy, and notes related forms for the act, agent, and transgressors. Al-Bursawī defines crossing the limit. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says Arabic applies it to dangerous overflowing water: 69:11 uses it for water, and al-ṭāghiya in 69:5 is glossed as Thamūd's destructive flood. She says most Qur'anic uses concern disobedience and disbelief, citing 2:15, 6:110, 7:186, 10:11, 23:71, 17:60, 5:64, 5:68, 38:55, and 78:22; she also cites 96:6, 89:11, 17:60, and 18:80 for tyranny, arrogance, and oppression. [7 notes: BINTSHATI+; BURSEVI+; JISHUMI+]

## 79:17/q08 What verbal forms accompany the two forms of the present of 'ṭaghā'?  [طَغَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: The two Arabic usages differ in whether the present and past take the yāʾ or wāw stem.
- 79:17/q08/p1 The present is yaṭghā, with past ṭaghaytu and verbal noun al-ṭughyān. — Al-Naḥḥās assigns these forms to those who say yaṭghā. [1 notes: NAHHAS]
- 79:17/q08/p2 The present is yaṭghū, with past ṭaghawtu. — Al-Naḥḥās assigns ṭaghawtu to those who say yaṭghū. [1 notes: NAHHAS]

## 79:17/q09 In what way had Pharaoh transgressed?  [إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: The notes describe the bounds he crossed in general moral and religious terms or specify his demand for worship.
- 79:17/q09/p1 He exceeded bounds through disbelief, corruption, arrogance, oppression, and disobedience. — Al-Wāḥidī names disbelief; al-Baghawī adds pride and arrogance; al-Ṭabrisī adds rebellion and corruption; al-Qurṭubī names disobedience; al-Nasafī names unbelief and corruption. Al-Bursawī distinguishes disbelief against God from arrogance and enslavement against people, contrasting these with truth toward God and good character toward people. Ibn ʿĀshūr stresses excessive arrogance and compares 78:22. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ notes tyranny, arrogance, and oppression in Qur'anic usage. [8 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL+; BINTSHATI+; BURSEVI+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; NASAFI-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+; TABRISI+; WAHIDI-WAJIZ+]
- 79:17/q09/p2 His transgression reached the claim to be worshiped himself. — Muqātil says Pharaoh made himself an object of worship and called people to worship him. He also reports Ibn Masʿūd's reading that Pharaoh had never worshiped an idol but summoned people to worship himself. [2 notes: MUQATIL(ابن مسعود); MUQATIL+]

## 79:17/q10 What does 'indeed, he transgressed' explain?  [إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether Pharaoh's transgression motivates the sending itself or Moses' obligation to obey the sending command.
- 79:17/q10/p1 It gives the reason for the command to go. — Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī allow this reading; Ibn ʿĀshūr says opening with inna stresses the reason; Ṭabāṭabāʾī adopts it. Al-Biqāʿī connects the command to the recompense deserved by Pharaoh's transgression. [5 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 79:17/q10/p2 It gives the reason Moses must comply with the command. — Al-Bursawī and al-Ālūsī present this as the other possible attachment of the explanation. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 79:17/q11 What does Pharaoh's conduct show about the origin of human acts?  [إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · theology]
- 79:17/q11/p1 Human acts originate from the servants themselves. — Al-Jishumī argues this from Pharaoh's own transgression, denial, turning away, and calling out. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]

## 79:17/q12 What inward condition does Pharaoh's transgression exemplify?  [إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · inward]
- 79:17/q12/p1 Egotism veiled him in selfhood and led to self-worship and tyranny. — Al-Qāshānī, reported by al-Bursawī, says Pharaoh's strength, wisdom, and learning did not prevent his claim to divine attributes and lordship. A Prophetic saying calls the worst person one upon whom Resurrection arrives while alive; al-Qāshānī applies it to Pharaoh's selfhood as a veil in the station of oneness of attributes. [2 notes: BURSEVI(القاشاني,النبي عليه السلام)]

## 79:17/q13 What report describes Moses' concern about going to Pharaoh?  [ٱذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ · reports]
- 79:17/q13/p1 Moses asked why he should go when Pharaoh would refuse, and God commanded him to proceed. — Ṣakhr ibn Juwayriyya is reported by ʿAbd al-Razzāq and al-Qurṭubī. God says twelve thousand angels in heaven seek knowledge of destiny or decree without attaining it; ʿAbd al-Razzāq's note mentions 79:19. [2 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(صخر بن جويرية); QURTUBI-FULL(صخر بن جويرية)]

## 79:17/q14 How does this command connect with the account in Ṭā Hā?  [ٱذْهَبْ إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ إِنَّهُۥ طَغَىٰ · links]
- 79:17/q14/p1 Ṭā Hā gives the parallel mission and adds the manner of invitation. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ points to 20:24 for the same command and declaration of transgression, and to 20:42–44 for Moses and Aaron's instruction to speak gently in hope that Pharaoh remembers or fears. [2 notes: BINTSHATI+]

## 79:17/q15 How does the Moses and Pharaoh account serve the sūra's argument and audience?  [* · links]
turns on: The notes draw different related lessons from the account: consolation and threat, resurrection and final fate, or proof of divine mission and lordship.
- 79:17/q15/p1 It consoles the Prophet and threatens opponents through Pharaoh's fate. — Al-Qurṭubī says Pharaoh was stronger than contemporary unbelievers yet God seized him, and will do likewise to them. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the account consoles the Prophet over rejection and threatens his opponents. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 79:17/q15/p2 It warns resurrection-deniers by showing humanity's final fate. — Al-Biqāʿī describes the sūra's purpose as establishing resurrection and gathering after the preceding sūras' full exposition, with oaths marking a decisive climax. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the account warns polytheists whose denial of resurrection undermines religious law. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL+; TABATABAI+]
- 79:17/q15/p3 Pharaoh's destruction demonstrates Moses' authorized mission and God's lordship over people. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says destruction of Pharaoh and his army proves Moses' divine authorization, which entails God's lordship against the polytheists' claim that lesser lords govern them. [1 notes: TABATABAI+]
