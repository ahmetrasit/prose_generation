# 36:14: 27 questions

## 36:14/q01 How does 'when We sent' connect grammatically to the preceding verse?  [إِذْ أَرْسَلْنَآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the second time clause restates the first or supplies the time of the messengers' arrival.
- 36:14/q01/p1 It is apposition to the earlier 'when they came' in 36:13, explaining the arrival as a sending. — Al-Wahidi calls it apposition; al-Nasafi and Bursevi identify the second idh as apposition to the first. Al-Tabataba'i and Islahi say it elaborates the earlier mention of messengers coming. Al-Razi reports this possible parsing. [6 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; RAZI-FULL]
- 36:14/q01/p2 The time clause modifies 'they came': they came when God sent them. — Al-Razi prefers this as clearer and stresses that the messengers did not arrive on their own initiative. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 36:14/q02 Who sent the messengers, and were they prophets or Jesus's disciples?  [أَرْسَلْنَآ مُّرْسَلُونَ · referent]
turns on: The force of God's 'We sent' and the people's objection to human messengers, weighed against reports that Jesus acted under God's command and whether the miracles prove prophethood.
- 36:14/q02/p1 Jesus sent his disciples under God's command, so God attributes their commission to Himself. — Qatada's account names Antioch and says Jesus sent two disciples then a third; Ibn Jurayj and others say an envoy of God's messenger can be called God's envoy. Al-Thalabi, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Bursevi, Elmalili and Study Quran explain the attribution through God's authorization. Al-Razi says their rejection consoles Muhammad as rejection of him. Al-Alusi answers the objection from their miracles: these may be Jesus's confirming signs through representatives. Al-Tabrisi cites Wahb and Ka'b; al-Ayyashi's variant says Jesus sent his legatee Shimon to rescue the first two. Bursevi infers that on this account the apostles were not prophets. Al-Razi and Bursevi compare an authorized subagent, whom the principal may dismiss but the intervening agent may not. [35 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(كعب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); TABRISI(العياشي,وهب وكعب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قتادة وابن جريج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وغيرهم من الحواريين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة بن دعامة); DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI(التكملة); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Katâde ve gayrıları); TABATABAI(المجمع); STUDYQURAN(Most commentators); ISLAHI-TADABBUR(exegetes); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 36:14/q02/p2 God sent prophets directly; the words describe divine messengers in their own right. — The apparent 'We sent' favors this reading for Ibn al-Jawzi, Elmalili and al-Alusi. Ibn Atiyya, Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi point to the people's objection that the men were mere humans; Islahi adds their sworn claim in 36:16. Al-Alusi cites miracles as an argument proper to prophets, and says one scholar saw them as confirming Jesus's law as Aaron supported Moses. Study Quran cites mursal for Muhammad in 36:3 and 2:252 and its use for other prophets. Al-Tabataba'i's transmitted variant distinguishes God's three envoys from Jesus's later dispatch of Shim'un. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب الأحبار ووهب بن منبه); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وكعب ووهب); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); ELMALILI; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله عليهما السلام); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Another opinion); IBNATIYYA-FULL+; STUDYQURAN+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 36:14/q02/p3 The first two are Moses and Aaron, with Moses the primary messenger and Aaron his assistant. — Islahi says they are the sole pair sent simultaneously to one people in prophetic history, as earlier surahs discuss; his account identifies the supporting third as Pharaoh's believing kinsman and treats the plural claim as collective embassy speech. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:14/q03 When were Jesus's disciples dispatched?  [أَرْسَلْنَآ · reports]
turns on: The transmitted chronology of Jesus's ascension and the disciples' dispersion.
- 36:14/q03/p1 They were sent after Jesus was raised to heaven. — Al-Kalbi dates the event to the raising. Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan add that one made to resemble Jesus was crucified and the disciples dispersed. Al-Qurtubi and Elmalili also place the sending after the raising. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة وغيره); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وغيرهم من الحواريين); ELMALILI(Katâde ve gayrıları)]
- 36:14/q03/p2 God ordered Jesus to send the apostles to cities before his ascension. — Kashf al-Asrar, as reported by Bursevi, says two went to Antioch before the ascension. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]

## 36:14/q04 To which place and people were the messengers sent?  [إِلَيْهِمُ إِلَيْكُم · referent]
turns on: Whether the traditional Antioch account fits the Quranic destruction and the later history of Christianity there.
- 36:14/q04/p1 They went to idol-worshipping Antioch and its people. — Al-Farra, Qatada, tafsir reported by al-Zajjaj, Ibn Kathir and many others name Antioch. Reports call its king a pagan pharaoh, or Antikhus son of Antikhus son of Antikhus. Bursevi and al-Alusi say the inhabitants worshipped idols; Kuranyolu reports an identification as Romans. Their king threatened them in the Ibn Abbas–Ka'b–Wahb report. [20 notes: FARRA; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب الأحبار ووهب بن منبه,قتادة); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); MAWARDI-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قتادة وغيرهم من الحواريين); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق,بريدة بن الحصيب، وعكرمة، وقتادة، والزهري,قتادة بن دعامة); BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR(exegetes); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(müfessirler); ISLAHI-TADABBUR-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 36:14/q04/p2 The town and its inhabitants remain unnamed and cannot securely be identified as Antioch. — Ibn Kathir notes leading scholars' objection. Islahi argues that no such destruction appears in Gospel or biblical history, Antioch embraced Jesus, Jesus's mission was to Israel, and he barred disciples from other nations. Kuranyolu cites Acts 11:19–26, 14:8–28 and 15:22, 35–36: Barnabas, Peter and Paul reached Antioch after the ascension, met no such resistance, and the city became a Christian center; neither Quran nor sound hadith names the town, inhabitants or envoys. Kuranyolu cites Ibn Atiyya's omission of conflicting unreliable accounts. [10 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye)]

## 36:14/q05 Why does the verse say 'to them' rather than 'to the town'?  [إِلَيْهِمُ · rhetoric]
- 36:14/q05/p1 Sending properly takes people as its destination, and naming the people prepares for their rejection. — Al-Alusi notes that rejection also attaches more clearly to people; the explanatory fa resembles the sequence after Moses struck the stone and water gushed forth in 2:60. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:14/q06 Who were the first two messengers?  [ٱثْنَيْنِ · referent]
turns on: Conflicting transmitted names; some commentators decline identification because no Quranic or sound-hadith name settles the matter.
- 36:14/q06/p1 They were Ṣādiq and Ṣadūq. — Ibn Abbas, Ka'b and Wahb give these names; spelling varies as Ṣadūq/Sadduq, and the third is Salūm/Shalūm. The reports through Ibn Ishaq also name Antioch and its king. Al-Nasafi includes their meeting with Habib. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب الأحبار ووهب بن منبه); THALABI(كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب الأحبار ووهب بن منبه); BAGHAWI-FULL(كعب); TABRISI(ابن عباس وكعب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وكعب); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب وكعب الأحبار); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ITQAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

- 36:14/q06/p3 They were Yaḥyā and Yūnus. — Wahb and al-Baydawi report this pair; Bursevi holds it and says the mission was by God's command. Al-Baydawi also reports the unelaborated alternative that they were others. [3 notes: THALABI(وهب); BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 36:14/q06/p4 They were Yūḥannā/John and Būluṣ/Paul. — Wahb and some later reports give this pair; Elmalili's reported combination makes Simon Peter the third. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(وهب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI(Bazıları)]
- 36:14/q06/p5 They were Tūmān and Yūnus. — Muqatil gives these names in his account. [1 notes: MUQATIL]

- 36:14/q06/p7 They were Tūmān and Mānūs. — Al-Tha'labi reports this as Muqatil's naming; the Muqatil notes elsewhere give Tūmān and Yūnus or Būluṣ, so the transmitted second name varies. [1 notes: THALABI(مقاتل)]
- 36:14/q06/p8 They were Simʿān and Yaḥyā. — Al-Naqqash supplies these names. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(النقاش)]
- 36:14/q06/p9 They were Tūmān and Būluṣ. — Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Alusi attribute this pair to Muqatil. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 36:14/q06/p10 They were Nāzūs and Mārūs. — Al-Alusi lists this as another reported pair. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 36:14/q06/p11 They were Moses and Aaron. — Islahi argues that only these two were sent simultaneously to one people and that Moses was primary while Aaron helped. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:14/q06/p12 Their names cannot be established from the Quran or sound hadith. — Kuranyolu says reports of the names and treatment conflict; it cites Ibn Atiyya's decision to omit unreliable material. ## 36:14/q07 Why were two messengers sent together?  [ٱثْنَيْنِ · interpretation]
- 36:14/q07/p1 Each could support and strengthen the other's mission. — Al-Biqa'i says the two mutually supported the call to faith; Islahi's Moses–Aaron reading likewise treats Aaron as Moses's assistant. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 36:14/q08 How did the townspeople first respond to the two messengers?  [فَكَذَّبُوهُمَا · reports]
turns on: The verse states denial; further reports differ on the physical treatment, while later words explain the people's objection to human envoys.
- 36:14/q08/p1 They denied the two, and reports say they beat or imprisoned them. — Ibn Abbas says both beating and imprisonment. Wahb says the king jailed them and ordered one hundred lashes; other accounts say beating or imprisonment. The denial persisted despite signs, according to al-Biqa'i. Kuranyolu notes the reports of treatment conflict; Ibn Atiyya omitted unreliable accounts. Al-Tabari's report has the pagan king threaten the three after the third joined. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب الأحبار ووهب بن منبه,قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل,وهب); NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(المجمع,وهب بن منبه); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(İbn Atıyye); STUDYQURAN(Some,commentators)]
- 36:14/q08/p2 The townspeople denied their divine commission because they were humans like themselves. — Yahya ibn Sallam and al-Qurtubi record the objection; al-Alusi and Ibn Atiyya use it to support a direct prophetic commission, while al-Alusi also records the apostolic response. [5 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب); ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:14/q09 What does fa-ʿazzaznā mean here?  [فَعَزَّزْنَا · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb denotes adding strength or prevailing over opponents, and whether the light and doubled forms carry one sense or distinct senses.
- 36:14/q09/p1 God strengthened and reinforced the pair or their mission by adding a third. — Mujahid glosses it as reinforcement; Ibn Zayd explains that making two into three supplies strength. Ibn Jurayj and Mujahid also say 'increased.' Ibn Qutayba compares strengthening a heart and firm camel flesh; al-Zamakhshari, Abu Hayyan, Bursevi and al-Alusi cite rain compacting earth or flesh hardening. Al-Mawardi and al-Tabrisi derive it from ʿizza, protected might, and cite the proverb about the victor taking spoils; al-Majaz cites al-Namir ibn Tawlab and a verse of Ali for related usage. Al-Jawhari and al-Asma'i cite al-Mutalammis's line for the shared strengthening sense. Al-Wahidi prefers this meaning as fitting the context, while al-Farra and al-Zajjaj allow both pronunciations to mean strengthening. Al-Samarrai specifies that the mission and call gain strength rather than the persons alone. [42 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(ابن مجاهد عن أبيه,السدي); FARRA(عاصم); MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج,مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق والمفسرون,الفراء والزجاج); DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة); QURTUBI-FULL(الأصمعي,الجوهري); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد وابن قتيبة); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الحسن,مجاهد); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 36:14/q09/p2 God overpowered or subdued the opponents by means of the third messenger. — Abu Ali and al-Mubarrad distinguish the light form from the doubled form, comparing 38:23; Jarir's line and the proverb 'whoever prevails takes' attest the sense. Al-Nahhas derives it from ʿāzanī fa-ʿazaztuhu and rejects a derivation from simple ʿazza yaʿizzu; al-Samin also cites the proverb. Al-Tabari says this sense fits the context poorly. Al-Farisi reports another explanation that reverses the form-to-sense assignment, giving strengthening to the light form and overcoming to the doubled, with 38:23 as evidence. Al-Biqa'i takes the result as bringing victory to God's cause after denial. Qiraat-er allows the light form to mean became mighty, overcame, transitively overpowered or made rare. [22 notes: NAHHAS(عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(بعضهم,جرير); THALABI(طلحة بن مصرف وعاصم عن حفص); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد,المبرد وأبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(قيل); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم في رواية المفضل عن أبي بكر); TABRISI(أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو بكر والمفضل عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL(أبو بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، وأبو حيوة، وأبو بكر، والمفضل، وأبان); SAMIN-DURR(أبو بكر); BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); QIRAAT-ER; TAB-FULL-; WAHIDI-BASIT-]

## 36:14/q10 How is the verb read, and what does each reading imply?  [فَعَزَّزْنَا · readings]
turns on: Transmission of doubling versus lightening, and whether lightening changes the meaning or only the verb form.
- 36:14/q10/p1 Most readers double the zāy: fa-ʿazzaznā, usually explained as reinforcing or strengthening. — Al-Tabari prefers it by the readers' consensus and because 'overcame' fits poorly. Ibn al-Jawzi lists Ibn Kathir, Nafi', Abu Amr, Ibn Amir, Hamza, al-Kisa'i and Hafs from Asim. Abu Ubayd instead explains his doubled recitation as overcoming; al-Farisi reports a similar reversed semantic distinction. Al-Zajjaj records both forms without excluding either. [12 notes: ZAJJAJ; FARISI-HUJJA(عاصم في رواية أبي بكر والمفضل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيد,المبرد وأبو علي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(جمهور القراء); TABRISI(أبو بكر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن قتيبة,ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وابن عامر وحمزة والكسائي وحفص عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(أبو بكر); TAB-FULL+]
- 36:14/q10/p2 A light-zāy reading, fa-ʿazaznā, is transmitted, chiefly from Abū Bakr and al-Mufaḍḍal from ʿĀṣim; its meaning is disputed. — Al-Tha'labi also attributes it to Talha ibn Musarrif and Hafs from Asim, while other sources put Hafs with the doubled reading. Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi add al-Hasan, Abu Haywa and Aban; al-Durr cites Mujahid. Al-Farra, al-Baghawi, al-Jawhari and al-Alusi allow the same strengthening sense; Abu Ali, al-Mubarrad, al-Nahhas, al-Tabrisi and others understand 'overcame,' supported by 38:23, Jarir and the proverb. Ibn Atiyya phrases its result as their prevailing over the messengers, whereas most overcomer explanations make God overcome the opponents. Qiraat-er says Form I may signify simple strength, victory, or rarity. [26 notes: FARRA(عاصم); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(عاصم); FARISI-HUJJA(بعضهم,عاصم في رواية أبي بكر والمفضل); THALABI(طلحة بن مصرف وعاصم عن حفص); WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج,المبرد وأبو علي); BAGHAWI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); IBNATIYYA-FULL(عاصم في رواية المفضل عن أبي بكر); TABRISI(أبو بكر,أبو علي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو بكر والمفضل عن عاصم); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم,الجوهري); BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL(أبو بكر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحسن، وأبو حيوة، وأبو بكر، والمفضل، وأبان); SAMIN-DURR(أبو بكر); NASHR(أبو بكر); BIQAI-FULL(أبو بكر عن عاصم); DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي,الحسن); QIRAAT-ER]

## 36:14/q11 What does the omitted object of fa-ʿazzaznā refer to?  [فَعَزَّزْنَا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the verb describes support of the two, support of the message, or victory over those who denied it.
- 36:14/q11/p1 It refers to the first two messengers, supplied by the preceding clause. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Nasafi say omission draws attention to Shimon and the subtle strategy by which truth prevailed; al-Zamakhshari compares omitting those judged when saying a ruler judged justly. Al-Baydawi says the context supplies the object and focuses on the helper. Abu Hayyan says the third reinforced the two and all prevailed through argument and tactful invitation. Bursevi and al-Alusi likewise recover the pair; al-Samin extends the omission to both readings. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 36:14/q11/p2 The mission or call, rather than the messengers personally, is what was strengthened. — Al-Zajjaj says the message was reinforced; al-Samarrai explicitly distinguishes the call from the persons. Al-Wahidi prefers this explanation for the passage. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق والمفسرون); SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; WAHIDI-BASIT+]
- 36:14/q11/p3 On the overcoming sense, the understood object is the opponents or the disbelievers. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Baydawi treat the third as the means of victory; Abu Ali says his proof defeated the opponents. Al-Samin's general statement instead calls the omitted object the two messengers even for the 'overcome' reading. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(أبو بكر); NASAFI-FULL(أبو بكر); ALUSI-FULL(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR]

## 36:14/q12 Was the third sent after the pair or had he been sent earlier?  [فَعَزَّزْنَا بِثَالِثٍۢ · reports]
turns on: Narrative order and whether 'third' can designate a man dispatched earlier but introduced after the pair.
- 36:14/q12/p1 The third was sent after the first two were rejected, to support them. — The sequence is explicit for Yahya ibn Sallam, Qatada, al-Tabari, al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi and others; Wahb says the third received news of the imprisoned pair and set out. Al-Samarrai explains why the verse does not simply say three were sent at once. Ibn al-Jawzi objects that a messenger sent before both would be called first, and al-Nahhas says the apparent wording favors a later third; al-Tabataba'i rejects transmitted versions that jar with Quranic sequence. [17 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي); ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب); QURTUBI-FULL(وهب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 36:14/q12/p2 The third had been sent before the pair; the narration mentions him later and speaks retrospectively of his strengthening. — Al-Farra identifies this earlier messenger as Shimon and says the strengthening came from what he taught the pair. Al-Nahhas reports his view but says an earlier man should have been introduced as 'the third' with the article. Ibn al-Jawzi rejects it because exegetes place the third later and because one sent first would not be third. [4 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); NAHHAS-; IBNJAWZI-ZAD-]

## 36:14/q13 Who is the third?  [بِثَالِثٍۢ · referent]
turns on: Incompatible name traditions and, for Islahi and al-Qashani, different identifications of the entire parable.
- 36:14/q13/p1 He was Shimʿūn/Simon, also called al-Ṣafā, al-Ṣakhra or al-Ṣaffār, Jesus's disciple or successor. — Muqatil's form Shamʿūn/Shaman and many other reports identify Simon; Ibn Abbas calls him al-Ṣafā. Al-Qurtubi calls him chief of the apostles and says he entered the royal court in disguise. Bursevi calls him their leader and Jesus's successor after the ascension. Al-Durr mentions a messengerless interval of 434 years. In one report God inspired him with the pair's plight; in others Jesus sent him. Al-Farra instead puts Simon before the pair. Elmalili's reported John–Paul pair makes Simon Peter third. [16 notes: MUQATIL; FARRA; THALABI(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل,وهب); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); ELMALILI(Bazıları); TABATABAI(وهب بن منبه)]
- 36:14/q13/p2 He was Salūm/Shalūm. — The Ibn Abbas–Ka'b–Wahb or Ka'b reports pair him with Ṣādiq and Ṣadūq; spelling varies. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب الأحبار ووهب بن منبه); THALABI(كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب ووهب); BAGHAWI-FULL(كعب); TABRISI(ابن عباس وكعب); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ITQAN(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس)]
- 36:14/q13/p3 He was Būluṣ/Paul. — Shuʿayb al-Jubbāʾī, Shu'ba and other reports pair him with Simon and John; al-Alusi notes a spelling with ṣād or sīn. Sa'id ibn Jubayr also names Paul, though he calls Simon son of John the helper and says all three were killed. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(شعيب الجبائي); TABRISI(شعبة); DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير,شعيب الجبائي); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس)]
- 36:14/q13/p4 He was Yūnus/Jonas. — Al-Mawardi attributes Yūnus to Shuʿayb; Study Quran reports Jonas as another identification; al-Alusi says Wahb and Ka'b also transmit Yūnus. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(شعيب); ALUSI-FULL(إبن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Some)]
- 36:14/q13/p5 He was Pharaoh's believing kinsman who defended Moses, though not a prophet in the technical sense. — Islahi identifies him with the believer of 40:28 and the speaker in 40:26–45. He hid his faith until Pharaoh proposed killing Moses, then defended Moses before the court; his family status gave his plea force. His arguments and courage earn him collective inclusion with Moses and Aaron, as a member of one embassy can speak with the others; Islahi compares him with Abu Bakr among Muhammad's followers. [10 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:14/q13/p6 In an inward reading, the third is intellect, joining spirit and heart. — Al-Qashani says intellect shares the self's interests and succeeds in influencing it toward what heart and spirit seek, whereas the body initially rejects spirit and heart because of its darkness and lack of affinity. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 36:14/q14 How does Arabic form and use ordinal expressions such as 'a third'?  [بِثَالِثٍۢ · grammar]
- 36:14/q14/p1 Ordinals use the fāʿil pattern, with count, gender and compound-number forms governed by Arabic usage. — Sibawayh cites ثاني اثنين (9:40) and ثالث ثلاثة (5:73) for ordinals two through ten in annexation; feminine fāʿila takes a feminine ending, while feminine counted numbers three through ten omit hāʾ. خامس أربعة means making four into five and is rare though analogical; Arabic does not say ثاني واحد. Eleven through nineteen have open compound parts, as حادي عشر and ثالث عشر; feminine حادية عشرة follows its feminine number pattern. He treats خامس خمسة عشر and حادي أحد عشر, explaining when حادي declines, and notes a less common expanded ثالث عشر ثلاثة عشر whose final count is often omitted. Masculine ordinals dominate mixed-gender counts; خامس أربع for four women plus a man is scarcely used; higher patterns include رابع ثلاثة عشر and بضعة عشر/بضعة عشرة inflect like تسعة عشر/تسع عشرة. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(بعضهم)]

## 36:14/q15 Is 'with a third' also read with a definite article?  [بِثَالِثٍۢ · readings]
- 36:14/q15/p1 The transmitted wording is 'with the third' (bi-l-thālith). — Al-Farra attributes it to Abd Allah and explains definiteness by prior mention of the messengers, comparing 'the two dirhams' after an earlier reference. Ibn Atiyya identifies Ibn Masud's codex; al-Samin and al-Alusi also report Abd Allah's reading. Al-Nahhas says this would have been the natural wording for al-Farra's theory that the third had been sent first. [4 notes: FARRA(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); SAMIN-DURR(عبد الله); ALUSI-FULL(عبدالله)]

## 36:14/q16 What purpose or effect did sending a third serve?  [فَعَزَّزْنَا بِثَالِثٍۢ · interpretation]
turns on: The literal mission, Islahi's Moses reading, or al-Qashani's inward reading determines the kind of support.
- 36:14/q16/p1 It reinforced the first two, increased their number, and made the proof and mission stronger. — Abu al-Aliya explicitly calls the third a stronger proof against the town; Ibn Zayd says making three is reinforcement. Ibn Atiyya says it completed the proof. Al-Biqa'i says God brought might to His cause after their weakening by denial. Al-Samarrai says rejection explains why support came later rather than all three at once. Al-Jishumi says one messenger may permissibly be strengthened by another; messengers sent together may share a law, while successively arriving messengers each require a new miracle and renewed law. [12 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن جريج); JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); BIQAI-FULL; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 36:14/q16/p2 The third's fearless defense of Moses is the assistance celebrated by God's wording. — Islahi calls the defender of 40:26–45 courageous and unmatched outside prophets and the righteous. Pharaoh proposed killing Moses and charged him with changing religion; Moses sought protection; the believer argued that a true messenger's warnings could fall on them, invoked earlier ruined peoples, Joseph's signs and the Day of Flight, and warned against tyranny. Pharaoh claimed sound guidance and ordered Haman's tower. The believer's royal status and concealed faith made his public defense forceful. [13 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR(Moses,Pharaoh,a believer from the Pharaoh’s family)]
- 36:14/q16/p3 The third's intellect mediated the inward call of spirit and heart to the self. — Al-Qashani says the body rejects spirit and heart because of opposed light and darkness; its bad omen is an aversion to discipline and spiritual struggle, and its stoning and torment of them are bodily impulses and animal appetites. [4 notes: QASHANI]

## 36:14/q17 What is reported about the envoys' encounter with Ḥabīb?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports differ over how many believed and over the later fate of Habib and the envoys.
- 36:14/q17/p1 The envoys met Ḥabīb the shepherd or carpenter, cured his sick son and others, and he believed. — Al-Zamakhshari says they met the carpenter tending goats near the city; Habib requested a sign, and the sick, blind and leprous, including a son ill for two years, were healed. Al-Qurtubi calls Habib the shepherd of a few sheep and identifies him with the man from the far end of town; his son is described as ill or possessed. Al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi and al-Tabataba'i recount the son's healing; al-Nasafi says he rose after their touch. News of many healings reached the king. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(المجمع)]
- 36:14/q17/p2 Only Ḥabīb al-Najjār believed; he left his work, later urged the townspeople to follow the messengers, and was killed. — Muqatil calls him an Israelite and former polytheist. Ibn Atiyya says a running believer was killed while the people persisted; Sa'id ibn Jubayr says Habib had concealed his faith, all three envoys were killed, and Habib was then killed in a well at al-Rass. Ibn Ishaq's version has Habib run from the city's far gate when the king and people resolve to kill the envoys. Kuranyolu treats his likely murder as part of the warning. [5 notes: MUQATIL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABATABAI(ابن إسحاق); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 36:14/q18 How did the envoys and their helper present signs to the king?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported sign narratives differ in the person revived and in what the signs establish about the envoys' status.
- 36:14/q18/p1 They healed a blind boy and revived a child dead seven days, exposing the idols' powerlessness. — Al-Zamakhshari describes Simon's disguise and court friendship, his challenge to the king's idol, the blind boy's missing eyes restored through prayer and two clay pellets, and revival after a seven-day death. The revived boy warned of seven valleys of Fire and said a handsome youth interceded for the three in heaven. Al-Qurtubi says the envoys prayed openly while Simon prayed secretly and the boy testified to God, Jesus and the envoys, naming Simon best; al-Tabrisi, al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi tell related versions. Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi say the king admitted his idols neither hear, see, harm nor benefit. Abu Hayyan cites healing the blind and lepers and raising the dead as signs of clear delivery. Al-Biqa'i says messengers are accompanied by credible signs and cites Tufayl ibn Amr's requested light first on his forehead, then his whip. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(طفيل بن عمرو الدوسي); TABATABAI(وهب بن منبه)]
- 36:14/q18/p2 Shamʿūn revived a girl from her grave before the people. — Muqatil says he struck the grave with his foot, called her by God's permission, and she emerged testifying that the emissaries were sent. She warned that acceptance brings forgiveness and refusal punishment, predicted disbelief, then returned to her grave at her request. [3 notes: MUQATIL(الجارية)]
- 36:14/q18/p3 Miracle reports may be Jesus's confirming wonder through the apostles, rather than proof they were prophets. — Al-Alusi records this answer to the claim that only prophets properly have miracles; the apostles may have spoken as direct envoys or representatives. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]

## 36:14/q19 How did the king and townspeople finally respond?  [* · reports]
turns on: Conflicting narratives of the king's conversion and whether any townspeople survived as believers.
- 36:14/q19/p1 The king and some people believed; Gabriel's cry killed those who refused. — Al-Zamakhshari and al-Baydawi say Simon's argument moved the king; al-Qurtubi says the revived boy's testimony led Simon to identify himself and the king and many believed. Al-Qushayri says the king believed but his people did not. Al-Nasafi includes Simon and the messengers among those who believed. Al-Tabataba'i's al-Ayyashi variant says the revived man was the king's son and king and people believed, though the destruction of unbelievers is recounted by other versions. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(القشيري); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; TABATABAI(أبو جعفر وأبو عبد الله عليهما السلام)]
- 36:14/q19/p2 The king remained an unbeliever and joined his people in persecuting the envoys; the unbelievers were destroyed. — Wahb and Ka'b say the king and people continued torture and killing. Bursevi prefers this account: if king or subjects had believed they would have aided the envoys as Habib did, especially with clear proof and a population that follows its king. Ibn Ishaq also says the king resolved to kill them; Ibn Atiyya says the townspeople persisted and a heavenly cry silenced them. [4 notes: BURSEVI(وهب بن منبه وكعب الأحبار); TABATABAI(ابن إسحاق); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BURSEVI+]

## 36:14/q20 How did the envoys understand the townspeople's language?  [* · reports]
turns on: Different reported means of overcoming the language barrier.
- 36:14/q20/p1 God put them to sleep, angels carried them to Antioch, and they awoke speaking its language. — Al-Qurtubi links this wonder to God's strengthening Jesus with the Holy Spirit in 2:87 and 2:253. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 36:14/q20/p2 An angel gave them luminous wine enabling comprehension of the local language. — Bursevi reports this from Kashf al-Asrar, alongside the claim that Jesus was told to dispatch apostles to cities before his ascension. [1 notes: BURSEVI(كشف الأسرار)]

## 36:14/q21 Who spoke the declaration 'we are sent to you'?  [فَقَالُوٓا۟ إِنَّآ إِلَيْكُم مُّرْسَلُونَ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural speech is simultaneous or a narrative summary of successive declarations.
- 36:14/q21/p1 All three messengers addressed the people together, or one spoke with the others' agreement. — Al-Tha'labi, al-Baghawi, al-Nasafi and Bursevi take the plural as the three. Al-Alusi allows plural attribution when one speaks for a concurring group. Bursevi says denial of the two amounts to denial of the third because their message is one. Islahi allows collective embassy language despite different ranks, making Moses primary, Aaron assisting and the believer preaching Moses's message. [10 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 36:14/q21/p2 The first two spoke before the third arrived, and the third spoke after them. — Yahya ibn Sallam reports this speaking sequence, although the verse gives a collective declaration after mentioning the reinforcement. [1 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM]

## 36:14/q22 What commission did they declare to the people?  [إِنَّآ إِلَيْكُم مُّرْسَلُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: The breadth of mursalūn as 'messengers' or 'message bearers' and the reported content of their call.
- 36:14/q22/p1 They were sent to call the people to worship the one God, abandon idols, and accept guidance and faith. — Al-Tabari says they renounced idols; Abu al-Aliya says worship God without partner. Al-Zamakhshari and al-Qurtubi report their replies to the king that God created him and his idols, does as He wills, rules as He chooses, and heals by His permission. Ibn Atiyya says the call was to guidance and faith; al-Biqa'i says the two announced faith in God. Al-Zajjaj and al-Qurtubi say their duty was clear delivery, not control of belief; Abu Hayyan says they entrust their truth to God. Muqatil's revived girl warned of forgiveness for acceptance and punishment for rejection. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(أبو العالية); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو العالية); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو العالية); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; MUQATIL(الجارية)]
- 36:14/q22/p2 The line can also be rendered 'we are message bearers unto you.' — Study Quran notes this translation for the declaration here and in 36:16. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 36:14/q23 Why is this first declaration emphasized with innā, and how does it differ from the later claim?  [إِنَّآ إِلَيْكُم مُّرْسَلُونَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the first utterance is truly initial information or already answers a request or denial.
- 36:14/q23/p1 The first claim begins with innā; after stronger denial, the later assertion adds lām and, in the fuller exchange, an oath. — Al-Zamakhshari and Abu Hayyan say the first is initial information and the later lām responds to denial. Al-Samin says plain denial receives inna whereas emphatic denial receives inna and lām; he distinguishes initial, requestive and denial contexts, and reports al-Kindi's point that the forms have distinct meanings, recalling his failed attempt to imitate the Quran at al-Ma'ida. Al-Suyuti lists other Quranic emphasis particles and says emphasis rises with doubt or denial; for Jesus's envoys, the later oath and lām follow stronger rejection. Bursevi says prior rejection explains the group's emphasis. [11 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو العباس الكندي,الشيخ); ITQAN; BURSEVI]
- 36:14/q23/p2 Calling the first speech merely 'initial' misclassifies a context in which the audience already seeks or denies proof. — Al-Samin rejects al-Shaykh's initial-versus-denial account for confusing requestive and initial contexts; he still holds that inna and the later lām mark different degrees of denial. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 36:14/q24 What does this account teach about successive messengers and evidence?  [* · theology]
- 36:14/q24/p1 A messenger can be supported by another; simultaneous messengers may share a law, while successive arrivals require renewed miracle and law. — Al-Jishumi draws the permissibility of reinforcing one messenger with another and distinguishes shared versus successive commissions. Al-Biqa'i says God sends no messenger without signs that can bring belief, directly or through another; he cites Tufayl ibn Amr's light as an example of a requested sign. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL(طفيل بن عمرو الدوسي)]

## 36:14/q25 What agency rule is drawn from God's attribution of the dispatch?  [أَرْسَلْنَآ · law]
- 36:14/q25/p1 An authorized subagent is the principal's agent: the principal may remove him but the intervening agent may not. — Al-Razi and Bursevi apply this rule to Jesus's sending under God's command. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 36:14/q26 Does an apostolic interpretation conflict with the saying that no prophet came between Jesus and Muhammad?  [مُّرْسَلُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether 'prophet' in the saying includes confirmation of an earlier law or only an independent new law.
- 36:14/q26/p1 On the apostles reading, the disciples were not prophets during or after Jesus's life. — Bursevi infers this from their status as Jesus's envoys and cites the Prophet's saying, 'There is no prophet between me and him,' taking 'him' as Jesus. [2 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]
- 36:14/q26/p2 The saying may exclude only a prophet bringing an independent law, leaving a prophet who confirms prior law possible. — Bursevi explicitly allows this narrower sense of the cited saying. [1 notes: BURSEVI(النبي)]

## 36:14/q27 How does the verse connect to its surrounding account and to Muhammad's audience?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the passage is read chiefly as a historical warning, a typological confirmation of Muhammad, or a discourse sequence.
- 36:14/q27/p1 The town's rejection is a warning and consolation for Muhammad and his Meccan opponents. — Al-Alusi says the sending completes the parable and consoles the Prophet. Islahi says Quraysh should see that rejecting messengers, blaming signs, treating warning as bad luck and threatening them leads to destruction. Kuranyolu says it warns Muhammad's deniers and strengthens followers; Study Quran says Muhammad is told to give Quraysh an example of people who treated earlier bearers as they treat him. Al-Razi adds that rejection of envoys sent with God's leave is rejection of the one who sent them. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 36:14/q27/p2 The third also suggests Muhammad's apostleship confirming and strengthening the missions of Moses and Jesus. — Elmalili prefers this representational aim and cites 2:97, 3:3 and 5:48. [1 notes: ELMALILI+]
- 36:14/q27/p3 The narrative unfolds through implied questions and answers throughout the passage. — Al-Jurjani reads the arrival, denial, threats and responses through this discourse pattern, comparing 15:57–58 and tracing it across 36:13, 15–17 and 19–20. [1 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL]
