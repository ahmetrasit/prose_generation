# 74:14: 12 questions

## 74:14/q01 Who is the person for whom God prepared these favors?  [لَهُۥ · referent]
turns on: Whether the passage addresses the named Quraysh notable alone, a class of affluent opponents, or the human condition more broadly.
- 74:14/q01/p1 Al-Walīd ibn al-Mughīra, the leading Quraysh notable. — Muqātil, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Qurṭubī and others name al-Walīd. Al-Jishumī reports Ibn ʿĀmir's identification as al-Walīd al-Makhzūmī, wealthy with sons and called al-Waḥīd. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursawī, al-Ālūsī and Elmalılı record al-Waḥīd and/or Rayḥānat Quraysh, explaining these titles by his precedence, leadership or prosperity. The Study Quran adds learning and eloquence. Iṣlāḥī says the passage applies to him, though its scope is wider. [24 notes: MUQATIL; IBNHISHAM(الوليد بن المغيرة); TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:14/q01/p2 The wealthy, high-status opponents of the message generally, including al-Walīd. — Iṣlāḥī names affluent leaders of Makkah and Ṭāʾif and says the passage describes their shared mentality; al-Walīd remains an instance of it. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ISLAHI-TADABBUR+]
- 74:14/q01/p3 The human recipient of God's gift of exceptional potentialities. — Asad explains the wide scope of life here as potentialities beyond those of other living beings; his note and rendering use the same account. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:14/q02 What does God's tamhīd for him mean or provide?  [وَمَهَّدتُّ لَهُۥ تَمْهِيدًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Which concrete gift the broad image of preparing and spreading denotes: material goods, standing, practical ease, lifespan, land, security, or human capacity.
- 74:14/q02/p1 God spread and eased his life, livelihood and circumstances, preparing things comfortably for him. — Al-Ṭabarī gives an easy worldly life and reports Sufyān's 'spread out'; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Wāḥidī al-Wajīz, Ibn ʿAṭiyya citing Sufyān, and al-Ṭabrisī stress broad comfort or complete provision. Al-Ṭabrisī also reports al-Ḥasan's well-ordered harmonious circumstances and an alternative gloss of ease. Al-Dāmghānī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya give the basic sense of establishing or preparing; al-Jishumī pairs tamhīd with tawṭiʾa, tadhليل and tashīl. Al-Qurṭubī compares making a child's bed; al-Ālūsī says the root is smoothing and preparing and is figurative for abundance; Ibn ʿĀshūr derives intensive mahhada from leveling ground and removing what hurts someone lying there. Ibn Kathīr specifies varieties of wealth, furnishings and worldly provision. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ includes well-ordered affairs. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(سفيان); THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL(سفيان); TABRISI(الحسن,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 74:14/q02/p2 God furnished him with extensive wealth and offspring, including wealth layered upon wealth. — Muqātil adds good fortune and elsewhere says the wealth was fully expanded. Mujāhid is reported by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī and al-Durr as naming wealth and children; al-Wāḥidī also cites Muqātil and al-Kalbī on expanded wealth. Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Thaʿlabī, al-Kalbī in al-Baghawī, and Mujāhid in al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī liken wealth piled in layers to spread bedding. Ibn Kathīr includes furnishings; Elmalılı says many means were prepared beyond wealth and sons. The Study Quran reports great wealth and seven or ten sons. [19 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي,مجاهد,مقاتل); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(مجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); ALUSI-FULL(مجاهد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
- 74:14/q02/p3 God established his leadership, prestige and authority among his people. — Ibn Shajara is reported by al-Māwardī. Al-Kashshāf, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, Bursawī and al-Ālūsī say rank completes wealth as the worldly ideal or perfection in worldly eyes; al-Bayḍāwī adds that he was deemed worthy of precedence. Al-Kashshāf's prayer for sustained tamhīd and al-Rāzī's idiom mean prolonged prestige and discretion. Al-Biqāʿī describes gradual elevation, possession of people's hearts, removal of obstacles and elders seeking his counsel. Elmalılı adds grandeur and good fortune; Iṣlāḥī explains that wealth and sons paved the way to dignity and influence. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ includes status. [15 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(ابن شجرة); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 74:14/q02/p4 God made his affairs easy to conduct and gave him freedom and influence to act. — Al-Qushayrī speaks of managing activities freely. Al-Wāḥidī explains the blessing 'may God continue your tamhīd' as extended ease and agency; al-Jishumī says preparation enables dealings. Al-Ṭabrisī reports facilitation of managing affairs. Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān place his discretionary action within comfort in his town. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the preparation a metaphor for easy affairs, influential speech and no difficult request. [7 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:14/q02/p5 God granted abundant livelihood and a long life. — Al-Wāḥidī and al-Rāzī report this as the view of some commentators. Al-Baghawī and Ibn al-Jawzī combine long life with easy, comfortable living; al-Biqāʿī also includes prolonged life in his account of extensive fortune. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(بعض المفسرين); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(بعض المفسرين); BIQAI-FULL]
- 74:14/q02/p6 God extended his territory or dominion from Yemen to Syria. — Al-Qurṭubī attributes ample territory to Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid; Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī and al-Ālūsī transmit Ibn ʿAbbās's Yemen-to-Syria extent. Al-Biqāʿī adds that his worldly fortune was completed and links his standing to his titles. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 74:14/q02/p7 God settled him safely and comfortably in his homeland. — Al-Māwardī offers security from fear and from being dislodged as a further possibility. Al-Qurṭubī says he lived securely in his town at discretion; Abū Ḥayyān says he remained settled there and could consult at ease. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 74:14/q02/p8 God gave human life wide scope through exceptional potentialities. — Asad says these potentialities exceed those of other living beings, in both his note and his rendering. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:14/q03 What is the grammatical function of تَمْهِيدًا?  [تَمْهِيدًۭا · grammar]
- 74:14/q03/p1 It is an accusative cognate verbal noun emphasizing 'I prepared.' — Al-Naḥḥās calls it an emphatic verbal noun. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the accusative cognate object and says its indefiniteness magnifies the preparation; emphasis need not exclude metaphor. [2 notes: NAHHAS; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:14/q04 What does the emphatic wording تَمْهِيدًا add?  [تَمْهِيدًۭا · rhetoric]
- 74:14/q04/p1 It presents the preparation and comfort as thorough and great. — Al-Biqāʿī says tamhīd stresses the full extent of what God prepared. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the indefinite cognate object magnifies it and that such emphasis is compatible with metaphor. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:14/q05 How does the verse ending settle into the preceding statement?  [تَمْهِيدًۭا · rhetoric]
- 74:14/q05/p1 The ending is prepared by the statement as a whole and completes its sense naturally. — Al-Zarkashī calls this tamkīn: removing the ending would disturb meaning or comprehension. He distinguishes four kinds of fitting Quranic endings—tamkīn, tawshīḥ, īghāl and taṣdīr—by cue placement and whether the cue is verbal or semantic. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 74:14/q06 What accusations against Muhammad or the Quran are reported in this setting?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reported wording and scope of opponents' allegations: al-Walīd's chosen public label or the broader allegations of human composition and borrowing.
- 74:14/q06/p1 Al-Walīd recommended calling Muhammad a magician because the message divided families. — Ibn Hishām says Quraysh sought one account for pilgrims; al-Walīd rejected soothsayer, madman, poet and sorcerer as accurate descriptions before choosing 'sorcerer' as a public label. Al-Jishumī's account similarly has him reject soothsayer, poet and madman and choose 'magician' because the Quran separated families. [2 notes: IBNHISHAM(الوليد بن المغيرة); JISHUMI]
- 74:14/q06/p2 Meccan opponents alleged the Quran was human fabrication, borrowed ancient lore or earlier writings dictated to Muhammad. — Bint al-Shāṭiʾ says the Quran records these repeated charges and reasons against their falsehood; she cites 68:10–15, 68:44–47, 25:4–10, 17:87–96, 6:7–9, 6:25–26 and 34:43–45. [1 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]

## 74:14/q07 How many sons does the reported account give al-Walīd?  [* · reports]
turns on: The alternative reported counts, seven and ten.
- 74:14/q07/p1 Seven sons. — The Study Quran reports seven or ten sons as alternative counts in its account of his wealth, learning and eloquence. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 74:14/q07/p2 Ten sons. — The Study Quran reports seven or ten sons as alternative counts in its account of his wealth, learning and eloquence. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]

## 74:14/q08 Why are these blessings recalled before the rebuke?  [* · interpretation]
- 74:14/q08/p1 They expose ingratitude and make disobedience despite God's gifts more culpable, preparing the warning of judgment. — Muqātil says the man still hoped for more after expanded wealth and resisted God's signs. Al-Jishumī says abundant favors aggravate disobedience. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the reminder preludes reproach and threats of worldly harm and afterlife punishment. Iṣlāḥī says he became conceited rather than grateful, that affluent leaders mistook wealth for divine favor and expected more, and that 74:11–17 warns such disbelievers of severe Judgment Day. Kur'an Yolu grounds gratitude in reason and justice and calls denying God, polytheism and injustice after material and spiritual gifts grave ingratitude. [8 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 74:14/q09 Can unbelievers receive genuine blessings from God?  [* · theology]
- 74:14/q09/p1 Yes; God grants blessings to unbelievers, and worldly conditions and goods are among His blessings. — Al-Jishumī states this against the Mujbira and explicitly counts worldly circumstances and possessions as blessings from God. [2 notes: JISHUMI; JISHUMI+]

## 74:14/q10 How does this passage connect with 68:10?  [* · links]
- 74:14/q10/p1 Its account of favors contrasts with 68:10's depiction of the man's vices, if that verse also refers to al-Walīd. — Ibn ʿĀshūr treats the identification in 68:10 as possible rather than certain; its own context warns against the man's harm and treachery. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:14/q11 How is this surah related to the preceding al-Muzzammil?  [* · links]
- 74:14/q11/p1 Al-Muddaththir is its counterpart, with essentially the same central themes and a similarly meaningful name. — Iṣlāḥī draws this surah-level pairing and the relation between the two names. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:14/q12 What inward reading is given to the man's provision and its end?  [* · inward]
- 74:14/q12/p1 The man symbolizes the solitary evil soul, whose dark powers and dominion are overcome by spiritual light. — Bursawī reports the Najmī allusion: al-Walīd is the solitary evil soul; wealth, bad traits, followers and prestige signify its sins and dark natural powers. Abū Bakr as the hidden, ʿUmar as spirit, ʿUthmān as secret and ʿAlī as heart erase its darkness, defeat its deeds and followers, and end its rule. [2 notes: BURSEVI(التأويلات النجمية)]
