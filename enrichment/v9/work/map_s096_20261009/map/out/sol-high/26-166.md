# 26:166: 17 questions

## 26:166/q01 What does tadharūn mean and how is its verb used?  [وَتَذَرُونَ · meaning]
- 26:166/q01/p1 It means leaving or discarding; its old perfect form is unused. — Jishumī equates yadharu with yadaʿ and says their past forms fell out of use because taraka sufficed and Arabic dislikes initial wāw. Bursevī adds that discarding suggests little regard for the object and says no perfect form is attested. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BURSEVI]

## 26:166/q02 What did they turn to instead of lawful intercourse with women?  [وَتَذَرُونَ مَا خَلَقَ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُم مِّنْ أَزْوَٰجِكُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the description includes anal intercourse with women as well as men, or singles out men, and whether the men were foreign.
- 26:166/q02/p1 They turned to anal intercourse with men and women. — Mujāhid specifies men's and women's rear openings in Mujahid, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, and al-Durr; Ibn Mujāhid's transmission and the Study Quran also report anal intercourse with both sexes. Al-Nasafī says they practiced it with wives too; Bursevī and al-Ālūsī say the partitive reading suggests this, while al-Ālūsī observes that the wording leaves the lesser offense against women implicit. [9 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(أبو مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(some)]
- 26:166/q02/p2 They turned from women's front openings to men's backsides. — Mujāhid is transmitted this way by al-Wāḥidī, al-Baghawī, and Ibn al-Jawzī; ʿIkrima is reported by al-Durr. Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies that pursuing males is the sinful act in view. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(عكرمة)]
- 26:166/q02/p3 The behavior involved foreign men. — The Study Quran reports this as another interpretation of the passage. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(some)]

## 26:166/q03 Did leaving their spouses mean abandoning women altogether?  [وَتَذَرُونَ أَزْوَٰجِكُم · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'leaving' describes their conduct during intercourse or a general avoidance of women.
- 26:166/q03/p1 No; they neglected lawful intercourse with wives during the sinful act. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya explicitly distinguishes abandonment during the act from total abandonment. Al-Biqāʿī says they turned to males while falsely treating 'leave' as total abandonment; Lot rejects that evasion. Al-Wāḥidī's al-Wajīz describes leaving wives without intercourse. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BIQAI-FULL+; IBNATIYYA-FULL+]
- 26:166/q03/p2 The context suggests that they avoided women. — The Study Quran takes this as the apparent sense of the context. Al-Iṣlāḥī says attraction to women disappears as a consequence of their perversion; al-Elmalılı describes Lot rebuking the men for abandoning their God-given spouses. [3 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN+; IBNATIYYA-FULL-; BIQAI-FULL-]

## 26:166/q04 What does God-created provision from their spouses refer to?  [مَا خَلَقَ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُم مِّنْ أَزْوَٰجِكُم · referent]
turns on: Whether mā names spouses generally or the lawful part of them.
- 26:166/q04/p1 The lawful female sexual organ or vaginal intercourse. — Muqātil, al-Suddī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Wāḥidī, and Mujāhid's reports specify women's genitals or front openings; al-Ṭabarī calls them lawful. Ibn Abī Ḥātim's Mujāhid explains 'suitable' as vaginal intercourse. Al-Ḍāmghānī says God made those parts lawful; Ibn al-Jawzī transmits al-Zajjāj's explanation of Ibn Masʿūd's reading. Al-Biqāʿī describes the act as intercourse with women. Al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī specify the lawful part under the partitive analysis. [17 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,مجاهد); TAB-FULL; IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:166/q04/p2 The wives God created for them, as women generally. — Al-Wāḥidī's al-Wajīz says God created their wives for them. Al-Bayḍāwī and Bursevī say God created spouses for enjoyment; al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, and al-Ālūsī allow the general female referent when min is explanatory. Al-Ālūsī also reports a reading with no implied intercourse: they simply disregard God's created provision. [8 notes: WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 26:166/q05 What does khalaqa mean here and in its cited Arabic uses?  [خَلَقَ · meaning]
turns on: The contextual sense of making provision versus the root's broader senses of creation and measured formation.
- 26:166/q05/p1 Here it means made, appointed, or made suitable and lawful. — Al-Ḍāmghānī gives 'made and permitted' for the women's sexual parts. Ibn al-Jawzī reports exegetes' 'made or appointed' for spouses; Mujāhid's 'made suitable' is reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim and al-Durr. [4 notes: DAMGHANI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 26:166/q05/p2 The root can denote bringing into being or shaping by measure and ordered design. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives creation and creation according to estimate and arrangement. He quotes Ibn Qutayba's 'estimation' with Zuhayr's poem about executing what was planned, and Ibn Fāris's example of measuring a hide for a waterskin. He adds usages for clouds becoming even (ikhlawlaqa), a level ground trace (makhlūlaq), and a fully formed man (mukhtalaq). [6 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,ابن قتيبة)]

## 26:166/q06 How can min azwājikum be construed?  [مِّنْ أَزْوَٰجِكُم · grammar]
turns on: Whether min explains the female kind or selects the lawful part, and whether intercourse must be supplied by ellipsis.
- 26:166/q06/p1 Min is explanatory: what was created refers to women as a kind. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī all allow this. Al-Ālūsī says the interpretation implies omitted wording for intercourse with their vulvas. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:166/q06/p2 Min is partitive: what was created means the lawful sexual part of wives. — Al-Rāzī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī, Abū Ḥayyān, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, and al-Ālūsī all allow this. Several infer anal intercourse with wives as well. Abū Ḥayyān says referring to the created organ requires an omitted noun for intercourse; al-Ālūsī says this analysis needs one omitted term. Al-Ālūsī adduces Ibn Masʿūd's 'mā aṣlaḥa lakum' reading in support. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 26:166/q06/p3 The phrase can be read without supplying an omitted noun. — Al-Ālūsī reports a view that they are blamed for abandoning and disregarding what God created for them before considering intercourse. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]

## 26:166/q07 What alternate reading is transmitted for mā khalaqa lakum rabbukum?  [مَا خَلَقَ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُم · readings]
- 26:166/q07/p1 Ibn Masʿūd reads mā aṣlaḥa lakum rabbukum, 'what your Lord made suitable for you.' — Al-Ṭabarī attributes the reading to ʿAbd Allāh; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Ālūsī name Ibn Masʿūd. Ibn al-Jawzī reports al-Zajjāj's explanation that it denotes women's sexual organs; al-Ālūsī takes it as support for the partitive interpretation. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); ALUSI-FULL(ابن مسعود)]

## 26:166/q08 What does bal do in the rebuke?  [بَلْ · rhetoric]
- 26:166/q08/p1 It shifts from describing their conduct to a judgment of transgression and further fault. — Al-Burhān says bal moves from pursuing males and neglecting wives to calling them transgressors; it does not retract those acts. He explains that after affirmative speech bal may correct a mistaken human claim or move to a more apt matter, while divine usage may refute claims or move from a past matter to what deserves mention; he cites 21:5, 27:66, 38:8 and relates 7:81. He rejects al-Basīṭ's and Ibn Mālik's general claim that Qur'anic bal only marks transition, because it can also retract and correct. Abū Ḥayyān calls this a further charge. [5 notes: BURHAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURHAN+]

## 26:166/q09 What does ʿādūn mean?  [عَادُونَ · meaning]
- 26:166/q09/p1 Transgressors who go beyond lawful or just limits. — Muqātil and Ibn Jurayj give the basic gloss. Yaḥyā says beyond God's command; al-Ṭabarī and al-Baghawī specify crossing from permitted to prohibited sexual relations. Al-Wāḥidī says from lawful to unlawful and obedience to sin, and in al-Wajīz calls it utmost injustice. Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Ibn al-Jawzī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Biqāʿī, Bursevī, al-Ālūsī, al-Elmalılı, and al-Iṣlāḥī describe exceeding limits, injustice, or proper human conduct. Jishumī equates ʿādī with muʿtadī and notes possible derivation from enmity or swift running. [19 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL(ابن جريج); WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن جريج); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:166/q10 How broad is the charge of transgression?  [بَلْ أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ عَادُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the charge is confined to the grave sexual offense, extends to all sin, describes abnormal desire, or stresses an entrenched disposition or emotive rebuke.
- 26:166/q10/p1 It condemns their sexual offense as a grave crossing of bounds. — Al-Ṭabarī says they cross from God-permitted sexual relations to prohibited ones. Al-Rāzī and al-Bayḍāwī allow that the enormity alone makes them especially deserving of the label; al-Nasafī likewise singles out the grave act. Al-Biqāʿī says Lot rejects their evasion because they turned from wives to males; al-Ālūsī says they pursued what was not created for sex and left what was. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:166/q10/p2 They transgress in all sins, with this act included. — Al-Rāzī offers this as one alternative. Bursevī says the bounds are exceeded in all sins, and al-Ālūsī says the omitted complement of 'transgress' may be general. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:166/q10/p3 They exceed the bounds of desire beyond other people and even animals. — Al-Bayḍāwī gives this as an alternative sense, while al-Ālūsī reports a version saying beyond people and most animals and allows a specific complement of excess in sexual desire. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 26:166/q10/p4 Transgression is a settled trait that keeps them beyond God's bounds. — Al-Biqāʿī calls it an entrenched disposition and says Lot's rebuke exposes that rooted condition. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 26:166/q10/p5 The wording conveys sorrow over conduct beyond human nature. — Al-Iṣlāḥī reads the address as expressing yearning and sorrow at their crossing the limits of human nature. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:166/q11 What does placing antum first add?  [أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ عَادُونَ · rhetoric]
- 26:166/q11/p1 The direct pronoun singles them out and emphasizes their notorious transgression. — Abū Ḥayyān says the address makes them alone conspicuous for exceeding limits; al-Ālūsī transmits Abū Ḥayyān's explanation. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 26:166/q12 What ruling on anal intercourse with wives or enslaved women is drawn from this verse?  [مَا خَلَقَ لَكُمْ رَبُّكُم مِّنْ أَزْوَٰجِكُم · law]
turns on: Whether the verse's restriction to the lawful part of spouses establishes prohibition; the notes also point to 2:223 and a hadith.
- 26:166/q12/p1 It is forbidden, and intercourse with wives that way is a major sin. — Al-Nasafī says the verse forbids it with wives and female slaves and strongly faults those permitting it. Bursevī derives the same ruling from the partitive reading and cites a hadith that one who has anal intercourse with a woman is disavowed from what was revealed to Muḥammad and will not be looked upon by God; he reports some Companions calling the offender an unbeliever. Al-Ālūsī identifies prohibition as the established Sunni position and points to 2:223. [5 notes: BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام,بعض الصحابة); ALUSI-FULL(أهل السنة)+; BURSEVI+; NASAFI-FULL+]
- 26:166/q12/p2 Anal intercourse with wives is permitted. — Al-Ālūsī reports this alternative and refers to his fuller discussion at 2:223; al-Nasafī expressly argues against those who permit it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL-]

## 26:166/q13 What penalty is assigned for male same-sex intercourse?  [بَلْ أَنتُمْ قَوْمٌ عَادُونَ · law]
turns on: The jurists differ over whether the act receives discretionary punishment, stoning in all cases, or the fornication rule.
- 26:166/q13/p1 Discretionary taʿzīr applies, without a fixed ḥadd. — Bursevī reports Abū Ḥanīfa's ruling and says his two disciples disagree. [1 notes: BURSEVI(أبو حنيفة)]
- 26:166/q13/p2 Both participants are stoned, married or unmarried. — Bursevī reports Mālik's ruling. [1 notes: BURSEVI(مالك)]
- 26:166/q13/p3 The penalty follows the legal rule for fornication. — Bursevī attributes this to al-Shāfiʿī and Aḥmad. [1 notes: BURSEVI(الشافعي وأحمد)]

## 26:166/q14 What does this rebuke imply about human freedom and divine creation of acts?  [وَتَذَرُونَ خَلَقَ عَادُونَ · theology]
turns on: Whether moral blame and 'leaving' require self-originated acts, or whether divine knowledge and the origin of motives make that inference fail.
- 26:166/q14/p1 People must originate their acts for blame, praise, commands, and prohibitions to be coherent. — Al-Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār argues that 'leaving' presumes an ability to act otherwise, whereas what God creates would be imposed; blaming divinely created transgression would resemble blaming skin color. Al-Rāzī summarizes that case and notes this story's special force beyond the commands and blame in the stories of Moses, Abraham, and Noah. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(القاضي عبد الجبار)+; RAZI-FULL-]
- 26:166/q14/p2 Divine knowledge and the cause of a decisive motive defeat that free-will argument. — Al-Rāzī answers that nonoccurrence of acts God knows will occur is impossible without making divine knowledge ignorance, so a command to avoid them would demand the impossible. A choice needs a preponderant motive: if a person originates that motive its causes regress infinitely; if God originates it, compulsion follows. He says these two arguments defeat al-Qāḍī's case. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 26:166/q15 What wider effects are attributed to their abandonment of wives?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Al-Iṣlāḥī names both a change in attraction and a social consequence, without treating them as rival explanations.
- 26:166/q15/p1 Their attraction to women disappears. — Al-Iṣlāḥī says leaving God-created wives reflects blindness and this consequence of the perversion. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 26:166/q15/p2 Family becomes an object of mockery and society becomes a herd ruled by unnatural desires. — Al-Iṣlāḥī presents these as social effects of such conduct. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 26:166/q16 How did Lot's people answer his direction toward wives?  [* · reports]
- 26:166/q16/p1 They threatened to expel him if he continued preaching. — Ibn Kathīr places the threat after Lot forbade their approach to males and directed them to wives. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 26:166/q17 How does this passage connect to other Lot passages?  [* · links]
- 26:166/q17/p1 Parallel passages also discuss Lot's people and illuminate the argument here. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Lot's preceding argument rests on demonstrative proof also reflected in the parallel wording at 29:29. The Study Quran directs readers to 7:80–81, 11:77–79, and 29:28–29. [2 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]
