# 53:13: 18 questions

## 53:13/q01 Who is the subject of 'saw him'?  [رَءَاهُ · referent]
turns on: Which pronoun's antecedent is supplied from the surrounding account.
- 53:13/q01/p1 Muhammad is the one who saw. — Al-Wahidi identifies the subject pronoun as Muhammad; Ibn Ashur relates it to 'your companion' in 53:2. This is assumed in the accounts of his seeing Gabriel or God. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:13/q01/p2 Gabriel saw God while Muhammad was at the Sidra. — Al-Qushayri explicitly makes Gabriel the seer and God the seen. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]

## 53:13/q02 Whom did Muhammad see again?  [رَءَاهُ · referent]
turns on: The antecedent of the object pronoun, the earlier vision, and whether the cited verses and reports allow seeing God.
- 53:13/q02/p1 Gabriel, in the form in which he was created. — Al-Nahhas prefers Gabriel because the preceding description calls him mighty in strength; Ibn Ashur identifies the object with Gabriel in the earlier scene. Aisha says the Prophet himself identified Gabriel and cites 81:23; she argues from 6:103 and 42:51 against seeing God. Reports place his two true-form appearances at the horizon and Sidra; most scholars are cited by al-Thalabi and al-Wahidi. Al-Thalabi adds that God has no location or descent and no prior God-vision preceded the Night Journey. Ibn Kathir gives Gabriel reports and says the vision was on the Night Journey; Elmalili connects it to Gabriel's lesser reach. [55 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود,الربيع,عائشة,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(عائشة); THALABI(عائشة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أكثر العلماء); WAHIDI-WAJIZ(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن مسعود وعائشة,عائشة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن مسعود وعائشة ومجاهد والربيع); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن مسعود); RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن مسعود، عائشة، مجاهد، الربيع); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن مسعود,ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد),عائشة,قتادة والربيع بن أنس,مجاهد); BIQAI-FULL(عائشة); DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,إبراهيم,ابن مسعود,عائشة,مرة الهمداني); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(birçokları); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ASAD-NOTES; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Aj,many commentators); NAHHAS+; THALABI(عائشة وأكثر العلماء)+]
- 53:13/q02/p2 God, whom Muhammad saw a second time. — Ibn Abbas connects the vision with 53:11 and reports God drawing near in 53:9–10. Ka'b contrasts Muhammad's two visions with Moses' two divine conversations; Ikrima invokes Abraham's friendship and Moses' speech as parallels. Ibn Abbas answers 6:103 by comparing partial sight of God to seeing the sky without encompassing it. Proponents cited by Study Quran locate two special self-disclosures above the Throne and at the Sidra. Al-Tabatabai prefers the divine referent as heart-sight. [48 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عكرمة,كعب); MAWARDI-FULL(كعب); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس والحسن وعكرمة,ابن عباس,عكرمة عن ابن عباس,غير مسمى); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وكعب الأحبار); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس,كعب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس، كعب الأحبار); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو صالح، السدي,ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,البغوي,كعب); BIQAI-FULL(ابن برجان,ابن عباس,ابن مرجان); DURR-FULL(أبو ذر,أنس,ابن عباس,عكرمة,كعب وابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Aj,many others,those who maintain the vision of God); TABATABAI+; TAB-FULL(عائشة)-; NAHHAS(عائشة)-; THALABI(عائشة,عائشة وأكثر العلماء)-; BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة)-; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة)-; BIQAI-FULL(عائشة)-; DURR-FULL(عائشة)-]

## 53:13/q03 How, if at all, was God seen?  [رَءَاهُ · theology]
turns on: How the reported visions and Abu Dharr's light report are understood, and the force of verses invoked against physical divine sight.
- 53:13/q03/p1 Muhammad saw God with his heart, twice. — Ibn Abbas's account is transmitted by Muslim through Abu al-Aliya according to al-Qurtubi and by Ibn Kathir; Abu Dharr's reports expressly exclude eyesight. Al-Tabatabai makes both descents heart-sight. Al-Biqai transmits the same twice-with-heart report. Al-Tirmidhi's Ibn Abbas report says divine eyes cannot be perceived when God manifests in His own light, graded hasan gharib. Ibn Kathir construes an unspecified Ibn Masud vision as heart-sight. [18 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو ذر,أبو صالح، السدي,ابن عباس,ابن مسعود); BIQAI-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(أبو ذر,ابن عباس); TABATABAI]
- 53:13/q03/p2 Muhammad saw God with his physical eyes. — Al-Baghawi names Anas, al-Hasan and Ikrima; al-Durr transmits an explicit Ibn Abbas eye-vision. Al-Biqai's Ibn Marjan calls the first vision heart-sight preparation and the second eye-sight. A report has God place a hand between Muhammad's shoulders, its coolness reaching his chest and knowledge of heaven and earth following. Ibn Kathir disputes both the soundness of physical-eyesight Companion reports and the attribution to those three authorities. [6 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أنس والحسن وعكرمة,ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(البغوي); BIQAI-FULL(ابن مرجان); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 53:13/q03/p3 There were two God-visions, one by eye and one by heart. — Ibn Abbas is reported with both faculties; Ibn Marjan specifies an initial heart-sight followed by the second eye-sight. [4 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); BIQAI-FULL(ابن مرجان); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(many others)]
- 53:13/q03/p4 Muhammad did not see God; he saw Gabriel. — Aisha calls the contrary a grave lie and cites 6:103 and 42:51; her fuller denial also cites 31:34 concerning the unseen future and 5:67 concerning conveying revelation. Ibn Kathir says Aisha asked after the Night Journey and was not told of a God-vision; he rejects the proposal that she was mistaken or merely addressed according to her understanding. [14 notes: TAB-FULL(عائشة); NAHHAS(عائشة); THALABI(عائشة,عائشة وأكثر العلماء); BAGHAWI-FULL(عائشة); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن خزيمة,عائشة); BIQAI-FULL(عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 53:13/q03/p5 The Prophet answered the question of seeing God by referring to light. — Abu Dharr reports both 'I saw a light' and 'there was light; how could I see Him?' Muslim transmits variant wordings. Ahmad was troubled by this hadith; Ibn Khuzayma questions its chain between Abdullah ibn Shaqiq and Abu Dharr. Ibn al-Jawzi locates Abu Dharr's question before the Night Journey; Ibn Kathir calls this timing very weak because Aisha asked after it. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو ذر); DURR-FULL(أبو ذر)]
- 53:13/q03/p6 The question of God-vision is left open. — Sa'id ibn Jubayr neither asserted nor denied it; al-Baghawi records a group disagreeing over heart versus eye. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); BAGHAWI-FULL(غير مسمى)]

## 53:13/q04 What does 'nazlatan ukhra' denote?  [نَزْلَةً أُخْرَىٰ · meaning]
turns on: Whether nazla's single-occurrence form is taken as time alone, a real descent, or an ellipsis for another vision.
- 53:13/q04/p1 Another occasion or time of seeing. — Al-Farra, al-Zajjaj, Ibn Atiyya and others gloss it as another time; al-Mawardi says it repeats what Muhammad saw first. Al-Samin says 'other' presupposes an earlier vision. Al-Nasafi explains 'once more' by tadmīn. Al-Elmalili says the adverbial reading points to another descent, whereas a circumstantial reading may mean another time. [9 notes: FARRA; NAHHAS(الفراء); MAWARDI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:13/q04/p2 One other descent, with descent and approach implicit. — Jishumi, Ibn Ashur and al-Tabatabai identify a noun of a single occurrence from descending. Ibn Ashur links 'another' to 'he approached and descended' in 53:8. Al-Baydawi and Alusi say this word conveys descent and nearness, unlike plain 'time' or marra; Samarra'i likewise contrasts nazla with marra and connects it to returning below Gabriel's limit. Al-Elmalili gives a final descent after multiple prayer journeys. [12 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(müfessirînin çoğu); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]
- 53:13/q04/p3 Another act of vision. — Abu al-Baqa permits a verbal-noun expansion as ru'ya ukhra; al-Samin and Alusi question that expansion, and al-Elmalili reports it as a possible construction. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); ELMALILI; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]

## 53:13/q05 How is 'nazlatan' parsed?  [نَزْلَةً · grammar]
turns on: How an accusative noun naming one descent relates syntactically to the seeing verb.
- 53:13/q05/p1 An accusative adverbial of time or occasion. — Al-Zamakhshari treats a noun of one occurrence as an adverb; al-Samin identifies this as al-Farra's school rather than the Basrans'. Al-Nasafi calls it accusative like 'once' by tadmīn. Al-Biqai and Bursawi derive the temporal function from the form meaning one descent. Al-Baydawi calls it the accusative of a single occurrence; Ibn Ashur also permits a temporal omitted noun. [9 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(müfessirînin çoğu); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:13/q05/p2 A verbal noun in circumstantial position: seeing him while he descended. — Al-Nahhas prefers this; al-Hawfi, Ibn Atiyya and Makki are cited for it by Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, Alusi and Elmalili. Al-Qurtubi gives the paraphrase 'saw him descending another time.' Al-Baydawi offers it as an alternative if Gabriel descended. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الحوفي وابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR(مكي); ALUSI-FULL(الحوفي وابن عطية); ELMALILI(Hûfî ve İbn Atıyye); NAHHAS+]
- 53:13/q05/p3 An emphatic or cognate accusative verbal noun, expanded as another occasion or vision. — Abu al-Baqa offers marra ukhra or ru'ya ukhra; al-Samin and Alusi object specifically to the vision expansion. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو البقاء); SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)]
- 53:13/q05/p4 A locative or temporal adverb through an omitted preposition or noun. — Ibn Ashur allows an elided preposition, a locative adverb, or an omitted noun meaning 'at the time of another descent.' [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 53:13/q06 Whose descent is meant?  [نَزْلَةً · referent]
turns on: Whether the action belongs to Muhammad, Gabriel, or a figurative divine approach; the Sidra and repeated prayer journeys are the cited tests.
- 53:13/q06/p1 Muhammad descended on his return from the heavenly ascent. — Al-Wahidi, al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi link it to repeated ascents and descents while requesting fewer prayers. Al-Razi reports Muhammad returning to Gabriel after passing beyond him, and suggests 'another' because of repeated prayer journeys. Elmalili prefers Muhammad's descent: Gabriel could not pass the Sidra without burning, and Muhammad later met him there. Al-Tabatabai makes the Prophet descend at the Sidra and see God with his heart. Samarra'i argues the word is more specific than 'time' and indicates descent back from beyond Gabriel's reach. [15 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(جبريل عليه السلام); ELMALILI+]
- 53:13/q06/p2 Gabriel descended to Muhammad in his true form. — Al-Nahhas's circumstantial parse makes Gabriel the descending one. Al-Razi offers Gabriel's two descents in his own form; al-Tabatabai reports a descent to take Muhammad heavenward. Al-Baghawi and al-Tabrisi speak of descent from heaven. Kuran Yolu calls this one reported reading; Elmalili objects that Gabriel could not descend from above the Sidra. [10 notes: NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI(birçokları); TABATABAI(قيل); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI-]
- 53:13/q06/p3 God drew near in mercy and favor, without physical movement. — Al-Razi treats nonphysical divine nearness as a proposed sense, even without the servant seeing God. Elmalili cites his explanation and relates Moses' request in 7:143 to lifting veils of majesty. Kuran Yolu says the scene's descent and nearness are figurative divine acts and attributes, without specifying how. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:13/q06/p4 God physically moved or relocated in a descent. — Al-Razi explicitly identifies and rejects this proposed reading; Elmalili repeats his rejection. Kuran Yolu judges a reading of God's descent weak. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-; ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî)-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 53:13/q06/p5 Descent marks the worldly ascent because it had a return, unlike the ascent of the hereafter. — Al-Razi calls descent the opposite of ascent and uses the worldly return to explain the designation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]
- 53:13/q06/p6 The Prophet descended from the Real to the station of spirit and saw Gabriel again. — Al-Qashani gives this inward account of the return and vision. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 53:13/q06/p7 The descent is imaged as leaving the mount of passion and vehicle of the self. — Al-Razi offers this figurative image specifically when the seen one is God. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 53:13/q07 Where and when was this second vision?  [رَءَاهُ نَزْلَةً أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The report of the Night Journey, the location of the Sidra, and which descent the verse describes.
- 53:13/q07/p1 At the Sidra during the Night Journey and Ascension. — Al-Zajjaj and al-Thalabi locate it at the Lote Tree; al-Nasafi says at al-Ma'wa. Al-Biqai places it after ascent through the stations where pen scratching was heard, shortly before the Hijra in prophethood's thirteenth year. Tafsir al-Qummi places the Sidra in the seventh heaven. Al-Tabatabai's reported rival reading makes Gabriel descend to take Muhammad to heaven; his own view has Muhammad descend at the tree. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(عائشة,عائشة وأكثر العلماء); BAGHAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,عائشة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(those who maintain the vision of God)]
- 53:13/q07/p2 One Gabriel sighting was in the heavens and one in descent filling the space between heaven and earth. — Aisha says the latter filled the horizon; Ibn Kathir reports the Prophet saying Gabriel's created form was seen only twice, one appearance descending. Al-Baghawi summarizes the two as one on earth and one in heaven. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(عائشة); NAHHAS(عائشة); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 53:13/q07/p3 The second sight followed the Prophet's repeated ascents concerning the prescribed prayers. — Ibn Abbas and later explainers describe repeated requests for reduction; al-Wahidi suggests a God-vision on one descent, whereas Bursawi and Elmalili have Gabriel waiting at the Sidra and seen on the return. [8 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:13/q08 Where or when was the earlier vision?  [أُخْرَىٰ · reports]
turns on: The varying reports of the first encounter, including its earthly site or precreation setting.
- 53:13/q08/p1 At the highest horizon or on earth, before the later heavenly sight. — Al-Thalabi places the first appearance at the highest horizon and the second at Sidra; al-Baghawi calls them earthly and heavenly. The Gabriel vision with six hundred wings is linked to 53:7 by al-Qurtubi. Ibn Kathir's unusual report says Gabriel first filled the horizon at Muhammad's request, then was seen again on the ascent. [6 notes: THALABI(عائشة وأكثر العلماء); BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد)); IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:13/q08/p2 At Mount Hira during the first revelation, before the Ascension. — Study Quran gives the two true-form appearances as Hira and the Ascension. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN]
- 53:13/q08/p3 At Ajyad, paired with the later sight at Sidra. — Aisha is reported by Ibn Kathir and al-Durr as naming Ajyad and Sidra; the order is not stated consistently in these summaries. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(عائشة); DURR-FULL(عائشة)]
- 53:13/q08/p4 At Muhammad's beginning when God created him. — Sahl's account places vision at the beginning. Another account has him as light in a column of light a million years before creation, already possessing innate faith, unveiling the unseen through the unseen and worshipping before God. [2 notes: TUSTARI]

## 53:13/q09 What appearance accompanied the Gabriel vision?  [رَءَاهُ · reports]
turns on: Which transmitted description of the visible sight is followed.
- 53:13/q09/p1 Gabriel's true form had six hundred wings, spanning or filling the horizon and shedding jewels. — Ibn Masud's reports describe pearls, rubies and other marvels falling from feathers; Ibn Kathir grades one chain good and strong and another hasan. Aisha's Ajyad/Sidra report adds six hundred wings. Bursawi says Gabriel was at the Sidra while Muhammad made prayer journeys. An unusual report graded strange by Ibn Kathir says Gabriel prostrated and the great signs were his creation. [7 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود,ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد)); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود,عائشة); BURSEVI]
- 53:13/q09/p2 Gabriel's leg or legs bore pearls like drops on greenery. — Ibn Masud's two accounts describe the pearled legs, one locating him at Sidra. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن مسعود)]
- 53:13/q09/p3 Gabriel appeared green and adorned with hanging pearls. — Murra al-Hamdani says Gabriel came in his true form only twice and gives the green and pearl detail. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(مرة الهمداني)]
- 53:13/q09/p4 A great light was seen at the Sidra. — Qatada gives this report without identifying the light as Gabriel or God. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(قتادة)]

## 53:13/q10 What does the repeated vision establish?  [وَلَقَدْ رَءَاهُ أُخْرَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: What significance is drawn from the second, heavenly sight and its emphatic wording.
- 53:13/q10/p1 It reinforces the certainty and reality of revelation and of the vision. — Al-Razi says the heavenly sight excludes the small residual possibility, despite certain angelic knowledge, that the earthly figure was a jinn: jinn and humans cannot be at the Sidra above the sixth heaven. He adds that unanswered doubts do not cancel certainty and treats the preceding challenge as asking how they dispute a sight without doubt. Al-Baydawi and Alusi say the later sight removes doubt. Elmalili takes the present tense of 'sees' as ongoing vision confirmed by the following oath. Islahi says the scene cannot be fantasy and that the angel is God's trustworthy revealer. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 53:13/q10/p2 It shows Muhammad's exceptional rank, including his ascent beyond Gabriel's reach. — Ibn Ashur says the oath lām and qad stress the unusual sight and ascent. Elmalili and Kuran Yolu connect the later descent with Muhammad outranking Gabriel. Gabriel reportedly said advancing a finger or inch farther would burn him. [5 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT(جبريل عليه السلام)]
- 53:13/q10/p3 It presents a higher unseen experience that calls for faith and restraint in describing it. — Ibn Ashur describes progress from denied earthly sight to the higher realm. Kuran Yolu warns against pressing sensory reason past its limits; if read as Mi'raj, its descriptions of appearance, horizon, descent and nearness are figurative divine acts and attributes. [3 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 53:13/q11 How do the vision and descent relate to divine location or nearness?  [رَءَاهُ نَزْلَةً · theology]
turns on: Whether spatial words are applied to God, to Muhammad's place, or to the angelic encounter.
- 53:13/q11/p1 The Sidra locates Muhammad's vision, not God; divine nearness need not be spatial. — Al-Razi rejects locating God at the Sidra, allowing Muhammad to be there when he saw; he calls nonphysical nearness mercy and favor. He rejects literal divine relocation. Al-Thalabi argues that God has no place and does not descend. Kuran Yolu understands the spatial language as figurative divine acts and attributes. Al-Razi prefers Muhammad as the one at Sidra even on the Gabriel reading. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; THALABI(عائشة وأكثر العلماء); ELMALILI(Fahreddin Râzî); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; RAZI-FULL+]
- 53:13/q11/p2 Words for being 'at' or 'near' can express literal proximity in this sighting. — Al-Burhan compares the Prophet's sighting at the Lote Tree in 53:13–15 with Joseph's master at the door in 12:25 to illustrate literal ʿind and lada; the report does not itself locate God. [1 notes: BURHAN]
- 53:13/q11/p3 God physically occupied the Sidra or physically descended. — Al-Razi identifies and rejects both implications of a spatial reading. [0 notes: RAZI-FULL-]

## 53:13/q12 How does this sighting connect with the earlier verses and the sura's argument?  [* · links]
turns on: Whether the notes trace the repeated event within the immediate vision narrative or explain its function in the sura's polemic.
- 53:13/q12/p1 It repeats and confirms an earlier encounter with the same one seen. — Al-Mawardi says Muhammad saw again what he saw first. Al-Samin notes that 'other' requires a previous vision. Al-Razi's God reading follows his interpretation of 53:11, while Ibn Ashur connects the other descent to 53:8 and identifies Muhammad through 53:2. Al-Baydawi refers back to his account of the earlier identity and approach. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; MAWARDI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:13/q12/p2 The opening vindicates revelation against charges of invention, soothsaying, or illusion. — Study Quran cites the accusation of fabrication in 52:33 and notes disagreement over the opening passage. Islahi says 1–18 establish the trustworthy angel as revealer and refute treating the Prophet's experience as fancy. Samarra'i distinguishes mirya, casting suspicion on the report, from conceptual argument and links the preceding challenge to 42:18. Kuran Yolu reads the passage against pagan material or spiritual intermediaries falsely made divine. [6 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; STUDYQURAN]

## 53:13/q13 How did the Night Journey and ascent occur?  [* · reports]
turns on: Reports surveyed by al-Tabatabai about dream or waking experience and, if waking, bodily or soul-only ascent.
- 53:13/q13/p1 The journey from the Sacred Mosque to Jerusalem took place in body and soul. — Sahib al-Manaqib as cited by al-Tabatabai attributes this to the Imamis and cites 17:1. [1 notes: TABATABAI(صاحب المناقب)]
- 53:13/q13/p2 The ascent from Jerusalem to heaven occurred in body and soul. — Al-Tabatabai reports this from a group and many Shi'a, alongside the view that the Isra was bodily. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قوم وكثير من الشيعة)]
- 53:13/q13/p3 The ascent from Jerusalem to heaven occurred by soul alone. — Al-Tabatabai reports some Shi'a and some later scholars inclining to this account. [1 notes: TABATABAI(بعض الشيعة وبعض المتأخرين)]
- 53:13/q13/p4 The Isra or ascent took place in a dream. — Al-Tabatabai records dream versus waking as a disputed description in his earlier review at Surat al-Isra, but rejects a dream interpretation of the Isra. [1 notes: TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 53:13/q13/p5 The ascent was night travel to other planets, stars, or galaxies. — Al-Tabatabai rejects this as incoherent with the verses and detailed reports. [0 notes: TABATABAI-]

## 53:13/q14 When did the ascent and the verses about it occur?  [* · sciences]
turns on: Whether verses 13–18 are later than the sura's early Meccan context and which reported date is followed.
- 53:13/q14/p1 Verses 13–18 allude to a later, late Meccan ascent near the Hijra. — Asad places it about a year before migration. Kuran Yolu says these verses are generally associated with a Mi'raj near the end of the Meccan period despite the sura's early Meccan setting. Study Quran says 13–18 may be later than that setting; al-Biqai dates the second descent shortly before the Hijra in the thirteenth year of prophethood. [4 notes: ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:13/q14/p2 The ascent most likely occurred in 619. — Study Quran attributes the late Meccan dating to many commentators. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(many commentators)]
- 53:13/q14/p3 The ascent occurred in 614, seven years before the Hijra. — Study Quran attributes this date to a minority. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(a minority)]

## 53:13/q15 How should reports challenging Aisha's denial be assessed?  [* · reports]
turns on: Hadith wording, timing, transmission, and how Aisha's post-Ascent inquiry bears on the reports.
- 53:13/q15/p1 The God-vision reports affirm at least a heart vision, while physical eyesight lacks sound Companion support. — Ibn Kathir accepts Ibn Abbas's Muslim report of heart-sight and construes an unrestricted Ibn Masud report that way; he calls eye-sight reports anomalous and doubts al-Baghawi's attributions to Anas, al-Hasan and Ikrima. Ibn Abbas's affirmation of a Night Journey vision has early and late followers, but other Companions and Successors disagree. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,جماعات من الصحابة والتابعين وغيرهم); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 53:13/q15/p2 Abu Dharr asked before the Night Journey, so his light answer does not exclude a later vision. — Ibn al-Jawzi proposes this timing and says a later question would have received affirmation; Ibn Kathir rejects it because Aisha asked after the Night Journey without receiving such an affirmation. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن الجوزي); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 53:13/q15/p3 Aisha's denial resulted from mistaken understanding or an answer tailored to it. — Ibn Kathir says Ibn Khuzayma and others made this claim, and rejects their criticism of Aisha. [0 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن خزيمة)-]
- 53:13/q15/p4 The Abu Dharr light hadith remains difficult or its chain is interrupted. — Al-Khallal reports Ahmad opposing the hadith and not knowing how to interpret it; Ibn Khuzayma points to a break between Abdullah ibn Shaqiq and Abu Dharr. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أحمد,ابن خزيمة)]

## 53:13/q16 What senses can 'seeing' carry?  [رَءَاهُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ru'ya denotes direct perception or one of Jishumi's figurative uses.
- 53:13/q16/p1 Direct perception. — Jishumi gives perception as the base sense of ru'ya. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 53:13/q16/p2 Illusion, by figurative extension. — Jishumi includes illusion among its transferred senses. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 53:13/q16/p3 Dream-conception in the heart, by figurative extension. — Jishumi lists this heart experience as another transferred use. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 53:13/q16/p4 Knowledge in the heart, by figurative extension. — Jishumi lists inner knowledge as a transferred use. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 53:13/q17 What report supports the Gabriel identification?  [رَءَاهُ · reports]
- 53:13/q17/p1 Ibn Masud and Abu Hurayra identify Gabriel as the one seen; Muslim authenticates the report. — Al-Qurtubi cites both authorities and the authentication in Muslim. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن مسعود وأبو هريرة)]

## 53:13/q18 Where are the differing Mi'raj reports reviewed?  [* · reports]
- 53:13/q18/p1 In al-Tabatabai's earlier opening commentary on Surat al-Isra. — Al-Tabatabai refers readers to his review there of the reports and their differing descriptions of the ascent, including dream or waking and bodily or soul-only accounts. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
