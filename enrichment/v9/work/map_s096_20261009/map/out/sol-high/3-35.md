# 3:35: 32 questions

## 3:35/q01 How is the opening إذ construed?  [إِذْ · grammar]
turns on: Whether idh needs an omitted command, attaches to choosing or divine attributes, is redundant, or independently opens the story; objections concern chronology, grammar, and coherence.
- 3:35/q01/p1 An omitted 'remember' governs it. — Al-Akhfash and al-Mubarrad supply udhkur; al-Samin calls idh its object; Nasafi also allows qālat as governor. [18 notes: ZAJJAJ(أبو الحسن الأخفش وأبو العباس محمد بن يزيد); NAHHAS(محمد بن يزيد); WAHIDI-BASIT(الأخفش والمبرد); JISHUMI(الأخفش وأبو العباس,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(المبرد والأخفش); TABRISI(الأخفش والمبرد); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المبرد، الأخفش); RAZI-FULL(الأخفش والمبرد); QURTUBI-FULL(محمد بن يزيد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الأخفش); SAMIN-DURR(أبو الحسن); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN]
- 3:35/q01/p2 It depends on the earlier choosing of Imran's family. — Al-Zajjaj connects 3:33 to this event; Abu Bakr and Ibn al-Anbari object that Adam and Noah were chosen earlier. Razi replies that each selection appears when its recipient exists and acts. Ibn Atiyya says the reading excludes Imran from this moment; al-Samin and Abu Hayyan make the link clause level. [14 notes: NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); JISHUMI(الزجاج); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR(الزجاج); ALUSI-FULL; ZAJJAJ+; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر)-; RAZI-FULL(ابن الأنباري)-]
- 3:35/q01/p3 It depends on the preceding Hearing and Knowing: God heard her when she spoke. — Tabari and Ali ibn Isa attach it to the attributes in 3:34. Abu Hayyan objects to interruption by ʿalīm, an adjectival governor, and apparent temporal restriction of divine attributes; al-Samin replies that adverbials allow flexibility. Razi distinguishes timeless attributes from time bound relations. [13 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); JISHUMI(علي بن عيسى,قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI(علي بن عيسى); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن جرير الطبري); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الطبري); SAMIN-DURR(ابن جرير الطبري); ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-]
- 3:35/q01/p4 It is an extra particle, leaving simply 'the wife of Imran said'. — Abu Ubayda and Ibn Qutayba are named for redundancy; grammarians reject canceling a meaningful past time expression and Ibn al-Anbari rejects discarding a Qur'anic particle. [7 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; NAHHAS(أبو عبيدة); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو عبيدة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(أبو عبيدة، ابن قتيبة); RAZI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو عبيدة); ZAJJAJ(أبو عبيدة,النحويون)-; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن الأنباري,الزجاج)-; JISHUMI(أبو عبيدة)-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو عبيدة معمر)-; TABRISI(أبو عبيدة)-; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج)-; SAMIN-DURR(أبو عبيدة)-]
- 3:35/q01/p5 It opens a new event through temporal foregrounding. — Ibn Ashur prefers an unjoined sentence whose initial time phrase links it to the earlier selection; he rejects remote links to al-maṣīr and yuḥadhdhirukum and an ill fitting governor in the prior verse. Biqai sees a response to questions about the family. [2 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعضهم)-]

## 3:35/q02 How does this account connect to the surrounding passage?  [إِذْ قَالَتِ ٱمْرَأَتُ عِمْرَٰنَ · links]
- 3:35/q02/p1 It unfolds the election of Imran's family and the extraordinary birth and honor of Mary and Jesus. — Abu Hayyan connects the family's beginning to the discussion of Najran's Christians. Biqai says earlier figures introduce Jesus and compares Adam's creation in al-Baqara. Alusi says the story explains the selection. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:35/q03 Who is the wife of Imran?  [ٱمْرَأَتُ ٱمْرَٰنَ · referent]
- 3:35/q03/p1 Hanna or Hanne, Mary's mother and Jesus' maternal grandmother. — Names vary in spelling and in her father Faqudh, Qafudha, Qadhudh or Faqhudh; Qur'an does not name her, says Kur'an Yolu. Abu Hayyan calls Hinnah Hebrew; Qurtubi says the woman's name is not known in Arabic, while other names and a Syrian monastery furnish parallels. Tabirsi's 'Mary's grandmother' conflicts with the usual identification. [32 notes: MUQATIL; MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري عن ابن إسحاق); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو نواس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(القرطبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة,ابن عباس,عكرمة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس، أبو هريرة); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]

## 3:35/q04 Which Imran is meant and what is his ancestry?  [عِمْرَٰنَ · referent]
turns on: The reported patronymic and lineage vary; Ibn Ashur explicitly disputes Solomonic descent.
- 3:35/q04/p1 Mary's father, distinct from the father of Moses and Aaron; often called son of Mathan. — Ibn Ishaq traces an Israelite line to Solomon and David; Muqatil calls Banu Mathan kings descended from David. Sources give 1,080 or 1,800 years of separation, and cite Zechariah's later guardianship. Ibn Ashur calls him a priest and notes Hebrew Amram; Christian books give Joachim, perhaps another name. [17 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL(كتب النصارى); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 3:35/q04/p2 His lineage is given as son of Ashham or al-Hashim. — Baghawi offers Ashham; Tabirsi names al-Hashim as an alternative to Mathan. The variants concern the father's name. [2 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI]
- 3:35/q04/p3 Mary's ancestry goes back to Aaron rather than Solomon. — Ibn Ashur rejects exegetes' Solomonic descent and names Aaron, Moses' brother. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرون)+]
- 3:35/q04/p4 Mary's ancestry descends from Solomon and David. — Ibn Ishaq's lineage and Muqatil's royal Mathan family support this; Ibn Ashur rejects it. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); MUQATIL; IBNASHUR-FULL(المفسرون)-]

## 3:35/q05 How are Mary's and Yahya's families related?  [ٱمْرَأَتُ ٱمْرَٰنَ · reports]
turns on: Whether Isha is Hanna's sister, her daughter, or both half sister and stepdaughter; Alusi checks the genealogical reports.
- 3:35/q05/p1 Hanna and Isha were sisters married to Imran and Zechariah, making Mary and Yahya maternal cousins. — Ibn Ishaq and others report the two marriages. Baydawi and Bursevi use Zechariah's kinship and guardianship to date the family. Alusi's Taqrib source explains the Miʿraj's 'sons of maternal aunts' figuratively. [9 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI(ابن عباس ومقاتل); RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(صاحب التقريب); ELMALILI]
- 3:35/q05/p2 Isha was Hanna's daughter and Mary's sister, making Yahya and Jesus cousins one generation differently. — Razi says Zechariah married Hanna's daughter Esha; Elmalili records a report calling Iysha Mary's sister. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 3:35/q05/p3 Isha was Hanna's maternal half sister and Mary's paternal sister. — This requires Imran to marry Hanna's mother before Hanna. Alusi rejects it because it conflicts with the report that Hanna and Isha were daughters of Faqudha and because neither marriage is supported. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 3:35/q06 How was the vow prompted and timed?  [إِذْ قَالَتِ ٱمْرَأَتُ عِمْرَٰنَ · reports]
turns on: Reports give different immediate prompts and different points in pregnancy for the vow.
- 3:35/q06/p1 After long childlessness, seeing a bird feed its chick led Hanna to pray for a child and vow temple service in gratitude. — Accounts differ on whether the vow preceded conception, followed recognition of pregnancy, or was conditional on safe delivery; some say Imran died during pregnancy. Ibn Abbas adds that menstruation resumed immediately after prayer. Ikrima mentions envy of mothers. [23 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,عكرمة وأبو بكر); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق); TABRISI(قالوا); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(عكرمة,محمد بن إسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bir rivayet); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ); MUQATIL]
- 3:35/q06/p2 A divine promise to Imran of a blessed miracle working son prompted the vow. — Abu Abd Allah and Imam al-Sadiq report a promised son who would heal the blind and leper and raise the dead by God's permission; Tabatabai says Jesus fulfilled the promise through Mary. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو عبد الله); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام)]
- 3:35/q06/p3 God inspired Hanna to make the vow. — Al-Hasan compares her inspiration with Abraham's understanding of the dream sacrifice and Moses' mother's prompting, without treating it as prophetic revelation. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحسن البصري)]

## 3:35/q07 Was Imran alive when Hanna made the vow?  [ٱمْرَأَتُ ٱمْرَٰنَ · reports]
turns on: The timing of his death conflicts with reports that he warned Hanna about a possible daughter.
- 3:35/q07/p1 He had died while Hanna was pregnant. — Ibn Ishaq and many subsequent accounts say he died during pregnancy; Tabatabai infers death from her independent dedication and 3:44. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن إسحاق); RAZI-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق); BAYDAWI-FULL(روي); BURSEVI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(bir rivayet); STUDYQURAN(R, Ṭ)]
- 3:35/q07/p2 He heard of or reacted to the vow while she was pregnant. — Muqatil and Ibn Abbas report his worry if the child proved a girl; Baghawi says both parents became distressed. [4 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]

## 3:35/q08 What is conveyed by قَالَتِ and the address رَبِّ?  [قَالَتِ رَبِّ · interpretation]
- 3:35/q08/p1 She voiced the vow as an appeal to her Lord, who owns her and all things. — Abu Hayyan distinguishes uttering the vow from merely intending it. Razi's lexical discussion allows qawl for belief or nonverbal expression, but here a spoken prayer is described. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL]

## 3:35/q09 What does نَذَرْتُ mean?  [نَذَرْتُ · meaning]
- 3:35/q09/p1 She imposed an obligation on herself, conditionally or without condition. — Thalabi cites Mary in 19:26, the idiom of vowing another's blood, and lines of al-Aʿsha and Jamil. Wahidi's linguists connect nadhr to warning and warding off feared harm. Abu Hayyan repeats the blood idiom. [16 notes: THALABI(الأعشى,جميل); WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل اللغة); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI]
- 3:35/q09/p2 The wording stresses a firm or unconditional declaration of her vow. — Alusi reports a move from conditional to direct wording; Kur'an Yolu renders the dedication as unconditional, and Alusi hears strong desire in its emphasis. [3 notes: ALUSI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:35/q10 What does لَكَ specify?  [لَكَ · grammar]
turns on: The lām is explained as dedication for a purpose or as an ownership relation.
- 3:35/q10/p1 It marks purpose: for God's worship, obedience, and sanctuary service. — Qurtubi supplies 'for Your worship'; Abu Hayyan and Alusi call the lām purposive or causal, implying the sanctuary. Alusi says putting the phrase early foregrounds concern for God. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:35/q10/p2 The lām can convey ownership or entitlement. — Sibawayh compares 'the boy is yours' and 'the slave is yours' to possessive constructions; this is a general linguistic parallel. [1 notes: SIBAWAYH]

## 3:35/q11 Why use مَا for the unborn child?  [مَا · rhetoric]
- 3:35/q11/p1 Its identity and sex are unknown in the womb, and the fetus is not yet treated as discerning. — Abu Hayyan also notes mā can be general and stand for man, attributed to Sibawayh. Al-Samin says those who allow mā for rational referents require no special explanation. [7 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(سيبويه); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 3:35/q12 Why say بَطْنِى here?  [بَطْنِى · rhetoric]
- 3:35/q12/p1 The womb wording marks a present pregnancy and the close maternal attachment relinquished by the vow. — Biqai's Haralli compares this early relinquishment with Abraham's later sacrifice; Tabatabai infers she spoke while pregnant. Burhan says substituting jawf for batn would lose the verse's apt sound. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); TABATABAI; BURHAN]

## 3:35/q13 What was Hanna dedicating the child to?  [مَا فِى بَطْنِى مُحَرَّرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the liberation describes institutional temple service, spiritual release, or both.
- 3:35/q13/p1 Worship and service in the Jerusalem sanctuary, freed from worldly work and parental claims. — Sources variously emphasize cleaning the church, studying and teaching scripture, obedience to God, and freedom from marriage. Al-Asamm says the vow renounced the parents' normal benefit from a grown child; Dahhak assigns the child to scholars. Tabatabai says no literal manumission of a free child occurred. - 3:35/q13/p2 The vow has an inward sense: freeing the soul or heart from desire and dependence on created things. — Tustari frees the child from caprice; Sulami's transmitters speak of worldly bondage, personal aims, earnings, and the mother's relinquishment of management. Alusi allegorizes the mother as soul and child as heart; Qashani relates parents' lawful food and sincere disposition to the child's spiritual formation. [11 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(النوري,جعفر,سهل بن عبد الله,محمد بن الفضل,محمد بن علي); QASHANI(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ALUSI-FULL]

## 3:35/q14 What does مُحَرَّرًۭا mean lexically?  [مُحَرَّرًۭا · meaning]
- 3:35/q14/p1 Freed, purified, and wholly set apart. — Thalabi, Wahidi, Razi, Qurtubi, and others compare freeing a slave, refining a book, a person free of encumbrance, and pure clay. Dhū al-Rumma's verse uses ḥurra for purity. Biqai's Haralli hears the intensive form as complete severance from human authority. [19 notes: MAJAZ; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن قتيبة); JISHUMI(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ذو الرمة,عكرمة ومجاهد); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الحرالي); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 3:35/q15 How is مُحَرَّرًۭا parsed?  [مُحَرَّرًۭا · grammar]
turns on: Whether the accusative describes the child directly, an implied pronoun or boy, or the act of freeing.
- 3:35/q15/p1 An accusative circumstance describing mā fī baṭnī, governed by nadhartu. — Tabari takes mā as 'the one who'; Nahhas, Qurtubi and Alusi prefer this. Nahhas invokes the later female birth and divine acceptance; al-Samin says freedom can be contemporaneous and temple service prospective. [16 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 3:35/q15/p2 An accusative circumstance of the pronoun in the prepositional phrase, governed by implied existence. — Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, and Alusi offer the implicit istaqarra or existential predicate. [3 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:35/q15/p3 An adjective of an omitted object, 'a consecrated boy'. — Makkī proposes this; Ibn Atiyya objects that nadhara already takes mā as its object, and Nahhas says it also requires an unusual deleted lām. [2 notes: NAHHAS; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(مكي); NAHHAS-; IBNATIYYA-FULL(مكي)-; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية)-; SAMIN-DURR(مكي بن أبي طالب)-]
- 3:35/q15/p4 An accusative verbal noun meaning 'a freeing' or an implicit clause 'that I make it free'. — Ibn Qutayba supplies the making free; Abu Hayyan and al-Samin cite passive participles used as verbal nouns, with Qur'an 34:19, 22:18 and poetry. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن قتيبة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية); SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)]

## 3:35/q16 What custom governed a vowed child and could a daughter serve?  [مُحَرَّرًۭا · reports]
turns on: The customary male restriction is set against Mary's reported exceptional acceptance.
- 3:35/q16/p1 The usual dedication was of boys, who served and cleaned the sanctuary until puberty and could then choose to remain or leave. — Girls were thought unsuitable because menstruation required leaving the sanctuary. Qatadah and others give this as the basis of 3:36's male and female contrast. Ibn Ishaq, al-Kalbi, Razi, Tabatabai and others describe the puberty option; Dahhak says boys were handed to scripture scholars after nursing. [26 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(الربيع,عكرمة وأبو بكر,قتادة); ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الضحاك); THALABI(الكلبي وابن إسحاق وغيرهما); BAGHAWI-FULL(الكلبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNHISHAM(ابن إسحاق); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,الضحاك,قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام,قيل)]
- 3:35/q16/p2 Mary was exceptionally accepted despite being a girl and served the worshippers. — Abu Hayyan says no girl had previously been accepted as a vow; a report in Tabatabai says she served until Zakariya was told to place a screen between her and worshippers. [3 notes: NAHHAS(ابن عباس); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); TABATABAI(أحدهما)]

## 3:35/q17 What did Hanna assume about the unborn child's sex?  [مَا فِى بَطْنِى مُحَرَّرًۭا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the masculine wording signals a real expectation or the fetus's sex remained unknown.
- 3:35/q17/p1 She expected, assumed, or hoped for a boy. — Suddi reports an assumption. Razi, Baydawi, Abu Hayyan and Bursevi offer assumption or a vow meant to obtain a boy. Tabatabai argues the masculine adjective and her grief at a girl imply belief in a male child; he says such certainty must rest on revelation, citing 13:8, 31:34 and 72:26. [11 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:35/q17/p2 She did not know whether the fetus was male or female when she vowed. — Ibn Kathir explicitly says so; Thalabi and Ibn Atiyya note the uncertainty, and Abu Hayyan takes mā to include either sex. [4 notes: THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 3:35/q18 What does فَتَقَبَّلْ mean?  [فَتَقَبَّلْ · meaning]
- 3:35/q18/p1 Receive favorably and with approval, as one accepts a gift. — Wahidi and Alusi derive it from meeting or requiting with recompense; Abu Hayyan says taqabbala here works like qabilla. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الواحدي); NASAFI-FULL(مدنى وأبو عمرو); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 3:35/q19 What does Hanna ask God to accept?  [فَتَقَبَّلْ مِنِّىٓ · interpretation]
turns on: The direct object is omitted; interpreters supply the vow, the child, or an implied request for a son.
- 3:35/q19/p1 Her vow or the consecrated child, as an acceptable act deserving God's pleasure. — Tabari says the vowed child; Muqatil, Ibn Kathir, Baydawi, and others say the vow. Ibn Atiyya adds reward; Razi sees sincerity and God's pleasure. Tabatabai notes the omitted object allows either, while 3:37 favors the child. [12 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 3:35/q19/p2 The request implicitly asks for a son who can be accepted for sanctuary service. — Bursevi argues that an accepted offering presupposes a qualified child under the custom barring girls. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 3:35/q20 How is the final yāʾ of مِنِّىٓ read?  [مِنِّىٓ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowel of the first-person yāʾ; the notes say the meaning is unchanged.
- 3:35/q20/p1 It is opened with fatḥa in the reported variant. — The two Medinan readers and Abu Amr are reported to open this yāʾ; Qiraat-ER says pronunciation and melody change without change in meaning. [3 notes: NASHR; QIRAAT-ER(Y-iḍ); NASAFI-FULL(مدنى وأبو عمرو)]
- 3:35/q20/p2 It remains in the basic quiescent form. — Qiraat-ER calls this the standard form; NASHR's list sets the opened reading against the other readers. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER; NASHR]

## 3:35/q21 What do ٱلسَّمِيعُ and ٱلْعَلِيمُ affirm here?  [ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ · theology]
- 3:35/q21/p1 God hears her spoken prayer and knows her inward intention, vow, and desire. — Tabari says nothing inward or outward is hidden; Razi distinguishes eternal hearing and knowledge from their time bound relation to this utterance. Abu Hayyan says the attributes fit her intention, utterance, and plea for acceptance. [14 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; QASHANI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 3:35/q21/p2 The emphatic exclusive wording expresses firm certainty and directs hope to God alone. — Alusi's Shaykh al-Islam says restriction cuts off hope in anything else; Alusi says hearing precedes knowledge because audible objects are fewer. Sibawayh supplies the general emphatic function of inna and compensating lām in its lightened form. [6 notes: SIBAWAYH; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(شيخ الإسلام)]

## 3:35/q22 How is the written ت of ٱمْرَأَتُ treated when pausing?  [ٱمْرَأَتُ · readings]
turns on: Whether pause follows the common feminine hāʾ sound or the written tāʾ.
- 3:35/q22/p1 Pause with hāʾ. — Al-Samin names Abu Amr and al-Kisa'i, despite the written tāʾ; Nashr discusses written feminine tāʾ as a class of pause forms. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو); NASHR]
- 3:35/q22/p2 Pause with tāʾ as written. — Al-Samin attributes this to Medinans and cites Arabic dialect and poetry; comparable wife forms occur at 12:30, 66:10 and 28:9. [4 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو عمرو,أهل المدينة); NASHR]

## 3:35/q23 What does the open tāʾ spelling of ٱمْرَأَتُ suggest?  [ٱمْرَأَتُ · rhetoric]
- 3:35/q23/p1 It marks outward marriage, and here an inwardly harmonious marriage as well. — Burhan compares five wife forms: four are inwardly estranged, while Imran's wife shares both outward and inward union with her husband, followed by honored offspring. [2 notes: BURHAN]

## 3:35/q24 What is the legal force of this dedication?  [نَذَرْتُ مُحَرَّرًۭا · law]
turns on: The legal dispute concerns parental authority, ownership of a free child, and differences between Israelite and Islamic law.
- 3:35/q24/p1 It was lawful and binding under Israelite custom, with children expected to fulfill parental vows. — Zajjaj and Wahidi say parents could bind children; Razi distinguishes Israelite from Islamic law. Al-Asamm and Bursevi say the vow redirected a child's normal service to parents. Thalabi quotes the Prophetic rule that vows of obedience are fulfilled, not vows of disobedience. [11 notes: ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الكلبي وابن إسحاق وغيرهما,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); RAZI-FULL(الأصم); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 3:35/q24/p2 An analogous Islamic vow can bind a parent to educate a young child in worship and religious learning. — Al-Qadi Abu Yaʿla validates raising the child for Qur'an, jurisprudence, and religious sciences. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(القاضي أبو يعلى)]
- 3:35/q24/p3 A free woman cannot make her child the object of a binding vow because she does not own it. — Ibn al-Arabi gives the ownership objection while also explaining Hanna's relinquishment of comfort and support. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 3:35/q24/p4 The vow releases parental claims but does not literally manumit the child. — Tabatabai distinguishes a parent's dedicating a free child to God from manumitting property. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 3:35/q25 What can be inferred about the credibility of the family reports?  [* · reports]
- 3:35/q25/p1 The Ahl al-Bayt reports are plausible from the verses, though weak solitary chains do not prove them. — Tabatabai answers commentators who dismiss reports on Imran's revelation, Mary's fruit and Zechariah's sign as unsupported by scripture, reason or reliable history; he says their dismissal is unargued. [1 notes: TABATABAI+; TABATABAI(بعض المفسرين)-]

## 3:35/q26 What examples illustrate releasing a child for worship?  [نَذَرْتُ مُحَرَّرًۭا · reports]
- 3:35/q26/p1 Dedication releases a child from ordinary family claims so the child can serve God. — Al-Hasan says Hanna vowed the child and then released her. Qurtubi recounts a Sufi son who asked his mother to release him for worship and study; she later said she gave him to God. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); QURTUBI-FULL(رجل من الصوفية)]

## 3:35/q27 What wider principles of Qur'anic eloquence are cited with the verse?  [* · rhetoric]
- 3:35/q27/p1 The Qur'an's arrangement and eloquence exceed human poetry and prose. — Baqillani contrasts sustained Qur'anic coherence with poets' inherited materials and uneven achievement, criticizing Imru' al-Qays's horse imagery while allowing some good lines. Bint al-Shati says his treatment often stays in theology and lengthy poetic criticism. Alusi lists foretelling and meaning as further signs, prefers the distinctive arrangement theory, and rejects pure sarfa. Samarrai cites the challenge to humans and jinn, hearers' response, deliberate word placement, letter distribution, and asserted paired word counts. [53 notes: BAQILLANI; ALUSI-FULL; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; SAMARRAI-ASRAR(الوليد بن المغيرة,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم,ملاك التأويل)]
- 3:35/q27/p2 Poetry remains legitimate evidence for Qur'anic language despite moral objections to some poems. — Jurjani answers critics of poetry: vice also occurs in prose, and scholars may quote objectionable lines for lexicon or grammar without endorsing them. He corrects al-Amidi's literal reading of Abu Tammam and notes that even long accepted interpretations can prove wrong. Burhan quotes Abu Tammam for dhat. [10 notes: JURJANI-DALAIL(الآمدي,من كان هذا رأيه); BURHAN(أبو تمام)]

## 3:35/q28 What general grammatical and lexical examples accompany the verse?  [* · grammar]
- 3:35/q28/p1 The sources add broader rules of morphology, clause structure, speech, and vocabulary. — Sibawayh inventories augmented verb patterns and exemplifies exclamations; Jurjani defines the two-term proposition and responsible speaker. Razi discusses qawl as utterance, doctrine or figurative expression, the dispute over inner speech, and Umar's inward prepared words; he rejects an inference from 63:1. Damghani lists eighteen senses of dhikr. Burhan describes causes of definiteness. [23 notes: SIBAWAYH; JURJANI-DALAIL; RAZI-FULL(الذين ينكرون كلام النفس,عمر,مثبتو كلام النفس); DAMGHANI; BURHAN]
- 3:35/q28/p2 The definite male and female in the next verse designate the expected boy and actual girl. — Burhan says the contrast concerns particular children, rather than all males and females. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 3:35/q29 What exegetical methods are discussed alongside this account?  [* · interpretation]
- 3:35/q29/p1 Sound Qur'anic study should trace language, readings, meaning, law, and reports with care. — Ibn Atiyya describes exegesis as a leading science, aims at concise attributed commentary, cites the command to write knowledge, and rejects strained esoteric readings. Itqan says occasions of revelation clarify meaning and legislation, discusses restrictions by cause, opening rhetoric, and the scope of abrogation. [19 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ITQAN(ابن تيمية,ابن دقيق العيد,القاضي أبو بكر,الواحدي)]
- 3:35/q29/p2 The passage uses intelligible ellipses in several places. — Abu Hayyan says omitted material must be supplied at points to make the sense clear. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 3:35/q30 What ancillary reading history and rules are reported?  [* · readings]
- 3:35/q30/p1 The notes preserve reader biographies and broader phonetic and orthographic rules. — Nashr records Abu Amr, his transmitters, Hamza and his transmitters; the articulation of ta, tha and jim; pause and hamza options; rules for personal yāʾ and extra yāʾ; particular readings of other words and names. Explicit transmission outweighs a manual's silence. [47 notes: NASHR(أبو حنيفة,أبو شامة,الأعمش,الحسن,الدارقطني,الداني,سفيان بن عيينة,سيبويه,يحيى بن عبد الملك)]
- 3:35/q30/p2 The next verse has a reading of waḍaʿtu as Hanna's quoted speech. — Jishumi attributes the marked tāʾ to Ali, Abu Bakr from Asim, Ibn Amir and Yaqub. [1 notes: JISHUMI(علي بن أبي طالب وأبو بكر عن عاصم وابن عامر ويعقوب)]

## 3:35/q31 What further details about the family or nearby verses are reported?  [* · reports]
- 3:35/q31/p1 Mary is counted among four women said to have attained perfection. — Biqai cites a Prophetic report naming Mary daughter of Imran. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL(النبي)]
- 3:35/q31/p2 Jishumi records that 76:7 was revealed about Ali's household. — The note concerns fulfillment of vows in a different verse. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
- 3:35/q31/p3 Waḍʿ in the following verse means giving birth. — Tabirsi derives the expression from putting down and notes other uses for place, lowliness, and easing pace. [1 notes: TABRISI]

## 3:35/q32 How are inner promptings and revelation distinguished in the extended commentary?  [* · theology]
- 3:35/q32/p1 Inner meanings may be called speech, but revelation is distinct and angelic promptings are discerned by their conformity with scripture. — Tabatabai distinguishes unuttered promptings, angelic and satanic effects on the heart, recognition by prophets, and unveiled revelation. Reports from al-Sadiq and al-Baqir distinguish a messenger seeing an angel, a prophet receiving dreams or heart revelation, and a muhaddath hearing a voice without seeing a form. He connects the heart's angel and Satan to 50:18 while allowing recording angels too. [12 notes: TABATABAI(الصادق عليه السلام,بريد عن الباقر والصادق عليهما السلام,زرارة عن أبي عبد الله)]
