# 2:114: 19 questions

## 2:114/q01 What does the charge of being most unjust mean?  [وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the superlative is literal, restricted by class, or rhetorical.
- 2:114/q01/p1 No one surpasses the offender in injustice; the question condemns the obstruction. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj and later grammarians parse man as subject and aẓlam as predicate. Elmalılı defines injustice as misplacing what is sacred; Ibn ʿĀshūr says the offenders violated worshippers’ rights. [26 notes: ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI]
- 2:114/q01/p2 Several kinds of unbelieving offenders can tie at the highest degree of injustice. — Abū Ḥayyān rejects restricting the phrase to classes: saying none is more unjust allows equals. Al-Ālūsī compares three equally learned people; the shared unbelief explains the tie. [13 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(أبو حيان); ALUSI-FULL(غير واحد); ELMALILI; ALUSI-FULL+; ITQAN(أبو حيان)+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:114/q01/p3 Each superlative is limited to offenders of its own kind. — Several grammarians, al-Burhān and al-Itqān use the relative clause to limit comparison to mosque blockers, forgers, or rejecters. [10 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض الناس); SAMIN-DURR(السائل); BURHAN(بعض المتأخرين,بعض مشايخنا); ITQAN; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 2:114/q01/p4 The earliest perpetrators are unsurpassed because they introduced the offense. — Reported by Abū Ḥayyān, al-Samīn, al-Burhān and al-Itqān; al-Itqān says this reduces to restriction by offense. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN; ITQAN; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:114/q01/p5 The superlative is figurative hyperbole expressing horror at the act. — Al-Zarkashī prefers this to a literal ranking; al-Ālūsī accepts it as an alternative. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; BURHAN+]

## 2:114/q02 Who are the mosque preventers and what event occasioned the verse?  [مِمَّن مَّنَعَ مَسَٰجِدَ ٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: Historical chronology, the context of 2:113, and whether ruin was physical or through obstructed worship.
- 2:114/q02/p1 Christians or Roman Christians desecrated and obstructed worship at Jerusalem. — Mujāhid reports dumped filth; Ibn ʿAbbās and others identify Christians. Reports name Titus and Roman forces, killing and enslaving Israelites, burning Torah, desecrating the Temple; many say it remained ruined until ʿUmar. Elmalılı details Titus, Hadrian, Helena and eventual Muslim rebuilding. Al-Ṭabarī prefers this due to physical ruin and context; Abū Bakr al-Rāzī and Kuran Yolu object that Christians postdate Nebuchadnezzar and revered the site. [69 notes: MUJAHID(ابن أبي نجيح); MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,مجاهد); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,كعب,مجاهد); THALABI; WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(ابن عباس، ومجاهد، والفراء,النصارى); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); TABRISI(الطبري); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس وآخرون); RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر الرازي,ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس,الغزنوي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس، مجاهد,كعب الأحبار); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,كعب,مجاهد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI(Cumhûr-i müfessirîn,İbn Abbas); IBNASHUR-FULL(بعضهم); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Taberî); STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī,some commentators); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن جرير)+; TAB-FULL+]
- 2:114/q02/p2 Meccan polytheists, especially Quraysh, barred the Prophet and Muslims from the Sacred Mosque. — Reports locate the bar at the Kaaba or al-Ḥudaybiyya, where sacrifice at Dhū Ṭuwā and a truce followed; others mention expulsion, Abu Bakr's prayer place, and harassment of the Prophet. Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr prefer this in context. Al-Ṭabarī objects that Quraysh did not demolish the Kaaba; Ibn Kathīr replies that idolatry and exclusion ruined it in substance. [57 notes: TAB-FULL(آخرون,ابن زيد); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); THALABI(عطاء وعبد الرحمن بن عوف); WAHIDI-ASBAB(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); JISHUMI(الأصم، وأبو علي، وأبو القاسم، وابن زيد,المشركون؛ عن الأصم وغيره); BAGHAWI-FULL(عطاء وعبد الرحمن بن زيد); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن زيد); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد وأبو مسلم,ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد,ابن عباس); SUYUTI-LUBAB(ابن عباس,زيد); BURSEVI(قيل); ALUSI-FULL(عطاء عن ابن عباس); ELMALILI(Bazı müfessirîn); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,سعد بن معاذ); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(R,some commentators); IBNASHUR-FULL+; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; TABATABAI+; TAB-FULL-; TABRISI(الطبري)-]
- 2:114/q02/p3 The immediate historical cases differ, but the wording includes every person and every mosque. — Al-Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān prefer the general wording. Al-Zajjāj cites the earth made a mosque; al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī compare 104:1, a general rebuke from a particular occasion. Elmalılı includes Jews, Christians and polytheists; Asad and Kuran Yolu extend protection to houses of worship. [30 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(بعض أهل اللغة); WAHIDI-BASIT(الزجاج); JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(others); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; QURTUBI-FULL+]
- 2:114/q02/p4 Nebuchadnezzar and his followers ruined Jerusalem, aided by Romans or Christians. — Qatāda, al-Suddī and others say hostility to Jews, sometimes after the killing of John son of Zechariah, prompted the alliance; variants name Jews who induced the attack. Abū Bakr al-Rāzī objects to the chronology. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports Nebuchadnezzar's three campaigns but rejects this occasion on contextual grounds. [29 notes: ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة والحسن والسدي); THALABI(السدي,قتادة,قتادة والسدي); WAHIDI-ASBAB(قتادة والسدي); JISHUMI(الحسن، وقتادة، والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(السدي,قتادة,قتادة والسدي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والسدي,قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(المفسرون,قتادة); RAZI-FULL(الحسن وقتادة والسدي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو بكر الرازي,قتادة,قتادة والسدي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,قتادة); DURR-FULL(السدي,قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL(بعضهم); STUDYQURAN(some commentators); RAZI-FULL(أبو بكر الرازي)-; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-; IBNASHUR-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 2:114/q02/p5 Jews after the qibla change sought to obstruct prayer toward the Kaaba and perhaps stirred attacks on it and the Prophet's Mosque. — Al-Rāzī prefers this because the nearby verses censure Jews and Christians; Kuran Yolu reports his fifth view. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî); RAZI-FULL+]

## 2:114/q03 What does منع mean and what obstruction counts here?  [مَّنَعَ · meaning]
- 2:114/q03/p1 It means interposing between a willing person and worship or access. — Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī compare ṣadd; Abū Ḥayyān cites al-Rāghib's protective sense. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies denying qualified worshippers appointed rites, while ordinary closure and exclusion of unqualified entrants are outside the charge. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الراغب); IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:114/q04 What are God's masājid, and why is the plural used?  [مَسَٰجِدَ ٱللَّهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether a particular sanctuary, all mosques, or the earth is intended; why a plural may refer to a single case.
- 2:114/q04/p1 Every mosque or place of worship is within the wording. — A mosque is a place of prostration, and the earth was made a mosque and purifier. Al-Kashshāf, al-Qurṭubī, Abū Ḥayyān and others distinguish the general rule from a specific occasion. Ibn ʿAṭiyya includes a Muslim city because the whole earth is a mosque. [19 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ(بعض أهل اللغة); JISHUMI; TABRISI(علي); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عطية,الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(others)]
- 2:114/q04/p2 Jerusalem's Temple and its prayer niches. — Muqātil, al-Ṭabarī's preferred context, al-Dāmghānī and others identify the sanctuary. The plural may include its many prayer spaces. [13 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; DAMGHANI; JISHUMI(النصارى); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(al-Ṭabarī,some commentators)]
- 2:114/q04/p3 Masjid literally means a place of prostration, conventionally a building for prayer. — Al-Ṭabarī gives singular/plural morphology, Ibn al-Jawzī and Kuran Yolu the technical use; Abū Ḥayyān says naming prostration reflects full submission and cites nearness to God in prostration. Turkish terms jāmiʿ, musallā and namāzgāh are distinguished. [17 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; QURTUBI-FULL(الفراء); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); SAMIN-DURR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(hadith); STUDYQURAN]
- 2:114/q04/p4 The Sacred Mosque in Mecca, or it and other Meccan worship places. — Abū Muslim makes one mosque plural; al-Rāzī and Ibn ʿĀshūr mention Abu Bakr's mosque, the Kaaba, Maqām Ibrāhīm, Ḥaṭīm, Khayf, Minā and Mashʿar. Others say the Kaaba is qibla of all mosques or the plural magnifies it. [15 notes: JISHUMI(المشركون؛ عن الأصم وغيره); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين); RAZI-FULL(أبو مسلم); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(غيره); IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]

## 2:114/q05 How does the clause 'that His name be mentioned' attach to 'prevented'?  [أَن يُذْكَرَ فِيهَا ٱسْمُهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether it is a second object, apposition, purpose clause or governed by an omitted preposition.
- 2:114/q05/p1 A preposition, usually min, is understood before it. — Al-Akhfash and others allow an omitted preposition, with accusative or genitive analyses; al-Naḥḥās notes length or the verb's sense licenses deletion. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; THALABI; JISHUMI(الأخفش); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الأخفش); QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI]
- 2:114/q05/p2 It is the second object of 'prevented'. — Al-Kashshāf compares 17:59 and 17:94; al-Wāḥidī calls it a verbal-noun clause. Some allow direct double-object government. [11 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:114/q05/p3 It is an apposition to 'mosques', particularly an inclusive apposition. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Zajjāj, al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn state this analysis. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(الأخفش); TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR]
- 2:114/q05/p4 It expresses the purpose or motive of prevention: lest God be remembered. — Al-Zajjāj makes dislike of God's name its governing motive; al-Samīn says an omitted noun such as 'entry' is needed. [9 notes: JISHUMI(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 2:114/q06 What does mentioning God's name there encompass?  [يُذْكَرَ فِيهَا ٱسْمُهُۥ · interpretation]
- 2:114/q06/p1 It encompasses monotheism, prayer, recitation and inner and outward worship. — Muqātil specifies affirming oneness; Abū Ḥayyān says even the easiest act of remembrance was barred. Bursevī adds glorification and sanctification. [7 notes: MUQATIL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 2:114/q07 What does saʿā mean and how does it work here?  [وَسَعَىٰ · meaning]
- 2:114/q07/p1 It is purposeful effort or action toward an end. — Al-Wāḥidī traces quick walking to walking then work, citing 28:20, 37:102, 62:9, 2:260, 17:19, 22:51 and 92:4. Jishumī, al-Ṭabrisī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ibn ʿĀshūr give walking, seeking, earning and causation; poetry is cited for livelihood and alms. It is coordinated with manaʿa and fī kharābihā attaches to it. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BURSEVI; NAHHAS; SAMIN-DURR]

## 2:114/q08 What does kharāb mean here and how is it caused?  [فِى خَرَابِهَآ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the ruin is physical destruction, loss of worship, or both.
- 2:114/q08/p1 Preventing prayer and remembrance is itself figurative ruin. — Ibn Zayd says barring pilgrims and maintainers empties the mosque; al-Rāzī and Ibn Kathīr say idolatry, expulsion and inactivity undo its purpose. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes literal blocking from metaphorical ruin in the Meccan account. [26 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(أبو مسلم); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن زيد); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(R)]
- 2:114/q08/p2 It is literal demolition and desecration of a sanctuary. — Jerusalem accounts name wrecking, burned Torah, corpses and pigs. Jishumī and Abū Ḥayyān define kharāb against construction; al-Samīn gives verbal-noun analyses, including object and subject annexation. [26 notes: MUQATIL; JISHUMI; THALABI; TABRISI(الأصمعي); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]

## 2:114/q09 How is the entry clause parsed and why does it shift to a plural?  [أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ مَا كَانَ لَهُمْ أَن يَدْخُلُوهَآ إِلَّا خَآئِفِينَ · grammar]
- 2:114/q09/p1 The demonstrative is subject; the entry clause is its predicate, and khāʾifīn is a circumstantial accusative. — Al-Samīn makes lahum the fronted predicate of kāna and an yadkhulūhā its subject; al-Ṭabrisī also allows kāna as redundant. The exception excludes all states except fear. The singular form of man governs earlier verbs and its collective sense governs the plural. [14 notes: TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 2:114/q10 How is the fearful-entry phrase read?  [إِلَّا خَآئِفِينَ · readings]
turns on: Variant forms and the reciters' arguments about hamza and vowel length.
- 2:114/q10/p1 Ubayy's codex reads khīfāʾ or khīfan, a plural conveying fear. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn compare ṣawm/ṣaym and explain wāw-to-yāʾ change. [3 notes: THALABI(أبي بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبي); SAMIN-DURR(أبي)]
- 2:114/q10/p2 The standard wording is khāʾifīn. — Ibn al-Jazarī rejects a reported shortest madd on ulāʾika: no imam adopts it and the following kasra agrees with khāʾifīn. Reciters differ in madd length and hamza pause treatment; Sībawayh's hamza rules explain easing and replacement. [45 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر بن مجاهد، أبو القاسم الطرسوسي، أبو الطاهر بن خلف,أبو بكر بن مهران,أبو عبد الله بن شريح,أبو محمد عبد الواحد بن أبي السداد المالقي,أبو هشام، خلف,ابن مهران,الجمهور,الداني,خلف عن سليم); SIBAWAYH(أبو الخطاب,أهل الحجاز,الخليل,العرب,بنو تميم)]
- 2:114/q10/p3 Abū ʿAbd Allāh reads khīfan. — Al-Kashshāf compares its form with ṣiyam. [1 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عبد الله)]
- 2:114/q10/p4 A reading attributed to ʿAbd Allāh gives 'except ḥanīfan'. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(عبدالله)]

## 2:114/q11 What does the fearful-entry clause mean?  [مَا كَانَ لَهُمْ أَن يَدْخُلُوهَآ إِلَّا خَآئِفِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether it states rightful reverence, predicts reversal of power, or commands exclusion.
- 2:114/q11/p1 Christians or Romans enter Jerusalem secretly fearing beating, execution or jizya. — Qatāda, al-Suddī, Ibn ʿAbbās and others describe disguise, severe beating, death or poll tax after the Muslim rebuilding. Al-Rāzī objects that Christians later held Jerusalem for more than a century; al-Ālūsī says one period suffices for the promise. [41 notes: MUQATIL; ABDURRAZZAQ(قتادة); IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(السدي,قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,قتادة,كعب); THALABI(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة ومقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI(قتادة، والسدي); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس,السدي,قتادة ومقاتل); KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة والسدي); TABRISI(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL(قتادة والسدي); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قتادة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(السدي,قتادة,كعب الأحبار); DURR-FULL(السدي,كعب); ALUSI-FULL(قتادة); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(some commentators)]
- 2:114/q11/p2 It predicts Muslim victory, recovery of mosques and their fearful entry. — Al-Zajjāj links 9:33; al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite the conquest, ban on polytheist pilgrimage and expulsion. Al-Ālūsī says Crusader control later does not negate a promise fulfilled once. Abū Ḥayyān objects that mā kāna lahum expresses propriety, not plain prediction. [19 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI(الزجاج); TABRISI(الزجاج); KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); THALABI(عطاء وعبد الرحمن بن عوف); BAGHAWI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ELMALILI; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-]
- 2:114/q11/p3 The declarative form commands Muslims to bar or expel the offenders. — Explanations compare 33:53; some demand jihad until fearful entry, others forbid enabling access. Ibn Kathīr specifies truce and jizya. Abū Ḥayyān finds this strained. [13 notes: THALABI(أهل المعاني); WAHIDI-BASIT(قيل); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قيل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 2:114/q11/p4 They ought to fear believers' punishment and must not seize mosques. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī say oppression prevented this rightful arrangement; al-Ālūsī reads lām as entitlement violated. [12 notes: TAB-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(بعضهم); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Fahreddin er-Râzî)]
- 2:114/q11/p5 The offenders ought to enter reverently, fearing God and His punishment. — Abū Ḥayyān prefers the mosque's dignity as the direct force of mā kāna lahum and rejects a simple future prediction and command. Al-Ālūsī says the lām marks fitting entitlement; Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects this because the demonstrative makes the condition a consequence of offense. [9 notes: RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(كثير من المفسرين); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]

## 2:114/q12 What access to mosques may non-Muslims have?  [أَن يَدْخُلُوهَآ · law]
turns on: The sanctity of the Sacred Mosque, scope of 9:28, reports of actual entry, and whether 2:114 proves exclusion.
- 2:114/q12/p1 They may enter no mosque, apart from reported necessity or judicial exceptions. — Mālik invokes 9:28 and purification from impurity. Al-Jubbāʾī allows a litigant brief fearful entry to ordinary mosques; Abū ʿAlī gives judicial authority. Al-Qurṭubī reads fear as expulsion or discipline. [11 notes: JISHUMI(أبو علي); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(الجبائي); RAZI-FULL(الشافعي); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(بعض الناس); ALUSI-FULL(مالك); TABRISI(أبو جعفر)+]

## 2:114/q13 What does khizy mean and what is their worldly disgrace?  [لَهُمْ فِى ٱلدُّنْيَا خِزْىٌۭ · interpretation]

- 2:114/q13/p1 Belligerents face killing or captivity; protected non-Muslims pay jizya in humiliation. — Qatāda, al-Zajjāj and many others distinguish the two legal statuses. ## 2:114/q14 What is the great punishment in the hereafter, and why is it great?  [وَلَهُمْ فِى ٱلْءَاخِرَةِ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌۭ · interpretation]
- 2:114/q14/p1 It is Hellfire, commensurate with unbelief and grave wrongdoing. — Al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī and many others identify Hell; al-Zajjāj, al-Rāzī and al-Ālūsī link greatness to the opening accusation. Abū Ḥayyān says its degree distinguishes unbelievers from believers and its fire mirrors their demolition; Ibn Kathīr cites desecration with idols and naked circumambulation. ## 2:114/q15 How does this verse connect with its neighbors and other verses?  [* · links]
turns on: The proposed historical addressee and how the passage turns to 2:115.
- 2:114/q15/p1 It follows the rebuke of Jews and Christians by exposing Christian offenses. — Al-Ṭabarī cites 2:113 and 2:116; al-Rāzī says Christian destruction contradicts their Paradise claim; al-Ālūsī makes this the first occasion's link. [6 notes: TAB-FULL; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 2:114/q15/p2 It turns from Jews and Christians to Arab polytheists with analogous conduct. — Jishumī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr and Ibn ʿĀshūr find the previous mention of those without knowledge or a digression. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the qibla dispute: exclusion from the sanctuary was graver than facing another direction. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 2:114/q15/p3 The passage is linked to other mosque and worship verses. — The sources cite 9:17–18 on mosque maintenance, 9:28 on barring polytheists, 22:40 on protection of all houses of worship and 72:18 on mosques belonging to God. [8 notes: RAZI-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ASAD-NOTES(Asad); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 2:114/q15/p4 Verse 2:115 consoles those denied Jerusalem or a mosque: east and west belong to God. — Al-Dahhān's explanation appears in al-Burhān and al-Itqān. [2 notes: BURHAN(أبو الحسين الدهان); ITQAN(أبو الحسن الدهان)]
- 2:114/q15/p5 The next statement about God taking a son connects to the mosque-preventer passage. — Al-Wāḥidī uses the Syrian codex reading of Ibn ʿĀmir without the waw before qālū to mark the connection. [2 notes: BURHAN(الواحدي); ELMALILI]

## 2:114/q16 What inward or spiritual analogy do the sources draw?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether inward allusion is legitimate and whether it replaces the outward meaning.
- 2:114/q16/p1 Mosques may analogize hearts, souls, spirits and inner secrets; ruin is desire and veiling. — Al-Qushayrī distinguishes ruined homes of worship, gnosis, love and witnessing. Qāshānī and al-Ālūsī read divine names manifesting according to the heart, desire and fanaticism clouding it, humbled entry, worldly exposure and afterlife veiling.
