# 12:33: 25 questions

## 12:33/q01 How does Joseph address God and present his response?  [قَالَ رَبِّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the address was heard by the women.
- 12:33/q01/p1 He turns from the women to God in a private prayer and appeal for aid. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya calls it complaint and request to remove affliction; Biqāʿī sees absorbed direct address without a request particle; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it an explanatory resumption of the crisis. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Joseph gave the women no answer, but turned to God. Biqāʿī defines duʿāʾ as an upward request to a superior, though its form resembles a command to a subordinate. [8 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Kl)]
- 12:33/q01/p2 He may have spoken aloud to discourage the women. — Ibn ʿĀshūr offers this alongside a private prayer. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:33/q02 How is 'Lord' read and parsed?  [رَبِّ ٱلسِّجْنُ · readings]
turns on: The vocalization of rabb and the case of al-sijn.
- 12:33/q02/p1 The common reading has rabbī with an omitted possessive yāʾ and makes al-sijn the nominative subject. — Al-Samīn says aḥabb is the predicate. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 12:33/q02/p2 A reported reading has rabbu with ḍamma and al-sijn in the genitive. — Al-Samīn reports an interpretation about facing the jailer or enduring imprisonment. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(بعضهم)]

## 12:33/q03 What does al-sijn denote?  [ٱلسِّجْنُ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the noun denotes the place or the act, and whether an omitted 'entering' is supplied.
- 12:33/q03/p1 With kasra it names the prison or place of confinement, with staying or entering understood. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Ṭabarī allow a place noun to stand for a verbal noun. Al-Zajjāj contrasts place and act. Al-Wāḥidī says a place itself cannot literally be preferred, so entering must be supplied or the word taken as a verbal noun. Al-Ṭabrisī defines prison as confinement restricting action. [11 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر,الزجاج); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والنحاس); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:33/q03/p2 It means imprisonment itself, a verbal noun or a place form used for the act. — Al-Farrāʾ permits the place form to substitute for the verbal noun; with fatḥa the act is unambiguous. Al-Wāḥidī reports this option from him; al-Samīn calls the subject imprisonment. [6 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء); SAMIN-DURR; QIRAAT-ER]

## 12:33/q04 How is al-sijn read?  [ٱلسِّجْنُ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted vowel and the authority assigned to the minority reading.
- 12:33/q04/p1 The majority read kasra on sīn, the noun for prison. — Al-Ṭabarī rejects fatḥa as contrary to the authoritative readers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nashr and Ibn ʿĀshūr give the majority reading. [7 notes: TAB-FULL; THALABI(يعقوب); BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب); IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(يعقوب); NASHR(يعقوب); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 12:33/q04/p2 Several readers, especially Yaʿqūb, read fatḥa on sīn as the verbal noun 'imprisonment'. — Reports name ʿUthmān, his servant Ṭāriq, Zayd b. ʿAlī, al-Zuhrī, Ibn Abī Isḥāq, Ibn Hurmuz, al-Aʿraj and Yaʿqūb, with attribution varying. Ibn al-Jawzī says Yaʿqūb reads fatḥa only here. Al-Ṭabarī rejects this reading on majority grounds. [17 notes: THALABI(يعقوب); NAHHAS; BAGHAWI-FULL(يعقوب); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(يعقوب); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(يعقوب); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عثمان بن عفان); BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عثمان ومولاه طارق وزيد بن علي والزهري وابن أبي إسحاق وابن هرمز ويعقوب); SAMIN-DURR(عثمان ومولاه طارق وزيد بن علي والزهري وابن أبي إسحاق وابن هرمز ويعقوب); NASHR(يعقوب); ALUSI-FULL(عثمان); IBNASHUR-FULL; QIRAAT-ER; TAB-FULL-]

## 12:33/q05 How does 'prison is dearer' express Joseph's choice?  [ٱلسِّجْنُ أَحَبُّ إِلَىَّ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether aḥabb is literal considered preference, idiom for the lesser evil, or a strategic reply to pressure.
- 12:33/q05/p1 It is a genuine preference for enduring prison over sin, grounded in reflection on their outcomes. — Al-Kashshāf weighs patience and God's reward against disobedience; al-Rāzī contrasts worldly blame and afterlife punishment with worldly praise and lasting reward. Bayḍāwī notes his natural aversion to prison. Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls the preference genuine and a performative acceptance of obedience; Study Quran contrasts fleeting pleasure with felicity. Al-Jubbāʾī speaks of preparing to endure prison. [25 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(ابن اسحاق,السدي); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي الجبائي); RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(يوسف); DURR-FULL(يوسف); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 12:33/q05/p2 It is an idiomatic comparison of two evils: prison is less painful, though neither is loved. — Al-Wāḥidī says the comparative is conditional on having to choose; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Qurṭubī say prison is easier to bear. Al-Rāzī calls imprisonment a wrong but the lesser evil. Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn deny a literal comparative; Biqāʿī sees deliberate paradox, since no one ordinarily loves prison. Ālūsī says a brief hardship leads to relief. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:33/q05/p3 The declaration breaks the women's hope that fear of prison will force compliance. — Ālūsī connects the wording to their threats; Ibn ʿĀshūr says concerted social pressure did not break his resolve. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:33/q06 What does ilayya do after aḥabb?  [أَحَبُّ إِلَىَّ · grammar]
- 12:33/q06/p1 Ilā identifies the person to whom something is dear in an elative or expression of love and dislike. — Ibn Mālik calls it explanatory; al-Samīn says ilayya depends on aḥabb. Al-Burhān also reports endpoint and additive senses elsewhere, and Ibn ʿAṣfūr's 'side' analysis, disputed by Ibn Ḥayyān. [6 notes: BURHAN(ابن عصفور,ابن مالك); ITQAN(ابن مالك); SAMIN-DURR]

## 12:33/q07 Who invited Joseph, and what did they ask of him?  [يَدْعُونَنِىٓ إِلَيْهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural marks one woman's speech, collective pressure to serve her, or separate invitations from each woman.
- 12:33/q07/p1 The Aziz's wife sought illicit intercourse; plural speech refers indirectly to her alone. — Al-Māwardī says the plural may honor or avoid naming her; al-Baghawī and al-Qurṭubī report this. Ibn al-Jawzī cites Arabic plural for a single ship as analogy. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:33/q07/p2 The gathered women urged him to comply with the Aziz's wife, threatening prison and shaming his refusal. — Wahb says she enlisted them; al-Ḥasan says they told him to obey their mistress; al-Rāzī lists beauty, wealth, gifts, seduction, threats and fear of violence. Ṭabāṭabāʾī favors this from the later words about not betraying her in absence. Study Quran reports their ultimatum. [20 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(وهب); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الحسن); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(R); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; TABATABAI+]
- 12:33/q07/p3 Each woman privately invited Joseph to herself. — ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn reports private messages requesting visits; another account says the wife let each woman meet him alone. Al-Qurṭubī says they used advice as a pretext. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 'incline toward them' suggests personal invitations but the verse does not decide. [9 notes: TABRISI(علي بن الحسين); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(علي بن الحسين); TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين)]
- 12:33/q07/p4 The plural can refer generally to the women's collective desire, or to the gathered women joining the wife. — Ibn ʿĀshūr links this plural to the later plural 'their plot' and 12:28; al-Māwardī and Ibn al-Jawzī report both broader and narrower possibilities. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 12:33/q08 What is the form of yadʿūnanī?  [يَدْعُونَنِىٓ · grammar]
- 12:33/q08/p1 It is the feminine plural verb pattern yafʿalna, distinct from masculine plural yadʿūna. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya draws the contrast. Al-Itqān describes nūn al-wiqāya before first-person yāʾ after verbs and particles. [2 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ITQAN]

## 12:33/q09 How is the possessive yāʾ in yadʿūnanī ilayhi recited?  [يَدْعُونَنِىٓ إِلَيْهِ · readings]
- 12:33/q09/p1 Readers agree on quiescence of this first-person yāʾ. — Al-Nashr counts it among nine such yāʾs; some explain the quiescence by successive heavy letters, while others give other explanations. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 12:33/q10 How does Joseph's prayer connect to the surrounding episode?  [* · links]
- 12:33/q10/p1 The wife's renewed threat and the women's growing pressure prompt his appeal; God then answers and averts their guile. — Al-Ṭabarī implies renewed seduction. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reads the next verse as the answer. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the group ordeal was harder than the private encounter at 12:23, so Joseph now prayed; God hears and knows their plotting. His imprisonment later brings him toward the king and Egyptian authority. [9 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 12:33/q10/p2 The story models chastity and divine guidance through a severe trial. — Ibn Kathīr cites the report of seven shaded by God, including one refusing a powerful beautiful woman. Kuranyolu identifies a model for virtuous youth; Asad contrasts the Qur'anic episodes absent from Genesis and sees God's surprising direction of affairs, citing 2:216. [6 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم); ASAD-NOTES; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 12:33/q11 What is the women's kayd?  [كَيْدَهُنَّ · referent]
turns on: Whether the plural denotes one principal plotter, her helpers, or separate advances.
- 12:33/q11/p1 It is the wife's attempt to procure the sexual sin. — Al-Māwardī reports this; al-Dāmghānī glosses kayd as plot or guile and links 12:34. [2 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; DAMGHANI]
- 12:33/q11/p2 It is the other women's effort to attract Joseph or induce compliance with the wife. — Al-Māwardī reports their attempts at his heart; Ibn Isḥāq calls it the danger Joseph feared. Al-Qurṭubī reports those who urged him to obey her. [4 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(ابن اسحاق); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:33/q11/p3 It is the Aziz's wife alone, with a plural used for stature or indirect reference. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī and al-Qurṭubī give the option; al-Māwardī says plural may honor her or avoid explicit mention. [6 notes: ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL]
- 12:33/q11/p4 It is the wife together with the women who saw Joseph or shared her plan. — Al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī explicitly identify both groups. [4 notes: ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 12:33/q12 What does 'unless You turn away' ask God to do?  [وَإِلَّا تَصْرِفْ عَنِّى كَيْدَهُنَّ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether diversion is protective aid to choice, a decisive divine cause of motivation, or special knowledge.
- 12:33/q12/p1 It implicitly petitions God to avert the scheme and protect Joseph from yielding. — Al-Wāḥidī compares 'unless you obey me, I will punish you,' which implies an imperative. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God granted this request in the next verse. Ibn Isḥāq says Joseph sought aid; Ibn Zayd says he had no protection of his own. [19 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,ابن زيد); IBNABIHATIM(ابن اسحاق,عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن اسلم); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو بكر); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(يوسف); DURR-FULL(ابن زيد); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Qu)]
- 12:33/q12/p2 God's subtle aid strengthens an active resolve to resist. — Al-Kashshāf and al-Ṭabrisī specify luṭf, since the guile had already occurred; Jishumī connects the plea to God's subtle aid and says people should seek protection during affliction. Bayḍāwī calls it strengthening his guard. Al-Kashshāf explicitly denies compulsion. [6 notes: JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود)]
- 12:33/q12/p3 Protection is continuously bestowed divine knowledge that preserves free choice. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the conditional shows ongoing dependence; 12:34 describes a fresh act. Knowledge of wrongdoing as ignorance, injustice and treachery guides Joseph; unlike ordinary knowledge it reliably changes conduct without forcing choice, as certain knowledge of a deadly drink does. [13 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:33/q13 What does the plea imply about God's action and human ability to avoid sin?  [وَإِلَّا تَصْرِفْ عَنِّى كَيْدَهُنَّ · theology]
turns on: Whether divine aid supplies decisive motives, helps voluntary resistance, or names a natural vulnerability.
- 12:33/q13/p1 A person escapes sin only through God's protection and enabling. — Al-Wāḥidī generalizes the prayer; Abū Ḥayyān and Study Quran say only God averts evil. Al-Rāzī says overpowering incentives required God to create countervailing motives; his Ashʿarī colleagues say the dominant motive is preferred by God. Qushayrī reads it through tawḥīd; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says created causes have no independent power. [11 notes: QUSHAYRI; WAHIDI-BASIT; RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(Qu)]
- 12:33/q13/p2 God's help works through voluntary choice and sustained insight, without compulsion. — Al-Kashshāf says prophets pray while resolving patience, not asking for compelled chastity; al-Ṭabrisī invokes luṭf. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says infallible choice remains voluntary; Holy Spirit, faith and resistance to Satan operate through agency. He rejects a competing divine will or angel overpowering inclination as contrary to experience and monotheism. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; TABATABAI; TABATABAI-]
- 12:33/q13/p3 His plea acknowledges a natural impulse and calls desperately for rescue. — Abū al-Suʿūd gives this explanation; Ālūsī notes al-Quṭb and others call it a Muʿtazilī evasion of an Ashʿarī inference. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو السعود)]

## 12:33/q14 How does illā work in this conditional?  [وَإِلَّا · grammar]
turns on: Whether illā here is the conditional contraction or explained through general restriction.
- 12:33/q14/p1 It is conditional in plus negative lā, 'if not' or 'unless,' with assimilation of n to lām. — Ālūsī says aṣbu is jussive as the consequent; the conditional follows a nominal declaration that conveys lasting preference. Al-Burhān distinguishes this construction from exceptive illā. [3 notes: BURHAN; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:33/q14/p2 Illā can instead be the exception particle or a general restriction to one state or item. — Al-Rummānī's broad account includes excluding alternatives, absence, presence or a particular circumstance. [1 notes: BURHAN(الرماني)]

## 12:33/q15 What does aṣbu ilayhinna mean?  [أَصْبُ إِلَيْهِنَّ · meaning]
turns on: Whether ṣabā conveys inclination, practical compliance, or the more intense ṣabābah.
- 12:33/q15/p1 I incline toward, long for, or turn toward the women through desire. — Abū ʿUbayda and al-Ṭabarī cite poetry about a heart inclining toward Hind; al-Thaʿlabī and al-Māwardī repeat it. Al-Zajjāj connects ṣabā to amusement; al-Kashshāf and al-Nasafī connect ṣabā to the pleasant east wind. Al-Rāghib adds yearning and childlike action; al-Majmaʿ describes softness of passion. Asad combines inclination, yearning and amorous indulgence. Abū Ḥayyān stresses inclination alone does not entail the forbidden act. [31 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; THALABI(يزيد بن ضبة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الطبري); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(أهل اللغة); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة); BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(الراغب,المجمع); ASAD-NOTES]
- 12:33/q15/p2 I follow, comply with, or yield to the women. — Qatāda and Ibn ʿAbbās gloss the consequence as following or complying; al-Māwardī includes both compliance and inclination. [5 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة); DURR-FULL(قتادة)]
- 12:33/q15/p3 I yearn intensely, as passion pours toward its object. — The minority derivation is from ṣabābah; al-Samīn likens it to water poured toward what is loved. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(فرقة); ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:33/q16 How is aṣbu read and derived?  [أَصْبُ · readings]
turns on: The consonant doubling and derivation.
- 12:33/q16/p1 The common reading has a light b and derives from ṣabā yaṣbū. — Al-Samīn and Abū Ḥayyān connect it with ṣabwah, desire or play. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 12:33/q16/p2 A group doubles b, deriving the word from ṣabābah, intense yearning. — Al-Samīn explains the image as love poured like water; al-Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī mention the variant. [5 notes: SAMIN-DURR(فرقة); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]

## 12:33/q17 What does 'among the ignorant' mean?  [ٱلْجَٰهِلِينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether ignorance is sinful conduct, unused knowledge, or foolishness.
- 12:33/q17/p1 Those who sin and disregard God's rights, limits and commands. — Al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Isḥāq identify disobedience; Ibn ʿAbbās says sinners and offenders; al-Baghawī and ʿAmr b. Murra say even knowing sinners are ignorant during the act. Bursevī calls adulterers lying ignoramuses. [9 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNABIHATIM(ابن اسحاق); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; DURR-FULL(عمرو بن مرة); BURSEVI]
- 12:33/q17/p2 Those who possess knowledge but fail to act on it. — Al-Kashshāf says they become like the ignorant; al-Nasafī and Ālūsī repeat the contrast. Bursevī says such knowledge has no practical value; Abū Ḥayyān calls it unbeneficial knowledge. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 12:33/q17/p3 Foolish or unwise people, deserving blame for irrational misconduct. — Al-Māwardī's people of meanings say 'deserving blame'; al-Ṭabrisī allows 'acting as though ignorant.' Al-Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Bursevī and Ālūsī say a wise person would not do this; a poetic line uses ignorance for folly. Ibn ʿĀshūr adds lack of forbearance. [10 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(أهل المعاني); KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:33/q18 Why say 'one of the ignorant' instead of simply 'ignorant'?  [وَأَكُن مِّنَ ٱلْجَٰهِلِينَ · rhetoric]
- 12:33/q18/p1 Joining their class is stronger than merely bearing the quality. — Ibn ʿĀshūr compares 'one of the disgraced' at 12:32. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 12:33/q19 Was Joseph asking to be imprisoned, and how was his prayer answered?  [ٱلسِّجْنُ وَإِلَّا تَصْرِفْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the first clause constitutes a request for prison or prepares the actual request for protection.
- 12:33/q19/p1 The declaration is a prayer or choice for imprisonment, and he was tested with what he asked for. — A report says God told him he confined himself; Ibn ʿUyayna's ant and rock report says prayer accords with decree and Joseph should have trusted God's care. Bayḍāwī cites Prophetic correction of one asking for patience; Baghawī and Qurṭubī say asking for well-being was preferable. Ibn ʿAbbās counts this desire as one of three slips, alongside asking the cupbearer and calling his brothers thieves. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عيينة); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); DURR-FULL(ابن عيينة); ALUSI-FULL(القرطبي); TABATABAI-]
- 12:33/q19/p2 He states his preference, then asks only that God avert their guile; the answer was that diversion. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says 12:34 explicitly names the diverted plot, while imprisonment was decided only later; Imam al-Sajjād's report places his refusal before the conditional plea. Ibn ʿAṭāʾ says he sought only escape from adultery and that leaving the means to God might have avoided trial. [8 notes: ALUSI-FULL(ابن عطاء); TABATABAI; TABATABAI+]

## 12:33/q20 What inward meaning is drawn from Joseph's choice and dependence?  [* · inward]
turns on: Whether the inward focus is the heart's vulnerability, prison's spiritual use, or love that overcomes temptation.
- 12:33/q20/p1 The heart needs continual divine protection from the soul's enduring attraction. — Qāshānī describes the heart's two faces toward spirit and soul; temporary illumination cannot secure it. He cites the Prophet's prayer for firmness and the feather turned by wind. Qushayrī sees choice followed by trial that proves truth. [6 notes: QASHANI(عليه السلام); QUSHAYRI]
- 12:33/q20/p2 Joseph preferred prison as intimacy and seclusion with God over distance caused by the women's invitation. — Ālūsī reports the view that prison allows conversation, witnessing and closeness. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 12:33/q20/p3 He sought prison to hide from Zulaykhā and redirect her love into spiritual love. — Ālūsī reports this separate inward interpretation. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 12:33/q20/p4 Divine love eclipsed sensual attraction and made Joseph seek only God. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī contrasts the outward contest with an inward one; Joseph did not invoke status, lineage, reward or punishment but God's presence alone, unlike Mary's phrasing at 19:18. Study Quran says human love remained real but illicit beside God's Love. [6 notes: TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN]

## 12:33/q21 What grammatical analogies for feminine and proper-name plurals appear with the verse's feminine plural?  [يَدْعُونَنِىٓ كَيْدَهُنَّ · grammar]
- 12:33/q21/p1 Arabic pluralizes names by sound or attested broken patterns, with form and established usage controlling the choice. — Sībawayh gives Zayd, ʿAmr, Bakr, Qays, Saʿd, Kaʿb and other names; women may take -āt or broken plurals, with dialectal heavy and light tāʾ. Names with tāʾ marbūṭa avoid masculine -ūn; Warqāʾ follows adjective patterns. He surveys Ibn, Ism, Imruʾ, Umm, Shāh, Ḍarb, Rubba, Ẓuba, ʿIdda, Shafa, Ama, Burda, faʿīl/faʿīla names, participles and ʿAjūz, preserving attested forms. Jarīr, Ruʾba, al-Farazdaq, Laqīṭ and others supply poetic witnesses; Yūnus and al-Khalīl attest frequency. He rejects unestablished forms such as ḥumr for Aḥmar and shafāt/umāt, and uses diminutives to support ʿUthmānūn rather than ʿathāmīn. [40 notes: SIBAWAYH(الخليل,الشاعر,الفرزدق,القتال الكلابي,جرير,رؤبة,لقيط بن زرارة,يونس والخليل)]

## 12:33/q22 What can waw do before a verb in a nonaffirmative construction?  [وَأَكُن · grammar]
- 12:33/q22/p1 It may introduce a subjunctive verb with an implied an, coordinate, or begin an independent raised verb, with different meanings. — Sībawayh says subjunctive waw can prohibit or require the combination of two acts, unlike jussive separation; waw alone does not assert sequence as fa does. His examples include al-Akhṭal, al-Ḥuṭayʾa, Durayd, al-Aʿshā, Kaʿb al-Ghanawī and Qays b. Zuhayr. 'Visit me and I visit you' distinguishes a committed raised clause from joint prospective action; noun plus verb under aḥabb requires implied an. Some examples admit raised attachment to a noun or relative clause. [13 notes: SIBAWAYH(الأخطل,الأعشى,الحطيئة,دريد بن الصمة,قيس بن زهير بن جذيمة,كعب الغنوي)]

## 12:33/q23 What wider principles of Qur'an recitation and pause are recorded alongside this verse?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The aims of recitation, pause criteria, and rival explanations or strength of the completion report.
- 12:33/q23/p1 Measured recitation with reflection is preferable to speed and volume. — Al-Nashr prefers understanding and practice; Mujāhid values the reader of less text with equal prayer movements. Early Muslims repeated one verse until dawn; al-Ghazālī says deliberate reading moves even a non-Arabic reader's heart. One scholar distinguishes reward quality from reward count, and another distinguishes taḥqīq for teaching from tartīl for reflection. [6 notes: NASHR(أبو حامد الغزالي,بعض أئمتنا,بعضهم,مجاهد); NASHR+]
- 12:33/q23/p2 More recitation can yield more merit by the number of letters. — Al-Nashr reports ten good deeds per letter, ʿUthmān's complete recitation in one prayer unit and abundant early practice. [1 notes: NASHR]
- 12:33/q23/p3 Pauses are classified by completeness of sense and syntactic connection; verse endings may be paused at by Prophetic practice. — Al-Nashr distinguishes complete, adequate, good and ugly pauses, criticizing overly elaborate schemes; Umm Salama reports verse-by-verse pausing, with Abū ʿAmr and al-Bayhaqī preferring it. Al-Itqān reports Nāfiʿ's semantic pauses, Ibn Kathīr and Ḥamza's breath pauses with three exceptions, ʿĀṣim and al-Kisāʾī's complete-statement pauses, and Abū ʿAmr's verse endings. [16 notes: NASHR(أم سلمة); ITQAN(أبو عمرو,أم سلمة,ابن كثير,بعضهم,عاصم والكسائي,نافع); ITQAN(البيهقي وآخرون)+; NASHR(أبو عمرو والبيهقي)+]
- 12:33/q23/p4 Recitation chains, completion rites and supplications have their own transmitted practices and disputed reports. — Al-Nashr gives chain heights for Qur'an and hadith; al-Burhān discusses completion by opening al-Fātiḥa and al-Baqara and differing verse counts. The ḥāl murtaḥil report is explained as returning to the Qur'an after finishing; Abū Shāma favors an original campaigning sense and questions Ṣāliḥ al-Murrī, while al-Nashr cites other routes and rejects sole dependence on him. Completion supplication and prostration are reported from Aḥmad and Ibn al-Mubārak, with cited prayers for guidance, relief, sound life and forgiveness. [33 notes: NASHR(أبو الشيخ ابن حيان,أبو شامة,أبو عمرو,أبو محمد مكي,أبي بن كعب,أحمد بن حنبل,ابن عباس,البيهقي,السخاوي,الطبراني,النبي ﷺ,صاحب الفردوس,عبد الله بن المبارك); BURHAN(الترمذي,قيل); NASHR+]

## 12:33/q24 What wider lessons about moral restraint are drawn from Joseph's ordeal?  [* · theology]
turns on: What grounds restraint and whether motives of fear, hope or love best explain it.
- 12:33/q24/p1 Law requires virtue and monotheistic awareness to restrain secret wrongdoing. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī traces righteous action through character and faith, comparing tawḥīd, virtues and deeds to a tree. Mutual dependence needs rights, fair distribution, penalties and enforcement; despotism, selfishness, colonial exploitation and empty moral slogans undermine them. Belief in an omniscient judging God provides inward restraint where reputation fails; Joseph's private trial exemplifies it. [14 notes: TABATABAI]
- 12:33/q24/p2 Piety can arise from fear, hope or love, with love orienting all action to God. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites worldly deception and afterlife judgment for fear and hope, then describes worship because God is worthy. Divine beauty evokes love; created beauty points beyond itself. Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq is reported to call religion love. Lovers see events as signs, accept affliction, avoid evil and gain knowledge, sincerity and purification. Infallibility is divine knowledge while choice remains intact. [16 notes: TABATABAI]

## 12:33/q25 What other lexical, narrative and Qur'anic notes accompany these excerpts?  [* · interpretation]
- 12:33/q25/p1 Protection is resistance to sin, sought from God, and Joseph's earlier refuge credits God as the source of care. — Jishumī defines istiʿṣām by God's protection, citing the verse denying a protector from God's command. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Joseph's household belongs to God, his earlier 'Lord' means God, and his refusal of the wife reflects God's care, not the husband's independent power. A report recounts the shirt test, women's gathering and eventual imprisonment. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says faith unmixed with injustice brings security (6:82), so Joseph sought refuge against sin; his saying God made his dwelling good attributes benefit to God, while calling it honor could imply personal status. [7 notes: JISHUMI; TABATABAI(علي بن الحسين); TABATABAI+]
- 12:33/q25/p2 The Qur'an is a source of guidance and blessing, and recitation carries duties. — Al-Itqān's reports call it a response to tribulation, dearer than creation, a protector before sleep, a source of good in homes and sufficiency; a reciter should avoid anger and ignorance. Other reports promise relief from terror and use the image of a fireproof hide, explained as the believer's heart. [9 notes: ITQAN(أبو عبيد,أبو هريرة,أنس,ابن عمر,شداد بن أوس,عبد الله بن عمرو,عقبة بن عامر,علي)]
- 12:33/q25/p3 Other grammatical examples define attention particles and a separate narrative report locates an episode at night. — Al-Burhān says alā draws attention and confirms a following oath-like clause; al-Itqān cites al-Qāḍī Jalāl al-Dīn on women going out at night, supported by ʿĀʾisha's slander report. [2 notes: BURHAN; ITQAN(القاضي جلال الدين)]
