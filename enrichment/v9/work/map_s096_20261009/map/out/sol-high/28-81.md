# 28:81: 31 questions

## 28:81/q01 What does the initial ف connect or explain?  [فَخَسَفْنَا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the particle emphasizes moral causation or sequence in the surrounding scene.
- 28:81/q01/p1 The sinking follows Qarun's arrogance and false claim of independent wealth. — Razi ties the fāʾ causally to his arrogance, insolence and excess. Biqāʿī connects it to the reasoning that led to disbelief. Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes it the outcome of wrongdoing, while the next fāʾ makes his lack of rescuers a consequence exposing his false independence. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:81/q01/p2 The fāʾ marks immediate sequence after Qarun's proud display and the crowd's wish to be like him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the scholars had just corrected the onlookers, who then witnessed the punishment. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:81/q02 What does خسف mean in this verse?  [فَخَسَفْنَا ٱلْأَرْضَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the event is understood as physical subsidence or as a possible metaphor for ruin.
- 28:81/q02/p1 God made Qarun and his surroundings sink into the earth. — Jishumī defines sinking of earth with what is on it; Qurṭubī distinguishes a place sinking from God causing someone to disappear into it and permits active and passive descriptions. Bursawī adds uses for the moon's lost light and a dulled eye; Thaʿlab distinguishes solar eclipse and lunar sinking; Qurṭubī adds diminution. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it inversion of the earth's surface by earthquake force, cites 16:45, and says eclipse is figurative. Wāḥidī refers to his discussion in al-Isrāʾ; Ālūsī says such sinking is possible in principle and occurrence. [9 notes: JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL(ثعلب); BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:81/q02/p2 The swallowing may figuratively describe sudden catastrophic loss of Qarun's wealth and grandeur. — Asad explicitly allows this metaphorical reading. [1 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 28:81/q03 How is the verb خسفنا read?  [فَخَسَفْنَا · readings]
turns on: The variant's active or passive voice; Abū ʿAlī says both convey the same event.
- 28:81/q03/p1 It is read actively with fatḥ on khāʾ and sīn. — Ṭabrisī names Ḥafṣ from ʿĀṣim, Yaʿqūb, Sahl, al-Ḥasan, al-Aʿraj, Shayba and Mujāhid. Abū ʿAlī says the active form follows the preceding mention of God. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي,الحسن والأعرج وشيبة ومجاهد,حفص عن عاصم ويعقوب وسهل)]
- 28:81/q03/p2 It is also read in the passive, with ḍamma on khāʾ and kasra on sīn. — Ṭabrisī assigns it to the remaining readers; Abū ʿAlī says the meaning is the same. [2 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي,الباقون)]

## 28:81/q04 How does the bāʾ in به وبداره work?  [بِهِۦ وَبِدَارِهِ · grammar]
turns on: Whether bāʾ is analyzed as transitivizing or as accompaniment.
- 28:81/q04/p1 It makes the verb transitive to Qarun and his house. — Bursawī says the preposition bāʾ supplies transitivity. [1 notes: BURSEVI]
- 28:81/q04/p2 It marks accompaniment: the earth sank together with Qarun and his house. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explicitly calls the bāʾ one of accompaniment. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:81/q05 Whom do به and داره refer to?  [بِهِۦ وَبِدَارِهِ · referent]
- 28:81/q05/p1 Both pronouns refer to Qarun. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī makes the reference explicit. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 28:81/q06 What events led to Qarun's destruction?  [فَخَسَفْنَا بِهِۦ · reports]
turns on: The traditions give different immediate provocations: slander, a prayer contest, priestly rebellion, rejection of zakat, or general arrogance.
- 28:81/q06/p1 After opposing Moses and resisting zakat, Qarun bribed a woman to accuse Moses falsely; she confessed and Moses appealed to God. — ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Ḥārith, Ibn ʿAbbās and al-Suddī give the plot; al-Suddī calls her Shirta and says she received 2,000 dirhams; Ṭabrisī says 2,000 and marked money. Thaʿlabī's variants give 1,000 dirhams, 1,000 dinars, a gold basin, or her price; Ālūsī gives 1,000 dinars or a gold basin of gold and says al-Ḥākim graded Ibn ʿAbbās's account sound. Wāḥidī, Bayḍāwī and Nasafī note Qarun's dismay at a levy of one per thousand and his claim Moses wanted Israelite wealth. Ibn ʿAbbās says Qarun was Moses' cousin, knowledgeable and jealous; the woman affirmed Moses' prophethood, sometimes swearing by the sea-splitting God. Moses prayed, sometimes prostrate, and received command over the earth. Abū Ḥayyān says God knows the long story best. Ṭabarī's Ibn ʿAbbās report cites 28:79, 23:117, 28:83; ʿAbd Allāh's report cites 27:38, 27:40. [26 notes: MUJAHID(عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الحارث); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي,عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل الهاشمي); THALABI(ابن عباس); TABRISI(السدي); RAZI-FULL(قيل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABATABAI(ابن عباس,ابن نوفل الهاشمي); STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ); IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 28:81/q06/p2 Qarun's conspicuous parade led to a prayer contest with Moses, whose prayer brought the sinking. — ʿAṭāʾ says he wore purple with 4,000 attendants to distract from Moses' teaching. Ibn Kathīr's alternative has gray mules and servants, with Qarun challenging Moses to mutual prayers. [2 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قول غير مسمى)]
- 28:81/q06/p3 His rebellion grew from rejecting the garment tassel command and resenting Aaron's priesthood; he dismissed the staff sign as magic. — Baghawī's historians say God ordered four blue threads at garment corners as reminders of revelation; Moses suggested green clothing, and God warned small disobedience foretells great. After the sea crossing Aaron received priesthood; Qarun demanded proof, then dismissed Aaron's sprouting almond staff. Thaʿlabī says Qarun called the event sorcery; Biqāʿī relays these reports and notes Qarun's boasts and grave accusation. [13 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل العلم بالأخبار); THALABI(قارون); BIQAI-FULL(أهل العلم بالأخبار)]
- 28:81/q06/p4 Korah and 250 leaders challenged Moses and Aaron over priestly authority; Moses said the earth opening would prove God's choice. — Biqāʿī's Numbers account identifies Korah as Qarun son of Izhar, Kohath and Levi, with Dathan, Abiram and On; the 250 incense bearers claim all Israel holy. Moses says God will show the rightful priest, reminds Levites of their privilege, and denies having wronged them. Dathan and Abiram reject his summons, charging that he brought them from a land of milk and honey to wilderness without promised farms and vineyards. Moses predicts ordinary death would disprove his mission. Ibn ʿĀshūr and Ṭabāṭabāʾī cite Numbers 16; Iṣlāḥī places a suppressed mutual imprecation after the Sāmirī episode by the worship tent. [11 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(سفر العدد); TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:81/q06/p5 Qarun mocked and expelled Moses when told the zakat ruling, then God sank him. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī attributes this to al-Qummī and regards the report as mawqūf with objectionable material. [1 notes: TABATABAI(القمي)]
- 28:81/q06/p6 His arrogance, disbelief and wrongdoing brought punishment. — Rāzī links the fāʾ to arrogance, insolence and excess; Biqāʿī links ruin to his reasoning toward disbelief. Ṭabāṭabāʾī stresses his injustice to Israelites, rather than only injury to Moses, and his acquired know-how as apparent means of wealth. Jishumī calls the sinking punishment; Kuran Yolu mentions disobedience, injustice and transgression. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; JISHUMI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:81/q07 What penalties did Moses state when accused?  [فَخَسَفْنَا بِهِۦ · law]
- 28:81/q07/p1 The reported law punished married adultery by stoning, including if Moses himself were guilty. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim transmits Moses' answer in al-Suddī's and Ibn ʿAbbās's accounts; Ibn ʿAbbās also names cutting off a thief's hand. Thaʿlabī adds 80 lashes for false accusation and 100 for an unmarried adulterer; Nasafī says these penalties were announced at the festival before the staged accusation. [4 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,موسى); THALABI(موسى); NASAFI-FULL]

## 28:81/q08 How did the earth swallow Qarun?  [فَخَسَفْنَا بِهِۦ وَبِدَارِهِ ٱلْأَرْضَ · reports]
turns on: The narratives differ in physical sequence and in whether they describe Moses' repeated commands or the earth opening at once.
- 28:81/q08/p1 At Moses' command the earth swallowed Qarun and his companions by stages despite their pleas. — Versions vary: ankles, knees, waist, navel, neck and then complete engulfment; some say a throne or bed sank first, and some explicitly include the house and treasures. Muqātil says Qarun invoked kinship; Yazīd al-Raqāshī adds Moses striking Qarun's face with sandals; some narrations count 70 pleas. Qatāda says God lent Moses command for an hour. The woman’s confessed plot often precedes the prayer. Ṭabarī includes Solomon's story at 27:38 and 27:40 by comparison. [35 notes: MUJAHID(عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل); MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس,عبد الله بن الحارث); ZAJJAJ(التفسير); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي,سعيد بن جبير,عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل الهاشمي,عبد الله بن عوف القاري,عطاء,قتادة,يزيد الرقاشي); THALABI(ابن عباس,موسى); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس,يزيد الرقاشي); BAGHAWI-FULL(وفي رواية); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي,قول غير مسمى); DURR-FULL(عون بن عبد الله القاري); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(ابن عباس); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:81/q08/p2 The earth opened and the rebels, households, livestock and property descended alive; fire consumed 250 incense bearers. — Biqāʿī quotes Numbers: Moses had Israel move away from the tents and touch no property; Israel fled when the earth closed. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites Numbers 16. Iṣlāḥī cites Numbers 16:27–34. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(سفر العدد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:81/q09 Who was caught in the sinking with Qarun?  [بِهِۦ وَبِدَارِهِ · referent]
turns on: The narratives differ over which associates are named and how the 250 died.
- 28:81/q09/p1 Qarun's immediate companions and supporters were swallowed with him. — Ṭabarī says his companions were in his house, shared hypocrisy and supported harming Moses. Ibn ʿĀshūr says this was localized, not a wide-area subsidence, and that Levite chiefs who supported his challenge perished. The reports variously call them followers, people, or household; Biqāʿī's Torah narrative adds households and livestock but says fire killed 250 incense bearers. [10 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن الحارث); IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; MUJAHID(عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل); IBNABIHATIM(عطاء); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قول غير مسمى)]
- 28:81/q09/p2 Korah's named fellow rebels were Dathan, Abiram and On, with 250 Israelite leaders. — Biqāʿī cites the Torah names and the 250 leaders' priesthood challenge; its account distinguishes those engulfed from incense bearers consumed by fire. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:81/q10 When and why was Qarun's house swallowed?  [وَبِدَارِهِ · reports]
turns on: Whether the house fell with Qarun or later in response to suspicion about Moses' motives.
- 28:81/q10/p1 His house and wealth sank three days later after Israelites suspected Moses wanted Qarun's estate. — Muqātil says the delay and loss of the estate ended the accusation; Thaʿlabī, Māwardī, Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Ibn al-Jawzī and Qurṭubī relay it, often saying Moses prayed again. Ṭabrisī says nothing was recovered. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Baghawī and Ālūsī also give the suspicion and subsequent prayer without an exact interval. [11 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; MAWARDI-FULL(مقاتل); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); TABRISI(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(مقاتل); QURTUBI-FULL(مقاتل); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BAGHAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:81/q10/p2 Qarun's house was swallowed together with him and his possessions. — Some versions explicitly say the house and treasures sank at Moses' staged command; Ālūsī records simultaneous sinking as an alternative and says the house had gold plates. Qarun's house is described with a gold door and gold-plated walls, frequented by Israelite leaders. Biqāʿī says its size matched his wealth and many occupants, precluding a reading that Moses looted it; al-Qummī's report says God sank Qarun and his house. [13 notes: TAB-FULL(عبد الله بن الحارث); IBNABIHATIM(السدي,عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل الهاشمي); THALABI; BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل العلم بالأخبار); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); DURR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI(القمي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:81/q11 How far and for how long did Qarun sink?  [فَخَسَفْنَا بِهِۦ ٱلْأَرْضَ · reports]
turns on: Different transmitted depths, daily measures and endpoints conflict; Rāzī questions both physical possibility and the reliability of isolated reports.
- 28:81/q11/p1 He sank to the seventh, lowest earth. — Ibn ʿAbbās identifies the earth or the depth as the seventh or lowest. Rāzī reports al-Qāḍī's view that such depth, or less, is possible if treated as deterrent rhetorical exaggeration. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); DURR-FULL(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(القاضي)]
- 28:81/q11/p2 He descends a man's height or stature each day until Resurrection without reaching the bottom. — Muqātil, Qatāda, Samura, Mālik b. Dīnār and Ibn Jurayj recur across sources; the unit is also called one qāma. Some reports specify that Qarun and his people share the descent. Ālūsī says the report is hard to reconcile with philosophers' earth diameter and lack of motion. Rāzī rejects specified rates and endless descent. [17 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(سمرة بن جندب,قتادة); THALABI(قتادة); MAWARDI-FULL(سمرة بن جندب); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(قتادة ومالك بن دينار); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن جريج,قيل); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سمرة بن جندب); DURR-FULL(ابن جريج,سمرة بن جندب,قتادة,مالك بن دينار); ALUSI-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:81/q11/p3 He sinks 100 fathoms each day and continues until Resurrection without reaching earth's bottom. — Ibn Jurayj specifies 100 fathoms; Mālik b. Dīnār repeats the daily 100-fathom measure without adding the endpoint. Rāzī's cited story uses 100 qāma per day, a different measure, and he rejects exact daily measures. [3 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن جريج,مالك بن دينار); RAZI-FULL(قيل); RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:81/q11/p4 He sinks one fathom a day without reaching the bottom before Resurrection. — Ṭabarī reports this from Qatāda; it differs from the 100-fathom and one-stature versions. Rāzī disputes the specified rates. [1 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:81/q11/p5 He will reach the lowest earth at Judgment Day, when the Trumpet is blown. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports Samura's version; Qurṭubī transmits the version that Isrāfīl blows the Trumpet when Qarun reaches the seventh earth's bottom. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(سمرة بن جندب); QURTUBI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:81/q11/p6 The fixed daily and endless-descent details cannot be established. — Rāzī argues any descent must end, exact measures are doubtful, and isolated conflicting reports give no certainty; this is no practical ruling, so the Qur'an suffices and the remainder is left to God. Ibn Kathīr passes over strange Israelite reports. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; RAZI-FULL+]

## 28:81/q12 What authority over the earth did Moses receive?  [فَخَسَفْنَا ٱلْأَرْضَ · reports]
turns on: The reports distinguish a temporary grant to Moses from claims about whether it could recur.
- 28:81/q12/p1 God made the earth obey Moses for the sinking. — Muqātil and many narrative reports say Moses received power to command it; Qatāda limits the obedience to one hour. Ibn ʿĀshūr calls the localized subsidence a miracle for Moses. [14 notes: MUQATIL; IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,سعيد بن جبير,عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل الهاشمي,قتادة); THALABI(ابن عباس); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:81/q12/p2 God declared that the earth would never again obey another person after Moses. — Abū ʿImrān al-Jawnī, Thaʿlabī, Baghawī and Qurṭubī transmit this declaration. In al-Durr, Abū ʿImrān's transmitted wording instead says no one would worship the earth after him. [6 notes: TAB-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني); IBNABIHATIM(أبو عمران الجوني); THALABI(الله سبحانه); BAGHAWI-FULL(بعض الآثار); QURTUBI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أبو عمران الجوني)]

## 28:81/q13 Would God have rescued those who appealed during the sinking?  [فَخَسَفْنَا بِهِۦ · theology]
turns on: Whether an appeal alone sufficed, whether repentance was required, and whether some versions speak of hungry Israelites rather than Qarun.
- 28:81/q13/p1 God would have answered one appeal to Him with mercy, though Moses ignored repeated pleas to himself. — Narratives say Qarun invoked kinship or pleaded up to 70 times; God rebuked Moses' severity, sometimes after Moses struck Qarun, and said one appeal to God would have sufficed. Moses says he acted from anger for God. Some versions refer instead to Israelites in post-sinking hunger: had they called on God rather than Moses, He would have answered. Qurṭubī also relays a strange account of Qarun meeting Jonah in the sea's darkness and saying Moses had refused his entrusted repentance. [24 notes: MUJAHID(عبد الله بن الحارث بن نوفل); TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNABIHATIM(ابن عباس,السدي,سعيد بن جبير,عبد الله بن عوف القاري,يزيد الرقاشي); THALABI(الله تعالى); MAWARDI-FULL(يزيد الرقاشي); BAGHAWI-FULL(الله,وفي رواية); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL(قيل); QURTUBI-FULL(يونس بن ميسرة بن حلبس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(الله); DURR-FULL(عكرمة,عون بن عبد الله القاري); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(ابن عباس)]
- 28:81/q13/p2 Mercy would require Qarun and his followers to repent, rather than merely call while persisting in wrongdoing. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya's transmitted report explicitly allows an appeal or repentance; Rāzī finds an unconditional rescue unlikely because Moses acted by God's command and the rebels remained wrongdoers. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL-]
- 28:81/q13/p3 The unconditional-appeal report is implausible. — Rāzī reasons God would not answer a plea without repentance while the wrongdoing continued and Moses was carrying out God's order. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL+]

## 28:81/q14 How securely are the detailed narratives established?  [فَخَسَفْنَا بِهِۦ · reports]
- 28:81/q14/p1 The reports are isolated, conflicting or otherwise uncertain; the Qur'anic account should anchor interpretation. — Rāzī says additional detail yields no certainty and no legal ruling depends on it. Ibn Kathīr sets aside strange Israelite accounts. Abū Ḥayyān says God knows the long plot story best. Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls the al-Qummī report mawqūf and objectionable and says Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn Nawfal accounts are also mawqūf; he contrasts the plot's injury to Moses with the Qur'an's emphasis on wronging Israelites and acquired know-how. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; TABATABAI; RAZI-FULL+]

## 28:81/q15 How is the pronoun in له read?  [لَهُۥ · readings]
turns on: Assimilation to the preceding vowel versus retention of the original vowel.
- 28:81/q15/p1 The common reading gives the pronoun kasra by assimilation to the preceding kasra. — Samīn al-Ḥalabī identifies the common reading and its phonetic cause. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 28:81/q15/p2 A Hijazi variant retains the pronoun's original ḍamma. — Samīn identifies this as a regional variant. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]

## 28:81/q16 What does فئة mean and how is it derived?  [فِئَةٍ · meaning]
turns on: The shared group sense is explained through differing roots: return, inclination or cutting.
- 28:81/q16/p1 A group or band whose members support one another and can be called upon for help. — Majāz and Ṭabarī cite Khuffāf's poem for a strong, discerning group standing firm in hardship. Ṭabarī and Ibn ʿAṭiyya derive the helping sense from returning for aid; Bursawī and al-Rāghib likewise link it to return, the latter positing an omitted middle radical. Bayḍāwī and one Ālūsī derivation link it to inclination, with an omitted final radical in the latter. Biqāʿī invokes birds returning quickly to their place. Jishumī instead says a common-purpose group is rooted in cutting, with diminutive فئية. Qatāda glosses it as an army; Study Quran cites 8:15–16 for battlefield support. [18 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الراغب); TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:81/q16/p2 It specifically evokes a gathered group united by a common cause. — Jishumī derives the term from cutting and gives the diminutive فئية; this differs from the return and inclination etymologies while sharing the collective sense. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 28:81/q17 How is ما كان له من فئة ينصرونه constructed?  [فَمَا كَانَ لَهُۥ مِن فِئَةٍۢ يَنصُرُونَهُۥ · grammar]
turns on: Whether كان is complete or incomplete, with a corresponding change in the function of له and ينصرونه.
- 28:81/q17/p1 With incomplete كان, من فئة is its subject and له or ينصرونه its predicate. — Samīn al-Ḥalabī gives these two predicate possibilities. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR]
- 28:81/q17/p2 With complete كان, من فئة is the subject, ينصرونه modifies فئة, and من is extra. — Samīn al-Ḥalabī states this analysis. Ālūsī also calls من extra; Biqāʿī notes that it intensifies the negation of any faction. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:81/q18 Why is the absence of any faction stated so forcefully?  [مِن فِئَةٍۢ يَنصُرُونَهُۥ · rhetoric]
- 28:81/q18/p1 The added من emphatically denies even one human faction's rescue, despite a notable person's expected supporters. — Biqāʿī supplies the social expectation; Asad stresses the literal 'no host at all.' Ālūsī treats من as extra in the construction. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:81/q19 Who could have been Qarun's rescuing faction, and why did it fail?  [فِئَةٍۢ يَنصُرُونَهُۥ مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the wording evokes an unavailable army, supporters destroyed with him, or wealth and strength treated as support.
- 28:81/q19/p1 His soldiers, retinue, servants or other supporters could not shield him from God's punishment. — Qatāda glosses faction as army; Muqātil, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bayḍāwī, Nasafī, Ibn Kathīr, Bursawī and Ālūsī state the inability. Ṭabrisī says Qarun had trusted them; Ṭabāṭabāʾī connects their failure to his false confidence in wealth and acquired strength. Iṣlāḥī says his people could not rescue him. [16 notes: MUQATIL; TAB-FULL(قتادة); IBNABIHATIM(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL(قتادة); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:81/q19/p2 The faction that had backed Qarun's challenge perished along with him; God's allies would not plead for him. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says Levite chiefs and others supported the challenge and were caught in the localized subsidence. Biqāʿī says those sharing his outlook perished while those fearing God would not petition for him, referring to 28:80. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:81/q19/p3 His wealth, property and his own strength could supply no help. — Ibn Kathīr includes his wealth, servants and entourage; Jishumī connects this to the preceding warning against false patrons; Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu make it an admonition against trusting wealth and power. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 28:81/q20 What does من دون الله add to the denial of rescue?  [مِن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ · interpretation]
- 28:81/q20/p1 No helper apart from or beyond God's aid could avert His decree. — Bursawī explicitly interprets the phrase as helpers beyond or apart from God's aid; Qatāda, Wāḥidī and Ṭabrisī say no army or faction could protect from God. [4 notes: BURSEVI; TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT; TABRISI]

## 28:81/q21 What does المنتصرين mean here?  [ٱلْمُنتَصِرِينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the failed intisār is self-defense against punishment, retaliation against Moses, or victory with allies.
- 28:81/q21/p1 People able to defend or save themselves against God's sinking. — Thaʿlabī, Baghawī, Wāḥidī, Qurṭubī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bayḍāwī, Bursawī, Biqāʿī, Ṭabāṭabāʾī and Ibn ʿĀshūr apply it to Qarun's inability to resist. Bayḍāwī, Nasafī and Bursawī explain naṣara/intāṣara as helping against an enemy and protecting oneself; Wāḥidī also defines al-muntaṣir as one attaining victory. [16 notes: MUQATIL; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:81/q21/p2 It can mean avenging himself on Moses. — Rāzī and Nasafī explicitly allow this alongside the sense of fending off divine punishment. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 28:81/q21/p3 It can stress that Qarun gained no victory even with helpers. — Ālūsī presents this as an alternative emphasis; Ibn ʿĀshūr defines intasara as obtaining victory. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:81/q22 What wider lesson does Qarun's ruin teach?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The sources emphasize different lessons about riches, reliance, punishment and divine favor.
- 28:81/q22/p1 Worldly wealth and power do not establish independent merit or safety; God's favor and final judgment decide the outcome. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī says Qarun's knowledge-based claim to riches was exposed when wealth and strength failed, showing God granted them. Iṣlāḥī calls him a wealthy Jewish capitalist and says the wretched credit themselves and idols; his fate warns people seduced by riches who forget God and the Hereafter. Kuran Yolu says envious onlookers repented and recognized God's provision and the failure of ingratitude; Biqāʿī warns Muhammad's community against rejecting the mercy of his message, for prophets cannot force guidance or shield the condemned. Jishumī links it to false patrons and leaders giving no help. [8 notes: TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BIQAI-FULL; JISHUMI(قيل)]
- 28:81/q22/p2 The destruction is punishment for wrongdoing. — Jishumī labels the sinking punishment; Rāzī and Biqāʿī tie it to arrogance, disbelief and the reasoning that led Qarun astray. [3 notes: JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:81/q23 How does Qarun's fall connect to nearby verses?  [* · links]
turns on: Which aspect of the surrounding passage supplies the link: the onlookers' reversal or the failure of false patrons.
- 28:81/q23/p1 It reverses the crowd's wish in 28:79 and prepares their regret in 28:82. — Ibn al-Jawzī says the people who wanted Qarun's position regret it in the next verse. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the same onlookers witnessed the immediate punishment. Kuran Yolu says the formerly envious acknowledge God's provision and the failure of the ungrateful. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNASHUR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:81/q23/p2 It demonstrates that following false patrons and leaders brings no protection. — Jishumī states this connection to the preceding lesson; Biqāʿī connects the failure of Qarun's party to God's allies in 28:80, who would not intercede for him. [2 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:81/q24 What does the account imply about divine لطف, the evil eye, and human acts?  [* · theology]
turns on: The theological applications address distinct consequences of the episode.
- 28:81/q24/p1 God can destroy an admired person's worldly gift as لطف, teaching the person and admirers not to rely on it. — Jishumī applies this to Qarun's house and adornment: the deserving person learns favor is temporary and avoids conceit. He reports Abū Hāshim's use of the principle to affirm the evil eye, because the admirer likewise learns not to rely on worldly life. [2 notes: JISHUMI(أبو هاشم)]
- 28:81/q24/p2 The narrative offers indications that people originate their own acts. — Jishumī states this conclusion without further detail in the note. [1 notes: JISHUMI]

## 28:81/q25 What other transmitted events illuminate swallowing or divine signs?  [* · reports]
turns on: The analogues are different narrated events and signs.
- 28:81/q25/p1 Hadith describe a swaggering man swallowed by the earth and continuing to sink until Resurrection. — Ibn Kathīr cites al-Bukhārī's Ibn ʿUmar and Abū Hurayra versions, Aḥmad's Abū Saʿīd version about two green cloaks, whose chain he grades ḥasan and says Aḥmad alone transmits, and Abū Yaʿlā's Anas version about two cloaks. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي سعيد,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أبي هريرة,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن أنس بن مالك,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن ابن عمر)]
- 28:81/q25/p2 A strange report tells of a proud young man in Najran who shrank and was carried off. — Ibn Kathīr says Nawfal b. Musāḥiq reports a handsome, tall youth who claimed God marveled at him, then became a handspan high; a relative carried him away. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(نوفل بن مساحق)]
- 28:81/q25/p3 Biqāʿī compares other prophetic signs: a yearning trunk, Surāqa's sinking horse, and earth rejecting a defector's corpse. — At Ḥunayn the trunk yearned and chose Paradise over restoration. Surāqa's horse sank to its belly three times on the Hijra route; Muhammad spared him and foretold Kisra's bracelets, after which Surāqa embraced Islam. Anas reports a former scribe from Banū al-Najjār whose buried body was expelled twice after defecting to People of the Book; a second Christian-man version has three expulsions. Biqāʿī attributes them to Ṣaḥīḥ collections. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أنس,أنس بن مالك)]

## 28:81/q26 How does the Torah account compare with the Qur'anic account?  [* · links]
turns on: The comparison turns on the rebellion's motive, participants and event sequence.
- 28:81/q26/p1 Numbers 16 supplies a fuller Korah rebellion narrative with priesthood dispute, earthquake and subsequent communal turmoil. — Biqāʿī quotes the tassel reminder from the fourth Torah book, then names Korah's lineage, Dathan, Abiram, On and 250 leaders. Dathan and Abiram accuse Moses of failed promises. Moses makes the earth-opening a test of his mission; households and property descend alive, 250 incense bearers die by fire, and Israel later blames Moses and Aaron. God then threatens the congregation, Aaron carries incense to stop a plague between living and dead, and 14,700 die besides those swallowed. The twelve staffs test the priesthood; Aaron's bears branches, leaves and almonds and is kept as a sign; later Aaron dies on Mount Hor and Eleazar succeeds him. Ibn ʿĀshūr summarizes Numbers 16; Iṣlāḥī says the Qur'an suppresses the mutual imprecation by the worship tent after Sāmirī. [17 notes: BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL(سفر العدد); ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:81/q26/p2 The present Torah's account differs from the Ibn ʿAbbās plot narrative. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī specifically contrasts Numbers 16's named rebels and 250 leaders with the accusation story; he also notes the latter's focus on injury to Moses differs from the Qur'an's stated wrong to Israelites. [2 notes: TABATABAI]

## 28:81/q27 What makes the wording of Qarun's destruction rhetorically striking?  [* · rhetoric]
- 28:81/q27/p1 The story compresses ruin and admonition into exceptionally eloquent, well-ordered language. — Bāqillānī calls the three phrases harmonious and exceptional, saying Qur'anic stories combine narrative, admonition, wisdom, oneness and transcendence more coherently than expanded prose. He locates inimitability in wording, order, composition, clarity and eloquence, citing 3:138, 16:89, 26:195; he says placement and openings and endings move listeners to grief, joy, courage or generosity, and speaker identity shapes reception. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ quotes three words of the ruin as rarer than red sulfur and presents Bāqillānī's balanced order, sound, ease, vividness and demonstrative force. Jurjānī explains grandeur through implied attributes and cites Ziyād al-Aʿjam's poem locating generosity in a patron's dome rather than directly naming the man's traits. [12 notes: BAQILLANI; BINTSHATI-IJAZ; JURJANI-DALAIL]

## 28:81/q28 What recitational change affects the hamza in this verse?  [فِئَةٍ · readings]
- 28:81/q28/p1 A ten-reader variant replaces hamza with yāʾ without changing the meaning. — Qirāʾāt Encyclopedia relates the change to dialectal avoidance of hamza. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:81/q29 What recitation rule applies before ٱلْأَرْضَ?  [ٱلْأَرْضَ · readings]
- 28:81/q29/p1 Transferring the vowel before alif-lām does not restore a preceding long-vowel letter or original sukūn. — Ibn al-Jazarī says reciters agree that the consonant moves and the madd is omitted despite Arabic alternatives; he adduces 20:21, 82:1, 4:59, 30:50, 10:51, 24:32, 4:58, 72:9, 77:16, 80:24, 6:103, 6:136, 15:3 and 3:139. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 28:81/q30 Where was this sūra revealed?  [* · sciences]
- 28:81/q30/p1 All of al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]

## 28:81/q31 What was Qarun's standing before his rebellion?  [* · reports]
- 28:81/q31/p1 He was a learned, wealthy Israelite close to Moses who later became arrogant and rebellious. — Thaʿlabī and Baghawī's historians portray him as Israel's most learned man after Moses and Aaron, a fine or leading Torah reciter, handsome, wealthy, and in Baghawī pleasant-voiced; Thaʿlabī says he allied hypocritically with Sāmirī. Ibn ʿAṭiyya places the episode after the sea crossing and says transmitters credited Qarun with preserving and reciting Torah. Biqāʿī says historical reporters place him among Moses' people and very near him. [4 notes: THALABI(العلماء بأخبار القدماء); BAGHAWI-FULL(أهل العلم بالأخبار); IBNATIYYA-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أهل الأخبار)]
