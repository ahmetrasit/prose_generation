# 74:26: 11 questions

## 74:26/q01 What does سأصليه mean in this threat?  [سَأُصْلِيهِ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the verb is being explained in its punitive context or by its wider attested usage around fire.
- 74:26/q01/p1 God will put him into the Fire, exposing him to its flames and heat. — Wāḥidī, al-Wajīz, al-Baghawī, Ibn al-Jawzī, and al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī explain it as admission to Hell. Ibn ʿAṭiyya specifies direct contact with its flames; al-Qurṭubī specifies suffering its heat. Al-Jishumī says iṣlāʾ consigns someone to the fire's place and has a root sense of staying or sticking there; he and al-Ṭabrisī add that the person remains there, al-Jishumī saying perpetually. Al-Biqāʿī calls the threat inevitable and soon to arrive. Asad places the suffering in the life to come. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites burning uses at 111:3, 92:14–15, and 4:10. [13 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; ASAD-NOTES]
- 74:26/q01/p2 The verb can also describe warming oneself by a fire. — Ibn ʿĀshūr cites al-Ḥārith ibn Ḥilliza's poetry for ṣilāʾ as experiencing fire's heat for warmth, and Ḥamīd ibn Thawr's line lā taṣṭalī al-nāra for the same usage; he also gives burning as a sense used in this threat. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:26/q02 Whose conduct prompts this threat?  [سَأُصْلِيهِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the immediate trigger is characterized as obstinate plotting generally or the specific slander of the Qurʾān.
- 74:26/q02/p1 The threatened person is the disbeliever just described, whose obstinacy and plotting incur punishment. — Al-Jishumī says the passage follows mention of the disbeliever with a threat. Al-Biqāʿī connects the heat attacking and melting the brain to the man's mental plotting to burn God's friends, and connects the preceding Ṣaʿūd punishment with his obstinacy. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the earlier fa in 'then he said' marks his immediate speech once intense deliberation produced the thought. [4 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:26/q02/p2 The threat answers his slander that the Qurʾān is transmitted magic or human speech. — Abū Ḥayyān assigns this Saqar threat to his claim about transmitted magic, after an earlier threat for defiance of the signs. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports an explanation that the threat confirms the preceding denial that the Qurʾān is human speech. Bint al-Shāṭiʾ surveys related accusations of outside help, copied ancient tales, bewitchment, and missing angelic or worldly signs at 25:4–10, 17:87–96, 6:7–9 and 6:25–26, and 34:43–45; she says the Qurʾān answers with revelation, God's knowledge, and earlier messengers. Ibn ʿĀshūr links the threat of Hell specifically to the man's attacks on the Qurʾān and denial of revelation, following 74:24–25. [5 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); TABATABAI(قيل); BINTSHATI-IJAZ; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 74:26/q03 What does سقر denote?  [سَقَرَ · referent]
turns on: Whether Saqar names Hell as a whole, a level or gate within it, a specified part, or an allegorical name for afterlife suffering; reports disagree over its numbered location.
- 74:26/q03/p1 Saqar is a name for Hell or its Fire. — Al-Farrāʾ compares the proper name Laẓā. Al-Zajjāj, al-Māwardī, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Baghawī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Nasafī, al-Bursawī citing al-Ṣiḥāḥ, and the Study Quran's cited authorities use it as a name of Hell or the Fire. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the majority of commentators use it synonymously with Jahannam, although it can name a place within Hell. Ibn al-Jawzī refers readers to 54:48. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows this alongside a level; the Turkish commentary cites al-Shawkānī for a name of the fire that also denotes a section with severe punishment. [16 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(الصحاح); IBNASHUR-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî); STUDYQURAN(Bg, Ṭ)]
- 74:26/q03/p2 Saqar is an unspecified level or section of Hell. — Al-Jishumī reports this alongside gate and name interpretations. Al-Ṭabrisī reports a level as an alternative to Hell and its gate. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows a named place in Hell; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī allows one of its levels. The Turkish commentary reports al-Shawkānī's description of a section with severe punishment; al-Biqāʿī describes a fiery level whose heat melts the brain. [6 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Şevkânî)]
- 74:26/q03/p3 Saqar is the sixth level or depth of Hell. — Al-Wāḥidī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Bursawī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr transmit this from Ibn ʿAbbās. Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports the sixth level; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes that Ibn ʿAṭiyya confines his explanation to the sixth depth. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNATIYYA-FULL; RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI(ابن عباس); IBNASHUR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن عطية)]
- 74:26/q03/p4 Saqar is an unspecified gate of Hell. — Al-Jishumī lists a gate among reported explanations; al-Ṭabrisī reports it as an alternative, and the Study Quran cites al-Ṭabarī for it. [3 notes: JISHUMI; TABRISI(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Ṭ)]
- 74:26/q03/p5 Saqar is Hell's fifth gate. — Muqātil specifies the fifth gate. [1 notes: MUQATIL]
- 74:26/q03/p6 Saqar is the lowest part of Hell, containing the tree of Zaqqūm. — Al-Suyūṭī transmits this from Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Durr al-Manthūr; it differs in detail from the sixth-level reports also attributed to him. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 74:26/q03/p7 Saqar is one of seven metaphorical names for self-incurred suffering in the afterlife. — Asad calls this its earliest Qurʾānic occurrence and attributes that suffering to sin and deliberate blindness to spiritual truth, mentioning 15:33; he reads descriptions here and from 74:28 onward allegorically. [2 notes: ASAD-NOTES]

## 74:26/q04 What is the derivation and lexical force of سقر?  [سَقَرَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the name can be derived from Arabic scorching language or is underived or borrowed.
- 74:26/q04/p1 It is Arabic-derived from the sun's scorching, melting, or burning heat. — Al-Naḥḥās cites the sun's scorching and al-sāqūr, heated iron used to cauterize a donkey. Al-Māwardī and al-Jishumī add its painful effect on the brain, with al-Jishumī saying the name conveys the Fire's intense pain; he relates the word for a falcon, saqr or sakr, to the severity of its attack. Al-Qurṭubī mentions scorching and burning skin. Al-Bursawī transmits the link with the sun from Ibn ʿAbbās. Elmalılı prefers the Arabic scorching derivation to a foreign origin. The Study Quran reports the expression saqarathu al-shams. [8 notes: NAHHAS; MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI; QURTUBI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); STUDYQURAN(Some commentators); ELMALILI+]
- 74:26/q04/p2 It is an Arabicized loanword of unidentified original form and language. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports al-Jawālīqī's view as cited in al-Itqān; Elmalılı mentions a non-Arabic explanation but favors derivation from the sun's heat. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL(الجواليقي); ELMALILI-]
- 74:26/q04/p3 The name has no derivation. — The Study Quran reports this from some commentators without an etymological argument. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(Some commentators)]

## 74:26/q05 Why is سقر not declined?  [سَقَرَ · grammar]
- 74:26/q05/p1 It is a feminine proper name, so it is diptote. — Al-Farrāʾ calls it a proper name like Laẓā; al-Zajjāj, al-Wāḥidī, al-Jishumī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Nasafī, and Ibn ʿĀshūr cite proper naming and feminine gender. Al-Naḥḥās prefers this over foreignness, arguing that a three-letter foreign name with a moving middle would normally decline. Ibn ʿĀshūr says feminine pronouns in 74:27 and 74:30 support the gender, whether Saqar names Hell itself or a place within it. [9 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NAHHAS+]

## 74:26/q06 Why choose the word سقر?  [سَقَرَ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes offer semantic force and the Qurʾān's choice of a rarer rhyming synonym as different explanations of the wording.
- 74:26/q06/p1 Its fierce scorching sense suits the threatened punishment. — Al-Jishumī says the name reflects the fire's intense pain; al-Biqāʿī ties its brain-melting heat to the man's plotting to burn God's friends. [2 notes: JISHUMI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 74:26/q06/p2 The Qurʾān may favor this rarer synonym to preserve the sūra's rhyme. — Al-Suyūṭī gives Saqar among rare synonymous choices with ḍīzā, al-ḥuṭama, Laẓā, and Hāwiya, and says rhyme often motivates such choices. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 74:26/q07 How does this threat relate to the earlier threat of Ṣaʿūd?  [* · links]
turns on: The syntactic link to 74:17 and surrounding statements, especially whether Ṣaʿūd is hardship or a physical mountain, and whether the two threats answer separate offenses.
- 74:26/q07/p1 It explains or substitutes for the earlier threat, with Saqar encompassing the ascent or its hardships. — Al-Zamakhsharī treats it as substitution for 74:17, as reported by Abū Ḥayyān and al-Samīn; al-Kashshāf itself says it replaces the earlier promise. Al-Nasafī says it explains that promise. Al-Samīn finds substitution clear if Ṣaʿūd means hardship, harder if it is a rock in Hell, except as a part-whole relation. Al-Bursawī calls Saqar an inclusionary apposition whether Ṣaʿūd is hardships or a mountain of fire. Al-Ālūsī calls it a partitive substitution because Saqar contains severe ordeals and, on one reading, a mountain of fire. Elmalılı calls it explication and an inclusive substitute. Ibn ʿĀshūr reports substitution as one possibility; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the clause explains or apposes 74:17. [9 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 74:26/q07/p2 The two are successive threats for two stages of the man's conduct. — Abū Ḥayyān associates Ṣaʿūd with obstinacy toward the signs and Saqar with calling the Qurʾān transmitted magic; al-Ālūsī reports this division but objects that the preceding discussion makes it problematic. Al-Biqāʿī describes the earlier obstinacy and Ṣaʿūd punishment, followed by naming Saqar as the afterlife recompense. Ibn ʿĀshūr distinguishes an earlier worldly threat responding to the man's worldly motives of leadership and envy from the Hell threat responding to his attacks on the Qurʾān and denial of revelation. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري)+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 74:26/q07/p3 It begins a new explanatory sentence prompted by the account of thought and calculation. — Ibn ʿĀshūr gives this as an alternative to substitution, referring to the preceding account at 74:18. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:26/q07/p4 It confirms the preceding denial that the Qurʾān is human speech, despite a difference in meaning. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī reports that some take the clauses as uncoordinated because both convey that denial. [1 notes: TABATABAI(قيل)]

## 74:26/q08 How do the surrounding verses heighten or recall Saqar's threat?  [سَقَرَ · links]
turns on: The notes address different surrounding clauses, one describing Saqar's dread and another possibly referring back to it.
- 74:26/q08/p1 The wording and following question magnify Saqar's dread. — Al-Bayḍāwī says the wording magnifies its dread. Ibn ʿĀshūr parses 'What will make you know what Saqar is?' as a circumstantial clause modifying Saqar and says no informant could make its reality known to the Prophet, magnifying its dreadful state. [3 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:26/q08/p2 A later feminine pronoun may refer back to Saqar as a reminder through its threat and horrors. — Ibn ʿĀshūr reports this as another possibility, with an omitted noun such as Saqar's mention or description understood. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 74:26/q09 What does a report say about the inhabitant of Saqar?  [سَقَرَ · reports]
- 74:26/q09/p1 God told Moses that the inhabitant of Saqar is the poorest servant. — Al-Qurṭubī transmits Abū Hurayra's account of Moses asking God which servant is poorest and God's reply; al-Thaʿlabī supplies no grading. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(النبي)]

## 74:26/q10 What does the verse's punishment teach in the sūra?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes draw distinct lessons from the threat: inescapability, personal moral causation, and the response of truth-seekers versus mockers.
- 74:26/q10/p1 The disbelievers cannot escape the horrors of Hell's punishment. — Ibn ʿĀshūr describes the sūra's terrors and says the passage makes disbelievers despair of escape. [2 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 74:26/q10/p2 Sin and deliberate blindness bring afterlife suffering on the person himself; its descriptions are allegorical. — Asad calls Saqar one of seven metaphorical names for this suffering, points to 15:33, and reads this passage and the descriptions from 74:28 onward allegorically. [3 notes: ASAD-NOTES]
- 74:26/q10/p3 The description rests on divine wisdom: truth-seekers benefit, while arrogant mockers ruin their fate. — Iṣlāḥī says disbelievers mock the description of Hell because they assume they know everything; the differing effects on sincere and arrogant readers express divine wisdom. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 74:26/q11 Which other infernal terms are discussed alongside Saqar?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: The notes distinguish a list of rarer synonymous words from a list of specifically named valleys.
- 74:26/q11/p1 Some rare Qurʾānic terms are treated as synonyms in a discussion including Saqar. — Al-Suyūṭī lists ḍīzā, al-ḥuṭama, Saqar, Laẓā, and Hāwiya as rarer synonymous choices, often serving the sūra's rhyme. [1 notes: ITQAN]
- 74:26/q11/p2 Several other named terms are classified as valleys in Hell. — Al-Suyūṭī lists Ghayy, Atham, Mwbq, Saʿīr, Wayl, Saʾil, and Suhaq as valleys. He reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr's more specific description of Saʿīr as a valley of pus and Suhaq as a valley in Hell. These notes do not classify Saqar as a valley. [2 notes: ITQAN(سعيد بن جبير)]
