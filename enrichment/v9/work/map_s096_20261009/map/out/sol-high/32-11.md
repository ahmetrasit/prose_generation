# 32:11: 30 questions

## 32:11/q01 What does the command to say this verse answer?  [قُلْ · links]
turns on: Whether the surrounding denial concerns resurrection, an encounter at death, or a natural account of death, and whether soul extraction supports the bodily analogy.
- 32:11/q01/p1 It answers denial of resurrection and warns that death is followed by return to God. — Muqātil and al-Qurṭubī connect it to the prior doubt; Rāzī says taking and preserving the soul answers the objection that scattered bodily parts cannot be restored. Biqāʿī argues that extracting a soul from a body proves God's power to distinguish mingled bodily particles; he sees recreation omitted as contextually clear. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the complete self is taken, so its burial does not lose it. Ālūsī reports this as an alternative contextual reading; the next verse describes punishment, Rāzī says. [9 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; TABATABAI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:11/q01/p2 It tells those denying the encounter at death that they will meet the Angel of Death and then be raised. — Ālūsī connects this reading to 32:10 and says the angel's mention promises precisely the encounter denied. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:11/q01/p3 It refutes the deniers' false claim, including a naturalistic account of death. — Bursevī calls the command a statement of truth and says the verse rejects death as an accidental consequence of animal constitution. Ālūsī judges the naturalistic reading weak as an inference from 32:10. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 32:11/q01/p4 Its extraction of soul from body argues that God can recover bodily parts mixed into earth. — Biqāʿī says the soul and intact body are more closely mingled than bodily particles and earth, yet an appointed servant separates them. Ālūsī calls this argument still more remote because the soul's hidden relation to the body is disputed. [3 notes: BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 32:11/q02 What does يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم mean?  [يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم · meaning]
turns on: Whether the expression is explained by complete taking of a soul or count, ordinary death, or completion of a lifespan.
- 32:11/q02/p1 It means taking the souls completely, or taking every person without omission. — Majāz cites Manẓūr al-Zubayrī's line about Quraysh not counting people; Ṭabarī and Ṭabrisī cite rajaz for numerical taking. Zajjāj, Abū Isḥāq via Naḥḥās and Wāḥidī, Māwardī, Kashshāf, Ibn ʿAṭiyya, Qurṭubī, Nāṣafī, Bursevī, Ālūsī, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī compare full collection of a number, property, or debt. Kashshāf cites 39:42 and أخرجوا أنفسكم; Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī also gloss it as none left alive. Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī say tafaʿʿul and istifʿāl forms can coincide. Biqāʿī emphasizes complete removal from the body; Bursevī also mentions severe striking of faces and backs. [25 notes: MAJAZ; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(أبو إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(الراجز); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; BIQAI-FULL]
- 32:11/q02/p2 It denotes death, the taking of a person's spirit. — Damghānī gives death as a sense and cites 40:77; Bursevī cites al-Ṣiḥāḥ's gloss that God takes the spirit. Ibn al-Jawzī records exegetes' use for the angel or angels taking souls, including those of unjust and virtuous people. [3 notes: DAMGHANI; BURSEVI(الصحاح); IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أهل التفسير)]
- 32:11/q02/p3 Death is termed wafāh because the person's allotted lifespan is complete. — Ibn al-Jawzī derives tawaffī from exhausting or completing a number and notes related verbal forms. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 32:11/q03 What is the nafs taken at death?  [يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم · theology]
turns on: Whether the self is a spiritual substance, a body or blood, or a nonindependent accident.
- 32:11/q03/p1 A created, subtle spiritual substance of light. — ʿAlī b. ʿUbayd Allāh prefers this account; he compares angels as subtle luminous bodies and contrasts fire-created jinn and devils. Ṭabāṭabāʾī argues the self denoted by 'I' is the human essence, distinct from and prior to the body, and the verse proves its immateriality. [3 notes: TABATABAI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(شيخنا علي بن عبيد الله)+]
- 32:11/q03/p2 A subtle body. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as one group's view. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(قوم)]
- 32:11/q03/p3 Blood. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as one group's view. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(قوم)]
- 32:11/q03/p4 A body distinct from blood. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as another view. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(آخرون)]
- 32:11/q03/p5 An accident rather than an independently existing entity. — Ibn al-Jawzī says proponents argue that it is not found independently. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(آخرون)]

## 32:11/q04 Are the nafs and spirit identical?  [يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم · theology]
turns on: Whether nafs names the same entity as spirit.
- 32:11/q04/p1 They are distinct. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes this to many people. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(كثير من الناس)]
- 32:11/q04/p2 They are the same. — Ibn al-Jawzī attributes this to others; Nāṣafī glosses the soul taken here as spirit. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(آخرون); NASAFI-FULL]

## 32:11/q05 Are human and animal souls of one kind?  [يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم · theology]
turns on: Whether animal and human souls share the same nature.
- 32:11/q05/p1 They are two kinds. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this as the view of many, linked to animal souls expiring without the angel. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(القائلون بأنها من جنسين,كثير من الناس)]
- 32:11/q05/p2 They are one kind. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports that this view links the angel's work to all animal souls. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(آخرون,القائلون بأنها من جنس واحد)]

## 32:11/q06 What happens to the nafs during sleep?  [يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم · theology]
- 32:11/q06/p1 It leaves while spirit and life remain, then returns unless God retains it and takes the spirit. — A scholar transmitted by Ibn al-Bannāʾ says the sleeping person can dream, move and breathe; death during sleep occurs if God keeps the nafs and takes the spirit. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(أبو علي ابن البناء)]

## 32:11/q07 Does the person survive bodily death, and what follows for the soul?  [يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم · theology]
turns on: How survival after death is described, especially the philosophers' heavenly-body account.
- 32:11/q07/p1 The complete self survives bodily dissolution and is kept for resurrection; pure and wicked souls have different states. — Rāzī reasons that tawaffī is taking and qabḍ is grasping, so neither denotes annihilation; souls are preserved to return to their bodies. He says pure souls grow clear and strong with angels like family, while wicked ones weaken like prisoners among strangers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the decaying body is separate from the preserved self that God restores for judgment. [5 notes: RAZI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 32:11/q07/p2 Pure souls attach to a superior heavenly body and perfect themselves, while wicked souls gain no perfection. — Rāzī reports this from philosophers and accepts it if it means what he has described about souls with angels; otherwise he leaves its truth dependent on their intended meaning. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL(الحكماء)]

## 32:11/q08 What does the verse mean by death?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · theology]
turns on: Whether death is created directly or is a natural accident.
- 32:11/q08/p1 Death is an existent quality created in opposition to life. — Bursevī states this account; Ibn ʿAṭiyya says God extinguishes animals' life and does the same in humans, while using angels for humans. [2 notes: BURSEVI; IBNATIYYA-FULL]
- 32:11/q08/p2 Death is a natural condition arising accidentally from an animal's constitution. — Bursevī rejects this account; Ālūsī regards reading the verse as its refutation as weak. [0 notes: BURSEVI-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 32:11/q09 Who is the Angel of Death?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · referent]
turns on: Whether the singular title names one appointed angel, a group, an angel for each person, or an inward universal soul.
- 32:11/q09/p1 A particular angel, commonly called ʿAzrāʾīl, appointed to take souls. — Muqātil and al-Kalbī describe him with four wings and world-spanning reach; Qurṭubī and Abū Ḥayyān gloss the name 'servant of God.' Ibn Kathīr finds a specific angel in the singular wording. Itqān transmits the name from Wahb through Abū al-Shaykh. Kuran Yolu notes its probable Hebrew origin and absence from the Qur'an and sound hadith; Jewish and Christian traditions also assign such work to an angel or angels. Study Quran gives the common identification. [14 notes: MUQATIL(they); THALABI(مقاتل والكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وغير واحد); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 32:11/q09/p2 The singular title can denote the class or leader of assisting angels. — Jishumī attributes many helpers to Qatāda, Muqātil, and al-Kalbī; Ṭabrisī calls the title collective in light of 6:61 and 4:97. Kuran Yolu compares the singular with plural angel verses and proposes leadership or helpers. [3 notes: JISHUMI(الكلبي); TABRISI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 32:11/q09/p3 An angel is appointed over each person. — Iṣlāḥī says this angel takes each person's soul and accompanies the deceased under God's sight until resurrection. [1 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 32:11/q09/p4 The universal human soul, receiving uncorrupted partial souls. — Qāshānī says souls retaining primordial nature return to it; angels of punishment separate those corrupted to the limit. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 32:11/q10 What is the nature of an angel here?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · theology]
- 32:11/q10/p1 An angel is a subtle luminous body capable of taking different forms. — Bursevī gives the definition, and reports investigators distinguishing angels entrusted with administration, including the Angel of Death, from human rulers. [2 notes: BURSEVI(بعض المحققين)]

## 32:11/q11 What is the Angel of Death commissioned to do?  [ٱلَّذِى وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ · interpretation]
- 32:11/q11/p1 He takes souls at their appointed times and knows or records their lifespans. — Ibn ʿAbbās through Wāḥidī, Ibn al-Jawzī, Bayḍāwī, Nāṣafī, Bursevī, and Ālūsī state the charge. Rāzī says he neither neglects nor delays anyone at their term and that this is his sole task. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the unrestricted verse may imply more than the standard gloss of causing death and taking souls. Itqān's Ibn Sābiṭ assigns him souls among three angels whose records agree at resurrection; Gabriel handles scriptures, revelation, victory and destructive events, and Michael rain and plants. [9 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ITQAN(ابن سابط); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 32:11/q12 How do the Angel of Death and other angels take souls?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether assistants participate, and if they do, whether they extract, seize or attend the souls.
- 32:11/q12/p1 The Angel of Death takes souls with assisting angels of mercy and punishment. — Qatāda, al-Kalbī, Muqātil and later transmitters report helpers. Ibn ʿAṭiyya says their participation honors humans and heightens the disbelievers' punishment. Kuran Yolu cites 8:50 and 16:32–33 for plural angels and contrasts their gentle greetings to protected believers with blows and harsh words for the wicked (4:97, 7:37, 47:27). Study Quran cites 4:97, 8:50, 16:32. [16 notes: TAB-FULL(قتادة); THALABI(مقاتل والكلبي); MAWARDI-FULL; JISHUMI(الكلبي); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL(قتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI(قتادة والكلبي); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية); IBNKATHIR-FULL(قتادة وغير واحد); DURR-FULL(قتادة); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN]
- 32:11/q12/p2 He summons the souls, they answer, and he directs assistants to seize them. — Thaʿlabī says angels grasp and draw spirits out while God creates death; Kashshāf, Nāṣafī and Burhān report the summons and command. Qurṭubī says he then hands souls to mercy or punishment angels. [5 notes: THALABI; KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); BURHAN(البغوي); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 32:11/q12/p3 Assistants draw souls from bodies; he receives them at the throat. — Baghawī's ladder report places him between heaven and earth. Ibn Kathīr calls this a hadith; Ālūsī says he takes the soul near its departure. [3 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 32:11/q12/p4 He takes souls by himself, without assistants. — Māwardī reports this as the alternative to taking them with assistants. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 32:11/q13 How can the angel, other angels, and God each be said to take souls?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · theology]
turns on: How to reconcile 39:42's divine attribution with this verse and passages naming plural angels.
- 32:11/q13/p1 God creates or ultimately causes death, while the angel and helpers carry out its taking. — Thaʿlabī distinguishes God's creation from the angels' grasping and drawing; Jishumī prefers the angel's seizure or command of helpers. Ṭabrisī cites 39:42 and says none but God controls death. Qurṭubī invokes 39:42 and 67:2; angelic execution accounts for 6:61 and 8:50. Nāṣafī says God commands and creates acts. Burhān reports Baghawī's summons-and-extraction account. Ālūsī gives Ashʿarī acquisition or divine permission and will. Ṭabāṭabāʾī describes levels of causation, like pen, hand and person each writing. Study Quran reports Qurṭubī's reconciliation and cites 6:61–62 and 7:37; Elmalılı poses the attribution question but his excerpt ends before an answer. [19 notes: THALABI; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURHAN(البغوي); ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN(al-Qurṭubī); ELMALILI; JISHUMI+]
- 32:11/q13/p2 God takes souls and orders angels to seize them. — Jishumī reports this alternative but explicitly prefers the previous account, where the angel seizes the life-bearing spirit. [1 notes: JISHUMI(قيل); JISHUMI-]

## 32:11/q14 Whose souls does the Angel of Death take?  [يَتَوَفَّىٰكُم مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the angel's charge covers animals and sea martyrs, or other angels or God take those souls.
- 32:11/q14/p1 Every creature's soul, including animals and even small insects. — Yaḥyā reports every land and sea creature; Ibn al-Jawzī connects this with the one-kind account of souls. Qurṭubī reports fleas and mosquitoes; Mālik answers yes about fleas and cites 39:42. The Anṣārī narration says he cannot take even a mosquito without God's command. Ālūsī calls this the majority position and names one angel with helpers. [6 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(القائلون بأنها من جنس واحد); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,مالك بن أنس); ALUSI-FULL(الجمهور)+]
- 32:11/q14/p2 Human souls go through the Angel of Death; God ends animals' lives without him. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya reports direct divine taking of animals and interprets it as extinguishing life, while humans receive angelic care as an honor; Qurṭubī repeats the report. Ibn al-Jawzī connects the view to a distinction between human and animal soul kinds. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(القائلون بأنها من جنسين); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عطية)]
- 32:11/q14/p3 Different angels take different creatures' souls. — Al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās assigns separate angels to humans, jinn, devils, birds, wild animals, beasts, fish and ants; Ālūsī leaves the report's soundness to God. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس)]
- 32:11/q14/p4 God personally takes martyrs at sea as a special exception. — Abū Umāmah transmits the Prophet's exception; al-Ḍaḥḥāk from Ibn ʿAbbās also gives the honor as its reason. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(أبو أمامة,الضحاك عن ابن عباس)]

## 32:11/q15 What is reported about the Angel of Death's reach?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
turns on: Which narrated image describes his access to people at death.
- 32:11/q15/p1 The earth is set before him like a basin, tray, platter, bowl or palm, so he reaches any place. — Mujāhid's recurring comparison varies in vessel wording; al-Suddī likens souls to grain picked by a bird. Ibn ʿAbbās also transmits the bowl account. Qurṭubī notes an ungraded Prophetic narration; Ibn Kathīr calls Zuhayr b. Muḥammad's similar Prophetic transmission mursal. [18 notes: MUJAHID(مجاهد); YAHYA-SALLAM(السدي,مجاهد); TAB-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(ابن عباس,مجاهد); BAGHAWI-FULL(مجاهد); KASHSHAF-FULL(مجاهد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(مجاهد); TABRISI(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); NASAFI-FULL(مجاهد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس,النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,مجاهد); DURR-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 32:11/q15/p2 His stride spans east to west. — Ibn ʿAbbās is cited by Thaʿlabī, Baghawī and Ṭabrisī; Ṭabrisī also joins it to the bowl report. [3 notes: THALABI(ابن عباس); BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس)]
- 32:11/q15/p3 He spans the world, with four wings and a body between heaven and earth. — Muqātil and al-Kalbī add that his legs span the world and souls can be taken as though the world were in his palm; Kuran Yolu says colossal accounts with countless limbs, faces, eyes and tongues lack Qur'anic or sound-hadith basis and arose from Isrāʾīliyyāt. [2 notes: MUQATIL(they); THALABI(مقاتل والكلبي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR-]
- 32:11/q15/p4 He carries an east-to-west spear and strikes those whose appointed term ends. — Muʿādh b. Jabal's report says he examines people or faces twice daily and tells the dead they join the army of the dead. [2 notes: THALABI(معاذ بن جبل); BAGHAWI-FULL(معاذ بن جبل)]
- 32:11/q15/p5 He stands on a ladder between heaven and earth. — Baghawī's report says helpers draw the soul out and he takes it at the throat. [1 notes: BAGHAWI-FULL]

## 32:11/q16 What happened to the man sent to India after seeing the Angel of Death?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
- 32:11/q16/p1 Solomon sent him there by wind, fulfilling the angel's order to take his soul in India. — Khaythamah and Shahr b. Ḥawshab report that the man requested the journey after seeing the angel, who was surprised because his assigned place of death was India. [1 notes: THALABI(خيثمة وشهر بن حوشب)]

## 32:11/q17 What does the Anṣārī deathbed report say?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
- 32:11/q17/p1 The angel is gentle with believers, visits every household five times daily, and cannot take even a mosquito without God's order. — The Prophet asks the angel to treat a believing Anṣārī gently; Māwardī and Ibn Kathīr transmit through Jaʿfar, Qurṭubī and Ālūsī transmit parallel versions, and Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites the account. Jaʿfar connects the five visits to prayer times; Ibn Kathīr says for one who kept prayer the angel repels Satan and prompts testimony of faith. Ālūsī cites similar transmissions by al-Ṭabarānī, Abū Nuʿaym and Ibn Mandah. [8 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(جعفر الصادق عن أبيه); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,جعفر بن علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جعفر بن محمد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي,الطبراني وأبو نعيم وابن منده); TABATABAI(ملك الموت)]

## 32:11/q18 How often does the Angel of Death inspect homes?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
turns on: The reports give incompatible counts of daily visits.
- 32:11/q18/p1 Five times daily, at prayer times. — The Anṣārī narration and Jaʿfar's gloss make this frequency; Ibn Kathīr adds that the angel repels Satan and prompts faith for a prayer-keeping dying person. [7 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(جعفر الصادق عن أبيه); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي,جعفر بن علي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم,جعفر بن محمد); ALUSI-FULL(أبو جعفر محمد بن علي); TABATABAI(ملك الموت)]
- 32:11/q18/p2 Twice daily. — Mujāhid says he visits tents and masonry houses alike; Muʿādh's spear report likewise says he examines each face twice daily. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(مجاهد); THALABI(معاذ بن جبل); BAGHAWI-FULL(معاذ بن جبل)]
- 32:11/q18/p3 Seven times daily. — Kaʿb al-Aḥbār says he stands at each inhabited home's door to see if anyone's term has arrived. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(كعب الأحبار)]

## 32:11/q19 Why do illnesses appear before death?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
- 32:11/q19/p1 They warn of death and prevent people from blaming the Angel of Death. — Ṭabrisī transmits the Prophet's account that illness and pain precede the final messenger, who neither wrongs the term nor the provision and will return until none remain. Qurṭubī reports the angel's complaint about being blamed and cursed and God's answer that illness would appear as the cause. [2 notes: TABRISI(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); QURTUBI-FULL]

## 32:11/q20 What happens to the Angel of Death at the end?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
- 32:11/q20/p1 He dies after all creatures, when God orders him between Paradise and Hell. — Bursevī's report has him as the sole survivor; al-Ḍaḥḥāk's report says he first takes other angels' souls and then dies. [2 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الضحاك عن ابن عباس)]

## 32:11/q21 Are spectacular descriptions of Azrāʾīl reliable?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
- 32:11/q21/p1 Colossal forms with innumerable limbs and faces, and a leaf naming the dead forty days ahead, lack Qur'anic or sound-hadith support. — Kuran Yolu traces the stories to Isrāʾīliyyāt spread by Jewish converts under Abū Bakr and ʿUmar; the reported four-wing and world-spanning descriptions are given by Muqātil and Thaʿlabī. [1 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]

## 32:11/q22 What does وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ mean?  [وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ · meaning]
- 32:11/q22/p1 He was entrusted as a deputy to carry out an assigned task for them. — Ṭabrisī defines tawkīl as committing an affair to another; Bursevī adds reliance and deputation. Rāzī says the assignment means no neglect or delay at the appointed term. [3 notes: TABRISI; BURSEVI; RAZI-FULL]

## 32:11/q23 Why is the agent of وُكِّلَ left unstated?  [وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ · rhetoric]
- 32:11/q23/p1 The passive foregrounds the ease and divine power behind commissioning the angel. — Biqāʿī says the unstated commissioner is the Creator and the angel His servant. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]

## 32:11/q24 Does the angel's appointment support legal agency or compelled seizure of rights?  [وُكِّلَ بِكُمْ · law]
turns on: Whether divine commissioning supplies a general legal analogy, or a narrower analogy for judicial seizure.
- 32:11/q24/p1 It supports the permissibility of appointing an agent. — Qurṭubī reports that some scholars infer agency from God's appointment of the Angel of Death. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(بعض العلماء); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)-]
- 32:11/q24/p2 It does not by itself establish ordinary legal agency. — Ibn al-ʿArabī says divine and human purposes differ; God's sending a Messenger or commanding alms likewise does not validate such analogies (7:158, 2:43). [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]
- 32:11/q24/p3 Read for its meaning, it supports a judge appointing someone to seize a due right by force. — Ibn al-ʿArabī says the seizure need not depend on the debtor's act or consent. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(ابن العربي)]

## 32:11/q25 What does returning to your Lord mean?  [ثُمَّ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكُمْ تُرْجَعُونَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether 'return' names resurrection and recompense or exclusive dependence on God.
- 32:11/q25/p1 Resurrection after death, followed by judgment and recompense from God. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, Wāḥidī, Baghawī, Bayḍāwī, Nāṣafī, Abū Ḥayyān, Ibn Kathīr, Bursevī and Ālūsī give this sense. Rāzī says return requires renewed life; Ibn al-Jawzī names the Day of Recompense. Ṭabrisī says the return is called 'to God' to magnify reward and punishment. Biqāʿī says the Creator and nurturer gathers all and does not neglect wrongdoers. Ṭabāṭabāʾī identifies the earlier 'meeting' of 32:10 and notes that ثم delays return after death. Iṣlāḥī stresses that God alone, not supposed gods or intercessors, judges; he says post-sight confession is useless because intellect and moral awareness were the trial. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; TABRISI; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 32:11/q25/p2 It can mean entering a state where only God can benefit or harm. — Māwardī reports this alongside the recompense interpretation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 32:11/q26 How is تُرْجَعُونَ read?  [تُرْجَعُونَ · readings]
turns on: Whether the verb is passive or active, changing the grammatical agent.
- 32:11/q26/p1 In the passive: 'you are returned.' — Abū Ḥayyān calls this the majority reading; Samīn calls it the common reading. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(العامة)]
- 32:11/q26/p2 In the active: 'you return.' — Abū Ḥayyān, Samīn and Ālūsī attribute this to Zayd b. ʿAlī; Qirāʾāt ER notes the active Form I shifts agency to the subjects. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR(زيد بن علي); ALUSI-FULL(زيد بن علي); QIRAAT-ER]

## 32:11/q27 What inward sense is given to death?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · inward]
- 32:11/q27/p1 It signifies repentance that kills the former self. — Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq cites 2:54, 'repent to your Maker and kill yourselves,' to explain the repentant as one who has killed himself. Bursevī quotes a Persian couplet urging washing off error before sudden seizure. [2 notes: BURSEVI(جعفر بن محمد الصادق)]

## 32:11/q28 What other angelic assignments are reported alongside the Angel of Death's?  [مَّلَكُ ٱلْمَوْتِ · reports]
turns on: The reports describe different angels' assignments and priorities.
- 32:11/q28/p1 Gabriel, Michael and the Angel of Death hold distinct charges recorded until resurrection. — Ibn Sābiṭ says Gabriel handles scriptures, revelation, wartime victory and destructive events; Michael rain and plants; and the Angel of Death souls, with their records agreeing at resurrection. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن سابط)]
- 32:11/q28/p2 Israfil accompanied the Prophet for three years before Gabriel's twenty years of Qur'anic revelation. — Al-Shaʿbī says Israfil taught words but no Qur'an; Ibn ʿAsākir says the Trumpet angel's assignment suits a mission heralding the Hour, and compares assignments to Dhū al-Qarnayn and Khālid b. Sinān. [2 notes: ITQAN(ابن عساكر,الشعبي)]
- 32:11/q28/p3 Gabriel will be first to be reckoned because he was trustee over God's messengers. — ʿAṭāʾ b. al-Sāʾib gives this rationale. [1 notes: ITQAN(عطاء بن السائب)]

## 32:11/q29 What reports describe Qur'anic passages no longer recited?  [* · sciences]
turns on: The reports concern different passages and different kinds of loss or withdrawal.
- 32:11/q29/p1 A forgotten surah resembled the Musabbiḥāt and warned against saying what one does not do. — Abū Mūsā says only this warning remained in memory. [1 notes: ITQAN(أبو موسى الأشعري)]
- 32:11/q29/p2 A recited warning called disowning one's father disbelief. — ʿUmar recalls it and says Zayd b. Thābit confirmed its wording. [1 notes: ITQAN(عمر)]
- 32:11/q29/p3 A command to strive as before was among what had been dropped. — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf answered ʿUmar's question about this revelation. [1 notes: ITQAN(عمر، عبد الرحمن بن عوف)]
- 32:11/q29/p4 Two passages absent from the codex promised success to emigrant strivers and hidden delight to their supporters. — Muslima b. Mukhallad recited them. [1 notes: ITQAN(مسلمة بن مخلد الأنصاري)]
- 32:11/q29/p5 A surah learned by two men became unrecitable because it was abrogated. — Ibn ʿUmar says the Prophet told them to leave it. [1 notes: ITQAN(ابن عمر)]
- 32:11/q29/p6 A passage about the Biʾr Maʿūna martyrs was recited and then withdrawn. — Anas recalls its message that they met their Lord and mutual pleasure followed. [1 notes: ITQAN(أنس)]
- 32:11/q29/p7 Only a quarter of Sūrat al-Tawba remains in recitation. — Ḥudhayfa makes this report. [1 notes: ITQAN(حذيفة)]

## 32:11/q30 What are the sūra's setting and themes around this verse?  [* · sciences]
- 32:11/q30/p1 Sūrat al-Sajdah is Makkan, and its first half treats revelation, God, human creation, resurrection and judgment. — Study Quran cites 32:2–14 for the thematic span, including this verse. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN]
