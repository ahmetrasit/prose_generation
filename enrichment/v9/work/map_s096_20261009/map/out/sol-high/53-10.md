# 53:10: 15 questions

## 53:10/q01 What does waḥy mean here?  [فَأَوْحَىٰٓ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the word is defined by its communicative function, by speech in this context, or by other Arabic usages.
- 53:10/q01/p1 A swift, private communication of meaning by indication or speech. — Jishumi defines it as private conveyance to the mind. Ibn Qutayba and Ibn Faris, as cited by Ibn al-Jawzi, allow writing, gesture, message, or any means that makes another know; an unnamed definition stresses the swiftest and gentlest delivery. Al-Qurtubi cites the Arabic expression waḥāʾ waḥāʾ for swift casting. [5 notes: JISHUMI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(ابن فارس,ابن قتيبة,بعضهم); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 53:10/q01/p2 Speech. — Ibn al-Jawzi glosses the word in this context as speech. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]
- 53:10/q01/p3 It can also denote swiftness or sound. — Ibn al-Jawzi cites istawḥaynāhum in the sense of calling them to cry out. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA]

## 53:10/q02 Who reveals to whom in فَأَوْحَىٰٓ إِلَىٰ عَبْدِهِۦ?  [فَأَوْحَىٰٓ إِلَىٰ عَبْدِهِۦ · referent]
turns on: The understood subject, the recipient called 'His servant,' the pronouns' antecedents, and whether the preceding scene continues with Gabriel or shifts to God.
- 53:10/q02/p1 God reveals to His servant Muhammad. — Ibn Abbas is transmitted by al-Tabari, al-Nahhas, and Abu Hayyan; Muqatil, Ibn Qutayba, al-Thaʿlabi, al-Qushayri, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, al-Samin, Ibn Ashur and others hold this subject and recipient. Al-Samin and Ibn Ashur say God is understood without an explicit prior name; Ibn Ashur cites 53:4. Al-Razi allows divine address or God creating necessary knowledge in Muhammad. Ibn Kathir accepts this along with Gabriel as subject. Elmalılı, al-Tabatabai and Kur'an Yolu favor this reading in the ascent setting, with direct revelation; al-Biqaʿi prefers the divine approacher in a cited report. Al-Tabatabai records a reading with all three pronouns referring to God; al-Baydawi and al-Biqaʿi also report all earlier pronouns as divine, al-Baydawi citing 51:58. [36 notes: MUQATIL; IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TAB-FULL(ابن عباس); NAHHAS(ابن عباس); SULAMI(جعفر); THALABI; QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قائلون بأن ذلك كان ليلة المعراج); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن عباس); SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(أنس,ابن عباس,سريج بن عبيد); IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭb); BIQAI-FULL+; ELMALILI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+; TABATABAI+]
- 53:10/q02/p2 Gabriel reveals to God's servant Muhammad. — Al-Farra places Gabriel within two bow lengths of Muhammad; al-Tabari prefers continuity with the sura's earlier account of Muhammad and Gabriel, and al-Nahhas uses the surrounding narrative likewise. Al-Hasan, al-Rabiʿ, Ibn Zayd, Qatada, Ibn Abbas via Ata, and others transmit the reading, often saying Gabriel passes on what God revealed to him. Al-Wahidi says al-Farra, al-Zajjaj and Ibn al-Anbari chose it. Al-Baydawi and al-Nasafi allow the pronoun before Muhammad's explicit name because he is known; al-Nasafi compares 'what it left on its back.' Al-Alusi favors Gabriel as subject of both verbs over Abu Zayd's mixed subjects; Elmalılı says Gabriel's true-form appearance authenticated the source before piecemeal delivery. Ibn Kathir accepts this reading too. [39 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الربيع); ZAJJAJ; THALABI(الحسن والربيع وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء والكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وعطاء والكلبي والحسن والربيع وابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن والربيع وابن زيد وعطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة والربيع وغيرهم,الربيع والحسن وابن زيد وقتادة); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو زيد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Q, Ṭb); ALUSI-FULL(الحسن)+; NAHHAS(ابن زيد)+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة والحسن وابن زيد)+]
- 53:10/q02/p3 God reveals to His servant Gabriel, who then conveys the message to Muhammad. — Al-Hasan is reported by al-Tabari and, with Aisha and Qatada, by al-Mawardi. Ibn Abbas is reported by Ibn Atiyya. Al-Razi reads this as giving Gabriel proof that the message to Muhammad is revelation, distinguishing an angel from a jinn; he takes mā as a verbal noun. Al-Alusi calls the reading implausible. [7 notes: TAB-FULL(الحسن); MAWARDI-FULL(عائشة والحسن وقتادة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(عائشة والحسن وقتادة); RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN; ALUSI-FULL(قيل)-]

## 53:10/q03 How is God's part in the communication related to Gabriel's?  [فَأَوْحَىٰٓ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the encounter is the direct Miʿraj communication or Gabriel's delivery of God's revelation.
- 53:10/q03/p1 God speaks directly to Muhammad without Gabriel as intermediary. — Jaʿfar in al-Sulami describes direct speech to Muhammad's heart and innermost secret, excepting the later grant of intercession. Al-Qashani locates it at the station of unity. Ibn al-Jawzi reports the Night Journey setting. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu favor it if this is the Miʿraj; Tafsir al-Qummi describes face-to-face speech. Al-Razi describes direct address or divinely created certain knowledge. [9 notes: SULAMI(جعفر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قائلون بأن ذلك كان ليلة المعراج); RAZI-FULL; QASHANI; TABATABAI(تفسير القمي); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ELMALILI+; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR+]
- 53:10/q03/p2 Gabriel conveys God's revelation to Muhammad. — Al-Rabiʿ says the revelation comes on Gabriel's tongue; Ibn Zayd, Ibn Abbas through Ata and others say Gabriel received it from God before conveying it. Ibn Kathir also allows the grammatical form 'God revealed through Gabriel.' Elmalılı says Gabriel's original appearance verified divine origin before gradual delivery. [28 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن زيد,الربيع); FARRA; THALABI(الحسن والربيع وابن زيد); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس والسدي); WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس عن عطاء والكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL(ابن عباس وعطاء والكلبي والحسن والربيع وابن زيد); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن زيد,الحسن); TABRISI(الحسن والربيع وابن زيد وعطاء عن ابن عباس); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عباس); QURTUBI-FULL(ابن عباس والحسن وقتادة والربيع وغيرهم,الربيع والحسن وابن زيد وقتادة); IBNKATHIR-FULL; DURR-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو زيد); ELMALILI; TABATABAI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Q, Ṭb); NAHHAS(ابن زيد)+; TAB-FULL+; WAHIDI-BASIT(قتادة والحسن وابن زيد)+]

## 53:10/q04 Why is Muhammad called عَبْدِهِۦ, and whose servant is he?  [عَبْدِهِۦ · rhetoric]
turns on: The notes give honor, the recoverable divine antecedent, and the scope of 'servant' as separate reasons for this wording.
- 53:10/q04/p1 The servant is Muhammad, honored by attribution to God. — Al-Burṣawī compares 17:1; Ibn Ashur says the noun replaces a pronoun to honor him. Al-Biqaʿi adds that Muhammad worshipped God alone, at Hira and elsewhere. Al-Nasafi says ʿAbd Allah is recognizable without prior naming. [4 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 53:10/q04/p2 The possessive refers to God though His name is unspoken, because the referent is clear. — Abu Hayyan and al-Burṣawī compare the implicit 'earth' in 35:45. Al-Alusi also cites 97:1. Al-Biqaʿi relates the hidden antecedent to revelation through a messenger in 42:51, suiting concealed speech. Elmalılı says this holds even if the preceding powerful one is Gabriel. [6 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ELMALILI]
- 53:10/q04/p3 The term servant denotes body and spirit together, supporting bodily as well as spiritual ascent. — Al-Samarrai cites 17:1 and argues that otherwise the disbelievers would not have denied the miracle. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 53:10/q05 How does مَآ أَوْحَىٰ work grammatically?  [مَآ أَوْحَىٰ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā denotes a thing revealed or the act of revealing.
- 53:10/q05/p1 Mā is a relative pronoun: 'what He revealed.' — Al-Tabari and al-Tabrisi give this parse. Al-Samin cites Ibn Malik's use of this verse and 20:78 against the common grammatical requirement that a relative clause refer to something already familiar to the listener. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR]
- 53:10/q05/p2 Mā is a verbal noun: 'His revealing' or 'revelation.' — Al-Tabari and al-Tabrisi allow this parse. Al-Razi uses it when God gives Gabriel evidence that the message is genuine revelation. [4 notes: TAB-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL]

## 53:10/q06 Why repeat أَوْحَىٰ and leave its object unnamed?  [مَآ أَوْحَىٰ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the phrasing chiefly heightens the revelation, guards private content, or asks for assent without specification.
- 53:10/q06/p1 The repetition and ambiguity magnify the revelation. — Ibn Atiyya, al-Qurtubi, al-Baydawi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, al-Samin, al-Alusi, Ibn Ashur and the Study Quran give this reason. Al-Qurtubi records a disagreement over whether the content is to be believed generally or is known and explained. [11 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN(JJ, Ṭb); ALUSI-FULL+]
- 53:10/q06/p2 The indeterminacy preserves a private, exclusive communication. — Al-Wasiti says only Muhammad received its concealed content while his public message was disclosed. Al-Sadiq and al-Thawri call it a secret between beloveds beyond speech or writing; al-Thaʿlabi's anonymous poem evokes intimacy, bewilderment and light. Al-Tabrisi cites similar poetry; al-Qushayri says other things in the encounter were shown to no one. Nishaburi says its secrets and knowledge were known only to God and Muhammad. [9 notes: SULAMI(الصادق,الواسطي); THALABI(أبو الحسن الثوري,الشاعر); QUSHAYRI; TABRISI(غير مسمى); ELMALILI(Nizameddin Nîsâbûrî)]
- 53:10/q06/p3 Its lack of specification allows general assent to an undisclosed revelation. — Al-Qurtubi records this as one side of the exegetes' disagreement with attempts to identify particular contents. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 53:10/q07 What was revealed in مَآ أَوْحَىٰ?  [مَآ أَوْحَىٰ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the indefinite phrase points to a specified gift or message, multiple gifts, or undisclosed knowledge.
- 53:10/q07/p1 The prescription of prayer, including the five daily prayers. — Ibn Atiyya and Abu Hayyan name prayer as what is known. Al-Biqaʿi quotes Bukhari's Anas report: fifty prayers were revealed and reduced to five with tenfold worth. Elmalılı calls prayer the known Miʿraj revelation. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(أنس); ELMALILI]
- 53:10/q07/p2 Reminders of God's care for Muhammad, from orphanhood to raised renown. — Saʿid or Saʿid ibn Jubayr is cited by al-Thaʿlabi, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi, linking 93:6 and 94:4. Al-Qushayri includes guidance and enrichment from 93:7–8; al-Qurtubi cites 94:1–4 as well. [6 notes: THALABI(سعيد); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); TABRISI(سعيد بن جبير); QURTUBI-FULL(سعيد بن جبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL(سعيد بن جبير)]
- 53:10/q07/p3 Paradise remains closed to other prophets and peoples until Muhammad and his community enter. — Reported by al-Thaʿlabi, al-Qushayri, al-Baghawi, al-Tabrisi, al-Qurtubi, al-Nasafi, Abu Hayyan, Ibn Kathir and Elmalılı. The last has a slight phrasing variation, 'his prophet and community.' [9 notes: THALABI(قيل); QUSHAYRI; BAGHAWI-FULL(غير مسمى); TABRISI(غير مسمى); QURTUBI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(قيل); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الزمخشري); IBNKATHIR-FULL(غيره); ELMALILI]
- 53:10/q07/p4 Glad tidings of the Basin and Kawthar. — Al-Qushayri reports this explanation. [1 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 53:10/q07/p5 Undisclosed burdens, secrets, realities, or knowledge reserved to Muhammad. — Al-Qushayri reports exclusive burdens or secrets and says there was more unseen by anyone else. Al-Wasiti distinguishes this private content from Muhammad's public preaching; Nishaburi describes secrets, realities and kinds of knowledge known only to God and His Messenger. Al-Sadiq and al-Thawri stress its intimate, ineffable character. [7 notes: SULAMI(الصادق,الواسطي); THALABI(أبو الحسن الثوري); QUSHAYRI; ELMALILI(Nizameddin Nîsâbûrî)]
- 53:10/q07/p6 Five prayers, the closing verses of al-Baqara, and forgiveness of grave sins for non-idolatrous members of the community. — Elmalılı cites a Muslim report from the Prophet, including 2:285–286. [1 notes: ELMALILI(Resûlullah)]
- 53:10/q07/p7 God disclosed what He willed, without naming its content. — Anas reports a great light behind a pearl-and-ruby curtain before this revelation; Surayj reports Gabriel prostrating until God completed it. Al-Qushayri allows multiple stated contents and additional hidden things. [3 notes: DURR-FULL(أنس,سريج بن عبيد); QUSHAYRI]

## 53:10/q08 In what event or setting did this revelation occur?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which earlier approach and vision the pronouns describe, and the strength of reports linking the verse to each occasion.
- 53:10/q08/p1 During the Night Journey and Ascension, with Muhammad receiving direct revelation. — Ibn al-Jawzi reports this setting. Elmalılı and Kur'an Yolu connect direct revelation to Muhammad's ascent and nearness; al-Biqaʿi cites Anas's Bukhari Isra report of the opened chest, ascent and prayers. Al-Qushayri calls that night uniquely Muhammad's. [8 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(قائلون بأن ذلك كان ليلة المعراج); QUSHAYRI; BIQAI-FULL(أنس); ELMALILI; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 53:10/q08/p2 The passage depicts Gabriel appearing in his true form and delivering revelation. — Ibn Ishaq says Gabriel descended at al-Abtah in his original form and revealed the sura. Al-Nashr favors the Gabriel vision because takbir suits a momentous or frightening occurrence. Study Quran reports many commentators understand two true-form visions, unlike Gabriel's usual human guise. Ibn Masud's tentatively attributed report has one horizon-filling appearance on request and another on the ascent, with Gabriel prostrating afterward; Ahmad transmits it and Ibn Kathir calls it strange. Ibn Masud's six-hundred-wing report is in Bukhari and Muslim. [5 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر محمد بن إسحاق); STUDYQURAN(many commentators); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد)); TABRISI(عبد الله بن مسعود); NASHR+]
- 53:10/q08/p3 The scene may relate to the first revelation at Hira. — Al-Biqaʿi tentatively associates the descent with Muhammad's fright, trembling return and request to be covered. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 53:10/q08/p4 The passage depicts Muhammad seeing God during the Night Journey, by heart or by eye and heart. — Study Quran reports many commentators' reading. An expanded Ibn Abbas version refers 53:8–11 to the heart's vision of God, but Ibn Kathir grades its chain weak. [2 notes: STUDYQURAN(many others); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]

## 53:10/q09 What do reports about the vision and divine disclosure add to this scene?  [* · reports]
turns on: The reports supply distinct visions, contents, and assessments of transmission.
- 53:10/q09/p1 A divine disclosure in a vision of the Highest Assembly's dispute. — Ibn Abbas describes a vision, probably in sleep, in which God put a hand between Muhammad's shoulders and granted knowledge of heaven and earth. Expiations were remaining in mosques after prayer, walking to Friday prayer and completing ablution in hardship; degrees were feeding others, spreading peace and night prayer. Muhammad was taught to pray for good works, avoidance of wrongs, love of the poor and protection from trial. Ibn Kathir points to a similar Muadh report at the end of Sad. [5 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 53:10/q09/p2 An expanded Ibn Abbas report gives undisclosed knowledge and a vision of God by the heart. — It connects 53:8–11 with this vision. Ibn Kathir explicitly grades the expanded chain weak. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن عباس); IBNKATHIR-FULL-]
- 53:10/q09/p3 Gabriel appeared in his original vast form, with six hundred wings or horizon-filling wings. — Ibn Masud's six-hundred-wing report is in Bukhari and Muslim according to al-Tabrisi. Surayj describes emerald, pearl and ruby wings between the horizons after Gabriel's prostration; he contrasts earlier appearances as Dihya al-Kalbi or through a sieve. Ibn Umar also reports Dihya's form. A tentative Ibn Masud attribution says Gabriel appeared in true form twice, with Ahmad transmitting it and Ibn Kathir calling it strange. [6 notes: TABRISI(عبد الله بن مسعود); DURR-FULL(ابن عمر,سريج بن عبيد); IBNKATHIR-FULL(ابن مسعود (على ظن محمد))]
- 53:10/q09/p4 The Prophet saw a vast light screened by pearls and rubies, followed by revelation. — Anas transmits this report in al-Durr. [1 notes: DURR-FULL(أنس)]
- 53:10/q09/p5 The Prophet was shown his community's future treasures and a thousand paradisal palaces. — Ibn Abbas's report is sound to Ibn Abbas, according to al-Nashr, and is treated as raised by convention; it cites 93:5. [1 notes: NASHR(ابن عباس)]

## 53:10/q10 How should divine approach and nearness in the surrounding scene be understood?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether approach is read as figurative transmission, an incomprehensible divine act, or inward nearness.
- 53:10/q10/p1 As a figurative, gentle transmission that entails no bodily location or likeness for God. — Al-Biqaʿi says divine approach and descent portray the ease of revelation while Muhammad remains connected to the holy realm. He uses a report from Uthman ibn Abi al-As comparing nightly divine descent to a king opening doors to intimates and then others, but calls it representational; God has no likeness, and he refers readers to discussion of 17:78. Kur'an Yolu says horizon, Sidra, descent and nearness can describe divine acts and attributes without asking how. Al-Baydawi construes God's approach as raising Muhammad's rank and drawing him wholly to the sacred presence. [6 notes: BIQAI-FULL(عثمان بن أبي العاص); KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; BAYDAWI-FULL(قيل)]
- 53:10/q10/p2 As God's own approach beyond comprehension and befitting divine majesty. — Al-Biqaʿi favors this with the Anas report and a later Jaʿfar al-Sadiq account, after created things reach their limit. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL+]
- 53:10/q10/p3 As intimate nearness in which awe is met by divine gentleness. — Al-Sadiq in al-Sulami says the Prophet reached utmost awe and only utmost gentleness could bear it; al-Qushayri emphasizes a uniquely exalted station and a hidden secret. [2 notes: SULAMI(الصادق); QUSHAYRI]

## 53:10/q11 What does the verse teach about revelation and the sura's opening?  [* · links]
turns on: How the scene answers denial of revelation and how much of its unseen reality it claims to disclose.
- 53:10/q11/p1 The revelation clause is the goal of the opening scene and establishes that revelation is real. — Ibn Ashur calls the preceding stages a prelude, depicting extraordinary events in familiar terms to answer polytheist denial; he links to 53:4. Islahi says 1–18 reject soothsaying, fortune telling and the charge that Muhammad's experiences were illusions. Study Quran links the opening to the accusation of invention at 52:33 and notes the opening's rhetorical power and disputed interpretation. [6 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 53:10/q11/p2 The trusted angel taught Muhammad closely and clearly. — Islahi compares Gabriel's approach to an affectionate teacher bending over a pupil; Muhammad fully grasped the instruction. Asad says the phrase alludes both to Gabriel's exceptional true-form manifestation and to revelation's general content. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ASAD-NOTES]
- 53:10/q11/p3 The mysterious scene calls for faith beyond sensory access and limits speculative reason. — Kur'an Yolu also says the passage condemns pagan efforts to concretize divinity by intermediaries or ascribe godhood to other beings. Asad says even chosen prophets do not receive ultimate mysteries of life, death, creation's purpose or the universe. [3 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; ASAD-NOTES]

## 53:10/q12 What are the reported readings of verse endings in this passage?  [* · readings]
- 53:10/q12/p1 Readers vary among full fatḥ, imāla, and an intermediate pronunciation. — Ibn Atiyya reports Ibn Kathir, Asim and Ibn Amir with fatḥ at all endings; Asim via Abu Bakr uses imāla on raʾā; Nafi and Abu Amr use an intermediate form; Hamza and al-Kisai use imāla throughout; Abu Amr via Ubayd uses it for al-aʿlā and tadallā. [1 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير وعاصم وابن عامر ونافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي)]

## 53:10/q13 How do reports explain takbīr around al-Ḍuḥā and al-Sharḥ?  [* · reports]
turns on: Which occasion or liturgical function the takbīr marks.
- 53:10/q13/p1 It expressed the Prophet's distress over his household's fate. — Al-Suddi transmits from Ibn Abbas that the prospect of a household member entering Hell prompted takbir. [1 notes: NASHR(السدي عن ابن عباس)]
- 53:10/q13/p2 It referred to the Prophet's intercession. — Al-Hasan and Abu Jaʿfar al-Baqir give this explanation. [1 notes: NASHR(الحسن وأبو جعفر الباقر)]
- 53:10/q13/p3 It marked the momentous or frightening vision of Gabriel. — Ibn Ishaq says Gabriel revealed the sura in original form at al-Abtah; al-Nashr favors this because takbir usually responds to something momentous or frightening. [2 notes: NASHR(أبو بكر محمد بن إسحاق); NASHR+]
- 53:10/q13/p4 It honors God and marks grateful completion of recitation or blessing. — Makki gives this explanation and Ali's instruction to say takbir at the end of the Mufassal supports it. Al-Nashr suggests the practice at al-Duha's end carries into al-Sharh, or takbir follows the completed list of blessings in al-Sharh; he found no earlier explanation for its beginning there. [3 notes: NASHR(مكي وعلي)]

## 53:10/q14 What force does the opening Qur'anic oath have?  [* · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the oath functions as ordinary emphasis or carries magnification or further philosophical meanings.
- 53:10/q14/p1 An oath emphasizes, affirms, and solemnizes its claim. — Bint al-Shati says these are the oath's proper rhetorical functions and cites 28:71–72, 17:12 and 10:6 as benefits of created things stated without an oath. [2 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ]
- 53:10/q14/p2 The oath's wāw magnifies what follows. — Bint al-Shati reports this common exegetical and rhetorical claim but objects that created things cannot be measured against the Creator's greatness. [0 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(المفسرون والبلاغيون)-]
- 53:10/q14/p3 The oath's wāw bears philosophical or inward meanings. — Bint al-Shati attributes such readings to al-Razi, al-Nishaburi, al-Tabrisi and Muhammad Abduh and rejects them as implausible for the particle. [0 notes: BINTSHATI-IJAZ(الفخر الرازي والنيسابورى والطبرسى والشيخ محمد عبده)-]

## 53:10/q15 How is divine speech traced through later transmitted teaching?  [* · links]
- 53:10/q15/p1 Each Imam's hadith traces through the Imams to the Prophet, whose hadith is God's speech. — Al-Tabatabai reports this from Imam al-Sadiq. [1 notes: TABATABAI(أبو عبد الله عليه السلام)]
