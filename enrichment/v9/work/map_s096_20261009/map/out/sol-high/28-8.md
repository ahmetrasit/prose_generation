# 28:8: 28 questions

## 28:8/q01 What does the opening fāʾ leave unstated?  [فَٱلْتَقَطَهُۥٓ · grammar]
- 28:8/q01/p1 It follows an omitted account of Moses' mother nursing him and casting him into the river. — Abū Ḥayyān supplies nursing and casting; Bursawī recalls the command and her protection of the chest; Ālūsī says the deletion conveys her prompt obedience. [4 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]

## 28:8/q02 What does iltiqāṭ mean here?  [فَٱلْتَقَطَهُۥٓ · meaning]
- 28:8/q02/p1 Finding and taking something without previously seeking it. — Ṭabarī cites an anonymous rajaz line about unexpectedly reaching water; Qurṭubī cites منهلا وردته التقاطا and relates the word to luqaṭa, found property; Jishumī compares a lost object. Baghawī is quoted by Biqāʿī. Ibn ʿĀshūr allows deliberate or inadvertent pickup of something cast away; Bursawī and Ālūsī stress subsequent deliberate care. [17 notes: TAB-FULL(الراجز); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(البغوي); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; NASAFI-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ]

## 28:8/q03 What does the verse imply about found property and an abandoned child?  [فَٱلْتَقَطَهُۥٓ · law]
- 28:8/q03/p1 A found object requires seeking its owner; an abandoned child in danger must be rescued. — Bursawī defines luqaṭa as unguarded property whose owner must be sought and laqīṭ as a child of unknown lineage abandoned through poverty or fear of illicit birth; rescue is obligatory if water, a predator, or similar danger threatens. Qurṭubī says found-property rules were discussed in Sūrat Yūsuf. [4 notes: BURSEVI; QURTUBI-FULL]

## 28:8/q04 Who or what is meant by Pharaoh's āl?  [ءَالُ فِرْعَوْنَ · referent]
turns on: Whether āl identifies the actual finder, a household group, or the people to whom the deed is attributed; reports name different finders.
- 28:8/q04/p1 His household, followers, and close adherents, including those who served him. — Ṭabarī retains the verse's general wording and declines to choose one reported identification; Bursawī includes kinship, companionship, and religious agreement. Ālūsī says restriction to the noble is usual rather than necessary. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the act is attributed to the household though one woman retrieved the chest. [5 notes: TAB-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; NASAFI-FULL]
- 28:8/q04/p2 His wife's maidservants or female attendants. — Yaḥyā reports Nile washerwomen; Suddī and Ibn ʿAbbās describe Asiya's maids, who found the chest among trees or while drawing water, sometimes expecting treasure. Rāzī identifies the family here with female servants; Ibn Kathīr says they carried the unknown basket to Asiya. [9 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM(يحيى); TAB-FULL(السدي); MAWARDI-FULL(ابن عباس); WAHIDI-BASIT(المفسرون); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(السدي); RAZI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي); BIQAI-FULL]
- 28:8/q04/p3 Pharaoh's daughter, or attendants around her. — Muḥammad ibn Qays and Ibn Abī Ḥātim say the daughter found or opened the chest; Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies a woman around the daughter, citing Exodus 2. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن قيس); IBNABIHATIM(أبو عبد الرحمن الحبلي); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(محمد بن قيس); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:8/q04/p4 Pharaoh's agents. — Ibn Isḥāq says Asiya first spotted the chest, then Pharaoh's agents retrieved it. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن إسحاق)]
- 28:8/q04/p5 Pharaoh's wife herself. — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd says she found the chest and was cured; Asad infers the wife from 28:9 and 66:11; Ṭabrisī says Asiya opened it. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد); ASAD-NOTES; TABRISI]
- 28:8/q04/p6 The entire royal family, young and old. — Iṣlāḥī reads āl as indicating collective discovery and carrying Moses into the palace. [2 notes: ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:8/q04/p7 A person named Tabuth. — Itqān transmits this name for the finder without further detail. [1 notes: ITQAN]

## 28:8/q05 How did Moses reach the royal household?  [فَٱلْتَقَطَهُۥٓ ءَالُ فِرْعَوْنَ · reports]
turns on: The accounts differ in the mother's timing, the chest's construction, and the events before and during its journey.
- 28:8/q05/p1 His mother placed him in a chest or basket in the Nile, which carried him to Pharaoh's house. — Ibn Kathīr says a feared visitor prompted her to put him in a basket, which she forgot to tie; Biqāʿī describes a waterproof pitch-coated chest caught by a tree. Iṣlāḥī says a papyrus box floated from Israelite settlements, and cites the Torah's prior hiding. Ṭabrisī and Muqātil mention the Nile, water and trees; Bursawī says she acted as commanded. [8 notes: MUQATIL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]
- 28:8/q05/p2 The child's earlier concealment included a protective miracle in a furnace. — Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by Ṭabrisī describes a friendly midwife, Moses' sister hiding him in a lit furnace God cooled, and a carpenter's failed exposure attempts. Ālūsī's report has the midwife see light between his eyes, tremble, love him, and advise concealment; his mother then hid him in a blazing oven. [2 notes: TABRISI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:8/q05/p3 His mother nursed him for three months before making a reed chest. — Ālūsī gives this as one of three conflicting durations; the chest was coated inside with pitch. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:8/q05/p4 His mother nursed him for four months before making a reed chest. — Ālūsī gives this as one of three conflicting durations; the chest was coated inside with pitch. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:8/q05/p5 His mother nursed him for eight months before making a reed chest. — Ālūsī gives this as one of three conflicting durations; the chest was coated inside with pitch. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:8/q05/p6 A carpenter made the chest with its lock inside. — Ālūsī reports Suddī's detail that the mother set it in the Nile near Pharaoh's house. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(السدي)]

## 28:8/q06 What happened when the royal household opened the chest?  [فَٱلْتَقَطَهُۥٓ ءَالُ فِرْعَوْنَ · reports]
turns on: The cure reports differ over whether the patient was daughter or wife and whether sight or saliva healed her.
- 28:8/q06/p1 The women found Moses and were drawn to him; Asiya sought to keep him alive. — Suddī says maids expecting treasure brought the chest to Asiya, who loved Moses and opposed Pharaoh's wish to kill an Israelite child. Ibn Isḥāq says God placed love for him in Pharaoh and Asiya. Thaʿlabī says servants could not open or break the chest, Asiya alone saw light and opened it, Moses had light between his eyes and milk from his thumb. Ibn Kathīr and Biqāʿī link love to 20:39; Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu relate Asiya's plea to 66:11. [18 notes: TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي); THALABI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(السدي,قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 28:8/q06/p2 Asiya pleaded that he might benefit them or be adopted, and Pharaoh yielded. — Thaʿlabī says the childless Asiya asked for him and Pharaoh said he had no need of him. Ālūsī's continuation reports advisers' suspicions before Pharaoh yielded; Iṣlāḥī and Kuran Yolu likewise describe her compassionate plea. [8 notes: TAB-FULL(السدي); THALABI; ALUSI-FULL(السدي,قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 28:8/q06/p3 The daughter with leprosy was cured by seeing Moses. — Muḥammad ibn Qays and one report in Ālūsī give cure at sight; the daughter declared Moses blessed and pleaded for his life. [2 notes: TAB-FULL(محمد بن قيس); ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:8/q06/p4 The daughter with leprosy was cured by Moses' saliva. — Thaʿlabī gives doctors' and magicians' predicted sea-found child and a specified Monday sunrise; Pharaoh and Asiya sat by the Nile. The daughter applied saliva and recovered. Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Ālūsī, and Bursawī transmit variants; Ālūsī says she was Pharaoh's only child. [6 notes: IBNABIHATIM(أبو عبد الرحمن الحبلي); THALABI; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس وغيره); BURSEVI]
- 28:8/q06/p5 Pharaoh's wife had leprosy and was cured by taking the infant. — ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Zayd's report places the illness on the wife, who called Moses blessed. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(عبد الرحمن بن زيد)]

## 28:8/q07 Why was the child named Moses?  [فَٱلْتَقَطَهُۥٓ · reports]
- 28:8/q07/p1 He was found amid water and trees; the name's parts mean water and tree in the reported language. — Muqātil calls the language Coptic and gives mo and si; Baghawī attributes naming to Asiya; Biqāʿī gives mu and sā. These are minor phonetic variants. [3 notes: MUQATIL; BAGHAWI-FULL(آسية); BIQAI-FULL]

## 28:8/q08 Who was Pharaoh?  [فِرْعَوْنَ · reports]
turns on: Reports differ over Pharaoh's name and origin.
- 28:8/q08/p1 He was al-Walīd ibn Muṣʿab. — Ibn Isḥāq, reported by Ibn Abī Ḥātim, calls him unusually rebellious, outspoken, and long-reigning. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(ابن إسحاق)]
- 28:8/q08/p2 He was a non-Arab from Hamadhān. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from al-Ḥasan. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(الحسن)]
- 28:8/q08/p3 He was Persian, from Istakhr. — Nasafī reports this from al-Zajjāj. [1 notes: NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج)]

## 28:8/q09 What is the lām in ليكون doing?  [لِيَكُونَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the expressed end belongs to the family's intention, a figurative presentation of the unintended outcome, or God's intended decree; some propose omitted or reordered syntax.
- 28:8/q09/p1 It marks the unintended consequence of taking Moses in: he later became their enemy and grief. — Ibn Qutayba says they took him as a son; Ṭabarī, Ibn Isḥāq, Zajjāj, Ṭabrisī, and others contrast their hope of benefit or comfort with the ruin that followed. Zajjāj compares wealth leading to death; Māwardī and poets compare birth and nurturing for death and building for ruin. Rāzī invokes 28:9, 20:39, and 7:179. Zarkashī says consequence still truthfully expresses causation and cites a proposed substitution of fa. Abū Ḥayyān supplies the omitted nursing sequence. Iṣlāḥī says they were unaware Moses would end Pharaoh's rule. [35 notes: IBNQUTAYBA-GHARIB; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق); ZAJJAJ; IBNABIHATIM(محمد بن إسحاق); MAWARDI-FULL(الشاعر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(شاعر); NASAFI-FULL(الزجاج,المفسرون); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق وغيره); BURHAN(ابن خالويه,ابن قتيبة); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI(قيل); ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:8/q09/p2 The purposive lām is figurative or ironic: the result is represented as if it motivated the family's act. — Zamakhsharī compares conventional purpose expressions and Rāzī prefers his account, using a lion metaphor. Bayḍāwī, Biqāʿī and Ibn ʿĀshūr likewise identify an imagined motive; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls it a dependent metaphor and differs from Sakkākī. Sāmin allows this or a consequence lām; Itqān calls it a derivative metaphor in the particle. Ālūsī reports a literal purpose with implicit comparison and rejects the objection that pickup is involuntary because taking and care were deliberate. Ālūsī reports Sakkākī and Taftāzānī's relation-level metaphor; Khaṭīb al-Dimashqī instead starts from a comparison involving the genitive, which ʿAbd al-Ḥakīm prefers because lām's meaning depends on it. [20 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف); SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; BURHAN(الزمخشري); ITQAN; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(الخطيب الدمشقي,السكاكي والتفتازاني,قيل); IBNASHUR-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(العلامة عبد الحكيم)+; RAZI-FULL(صاحب الكشاف)+]
- 28:8/q09/p3 The lām expresses God's intended decree and wisdom in their taking Moses. — Ibn Kathīr prefers this from 28:6: God made their precautions fail. ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz cites God's prior promise and knowledge. Zarkashī argues an outcome lām suits an ignorant agent but God's acts carry wisdom; speaking of the family's act heightens regret. Ālūsī rejects supplying an explicit 'He decreed the taking so that...' as unsuitable. [5 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عمر بن عبد العزيز); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز); BURHAN; IBNKATHIR-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)-]
- 28:8/q09/p4 A literal purpose can be supplied: they took him for fear he would become an enemy. — Itqān reports Abū Ḥayyān's preferred omitted noun, and Ibn al-Dahhān proposes their aversion to that outcome as the motive. [2 notes: BURHAN(ابن الدهان); ITQAN(أبو حيان)+]
- 28:8/q09/p5 On an unusual reordered parsing, 'enemy and grief' is a circumstance and they took him to possess him. — Zarkashī reports Ibn al-Dahhān's rearrangement without adopting it. [1 notes: BURHAN(ابن الدهان)]

## 28:8/q10 How does the purpose construction work grammatically?  [لِيَكُونَ · grammar]
- 28:8/q10/p1 Lām governs a subjunctive verb through an understood verbal noun or kay. — Naḥḥās calls ليكون accusative under the purpose lām and dismisses weak grammarians' objections. Zarkashī says kay may be understood with lām and discusses its occurrence with or without lām. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies lām kay governing a verbal noun understood from an plus the subjunctive, ordinarily denoting the agent's motive. [3 notes: NAHHAS; BURHAN; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:8/q11 In what sense would Moses become their enemy and grief?  [عَدُوًّۭا وَحَزَنًا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether enmity concerns religion and destruction generally or distinct effects on men and women.
- 28:8/q11/p1 He would oppose their religion and bring grief through their defeat and destruction. — Qatāda speaks of religious opposition and what befell them; Muqātil specifies destruction and anger over firstborn, connecting 26:55. Ibn Jawzī reports harm to them; Biqāʿī mentions the loss of kingdom, liberation of Israel, death of Egyptian firstborn, and drowning. Wāḥidī reports Muqātil; Kuran Yolu calls it recompense for persecuting Israel. [8 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة); TAB-FULL(قتادة); WAHIDI-BASIT(مقاتل); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BIQAI-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:8/q11/p2 He was the men's enemy and the women's grief. — Ibn Jawzī reports men drowned and women enslaved; Kāshifī as reported by Bursawī has men drowned and women grieved because their sons were enslaved. These accounts differ in who was enslaved. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(المفسرون); BURSEVI(الكاشفي)]
- 28:8/q11/p3 The family's expected joy and benefit became grief according to divine destiny. — Tustarī and Sahl stress their ignorance of the hidden decree; Ṭabarī compares a seemingly helpful act ending in harm. Wāḥidī and Study Quran repeat the reversal. [6 notes: TUSTARI; SULAMI(سهل); TAB-FULL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:8/q12 To whom does لهم refer across Moses' later life?  [لَهُمْ · referent]
turns on: Whether the pronoun denotes the initial household narrowly or a continuing dynasty.
- 28:8/q12/p1 It refers to Pharaoh's ruling house, state, or nation, including a later Pharaoh. — Ibn ʿĀshūr says the Pharaoh whom Moses opposed was later than the one whose household found him. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:8/q12/p2 It refers to the household that found and raised him. — Most accounts speak of the rescuing household becoming the target of his enmity and their own loss; no separate generational distinction is made. [3 notes: TAB-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; IBNKATHIR-FULL(محمد بن إسحاق وغيره)]

## 28:8/q13 Why call Moses himself grief?  [وَحَزَنًا · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the verbal noun directly intensifies the description or has an omitted 'for them' relation.
- 28:8/q13/p1 It emphatically calls the cause of grief 'grief' itself. — Bursawī and Ālūsī say the wording intensifies sorrow; Ṭabāṭabāʾī calls grief the cause by figurative intensification. [3 notes: BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 28:8/q13/p2 It is a verbal noun with an omitted relation, meaning grief for them. — Ibn ʿĀshūr rejects analysis as a source noun used adjectivally for emphasis and calls the attribution to Moses a causal metaphor. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:8/q14 How is حزن read?  [وَحَزَنًا · readings]
turns on: The two attested vowel patterns and their dialectal or noun-form analysis.
- 28:8/q14/p1 With fatḥa on both ḥāʾ and zāy: ḥazanan. — The majority or remaining readers use this form; Abū ʿUbayd selects it, and Abū Ḥayyān and Ālūsī call it Quraysh usage. Ṭabarī finds both readings correct and close in sense; Qirāʾāt ER says both are attested verbal-noun patterns. [10 notes: IBNMUJAHID(حمزة والكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); JISHUMI; RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش ويحيى والمفضل وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); ALUSI-FULL(جمهور); TAB-FULL; QIRAAT-ER]
- 28:8/q14/p2 With ḍamma on ḥāʾ and sukūn on zāy: ḥuznan. — Ḥamza and al-Kisāʾī are repeatedly named; Nashr and Ibn ʿĀshūr include Khalaf, Qurṭubī also names Aʿmash, Yaḥyā, and Mufaḍḍal, and Ālūsī names Ibn Waththāb and Ibn Saʿdān. Abū Ḥātim favors emphatic pronunciation. Ṭabarī distinguishes noun ḥuzn from verbal noun ḥazan; Fārisī, Wāḥidī, Zamakhsharī, Nasafī, and others treat the forms as synonymous dialect variants, comparing ʿadam/ʿudm, sickness, guidance, Arab, and ʿajam pairs. [22 notes: TAB-FULL(قراء الكوفة); ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(حمزة والكسائي); FARISI-HUJJA(حمزة والكسائي); WAHIDI-BASIT; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); QURTUBI-FULL(الأعمش ويحيى والمفضل وحمزة والكسائي وخلف); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL(حمزة); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(العامة); NASHR(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); ALUSI-FULL(ابن وثاب والأعمش وحمزة والكسائي وابن سعدان); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة والكسائي وخلف); TABATABAI; QIRAAT-ER]

## 28:8/q15 How should the doubled wāw at عدوًّا وحزنًا be recited?  [عَدُوًّۭا وَحَزَنًا · readings]
- 28:8/q15/p1 Keep each wāw clear rather than chewing or blurring it. — Ibn al-Jazarī in Nashr applies the rule to vowelled or repeated wāw and names this phrase as a geminated example. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 28:8/q16 What does جنود mean?  [وَجُنُودَهُمَا · meaning]
- 28:8/q16/p1 It is the plural of jund, a large assembled multitude. — Ibn al-Jawzī gives the lexical sense and reports the assembled multitudes, including Pharaoh, Haman, and their troops, as an interpretive use. [2 notes: IBNJAWZI-NUZHA(بعض المفسرين)]

## 28:8/q17 Why are the troops attributed to both Pharaoh and Haman?  [وَجُنُودَهُمَا · grammar]
- 28:8/q17/p1 The king commanded and compelled the troops; his minister raised the funds needed to maintain them. — Abū Ḥayyān says Haman did not have troops of his own, yet both were linked to the army because of their respective roles. [1 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 28:8/q18 Who was Haman and what was his military role?  [هَٰمَٰنَ وَجُنُودَهُمَا · reports]
turns on: The notes report different aspects of Haman's identity and military function.
- 28:8/q18/p1 He was Pharaoh's Coptic minister. — Qurṭubī identifies his office and origin. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]
- 28:8/q18/p2 He commanded a vast vanguard of 1,700,000 horses. — Ibn Abī Ḥātim reports this from Suddī. [1 notes: IBNABIHATIM(السدي)]

## 28:8/q19 What does خاطئين say about Pharaoh, Haman, and their troops?  [خَٰطِـِٔينَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether khāṭiʾīn names deliberate sin and its punishment or their disastrous misjudgment; several sources allow both.
- 28:8/q19/p1 They were culpable sinners, especially for unbelief, shirk, and oppression; God made them raise their destroyer as punishment. — Yaḥyā calls them polytheists; Wāḥidī, Ṭabrisī, Qurṭubī, and others say disobedient. Damghānī and Ibn al-Jawzī cite 69:37 for shirk. Rāzī's majority view and Bayḍāwī make their self-defeating adoption a punishment; Abū Ḥayyān says their crimes persisted. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies oppression of Israel and idol worship; Elmalılı says otherwise they would not have taken him in. [21 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; WAHIDI-BASIT; DAMGHANI; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-NUZHA; RAZI-FULL(جمهور المفسرين); QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL; STUDYQURAN]
- 28:8/q19/p2 They were habitually mistaken or wrong in their actions, shown by raising the very child they sought to kill. — Kashshāf and Bayḍāwī say they were wrong in everything; Bayḍāwī notes thousands killed. Rāzī reports Ḥasan's view that this was mistaken appraisal, not sin. Mubarrad says they wronged themselves in taking him; others blame child-killing or raising him. Biqāʿī, Bursawī, Ālūsī and Iṣlāḥī stress the irony of slaughtering boys while raising Moses. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الحسن); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(المبرد); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(a minority of commentators)]
- 28:8/q19/p3 They were sinners and also wrong in all their affairs; the two explanations can reinforce each other. — Nasafī combines the usual error and punishment senses; Ālūsī allows judgmental error or deliberate sin. Study Quran gives both senses without preferring one. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; STUDYQURAN]

## 28:8/q20 What is the distinction between khaṭiʾa and akhṭaʾa?  [خَٰطِـِٔينَ · meaning]
turns on: Whether the forms distinguish intent and culpability or overlap in Arab usage.
- 28:8/q20/p1 Khaṭiʾa is deliberate culpable sin; akhṭaʾa or mukhṭiʾ refers to unintended error. — Abū Ḥayyān contrasts intentional khāṭiʾ and mukhṭiʾ; Bursawī describes a person intending good but slipping. Amwī distinguishes wrong aim from missing despite aiming right. Elmalılı derives the verse's form from khatīʾa. Ibn ʿĀshūr cites 96:16, 17:31, 33:5 and reports Nuftawayh, Jawharī, and Ḥarīrī's separate usage; Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 17:31 and 12:91. [8 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL(الأموي); BURSEVI; ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL(نفطويه والجوهري والحريري); TABATABAI]
- 28:8/q20/p2 The two verbs can be synonymous dialect variants, or either can denote failure to hit the right course. — Abū ʿUbayd and Ibn Qutayba, reported by Ibn ʿĀshūr, treat them as synonymous; Biqāʿī reports Abū ʿUbayd's dialect account, Ibn ʿArafa's allowance for intended or unintended wrong paths, and Ibn Ẓarīf's 'missing the mark' gloss. Ibn ʿĀshūr says Zamakhsharī appears to follow this view. [4 notes: BIQAI-FULL(أبو عبيد,ابن ظريف,ابن عرفة); IBNASHUR-FULL(أبو عبيد وابن قتيبة)]

## 28:8/q21 How is خاطئين read and derived?  [خَٰطِـِٔينَ · readings]
turns on: Whether loss of hamza is phonetic easing or reflects the separate root 'to step'.
- 28:8/q21/p1 The common reading retains hamza in khāṭiʾīn, from khaṭiʾa, error or sin. — Sāmin identifies the ordinary reading and derivation; Ibn ʿĀshūr gives its active participle and verbal noun khaṭʾ. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR(العامة); IBNASHUR-FULL]
- 28:8/q21/p2 A hamza-less reading lightens the same word while keeping its meaning. — Nasafī names Abū Jaʿfar; Kashshāf, Rāzī, Bayḍāwī, Abū Ḥayyān, Sāmin, and Ālūsī report deletion or softening. Qirāʾāt ER calls it a canonical ten-reader form with vowel compensation and says this is the dominant analysis. [8 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL(أبو جعفر); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(قُرِئَ); QIRAAT-ER]
- 28:8/q21/p3 A hamza-less form may derive from khaṭā yakhtū, stepping or straying past the right course. — Kashshāf and the later grammarians offer this alternate derivation; Qirāʾāt ER notes a literal 'those who tread' proposal but favors the pronunciation-variant account. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(غير مسمى); ALUSI-FULL(قُرِئَ); QIRAAT-ER(some grammarians)]

## 28:8/q22 What does the closing clause do rhetorically?  [إِنَّ فِرْعَوْنَ وَهَٰمَٰنَ وَجُنُودَهُمَا كَانُوا۟ خَٰطِـِٔينَ · rhetoric]
turns on: Whether the final sentence chiefly reinforces the error already shown or explains the punishment caused by their sins.
- 28:8/q22/p1 It reinforces the preceding event as a tadhyīl or emphatic closing. — Bāqillānī defines tadhyīl as emphatic reinforcement and treats this narrative as an example; Qāḍī Abū Bakr, reported by Zarkashī, specifically counts this close as tadhyīl. Bayḍāwī and Ālūsī say it emphasizes their error. [4 notes: BAQILLANI; BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر); BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL]
- 28:8/q22/p2 It explains why they were afflicted with raising their enemy. — Zarkashī allows the close as explanation rather than tadhyīl. Kashshāf, Bayḍāwī, Ibn ʿĀshūr, and Elmalılı connect their crimes to God's punishment. Ālūsī reports a parenthetical explanation or explanatory resumption; the choice depends on whether affliction names Moses' enmity and grief. [7 notes: BURHAN(القاضي أبو بكر); KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(قيل); ELMALILI; IBNASHUR-FULL]

## 28:8/q23 What does the verse teach about God's decree and Pharaoh's precautions?  [* · theology]
turns on: The notes emphasize distinct facets of divine decree: the fixed outcome, Moses' protection, and punishment for oppression.
- 28:8/q23/p1 God's prior knowledge and decree made Moses Pharaoh's enemy despite Pharaoh's precautions. — ʿUmar ibn ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz argues against a Qadari claim that Pharaoh could have supported Moses, invoking 20:43 and the promise in 28:6. Ibn Kathīr says God's arrangement defeated the regime's precautions more fully. Biqāʿī contrasts the slaughter of sons with Moses being raised in Pharaoh's own household; Iṣlāḥī says their power could not perpetuate oppression. [7 notes: IBNABIHATIM(عمر بن عبد العزيز); IBNKATHIR-FULL(عمر بن عبد العزيز); BURHAN; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]
- 28:8/q23/p2 Love placed in hearts protected Moses under divine care. — Ibn Kathīr connects the love he aroused from birth to 20:39; Kuran Yolu says God surrounded him with love so he could be protected, raised, and matured, again citing 20:39. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR]
- 28:8/q23/p3 Their persecution of Israel was met by raising their enemy within their house. — Kuran Yolu calls this recompense; Ibn ʿĀshūr names oppression and idolatry, and Biqāʿī and Iṣlāḥī stress the reversal of Pharaoh's plans. [4 notes: KURANYOLU-TEFSIR; IBNASHUR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR]

## 28:8/q24 How many infants were killed in the search for Moses?  [* · reports]
turns on: The sources give either a reported precise figure or an unspecified multitude.
- 28:8/q24/p1 Ninety thousand newborn boys were slaughtered. — Ālūsī transmits the figure as a report. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(قيل)]
- 28:8/q24/p2 Thousands were killed, without an exact number. — Bayḍāwī and Bursawī use the unspecified number to underscore the folly of subsequently raising Moses. [2 notes: BAYDAWI-FULL; BURSEVI]

## 28:8/q25 What was Pharaoh's policy toward Israelite births before Moses' discovery?  [* · reports]
turns on: These reports describe different elements of the birth policy.
- 28:8/q25/p1 He ordered boys killed in alternating years, with Aaron born in a spared year and Moses in a killing year. — Ibn Kathīr says Copts warned that perpetual slaughter would remove their labor force as elders died and women could not replace men. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 28:8/q25/p2 He used midwives, watchers, and executioners to inspect births and kill male infants. — Ibn Kathīr reports that Coptic women attended Israelite deliveries; girls were spared, boys were killed by armed men. Moses' pregnancy had no usual signs, escaping the midwives. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 28:8/q26 How does this verse connect to the preceding promise and the next scene?  [* · links]
turns on: The sources connect the verse to God's earlier promise, its immediate narrative sequence, or the Qur'an's repeated Moses accounts.
- 28:8/q26/p1 It shows the feared child entering Pharaoh's own house, fulfilling the reversal announced in 28:6. — Ibn Kathīr prefers divine purpose in light of 28:6; Biqāʿī says Pharaoh's slaughter failed to prevent the feared child being raised by him. Iṣlāḥī sees a rebuke to confidence in oppressive power; Kuran Yolu relates the later lack of awareness to what Moses would do. [4 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; KURANYOLU-TEFSIR(Râzî)]
- 28:8/q26/p2 The narrative gives its ending early, then returns to the household and Asiya's appeal in 28:9. — Biqāʿī says the anticipatory outcome frames and arouses interest in the details; Ṭabarī's and Thaʿlabī's discovery reports lead into Asiya's plea. [5 notes: BIQAI-FULL; TAB-FULL(ابن إسحاق,السدي,محمد بن قيس); THALABI]
- 28:8/q26/p3 Repeated tellings of Moses and Pharaoh vary in wording, order, and detail to convey distinct nuances. — Zarkashī contrasts the several tellings from 28:3–43 with Joseph's story gathered in one sūra, unlike earlier scripture's separate tellings. [1 notes: BURHAN]

## 28:8/q27 What inward meaning is drawn from Pharaoh's household and its enemy?  [* · inward]
- 28:8/q27/p1 The household signifies dominating psychic faculties, while the true inner enemy is the self to be subdued. — Qāshānī says discernment and protection use imagination, estimation, and inner and outer senses; discipline, restraint, breaking, and mortification overcome the self and its helpers. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 28:8/q28 Was the sūra containing this verse revealed in Mecca?  [* · sciences]
- 28:8/q28/p1 All of al-Qaṣaṣ is Meccan. — Ibn al-Jawzī reports this from al-Ḥasan, ʿAṭāʾ, and ʿIkrima. [1 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الحسن وعطاء وعكرمة)]
