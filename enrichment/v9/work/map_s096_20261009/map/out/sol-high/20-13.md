# 20:13: 24 questions

## 20:13/q01 How is 'I chose you' read?  [وَأَنَا ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · readings]
turns on: Singular versus majestic plural, light versus doubled nūn, and open versus broken initial hamza; the singular is supported by the written form and nearby direct address, while the plural has the 17:1–2 parallel.
- 20:13/q01/p1 Wa-anā ikhtartuka: a light nūn and singular 'I chose you.' — Ibn Mujāhid names Ibn Kathīr, Nāfiʿ, ʿĀṣim, Abū ʿAmr, Ibn ʿĀmir and al-Kisāʾī; al-Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī name the Medinans, Abū ʿAmr, ʿĀṣim and al-Kisāʾī. Ibn Khālawayh parses anā as subject, ikhtartu as predicate, its tāʾ as agent and kāf as object. Al-Naḥḥās and Qurṭubī favor the written form and direct-address sequence; al-Fārisī favors agreement with 'I am your Lord' and Ubayy's reported text, though he acknowledges the plural parallel in 17:1–2. Ibn ʿAṭiyya likewise notes harmony with 'I am your Lord.' [16 notes: FARRA; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر والكسائي); NAHHAS(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو وعاصم والكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير ونافع وأبو عمرو وعاصم وابن عامر والكسائي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(السبعة غير حمزة); TABRISI(حمزة); QURTUBI-FULL(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو وعاصم والكسائي); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الباقون); NASHR(حمزة); FARISI-HUJJA+; NAHHAS+; QURTUBI-FULL(النحاس,حمزة)+]
- 20:13/q01/p2 Wa-annā ikhtarnāka: a doubled nūn and majestic plural 'We chose you,' with an open hamza. — Ibn Mujāhid and many later sources attribute it to Ḥamza; Ibn al-Jawzī also names al-Mufaḍḍal, al-Samīn says Ḥamza and others, and al-Ālūsī names Ṭalḥa, Ibn Abī Laylā, Ḥamza, Khalaf and one transmission from al-Aʿmash. Ibn Khālawayh explains the plural as the royal 'we' of rulers; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Ṭabrisī cite the singular-to-plural shift in 17:1–2. Al-Naḥḥās says its sense equals the singular. Some reports describe Ḥamza's initial form as wa-innā rather than wa-annā. Ibn ʿĀshūr attributes the majestic-plural reading to Ḥamza alone. [28 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNMUJAHID(ابن كثير ونافع وعاصم وأبو عمرو وابن عامر والكسائي,حمزة); NAHHAS(أهل المدينة وأبو عمرو وعاصم والكسائي); IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; FARISI-HUJJA(الأعمش وأبي,حمزة); THALABI(حمزة); JISHUMI(حمزة); BAGHAWI-FULL(حمزة); KASHSHAF-FULL(حمزة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(حمزة); TABRISI(حمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(حمزة والمفضل); RAZI-FULL(حمزة وأبي بن كعب); QURTUBI-FULL(حمزة); BAYDAWI-FULL(حمزة); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(حمزة في آخرين); NASHR(حمزة); BURSEVI(حمزة); ALUSI-FULL(طلحة وابن أبي ليلى وحمزة وخلف والأعمش); QIRAAT-ER(Pf|Dot); IBNASHUR-FULL(حمزة)]
- 20:13/q01/p3 Wa-innā ikhtarnāka: the plural with a kasra on the initial hamza. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya attributes it to a group; al-Samīn names al-Sulamī, al-Aʿmash and Ibn Hurmuz; al-Ālūsī names those readers but distinguishes one of two transmissions from al-Aʿmash. Ibn Khālawayh explains the doubled nūn as assimilation of two nūns, and al-Zajjāj permits an independent or quoted clause. [7 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; IBNATIYYA-FULL(فرقة); SAMIN-DURR(السلمي والأعمش وابن هرمز); ALUSI-FULL(السلمي وابن هرمز والأعمش)]
- 20:13/q01/p4 Ubayy's reported singular wa-annī or wa-innī ikhtartuka. — Al-Fārisī reports Ubayy's codex as wa-annī ikhtartuka and uses it to support the singular; Ibn ʿAṭiyya and al-Samīn report wa-annī, whereas al-Rāzī reports wa-innī. Al-Samīn says its open hamza follows the coordination of Ḥamza's reading. Abū Ḥayyān also mentions Ubayy's yāʾ reading. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(الأعمش وأبي); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبي بن كعب); RAZI-FULL(حمزة وأبي بن كعب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(أبي)]
- 20:13/q01/p5 A proposed open yāʾ in Abū Jaʿfar's reading is inferred, without explicit transmission. — Ibn al-Jazarī says Abū Jaʿfar's general rule would open yāʾ where he cuts the hamza, but he found no explicit report for this inference. [1 notes: NASHR]

## 20:13/q02 How does the clause 'and I chose you' connect grammatically to the preceding speech?  [وَأَنَا ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · grammar]
turns on: The vowel of the initial hamza and whether the clause coordinates with the preceding call, resumes speech, or supplies an implied cause.
- 20:13/q02/p1 It is coordinated with the preceding declaration of God's lordship or call to Moses. — Al-Farrāʾ attaches the singular clause to nūdiya; Ibn Khālawayh continues the open-hamza plural from 'I am your Lord' in 20:12. Al-Samīn prefers coordination with the preceding 'I am your Lord' and says the open or broken hamza follows the preceding reading; al-Ālūsī allows coordination whether the earlier clause attaches to nūdiya or an implied 'know.' The Qirāʾāt Encyclopedia glosses anna as subordinate to the preceding verb, 'and that I chose you.' [6 notes: FARRA; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; QIRAAT-ER(Gm); SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:13/q02/p2 The kasra form begins a new, independent clause or quoted speech. — Al-Farrāʾ calls it a new sentence; al-Zajjāj permits resumption or a quotation of what Moses was told, 'We chose you.' Ibn Khālawayh treats the doubled-nūn particle as opening the clause. Al-Ālūsī also reports an analysis treating the clause as an independent address with no syntactic governor. [4 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; IBNKHALAWAYH-HUJJA; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:13/q02/p3 The open-hamza clause can depend on an implied 'know' or on the preceding command to remove the sandals. — Al-Ālūsī reports both analyses, and says the open clause can coordinate with the earlier 'I am your Lord' under either nūdiya or implied aʿlam; he ascribes an implied aʿlam to the open plural reading. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(طلحة وابن أبي ليلى وحمزة وخلف والأعمش)]
- 20:13/q02/p4 An implied 'because' makes the clause the reason to listen. — Abū al-Baqāʾ proposes 'because I chose you, so listen,' linking it to istamiʿ; al-Ālūsī reports and dismisses this implied-lām construction. [1 notes: SAMIN-DURR(أبو البقاء); ALUSI-FULL(أبو البقاء)-]

## 20:13/q03 What does 'I chose you' mean?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · meaning]
- 20:13/q03/p1 God selected Moses; choice selects the one best suited to its purpose. — Al-Thaʿlabī and Ibn al-Jawzī gloss ikhtartuka as 'I selected you.' Ṭabāṭabāʾī derives choice from khayr: an agent weighs alternatives and selects the best for a purpose, and God found Moses best suited to receive prophecy and mission. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains ikhtiyār as making an effort to seek what is good; its form here expresses excellent pursuit of that good. [4 notes: THALABI(حمزة); IBNJAWZI-ZAD; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:13/q04 For what did God choose Moses?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the choice confers the prophetic mission here or marks an earlier stage in an inward sequence.
- 20:13/q04/p1 For prophethood and messengership, including the privilege of divine speech. — Muqātil, Yaḥyā, al-Kalbī, al-Baghawī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr and the Study Quran explicitly include the message, speech, or both; the Study Quran connects direct address with Moses' title Kalīm Allāh. Ibn Kathīr and the Study Quran cite 7:144. Al-Wāḥidī reports al-Kalbī's purpose: Moses is to carry out God's command. Ṭabāṭabāʾī says God chose Moses as suited to prophecy and mission; al-Iṣlāḥī describes the appointment as honor and great responsibility. Ibn ʿĀshūr specifies that Moses is selected to receive what God will reveal. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT(الكلبي); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; STUDYQURAN(R); IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 20:13/q04/p2 In the inward reading, this is an election before prophetic commissioning by revelation. — Al-Qāshānī distinguishes disclosure of attributes before Essence, says Moses is sent at this stage without commissioning by revelation, and interprets the order as spiritual discipline, presence and vigilance. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:13/q05 From among whom was Moses chosen?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · referent]
turns on: The unstated comparison group: all humanity, Moses' people, or those alive in his time.
- 20:13/q05/p1 From humanity at large. — Al-Ālūsī gives humanity as one scope of God's selection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:13/q05/p2 From his own people. — Al-Samīn says a second object of ikhtartuka is omitted, yielding 'I chose you from among your people'; al-Ālūsī also gives this narrower scope. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]
- 20:13/q05/p3 Above all people alive in Moses' time. — Ibn Kathīr grounds the temporal scope and distinction in 7:144, where God selects Moses above the people by His messages and words. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL]

## 20:13/q06 Was Moses' selection granted at this moment or reported as already complete?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the clause performs a new appointment or describes a stage in a prior and later sequence of election.
- 20:13/q06/p1 The utterance itself confers prophecy and mission at this moment. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the clause as a performative appointment, not a report of an earlier choice; the command to listen follows from this appointment. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:13/q06/p2 This choice is an intermediate inward election near the original election in 20:122. — Al-Qāshānī places it between the original election of 20:122 and a later selection, within his account of Moses' self-disclosure and awakening. [1 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:13/q07 What does Moses' selection teach about the source of prophethood?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · theology]
- 20:13/q07/p1 Prophethood comes from God's initial choice, not human entitlement. — Al-Rāzī infers this from God's choosing Moses for prophethood and privileged speech. [1 notes: RAZI-FULL]

## 20:13/q08 Why was Moses singled out for direct speech?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · reports]
- 20:13/q08/p1 He humbled himself before God more than anyone else. — Ibn Kathīr transmits an unnamed dialogue in which God asks Moses the reason, Moses says he does not know, and God gives Moses' unique humility as the answer. The Study Quran reports Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī's statement that Moses' heart was the most humble among Adam's children. [2 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(قيل); STUDYQURAN(Abū Sulaymān al-Dārānī)]

## 20:13/q09 What inward effect does God's choice have on Moses or the chosen servant?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · inward]
turns on: Different accounts of what selection does inwardly: preparation, purification and effacement, or awakening after self-disclosure.
- 20:13/q09/p1 The chosen servant is nurtured for revelation, loved, articulate and made steadfast. — Al-Sulamī reports al-Wāsiṭī: God's choice nurtures and selects the servant, makes God's servants love him, enables clear speech, and eases his affairs so he can stand before revelation. [1 notes: SULAMI(الواسطي)]
- 20:13/q09/p2 Choice purifies Moses of illusion and effaces his self so that he belongs to God and acts through Him. — Al-Qushayrī says God's choice is made with knowledge of Moses and strips away what clouds clarity; he also reports Moses' annihilation after selection. [2 notes: QUSHAYRI]
- 20:13/q09/p3 Choice follows the collapse of Moses' separate existence and awakening through true existence. — Al-Qāshānī reads 7:143–144 as Moses' mountain-like existence crumbling in annihilation, his stunned fall, and subsequent awakening; he calls this disclosure of attributes before Essence. [2 notes: QASHANI]

## 20:13/q10 Why does the command to listen follow the announcement of selection?  [فَٱسْتَمِعْ · rhetoric]
- 20:13/q10/p1 Selection gives the reason for listening: Moses must attend to the revelation of his new calling. — Al-Ālūsī says the fāʾ makes listening follow from selection. Al-Ṭabrisī says the instruction to hear revelation follows the announcement of prophethood; Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the command follows the conferral of prophecy and mission. Al-Iṣlāḥī says attentive listening focuses Moses and marks both honor and new responsibility. Al-Biqāʿī hears honor in the private, intimate-sounding address even though it is heard openly. Ibn ʿĀshūr grounds the following command in the choice of Moses to receive revelation. [6 notes: TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:13/q11 What does 'listen' ask Moses to do?  [فَٱسْتَمِعْ · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the command is explained chiefly as concentrated hearing or as obedient action flowing from it.
- 20:13/q11/p1 Attend silently and intently to the revelation, ready to understand it. — Ibn al-Anbārī glosses listening as silence and attention. Al-Rāzī says Moses must ready his whole mind for a grave matter; choice gives hope and mercy while the command gives awe and fear. Al-Ṭabrisī adds careful, steady attention. Qurṭubī calls attentive listening a discipline that brings understanding, citing 39:18, 17:47 and 7:204; Sufyān b. ʿUyayna orders listening before understanding, memorizing, acting and teaching, and says sincere listening brings light. Wahb specifies still limbs, lowered gaze, attentive hearing, present mind and intent to understand and act. Al-Iṣlāḥī says attention focuses Moses for revelation. Al-Ālūsī says the command signals the greatness of the revelation and the need to prepare for it; he reports Wahb's account of still limbs, lowered gaze, focused hearing and present understanding. Ibn ʿĀshūr says istamiʿ can convey aṣghi, attentive listening. [19 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن الأنباري); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(سفيان بن عيينة,وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب); IBNKATHIR-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; BURSEVI; TABATABAI; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; ALUSI-FULL(وهب); ALUSI-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+]
- 20:13/q11/p2 Listen so as to carry out the revealed commands and avoid prohibitions. — Al-Wāḥidī explicitly interprets listening as acting on God's commands and prohibitions; Wahb includes a resolve to act in proper listening, and al-Bursawī says Moses listens to revealed commands and prohibitions. Al-Ālūsī reports Wahb's inclusion of a resolve to act on what God loves in attentive listening. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT; QURTUBI-FULL(وهب بن منبه); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(وهب); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(وهب)]

## 20:13/q12 How did Moses physically listen at this moment?  [فَٱسْتَمِعْ · reports]
- 20:13/q12/p1 He stood at or leaned on a stone, hands folded, chin lowered, dressed in wool. — Ibn ʿAṭiyya transmits Abū al-Faḍl al-Jawharī; Qurṭubī cites Ibn ʿAṭiyya's father citing al-Jawharī; Abū Ḥayyān repeats the account. The reports vary slightly between standing against a stone, standing by one and leaning on another, and leaning on a stone. Al-Ālūsī reports Abū al-Faḍl al-Jawharī's details that Moses stood on one stone, leaned on another, placed his right hand over his left, lowered his chin and listened intently. [4 notes: IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو الفضل بن الجوهري); QURTUBI-FULL(أبو الفضل الجوهري); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(أبو الفضل الجوهري); ALUSI-FULL(أبو الفضل الجوهري)]

## 20:13/q13 What is Moses to listen to?  [لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · referent]
turns on: Whether the phrase points specifically to the declaration immediately following or to the revelation and instructions more broadly.
- 20:13/q13/p1 God's declaration of exclusive divinity and the command to worship Him in 20:14. — Muqātil calls it God's declaration of oneness; Ibn al-Jawzī quotes 'I am God; there is no god but Me, so worship Me'; Abū Ḥayyān likewise identifies God's declaration of exclusive divinity. [3 notes: MUQATIL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:13/q13/p2 The revelation now being spoken to Moses, including its commands and prohibitions. — Yaḥyā, al-Baghawī and Ibn Kathīr identify the revelation being given now; al-Bursawī specifies commands and prohibitions, while al-Wāḥidī says listening includes acting on both. Ibn ʿĀshūr identifies the immediate object as the revelation then being delivered and says listening to later revelations follows a fortiori. [8 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; WAHIDI-BASIT; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; BAGHAWI-FULL; TABRISI; IBNKATHIR-FULL; BURSEVI; IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:13/q14 Does mā mean revealed content or the act of revelation?  [لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether mā is a relative pronoun or a verbal-noun particle.
- 20:13/q14/p1 A relative pronoun: that which is revealed. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī offer this as one sense; Abū Ḥayyān favors it as the natural meaning; al-Samīn identifies the relative-pronoun parsing. Al-Ālūsī reports the relative reading of mā as 'what is revealed' within the independent-address analysis. [6 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL+]
- 20:13/q14/p2 A verbal noun: revelation itself. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī offer revelation itself as the alternative; al-Samīn calls this the verbal-noun parsing. Al-Ālūsī also reports the verbal-noun reading, 'revelation,' within that analysis. [5 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL]

## 20:13/q15 Which verb governs the lām phrase 'to what is revealed'?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ فَٱسْتَمِعْ لِمَا يُوحَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the phrase is governed by the command to listen or by the verb of selection; the objection to technical dual government is the missing repeated pronoun.
- 20:13/q15/p1 It attaches to 'listen'; the lām can function as an added preposition before its object. — Al-Bayḍāwī, al-Nasafī and al-Samīn offer this attachment; al-Samīn prefers it as apparent, as does Abū Ḥayyān. Al-Bursawī calls the lām added before the object, comparing radifa lakum in 27:72; al-Samīn also cites 27:72 for an extra lām. Abū Ḥayyān argues that assigning technical government to 'chose' violates the two-governor rule without a repeated pronoun; al-Samīn reports his expected form as fastamiʿ lahu limā yūḥā. Al-Ālūsī cites this objection but also reports a reply allowing alternative coordination. Ibn ʿĀshūr says the lām reinforces istamiʿ's link to its object; alternatively, taking istamiʿ in the sense of aṣghi explains the lām with attentive listening. [12 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان); ABUHAYYAN-FULL+; IBNASHUR-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:13/q15/p2 It can attach to 'I chose you': Moses was chosen for revelation and must listen to it. — Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī allow the attachment; al-Samīn reports al-Zamakhsharī and others as describing tanāzuʿ, but clarifies they mean semantic suitability rather than shared technical government. Al-Ṭabrisī reports Abū ʿAlī's special parsing of the plural as wa-li-annā ikhtarnāka, making that prepositional phrase the object of 'listen.' Al-Ālūsī records a reply permitting either attachment by alternative coordination. Ibn ʿĀshūr offers the attachment to 'I chose you for revelation' and treats the intervening command as parenthetical. [7 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; TABRISI(أبو علي); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; SAMIN-DURR(الزمخشري وغيره); IBNASHUR-FULL+; ABUHAYYAN-FULL-; SAMIN-DURR(الشيخ)-; ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)-]

## 20:13/q16 Why is 'is revealed' passive?  [يُوحَىٰٓ · rhetoric]
- 20:13/q16/p1 Its agent is already known, and the passive suits the verse ending. — Abū Ḥayyān says the known agent is suppressed and the passive fits the verse's final sound. Al-Ālūsī adds that an active verb would not supply the verse ending furnished by the passive. [2 notes: ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:13/q17 How does the call unfold across the following verses?  [* · links]
- 20:13/q17/p1 The passage first states Moses' prophethood, then develops his mission through the staff question and command to go to Pharaoh. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī reads the next eleven verses as containing both prophecy and mission; the mission begins with the staff question and ends with the order to approach Pharaoh, and 19:51 names Moses both prophet and messenger. [1 notes: TABATABAI]
- 20:13/q17/p2 The call begins with kindly choice and ends with a severe warning, teaching that mercy precedes wrath and the servant lives between hope and fear. — Al-Ālūsī contrasts the opening choice in 20:13 with the closing warning in 20:16 and draws the lessons of mercy before wrath and hope alongside fear. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]
- 20:13/q17/p3 The address reasons from God's identity and oneness to worship and prayer, then the Hour and requital, warning against deniers, and the ruin that follows disobedience. — Ibn ʿĀshūr traces how each command and prohibition in the address follows from the preceding point. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 20:13/q18 Can Moses' call be God's revelation if a veil or intermediary is involved?  [* · theology]
- 20:13/q18/p1 Yes; attention passes beyond the unseen intermediary, so Moses experiences the message as God's. — Ṭabāṭabāʾī compares passages describing a veil or intermediary in Moses' call, citing 19:52 and 28:30 along with 20:13. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

- 20:13/q19/p1 God spoke to Moses twice, while Muhammad saw his Lord twice and Adam disobeyed twice. — Muqātil reports this from Kaʿb without grading it. [1 notes: MUQATIL(كعب)]

## 20:13/q21 What does the adjacent divine istiwāʾ mean?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether istiwāʾ denotes power or a distinct act or attribute.
- 20:13/q21/p1 It means power. — Al-Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's explanation as sounder and calls the Kullābī account unintelligible. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]
- 20:13/q21/p2 It is an act or attribute called istiwāʾ. — Al-Jishumī attributes this account to the Kullābīs and argues against its intelligibility. [0 notes: JISHUMI-]

## 20:13/q22 How is the nearby command to establish prayer for remembrance understood?  [* · interpretation]
turns on: Whether remembrance is within prayer, its entry, or recall of a forgotten prayer.
- 20:13/q22/p1 Remember God during prayer. — Al-Māwardī reports Mujāhid's explanation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL(مجاهد)]
- 20:13/q22/p2 Begin prayer by remembering God, since prayer is entered only through His remembrance. — Al-Māwardī reports this as another interpretation. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 20:13/q23 Does God's direct speech to Moses establish his superiority over Muhammad?  [ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · theology]
- 20:13/q23/p1 No; God addressed Muhammad directly on the Night Journey, unveiled and with a vision that Moses was not granted. — Al-Ālūsī argues from the direct address to Muhammad on the Night Journey while discussing God's speech to Moses; he mentions 4:164 and 17:1. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL+]

## 20:13/q24 What does the predication in 'I chose you' accomplish?  [وَأَنَا ٱخْتَرْتُكَ · rhetoric]
- 20:13/q24/p1 It reinforces the surprising announcement of choice so Moses will not doubt it; it does not restrict the assertion. — Ibn ʿĀshūr explains that verbal predication strengthens unexpected news without making it exclusive. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]
