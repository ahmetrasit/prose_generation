# 18:39: 26 questions

## 18:39/q01 What force does walawlā have here?  [وَلَوْلَآ · interpretation]
- 18:39/q01/p1 It reproaches and exhorts the owner: why did you not say this upon entering? — Yaḥyā glosses it with hallā; al-Zajjāj calls it rebuke; al-Samīn notes that the past verb after exhortative lawlā gives censure. Ibn ʿĀshūr connects it to the earlier denial and the owner's boasting; Asad renders it 'alas' and refers to 10:98. [14 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; ZAJJAJ; WAHIDI-BASIT; IBNATIYYA-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ASAD-NOTES; MUQATIL; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; ELMALILI]

## 18:39/q02 How does lawlā govern the intervening phrase?  [وَلَوْلَآ إِذْ دَخَلْتَ قُلْتَ · grammar]
- 18:39/q02/p1 It governs qult(a), with idh dakhalta attached to that verb despite intervening between the particle and its complement. — Al-Samīn says the adverbial is part of the construction, not a foreign interruption; Abū Ḥayyān also calls lawlā exhortative and notes the separation. [2 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ABUHAYYAN-FULL]

## 18:39/q03 How is idh pronounced before dakhalta?  [إِذْ دَخَلْتَ · readings]
turns on: The transmitted readers and routes differ between assimilation and distinct articulation.
- 18:39/q03/p1 The consonant of idh is assimilated into the following dāl. — Ibn al-Jazarī defines small assimilation as involving a quiescent first consonant and lists idh before six letters. He reports assimilation at dāl for Abū ʿAmr, Hishām, Ḥamza, Khalaf, al-Kisāʾī and Khallād, with route-specific reports for Ibn Dhakwān and idh dakhalta alone; al-Itqān likewise places dāl among the disputed six. [11 notes: NASHR(أبو العز عن زيد عن الرملي، هبة الله عن الأخفش,أبو عمرو وهشام,ابن ذكوان,الكرزيني، صاحب المبهج، أبو معشر عن رويس,الكسائي وخلاد,النهرواني عن الأخفش، الفارسي عن الحمامي,حمزة وخلف); ITQAN]
- 18:39/q03/p2 The consonant of idh is pronounced distinctly before dāl. — Ibn al-Jazarī reports this from Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, ʿĀṣim, Abū Jaʿfar, Yaʿqūb, and the al-Ṣūrī and Hibat Allāh routes, while other routes distinguish idh dakhalta from three idh dakhalū sites. [5 notes: NASHR(أبو العز عن زيد عن الرملي، هبة الله عن الأخفش,ابن ذكوان,النهرواني عن الأخفش، الفارسي عن الحمامي,نافع وابن كثير وعاصم وأبو جعفر ويعقوب); ITQAN]

## 18:39/q04 What is the garden called jannataka?  [جَنَّتَكَ · meaning]
- 18:39/q04/p1 It is an orchard, including its fruits and crops. — Al-Zajjāj glosses janna as orchard; al-Ṭabrisī names the fruits and crops, while al-Qaffāl points to the objects visibly present in it. [3 notes: ZAJJAJ; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL(القفال)]

## 18:39/q05 When should the invocation in this verse be said?  [دَخَلْتَ جَنَّتَكَ قُلْتَ مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ · law]
turns on: The reports and commentators differ in how broadly they extend the garden-entry example.
- 18:39/q05/p1 On entering a garden, house, or property and when admiring one's blessings. — The garden scene supplies the model. ʿUrwa said it at his gardens or property, opened a wall for visitors at date harvest, and Ibn Shihāb said it on entering properties. Mālik used it on entering home; Wahb wrote it above his door. Ibn al-ʿArabī inferred a home-entry recommendation. Early authorities extend it to condition, wealth, and children; al-Ālūsī extends it to anything pleasing, one's own or another's. [20 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TAB-FULL; THALABI(رسول الله); BAGHAWI-FULL(هشام بن عروة); KASHSHAF-FULL(عروة بن الزبير); IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(ابن وهب,مالك); DURR-FULL(ابن شهاب,عروة,مالك,وهب بن منبه); ALUSI-FULL(عروة,مالك); ELMALILI; STUDYQURAN(Ibn Kathīr)]
- 18:39/q05/p2 It should be said with inward acknowledgment of God's will and power. — Al-Qurṭubī says the owner should say it with his heart; al-Ṭabrisī calls for thanks on seeing fruit and crops; al-Jishumī says gratitude and reliance help a blessing last. [3 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; JISHUMI]

## 18:39/q06 What is the syntax and force of mā shāʾ Allāh?  [مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · grammar]
turns on: The analyses differ over whether mā is relative, conditional or indefinite, and whether an unspoken clause precedes or follows it.
- 18:39/q06/p1 Mā refers to what God willed, with an understood subject or predicate: the matter or garden is what God willed. — Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj allow a nominative mā and implied huwa; al-Ṭabarī reports the same. Al-Zamakhsharī calls it relative; Ibn ʿAṭiyya also offers an omitted-subject report. Al-Qaffāl points to the visible garden objects. Al-Wāḥidī says it answers the owner's claim that the garden will never perish. Ibn ʿĀshūr explains it as what God willed to give the owner. [23 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL(القفال); QURTUBI-FULL(الزجاج والفراء); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; IBNASHUR-FULL-]
- 18:39/q06/p2 Mā is conditional or an object of shāʾ, with the result omitted: whatever God wills comes to pass. — Al-Farrāʾ, al-Naḥḥās, al-Wāḥidī and Ibn al-Jawzī compare the ellipsis at 6:35. Al-Biqāʿī says the missing answer makes it cover present and future, seen and unseen; al-Ālūsī says what God does not will cannot occur. Al-Ṭabarī adopts the accusative object parsing; other grammarians also allow it. [25 notes: FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; THALABI; WAHIDI-BASIT(الفراء والزجاج); JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الفراء); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; BIQAI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI]
- 18:39/q06/p3 Mā is an indefinite noun qualified by shāʾ Allāh: this is something God willed for me. — Ibn ʿĀshūr prefers this to the relative analysis. [1 notes: IBNASHUR-FULL+]

## 18:39/q07 How far does God's will in mā shāʾ Allāh extend?  [مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: Whether the expression is universal or limited to visible creation or direct divine acts, especially in relation to human wrongdoing.
- 18:39/q07/p1 Every event and created condition depends on God's will; what He does not will does not occur. — The Ashʿarites infer that God did not will the unbeliever's faith; al-Ālūsī calls this the natural scope and rejects Muʿtazilite restrictions. Abū Ḥayyān includes poverty, riches, support and abandonment; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ calls the universal sense best for the argument; the Study Quran cites 7:188 and 10:49. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(أصحابنا); ABUHAYYAN-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BIQAI-FULL; RAZI-FULL(الكعبي)-; ALUSI-FULL(القفال,الكعبي والجبائي)-]
- 18:39/q07/p2 It points to the garden's existing, observed contents, without claiming universal scope. — Al-Qaffāl argues from ordinary demonstrative use. Al-Rāzī prefers this over al-Jubbāʾī and al-Kaʿbī but says a stolen or unjustly built garden remains a difficulty on Muʿtazilite premises. Al-Ālūsī reports the narrower fruit-only version; al-Rāzī rejects restricting the text to fruit growth without evidence. [4 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); ALUSI-FULL(القفال); RAZI-FULL+; RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]
- 18:39/q07/p3 It covers what God directly does, while forbidden human acts can occur without being willed by Him. — Al-Kaʿbī compares this with God's command not being nullified, and al-Jubbāʾī is also named. Al-Rāzī objects that this departs from the apparent sense and confuses will with command; al-Ālūsī likewise objects. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(الكعبي); ALUSI-FULL(الكعبي والجبائي); RAZI-FULL-; ALUSI-FULL-]

## 18:39/q08 What does the garden owner's failure to say the formula imply about human choice?  [قُلْتَ مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ · theology]
turns on: The notes distinguish responsibility for an omitted utterance from attribution of wealth and status.
- 18:39/q08/p1 He could have said it; reproach for omitting it implies capacity before action. — Al-Jishumī uses the reproach against the Mujabira and says disbelief, association and wrongdoing are accountable human acts. [2 notes: JISHUMI]
- 18:39/q08/p2 Human status and fortune are God's dispensation, so faith calls for gratitude, patience and freedom from pride. — Al-Qaffāl says the rich man's self-attribution effectively makes a partner in granting honor and affluence; his denial of resurrection also likens God to incapable creatures. Alternatively he may be an idolater whom the believer answers by affirming divine uniqueness. [6 notes: RAZI-FULL(القفال); THALABI; JISHUMI]

## 18:39/q09 What is the case and construction of lā quwwata?  [لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ · grammar]
turns on: The grammatical case after lā, absolute negation versus nominative subject.
- 18:39/q09/p1 Quwwata is accusative under lā of absolute negation, with illā billāh supplying its predicate. — Al-Zajjāj prefers this total negation; Ibn al-Jawzī compares lā rayba fīhā. Al-Samīn calls the entire utterance a second object of qult. [3 notes: IBNJAWZI-ZAD(الزجاج); SAMIN-DURR; ZAJJAJ+]
- 18:39/q09/p2 Quwwatun may be nominative as a subject: no one has any power except through God. — Al-Zajjāj permits it; Ibn al-Jawzī supplies billāh as predicate and applies it to bodily and material strength. [2 notes: ZAJJAJ; IBNJAWZI-ZAD]

## 18:39/q10 What power does lā quwwata illā billāh deny to creatures?  [لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ · theology]
- 18:39/q10/p1 All creaturely ability depends on God's aid and never operates independently. — Muqātil and al-Wāḥidī apply it to the owner's garden; al-Qurṭubī says God could remove its bounty. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Nasafī, al-Burṣawī and al-Ālūsī apply it to cultivation and management. Ibn ʿĀshūr says God-given means and tools enable making it; al-Ṭabāṭabāʾ cites 2:165. Al-Biqāʿī uses it against independent natural properties. Al-Ṭabarī and Sahl apply it to obedience; Sahl also to safety and a good end. Abū Isḥāq and al-Baghawī apply it to preserving goods and warding harm. Ibn Masʿūd and Ibn ʿAbbās distinguish God's protection from sin and aid for obedience; Zuhayr speaks of attaining what is loved and avoiding what is disliked. [40 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM; TUSTARI; TAB-FULL; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق); WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL; TABRISI; RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; SAMIN-DURR; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; BIQAI-FULL; DURR-FULL(ابن عباس,ابن مسعود,زهير بن محمد); BURSEVI(النبي عليه السلام); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI; STUDYQURAN; BUKHARI(أبو موسى الأشعري); MUSLIM(أبو موسى الأشعري,رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم)]

## 18:39/q11 What reports describe the formula's spiritual merit?  [لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: The reports ascribe several distinct merits, and sometimes quote different lengths of the formula.
- 18:39/q11/p1 It is a treasure of Paradise, sometimes specified as beneath the Throne. — Abū Hurayra distinguishes the verse's exact lā quwwata wording from lā ḥawla wa-lā quwwata; Aḥmad, Ibn Kathīr and al-Ālūsī transmit it. Abū Mūsā's sound report uses the longer lā ḥawla formula, sometimes adding al-ʿAliyy al-ʿAẓīm; Muslim records wording variants. Further reports come from Abū Dharr, Abū Umāma, Abū Ayyūb, Zayd and Abū Hurayra. [16 notes: MUSNAD-AHMAD(أبو هريرة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(أبو موسى,أبو هريرة); QURTUBI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(النبي); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو هريرة,النبي); DURR-FULL(أبو أمامة,أبو أيوب الأنصاري,أبو ذر,أبو هريرة,زيد بن ثابت); ALUSI-FULL(أبو هريرة); STUDYQURAN(the Prophet)]
- 18:39/q11/p2 It is a gate of Paradise. — Reports from Muʿādh and Qays ibn Saʿd transmit the Prophet's description; Qays had served him. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(قيس بن سعد بن عبادة,معاذ بن جبل)]
- 18:39/q11/p3 It remedies ninety-nine ailments, of which worry is the least. — Abū Hurayra transmits the report; al-Burṣawī repeats it. [2 notes: DURR-FULL(أبو هريرة); BURSEVI(رُوي)]
- 18:39/q11/p4 Angels utter the longer formula, and the shorter formula drives away devils. — Ṣafwān says no angel ascends without lā ḥawla; Yaḥyā ibn Sulaym reports angels use mā shāʾ Allāh against devils overhearing heaven. Jarīr's spirit story says his repeated formula drove it off until it shrank to a fly. [5 notes: DURR-FULL(جرير,صفوان بن سليم,يحيى بن سليم الثقفي); QURTUBI-FULL]
- 18:39/q11/p5 Mā shāʾ Allāh is among the best and most successful supplications. — ʿUmar ibn Murra praises it; Ibrāhīm ibn Adham says no petition succeeds more; Moses reportedly received his delayed request after saying it and was told its efficacy. [4 notes: DURR-FULL(إبراهيم بن أدهم,عمر بن مرة,موسى); ALUSI-FULL(عمرو بن مرة)]

## 18:39/q12 Does saying the invocation protect an admired blessing?  [مَا شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِٱللَّهِ · reports]
turns on: The report's protection claim and al-Azdī's judgment on one transmission route.
- 18:39/q12/p1 Yes: it protects what one admires or receives from harm, calamity or the evil eye, at least until death. — Anas reports versions concerning family, wealth, children and property; al-Thaʿlabī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Durr, al-Itqān and al-Ālūsī transmit them. The Burṣawī records a version about another person's blessing. ʿUqba ibn ʿĀmir cites the garden verse for preserving blessings. [13 notes: THALABI(رسول الله); BAYDAWI-FULL(النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم); IBNKATHIR-FULL(النبي); DURR-FULL(أنس,عقبة بن عامر); ITQAN(أنس); BURSEVI(الحديث); ALUSI-FULL(أنس); IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الفتح الأزدي)-]
- 18:39/q12/p2 The Anas protection report through ʿĪsā ibn ʿAwn and ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Zurāra is unsound. — Abū al-Fatḥ al-Azdī rejects that route. [1 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL(أبو الفتح الأزدي)]

## 18:39/q13 What is the syntax of in taranī and its response?  [إِن تَرَنِ أَنَا۠ أَقَلَّ · grammar]
turns on: The verb's sense controls whether aqalla is a second object or a circumstantial accusative.
- 18:39/q13/p1 The conditional verb is jussive and fa-ʿasā in the next verse answers the condition. — Al-Qurṭubī gives this analysis; al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn identify fa-ʿasā as answer. Al-Itqān explains that fāʾ introduces a response that could not itself serve as a condition, illustrating the rule with other verses. [4 notes: QURTUBI-FULL; TABRISI; SAMIN-DURR; ITQAN]
- 18:39/q13/p2 Taranī can mean cognitive seeing and take two objects, or visual seeing with aqalla as a circumstantial phrase. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī give both senses; al-Samīn says a separator requires cognitive seeing, whereas visual seeing allows anā only as emphasis. Al-Burṣawī likewise distinguishes the two. [11 notes: SIBAWAYH; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور,عيسى بن عمر); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر)]

## 18:39/q14 How is the final yāʾ of taranī read?  [تَرَنِ · readings]
turns on: Readers and routes differ on retaining or omitting the final yāʾ in connection and at pause.
- 18:39/q14/p1 It is retained in both connected and paused recitation. — Ibn Kathīr is consistently named, with Yaʿqūb in al-Nashr; al-Fārisī calls retention the original analogical form. Other listed Kahf words accompany this pattern. [5 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن كثير); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن كثير); NASHR(أبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وقالون والأصبهاني عن ورش، ابن كثير ويعقوب,ابن كثير ويعقوب)]
- 18:39/q14/p2 It is retained in connection and omitted at pause. — Nāfiʿ and Abū ʿAmr are named by early sources; al-Nashr specifies Abū Jaʿfar, Qālūn and al-Aṣbahānī from Warsh, and excludes al-Azraq from a broader group. The ending resembles verse-rhyme; broader lists of additional and annexation yāʾs give the classification and exceptions. [13 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(نافع وأبو عمرو); IBNATIYYA-FULL(نافع وأبو عمرو); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(نافع وأبو عمرو); NASHR(أبو جعفر وأبو عمرو وقالون والأصبهاني عن ورش، ابن كثير ويعقوب,الخمسة المذكورون سوى الأزرق عن ورش,الكسائي,المدنيان، أبو عمرو والكسائي، ابن كثير ويعقوب,المدنيان، ابن كثير، أبو عمرو,نافع وأبو عمرو وحمزة والكسائي وأبو جعفر,نافع، ابن كثير، أبو عمرو، أبو جعفر، يعقوب)]
- 18:39/q14/p3 It is omitted in both connected and paused recitation. — Ibn ʿĀmir and ʿĀṣim are consistently named; early lists also name Ḥamza, while al-Nashr's general list names Khalaf. Al-Fārisī compares non-rhyme omission at 11:105. [6 notes: FARISI-HUJJA(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة); IBNATIYYA-FULL(ابن كثير); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن عامر وعاصم وحمزة); NASHR(ابن عامر وعاصم وخلف); SAMARRAI-ASRAR; QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:39/q14/p4 Retaining yāʾ lengthens and emphasizes the moment of seeing; dropping it in pause clips and emphasizes the conditional. — The recitation-effects notes assign opposite prosodic effects to retention and pausal omission. [2 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 18:39/q15 Why can taranī lose its yāʾ?  [تَرَنِ · grammar]
- 18:39/q15/p1 The yāʾ is the underlying pronoun, but the preceding kasra indicates it when deleted. — Al-Naḥḥās and al-Qurṭubī say preserving it is the good original form because nūn is secondary; Ibn ʿĀshūr calls deletion for ease common. Al-Sāmarrāʾī compares other Kahf deletions at 18:40, 18:64, 18:66 and 18:17. [5 notes: NAHHAS; QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); IBNASHUR-FULL; SAMARRAI-ASRAR; BURSEVI]

## 18:39/q16 How is anā aqalla parsed and read?  [أَنَا۠ أَقَلَّ · grammar]
turns on: Accusative versus nominative aqall, and whether anā is emphasis, separation, or a true subject.
- 18:39/q16/p1 With accusative aqalla, anā reinforces the object pronoun or separates it from the second object; aqalla is the second object. — This is the majority reading and al-Ṭabarī's choice. Sībawayh allows separation or emphasis but rejects an adjectival pronoun with an overt noun; he compares verbs of judgment, transformation and predication and explains when the separator cannot be used. Al-Iṣlāḥī calls it an auxiliary element between objects; al-Itqān says separation marks predication, emphasis and possible restriction, citing 2:5. Al-Burhān distinguishes it from a pronoun of affair and defends grammatical emphasis against Ibn al-Ḥājib; al-Kisāʾī's allocation of emphasis to individual sentence parts is criticized. Al-Jishumī paraphrases the construction as in taranī aqalla and calls anā an addition after in. [40 notes: SIBAWAYH(ناس); FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); BAGHAWI-FULL; KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(الجمهور); TABRISI(الزجاج); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BURHAN(ابن الحاجب,الكسائي,بعضهم,سيبويه); ITQAN(الزمخشري); ALUSI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; ISLAHI-TADABBUR; JISHUMI; TAB-FULL+]
- 18:39/q16/p2 With accusative aqalla, anā emphasizes the first-person object and aqalla may be a circumstance if seeing is visual. — Abū Ḥayyān and al-Ālūsī state the visual-seeing alternative; al-Samīn says separation itself is then impossible. Al-Burṣawī derives the form from an underlying taranī. [7 notes: THALABI; NAHHAS; TABRISI(الزجاج); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور); SAMIN-DURR; BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL]
- 18:39/q16/p3 With nominative aqallu, anā begins a nominal clause, with aqallu its predicate and the clause the second object. — ʿĪsā ibn ʿUmar and Ibn Abī ʿAbla transmit it; al-Kisāʾī is also named. Sībawayh records many Arabs and Ruʾba using a parallel raised-clause analysis, and supports it with Abū ʿAmr's wording, a hadith about parents, and poetry of Qays ibn Dhurayḥ and Banū ʿAbs. He says a changed referent requires a real nominal clause. Al-Farrāʾ and al-Zajjāj allow this parse; several commentators say the verb denotes knowledge here. [24 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو عمرو,رؤبة); FARRA; TAB-FULL; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(عيسى بن عمر); WAHIDI-BASIT(أبو إسحاق,الفراء); KASHSHAF-FULL; IBNATIYYA-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); TABRISI(عيسى بن عمر); IBNJAWZI-ZAD(ابن أبي عبلة); RAZI-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); NASAFI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL(عيسى بن عمر); SAMIN-DURR(عيسى بن عمر); ALUSI-FULL(عيسى بن عمر)]
- 18:39/q16/p4 Anā can be analyzed as an adjective or emphatic noun in a minority grammatical account. — Sībawayh reports the adjective analysis but rejects it because Arabs do not use it with an overt noun; al-Thaʿlabī calls it nominative subject emphasis. ## 18:39/q17 How can a raised nominal clause follow a predicate-taking verb?  [أَنَا۠ أَقَلَّ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the pronoun is an independent subject or a sentence-internal separator.
- 18:39/q17/p1 The pronoun can begin a genuine nominal clause, which the verb then takes as its predicate or object. — Sībawayh cites Ruʾba, Abū ʿAmr, Qays ibn Dhurayḥ, a hadith and two poems, and says removal of a true subject pronoun can change the meaning. He contrasts separators with verbs requiring a following predicate and with mere circumstantial phrases. [10 notes: SIBAWAYH(أبو عمرو,رؤبة)]
- 18:39/q17/p2 A separator leaves the following predicate's case unchanged, unlike a nominal-clause subject. — Sībawayh includes inna and its sisters, cites the proverb man kadhiba kāna sharran lahu, and excludes adjectival use with overt nouns. Al-Itqān explains matching the antecedent in person and number. [5 notes: SIBAWAYH(ناس); ITQAN]

## 18:39/q18 What effect do short and extended anā have in recitation?  [أَنَا۠ · readings]
turns on: The length of the alif changes the reported rhetorical effect.
- 18:39/q18/p1 A short alif makes the pronoun minimal and may lessen its emphatic or separating force. — The recitation-effects note interprets the shortened pronunciation this way. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]
- 18:39/q18/p2 Extended madd lengthens anā and strengthens its emphatic or separating force. — The recitation-effects note interprets lengthening this way. [1 notes: QIRAAT-ER]

## 18:39/q19 What is the case of mālan wa waladan?  [مَالًۭا وَوَلَدًۭا · grammar]
- 18:39/q19/p1 Both are accusative specifications of the respect in which the speaker is less. — Al-Samīn and al-Ālūsī explicitly call both tamyīz; al-Ṭabrisī also mentions specification in the following verse. [3 notes: SAMIN-DURR; ALUSI-FULL; TABRISI]

## 18:39/q20 What does walad include here?  [وَوَلَدًۭا · meaning]
- 18:39/q20/p1 It can mean offspring of either sex, one or several; here it can denote several children. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī cites this verse as an example of plural use. The narrative sources speak of family, dependents and children. [4 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT; MUQATIL; TABRISI; BIQAI-FULL]

## 18:39/q21 Who speaks the comparison, and what is his worldly position?  [إِن تَرَنِ أَنَا۠ أَقَلَّ مِنكَ مَالًۭا وَوَلَدًۭا · referent]
- 18:39/q21/p1 The believing companion addresses the wealthy garden owner and concedes having less wealth and children. — Al-Ṭabarī compares him to Salmān, Ṣuhayb and Khabbāb; Ibn ʿAbbās says he first turned back to himself. Abū Ḥayyān infers that he still had some wealth and followers, against accounts depicting a destitute petitioner. Al-Biqāʿī calls children one's dearest dependents. The believer hopes God may give him a better garden and reverse their fortunes. ## 18:39/q23 How does the believer's reply relate to the owner's boast and the surrounding verses?  [* · links]
- 18:39/q23/p1 It answers the boast of superior wealth and supporters with their reversibility and a better outcome for faith. — The owner had assumed his garden would never perish and doubted the Hour; the believer anticipates a better garden and the other's loss. Ibn ʿĀshūr links it to 18:34; al-Biqāʿī relates remembering God's will to 18:23. Al-Jishumī says worldly enjoyment is transient and each side receives its due; conjecture is exposed as false. [14 notes: THALABI; WAHIDI-WAJIZ; JISHUMI; KASHSHAF-FULL; RAZI-FULL; ABUHAYYAN-FULL; BIQAI-FULL; IBNASHUR-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 18:39/q24 What inward meaning is drawn from entering a garden?  [دَخَلْتَ جَنَّتَكَ · inward]
- 18:39/q24/p1 The believing heart is God's garden and throne of knowledge; it must be cleared of Satanic whispering by seeking God's protection. — Al-Rāzī contrasts the heart's nobility with earthly gardens, cites 35:10 and 54:55, and invokes the saying that a righteous person's grave is a garden. In his imagined dialogue the servant admits neglecting God's garden while entering his own. The people of inward allusion say divine knowledge is its king. [7 notes: RAZI-FULL(أهل الإشارة,النبي)]

## 18:39/q25 What other illustrative material is attached to discussions of this verse?  [* · reports]
- 18:39/q25/p1 Some sources use wider examples about obedience, human servanthood, oaths, vocatives and nearby wording. — Abū Bakr's address conditions obedience to him on obedience to God; ʿUmar's transmitted sermon treats stoning and allegiance, while Abū Ḥātim explains a phrase about Saʿd. A Prophetic report forbids excessive praise. Abū Mūsā's camel-oath reports attribute provision to God and teach expiation for the better course. Sībawayh's vocative examples range over tribal boasts and poems, highlighting address, identification and ellipsis. Al-Jishumī discusses the neighboring lakinnā and its readings; al-Ṭabrisī notes the nearby ghawran form. Al-Itqān's reading survey includes qad, hal, bal and nearby articulation cases. Al-Sāmarrāʾī's introduction and counsel to Abū Dharr, and his comparison of Zechariah's and Mary's wording, are likewise included as surrounding source material. ## 18:39/q26 What outlook does acknowledging God's will cultivate?  [* · interpretation]
- 18:39/q26/p1 One sees every blessing as dependent on God's will and thanks Him instead of claiming personal permanence. — Al-Zamakhsharī says the garden's existence and every good in it depend on God's will and grace; God can preserve or ruin it. Al-Nasafī and al-Ālūsī concur. Sahl connects divine will with God's pre-eternal knowledge, known fully only to Him. [4 notes: KASHSHAF-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL; TUSTARI]
- 18:39/q26/p2 One treats the afterlife as the foremost concern rather than trusting worldly enjoyment. — Al-Jishumī makes this the accountable person's priority. [1 notes: JISHUMI]
