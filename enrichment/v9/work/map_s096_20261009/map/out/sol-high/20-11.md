# 20:11: 28 questions

## 20:11/q01 Does faʾ require the call to follow Moses' arrival without an interval?  [فَلَمَّآ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the sequential faʾ permits the short interval related in reports.
- 20:11/q01/p1 A brief interval may have passed before the call. — Al-Rāzī says the sequential faʾ does not preclude a short interval. Al-Ālūsī says the interval in the transmitted accounts does not defeat them. [2 notes: RAZI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL+; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المعتزلة)-]
- 20:11/q01/p2 The verse's apparent immediate sequence excludes reports of an interval. — Some Muʿtazilites appeal to the apparent sequence of arrival and call. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:11/q02 Why is Moses' arrival expressed with atā here?  [أَتَىٰهَا · rhetoric]
- 20:11/q02/p1 It fits the sūra's preference for atyan over majiʾ. — Al-Sāmarrāʾī counts more than fifteen uses of atyan and four of majiʾ in Ṭā Hā, against eight of majiʾ and thirteen of atyan in al-Naml; compare 27:8. [1 notes: SAMARRAI-LAMASAT-HALAQAT]

## 20:11/q03 What does the pronoun in atāhā refer to?  [أَتَىٰهَا · referent]
turns on: Whether the feminine pronoun points back to the seen fire or to the tree at the call.
- 20:11/q03/p1 The fire Moses had seen or thought he saw. — Yaḥyā says it was what Moses thought was fire; al-Māwardī calls it fire that was light. Ibn al-Jawzī and al-Qurṭubī explicitly identify the feminine pronoun with fire. Study Quran cites 20:10 and reports a version in which the fire consumed the tree. [7 notes: YAHYA-SALLAM; MAWARDI-FULL; IBNJAWZI-ZAD; QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭ, Bg, R); BAYDAWI-FULL; IBNKATHIR-FULL]
- 20:11/q03/p2 The tree from which God called Moses. — Study Quran reports this interpretation from JJ. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(JJ)]

## 20:11/q04 Where did Moses arrive?  [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
- 20:11/q04/p1 At the valley named Tuwa, the purified valley. — Muqātil gives Tuwa as the valley's name and explains it as 'purified.' [1 notes: MUQATIL]

## 20:11/q05 What kind of tree held the apparent fire?  [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
turns on: The reports identify the tree by different species; al-Būrsawī says the listed trees do not contain fire like other trees.
- 20:11/q05/p1 A jujube tree. — Ibn ʿAbbās reports a jujube in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ālūsī; al-Ṭabrisī reports him likewise. Al-Nasafī offers jujube or osage; al-Būrsawī lists jujube among several identifications. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس)]
- 20:11/q05/p2 An ʿawsaj or osage tree. — Al-Nasafī allows osage; al-Būrsawī lists ʿawsaja; al-Ālūsī reports awsj. [3 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم)]
- 20:11/q05/p3 A samura tree. — Al-Būrsawī lists green samura; al-Ālūsī transmits it from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd. [2 notes: BURSEVI(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(عبد الله بن مسعود)]
- 20:11/q05/p4 An al-ʿalīq or blackberry-like thorn tree. — Al-Būrsawī's list describes a blackberry-like thorn tree; al-Ālūsī's Wahb report calls the great green tree al-ʿalīq. These descriptions vary. [2 notes: BURSEVI(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منية)]

## 20:11/q06 What did the fire and tree look like together?  [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
turns on: Whether the fire preserved the green tree or consumed it.
- 20:11/q06/p1 Brilliant white fire or light appeared in a green tree without consuming it or being dimmed by its moisture. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabrisī, al-Rāzī, and al-Būrsawī describes the bright flame and living green tree; al-Rāzī says the glow ran bottom to top. Al-Bayḍāwī and al-Nasafī describe white flame in green growth. Wahb in al-Ālūsī says the great flame grew without burning the tree. [7 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس); RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BAYDAWI-FULL; NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منية)]
- 20:11/q06/p2 The fire consumed the tree. — Study Quran reports this interpretation from IK, R, Ṭ, Bg, and R, with reference to 20:10. [1 notes: STUDYQURAN(IK, R, Ṭ, Bg, R)]

## 20:11/q07 How did the apparent fire behave as Moses approached?  [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
- 20:11/q07/p1 It withdrew as he approached and came toward him when he drew back or stopped seeking it. — Wahb in al-Ṭabarī says Moses feared this motion. Al-Rāzī's Wahb report adds that Moses carried dry sticks, the fire repeatedly lured him, then vanished as a branch and dazzling light shone skyward. Al-Nasafī reports the reciprocal movement. Al-Ālūsī's Wahb report has repeated advance and retreat, a pillar of light reaching the sky, Moses' fear, angelic praise, and calm; it states no grading. [4 notes: TAB-FULL(وهب بن منبه); RAZI-FULL(وهب); NASAFI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منية)]

## 20:11/q08 What else did Moses perceive at the fire?  [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
- 20:11/q08/p1 He saw intense light, heard angelic praise, and was awed before being calmed. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Rāzī reports great light and angels glorifying God. Al-Būrsawī adds light too intense for the eyes, Moses covering his eyes, fear and astonishment, and subsequent calm. Wahb in al-Ālūsī also culminates his report in angelic praise and calm. [3 notes: RAZI-FULL(ابن عباس); BURSEVI; ALUSI-FULL(وهب بن منية)]
- 20:11/q08/p2 He found no person beside the fire. — Al-Nasafī and Ibn ʿAbbās as reported by al-Būrsawī say no one was there. [2 notes: NASAFI-FULL; BURSEVI(ابن عباس)]

## 20:11/q09 Was the phenomenon real fire or light?  [أَتَىٰهَا · interpretation]
turns on: Whether the visible phenomenon was actual veiling fire or divine light perceived and named as fire.
- 20:11/q09/p1 It was light, called fire because Moses perceived it so. — Al-Māwardī describes the fire as light. Al-Ālūsī reports Ibn ʿAbbās calling it light from God's light and Abū Ḥayyān preferring light to actual fire; he says al-Māwardī also adopts that view. [3 notes: MAWARDI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(ابن عباس); ALUSI-FULL(أبو حيان)+]
- 20:11/q09/p2 It was fire itself and one of God's veils. — Al-Ālūsī reports Saʿīd ibn Jubayr. He also cites Abū Mūsā's Prophetic report that God's veil is fire and its unveiling would burn creatures reached by His face's radiance; al-Baghawī cites it and al-Khāzin says Muslim recorded it. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(النبي,سعيد بن جبير)]

## 20:11/q10 How is Moses' fire classified by eating and drinking?  [أَتَىٰهَا · interpretation]
- 20:11/q10/p1 It neither eats nor drinks. — Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī report a fourfold scheme: ordinary fire eats but does not drink, green-tree fire drinks but does not eat, Hellfire does both, and Moses' fire does neither. [2 notes: BURSEVI(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(قالوا)]

## 20:11/q11 How is Moses' fire classified by light and burning?  [أَتَىٰهَا · interpretation]
- 20:11/q11/p1 It gives light without burning. — Al-Būrsawī and al-Ālūsī report a fourfold contrast: ordinary fire has both light and burning, Hellfire burns without light, and Moses' fire shines without burning. Al-Būrsawī says trees have neither; al-Ālūsī specifies tree fire as neither. [2 notes: BURSEVI(قالوا); ALUSI-FULL(قالوا)]

## 20:11/q12 What happened with Moses' sandals when he arrived?  [أَتَىٰهَا · reports]
- 20:11/q12/p1 He removed sandals made from the hide of an unritually dead donkey. — Muqātil says God ordered their removal at Tuwa; Qatāda as reported by Yaḥyā says Moses removed them and then approached. [2 notes: MUQATIL; YAHYA-SALLAM(قتادة)]

## 20:11/q13 What does nūdiya mean here?  [نُودِىَ · meaning]
- 20:11/q13/p1 He was told or addressed. — Al-Naḥḥās uses 'he was told' to explain the variant readings of yā Mūsā annī. Al-Nasafī and Asad give the literal passive sense 'he was called.' [3 notes: NAHHAS; NASAFI-FULL; ASAD-NOTES]

## 20:11/q14 What is the passive subject of nūdiya?  [نُودِىَ · grammar]
turns on: Whether the passive promotes Moses, an understood verbal noun, or the quoted clause; the last is challenged because a clause cannot be the subject.
- 20:11/q14/p1 Its implicit subject is Moses. — Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī allows Moses because he was already mentioned. Al-Samīn prefers this, as does al-Ālūsī as the apparent reading. [3 notes: TABRISI(أبو علي); ALUSI-FULL+; SAMIN-DURR+]
- 20:11/q14/p2 An implicit verbal noun is the subject: the calling occurred. — Al-Ālūsī reports this proposal; al-Samīn calls the equivalent 'the call was called' weak. Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī also allows an implicit pronoun rather than the quoted clause. [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI(أبو علي); SAMIN-DURR-]
- 20:11/q14/p3 The quoted words yā Mūsā function as the subject. — Al-Ālūsī reports that the call is taken to include speech and the quoted wording to be its intended message. Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī and al-Samīn object that a clause cannot be a passive subject. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعضهم); TABRISI(أبو علي)-; SAMIN-DURR-]

## 20:11/q15 How is the quoted address attached to nūdiya?  [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · grammar]
turns on: Whether 'called' itself introduces speech or an omitted verb of saying is supplied.
- 20:11/q15/p1 Calling itself has the force of saying, so the words are the call's speech. — Al-Ālūsī attributes this construction to the Kūfans. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكوفيون)]
- 20:11/q15/p2 An unspoken verb 'he was told' introduces the quoted address. — Al-Ālūsī attributes the omitted saying verb to the Basrans: Moses was called and told 'O Moses.' Al-Naḥḥās glosses nūdiya as 'he was told.' [2 notes: ALUSI-FULL(الكوفيون); NAHHAS]

## 20:11/q16 What effect does the passive wording of nūdiya have?  [نُودِىَ · rhetoric]
- 20:11/q16/p1 Concealing the caller makes the summons greater when Moses is named. — Al-Biqāʿī points to the sequence from an unnamed caller to Moses' explicit name. [1 notes: BIQAI-FULL]
- 20:11/q16/p2 Omitting the agent subtly suggests the sign appeared suddenly to Moses. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī links the passive's omission to the sudden appearance of the sign. [1 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:11/q17 Who produced the call to Moses?  [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether God's address was unmediated or made audible through light.
- 20:11/q17/p1 God addressed Moses directly and alone. — Al-Māwardī reports this view. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]
- 20:11/q17/p2 God made His inseparable light voice the call. — Al-Māwardī reports that the light spoke to calm Moses enough to bear God's command. [1 notes: MAWARDI-FULL]

## 20:11/q18 From where was the call heard?  [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · reports]
- 20:11/q18/p1 From the tree or its vicinity. — Ibn ʿAbbās in al-Wāḥidī and al-Ṭabrisī says Moses heard the tree call; Wahb in al-Ṭabrisī says Moses heard a voice without seeing its place. Al-Qurṭubī specifies the tree's direction and vicinity and compares Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ. Study Quran reports God called from a tree in the valley. [5 notes: WAHIDI-BASIT(ابن عباس); TABRISI(ابن عباس,وهب); QURTUBI-FULL; STUDYQURAN(Q, Ṭ)]

## 20:11/q19 How did Moses know the caller was his Lord?  [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · theology]
turns on: Whether recognition at the first revelation requires evidentiary inference or is immediate in the experience of revelation.
- 20:11/q19/p1 He recognized God by a miracle or divinely created knowledge. — Wahb in al-Ṭabrisī says Moses heard but could not locate the voice and knew the caller by the response and a miracle. Abū Ḥayyān allows knowledge created by God or a miracle; he says Muʿtazilites require miraculous proof because necessary knowledge would conflict with accountability. [2 notes: TABRISI(وهب); ABUHAYYAN-FULL]
- 20:11/q19/p2 Revelation gave immediate certainty of the divine speaker. — Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī says the addressee apprehends no other speaker, citing the Quran's account of divine communication at 42:51. A prophet's first reception of mission must be immediate; acquired rational proof would not be reception from the unseen. He says an unseen veil or intermediary is compatible with this experience, citing 19:52, 28:30, and 20:13. [3 notes: TABATABAI]

## 20:11/q20 How is the marvel before Moses' commission classified?  [نُودِىَ · theology]
turns on: Whether a marvel may precede Moses' prophetic commission as his own precursor miracle.
- 20:11/q20/p1 It is an irhāṣ, a precursor miracle. — Al-Ālūsī classifies the pre-prophetic marvel this way and says Muʿtazilites reject it. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL; ALUSI-FULL(بعض المعتزلة)-]
- 20:11/q20/p2 A miracle cannot occur before Moses' commission unless it belongs to another prophet. — Al-Ālūsī reports this Muʿtazilite objection. [1 notes: ALUSI-FULL(بعض المعتزلة); ALUSI-FULL-]

## 20:11/q21 How is annī or innī in the following divine speech read and construed?  [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · readings]
turns on: Whether the hamza has fatḥa and attaches the following clause to the call, or kasra and starts quoted speech; reports differ on the named readers and nuances of attachment.
- 20:11/q21/p1 With fatḥa, annī: he was called with or because of the words 'I am your Lord.' — Al-Zajjāj and al-Naḥḥās take an to be accusative; Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī says calling can take a preposition. Al-Jishumī glosses 'because I am your Lord.' Named readers vary: al-Naḥḥās includes al-Ḥasan, Abū Jaʿfar, Abū ʿAmr; al-Jishumī and al-Ṭabrisī list Abū Jaʿfar, Ibn Kathīr, Abū ʿAmr; al-Kashshāf lists Abū ʿAmr and Ibn Kathīr. Abū Ḥayyān gives Ibn Kathīr and Abū ʿAmr's wa-annī. [8 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS(الحسن وأبو جعفر وأبو عمرو); JISHUMI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن كثير); TABRISI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو عمرو,أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]
- 20:11/q21/p2 With kasra, innī begins direct quoted speech: 'O Moses, I am your Lord.' — Al-Zajjāj, al-Jishumī, al-Kashshāf, Abū ʿAlī in al-Ṭabrisī, and Abū Ḥayyān describe a fresh quotation or supplied saying verb; Abū Ḥayyān calls it the majority reading. Al-Farrāʾ allows the call to include annī and Mūsā. Al-Naḥḥās reports an interpretation in which Moses says 'I am.' [8 notes: FARRA; ZAJJAJ; NAHHAS; JISHUMI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو عمرو); KASHSHAF-FULL(أبو عمرو وابن كثير); TABRISI(أبو جعفر وابن كثير وأبو عمرو,أبو علي); ABUHAYYAN-FULL(الجمهور)]

## 20:11/q22 Is the final yāʾ of innī opened or left still?  [نُودِىَ يَٰمُوسَىٰٓ · readings]
turns on: The reported recitation of the final yāʾ in the following innī.
- 20:11/q22/p1 Abū Jaʿfar, Nāfiʿ, Ibn Kathīr, and Abū ʿAmr open the final yāʾ. — Al-Jishumī gives this reader list. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو)]
- 20:11/q22/p2 The remaining readers leave the final yāʾ quiescent. — Al-Jishumī contrasts them with the four who open it. [1 notes: JISHUMI(أبو جعفر ونافع وابن كثير وأبو عمرو)]

## 20:11/q23 How does this call connect with other accounts of Moses' call?  [* · links]
- 20:11/q23/p1 It is the call from the blessed spot at the valley's right side and from the tree. — Ibn Kathīr compares 28:30. Al-Qurṭubī also refers to Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ for the direction of the call. Al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī cites 19:52, 28:30, and 20:13 to say an intermediary or veil can accompany divine revelation. [3 notes: IBNKATHIR-FULL; QURTUBI-FULL; TABATABAI]

## 20:11/q24 What inward significance is given to Moses' encounter and call?  [* · inward]
- 20:11/q24/p1 The divine call enveloped Moses and overwhelmed his selfhood; God alone bears and answers His own address. — Jaʿfar in al-Sulamī says every hair perceived the call from every direction, awe and majesty confirmed it, and God's self-identification left Moses stunned and annihilated as an intermediary locus. [1 notes: SULAMI(جعفر)]
- 20:11/q24/p2 The fire is a veil of majesty through which God's manifestation addresses Moses. — Al-Qāshānī says Moses connects with the fire and is called beyond fiery curtains; 'I am your Lord' is the divine manifestation in fiery form. [1 notes: QASHANI]
- 20:11/q24/p3 Fire figures ardent love and its light dissolves Moses' selfhood into single-hearted orientation. — Al-Būrsawī distinguishes light as love from fire as longing: their fullness lets essential light appear as attribute-fire and burn away selfhood. [1 notes: BURSEVI]

## 20:11/q25 What do qabas and miqbās mean in the preceding request for fire?  [* · meaning]
- 20:11/q25/p1 Qabas is a flame; miqbās is a firebrand or tool for taking fire. — Al-Qurṭubī supplies these lexical meanings in the discussion attached to the call. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:11/q26 Can verbs from qabas describe taking both fire and knowledge?  [* · meaning]
turns on: Whether the two verbal forms keep fire and knowledge distinct or both extend to either.
- 20:11/q26/p1 The uses are distinguished: qabastu is for fire, while aqbastu someone knowledge is for seeking knowledge for that person. — Al-Yazīdī as reported by al-Qurṭubī draws this distinction. Al-Qurṭubī also says qabistu and iqtibās can mean taking fire, while iqtibās can mean acquiring knowledge. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(اليزيدي)]
- 20:11/q26/p2 Both aqbastuhu and qabastuhu can apply to fire or knowledge. — Al-Kisāʾī as reported by al-Qurṭubī permits both verbs for either object. Al-Qurṭubī also notes iqtibās can refer to fire-taking or knowledge acquisition. [2 notes: QURTUBI-FULL(الكسائي)]

## 20:11/q27 What does hudā mean in the preceding search?  [* · meaning]
- 20:11/q27/p1 It means hādiyan, a guide. — Al-Qurṭubī supplies the gloss. [1 notes: QURTUBI-FULL]

## 20:11/q28 What view of divine istiwāʾ is noted in the preceding discussion?  [* · theology]
turns on: Whether istiwāʾ is understood as power or as a distinct act or attribute; al-Jishumī says the latter is unintelligible.
- 20:11/q28/p1 It means power. — Al-Jishumī prefers Abū ʿAlī's view as sounder and finds the Kullābī account unintelligible. [1 notes: JISHUMI+]
- 20:11/q28/p2 It is an act or attribute called istiwāʾ. — Al-Jishumī attributes this to the Kullābīs and calls it unintelligible. [0 notes: JISHUMI-]
